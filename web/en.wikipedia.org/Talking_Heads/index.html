Talking Heads

Talking Heads were an American rock band formed in 1975 in New York City, comprising David Byrne (lead vocals, guitar), Chris Frantz (drums), Tina Weymouth (bass), and Jerry Harrison (keyboards, guitar). Described as "one of the most critically acclaimed bands of the 1980s", the group helped pioneer new wave music by combining elements of punk, art rock, funk, and world music with an anxious, art-school sensibility.

History

Byrne, Frantz, and Weymouth met at the Rhode Island School of Design and moved to New York, where they became fixtures at CBGB alongside the Ramones and Patti Smith. Harrison, formerly of the Modern Lovers, joined in 1977, the year of their debut album Talking Heads: 77, which included "Psycho Killer". Working with producer Brian Eno on More Songs About Buildings and Food (1978), Fear of Music (1979), and Remain in Light (1980), the band absorbed African polyrhythms — particularly the influence of Fela Kuti — and produced the hit single "Once in a Lifetime".

Speaking in Tongues (1983) yielded their only US top-ten single, "Burning Down the House", and the subsequent tour was filmed by Jonathan Demme as Stop Making Sense (1984), widely regarded as one of the finest concert films ever made. Little Creatures (1985) brought further hits ("And She Was", "Road to Nowhere"), followed by True Stories (1986) and the Afro-Cuban-influenced Naked (1988). The band effectively ended in 1991 without a formal announcement; Byrne, Frantz, and Weymouth continued in projects including Tom Tom Club and the Heads. Talking Heads were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2002, performing together for the occasion.

Legacy

Four of the band's albums appear on Rolling Stone's list of the 500 greatest albums of all time, and "Once in a Lifetime" and "This Must Be the Place" remain staples of alternative rock. Their influence extends across indie and dance music, and Stop Making Sense received a celebrated 4K restoration and theatrical re-release in 2023.
