Mary Mallon

Mary Mallon (1869–1938), known as Typhoid Mary, was an Irish-born American cook who was the first identified asymptomatic carrier of typhoid fever in the United States. Working for wealthy New York families, she is attributed with infecting at least 53 people, three of whom died.

Investigation

Sanitary engineer George Soper traced outbreaks to Mallon in 1907 and identified her as a healthy carrier of Salmonella typhi. She refused to believe she could spread a disease she never had, and resisted testing and examination.

Quarantine

Health authorities quarantined her on North Brother Island from 1907 to 1910, releasing her on condition she stop cooking. She resumed cooking under an assumed name, causing further outbreaks, and was quarantined again in 1915 for the remaining 23 years of her life. Her case became a landmark — and contested — example of public-health power over individual liberty.

Legacy

"Typhoid Mary" entered the language for anyone who unwittingly spreads disease or harm. Modern discussion weighs prejudice against an Irish immigrant servant alongside genuine epidemiological risk.
