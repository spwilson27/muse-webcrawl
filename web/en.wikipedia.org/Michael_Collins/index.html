Michael Collins

Born16 October 1890, Woodfield, County Cork
Died22 August 1922, Béal na Bláth, County Cork
Known forIrish revolutionary leader; Chairman of the Provisional Government

Michael Collins (Irish: Mícheál Ó Coileáin; 16 October 1890 – 22 August 1922) was an Irish revolutionary, soldier and politician who was a leading figure in the early-20th-century struggle for Irish independence. He directed intelligence operations during the Irish War of Independence, was Minister for Finance in the First Dáil, and negotiated the Anglo-Irish Treaty of 1921.

Career

Collins fought in the Easter Rising of 1916, was interned, and afterwards rebuilt the Irish Republican Brotherhood and Sinn Féin networks. As Director of Intelligence of the IRA he organised the "Squad" and outwitted British intelligence in Dublin. Sent to London in 1921, he signed the treaty establishing the Irish Free State, which he defended as "the freedom to achieve freedom".

Death

As Chairman of the Provisional Government and Commander-in-chief of the National Army, Collins was killed in an ambush by anti-Treaty forces at Béal na Bláth during the Irish Civil War, aged 31.
