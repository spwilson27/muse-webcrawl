Prison

A prison, also known as a jail, gaol, penitentiary, detention center, or correctional facility, is a facility where people are confined against their will and usually denied a range of freedoms under the authority of the state, generally as punishment for various crimes.

History

Imprisonment as a standard punishment developed in the early modern period, replacing corporal and capital punishments for many offences. The Pennsylvania and Auburn systems of the 19th-century United States pioneered rival models of solitary confinement and congregate labour.

Purposes

The stated aims of imprisonment include retribution, deterrence, incapacitation and rehabilitation, the balance among which varies by jurisdiction. Worldwide, more than 11 million people are held in penal institutions.
