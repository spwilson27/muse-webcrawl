The Isle of Mull (Scottish Gaelic: Muile) is the second-largest island of the Inner Hebrides, off the west coast of Scotland in the council area of Argyll and Bute. With an area of about 875 square kilometres, it is the fourth-largest island in Scotland and around Great Britain. Its population is roughly 2,800. The main town is Tobermory, famous for its brightly painted harbourfront buildings and as the setting of the children's television programme Balamory.

Geography

Mull has a coastline of about 480 kilometres, deeply indented by sea lochs including Loch na Keal, Loch Scridain, and Loch Buie. The island is mountainous at its core: Ben More, at 966 metres, is the highest peak and the only Munro on a Scottish island apart from those on Skye. The geology is dominated by Tertiary volcanic rocks — Mull was the site of an ancient volcano — with basalt columns visible at places such as the island of Staffa offshore, famed for Fingal's Cave.

History

Mull has been inhabited since around 6000 BC. Bronze Age remains include standing stones at Lochbuie. The island came under Norse influence, then passed into the Lordship of the Isles before Maclean and Campbell ascendancy in the later medieval period; Duart Castle, seat of Clan Maclean, overlooks the Sound of Mull. In 1588 a Spanish Armada galleon, the Florencia, was blown up in Tobermory Bay, generating enduring treasure legends. The 19th century saw clearances and emigration, followed by crofting reform.

Economy and tourism

Tourism is the mainstay of the economy, drawn by wildlife — Mull is one of the best places in Britain to see white-tailed eagles, golden eagles, otters, and marine life — and by scenery and heritage. Tobermory distillery (founded 1798) produces single malt whisky. The island is also a gateway to Iona, the small island off Mull's southwest tip from which St Columba spread Christianity in Scotland, reached by ferry from Fionnphort.

Transport

Caledonian MacBrayde ferries link Craignure on Mull with Oban on the mainland (the principal route), Fishnish with Lochaline, and Tobermory with Kilchoan on Ardnamurchan.
