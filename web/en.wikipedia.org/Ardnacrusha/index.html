Ardnacrusha

Location: County Clare, Ireland
Coordinates: near Parteen, River Shannon
Opened: 1929
Operator: ESB
Installed capacity: 86 MW

Ardnacrusha is a hydroelectric power station on the River Shannon near the village of Ardnacrusha in County Clare, Ireland, close to the border with Limerick. Built as the centrepiece of the Shannon hydroelectric scheme, it was the Irish Free State's flagship engineering project and, when it opened in 1929, supplied the great majority of the country's electricity.

The Shannon Scheme

The scheme, promoted by the young Free State government under W. T. Cosgrave and engineered with the German firm Siemens-Schuckert, diverted water from the Shannon at Parteen Villa weir along a purpose-built headrace canal to the power station at Ardnacrusha, exploiting a fall of about 30 metres between the Shannon and Lough Derg level and the lower river. The project cost roughly £5 million — a substantial share of the new state's annual budget — and employed some five thousand workers at its peak.

Power station

The station originally housed three vertical Francis turbines; a fourth Kaplan unit was added in 1934, bringing capacity to about 86 MW. For a time Ardnacrusha was among the largest hydroelectric stations in the world, and at its opening it could meet over 90 percent of Irish electricity demand. A salmon pass and an eel management programme address the scheme's effects on the river's fisheries, and the Ardnacrusha canal allows navigation between the upper and lower Shannon.

Heritage

The power station, with its distinctive turbine hall, remains in operation under the ESB and is a protected structure and a landmark of Ireland's industrial heritage; guided tours are offered, and the site received an international engineering heritage award from the American Society of Civil Engineers.
