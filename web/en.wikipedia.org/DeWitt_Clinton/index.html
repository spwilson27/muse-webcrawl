DeWitt Clinton (2 March 1769 – 11 February 1828) was an American politician and naturalist. He served as a United States senator, as mayor of New York City, and as the sixth governor of New York. In the last capacity, he was largely responsible for the construction of the Erie Canal, the great waterway linking the Hudson River with the Great Lakes.

Early life

Clinton was born in Little Britain, New York, the second son of Major-General James Clinton and Mary De Witt. He was educated at Columbia College and studied law, entering politics as secretary to his uncle, Governor George Clinton of New York.

Political career

Clinton served in the New York State Assembly and Senate before a brief term in the United States Senate (1802–1803). He was mayor of New York City for most of the period 1803–1815, during which he promoted public education, sanitation, and the arts, helping to found institutions including the New-York Historical Society. In the presidential election of 1812, Clinton ran as the candidate of a coalition of Federalists and dissident Democratic-Republicans against incumbent James Madison, narrowly losing. He served as governor of New York from 1817 to 1822 and again from 1825 until his death in 1828.

The Erie Canal

Clinton championed the construction of a canal from the Hudson River to Lake Erie as early as 1810, serving on the canal commission that surveyed the route. Derided by opponents as "Clinton's Ditch" and "Clinton's Folly," the 363-mile canal was completed in 1825. Clinton presided over its opening, travelling by canal boat from Buffalo to New York City and pouring a keg of Lake Erie water into the Atlantic in the "Wedding of the Waters" ceremony. The canal dramatically cut freight costs, spurred settlement of the interior, and cemented New York City's position as the nation's leading port.

Natural history

Beyond politics, Clinton was an amateur naturalist who wrote on the fishes and antiquities of New York and delivered a noted 1814 discourse on the state's natural history. Numerous places, schools, and the Clinton counties of several states bear his name.
