The Battle of the Coral Sea, fought from 4 to 8 May 1942, was a major naval battle in the Pacific Theater of World War II between the Imperial Japanese Navy and naval and air forces of the United States and Australia. It was the first battle in which aircraft carrier groups engaged one another, and the first naval battle fought entirely by aircraft, with the opposing ships never sighting or firing on each other directly.

Background

Japan planned Operation Mo, a seaborne invasion of Port Moresby in New Guinea, which would threaten Australia. Allied codebreakers learned of the plan, and two American carrier groups under Rear Admiral Frank Jack Fletcher were sent to intercept.

The battle

After Japanese forces took Tulagi in the Solomons, American aircraft sank the light carrier Shōhō on 7 May. On 8 May the main carrier forces found each other: Japanese planes badly damaged Yorktown and hit Lexington, which was later scuttled after gasoline explosions; American planes damaged Shōkaku and depleted Zuikaku's air group.

Aftermath

Although the Japanese inflicted heavier ship losses, the Port Moresby invasion was called off — the first Japanese offensive of the war to be turned back. Neither Shōkaku nor Zuikaku could take part in the Battle of Midway a month later, a consequence often counted among the decisive factors of that battle.
