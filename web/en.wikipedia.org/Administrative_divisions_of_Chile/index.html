The administrative divisions of Chile form a three-tier structure. Chile is a unitary state divided into 16 regions (regiones), each headed by a regional government; regions are subdivided into 56 provinces (provincias), and provinces into 346 communes (comunas), which are the basic unit of local administration, each governed by a municipality led by an elected mayor (alcalde) and council.

Regions

Regionalization was carried out in 1974 under the military government, when the country was organized into regions numbered I to XII plus the Santiago Metropolitan Region. New regions were later created: Arica y Parinacota and Los Ríos in 2007, and Ñuble in 2018, bringing the total to 16. Regional governors (gobernadores regionales) have been directly elected since 2021.

Provinces and communes

Each province is headed by a provincial presidential delegate appointed by the President. Communes may group several settlements; some large urban areas, such as Greater Santiago, are divided among dozens of communes. A commune does not necessarily correspond to a single town.
