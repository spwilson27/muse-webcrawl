Late harvest wine

Late harvest wine is wine made from grapes left on the vine longer than usual. Late-harvest grapes are often more similar to raisins, but have been naturally dehydrated while still on the vine, concentrating their sugars and flavors. The wines produced are typically sweet dessert wines.

Styles

The most celebrated late harvest wines are made from grapes affected by Botrytis cinerea, the "noble rot" that shrivels berries and concentrates sugar and acidity, producing Sauternes and Barsac in Bordeaux, Tokaji Aszú in Hungary, and German Beerenauslese and Trockenbeerenauslese. In the German Prädikat system, Spätlese (literally "late harvest"), Auslese, Beerenauslese, and Trockenbeerenauslese denote progressively later and more selective pickings. Alsace uses the term Vendange Tardive.

Risks and production

Leaving fruit on the vine exposes it to birds, hail, and grey rot rather than noble rot, so yields are small and production is risky and labor-intensive, often requiring multiple passes (tries) through the vineyard to pick only botrytised berries. Related methods include ice wine, made from grapes frozen on the vine, and straw wine, made from grapes dried off the vine.
