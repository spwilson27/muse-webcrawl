Levittown, New York

Levittown is a hamlet and census-designated place in the Town of Hempstead in Nassau County, on Long Island, New York. Built between 1947 and 1951 by Levitt & Sons, it is widely regarded as the first mass-produced suburb in the United States and a symbol of post-war suburbanisation.

History

Using assembly-line methods on former potato fields, Levitt's crews could complete dozens of nearly identical Cape Cod and ranch houses a day, sold at prices returning Second World War veterans could afford with GI Bill mortgages. The development transformed American housing and suburban life.

Criticism and legacy

Levittown was also criticised for monotonous design and for racially restrictive sales practices that excluded Black buyers, contributing to segregated suburbs. Later Levittowns were built in Pennsylvania, New Jersey and elsewhere; the original remains a large residential community.
