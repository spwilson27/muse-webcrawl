Quantum algorithm

In quantum computing, a quantum algorithm is an algorithm that runs on a quantum computer and typically exploits quantum mechanical phenomena such as superposition and entanglement to solve problems more efficiently than is known to be possible classically, or to solve problems intractable for classical machines.

Notable algorithms

Shor's algorithm (1994) factors integers and computes discrete logarithms in polynomial time, threatening widely used public-key cryptography. Grover's algorithm (1996) gives a quadratic speedup for unstructured search. Other major examples include the Deutsch–Jozsa algorithm, the Bernstein–Vazirani and Simon algorithms, quantum phase estimation, and variational algorithms such as VQE and QAOA for near-term hardware.

Complexity

The class of problems efficiently solvable by quantum computers is BQP. Quantum algorithms are usually described as quantum circuits acting on qubits and measured at the end.
