Radiohead

Radiohead performing live.

Radiohead are an English rock band formed in Abingdon, Oxfordshire, in 1985. The band consists of Thom Yorke (vocals, guitar, piano), brothers Jonny Greenwood (lead guitar, keyboards) and Colin Greenwood (bass), Ed O'Brien (guitar, backing vocals) and Philip Selway (drums). They have worked with producer Nigel Godrich since the mid-1990s.

Career

Radiohead's debut single "Creep" (1992) became a worldwide hit after the release of their first album, Pablo Honey (1993). Their third album, OK Computer (1997), is widely acclaimed as one of the greatest albums of all time, with themes of modern alienation. Kid A (2000) marked a radical shift toward electronic music, jazz and krautrock influences and topped charts despite minimal promotion. In Rainbows (2007) was famously released as a pay-what-you-want download, pioneering new distribution models. Later albums include The King of Limbs (2011) and A Moon Shaped Pool (2016).

Legacy

Radiohead have sold more than 30 million albums worldwide and were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2019. They are noted for their experimental approach, environmental activism, and resistance to music-industry conventions.
