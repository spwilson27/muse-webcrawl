The Battle of Makin was an engagement of the Pacific campaign of World War II, fought from 20 to 24 November 1943 on and around Butaritari Island in the Makin Atoll of the Gilbert Islands. It formed the northern half of Operation Galvanic, the American seizure of the Gilberts, whose southern and bloodier objective was Tarawa.

Background

After the Battle of Midway, the United States began planning an advance across the Central Pacific toward the Mariana Islands. The Gilbert Islands, held by Japan since December 1941, were the first stepping stone. In August 1942, U.S. Marine Raiders under Evans Carlson had raided Makin Atoll, an episode that prompted the Japanese to fortify the atoll's main island, Butaritari.

The battle

The assault force, built around the U.S. Army's 165th Regimental Combat Team of the 27th Infantry Division, landed on Butaritari on the morning of 20 November 1943 after a heavy naval and air bombardment. The Japanese garrison, roughly 800 men including labour troops under Seizou Ishikawa, resisted from bunkers and pillboxes but could not contest the beaches. Progress was slow: the inexperienced Army troops advanced cautiously, and snipers and night infiltrations harassed the perimeter. The island was declared secure on 24 November.

The sinking of Liscome Bay

On the morning of 24 November, the Japanese submarine I-175 torpedoed the escort carrier Liscome Bay off Makin. A torpedo detonated her bomb magazine, and she sank within about half an hour with the loss of more than 700 men, including Rear Admiral Henry Mullinix and Pearl Harbor hero Doris Miller. The carrier's loss made Makin far costlier for the Navy than the ground fighting was for the Army.

Aftermath

American ground casualties were comparatively light — about 66 killed — but the sinking of Liscome Bay pushed total U.S. losses above 800 dead. The slow pace of the Army's advance at Makin, contrasted with the Marines' rapid but costly victory at Tarawa, fuelled an interservice controversy over amphibious doctrine that influenced later operations. Makin became a supporting airbase for the subsequent invasion of the Marshall Islands.
