Foundation and Empire is a science fiction novel by American writer Isaac Asimov, originally published by Gnome Press in 1952. It is the second book in the Foundation series, assembled from stories first published in Astounding Science Fiction in 1945. The book consists of two novellas, "The General" and "The Mule", set centuries after the fall of the Galactic Empire begins.

Plot

In "The General", the Foundation faces the last great general of the dying Empire, Bel Riose, whose campaign is defeated not by military genius but by the psychohistorical inevitability of the Seldon Plan and the Empire's internal politics. In "The Mule", a mysterious warlord conquers world after world with inexplicable ease, defeating the Foundation itself; he is revealed to be a mutant able to manipulate human emotions, a factor the Seldon Plan could not foresee, and the book ends with the clandestine Second Foundation setting out to counter him.

Reception

The Foundation trilogy won the one-time Hugo Award for "Best All-Time Series" in 1966. "The Mule" in particular is widely regarded as one of Asimov's finest novellas, and the character's disruption of a seemingly deterministic history is central to the series' themes.
