Spitz

A spitz (German for "pointed") is a type of domestic dog characterised by a thick, long and often white coat, pointed ears, and a tail that curls over the dog's back. The exact origins of spitz-type dogs are not known, though most of those seen today originate from the Arctic region or Siberia.

Characteristics

Spitz breeds share a wolf-like appearance: a wedge-shaped head, triangular erect ears, a dense double coat with a thick undercoat, and a tail carried curled over the back. Sizes range from the tiny Pomeranian (about 2 kg) to large sled dogs such as the Alaskan Malamute.

Breeds

Recognised spitz breeds include the Samoyed, Siberian Husky, Alaskan Malamute, Akita, Shiba Inu, Chow Chow, German Spitz, Pomeranian, Finnish Spitz, Keeshond, Norwegian Elkhound, and Icelandic Sheepdog.

Uses

Spitz types were developed for herding, hunting, guarding, and pulling sledges in cold northern climates; smaller varieties became companion dogs. Their thick coats require regular grooming and shed heavily.
