Krupp was a German family business dynasty and industrial conglomerate centred in Essen, famous for steel production and armaments. Founded in 1811 by Friedrich Krupp to manufacture cast steel, the firm grew under his son Alfred Krupp into one of Europe's largest industrial enterprises, supplying rails, wheels, artillery and armour plate.

Rise

Alfred Krupp (1812–1887) perfected seamless railway tyres, secured orders from Prussia and foreign governments, and built the vast Essen works with their workers' housing colonies and welfare schemes. Krupp steel guns outclassed bronze artillery and became the backbone of German and many other armies; the family's Villa Hügel overlooked the factories. The firm remained family-owned through the marriage of Bertha Krupp to Gustav von Bohlen und Halbach in 1906, by imperial decree adding "Krupp" to his name.

World wars

Krupp armed Germany in both world wars, producing the Paris Gun and Big Bertha howitzers in the First, and tanks, guns and U-boat steel in the Second, extensively using forced and concentration-camp labour. After 1945 Alfried Krupp was convicted at the Nuremberg Krupp Trial of crimes against humanity for the use of slave labour; he was later released under a 1951 amnesty and restored to control of the company.

Later history

In 1968 the firm became a corporation owned by the Alfried Krupp von Bohlen und Halbach Foundation. In 1999 it merged with Thyssen AG to form ThyssenKrupp, one of the world's largest steel and engineering groups, while the Krupp name endures in the charitable foundation and in Essen's industrial heritage.
