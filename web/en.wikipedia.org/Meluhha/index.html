Meluhha

Meluhha (Sumerian: Me-luh-ha) is the Sumerian name of a prominent trading partner of Sumer during the Middle Bronze Age. Its identification remains an open question, but most scholars associate it with the Indus Valley Civilisation.

Trade with Mesopotamia

Akkadian and Sumerian texts describe Meluhha as a source of gold, carnelian, exotic woods, ivory, peacocks and other luxury goods, reached by sea through Dilmun (Bahrain) and Magan (Oman). Inscriptions say that boats from Meluhha docked at Akkad, and there is mention of a Meluhhan village in Sumer and of Meluhhan interpreters. Indus seals, etched carnelian beads and other Indus artefacts have been found at Mesopotamian sites.

Decline

Trade with Meluhha flourished between about 2600 and 1900 BC and then declined along with the Indus cities. In later texts the name Meluhha shifted in meaning and was applied to regions such as Nubia and Ethiopia.
