The Radfan Campaign was a series of British military operations in the Radfan mountains of South Arabia in 1964, during the Aden Emergency. The campaign aimed to secure the Dhala road, the main route between Aden and Dhala, against dissident tribesmen supported by the National Liberation Front (NLF).

Background

The Radfan region, about 100 km north of Aden, was inhabited by the Qutaibi and other tribes who resisted the Federal government and were encouraged by Egypt-supported rebel movements in Yemen. Attacks on convoys along the Dhala road prompted a major operation.

Operations

After an initial operation in January 1964, a larger offensive, sometimes known as Operation Nutcracker, began in late April 1964 under Brigadier (later General) Charles Harington's overall direction. British and Federal Regular Army units, supported by RAF aircraft including Hawker Hunters and helicopters, fought through rugged highland terrain to occupy positions such as Jebel Huriyah. Fighting continued into May, after which the main strongpoints were held, though low-level resistance persisted for years.
