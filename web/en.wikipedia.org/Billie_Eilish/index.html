Billie Eilish

Billie Eilish Pirate Baird O'Connell (born December 18, 2001) is an American singer-songwriter. She first gained public attention in 2015 with her debut single "Ocean Eyes", written and produced by her brother Finneas O'Connell, with whom she collaborates on music and live shows. She is one of the most successful artists of her generation, distinguished by a whispered vocal style, darkly atmospheric electropop, and an oversized, avant-garde fashion image.

Career

After the EP Don't Smile at Me (2017), her debut studio album When We All Fall Asleep, Where Do We Go? (2019) debuted atop the Billboard 200 and produced the worldwide hit "Bad Guy", making Eilish the first artist born in the 21st century to top the Billboard Hot 100. At the 2020 Grammy Awards she became the youngest person and second artist ever to win all four general-field categories — Best New Artist, Record of the Year, Album of the Year, and Song of the Year — in a single ceremony.

Her subsequent albums Happier Than Ever (2021) and Hit Me Hard and Soft (2024) both reached number one in many countries. She won Academy Awards for Best Original Song for "No Time to Die" (2021), the James Bond theme, and "What Was I Made For?" (2023), written for the film Barbie, becoming the youngest two-time Oscar winner in history. Eilish is also known for environmental advocacy, championing sustainable touring and fashion practices.
