'Salem's Lot is a 1975 horror novel by American author Stephen King. It was his second published novel and tells the story of writer Ben Mears, who returns to the town of Jerusalem's Lot (abbreviated to 'Salem's Lot) in Maine, where he lived from the age of five until he was ten, only to discover that the residents are becoming vampires. The town would reappear in the short stories "Jerusalem's Lot" and "One for the Road", both collected in King's 1978 short story collection Night Shift.

The novel was nominated for the World Fantasy Award in 1976 and the Locus Award for the All-Time Best Fantasy Novel in 1987. It has been adapted several times, including a 1979 television miniseries directed by Tobe Hooper, a 2004 television miniseries, and a feature film released in 2024.

Plot summary

Ben Mears, a successful writer grieving the death of his wife in a motorcycle accident, returns to Jerusalem's Lot, Maine, intending to write a book about the Marsten House, an abandoned mansion on a hill overlooking the town. As a child, Mears entered the house on a dare and believed he saw the hanged corpse of its owner, Hubie Marsten, open its eyes. He plans to rent the house, but learns it has just been purchased by two newcomers: Kurt Barlow, an Austrian immigrant who presents himself as an antique dealer, and his business partner, Richard Straker.

Shortly after their arrival, a series of deaths and disappearances begins. A dog is found impaled on a cemetery gate, a young boy, Ralphie Glick, vanishes, and his brother Danny dies of a mysterious illness and returns as a vampire who begins turning others. Mears allies himself with schoolteacher Matt Burke, high-school student Mark Petrie, physician Jimmy Cody, Father Donald Callahan, and Susan Norton, the young woman Mears has begun to fall for. Together they deduce that Barlow is an ancient and powerful vampire and that Straker is his human familiar.

As the contagion spreads through the town, the small band of hunters suffers devastating losses: Susan is turned and must be staked by Ben himself, Matt dies of a heart attack, Jimmy is killed by a booby trap, and Father Callahan is forced to drink Barlow's blood, which damns him and drives him from the town. Ben and Mark ultimately destroy Barlow in the cellar of the boarding house where he has taken refuge, then burn the town to cleanse it. A prologue and epilogue frame the story with Ben and Mark returning a year later to finish the work, as the fire's survivors — now vampires — continue to spread.

Background and publication

King drew inspiration from Bram Stoker's Dracula, wondering what would happen if Dracula came to a small town in modern-day America, and from Shirley Jackson's The Haunting of Hill House, which influenced the Marsten House. He also cited the EC horror comics of his youth. In his non-fiction book Danse Macabre (1981), King discusses the novel's origins and themes, including its undercurrent of anxiety about American small-town decline and conformity. The novel was published by Doubleday on October 17, 1975, following the success of Carrie (1974). King has named it among his personal favorites of his own books.

Themes

Critics have read the novel as an elegy for small-town America: Jerusalem's Lot is depicted as a decaying, insular community whose rot makes it easy prey for Barlow. The vampire functions both as literal monster and as metaphor for the evil already latent in the town — gossip, cruelty, adultery, and indifference. The portrait of the town itself, rendered through dozens of minor characters, anticipates the ensemble technique King would use throughout his later work.

Adaptations

  • Salem's Lot (1979), a CBS television miniseries directed by Tobe Hooper, starring David Soul as Ben Mears and James Mason as Straker; a shortened version was released theatrically overseas as Salem's Lot: The Movie. A sequel, A Return to Salem's Lot, followed in 1987.
  • Salem's Lot (2004), a TNT miniseries starring Rob Lowe as Ben Mears and Donald Sutherland as Straker.
  • Salem's Lot (2024), a feature film written and directed by Gary Dauberman, released on Max, starring Lewis Pullman as Ben Mears.
  • A 1995 BBC Radio 4 dramatisation of the novel.

Connections to King's other works

Father Callahan reappears as a major character in the later volumes of King's The Dark Tower series. The town and its characters also recur in the Night Shift stories "Jerusalem's Lot" (a prequel in epistolary form, set in the 19th century) and "One for the Road" (a sequel set a few years after the novel).
