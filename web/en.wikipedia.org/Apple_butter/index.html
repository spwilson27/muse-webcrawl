Apple butter

Apple butter

TypeFruit butter / spread
Region of originBelgium, Netherlands, Germany; popularized in Appalachia and Pennsylvania Dutch Country
Main ingredientsApples, apple cider or water, sugar, spices (cinnamon, cloves, allspice)

Apple butter is a highly concentrated form of apple sauce produced by long, slow cooking of apples with cider or water to a point where the sugar in the apples caramelizes, turning the apple butter a deep brown. The concentration of sugar gives apple butter a much longer shelf life than apple sauce. Despite its name, it contains no butter; the name refers to its thick, soft consistency and its use as a spread for breads.

Traditional apple butter simmering in a copper kettle

History

Apple butter originated in Limburg (Belgium and the Netherlands) and the Rhineland (Germany) during the Middle Ages, when monasteries kept extensive orchards. The production was a perfect way to conserve part of the year's fruit production. German settlers brought the technique to colonial America, and apple butter became a staple in Pennsylvania Dutch Country and Appalachia, where communal "apple butter boilings" with large copper kettles stirred for many hours became social events.

Preparation

Apples are peeled, cored, and cooked down slowly — traditionally outdoors in a huge kettle over an open fire, today often in a slow cooker — until thick and dark. Sugar and spices such as cinnamon, cloves, and allspice are added. The finished butter is canned or jarred. It is served on bread, toast, biscuits, and pork dishes, and used in baking, including shoofly pie variations and spice cakes.

Similar products

Apple butter belongs to the broader family of fruit butters, which includes pear butter and pumpkin butter. It resembles the Belgian sirop de Liège, a concentrated apple-and-pear syrup, and Dutch appelstroop.
