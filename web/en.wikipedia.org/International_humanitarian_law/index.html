International humanitarian law

International humanitarian law (IHL), also referred to as the laws of armed conflict, is the law that regulates the conduct of war. It seeks to limit the effects of armed conflict for humanitarian reasons, protecting persons who are not participating in hostilities and restricting means and methods of warfare.

International humanitarian law
Key treatiesGeneva Conventions 1949, Additional Protocols 1977
PrinciplesDistinction, proportionality, necessity, humanity

Sources

IHL derives from the Hague Conventions, the four Geneva Conventions — universally ratified — and customary international law. Grave breaches may be prosecuted as war crimes.

Application

It distinguishes combatants from civilians, protects prisoners of war, the wounded, medical personnel such as MSF, and refugees, and prohibits torture, chemical weapons and attacks on civilian objects.
