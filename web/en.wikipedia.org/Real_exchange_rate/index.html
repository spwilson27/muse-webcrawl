Real exchange rate

The real exchange rate (RER) is the purchasing power of one currency relative to another, adjusted for differences in price levels. If E is the nominal exchange rate (foreign currency per unit of domestic currency), P the domestic price level, and P* the foreign price level, the real exchange rate is E·P/P* — the relative price of domestic goods in terms of foreign goods.

Interpretation

A rise in the real exchange rate (real appreciation) means domestic goods have become more expensive relative to foreign goods, which tends to reduce export competitiveness and increase imports; a real depreciation has the opposite effect. Under purchasing power parity the real exchange rate would be constant, but in practice it fluctuates substantially in the short and medium run.

Determinants

Long-run real exchange rates are influenced by productivity differences (the Balassa–Samuelson effect), terms of trade, government spending, and net foreign asset positions. Real effective exchange rates (REER) average bilateral real rates across trading partners and are published by institutions such as the IMF and BIS as competitiveness indicators.
