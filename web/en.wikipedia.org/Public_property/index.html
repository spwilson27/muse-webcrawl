Public property

Public property is property that is dedicated to public use, owned collectively by the community or held by the state on its behalf, as distinct from private property owned by individuals or corporations. Examples include public parks, libraries, roads, government buildings and publicly owned land and infrastructure.

Concepts

In Roman law, res publica denoted things held for common use, such as public roads and harbours, alongside res nullius (things owned by nobody) and crown property. Modern legal systems distinguish public property from state or crown property in some contexts, and public ownership ranges from municipal assets to federally held lands such as national parks and forests.

Use and management

Public property is generally open to all members of the public, subject to regulations protecting the resource and other users. Debates over privatisation, public access rights, and the maintenance or deferred upkeep of public assets recur in policy. The "tragedy of the commons" describes how shared resources can be overused when access is unregulated.
