Lech Wałęsa (born 29 September 1943) is a Polish statesman, dissident, and Nobel Peace Prize laureate. A shipyard electrician in Gdańsk, he co-founded and led Solidarity (Solidarność), the first independent trade union in the Soviet bloc, whose strikes and negotiations helped bring about the peaceful end of communist rule in Poland and hastened the fall of communism in Central and Eastern Europe.

Solidarity

Wałęsa emerged as a workers' leader during strikes at the Lenin Shipyard in Gdańsk, having earlier participated in the protests of 1970. In August 1980 he climbed the shipyard gate to join and lead a strike that won the Gdańsk Agreement, legalising independent unions. When martial law was imposed in December 1981, he was interned for nearly a year. Awarded the Nobel Peace Prize in 1983, he remained the symbol of resistance until Round Table talks in 1989 led to semi-free elections and the formation of a Solidarity-led government.

Presidency

Wałęsa won the 1990 presidential election and served as President of Poland from 1990 to 1995, presiding over the transition to a market economy and the withdrawal of Soviet/Russian troops. He narrowly lost re-election in 1995 to Aleksander Kwaśniewski, and a 2000 comeback attempt failed. He remains an active public commentator on Polish and European politics.
