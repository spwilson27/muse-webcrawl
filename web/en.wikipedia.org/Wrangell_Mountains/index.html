Wrangell Mountains

Highest pointMount Blackburn, 16,390 ft (4,996 m)
LocationEastern Alaska, United States; partly Yukon, Canada
Protected areaWrangell–St. Elias National Park and Preserve

The Wrangell Mountains are a high mountain range of eastern Alaska in the United States, extending into the Yukon Territory of Canada. Largely volcanic in origin, they are named after Ferdinand von Wrangel, a Baltic German explorer and president of the Russian-American Company. Much of the range lies within Wrangell–St. Elias National Park and Preserve, the largest national park in the United States.

Geography and geology

The range is a huge volcanic field formed by subduction of the Pacific Plate. Mount Blackburn, an eroded shield volcano, is the highest peak. Other major summits include Mount Sanford, Mount Wrangell — one of the largest active andesitic shield volcanoes in the world — and Mount Drum. Extensive icefields and glaciers, including parts of the Bagley Icefield region, mantle the higher elevations.

History

The area was prospected for copper in the early 20th century; the Kennecott mines, fed by exceptionally rich copper ore, operated from 1911 to 1938 and are now a National Historic Landmark at McCarthy. The park was established in 1980 and, together with adjoining Canadian parks, forms a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
