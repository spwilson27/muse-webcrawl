Date16 December 1740 – 18 October 1748
LocationEurope, North America, India
BelligerentsHabsburg Austria, Britain, Dutch Republic vs Prussia, France, Spain, Bavaria

The War of the Austrian Succession (1740–1748) was a major European conflict sparked by the death of the Habsburg emperor Charles VI and the accession of his daughter Maria Theresa, whose right to inherit the Habsburg lands under the Pragmatic Sanction of 1713 was challenged by rival claimants. Frederick II of Prussia opened the war by invading the rich province of Silesia in December 1740.

Course of the war

The war drew in most of Europe: France, Spain and Bavaria supported the anti-Habsburg cause, while Britain and the Dutch Republic backed Austria, chiefly to check French power. Fighting extended to colonial theatres — in North America as King George's War and in India as the First Carnatic War. Notable battles included Mollwitz (1741), Dettingen (1743), where George II became the last British king to lead troops in battle, and Fontenoy (1745).

Settlement

The Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle (1748) largely restored the status quo ante, with the great exception that Prussia kept Silesia — a loss Maria Theresa never accepted, setting the stage for the Seven Years' War eight years later.
