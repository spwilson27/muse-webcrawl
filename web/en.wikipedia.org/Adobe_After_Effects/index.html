Adobe After Effects is a digital visual effects, motion graphics, and compositing application developed by Adobe Inc. It is used in film, television, and web production for creating titles, animations, and effects, layering video and images over a timeline with keyframing, masks, and effects.

History

After Effects was created by the Company of Science and Art (CoSA) in Providence, Rhode Island, with version 1.0 released in 1993. Aldus acquired CoSA in 1993, and Adobe acquired Aldus in 1994; Adobe then developed the program into an industry standard. The software became part of the Adobe Creative Suite and later Creative Cloud subscription offerings.

Features

Users animate properties such as position, scale, rotation, and opacity with keyframes and expressions (JavaScript-based scripts). After Effects supports a large ecosystem of plug-ins, 3D camera tracking, rotoscoping tools, and integration with Adobe Premiere Pro and Cinema 4D. Common uses include broadcast graphics, title sequences, explainer animations, and compositing CGI into live-action footage.
