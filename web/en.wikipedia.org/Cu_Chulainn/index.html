Cú Chulainn (Irish: [kuː ˈxʊlˠɪn̠ʲ], "Culann's Hound"), also spelled Cú Chulaind or Cúchulainn, is an Irish mythological demigod and the central hero of the Ulster Cycle of Irish mythology. The son of the god Lugh and the mortal Deichtine, he is best known from the epic Táin Bó Cúailnge ("The Cattle Raid of Cooley"), in which, at seventeen, he single-handedly defends Ulster against the armies of Queen Medb of Connacht.

Legend

Born Sétanta, the boy earned his famous name after killing the fierce guard hound of the smith Culann in self-defence and offering to take the dog's place until a replacement could be reared. Trained in arms by the warrior woman Scáthach in Alba (Scotland), he mastered the terrible spear called the Gáe Bulg. In battle he was subject to a ríastrad, a "warp spasm" in which his body contorted into a monstrous, unrecognisable form.

The Táin

During the Cattle Raid, Cú Chulainn invoked the right of single combat at fords, defeating a champion of Connacht each day, and was forced to fight his beloved foster-brother Ferdiad, whom he killed with the Gáe Bulg. Mortally wounded in his final battle, he tied himself to a standing stone so that he would die on his feet, and his enemies only dared approach when a raven — the Morrígán — landed on his shoulder, proving he was dead.

Legacy

Cú Chulainn remains one of the most enduring figures of Irish culture; a bronze statue of his death stands in the General Post Office in Dublin as a memorial to the Easter Rising of 1916.
