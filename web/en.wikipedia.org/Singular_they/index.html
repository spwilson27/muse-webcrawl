Singular they

Singular they, along with its inflected forms them, their, theirs and themselves, is the use of the pronoun they with a singular antecedent. It occurs in two main settings: with indefinite or generic antecedents ("someone left their umbrella"), and as the personal pronoun of a specific individual whose gender is unknown, irrelevant, or nonbinary.

History

Singular they is attested in English from the 14th century and appears in Chaucer, Shakespeare, Austen, and the King James Bible tradition of usage. From the 18th century, prescriptive grammarians promoted generic he instead, and 19th-century legislation in Britain even codified masculine pronouns as covering women. Usage of singular they nevertheless persisted in speech and much writing.

Modern acceptance

Since the early 21st century most major style guides — including the Associated Press, Chicago Manual of Style, APA style, and Merriam-Webster (which made singular they its 2019 Word of the Year) — have accepted or recommended it in at least some contexts, particularly to avoid assuming gender and to respect individuals who use they/them pronouns. Grammatically, singular they behaves like singular you: it takes plural verb forms ("they are") even when referring to one person.
