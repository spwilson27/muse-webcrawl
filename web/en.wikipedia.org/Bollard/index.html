Bollard

A bollard is a short vertical post used to control or direct traffic, protect pedestrians and property, or moor vessels. The word originally described the stout posts on a wharf or ship's deck to which mooring lines are fastened; today it most commonly refers to the street furniture used to manage road traffic.

Maritime bollards

In maritime use, a bollard is a wooden, iron or steel post on a dock or on board a vessel, around which ropes (hawsers) are secured to moor the ship. On ships, pairs of vertical posts mounted together are called bitts. Mooring bollards are rated for the loads they must bear and are a standard feature of ports and quaysides.

Traffic and security bollards

Road bollards delineate lanes, protect buildings and pedestrian areas, and prevent vehicle access. Types include:

  • Fixed bollards — permanently set in concrete, often concrete-filled steel pipe.
  • Removable and retractable bollards — which can be lowered or lifted to allow authorised vehicles through.
  • Flexible bollards — made of plastic that bends on impact, used to delineate traffic lanes without damaging vehicles.
  • Crash-rated bollards — engineered and certified to stop a vehicle of a given mass at a given speed, used for hostile-vehicle mitigation at airports, government buildings and crowded places.

Since the early 2000s, security bollards have become ubiquitous in response to vehicle-ramming attacks and fears of car bombs. Historically, London streets were lined with bollards made from captured French cannon after the Napoleonic Wars, and some of these survive.

Lighting bollards

Short posts containing a light source are used to illuminate paths, gardens and parking areas. Solar-powered and LED versions are common.
