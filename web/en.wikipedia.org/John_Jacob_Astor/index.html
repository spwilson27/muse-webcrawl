John Jacob Astor (born Johann Jakob Astor; July 17, 1763 – March 29, 1848) was a German-American businessman, merchant and investor who built a fortune in the fur trade and New York City real estate. He founded the American Fur Company and is often described as the first multimillionaire in the United States, leaving an estate estimated at $20–30 million.

Early life and fur trade

Astor was born in Walldorf, Germany, the son of a butcher. He emigrated via London to New York in 1784 and entered the fur trade, travelling into the interior to buy pelts and selling them in London and China. By 1800 he was the leading fur merchant in the United States. His American Fur Company (1808) dominated the trade in the Great Lakes and the West; his Pacific Fur Company founded Fort Astoria at the mouth of the Columbia River in 1811, though the post was sold to the British during the War of 1812.

Real estate and later years

From about 1834 Astor withdrew from the fur trade and invested heavily in Manhattan real estate, buying land ahead of the city's growth and becoming its largest landlord. His wealth funded the Astor Library, which later became part of the New York Public Library. He died in 1848, the richest man in America; his fortune founded the Astor family dynasty and gave its name to Astor Place, Astoria in Queens and Astoria, Oregon.
