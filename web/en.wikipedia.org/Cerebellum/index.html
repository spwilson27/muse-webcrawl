Cerebellum

Cerebellum
Location: Hindbrain, behind brainstem
Neurons: About 69 billion in humans
Function: Motor coordination, balance

The cerebellum (Latin for little brain) is a major structure of the hindbrain, located beneath the occipital lobes and behind the brainstem. Although only about 10% of brain volume, it contains roughly 80% of the brain's neurons, mostly granule cells.

Function

The cerebellum fine-tunes voluntary movement, posture, balance and motor learning by comparing intended movements with sensory feedback and correcting errors. It also contributes to timing, language and cognition. Damage causes ataxia — unsteady gait, tremor and slurred speech — rather than paralysis.

Anatomy

It has two hemispheres, a midline vermis, a finely folded cortex, and deep nuclei, connected to the brainstem by three pairs of peduncles. See Brain and Central nervous system.
