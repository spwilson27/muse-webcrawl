University Circle is a district on the east side of Cleveland, Ohio, in the United States. Often described as one of the densest concentrations of cultural attractions and educational institutions in the country, the roughly one-square-mile district is home to Case Western Reserve University, the Cleveland Museum of Art, the Cleveland Orchestra at Severance Hall, the Cleveland Museum of Natural History, and major medical centres including the Cleveland Clinic and University Hospitals.

History

The district takes its name from a circular streetcar turnaround at Euclid Avenue and Doan Brook. In the 1880s Western Reserve University and the Case School of Applied Science relocated to the area, followed by cultural institutions attracted by the pastoral setting around Wade Park, donated by industrialist Jeptha Wade. The two universities federated in 1967 as Case Western Reserve University.

Institutions

University Circle hosts more than forty institutions, including the Cleveland Institute of Art, the Cleveland Institute of Music, the Western Reserve Historical Society and the Museum of Contemporary Art Cleveland. Its hospitals form one of the largest employment centres in Ohio. The district is managed in part by University Circle Inc., a non-profit community service corporation founded in 1957.

Transportation

The area is served by the RTA Rapid Transit Red Line and the HealthLine bus rapid transit along Euclid Avenue, one of the first BRT corridors in the United States.
