Paris–Nice is a professional cycling stage race held each year in France in early March. Nicknamed the "Race to the Sun", it runs from the Paris region south to Nice on the Mediterranean coast, and is traditionally the first major stage race of the European season. Founded in 1933 by the newspaper owner Albert Lejeune, it is now organised by the Amaury Sport Organisation and forms part of the UCI World Tour.

History

The race was created in 1933 to promote Lejeune's newspapers Le Petit Journal and Le Petit Niçois. After interruptions during the Second World War and a hiatus in the late 1940s, it became an established fixture of the calendar. Irish rider Sean Kelly holds the record with seven consecutive victories between 1982 and 1988. Other notable multiple winners include Jacques Anquetil, Eddy Merckx, Raymond Poulidor, Joop Zoetemelk and Miguel Induráin.

Route

The course typically begins with flat or rolling stages in northern France, often in cold and windy conditions, before reaching the hills of Provence and the Alps-Maritimes. Mountain stages around the Col de Turini, La Colmiane or the Col d'Èze, and a final time trial or hilly stage ending on the Promenade des Anglais in Nice, usually decide the general classification. The leader wears a yellow jersey.

Winners

In the modern era the race has been won by riders such as Alberto Contador, Richie Porte, Geraint Thomas, Marc Soler, Primož Roglič and Tadej Pogačar, who triumphed in 2023. The race is regarded as an important early-season test of form ahead of the spring classics and the Grand Tours.
