The sugar maple (Acer saccharum) is a species of flowering plant in the soapberry family native to the hardwood forests of eastern Canada and the eastern United States. It is best known as the primary source of maple syrup and for its brilliant autumn foliage in shades of orange and red.

Description

Sugar maple is a large deciduous tree, commonly reaching 25–35 metres in height, with a dense, rounded crown. Its five-lobed leaves are the model for the maple leaf on the flag of Canada. The bark is grey-brown and develops long vertical plates with age.

Maple syrup production

In late winter and early spring, the tree's sap rises and can be tapped by drilling holes in the trunk; the sap is collected and boiled down to produce maple syrup, roughly forty litres of sap yielding one litre of syrup.

Uses and ecology

The hard, heavy wood ("hard maple") is prized for furniture, flooring, and bowling pins. The tree is an important component of northern hardwood forests and a major contributor to autumn tourism in New England and eastern Canada.
