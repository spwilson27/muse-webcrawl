Charles Boycott

Charles Cunningham Boycott (12 March 1832 – 19 June 1897) was an English land agent in Ireland whose ostracism by his neighbours in County Mayo in 1880 gave the English language the word "boycott".

Life

Boycott was born in Burgh St Peter, Norfolk, and served in the British Army before settling in Ireland, where he became agent for the Earl of Erne's estates around Lough Mask in County Mayo. During the Land War, tenant farmers demanded rent reductions after poor harvests. When Boycott refused and began eviction proceedings in 1880, the local branch of the Irish National Land League organised a complete withdrawal of labour and services: his workers, servants, and local tradespeople refused to deal with him, following advice given by Charles Stewart Parnell that unpopular landlords' agents should be shunned rather than attacked.

The campaign

The campaign drew international press attention. Crops on the estate were eventually harvested by fifty Orangemen brought in from Cavan and Monaghan under military escort, at a cost far exceeding the value of the harvest. The word "boycotting" was coined by the local priest Father John O'Malley and spread by journalist James Redpath, entering general usage within months. Boycott left Ireland in December 1880 and later worked as a land agent in Suffolk, England, where he died.
