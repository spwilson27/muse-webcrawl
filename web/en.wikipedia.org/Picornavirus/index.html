Picornavirus

Picornaviruses are a family of small, non-enveloped, positive-sense single-stranded RNA viruses, the Picornaviridae. The name combines "pico" (very small) with "RNA". Their virions are about 30 nanometres in diameter, with an icosahedral capsid made of 60 copies each of four structural proteins (VP1–VP4) surrounding a genome of roughly 7,500 nucleotides.

Genera and diseases

The family includes many important human and animal pathogens: poliovirus (poliomyelitis), rhinoviruses (the common cold), enteroviruses such as coxsackieviruses and echoviruses, hepatitis A virus, and foot-and-mouth disease virus of livestock. Transmission is usually faecal–oral or respiratory, and infections range from asymptomatic to severe disease of the nervous system, heart, liver and skin.

Replication

The viral RNA acts directly as messenger RNA upon entry into a cell; a single polyprotein is translated and cleaved by viral proteases into functional proteins. Genome replication takes place on rearranged intracellular membranes via a negative-strand intermediate.
