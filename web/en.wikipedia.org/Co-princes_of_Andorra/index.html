The co-princes of Andorra are jointly the heads of state of the Principality of Andorra, a small landlocked country in the Pyrenees between France and Spain. Uniquely in the world, the principality has two co-princes: the Bishop of Urgell in Catalonia, Spain, and the President of the French Republic. The arrangement dates from the thirteenth century and makes the French president, uniquely, an elected head of state of a foreign country as well as of his own.

History

The co-principality originated in disputes between the Bishop of Urgell and the Count of Foix over the Andorran valleys, settled by the Paréages of 1278 and 1288, which established joint lordship. The rights of the Counts of Foix passed through marriage to the kings of Navarre and thence, with Henry IV, to the French crown; after the Revolution they devolved on the French head of state, and since 1848 on the President of France. Andorra paid a symbolic tribute, the questia, to the co-princes until 1993.

Constitutional role

The Constitution of Andorra of 1993 formalized the diarchy while transforming Andorra into a sovereign parliamentary democracy. The co-princes are heads of state jointly and indivisibly; their acts are largely formal, requiring countersignature, and most are exercised through personal representatives. They sanction and promulgate laws, accredit ambassadors jointly, and serve as guarantors of Andorra's independence and of its treaties with France and Spain. Actual government is exercised by the elected General Council and the Head of Government.

Notable occupants

The French co-principality has been held by kings, emperors and presidents; every French president since 1848 has also been co-prince of Andorra. On the episcopal side, Joan Enric Vives i Sicília served from 2003 to 2025, resigning on reaching the age limit, and was succeeded by Josep-Lluís Serrano Pentinat. The long-serving bishop Justí Guitart welcomed exiled institutions in the Spanish Civil War era, and the co-princes' neutrality helped Andorra remain outside both World Wars.
