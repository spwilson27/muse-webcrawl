Transportation in New York City

The transportation system of New York City is a network of complex infrastructural systems, the largest in the United States. Unlike most American cities, a majority of New Yorkers use public transport rather than private cars, and many households do not own a car.

Mass transit

The New York City Subway, operated by the Metropolitan Transportation Authority (MTA), is one of the world's largest rapid transit systems, running 24 hours a day. Commuter railroads — the Long Island Rail Road, Metro-North Railroad, and NJ Transit — converge on Penn Station and Grand Central Terminal. The MTA bus network, ferries including the Staten Island Ferry, and the Port Authority's PATH trains to New Jersey complete the system.

Roads and bridges

Iconic crossings such as the Brooklyn Bridge, George Washington Bridge, and the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge link the five boroughs, while tunnels including the Holland and Lincoln tunnels connect Manhattan to New Jersey. Congestion pricing for Manhattan below 60th Street was introduced in 2025, a first in the United States.

Air

The region is served by John F. Kennedy International, LaGuardia, and Newark Liberty International airports, among the busiest airport systems in the world.
