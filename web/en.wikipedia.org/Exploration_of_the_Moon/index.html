Exploration of the Moon

Apollo 11's lunar module Eagle on the surface at Tranquility Base, 1969.

The exploration of the Moon began with robotic spacecraft in 1959 and reached its most famous milestone when Apollo 11 landed the first humans on the lunar surface in July 1969. The Moon remains the only celestial body beyond Earth visited by humans.

Robotic pioneers

The Soviet Luna programme achieved the first successful flyby (Luna 1), first impact (Luna 2) and first photographs of the far side (Luna 3), all in 1959, followed by the first soft landing (Luna 9, 1966) and the first robotic sample returns. American Ranger, Lunar Orbiter and Surveyor probes mapped landing sites ahead of the crewed Apollo missions.

Crewed landings and later missions

Six Apollo missions landed twelve astronauts between 1969 and 1972, returning 382 kg of lunar rock and soil. After a long lull, orbiters and landers from several nations resumed lunar exploration: China's Chang'e programme achieved the first far-side landing (Chang'e 4, 2019) and sample returns from both near and far sides, India's Chandrayaan-3 landed near the south pole in 2023, and NASA's Artemis programme aims to return crews to the Moon in the late 2020s.
