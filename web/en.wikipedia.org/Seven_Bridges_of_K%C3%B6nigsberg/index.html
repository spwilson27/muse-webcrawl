Seven Bridges of Königsberg

The Seven Bridges of Königsberg is a historically notable problem in mathematics. The city of Königsberg in Prussia (now Kaliningrad, Russia) was set on both sides of the Pregel River and included two large islands connected to each other and to the mainland by seven bridges.

The problem

Citizens wondered whether it was possible to walk through the city crossing each bridge exactly once. In 1736, Leonhard Euler proved that it was impossible and, in doing so, laid the foundations of graph theory and anticipated topology.

Euler's solution

Euler abstracted the land masses as vertices and the bridges as edges. Such a walk (an Eulerian path) exists only if the graph has zero or two vertices of odd degree; in Königsberg all four land areas had odd degree, so no such route could exist.
