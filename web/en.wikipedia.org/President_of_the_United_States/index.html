The President of the United States (POTUS) is the head of state and head of government of the United States, directing the executive branch of the federal government and serving as commander-in-chief of the United States Armed Forces. Article II of the US Constitution vests executive power in the president. The office is widely considered among the most powerful political positions in the world.

Powers and duties

The president enforces federal law, appoints cabinet secretaries, ambassadors, and federal judges (including Supreme Court justices) with the advice and consent of the Senate, issues executive orders and pardons for federal offenses, negotiates treaties, and commands the military. The president may veto legislation, subject to override by two-thirds of both houses of Congress, addresses Congress in the State of the Union, and represents the United States in foreign affairs.

Election

Presidents are chosen indirectly through the Electoral College: voters in each state select electors, whose numbers equal the state's congressional delegation plus three for the District of Columbia, for a total of 538; 270 electoral votes are needed to win. Elections occur every four years on the Tuesday after the first Monday in November, with inauguration on 20 January.

Qualifications and term

A president must be a natural-born citizen of the United States, at least 35 years old, and a resident for at least 14 years. George Washington set a two-term precedent followed until Franklin D. Roosevelt won four elections; the Twenty-second Amendment (ratified 1951) now limits presidents to two elected terms.

Succession and removal

The vice president succeeds to the presidency upon the president's death, resignation, or removal; the Twenty-fifth Amendment governs disability and vacancies in the vice presidency. Eight presidents have died in office and one, Richard Nixon, resigned. The House of Representatives may impeach the president, and conviction by two-thirds of the Senate removes them; Andrew Johnson, Bill Clinton, and Donald Trump (twice) were impeached but acquitted.
