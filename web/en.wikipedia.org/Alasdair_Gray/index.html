Alasdair Gray

Born: 28 December 1934, Glasgow, Scotland

Died: 29 December 2019, Glasgow

Occupation: Novelist, artist, playwright, poet

Notable work: Lanark (1981)

Alasdair Gray (28 December 1934 – 29 December 2019) was a Scottish writer and artist, regarded as a central figure in the renaissance of Scottish literature in the late twentieth century. His novel Lanark: A Life in Four Books (1981), written over nearly thirty years, is often described as one of the greatest Scottish novels of the century.

Life and work

Gray studied at the Glasgow School of Art and worked as a painter, muralist, and teacher before publishing Lanark, a work combining realist Glasgow narrative with dystopian fantasy about the city of Unthank. His subsequent books include the short-story collection Unlikely Stories, Mostly (1983), 1982, Janine (1984), Poor Things (1992) — which won the Guardian Fiction Prize and was adapted into an acclaimed film in 2023 — and A History Maker (1994). Gray designed and illustrated his own books with distinctive typography and artwork, and his murals decorate sites across Glasgow, including the Òran Mór and Hillhead subway station.

Politics

Gray was a Scottish nationalist and republican, famous for the maxim "Work as if you live in the early days of a better nation," adapted from a line by Canadian poet Dennis Lee and now inscribed on the wall of the Scottish Parliament building. He died in Glasgow the day after his 85th birthday.
