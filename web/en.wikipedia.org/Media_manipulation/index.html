Media manipulation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Media manipulation refers to a range of techniques in which partisans create an image or argument that favours their particular interests. Such tactics may include the use of propaganda techniques, suppression of information, distortion of facts, and the manipulation of public opinion through agenda-setting, framing and priming. Media manipulation is closely related to mass media and is often discussed in connection with politics, advertising and public relations.

Techniques

Common techniques include selective reporting, in which favourable facts are emphasised and unfavourable ones omitted; the use of loaded language; astroturfing, in which a campaign is made to appear grassroots; and the repetition of slogans or claims until they are accepted as true. In the digital era, manipulation may also involve fake accounts, bots and coordinated inauthentic behaviour on social media platforms.

History

Manipulation of information is as old as organised communication, from wartime propaganda to the yellow journalism of the late nineteenth century. The twentieth century saw state propaganda ministries and sophisticated advertising industries, while the rise of the internet and algorithmic feeds created new opportunities and risks in the twenty-first century.

Countermeasures

Media literacy education, fact-checking organisations, transparency requirements for advertising and independent journalism are all proposed as countermeasures against manipulation.
