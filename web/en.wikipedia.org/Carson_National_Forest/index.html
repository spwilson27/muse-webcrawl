Carson National Forest is a national forest in northern New Mexico, United States, comprising 1.5 million acres (6,070 km²). It was established in 1908 by combining the former Taos National Forest and part of the Jemez National Forest, and is named for the frontiersman Kit Carson. The forest is administered by the United States Forest Service from its headquarters in Taos.

High country in Carson National Forest, near Wheeler Peak.

Geography

The forest spans four ranger districts on both sides of the Rio Grande, from high desert and piñon–juniper woodland to subalpine spruce–fir forest. It includes Wheeler Peak (13,161 ft), the highest point in New Mexico, protected within the Wheeler Peak Wilderness, as well as the Latir Peak and Cruces Basin wilderness areas and the Valle Vidal unit, a former ranch donated in 1982 known for its elk herd.

History and communities

Carson is unusual among national forests for the many land grants and long-established Hispano and Pueblo communities within and around its boundaries, whose grazing, irrigation (acequia), and firewood traditions predate the forest. Land-grant disputes over forest lands were a defining issue of 20th-century northern New Mexico politics, culminating in the 1967 Tierra Amarilla courthouse raid by Reies Tijerina's Alianza Federal de Mercedes.

Recreation

The forest contains the Taos Ski Valley and Red River ski areas, the Enchanted Circle scenic byway, hundreds of miles of trails, and fishing waters including tributaries of the Rio Grande and the Rio Chama. It also contains the historic Cumbres and Toltec Scenic Railroad corridor near its northern edge.
