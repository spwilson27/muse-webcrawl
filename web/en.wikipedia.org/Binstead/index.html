Binstead

LocationIsle of Wight, England
Civil parishRyde
Ceremonial countyIsle of Wight

Binstead is a village in the civil parish of Ryde on the Isle of Wight in southern England. It lies to the west of Ryde, close to the island's north-east coast, and has effectively become a suburb of the town while retaining a village character.

History

Binstead is best known for its stone: Binstead stone, a hard limestone quarried locally since at least Norman times, was used in the construction of Winchester Cathedral and other churches. The village church, the Church of the Holy Cross, dates substantially from the 12th and 13th centuries and was rebuilt in the 19th century after a fire.

Features

The village contains the remains of Quarr Abbey's medieval grange; the modern Quarr Abbey, a Benedictine monastery built in brick in the early 20th century, stands nearby. Binstead has a primary school, a village hall, and recreational grounds, and adjoins woodland along the coast near Fishbourne.
