SoftBank Group

SoftBank Group Corp. is a Japanese multinational investment holding company headquartered in Minato, Tokyo, founded in 1981 by Masayoshi Son. Begun as a software distributor, it became a telecommunications operator and, especially after 2016, one of the world's largest technology investors, best known for its early investment in Alibaba and for the nearly $100 billion SoftBank Vision Fund launched in 2017.

History

Son founded the company as a PC software wholesaler, expanded into publishing and trade shows, and listed it in 1994. A series of bold investments followed: a stake in Yahoo! and the founding of Yahoo! Japan, purchases of Ziff Davis and Comdex, and in 2000 a $20 million investment in the Chinese e-commerce startup Alibaba that grew to be worth tens of billions. SoftBank built a telecom business by acquiring Japan Telecom and Vodafone Japan (2006), took control of U.S. carrier Sprint (2013; later merged into T-Mobile US), and acquired British chip designer ARM Holdings in 2016 for about £24 billion.

Vision Fund and investments

The Vision Fund, backed by Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, Abu Dhabi's Mubadala, Apple, and others, made very large late-stage bets — Uber, DoorDash, ByteDance, Coupang, and WeWork among them. The WeWork debacle of 2019, in which SoftBank rescued the company after its failed IPO, and broad markdowns in 2022 produced record losses, prompting asset sales (including much of the Alibaba stake) and a more defensive posture. A second Vision Fund, funded by SoftBank itself, invested in hundreds of smaller positions. ARM's successful Nasdaq IPO in 2023 restored a centerpiece asset, and Son has since refocused the group on artificial intelligence investments, including participation in the announced Stargate data-center venture.

Structure

SoftBank Group acts as a strategic investment holding company; its Japanese telecom business operates through listed subsidiary SoftBank Corp. Son, its chairman and CEO, remains the dominant shareholder and public face.
