Context (language use)

In linguistics and related fields, context refers to the relevant circumstances — linguistic, situational and social — that surround an utterance or text and contribute to its meaning. The study of how context affects meaning is central to pragmatics, sociolinguistics and discourse analysis.

Linguistic context

The linguistic context (or co-text) consists of the words and sentences surrounding an expression. It resolves ambiguity: the meaning of a word such as "bank" is fixed by the sentences around it. Anaphora, ellipsis and many other phenomena depend on co-text for interpretation.

Situational and social context

The situational context includes the physical setting, the participants and their relationships, the purpose of the communication, and shared background knowledge. Bronisław Malinowski coined the phrase "context of situation" (1923), and J. R. Firth and later M. A. K. Halliday developed the idea into register theory, analysing language variation by field, tenor and mode. Dell Hymes' SPEAKING model catalogued the components of speech events.

Contextual meaning

Many linguistic meanings are context-dependent: deictic expressions such as "I", "here" and "tomorrow" pick out their referents only relative to the situation of utterance. Pragmatic theories — including speech act theory, Gricean implicature and relevance theory — explain how hearers use context to infer what speakers mean beyond what they literally say.
