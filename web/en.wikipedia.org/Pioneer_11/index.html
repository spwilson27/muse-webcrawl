Pioneer 11

Pioneer 11 (also known as Pioneer G) is a NASA robotic space probe launched on 5 April 1973 to study the asteroid belt, the environment around Jupiter and Saturn, the solar wind, and cosmic rays. It was the second mission of the Pioneer program and the sister craft of Pioneer 10.

Mission

After surviving the asteroid belt, Pioneer 11 flew by Jupiter on 2 December 1974, passing within about 42,500 km of the cloud tops and imaging the Great Red Spot and the polar regions. Using Jupiter's gravity, it was retargeted to Saturn, which it reached on 1 September 1979, becoming the first spacecraft to visit that planet. It passed within about 21,000 km, discovered a small moon and an additional ring (the F ring), and measured Saturn's magnetic field and temperature.

Later years

Pioneer 11 then headed out of the Solar System in roughly the direction opposite to Pioneer 10. Its radioisotope power sources gradually decayed; the last routine contact was in September 1995, and the mission formally ended that year. Like its sister, it carries the Pioneer plaque, a gold-anodized message from humanity designed by Carl Sagan and Frank Drake and drawn by Linda Salzman Sagan.
