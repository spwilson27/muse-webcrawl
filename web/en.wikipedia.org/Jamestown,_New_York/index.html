Jamestown is a city in Chautauqua County, New York, United States, situated at the southern end of Chautauqua Lake near the Pennsylvania border. It was the home of comic actress Lucille Ball and naturalist Roger Tory Peterson, and historically a leading centre of wooden furniture manufacture.

History

The first settler, James Prendergast — after whom the settlement was named "Jamestown" — built a sawmill on the outlet of Chautauqua Lake in 1810–11. Water power drew lumber, furniture and textile industries; by the late 19th century Jamestown was one of the largest furniture-producing cities in the United States, drawing large Swedish and Italian immigrant communities. The city was incorporated in 1886.

People and culture

Jamestown was the hometown of Lucille Ball (born 1911), star of I Love Lucy; the city is home to the Lucille Ball Desi Arnaz Museum and, since 2018, the National Comedy Center. Ornithologist and artist Roger Tory Peterson, author of the Peterson Field Guides, was born in Jamestown and is commemorated by the Roger Tory Peterson Institute. US Supreme Court justice Robert H. Jackson, chief prosecutor at Nuremberg, grew up nearby and is buried in Frewsburg.

Geography and economy

The city lies in the Allegheny Plateau country of western New York, about 75 miles (120 km) southwest of Buffalo. The Chadakoin River flows through the city from Chautauqua Lake. After the decline of furniture manufacturing, the economy has centred on machinery, metal products, health care and tourism linked to the lake and the Chautauqua Institution up the shore.
