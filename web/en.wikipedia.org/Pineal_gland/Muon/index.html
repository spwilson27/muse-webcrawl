Muon

Symbolμ−
Antiparticleμ+
Mass105.66 MeV/c²
Mean lifetime2.197 μs

A muon (symbol μ) is an elementary particle similar to the electron, with an electric charge of −1 e and a spin of 1/2, but with a much greater mass — about 207 times that of the electron. It is classified as a lepton. Like all elementary particles, it has a corresponding antiparticle of opposite charge but equal mass and spin: the antimuon.

History

Muons were discovered by Carl D. Anderson and Seth Neddermeyer at Caltech in 1936 while studying cosmic radiation. Because muons were not expected, I. I. Rabi famously remarked, "Who ordered that?" Muons produced in the upper atmosphere by cosmic rays reach the ground thanks to time dilation.

Decay and uses

Muons decay with a mean lifetime of 2.2 microseconds into an electron, an electron antineutrino and a muon neutrino. They are used in muon tomography to image the interior of large structures, in muon-catalysed fusion research, and in experiments probing for physics beyond the Standard Model, such as measurements of the muon's anomalous magnetic moment (g−2).
