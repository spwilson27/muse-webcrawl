Meson

CompositionOne quark and one antiquark
StatisticsBosonic
InteractionsStrong, weak, electromagnetic, gravity

In particle physics, mesons are hadronic subatomic particles composed of an equal number of quarks and antiquarks, usually one of each, bound together by strong interactions. Because mesons are composed of quark subparticles, they have a meaningful physical size, roughly one femtometre in diameter. All mesons are unstable, with the longest-lived lasting for only a few tens of nanoseconds.

History

The existence of mesons was proposed by Hideki Yukawa in 1935 to explain the strong nuclear force; the pion was identified in cosmic rays in 1947. Yukawa received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1949 for the prediction.

Types

The lightest mesons are the pions (π), followed by kaons (K), the eta mesons, and heavier states containing charm and bottom quarks such as the D and B mesons. Mesons are classified by their flavour quantum numbers, total angular momentum and parity in the quark model.
