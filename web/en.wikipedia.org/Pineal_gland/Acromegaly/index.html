Acromegaly

SpecialtyEndocrinology
SymptomsEnlargement of hands, feet, forehead, jaw and nose
CauseExcess growth hormone, usually from a pituitary adenoma
TreatmentSurgery, medication, radiation

Acromegaly is a disorder that results from excess growth hormone (GH) after the growth plates have closed. It is most commonly caused by a benign tumour (adenoma) of the pituitary gland. When the same process occurs before the growth plates close, it produces gigantism.

Signs and symptoms

The initial symptom is typically enlargement of the hands and feet. People may also notice enlargement of the forehead, brow and nose, protrusion of the lower jaw, and deepening of the voice. Because onset is slow, the condition is often not diagnosed for years. Complications can include type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, increased risk of colon polyps and cardiovascular disease.

Cause and diagnosis

In more than 95% of cases, acromegaly results from a pituitary adenoma that secretes growth hormone; the hormone in turn raises levels of insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1). Diagnosis is generally based on blood tests for IGF-1 and failure of GH to suppress after a glucose load, followed by MRI of the pituitary to locate the tumour.

Treatment

Transsphenoidal surgery to remove the adenoma is usually the first-line treatment. Somatostatin analogues, dopamine agonists and the GH receptor antagonist pegvisomant may be used when surgery is insufficient, and radiation therapy is a further option. Left untreated, acromegaly reduces life expectancy, but with treatment life expectancy usually returns close to normal.
