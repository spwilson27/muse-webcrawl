Growth hormone

SymbolGH1 / somatotropin
SourceAnterior pituitary gland
TargetsLiver, bone, muscle, adipose tissue

Growth hormone (GH) or somatotropin is a peptide hormone that stimulates growth, cell reproduction and cell regeneration in humans and other animals. It is a single chain of 191 amino acids synthesised, stored and secreted by somatotropic cells in the anterior pituitary gland.

Function

Growth hormone stimulates hepatic production of insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1), which drives growth of bone and tissues. GH also promotes protein synthesis, increases fat breakdown, and influences carbohydrate metabolism. Its secretion is controlled by the hypothalamus through growth hormone–releasing hormone and somatostatin, and peaks during puberty and during deep sleep.

Clinical significance

Deficiency of growth hormone in childhood causes short stature and is treated with recombinant human GH. Excess secretion, usually from a pituitary adenoma, causes gigantism in children and acromegaly in adults. Recombinant GH is also misused as a performance-enhancing drug in sport, where it is banned.
