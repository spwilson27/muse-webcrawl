Hypothalamus

Part ofDiencephalon
FunctionLinks nervous system to endocrine system; regulates homeostasis

The hypothalamus is a small region of the brain located below the thalamus and just above the brainstem. It is the principal link between the nervous system and the endocrine system, chiefly through its control of the pituitary gland.

Function

The hypothalamus regulates many homeostatic processes: body temperature, hunger, thirst, fatigue, sleep and circadian rhythms. Its neurons secrete releasing and inhibiting hormones that govern pituitary secretion, including control of growth hormone; magnocellular neurons also produce oxytocin and vasopressin, released from the posterior pituitary. The suprachiasmatic nucleus receives light information from the retina and helps entrain the body's daily rhythms, interacting with melatonin signals from the pineal gland.

Structure

Despite being roughly the size of an almond, the hypothalamus contains many distinct nuclei, among them the paraventricular, supraoptic, arcuate, ventromedial, dorsomedial and suprachiasmatic nuclei, each with specialised roles in appetite, stress response, reproduction and autonomic control.
