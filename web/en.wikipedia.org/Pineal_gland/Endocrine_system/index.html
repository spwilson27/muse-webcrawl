Endocrine system

The endocrine system is a messenger system in an organism comprising feedback loops of hormones that are released by internal glands directly into the circulatory system and that target and regulate distant organs. In vertebrates, the hypothalamus is the neural control centre for all endocrine systems.

Major glands

The principal endocrine glands include the pituitary gland, the pineal gland, the thyroid and parathyroid glands, the adrenal glands, the pancreas, and the ovaries and testes. The pituitary, often called the "master gland", secretes hormones such as growth hormone that regulate other glands. The pineal gland produces melatonin, which helps regulate sleep-wake cycles.

Hormones and signalling

Endocrine glands release hormones into the bloodstream, in contrast to exocrine glands, which secrete through ducts. Hormones act on target cells bearing specific receptors, and their release is typically controlled by negative feedback. Major classes of hormones include peptides, steroids and amino-acid derivatives.

Disorders

Diseases of the endocrine system include diabetes mellitus, thyroid disorders, and conditions of hormone excess such as acromegaly and Cushing's syndrome. The medical specialty concerned with the endocrine system is endocrinology.
