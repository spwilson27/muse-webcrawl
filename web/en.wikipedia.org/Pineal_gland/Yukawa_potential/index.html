Yukawa potential

A Yukawa potential (also called a screened Coulomb potential) is a potential of the form V(r) = −g² (e^(−kmr) / r), named after the Japanese physicist Hideki Yukawa. Yukawa showed in 1935 that such a potential arises from the exchange of a massive scalar field, and used it to model the strong nuclear force between nucleons, predicting the existence of the pion as the exchange particle.

Properties

Unlike the Coulomb potential, which falls off as 1/r, the Yukawa potential decays exponentially with distance, with a range set by the inverse mass of the exchanged particle. For a pion mediator this gives a range of about 1.4 femtometres, matching the size of atomic nuclei. In the limit of zero mediator mass, the Yukawa potential reduces to the Coulomb potential.

Applications

The same screened-potential form appears in plasma physics and electrolytes as the Debye–Hückel potential, and Yukawa-type interactions are used throughout condensed-matter and particle physics to describe short-range forces.
