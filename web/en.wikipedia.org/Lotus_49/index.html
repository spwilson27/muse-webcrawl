Lotus 49

The Lotus 49 was a Formula One racing car designed by Colin Chapman and Maurice Philippe for the 1967 season. It was the first car powered by the Ford Cosworth DFV engine, which it introduced at the Dutch Grand Prix at Zandvoort — where Jim Clark promptly won.

The 49 pioneered the use of the engine as a stressed structural member bolted to the monocoque, reducing weight and becoming the standard Formula One layout. Clark and Graham Hill drove the cars; Hill won the 1968 World Championship in the updated 49B, despite Clark's death that year in a Formula 2 accident. Variants of the 49 won races into 1970, and the DFV went on to dominate the sport for over a decade.
