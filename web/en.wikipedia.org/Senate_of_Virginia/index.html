Senate of Virginia

Senate of Virginia
Type: upper house, Virginia General Assembly
Seats: 40
Term: 4 years
Presiding officer: Lieutenant Governor of Virginia (President)
Meeting place: Virginia State Capitol, Richmond

The Senate of Virginia is the upper house of the Virginia General Assembly, the state legislature of the Commonwealth of Virginia. It consists of 40 senators elected from single-member districts for four-year terms, with elections staggered so that all seats are contested in years ending in 3 and 7. It shares legislative power with the Virginia House of Delegates.

Leadership and powers

The Lieutenant Governor of Virginia serves as President of the Senate, voting only to break ties; day-to-day floor leadership rests with the President pro tempore and the majority and minority leaders. The Senate confirms gubernatorial appointments, tries impeachments brought by the House, and must concur in legislation and budgets. Senators must be at least 21 years old, residents of Virginia for one year and of their district at election.

History

The Senate descends from the colonial Council and was established in its modern elected form under the Constitution of 1776. Its chamber in the Virginia State Capitol in Richmond was part of Thomas Jefferson's Capitol design as executed and later expanded.
