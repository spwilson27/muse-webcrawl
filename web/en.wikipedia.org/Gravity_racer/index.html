Gravity racer

Also known as: Soapbox car, soapbox racer, billy cart

Power source: Gravity only (no engine)

A gravity racer or soapbox car is a motorless vehicle which is propelled entirely by gravity. Races are held on downhill courses, and the vehicles — built in styles ranging from simple wooden carts to sophisticated streamlined machines — compete against the clock or in heats. In Australia they are commonly known as billy carts.

History

Soapbox racing grew out of children building carts from wooden crates — including soap boxes — in the early 20th century. The American Soap Box Derby began in Dayton, Ohio in 1933, after journalist Myron Scott observed boys racing homemade cars, and the first All-American Soap Box Derby was held in 1934. The event moved to Akron, Ohio, where the permanent Derby Downs track was built. Youth divisions and kit-car rules standardised the sport in the United States.

Design

Gravity racers have no propulsion system; speed comes from the slope, low rolling resistance and good aerodynamics. Rules typically limit total weight of car and driver, wheel size and materials, and require effective brakes and steering. Modern racers may use fibreglass or composite shells, precision bearings and carefully aligned axles, while traditional classes use wooden construction. Events exist for children, adults and corporate or charity teams.

Racing today

In addition to the Soap Box Derby, gravity racing takes place at many local festivals and hill events worldwide, and televised novelty races such as the Red Bull Soapbox Race emphasise creative vehicle designs and entertainment. Physics plays a central role: heavier cars carry more momentum, but friction, air drag and course handling determine results.
