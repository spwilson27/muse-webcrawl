The Araguari River (Portuguese: Rio Araguari) is a river in northern Brazil, flowing through the state of Amapá and forming part of the border region with Pará before emptying into the Atlantic Ocean north of the mouth of the Amazon. It is approximately 617 kilometres long.

Course

The river rises in the Tumucumaque Mountains near the border with French Guiana and Suriname, flows southeastward through rainforest, and receives tributaries including the Amapari and Falsino rivers. The town of Ferreira Gomes lies on its course, where a hydroelectric dam (Ferreira Gomes) generates power.

Pororoca

The Araguari was famous for the pororoca, a tidal bore that travelled upriver at the new and full moons, producing a continuous wave that could be surfed for long distances; Brazilian surfers rode it for record durations. Changes to the river in the 2010s, including buffalo ranching channels, siltation, and hydroelectric development, greatly diminished the bore on the Araguari, shifting attention to other Amazonian tidal bores.
