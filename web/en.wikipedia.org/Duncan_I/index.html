Duncan I (Scottish Gaelic: Donnchadh mac Crìonain; c. 1001 – 1040), nicknamed An t-Ilgarach, "the Diseased", was King of Scotland (Alba) from 1034 to 1040. He is the historical basis for the King Duncan of Shakespeare's tragedy Macbeth.

Life and reign

Duncan was the son of Crínán, lay abbot of Dunkeld, and Bethóc, daughter of King Malcolm II. He succeeded his grandfather Malcolm II on 25 November 1034, the succession passing through his mother because Malcolm had no surviving sons. Unlike the fictional Duncan of Shakespeare, who is an old man, the historical king was a young man in his thirties.

His reign included unsuccessful military ventures. In 1039 he led a major raid into northern England and besieged Durham, but the expedition ended in defeat. The following year he marched into Moray, the territory of his cousin and rival Macbeth, mormaer of Moray. Duncan was killed in battle at Bothnagowan, near Elgin, on 14 August 1040, and Macbeth succeeded him as king.

Family

Duncan's wife, whose name is uncertain (sometimes given as Suthen), bore him at least two sons: Malcolm, who became Malcolm III ("Canmore"), and Donald, later Donald III. Both fled after their father's death and returned to claim the throne after Macbeth's death in 1057.

In fiction

Shakespeare's Macbeth draws on Holinshed's Chronicles, which distorted the story: the play's Duncan is murdered in his sleep by Macbeth, whereas the historical king died in open battle. The kindly old king of the play has largely displaced the historical figure in popular imagination.
