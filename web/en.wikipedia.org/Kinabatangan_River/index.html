The Kinabatangan River (Malay: Sungai Kinabatangan) is the longest river in Sabah, Malaysian Borneo, flowing about 560 kilometres from the mountains of the interior eastward to the Sulu Sea. Its floodplain supports one of the most concentrated wildlife habitats in Southeast Asia.

Course

The river rises in the highlands of southwestern Sabah and flows northeast and east through lowland dipterocarp rainforest, passing the towns of the Kinabatangan District before reaching a broad delta of mangrove swamps on the Sulu Sea coast near Sandakan. The lower basin, some 23,000 hectares of floodplain forest, oxbow lakes, and swamp, is Sabah's largest remaining wetland.

Wildlife

The lower Kinabatangan is famed for wildlife viewing: it is one of only two places on Earth where ten primate species coexist, including proboscis monkeys, orangutans, and Bornean gibbons. Bornean pygmy elephants migrate along its banks, and the river hosts saltwater crocodiles, hornbills, and hundreds of bird and fish species. The Lower Kinabatangan-Segama Wetlands were designated a Ramsar site in 2008, and river safaris from villages such as Sukau and Bilit are a mainstay of Sabah ecotourism.

Conservation

Much of the surrounding forest has been converted to oil palm plantations, fragmenting habitat and confining wildlife to a riparian corridor protected in part as the Kinabatangan Wildlife Sanctuary (established 2005). Conservation groups work with plantation companies and villages on reforestation and wildlife corridors, particularly for elephants.
