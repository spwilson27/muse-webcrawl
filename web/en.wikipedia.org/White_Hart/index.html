White Hart

A white hart is a red deer stag of unusual white coloration, long associated in English folklore and heraldry with rarity, purity and adventure. The white hart was the personal badge of King Richard II of England, and it remains one of the most common names for inns and public houses in England, with hundreds of establishments called "The White Hart".

In legend

In Arthurian legend, the white hart (or white stag) often appears as a questing beast that knights pursue but can never catch, symbolizing an unattainable spiritual goal. In the Welsh Mabinogion, a white stag leads Pwyll into the Otherworld of Annwn. White deer also appear in Hungarian and other European mythologies as guides to new homelands.

Heraldry and pub names

Richard II adopted the white hart as his livery badge, depicting it crowned and chained with a gold coronet; carved examples survive in Westminster Hall and Wilton Diptych imagery. Because inn signs reproduced royal and noble badges, the White Hart became a widespread inn name from the medieval period onward.
