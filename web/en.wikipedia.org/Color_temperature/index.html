Color temperature

Unitkelvin (K)
Candle flame~1,900 K
Sunrise / sunset~3,200 K
Daylight~5,600 K
Overcast sky~6,500 K

Color temperature is a characteristic of visible light, expressed in kelvins, that describes the hue of a light source by comparison with the color radiated by an ideal black body heated to that temperature. Warm, reddish light has a low color temperature; cool, bluish light has a high one.

Principle

As a black body is heated it glows first dull red, then orange, yellow, and white, and eventually bluish white. The temperature at which its color most closely matches a given light source defines that source's correlated color temperature. The scale is used because many natural and artificial sources approximate black-body radiation.

Applications

Photographers and cinematographers use color temperature to set white balance so that colors record naturally. Lighting designers specify lamps by temperature: warm white bulbs around 2,700 K for homes, neutral white near 4,000 K for offices, and daylight-balanced tubes around 5,500–6,500 K for color-critical work. Astronomers apply the same idea to classify stars, whose surface colors range from red dwarfs near 3,000 K to blue giants above 20,000 K.
