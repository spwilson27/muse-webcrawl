Moped

A moped is a type of small motorcycle with an engine typically no larger than 50 cc and a maximum design speed of about 45–50 km/h (28–31 mph). The word is a portmanteau of the Swedish motor and pedaler ("pedals"): classic mopeds such as the French VéloSoleX and the Puch Maxi had bicycle pedals used for starting and to assist the engine, though many modern machines classed as mopeds lack pedals entirely.

A classic pedal-equipped moped.

History

Motorised bicycles appeared in the early 20th century, but the moped boomed after the Second World War as cheap transport in Europe: French Motobécane Mobylettes, German Kreidler and Zündapp machines, Italian models and later Japanese products filled the roads. In the 1970s, marques such as Puch, Tomos and Garelli sold widely in North America during the fuel crises. Famous non-pedal designs that blurred the category include the Honda Super Cub — strictly an underbone motorcycle — and step-through scooters descended from the Vespa.

Regulation

Most jurisdictions regulate mopeds more lightly than motorcycles: riders may need only a car or provisional licence, and minimum ages are as low as 14–16 in many places. In the European Union, the AM category covers two-wheelers up to 45 km/h. Electric mopeds have revived the category in the 21st century, and moped-style e-bikes occupy a similar niche.
