Image segmentation

Segmentation partitions an image into regions; here each object is assigned its own label and colour.

In digital image processing and computer vision, image segmentation is the process of partitioning a digital image into multiple image segments, also known as image regions or image objects (sets of pixels). The goal is to simplify or change the representation of an image into something more meaningful and easier to analyse: each pixel is assigned a label so that pixels with the same label share certain characteristics. Segmentation is a critical preprocessing step in object detection, medical imaging, and scene understanding.

Types of segmentation

Semantic segmentation assigns a class label (such as "road", "car" or "person") to every pixel, without distinguishing individual objects of the same class. Instance segmentation goes further, separating each distinct object instance. Panoptic segmentation, introduced in 2018, unifies the two tasks. Results are typically evaluated with metrics such as intersection over union (IoU).

Methods

Classical methods include thresholding (for example Otsu's method), edge detection followed by boundary completion, region growing and splitting-and-merging, clustering methods such as k-means and mean shift, graph cuts, active contours (snakes), and the watershed transform. Since the 2010s, deep learning has dominated: fully convolutional networks, U-Net (widely used for biomedical images), DeepLab, and Mask R-CNN for instance segmentation achieved large gains on benchmarks such as PASCAL VOC and COCO. Transformer-based models and foundation models such as the Segment Anything Model (2023) extended segmentation to open-vocabulary and promptable settings.

Applications

Segmentation is used in medical image analysis (tumor and organ delineation), autonomous driving, satellite and aerial image interpretation, video surveillance, industrial inspection and content-based image retrieval.
