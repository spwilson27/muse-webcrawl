In microeconomics, a consumer's Hicksian demand function (or compensated demand function) gives the quantity of a good the consumer would demand at given prices if their income were adjusted — "compensated" — so as to keep their utility constant at a specified level. It is named after the economist John Hicks, who introduced the concept in Value and Capital (1939) building on the work of Eugen Slutsky.

Definition

Hicksian demand is the solution to the expenditure minimization problem: the cheapest consumption bundle that achieves a target utility level ū at prices p:

h(p, ū) = arg minx p · x   subject to   u(x) ≥ ū

The minimized value of this problem is the expenditure function e(p, ū). By Shephard's lemma, the Hicksian demand for a good equals the partial derivative of the expenditure function with respect to that good's price. Hicksian and Marshallian (ordinary) demand functions are linked by the identities h(p, ū) = x(p, e(p, ū)) and x(p, w) = h(p, v(p, w)), where v is the indirect utility function.

Properties

Because utility is held constant, a Hicksian demand curve reflects only the substitution effect of a price change: when a good's own price rises, its compensated demand never increases (the compensated law of demand), since the consumer substitutes toward relatively cheaper goods. Hicksian demand is homogeneous of degree zero in prices and is not directly observable, because utility cannot be measured; it is nonetheless central to welfare analysis. Compensating variation and equivalent variation — money measures of welfare change — are computed by integrating the Hicksian demand or, equivalently, from differences in the expenditure function.

Slutsky equation

The Slutsky equation decomposes the total effect of a price change on Marshallian demand into the substitution effect (given by the slope of Hicksian demand) plus an income effect. The symmetry and negative semi-definiteness of the Slutsky substitution matrix, whose entries are the price derivatives of Hicksian demand, are key testable implications of utility maximization.
