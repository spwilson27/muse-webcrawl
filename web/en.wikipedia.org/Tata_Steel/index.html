Tata Steel Limited is an Indian multinational steel-making company based in Mumbai, Maharashtra, with its primary steel plants in India. It is a subsidiary of the Tata Group. Formerly known as the Tata Iron and Steel Company (TISCO), it was established by Jamsetji Tata and founded by Dorabji Tata on 26 August 1907, and its plant at Jamshedpur in Jharkhand was the first integrated steel plant in Asia.

History

Jamsetji Tata conceived an Indian steel industry in the 1880s, inspired in part by a lecture on iron and steel he heard in Manchester. After extensive geological surveys, the site at Sakchi — later renamed Jamshedpur — was chosen for its proximity to iron ore, coal, and water. Production of steel began in 1912. The company supplied rails for the Allied war effort in the First World War and grew steadily through the twentieth century.

International expansion

In 2007 Tata Steel acquired the Anglo-Dutch steelmaker Corus Group for about US$12 billion, then one of the largest overseas acquisitions by an Indian company, creating one of the world's largest steel producers. Its European operations centred on plants at IJmuiden in the Netherlands and Port Talbot in Wales. The company has since restructured its European business and expanded capacity in India, including the Kalinganagar plant in Odisha.

Operations

Tata Steel's crude steel capacity is around 35 million tonnes per annum across its Indian and international operations. It is among the world's top steel companies by production volume and is noted for its raw-material security, owning iron ore and coal mines in India.
