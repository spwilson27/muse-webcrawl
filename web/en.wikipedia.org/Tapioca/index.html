Tapioca

Tapioca is a starch extracted from the storage roots of the cassava plant (Manihot esculenta), a species native to Brazil that spread through South America and is now cultivated worldwide. The name derives from the Tupi word tipi'óka. Tapioca is a staple food for millions of people in tropical countries and is naturally gluten-free.

Forms and uses

Tapioca is sold as flakes, sticks, and pearls. Tapioca pearls are the signature ingredient of bubble tea (boba tea), originating in Taiwan, and are also used in puddings — tapioca pudding being a classic dessert in Britain and North America. In Brazil, tapioca starch is used to make beiju crêpes and pão de queijo (cheese bread), while cassava flour is toasted as farinha.

Production

Because bitter cassava varieties contain cyanogenic compounds, traditional processing — grating, pressing, washing, and drying — removes the toxins before the starch is dried and sifted into its commercial forms.
