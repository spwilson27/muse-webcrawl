Corpus linguistics

Corpus linguistics is the study of language as expressed in corpora (collections) of "real world" text. It is an empirical approach in which hypotheses about language are tested against large, structured collections of naturally occurring spoken and written material, typically analysed with the help of computers.

Methods

Corpus linguists use tools such as concordancers to examine keywords in context (KWIC), word frequencies, collocations (words that habitually co-occur), and colligations. Corpora are often annotated with part-of-speech tags and other linguistic information, and may be balanced to represent a language variety, as with the British National Corpus of 100 million words.

History

Although concordances of religious texts were compiled by hand for centuries, modern corpus linguistics began with the Survey of English Usage (founded 1959) and the Brown Corpus of 1961, the first major computerised corpus. John Sinclair's work at Birmingham and the COBUILD project of the 1980s used corpus evidence to produce dictionaries based on actual usage.

Applications

Corpus methods underpin modern lexicography, language teaching materials, translation studies, forensic linguistics, discourse analysis and natural language processing, where corpora provide training and evaluation data for language technology.
