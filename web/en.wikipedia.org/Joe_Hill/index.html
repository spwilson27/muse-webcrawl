Joe Hill

[Image placeholder: portrait of Joe Hill]

Joe Hill (born Joel Emmanuel Hägglund; 7 October 1879 – 19 November 1915), also known as Joseph Hillström, was a Swedish-American labor activist, a member of the Industrial Workers of the World (IWW), and a songwriter. He wrote many of the union's best-known songs, including "The Preacher and the Slave" (which introduced the phrase "pie in the sky"), "Casey Jones—the Union Scab", and "There Is Power in a Union".

Hägglund was born in Gävle, Sweden, and emigrated to the United States in 1902. He worked at a succession of jobs across the country — as a labourer, miner, and seaman — and joined the IWW around 1910. His songs, published in the IWW's Little Red Songbook, were sung at strikes and rallies throughout the country.

Trial and execution

In January 1914, a Salt Lake City grocer and his son were murdered by two men. Hill was arrested and charged, largely on circumstantial evidence: he had been treated that night for a gunshot wound, which he said he had received in a quarrel over a woman whom he refused to name. Despite an international campaign for clemency — including appeals from the Swedish government and from President Woodrow Wilson — Hill was convicted and executed by firing squad in Utah on 19 November 1915. His trial has been widely criticised as unfair, and his guilt remains disputed.

Legacy

Before his execution Hill sent a famous telegram to IWW leader Big Bill Haywood: "Don't waste any time in mourning. Organize!" His ashes were scattered in every state and on several continents. He became a folk hero of the labour movement; his life inspired the song "Joe Hill" (with the line "I dreamed I saw Joe Hill last night"), later performed by Paul Robeson, Pete Seeger, and Joan Baez, among others.
