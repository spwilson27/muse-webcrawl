Heavy cavalry

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Heavy cavalry was a class of cavalry intended to deliver a decisive shock charge against enemy formations. Heavy cavalrymen and their horses were typically protected by substantial armour, and the troopers were armed with weapons such as lances, swords, maces, or war hammers suited to close combat. Unlike light cavalry, which was used for reconnaissance, skirmishing, and pursuit, heavy cavalry was reserved for the battlefield clash itself.

History

Antiquity

The earliest recognisable heavy cavalry were the cataphracts of Parthia and later Sasanian Persia, in which both rider and horse wore complete armour of scale or mail. Hellenistic and Roman armies adopted similar fully armoured lancers, and the Byzantine Empire maintained cataphract units for centuries. Chinese dynasties likewise fielded armoured heavy cavalry during the Wei and Tang periods.

Middle Ages

In medieval Europe, the armoured knight — supported by the feudal system and equipped with the couched lance, stirrups, and a high-cantled saddle — dominated battlefields from the 11th to the 14th century. Charges by massed knights could shatter infantry formations, but disciplined spearmen, longbowmen, and unfavourable terrain increasingly blunted their effectiveness, as at Courtrai (1302), Crécy (1346), and Agincourt (1415).

Early modern and Napoleonic periods

With the rise of gunpowder, armour shrank to the cuirass and helmet, but heavy cavalry regiments — cuirassiers, carabiniers, and household cavalry — remained a battlefield arm. Napoleonic warfare saw massed heavy cavalry charges, such as those at Eylau (1807) and Waterloo (1815), where French cuirassiers attacked British infantry squares.

Decline

By the late 19th century, rifled firearms, machine guns, and artillery made the frontal charge prohibitively costly. Heavy cavalry survived into the First World War largely as a ceremonial and exploitation force, and its shock role passed to armoured vehicles; many historic heavy cavalry regiments converted to tanks while retaining their cavalry titles and traditions.

Equipment

Heavy cavalry equipment typically included body armour (mail, plate, or a cuirass), a helmet, a shield in earlier periods, a primary shock weapon such as a lance or kontos, and sidearms for melee. Horses were often larger and stronger than those of light cavalry and were sometimes armoured with a chanfron (face plate) and barding.
