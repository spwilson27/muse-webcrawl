Aneurin "Nye" Bevan (15 November 1897 – 6 July 1960) was a Welsh Labour politician, best remembered as the Minister of Health in Clement Attlee's post-war government who spearheaded the creation of the National Health Service (NHS), Britain's publicly funded healthcare system.

Early life

Bevan was born in Tredegar, Monmouthshire, one of ten children of a coal miner. He left school at 13 to work in the pits, became a trade union activist and miners' agent, and studied at the Central Labour College in London. Elected to Parliament for Ebbw Vale in 1929, he became one of the most brilliant and combative orators on the Labour left, editing the socialist paper Tribune and clashing repeatedly with his own leadership.

The NHS

As Minister of Health from 1945, Bevan also oversaw a vast public housing programme, but his monument is the NHS, launched on 5 July 1948. Against fierce opposition from the British Medical Association — which he overcame, in his phrase, by "stuffing their mouths with gold" through concessions to consultants — he nationalised Britain's hospitals and created a service free at the point of use, funded from general taxation. The NHS became one of the most popular institutions in British life and the defining achievement of the post-war settlement.

Later career

Bevan resigned from the Cabinet in 1951, alongside Harold Wilson, over the introduction of NHS charges for spectacles and dental work to help fund rearmament. As leader of the Labour left ("Bevanites"), he contested the leadership, and later served as shadow foreign secretary, famously renouncing unilateral nuclear disarmament at the 1957 party conference. He died of cancer in 1960. In a 2004 poll he was voted the greatest Welsh hero of all time.
