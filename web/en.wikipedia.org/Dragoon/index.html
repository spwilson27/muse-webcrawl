Dragoon

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Dragoons were originally a class of mounted infantry, who used horses for mobility, but dismounted to fight on foot. From the early modern period onward, dragoons evolved into conventional cavalry units in most armies, while retaining their infantry origins in organisation and sometimes equipment. The name is said to derive from a type of firearm carried by French dragoons: a short musket or carbine called a "dragon" — a blunderbuss-like weapon that spat fire like the mythical beast.

History

Mounted infantry appear in the mid-16th century; Gustavus Adolphus of Sweden employed dragoons effectively in the Thirty Years' War, and the type spread to all European armies. Early dragoons were essentially infantry provided with cheap horses for marching speed; they were cheaper to raise than true cavalry and useful for skirmishing, raiding, reconnaissance, and occupying ground quickly. The word also became a verb — to "dragoon" someone is to coerce them, recalling the dragonnades of Louis XIV, in which dragoons were billeted on Protestant households to force conversion.

During the 18th century the distinction between dragoons and other cavalry blurred. In Britain, several regiments originally raised as dragoons were converted to light dragoons, hussars and lancers; heavy regiments retained the dragoon name, including the Royal Scots Greys and the regiments that survive today as the Royal Dragoon Guards. France under Napoleon fielded up to 30 regiments of dragoons, and uniquely also raised dragoons who could fight on foot in emergencies.

Modern use

Although horse cavalry disappeared after the world wars, the dragoon title survives in several armies: the United States Army's 2nd Cavalry Regiment is styled "Second Dragoons", French armoured units include dragoon regiments, and mounted ceremonial duties in Britain and elsewhere are performed by dragoon-named regiments. In modern usage the name is largely honorary, attached to armoured reconnaissance and mechanised infantry formations.
