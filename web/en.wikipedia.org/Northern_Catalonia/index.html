Northern Catalonia

Northern Catalonia (Catalan: Catalunya del Nord; French: Catalogne du Nord), sometimes called French Catalonia, is the Catalan-speaking and Catalan-culture territory ceded by Spain to France under the Treaty of the Pyrenees in 1659. It corresponds roughly to the modern department of Pyrénées-Orientales in the Occitanie region.

Perpignan, the historic capital of Roussillon

History

The area comprises the historic counties of Roussillon, Vallespir, Conflent, Capcir, and upper Cerdanya, which had formed part of the Principality of Catalonia within the Crown of Aragon. After the Reapers' War, the 1659 treaty transferred them to Louis XIV's France; the village of Llívia remained Spanish as an exclave. French assimilation policies, especially through schooling in the 19th and 20th centuries, sharply reduced everyday use of Catalan, though the language survives and is taught in immersion schools such as La Bressola.

Identity

In 2007 the departmental council of Pyrénées-Orientales officially recognized Catalan alongside French in its charter for the language. Catalan nationalist usage treats Northern Catalonia as one of the territories of the "Catalan Countries" (Països Catalans), a claim with cultural rather than administrative standing in France.
