A-series and B-series

The A-series and B-series are families of standardised paper sizes defined by the international standard ISO 216, used throughout most of the world (notably excluding the United States and Canada, which use Letter and related sizes). Both series are based on a rectangle with an aspect ratio of 1:√2, so that halving a sheet along its long side produces two sheets of the next smaller size with the same proportions.

A series

The largest size, A0, has an area of exactly one square metre (841 × 1189 mm). Each successive size halves the area: A1 is half of A0, A2 half of A1, and so on. A4 (210 × 297 mm) is the world's most common office paper size, used for letters, documents and printing; A3 is twice A4, and A5 is half of A4, common for notebooks and booklets.

B series

The B series provides intermediate sizes between A sizes: each B size is the geometric mean of the corresponding A size and the next larger A size. B0 measures 1000 × 1414 mm. B sizes are frequently used for posters, books and envelopes where an A size is not suitable. Japan uses a variant, the JIS B series, defined differently. Related C-series sizes, defined in ISO 269, are used for envelopes designed to hold A-series sheets.
