Chauri Chaura incident

The Chauri Chaura incident occurred on 4 February 1922 in Chauri Chaura, in the Gorakhpur district of the United Provinces, British India, during the Non-cooperation movement.

A crowd of protesters clashed with police, who fired on them. In retaliation, demonstrators attacked and set fire to the police station, killing 22 or 23 policemen trapped inside.

In response, Mahatma Gandhi, who insisted on strict nonviolence, called off the Non-cooperation movement at the national level on 12 February 1922 — a decision that disappointed many Congress leaders. The British authorities prosecuted 225 people; 19 were hanged and others imprisoned or transported for life, though sentences were reduced on appeal after intervention by Madan Mohan Malaviya.

The incident is remembered as a turning point that delayed India's independence struggle strategy and is commemorated by a memorial in Chauri Chaura.
