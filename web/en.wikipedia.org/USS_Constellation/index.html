USS Constellation is a sloop-of-war, the last sail-only warship designed and built by the United States Navy. She was constructed at the Gosport Shipyard in Portsmouth, Virginia, launched in 1854 and commissioned in 1855. She is now preserved as a museum ship in Baltimore's Inner Harbor, Maryland.

The ship was long claimed — and for many years promoted — to be the same vessel as the famous frigate Constellation of 1797, one of the original six frigates of the US Navy. Documentary research in the late 20th century established that the surviving ship is a distinct, later vessel, though built in part with timbers and materials salvaged from the earlier frigate when it was broken up in 1853.

Service history

After commissioning, Constellation served in the Mediterranean and then, from 1859 to 1861, as flagship of the US Africa Squadron, patrolling against the illegal transatlantic slave trade off the Congo River; in 1860 she captured the brig Cora, freeing hundreds of captive Africans.

During the American Civil War she returned to the Mediterranean to protect Union shipping from Confederate commerce raiders. In later decades she served in various roles: a receiving ship, a training ship for naval cadets at Annapolis and Newport, and a transport that carried relief supplies to Ireland during the famine of 1880 and exhibits to the Paris Exposition of 1878.

Recommissioned during the Second World War as a reserve flagship and harbour training ship, she served as relief flagship for the Atlantic Fleet. She was decommissioned for the last time in 1955, exactly 100 years after first commissioning.

Museum ship

Constellation was brought to Baltimore in 1955 and designated a National Historic Landmark. After periods of deterioration and extensive restoration work in the 1990s, she is now operated as part of the Historic Ships in Baltimore collection, moored at Pier 1 in the Inner Harbor, where visitors can tour her spar, gun and berth decks. She is the second ship of the US Navy to bear the name Constellation in her own right, after the 1797 frigate, and the name was later given to an aircraft carrier and a class of guided-missile frigates.
