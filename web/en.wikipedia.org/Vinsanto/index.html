Vin Santo (also written Vinsanto) is a style of Italian dessert wine traditionally made in Tuscany from dried grapes. The name means "holy wine", possibly because the grapes were traditionally dried until Holy Week, or because the wine was used in the Mass. A related wine, Vinsanto, is produced on the Greek island of Santorini.

Production

Grapes — typically Trebbiano and Malvasia — are harvested and dried on straw mats or hanging racks in well-ventilated rooms (the appassimento process) for several months, concentrating their sugars. The raisins are pressed and fermented in small sealed barrels called caratelli, which are stored in lofts subject to wide temperature swings and left undisturbed for three to ten years, during which the wine develops its amber colour and flavours of nuts, honey, dried apricot and spice. Rosé versions made from Sangiovese are called Occhio di Pernice ("partridge eye").

Serving

In Tuscany Vin Santo is famously served with almond cantucci biscuits, which are dunked in the wine. Styles range from bone-dry (secco) to very sweet.
