Ten Great Campaigns

The Ten Great Campaigns (Chinese: 十全武功) were a series of military campaigns launched by the Qing dynasty of China in the mid–late 18th century under the Qianlong Emperor. The campaigns extended Qing control across Inner Asia and demonstrated the empire's power, though at enormous financial cost.

The campaigns

The ten campaigns comprised: two campaigns against the Dzungar Khanate (1755–1757) that ended with the conquest and destruction of the Dzungars; the pacification of Xinjiang (1758–1759); three frontier campaigns — in Sichuan against the Jinchuan tribes (1747–1749 and 1771–1776, counted as two), Burma (1765–1769), Taiwan (1786–1788) and Vietnam (1788–1789); and two campaigns against the Gurkhas of Nepal (1790 and 1791–1792).

Legacy

Qianlong celebrated the campaigns as the "Ten Complete Victories" and adopted the title "Old Man of the Ten Completed [Great Campaigns]". The conquests doubled the territory under Qing control, but campaigns such as those in Burma and Jinchuan were costly failures or pyrrhic victories, and the treasury was severely drained.
