Liu Bei (161 – 10 June 223), courtesy name Xuande, was a warlord of the late Eastern Han dynasty who founded the state of Shu Han in the Three Kingdoms period. A distant descendant of the imperial Liu clan, he rose from humble origins as a weaver of straw mats and shoes to become one of the three great rivals contending for control of China after the Han collapse.

Early life and rise

Liu Bei was born in Zhuo County (present-day Zhuozhou, Hebei). Though descended from Liu Sheng, Prince of Zhongshan, his branch of the family had fallen into poverty. As a youth he studied under the scholar Lu Zhi and formed friendships with figures such as Gongsun Zan. When the Yellow Turban Rebellion broke out in 184, Liu Bei raised volunteers and won early recognition for his service. In popular tradition—immortalized in the 14th-century novel Romance of the Three Kingdoms—he swore the Oath of the Peach Garden with his close companions Guan Yu and Zhang Fei.

Path to power

For two decades Liu Bei served under or alongside a succession of warlords, including Gongsun Zan, Tao Qian, Cao Cao, Yuan Shao, and Liu Biao, repeatedly rebuilding after defeats. After Liu Biao's death, Liu Bei allied with Sun Quan, and their combined forces defeated Cao Cao at the Battle of Red Cliffs (208), the turning point that divided China into three spheres. With the strategist Zhuge Liang, recruited during the famous "three visits to the thatched cottage," Liu Bei took control of Jing Province and then conquered Yi Province (modern Sichuan) from Liu Zhang in 214. In 219 his general Guan Yu was captured and executed by Sun Quan's forces, and the alliance collapsed. In 219 Liu Bei defeated Cao Cao's forces at Hanzhong and proclaimed himself King of Hanzhong; when Cao Pi usurped the Han throne in 220 and founded Wei, Liu Bei declared himself emperor in 221, claiming to continue the Han line.

Final campaign and death

Determined to avenge Guan Yu and recover Jing Province, Liu Bei launched a major campaign against Sun Quan's state of Wu in 221 despite Zhuge Liang's reservations. After initial gains, his army was decisively defeated by the Wu commander Lu Xun at the Battle of Xiaoting (Yiling) in 222, when fire attacks destroyed his linked camps. Liu Bei retreated to Baidicheng, where he died in 223. On his deathbed he entrusted his son and successor Liu Shan to Zhuge Liang in a celebrated act of confidence. He received the posthumous name Emperor Zhaolie.

Legacy

Historians note Liu Bei's resilience, charisma, and talent for attracting able followers; the official history Records of the Three Kingdoms portrays him as benevolent though less virtuous than his fictional image. In Chinese popular culture he is the archetypal righteous, people-loving ruler, contrasted with the cunning Cao Cao and the imperious Sun Quan.
