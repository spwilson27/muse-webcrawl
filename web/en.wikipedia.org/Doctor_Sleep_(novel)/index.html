Doctor Sleep is a 2013 horror novel by American author Stephen King and the sequel to his 1977 novel The Shining. The book reached the first position on The New York Times Best Seller list for print and ebook fiction (combined), print fiction, and hardcover fiction. Doctor Sleep won the 2013 Bram Stoker Award for Best Novel.

Background

King first mentioned the idea in November 2009 and confirmed in 2011 that he was writing a sequel following Danny Torrance as an adult. The novel explores what became of Danny, the child protagonist of The Shining, and themes of alcoholism and recovery that mirror the earlier book's portrayal of his father, Jack Torrance.

Plot

Following the events at the Overlook Hotel, young Danny Torrance is visited by the ghost of Dick Hallorann, who teaches him to imprison the hotel's lingering spirits in mental "lockboxes". As an adult, Dan descends into alcoholism to suppress his shining, drifting until he lands in Frazier, New Hampshire, where he joins Alcoholics Anonymous and finds work at a hospice, comforting the dying and gaining the nickname "Doctor Sleep".

A group of nomadic quasi-immortals known as the True Knot sustain themselves by consuming "steam", a psychic essence released when children with the shining are tortured and killed. Dan connects telepathically with Abra Stone, a girl with an extraordinarily strong shining, and together with friends they battle the True Knot, led by the charismatic Rose the Hat.

Adaptation

The novel was adapted into the 2019 film of the same name directed by Mike Flanagan and starring Ewan McGregor as Dan Torrance.
