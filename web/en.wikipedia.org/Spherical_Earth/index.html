Spherical Earth

The concept of a spherical Earth — that the Earth is approximately a sphere — dates to ancient Greek philosophy in about the 6th century BC and remained a matter of philosophical speculation until the 3rd century BC, when Hellenistic astronomy established the spherical shape as a physical fact.

Evidence in antiquity

Aristotle gave empirical arguments, including the circular shape of Earth's shadow on the Moon during lunar eclipses and the changing visibility of stars when travelling north or south. Around 240 BC, Eratosthenes estimated Earth's circumference with remarkable accuracy by comparing shadows at Syene and Alexandria.

Later history

The spherical Earth was accepted throughout the medieval world, in Islamic astronomy and in European scholasticism; the popular notion that medieval people universally believed in a flat Earth is a modern misconception. Globes such as Martin Behaim's Erdapfel (1492) and the circumnavigations of the sixteenth century further confirmed the idea. Modern geodesy refines the shape as an oblate spheroid and geoid.
