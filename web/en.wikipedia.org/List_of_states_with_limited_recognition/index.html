List of states with limited recognition

A number of polities have declared independence and sought diplomatic recognition as sovereign states, but have not achieved universal recognition. These entities often exercise de facto control over a territory and population but are not members of the United Nations, or are recognised by only some of its members.

UN member states not recognised by some states

Several United Nations member states are not recognised by at least one other member state. Examples include Israel, which is not recognised by a number of Arab and Muslim-majority states; North Korea and South Korea, which do not recognise each other; and Armenia, which is not recognised by Pakistan. China and Taiwan likewise do not recognise each other's claim to represent China, although Taiwan is listed separately below because it is not a UN member.

States recognised by no UN member

Somaliland, which declared independence from Somalia in 1991, and Transnistria, which broke away from Moldova, maintain functioning governments but lack recognition from any UN member state.
