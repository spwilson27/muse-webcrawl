Folk high school

Folk high schools (also called people's colleges) are institutions for adult education that emerged in Scandinavia and are particularly associated with Denmark, where the first was founded in 1844 at Rødding, inspired by the educational ideas of the clergyman and philosopher N. F. S. Grundtvig.

Characteristics

Folk high schools typically offer residential courses ranging from a few days to several months in subjects such as arts, crafts, music, politics, outdoor life and personal development. They generally have no exams, no grades and no formal entry qualifications; learning is based on dialogue, community life and the "living word" rather than textbooks, in line with Grundtvig's philosophy of education for life (livsoplysning).

Spread

The movement spread from Denmark to Norway, Sweden, Finland and Iceland, and influenced adult education elsewhere, including the Highlander Folk School in Tennessee in the United States. Today there are hundreds of folk high schools in the Nordic countries, many receiving state support.
