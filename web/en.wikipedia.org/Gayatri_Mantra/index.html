The Gāyatrī Mantra, also called the Sāvitrī Mantra, is a highly revered mantra from the Rigveda (Mandala 3.62.10), dedicated to the Vedic solar deity Savitṛ. It is named after its metre, gāyatrī — three lines (pādas) of eight syllables each. The mantra is traditionally attributed to the sage Viśvāmitra and occupies a central place in Hindu daily prayer.

Text and translation

The mantra reads: oṃ bhūr bhuvaḥ svaḥ / tát savitúr váreṇyaṃ / bhárgo devásya dhīmahi / dhíyo yó naḥ pracodáyāt — preceded by the invocation oṃ and the vyāhṛtis ("earth, atmosphere, heaven"). A common translation is: "We meditate on the glorious splendour of the divine Savitṛ; may he illumine and inspire our minds." William Jones, Monier Monier-Williams and many others have produced renderings, varying in literalness.

Usage

The mantra is traditionally recited at dawn, noon and dusk (the sandhyā prayers) by those initiated in the upanayana (sacred thread) ceremony, and is often repeated 108 times with a mālā (rosary). Historically restricted to initiated males of the "twice-born" varṇas, its recitation has been opened in modern times — reform movements such as the Arya Samaj and the Gayatri Pariwar promoted its universal use, and it is now chanted widely in temples, schools and public ceremonies in India and the Hindu diaspora.

Significance

Gayatri is also personified as a goddess, the consort of Brahmā and mother of the Vedas. The mantra is regarded in Hindu tradition as a distillation of the entire Veda and a prayer for the awakening of intellect and spiritual illumination.
