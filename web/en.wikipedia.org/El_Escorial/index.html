El Escorial

The Monastery and Royal Site of San Lorenzo de El Escorial

The Royal Site of San Lorenzo de El Escorial, commonly known as El Escorial, is a historical residence of the king of Spain built between 1563 and 1584 by King Philip II. Located in the town of San Lorenzo de El Escorial, about 45 kilometres northwest of Madrid, it is the largest Renaissance building complex in the world.

History and design

Philip II commissioned the building to commemorate the Spanish victory at the Battle of St. Quentin in 1557 and to serve as a royal mausoleum. Designed by Juan Bautista de Toledo and completed by Juan de Herrera, the austere granite complex follows a gridiron plan, traditionally said to honour Saint Lawrence, who was martyred on a gridiron. It combines a monastery, basilica, royal palace, pantheon, library, and museum.

The Royal Pantheon contains the tombs of most Spanish monarchs of the Habsburg and Bourbon dynasties. The library holds tens of thousands of volumes, including illuminated manuscripts, and its ceiling is painted with frescoes representing the liberal arts. El Escorial was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list in 1984.
