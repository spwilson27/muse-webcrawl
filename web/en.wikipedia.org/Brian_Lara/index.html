Brian Lara

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Lara batting for the West Indies

Brian Charles Lara (born 2 May 1969) is a Trinidadian former international cricketer, widely regarded as one of the greatest batsmen of all time. The left-handed batsman captained the West Indies and holds the world record for the highest individual score in Test cricket — 400 not out against England in 2004 — as well as the highest score in first-class cricket, 501 not out for Warwickshire in 1994.

Career

Lara made his Test debut against Pakistan in 1990. His 277 against Australia at Sydney in 1992–93 announced him as a major talent. In 1994 he broke Garfield Sobers' 36-year-old Test record with 375 against England at Antigua; after Matthew Hayden briefly took the record in 2003, Lara reclaimed it with his unbeaten 400 at the same ground in April 2004, the only quadruple century in Test history. Within weeks of the 375, he scored 501 not out for Warwickshire against Durham, still the only quintuple century in first-class cricket.

Captaincy and retirement

Lara captained the West Indies in three spells during a period of decline for Caribbean cricket, leading them to victory in the 2004 ICC Champions Trophy in England. He retired after the 2007 World Cup in the Caribbean, having scored 11,953 Test runs at an average of 52.88 and 10,405 runs in One Day Internationals. He was inducted into the ICC Cricket Hall of Fame in 2012. The Brian Lara Cricket Academy stadium in Trinidad is named in his honour.
