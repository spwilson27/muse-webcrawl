SpaceX Merlin

Merlin 1D engines on a Falcon 9 first stage

Merlin is a family of rocket engines developed by SpaceX for use on its Falcon 1, Falcon 9 and Falcon Heavy launch vehicles. Merlin engines use RP-1 and liquid oxygen as rocket propellants in a gas-generator power cycle. The Merlin engine was originally designed for recovery and reuse.

Design

The Merlin uses a pintle injector, similar to that of the Apollo Lunar Module descent engine, and a single-shaft turbopump fed by a gas generator. The engine was designed for simplicity and low cost, emphasising performance and reliability. The Merlin 1D, introduced in 2013, has a sea-level thrust of about 845 kN in its later "Full Thrust" form and one of the highest thrust-to-weight ratios of any rocket engine.

Variants

Variants include the Merlin 1A (flown on the first two Falcon 1 launches), the ablatively cooled Merlin 1B, the regeneratively cooled Merlin 1C (used on Falcon 1 and early Falcon 9), the Merlin 1D and the Merlin Vacuum (MVac), a larger-nozzle version optimised for the upper stage. Nine Merlin engines power the first stage of the Falcon 9 in an "Octaweb" arrangement; the Falcon Heavy uses 27 Merlin 1D engines across its three cores.

Operational history

Merlin engines first flew in 2006 on the Falcon 1. Famously, a Falcon 9 first stage suffered a Merlin 1C engine failure during the October 2012 CRS-1 mission but completed its primary mission, demonstrating the vehicle's engine-out capability. Merlin-powered boosters have since been routinely recovered and reflown.
