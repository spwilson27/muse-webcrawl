The Soviet–Japanese War, known in Mongolia as the Liberation War of 1945, was a military conflict within the Second World War beginning soon after midnight on 9 August 1945, when the Soviet Union invaded the Japanese puppet state of Manchukuo. The Soviet Union and Mongolian forces defeated the Japanese Kwantung Army in less than a month, in one of the largest campaigns of the war.

Background

At the Yalta Conference in February 1945, Stalin pledged to enter the war against Japan within three months of Germany's surrender, in exchange for concessions including southern Sakhalin and the Kuril Islands. The Soviet–Japanese Neutrality Pact of 1941 was denounced in April 1945. Over the summer, the USSR quietly transferred over a million men, thousands of tanks and aircraft across Siberia along the Trans-Siberian Railway.

The campaign

The invasion, commanded by Marshal Aleksandr Vasilevsky, struck on three fronts. The 1st Far Eastern Front advanced from near Vladivostok, the 2nd Far Eastern Front crossed the Amur and Ussuri rivers, and the Transbaikal Front swept from Mongolia across desert and the Greater Khingan mountains in a vast pincer movement. The Kwantung Army, hollowed out by transfers to the Pacific and home islands, was rapidly outmanoeuvred. Within days Soviet forces had taken Harbin and Mukden, landed in northern Korea, and begun operations to seize South Sakhalin and the Kurils — where the Battle of Shumshu, the campaign's last major fight, raged even after Japan's surrender announcement.

Outcome

Emperor Hirohito cited the Soviet entry, alongside the atomic bombings, in his surrender rescript to the armed forces. The campaign restored southern Sakhalin and gave the USSR the Kuril Islands — a transfer whose southernmost islands remain disputed by Japan as the Northern Territories. Hundreds of thousands of Japanese prisoners were taken to Siberian labour camps, many held for years. Manchuria's industrial plant was partly dismantled and removed, and the region was handed over to Chinese control, materially aiding Mao Zedong's forces in the resumed Chinese Civil War.
