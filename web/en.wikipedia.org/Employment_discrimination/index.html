Employment discrimination

Employment discrimination is a form of illegal discrimination in hiring, promotion, job assignment, termination, and compensation based on protected characteristics such as race, gender, age, religion, national origin, disability, or sexual orientation, depending on jurisdiction.

United States law

Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 prohibits discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, and national origin. Other statutes include the Age Discrimination in Employment Act, the Americans with Disabilities Act, and the Equal Pay Act. The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission enforces federal laws. Doctrines include disparate treatment and disparate impact, the latter established in Griggs v. Duke Power Co. (1971).

Other jurisdictions

The United Kingdom's Equality Act 2010 and European Union directives similarly prohibit workplace discrimination on enumerated grounds, with tribunals and commissions to hear claims.
