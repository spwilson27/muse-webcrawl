Inigo Jones

Inigo Jones (15 July 1573 – 21 June 1652) was the first significant architect in England in the early modern period and the first to employ the classical proportions and symmetry of Italian Renaissance architecture, derived from Andrea Palladio, in English buildings. He is also remembered as a pioneering stage designer for the court masques of James I and Charles I.

Jones introduced Palladian classicism to England with buildings such as the Queen's House, Greenwich.

Career

The son of a London clothmaker, Jones travelled in Italy (c. 1598–1603 and again 1613–14), studying the works of Palladio and Scamozzi and collecting drawings. From 1605 he designed sets and costumes for masques written by Ben Jonson — a collaboration that famously ended in a quarrel over whether poetry or scenery mattered more. Appointed Surveyor of the King's Works in 1615, he produced his two masterpieces: the Queen's House at Greenwich (begun 1616), the first strictly classical building in England, and the Banqueting House at Whitehall (1619–22), with its Rubens ceiling. He also laid out Covent Garden, London's first residential square, and designed St Paul's Church there.

Later life and legacy

Jones's career ended with the English Civil War; his royalist service led to fines and the loss of his posts, and he died in 1652. His influence, transmitted through his pupil John Webb and his annotated copy of Palladio's Quattro Libri, was foundational: a century later the Palladian revival of Lord Burlington consciously modelled itself on his example, shaping Georgian architecture in Britain and colonial America.
