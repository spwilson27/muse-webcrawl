Russo-Turkish Wars

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Russo-Turkish wars (or Russo-Ottoman wars) were a series of twelve wars fought between the Russian Empire and the Ottoman Empire between the 17th and 20th centuries. They rank among the longest series of conflicts in European history and drove the gradual Ottoman retreat from south-eastern Europe and the Caucasus as Russia advanced toward the Black Sea and the Balkans.

Overview of the wars

  • 1568–1570 — Ottoman attempt to take Astrakhan; Russian victory.
  • 1676–1681 — fought over Ukraine; inconclusive, Treaty of Bakhchisarai.
  • 1686–1700 — Russia joined the Holy League and captured Azov.
  • 1710–1711 — the Pruth River campaign; Ottoman victory.
  • 1735–1739 — limited Russian gains, largely reversed at Belgrade.
  • 1768–1774 — decisive Russian victory; Treaty of Küçük Kaynarca gave Russia Black Sea access and a claim to protect Ottoman Christians.
  • 1787–1792 — Treaty of Jassy; Russian frontier advanced to the Dniester.
  • 1806–1812 — Treaty of Bucharest; Bessarabia ceded to Russia.
  • 1828–1829 — Treaty of Adrianople; Greek independence effectively secured.
  • 1853–1856 — the Crimean War; Russia defeated by an Ottoman–British–French alliance.
  • 1877–1878 — Treaty of San Stefano and Congress of Berlin reshaped the Balkans.
  • 1914–1918 — the Caucasus campaign of the First World War ended both empires.

Significance

Across three and a half centuries the wars transferred Crimea, the northern Black Sea steppe, Bessarabia and parts of the Caucasus to Russia, eroded Ottoman control of the Balkans, and fuelled the "Eastern Question" — the diplomatic problem of managing Ottoman decline — until both empires collapsed in the First World War.
