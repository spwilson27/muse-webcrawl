Barcelona

Barcelona (Catalan: [bəɾsəˈlonə]) is the capital and largest city of the autonomous community of Catalonia in northeastern Spain, and the country's second most populous municipality, with a population of about 1.6 million within city limits and some 5.6 million in the metropolitan area. It lies on the Mediterranean coast between the mouths of the Llobregat and Besòs rivers.

History

Founded as a Roman colony, Barcino, the city grew into a major Mediterranean trading power under the Crown of Aragon. After declining in the early modern period, nineteenth-century industrialisation revived it; the Eixample grid district and Catalan modernisme produced the works of Antoni Gaudí, including the Sagrada Família basilica, Park Güell and Casa Batlló. Barcelona endured siege and bombardment during the Spanish Civil War, hosted the 1992 Summer Olympics, whose regeneration of the waterfront reshaped the modern city, and remains a centre of the Catalan independence movement.

Culture and landmarks

The Gothic Quarter preserves the medieval cathedral and Roman remains; La Rambla runs from Plaça de Catalunya to the port. Nine buildings are UNESCO World Heritage Sites, including Gaudí's works and the Palau de la Música Catalana. The city is home to FC Barcelona, whose Camp Nou is the largest football stadium in Europe. Barcelona is a global centre for tourism, design, publishing and technology start-ups.

Geography and climate

Barcelona sits on a plain between the Collserola ridge and the sea, with a Mediterranean climate of mild winters and warm, humid summers.
