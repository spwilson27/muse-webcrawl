Cameraless animation


Placeholder image: A strip of film painted for cameraless animation

Cameraless animation, also known as direct animation or animation without camera, is an animation technique in which images are produced directly on film stock, as opposed to any other type of animation where the images or objects are photographed frame by frame with an animation camera.

Techniques

Artists draw, paint, scratch or stamp directly onto clear ("blank leader") or black film stock, or apply materials such as sand, leaves or found objects to the film surface. Black film can be scratched with needles and blades to produce white lines and shapes when projected. The resulting work is often abstract and closely synchronised with music.

Practitioners

Pioneers include Len Lye, whose film A Colour Box (1935) was painted directly onto film, and Norman McLaren, whose works such as Begone Dull Care (1949) and Blinkity Blank (1955) were produced at the National Film Board of Canada. Other notable practitioners include Stan Brakhage, who painted and scratched film and even glued moth wings to stock for Mothlight (1963).
