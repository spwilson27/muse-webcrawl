The Kochi-Muziris Biennale is an international exhibition of contemporary art held in Kochi, Kerala, India. It is the first biennale of its kind in India and one of the largest contemporary art events in South Asia. The exhibition takes its name from Kochi and from Muziris, the legendary ancient seaport believed to have been located near the present-day city, a hub of trade with Arabia, Rome and China two thousand years ago.

History

The Biennale was founded by artists Bose Krishnamachari and Riyas Komu, who established the Kochi Biennale Foundation in 2010 with support from the Government of Kerala. The first edition opened on 12 December 2012, deliberately chosen as 12/12/12, and ran for about three months, drawing roughly 400,000 visitors. Subsequent editions were curated by artists including Sudarshan Shetty (2016), Anita Dube (2018) — the first woman to curate the Biennale — and Shubigi Rao (opened December 2022, after delays caused by the COVID-19 pandemic).

Venues and character

Exhibitions are staged in heritage properties and disused warehouses across Fort Kochi, Mattancherry and Ernakulam, most prominently the Aspinwall House spice warehouse, Pepper House, David Hall and Durbar Hall. Much of the art is site-specific, responding to Kochi's layered history of Portuguese, Dutch and British colonialism, spice trade and migration. The Biennale is noted for its strong public engagement: free or cheap entry, an extensive students' and children's programme, the Students' Biennale, and the Art By Children initiative.

Significance

The event has been credited with transforming Kochi into a major cultural destination and with boosting the region's tourism and creative economy. Despite recurring funding difficulties and controversies over management, it remains a landmark platform for contemporary art from the Global South.
