Winchester

Winchester is a cathedral city in Hampshire, England, lying on the River Itchen about 60 miles (97 km) south-west of London. Once the capital of Anglo-Saxon England, it is known for Winchester Cathedral, one of the largest Gothic cathedrals in Europe, and for Winchester College, founded in 1382.

History

The site began as the Roman town of Venta Belgarum. Under Alfred the Great in the ninth century it became a fortified burh and the principal city of Wessex; later Anglo-Saxon and early Norman kings were crowned and buried there. The Domesday Book was compiled at Winchester. After the Norman Conquest its importance gradually yielded to London, but it remained a major ecclesiastical and trading centre, famous for its wool trade and St Giles Fair.

Landmarks

Winchester Cathedral, begun in 1079, has the longest nave of any Gothic cathedral in Europe and contains the tomb of the novelist Jane Austen. The Great Hall of Winchester Castle preserves a medieval Round Table associated with the Arthurian legends. Other notable sites include Wolvesey Castle, the Hospital of St Cross, and Winchester College.

Modern city

Today Winchester is an affluent market and administrative city, the county town of Hampshire, home to the University of Winchester, and consistently rated among the most desirable places to live in the United Kingdom.
