EDGAR (Electronic Data Gathering, Analysis, and Retrieval) is the online database maintained by the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) through which publicly traded companies and other filers submit required disclosure documents. Since the mid-1990s most SEC filings — annual reports on Form 10-K, quarterly reports on Form 10-Q, current reports on Form 8-K, registration statements and insider trading reports — have been filed electronically through EDGAR and made freely available to the public.

History

The SEC began developing EDGAR in 1983 to automate the collection and dissemination of corporate filings, which had previously been available only on paper or microfiche. Electronic filing was phased in between 1993 and 1996, after which nearly all domestic reporting companies filed through the system. From 2002, filings began to be posted to the SEC website in near real time, and later enhancements introduced full-text search, interactive data in XBRL format, and a modernised EDGAR Filing interface.

Use

Investors, journalists and researchers use EDGAR to examine financial statements, proxy statements, beneficial ownership reports and material-event disclosures. Companies are identified in the system by a Central Index Key (CIK) number. The database is one of the world's most heavily used sources of corporate information.
