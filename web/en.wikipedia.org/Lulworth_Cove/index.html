Lulworth Cove

Lulworth Cove is a cove near the village of West Lulworth, on the Jurassic Coast in Dorset, southern England. Its almost perfectly circular shape formed where the sea breached a band of resistant Portland limestone and eroded the softer Wealden clays behind it, a classic example used to teach coastal geomorphology.

Geology and tourism

The cove lies within the Jurassic Coast UNESCO World Heritage Site, near Durdle Door and the fossil-rich cliffs of the Purbeck coast, and the nearby Lulworth Estate and Lulworth Castle. It attracts hundreds of thousands of visitors a year; offshore lie the remains of a submerged fossil forest visible at low tide.
