Aras an Uachtarain

Aras an Uachtarain (Irish: Áras an Uachtaráin) is the official residence and principal workplace of the President of Ireland. It is located in Phoenix Park in Dublin. The building, originally known as the Viceregal Lodge, was completed in 1751 to a design attributed to Nathaniel Clements.

History

The house served as the summer residence of the Lord Lieutenant of Ireland, the British viceroy, and later became the official residence of the Governor-General of the Irish Free State. When the office of President of Ireland was created by the 1937 Constitution, Douglas Hyde became the first president to take up residence in 1938. Successive presidents have lived there since, and the house hosts state visits, official ceremonies and the signing of bills into law.

The building and grounds

The residence combines a 1750s core with additions by Francis Johnston and later architects, including a wing built for the visit of Queen Victoria. State rooms display Irish art and craftsmanship. Guided tours of the house are offered to the public on Saturdays. In the grounds stands a bell that was rung by presidents, and a candle is traditionally kept lit in a window as a symbol of welcome to the Irish diaspora.
