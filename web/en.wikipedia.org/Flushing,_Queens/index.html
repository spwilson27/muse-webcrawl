Flushing, Queens

Flushing is a neighborhood in the north-central portion of the New York City borough of Queens. The neighborhood is the fourth-largest central business district in New York City and is served by the Flushing station of the Long Island Rail Road and the Flushing–Main Street station of the New York City Subway's 7 and <7> trains.

History

Flushing was founded in 1645 by Dutch colonists as Vlissingen, named after the city in the Netherlands, and settled under the Dutch West India Company. It is the site of the 1657 Flushing Remonstrance, a petition by English settlers protesting Governor Peter Stuyvesant's ban on Quaker worship — an early landmark in the American history of religious freedom. The Bowne House, where Quakers met in defiance of the ban, still stands.

Demographics

Flushing is one of the most ethnically diverse neighborhoods in the United States. Since the 1970s it has developed a large East Asian population; Downtown Flushing's Chinatown is among the largest Chinese enclaves in the Western Hemisphere, and the neighborhood also has substantial Korean, South Asian, and Latino communities. Main Street and Kissena Boulevard are lined with Asian restaurants, bakeries, and markets.

Landmarks

Nearby Flushing Meadows–Corona Park, created for the 1939 and 1964 World's Fairs, contains the Unisphere, the Queens Museum, Citi Field (home of the New York Mets), and the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center, host of the US Open. Other landmarks include Flushing Town Hall, the Voelker Orth Museum, and St. George's Church.

Transportation

The neighborhood is a major transit hub: the 7 train's eastern terminus, numerous MTA bus routes, the Long Island Rail Road's Port Washington Branch, and proximity to the Long Island Expressway and Van Wyck Expressway connect it to Manhattan and Long Island.
