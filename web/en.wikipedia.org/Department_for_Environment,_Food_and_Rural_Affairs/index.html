The Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs (Defra) is the department of His Majesty's Government in the United Kingdom responsible for environmental protection, food production and standards, agriculture, fisheries and rural communities.

History

Defra was created in June 2001 under Secretary of State Margaret Beckett, by merging the Ministry of Agriculture, Fisheries and Food (MAFF) with part of the Department of the Environment, Transport and the Regions. Its creation followed the 2001 foot-and-mouth disease outbreak, which had exposed weaknesses in MAFF.

Responsibilities

The department oversees policy on climate change adaptation, biodiversity, waste, water, air quality, animal health and welfare, plant health, farming, fisheries and rural affairs. Many functions are devolved in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. It sponsors executive agencies including the Environment Agency, Natural England and the Animal and Plant Health Agency.
