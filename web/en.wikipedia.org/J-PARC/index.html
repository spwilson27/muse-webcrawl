J-PARC

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

J-PARC (Japan Proton Accelerator Research Complex) is a high-intensity proton accelerator complex in Tokai, Ibaraki Prefecture, Japan. It is a joint project of the High Energy Accelerator Research Organization (KEK) and the Japan Atomic Energy Agency (JAEA).

Facilities

Protons are accelerated by a linear accelerator and two synchrotrons (a 3 GeV rapid-cycling synchrotron and a 50 GeV main ring). The beams feed a Materials and Life Science Experimental Facility with neutron and muon sources, a hadron experimental facility, and a neutrino beamline.

Experiments

J-PARC's neutrino beam is sent 295 km to the Super-Kamiokande detector as the T2K experiment, which observed electron-neutrino appearance from a muon-neutrino beam. The facility supports research in particle and nuclear physics, materials science, and transmutation of nuclear waste.
