Plagiarism detection

Plagiarism detection is the process of locating instances of plagiarism within a work or document. The widespread use of computers and the advent of the Internet have made it easier to plagiarise the work of others, and correspondingly have spurred the development of computer-assisted detection. Most approaches can be grouped into extrinsic detection, which compares a suspicious document against external reference collections, and intrinsic detection, which analyses the text itself for shifts in writing style (stylometry).

Methods

Extrinsic systems fingerprint documents using character n-grams, citation patterns, or vector-space similarity, then search large databases of published and previously submitted work. Commercial services used in education, such as Turnitin and iThenticate, maintain vast corpora of student papers, journals, and web pages and return similarity reports highlighting matched passages. Intrinsic methods look for inconsistencies in vocabulary, sentence length, and punctuation within a single document.

Limitations

Similarity is not proof of plagiarism: correctly quoted and referenced material, common phrases, and bibliographies inflate scores, so reports require human judgement. Detection is weakest against paraphrasing, translation plagiarism, and text generated or heavily edited with automated tools, which has prompted ongoing research in authorship analysis and AI-text detection.

Legal and academic context

Plagiarism is an academic and ethical offence rather than a crime in most jurisdictions, though it may overlap with copyright infringement. Universities typically treat confirmed plagiarism as academic dishonesty, with penalties from failing grades to expulsion.
