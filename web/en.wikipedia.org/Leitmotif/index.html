Leitmotif

A leitmotif or leitmotiv (from German Leitmotiv, "leading motif") is a short, recurring musical phrase associated with a particular person, place, idea or emotion in a dramatic work, especially opera. Each recurrence can reinforce the association, and motifs may be transformed — varied in harmony, rhythm or orchestration — to reflect developments in the drama. The term is most closely associated with the operas of Richard Wagner, above all the four-opera cycle Der Ring des Nibelungen, which weaves dozens of leitmotifs into a continuous symphonic fabric.

Development

Predecessors include the recurring "reminiscence motifs" in operas by Weber and others. Wagner's commentator Hans von Wolzogen popularised the term and published thematic guides naming the Ring's motifs — the Sword, the Ring, Valhalla, the Curse, and many more. Wagner himself preferred to speak of Grundthemen (basic themes) and warned against treating the labels too literally. Later composers such as Richard Strauss, Puccini and Mahler used the technique extensively.

In other media

Film composers adopted the leitmotif wholesale: Max Steiner's score for King Kong, John Williams's themes for Darth Vader and other characters in Star Wars, and Howard Shore's interlocking themes for The Lord of the Rings are standard examples. The term has also migrated into literature and criticism to denote any recurring image, phrase or theme associated with an idea.
