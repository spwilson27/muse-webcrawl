Innsbruck

Innsbruck (German: [ˈɪnsbʁʊk]) is the capital of Tyrol and the fifth-largest city in Austria. At the foot of the Karwendel Alps range and on the Inn River, it is well known for its winter sports. With a population of about 130,000, Innsbruck is a major centre for skiing and mountaineering, having twice hosted the Winter Olympics, in 1964 and 1976.

Geography

Innsbruck lies in the Inn Valley where the Wipp Valley joins it, near the Brenner Pass, about 30 km (19 mi) north of the Italian border. The Nordkette range rises directly above the city, and a funicular and cable car carry visitors from the centre to high alpine terrain.

History

The name derives from "bridge over the Inn". First documented in the 12th century, Innsbruck became a residence of the Habsburg dukes of Tyrol. Under Emperor Maximilian I it rose to prominence; his era left landmarks such as the Golden Roof (Goldenes Dachl), built around 1500 with 2,657 fire-gilded copper tiles, and the Imperial Palace (Hofburg). The city was strongly affected by the Napoleonic Wars and the Tyrolean rebellion of 1809 led by Andreas Hofer.

Sights and sport

Landmarks include the old town, Ambras Castle, the Hofkirche with Maximilian's cenotaph, and the Bergisel ski jump, redesigned by Zaha Hadid. Besides two Winter Olympics, Innsbruck hosted the first Winter Youth Olympics in 2012 and stages the annual Four Hills Tournament.
