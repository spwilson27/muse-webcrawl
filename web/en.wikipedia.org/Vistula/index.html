The Vistula (Polish: Wisła) is the longest river in Poland and the longest river flowing into the Baltic Sea. It is 1,047 kilometres (651 mi) long, with a drainage basin of 193,960 km², most of it within Poland. The river rises at Barania Góra in the south of Poland, in the Silesian Beskids, and flows north past Kraków, Sandomierz, Warsaw, Płock, Włocławek, Toruń, Bydgoszcz, Grudziądz and Tczew before emptying into the Vistula Lagoon and Gdańsk Bay near Gdańsk.

History

The Vistula valley was a major trade route from Roman times, when amber from the Baltic coast was carried south along it. In the early modern period the river was the artery of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth's grain trade, with huge quantities of grain floated down to Gdańsk for export to western Europe. In the Second World War the river was a major defensive line: the Soviet Vistula–Oder Offensive of January 1945 broke the German front along it.

Delta and branches

Near its mouth the Vistula divides into branches, the main one being the Leniwka and the eastern branch the Nogat, forming the Żuławy delta plain. In 1895 an artificial cut (Przekop Wisły) was dug to give the main stream a direct outlet to the Baltic and reduce flooding and ice jams.

Ecology

The Vistula is unusual among large European rivers for its relatively natural, braided middle course, a habitat for many bird species; much of it is protected under the Natura 2000 network. Historically it supported migratory fish such as Atlantic salmon and sturgeon, whose runs declined with damming, notably at the Włocławek barrage completed in 1970.
