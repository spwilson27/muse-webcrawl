Bánh mì (Vietnamese: [ɓǎjŋ̟ mî]) is a short baguette with a thin, crisp crust and a soft, airy interior, and by extension the sandwich traditionally filled with it. It is a product of French colonial rule in Indochina, combining French ingredients — the baguette, pâté, mayonnaise — with native Vietnamese fillings such as coriander, cucumber and pickled carrots.

History

Baguettes were introduced to Vietnam by the French in the mid-19th century. After the First Indochina War and the partition of Vietnam in 1954, migrants carried the sandwich south; in Saigon, shops such as Hòa Mã (opened 1958) popularised the modern filled bánh mì. Mass emigration after 1975 spread it to France, the United States, Australia and Canada, where it is now a staple of Vietnamese bakeries worldwide.

Typical fillings

The classic bánh mì thịt combines sliced pork (often chả lụa sausage, pork belly or grilled pork), liver pâté, head cheese, pickled carrot and daikon radish, cucumber, coriander leaves, chillies, mayonnaise and soy or Maggi seasoning. Breakfast versions use fried eggs (bánh mì ốp la), and vegetarian versions use tofu or seitan. The bread itself is often made with a proportion of rice flour, giving an exceptionally light, crackly crust.
