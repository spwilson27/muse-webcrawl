The Gentle Shepherd

AuthorAllan Ramsay
LanguageScots
GenrePastoral comedy / ballad opera
First published1725

The Gentle Shepherd is a pastoral comedy in Scots by the Scottish poet Allan Ramsay, first published in 1725. Set in the Pentland Hills near Edinburgh, it follows two young shepherds, Patie and Roger, and their loves Peggy and Jenny, in a plot of concealed identities resolved by the return of an exiled laird.

Background

Ramsay first issued the play in 1725; in 1729 he republished it with songs set to familiar Scottish tunes, turning it into one of the earliest ballad operas. Written largely in the Scots vernacular, the work was a landmark in the revival of Scottish literature after the Union of 1707 and remained popular on the Scottish stage for over a century.

Influence

The play influenced later Scottish writers, including Robert Fergusson and Robert Burns, and its shepherds' names became stock figures in Scots pastoral poetry.
