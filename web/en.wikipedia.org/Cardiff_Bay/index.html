Cardiff Bay

LocationCardiff, Wales
Created byCardiff Bay Barrage (1999)
Area~200 hectares (500 acres) of freshwater lake

Cardiff Bay (Welsh: Bae Caerdydd) is a body of water in the south of Cardiff, Wales, created by the Cardiff Bay Barrage in 1999, along with the wider waterfront district that surrounds it. The bay was formerly a tidal mudflat at the mouths of the rivers Taff and Ely, exposed at low tide, and was central to Cardiff's docks, once among the busiest coal-exporting ports in the world.

Development

The Cardiff Bay Development Corporation was established in 1987 to regenerate the derelict docklands. Its centerpiece, the barrage completed in 1999, impounded a 200-hectare freshwater lake and created some 13 kilometres of waterfront. The area is now home to the Senedd building, the Wales Millennium Centre, the Pierhead Building, and Mermaid Quay's restaurants and bars.

Ecology

Barrage construction destroyed tidal mudflat habitat used by wading birds; compensatory wetlands were created at Newport (the Newport Wetlands reserve). The freshwater bay itself has developed its own ecology and hosts leisure sailing and water sports.

Culture

Cardiff Bay features in the television series Doctor Who and its spin-off Torchwood, in which Roald Dahl Plass and a fictional rift in time are located there.
