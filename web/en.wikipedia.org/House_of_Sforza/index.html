House of Sforza

The Castello Sforzesco in Milan, rebuilt by Francesco Sforza after he became duke in 1450.

The House of Sforza was a ruling family of Renaissance Italy, sovereign rulers of the Duchy of Milan from 1450 to 1535. The family was founded by the condottiero Muzio Attendolo, nicknamed "Sforza" (from sforzare, "to force"), whose son Francesco seized Milan after the extinction of the Visconti line.

Rule of Milan

Francesco Sforza became Duke of Milan in 1450 and turned the duchy into a major Italian power, helping establish the balance of power formalized by the Peace of Lodi in 1454. His son Galeazzo Maria was assassinated in 1476; the most famous Sforza ruler, Ludovico Sforza ("il Moro"), governed from 1494 until the French invasion of 1499 during the Italian Wars.

Patronage

The Sforza court was a center of Renaissance art and learning. Ludovico employed Leonardo da Vinci, who painted The Last Supper in Milan, and the architect Donato Bramante. The family also commissioned the Certosa di Pavia and embellished the Castello Sforzesco.

Decline

Milan became a battleground between France and the Habsburgs. The duchy was briefly restored to Ludovico's sons, Massimiliano and Francesco II, but on Francesco's death in 1535 Milan passed to the Spanish Habsburgs. Branches of the family, such as the Sforza of Santa Fiora, survived for centuries.

Notable members

  • Muzio Attendolo Sforza, condottiero and founder
  • Francesco Sforza, first Sforza duke of Milan
  • Ludovico Sforza
  • Caterina Sforza, countess of Forlì
  • Bona Sforza, queen of Poland
