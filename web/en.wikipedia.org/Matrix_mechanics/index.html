Matrix mechanics

Matrix mechanics
Introduced: 1925
Founders: Werner Heisenberg, Max Born, Pascual Jordan
Key relation: [x, p] = iħ
Relation to wave mechanics: Mathematically equivalent (Schrödinger, Dirac, von Neumann)

Matrix mechanics is a formulation of quantum mechanics created by Werner Heisenberg, Max Born and Pascual Jordan in 1925. It was the first complete and correct formulation of quantum mechanics, extending the Bohr model by abandoning unobservable electron orbits and instead describing physical quantities by arrays of numbers — soon recognized as infinite matrices — whose non-commutative multiplication encodes the new physics.

Development

In July 1925 Heisenberg, seeking a theory built only on observable quantities such as spectral line frequencies and intensities, drafted his "Umdeutung" (reinterpretation) paper while on the island of Heligoland. Born recognized that Heisenberg's multiplication rule for the arrays was matrix multiplication, and with Jordan published the systematic "three-man paper" later that year. Paul Dirac independently developed his own version, q-number algebra. Wolfgang Pauli used the formalism to derive the hydrogen spectrum in 1926, a decisive confirmation. Heisenberg's introduction of the commutation relation

xp − px = iħ

led directly to the uncertainty principle in 1927. Erwin Schrödinger's wave mechanics, published in 1926, appeared entirely different, but Schrödinger and others soon proved the two theories mathematically equivalent; the modern Hilbert-space formulation of Dirac and John von Neumann unifies both.

Formalism

In matrix mechanics, observables are represented by Hermitian matrices (operators), and the state of a system by a vector. Time evolution can be viewed in the Heisenberg picture, where operators evolve according to the Heisenberg equation of motion while states remain fixed — the opposite arrangement to the Schrödinger picture, and still the preferred language in much of quantum field theory.
