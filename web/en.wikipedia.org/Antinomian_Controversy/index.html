Antinomian Controversy

The Antinomian Controversy, also known as the Free Grace Controversy, was a religious and political conflict in the Massachusetts Bay Colony from 1636 to 1638. It pitted most of the colony's ministers and magistrates against the followers of Anne Hutchinson and John Wheelwright, who emphasized the "covenant of grace" over the "covenant of works".

Background

Puritan colonists debated how a person received God's grace and how sanctification — evidence of salvation — related to justification. Hutchinson held meetings in her Boston home reviewing sermons and increasingly criticized ministers, particularly John Wilson, whom she accused of preaching a covenant of works. Her brother-in-law Wheelwright preached a fast-day sermon in January 1637 that escalated tensions with the authorities under Governor John Winthrop.

Trial and aftermath

Hutchinson was tried by the General Court in November 1637 and banished for her teachings; a church trial followed in March 1638, after which she was excommunicated and left for Rhode Island, helping to found Portsmouth. Wheelwright was also banished and founded Exeter, New Hampshire. Many of Hutchinson's supporters were disenfranchised or disarmed.

Significance

Historians regard the controversy as a key episode in the history of religious freedom, dissent, and women's roles in colonial America, and as a defining moment in the development of Puritan New England.
