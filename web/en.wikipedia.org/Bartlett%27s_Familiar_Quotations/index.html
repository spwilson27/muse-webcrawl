Bartlett's Familiar Quotations, often simply called Bartlett's, is an American reference work of quotations first published in 1855 by John Bartlett, a bookseller and publisher from Cambridge, Massachusetts. It is one of the oldest and most enduring continuously published collections of quotations in English.

History

John Bartlett compiled the first edition, subtitled A Collection of Passages, Phrases, and Proverbs Traced to Their Sources in Ancient and Modern Literature, largely from notes he kept while working in a university bookstore, where he was known for his memory of literary passages. The book proved popular and went through nine editions in Bartlett's lifetime. After his death, the publisher Little, Brown and Company continued the work with successive editors, expanding its scope to include speeches, songs, and contemporary figures.

Organization

Quotations are arranged chronologically by author, beginning with ancient sources such as Homer and the Bible and proceeding through modern writers, politicians, and public figures. Extensive author and keyword indexes allow readers to trace a phrase by subject or by a distinctive word. Later editions, including the eighteenth edition of 2012, added quotations from figures of popular culture and politics.

Legacy

Bartlett's has become a standard reference on library and newsroom shelves and is frequently cited as an authority on the origin and exact wording of famous sayings. Its name is used generically for quotation dictionaries in American English.
