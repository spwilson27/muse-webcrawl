Historic Houses Association

Formation: 1973

Type: Membership organisation

Headquarters: London, United Kingdom

Purpose: Representing independently owned historic houses, castles and gardens

The Historic Houses Association (HHA) is a British not-for-profit membership organisation that represents independently owned historic houses, castles and gardens across the United Kingdom. Founded in 1973, it acts as a voice for private owners of listed buildings and heritage properties, lobbying government on matters such as taxation, planning, tourism and conservation policy.

History

The association was established in 1973 at a time when many country houses faced severe financial pressure from maintenance costs, death duties and changing social conditions. Its founders sought to give private owners a collective voice comparable to that of larger heritage bodies such as the National Trust, while emphasising that privately owned houses are often lived-in family homes as well as heritage assets.

Membership and activities

Members include the owners of several hundred historic houses and gardens, many of which are open to the public for part of the year. The association publishes a members' handbook and operates a Friends scheme under which members of the public can visit participating properties, often free of charge on certain days. It also runs an annual garden of the year award, sponsored for many years by Christie's, and an award recognising excellence in education work at historic properties.

Role in heritage policy

The HHA campaigns on issues affecting heritage owners, including the maintenance relief and tax arrangements that encourage conservation, the impact of climate policy on old buildings, and the role of historic houses in rural economies and tourism. It works alongside organisations such as Historic England, the National Trust and the Country Land and Business Association.
