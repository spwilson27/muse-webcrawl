Ležáky

Ležáky was a small Czech village in the Pardubice Region, destroyed by Nazi forces on 24 June 1942 in reprisal for the assassination of Reinhard Heydrich, two weeks after the better-known massacre at Lidice.

Destruction

Ležáky was linked to the Silver A parachutist group: a resistance radio transmitter, code-named Libuše, operated from the village mill. After the Gestapo uncovered the connection, SS and police units surrounded Ležáky, set its eight houses ablaze and shot all 34 adults — men and women — at Pardubice that evening. Eleven children were sent to Chełmno extermination camp and gassed; two sisters deemed suitable for Germanisation survived the war in German families.

Memorial

Unlike Lidice, Ležáky was never rebuilt. Its site is a national cultural monument: grassed outlines and "hrobodomy" markers trace the destroyed homes, with a museum and annual commemorations each June.
