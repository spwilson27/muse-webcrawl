Stay-on tab

A stay-on tab (also called a stay-tab or ecology tab) is a type of opening mechanism for aluminium beverage cans in which the pull tab remains attached to the can after opening. It replaced the earlier detachable ring-pull tab, whose discarded rings created litter and a hazard to wildlife.

History

The detachable pull-tab was introduced in the early 1960s by Ermal Fraze. By the 1970s the discarded tabs had become a notorious form of litter. The stay-on tab was invented by Daniel F. Cudzik of Reynolds Metals and patented in 1975; it was first used commercially on Falls City beer cans and quickly became the industry standard worldwide.

Mechanism

The tab functions as a lever: lifting it first vents the can by breaking a small scored section, then continued pressure folds the larger scored opening into the can. Because the riveted tab never separates, it stays with the can for recycling, which is why it was promoted as an environmental improvement and is sometimes called the "ecology tab".

Variations

Variants include wide-mouth openings for smoother pouring and enlarged tabs for accessibility, as well as resealable designs used on some energy-drink and canned-coffee products.
