Liebig's law of the minimum

Liebig's law of the minimum, often simply called Liebig's law or the law of the minimum, is a principle in agricultural science stating that growth is dictated not by the total resources available, but by the scarcest resource (the limiting factor).

History

The principle was developed by the German chemist Justus von Liebig, building on earlier observations by Carl Sprengel. Liebig popularized the idea that a plant's yield depends on the nutrient present in the smallest amount relative to its needs, which drove his work on mineral fertilizers.

"Liebig's barrel"

The concept is often illustrated by "Liebig's barrel": a barrel with staves of unequal length can hold water only up to the level of the shortest stave, just as plant growth is limited by the nutrient in shortest supply. Adding more of an abundant nutrient does nothing until the limiting one is increased.

Applications

The law applies in ecology, where populations are limited by scarcest resources, and in economics and management by analogy. Modern research notes that the law is a simplification: factors interact, organisms adapt, and co-limitation by several nutrients can occur.
