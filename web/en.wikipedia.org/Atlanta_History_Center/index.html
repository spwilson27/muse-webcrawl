Atlanta History Center
Founded: 1926 (as Atlanta Historical Society)
Location: Buckhead, Atlanta, Georgia
Size: 33 acres
Includes: Swan House, Smith Farm, Cyclorama

The Atlanta History Center is a history museum and research center located in the Buckhead district of Atlanta, Georgia. Founded in 1926 as the Atlanta Historical Society, the center occupies 33 acres of gardens and trails and operates one of the largest history museums in the Southeast.

Attractions

The center's main museum building houses permanent exhibitions on the history of Atlanta, the American Civil War, and Southern folk art. The grounds include the Swan House, a 1928 mansion designed by Philip Trammell Shutze for Edward and Emily Inman, and the Smith Farm, an 1860s antebellum farmhouse complex. In 2018 the center opened the Lloyd and Mary Ann Whitaker Cyclorama Building, housing the restored painting The Battle of Atlanta, one of only two surviving cycloramas in the United States, along with the restored steam locomotive Texas, famous for the Great Locomotive Chase of 1862.

Archives

The Kenan Research Center holds millions of documents, photographs, and artifacts relating to Atlanta and Southern history, and the Cherokee Garden Library is one of the largest horticultural libraries in the country.
