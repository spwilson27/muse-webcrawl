Varuna

Varuna (Sanskrit: वरुण, Varuṇa) is a Hindu deity associated with the sky, oceans, water, and the moral and cosmic order (ṛta). In the earliest layer of the Vedas he is a supreme sovereign god, an Asura who upholds truth and punishes sin; in later Hinduism he becomes chiefly the god of the oceans and guardian of the western direction.

Vedic Varuna

The Rigveda praises Varuna, often jointly with Mitra, as the all-seeing king who set the sun in the sky, maintains the rivers, and binds wrongdoers with his nooses (pāśa). Worshippers confess their sins to him and beg release from guilt. Scholars have noted parallels with the Avestan Ahura Mazda and Greek Ouranos.

Later role

In post-Vedic religion Varuna's importance declined relative to Indra and the later gods, and he became one of the Adityas and a Dikpala (guardian of the west), riding the sea creature makara and presiding over rain, rivers, and the ocean. He appears in the Ramayana and Mahabharata, and in Tamil tradition as a rain god. Varuna is also found in Buddhism and Jainism as a directional deity, and a Japanese Buddhist form (Suiten) derives from him.
