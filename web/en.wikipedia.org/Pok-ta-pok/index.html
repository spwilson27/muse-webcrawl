Pok-ta-pok

OriginMaya civilisation, Mesoamerica
EquipmentSolid rubber ball
VenueStone ballcourt (tlachtli-type court)
RelatedMesoamerican ballgame

Pok-ta-pok (pitz in Classic Maya) is the Maya name for the Mesoamerican ballgame played by the Maya peoples of pre-Columbian Central America. Players attempted to pass a heavy solid rubber ball through a vertical stone ring set high on the wall of a stone ballcourt, striking the ball with their hips.

Rules and play

The game was played between two teams on a long I-shaped stone court. The ball, made of solid rubber and weighing several kilograms, could not be touched with the hands; players propelled it with hips, thighs, and knees, wearing protective padding. Scoring through the ring was rare and decisive; more commonly points were scored by faults.

Ritual significance

The ballgame had deep cosmological and ritual meaning, re-enacting the Maya creation myth in which the Hero Twins defeated the lords of the underworld Xibalba at ball. Ballcourts stood at the heart of major Maya cities such as Chichén Itzá, whose Great Ball Court is the largest in Mesoamerica. In some accounts, the losing or winning captain was sacrificed, linking the game to fertility and the movement of the sun.

Modern revivals

A modern version of the game is still played in parts of Mexico and Belize, and Maya communities have revived pok-ta-pok tournaments in Yucatán and Guatemala.
