Post-9/11 G.I. Bill

The Post-9/11 Veterans Educational Assistance Act of 2008, commonly known as the Post-9/11 G.I. Bill, is a United States law providing education benefits for military veterans who served on or after September 11, 2001. It substantially expanded the benefits of the original G.I. Bill, covering full tuition and fees at public institutions, a monthly housing allowance, and a stipend for books and supplies.

Provisions

The level of benefits depends on the length of active-duty service after 10 September 2001, with the full benefit available to those with at least 36 months of service or a service-connected disability discharge. Benefits may be transferred to a spouse or dependent children under certain conditions, and the Yellow Ribbon Program allows additional contributions toward tuition at private or out-of-state institutions.

History

The bill was championed by Senator Jim Webb and signed into law on June 30, 2008, with most provisions taking effect on August 1, 2009. The Harry W. Colmery Veterans Educational Assistance Act of 2017 (the "Forever GI Bill") removed the 15-year time limit on using benefits for many veterans and made other expansions.
