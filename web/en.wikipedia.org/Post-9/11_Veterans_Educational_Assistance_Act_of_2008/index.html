Post-9/11 Veterans Educational Assistance Act of 2008

Post-9/11 GI Bill
Enacted by110th United States Congress
Signed into law30 June 2008
Effective1 August 2009
Codified38 U.S.C. ch. 33
The act provides education benefits to veterans of service after 11 September 2001.

The Post-9/11 Veterans Educational Assistance Act of 2008 is Title V of the Supplemental Appropriations Act of 2008 (Public Law 110-252), which was signed into law on 30 June 2008. The law is an effort to pay for veterans' college expenses to a similar extent as the original G.I. Bill did after the Second World War, and it is commonly called the Post-9/11 GI Bill. Benefits took effect on 1 August 2009.

Provisions

The act provides benefits for members of the military who have served on active duty for at least 90 aggregate days after 10 September 2001, or who were discharged with a service-connected disability after 30 days. Benefits are tiered by length of service; those with 36 months of service qualify for the maximum benefit, which includes the full cost of tuition and fees at public institutions (and a capped amount at private institutions), a monthly housing allowance, and a stipend for books and supplies. Benefits may be transferred to spouses and children under certain conditions.

History and amendments

The bill was championed by Senator Jim Webb of Virginia, who introduced it on his first day in the Senate in January 2007. The Post-9/11 Veterans Educational Assistance Improvements Act of 2010 extended benefits to vocational and other non-degree programs. The Harry W. Colmery Veterans Educational Assistance Act of 2017 — the "Forever GI Bill" — removed the 15-year time limit on using benefits for those who left service after 1 January 2013.
