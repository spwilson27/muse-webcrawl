Montgomery GI Bill and the Post-9/11 era

The Montgomery GI Bill (MGIB) is a United States education benefit program for service members and veterans, established in 1984 and named after Congressman G. V. "Sonny" Montgomery. Service members who enrol contribute $100 per month for the first twelve months of service in exchange for monthly education payments after completing their service obligation.

Relationship to the Post-9/11 G.I. Bill

After the enactment of the Post-9/11 G.I. Bill in 2008, most veterans with service after September 11, 2001 became eligible for both programs and must elect which benefit to use; the election is generally irrevocable for a given period of service. The Post-9/11 benefit usually provides greater value for full-time students because it pays tuition directly to the school plus a housing allowance, whereas the Montgomery GI Bill pays a flat monthly amount to the student.

Eligibility

Eligibility generally requires a high school diploma or equivalent, an honourable discharge, and completion of the initial service obligation. Benefits are typically usable for up to ten years after separation and cover college degrees, technical training, apprenticeships, and licensing and certification tests.
