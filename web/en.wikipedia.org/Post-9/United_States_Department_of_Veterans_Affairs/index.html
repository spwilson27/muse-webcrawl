The United States Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) is a Cabinet-level executive branch department of the federal government charged with providing lifelong healthcare services to eligible military veterans at VA medical centres and outpatient clinics across the nation, as well as benefits including disability compensation, pensions, education assistance, home loans, burial and memorial services.

History

The department's origins reach back to benefits systems for veterans of the Revolutionary and Civil Wars. The Veterans Administration was created in 1930 to consolidate federal veterans' programmes, and it was elevated to cabinet status as the Department of Veterans Affairs on March 15, 1989, under President George H. W. Bush.

Organisation

The VA is organised into three main administrations: the Veterans Health Administration, one of the largest integrated healthcare systems in the United States; the Veterans Benefits Administration, which administers compensation, education (including the Post-9/11 G.I. Bill and Montgomery GI Bill), and loan programmes; and the National Cemetery Administration. It is among the largest federal employers, with several hundred thousand staff.
