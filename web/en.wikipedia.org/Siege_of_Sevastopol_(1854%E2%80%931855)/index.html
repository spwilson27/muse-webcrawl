Siege of Sevastopol (1854–55)

Date: 17 October 1854 – 9 September 1855
Location: Sevastopol, Crimea
Result: Allied victory

The siege of Sevastopol (October 1854 – September 1855) was the central operation of the Crimean War, in which British, French, Ottoman and later Sardinian forces besieged the Russian Black Sea naval base of Sevastopol.

Course

After landing in the Crimea and winning the Battle of the Alma, the allies invested Sevastopol but delayed a full assault, allowing the Russians under engineer Eduard Totleben to strengthen formidable earthworks. The siege featured the battles of Balaclava (with the Charge of the Light Brigade) and Inkerman in 1854, a harsh winter that devastated allied armies through disease and supply failure, and repeated bombardments in 1855.

Fall

On 8 September 1855 French troops stormed the Malakoff redoubt; the Russians evacuated the south side of the city that night, scuttling their fleet. The fall of Sevastopol led toward the Treaty of Paris (1856), which neutralised the Black Sea.
