Bolgar (Tatar: Болгар) is a town and archaeological site in the Spassky District of Tatarstan, Russia, on the left bank of the Volga River near its confluence with the Kama. It was one of the capitals of Volga Bulgaria, the medieval state of the Volga Bulgars, and is regarded as a holy place by Tatar Muslims as the site where Islam was accepted in the Volga region in 922 CE.

History

The city flourished from the 10th century as a major trading hub connecting Rus', Scandinavia, Central Asia, and the Islamic world, dealing in furs, slaves, amber, grain, and metalwork. In 922 the ruler Almish received an embassy from the Abbasid caliph al-Muqtadir — the mission famously recorded by the traveler Ahmad ibn Fadlan — and adopted Islam. Bolgar was destroyed by Batu Khan's Mongols in 1236 but was rebuilt under the Golden Horde, when its monumental stone architecture, including the surviving cathedral mosque and mausolea, was constructed.

After repeated sackings by Rus' princes and Timur's forces in the late 14th and early 15th centuries, the city's role passed to Kazan. The modern town of Bolgar, formerly known as Spassk, adjoins the ancient site.

Heritage

The Bolgar Historical and Archaeological Complex was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2014. It includes the 13th–14th century cathedral mosque with its Great Minaret, the Black Chamber, the White Chamber bathhouse, and several mausolea. Each year the site hosts commemorations of the adoption of Islam by Volga Bulgaria.
