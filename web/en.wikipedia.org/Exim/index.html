Exim

Exim relays messages between mail servers over the SMTP protocol.

Exim is a mail transfer agent (MTA) used on Unix-like operating systems. It is free software released under the terms of the GNU General Public License, and it aims to be a general and flexible mailer with extensive facilities for checking incoming email. Exim has been ported to most Unix-like systems and is the default MTA in Debian GNU/Linux.

History

Exim was written in 1995 by Philip Hazel at the University of Cambridge Computing Service, taking its name from EXperimental Internet Mailer. It grew out of experience with the Smail 3 MTA but was written as a new code base. Hazel maintained it until retiring in 2007, after which development continued under a team of maintainers.

Design

Exim uses a single large configuration file with a powerful string-expansion language, allowing administrators to express complex routing, rewriting, transport and access-control policies. Its built-in content scanning, support for SMTP authentication, TLS, DKIM, SPF and DMARC, and its access control lists evaluated at SMTP time made it a popular choice for Internet-facing mail servers. Internet surveys have repeatedly found Exim to be the most widely deployed MTA on publicly reachable mail servers.
