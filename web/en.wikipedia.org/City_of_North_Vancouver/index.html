City of North Vancouver

North Vancouver (city)
Province: British Columbia, Canada
Region: Metro Vancouver
Incorporated: 1907
Area: approx. 11.8 km²

The City of North Vancouver is a waterfront municipality on the north shore of Burrard Inlet, directly across from the city of Vancouver in British Columbia, Canada. It is the smallest of the three North Shore municipalities by land area, and is surrounded on three sides by the much larger District of North Vancouver.

History

The city was incorporated in 1907. Early growth centred on the shipyards and the ferry service to Vancouver; the Wallace Shipyards and later Burrard Dry Dock were major employers, building many ships during both world wars. The waterfront shipyards site has been redeveloped as the Shipyards District, with the Polygon Gallery and a public market at Lonsdale Quay.

Geography and demographics

The city occupies the lower slope of the North Shore Mountains along Burrard Inlet. Its population is in the range of 55,000–60,000 residents, making it one of the densest municipalities in Metro Vancouver, with significant recent condominium development around Lower Lonsdale.

Transportation

The SeaBus passenger ferry links Lonsdale Quay with Waterfront Station in downtown Vancouver. The city is served by TransLink bus routes; the nearest SkyTrain station is across the inlet.
