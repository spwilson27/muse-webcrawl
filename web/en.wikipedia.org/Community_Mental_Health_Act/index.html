Community Mental Health Act

Community Mental Health Act
Enacted: 31 October 1963 (United States)
Signed by: President John F. Kennedy
Official name: Mental Retardation Facilities and Community Mental Health Centers Construction Act of 1963

The Community Mental Health Act of 1963 is a United States federal law that provided funding for community mental health centers and research facilities in an effort to move treatment of mental illness out of large state asylums and into local communities.

Background

Post-war investigations exposed overcrowding and neglect in state mental hospitals, and new antipsychotic drugs made community treatment seem feasible. President John F. Kennedy, whose sister Rosemary had been institutionalised, proposed a "bold new approach" to Congress in February 1963.

Provisions

The act authorised federal grants for constructing community mental health centres that would provide inpatient and outpatient care, emergency services, partial hospitalisation and consultation, alongside facilities for people with intellectual disabilities.

Legacy

The law accelerated deinstitutionalisation: the state hospital population fell from over 550,000 in 1955 to a small fraction of that figure. Critics note that promised community services were chronically underfunded, contributing to homelessness and the criminalisation of mental illness; supporters credit the act with ending the era of mass institutional confinement.
