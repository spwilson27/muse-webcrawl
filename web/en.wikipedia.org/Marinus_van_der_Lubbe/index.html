Marinus van der Lubbe (1909–1934) was a Dutch communist and unemployed bricklayer who was tried, convicted and executed for setting fire to the Reichstag building in Berlin on 27 February 1933, an event the Nazi regime used to justify the Reichstag Fire Decree and the suppression of civil liberties.

Trial and execution

Arrested inside the burning building, Van der Lubbe confessed to acting alone, though the prosecution alleged a communist conspiracy and tried him alongside Ernst Torgler and three Bulgarian communists, who were acquitted. He was sentenced to death and beheaded by guillotine in January 1934. Historians continue to debate whether he acted alone or was set up by the Nazis; in 2008 Germany posthumously pardoned him under a law annulling unjust Nazi-era verdicts.
