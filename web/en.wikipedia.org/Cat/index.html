The cat (Felis catus), also called the domestic cat or house cat, is a small domesticated carnivorous mammal and the only domesticated species in the family Felidae. It is commonly kept as a pet, as a working animal on farms, and as a companion; global populations are estimated at 600–700 million. Cats were domesticated from the African wildcat (Felis lybica) roughly 9,000–10,000 years ago in the Fertile Crescent, most likely as grain stores attracted rodents and the cats that hunted them.

Biology

Cats have strong, flexible bodies, retractable claws, keen night vision (aided by a tapetum lucidum), excellent hearing extending into ultrasound, and a highly developed sense of smell. As obligate carnivores they require nutrients found in animal tissue, such as taurine, and cannot taste sweetness. Their shearing cheek teeth are the carnassials. Cats communicate through meowing (directed largely at humans), purring, trilling, hissing, body posture, and scent marking. They sleep 12–16 hours a day on average and spend much of their waking time grooming.

Domestication and breeds

Archaeological evidence includes a cat buried with a human on Cyprus about 9,500 years ago and the elaborate reverence for cats in ancient Egypt, where the goddess Bastet was depicted as a cat. Selective breeding has produced some 70 recognised breeds, from the Maine Coon to the hairless Sphynx, though most pet cats are non-pedigree "domestic shorthairs". Genetic studies show domestic cats differ only slightly from wildcats and continue to interbreed with them.

Behaviour

Cats are territorial, crepuscular hunters with a strong predatory drive toward small mammals and birds — a trait that makes free-ranging cats a significant threat to wildlife in many ecosystems, and a source of conservation controversy. Socially they are more flexible than once thought: feral cats form colonies around food sources, and domestic cats form strong attachments to people.

Health

Indoor cats commonly live 13–17 years; the oldest recorded cat, Creme Puff, reached 38. Common health issues include chronic kidney disease, hyperthyroidism, dental disease and obesity. Cats are routinely vaccinated against rabies and feline viral diseases, and neutering is widely practised to control feral populations.

In culture

Cats have been revered, feared and adored across cultures: worshipped in Egypt, associated with witchcraft in medieval Europe, symbols of good luck in Japan (the maneki-neko), and fixtures of the modern internet. They are consistently among the world's most popular pets.
