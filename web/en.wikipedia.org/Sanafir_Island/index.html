Sanafir Island

Sanafir Island (Arabic: جزيرة صنافير) is an island in the Strait of Tiran, at the entrance to the Gulf of Aqaba in the Red Sea. It lies east of the larger Tiran Island and covers roughly 33 square kilometres. Together, the two islands control the straits through which shipping reaches the ports of Aqaba in Jordan and Eilat in Israel.

Sovereignty dispute

Sanafir, like Tiran, was administered by Egypt for most of the 20th century, having been placed under Egyptian control in the early 1950s with Saudi agreement. The islands were occupied by Israel during the 1967 Six-Day War and returned to Egypt under the Egypt–Israel peace treaty.

In April 2016 Egypt and Saudi Arabia signed a maritime border agreement recognising Tiran and Sanafir as Saudi territory. The deal provoked protests in Egypt and legal challenges arguing that the islands were historically Egyptian, but Egypt's parliament ratified the agreement in 2017 and the transfer was upheld. The transfer became central to regional diplomacy, as Saudi recognition of the islands' status under the peace treaty framework bears on freedom of navigation through the strait and on potential Saudi–Israeli normalisation.

Geography

The island is arid, uninhabited and fringed by reefs; the surrounding waters are noted for coral reefs and diving sites. It forms part of the Ras Muhammad protected area system administered by Egypt.
