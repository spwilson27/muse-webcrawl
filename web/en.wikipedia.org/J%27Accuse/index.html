J'Accuse

J'Accuse...!
AuthorÉmile Zola
Published13 January 1898, in L'Aurore
SubjectThe Dreyfus affair

"J'Accuse...!" ("I accuse...!") is an open letter by the French novelist Émile Zola, published on the front page of the Paris newspaper L'Aurore on 13 January 1898. Addressed to President Félix Faure, it accused the French government and army of antisemitism and of wrongfully convicting Captain Alfred Dreyfus of treason.

Content

Zola argued that Dreyfus's conviction rested on forged evidence, that the real traitor, Ferdinand Walsin Esterhazy, had been acquitted by a military court acting under orders, and that army leaders and handwriting "experts" had conspired to conceal the truth. The letter's headline, devised by editor Georges Clemenceau, and its repeated refrain "J'accuse" made it famous.

Consequences

Zola was prosecuted for libel, convicted, and fled to England to avoid prison. The letter polarized France, galvanized the Dreyfusard camp, and helped force a retrial; Dreyfus was pardoned in 1899 and fully exonerated in 1906. "J'accuse" has since become a general expression for a forceful public denunciation of injustice.
