DMAIC
Type: Process improvement methodology
Origin: Six Sigma
Phases: Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, Control

DMAIC (pronounced duh-MAY-ick) is a data-driven improvement cycle used for improving, optimizing and stabilizing business processes and designs. It forms the core process-improvement methodology of Six Sigma.

Phases

The five phases are Define the problem and project goals, Measure key aspects of the current process, Analyze the data to find root causes, Improve the process based on that analysis, and Control the future process to hold the gains.

History

DMAIC evolved from the plan-do-check-act cycle and from quality practices developed at Motorola in the 1980s, later popularized by General Electric under Jack Welch in the 1990s.

Use

It is used on existing processes, whereas DMADV is used for designing new products or processes. Tools include process mapping, Pareto charts, fishbone diagrams, and statistical process control.
