Killers of the Flower Moon (book)

Author: David Grann
Published: 2017 (Doubleday)
Genre: True crime, history
Pages: 400

Killers of the Flower Moon: The Osage Murders and the Birth of the FBI is a 2017 nonfiction book by American journalist David Grann. It investigates a series of murders of wealthy Osage Nation members in Oklahoma during the 1920s, after oil was discovered beneath their land.

Background

Following the discovery of oil on Osage land, tribal members became among the wealthiest people per capita in the world. Under a system of court-appointed guardians, however, many Osage were required to have white guardians manage their money. Between 1921 and 1926 dozens of Osage were murdered or died under suspicious circumstances in what became known as the Osage "Reign of Terror."

Investigation

The newly formed Bureau of Investigation, led by J. Edgar Hoover, sent a team of undercover agents to Osage County, including Tom White, a former Texas Ranger. Their investigation uncovered a conspiracy orchestrated by rancher William Hale, the self-styled "King of the Osage Hills," who arranged the murders of members of Mollie Burkhart's family to seize their headrights. Hale and several accomplices were eventually convicted.

Reception and adaptation

The book was a bestseller and a finalist for the National Book Award. It was adapted into a 2023 film directed by Martin Scorsese, starring Leonardo DiCaprio, Lily Gladstone, and Robert De Niro.
