Muzzle velocity

Muzzle velocity is the speed of a projectile with respect to the muzzle at the moment it leaves the barrel of a gun or other firearm. It is typically measured in metres per second (m/s) or feet per second (ft/s) and is a key figure in ballistics, determining a projectile's trajectory, range, and kinetic energy.

Typical values

Velocities vary enormously: subsonic pistol rounds may travel at around 260–350 m/s, typical intermediate rifle cartridges at 700–900 m/s, and tank armour-piercing rounds can exceed 1,700 m/s. The fastest projectiles are produced not by guns but by light-gas guns used in research, which reach several kilometres per second.

Factors

Muzzle velocity depends on the propellant type and charge, barrel length (longer barrels generally allow more complete gas expansion), projectile mass (lighter projectiles are faster for the same energy), and internal friction and fit. It is measured with chronographs, Doppler radar, or high-speed photography.

Importance

Higher muzzle velocity produces a flatter trajectory, reducing the need to compensate for bullet drop and wind drift, and increases the energy delivered to the target. For a given projectile mass, kinetic energy grows with the square of velocity.
