Samatha

Samatha (Pāli; Sanskrit: śamatha, "calm abiding") is a Buddhist meditation practice focused on calming and unifying the mind through sustained concentration on a single object. It is traditionally paired with vipassanā (insight meditation): samatha develops serenity and the absorptions (jhāna), while vipassanā develops insight into the nature of phenomena.

Practice

The meditator selects one of the traditional meditation subjects — classically forty, listed in texts such as the Visuddhimagga — including the breath (ānāpānasati), coloured discs (kasiṇa), contemplation of the body, and the four divine abidings (brahmavihāra) such as loving-kindness (mettā). Attention is returned repeatedly to the object until the five hindrances are suppressed and the mind enters access concentration and then the jhānas, characterized by increasing stillness, rapture and equanimity.

Role in the path

Samatha is associated with the development of right concentration in the Noble Eightfold Path. Commentarial tradition holds that the calm and stability it produces form the basis for effective insight practice, though traditions differ on how much samatha is required before turning to vipassanā; some modern movements teach "dry insight" with only momentary concentration. Analogous calm-abiding practices exist in Tibetan Buddhism (shiné) and East Asian traditions.
