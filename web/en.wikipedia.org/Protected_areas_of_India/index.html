Protected areas of India

The protected areas of India comprise a network of national parks, wildlife sanctuaries, conservation reserves, and community reserves established under the Wildlife (Protection) Act of 1972 and related legislation. As of the mid-2020s the network includes more than one hundred national parks, over five hundred wildlife sanctuaries, and dozens of conservation and community reserves, together covering roughly five percent of India's geographical area. Many of the reserves protect habitats of flagship species such as the Bengal tiger, Indian elephant, Asiatic lion, and greater one-horned rhinoceros.

Categories

National parks receive the strictest protection: grazing and most human activity are prohibited, and rights within them must be settled or extinguished. Wildlife sanctuaries protect species and habitat while permitting certain regulated activities. Conservation reserves and community reserves, introduced by a 2002 amendment, buffer existing parks and protect corridors, often on government or community land. Overlaid on this system are Project Tiger reserves (launched 1973), biosphere reserves under UNESCO's Man and the Biosphere programme, and Ramsar-listed wetlands.

History

India's first national park, Hailey National Park — today Jim Corbett National Park — was established in 1936 in what is now Uttarakhand. The post-independence framework took shape with the Wildlife (Protection) Act of 1972, which standardized categories and banned hunting of most wild animals. The tiger reserves created under Project Tiger now number more than fifty and anchor much of the protected-area network; India's wild tiger population, surveyed by camera traps, has become a high-profile conservation success, though pressures from habitat fragmentation, infrastructure, and human–wildlife conflict remain intense.
