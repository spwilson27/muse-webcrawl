Davis Strait

The Davis Strait is a northern arm of the Atlantic Ocean lying between mid-western Greenland and Baffin Island in Nunavut, Canada. It connects Baffin Bay to the north with the Labrador Sea to the south, and forms part of the route of the Northwest Passage. The strait is named after the English explorer John Davis, who sailed its waters in the 1580s while searching for a northwest passage to Asia.

Geography

The strait is roughly 650 kilometres long and between about 340 and 1,075 kilometres wide. Its waters are relatively shallow compared with Baffin Bay because of a submarine ridge running between Greenland and Baffin Island at a depth of a few hundred metres. The cold Baffin Island Current flows southward along the Canadian side, carrying icebergs calved from Greenland's glaciers toward the Labrador Sea, while the warmer West Greenland Current flows north along the Greenland coast.

History and use

Indigenous peoples have lived along the strait's shores for millennia, and Norse and later European explorers travelled it in search of a route to the Pacific. In the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries the strait was an important whaling ground. Today it is fished commercially, including for Greenland halibut and shrimp, and the waters are considered prospective for oil and gas, though exploration has been limited. The strait separates the Canadian territory of Nunavut from Greenland, an autonomous territory of Denmark.
