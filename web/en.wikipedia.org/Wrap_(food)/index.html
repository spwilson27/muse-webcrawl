Wrap (food)

Wrap
TypeSandwich
CourseMain course, snack
Place of originUnited States (modern form)
Main ingredientsFlatbread (usually a flour tortilla), fillings, sauce

A wrap is a form of sandwich made with a soft flatbread rolled around a filling. The usual flatbread is a flour tortilla, but wraps can also be made with lavash, pita, naan, or other soft flatbreads. Fillings typically include sliced meat or poultry, lettuce and other salad vegetables, cheese, and a sauce or dressing. A wrap is distinguished from a burrito principally in that burritos are usually served hot with cooked fillings such as rice and beans, while wraps were popularized as cold or room-temperature deli-style sandwiches; in practice the terms overlap considerably.

History

Rolling food in flatbread is ancient worldwide — Mexican burritos and tacos, Greek gyros and souvlaki in pita, Middle Eastern shawarma in flatbread, Turkish dürüm, and Armenian lavash rolls are all antecedents. The modern "wrap" as a named menu category emerged in the United States in the early 1980s–1990s. One claimant is the Stamford, Connecticut, restaurant Bobby Valentine's Sports Gallery Cafe, credited with serving a "wrap" around 1982; the form spread through California and New York delis and cafes in the 1990s, marketed as a lighter alternative to the sliced-bread sandwich, and major sandwich chains added wraps to their menus. Bobby Valentine himself has popularly been credited with coining the term.

Variations

Wraps are commonly made with spinach-, tomato- or herb-flavoured tortillas, grilled briefly to seal, and sliced diagonally. Popular fillings include Caesar chicken, turkey club, buffalo chicken, tuna salad, falafel and hummus for vegetarian versions, and breakfast fillings of egg and bacon. Lettuce wraps, using large leaves in place of bread, are a low-carbohydrate variant associated with Asian cuisines such as Chinese san choy bau. In the United Kingdom the wrap became a standard supermarket sandwich-format item from the late 1990s onward.
