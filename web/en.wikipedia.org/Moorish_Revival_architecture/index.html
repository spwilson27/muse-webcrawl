Moorish Revival architecture

Moorish Revival or Neo-Moorish is one of the exotic revival architectural styles that were adopted by architects of Europe and the Americas in the wake of Romanticist interest in Islamic architecture. It peaked in popularity in the mid-19th century, drawing on the architecture of Moorish Spain and the wider Islamic world.

StyleExotic revival architecture
Period19th – early 20th century
FeaturesHorseshoe arches, minaret-like towers, arabesque ornament

Characteristics

Typical features include horseshoe and ogee arches, scalloped domes and onion domes, slender minaret-like towers, pierced screens, and richly patterned tile and stucco ornament inspired by buildings such as the Alhambra in Granada.

Spread

The style was especially popular for synagogues in Central Europe and the United States, where it evoked a golden age of Jewish life in medieval Spain; examples include the Dohány Street Synagogue in Budapest (1859). It was also used for theatres, exhibition halls, and bathhouses, and the related Mudéjar Revival flourished in Spain itself.
