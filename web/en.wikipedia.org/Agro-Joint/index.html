Agro-Joint

The Agro-Joint, formally the American Jewish Joint Agricultural Corporation, was an organisation established in 1924 by the American Jewish Joint Distribution Committee (JDC) to settle Soviet Jews on agricultural colonies in Ukraine and Crimea. Working under agreements with the Soviet government, it was one of the most unusual collaborations between an American relief organisation and the USSR.

Background

After the Russian Revolution and Civil War, hundreds of thousands of Jews in the former Pale of Settlement were impoverished and legally barred from many trades. The Soviet authorities, seeking to "productivise" the Jewish population, permitted a programme of Jewish agricultural settlement, coordinated on the Soviet side by KOMZET and its implementing body OZET. The agronomist Joseph A. Rosen, who had directed JDC agricultural relief work in Russia, championed the scheme in America.

Activities

Agro-Joint supplied the colonies with tractors, seed, livestock, wells, housing and technical supervision. At its height it supported some 160 colonies and tens of thousands of settlers on hundreds of thousands of hectares. It also pioneered the introduction of modern machinery, credit cooperatives and agronomic training. Beginning in 1933, Agro-Joint quietly redirected part of its work to assist German Jews fleeing Nazi persecution.

End

As Stalin's purges intensified, Soviet partners of Agro-Joint were arrested; its director Joseph Rosen was denounced, and its Moscow office closed in 1938. Many colonies were destroyed during the Nazi occupation of 1941–1944. The settlements' surviving equipment and organisations were absorbed into the Soviet collective farm system.
