Equestrianism

Equestrianism (from Latin equester, "horseman") commonly refers to the skill of riding, driving and training horses, encompassing a wide range of competitive sports and recreational activities. Equestrian sports are governed internationally by the Fédération Équestre Internationale (FEI), founded in 1921.

Olympic disciplines

Three equestrian disciplines appear at the Olympic Games: dressage, in which horse and rider perform a series of predetermined movements; show jumping, over a course of obstacles against time and faults; and eventing, a three-phase combination of dressage, cross-country jumping and show jumping. Equestrian events were first held at the 1900 Paris Olympics and in their modern form from 1912; uniquely among Olympic sports, men and women compete directly against each other, and the horse is regarded as an athlete alongside the rider.

Other disciplines

The FEI also recognizes endurance riding, driving, vaulting, reining and para-equestrian sport. Beyond FEI competition lie horse racing, polo, rodeo events, tent pegging, and many regional traditions. Horses are ridden in two broad styles: English riding, derived from European cavalry and hunting traditions, and Western riding, developed on the cattle ranches of the Americas.

History

Horses were domesticated around 3500 BC on the Eurasian steppe, and formal treatises on horsemanship go back to Xenophon's On Horsemanship (c. 355 BC). The classical dressage of the Renaissance academies of Naples and Vienna — preserved today at the Spanish Riding School — laid the foundations of the modern sport.
