Battle of Fulford
Date: 20 September 1066
Location: Fulford, near York, England
Result: Norwegian victory

The Battle of Fulford (also known as the Battle of Gate Fulford) was fought on 20 September 1066 at Fulford, just south of York, between the invading army of King Harald Hardrada of Norway, supported by Tostig Godwinson, and the northern earls Edwin of Mercia and Morcar of Northumbria. It was the first of the three great battles of 1066 and ended in a Norwegian victory.

Background

After the death of Edward the Confessor, Harald Hardrada claimed the English throne and sailed up the Humber and Ouse with a large fleet in September 1066, joining forces with Harold Godwinson's exiled brother Tostig. The northern earls marched out of York to block the invaders along the south bank of the Ouse, anchoring their line between the river and marshy ground at Germany Beck.

The battle

The English initially held their ground and pushed back the weaker Norwegian right wing, but Hardrada brought up reinforcements from his reserves along the line and gradually forced the earls' army back into the marsh and beck, where many were killed or drowned. The Anglo-Saxon Chronicle says the Norwegians had the mastery but at heavy cost. York surrendered and agreed to supply the invaders, who withdrew to Stamford Bridge to await hostages and provisions.

Aftermath

Five days later King Harold Godwinson, having force-marched from the south, surprised and destroyed Hardrada's army at the Battle of Stamford Bridge. Harold then had to turn south at once to face the Norman invasion, culminating at Hastings.
