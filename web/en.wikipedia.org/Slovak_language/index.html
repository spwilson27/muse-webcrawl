Slovak language

Slovak is the official language of Slovakia

Slovak (slovenčina) is a West Slavic language of the Indo-European family, closely related to Czech, with which it is largely mutually intelligible. It is the official language of Slovakia, where it is spoken by about 4.6 million people, and is also spoken by Slovak communities abroad and recognised as a minority language in several neighbouring countries.

History

Slovak developed from Proto-Slavic in the region of present-day Slovakia. Attempts to codify a literary Slovak began in the late 18th century with Anton Bernolák's standard based on western dialects. The modern standard derives from Ľudovít Štúr's codification of 1843, based on central Slovak dialects, later reformed by Martin Hattala. Slovak was long overshadowed by Czech in education and administration under Hungary and later Czechoslovakia, but it became the sole official language of an independent Slovakia in 1993.

Features

Slovak uses the Latin alphabet with diacritics, including the unique letter ä and the long syllabic consonants ĺ and ŕ. It has six cases for nouns, three genders, and a rhythmic law that generally prevents two consecutive long syllables. Its phoneme inventory includes the distinctive ľ sound.
