Standard instrument departure

A standard instrument departure (SID) is a published flight procedure that an aircraft flying under instrument flight rules (IFR) follows after take-off to join the en-route airways structure in a safe, orderly and predictable way. SIDs are designed by national aviation authorities, published on aeronautical charts, and assigned by air traffic control as part of an IFR clearance.

Purpose

SIDs guarantee obstacle clearance during the climb-out, separate departing traffic flows, reduce pilot–controller communication workload, and can incorporate noise-abatement routing around populated areas. Each SID specifies a sequence of waypoints, headings, altitude and speed restrictions, and the minimum climb gradient the aircraft must achieve.

Types

Pilot-nav SIDs give the pilot all the information needed to fly the procedure using onboard navigation. Vector SIDs take the aircraft to a point from which radar controllers issue headings. Modern procedures are increasingly designed for area navigation (RNAV) and satellite-based performance-based navigation (PBN/RNP), allowing precise curved paths and continuous climb profiles.

Naming

SIDs are named for a fix or waypoint at or near their end, with a number and letter indicating the version and the runway or transition — for example, a departure ending at a waypoint called LAM may be designated "LAM 4M". The counterpart procedure flown on arrival is the standard terminal arrival route (STAR).
