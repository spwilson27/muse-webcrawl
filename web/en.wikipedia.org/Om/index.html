Om (ॐ, IAST: Oṃ, sometimes written Aum) is a sacred sound, syllable, mantra and icon in Hinduism. Its position and utterance at the beginning and end of most Sanskrit recitations, prayers and texts is considered spiritually supreme in Hinduism and other dharmic religions, including Buddhism, Jainism and Sikhism.

The syllable Om is first mentioned in the Upanishads. It has variously been associated with concepts of "cosmic sound" or "mystical syllable" or "affirmation to something divine", or as symbolism for abstract spiritual concepts in the Upanishads. In the Chandogya Upanishad, for example, Om is an important part of the study of the Self (atman), the universal principle underlying the cosmos.

Etymology and origins

The syllable is often analysed as consisting of three phonemes, "a-u-m", a triad commonly understood to represent various fundamental triads such as the three Vedas, the three states of consciousness (waking, dreaming and deep sleep), or the three principal deities Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva. The earliest surviving specimens of the Om sign are found in inscriptions and manuscripts of the first millennium BCE and later.

Hinduism

In Hinduism, Om is one of the most important spiritual symbols (pratika). It refers to Atman (the Self within) and Brahman (ultimate reality, the entirety of the universe, truth, divine, supreme spirit). The syllable is often found at the beginning and the end of chapters in the Vedas, the Upanishads, and other Hindu texts. It is a sacred invocation made before and during the recitation of spiritual texts, during puja and private prayers, in ceremonies of rites of passage (samskara) such as weddings, and during meditative and spiritual activities such as yoga.

Other religions

Om is part of the iconography found in ancient and medieval era manuscripts, temples, monasteries and spiritual retreats in Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, and Sikhism. In Buddhism it begins the well-known mantra Om mani padme hum. In Jainism, Om is a condensed reference to the five supreme beings (Panch Parameshti). In Sikhism, the related term Ik Onkar names God as one and eternal.
