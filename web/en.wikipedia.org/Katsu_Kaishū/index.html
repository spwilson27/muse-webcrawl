Katsu Kaishū (勝 海舟, 1823–1899), born Katsu Yoshikuni, was a Japanese naval officer and statesman who played a central role in his country's transition from the Tokugawa shogunate to the Meiji Restoration.

Career

A low-ranking samurai official's son, Katsu studied Dutch and Western military science (rangaku) and trained at the shogunate's naval school in Nagasaki under Dutch instructors. In 1860 he captained the warship Kanrin Maru across the Pacific to San Francisco — the first Japanese-crewed transpacific voyage — escorting Japan's first embassy to the United States. He went on to direct naval affairs for the shogunate, founded the Kobe naval school (where the reformer Sakamoto Ryōma studied), and argued for a modern national navy transcending domain loyalties.

The surrender of Edo

Katsu's most famous act came in 1868 during the Boshin War, when, as the shogunate's army minister, he negotiated with Saigō Takamori the peaceful surrender of Edo Castle, sparing the capital from a destructive assault. In the Meiji government he served as Navy Minister and privy councillor and was ennobled as a count, while writing voluminous memoirs and commentaries that remain key sources for the period.
