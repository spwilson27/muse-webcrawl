The NTNU University Museum (Norwegian: NTNU Vitenskapsmuseet) is a university museum in Trondheim, Norway, forming part of the Norwegian University of Science and Technology. It is responsible for the university's collections in natural history, archaeology, and cultural history, and conducts research and public outreach based on them.

History

The museum's collections originate with the Royal Norwegian Society of Sciences and Letters, founded in 1760, whose museum opened in the 1760s and is among the oldest in Norway. The museum became part of the University of Trondheim in 1968 and of NTNU when the university was restructured in 1996.

Collections and work

The museum holds extensive zoological, botanical, and geological collections, as well as archaeological material from central Norway, for which it carries statutory responsibility under Norwegian cultural heritage law — including archaeological excavations ahead of development. Its exhibitions in the historic museum buildings near Nidaros Cathedral cover subjects from Stone Age settlement and medieval Trondheim to biodiversity and evolution. The museum also manages the Ringve Botanical Garden and other scientific collections.
