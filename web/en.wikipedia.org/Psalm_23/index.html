Psalm 23 is the 23rd psalm of the Book of Psalms, beginning in the King James Version, "The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want." It is one of the best-known passages of the Bible, frequently read at funerals and in times of distress. The psalm is traditionally attributed to King David, and its superscription reads "A Psalm of David."

Text and themes

The six verses develop two images: God as a shepherd who leads the speaker to green pastures and still waters, restores the soul, and guides through "the valley of the shadow of death" with rod and staff; and God as a host who prepares a table "in the presence of mine enemies", anoints the head with oil, and fills the cup to overflowing. The psalm concludes with the assurance that "goodness and mercy shall follow me all the days of my life" and that the speaker will "dwell in the house of the LORD for ever."

Use and settings

Psalm 23 is used in Jewish, Catholic, Orthodox, and Protestant liturgy, and forms part of the traditional Office of the Dead. It has been set to music many times, in hymn paraphrases such as "The King of Love My Shepherd Is" (to the tune St. Columba) and "The Lord's My Shepherd" (Crimond), and in classical works by composers including Schubert, Bernstein (in the Chichester Psalms), and John Rutter.
