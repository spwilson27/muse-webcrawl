Pallor mortis

TypePost-mortem change
OnsetWithin minutes of death
StageFirst sign after death

Pallor mortis (Latin: pallor "paleness", mortis "of death") is the first stage of post-mortem change in a corpse, in which the skin takes on an unusually pale appearance. It occurs almost immediately after death — usually within the first 15 to 25 minutes — as a result of the cessation of capillary circulation, so that blood drains from the smaller veins in the skin.

Characteristics

Paleness develops in nearly all corpses regardless of skin tone, though it is more readily apparent in light-skinned individuals. Pallor mortis is usually not significant in determining the time of death, since it appears so quickly after death, but it helps confirm that circulatory arrest has occurred. It is followed by the other early post-mortem changes: algor mortis (cooling), rigor mortis (stiffening), and livor mortis (settling of blood).
