Theresienwiese

LocationLudwigsvorstadt-Isarvorstadt, Munich, Germany
Area42 hectares (100 acres)
Known forOktoberfest; Bavaria statue and Ruhmeshalle
Named afterTherese of Saxe-Hildburghausen

Theresienwiese is an open space in the Munich quarter of Ludwigsvorstadt-Isarvorstadt, best known as the site of the Oktoberfest, the world's largest beer festival. The 42-hectare field lies southwest of Munich's city centre and is served by its own station on the Munich U-Bahn.

History

The meadow takes its name from Therese of Saxe-Hildburghausen, whose marriage to the Bavarian Crown Prince Ludwig (later King Ludwig I) in October 1810 was celebrated with horse races on the field. The festivities were repeated in subsequent years and developed into the Oktoberfest. In the mid-nineteenth century the Bavaria statue — a monumental bronze female figure personifying the Bavarian state — and the Ruhmeshalle (Hall of Fame) were erected on the field's western edge, overlooking the festival grounds.

Events

Besides the Oktoberfest (mid-September to early October), the Theresienwiese hosts the Munich Spring Festival (Frühlingsfest), the Tollwood Winter Festival, circuses, flea markets, and other large gatherings. In 1980 the field was the site of the Oktoberfest bombing, a right-wing terrorist attack at the festival's main entrance that killed 13 people including the perpetrator.
