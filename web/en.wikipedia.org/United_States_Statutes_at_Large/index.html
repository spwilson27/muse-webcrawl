United States Statutes at Large

The United States Statutes at Large, commonly abbreviated Stat., is the permanent official collection of laws and resolutions enacted by the United States Congress. Every public law and most private laws are published in chronological order in bound volumes, constituting legal evidence of the laws in all U.S. courts.

Publication process

After enactment, a law is first issued as a slip law, then collected in the session laws at the end of a congressional session, and finally bound into the Statutes at Large. Since 1947 the volumes have been prepared under the authority of the Archivist of the United States.

Contents

Beyond public and private laws, the volumes include concurrent resolutions, presidential proclamations, proposed constitutional amendments and, historically, treaties and international agreements. Marginal notes give cross-references to United States Code sections.

History

Publication began in 1845 under authority of Congress, compiled initially by the publishers Little, Brown and Company to standardize the scattered early session laws; the series now extends well past one hundred volumes.
