Supravalvular aortic stenosis

Supravalvular aortic stenosis (SVAS) is a congenital narrowing of the ascending aorta just above the aortic valve (at the sinotubular junction), which obstructs blood flow from the left ventricle. It is a form of left ventricular outflow tract obstruction distinct from valvar and subvalvar aortic stenosis.

Causes

SVAS is caused by disruption of the elastin gene (ELN) on chromosome 7. It occurs as part of Williams syndrome, a contiguous gene deletion syndrome, or in familial form inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern without the other features of Williams syndrome. Loss of elastin leads to thickened, less compliant arterial walls.

Clinical features and treatment

Severity ranges from mild narrowing to diffuse hypoplasia of the ascending aorta; associated findings can include coronary ostial involvement and peripheral pulmonary artery stenosis. Diagnosis is by echocardiography and cross-sectional imaging. Significant obstruction is treated surgically by patch aortoplasty to enlarge the narrowed segment.
