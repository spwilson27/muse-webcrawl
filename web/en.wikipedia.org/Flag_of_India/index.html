Flag of India

The flag of India, popularly called the Tiranga ("tricolour"), is a horizontal rectangular tricolour of deep saffron, white and India green, with the Ashoka Chakra — a 24-spoke wheel in navy blue — at its centre. It was adopted in its present form during a meeting of the Constituent Assembly on 22 July 1947, and became the official flag of the Dominion of India on 15 August 1947.

Symbolism

Mahatma Gandhi interpreted the colours as representing qualities: saffron for strength and courage, white for truth and peace, and green for fertility and the land's auspiciousness; the Chakra represents the eternal wheel of law (dharma) and the principle that there is life in movement and death in stagnation. The wheel replaced the spinning wheel (charkha) of earlier Congress flags.

History

The flag evolved from flags of the Indian independence movement. A prototype designed by Pingali Venkayya — saffron, white and green bands with a spinning wheel — was adopted by the Indian National Congress in 1931. After independence, the Constituent Assembly replaced the charkha with the Ashoka Chakra, taken from the Lion Capital of Ashoka at Sarnath.

Use

Display of the flag is governed by the Flag Code of India, liberalized in 2002 to allow citizens to fly it year-round subject to rules of dignity, and promoted by the Har Ghar Tiranga campaign of 2022. By law the flag must be made of khadi (hand-spun cloth) for official use, a provision later amended to permit machine-made flags.
