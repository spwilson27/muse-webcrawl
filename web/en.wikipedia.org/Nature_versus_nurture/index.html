Nature versus nurture

Nature versus nurture is a long-standing debate in biology and psychology concerning the relative contributions of genetic inheritance ("nature") and environmental factors ("nurture") to human development and behaviour.

History

The phrase was popularised by Francis Galton, drawing on Shakespeare's The Tempest. Thinkers from Plato and Descartes, who favoured innate ideas, to John Locke, who proposed the mind as a tabula rasa (blank slate), took opposing positions.

Scientific approaches

Behavioural genetics studies twins, adoptees, and families to estimate heritability — the proportion of variation in a trait attributable to genetic differences within a population. Modern research emphasises interaction: genes and environment influence each other, and epigenetic mechanisms can switch genes on or off in response to experience.

Consensus

Most scientists today reject a simple either/or framing. Traits such as intelligence, personality, and susceptibility to mental illness reflect complex interactions between genes and environments across development.
