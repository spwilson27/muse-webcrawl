Guiding Principles on Internal Displacement

Internally displaced persons remain within their own country and under its legal protection.

The Guiding Principles on Internal Displacement are a set of 30 principles presented to the United Nations Commission on Human Rights in 1998 by Francis Deng, the Secretary-General's Representative on Internally Displaced Persons. They identify the rights and guarantees relevant to the protection of internally displaced persons (IDPs) — people forced to flee their homes but who, unlike refugees, have not crossed an international border.

Content

The Principles are organised around the phases of displacement: protection against arbitrary displacement; protection and assistance during displacement; humanitarian access; and return, resettlement and reintegration. They restate existing international human rights and humanitarian law rather than creating new obligations, and affirm that national authorities bear the primary duty to protect and assist IDPs.

Influence

Although not a binding treaty, the Principles have been widely endorsed by UN bodies and incorporated into national laws and policies in dozens of countries. They also underpin the African Union's Kampala Convention of 2009, the first binding regional treaty on internal displacement.
