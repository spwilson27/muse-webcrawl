The Road to Serfdom is a book by the Austrian-British economist and philosopher Friedrich Hayek, published in 1944, in which he warned that central economic planning leads inevitably toward totalitarianism.

Written during the Second World War while Hayek was at the London School of Economics, the book was aimed at British advocates of socialist planning.

Argument

Hayek argued that abandoning individualism and classical liberalism in favour of collectivist planning requires the state to impose a single scale of values, concentrates power, and creates conditions in which "the worst get on top". He maintained that Nazism itself grew from socialist roots.

Reception and influence

A condensed version in Reader's Digest (1945) and a popular US tour made the book a bestseller. It profoundly influenced conservative and libertarian thought, including Margaret Thatcher, and remains one of the most influential political books of the 20th century.
