Non-cooperation movement (1919-22)

Dates1920 – February 1922
LocationBritish India
LeaderMahatma Gandhi
AimSwaraj (self-rule)

The non-cooperation movement of 1919–22 was the first nationwide satyagraha campaign led by Mahatma Gandhi against British rule in India. Adopted by the Indian National Congress in 1920, it urged Indians to withdraw cooperation from the colonial state—boycotting its courts, schools, titles, and imported goods—until grievances were redressed and swaraj attained.

Background

Post-war discontent, the repressive Rowlatt Acts, the Amritsar massacre of April 1919, and Muslim concern over the Khilafat created the conditions for a united mass campaign. Gandhi's alliance with the Khilafat movement briefly brought Hindus and Muslims together in a common struggle.

End of the movement

The campaign shook the Raj but was called off by Gandhi in February 1922 after the Chauri Chaura incident, in which protesters killed 22 policemen. Its legacy—mass mobilization, the khadi symbol, and Congress's transformation into a popular movement—shaped all subsequent phases of the freedom struggle.
