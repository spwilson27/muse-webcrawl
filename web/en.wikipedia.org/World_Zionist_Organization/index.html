World Zionist Organization

The World Zionist Organization (Hebrew: ההסתדרות הציונית העולמית), or WZO, was founded as the Zionist Organization (ZO) in 1897 at the First Zionist Congress in Basel, Switzerland, convened by Theodor Herzl. It served as the umbrella organisation of the Zionist movement, whose goal was the establishment of a Jewish state in Palestine.

History

The Zionist Organization was created to coordinate the activities of Zionist federations worldwide and to pursue the Basel Program, which called for a home for the Jewish people in Palestine secured under public law. After Herzl's death in 1904, leadership passed through figures including David Wolffsohn and Otto Warburg; Chaim Weizmann became president in 1921 and led the movement through the British Mandate period.

The organisation founded key institutions including the Jewish National Fund (1901) and the Jewish Agency (1929). Following the establishment of the State of Israel in 1948, the WZO redefined its role under the Jerusalem Program, focusing on immigration (aliyah), Jewish education, and ties between Israel and the Jewish diaspora.

Structure

The World Zionist Congress, elected by Zionist federation members in dozens of countries, is the WZO's supreme body. Between congresses, affairs are managed by the Zionist General Council and the Zionist Executive in Jerusalem.
