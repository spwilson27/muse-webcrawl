The Russo-Turkish War of 1877–1878 was a conflict between the Ottoman Empire and a coalition led by the Russian Empire. It arose from the Balkan crisis of 1875–1876 — uprisings in Herzegovina and Bulgaria, the Ottoman suppression of the April Uprising, and the Serbian–Ottoman war — and from Russia's aim to regain influence lost after the Crimean War and to champion the Orthodox Christian peoples of the Balkans under the banner of Pan-Slavism.

Course of the war

Russia declared war in April 1877. Its armies crossed the Danube at Svishtov, seized the Shipka Pass, and advanced toward Constantinople, but were checked for five months by Osman Pasha's defence of Plevna, which fell in December 1877 after a celebrated siege. In the Caucasus, Russian forces captured Kars. In January 1878 Russian troops advanced through the Balkans in winter, won at Philippopolis, and occupied Adrianople, compelling the Ottomans to seek an armistice.

Settlements

The Treaty of San Stefano (March 1878) created a large autonomous Bulgaria and recognised the full independence of Romania, Serbia and Montenegro. Alarmed by Russian gains, Britain and Austria-Hungary forced a revision at the Congress of Berlin (July 1878): Bulgaria was divided, Macedonia was returned to Ottoman rule, Austria-Hungary occupied Bosnia and Herzegovina, and Britain obtained Cyprus.

Legacy

The war completed the independence of the Balkan states and reduced Ottoman Europe to a rump, but the Berlin settlement left grievances — above all over Macedonia and Bosnia — that contributed to the Balkan Wars and ultimately to the First World War. In Russia, the costly victory heightened both Slavophile enthusiasm and awareness of the empire's relative backwardness.
