Sir Winston Leonard Spencer Churchill (30 November 1874 – 24 January 1965) was a British statesman, soldier and writer who served twice as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom, from 1940 to 1945, during the Second World War, and again from 1951 to 1955. He led Britain to victory against the Axis powers and was among the architects of the post-war order.

Early life and career

Churchill was born at Blenheim Palace, the son of Lord Randolph Churchill and the American heiress Jennie Jerome. After Harrow and Sandhurst he served as a cavalry officer and war correspondent in Cuba, India, the Sudan and South Africa, where his escape from a Boer prisoner-of-war camp made him a national hero. He entered Parliament in 1900 and held numerous cabinet posts, including Home Secretary and First Lord of the Admiralty, where his support for the disastrous Gallipoli campaign forced his resignation in 1915.

Second World War

Out of office through most of the 1930s, Churchill warned against Nazi Germany and opposed appeasement. He became Prime Minister on 10 May 1940, the day Germany invaded the Low Countries and France. His speeches — "blood, toil, tears and sweat", "we shall fight on the beaches", "their finest hour" — rallied Britain through the Battle of Britain and the Blitz. He forged the Grand Alliance with the United States and the Soviet Union, meeting Roosevelt and Stalin at Tehran and Yalta.

Later years

Defeated in the 1945 election despite his wartime standing, Churchill led the opposition and delivered the famous "Iron Curtain" speech at Fulton, Missouri, in 1946. He returned as Prime Minister in 1951, won the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1953, and resigned in 1955. He died in London in 1965 and received a state funeral; he is buried at Bladon, near Blenheim.
