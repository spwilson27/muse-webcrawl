Aristarchus of Samos
Bornc. 310 BC, Samos
Diedc. 230 BC, probably Alexandria
FieldsAstronomy, mathematics
Known forFirst known heliocentric model; sizes and distances of the Sun and Moon

Aristarchus of Samos (c. 310 – c. 230 BC) was an ancient Greek astronomer and mathematician who proposed the first known heliocentric model of the universe, placing the Sun — rather than the Earth — at its centre, with the Earth revolving around it once a year and rotating on its axis daily. His ideas anticipated Copernicus by some eighteen centuries.

Heliocentric hypothesis

Aristarchus's heliocentric work is lost, but it is described by Archimedes in The Sand Reckoner and mentioned by Plutarch. Aristarchus held that the sphere of the fixed stars is vastly larger than the Earth's orbit — an answer to the objection that no stellar parallax could be observed — and that the Sun and stars remain unmoved while the Earth travels around the Sun. The hypothesis found little ancient support: motion of the Earth offended physical intuition and no parallax was detectable, and one source, Plutarch, reports that Cleanthes thought Aristarchus should be indicted for impiety.

On the Sizes and Distances of the Sun and Moon

His only surviving treatise, On the Sizes and Distances of the Sun and Moon, works within a geocentric framework and uses geometry to estimate relative distances: at half-moon, the angle between the Sun and Moon as seen from Earth lets one compute the ratio of their distances. Aristarchus's measured angle of 87° implied the Sun was about 18–20 times farther than the Moon (the true value is near 390), but the method was sound and his conclusion that the Sun is much larger than the Earth helped motivate the heliocentric idea — it seemed implausible that the greater body should orbit the lesser.

Legacy

Aristarchus also reportedly improved the sundial and estimated the sizes of the Sun and Moon relative to the Earth. Copernicus knew of his predecessor and mentioned him in a manuscript passage later deleted. The lunar crater Aristarchus is named after him.
