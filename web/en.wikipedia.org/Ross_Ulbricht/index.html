Ross Ulbricht

Ross William Ulbricht (born March 27, 1984) is an American who created and operated the darknet market website Silk Road from 2011 until his arrest in 2013. Operating under the pseudonym "Dread Pirate Roberts", he ran the site as a Tor hidden service that used Bitcoin as currency and facilitated the sale of narcotics and other illicit goods and services.

Silk Road

Ulbricht launched Silk Road in February 2011, designing it to operate outside government regulation using Tor for anonymity and Bitcoin for payment. The site grew into the best-known darknet market, with sales revenues estimated at over $200 million. Libertarian ideas, drawn in part from Austrian economics and the works of Samuel Edward Konkin III, influenced his stated philosophy for the site.

Arrest, trial and pardon

He was arrested by the FBI on October 1, 2013, at the Glen Park branch of the San Francisco Public Library. In February 2015 a federal jury convicted him on seven counts; he was sentenced in May 2015 to two life sentences plus forty years without parole. His appeals were rejected, including by the U.S. Supreme Court in 2018. On January 21, 2025, President Donald Trump granted Ulbricht a full and unconditional pardon, fulfilling a campaign pledge, and he was released from prison.
