Richemont


Cartier, Richemont's largest maison

Compagnie Financière Richemont AG, commonly known as Richemont, is a Swiss-based luxury goods holding company founded in 1988 by South African businessman Johann Rupert. Through its maisons — historic brands it owns — Richemont is one of the world's largest luxury groups, specialising in jewellery, watches, writing instruments and fashion. Its shares are listed on the SIX Swiss Exchange and the Johannesburg Stock Exchange.

History

Richemont was created by spinning off the international assets of South Africa's Rembrandt Group, including its stakes in Cartier Monde and the Rothmans tobacco business. During the 1990s the group separated its luxury and tobacco interests (the latter merging into British American Tobacco, in which Richemont long retained a stake) and consolidated around luxury brands. Under Rupert's control — exercised through a dual-class share structure giving the Rupert family majority voting power — the group acquired many of its current houses.

Maisons

Richemont's portfolio is headed by Cartier, the world's largest jewellery brand, and includes Van Cleef & Arpels and Buccellati in jewellery; the watchmakers A. Lange & Söhne, Baume & Mercier, IWC Schaffhausen, Jaeger-LeCoultre, Officine Panerai, Piaget, Roger Dubuis and Vacheron Constantin; the writing-instrument house Montblanc; and fashion and accessories brands such as Chloé, Dunhill, Alaïa and Delvaux. The group has also held major stakes in the online luxury retailer YOOX Net-a-Porter.

Position

Together with LVMH and Kering, Richemont forms the trio of dominant global luxury conglomerates; it is typically ranked second or third by revenue, with annual sales in excess of €20 billion in recent years, and is especially strong in "hard luxury" — jewellery and watches.
