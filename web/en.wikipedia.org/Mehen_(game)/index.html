Mehen (game)

Mehen is an ancient Egyptian board game played from the predynastic period to at least the end of the Old Kingdom (about 2613–2160 BC). It takes its name from Mehen, a serpent god who coiled around the sun god Ra.

The game

The board depicts a coiled snake whose segmented body forms a spiral of squares. Evidence for the game includes board depictions in tombs and gaming pieces such as lion figures and marbles. The exact rules are unknown, but reconstructions suggest a race game in which pieces travelled along the snake's body from tail to head, possibly connected with the journey of Ra through the night.

Decline

Mehen disappeared from Egypt after the Old Kingdom, though it may have continued to be played in Nubia and the Levant. It is one of the earliest known board games, alongside senet and the Royal Game of Ur.
