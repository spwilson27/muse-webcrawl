Rheinsberg Palace

Rheinsberg Palace (German: Schloss Rheinsberg) is a palace in the municipality of Rheinsberg in the Ruppiner Land district of Brandenburg, Germany, on the eastern shore of Lake Grienerick (Grienericksee). It is chiefly famous as the residence of Crown Prince Frederick of Prussia — later Frederick the Great — from 1736 to 1740, and afterwards of his brother Prince Henry of Prussia.

History

A moated castle stood at Rheinsberg from the Middle Ages; the Knesebeck family built a Renaissance manor house on the site in 1566. In 1734 Crown Prince Frederick acquired the estate, and the architect Kemmeter, followed by Knobelsdorff, remodelled the house into an elegant single-wing palace with a colonnade — an early rehearsal for the Frederician Rococo later seen at Sanssouci. At Rheinsberg Frederick gathered a court of musicians, actors and philosophers, calling these his happiest years; he left on becoming king in 1740.

Prince Henry's era

Frederick gave Rheinsberg to his younger brother, Prince Henry, in 1744. Henry expanded the palace and gardens over five decades, adding a theatre, an obelisk commemorating heroes of the Seven Years' War, grottoes, and an Egeria grotto, making Rheinsberg a significant cultural court in its own right. Henry died here in 1802 and is buried in a pyramid tomb in the garden.

Later history

The palace passed through various owners and uses; in the GDR era it served as a clinic for diabetics. Kurt Tucholsky's 1912 story Rheinsberg: A Picture Book for Lovers made the town a byword for romantic excursions. Today the palace is a museum administered by the Prussian Palaces and Gardens Foundation Berlin-Brandenburg, with the state rooms restored and a literature museum honouring Tucholsky; the palace theatre hosts the Rheinsberg Chamber Opera festival.
