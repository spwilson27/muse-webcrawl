The Premier of the Soviet Union was the head of government of the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics (USSR). From 1923 to 1946 the office was titled Chairman of the Council of People's Commissars, and from 1946 to 1991 Chairman of the Council of Ministers. Though nominally the head of government, the premier was in practice subordinate to the General Secretary of the Communist Party, who was usually the country's true leader.

Holders

Vladimir Lenin was the first head of the Soviet government, followed by Alexei Rykov, Vyacheslav Molotov, Joseph Stalin, Georgy Malenkov, Nikolai Bulganin, Nikita Khrushchev, Alexei Kosygin (the longest-serving, 1964–1980), Nikolai Tikhonov, and Nikolai Ryzhkov. Valentin Pavlov served briefly in 1991 as "Prime Minister" before the August coup, and Ivan Silayev was the final officeholder as the USSR dissolved at the end of 1991.

Role

The premier directed the work of the Council of Ministers, supervising the enormous Soviet ministerial bureaucracy responsible for the planned economy, and represented the government in foreign affairs when the party leader did not. Stalin and Khrushchev combined the premiership with party leadership, consolidating power in both posts.
