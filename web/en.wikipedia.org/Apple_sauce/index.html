Apple sauce

Apple sauce (also spelled applesauce) is a purée made of apples. It can be made with peeled or unpeeled apples and may be spiced, for example with cinnamon, or sweetened. Apple sauce is inexpensive and widely consumed by children and adults alike, and is a common food for infants and invalids because of its soft texture and easy digestibility.

Preparation

Apple sauce is prepared by cooking apples with water or apple cider until they soften and break down, then mashing or milling them to the desired consistency. Commercial production commonly uses varieties such as McIntosh, Golden Delicious, or Fuji, sometimes with added sugar and ascorbic acid to preserve colour.

Uses

It is eaten as a dessert or snack, served as an accompaniment to savoury dishes such as roast pork and potato pancakes (latkes), used as an ingredient in baking — including as a partial substitute for fat or eggs — and served with couscous or as part of the Dutch and German dish known as Himmel un Ääd in the Rhineland. In Sweden, äppelmos accompanies roasted pork and is used in desserts.

History

Puréed apple preparations have a long history in European cooking; methods for preserving apples as sauce were described in early modern cookery books, and apple butter and sauce became staples in German, Dutch, and colonial American kitchens.
