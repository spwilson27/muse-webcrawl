Lugu Lake

Lugu Lake and its shoreline villages

Location: Yunnan–Sichuan border, China

Surface area: 48.5 km²

Max. depth: 93.5 m

Surface elevation: 2,685 m

Lugu Lake (Chinese: 泸沽湖; pinyin: Lúgū Hú) is a high-altitude freshwater lake on the border between Ninglang County in Yunnan and Yanyuan County in Sichuan, China. Lying on the northwestern Yunnan plateau at an elevation of about 2,685 metres, it is the third deepest freshwater lake in China and is ringed by mountains and dotted with small islands and peninsulas.

Geography

The lake covers roughly 48.5 square kilometres and reaches a maximum depth of about 93.5 metres. Its clear waters are fed by rainfall and mountain streams; outflow is seasonal through the Gaizu River into a tributary of the Yalong River. Villages around the shore include Luoshui, Lige, and Daluoshui, set among fields and forested slopes.

People and culture

The shores of Lugu Lake are the homeland of the Mosuo people, famous for their matrilineal households and the custom of "walking marriages" (tisese), in which partners remain in their maternal homes and men visit at night. The lake is sacred in Mosuo tradition; Gemu Mountain, a prominent peak on the northern shore, is venerated as a goddess. Other communities, including Yi, Pumi, and Lisu peoples, also live in the lake basin.

In recent decades the lake has become a popular destination for domestic tourism, drawn by its scenery and the mystique of Mosuo culture, prompting debate about development, water quality, and cultural change.
