Veliko Tarnovo (Bulgarian: Велико Търново) is a city in north-central Bulgaria and the administrative centre of Veliko Tarnovo Province. Often referred to as the "City of the Tsars", it is picturesquely situated on the Yantra River and is famous as the historical capital of the Second Bulgarian Empire (1185–1393), whose Tsarevets fortress dominates the old town.

History

Tarnovo became capital after the uprising of Asen and Peter in 1185 restored Bulgarian statehood. Under tsars such as Ivan Asen II it was a major political and cultural centre of southeastern Europe. The city fell to the Ottomans in 1393 after a three-month siege. In 1878, after liberation, the Constituent Assembly in Tarnovo adopted Bulgaria's first constitution (the Tarnovo Constitution, 1879). Prince Ferdinand declared Bulgaria's full independence in the city in 1908.

Sights

Major landmarks include the reconstructed Tsarevets fortress and patriarchal cathedral, the medieval churches on Trapezitsa hill, the old merchant street Samovodska Charshia, and the university "St. Cyril and St. Methodius". A sound-and-light show is projected on Tsarevets on festival evenings.
