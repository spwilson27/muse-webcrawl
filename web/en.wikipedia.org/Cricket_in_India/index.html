Cricket in India

Cricket is the most popular sport in India. It is played by many people all over the country and is an important part of Indian culture, watched by hundreds of millions of television viewers. The Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI) is the governing body for cricket in India and is one of the richest sporting organisations in the world. The Indian national cricket team is among the most successful in international cricket, having won the Cricket World Cup in 1983 and 2011, the T20 World Cup in 2007 and 2024, and the ICC Champions Trophy in 2013.

History

Cricket was introduced to India by British sailors and merchants in the early 18th century; the first recorded match took place in 1721, and the Calcutta Cricket Club was founded in 1792. Indians gradually took up the game, with the Parsi community of Bombay among the first to tour England, in 1886. The Ranji Trophy, named after Ranjitsinhji, began in 1934 as the national domestic championship. India played its first Test match at Lord's in 1932 and won its first Test series in 1952, against Pakistan and England.

International cricket

India's 1983 World Cup victory under Kapil Dev transformed cricket into a mass phenomenon in the country. Later landmarks include the 2007 inaugural World Twenty20 win, the 2011 World Cup triumph on home soil under M. S. Dhoni, and consistent success across formats led by players such as Sachin Tendulkar — the most prolific run-scorer in international cricket history — Sunil Gavaskar, Rahul Dravid, Anil Kumble, Virat Kohli, and Rohit Sharma. The women's national team has likewise grown in prominence, reaching World Cup finals and winning a large following.

Domestic cricket

The premier domestic competitions include the Ranji Trophy (first-class), the Vijay Hazare Trophy (one-day), and the Syed Mushtaq Ali Trophy (Twenty20). Since 2008 the Indian Premier League (IPL), a franchise-based Twenty20 league, has become one of the most valuable sports leagues in the world, attracting leading international players and enormous broadcast revenues. The Women's Premier League was launched in 2023.

Popularity and culture

Cricket cuts across India's linguistic, regional, and religious diversity; major matches are public events followed in workplaces, streets, and homes. The sport receives the overwhelming share of sports media coverage and sponsorship in India, and successful cricketers become national celebrities and major advertising figures.
