Chief of the Army Staff (India)

The Chief of the Army Staff (COAS) is the professional head and highest-ranking officer of the Indian Army. A four-star general, the COAS commands the army from Army Headquarters in New Delhi and is appointed by the Appointments Committee of the Cabinet, conventionally from among the army's senior-most lieutenant generals.

History

The office descends from the Commander-in-Chief, India of British rule. The first Indian to hold the post was General (later Field Marshal) K. M. Cariappa, who took over from General Sir Roy Bucher on 15 January 1949 — a date commemorated as Army Day. The title of Chief of the Army Staff was adopted in 1955. Only two holders, K. M. Cariappa and Sam Manekshaw, have been promoted to the five-star rank of field marshal.

Tenure and succession

The COAS normally serves a term of three years or until age 62, whichever is earlier. Since 2020 the army chief has ranked below the Chief of Defence Staff in protocol, though the COAS retains full command of the army.

Role

The COAS is responsible for the army's readiness, modernization, and operations, and advises the government on land warfare. The office's staff includes the Vice Chief of the Army Staff and seven army commands led by General Officers Commanding-in-Chief.
