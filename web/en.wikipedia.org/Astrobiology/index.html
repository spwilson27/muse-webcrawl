Astrobiology

Astrobiology is a scientific field within the life and environmental sciences that studies the origins, early evolution, distribution, and future of life in the universe by investigating its deterministic conditions and contingent events. It addresses questions such as whether life exists beyond Earth, how it might be detected, and how life originated on Earth.

Overview

Astrobiology draws on molecular biology, biophysics, biochemistry, chemistry, astronomy, physical cosmology, exoplanetology, and geology. The field examines habitable environments in the Solar System and on exoplanets, the origin of prebiotic compounds, laboratory and field studies of extremophiles, and biosignatures that might indicate life.

Key topics

Central topics include the abiogenesis of life on Earth, the potential habitats of Mars, the subsurface oceans of Europa and Enceladus, the chemistry of Titan, and the detection of atmospheric biosignatures on exoplanets. Space missions such as the Mars rovers and the Europa Clipper carry astrobiological objectives.

Extremophiles

The study of organisms that thrive in extreme environments on Earth — in deep-sea hydrothermal vents, acidic lakes, and Antarctic dry valleys — has broadened the range of conditions considered compatible with life.
