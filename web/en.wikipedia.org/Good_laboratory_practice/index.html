Good laboratory practice

AbbreviationGLP
ScopeQuality system for non-clinical health and environmental safety studies
Key frameworksOECD Principles of GLP; U.S. FDA 21 CFR Part 58; EPA 40 CFR Part 160

Good laboratory practice (GLP) is a quality system concerned with the organisational process and the conditions under which non-clinical health and environmental safety studies are planned, performed, monitored, recorded, archived, and reported. It underpins regulatory submissions for pharmaceuticals, pesticides, food additives, chemicals, and medical devices.

Origins

GLP arose in the 1970s after investigations — most notoriously of Industrial Bio-Test Laboratories in the United States — revealed fraudulent and poorly documented toxicology studies submitted to regulators. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration issued GLP regulations in 1978, and the OECD published its Principles of Good Laboratory Practice in 1981, enabling mutual acceptance of safety data among member countries.

Requirements

GLP requires a defined study plan, a designated study director, an independent quality assurance unit, standard operating procedures, trained personnel, calibrated equipment, characterised test items, full raw-data retention, and archived final reports that allow an independent reconstruction of the study. National monitoring authorities inspect test facilities for compliance.

Scope

GLP applies to regulatory safety studies — such as toxicity, mutagenicity, and environmental fate testing — and is distinct from good manufacturing practice (GMP) and good clinical practice (GCP), which govern production and human trials respectively.
