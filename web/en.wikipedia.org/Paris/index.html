Paris

Paris is the capital and most populous city of France, with a population of around two million within the city proper and over ten million in its metropolitan area. Situated on the River Seine in northern France, it is a global centre of art, fashion, gastronomy, finance, and culture, famous for landmarks such as the Eiffel Tower, Notre-Dame Cathedral, and the Louvre.

History

Paris began as Lutetia, a settlement of the Celtic Parisii tribe on the Île de la Cité, conquered by Julius Caesar in 52 BCE. It became the capital of the Frankish kingdom under Clovis I around 508 CE. In the Middle Ages it grew into one of Europe's largest cities and a centre of learning around the University of Paris. The city was transformed in the 19th century by Baron Haussmann's renovation under Napoleon III, which created its broad boulevards. Paris hosted the 1900 and 1924 Olympic Games and again in 2024.

Geography and climate

The city occupies about 105 square kilometres in the Paris Basin. Its climate is oceanic, with mild winters and warm summers. The Seine divides Paris into the Left Bank (Rive Gauche) and Right Bank (Rive Droite).

Culture

Paris is home to world-renowned museums including the Louvre — the world's most-visited museum — the Musée d'Orsay, and the Centre Pompidou. The Eiffel Tower, completed in 1889 for the World's Fair, is the city's most iconic symbol. Paris is divided into 20 arrondissements spiralling out from the centre. Transport

The Paris Métro, opened in 1900, is one of the densest underground networks in the world. The city is served by Charles de Gaulle and Orly airports and by high-speed TGV trains from stations such as Gare du Nord.
