Zapovednik

A zapovednik (Russian: заповедник, from zapoved, "sacred commandment" or "solemn prohibition") is a category of strictly protected nature reserve established in the former Soviet Union and maintained today in Russia and other post-Soviet states. Zapovedniks are the strictest form of environmental protection in that system, corresponding to IUCN protected area category Ia (strict nature reserve): the land is set aside for scientific research and the preservation of ecosystems in their natural state, and public access and economic use are generally prohibited.

Principles

The zapovednik concept rests on the idea of the etalon — a benchmark sample of an undisturbed natural ecosystem, kept intact forever so that scientists can study natural processes without human interference and compare them with managed landscapes. Economic activity, tourism, and usually even passage through the territory are forbidden; staff scientists conduct long-term ecological monitoring, often summarized in annual "Chronicles of Nature" (Letopis Prirody), maintained at some reserves for many decades.

History

The first zapovednik, Barguzinsky Zapovednik on the northeast shore of Lake Baikal, was established in 1916 (formally decreed in early 1917) to protect the sable population. The system expanded rapidly in the Soviet Union in the 1920s–1940s under scientists such as Franz Shillinger. In 1951, however, a Stalin-era decree abolished most zapovedniks and opened their lands to logging and hunting; the network was partly rebuilt in later decades. Today Russia has more than one hundred federal zapovedniks covering over 1% of its territory, including vast Arctic and Siberian reserves such as the Great Arctic Zapovednik, and comparable systems exist in Ukraine, Belarus, Kazakhstan, and other successor states.

Distinction from national parks

Unlike national parks (natsionalny park), which permit recreation and tourism, or zakazniks, which are partial reserves protecting particular species or habitats, zapovedniks exclude the public entirely except by special permit, and their staff are primarily scientists and guards rather than tourism operators.
