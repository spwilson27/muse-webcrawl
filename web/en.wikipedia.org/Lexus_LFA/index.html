The Lexus LFA, the marque's limited-production V10 halo car.

The Lexus LFA is a two-seat sports car manufactured by Lexus, the luxury vehicle division of Toyota. It is the second model in the F performance marque line after the IS F, and the company's first standalone supercar. Production ran from December 2010 to December 2012 at Toyota's Motomachi plant, limited to 500 units, each effectively hand-built by a dedicated team of engineers and craftspeople.

Development

Development began around 2000 under chief engineer Haruhiko Tanahashi, with the aim of creating a world-class supercar that would redefine the Lexus brand. Concept cars were shown at the North American International Auto Show in 2005 and 2007. The production car was revealed at the Tokyo Motor Show in October 2009. The body uses carbon-fibre-reinforced polymer (CFRP) for the monocoque and much of the bodywork, a decision taken mid-development that required new in-house carbon-fibre production technology.

Engine and performance

The LFA is powered by a 4.8-litre V10 engine (Toyota 1LR-GUE) co-developed with Yamaha, producing 552 hp at 8,700 rpm and revving to 9,000 rpm. Its exhaust note was famously tuned with input from Yamaha's musical instrument division. The front-mid-engined, rear-transaxle layout gives a 48:52 weight distribution. The car accelerates from 0–100 km/h in about 3.7 seconds, with a top speed of 325 km/h (202 mph). A Nürburgring Package edition set a lap time of 7:14.64 at the Nürburgring Nordschleife in 2011, among the fastest for a production car at the time.

Reception and legacy

Although sold at a loss, the LFA was widely praised by journalists for its engine sound, handling balance, and build quality, and it has become a sought-after collector car, with values rising above its original price of about US$375,000.
