Brasenose College (BNC) is a constituent college of the University of Oxford in England, situated on Radcliffe Square opposite the Radcliffe Camera. It was founded in 1509 by William Smyth, Bishop of Lincoln, and Sir Richard Sutton, on the site of the medieval Brasenose Hall.

Name and history

The unusual name is thought to derive from a "brazen nose" — a brass door-knocker in the shape of a nose (or a beast's snout) that served as the hall's emblem. A 13th-century knocker associated with the college was long kept at Stamford, Lincolnshire, and returned to Brasenose in 1890; it is displayed in the college hall. The college grew through successive building campaigns, adding its chapel and library in the 17th century and New Quad in the 19th and 20th centuries.

The college today

Brasenose has roughly 370 undergraduates and 210 postgraduates. Alumni include former prime minister David Cameron, Australian prime minister Malcolm Turnbull, the diarist and theologian John Buchan, and the comedian Michael Palin. The college is known for traditions including its Ale Verses and for strong showings in intercollegiate sport and the Norrington Table of degree results.
