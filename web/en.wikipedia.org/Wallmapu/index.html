Wallmapu

Wallmapu is the name given in Mapudungun to the ancestral territory of the Mapuche people, spanning parts of south-central Chile and southwestern Argentina. The term combines wall, meaning "surrounding" or "entire," and mapu, meaning "land," and is often rendered as "the surrounding land" or "the universe of the land."

Geography

Wallmapu is traditionally divided by the Andes into Gulu Mapu, the western lands in present-day Chile between the Itata and Toltén rivers and the Pacific Ocean, and Puel Mapu, the eastern lands in present-day Argentina extending toward the pampas.

History

The Mapuche maintained their independence from the Inca Empire and later from the Spanish Empire, which recognized a frontier at the Biobío River through a series of parliaments beginning with the Parliament of Quillín in 1641. Wallmapu was incorporated into Chile and Argentina only in the late nineteenth century, through Chile's Occupation of Araucanía (1861–1883) and Argentina's Conquest of the Desert.

Contemporary usage

Today "Wallmapu" is widely used by Mapuche organizations and in political discourse concerning land restitution, autonomy, and recognition within Chile and Argentina. The term gained renewed international attention during Chile's constitutional process in 2021–2022, when Mapuche leader Elisa Loncón presided over the Constitutional Convention.
