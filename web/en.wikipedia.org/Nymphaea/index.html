Nymphaea

Nymphaea is a genus of hardy and tender aquatic plants in the family Nymphaeaceae. The genus has a cosmopolitan distribution, with many species cultivated as ornamentals, and many are familiar plants in water gardening. All water lilies are in the order Nymphaeales. Its common name is water lily, shared with members of related genera. The genus name is from the Greek νυμφαία, nymphaia, and Latin nymphaea, which mean "water lily" and were inspired by the nymphs of Greek and Latin mythology.

Description

Leaves are round, with a radial notch in Nymphaea and Nuphar, but fully circular in Victoria. The flowers are large and showy in most species, with colors ranging from white and yellow to pink, red, blue, and purple. Many species are fragrant and are pollinated by beetles and other insects. The fruit is berry-like and borne on a curving stalk, ripening underwater.

Cultivation

Water lilies are popular garden plants for ponds and water gardens. Hardy species can survive temperate winters, while tropical species require warm water. Cultivars have been bred for flower color, size, and bloom time, and many have received horticultural awards.

Species

About 60 species are recognized, including Nymphaea alba (European white water lily), N. caerulea (blue lotus of Egypt), N. lotus, N. odorata, and N. mexicana.
