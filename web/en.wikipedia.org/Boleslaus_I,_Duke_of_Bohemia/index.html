Boleslaus I, nicknamed "the Cruel"

Boleslaus I (Czech: Boleslav I.; c. 908/910 – 15 July 972), nicknamed the Cruel, was Duke of Bohemia from 935 (or 929 in some traditions) until his death. A member of the Přemyslid dynasty, he came to power after the murder of his elder brother, Wenceslaus I — later canonized as Saint Wenceslaus — an act in which later chroniclers, especially Cosmas of Prague, implicated him.

Rise to power

On 28 September 935 (traditionally 929), Wenceslaus was murdered at Stará Boleslav by a party of nobles associated with Boleslaus, who succeeded him as duke. Whatever Boleslaus's direct role, chroniclers writing centuries later fixed his reputation as a fratricide, in contrast to the martyr cult of his brother.

Reign

Boleslaus proved an energetic ruler. He fought a long war against the German king Otto I, which ended in 950 with Boleslaus accepting Otto's overlordship and paying tribute; thereafter Bohemian troops fought alongside Otto's, including at the Battle of Lechfeld in 955 against the Magyars. He expanded Bohemian power eastward into Moravia and parts of Silesia and the Kraków region, built a network of strongholds administered by ducal officials, and minted the first Bohemian denarii bearing his name. Despite his reputation, he consolidated the church organization of the duchy, and his daughter Doubravka's marriage to Mieszko I of Poland in 965 helped bring Christianity to Poland; another daughter, Mlada, helped secure the founding of the Prague bishopric, established shortly after his death.

Death and succession

Boleslaus died in 972 and was succeeded by his son Boleslaus II, called the Pious. The contrast between the brothers' reputations — saint and fratricide — shaped Czech historical memory throughout the Middle Ages.
