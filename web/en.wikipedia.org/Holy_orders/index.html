Holy orders

Holy orders
OrdersBishop, priest, deacon
TraditionsCatholic, Orthodox, Anglican, some Lutheran
RiteLaying on of hands and prayer of consecration

Holy orders is the sacrament (in Catholic and Orthodox theology) or rite by which men — and in some churches women — are ordained as bishops, priests or deacons in the Christian ministry. The term derives from the Latin ordo, denoting an established order of ministry.

The three orders

Since the early church, ordained ministry has been structured in three degrees: the episcopate (bishops), the presbyterate (priests) and the diaconate (deacons). Ordination is conferred by a bishop through the laying on of hands accompanied by a consecratory prayer. Catholic theology holds that ordination imprints an indelible sacramental character and that bishops stand in apostolic succession.

Practice across churches

Eastern Orthodox and Oriental Orthodox churches share the threefold structure and sacramental understanding. Anglican churches retain bishops, priests and deacons but differ over women's ordination, admitted in many provinces since the 1970s. The Catholic Church restricts ordination to men and requires celibacy of Latin-rite priests. Most Protestant traditions, lacking a sacramental view of orders, ordain ministers by congregational or denominational procedures instead.
