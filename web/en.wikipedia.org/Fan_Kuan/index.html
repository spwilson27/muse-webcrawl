Fan Kuan

Fan Kuan (Chinese: 范寬; c. 960 – c. 1030), courtesy name Zhongli, was a Chinese landscape painter of the Northern Song dynasty. He is regarded, with Li Cheng and Guan Tong, as one of the "three great rivals" of early Song landscape painting, and his monumental style — towering mountains rendered with dense texture strokes — set a standard emulated for centuries.

Life and work

Little is known of Fan Kuan's life. He is said to have been a native of Huayuan (in modern Shaanxi), to have studied first under Li Cheng and then to have turned directly to nature, living reclusively in the Zhongnan and Taihua mountains and observing their changing weather and moods. His mature manner used "raindrop" texture strokes (yudian cun) to build massive, dark mountain forms of overwhelming presence, beneath which tiny human figures emphasise nature's scale.

Travelers Among Mountains and Streams

His only widely accepted surviving masterpiece is the hanging scroll Travelers Among Mountains and Streams (谿山行旅圖), now in the National Palace Museum in Taipei. One of the most famous paintings in Chinese art, it depicts a colossal central peak rising above mists, with a train of muleteers barely visible at lower right; Fan Kuan's signature was discovered hidden in the foliage in 1958. The painting exemplifies the Northern Song ideal of landscape as a vision of cosmic order.
