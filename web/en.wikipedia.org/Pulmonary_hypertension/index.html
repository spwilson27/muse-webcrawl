Pulmonary hypertension

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Pulmonary hypertension
Specialty: Cardiology, pulmonology
Definition: mean pulmonary arterial pressure > 20 mmHg at rest
Symptoms: breathlessness, fatigue, syncope, chest pain
Complication: right heart failure (cor pulmonale)

Pulmonary hypertension (PH) is a condition of increased blood pressure in the arteries of the lungs. It is defined haemodynamically as a mean pulmonary arterial pressure greater than 20 mmHg at rest, measured by right heart catheterisation. The increased load progressively strains the right ventricle and, untreated, leads to right heart failure and death.

Classification

The World Health Organization classification divides PH into five groups: (1) pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH), including idiopathic, heritable and drug-induced forms and disease associated with connective tissue disorders, HIV infection and congenital heart disease; (2) PH due to left heart disease; (3) PH due to lung diseases or hypoxia; (4) chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension (CTEPH); and (5) PH with unclear or multifactorial mechanisms.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms are non-specific and often delay diagnosis: exertional breathlessness, fatigue, palpitations, exertional syncope and signs of right heart failure such as peripheral oedema. A loud pulmonary component of the second heart sound and a parasternal heave may be found on examination.

Diagnosis and treatment

Suspicion usually arises from echocardiography; confirmation requires right heart catheterisation. Treatment depends on the group: PAH-specific therapies include endothelin receptor antagonists, phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors, prostacyclin analogues and soluble guanylate cyclase stimulators; CTEPH may be cured by pulmonary endarterectomy. Supportive measures include diuretics, oxygen and anticoagulation, and lung or heart–lung transplantation is considered in advanced disease.
