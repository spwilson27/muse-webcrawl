Glock 17

The Glock 17 is a polymer-framed, short recoil-operated, striker-fired semi-automatic pistol designed and produced by the Austrian manufacturer Glock Ges.m.b.H. Introduced in 1982, it entered service with the Austrian military and police as the P80, and went on to become one of the most widely used service pistols in the world, adopted by law-enforcement agencies and armed forces in dozens of countries.

Development

In the late 1970s the Austrian Federal Army announced a competition for a new service pistol. Gaston Glock, whose company made curtain rods and later field knives and entrenching tools, had no prior experience designing firearms but assembled a team of small-arms experts. The resulting design used a polymer frame — then a radical choice — and only 34 components. The pistol passed Austria's demanding trials, reportedly including being dropped from a height and run over, and was adopted in 1982. Contrary to a persistent myth, the "17" designation refers to Glock's 17th patent rather than the magazine capacity, though the magazine does hold 17 rounds.

Design

The Glock 17 uses a modified Browning short-recoil locked-breech action with a striker-fired mechanism and Glock's "Safe Action" system of three passive safeties: a trigger safety, a firing pin safety and a drop safety. There is no manual safety lever or decocker. The frame is made of a proprietary nylon-based polymer, while the slide and barrel are steel finished with a ferritic nitrocarburizing treatment for corrosion resistance.

Variants and adoption

The Glock 17 spawned an extensive family of models in various calibres and sizes, including the long-slide Glock 17L, the compact Glock 19, and the select-fire Glock 18. The pistol has been produced in several generations with changes to grip texture, recoil spring assembly and magazine design. By the 21st century Glocks were carried by a large share of American police officers, and the design profoundly influenced the broader handgun market toward striker-fired, polymer-framed duty pistols.
