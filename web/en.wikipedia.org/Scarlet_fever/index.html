The characteristic red rash of scarlet fever

Scarlet fever, also known as scarlatina, is an infectious disease caused by group A Streptococcus bacteria, the same organism responsible for strep throat. It most commonly affects children between five and fifteen years of age and is characterised by a sore throat, fever and a distinctive bright red rash.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms usually begin one to four days after exposure. They include fever, sore throat, headache and swollen lymph nodes, followed within a day or two by a fine, sandpaper-like red rash that typically starts on the trunk and spreads. The tongue may appear coated and then bright red, a feature known as "strawberry tongue". As the illness resolves, the skin may peel.

Cause and transmission

Scarlet fever is caused by strains of Streptococcus pyogenes that produce erythrogenic (pyrogenic) exotoxins. It spreads through respiratory droplets and close contact. Untreated, it can lead to complications such as rheumatic fever, kidney inflammation (post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis), ear infections and abscesses.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is usually clinical and can be confirmed by throat culture or a rapid antigen test. Antibiotics such as penicillin or amoxicillin shorten the illness, reduce transmission and prevent most complications. Before antibiotics, scarlet fever epidemics caused significant childhood mortality, though the disease is generally milder today.
