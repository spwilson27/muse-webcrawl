Amitai Etzioni (born Werner Falk; January 4, 1929 – May 31, 2023) was a German-born Israeli-American sociologist best known as a founder of the communitarian movement in the early 1990s and as founder of the Communitarian Network, a non-profit, non-partisan organization dedicated to supporting the moral, social, and political foundations of society.

Life and career

Born in Cologne, Etzioni fled Nazi Germany with his family and grew up in Mandatory Palestine. He studied at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem and received his PhD from the University of California, Berkeley. He taught at Columbia University for two decades, served as a senior advisor in the Carter White House, and from 1980 was a professor at George Washington University.

Work

Etzioni's early work, including A Comparative Analysis of Complex Organizations and The Active Society, addressed organizational theory and social guidance. His communitarian writings, such as The Spirit of Community (1993), argued for a balance between individual rights and social responsibilities. He also wrote on privacy, security, and the "I & We" paradigm in social theory.
