Tigrinya language

Tigrinya (ትግርኛ, Tigriñña), sometimes also spelled Tigrigna, is an Ethiopian Semitic language spoken in the Tigray Region of northern Ethiopia and in Eritrea, where it is one of the most widely spoken languages and a working language of government. It is closely related to Amharic and to the ancient liturgical language Ge'ez, from which its script descends.

Tigrinya is the most widely spoken language in Eritrea and the third most spoken in Ethiopia. Large diaspora communities speak it in Sudan, Israel, Europe and North America. Along with Arabic and English, it serves as a working language of the Eritrean state, and it is the medium of primary education in much of Eritrea.

History

Tigrinya developed from Ge'ez, the language of the ancient Kingdom of Aksum. While Ge'ez ceased to be spoken as a vernacular by roughly the early medieval period, it remained the liturgical language of the Ethiopian and Eritrean Orthodox churches, and its script — an abugida in which each symbol represents a consonant–vowel syllable — was adapted for Tigrinya, Amharic and other Ethiopian languages. The earliest surviving written examples of Tigrinya date to the 13th century. During the Italian colonial period in Eritrea (1890–1941), Tigrinya gained a printed literature, newspapers and schoolbooks, which helped standardise the language.

Phonology

Tigrinya has a consonant system typical of Ethiopian Semitic languages, including ejective consonants such as /t'/, /k'/, /s'/ and /tʃ'/. It preserves pharyngeal consonants /ħ/ and /ʕ/ inherited from Ge'ez, though in many dialects these are weakened in everyday speech. The vowel system has seven vowels, and vowel length is not contrastive. Stress is generally weak and non-phonemic.

Grammar

Like other Semitic languages, Tigrinya builds words from consonantal roots interwoven with vowel patterns. The basic word order is subject–object–verb (SOV), unusual among Semitic languages and attributed to long contact with Cushitic languages. Verbs agree with their subjects and may incorporate object pronouns as suffixes. The language distinguishes masculine and feminine gender in the second and third person, and it has a rich system of derived verb stems expressing passive, causative, reciprocal and frequentative meanings.

Writing system

Tigrinya is written with the Ge'ez script, an abugida of more than 200 basic characters once the full set of consonant–vowel combinations is counted. The script is written left to right and traditionally marks word boundaries with two vertical dots, though modern usage mostly follows European punctuation. Because the script was designed for Ge'ez, Tigrinya spelling is largely phonemic but preserves some historical conventions.

Dialects

Pronunciation and vocabulary vary between Eritrea and Tigray and from town to town; the dialects of Asmara and of the Tigrayan city of Adwa are often cited as reference varieties. Differences are generally not an obstacle to mutual intelligibility, and a common written standard is used in media and education.
