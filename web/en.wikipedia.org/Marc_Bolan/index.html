Marc Bolan

Born: Mark Feld, 30 September 1947, London
Died: 16 September 1977, London (aged 29)
Occupation: Singer, songwriter, guitarist
Known for: Frontman of T. Rex; pioneer of glam rock

Marc Bolan (born Mark Feld; 30 September 1947 – 16 September 1977) was an English singer, songwriter and guitarist, best known as the frontman of the band T. Rex. He was a pioneer of glam rock in the early 1970s, when "T. Rextasy" rivalled Beatlemania in British press coverage.

Career

After a mod adolescence, a brief stint in John's Children and experiments in modelling and acting, Bolan formed the psychedelic folk duo Tyrannosaurus Rex with percussionist Steve Peregrin Took in 1967. Shortening the name to T. Rex and switching to electric guitar, he scored a string of UK hits from 1970 — "Ride a White Swan", "Hot Love", "Get It On (Bang a Gong)", "Telegram Sam", "Metal Guru" and "20th Century Boy" — and classic albums including Electric Warrior (1971) and The Slider (1972). His style — glitter, satin and feather boas over boogie riffs and whimsical lyrics — defined glam rock's first wave.

Later years and death

By the mid-1970s Bolan's commercial fortunes declined amid personal problems, though a 1977 television series, Marc, and the punk-influenced album Dandy in the Underworld signalled a comeback. On 16 September 1977, two weeks before his 30th birthday, he was killed when the car driven by his partner Gloria Jones struck a tree in Barnes, London. The site is now a memorial shrine.

Legacy

Bolan's music influenced glam, punk, Britpop and alternative rock; T. Rex was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2020.
