Batting (cricket)

RoleBatsman (batter)
EquipmentBat, pads, gloves, helmet
ObjectiveScore runs; protect the wicket
Governing lawsLaws of Cricket (MCC), esp. Laws 25–31

In the sport of cricket, batting is the act or skill of hitting the ball with a bat to score runs or prevent the loss of one's wicket. A player who is currently batting is denoted a batsman (in modern usage, also "batter"), while the act of hitting the ball is called a shot or stroke. The term "batsman" is applied regardless of whether the player is male or female, though "batter" is increasingly used, notably by the Marylebone Cricket Club since 2021.

Technique

The batsman stands in front of the stumps at one end of the pitch, facing the bowler. Orthodox strokes include the defensive block, the drive, the cut, the pull, the hook, the glance and the sweep; modern limited-overs cricket has added unorthodox shots such as the reverse sweep, the switch hit, the scoop or ramp shot, and the helicopter shot. Batsmen wear protective equipment including pads, gloves, a thigh guard and, against fast bowling, a helmet. Two batsmen are on the field at once, one at each end of the pitch, crossing to score runs; hitting the ball to the boundary scores four runs, or six if it crosses without bouncing.

Tactics and roles

Teams arrange their batting order from specialist opening batsmen through the middle order to the tail-enders, who are usually bowlers with lesser batting ability. The innings ends when ten wickets fall or the allotted overs are exhausted. Statistics such as the batting average (runs scored per dismissal) and strike rate (runs per 100 balls faced) measure a batsman's performance. In Test cricket Donald Bradman's career average of 99.94 is regarded as one of the greatest achievements in any sport.

Dismissals

A batsman can be dismissed by being bowled, caught, leg before wicket (LBW), run out, stumped, hit wicket, handled the ball, obstructing the field, hit the ball twice, timed out, or by retiring out.
