Setouchi Triennale

A site-specific installation on one of the Triennale's island venues.

The Setouchi Triennale is a contemporary art festival held every three years on islands and coastal areas of the Seto Inland Sea in Japan. First staged in 2010, it is presented in spring, summer and autumn sessions across venues including Naoshima, Teshima, Inujima, Shodoshima, Ogijima, Megijima and the ports of Takamatsu and Uno.

Background

The festival grew out of art projects developed on Naoshima from the late 1980s by the Benesse Corporation and architect Tadao Ando, which transformed former industrial land with museums such as the Chichu Art Museum. The Triennale extended that model across the region under artistic director Fram Kitagawa, who had previously organised the Echigo-Tsumari Art Triennale. Its stated theme is the "restoration of the sea": using art to revitalise island communities affected by depopulation, ageing and industrial pollution, and to reconnect visitors with the Inland Sea's landscape and history.

Format and impact

Artists create site-specific works in abandoned houses, schools and factories as well as outdoor installations, and many projects involve local residents as volunteers and guides. Attendance has grown to around a million visitors per edition. The festival is credited with boosting tourism and encouraging some migration to the islands, while also prompting debate about overtourism, ferry capacity and the balance between art-led regeneration and residents' daily lives. Several works remain on display between editions, making the islands a year-round destination.
