Pikillaqta

Location
Cusco Region, Peru
Culture
Wari (Huari) Empire
Period
c. 550–1100 AD

Pikillaqta (Quechua for "flea town" or "small town") is an archaeological site in south-central Peru, about 20 km east of Cusco. It was one of the most important administrative centres of the Wari (Huari) culture, which dominated the region between roughly 550 and 1100 AD, before the rise of the Inca.

Layout

The site covers about 50 hectares and is laid out in a strict geometric grid of streets and rectangular blocks — a hallmark of Wari planning. It contains hundreds of structures, many of them two-storey buildings of fieldstone set in mud mortar, originally plastered and painted white. Large walled compounds likely served administrative, ceremonial, and storage functions rather than ordinary housing.

Finds and significance

Excavations have uncovered offerings including small turquoise figurines — a famous cache of 40 stylized figures interpreted as depictions of Wari mythical ancestors. Pikillaqta guarded the southern entrance to the Cusco valley and formed part of a chain of Wari provincial capitals that anticipated Inca imperial administration.
