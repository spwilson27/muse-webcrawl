Cricket World Cup

The ICC Cricket World Cup is the international championship of One Day International (ODI) cricket. The event is organised by the International Cricket Council (ICC) every four years, with preliminary qualification rounds leading to a finals tournament. The tournament is one of the world's most viewed sporting events and is considered the flagship event of the international cricket calendar.

The Cricket World Cup trophy

History

The first World Cup was held in England in 1975, contested over 60 overs per side and won by the West Indies, who also won the second tournament in 1979. India upset the West Indies in the 1983 final at Lord's. Subsequent tournaments introduced 50-over matches, coloured clothing, white balls, and day/night matches from 1992. The 1987 tournament was the first held outside England, in India and Pakistan. The tournament has expanded and contracted in format over the years, with between 9 and 16 teams participating across different editions.

Australia is the most successful team, with six titles. India and the West Indies have each won twice, while Pakistan, Sri Lanka, and England have won once each. England's 2019 victory, in a final against New Zealand decided on boundary countback after a tied match and a tied Super Over, is regarded as one of the greatest matches ever played.

Format and qualification

Teams qualify through the ICC's league structures, principally the Cricket World Cup Super League and qualifier tournaments. Recent editions have used a round-robin group stage followed by semi-finals and a final, with ten teams contesting the 2019 and 2023 tournaments.
