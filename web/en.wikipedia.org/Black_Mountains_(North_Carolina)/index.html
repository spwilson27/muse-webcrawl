The Black Mountains are a mountain range in western North Carolina, part of the Blue Ridge Mountains within the Appalachian system. Though only about 15 miles long, the range contains the highest peaks in the eastern United States, including Mount Mitchell at 6,684 feet, the highest point east of the Mississippi River.

Geography

Six of the range's peaks exceed 6,300 feet. Notable summits besides Mount Mitchell include Mount Craig, Big Tom, Balsam Cone, and Celo Knob. The range forms a rough J shape enclosing the Cane River valley. Much of the range lies within Pisgah National Forest and Mount Mitchell State Park, North Carolina's first state park, established in 1915.

Ecology

Slopes are covered by dense forests that appear dark from a distance — the origin of the name — with spruce-fir forests at high elevations similar to those of Canada, grading into rich cove hardwood forests below. The high-elevation ecosystem hosts many endemic and disjunct species, though Fraser fir stands have suffered heavily from the balsam woolly adelgid.
