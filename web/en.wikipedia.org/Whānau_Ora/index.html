Whānau Ora

Whānau Ora is a public policy and service-delivery approach in New Zealand that places whānau — extended families — at the centre of health and social support. Rather than treating individuals through separate agencies, it supports families to set goals and navigate services across wellbeing, education, housing, income and culture.

Development

The approach grew from Māori health and social development thinking and was implemented nationally from around 2010. Commissioning agencies contract providers, many of them Māori and Pacific organisations, to deliver flexible support coordinated around whānau aspirations.

Debate

Supporters credit Whānau Ora with culturally grounded, integrated help. Critics and reviewers have examined accountability, measurement and value for money. It remains a distinctive example of Indigenous-informed public service design.
