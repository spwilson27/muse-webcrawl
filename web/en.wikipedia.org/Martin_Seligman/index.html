Martin Seligman

Born: 12 August 1942, Albany, New York
Occupation: Psychologist, University of Pennsylvania
Known for: Learned helplessness, positive psychology, PERMA model

Martin Elias Peter Seligman is an American psychologist, a founder of positive psychology and author of research on learned helplessness.

Learned helplessness

In experiments in the 1960s–70s, Seligman found animals exposed to inescapable shocks later failed to escape avoidable ones – "learned helplessness" – a model applied to depression and later reformulated around explanatory style (optimism vs pessimism).

Positive psychology

As president of the American Psychological Association in 1998, Seligman promoted studying strengths and wellbeing alongside pathology. His books include Learned Optimism (1991), Authentic Happiness (2002) and Flourish (2011), which proposed the PERMA model: Positive emotion, Engagement, Relationships, Meaning, Accomplishment.
