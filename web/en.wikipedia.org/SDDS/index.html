SDDS

SDDS may refer to several things:

  • Sony Dynamic Digital Sound – a digital surround sound format introduced by Sony for theatrical film presentation in 1993, competing with Dolby Digital and DTS. SDDS encodes up to eight channels of audio (a "7.1" layout with five front channels, two surround channels and a low-frequency effects channel) and prints the digital data along both outer edges of 35 mm film prints, outside the sprocket holes. It was first used on the film Last Action Hero (1993).
  • Self-Describing Data Sets – a file format and toolkit developed at Argonne National Laboratory for storing and processing scientific data, used in accelerator physics and other fields.
  • Special Data Dissemination Standard – a standard established by the International Monetary Fund (usually abbreviated SDDS) guiding member countries in providing economic and financial data to the public.
