H.264

H.264 / MPEG-4 AVC
Developed by: ITU-T VCEG and ISO/IEC MPEG (Joint Video Team)
Initial release: 2003
Status: Widely deployed

H.264, also known as MPEG-4 Part 10, Advanced Video Coding (MPEG-4 AVC), is a video compression standard based on block-oriented, motion-compensated coding. It was developed by the Joint Video Team of the ITU-T Video Coding Experts Group and the ISO/IEC MPEG group, with the first version completed in 2003.

Features

H.264 achieves substantially better compression than its predecessors MPEG-2 and MPEG-4 Part 2, roughly halving the bit rate for equivalent quality. Its tools include variable block-size motion compensation, multiple reference frames, in-loop deblocking filtering, and entropy coding methods CABAC and CAVLC. Profiles such as Baseline, Main and High target applications from videoconferencing to broadcast.

Applications

The standard became ubiquitous in Blu-ray Discs, broadcast television, web video, video sharing platforms, surveillance and mobile devices. Although newer standards such as HEVC (H.265) and AV1 offer greater efficiency, H.264 remains among the most widely supported codecs.
