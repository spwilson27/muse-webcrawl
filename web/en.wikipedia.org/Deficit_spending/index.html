Deficit spending

Deficit spending is the amount by which spending by a government exceeds its revenue over a particular period of time, also called simply deficit, or budget deficit; the opposite is budget surplus. The term may be applied to the budget of a government, a private company, or an individual.

Economic views

Keynesian economics holds that deliberate deficit spending can stimulate demand and lift an economy out of recession, with the deficit shrinking again as growth restores tax revenue. Critics from monetarist and Austrian schools argue that borrowing crowds out private investment, raises interest rates and burdens future taxpayers. Modern Monetary Theory contends that a sovereign currency issuer's constraint is inflation rather than solvency.

Deficits and debt

Repeated annual deficits accumulate into the national debt, which must be serviced through interest payments. Deficits are commonly expressed as a percentage of GDP to compare them across countries and over time. Some constitutions and fiscal rules — such as Germany's debt brake or the EU's Stability and Growth Pact — limit structural deficits.
