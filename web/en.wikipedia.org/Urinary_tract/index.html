Urinary tract

The human urinary tract: kidneys, ureters, bladder and urethra

The urinary tract is the system of organs that produces, stores and eliminates urine from the body. It consists of the kidneys, which filter blood to form urine; the ureters, muscular tubes that propel urine to the bladder; the urinary bladder, a hollow muscular organ that stores urine; and the urethra, through which urine leaves the body. Together with the kidneys it forms the urinary system.

Structure

The upper urinary tract comprises the kidneys and ureters; the lower tract comprises the bladder and urethra. Most of the tract is lined by transitional epithelium (urothelium), a stretchable, impermeable lining adapted to contact with urine. In males the urethra also carries semen and passes through the prostate gland; in females it is much shorter, which partly explains the higher incidence of urinary tract infections in women.

Function

Beyond excretion, the urinary system regulates blood volume and pressure, electrolyte and acid–base balance, and produces hormones such as erythropoietin and the active form of vitamin D. Urine flows from the renal pelvis down the ureters by peristalsis, is stored in the bladder (typically 300–500 ml in adults), and is expelled by the micturition reflex coordinated by the pontine micturition centre.

Clinical significance

Common disorders include urinary tract infections (usually caused by Escherichia coli), urinary stones (urolithiasis), obstruction from benign prostatic hyperplasia, incontinence, and cancers of the bladder, kidney and prostate. Imaging of the tract includes ultrasound, CT urography and cystoscopy.
