Nordschleife

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Nordschleife ("North Loop") is the northern circuit of the Nürburgring in the Eifel mountains of Germany. Built in the 1920s and opened in 1927, it is a 20.8 km (12.9 mile) road course with more than 70 corners and around 300 metres of elevation change, winding through forest past villages such as Adenau. Jackie Stewart famously nicknamed it "The Green Hell".

Racing history

The Nordschleife hosted the German Grand Prix until 1976, when Niki Lauda's near-fatal fiery accident — after he had campaigned for a boycott on safety grounds — ended Formula One racing there. The shorter Grand Prix circuit was later built beside it. The Nordschleife still hosts the Nürburgring 24 Hours endurance race and rounds of touring car series.

Public use

On "Touristenfahrten" (tourist ride) days the track operates as a one-way toll road open to the public, and manufacturers use it intensively for testing and for setting production-car lap records. Its length, blind crests, and changeable weather make it one of the most demanding circuits in the world.
