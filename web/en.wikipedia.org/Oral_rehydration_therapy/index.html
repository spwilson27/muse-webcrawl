Oral rehydration therapy

Oral rehydration therapy (ORT) is a treatment for dehydration involving drinking water mixed with sugar and salts (oral rehydration salts, ORS). It is used worldwide to prevent and treat dehydration from diarrhoea, including cholera, and is credited with saving millions of lives, particularly of children in developing countries.

Mechanism

ORT exploits glucose-coupled sodium transport in the small intestine: when glucose and sodium are present together, water follows them across the intestinal wall even during secretory diarrhoea, restoring fluid and electrolytes.

Formulation

The World Health Organization's reduced-osmolarity ORS contains sodium chloride, potassium chloride, trisodium citrate, and anhydrous glucose dissolved in clean water. A homemade approximation combines six teaspoons of sugar and half a teaspoon of salt per litre of water.

Impact

The Lancet has described ORT as potentially the most important medical advance of the 20th century. Its widespread promotion by WHO and UNICEF from the 1970s onward dramatically reduced child deaths from diarrhoeal disease.
