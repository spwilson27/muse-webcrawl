Configuration management

Configuration management (CM) is a systems engineering process for establishing and maintaining consistency of a product's performance, functional, and physical attributes with its requirements, design, and operational information throughout its life. In the technology world, configuration management is an IT management process that tracks individual configuration items (CIs) of an IT service.

History

Configuration management originated in the United States Department of Defense in the 1950s as a technical management discipline for complex military systems, setting standards for identifying, controlling, and auditing changes to hardware and software configurations. Standards such as MIL-STD-973 and later ANSI/EIA-649 formalized the practice.

Activities

The core activities of configuration management include configuration identification (selecting and documenting the physical and functional characteristics of configuration items), configuration control (evaluating and approving changes), configuration status accounting (recording and reporting change processing), and configuration auditing (verifying that a product matches its documentation). In software, version control systems and infrastructure-as-code tools such as Ansible, Puppet, and Chef automate configuration management at scale.
