The House of Valois was a cadet branch of the Capetian dynasty that ruled France from 1328 to 1589, succeeding the direct Capetians after the succession crisis resolved by the Salic law.

Early Valois and the Hundred Years' War

Philip VI, son of Charles of Valois, took the throne in 1328; Edward III of England's rival claim through his mother sparked the Hundred Years' War. Despite defeats at Crécy and Poitiers, the Valois recovered under Charles V and Charles VII, aided by Joan of Arc, expelling the English from all but Calais by 1453.

Later Valois

Louis XI consolidated royal power; Francis I made France a Renaissance power but began long wars with the Habsburgs in Italy. The cadet lines of Orléans and Angoulême continued the house. The last Valois kings — Francis II, Charles IX and Henry III — reigned through the French Wars of Religion; Henry III's assassination in 1589 ended the dynasty, and the crown passed to the Bourbon Henry IV.

Burgundian branch

A junior line, Valois-Burgundy, held the Duchy of Burgundy from 1363 to 1477 and built a quasi-royal state between France and the Empire.
