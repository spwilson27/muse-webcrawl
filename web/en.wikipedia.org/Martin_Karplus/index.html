Martin Karplus (15 March 1930 – 28 December 2024) was an Austrian-American theoretical chemist. He shared the 2013 Nobel Prize in Chemistry with Michael Levitt and Arieh Warshel "for the development of multiscale models for complex chemical systems".

Life and career

Karplus was born in Vienna and fled Austria with his family after the Anschluss in 1938, emigrating to the United States. He studied at Harvard and earned his PhD in 1953 under Linus Pauling at Caltech. After posts at Oxford, the University of Illinois and Columbia, he joined the Harvard faculty in 1966, later also holding a professorship at the University of Strasbourg.

Contributions

He is known for the Karplus equation relating NMR coupling constants to dihedral angles, and he led the development of CHARMM, a widely used program for molecular dynamics simulations of biomolecules. His group's 1977 simulation of the small protein BPTI helped launch the field of computational protein dynamics. His multiscale QM/MM work made it possible to model reactions in enzymes by treating the active site quantum mechanically and the surrounding protein classically.
