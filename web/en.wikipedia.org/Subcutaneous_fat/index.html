Subcutaneous fat

Subcutaneous fat is the layer of adipose tissue stored directly beneath the skin. It is one of the body's main fat depots, distinct from visceral fat that surrounds the internal organs. Subcutaneous fat provides insulation, cushioning, and energy storage.

Subcutaneous fat
Type: Adipose tissue
Location: Beneath the skin
Function: Insulation, energy store, cushioning

Distribution

The amount and pattern of subcutaneous fat vary with sex, age, genetics, and hormones. Women typically carry a higher proportion, especially around the hips and thighs, while men accumulate more abdominal fat. Its thickness can be estimated with skinfold calipers and imaging.

Health

Excess body fat is associated with metabolic disease, but subcutaneous fat—particularly in the lower body—appears less harmful than visceral fat and may even be protective, as it secretes hormones such as leptin and adiponectin.

Measurement and modification

Diet, exercise, and overall energy balance affect subcutaneous fat levels. Procedures such as liposuction remove it directly, though they do not confer the metabolic benefits of lifestyle change.
