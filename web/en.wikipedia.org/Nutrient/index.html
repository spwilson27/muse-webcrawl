Nutrient

A nutrient is a substance used by an organism to survive, grow, and reproduce. In nutrition, nutrients are divided into macronutrients — carbohydrates, proteins, and fats, needed in large amounts and supplying energy — and micronutrients, the vitamins and minerals needed in small quantities. Water and dietary fibre are also commonly counted as essential dietary components.

Classification

Essential nutrients cannot be synthesised by the organism in sufficient quantity and must be obtained from food: these include nine amino acids, two fatty acids (linoleic and alpha-linolenic), thirteen vitamins, and a number of minerals such as calcium, iron, potassium, and zinc. Non-essential nutrients can be manufactured by the body, though they may still be obtained from the diet.

Plant nutrients

For plants, the primary macronutrients are nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium (NPK), supplemented by calcium, magnesium, and sulfur and by trace elements including iron, manganese, and boron. Deficiencies limit growth according to Liebig's law of the minimum. Excess nutrients from fertiliser run-off can cause eutrophication of waterways.

Deficiency and excess

Malnutrition spans both undernutrition — deficiencies such as scurvy (vitamin C), iron-deficiency anaemia, or iodine deficiency — and overnutrition, which contributes to obesity and metabolic disease. Dietary reference intakes published by health authorities specify recommended amounts for each nutrient and age group.
