Punjabi language

Punjabi (Gurmukhi: ਪੰਜਾਬੀ; Shahmukhi: پنجابی) is an Indo-Aryan language spoken by about 113 million people, principally in the Punjab region of Pakistan and India, making it one of the ten most widely spoken native languages in the world. It is the official language of the Indian state of Punjab and the most widely spoken language in Pakistan, though it lacks official status there. Unusually among Indo-Aryan languages, Punjabi is tonal: the historical loss of voiced aspirated consonants produced contrasting low, mid, and high tones.

Scripts

In India, Punjabi is written in the Gurmukhi script, standardized by Guru Angad in the sixteenth century and used for the Sikh scriptures. In Pakistan it is written in Shahmukhi, a Perso-Arabic script closely related to that of Urdu. This script division, formalized at the 1947 Partition, parallels the region's religious and political geography.

History and dialects

Punjabi developed from the Prakrits of the northwestern subcontinent, with early literature in the Nath yogi tradition and the verses of Baba Farid (12th–13th centuries). Major dialects include Majhi (the prestige standard of Lahore and Amritsar), Doabi, Malwai, and Puadhi in India, and Multani/Saraiki, Pothohari, and Hindko in Pakistan—some of which are claimed as separate languages. Sikh scripture, the Guru Granth Sahib, preserves a sacred literary register, while Sufi poets such as Bulleh Shah and Waris Shah, author of the epic Heer (1766), anchor its classical canon.

Status and diaspora

Punjabi is an official language of India at the state level in Punjab, Haryana, and Delhi, and it is a scheduled language of the Indian constitution. Large diaspora communities in Canada, the United Kingdom, and the United States maintain Punjabi media, and it is one of the most spoken immigrant languages in Canada. Punjabi popular music, especially bhangra, has achieved global reach.
