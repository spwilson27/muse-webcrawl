Quantum complexity theory

Quantum complexity theory is the subfield of computational complexity theory that deals with complexity classes defined using quantum computers, the quantum analogue of the Turing machine model. It studies the resources — time, space, and queries — needed for quantum algorithms and how quantum complexity classes relate to classical ones.

Key classes

The central class is BQP (bounded-error quantum polynomial time), the set of decision problems solvable by a quantum computer in polynomial time with error probability at most 1/3. BQP is known to contain BPP and to be contained in PSPACE; whether it contains NP-complete problems is a major open question, though Shor's algorithm places factoring in BQP. QMA is the quantum analogue of NP, and query-complexity classes capture the speedups of algorithms like Grover's.

Oracle separations

Relative to oracles, quantum classes can be separated from classical ones — for example Simon's problem gives an exponential separation — guiding intuition about where quantum advantage may lie.
