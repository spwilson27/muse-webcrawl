Roadster (automobile)

A roadster (also called a spider or spyder) is an open two-seat car with emphasis on a sporting appearance or character. Historically the term described a two-seat car without weather protection; in modern usage it generally refers to a two-seat convertible without fixed side windows.

History

In the 19th century "roadster" described a horse suited to travel on ordinary roads. Early automobiles adopted the term for open cars with a single row of seats, often with a folding top and a rumble seat at the rear. British sports cars such as the MG T-series popularised the type, and after the Second World War roadsters became a staple of European and Japanese manufacturers.

Modern examples

Well-known modern roadsters include the Mazda MX-5 (Miata), introduced in 1989 and the best-selling two-seat convertible in history, the BMW Z series, the Honda S2000 and the Tesla Roadster, an all-electric sports car produced from 2008 to 2012.
