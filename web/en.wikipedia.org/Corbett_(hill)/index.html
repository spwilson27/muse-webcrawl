Corbett (hill)

A Corbett is a mountain in Scotland that is between 2,500 and 3,000 feet (762–914 m) high, with a prominence (relative height) of at least 500 feet (152 m) on all sides. The classification is one of several used by hillwalkers to catalogue Scotland's peaks.

History

The list was compiled in the 1920s by John Rooke Corbett, a Bristol-based climber and founding member of the Scottish Mountaineering Club, and was published posthumously in 1953. There are 222 Corbetts in the current list.

Bagging

Climbers who ascend all the Corbetts are known as Corbetteers; the first recorded completion was by Corbett himself. Corbetts sit between the higher Munros (over 3,000 feet) and the Grahams (2,000–2,500 feet) in the hierarchy of Scottish hill lists, and many are regarded as finer viewpoints than their taller neighbours owing to their greater relative isolation.
