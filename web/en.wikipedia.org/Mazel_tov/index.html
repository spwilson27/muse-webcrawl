Mazel tov (Hebrew: מַזָּל טוֹב, romanized mazzāl ṭōḇ; Yiddish: מזל טוב) is a Jewish phrase used to express congratulations on a happy or significant occasion or event. Literally "good fortune", its conventional meaning in use is closer to "good luck has occurred" — that is, a wish that good things have come one's way.

Etymology

The word mazal derives from a Semitic root associated with the drip or flow of constellations and came to mean a constellation, a planet's influence, or fortune in general; tov means "good". In Mishnaic Hebrew the phrase reflected astrology, and a person's mazal was the fortune assigned by the stars.

Usage

"Mazel tov" is shouted at Jewish weddings after the groom breaks a glass, at bar and bat mitzvahs, at births and brit milah ceremonies, and informally for achievements such as a new job or graduation. Strictly, it congratulates someone on good fortune that has already arrived rather than wishing luck for the future. The phrase has passed into general English usage, and the response is typically simply to say thank you.
