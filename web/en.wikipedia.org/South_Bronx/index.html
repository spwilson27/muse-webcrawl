South Bronx

BoroughThe Bronx, New York City
NeighbourhoodsMott Haven, Melrose, Hunts Point, Morrisania, Concourse
Known forBirthplace of hip hop; Yankee Stadium (Concourse)

The South Bronx is an area of the New York City borough of the Bronx. Roughly the southwestern portion of the borough, it includes neighbourhoods such as Mott Haven, Melrose, Morrisania, Hunts Point, and Concourse, near the Harlem River.

History

Once a prosperous area of apartment houses and industry, the South Bronx suffered severe decline in the 1970s, marked by landlord abandonment, arson, and population loss; the phrase "the Bronx is burning" — associated with a 1977 World Series broadcast showing a nearby fire — became a national symbol of urban crisis. President Jimmy Carter's 1977 visit to Charlotte Street highlighted the devastation, and subsequent federal, city, and community efforts slowly rebuilt housing.

Culture

The South Bronx is the birthplace of hip hop: DJ Kool Herc's parties at 1520 Sedgwick Avenue in 1973 are widely credited as the genre's founding events, and the area produced pioneers including Afrika Bambaataa and Grandmaster Flash. The Hunts Point food distribution centre is among the largest in the world.

Revitalisation

Since the 1990s the population has rebounded, new housing and retail have arrived — including around the reconstructed Yankee Stadium (opened 2009) — while debates continue over gentrification and the area's historically high poverty and asthma rates.
