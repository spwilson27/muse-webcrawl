Complementary DNA

Complementary DNA (cDNA) is DNA synthesized from a single-stranded RNA template, typically messenger RNA, in a reaction catalyzed by the enzyme reverse transcriptase. It is frequently used to clone eukaryotic genes in prokaryotes.

Synthesis

cDNA is produced by isolating mRNA, annealing a primer, and using reverse transcriptase to create an RNA-DNA hybrid. The RNA strand is then degraded and a second DNA strand synthesized, producing double-stranded cDNA lacking introns.

Applications

Because cDNA contains only expressed sequences, libraries of cDNA are used to study gene expression, produce recombinant proteins, and, in the form of expressed sequence tags, to identify genes. Quantitative PCR often begins with cDNA made from RNA samples.
