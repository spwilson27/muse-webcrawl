Patristics

Patristics is the academic study of the Church Fathers — the early Christian writers, roughly from the end of the 1st century to the 8th century, whose theology shaped the doctrines of Christianity. The field examines their writings in Greek, Latin, Syriac, and other languages.

Periods

  • Apostolic Fathers — Clement of Rome, Ignatius of Antioch, Polycarp
  • Apologists — Justin Martyr, Irenaeus, Tertullian
  • Alexandrian and Cappadocian schools — Origen, Athanasius, Basil of Caesarea, Gregory of Nyssa
  • Latin Fathers — Ambrose, Jerome, Augustine of Hippo
  • Later Fathers — John of Damascus in the East; Gregory the Great and Bede in the West

Themes

Patristic study addresses the formation of the biblical canon, Trinitarian and Christological controversies, liturgy, monasticism, and the Fathers' use of classical philosophy. Major editions include Migne's Patrologia Graeca and Patrologia Latina.
