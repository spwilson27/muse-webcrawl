Theda Bara


Theda Bara, c. 1921

Theda Bara (born Theodosia Burr Goodman; July 29, 1885 – April 7, 1955) was an American silent film and stage actress. Known as one of the earliest sex symbols of cinema, she was widely promoted under the nickname "The Vamp", a figure of the seductive woman who lures men to their ruin. Her popularity helped define the femme fatale archetype in early Hollywood.

Bara was born in Cincinnati, Ohio, to a Jewish Polish immigrant tailor father and a Swiss-French mother. She studied at the University of Cincinnati before moving to New York City to pursue acting, performing on stage in regional companies and on tour.

Her film breakthrough came in A Fool There Was (1915), based on Rudyard Kipling's poem "The Vampire". The film was a sensation, and the studio, Fox Film Corporation, mounted an elaborate publicity campaign claiming she was an exotic Egyptian-born clairvoyant; her name was promoted as an anagram of "Arab death". She went on to make more than forty films for Fox, including Carmen (1915), Camille (1917) and Cleopatra (1917), the latter now mostly lost.

When her contract with Fox ended in 1919, her career declined; the public had grown tired of the vamp type. She retired from film in 1926 after making Madame Mystery, a comedy spoofing her own image. She married film director Charles Brabin in 1921. Because nearly all of her films were destroyed in the 1937 Fox vault fire, only a handful of her works survive, including A Fool There Was and The Unchastened Woman.

Bara died of abdominal cancer in Los Angeles in 1955. She has a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame.
