Tozinameran

Brand name: Comirnaty
Type: mRNA vaccine
Developers: BioNTech and Pfizer
Target: SARS-CoV-2 (COVID-19)

Tozinameran, sold under the brand name Comirnaty, is an mRNA-based COVID-19 vaccine developed by the German company BioNTech in partnership with the American company Pfizer. It was the first COVID-19 vaccine to receive emergency authorization in the United Kingdom and United States (in December 2020) and the first to receive full approval from the US Food and Drug Administration (August 2021).

Mechanism

The vaccine contains nucleoside-modified messenger RNA encoding the spike protein of SARS-CoV-2, packaged in lipid nanoparticles. After intramuscular injection, cells temporarily produce the spike protein, prompting the immune system to generate antibodies and T-cell responses against it. The vaccine does not contain live virus and the mRNA does not enter the cell nucleus or alter DNA.

Efficacy and use

In its phase III trial of more than 43,000 participants, tozinameran showed about 95% efficacy against symptomatic COVID-19 from the original virus strain, with strong protection against severe disease. It was originally given as two doses three weeks apart, with booster doses added later, and updated formulations were developed to target Omicron-lineage variants. The vaccine requires frozen or refrigerated storage, with later formulations allowing longer refrigerator storage.

Safety

Common side effects include injection-site pain, fatigue, headache, and fever, usually resolving within a few days. Rare serious adverse events identified through monitoring include anaphylaxis and myocarditis/pericarditis, the latter occurring mainly in adolescent and young adult males after the second dose; health authorities concluded that the benefits of vaccination outweigh these risks.
