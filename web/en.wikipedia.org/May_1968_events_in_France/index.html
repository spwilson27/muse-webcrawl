May 1968 events in France

The May 1968 events in France were a period of civil unrest, student occupation protests, and general strikes that brought France to the verge of revolution and briefly shook the government of President Charles de Gaulle. Beginning with student protests in Paris, the movement expanded into a general strike involving some ten million workers — roughly two-thirds of the workforce — the largest strike in French history.

Background

France in the 1960s enjoyed rapid growth, but universities were overcrowded and authoritarian, and young workers faced low wages and insecure jobs. At Nanterre University, protests led by the Movement of 22 March (including Daniel Cohn-Bendit) over dormitory rules and the Vietnam War spread in early May 1968; police eviction of students from the Sorbonne on 3 May triggered riots, and on the night of 10–11 May students built barricades in the Latin Quarter.

The general strike

Trade unions called a one-day general strike on 13 May; factory occupations followed, beginning at Sud-Aviation and Renault plants, until about ten million workers were on strike and much of the country was paralysed — no trains, no post, fuel shortages. De Gaulle briefly and secretly left France on 29 May to consult General Massu at Baden-Baden, then returned, dissolved the National Assembly, and called elections.

Outcome

The Grenelle agreements raised the minimum wage by 35%, and the June elections returned a Gaullist landslide as voters reacted against disorder. Yet May 68 left a lasting cultural and social legacy in France — in feminism, education, environmentalism, and attitudes to authority — although de Gaulle himself resigned a year later after losing a referendum.
