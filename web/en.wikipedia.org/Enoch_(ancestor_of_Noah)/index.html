Enoch (Hebrew: חֲנוֹךְ, Ḥănōḵ) is a biblical figure and patriarch prior to Noah's flood, the son of Jared and father of Methuselah. He was of the Antediluvian period in the Hebrew Bible. The text of the Book of Genesis says Enoch lived 365 years before he was taken by God. The text reads: "Enoch walked with God: and he was no more; for God took him." (Genesis 5:24) This is interpreted in some Jewish and Christian traditions as Enoch entering heaven alive.

In Genesis

Enoch appears in the genealogy of Seth in Genesis 5, the seventh generation from Adam. He is unusual among the patriarchs both for his comparatively short lifespan — 365 years, a solar number — and for the absence of the formula "and he died" used of everyone else in the list. His son Methuselah became the longest-lived figure in the Bible, and through Lamech, Enoch is the great-grandfather of Noah.

The Books of Enoch

Enoch is the subject of many Jewish and Christian traditions and is considered the author of the Book of Enoch (1 Enoch), an ancient Hebrew apocalyptic text that is canonical only in the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church. The book describes the fall of the Watchers, angels who descended to earth, Enoch's journeys through heaven and the cosmos, and coming judgement. Two further works, 2 Enoch and 3 Enoch, extend the tradition, the latter identifying Enoch with the angel Metatron.

In later tradition

The New Testament mentions Enoch in the genealogy of Jesus (Luke 3), praises his faith in Hebrews 11, and the Epistle of Jude quotes 1 Enoch as prophecy. In Islam, Enoch is commonly identified with the prophet Idris, mentioned in the Quran as a man of truth raised to a high place. In the Latter Day Saint movement, Enoch founded the righteous city of Zion.
