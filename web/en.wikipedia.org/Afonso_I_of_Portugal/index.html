Afonso I (c. 1106/1109/1111 – 6 December 1185), also known as Afonso Henriques and by the Muslim epithet Ibn Arrik ("son of Henry"), was the first king of Portugal. He achieved the independence of the County of Portugal, founding the Kingdom of Portugal and greatly expanding its territory southward at the expense of the Moors during the Reconquista.

Early life

Afonso was the son of Henry of Burgundy, Count of Portugal, and Teresa, the illegitimate daughter of King Alfonso VI of León and Castile. After his father's death in 1112, his mother ruled the county. In 1128, Afonso defeated his mother's forces at the Battle of São Mamede near Guimarães and assumed control of the county.

Independence and kingship

On 25 July 1139, after defeating a Muslim force at the Battle of Ourique, Afonso was proclaimed king by his troops. In 1143, by the Treaty of Zamora, Alfonso VII of León recognised Portuguese independence. Papal recognition followed in 1179 with the bull Manifestis Probatum issued by Pope Alexander III.

Expansion

Afonso captured Santarém and Lisbon in 1147, the latter with the help of northern European crusaders on their way to the Holy Land during the Second Crusade. He continued to push the frontier southward to the Alentejo, although the Algarve was only conquered by his descendants. He founded monasteries, including Santa Cruz in Coimbra and the Cistercian Alcobaça Abbey, and granted fueros (charters) to encourage settlement.

Death and legacy

Afonso died in Coimbra in 1185 and was succeeded by his son Sancho I. He is remembered as the founder of Portugal and a national hero; his tomb lies in the Monastery of Santa Cruz in Coimbra.
