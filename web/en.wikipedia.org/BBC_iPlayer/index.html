BBC iPlayer

Launched 25 December 2007
Owner BBC
Available in United Kingdom

BBC iPlayer is a video on demand service from the BBC. The service makes television and radio programmes produced by the BBC available online for a period after broadcast, and includes live streaming of the BBC's channels. It is available on a wide range of devices including computers, smartphones, tablets, smart TVs, and games consoles.

History

After a long approval and consultation process, iPlayer launched in beta in 2007 and officially on 25 December 2007. The launch version offered catch-up streaming and downloads of programmes from the previous seven days. The service grew rapidly in popularity and is credited with transforming viewing habits in the United Kingdom; the catch-up window was later extended, first to 30 days and then, for many programmes, to 12 months or longer.

Features

iPlayer offers streaming in high definition and increasingly in 4K Ultra HD, downloads for offline viewing, subtitles and audio description, personalised recommendations, and children's profiles. Since September 2016, users in the UK have required a TV licence to use the service, and access is restricted to the United Kingdom.

Impact

The service regularly records billions of programme requests a year and is consistently among the UK's most used streaming platforms, competing with global services while remaining free at the point of use for licence-fee payers.
