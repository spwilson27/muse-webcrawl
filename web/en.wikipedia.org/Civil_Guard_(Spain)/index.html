Civil Guard (Spain)

Native name: Guardia Civil
Formed: 1844
Jurisdiction: Spain
Type: Gendarmerie
Parent: Ministry of the Interior

Civil Guard officers, recognisable historically by the tricorn hat.

The Civil Guard (Spanish: Guardia Civil) is Spain's national gendarmerie force, founded in 1844. It is a military corps charged with civilian policing duties, primarily in rural areas and small towns, on highways, and in matters such as border control, coastal surveillance, environmental protection and weapons licensing. Together with the National Police Corps, it forms one of Spain's two national police forces. Its members have traditionally been known as benemérita ("the meritorious"), an official honorific granted in 1929.

History

The Civil Guard was created in 1844 during the reign of Isabel II, modelled on the French gendarmerie, to combat banditry and provide a reliable state presence in the countryside. Its first director, the Duke of Ahumada, imposed a strict code of conduct (the Cartilla del Guardia Civil). During the Spanish Civil War the corps split between the Republican and Nationalist sides. Under Franco it remained a central instrument of rural order; after the transition to democracy it was reformed and placed under the Ministry of the Interior while retaining military status, playing roles in counter-terrorism against ETA and in international peacekeeping missions.

Organisation and duties

The force is organised into zones, commands and companies, with specialised units including the Traffic Group (Agrupación de Tráfico), the Nature Protection Service (SEPRONA), the Maritime Service, the Mountain Rescue Service (GREIM), the Rapid Action Group (GAR) and the Central Operative Unit (UCO) for serious crime investigation. Civil Guards live in barracks-houses (casas cuartel) in many towns, a longstanding feature of the institution. Its traditional headgear, the black tricorn (tricornio), remains its best-known symbol, though everyday service now usually uses other caps.
