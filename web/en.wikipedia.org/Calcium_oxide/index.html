Calcium Oxide

Calcium oxide (formula CaO), commonly known as quicklime or burnt lime, is a widely used chemical compound. It is a white, caustic, alkaline, crystalline solid at room temperature.

Preparation

Quicklime is made by the thermal decomposition of limestone or seashells in a lime kiln at about 900 °C: CaCO3 → CaO + CO2. The process, called calcination, is among the oldest chemical operations of humanity.

Reactions

With water CaO reacts exothermically to give slaked lime, Ca(OH)2, releasing enough heat to hiss and boil — the slaking of lime. Left in air it slowly absorbs CO2 and reverts to carbonate.

Uses

Lime is essential in cement and mortar, steelmaking (as a flux removing impurities), paper production, water treatment, soil stabilisation and agriculture, flue-gas desulfurisation, and food processing (food additive E529). Heated to incandescence it produced the intense 'limelight' of 19th-century theatres.
