Identity by descent

A DNA segment shared identically by descent from a common ancestor.

Identity by descent (IBD) is a term used in genetic genealogy and population genetics. A segment of DNA is identical by descent in two or more individuals if those individuals have inherited it from a common ancestor without intervening recombination. In contrast, a segment is identical by state (IBS) if the individuals merely have the same sequence by chance or through a very old common origin.

Detection and use

IBD segments are detected from genome-wide SNP data as long runs of matching alleles uninterrupted by recombination breakpoints. Because recombination breaks up shared segments with every generation, the length distribution of IBD segments carries information about how many generations ago the common ancestor lived: close relatives share long segments, while distant cousins share short ones. Consumer genetic genealogy services use shared IBD to estimate relationships between customers, and population geneticists use IBD sharing patterns to infer recent demography, migration, and population structure.

Applications

IBD mapping exploits the fact that affected relatives are more likely to share a disease-causing region identically by descent, aiding gene discovery for inherited disorders. Runs of homozygosity within a single genome — where the two copies of a segment are IBD from a recent common ancestor of the parents — are used to measure inbreeding and to locate recessive disease genes through autozygosity mapping.
