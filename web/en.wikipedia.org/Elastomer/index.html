Elastomer
ClassPolymer
Key propertyLarge reversible (elastic) deformation

An elastomer is a polymer with viscoelasticity and very weak intermolecular forces, generally having a low Young's modulus and a high failure strain compared with other materials. Elastomers can be stretched to several times their original length and return to their original shape when the stress is removed. The term is a portmanteau of "elastic" and "polymer". Rubber is the best-known example.

Structure and mechanism

An elastomer consists of long, coiled polymer chains held together by sparse cross-links — in natural rubber, sulphur bridges formed during vulcanisation. Above its glass transition temperature the chain segments between cross-links move freely; stretching uncoils the chains, lowering entropy, and the entropic restoring force pulls the material back when the load is released. This entropy elasticity also explains why stretched rubber warms and why its stiffness increases with temperature.

Types

Elastomers may be thermosets (vulcanised natural rubber, neoprene, styrene-butadiene rubber, EPDM, silicone rubber), which cannot be remelted, or thermoplastic elastomers (such as SBS block copolymers and thermoplastic polyurethanes), whose physical cross-links soften on heating and can be reprocessed. Unsaturated elastomers are vulnerable to ozone and oxidation; saturated backbones such as EPDM and silicone are more weather-resistant.

Applications

Tyres consume the largest share of elastomer production; other uses include seals, gaskets, hoses, belts, gloves, adhesives, footwear, vibration mounts and medical devices.
