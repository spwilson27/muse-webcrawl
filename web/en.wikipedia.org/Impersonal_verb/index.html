Impersonal verb

An impersonal verb is a verb that cannot take a true subject, or has no logical subject. In sentences with impersonal verbs, a dummy pronoun such as English "it" often fills the subject position without referring to anything.

Weather verbs

The classic examples are weather verbs: "It rains," "It is snowing." Many languages express such ideas without any subject at all — Spanish llueve ("rains") consists of the verb alone — while Latin used third-person forms such as pluit. Finnish and other subject-optional languages likewise dispense with the dummy.

Other uses

Impersonal constructions also include modal and evaluative expressions such as "it seems," "it appears," and "it is necessary," and in Latin verbs of emotion such as pudet ("it shames") and oportet ("it is fitting"). Some grammarians also treat passive constructions without an expressed agent as impersonal passives, common in German and Dutch ("Es wird getanzt" — "there is dancing").

Theoretical treatment

Generative grammar treats the dummy "it" in such sentences as an expletive inserted to satisfy the requirement that clauses have subjects (the Extended Projection Principle), while the verb's argument structure assigns no thematic role to the subject position.
