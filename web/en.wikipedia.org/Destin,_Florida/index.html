Destin is a city in Okaloosa County, Florida, United States, located on the Emerald Coast of the Florida Panhandle. It is a principal city of the Crestview–Fort Walton Beach–Destin metropolitan area. Known for its white beaches and emerald green waters, Destin originated as a small fishing village and is now a major resort destination.

History

The city is named after Leonard Destin, a fishing captain from Connecticut who settled in the area in the 1840s and established a fishing camp. For more than a century the local economy centered on commercial and charter fishing; the Destin Fishing Rodeo, held every October since 1948, remains a signature event. The city was incorporated in 1984.

Geography

Destin occupies a peninsula separating Choctawhatchee Bay from the Gulf of Mexico. East Pass, the only outlet of the bay to the Gulf for many miles, lies at Destin's western end, and dredging maintains it for navigation. Henderson Beach State Park preserves a stretch of undeveloped shoreline within the city.

Economy and tourism

Tourism is the dominant industry. Visitors are drawn by the beaches—whose exceptionally fine white sand is composed of Appalachian quartz washed down to the coast—as well as charter fishing, golf, and the shops and restaurants of Destin Harbor and HarborWalk Village. Destin's fishing fleet is the largest in Florida, supporting its billing as "The World's Luckiest Fishing Village."
