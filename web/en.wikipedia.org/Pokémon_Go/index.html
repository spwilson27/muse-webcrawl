Pokémon Go

DeveloperNiantic
PublishersNiantic, The Pokémon Company
ReleaseJuly 6, 2016
GenreAugmented reality, location-based game
PlatformsiOS, Android

Pokémon Go (stylized as Pokémon GO) is a 2016 augmented reality (AR) mobile game, part of the Pokémon franchise, developed and published by Niantic in collaboration with Nintendo and The Pokémon Company for iOS and Android devices. The game uses GPS to locate, capture, train, and battle virtual creatures, called Pokémon, which appear as if they are in the player's real-world location.

Gameplay

Players create an avatar and explore a map based on their real surroundings. Pokémon appear in the wild and can be caught by throwing Poké Balls. Landmarks designated as PokéStops supply items, while Gyms host battles and, from 2017, cooperative Raid Battles against powerful Pokémon. The game follows a free-to-play model supported by in-app purchases.

Release and reception

The game was released in July 2016 to mixed reviews for its technical issues but widespread praise for popularizing location-based and AR gaming and encouraging physical activity. It became a global phenomenon: by the end of 2016 it had been downloaded more than 500 million times and was one of the most profitable mobile games ever, grossing over $6 billion by 2020.

Cultural impact and criticism

Pokémon Go was credited with boosting local businesses near PokéStops and prompting people to explore their communities. It also attracted criticism and legal action over accidents, trespassing, and the placement of game locations at sensitive sites. The game coined renewed interest in the Pokémon franchise and is frequently cited as a landmark in mainstream augmented reality.

Legacy

Niantic continued to support the game with new generations of Pokémon, trading, player-versus-player battles, and live community events. Its success influenced subsequent location-based titles, including Niantic's own Harry Potter: Wizards Unite and Pikmin Bloom.
