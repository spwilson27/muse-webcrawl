Duality (physics)

In physics, a duality is a mathematical equivalence between two theories or models that appear different but describe the same physical phenomena. Dualities are powerful tools because calculations that are difficult in one description may be easy in the dual one.

Wave–particle duality

Quantum mechanics exhibits wave–particle duality: entities such as electrons and photons display both wave-like behaviour (interference and diffraction) and particle-like behaviour (localised detection events), depending on the experimental arrangement. This principle was central to the development of quantum theory in the 1920s.

Electromagnetic duality

Maxwell's equations in vacuum are nearly symmetric under the interchange of electric and magnetic fields. Extensions of this idea lead to Montonen–Olive duality in supersymmetric gauge theories, relating electric charges to magnetic monopoles.

String dualities

String theory contains a web of dualities, including T-duality (relating strings on small and large circles), S-duality (relating strong and weak coupling), and the AdS/CFT correspondence, which equates a gravitational theory in anti-de Sitter space with a conformal field theory on its boundary.
