Fresno, California

Fresno (Spanish for "ash tree") is a city in the San Joaquin Valley of California, United States, and the county seat of Fresno County. It is the fifth-most populous city in California and one of the largest inland cities in the state, serving as the economic hub of the central San Joaquin Valley, one of the most productive agricultural regions in the world.

History

Fresno was founded in 1872 as a station of the Central Pacific Railroad and incorporated in 1885. Irrigation transformed the surrounding valley into intensive farmland noted for grapes, raisins and other crops; the raisin industry was famously boosted after 1873, when grape grower Francis T. Eisen's dried-on-the-vine grapes were marketed commercially. The city grew through the 20th century as an agricultural processing and distribution centre.

Geography and economy

Fresno lies roughly midway between Los Angeles and San Francisco and is the closest major city to Yosemite National Park's southern approaches and to Kings Canyon and Sequoia national parks. Agriculture, food processing, health care and education — including California State University, Fresno — are major employers. Landmarks include the Forestiere Underground Gardens and the Tower District.
