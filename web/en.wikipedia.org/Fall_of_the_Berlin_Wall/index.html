Fall of the Berlin Wall

Date9 November 1989
LocationBerlin, East Germany
Wall built13 August 1961
OutcomeBorder crossings opened; path to German reunification (3 October 1990)

The fall of the Berlin Wall (German: Mauerfall) on 9 November 1989, during the Peaceful Revolution, marked the destruction of the Berlin Wall and the figurative Iron Curtain, and was a watershed event in world history. It paved the way for German reunification less than a year later, and is considered one of the pivotal events marking the beginning of the end of the Cold War.

Background

The Berlin Wall had divided the city since 1961 and prevented East Germans from emigrating to the West. By 1989, reforms in the Soviet Union under Mikhail Gorbachev and openings elsewhere in the Eastern Bloc — Hungary dismantling its border fence with Austria and the Pan-European Picnic of August 1989 — let tens of thousands of East Germans flee westward. Mass protests, above all the Monday demonstrations in Leipzig, mounted pressure on the East German regime.

The night of 9 November

At an evening press conference, government spokesman Günter Schabowski, reading from new travel regulations he had not been fully briefed on, announced that East Germans could cross the border "immediately, without delay" — although the rules were meant to take effect the next day and required visas. Huge crowds gathered at checkpoints such as Bornholmer Straße, demanding passage. Overwhelmed and without clear orders, border guards under Lt. Col. Harald Jäger opened the crossing late that night; other checkpoints followed. Berliners from both sides climbed the Wall, celebrated, and began chipping away at it.

Aftermath

In the following days millions of East Germans visited the West. The Wall was progressively demolished — souvenired pieces were sold worldwide — and official dismantling was completed by 1992, with sections preserved as memorials including the East Side Gallery. Germany was formally reunified on 3 October 1990. See also: Berlin Wall, German reunification, Cold War, Peaceful Revolution.
