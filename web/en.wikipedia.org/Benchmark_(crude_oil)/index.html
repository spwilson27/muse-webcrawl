Benchmark (crude oil)

Major benchmarksBrent Crude; West Texas Intermediate (WTI); Dubai/Oman
PurposeReference pricing for crude oil grades worldwide

A benchmark crude oil or marker crude is a specific grade of petroleum that serves as a reference price for buyers and sellers of crude oil. Because there are many varieties and grades of crude, benchmarks allow the hundreds of streams traded globally to be priced as a differential to a standard reference.

Major benchmarks

The three primary benchmarks are Brent Crude, from the North Sea, used to price most internationally traded waterborne crude; West Texas Intermediate (WTI), a light sweet crude delivered at Cushing, Oklahoma, the reference for North American oil; and the Dubai/Oman average, used for Middle Eastern crude sold into Asia. Brent and WTI futures, traded on ICE and NYMEX respectively, are among the most liquid commodity contracts in the world.

Pricing

Other crudes — such as Nigeria's Bonny Light or Russia's Urals — are quoted at a premium or discount to a benchmark reflecting quality (density and sulfur content) and location. Regional benchmarks have occasionally decoupled: in 2011–2014 WTI traded at a large discount to Brent because of pipeline congestion at Cushing. See also Brent Crude and West Texas Intermediate.
