Heraklion

Country: Greece
Region: Crete
Population: ~180,000 (municipality)
Known for: Knossos, Venetian harbour

Heraklion (Greek: Ηράκλειο, Irákleio) is the largest city and the administrative capital of the island of Crete, Greece. It is the fifth largest city in Greece, with a population of roughly 180,000 in its municipality. The city is a major port and ferry hub connecting Crete with mainland Greece and the Aegean islands, and its airport is one of the busiest in the country.

History

The area was settled in Minoan times; the palace of Knossos, about 5 km southeast, was the ceremonial and political centre of the Minoan civilisation. The modern city grew on a site near the ancient port of Heracleum. Under Arab rule from 824 it was known as Rabd el-Khandaq ("fortress of the moat"), which became "Candia" under the Venetians, who held Crete from 1204 to 1669. The Venetians ringed the city with massive fortification walls and built the harbour fortress of Koules. After a siege lasting 21 years — one of the longest in history — the Ottomans took the city in 1669. Heraklion joined the modern Greek state in 1913.

Sights

The Heraklion Archaeological Museum holds the finest collection of Minoan art in the world, including the Phaistos Disc and the Bull-Leaping Fresco. Other landmarks include the Loggia, the Morosini Fountain in Lion Square, the cathedral of Saint Minas, and the tomb of writer Nikos Kazantzakis on the city walls. The reconstructed palace of Knossos, partly restored by Sir Arthur Evans, is the island's most visited archaeological site.
