János Kádár (1912–1989) was a Hungarian communist leader who governed Hungary from 1956 until 1988. Installed after the suppression of the Hungarian Revolution of 1956, he consolidated power through repression and then pursued a comparatively pragmatic and consumer-oriented form of socialism often called "Goulash Communism".

Early life and rise

Born János Csermanek in Fiume, Kádár joined the illegal communist movement as a young man and was repeatedly imprisoned. After the Second World War he rose in the Hungarian Communist Party, served as interior minister, and was himself imprisoned during the Rákosi-era purges before being rehabilitated.

The Kádár era

Kádár sided with the Soviet intervention that crushed the 1956 uprising led by Imre Nagy, who was later executed. After an initial period of reprisals, Kádár proclaimed that "whoever is not against us is with us", allowed greater cultural and economic flexibility, and introduced the New Economic Mechanism of 1968, a limited market reform. Hungary became known as "the happiest barracks in the socialist camp", though it remained a one-party state in the Warsaw Pact.

Fall and legacy

Aging and increasingly out of touch amid economic stagnation, Kádár was removed as party leader in May 1988 and died in July 1989, weeks before the end of communist rule in Hungary. His legacy remains contested, balancing relative prosperity and stability against his role in 1956 and its aftermath.
