Smartwatch

A smartwatch is a wearable computer in the form of a wristwatch. Modern smartwatches provide a local touchscreen interface for daily use, while an associated smartphone app provides management and telemetry, such as long-term biomonitoring. While early models could perform basic tasks such as calculations, digital time telling, translations, and game-playing, smartwatches released since 2015 generally have more general functionality closer to smartphones, including mobile apps, a mobile operating system, and WiFi and Bluetooth connectivity.

History

Early devices with timekeeping and computing functions include the 1972 Pulsar LED watch and the data-entry watches of the 1980s, such as the Seiko RC series. The first watch capable of running installable software is often considered to be the Timex Datalink of 1994. The category gained mainstream attention with the Pebble smartwatch, which raised over $10 million on Kickstarter in 2012, and with the launch of Android Wear (now Wear OS) in 2014 and the Apple Watch in 2015.

Features

Most smartwatches include fitness tracking through accelerometers, heart-rate sensors, and GPS receivers. Common features include notifications mirrored from a paired phone, contactless payments via NFC, sleep tracking, electrocardiogram (ECG) and blood-oxygen (SpO2) measurement, and voice assistants. Battery life typically ranges from one day to two weeks depending on the display technology and usage.

Operating systems

The principal smartwatch operating systems are Apple's watchOS, Google's Wear OS (developed with Samsung), and proprietary systems from manufacturers such as Garmin, Fitbit, and Huawei. See also wearable technology and activity tracker.
