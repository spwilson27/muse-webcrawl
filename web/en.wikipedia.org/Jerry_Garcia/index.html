Jerry Garcia

DiedAugust 9, 1995 (aged 53), Forest Knolls, California, U.S.

Jerome John Garcia (August 1, 1942 – August 9, 1995) was an American musician best known for being the principal songwriter, lead guitarist, and a vocalist with the rock band the Grateful Dead, which he co-founded and which became famous during the counterculture of the 1960s. Garcia performed with the Grateful Dead for their entire 30-year career, from 1965 to 1995.

Musical career

Raised in San Francisco, Garcia took up guitar at 15—having earlier lost the middle finger of his right hand in a childhood wood-chopping accident with his brother. He played in jug and bluegrass bands in the early 1960s Palo Alto folk scene before forming the band that became the Grateful Dead. Renowned for his melodic, improvisational guitar style, he also led the Jerry Garcia Band, played pedal steel guitar (including the famous part on Crosby, Stills, Nash & Young's "Teach Your Children"), and collaborated with mandolinist David Grisman. With lyricist Robert Hunter he wrote many of the Dead's signature songs, including "Dark Star", "Scarlet Begonias", and "Touch of Grey".

Legacy

Garcia was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame as a member of the Grateful Dead in 1994 and is consistently ranked among the greatest guitarists of all time. His cultural afterlife includes the Ben & Jerry's ice cream flavour Cherry Garcia and a devoted fan culture that outlived him.
