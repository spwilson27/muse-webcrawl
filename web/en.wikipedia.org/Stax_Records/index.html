Stax Records

Founded: 1957 (as Satellite Records)
Founders: Jim Stewart, Estelle Axton
Location: Memphis, Tennessee, US
Genre: Soul, R&B, funk, blues

Stax Records is an American record company, originally based in Memphis, Tennessee. Founded in 1957 as Satellite Records, the label changed its name to Stax in 1961 — combining the surnames of its founders, Jim Stewart and Estelle Axton. Along with Motown, it was one of the most influential soul music labels of the 1960s.

History

Operating from a converted movie theatre at 926 East McLemore Avenue, Stax developed a raw, horn-driven sound defined by its integrated house band, Booker T. & the M.G.'s, and the songwriting team of Isaac Hayes and David Porter. The label released classic recordings by Otis Redding, Sam & Dave, Carla Thomas, Rufus Thomas, Wilson Pickett and Albert King, distributed nationally through Atlantic Records.

The label's fortunes declined after the assassination of Martin Luther King Jr. in Memphis in 1968 and the loss of Otis Redding in a plane crash in 1967. Under Al Bell, Stax expanded into film and events such as the 1972 Wattstax concert, but financial problems forced it into involuntary bankruptcy in 1975. The Stax catalogue later passed through several owners; the label was revived under Concord Records, and the original site now houses the Stax Museum of American Soul Music, opened in 2003.
