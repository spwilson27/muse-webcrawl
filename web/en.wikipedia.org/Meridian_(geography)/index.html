Meridian (geography)

A meridian or line of longitude is the half of an imaginary great circle on the Earth's surface terminated by the North and South Poles, connecting points of equal longitude. The position of a point along the meridian is given by its latitude.

Prime meridian

The prime meridian is the meridian of 0° longitude from which other longitudes are measured. The internationally adopted prime meridian passes through the Royal Observatory in Greenwich, London, and was agreed at the International Meridian Conference of 1884.

Properties

All meridians converge at the poles. Together with its antipodal meridian, each meridian forms a full great circle. One degree of longitude along a meridian corresponds to roughly 111 kilometres at the equator, decreasing toward the poles.

Astronomical meridian

In astronomy, the meridian is the great circle passing through the celestial poles and the zenith of an observer's location; a celestial object is said to transit when it crosses the observer's meridian.
