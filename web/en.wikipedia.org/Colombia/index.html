Colombia, officially the Republic of Colombia, is a country primarily located in South America with insular regions in North America. It is bordered by the Caribbean Sea to the north, Venezuela to the east, Brazil to the southeast, Peru to the south, Ecuador to the southwest, the Pacific Ocean to the west, and Panama to the northwest. Colombia is divided into 32 departments, plus the Capital District of Bogotá, its capital and largest city.

Geography

Colombia is one of the world's most biodiverse countries, with territory spanning Amazon rainforest, highlands, grasslands, deserts, and coasts on both the Pacific and the Atlantic. The Andes split into three ranges (Cordilleras Occidental, Central, and Oriental) across the country. Its location near the equator gives it varied climates by elevation rather than by season.

History

Indigenous peoples including the Muisca, Quimbaya, and Tairona inhabited the territory before Spanish colonization began in 1499. The colony of New Granada became part of Spain's empire; independence was proclaimed in 1810 and secured after Simón Bolívar's victory at the Battle of Boyacá in 1819. Colombia briefly formed part of Gran Colombia with Venezuela, Ecuador, and Panama. The 20th century saw the civil conflict period known as La Violencia and, from the 1960s, an armed conflict involving guerrilla groups such as FARC, paramilitaries, and drug cartels. A 2016 peace accord with FARC formally ended that group's insurgency, though other armed groups remain active.

Economy and culture

Colombia is Latin America's fourth-largest economy; major exports include petroleum, coffee (Colombia is famed for its arabica), cut flowers, emeralds, coal, and bananas. Colombian culture blends Indigenous, Spanish, and African influences and is renowned for writers such as Gabriel García Márquez, artists such as Fernando Botero, and music genres including cumbia and vallenato.
