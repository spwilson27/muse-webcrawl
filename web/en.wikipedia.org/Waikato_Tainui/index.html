Waikato Tainui is a confederation of Māori iwi (tribes) and hapū (sub-tribes) in the Waikato region of New Zealand's North Island, whose members descend from the voyagers of the Tainui migration canoe (waka). Its constituent tribes include Waikato proper, Ngāti Hauā, Ngāti Korokī Kahukura, Ngāti Māhanga and others, numbering some 68 hapū and 68 marae.

History

The Tainui waka is said to have arrived from Hawaiki around the 14th century, making landfall at Whangaparāoa and later at Kāwhia Harbour, where its anchor stone remains. The Waikato tribes were central to the creation of the Kīngitanga (Māori King movement) in 1858, when Pōtatau Te Wherowhero of Waikato became the first Māori king. Following the invasion of the Waikato in 1863–64 during the New Zealand Wars, the Crown confiscated about 1.2 million acres of Waikato land under the New Zealand Settlements Act 1863 — the raupatu.

Settlement and governance

In 1995 Waikato Tainui became the first iwi to settle historical claims with the Crown under the Treaty of Waitangi settlement process: the Waikato Raupatu Claims Settlement Act 1995 returned some lands and provided $170 million in cash and assets, together with a formal Crown apology for the invasion and confiscations. A further settlement in 2008 established a co-governance framework for the Waikato River.

The iwi is governed by Te Whakakitenga o Waikato, a parliament of marae representatives, with an executive board, Te Arataura. Its commercial arm, Tainui Group Holdings, manages a substantial asset base in property, hotels, fisheries and equities centred on Hamilton. The tribal headquarters are at Hopuhopu near Ngāruawāhia, and the seat of the Kīngitanga is Tūrangawaewae Marae.
