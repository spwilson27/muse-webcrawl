Irish dance

Origin: Ireland

Forms: Step dance, céilí, sean-nós, set dancing

Irish dance refers to the traditional dance forms of Ireland, including both solo and group dancing. Its roots lie in a mixture of native Irish tradition and influences from England, Scotland, and continental Europe. The best-known form internationally is Irish step dance, popularised worldwide by shows such as Riverdance (1995) and Lord of the Dance.

Forms

Step dance is characterised by a stiff upper body, straight arms held at the sides, and rapid, precise footwork. Dancers perform in soft shoes (ghillies) for light dances — reels, light jigs, slip jigs and single jigs — and in hard shoes for hornpipes, treble jigs and treble reels, in which percussive sounds are made with the toe and heel. Céilí dances are group dances for two to sixteen people performed at social gatherings (céilithe). Set dancing, derived from French quadrilles, is danced by sets of four couples. Sean-nós ("old style") dancing is a relaxed, improvisational solo style from Connemara.

Competition and organisation

Competitions, called feiseanna, are governed by bodies such as An Coimisiún Le Rincí Gaelacha (CLRG), founded in 1927, which also certifies teachers. Competitors wear elaborate embroidered costumes, and female dancers traditionally wear curled wigs. The World Irish Dancing Championships (Oireachtas Rince na Cruinne) have been held annually since 1970.
