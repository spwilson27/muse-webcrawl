List of mountains in Greece

Mount Olympus (2,918 m), mythical home of the Greek gods and Greece's highest summit.

Greece is a predominantly mountainous country: about 80 per cent of its territory consists of mountains and hills, making it one of the most mountainous countries in Europe. The Pindus range forms the backbone of the mainland, running from the Albanian border southeast toward the Gulf of Corinth, with further ranges in Macedonia, Thrace, the Peloponnese and on the islands, notably Crete. This is a list of the most notable mountains and highest peaks in Greece.

Notable ranges and mountains

  • Pindus — the largest mountain range in Greece, called the "spine of Greece", extending some 160 km and home to the Vikos–Aoös National Park and its famous gorge.
  • Mount Olympus — in Greek mythology the home of the twelve Olympian gods; a national park and UNESCO Biosphere Reserve.
  • Taygetus — the dramatic range above Sparta, the highest in the Peloponnese.
  • Mount Pelion — mythological home of the centaurs, above Volos in Thessaly.
  • Mount Athos — the autonomous monastic peninsula (Holy Mountain) in Chalkidiki.
  • Mount Parnassus — sacred to Apollo and the Muses, site of Delphi on its slopes.
  • Lefka Ori and Psiloritis — the high ranges of Crete, including the Samaria Gorge.
  • Mount Dirfys — the highest peak on the island of Euboea (1,743 m).
