Alfonso XII

Alfonso XII (28 November 1857 – 25 November 1885), nicknamed "the Peacemaker" (el Pacificador), was King of Spain from 1874 until his death in 1885. His accession restored the Bourbon monarchy after the brief First Spanish Republic.

Early life and exile

The son of Queen Isabella II, Alfonso went into exile when his mother was deposed in the Glorious Revolution of 1868. He studied in Austria and at the Royal Military College, Sandhurst, in England. In the Sandhurst Manifesto of December 1874 he presented himself as a constitutional and Catholic monarch for a modern Spain.

Reign

Proclaimed king after a pronunciamiento by General Martínez Campos, Alfonso presided over the end of the Third Carlist War (1876) and the Ten Years' War in Cuba (1878), earning his nickname. Under the Constitution of 1876 and the statesmanship of Antonio Cánovas del Castillo, his reign established the turno pacífico, the managed alternation of Conservative and Liberal governments.

Marriages and death

His first wife, Mercedes of Orléans, died months after their wedding in 1878. He remarried Maria Christina of Austria, who was pregnant with their son — the future Alfonso XIII — when Alfonso XII died of tuberculosis and dysentery at age 27. Maria Christina served as regent until 1902.
