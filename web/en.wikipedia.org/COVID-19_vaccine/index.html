COVID-19 vaccine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A COVID-19 vaccine is a vaccine intended to provide acquired immunity against severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2), the virus that causes COVID-19. Developed at unprecedented speed during 2020, COVID-19 vaccines dramatically reduced severe illness, hospitalisation and death during the pandemic, and more than 13 billion doses had been administered worldwide by 2023.

Types

Several technology platforms were deployed. The mRNA vaccines of Pfizer–BioNTech (Comirnaty) and Moderna (Spikevax) were the first authorised in Western countries and the first mRNA vaccines ever approved for general use. Viral vector vaccines included Oxford–AstraZeneca and Janssen (Johnson & Johnson); inactivated vaccines included China's CoronaVac and Sinopharm products and India's Covaxin; protein subunit vaccines included Novavax.

Development and rollout

The first authorisations came in December 2020, less than a year after the virus's genome was published — a process accelerated by prior research on SARS and MERS, large public investment, and overlapping trial phases. Rollout priorities generally favoured healthcare workers, older people and the clinically vulnerable. Booster doses and updated formulations followed as variants such as Delta and Omicron emerged.

Effectiveness and safety

Vaccines proved highly effective against severe disease and death, though protection against infection wanes over time and is reduced by immune-evading variants. Serious adverse events are rare; they include anaphylaxis, myocarditis after mRNA vaccines in young males, and a clotting syndrome associated with adenoviral vector vaccines, which led several countries to restrict their use.
