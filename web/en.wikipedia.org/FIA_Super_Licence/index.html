The FIA Super Licence is a driver's qualification allowing the licence holder to compete in the Formula One World Championship. Introduced as a formal requirement by the FIA, it is the highest class of racing licence issued by the governing body.

Since 2016, applicants must be at least 18 years old, hold an International Grade A licence, have completed at least 80% of two seasons in eligible single-seater championships, and have accumulated 40 Super Licence points over the previous three seasons from results in junior categories such as Formula 2, Formula 3, IndyCar and Formula E.

Criteria

Besides age and points, drivers must hold a valid road driving licence from their home country, pass a test on the F1 sporting regulations, and demonstrate relevant experience — including, for a Free Practice Only licence, a lower points threshold. A driver must also not be disqualified from racing for disciplinary reasons.

Points system

Championships award points to their top finishers; Formula 2 carries the most weight (40 points for its champion), while other series award fewer. The system is intended to ensure drivers reach F1 via proven success, and it has occasionally blocked prominent drivers from other disciplines, prompting debate — notably the case of IndyCar driver Colton Herta in 2022, whose points total fell short.

Penalty points

During a season, drivers accumulate penalty points for on-track offences; reaching 12 points within 12 months brings an automatic one-race suspension. Kevin Magnussen became the first driver to trigger the ban, in 2024.

Fees

The licence carries an annual fee plus a per-point levy based on the driver's previous-season championship points, so the world champion pays the most — over €1 million in recent seasons.
