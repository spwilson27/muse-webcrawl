Analysis of variance

Analysis of variance (ANOVA) is a collection of statistical models used to analyze differences among group means in a sample. Developed by Ronald Fisher in the early 20th century, ANOVA partitions the observed variance in a variable into components attributable to different sources of variation, and uses an F-test to determine whether the group means differ significantly.

Models

In one-way ANOVA, a single categorical factor with two or more levels is examined. Two-way ANOVA studies two factors and their interaction. Repeated-measures ANOVA handles measurements taken repeatedly on the same subjects, while MANOVA extends the method to multiple dependent variables.

Assumptions

Standard ANOVA assumes that observations are independent, that residuals are approximately normally distributed, and that groups have similar variances (homoscedasticity). Violations of these assumptions can be addressed with transformations, nonparametric alternatives such as the Kruskal–Wallis test, or Welch's ANOVA.

Applications

ANOVA is widely used in experimental design across psychology, medicine, agriculture, and industry. It generalizes the two-sample t-test to comparisons of more than two groups, avoiding the inflated error rate that would result from performing many pairwise tests.
