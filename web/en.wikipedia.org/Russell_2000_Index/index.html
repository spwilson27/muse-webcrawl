The Russell 2000 is the best-known benchmark of small-cap U.S. equities.

The Russell 2000 Index is a small-cap U.S. stock market index that makes up the smallest 2,000 stocks in the Russell 3000 Index. It was started by the Frank Russell Company in 1984 and is maintained by FTSE Russell, a subsidiary of the London Stock Exchange Group. The index is widely regarded as the most important benchmark for small-cap companies in the United States.

Overview

The Russell 2000 measures the performance of the small-cap segment of the U.S. equity market, representing roughly 7 percent of the total market capitalization of the Russell 3000. Its constituents have a median market capitalization of around $1 billion. The index is reconstituted annually each June, when FTSE Russell ranks eligible companies by market capitalization and assigns them to the appropriate index.

Investing

Many index funds and exchange-traded funds track the Russell 2000, most notably the iShares Russell 2000 ETF (ticker IWM). Mutual funds that benchmark against the index are commonly used by investors seeking diversified exposure to smaller American companies, which historically have offered higher growth potential alongside greater volatility than large-cap stocks.
