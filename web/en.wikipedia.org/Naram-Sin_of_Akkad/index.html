Naram-Sin of Akkad

Naram-Sin (Akkadian: 𒈾𒆠𒈗, "Beloved of Sin"; reigned c. 2254–2218 BCE, middle chronology) was the fourth king of the Akkadian Empire, grandson of its founder Sargon of Akkad. Under him the empire reached its greatest extent, and he became the first Mesopotamian king to claim divinity during his own lifetime.

Reign

Naram-Sin campaigned from Elam and the Zagros to Syria and Anatolia, styling himself "King of the Four Quarters of the World". His reign saw administrative standardisation across the empire and building works at Nippur and other cities. His most famous monument, the Victory Stele now in the Louvre, shows the deified king — wearing the horned helmet of a god — triumphing over the Lullubi mountain people.

Legend

After the empire's fall, Mesopotamian legend blamed Naram-Sin's supposed impiety: the composition known as The Curse of Agade tells how his sack of Enlil's temple at Nippur brought divine wrath and the Gutian invasions that destroyed Akkad. In reality the empire endured revolts and external pressure, and it collapsed under his son Shar-Kali-Sharri.
