Dantokpa Market

LocationCotonou, Benin
AreaOver 20 hectares
Daily visitorsOver 1 million (estimates)
SignificanceLargest open-air market in West Africa

The Dantokpa Market, or simply Dantokpa, is the largest open-air market in West Africa. It is located in Cotonou, the economic capital of Benin, on the bank of the Cotonou lagoon near the Saint Michel bridge. Covering more than 20 hectares, it draws traders and buyers from across Benin and from neighbouring Nigeria, Niger, Togo, and beyond, with daily turnover estimated in the billions of CFA francs.

History and name

The market grew from the Tokpa market near the lagoon. Its name derives from "Dan," a deity in the local Vodun tradition whose shrine stood in the area, and "Tokpa," referring to the site by the lagoon; a fetish of Dan remains near the market. It expanded greatly in the later 20th century as Cotonou became the hub of Benin's large informal re-export trade, especially the commerce in used cars, textiles, and fuel-linked goods flowing to Nigeria.

Organization

The market is largely run by women traders, continuing the tradition of West African "market women" who dominate retail commerce. Goods range from foodstuffs, wax-print fabrics, and cosmetics to electronics, jewelry, and building materials, sold in both retail and wholesale sections. Specialized districts handle particular commodities, and trade associations and the SOGEMA (the market management company) organize stalls and services. Periodic fires have caused major damage, prompting government plans for modernization and relocation of parts of the market.
