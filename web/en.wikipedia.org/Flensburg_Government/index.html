Flensburg Government

The Flensburg Government (German: Flensburger Regierung), also known as the Dönitz Government, was the short-lived government of Nazi Germany during the last weeks of the Second World War in Europe, from 30 April to 23 May 1945. Based in the northern port city of Flensburg, it was headed by Grand Admiral Karl Dönitz, whom Adolf Hitler had named his successor in his political testament shortly before his suicide.

Formation and surrender

Hitler's testament expelled Hermann Göring and Heinrich Himmler and appointed Dönitz as Reich President and Joseph Goebbels as Chancellor. After Goebbels's suicide on 1 May, Dönitz formed a cabinet under Lutz Graf Schwerin von Krosigk as leading minister, retaining figures such as Albert Speer. The government attempted to negotiate a partial surrender to the Western Allies while continuing the war against the Soviet Union, but the Allies insisted on unconditional surrender on all fronts, signed at Reims on 7 May and ratified in Berlin on 8 May 1945.

Dissolution

The Western Allies tolerated the Flensburg administration for about two weeks, using it to transmit orders for the surrender and demobilisation of German forces. On 23 May 1945, British troops arrested Dönitz and his ministers aboard the liner Patria and at the Mürwik naval academy, formally ending the last government of the German Reich. Its members were subsequently held for trial at Nuremberg.
