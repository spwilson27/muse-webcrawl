5-Methylcytosine

5-Methylcytosine
FormulaC₅H₇N₃O
Derived fromCytosine (methylation at carbon 5)
EnzymesDNA methyltransferases (DNMT1, DNMT3A, DNMT3B)
Sequence contextMainly CpG dinucleotides (in animals)
Nicknamed"The fifth base"

5-Methylcytosine (5mC) is a methylated form of the DNA base cytosine, created when a methyl group is attached to the 5-carbon of the pyrimidine ring. It is the principal carrier of epigenetic information in DNA: although it does not change the underlying sequence, its pattern — DNA methylation — regulates gene expression, genomic imprinting, and X-chromosome inactivation.

Biology

In vertebrates, DNA methyltransferase enzymes add the methyl group almost exclusively at CpG sites, where a cytosine is followed by guanine. Dense clusters called CpG islands occur at many gene promoters; their methylation is generally associated with transcriptional silencing. Maintenance methylation by DNMT1 copies the pattern onto newly replicated DNA, making it heritable through cell division.

Mutability and detection

5-Methylcytosine spontaneously deaminates to thymine, producing a G–T mismatch; this makes methylated CpGs mutation hotspots and has depleted CpGs from vertebrate genomes over evolution. Bisulfite sequencing exploits a related reaction — bisulfite converts unmethylated cytosine to uracil but leaves 5mC intact — to map methylation at single-base resolution. TET enzymes can oxidize 5mC to 5-hydroxymethylcytosine as the first step of demethylation.
