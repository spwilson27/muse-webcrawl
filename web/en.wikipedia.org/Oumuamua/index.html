ʻOumuamua

Designation1I/2017 U1
Discovered19 October 2017, Pan-STARRS
TypeInterstellar object
Perihelion0.256 AU

ʻOumuamua is the first known interstellar object detected passing through the Solar System. Discovered on 19 October 2017 by Robert Weryk using the Pan-STARRS telescope in Hawaii, after it had already passed perihelion, it was designated 1I/2017 U1 — the "1I" denoting the first interstellar object. Its name, chosen with the help of Hawaiian language experts, roughly means "scout" or "messenger from afar arriving first".

Characteristics

Observations showed a highly elongated shape — often compared to a cigar or a pancake — tumbling in rotation, a reddish colour, and an apparent small non-gravitational acceleration as it left the Solar System, without any visible coma. These properties led to debate about whether it was a comet outgassing invisibly, a fragment of nitrogen or hydrogen ice, or, in a minority speculation, an artificial object.

Origin

Its hyperbolic trajectory, with eccentricity about 1.2 and an incoming speed of roughly 26 km/s relative to the Sun, proved it came from outside the Solar System and was not gravitationally bound to it. It was followed in 2019 by a second interstellar visitor, the comet 2I/Borisov.
