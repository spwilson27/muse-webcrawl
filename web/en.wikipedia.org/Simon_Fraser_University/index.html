Simon Fraser University

Simon Fraser University (SFU) is a public research university in British Columbia, Canada, with campuses in Burnaby, Vancouver, and Surrey. Founded in 1965 and named after the explorer Simon Fraser, it is known for its mountaintop Burnaby campus designed by Arthur Erickson and for consistently ranking among Canada's top comprehensive universities.

History

SFU opened in September 1965, admitting its first class just 18 months after approval — earning a reputation as a radical, fast-built campus. Architect Arthur Erickson's design, a linear megastructure along the ridge of Burnaby Mountain, won international acclaim. The university adopted a trimester system and an interdisciplinary outlook from its early years.

Academics and athletics

SFU has eight faculties, including computing science, business (Beedie School), and health sciences. Its sports teams, the Red Leafs, are unique in Canada for competing in the NCAA (Division II), and its football and swimming programmes have a long cross-border history.
