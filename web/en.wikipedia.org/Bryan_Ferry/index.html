Bryan Ferry

Bryan Ferry (born 26 September 1945) is an English singer and songwriter. He came to prominence as the lead vocalist and principal songwriter of the rock band Roxy Music, and has also maintained a successful solo career distinguished by his elegant stage image and smooth baritone voice.

Early life

Ferry was born in Washington, County Durham, and studied fine art at Newcastle University under the pop artist Richard Hamilton. While teaching pottery in London, he formed Roxy Music in 1970 with a group of friends including Brian Eno.

Roxy Music

Roxy Music's self-titled debut album appeared in 1972. The band blended glam rock, art-school experimentalism and retro romanticism on albums such as For Your Pleasure (1973), Stranded (1973), Country Life (1974) and Siren (1975), the last containing the hit "Love Is the Drug". After a hiatus, the smoother later albums Manifesto (1979), Flesh and Blood (1980) and Avalon (1982) brought greater commercial success before the group disbanded in 1983. Roxy Music has reunited for tours several times since 2001 and was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2019.

Solo career

Ferry's solo work began with These Foolish Things (1973), an album of covers. Later solo releases include Boys and Girls (1985), Bête Noire (1987), Mamouna (1994) and As Time Goes By (1999), a collection of standards, as well as Dylanesque (2007), devoted to songs by Bob Dylan.

Style and legacy

Ferry is regarded as one of British pop's great stylists, combining a crooner's phrasing with avant-garde sensibilities. His influence extends across glam rock, new wave and sophisticated pop.
