16S ribosomal RNA

16S ribosomal RNA (16S rRNA) is the RNA component of the 30S small subunit of the prokaryotic ribosome. About 1,500 nucleotides long, it binds the Shine–Dalgarno sequence of messenger RNA and helps position the ribosome at the start codon.

Structure and function

The 16S rRNA folds into a complex three-dimensional structure that forms most of the small subunit. Its 3′ end contains the anti-Shine–Dalgarno sequence. Many antibiotics, including tetracyclines and aminoglycosides, act by binding the 30S subunit at or near the 16S rRNA.

Use in phylogenetics

The gene encoding 16S rRNA contains both highly conserved regions and nine hypervariable regions (V1–V9), making it ideal for identifying bacteria and reconstructing evolutionary relationships. Carl Woese's use of 16S rRNA sequences in the 1970s revolutionised taxonomy and led to the recognition of the Archaea as a separate domain of life. Sequencing 16S genes from environmental samples is a standard tool of microbiome and metagenomics research.
