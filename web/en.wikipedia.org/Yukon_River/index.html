The Yukon River is a major watercourse of northwestern North America. Rising in British Columbia, Canada, it flows through the Yukon Territory and across Alaska to empty into the Bering Sea. It is the longest river in Alaska and among the longest in North America.

Course and history

From its headwaters the river flows northwest past Whitehorse and Dawson City, then west across Alaska, passing Circle, Fort Yukon, and Tanana before reaching its delta on Norton Sound. During the Klondike Gold Rush of the late 1890s, the Yukon was a principal route to the goldfields, served by fleets of sternwheel paddle steamers. Its name derives from a Gwich'in word meaning "great river."

Ecology

The river supports important salmon runs, including Chinook and chum salmon, vital to Indigenous communities along its banks, though runs have declined in recent decades. Much of the river freezes for over half the year and remains one of the least developed major rivers in North America.
