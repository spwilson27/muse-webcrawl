Fuchsian group

In mathematics, a Fuchsian group is a discrete subgroup of PSL(2, R), the group of orientation-preserving isometries of the hyperbolic plane. Fuchsian groups are used to model hyperbolic surfaces and arise in the theory of automorphic forms and Riemann surfaces.

Definition

A subgroup Γ of PSL(2, R) is a Fuchsian group if it acts properly discontinuously on the upper half-plane. Equivalently, Γ is discrete in the topology of PSL(2, R). The quotient of the hyperbolic plane by a Fuchsian group is a hyperbolic orbifold, which is a surface when the group is torsion-free.

History

The groups are named after Lazarus Fuchs, whose work on differential equations inspired Henri Poincaré's investigations. Poincaré studied the functions invariant under such groups, leading to the theory of automorphic functions.

Examples

The modular group PSL(2, Z) is a classical example. Triangle groups, generated by reflections or rotations about the vertices of a hyperbolic triangle, are another important class. Finitely generated Fuchsian groups are classified by their signature, which records the genus of the quotient surface and the orders of its cone points.
