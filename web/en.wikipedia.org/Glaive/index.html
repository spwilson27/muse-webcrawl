A glaive is a European polearm consisting of a single-edged blade mounted on the end of a long pole. The blade is typically around 45 to 60 centimetres (18 to 24 inches) long and resembles a large knife or short sword blade, attached to a shaft of roughly two metres (6–7 feet) by a socket tang. Some blades bore small hooks or lugs on the unsharpened back to catch weapons or riders.

History

Glaives were used by infantry in medieval and early modern Europe. They are documented in 13th- and 14th-century sources, and the 14th-century Welsh gwaywffon and various German and Italian weapons were similar. The glaive is among the polearms depicted in Hans Talhoffer's fencing treatises. It remained in ceremonial and military use into the 16th century.

Use

The glaive combined the reach of a spear with the cutting power of a sword, effective against both foot soldiers and cavalry. Its design influenced later polearms and, some argue, the socket bayonet.

In popular culture

In modern fantasy literature and games, "glaive" is sometimes applied to bladed throwing weapons quite unlike the historical polearm.
