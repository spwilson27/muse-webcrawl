Kama Sutra

The Kama Sutra (Sanskrit: Kāmasūtra) is an ancient Indian Sanskrit text on sexuality, eroticism and emotional fulfilment, attributed to the sage Vātsyāyana and commonly dated to between roughly 400 BCE and 300 CE, most likely around the 2nd–3rd century CE. It treats kāma — desire and pleasure — as one of the legitimate aims of life in Hindu philosophy, alongside dharma (duty), artha (prosperity) and moksha (liberation).

Contents

The work consists of short aphoristic verses arranged in seven parts and 36 chapters, covering general principles of cultured living, courtship and marriage, sexual union (the famous catalogue of positions occupies only one portion of one part), relations with the wives of others, courtesans, and means of attraction. Much of the text is a practical guide to refined urban life — the education, home and daily routine of the cultivated man-about-town (nagarika).

Transmission and reception

The most influential commentary is the 13th-century Jayamangala of Yashodhara. The Kama Sutra became famous in the West through the 1883 English translation published under the auspices of Sir Richard Burton (prepared largely by F. F. Arbuthnot and the pandit Bhagavanlal Indraji). Modern translations, such as Wendy Doniger and Sudhir Kakar's (2002), have emphasised its character as a text on intimacy and relationships rather than merely an erotic manual.
