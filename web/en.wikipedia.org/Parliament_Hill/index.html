Parliament Hill

Parliament Hill (French: Colline du Parlement), colloquially known as The Hill, is an area of Crown land on the southern bank of the Ottawa River in downtown Ottawa, Ontario, Canada. Its Gothic Revival buildings house the Parliament of Canada, including the Senate and the House of Commons, and the site is the symbolic centre of Canadian political life, hosting annual Canada Day celebrations and the daily Changing of the Guard in summer.

History

The limestone bluff was originally a military site known as Barrack Hill, overlooking the Rideau Canal and the Ottawa River. When Queen Victoria chose Ottawa as the capital of the Province of Canada in 1857, the hill was selected for the new parliament buildings. Construction of the original Centre Block began in 1859, and the legislature first sat there in 1866, a year before Confederation. On 3 February 1916 a fire destroyed the Centre Block; only the Library of Parliament survived, thanks to its iron doors. The present Centre Block, crowned by the 98-metre Peace Tower, was completed in 1927, and the tower's Memorial Chamber commemorates Canadians killed in the First World War.

Buildings and grounds

The precinct comprises the Centre Block, the East Block (housing senators' and ministers' offices and restored 19th-century rooms), and the West Block. The grounds, landscaped in the picturesque style, contain statues of monarchs, prime ministers, and other national figures, the Centennial Flame lit in 1967, and the Summer Pavilion overlooking the river. Since 2019 the Centre Block has been closed for a decade-long rehabilitation, with the House of Commons meeting in a converted West Block courtyard and the Senate in the former railway station across the street.
