Districts of Libya

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Libya is divided into 22 districts (shabiyat, singular shabiyah), which are the primary administrative subdivisions of the country. The district system was introduced in 1995 and reorganised in 2007, replacing the earlier system of governorates and municipalities. Districts are further divided into Basic People's Congresses (municipalities), and the district capital typically gives the district its name.

List of districts

The 22 districts include Butnan, Derna, Jabal al Akhdar, Marj, Benghazi, Al Wahat, Kufra, Surt, Misrata, Murqub, Tripoli, Jafara, Zawiya, Nuqat al Khams, Jabal al Gharbi, Nalut, Ghat, Wadi al Hayaa, Sabha, Wadi al Shatii, Jufra and Murzuq. The largest by area are the Saharan districts of Kufra and Murzuq, while the most populous are the coastal districts of Tripoli and Benghazi, where most of Libya's roughly seven million people live.

Historical divisions

Historically, Libya comprised three regions: Tripolitania in the northwest, Cyrenaica in the east, and Fezzan in the southwest, a tripartition that dates to antiquity and the Ottoman era and remains culturally and politically significant, especially since the 2011 civil war and subsequent fragmentation.
