The Gaelic League (Irish: Conradh na Gaeilge, pronounced [ˈkɔnˠɾˠəɣ n̪ˠə ˈɡeːlʲɟə]) is a social and cultural organisation which promotes the Irish language in Ireland and worldwide. Founded in Dublin on 31 July 1893 by Eoin MacNeill with the support of Douglas Hyde and others, it grew out of the earlier Gaelic Union and became the leading institution of the Gaelic revival.

History

The League was founded in the wake of Hyde's 1892 lecture "The Necessity for De-Anglicising Ireland". It presented itself as strictly non-political and non-sectarian, welcoming Protestants and unionists alongside Catholics and nationalists, and aimed principally to keep Irish alive as a spoken language. Within a decade it had hundreds of branches running evening classes, lectures, and céilí dances. Its newspaper, An Claidheamh Soluis (edited for a time by Patrick Pearse), promoted Irish literature, and the League campaigned successfully for Irish in schools and for a chair of Irish at the National University.

Influence

Although formally non-political, the League's classes and social networks drew many members toward Irish nationalism: future leaders of the 1916 Easter Rising and the independence struggle, including Pearse, Éamon de Valera, and Michael Collins, passed through its ranks. Hyde resigned the presidency in 1915 when the League adopted a political aim at its annual congress. After independence, the League continued as the principal voluntary Irish-language body, founding the publishing house An Gúm (1926), the theatre An Damer, and the Oireachtas festival of Irish arts.

Modern organisation

Today Conradh na Gaeilge runs language classes, campaigns such as Seachtain na Gaeilge, and advocacy for Irish-language rights in Ireland and the European Union. It remains headquartered at 6 Harcourt Street, Dublin.
