J. R. R. Tolkien

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

John Ronald Reuel Tolkien (3 January 1892 – 2 September 1973) was an English writer and philologist. He was the author of the high fantasy works The Hobbit and The Lord of the Rings.

Life

Born in Bloemfontein, South Africa, Tolkien moved to England as a child. He served as a signals officer in the First World War, fighting at the Somme. He was Rawlinson and Bosworth Professor of Anglo-Saxon at Oxford and later Merton Professor of English Language and Literature, and was a close friend of C. S. Lewis; both were members of the Inklings literary group.

Works and legacy

From 1917 Tolkien developed the legendarium of Middle-earth: its histories, languages (notably Quenya and Sindarin), and peoples. The Hobbit appeared in 1937 and The Lord of the Rings in 1954–55; The Silmarillion was published posthumously by his son Christopher in 1977. Tolkien's works popularized the fantasy genre and are widely regarded as foundational to modern fantasy literature; he is often called the "father" of modern fantasy.
