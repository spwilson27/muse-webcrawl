Barkerville


Main street of historic Barkerville, British Columbia.

Barkerville was the main community of the Cariboo Gold Rush in British Columbia, Canada. It is preserved as a historic town and is now Barkerville Historic Town & Park, a provincial heritage site. The town is located on the north slope of the Cariboo Plateau near the headwaters of the Williams Creek, west of the city of Quesnel.

History

Barkerville is named after Billy Barker, an English prospector from Cambridgeshire, who was among those who first struck gold at the region in 1862. His claim, at about 1,460 feet elevation, yielded some 37,000 ounces of gold. A town quickly grew around the strikes on Williams Creek, and by the mid-1860s Barkerville had a population of about 5,000, including a significant Chinese community, and for a time was reputed to be the largest town west of Chicago and north of San Francisco.

In September 1868, a fire destroyed much of the town, but it was rapidly rebuilt. As surface gold declined and deeper leads were worked, the population gradually dwindled, though mining continued in the area for decades.

Historic site

In 1957 the Government of British Columbia created Barkerville as a historic park. Today more than 100 historic buildings, many original, line its streets, with interpreters in period dress, a theatre, restaurants, and demonstrations of gold rush-era life. Nearby is the still-active town of Wells.
