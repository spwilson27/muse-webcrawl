Horned deity

A horned deity is a god or goddess depicted with horns, a motif found in many ancient religions and mythologies across the world. Horns generally symbolise power, fertility, virility, and a connection with wild animals and nature. Horned figures appear in art from the Palaeolithic period onward, including the famous "Sorcerer" figure in the Cave of the Trois-Frères in France.

In various cultures

In Celtic religion, the antlered god Cernunnos is shown seated cross-legged wearing the antlers of a stag, surrounded by animals. In ancient Egypt, deities such as Khnum and Amun were associated with rams, while Hathor and Isis wore bovine horns. In the ancient Near East, horned crowns denoted divinity in Mesopotamian art, and the number of horn pairs could indicate a god's rank. The Hebrew Bible's description of Moses was mistranslated in the Latin Vulgate as suggesting he had horns, which influenced later artistic depictions.

Interpretation

Scholars have proposed that horned imagery reflects the importance of cattle and wild game to early agricultural and hunting societies. In modern neopagan traditions, particularly Wicca, the Horned God is a central deity representing nature, wilderness, sexuality, and the cycle of life and death, often syncretised from figures such as Cernunnos and the Greek god Pan.
