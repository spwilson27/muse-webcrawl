ARD is a joint organisation of Germany's regional public-service broadcasters. It was founded in 1950 in West Germany to represent the common interests of the new, decentralised public broadcasting services — particularly in radio. It also provides the main public-service television network, Das Erste, and co-operates with ZDF and Deutschlandradio on additional channels.

The full name is Arbeitsgemeinschaft der öffentlich-rechtlichen Rundfunkanstalten der Bundesrepublik Deutschland ("Working group of public broadcasters of the Federal Republic of Germany"). ARD is mainly funded by a licence fee, the Rundfunkbeitrag, levied per household.

History

After the Second World War, the Allied occupying powers established regional broadcasting corporations in their zones, deliberately structured to avoid the centralised state broadcasting of the Nazi era. On 9 June 1950 the regional broadcasters of the Federal Republic of Germany formed ARD as their umbrella organisation. The joint television programme, later known as Das Erste, began broadcasting on 25 December 1952, initially under the name Deutsches Fernsehen. Following German reunification in 1990, the broadcasters of the former East Germany were dissolved and new regional corporations (MDR and ORB, the latter later merged into rbb) joined ARD.

Member broadcasters

ARD has nine member broadcasters serving Germany's regions: Bayerischer Rundfunk (BR), Hessischer Rundfunk (hr), Mitteldeutscher Rundfunk (MDR), Norddeutscher Rundfunk (NDR), Radio Bremen, Rundfunk Berlin-Brandenburg (rbb), Saarländischer Rundfunk (SR), Südwestrundfunk (SWR) and Westdeutscher Rundfunk (WDR). Together they operate numerous regional television channels ("die Dritten") and dozens of radio stations. Deutsche Welle, Germany's international broadcaster, is also associated with ARD.

Programming

Das Erste is known for the Tagesschau, Germany's most-watched television news programme, produced by ARD-aktuell in Hamburg. Other prominent output includes the crime series Tatort, political magazines, talk shows, documentaries, and major sports coverage such as the Olympic Games and football World Cups, shared with ZDF. ARD and ZDF jointly operate the children's channel KiKA, the culture channel Arte (with France), the documentary channel Phoenix and the youth service funk.
