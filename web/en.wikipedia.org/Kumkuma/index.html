Kumkuma

Kumkuma is a powder used for social and religious markings in India. It is made from turmeric or any other local materials; the turmeric is dried and powdered with a bit of slaked lime, which turns the rich yellow powder into a red colour.

Use

Kumkuma is most often applied by Indians to the forehead. It is a central part of many Hindu rituals and offerings, applied by married women as a sign of their marital status, and offered to deities in temples. The colour red represents blood and purity, and the powder is associated with the goddess and with good fortune. Kumkuma is also used in the Holi festival and in welcoming guests, when a tilak mark may be applied to the forehead.

Related powders

Kumkuma is related to, but distinct from, sindoor, the orange-red powder traditionally worn by married Hindu women along the hair parting, and from vibhuti, the sacred ash used by Shaivites.
