Probable prime

In number theory, a probable prime is an integer that satisfies a specific condition also satisfied by all prime numbers, but that a composite number can satisfy only rarely. Such conditions come from primality tests: a number passing the test is a probable prime with respect to that test.

Types

A Fermat probable prime to base a satisfies an−1 ≡ 1 (mod n). Carmichael numbers pass for every coprime base yet are composite. Strong probable primes, from the Miller–Rabin test, give stronger guarantees; Euler probable primes and Euler–Jacobi probable primes refine Fermat's condition.

Use

Cryptography, notably RSA key generation, relies on probabilistic tests because deterministic tests are slower for very large candidates. Baillie–PSW, combining Miller–Rabin and Lucas tests, has no known counterexamples. A probable prime that also passes a proof is simply prime; in practice, error probabilities below 2−100 are accepted.
