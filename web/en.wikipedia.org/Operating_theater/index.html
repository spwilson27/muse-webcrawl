Operating theater

An operating theater (also known as an operating room, operating suite, operation theatre, or operation suite) is a facility within a hospital where surgical operations are carried out in an aseptic environment. Historically, the term "operating theatre" referred to a non-sterile, tiered theatre or amphitheatre in which students and other spectators could watch surgeons perform surgery; the raised seating explains the name, and preserved examples such as the Old Operating Theatre in London date from the era before anaesthesia and antisepsis.

Design

Modern operating theatres are designed to minimize infection risk. They use smooth, easily cleaned surfaces, controlled ventilation often with laminar airflow over the operating table, and filtered air maintained at positive pressure relative to surrounding corridors. Surgical lights, anaesthetic machines, and monitoring equipment are arranged around the operating table, and access is restricted to staff in sterile attire.

Types

Theatres may be dedicated to specialties such as cardiothoracic, orthopaedic, or neurosurgery. Hybrid operating theatres combine a conventional operating room with advanced medical imaging devices such as fixed angiography systems, CT, or MRI scanners, enabling complex minimally invasive and image-guided procedures.
