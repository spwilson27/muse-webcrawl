Welsh Pony and Cob

OriginWales
SectionsA (Welsh Mountain Pony), B (Welsh Pony), C (Welsh Pony of Cob Type), D (Welsh Cob)
TraitsHardy, intelligent, good temperament; dished face, large eyes

The Welsh Pony and Cob is a group of four closely related horse breeds originating in Wales: the Welsh Mountain Pony (Section A), the Welsh Pony (Section B), the Welsh Pony of Cob Type (Section C) and the Welsh Cob (Section D). They are known for hardiness, intelligence and versatility, and are popular worldwide as children's ponies, driving horses and show animals.

History

Mountain ponies roamed the Welsh hills from ancient times; Arabian and Thoroughbred blood was introduced over the centuries, and the larger cobs were used for farm work, transport and military remounts. The Welsh Pony and Cob Society was founded in 1901 and its stud book established the four sections by height and type.

Sections

Section A, the Welsh Mountain Pony, stands up to 12 hands (121.9 cm). Section B, the Welsh Pony, is a lighter riding pony up to 13.2 hands. Section C, the Welsh Pony of Cob Type, is a stockier animal also up to 13.2 hands. Section D, the Welsh Cob, exceeds 13.2 hands with no upper limit and is noted for its powerful trot.

Uses

Welsh ponies and cobs excel in showing, dressage, jumping, combined driving and trekking. Crosses with Thoroughbreds and other breeds produce successful sport ponies and hunters.
