Indian nationality law

Indian nationality law governs the acquisition, transmission, and loss of citizenship of India, principally through the Citizenship Act, 1955, enacted under Articles 5–11 of the Constitution of India. India has a single, unified citizenship: there is no separate state citizenship.

Acquisition

Citizenship may be acquired by birth, descent, registration, and naturalization. Rules for citizenship by birth have tightened over time: those born in India before 3 December 2004 were generally citizens by birth, but since that date a person born in India is a citizen only if both parents are citizens, or one parent is a citizen and the other is not an illegal migrant. Naturalization generally requires twelve years of residence (including one continuous year immediately preceding application), good character, and knowledge of a language in the Eighth Schedule.

Controversies and amendments

The Citizenship (Amendment) Act, 2019 (CAA) provides a path to citizenship for Hindu, Sikh, Buddhist, Jain, Parsi, and Christian migrants from Afghanistan, Bangladesh, and Pakistan who entered India before 2015, and drew widespread protests and legal challenge for excluding Muslims. India does not permit dual citizenship, though the Overseas Citizen of India (OCI) card provides lifelong visa-free travel and residence rights to many persons of Indian origin. Citizenship may be lost by renunciation, termination upon acquiring another nationality, or deprivation for fraud or disloyalty.
