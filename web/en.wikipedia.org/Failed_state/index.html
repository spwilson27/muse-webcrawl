Failed state

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A failed state is a state that has lost its ability to fulfil fundamental security and development functions, lacking effective control over its territory and borders. Common characteristics of a failing state include a central government so weak or ineffective that it has little practical control over much of its territory; non-provision of public services; widespread corruption and criminality; the presence of refugees and involuntary population movements; sharp economic decline; and foreign military intervention.

The term first came into widespread use in the early 1990s, following the collapse of Somalia and the wars in the former Yugoslavia. Levels of state weakness are measured by indices such as the Fragile States Index, published annually by the Fund for Peace since 2005, which ranks countries on social, economic and political indicators of pressure on the state.

Definitions and characteristics

Political scientists generally describe state failure as the collapse or near-collapse of core state institutions: the government can no longer monopolise the legitimate use of physical force (see Max Weber), collect taxes, maintain infrastructure, or enforce laws across its claimed territory. In such circumstances, non-state actors — militias, warlords, criminal organisations or insurgent groups — may control substantial regions and provide (or withhold) basic services.

The concept is contested. Critics argue that "failed state" is an imprecise label that can be applied inconsistently, that it measures states against a Weberian ideal few countries fully meet, and that the term has sometimes been used to justify external intervention.

Examples and measurement

In annual Fragile States Index rankings, countries such as Somalia, Yemen, Syria, South Sudan, Sudan and the Democratic Republic of the Congo have repeatedly appeared among the most fragile. At the other end of the scale, countries such as Finland, Norway and Switzerland are ranked among the most stable and sustainable.

Related concepts include the fragile state, an ungoverned space, and state collapse. International responses to state failure have included peacekeeping missions, state-building programmes, humanitarian aid and, controversially, trusteeship-like arrangements.
