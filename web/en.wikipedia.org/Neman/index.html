The Neman (Lithuanian: Nemunas; Belarusian: Nioman; German: Memel) is a major Eastern European river rising in central Belarus and flowing through Lithuania before draining into the Curonian Lagoon and the Baltic Sea. At 937 km it is the largest river in Lithuania and the fourth-longest in the Baltic region.

Course and history

The river rises near Minsk, flows west past Grodno, then turns north through Lithuania — passing Druskininkai, Alytus, and Kaunas, where it receives the Neris — before forming part of the border between Lithuania and Russia's Kaliningrad Oblast and entering the Curonian Lagoon near Rusnė. Historically, the river marked the meeting of Napoleon and Tsar Alexander I on a raft at Tilsit in 1807, and its lower course long formed a frontier between Germany and Lithuania; the Lithuanian national revival treasured it in song and poetry.

Use

The Kaunas Hydroelectric Power Plant dams the river near Kaunas. Its delta is a protected wetland of international importance for migratory birds.
