Feral cat

A feral cat or stray cat is an unowned domestic cat (Felis catus) that lives outdoors and avoids human contact: it does not allow itself to be handled or touched, and usually remains hidden from humans. Feral cats may breed over dozens of generations and become an aggressive local apex predator in urban, savannah and bushland environments, and have significant impacts on native species.

Definitions

Terminology distinguishes the feral cat (born and raised without human contact, or a pet that has reverted) from the stray cat (a lost or abandoned pet that remains socialised to people) and from community cats managed in colonies by caretakers. An ear that has been "tipped" (the tip surgically removed) marks a feral cat that has been sterilised under a trap–neuter–return (TNR) programme.

Population and management

Estimates of the unowned cat population vary widely; in the United States, estimates range into the tens of millions. Management methods include trap–neuter–return, adoption of socialised individuals, and, controversially, lethal control, especially on islands where feral cats threaten endemic wildlife.

Ecological impact

Feral and free-ranging cats are estimated to kill billions of birds and small mammals annually, and have contributed to the extinction of many island species; the IUCN lists the domestic cat among the world's worst invasive species.
