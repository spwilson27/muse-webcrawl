Velites
Allegiance: Roman Republic
Type: Light infantry, skirmishers
Period: c. 3rd–2nd centuries BC
Weapons: hastae velitares (javelins), gladius, parma shield

Velites (singular veles) were a class of light infantry in the army of the mid-Roman Republic. Drawn from the youngest and poorest citizens who could not afford the equipment of the heavy infantry, each legion fielded about 1,200 velites.

A veles, distinguished by the wolf-skin worn over his helmet.

Equipment and role

Velites carried several light javelins (hastae velitares, about 3 feet long, designed to bend on impact), a short sword, and a small round shield (parma). Many wore a wolf's skin over their helmets so officers could recognize acts of bravery. In battle they screened the heavy infantry of the hastati, harassing the enemy with missiles, duelling with opposing skirmishers and elephant handlers, and then retiring through the ranks as the main lines closed.

Decline

As Roman equipment standardized, the distinctions among velites, hastati, principes, and triarii faded. The velites effectively disappeared with the Marian reforms of the late 2nd century BC, their skirmishing role passing to auxiliary troops.
