ICE 3

The ICE 3 is a family of high-speed electric multiple-unit trains operated by Deutsche Bahn in Germany and by NS International in the Netherlands. It is the third generation of Intercity Express (ICE) trains, manufactured by Siemens and Bombardier and in service since 2000. Unlike the earlier ICE 1 and ICE 2, the ICE 3 uses distributed traction: electric motors are mounted under the floors of several cars throughout the train rather than concentrated in power cars at each end.

Design

A standard ICE 3 trainset consists of eight cars, four of which are powered, giving an installed output of 8,000 kW (about 10,700 hp). The trains are designed for a maximum speed of 330 km/h (205 mph) and are approved for 300 km/h in regular service in Germany and, on certain French lines, 320 km/h. Distributed traction gives the train a comparatively good power-to-weight ratio and allows a panoramic lounge behind the driver's cab in the end cars, where passengers can watch the line ahead through a glass partition.

German domestic trains are designated Class 403, while the multi-system variant for cross-border services is Class 406 (ICE 3M) and can operate under four different overhead voltages and signalling systems. A smaller number of Class 406 sets were built for services to Amsterdam and Brussels.

Operations

ICE 3 trains operate flagship routes such as Cologne–Frankfurt and Munich–Berlin, and international services to Amsterdam, Brussels and (formerly in cooperation with SNCF) Paris. On the Cologne–Frankfurt high-speed line, designed with gradients of up to 4 per cent, the ICE 3 was for a long time the only train type capable of operating the full service.

Derivatives

The ICE 3's design forms the basis of Siemens's Velaro family, variants of which have been sold to Spain (Velaro E), China (CRH3), Russia (Velaro RUS), Turkey and Egypt. In Germany, later Class 407 (Velaro D) and Class 408 (ICE 3neo) trains supplement the original fleet.
