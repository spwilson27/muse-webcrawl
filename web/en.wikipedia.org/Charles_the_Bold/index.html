Charles the Bold

Charles I (10 November 1433 – 5 January 1477), commonly called Charles the Bold, was Duke of Burgundy from 1467 to 1477. He was the last Valois Duke of Burgundy and sought to turn his lands between France and the Holy Roman Empire into a kingdom.

Early life

Son of Philip the Good and Isabella of Portugal, Charles was born at Dijon. He married Margaret of York, sister of Edward IV of England, in 1468, cementing an Anglo-Burgundian alliance against Louis XI of France.

Reign and wars

Charles expanded Burgundian territory, reformed the army and court, and campaigned against Liège and Lorraine. His ambitions drew in the Swiss Confederates, who defeated him at Grandson and Morat in 1476.

Death

Charles was killed at the Battle of Nancy on 5 January 1477. His only child, Mary of Burgundy, inherited his lands, and her marriage to Maximilian of Habsburg transferred much of the Burgundian inheritance to the Habsburgs.
