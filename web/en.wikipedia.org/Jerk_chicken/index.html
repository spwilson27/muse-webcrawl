Jerk chicken

Jerk chicken is a spicy grilled chicken dish from Jamaica. The chicken is coated in spices and slow-cooked over a fire or grill traditionally composed of green pimento wood positioned in a half metal barrel, covered with a piece of zinc and left to cook. The smoky flavour and dark, caramelised crust are hallmarks of the dish.


Jerk chicken on a drum grill

Preparation

The chicken is marinated in jerk seasoning — built on allspice and Scotch bonnet peppers, with thyme, scallions, garlic, ginger and other spices — typically overnight. It is then grilled slowly over pimento wood, whose smoke gives the meat its characteristic flavour, and chopped into pieces with a cleaver before serving.

Serving

Jerk chicken is commonly served with hard-dough bread, Jamaican festival (a fried sweet dumpling), rice and peas, or fried plantain. Boston Beach in Portland Parish, Jamaica, is regarded as a home of jerk cooking, where vendors serve jerk chicken, pork and seafood from roadside stands.

Popularity

Along with jerk pork, jerk chicken has become one of Jamaica's best-known culinary exports, served in Caribbean restaurants around the world and sold at street food stalls, festivals and markets throughout the Jamaican diaspora.
