Feminist ethics

Feminist ethics seeks to reform traditional ethical theories that devalue women's experience.

Feminist ethics is an approach to ethics that builds on the belief that traditionally, ethical theorising has undervalued and/or underappreciated women's moral experience, and therefore chooses to reimagine ethics through a holistic feminist approach to transform it.

Feminist ethicists argue that traditional moral theory focuses on abstract rules, universal principles, and the autonomous individual in ways that reflect historically masculine concerns, while neglecting relationships, care-giving, and the concrete circumstances that have characterised much of women's moral labour. A central strand of the field is the ethics of care, developed from Carol Gilligan's research in In a Different Voice (1982), which distinguished a care perspective — emphasising empathy, responsibility within relationships, and responsiveness to need — from a justice perspective emphasising rights and fairness.

Themes

Feminist ethics examines how power and oppression shape moral life; criticises the public/private distinction that has insulated domestic life from ethical scrutiny; and attends to intersectionality — the interaction of gender with race, class, disability, and other structures. Feminist ethicists disagree among themselves: some seek to supplement traditional theories such as Kantianism, utilitarianism, and virtue ethics, while others argue for more thoroughgoing revision. The field has influenced bioethics, environmental ethics, and global ethics.
