Sorrento (Neapolitan: Surriento) is a town in Campania, southern Italy, overlooking the Bay of Naples from cliffs on the Sorrentine Peninsula. A resort destination since the 18th century, it is famous for its lemons, limoncello liqueur, marquetry (inlaid wood) workshops, and views across the water to Naples, Mount Vesuvius, and the island of Capri. (For the logical puzzle with a similar name, see sorites paradox.)

History

The site was settled by Greeks and Oscans and became the Roman Surrentum, a fashionable retreat with imperial villas. In the Middle Ages it passed among Byzantine, Lombard, and local duchies, suffering Saracen raids; it later joined the Kingdom of Naples. The poet Torquato Tasso was born in Sorrento in 1544. From the era of the Grand Tour, Sorrento drew writers and artists including Goethe, Byron, Ibsen, and Nietzsche.

Economy and culture

Tourism dominates the modern town, served by hydrofoils to Naples and Capri and the Circumvesuviana railway. Sorrento lemons (Limone di Sorrento IGP), grown in terraced gardens under chestnut pergolas, flavour the region's limoncello. The town is celebrated in the Neapolitan song "Torna a Surriento" ("Come Back to Sorrento", 1902).
