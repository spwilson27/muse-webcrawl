Canals of Ireland
Principal canals: Grand Canal, Royal Canal, Shannon–Erne Waterway, Newry Canal

The canals of Ireland were built for freight and passenger transport during the 18th and 19th centuries. The two great canals of Dublin, the Grand Canal (opened in stages from 1779) and the Royal Canal (completed 1817), link the capital with the River Shannon.

Major canals

The Grand Canal runs from Dublin to Shannon Harbour in County Offaly. The Royal Canal runs from Dublin to the Shannon near Termonbarry. The Newry Canal (1742), the first summit-level canal in Ireland or Britain, linked Lough Neagh with the Irish Sea at Newry. The Shannon–Erne Waterway reopened in 1994, connecting the two river systems.

Decline and revival

Railway competition ended most commercial traffic by the mid-20th century, but many canals survive for leisure cruising and are administered by Waterways Ireland.
