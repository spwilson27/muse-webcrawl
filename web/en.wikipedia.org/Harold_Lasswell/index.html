Harold Lasswell

Born: 13 February 1902, Donnellson, Iowa, U.S.

Died: 18 December 1978, New York City, U.S.

Fields: Political science, communication theory

Institutions: University of Chicago, Yale University

Harold Dwight Lasswell (1902–1978) was an American political scientist and communications theorist, one of the most influential and prolific social scientists of the twentieth century. A student of Charles Merriam at the University of Chicago, he pioneered the study of propaganda, political psychology, and the policy sciences.

Contributions

Lasswell's doctoral dissertation, published as Propaganda Technique in the World War (1927), was a landmark study of wartime persuasion. He is famous for defining communication as "Who (says) What (in) Which channel (to) Whom (with) What effect" — Lasswell's model of communication (1948) — and for defining politics itself as "who gets what, when, how", the title of his 1936 book. In Psychopathology and Politics (1930) he applied psychoanalytic ideas to political leadership.

Policy sciences

After the Second World War, during which he directed wartime communications research at the Library of Congress, Lasswell taught at Yale and advocated the "policy sciences": an interdisciplinary, problem-oriented approach to public policy that anticipated modern policy analysis.
