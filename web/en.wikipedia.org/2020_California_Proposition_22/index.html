2020 California Proposition 22

Election3 November 2020
SubjectApp-based drivers as independent contractors
ResultApproved (about 59% Yes)
SupportersUber, Lyft, DoorDash and others

Proposition 22 was a ballot proposition passed in the US state of California on 3 November 2020, which classified app-based delivery and transportation (rideshare) drivers as independent contractors rather than employees, exempting the companies from providing standard employment protections under California law.

Background

The measure was a response to Assembly Bill 5 (AB 5), a 2019 California law that codified a strict "ABC test" for employee classification, under which most app-based drivers would be employees. App-based companies funded a campaign of more than $200 million — the most expensive ballot-measure campaign in California history — to pass Proposition 22, which instead granted drivers limited benefits such as a healthcare subsidy and an earnings floor for engaged time.

Legal challenges

In August 2021 a California superior court judge ruled the proposition unconstitutional, but in March 2023 a state appeals court largely upheld it. In July 2024 the California Supreme Court upheld Proposition 22 in full.
