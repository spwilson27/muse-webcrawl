Pan Jianwei (Chinese: 潘建伟; born 4 November 1967) is a Chinese quantum physicist and a leading figure in quantum information science. He is a professor at the University of Science and Technology of China (USTC) in Hefei, an academician of the Chinese Academy of Sciences, and is sometimes called China's "father of quantum".

Career

Pan studied at USTC and earned his doctorate at the University of Vienna in 1999 in the group of Anton Zeilinger, working on experimental quantum teleportation and multi-photon entanglement. Returning to China, he built a research programme that produced a series of record results in multi-photon entanglement and quantum communication.

Major achievements

Pan led the scientific programme of Micius (the Quantum Experiments at Space Scale, QUESS), the world's first quantum communications satellite, launched in 2016, which demonstrated satellite-to-ground quantum key distribution, entanglement distribution over more than 1,200 km, and intercontinental quantum communication. His team also built metropolitan and intercity fibre quantum-communication networks in China, and contributed to photonic quantum-computing demonstrations, including the Jiuzhang photonic computer that reported a quantum computational advantage in Gaussian boson sampling in 2020.

Recognition

Pan has received numerous honours, including election to the Chinese Academy of Sciences (2011) and the World Academy of Sciences. Nature included him in its "Nature's 10" list in 2017, and his satellite work has been recognised by major physics prizes and year-end science lists internationally.
