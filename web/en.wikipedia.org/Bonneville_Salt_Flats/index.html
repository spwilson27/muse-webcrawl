Bonneville Salt Flats

The Bonneville Salt Flats are a densely packed salt pan in Tooele County in northwestern Utah, covering about 30,000 acres (121 km²). A remnant of the Pleistocene Lake Bonneville, the flats are famous as the venue for land speed records, most notably during the annual Speed Week event. They lie along Interstate 80 near the town of Wendover, close to the Nevada border, and are administered by the Bureau of Land Management.

Formation

Lake Bonneville was a vast pluvial lake that covered much of the Great Basin during the last ice age. As it dried up some 14,500 years ago, it left behind thick deposits of salt and other minerals in its lowest basins. The resulting crust at Bonneville is up to several feet thick at the centre and so flat that the curvature of the Earth can be perceived standing on it. The explorer and fur trader era gave the lake and flats the name of Benjamin Bonneville, a U.S. Army officer. The flats nearly wrecked the Donner Party in 1846, whose wagons lost crucial days and animals crossing them.

Land speed racing

The flats' hard, level surface was first used for racing in 1912, and in 1914 Teddy Tetzlaff set an unofficial record of 141 mph there. From 1935, when Malcolm Campbell first brought his Blue Bird to Bonneville, through the 1960s, the flats hosted nearly all world land speed records, including Campbell's 301 mph, John Cobb's 394 mph, and the jet-car battles of Craig Breedlove (600 mph, 1965) and Gary Gabelich (622 mph, 1970), before the absolute record moved to Nevada's Black Rock Desert.

Each August, the Southern California Timing Association's Speed Week draws hundreds of cars, motorcycles and streamliners to courses laid out on the salt; other meets include World of Speed and the World Finals. In recent decades racers and conservationists have raised concerns about the thinning salt crust, attributed partly to nearby potash mining, and restoration projects pump brine back onto the flats.
