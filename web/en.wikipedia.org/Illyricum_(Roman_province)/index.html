Illyricum (Roman province)

Region: Western Balkans, eastern Adriatic coast
Established: after the Illyrian Wars, formally a province from 27 BC
Later divided into: Dalmatia and Pannonia

Illyricum was a Roman province covering the territory of the former Illyrian kingdom and neighbouring lands along the eastern Adriatic coast and into the Balkan interior, corresponding roughly to parts of modern Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Montenegro, Albania, Serbia, Hungary, and Austria. Rome conquered the region through the Illyrian Wars of the third and second centuries BC.

History

After intermittent conflict, Rome annexed Illyria following the campaigns against Queen Teuta and later rulers. The Great Illyrian Revolt of AD 6–9, led by Bato the Daesitiate, was one of the most serious uprisings faced by the early Roman Empire and required a massive military effort to suppress. In the aftermath, Illyricum was divided into the provinces of Dalmatia (the coastal and southern region) and Pannonia (the northern plain).

Significance

The region became one of the empire's most important sources of soldiers; in the third century AD many emperors, including Aurelian, Probus, Diocletian, and Constantine the Great, came from Illyricum or the Danubian provinces. Administratively, the name Illyricum later denoted a large praetorian prefecture of the late empire.
