Circumflex

The circumflex (◌̂) is a diacritical mark used in the written forms of many languages, including French, Portuguese, Romanian, Vietnamese, Welsh, and Turkish, as well as in transliteration systems. It has the form of a chevron or "hat" placed above a letter.

Uses

In Ancient Greek the circumflex (perispomeni) marked a pitch accent—a rise and fall of tone on a long vowel or diphthong. In French, the circumflex often indicates the historical loss of a following s (for example forêt from "forest") or vowel length. In Romanian it marks central vowels, and in Turkish it can lengthen vowels or palatalize preceding consonants. In mathematics and computing the caret symbol ^ derived from the circumflex is used for exponentiation and other operations.

History

The mark originated in Hellenistic scholarship, where it was written as a combination of the acute and grave accents. Its name derives from the Latin circumflexus, "bent around."
