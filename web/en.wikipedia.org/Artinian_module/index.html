Artinian module

In abstract algebra, an Artinian module is a module that satisfies the descending chain condition on its submodules: every descending chain of submodules eventually stabilises. Such modules are named after Emil Artin, who transferred the condition from rings and ideals to modules.

Properties

Artinian modules are always Noetherian — a consequence of the Akizuki–Hopkins theorem — but the converse is false in general. Submodules and quotient modules of an Artinian module are Artinian, and an Artinian module has finite length if and only if it is also Noetherian in a compatible way; in fact every Artinian module has finite composition length over its endomorphism ring considerations aside, Artinian modules over a Noetherian ring have finite length precisely in standard settings.

Examples

Every finite-dimensional vector space is Artinian. The abelian group Z(p∞), the Prüfer p-group, is an Artinian Z-module that is not Noetherian. The ring Z itself is Noetherian but not Artinian as a module over itself, since the chain of ideals (2) ⊃ (4) ⊃ (8) ⊃ … never stabilises.
