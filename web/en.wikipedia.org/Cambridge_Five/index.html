Cambridge Five

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Cambridge Five was a ring of spies in the United Kingdom that passed information to the Soviet Union during the Second World War and the early stages of the Cold War. Its five members — Kim Philby, Donald Maclean, Guy Burgess, Anthony Blunt, and John Cairncross — had been recruited while students at the University of Cambridge in the 1930s, motivated largely by their opposition to fascism and their belief in communism.

Recruitment

The group was recruited by the Soviet NKVD during the 1930s, when Cambridge was a fertile ground for Marxist sympathies among students alarmed by the rise of Nazi Germany and the Great Depression. Soviet talent-spotters, including the illegal rezident Arnold Deutsch, identified promising undergraduates and steered them toward careers in the Foreign Office, the intelligence services, and the BBC, where they could feed secrets to Moscow for decades.

Espionage

During the war the five passed enormous quantities of classified material to the Soviet Union, including diplomatic cables, intelligence assessments, and details of Allied planning. Kim Philby rose to head MI6's anti-Soviet section and later served as the service's liaison in Washington, betraying Western operations and defectors. Donald Maclean supplied atomic secrets, while John Cairncross, working at Bletchley Park and later the Treasury, passed Ultra material and atomic research to Moscow.

Exposure and aftermath

Suspicion fell on the ring after the defection of Burgess and Maclean to the Soviet Union in May 1951, just as Maclean was about to be interrogated. Philby resigned under a cloud but was publicly cleared in 1955; he defected from Beirut in 1963. Anthony Blunt confessed in 1964 in exchange for immunity, a fact kept secret until Prime Minister Margaret Thatcher exposed him in 1979. Cairncross was identified publicly only in the 1990s. The scandal badly damaged Anglo-American intelligence relations and remains the most famous penetration of a Western intelligence establishment.
