Stem

Cross-section of a flax stem, showing the arrangement of supporting and conducting tissues.

Stem most commonly refers to the main structural axis of a vascular plant, which supports leaves, flowers and fruit and conducts water and nutrients between the roots and the rest of the plant. The word "stem" is also used in many other senses, listed below.

Plant stem

In botany, the stem is one of two main structural axes of a vascular plant, the other being the root. It is divided into nodes, where leaves and buds arise, and internodes between them. Stems grow at apical meristems, thicken by secondary growth in woody plants, and contain vascular tissues — xylem carrying water upward and phloem distributing the products of photosynthesis. Modified stems include rhizomes, tubers, bulbs, runners and thorns.

Other uses

In linguistics, a stem is the part of a word to which affixes are added. In music notation, the stem is the vertical line attached to a note head. In science education, STEM is an acronym for science, technology, engineering and mathematics. A wine glass stem, a ship's stem, and the stem of a watch or pipe are further unrelated uses of the term.
