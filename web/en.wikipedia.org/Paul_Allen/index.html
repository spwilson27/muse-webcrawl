Paul Allen

Paul Gardner Allen (January 21, 1953 – October 15, 2018) was an American businessman and philanthropist who co-founded Microsoft with Bill Gates in 1975. Through his investment firm Vulcan Inc., he pursued ventures in technology, real estate, spaceflight, and the arts.

Microsoft

Allen met Gates at Lakeside School in Seattle. In 1975 the two founded Microsoft in Albuquerque, New Mexico, after developing a BASIC interpreter for the Altair 8800. Allen coined the company's name and negotiated the acquisition of the operating system that became MS-DOS. He left his operational role in 1983 following a diagnosis of Hodgkin's lymphoma but retained his equity, becoming one of the world's wealthiest individuals.

Later ventures and philanthropy

Allen owned the Seattle Seahawks (NFL) and Portland Trail Blazers (NBA), funded the Allen Institute for Brain Science and the Allen Institute for Artificial Intelligence, supported SpaceShipOne, the first privately funded crewed spaceflight, and co-founded Stratolaunch. The Paul G. Allen Family Foundation gave extensively to science, the arts, and conservation. He died in Seattle in 2018 from complications of non-Hodgkin lymphoma.
