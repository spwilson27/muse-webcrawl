Operation Rheinübung

Operation Rheinübung ("Exercise Rhine") was the sortie of the German battleship Bismarck and heavy cruiser Prinz Eugen into the Atlantic Ocean in May 1941, the last major surface-ship commerce-raiding operation of the Kriegsmarine in the Second World War.

Course of the operation

The ships, under Admiral Günther Lütjens, sailed from Gotenhafen on 19 May 1941. Detected by Swedish and British intelligence, they were intercepted in the Denmark Strait, where on 24 May Bismarck sank the battlecruiser HMS Hood with the loss of all but three of her crew. Prinz Eugen separated and eventually reached Brest, while Bismarck, damaged and leaking fuel, headed for France.

Sinking of Bismarck

After a torpedo attack by Fairey Swordfish aircraft from HMS Ark Royal jammed her rudders, Bismarck was overwhelmed by British battleships on 27 May 1941 and sank with most of her crew, including Lütjens. Germany never again risked a capital ship on an Atlantic sortie.
