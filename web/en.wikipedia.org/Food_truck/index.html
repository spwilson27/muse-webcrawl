Food truck

A food truck is a large motorised vehicle or trailer equipped to store, cook, prepare and sell food. Some, including ice cream trucks, sell frozen or prepackaged food; others have onboard kitchens and prepare food from scratch. Food trucks are a fixture of street food culture in many cities and are commonly found at festivals, construction sites, office parks and events.

History

Precursors include the chuck wagons of nineteenth-century cattle drives and the lunch wagons that served night workers in American cities. In the twentieth century, mobile canteens and taco trucks became established, particularly in the southwestern United States. A gourmet food-truck boom began in the late 2000s, popularised by trucks such as Los Angeles' Kogi Korean BBQ, which used social media to announce locations and drew long queues.

Regulation and economics

Food trucks typically require health permits, commissary kitchens for preparation and cleaning, and compliance with municipal rules on parking and vending locations — rules that have been contested by established restaurants in some cities. Lower start-up costs than a fixed restaurant make trucks an entry point for chefs, though margins are affected by weather, seasonality and limited capacity.
