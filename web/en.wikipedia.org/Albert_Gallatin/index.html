Albert Gallatin

Born: 29 January 1761, Geneva, Switzerland
Died: 12 August 1849, Astoria, New York
Office: 4th U.S. Secretary of the Treasury (1801–1814)
Party: Democratic-Republican

Abraham Alfonse Albert Gallatin (29 January 1761 – 12 August 1849) was a Genevan-American politician, diplomat, ethnologist, and linguist. He served as the fourth and longest-serving United States Secretary of the Treasury, from 1801 to 1814, under Presidents Thomas Jefferson and James Madison, and is known as the "Swiss Founding Father".

Early life

Born into a wealthy Genevan family, Gallatin graduated from the Academy of Geneva in 1779 and emigrated to the United States in 1780. He settled in western Pennsylvania, entered politics as an opponent of Federalist financial policy, and served in the Pennsylvania legislature and the U.S. Senate — from which he was removed in 1794 over a citizenship dispute — before election to the House of Representatives, where he became a leader of the Democratic-Republican opposition and helped create the House Ways and Means Committee.

Secretary of the Treasury

As Treasury Secretary, Gallatin reduced the national debt despite funding the Louisiana Purchase, established systematic budgeting practices, and championed internal improvements, notably in his 1808 Report on Roads and Canals. He opposed the War of 1812's financing difficulties and left office to negotiate the Treaty of Ghent (1814), ending the war.

Later life

Gallatin served as Minister to France and to Great Britain, then settled in New York. He helped found New York University in 1831 and became a pioneering scholar of Native American languages, founding the American Ethnological Society in 1842. His home, Friendship Hill in Pennsylvania, is now a National Historic Site.
