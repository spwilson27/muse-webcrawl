The University of Arizona (UArizona) is a public land-grant research university in Tucson, Arizona, United States. Founded in 1885 by the 13th Arizona Territorial Legislature, it was the first university in the Arizona Territory. It is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities — Very high research activity" and is a member of the Association of American Universities.

Academics and research

The university enrolls more than 50,000 students across its Tucson and Phoenix-area programs. It is renowned for astronomy and space sciences: its Lunar and Planetary Laboratory played central roles in NASA missions, and the university led the Phoenix Mars Lander mission (2008) and the OSIRIS-REx asteroid sample-return mission. The Steward Observatory Mirror Laboratory casts giant telescope mirrors beneath the football stadium. The university is also strong in optics, arid-lands studies, anthropology, and the health sciences through the University of Arizona College of Medicine – Tucson and the College of Medicine – Phoenix.

Campus

The main campus covers about 380 acres in central Tucson. Landmarks include Old Main, the Arizona State Museum (the oldest and largest anthropology museum in the Southwest), and the Center for Creative Photography, founded with Ansel Adams.

Athletics

The Arizona Wildcats compete in NCAA Division I; the men's basketball team won the national championship in 1997 under coach Lute Olson. In 2024 the university moved to the Big 12 Conference.
