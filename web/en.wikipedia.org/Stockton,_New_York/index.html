Stockton is a town in Chautauqua County, New York, United States. It lies in the western part of the county, east of Lake Erie and southwest of Buffalo, and had a population of roughly 2,200 at recent censuses. The town includes the hamlet of Stockton and lies near Cassadaga Lake and the village of Cassadaga to its east.

History

The town was first settled around 1810 and was formed from the town of Chautauqua in 1821. It was named in honour of Richard Stockton, a signer of the Declaration of Independence from New Jersey. Agriculture — especially dairy farming and grapes — shaped the town's development, and in the 19th century it was also known for its religious camp meetings and early Spiritualist activity in the surrounding area, which led to the founding of nearby Lily Dale.

Geography

Stockton is a rural, rolling town of about 47 square miles drained by tributaries of Cassadaga Creek. New York State Route 60 and Route 380 cross the town, connecting its hamlets with Jamestown to the south and Fredonia and Dunkirk on Lake Erie to the north.
