Digital Negative

Digital Negative (DNG) is an open, lossless raw image format developed by Adobe and used for digital photography. It was launched on September 27, 2004, alongside the Adobe DNG Converter. The format is based on the TIFF/EP standard and mandates the inclusion of significant metadata.

Purpose

Adobe created DNG in response to the proliferation of hundreds of incompatible proprietary camera raw formats, which raised concerns about long-term archival access to digital photographs. DNG aims to be a publicly documented, widely supported standard that camera manufacturers and software developers can adopt, so raw files remain readable in the future. The specification is freely licensed, and Adobe submitted DNG for standardization by the International Organization for Standardization.

Features

A DNG file contains the sensor data from one raw image, a JPEG preview, and standardized metadata describing the camera's color profile and the corrections needed to render the image. Some manufacturers — including Leica, Hasselblad, Pentax, and Ricoh — have offered DNG as a native in-camera format, and smartphone makers support it in "ProRAW"-style workflows.
