In the Plex

In the Plex: How Google Thinks, Works, and Shapes Our Lives is a 2011 book by American technology journalist Steven Levy about the history and inner workings of Google. Based on more than a hundred interviews, including unprecedented access to the company's Mountain View headquarters — the "Googleplex" — it is widely regarded as one of the most detailed accounts of Google's first decade.

Contents

The book traces Google from the Stanford research project of Larry Page and Sergey Brin through its founding in 1998, the development of AdWords and AdSense, the acquisitions of YouTube and Android, the launch of Gmail and Google Books, and the company's expansion into and withdrawal from China. It details Google's engineering culture, its reliance on data-driven decisions, and practices such as "20 percent time."

Reception

Reviews praised Levy's access and narrative detail, making the book a standard reference on Google's culture and business model, though some critics noted its generally admiring tone toward the company.
