Charles Albert (Italian: Carlo Alberto; 2 October 1798 – 28 July 1849) was King of Sardinia (Piedmont-Sardinia) from 1831 until his abdication in 1849. He granted the Statuto Albertino, the constitution that later became the constitution of the unified Kingdom of Italy, and led Piedmont into the First Italian War of Independence against Austria, whose failure drove him from the throne.

Early life

Charles Albert was born in Turin to Charles Emmanuel, Prince of Carignano, and Maria Cristina of Saxony. The Carignano line was a cadet branch of the House of Savoy. Raised partly in Geneva and Paris in a relatively liberal atmosphere, he served as a young officer in the Restoration period and in 1821, as regent for a few days during the Piedmontese revolution, briefly assented to a liberal constitution before the returning king, Charles Felix, repudiated it — an episode that shadowed his reputation among both liberals and reactionaries.

Reign

Succeeding Charles Felix in 1831, Charles Albert at first governed conservatively but progressively reformed the kingdom: he promulgated new civil and criminal codes, modernised the army, encouraged railways and industry, and reduced clerical privilege. Under pressure from the revolutions of 1848, he promulgated the Statuto Albertino on 4 March 1848, a constitutional charter granting civil liberties and establishing a bicameral parliament; it remained Italy's constitution, in amended form, until 1948.

War with Austria and abdication

When Lombardy rose against Austrian rule in March 1848, Charles Albert declared war on Austria, hoping to lead Italian unification under the House of Savoy. Piedmontese forces won initial successes (Goito, Peschiera) but were defeated by Marshal Radetzky at Custoza in July 1848. Resuming hostilities in March 1849, Charles Albert was decisively beaten at Novara on 23 March 1849; that night he abdicated in favour of his son, Victor Emmanuel II, and went into exile in Porto, Portugal, where he died four months later.

Legacy

Though his war failed, Charles Albert's constitution and his commitment of the Savoy monarchy to the Italian national cause laid the foundation on which his son and Cavour completed unification in 1861.
