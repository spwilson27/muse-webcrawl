Scopes Trial

The Scopes Trial, formally The State of Tennessee v. John Thomas Scopes and commonly known as the Scopes Monkey Trial, was an American legal case in July 1925 in which a high school teacher, John T. Scopes, was accused of violating Tennessee's Butler Act, which had made it illegal to teach human evolution in any state-funded school.

Background

The Butler Act, passed in March 1925, prohibited teachers in public schools from denying the biblical account of mankind's origin by teaching that human beings descended from a lower order of animals. The American Civil Liberties Union offered to defend anyone accused of teaching evolution, and civic leaders in Dayton, Tennessee, recruited Scopes to stand as the defendant in a test case that would also bring publicity to their town.

The trial

The trial in Dayton drew national attention. Former presidential candidate William Jennings Bryan joined the prosecution, while famed trial lawyer Clarence Darrow led the defence. In the trial's most famous episode, Darrow called Bryan to the stand as an expert on the Bible and questioned him about literal interpretations of scripture. Scopes was found guilty and fined $100, though the verdict was later overturned on a technicality by the Tennessee Supreme Court.

Aftermath and legacy

Bryan died five days after the trial ended. The case was widely reported and is regarded as a pivotal moment in the American creation–evolution controversy. The Butler Act remained in force until 1967. The trial was dramatised in the 1955 play and 1960 film Inherit the Wind.
