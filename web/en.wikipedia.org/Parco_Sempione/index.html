Parco Sempione

Parco Sempione
LocationMilan, Italy
Area38.6 hectares (95 acres)
Created1888–1894

Parco Sempione ("Simplon Park") is the largest city park in Milan, Italy, covering about 38.6 hectares between the Castello Sforzesco and the Arco della Pace. Laid out in the English landscape style by architect Emilio Alemagna, it was built between 1888 and 1894 on the former parade ground of the castle.

Features

The park contains lawns, a pond, and notable buildings. The Palazzo dell'Arte (1933), by Giovanni Muzio, hosts the Triennale design museum. The Torre Branca, a 108-metre steel tower designed by Gio Ponti in 1933, offers panoramic views. The park also contains the Arena Civica (1807), one of the oldest sports venues in Italy, a public aquarium dating to 1906, and the Biblioteca del Parco, a glass-walled library pavilion.

Surroundings

The park forms the green heart of a monumental axis: the Castello Sforzesco at its southeast end and the neoclassical Arco della Pace, begun under Napoleon, at the northwest.
