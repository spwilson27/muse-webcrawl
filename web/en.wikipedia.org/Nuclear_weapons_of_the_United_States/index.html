Nuclear weapons of the United States

The United States was the first country to manufacture nuclear weapons and is the only country to have used them in combat, with the bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki in August 1945. Before and during the Cold War, it conducted over 1,000 nuclear tests. The United States maintains a nuclear triad of land-based intercontinental ballistic missiles, submarine-launched ballistic missiles, and strategic bombers.

Development

The weapons program began with the Manhattan Project during World War II. After the war, the Atomic Energy Commission and later the Department of Energy supervised production at sites including Los Alamos, Oak Ridge, Hanford, and Pantex. The United States tested the first thermonuclear device, Ivy Mike, in 1952.

Stockpile

The US stockpile peaked at about 31,000 warheads in 1967. Following arms-control agreements and the end of the Cold War, numbers fell steadily; recent official disclosures have placed the stockpile at roughly 3,700 warheads, with additional retired warheads awaiting dismantlement.

Policy

The United States maintains a policy of deterrence and extends a "nuclear umbrella" to allies. It has not conducted an explosive nuclear test since 1992 and observes a testing moratorium. Command and control of the weapons rests with the President, and the arsenal is managed by the National Nuclear Security Administration.
