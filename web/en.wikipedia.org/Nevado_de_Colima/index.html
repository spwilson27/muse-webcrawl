Nevado de Colima

The Nevado de Colima, also known as Tzapotépetl, is a stratovolcano in western Mexico. At about 4,339 metres (14,236 ft) above sea level, it is one of the highest peaks in Mexico — the seventh-highest by most lists. It lies in the states of Jalisco and Colima, within the Nevado de Colima National Park, a few kilometres north of its younger and still-active neighbour, the Volcán de Colima (Volcán de Fuego).

Geography

The Nevado forms the older, northern part of the Colima volcanic complex at the western end of the Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt. Unlike the frequently erupting Volcán de Colima to its south, the Nevado is dormant and is not considered to have erupted in historical times. Its summit area carries the scars of large ancient landslides and debris avalanches.

Climbing

The mountain is a popular climb, typically approached from the national park roads on its flanks. Above roughly 4,000 metres the terrain is volcanic ash and scree; seasonal snow gives the peak its name (nevado means "snow-capped"). From the summit, on clear days, both the Pacific coast and the active crater of Volcán de Colima can be seen.
