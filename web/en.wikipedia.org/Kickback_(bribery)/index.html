Kickback (bribery)

A kickback is a form of negotiated bribery in which a commission is paid to a decision-maker in return for awarding business, contracts or favourable treatment. Unlike an open commission or referral fee, a kickback is illicit because it is concealed from the employer or principal whose interests the decision-maker is supposed to serve, and the inflated cost is usually passed on to the victim of the arrangement.

Mechanism

In a typical scheme, a supplier inflates its invoice to a purchasing organisation; the buyer or official who selected the supplier secretly receives a percentage of the inflated amount. Kickbacks occur in government procurement, construction, healthcare and defence contracting. They distort competition, raise prices and can compromise quality when contracts go to the most generous rather than the most competent bidder.

Legal treatment

Most jurisdictions criminalise kickbacks under bribery, fraud and racketeering statutes. In the United States, the federal Anti-Kickback Statute specifically targets healthcare programmes, while the Foreign Corrupt Practices Act addresses bribes to foreign officials; procurement kickbacks are also covered by the Anti-Kickback Act of 1986. Many countries regulate the practice under laws implementing the OECD Anti-Bribery Convention and the UN Convention against Corruption.
