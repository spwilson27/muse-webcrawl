Essential medicines, as defined by the World Health Organization (WHO), are medicines that "satisfy the priority health care needs of the population". In 1977 the WHO published the first Model List of Essential Medicines; the list, updated every two years, now contains several hundred medicines and serves as a guide for national formularies in most countries.

Selection

Medicines are selected with regard to disease prevalence, evidence of efficacy and safety, and comparative cost-effectiveness. A separate list is maintained for children. The list comprises a core list of minimum needs for a basic health system and a complementary list for priority diseases requiring specialised facilities or training.

Access

The concept underpins global efforts to improve access to medicines: the WHO estimates that around two billion people lack regular access to essential medicines. Inclusion of high-cost treatments, such as direct-acting antivirals for hepatitis C and newer cancer drugs, has fuelled debate about affordability and patent policy.
