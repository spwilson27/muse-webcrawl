Hi-hat

A hi-hat (hihat, high-hat) is a pair of cymbals mounted facing each other on a stand, forming a standard part of the modern drum kit. The drummer strikes the cymbals with a stick and can bring them together using a foot pedal, producing sounds ranging from a short, crisp "chick" when closed to a sustained sizzle when open.

History

The hi-hat evolved in the 1920s from the low "sock cymbal" pedal into its raised, stand-mounted form, enabling drummers in jazz bands to play the cymbals with sticks as well as the foot. It became the principal timekeeping instrument in swing, bebop, rock, and funk drumming.

Technique

Players articulate patterns with the right hand (for right-handed kits), open the hats slightly for accent effects, "bark" them by striking while closing the pedal, and use the foot alone for the closing chick on beats two and four in jazz. Common sizes are 13 and 14 inches. See also cymbal and ride cymbal.
