Knickerbocker Trust Company

The Knickerbocker Trust Company was an American trust company based in New York City. Its failure in October 1907, after a run on its deposits, was the central event that triggered the Panic of 1907.

Founded1884
Defunct1907 (suspended payments)
HeadquartersNew York City
IndustryBanking, trust services

History

Founded in 1884, the Knickerbocker grew into one of the largest trust companies in the United States, holding deposits of more than $60 million by 1907. Its president, Charles T. Barney, was forced to resign after his involvement with speculator F. Augustus Heinze became public.

Failure

On 22 October 1907 a run on the Knickerbocker forced it to suspend payments. The panic spread to other trust companies and banks, and was only contained after financier J. P. Morgan organized rescues and liquidity support. The crisis contributed to the eventual creation of the Federal Reserve System in 1913.
