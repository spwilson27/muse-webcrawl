The Academy of Music, also known as American Academy of Music, is a concert hall and opera house located at 240 S. Broad Street in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. It is the oldest opera house in the United States still used for its original purpose, and is known as the "Grand Old Lady of Broad Street".

History

Designed by the Philadelphia firm of Napoleon LeBrun and Gustavus Runge, the Academy opened on 26 January 1857 with a grand ball. Its ornate, horseshoe-shaped auditorium — seating around 2,400–2,900 — was modelled on European opera houses, with open balconies on columns rather than enclosed boxes. The building hosted the 1872 Republican National Convention and, for over a century, was the home of the Philadelphia Orchestra until the orchestra moved to the Kimmel Center in 2001.

Today

The Academy is now owned by the Philadelphia Orchestra and Ensemble Arts and operated as part of the Kimmel Center complex. It is the home stage of Opera Philadelphia and the Pennsylvania Ballet, hosts Broadway touring productions and the annual Academy Ball, and was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1962.
