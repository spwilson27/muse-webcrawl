Great Pyramid of Giza

The Great Pyramid of Giza is the largest Egyptian pyramid and served as the tomb of pharaoh Khufu, who initiated its construction in the early 26th century BC during the Fourth Dynasty of the Old Kingdom. Built over a period of about 27 years, it is the oldest of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World and the only one that remains largely intact. It was the tallest human-made structure in the world for more than 3,800 years.

Construction

The pyramid consists of an estimated 2.3 million blocks of stone, most quarried locally from the Giza plateau, with fine white Tura limestone for the outer casing and granite from Aswan, some 800 km away, for the inner chambers. Egyptologists generally agree that the workforce consisted of seasonal conscripted labourers rather than slaves; estimates commonly suggest around 20,000–30,000 workers. A workers' town, bakeries and cemeteries have been excavated south of the plateau. The method by which blocks were raised remains debated, with ramps of various designs the leading proposals.

Interior

Three main chambers are known: an unfinished subterranean chamber cut into the bedrock, the Queen's Chamber, and the King's Chamber above it, reached by the corbelled Grand Gallery. The King's Chamber contains a granite sarcophagus and is roofed by five relieving chambers designed to distribute the weight of the masonry above. Narrow shafts extend from both upper chambers; their purpose — possibly ritual or astronomical — is still debated. In 2017 the ScanPyramids project announced a large void above the Grand Gallery detected by muon tomography.

History and exploration

The pyramid was robbed in antiquity and the Middle Ages; the caliph al-Ma'mun is traditionally said to have forced an entrance around AD 820, the passage still used by visitors today. The smooth white casing stones were largely stripped for building projects in medieval Cairo, leaving the stepped core visible today and reducing the height. Systematic surveys were made by Flinders Petrie in the 1880s, whose measurements remain a benchmark. The pyramid forms the centrepiece of the Giza pyramid complex, alongside the pyramids of Khafre and Menkaure, the Great Sphinx, temples and subsidiary pyramids.
