Boeing F4B

The Boeing F4B was a United States Navy carrier-based biplane fighter of the 1930s, the naval version of the Army's P-12. A compact single-seat biplane of metal construction with fabric covering, powered by a Pratt & Whitney R-1340 Wasp radial engine, it was among the last biplane fighters in U.S. service.

Development and service

Derived from the Boeing Model 83/89 prototypes of 1928, the F4B went through variants F4B-1 to F4B-4, the last introducing a larger fin, a Townend ring cowling and a semi-monocoque metal rear fuselage. Deliveries began in 1929; roughly 586 P-12/F4B family aircraft were built. The type equipped Navy and Marine Corps fighter squadrons aboard carriers such as Langley, Saratoga and Lexington before replacement by monoplanes in the late 1930s.

Operators and survivors

Export versions served in Brazil, China, the Philippines, Siam/Thailand and Spain. Several examples survive in museums, including the National Naval Aviation Museum at Pensacola.
