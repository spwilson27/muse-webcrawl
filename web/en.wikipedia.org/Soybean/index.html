The soybean, soy bean, or soya bean (Glycine max) is a species of legume native to East Asia, widely grown for its edible bean, which has numerous uses. Traditional unfermented food uses of soybeans include soy milk, from which tofu and tofu skin are made. Fermented soy foods include soy sauce, fermented bean paste, nattō, and tempeh. Fat-free (defatted) soybean meal is a significant and cheap source of protein for animal feeds and many packaged meals. Soy vegetable oil is another product of processing the soybean crop.

Composition and cultivation

Dry soybeans contain about 36% protein, 30% carbohydrates, 20% fat, and 9% water, and they are unusual among plant foods for providing all essential amino acids in useful proportions. Like other legumes, soy fixes nitrogen through root-nodule bacteria (Bradyrhizobium japonicum), improving soil fertility. The wild ancestor Glycine soja grows in China, Japan, Korea, and the Russian Far East; soybean was cultivated in China by at least 1100 BC and reached Europe and North America in the 18th–19th centuries, becoming a major American crop in the 20th.

Production and issues

Global production exceeds 350 million tonnes annually, concentrated in Brazil, the United States, and Argentina; most of the harvest is crushed for animal feed meal and oil, with direct human consumption a minority share. A large majority of soy grown in the Americas is genetically modified for herbicide tolerance. Soy expansion has been linked to deforestation, especially in the Brazilian Cerrado and Amazon fringe, making it a focus of sustainability certification efforts.
