Anencephaly is a serious birth defect in which a baby is born without major parts of the brain and skull. It is a neural tube defect that occurs when the rostral (head) end of the neural tube fails to close, usually between the 23rd and 26th day of conception.

Signs and characteristics

A newborn with anencephaly lacks a major portion of the brain, skull and scalp. The cerebral hemispheres are typically absent, although the brainstem may be present, so reflex actions such as breathing and responses to sound or touch can occur. The condition is uniformly fatal: most affected infants are stillborn or die within hours to days of birth.

Causes and prevention

The exact cause is multifactorial, involving genetic and environmental factors. The best-established preventive measure is adequate intake of folic acid (vitamin B9) before conception and during early pregnancy, which substantially reduces the risk of neural tube defects; many countries therefore fortify flour with folic acid and recommend supplementation for women of childbearing age. Other risk factors include certain medications, maternal diabetes and obesity.

Diagnosis

Anencephaly can be detected prenatally by ultrasound and by elevated alpha-fetoprotein levels in maternal blood or amniotic fluid.
