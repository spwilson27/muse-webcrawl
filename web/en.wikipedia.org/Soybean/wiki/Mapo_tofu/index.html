Mapo tofu (Chinese: 麻婆豆腐; pinyin: mápó dòufu) is a popular Chinese dish from Sichuan cuisine consisting of tofu cubes simmered in a spicy, oily sauce based on doubanjiang (fermented broad bean and chilli paste) and douchi (fermented black beans), typically with minced pork or beef. Its defining character is mala: the combination of chilli heat and the numbing tingle of Sichuan pepper.

History

The dish is traditionally dated to 1862 in Chengdu, where a small restaurant run by a Mrs. Chen — whose pockmarked face (ma) earned her the name "Mapo", "pockmarked old woman" — served the tofu dish to oil porters and workers. Her restaurant, Chen Mapo Tofu, still exists in Chengdu.

Preparation

Soft tofu is cut into cubes and simmered in a sauce of chilli oil, doubanjiang, fermented black beans, garlic, ginger and minced meat, thickened with starch and finished with ground roasted Sichuan pepper and scallions. It is usually served with steamed rice. Vegetarian versions omit the meat, and the dish has been adapted across China, Japan (as mābō-dōfu) and Korea, often in milder forms.
