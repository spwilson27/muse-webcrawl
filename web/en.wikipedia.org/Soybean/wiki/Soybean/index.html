The soybean (Glycine max) is a species of legume native to East Asia, widely grown for its edible bean, which has numerous uses. One of the world's most important crops, it is a major source of vegetable protein and oil, and the great majority of the harvest is processed into soybean meal and oil, much of it used as livestock feed.

History

Soybeans were domesticated in China several thousand years ago — Chinese tradition credits the legendary emperor Shennong, and the crop was among the sacred grains of ancient China. From East Asia it spread through trade, reaching Europe in the eighteenth century and becoming a major crop in the United States in the twentieth century. The largest producers today are the United States, Brazil and Argentina.

Cultivation

Like other legumes, soybeans fix atmospheric nitrogen through symbiotic bacteria in root nodules, enriching the soil. The plants grow in pods containing one to four seeds, and are typically grown in warm climates with well-distributed rainfall, often in rotation with maize.

Uses

Traditional foods include tofu, soy sauce, tempeh, miso, natto and soy milk. Industrial uses include biodiesel, printing inks, plastics and cosmetics. The seed contains about 36–40% protein and 20% oil by weight.
