Folic acid is the synthetic, oxidised form of folate, also known as vitamin B9. It is used in dietary supplements and food fortification, while "folate" refers to the various naturally occurring forms found in food. Folate is essential for the synthesis of DNA and RNA and for the metabolism of amino acids required for cell division.

Health roles

Because rapidly dividing cells need folate, it is especially important during pregnancy and infancy. Inadequate folate in early pregnancy greatly increases the risk of neural tube defects such as spina bifida and anencephaly in the developing baby. Health authorities recommend that women who could become pregnant consume 400 micrograms of folic acid daily. Deficiency in adults causes megaloblastic anaemia.

History

Folate was identified in the 1930s–40s through research on anaemia in pregnancy; Lucy Wills showed a yeast extract cured the condition, and the compound was isolated from spinach leaves (hence the name, from Latin folium, leaf) and synthesised in 1945.

Fortification

Many countries, including the United States (since 1998) and Canada, require folic acid to be added to enriched grain products, a policy followed by measurable declines in neural tube defect rates.
