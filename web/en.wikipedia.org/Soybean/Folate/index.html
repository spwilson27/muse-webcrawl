Folate in soybeans

Folate (vitamin B9)
Soybeans are among the richest plant sources of folate
NutrientFolate (vitamin B9)
Adult RDA400 µg dietary folate equivalents (DFE)/day
Edamame content~300–480 µg per cup (varies by preparation)
Key roleDNA synthesis; prevention of neural tube defects

Folate is the natural form of vitamin B9 found in foods; its synthetic form, used in supplements and fortified foods, is folic acid. Soybeans and soy foods are among the richest plant sources: a cup of cooked edamame supplies a large fraction of the adult daily requirement, and mature cooked soybeans provide roughly 90–180 µg per half cup depending on preparation.

Function

Folate coenzymes carry one-carbon units needed for synthesizing DNA and RNA, for amino-acid metabolism, and for forming red blood cells. Adequate intake before conception and in the first weeks of pregnancy sharply reduces the risk of neural tube defects such as spina bifida and anencephaly, which is why many countries mandate folic-acid fortification of flour and recommend 400–800 µg supplements for women who may become pregnant.

Deficiency and sources

Deficiency causes megaloblastic anaemia and, in pregnancy, birth defects. Besides soy, good sources include leafy green vegetables (the name "folate" comes from Latin folium, leaf), legumes, asparagus, eggs and liver. Folate is water-soluble and heat-sensitive, so boiling leaches it into cooking water; gentler cooking preserves more.
