Snakehead (gang)

Snakeheads (Chinese: 蛇頭; pinyin: shé tóu) are Chinese gangs involved in the smuggling of people to other countries. The name derives from the way a line of smuggled migrants follows its guide, like the body of a snake behind its head. Snakehead organizations operate largely out of Fujian province in China and transport their customers to wealthier nations in North America, Western Europe, and Australasia, as well as to Taiwan and Japan.

Migrants have been smuggled in shipping containers and freighter holds

Operations

Snakeheads charge large fees — historically tens of thousands of US dollars per person — often paid through family networks or worked off as debt after arrival in restaurants and garment workshops. Transport may be by air with fraudulent documents, by sea in modified freighters, or overland in stages. The trade is decentralized: brokers recruit clients, while separate specialists arrange documents, transit, and safe houses.

Notable cases

The most prominent snakehead was Cheng Chui Ping, known as "Sister Ping", who ran a smuggling empire from New York's Chinatown. In 1993 the freighter Golden Venture, carrying 286 undocumented Chinese migrants organized by snakehead networks, ran aground off Queens, New York, and ten passengers drowned. Sister Ping was convicted in 2005 and sentenced to 35 years in prison. In 2000, 58 Chinese migrants suffocated in a lorry at Dover, England, an event that drew international attention to the trade.
