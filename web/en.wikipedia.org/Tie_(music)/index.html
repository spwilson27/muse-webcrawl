Tie (music)

In music notation, a tie is a curved line connecting the heads of two notes of the same pitch, indicating that they are to be played as a single note with a duration equal to the sum of the individual notes' values. A tie is similar in appearance to a slur, but a slur joins notes of different pitches and indicates a phrase, whereas a tie always joins notes of the same pitch and merges their durations.

Usage

Ties are used when a note must be sustained across a bar line, when a duration cannot be expressed with a single note value (for example, a note lasting five quarter notes), or to observe the metrical structure of a measure by splitting long notes at the half-measure or beat level. The second note of a tied pair is not re-articulated; its sound simply continues.

Direction and placement

Ties are normally drawn on the opposite side of the note heads from the stems: above the notes when stems point down, and below when stems point up. In music with multiple voices on one staff, ties follow the voice they belong to. A chain of three or more notes of the same pitch may be tied successively to produce very long sustained values.

History

The tie developed in mensural and early modern notation as scribes needed to prolong notes across bar lines and into values not available as single symbols. Its modern form — and the convention distinguishing it from the phrase slur — became standard during the Baroque and Classical eras with the regularization of printed notation.
