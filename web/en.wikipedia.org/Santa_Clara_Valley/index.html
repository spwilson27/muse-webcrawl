Santa Clara Valley

The Santa Clara Valley is a valley in Northern California that runs southeast from the southern end of San Francisco Bay. It corresponds roughly to Santa Clara County and contains the cities of San Jose, Santa Clara, Sunnyvale, Cupertino, Mountain View, Palo Alto and others. Since the 1970s the valley has been universally known as Silicon Valley.

Geography

The flat valley floor is flanked by the Santa Cruz Mountains to the southwest and the Diablo Range to the northeast. It is drained by the Guadalupe River, Coyote Creek and other streams flowing north into the bay. The valley's Mediterranean climate — warm dry summers and mild wet winters — once made it ideal for orchards and vineyards.

History

Long home to Ohlone peoples, the valley was colonised by Spain with the founding of Mission Santa Clara (1777) and the pueblo of San Jose. After US annexation it became one of the world's great fruit-growing regions, the "Valley of Heart's Delight", covered in prune, apricot and cherry orchards and canneries. In the mid-20th century, electronics firms — growing from defence contracts, Stanford University's research park and the semiconductor industry — progressively replaced agriculture. Almost all the orchards had vanished by the 1990s, and the valley's name itself was superseded in common use by "Silicon Valley".

Water and environment

The Santa Clara Valley Water District manages the valley's water supply, including reservoirs in the surrounding hills and one of the largest groundwater basins in California; historic over-pumping caused notable land subsidence near the bay shore. Coyote Valley, a largely open corridor at the valley's southern end, is a focus of conservation efforts.
