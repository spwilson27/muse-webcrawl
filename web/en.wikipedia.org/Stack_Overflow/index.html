Stack Overflow, the flagship site of the Stack Exchange Network.

Stack Overflow is a question-and-answer website for computer programmers. It is the flagship site of the Stack Exchange Network. It was created in 2008 by Jeff Atwood and Joel Spolsky as a more open alternative to earlier question-and-answer websites such as Experts Exchange. The name of the website was chosen by voting in April 2008 by readers of Atwood's blog, Coding Horror, and is a pun on the stack overflow programming error.

How it works

Registered users can post questions about specific programming problems, algorithms, or software tools, and other users post answers. Content is voted up or down, and users earn reputation points and badges for valued contributions; higher reputation unlocks moderation privileges, so the site is largely community-moderated. Questions and answers are licensed under Creative Commons (CC BY-SA). By the early 2020s the site had accumulated tens of millions of questions and well over 100 million visitors a month, becoming the largest programming Q&A community in the world.

History

Stack Overflow launched in beta in 2008 and rapidly drew programmers away from forums and subscription sites. Its model spawned the Stack Exchange network of topic-specific Q&A communities (including Super User, Server Fault, MathOverflow-style mathematics sites, and many more). In 2018 the company launched Stack Overflow for Teams, a private version for companies, and in June 2021 the technology investment firm Prosus acquired Stack Overflow for approximately US$1.8 billion. The emergence of large language model chatbots in the 2020s sharply reduced new question traffic, as programmers increasingly asked AI assistants instead.

Criticism

The site has been criticised for perceived hostility to newcomers, aggressive closure of questions as duplicates or off-topic, and duplication and ageing of highly-ranked answers. Its own surveys and moderation debates reflect long-running tension between maintaining a high-quality reference library and being welcoming to beginners.
