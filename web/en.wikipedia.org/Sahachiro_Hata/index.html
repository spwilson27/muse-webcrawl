Sahachiro Hata

Born23 March 1873, Masuda, Shimane, Japan
Died22 November 1938, Tokyo, Japan
Known forCo-discovery of arsphenamine (Salvarsan)
FieldsBacteriology, chemotherapy

Sahachiro Hata (1873–1938) was a Japanese bacteriologist who, working with Paul Ehrlich in Frankfurt, co-developed arsphenamine (marketed as Salvarsan) in 1909, the first effective treatment for syphilis and a landmark in the history of chemotherapy.

Career

Hata studied medicine in Okayama and worked at the Kitasato Institute for Infectious Diseases in Tokyo under Shibasaburo Kitasato, researching plague and other infectious diseases. In 1907 he travelled to Germany to work in Ehrlich's laboratory, where he developed a method of infecting rabbits with syphilis, enabling systematic screening of arsenical compounds. Compound 606, arsphenamine, proved effective and became known as Salvarsan, the "magic bullet."

Later life

Returning to Japan, Hata continued research on spirochaetes and relapsing fever and taught at Keio University. He was nominated several times for the Nobel Prize but never received it.
