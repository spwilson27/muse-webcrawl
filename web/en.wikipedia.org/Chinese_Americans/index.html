Chinese Americans


San Francisco's Chinatown, the oldest in North America

Chinese Americans are Americans of Chinese ancestry. Chinese Americans constitute a subgroup of East Asian Americans, in turn a subgroup of Asian Americans. Many Chinese Americans have ancestors from mainland China, Hong Kong, Macau, Malaysia, Singapore, Taiwan, as well as other regions where the Chinese diaspora is present. With a population of roughly 5.5 million, Chinese Americans form the largest single group among Asian Americans and about 1.6% of the total United States population.

History

Significant Chinese immigration began during the California Gold Rush of the 1850s. Chinese labourers later played a central role in building the western portion of the first transcontinental railroad. Growing anti-Chinese sentiment culminated in the Chinese Exclusion Act of 1882, the first US law to bar immigration by a named nationality, which was not repealed until 1943 (the Magnuson Act). Immigration reforms in 1965, followed by the normalisation of US–China relations in the 1970s, led to large new waves of immigration from mainland China, Taiwan and Hong Kong.

Demographics and culture

Large Chinese American communities are found in New York City, the San Francisco Bay Area, Los Angeles, Houston, Chicago and Boston, often centred on historic Chinatowns. Chinese Americans are among the most highly educated groups in the United States, with high rates of college and advanced-degree attainment, and are prominent in science, technology, medicine, business, academia and the arts. Languages spoken include Mandarin, Cantonese, Taishanese, Hokkien and other Chinese varieties alongside English.

Notable Chinese Americans include architect I. M. Pei, cellist Yo-Yo Ma, physicist Steven Chu, former Secretary of Commerce Gary Locke, and author Amy Tan.
