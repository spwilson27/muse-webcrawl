The Olympic Mountains are a mountain range on the Olympic Peninsula of the U.S. state of Washington. Their highest summit is Mount Olympus (2,432 m), named in 1788 by the English captain John Meares, who thought the peak looked like a home for the gods. Most of the range lies within Olympic National Park.

Geology and climate

The range is composed mainly of uplifted basalt and sedimentary rocks accreted against the North American plate. Its western slopes are among the wettest places in the contiguous United States, receiving over 3.5 metres of rain a year and supporting the Hoh and Quinault temperate rainforests. Glaciers cap Mount Olympus and other high peaks.

Ecology

The isolated range hosts endemic species including the Olympic marmot and the Olympic torrent salamander. Roosevelt elk, black-tailed deer, and mountain goats inhabit the park, and the Elwha River valley (drained by the range) saw the largest dam-removal project in history in 2011–2014.
