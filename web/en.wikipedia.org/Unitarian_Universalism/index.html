Unitarian Universalism (UU) is a liberal religious movement characterized by a "free and responsible search for truth and meaning". Unitarian Universalists assert no creed; instead they are unified by a shared search for spiritual growth and by a covenant to affirm and promote seven principles, including the inherent worth and dignity of every person and respect for the interdependent web of all existence.

History

The movement formed in 1961 from the consolidation of two historically Christian denominations in the United States: the American Unitarian Association (1825), which affirmed the unity of God over the doctrine of the Trinity, and the Universalist Church of America (1793), which taught universal salvation. Its deeper roots reach back to radical Reformation figures such as Michael Servetus and the Polish Socinians.

Beliefs and practices

Members may hold beliefs drawn from Christianity, Judaism, Buddhism, paganism, humanism, atheism, and other traditions. Congregations are self-governing (congregational polity) and gather for worship that typically includes hymns, readings, sermons, and the lighting of the flaming chalice. The UUA reports roughly a thousand congregations and some 200,000 members, mainly in the United States.

Social activism

Unitarian Universalists have a long record of involvement in abolitionism, women's suffrage, civil rights, LGBTQ rights, and environmental causes.
