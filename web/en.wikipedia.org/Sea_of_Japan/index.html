The Sea of Japan is the marginal sea between the Japanese archipelago, Sakhalin, the Korean Peninsula and the mainland of the Russian Far East. The Japanese archipelago separates the sea from the Pacific Ocean. Like the Mediterranean Sea, it has almost no tides due to its nearly complete enclosure from the Pacific Ocean. This isolation also affects faunal diversity and salinity, both of which are lower than in the open ocean. The sea has no large islands, bays or capes. Its water balance is mostly determined by the inflow and outflow through the straits connecting it to the neighboring Pacific Ocean and East China Sea.

Geography

The sea is bounded by Russia and Sakhalin Island to the north, by Primorsky Krai, Khabarovsk Krai and Sakhalin Oblast to the northwest, by North Korea to the west, by South Korea to the southwest, and by Japan's Honshu island to the east and southeast. In the south it is connected to the East China Sea via the Tsushima Strait and the Korea Strait; in the north it is connected to the Sea of Okhotsk via the Strait of Tartary and the La Perouse Strait.

Naming dispute

The naming of the sea is a source of contention. South Korea argues that the most commonly used name, "Sea of Japan", was spread during the period of Japanese colonial rule, and that the historical name "East Sea" (Donghae) should be used instead. North Korea also advocates for "East Sea of Korea". Japan maintains that "Sea of Japan" is the internationally established name and that the term has been used on Western maps since the late 18th and early 19th centuries.

Economy

The sea is important for fishing, with crab, sardines, squid and other species taken commercially. It is also a major shipping route between ports of Japan, Korea, Russia and China.
