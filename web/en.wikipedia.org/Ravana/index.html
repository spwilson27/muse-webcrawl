Ravana

Ravana
Title: King of Lanka
Abode: Lanka (Sri Lanka)
Parents: Vishrava and Kaikesi
Spouse: Mandodari
Opponent: Rama

Ravana was a multi-headed rakshasa king of the island of Lanka in Hindu mythology, the primary antagonist of the epic Ramayana. He is traditionally depicted with ten heads and twenty arms, symbolising his mastery of the scriptures and the six shastras and four Vedas — though some traditions interpret the ten heads as representing the ten senses or negative emotions.

In the Ramayana

Ravana, a grandson of the sage Pulastya and son of Vishrava, won a boon of near-invulnerability from Brahma after severe penance, and usurped Lanka from his half-brother Kubera. Learning of the beauty of Sita, wife of the exiled prince Rama, Ravana abducted her with the help of the demon Maricha and carried her to Lanka in his flying chariot, the Pushpaka Vimana. Rama allied with the vanara king Sugriva and the monkey general Hanuman, built a bridge across the sea, and after a great battle slew Ravana — an event celebrated in the festival of Dussehra (Vijayadashami), when effigies of Ravana are burned.

Character and worship

Ravana is a complex figure: a tyrannical abductor, yet also a great scholar, a master of the veena, and a devotee of Shiva to whom the Shiva Tandava Stotra is attributed. A verse in some texts credits him with composing the hymn after offering his heads in sacrifice. In parts of India and Sri Lanka he is venerated — temples at places such as Kakinada and in Madhya Pradesh honour him, and some Sri Lankan traditions regard him as a great native king and physician.

In other traditions

Versions of Ravana appear in Jain and Buddhist literature and in the Ramayana retellings of Southeast Asia, including the Thai Ramkien, where he is called Thotsakan. His brother Vibhishana, who defected to Rama, and his giant brother Kumbhakarna are also prominent in the epic.
