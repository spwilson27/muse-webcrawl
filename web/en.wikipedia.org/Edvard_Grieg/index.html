Edvard Grieg

Edvard Hagerup Grieg (15 June 1843 – 4 September 1907) was a Norwegian composer and pianist of the Romantic era. He is widely considered one of the leading Romantic composers, and his music is part of the standard classical repertoire worldwide. His incidental music to Ibsen's Peer Gynt — including "Morning Mood" and "In the Hall of the Mountain King" — and his Piano Concerto in A minor are among his best-known works.

Life

Grieg was born in Bergen to a musical family and was sent to the Leipzig Conservatory at fifteen. Disliking the discipline there, he nonetheless developed into a fine pianist. In Copenhagen he met Rikard Nordraak, whose enthusiasm for Norwegian folk culture inspired Grieg to draw on folk melodies and rhythms. He settled at Troldhaugen, his villa near Bergen, from 1885, and died there in 1907.

Music

Grieg excelled in miniatures: ten volumes of Lyric Pieces for piano, songs, and the Holberg Suite. His Piano Concerto in A minor, Op. 16 (1868), written at age 25, is one of the most popular of all concertos. His incidental music for Henrik Ibsen's play Peer Gynt (1875), arranged into two orchestral suites, achieved worldwide fame.

Legacy

Grieg's harmonies influenced composers such as Debussy and Ravel, and he helped define a distinctly Norwegian national style in classical music. His home Troldhaugen is now a museum.
