Cathelicidin

Cathelicidins are a family of antimicrobial peptides forming part of the innate immune system of vertebrates. They are characterised by a conserved N-terminal "cathelin" domain and a variable C-terminal peptide that is cleaved off to become active. Cathelicidins are stored in the granules of neutrophils and are also produced by epithelial cells of the skin, gut and airways.

The human cathelicidin

Humans have a single cathelicidin gene, CAMP, encoding the precursor hCAP18, which is processed to the active peptide LL-37. Expression is induced by vitamin D. LL-37 disrupts bacterial membranes, neutralises endotoxin, and also modulates inflammation and wound healing. Abnormal processing of LL-37 is implicated in skin conditions such as rosacea and psoriasis, while deficiency is associated with atopic eczema and increased infection. Other mammals have multiple cathelicidins; pigs, cattle and horses produce several, some of which have been studied as models for new antibiotics.
