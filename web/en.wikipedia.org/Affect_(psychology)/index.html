Affect (psychology)


The circumplex model plots affect by valence and arousal

Affect, in psychology, is the underlying experience of feeling, emotion or mood. It is a fundamental concept in affective science and is often used to describe the observable expression of emotion. Affect is typically characterised along dimensions such as valence (positive or negative) and arousal (high or low), as in Russell's circumplex model of affect.

Terminology

Researchers distinguish affect from related terms: an emotion is a relatively brief, intense response to a specific stimulus; a mood is a longer-lasting, lower-intensity state not necessarily tied to a single stimulus; and affect is the umbrella term covering both. "Core affect" refers to the neurophysiological state that is consciously accessible as a simple feeling of pleasure or displeasure with activation. In clinical settings, a patient's affect is assessed by its range, intensity, lability and appropriateness; abnormalities such as blunted, flat or incongruent affect are features of several psychiatric conditions.
