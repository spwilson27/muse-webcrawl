Immersed tube

An immersed tube (or immersed tunnel) is a type of underwater tunnel composed of prefabricated sections, built on land or in a dry dock, floated to the tunnel site, and sunk into a dredged trench on the river- or seabed, where they are joined together and covered. The method is an alternative to bored tunnels and is well suited to wide, shallow water crossings.

Construction

Tunnel elements, typically steel or reinforced concrete and often more than 100 metres long, are sealed with bulkheads, floated out, and lowered into place with centimetre precision. Divers or remote systems connect each element to the previous one; water is pumped from the joint, and water pressure seals the connection with a rubber gasket (the Gina gasket). The completed tube is buried under a protective layer of rock and fill.

Examples

Notable immersed tube tunnels include the Detroit–Windsor Tunnel (1930), the Chesapeake Bay Bridge–Tunnel in Virginia, the Øresund crossing between Denmark and Sweden, the Busan–Geoje Fixed Link in South Korea, and the Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macau Bridge tunnel, one of the longest immersed tubes in the world. The Fehmarn Belt Fixed Link between Denmark and Germany is planned as the longest.
