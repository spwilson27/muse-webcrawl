Orienteering

Orienteering is a group of sports that involve using a map and compass to navigate from point to point in unfamiliar terrain as quickly as possible. Participants are given a topographical map, usually a specially prepared orienteering map, which they use to find control points marked in the terrain by orange-and-white flags. The classic form is foot orienteering; other disciplines include ski orienteering, mountain bike orienteering, and trail orienteering for competitors with mobility impairments.

History

Orienteering began in the late 19th century in Scandinavia as a military training exercise; the first civilian competition was held in Norway in 1897, and the term "orientering" was in use by 1886. The sport spread through Sweden and Finland, and the International Orienteering Federation (IOF) was founded in 1961. The World Orienteering Championships have been held since 1966. Orienteering is especially popular in Scandinavia and central Europe, and is recognised by the International Olympic Committee, though it is not part of the Olympic programme.

Competition

Courses are set in forest or urban terrain in formats including long distance, middle distance, sprint, and relay. Competitors carry an electronic punching device (such as SPORTident) to record visits to each control. Success depends on route choice, map reading at speed, and endurance. Specialised maps use IOF symbols at scales of 1:15,000 or 1:10,000 (1:4,000 for sprints), with five-metre contours and standardised colours for runnability and features.
