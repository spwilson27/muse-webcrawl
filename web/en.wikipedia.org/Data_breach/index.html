Data breach

A data breach is the unauthorised access to or disclosure of sensitive, protected, or confidential data. The data may include personal information such as names, addresses, financial details, health records or credentials. Breaches can result from deliberate attacks by hackers or insiders, or from accidental exposure such as misconfigured databases and lost devices.

Causes and process

Attackers typically proceed through research, intrusion (via phishing, malware or exploited vulnerabilities), escalation of privileges, and exfiltration of data. Criminal groups monetise stolen data through identity theft, fraud and extortion, including ransomware attacks in which data is both encrypted and threatened with publication ("double extortion").

Consequences and regulation

Notable breaches include the 2017 Equifax breach affecting around 147 million people, the Yahoo breaches disclosed in 2016 affecting billions of accounts, and the 2013 Target breach of payment-card data. Many jurisdictions impose notification duties: the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) requires notification of supervisory authorities within 72 hours in many cases, and US states have breach-notification laws beginning with California's in 2002. Organisations face regulatory fines, litigation, remediation costs and reputational damage.
