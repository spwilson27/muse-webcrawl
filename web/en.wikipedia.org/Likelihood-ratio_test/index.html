Likelihood-ratio test

In statistics, the likelihood-ratio test assesses the goodness of fit of a statistical model based on the ratio of maximum likelihoods — comparing the likelihood under a more restricted (null) model with that under a fuller (alternative) model. If the restriction is erroneous, the restricted model fits noticeably worse, and the ratio is small.

Definition

The likelihood ratio is the supremum of the likelihood over the restricted parameter space divided by the supremum over the full space. Its logarithm, multiplied by −2, gives the deviance statistic. By Wilks' theorem, under the null hypothesis and regularity conditions, this statistic is asymptotically chi-squared distributed with degrees of freedom equal to the difference in dimensionality between the two parameter spaces.

Use

Likelihood-ratio tests are widely used for comparing nested models — for example in logistic regression, analysis of variance with generalised linear models, and model selection generally. Related procedures include the Wald test and the score (Lagrange multiplier) test, which are asymptotically equivalent but differ in computation.
