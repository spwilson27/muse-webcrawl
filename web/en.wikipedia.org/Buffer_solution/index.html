Buffer solution

A buffer solution is an aqueous solution consisting of a mixture of a weak acid and its conjugate base, or vice versa. Its pH changes very little when a small amount of strong acid or base is added to it. Buffer solutions are used as a means of keeping pH at a nearly constant value in a wide variety of chemical applications.

Principles

The buffering effect arises from the equilibrium between the weak acid (HA) and its conjugate base (A⁻). Added hydrogen ions are absorbed by the base, and added hydroxide ions react with the acid, so the pH shifts only slightly. The relationship between pH and composition is given by the Henderson–Hasselbalch equation: pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA]).

Buffer capacity

Buffer capacity is a quantitative measure of the resistance of a buffer solution to changes in pH. It is greatest when the concentrations of acid and conjugate base are equal (pH = pKa) and falls off as the pH of the solution moves more than about one unit from the pKa.

Examples and applications

The bicarbonate buffer system maintains the pH of human blood near 7.4. Common laboratory buffers include acetate, phosphate, citrate, and Tris buffers, used in biochemistry, molecular biology, and the calibration of pH meters.
