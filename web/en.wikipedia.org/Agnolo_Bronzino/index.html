Agnolo Bronzino

Born: 17 November 1503, Monticelli, Florence
Died: 23 November 1572, Florence
Movement: Mannerism
Known for: Portraiture

Agnolo di Cosimo (17 November 1503 – 23 November 1572), usually known as Bronzino, was an Italian Mannerist painter from Florence. His sobriquet, "Bronzino", may derive from his relatively dark skin or reddish hair. He lived all his life in Florence and from his late thirties was kept busy as the court painter of Cosimo I de' Medici, Grand Duke of Tuscany.

Life and career

Bronzino was the pupil and adopted son of the painter Pontormo, whose expressive style he gradually transformed into something cooler, more polished and aristocratic. His portraits – of Cosimo I, Eleanor of Toledo, and members of the Medici court – are celebrated for their meticulous rendering of sumptuous fabrics, jewels and armour, and for an air of elegant detachment and stillness.

Among his best-known works are the Portrait of Eleanor of Toledo and her son Giovanni (c. 1545), the Allegory with Venus and Cupid (c. 1545, National Gallery, London), a complex erotic allegory made for presentation to King Francis I of France, and frescoes in the chapel of Eleanor of Toledo in the Palazzo Vecchio.

Bronzino was also a poet, writing burlesque verse in the tradition of Berni, and was a founding figure of the Florentine Academy of Art (Accademia delle Arti del Disegno) in 1563. His pupil Alessandro Allori became his artistic heir.
