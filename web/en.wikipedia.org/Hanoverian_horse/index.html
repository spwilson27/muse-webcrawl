Hanoverian horse

Country of origin: Germany (Lower Saxony)

Use: Dressage, show jumping, eventing

Height: 15.3–17.2 hands

The Hanoverian is a German warmblood horse breed originating in the Lower Saxon state stud at Celle. It is one of the oldest, most numerous, and most successful of the warmblood breeds, renowned worldwide for its athleticism, temperament, and success in the Olympic equestrian disciplines of dressage, show jumping, and eventing.

History

The breed traces its origins to 1735, when George II, King of Great Britain and Elector of Hanover, founded the state stud at Celle. Local mares were crossed with Holsteiner and Thoroughbred stallions to produce a versatile horse suited to agriculture, cavalry, and carriage work. After the Second World War, breeding goals shifted decisively toward the modern sport horse, with infusions of Thoroughbred, Trakehner, and Arabian blood refining the breed. The Hanoverian studbook, administered by the Verband Hannoverscher Warmblutzüchter, maintains strict inspection and performance-testing standards.

Characteristics

Hanoverians are elegant, strongly built horses standing roughly 15.3 to 17.2 hands. They are bred for elastic, ground-covering gaits — especially an expressive trot and canter — together with a willing, rideable temperament. All solid colours occur, most commonly bay, chestnut, black, and grey.

Uses

Hanoverian horses have won medals at every Olympic Games for decades. Famous representatives include the dressage stallion Donnerhall and the show jumper Warwick Rex. The annual elite auctions at Verden are a major marketplace for the breed.
