Merman

A merman (plural mermen), sometimes called a merlad or merboy, is the male counterpart of the mermaid, a legendary creature that is human from the waist up and fish-like from the waist down. Mermen appear in folklore and mythology around the world, including in close association with the more familiar mermaids.

In mythology

Male water beings in older traditions include the Mesopotamian god Ea (Oannes), depicted as a fish-man, the Greek sea god Triton — son of Poseidon, who blew a conch shell trumpet — and the Irish merrow or murdúchann. In Irish folklore the merrow was said to be ugly, with green teeth and hair, while in Scottish and English tradition mermen could be wild, storm-raising figures who lured sailors to their deaths.

Depictions

The conventionally handsome merman of modern popular culture, often paired with mermaids in fantasy fiction, contrasts with older depictions of mermen as ugly or dangerous. The barnum "Fiji mermaid" hoax of the nineteenth century was actually a monkey's torso sewn to a fish tail, billed as a mermaid rather than a merman. Mermen feature in works such as Hans Christian Andersen's "The Little Mermaid" (whose sea king is a widower merman) and in contemporary fantasy and film.
