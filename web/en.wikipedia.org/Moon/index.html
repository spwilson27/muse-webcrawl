The Moon is Earth's only natural satellite. It orbits Earth at an average distance of 384,400 kilometres (238,900 mi), about 30 times Earth's diameter. Its orbital period is 27.3 days, matching its rotation period, so the same side always faces Earth (tidal locking). The Moon is the fifth-largest satellite in the Solar System and the largest and most massive relative to its parent planet, with a diameter about one-quarter that of Earth.

Formation

The leading explanation is the giant-impact hypothesis: about 4.5 billion years ago, a Mars-sized body named Theia collided with the proto-Earth, and the ejected debris accreted in orbit to form the Moon. Lunar samples returned by the Apollo missions show isotopic similarities to Earth's mantle that support this theory. The Moon is gradually receding from Earth at about 3.8 cm per year, as measured by laser ranging to retroreflectors left on its surface.

Surface

The lunar surface is divided into light-coloured highlands and dark basaltic plains called maria (Latin for "seas"), the remnants of ancient lava flows filling large impact basins. The surface is covered by regolith, a layer of pulverised rock produced by billions of years of impacts, and is marked by craters such as Tycho and Copernicus. Water ice is confirmed in permanently shadowed polar craters.

Effects on Earth

The Moon's gravity produces the ocean tides; the Moon also stabilises Earth's axial tilt, moderating the climate over geological timescales. The changing illumination of the sunlit portion of the Moon as seen from Earth produces the lunar phases, and alignments of the Sun, Earth, and Moon cause solar and lunar eclipses.

Exploration

The Soviet Luna 2 was the first spacecraft to reach the surface (1959), and Luna 9 made the first soft landing (1966). The United States' Apollo programme achieved six crewed landings between 1969 and 1972, beginning with Apollo 11's landing on 20 July 1969, when Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin walked on the surface. More recent missions include China's Chang'e sample-return missions and the planned Artemis programme to return astronauts to the Moon.
