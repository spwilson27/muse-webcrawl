Incest

Incest is human sexual activity between family members or close relatives. Nearly all human societies prohibit it in some form, making the incest taboo one of the most widespread of all cultural norms, though the exact range of prohibited relationships varies.

The incest taboo

Anthropologists have long debated the origins of the taboo. Explanations include the Westermarck effect — a natural aversion to mating with those one grew up with — genetic costs of inbreeding, and the social need, emphasised by Claude Lévi-Strauss, to build alliances between families through exogamy.

Law

Most jurisdictions criminalise sexual relations between specified close relatives, such as parents and children or siblings; the defined degrees of prohibited kinship differ from place to place. Sexual activity between an adult and a minor relative is also treated as child sexual abuse.

Genetic considerations

Children of closely related parents face an elevated risk of autosomal recessive disorders because both parents are more likely to carry the same harmful recessive alleles.
