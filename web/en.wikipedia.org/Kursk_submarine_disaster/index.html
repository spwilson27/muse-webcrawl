Kursk submarine disaster

The Kursk submarine disaster occurred on 12 August 2000, when the Russian nuclear-powered cruise missile submarine Kursk (K-141), an Oscar II-class vessel, sank in the Barents Sea during a naval exercise, killing all 118 personnel on board.

The sinking

An explosion in the torpedo compartment — caused by a leaking, faulty practice torpedo using high-test peroxide fuel — triggered a second, far larger detonation of torpedo warheads about two minutes later, recorded by seismic stations across northern Europe. The submarine sank to the seabed at a depth of about 108 metres. At least 23 crew members survived the initial explosions and gathered in the aft compartment, but died before rescue.

Rescue and aftermath

Russian rescue attempts failed, and foreign assistance (from Norway and the United Kingdom) was accepted only after several days; by then there were no survivors. The disaster drew criticism of the Russian navy's equipment and secrecy and of President Vladimir Putin's handling of the crisis. The wreck was later raised, and most of the crew's bodies were recovered.
