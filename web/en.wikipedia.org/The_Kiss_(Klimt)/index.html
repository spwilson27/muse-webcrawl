The Kiss (Klimt)

ArtistGustav Klimt
Year1907–1908
MediumOil and gold leaf on canvas
Dimensions180 cm × 180 cm
LocationBelvedere, Vienna

The Kiss (German: Der Kuss) is an oil-on-canvas painting with added gold leaf, silver, and platinum by the Austrian Symbolist painter Gustav Klimt. Painted in 1907–1908, at the height of Klimt's "Golden Phase", it depicts a couple embracing on a flowering meadow, their bodies entwined in ornate, gilded robes.

Description

The man, his robe patterned with black-and-white rectangles, bends to kiss the woman's cheek; she kneels with closed eyes, her dress covered in circular floral motifs. The composition draws on Art Nouveau, the Arts and Crafts movement, and Byzantine mosaics Klimt had seen in Ravenna.

Reception and legacy

Purchased by the Austrian state in 1908 before it was even finished, the painting hangs in the Belvedere in Vienna. It is widely regarded as Klimt's most popular work and as a national treasure of Austria, reproduced on coins, posters, and countless souvenirs.
