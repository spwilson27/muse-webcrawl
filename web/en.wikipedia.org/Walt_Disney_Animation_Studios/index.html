Walt Disney Animation Studios (WDAS), sometimes shortened to Disney Animation, is an American animation studio that creates animated features and short films for the Walt Disney Company. Founded on October 16, 1923, by brothers Walt and Roy O. Disney, it is the oldest-running animation studio in the world and the oldest division of the Walt Disney Company. The studio has produced more than 60 feature films, beginning with Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs in 1937, one of the first full-length animated features and the first produced in the United States.

History

Early years and the Golden Age

After the success of Mickey Mouse, introduced in Steamboat Willie (1928), the studio developed the Silly Symphonies series and pioneered techniques including the multiplane camera, Technicolor animation, and storyboarding. The Golden Age produced Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs (1937), Pinocchio and Fantasia (1940), Dumbo (1941), and Bambi (1942). Wartime and postwar periods followed, with productions increasingly shaped by package films and, from Cinderella (1950), a return to feature fairy tales such as Sleeping Beauty (1959) and One Hundred and One Dalmatians (1961).

After Walt Disney

Following Walt Disney's death in 1966, the studio passed through a leaner Silver and Bronze Age, with films such as The Jungle Book (1967) and The Rescuers (1977), and the darker experimental period of the early 1980s. The Disney Renaissance (1989–1999) restored the studio's pre-eminence with musical features The Little Mermaid, Beauty and the Beast, Aladdin, and The Lion King.

Computer animation era

In the 2000s the studio transitioned from traditional hand-drawn animation to computer animation, with Chicken Little (2005) its first fully CGI feature, and enjoyed a revival from Tangled (2010) through Frozen (2013), Zootopia (2016), Moana (2016), and Encanto (2021). The studio's films are produced at its Burbank headquarters, in the Roy E. Disney Animation Building opened in 1995, recognizable by its giant Sorcerer's Hat.

Legacy

The studio's logo sequence, its "Disney Vault" release strategy, and the Walt Disney Animation Studios film canon are fixtures of popular culture. Its artists—including the "Nine Old Men"—defined much of the craft grammar of character animation.
