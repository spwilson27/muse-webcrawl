Viroid

Viroids are small single-stranded, circular RNAs that are infectious pathogens of plants. Unlike viruses, they lack a protein coat and code for no proteins at all; the viroid RNA itself is the entire infectious agent. Viroids are the smallest known infectious agents, with genomes of only about 246–434 nucleotides.

Discovery and structure

The first viroid, the potato spindle tuber viroid, was characterised in 1971 by Theodor O. Diener, who showed that the agent causing potato spindle tuber disease was a free RNA molecule far smaller than any virus. The RNA folds into a compact rod-like or quasi-rod structure with extensive internal base pairing, which protects it from degradation and allows it to interact with host enzymes.

Replication and disease

Viroids replicate in the nucleus or chloroplasts of plant cells using host RNA polymerases via a rolling-circle mechanism, with some viroids using a hammerhead ribozyme to cleave the multimeric RNA copies. They cause economically important diseases of potato, tomato, cucumber, citrus, coconut (cadang-cadang) and avocado (sunblotch). Transmission is mainly mechanical, by tools, seeds or pollen. Control relies on certification of clean planting stock and sanitation.
