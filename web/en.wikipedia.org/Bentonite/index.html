Bentonite

Bentonite is an absorbent swelling clay composed mostly of montmorillonite, formed from the weathering of volcanic ash in seawater. Named after Fort Benton, Wyoming, it is mined extensively in the United States, Greece, India and elsewhere.

Properties

Sodium bentonite can absorb many times its dry mass in water and swells dramatically, forming gels and seals; calcium bentonite swells less but can be converted by ion exchange. The clay is strongly thixotropic and has a high cation-exchange capacity.

Uses

Bentonite is used in drilling muds, as a binder for foundry sands and iron-ore pellets, in cat litter, as a clarifying agent for wine and oils, in pond and landfill liners, and in cosmetics and pharmaceuticals.
