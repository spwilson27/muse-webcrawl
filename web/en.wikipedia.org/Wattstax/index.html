DocumentaryWattstax (1973)

Wattstax was a benefit concert organized by Memphis-based Stax Records to commemorate the seventh anniversary of the 1965 Watts riots. It was held on 20 August 1972 at the Los Angeles Memorial Coliseum and drew a crowd of more than 100,000 people, with tickets priced at just one dollar so that the local Black community could afford to attend. The concert is often described as the "Black Woodstock."

The concert

The all-day event featured the cream of the Stax roster, including Isaac Hayes, Rufus Thomas, Carla Thomas, the Staple Singers, Albert King, the Bar-Kays, William Bell, Eddie Floyd, Johnnie Taylor and Luther Ingram. The Reverend Jesse Jackson served as an unofficial master of ceremonies, leading the crowd in his "I Am – Somebody" chant. Rufus Thomas famously jumped into the crowd, and Isaac Hayes closed the show headlining in his gold-chain regalia.

Film and albums

The concert was filmed and released in 1973 as the documentary Wattstax, directed by Mel Stuart. Because of licensing issues, some Los Angeles footage was supplemented with performances staged later and with interludes featuring comedian Richard Pryor, whose monologues — filmed in a barbershop set — became a celebrated part of the film. Stax also issued live albums drawn from the event. The film was added to the United States National Film Registry in 2020 for its cultural and historical significance.

Legacy

Wattstax is remembered as a landmark celebration of African-American music and community pride in the post-civil-rights era, and as Stax Records' most ambitious undertaking before the label's collapse in 1975.
