Pad thai

CourseMain course
Place of originThailand
Main ingredientsRice noodles, eggs, tofu, shrimp or chicken, peanuts, bean sprouts, tamarind
ServingHot, with lime wedge and crushed peanuts

Pad thai (often written phat thai) is a stir-fried rice noodle dish commonly served as street food and in Thai restaurants worldwide. It is generally considered Thailand's national dish and is known for its balance of sweet, sour, salty, and savoury flavours.

Ingredients and preparation

The dish consists of soaked dried rice noodles stir-fried with eggs and chopped firm tofu, flavoured with a sauce of tamarind paste, fish sauce, and palm sugar. It commonly includes shrimp or chicken, crushed roasted peanuts, bean sprouts, garlic chives, and roasted red chilli, and is served with a lime wedge and sometimes a banana leaf. Some versions are wrapped in a thin omelette.

History

Although stir-fried noodles have older roots in Chinese-influenced cooking in the region, pad thai in its modern form was popularised in the 1930s and 1940s under Prime Minister Plaek Phibunsongkhram, whose nation-building campaigns promoted noodle consumption — partly to reduce rice consumption during wartime shortages and to foster a distinct Thai national identity. The dish spread internationally with the Thai diaspora and government-backed restaurant promotion in the 2000s.

Variations

Regional and modern variations include vegetarian versions using soy sauce instead of fish sauce, versions with pork or crab, and sweeter Bangkok-style preparations with a reddish colour from chilli paste. In 2017 pad thai appeared on CNN's list of the world's 50 best foods, voted by readers.
