Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Areas Act 1979

The Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Areas Act 1979 is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that provides legal protection for scheduled monuments and designated areas of archaeological importance in England, Scotland, and Wales. It consolidated earlier ancient monuments legislation going back to 1882 and remains the principal statute protecting scheduled archaeological sites.

Provisions

Under the Act, the Secretary of State maintains a Schedule of monuments of national importance. Carrying out works that damage, alter, or demolish a scheduled monument without Scheduled Monument Consent is a criminal offence. The Act also provides for guardianship monuments, for areas of archaeological importance (designated in Canterbury, Chester, Exeter, Hereford, and York) in which developers must allow archaeological investigation before construction, and for controls on metal detecting at protected sites.

Administration

The Act is administered in England by Historic England, in Scotland by Historic Environment Scotland, and in Wales by Cadw. Scheduling protects archaeological remains regardless of visibility, and complements the separate system of listing historic buildings under planning legislation.
