Underground Resistance

Underground Resistance (often abbreviated UR) is a musical collective and record label from Detroit, Michigan, founded in 1989 by "Mad" Mike Banks, Jeff Mills, and Robert Hood. It is one of the most influential acts in techno music.

UR presented itself in paramilitary terms as a "resistance" against commercial, mainstream entertainment, championing self-determination for Detroit's Black community and anonymity for its members, who often performed masked and gave few interviews. Early releases such as "Sonic Destroyer" and the work of Mills and Hood forged a harder, faster, more militant strain of Detroit techno; the group's jazz-inflected Galaxy 2 Galaxy project (A High Tech Jazz Album, 1993) and the Drexciya project of James Stinson and Gerald Donald expanded its mythology and sound.

Mills and Hood left in the early 1990s for solo careers, while Banks has continued to lead Underground Resistance from Detroit, releasing music, touring with live line-ups, and running the associated Submerge distribution and record shop.
