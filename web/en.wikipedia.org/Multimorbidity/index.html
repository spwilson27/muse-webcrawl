Multimorbidity

FieldMedicine, epidemiology
DefinitionCoexistence of two or more long-term conditions

Multimorbidity is the coexistence of two or more chronic conditions in the same person, where no single condition is regarded as the index disease. It becomes increasingly common with age and is associated with reduced quality of life, higher healthcare use, polypharmacy and increased mortality.

Distinction from comorbidity

Comorbidity describes additional conditions in relation to one primary or index condition, whereas multimorbidity treats all conditions equally. Multimorbidity poses challenges for clinical guidelines, which are usually written for single diseases and may recommend conflicting treatments.

Management

Recommended approaches emphasise patient priorities, medication review, coordinated care and reducing treatment burden rather than applying multiple disease-specific targets mechanically.
