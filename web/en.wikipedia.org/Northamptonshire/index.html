Northamptonshire (abbreviated Northants) is a ceremonial county in the East Midlands of England. It is bordered by Warwickshire, Leicestershire, Rutland, Lincolnshire, Cambridgeshire, Bedfordshire, Buckinghamshire, and Oxfordshire — its border with Lincolnshire, at just 19 metres (20 yards), is among the shortest county boundaries in England. The county town is Northampton.

Geography and history

The county is drained by the River Nene, which rises near Daventry and flows northeast to the Wash. Ironstone hills in the north and west supported Roman settlement and later an iron and steel industry around Corby. Northampton grew as a centre of shoemaking, an industry that defined the county town for centuries and still survives in high-end footwear firms. The Battle of Naseby, the decisive engagement of the English Civil War, was fought in the county in 1645.

Local government

In 2021 the former county council, which had effectively collapsed financially in 2018, was replaced by two unitary authorities: North Northamptonshire (covering Corby, Kettering, Wellingborough, and East Northamptonshire) and West Northamptonshire (Northampton, Daventry, and South Northamptonshire).

Landmarks

Attractions include Althorp, the Spencer family home and resting place of Diana, Princess of Wales; Silverstone Circuit, home of the British Grand Prix; Rockingham Castle; and numerous country houses that gave the county its reputation as a land of "squires and spires."
