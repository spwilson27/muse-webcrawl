College of Cardinals

The College of Cardinals, more formally the Sacred College of Cardinals, is the body of all cardinals of the Catholic Church. Cardinals are appointed by the pope and typically hold a senior position within the Church, such as governing a major archdiocese or leading a department of the Roman Curia. Members under the age of 80 are eligible to participate in a papal conclave to elect a new pope.

Cardinals gathered in St. Peter's Basilica.

History

The college grew out of the clergy of Rome: the cardinal priests of the Roman parishes, the cardinal deacons who administered the Church's charity districts, and the cardinal bishops of the suburban sees around Rome. By the eleventh century the college had become the pope's principal advisory body. The decree In Nomine Domini of 1059 restricted the election of the pope principally to the cardinal bishops, and in 1179 the Third Lateran Council extended the franchise to all cardinals equally, requiring a two-thirds majority.

For centuries the number of cardinals was small. Sixtus V fixed the membership at 70 in 1586. In the twentieth century the college was internationalised, and Paul VI set the number of electors at 120. The maximum size of the whole college has since grown well beyond 200, and its membership is now drawn from every continent.

Organisation

The college is headed by a Dean, elected from among the cardinal bishops and approved by the pope, and a Vice-Dean. The Camerlengo of the Holy Roman Church administers the temporal goods of the Holy See during a vacancy of the papal office. Cardinals are divided into three orders — bishops, priests and deacons — a ranking that is now largely honorary, since most cardinal priests and deacons are in fact bishops.

Conclave

When the Apostolic See becomes vacant, the cardinals gather in Rome for general congregations and then enter the conclave in the Sistine Chapel. Under rules consolidated by John Paul II in Universi Dominici Gregis (1996), a two-thirds majority is required for election. Only cardinals who have not reached their eightieth birthday before the vacancy begins may vote.
