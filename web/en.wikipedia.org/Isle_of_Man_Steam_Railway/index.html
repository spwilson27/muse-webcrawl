Isle of Man Steam Railway

The line is the longest narrow gauge steam line in Britain still using its original locomotives and carriages.

The Isle of Man Steam Railway (Manx: Raad-Yiarn Smuide Ellan Vannin) is a narrow gauge steam-operated railway on the Isle of Man in the Irish Sea. It is the remainder of a once extensive network built to the island's distinctive 3 ft (914 mm) gauge, and runs for about 15.3 miles (24.6 km) between Douglas and Port Erin.

History

The Douglas to Peel and Douglas to Port Erin lines opened in 1873 and 1874 respectively, built by the Isle of Man Railway Company. A third line to Ramsey followed under a separate company. At its peak the system carried holiday traffic across the island. Closures in the 1960s left only the southern line, which was taken into public ownership when the island's government bought the railway in 1978.

Operation

Trains are hauled by original Beyer, Peacock and Company 2-4-0T locomotives dating from 1873 onward, with historic wooden-bodied carriages. The railway operates as a heritage and commuter attraction under Isle of Man Transport, with stations including Castletown, Ballasalla, Santon and Port Erin, where a railway museum is located.
