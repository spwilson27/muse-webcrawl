LGBT rights movement

The LGBT rights movement (also gay rights movement) is a civil rights movement advocating for the legal and social equality of lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender and queer people. Its goals have included decriminalisation of homosexuality, anti-discrimination protections, recognition of same-sex relationships and marriage, adoption rights, and protections for gender identity and expression.

History

Early organising dates to the late 19th century, including Magnus Hirschfeld's Scientific-Humanitarian Committee in Germany (1897). In the United States, mid-20th-century "homophile" groups such as the Mattachine Society and the Daughters of Bilitis preceded the more confrontational gay liberation movement catalysed by the 1969 Stonewall riots.

Milestones

Landmarks include the removal of homosexuality from the DSM in 1973, the Netherlands becoming the first country to legalise same-sex marriage in 2001, and court decisions such as Obergefell v. Hodges (2015) in the United States. Progress remains uneven: same-sex relations remain criminalised in dozens of countries.
