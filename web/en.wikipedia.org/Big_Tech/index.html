Big Tech

Big Tech, also known as the Tech Giants or similar terms, is a collective term for the largest and most influential technology companies in the world. The label most often refers to five American firms — Alphabet (Google), Amazon, Apple, Meta (Facebook), and Microsoft — which dominate online services, software, consumer electronics, and cloud computing.

Terminology

Various acronyms have been used over time, including GAFA, GAFAM, FAANG (which instead included Netflix), and "the Magnificent Seven" in stock-market commentary. Comparable groupings exist elsewhere, such as BAT (Baidu, Alibaba, Tencent) in China.

Areas of dominance

The companies lead markets including web search, social networking, e-commerce, mobile operating systems, digital advertising, and cloud infrastructure. Their scale gives them extensive data resources and network effects, and several have reached market capitalisations above one trillion dollars.

Criticism and regulation

Big Tech firms face scrutiny over market power, privacy practices, taxation, content moderation, and labour conditions. Regulators in the European Union have enacted measures such as the Digital Markets Act and Digital Services Act, and antitrust cases have been brought in the United States and elsewhere. Defenders argue the firms deliver consumer benefits and compete vigorously with one another.
