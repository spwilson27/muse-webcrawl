Józef Klemens Piłsudski (5 December 1867 – 12 May 1935) was a Polish statesman who served as Chief of State (1918–1922) and Marshal of Poland. Widely regarded as the chief architect of the Second Polish Republic, he led Poland to independence in 1918 after more than a century of partitions, and from the May Coup of 1926 until his death in 1935 he dominated Polish politics as the power behind the Sanation regime.

Early life and revolutionary activity

Piłsudski was born at Zułów in the Vilnius region, then in the Russian Empire, into a Polish-Lithuanian noble family. Expelled from medical studies at Kharkov for political activity, he was exiled to Siberia for five years (1887–1892) for alleged involvement in a plot against Tsar Alexander III. Returning to Vilnius, he became a leader of the Polish Socialist Party (PPS), edited the underground newspaper Robotnik, organised the party's fighting squads, and in 1908 carried out the famous Bezdany train robbery to fund the movement.

Legions and independence

Before 1914 Piłsudski built Polish paramilitary Riflemen's Associations in Austrian Galicia; on the outbreak of the First World War these became the Polish Legions fighting alongside Austria-Hungary against Russia. Refusing in 1917 to swear loyalty to the Central Powers, he was imprisoned by the Germans at Magdeburg — which confirmed his patriotic credentials. Released in November 1918, he took power in Warsaw and became Chief of State of reborn Poland on 14 November 1918.

Polish–Soviet War and later rule

As commander-in-chief in the Polish–Soviet War (1919–1921), Piłsudski pursued his Międzymorze (Intermarium) federalist vision and halted the Red Army at the Battle of Warsaw in August 1920 — the "Miracle on the Vistula". He retired from active politics in 1923 after the assassination of President Narutowicz, but dismayed by parliamentary instability he seized power in the May Coup of 1926. Thereafter, through the Sanation ("healing") regime, he governed authoritarian-ly behind formally democratic institutions, serving as Minister of Military Affairs throughout and twice as prime minister. He died of liver cancer at the Belweder Palace on 12 May 1935.

Legacy

Piłsudski's heart was buried in his mother's grave in Vilnius's Rasos Cemetery, and his body in the crypt of Wawel Cathedral in Kraków. His legacy in Poland is contested: honoured as the father of independence, he is also criticised for the 1926 coup and the authoritarianism of the Sanation era.
