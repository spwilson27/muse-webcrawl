Vladivostok (Russian: Владивосто́к, lit. 'Ruler of the East') is the largest city and the administrative centre of Primorsky Krai and the capital of the Far Eastern Federal District of Russia, located around the Golden Horn Bay on the Sea of Japan, near the borders with China and North Korea. It is the largest Russian port on the Pacific Ocean, the home port of the Russian Pacific Fleet, and the eastern terminus of the Trans-Siberian Railway.

History

The territory was ceded by Qing China to the Russian Empire under the Convention of Peking (1860), and a military outpost was founded the same year and named Vladivostok. It became a free port in 1862 and grew after the completion of the Ussuri Railway and, in 1903, the Trans-Siberian link to Moscow — at 9,289 km, the world's longest railway line. During the Russian Civil War the city was occupied at various times by Allied, including American and Japanese, forces, the last major stronghold of the White movement until 1922.

Because of its naval significance, Vladivostok was a closed city to foreigners during most of the Soviet era, reopening in 1992. For the 2012 APEC summit, held on Russky Island, the Russian government built the Russky Bridge — one of the world's longest cable-stayed bridges — and the new campus of Far Eastern Federal University. In 2018 Vladivostok replaced Khabarovsk as capital of the Far Eastern Federal District.

Geography and climate

The city sprawls over the hills of the Muravyov-Amursky Peninsula, which divides Amur Bay from Ussuri Bay. Its climate is a monsoon-influenced humid continental climate: cold, dry, sunny winters dominated by Siberian air masses (January average around −12 °C) and warm, humid, foggy summers with an August average near 20 °C, when most of the roughly 840 mm of annual precipitation falls.

Economy and transport

Fishing, shipping, ship repair and the naval base underpin the economy; the commercial port handles containers, vehicles and bulk cargo, and the city is a major importer of Japanese and Korean used cars. Vladivostok International Airport and the Trans-Siberian passenger trains — the Moscow journey takes about six days — link the city across Russia, while ferries connect it to South Korea and Japan.
