The Dhegiha languages are a group of Siouan languages spoken historically along the central Mississippi and Missouri river valleys of North America. The group comprises Omaha–Ponca, Kansa (Kaw), Osage, and Quapaw (Arkansas), with Ponca often treated as a dialect of Omaha.

Historical distribution of the Dhegiha languages.

Classification

Dhegiha belongs to the Mississippi Valley branch of the Siouan family. Linguistic and oral-historical evidence suggests the Dhegiha peoples migrated westward from the Ohio Valley region, splitting into separate nations as they settled: the Omaha and Ponca in present-day Nebraska, the Kansa in Kansas, the Osage in Missouri and Oklahoma, and the Quapaw further south along the Mississippi in Arkansas.

Features

The Dhegiha languages are closely related and share characteristic sound correspondences that distinguish them from other Siouan languages. They preserve a rich verb morphology typical of the family, including instrumental prefixes and positional auxiliaries. All Dhegiha languages are now endangered or undergoing revitalisation, with tribal language programmes working to document and teach them.
