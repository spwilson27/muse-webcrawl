Melanie Klein (née Reizes; 30 March 1882 – 22 September 1960) was an Austrian-British psychoanalyst known for her work in child analysis. She was a pioneer of object relations theory and developed the "play technique" for analysing young children, work that extended psychoanalytic theory to the earliest months of life and profoundly influenced the development of psychotherapy.

Life

Born in Vienna, Klein was analysed by Sándor Ferenczi in Budapest, who encouraged her interest in analysing children. In 1921 she joined the Berlin Psychoanalytic Society and worked with Karl Abraham. Invited to lecture in London in 1925, she settled there in 1926 and became a central, controversial figure in the British Psychoanalytical Society, notably during the "Controversial Discussions" of the 1940s with Anna Freud's followers.

Theory

Klein proposed that infants in the first months of life pass through the paranoid-schizoid position, characterised by splitting of the object into "good" and "bad," and then the depressive position, in which the infant recognises the mother as a whole object and feels guilt and a wish to make reparation. She placed unconscious phantasy at the centre of mental life from birth and emphasised envy, projection and projective identification.

Legacy

Her ideas were developed by followers including Wilfred Bion and Donald Winnicott (initially) and remain influential in psychoanalytic and psychotherapeutic practice worldwide.
