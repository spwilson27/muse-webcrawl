The Council of Ephesus was a council of Christian bishops convened in Ephesus (near present-day Selçuk in Turkey) in AD 431 by the Roman Emperor Theodosius II. It was the third ecumenical council and was called to resolve the Christological controversy between Nestorius, Patriarch of Constantinople, and Cyril of Alexandria.

Proceedings

Nestorius taught that Mary should be called Christotokos ("Christ-bearer") rather than Theotokos ("God-bearer"), reflecting his view of Christ's two natures. Cyril's council, meeting before the Antiochene bishops arrived, condemned Nestorius and affirmed the title Theotokos. Rival sessions deposed both men before imperial intervention; in the end Nestorius was exiled and Cyril's position prevailed. The Formula of Reunion (433) partially reconciled the Alexandrian and Antiochene parties.

Legacy

The council's condemnation of Nestorianism contributed to a lasting schism with the Church of the East. Its affirmation of Theotokos became a defining point of Marian doctrine in the Catholic and Orthodox churches.
