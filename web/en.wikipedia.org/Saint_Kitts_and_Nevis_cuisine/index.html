Saint Kitts and Nevis cuisine

Saint Kitts and Nevis cuisine is the food culture of the twin-island Caribbean nation of Saint Kitts and Nevis. It blends West African, Creole, British and broader Caribbean influences, built around locally available seafood, tropical produce and slow-cooked stews.

National dish

The national dish is stewed saltfish with spicy plantains, coconut dumplings and seasoned breadfruit. Saltfish — salted cod preserved for storage in the pre-refrigeration era — is soaked, flaked and stewed in a sauce of tomatoes, onions, peppers and herbs.

Common dishes

Goat water is a rich stew of goat meat, breadfruit, green papaya and dumplings in a tomato-based broth, traditionally served at weddings and celebrations. Cook-up (pelau) combines rice, pigeon peas, coconut milk and meat in one pot. Conch chowder, bullfoot soup, black pudding and johnny cakes are also widely eaten. A popular breakfast is saltfish with spiced breadfruit and boiled eggs.

Ingredients and produce

Breadfruit, plantain, yam, sweet potato, tannia and dasheen are staple starches. Mangoes, soursop, guava, passion fruit and golden apples feature in juices and desserts. Seafood includes conch, lobster, snapper and mahi-mahi.

Drinks

Ting, a grapefruit soda from Jamaica, is often mixed with local rum. Mauby, brewed from tree bark, sorrel drink and ginger beer are traditional non-alcoholic options. The islands' rum heritage dates to the sugar plantation era; Brinley Gold rum is produced on Saint Kitts.
