The Airbus A321XLR (Extra Long Range) is a long-range variant of the A321neo single-aisle airliner developed by Airbus. Launched at the Paris Air Show in June 2019, it offers a range of up to 4,700 nautical miles (8,700 km) — the longest of any single-aisle airliner — enabling airlines to operate thinner transatlantic and other long-haul routes economically with a narrow-body aircraft.

Development

The XLR adds a permanent rear centre fuel tank, an optional forward additional tank, a strengthened landing gear, and a higher maximum takeoff weight of 101 tonnes, while burning a claimed 30% less fuel per seat than previous-generation aircraft on comparable routes. Its first flight took place on 15 June 2022 from Hamburg. After certification delays centered on fire protection for the rear centre tank, the type received EASA certification in July 2024 and entered service with Iberia in November 2024, initially on European routes and then on Madrid–Boston services.

Market

Orders came from airlines including American Airlines, United, IndiGo, Qantas, Air Canada, and Aer Lingus. The aircraft is seen as filling the "middle of the market" gap left by the Boeing 757 and 767, and its economics pressure Boeing, which has no direct new competitor in production.
