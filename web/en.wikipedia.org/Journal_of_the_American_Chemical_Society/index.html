Journal of the American Chemical Society

The Journal of the American Chemical Society (commonly abbreviated JACS) is a peer-reviewed scientific journal published weekly by the American Chemical Society. Founded in 1879, it is one of the most prestigious and widely cited journals in chemistry, publishing original research across all fields of the discipline, including organic, inorganic, physical, analytical, and biological chemistry.

History

The journal was established in 1879, three years after the founding of the American Chemical Society itself. In 1893 it absorbed the Review of American Chemical Research, and in the following decade it developed its distinctive format of full articles and short communications. Under long-serving editors such as William A. Noyes and later Arthur C. Cope, JACS grew into the flagship journal of the society. The companion journal JACS Au, a fully open-access title, was launched in 2021.

Scope and article types

JACS publishes Communications (short reports of urgent, novel results), Articles (full-length research papers), and Perspectives and reviews. Papers undergo rigorous peer review, and the journal is known for a high rejection rate and for publishing landmark work, including many papers by Nobel laureates. Its impact factor has consistently ranked among the highest for general chemistry journals.

Abstracting and indexing

The journal is abstracted and indexed in Chemical Abstracts Service, Scopus, the Science Citation Index, PubMed, and other major databases.
