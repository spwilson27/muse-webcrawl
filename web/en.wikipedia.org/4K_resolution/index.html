4K resolution

4K resolution refers to a horizontal display resolution of approximately 4,000 pixels. Several different resolutions are marketed as 4K, principally the television standard 3840 × 2160 (also called 4K UHD, with a 16:9 aspect ratio and four times the pixel count of 1080p Full HD) and the digital cinema standard 4096 × 2160 (DCI 4K).

History

The term originated in digital cinema, where the Digital Cinema Initiatives consortium standardised a 4096 × 2160 container in the mid-2000s. For consumer electronics, the industry adopted 3840 × 2160 — exactly double the 1920 × 1080 Full HD standard in each dimension — and promoted it first as "4K" and later under the label Ultra HD, formally defined by the Consumer Electronics Association in 2012. The first consumer 4K televisions and cameras appeared in the early 2010s, and by the late 2010s 4K had become the standard for new television sets, streaming services, and games consoles.

Standards and formats

Broadcast and streaming 4K is governed by the ITU-R Rec. 2020 colour space and the Ultra HD Blu-ray format, and is typically accompanied by high dynamic range (HDR) formats such as HDR10 and Dolby Vision, which many viewers perceive as a larger improvement than the resolution increase alone. Successor resolutions include 5K, used in some monitors, and 8K UHD (7680 × 4320).
