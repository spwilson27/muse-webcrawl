The Spain national football team (Spanish: Selección Española de Fútbol) has represented Spain in men's international football competition since 1920. It is governed by the Royal Spanish Football Federation, the governing body for football in Spain. Spain is one of the most successful national teams in the world, having won the FIFA World Cup in 2010 and the UEFA European Championship in 1964, 2008, 2012 and 2024.

History

Spain made its debut at the 1920 Olympic Games in Antwerp, winning silver. The team won its first major title at Euro 1964 on home soil, defeating the Soviet Union in the final. Long regarded as underachievers at major tournaments, Spain entered a golden age under Luis Aragonés and Vicente del Bosque, winning Euro 2008, the 2010 World Cup in South Africa — defeating the Netherlands 1–0 in the final through an extra-time goal by Andrés Iniesta — and Euro 2012, becoming the first team to win three consecutive major tournaments.

Style of play

Spain's golden generation was famed for tiki-taka, a style based on short passing, movement and domination of possession, built around midfielders such as Xavi, Andrés Iniesta and Sergio Busquets. Under coach Luis de la Fuente, Spain won Euro 2024 in Germany, defeating England 2–1 in the final, and had earlier won the 2023 UEFA Nations League.

Records

Sergio Ramos is Spain's most capped player, while David Villa is the team's all-time top scorer. Spain held the joint record of 35 consecutive matches unbeaten between 2007 and 2009 and spent several years ranked first in the FIFA World Rankings.
