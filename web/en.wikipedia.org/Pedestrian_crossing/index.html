Pedestrian crossing

A pedestrian crossing (or crosswalk in American and Canadian English) is a place designated for pedestrians to cross a road, street or avenue. The term is also used in some treaties for facilities for cyclists. Crossings may be marked with painted stripes, controlled by traffic signals, or raised, and are intended to concentrate pedestrian crossings at safer locations.

Pedestrian crossing
Types: Zebra, pelican, puffin, toucan, signalized, uncontrolled

Types

The zebra crossing, with longitudinal white stripes, is internationally familiar. In the United Kingdom a family of signal-controlled crossings—pelican, puffin and toucan (the last shared with cyclists)—uses lights and sensors. North American crosswalks commonly use transverse "continental" markings and pedestrian signals.

Safety

Markings alone do not guarantee safety: signalization, lighting, refuges and raised tables improve outcomes, while crossing design remains a major topic in traffic engineering and Vision Zero policies.

History

Stepping-stone crossings existed in Roman streets such as Pompeii's. Modern marked crossings spread in the mid-20th century; the zebra crossing was introduced in Britain in 1951.
