Arkansas River

Length: 1,469 mi (2,364 km)
Source: near Leadville, Colorado
Mouth: Mississippi River, near Napoleon, Arkansas
Basin size: about 168,000 sq mi
States: Colorado, Kansas, Oklahoma, Arkansas

The Arkansas River is a major tributary of the Mississippi River, flowing generally east and southeast through Colorado, Kansas, Oklahoma, and Arkansas in the United States. At 1,469 miles (2,364 km) it is the sixth-longest river in the United States and the second-longest tributary of the Mississippi–Missouri system after the Missouri River itself.

Course

The river rises in the Sawatch Range of the Rocky Mountains near Leadville, Colorado, fed by snowmelt. It descends through the Royal Gorge and Bighorn Sheep Canyon, exits the mountains at Cañon City and Pueblo, and crosses the Great Plains through Garden City and Dodge City in Kansas, Wichita, and Tulsa in Oklahoma, entering Arkansas past Fort Smith to Little Rock and its confluence with the Mississippi near the historic town site of Napoleon.

History

Coronado's expedition crossed the river in 1541, and French traders gave it the name of the Arkansas (Quapaw) people living near its mouth. In the nineteenth century the upper Arkansas formed part of the boundary between the United States and Spanish, then Mexican, territory, and the Santa Fe Trail followed its valley across Kansas. Zebulon Pike's 1806 expedition explored its headwaters.

Navigation and water use

The McClellan–Kerr Arkansas River Navigation System, completed in 1971, channels the lower 445 miles with a series of locks and dams, allowing barge traffic from the Mississippi to the Port of Catoosa near Tulsa — the most inland ice-free port in the United States. The river is heavily used for irrigation on the plains, where groundwater pumping and upstream diversions have left some Kansas reaches dry for much of the year, long a subject of interstate litigation between Kansas and Colorado.

Recreation

The Colorado headwaters support famous whitewater rafting through Browns Canyon National Monument and the Royal Gorge, and the river below Leadville is a noted trout fishery.
