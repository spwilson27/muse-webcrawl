Malayalam cinema, also known by the portmanteau Mollywood, is the segment of Indian cinema dedicated to films in the Malayalam language, produced mainly in the state of Kerala. It is one of the four major South Indian film industries, alongside Tamil, Telugu, and Kannada cinema, and is especially noted for strong screenwriting, realistic themes, and critically acclaimed "parallel cinema".

History

The industry began with J. C. Daniel's silent film Vigathakumaran (1928), now lost, and its first talkie, Balan (1938). The 1950s and 1960s brought landmark works such as Newspaper Boy (1955) and Ramu Kariat's Chemmeen (1965), the first Malayalam film to win the National Film Award for Best Feature Film. From the 1970s a new wave of auteurs — Adoor Gopalakrishnan, G. Aravindan, John Abraham — won international festival recognition, while popular stars such as Prem Nazir, and later Mohanlal and Mammootty, dominated commercial cinema. Malayalam industry figures such as cinematographer–director Shaji N. Karun (Piravi, 1988) won prizes at Cannes.

Characteristics

Working with budgets far smaller than Bollywood's, Malayalam films typically emphasise story, performance, and social realism, and draw heavily on Kerala's rich literary tradition. Since the 2010s, a "new generation" movement — with films like Drishyam (2013), Premam (2015), and Manjummel Boys (2024) — has brought the industry renewed pan-Indian and streaming success, and many Malayalam hits are remade in other languages.

Awards and recognition

Malayalam cinema has won numerous National Film Awards, including multiple Best Actor awards for Mohanlal and Mammootty, and Kerala State Film Awards are presented annually by the state government. The International Film Festival of Kerala (IFFK), held in Thiruvananthapuram, is one of India's leading film festivals.
