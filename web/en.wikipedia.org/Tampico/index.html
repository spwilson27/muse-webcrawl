Tampico

Tampico is a city and port in the southeastern part of the state of Tamaulipas, Mexico, on the north bank of the Pánuco River near its mouth on the Gulf of Mexico. Together with Ciudad Madero and Altamira it forms a metropolitan area of roughly 900,000 people. It is one of Mexico's oldest and most important oil ports.

History

The site was settled by Huastec peoples and visited by Spanish expeditions from 1522; a permanent Spanish town was founded in the 16th century but suffered pirate attacks, including sacks by Dutch and French buccaneers. The modern city was refounded in 1823 and grew rapidly after the Mexican oil boom began around 1901, when foreign companies developed the Ébano and Pánuco fields. During the early 20th century Tampico was among the world's busiest oil-exporting ports, with a large foreign community and grand commercial architecture in its historic centre.

In April 1914, the arrest of American sailors in Tampico — the Tampico Affair — became a pretext for the United States occupation of Veracruz during the Mexican Revolution.

Economy

Oil refining and petrochemicals, centred on the Francisco I. Madero refinery, shipping, fishing, and shrimp processing dominate the economy. The port handles petroleum products, metals, and agricultural goods. Nearby Altamira hosts a deep-water industrial port complex.

Geography and culture

Tampico has a tropical savanna climate, hot and humid, and is exposed to Gulf hurricanes. The city is known for its seafood cuisine, its historic customs house, and El Carpintero lagoon, famous for its urban population of Morelet's crocodiles. It is the namesake of the Tampico fiber (ixtle) once exported through the port.
