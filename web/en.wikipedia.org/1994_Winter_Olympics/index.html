The 1994 Winter Olympics, officially the XVII Olympic Winter Games, were held from 12 to 27 February 1994 in Lillehammer, Norway. They were the first Winter Games to be staged in a different year from the Summer Olympics, following the IOC's decision to alternate the two events on a two-year cycle, and the only Winter Games held two years after the previous edition (Albertville 1992). They are widely regarded among the most successful Winter Olympics ever staged.

Highlights

Norwegian speed skater Johann Olav Koss became the hero of the Games, winning three gold medals, each in world-record time, and donating his winnings to Olympic Aid for Sarajevo, the besieged former Olympic host city. The Games had a strongly environmental profile, and for the first time professional figure skaters were allowed to reinstate amateur status: the ladies' singles, won by Ukraine's Oksana Baiul over Nancy Kerrigan, drew enormous attention after the January 1994 attack on Kerrigan connected to rival Tonya Harding — the event set U.S. television records.

Norway topped the medal table by golds on home ice, speed skater Bonnie Blair won two more golds for the United States, and Italy won the prestigious men's cross-country skiing relay over Norway in front of a stunned home crowd. Sweden won the ice hockey tournament, defeating Canada in the first Olympic shootout in the final. Russia, competing as an independent nation for the first time at a Winter Games, finished with the most medals overall, 23.
