The University of Zurich (UZH, German: Universität Zürich) is a public research university in the city of Zurich, Switzerland. Founded in 1833 by the cantonal government as the first university in Europe established by a democratic state rather than by a monarch or the church, it is the largest university in Switzerland, with some 28,000 students.

The main building of the University of Zurich, designed by Karl Moser.

History

The university was founded in 1833 from the merger of existing colleges of theology, law and medicine with a new faculty of philosophy. It was among the first universities in Europe to admit women, accepting its first female students in the 1860s; in 1867 Nadezhda Suslova became the first woman in Europe to receive a medical degree there. Albert Einstein studied and taught in Zurich (though his doctorate was from the university in 1905 while he worked at the patent office in Bern), and Erwin Schrödinger held a chair at Zurich when he formulated wave mechanics. The university counts numerous Nobel laureates among its alumni and faculty, including Wilhelm Röntgen, Theodor Mommsen, Alfred Werner and Kurt Wüthrich.

Faculties

UZH comprises seven faculties: Theology; Law; Business, Economics and Informatics; Medicine; Veterinary Medicine (Vetsuisse, jointly with Bern); Arts and Social Sciences; and Science. The University Hospital of Zurich and the children's hospital are closely affiliated. Research priorities include medicine, neuroscience, economics and finance, linguistics, and evolutionary biology; the university is a member of the League of European Research Universities (LERU).

Campus

The university's buildings are spread across the city of Zurich, centred on the historic main building (1914) by Karl Moser near the old town, the Irchel campus for the natural sciences on the Zürichberg, and facilities in Oerlikon. The university maintains museums including the Zoological Museum and the Museum of Anthropology, and its library (ZB Zürich) is among the largest in Switzerland.
