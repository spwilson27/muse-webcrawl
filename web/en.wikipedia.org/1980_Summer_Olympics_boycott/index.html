The 1980 Summer Olympics boycott was a protest by the United States and allied nations against the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan in December 1979. Led by President Jimmy Carter, the boycott kept American athletes out of the 1980 Summer Olympics in Moscow and persuaded or pressured dozens of other countries to stay away, substantially reducing the field at the Games.

Background

On 20 January 1980, Carter announced that the United States would boycott the Moscow Olympics unless Soviet troops withdrew from Afghanistan by 20 February. The deadline passed without withdrawal. The United States Olympic Committee voted to boycott in April, under intense government pressure that included threats to revoke the committee's finances and tax status.

Participation

Sixty-six eligible nations declined their invitations, though not all absences were boycott-related. Many Western European nations, including the United Kingdom, France and Italy, allowed their athletes to decide individually; British and French teams marched under the Olympic flag rather than their national flags at the opening ceremony. In total 80 nations competed, the smallest number since the 1956 Melbourne Games.

Effects on athletes

The boycott fell hardest on athletes in individual sports whose competitive windows were short; many never had another chance to compete at an Olympics. American swimmers, gymnasts and track athletes were particularly affected, and the United States organised the alternative Liberty Bell Classic in Philadelphia for boycotting nations. Congress later awarded the would-be Olympic team gold-plated medals in recognition.

Retaliation and legacy

Four years later the Soviet Union and most of its allies boycotted the 1984 Summer Olympics in Los Angeles, citing security concerns and alleged American hostility. The twin boycotts are generally judged to have damaged the Olympic movement and the careers of a generation of athletes without altering Soviet policy — Soviet forces remained in Afghanistan until 1989. The boycotts remain the largest in Olympic history and a defining episode of late Cold War sport.
