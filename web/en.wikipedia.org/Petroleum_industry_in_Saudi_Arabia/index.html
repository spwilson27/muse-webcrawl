The petroleum industry in Saudi Arabia is by far the country's dominant economic sector, accounting for the majority of government revenue and export earnings. Saudi Arabia possesses around 17% of the world's proved petroleum reserves and is one of the largest producers and exporters of crude oil. The industry is dominated by Saudi Aramco, the state-owned oil company.

History

Oil was discovered in commercial quantities in 1938 at Dammam No. 7, drilled by the California-Arabian Standard Oil Company (CASOC), a subsidiary of Standard Oil of California. Production grew rapidly after World War II. In 1944 the operating company was renamed the Arabian-American Oil Company (Aramco). The giant Ghawar field, discovered in 1948, is the largest conventional onshore oil field in the world; the offshore Safaniya field, discovered in 1951, is the largest offshore field.

Saudi Arabia was a founding member of OPEC in 1960. During the 1973 Arab oil embargo, Saudi Arabia led production cuts that quadrupled oil prices. The Saudi government gradually acquired ownership of Aramco, completing the takeover by 1980; in 1988 the company was renamed Saudi Aramco. In December 2019, Saudi Aramco listed about 1.5% of its shares on the Tadawul stock exchange in the world's largest initial public offering at the time.

Production and reserves

Saudi Arabia's maximum sustainable production capacity is around 12 million barrels per day. Major fields include Ghawar, Safaniya, Khurais, Manifa, Shaybah, Qatif and Abqaiq. The country exports crude through terminals at Ras Tanura and Juaymah on the Persian Gulf and Yanbu on the Red Sea, connected by the East–West Pipeline (Petroline).

Role in the economy

Oil revenue funds the Saudi state and its sovereign wealth. Under the Vision 2030 program launched in 2016, the kingdom aims to diversify its economy away from dependence on hydrocarbons, expanding sectors such as tourism, mining, technology and renewable energy, while Aramco invests in petrochemicals and natural gas.
