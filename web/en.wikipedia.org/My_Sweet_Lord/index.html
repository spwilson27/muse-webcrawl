My Sweet Lord

ArtistGeorge Harrison
AlbumAll Things Must Pass
ReleasedNovember 1970
GenreFolk rock, gospel

"My Sweet Lord" is a song by English musician George Harrison, released in November 1970 on his triple album All Things Must Pass. A prayer-like fusion of the Christian "hallelujah" and the Hindu "Hare Krishna" mantra, it became the first number-one single by an ex-Beatle and one of the best-selling singles of the 1970s.

Background and recording

Harrison wrote the song in December 1969 while touring with Delaney & Bonnie, drawing on the Edwin Hawkins Singers' gospel hit "Oh Happy Day". Co-produced with Phil Spector, the recording layers slide guitars, strings, and a massed chorus, shifting midway from "hallelujah" to the Hare Krishna mantra and a Sanskrit verse from the Vedas.

Copyright lawsuit

In 1971 Harrison was sued for copyright infringement over the song's similarity to "He's So Fine", a 1963 hit by the Chiffons written by Ronnie Mack. In 1976 a US judge ruled that Harrison had "subconsciously plagiarised" the earlier song; the prolonged legal aftermath — complicated by Harrison's former manager Allen Klein buying the plaintiff's rights — lasted years and inspired Harrison's wry response song "This Song".

Legacy

The song returned to number one in the UK in 2002, when it was reissued after Harrison's death. It appears on numerous lists of the greatest songs of all time and remains the defining statement of Harrison's spiritual songwriting.
