Corporate group

A corporate group or group of companies is a collection of parent and subsidiary corporations that function as a single economic entity through a common source of control. The concept of a group is frequently used in tax law, accounting and (less frequently) company law. Each company in the group remains a separate legal person, but control is exercised by the parent company, usually through majority voting shareholdings.

Structure and control

Groups may be organised as flat structures, in which the parent directly owns each subsidiary, or as pyramids, in which subsidiaries own further subsidiaries, allowing control of a large group with a relatively small equity stake at the top. A parent that carries on no trade of its own is a pure holding company. Conglomerates are groups whose subsidiaries operate in diverse, unrelated industries, while vertically and horizontally integrated groups combine businesses at different stages of, or at the same stage of, a supply chain.

Legal and accounting treatment

Because each member of a group is a distinct legal entity, the doctrine of limited liability generally shields one member from the debts of another, though courts may intervene in cases of fraud or where the subsidiary is a mere façade. For financial reporting, groups prepare consolidated financial statements that present the parent and subsidiaries as a single enterprise, eliminating intra-group transactions. Tax systems variously allow group loss relief and tax consolidation, and competition regulators treat a group as a "single economic unit" for many antitrust purposes. Related forms include the Japanese keiretsu and Korean chaebol, networks of affiliated firms with cross-shareholdings.
