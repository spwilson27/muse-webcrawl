Horse gait

Horse gaits are the various ways in which a horse can move, either naturally or as a result of specialised training by humans. Gaits are typically categorised by their footfall pattern and number of beats — the number of separate hoof impacts heard between moments when the pattern repeats.

Natural gaits

All horses can perform four natural gaits. The walk is a four-beat gait averaging about 6 km/h, in which each foot strikes the ground separately. The trot (jog) is a two-beat gait in which diagonal pairs of legs move together, with a moment of suspension between beats. The canter (lope) is a three-beat gait, and the gallop is a four-beat gait and the horse's fastest, in which all four feet may leave the ground at once; at racing speed a gallop can exceed 60 km/h.

Ambling gaits

Some horses can perform smooth four-beat lateral gaits collectively called ambling gaits, such as the running walk of the Tennessee Walking Horse, the rack, the fox trot, and the tölt of the Icelandic horse. These gaits are inherited in certain breeds and are associated with a mutation in the DMRT3 gene, sometimes called the "gait keeper" gene, which affects the coordination of leg movements.

Harness racing gaits

In harness racing, horses race either at the trot or at the pace, a two-beat lateral gait in which the legs on the same side move together. Pacers often wear hobbles to help maintain the gait.
