Digital Video Broadcasting

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Digital Video Broadcasting (DVB) is a set of international open standards for digital television maintained by the DVB Project, an industry consortium founded in 1993 and based in Geneva, with its technical specifications published by ETSI. DVB standards are used for broadcast television on every continent, with well over a billion receivers deployed.

Standards family

The DVB transmission standards share a common MPEG transport stream and differ mainly in modulation:

  • DVB-S and DVB-S2 (and S2X) — satellite broadcasting.
  • DVB-C and DVB-C2 — cable television.
  • DVB-T and DVB-T2 — terrestrial broadcasting using COFDM; T2 is used for HD and UHD services in much of Europe and beyond.
  • DVB-H and DVB-SH — handheld and satellite-to-handheld delivery (largely superseded by mobile broadband).
  • DVB-IPTV and DVB-I — delivery over managed IP networks and over the open internet.

Common elements

All DVB systems carry audio, video, and data in MPEG transport streams, use Service Information (DVB-SI) tables to describe networks and electronic programme guides, and support conditional access through the DVB Common Scrambling Algorithm and the Common Interface (DVB-CI/CI+) for pay-TV modules. Video codecs have evolved from MPEG-2 through H.264/AVC and HEVC to VVC, and audio from MPEG-1 Layer II to AAC and AC-4.

Adoption

DVB-T/T2 is the dominant terrestrial platform in Europe, Africa, much of Asia, and Oceania, while DVB-S2 dominates direct-to-home satellite worldwide. North America, Japan, and parts of Latin America instead use rival standards (ATSC and ISDB), though DVB is common in their cable and satellite sectors.
