Vincenzo Florio

Vincenzo Florio Jr. (March 18, 1883 – January 6, 1959) was an Italian entrepreneur and sportsman, a member of the wealthy Florio family of Sicily. He is best remembered as the founder of the Targa Florio, one of the oldest and most famous automobile races in the world, first held in Sicily in 1906.

Family and business

The Florio family had built one of Sicily's great fortunes in the nineteenth century, with interests in shipping, banking, tuna fishing, sulphur and Marsala wine. Vincenzo Florio Jr. inherited this commercial empire together with his elder brother Ignazio, but his own passions lay chiefly in sport and motoring. He competed in races himself and became one of the great patrons of early motor racing.

The Targa Florio

In 1906, aged just 23, Florio organized a race over the tortuous mountain roads of the Madonie in Sicily and offered a trophy — the targa, an ornate plaque — designed by the Paris jeweller René Lalique. The first edition, run on May 6, 1906, covered three laps of a 92-mile circuit and was won by Alessandro Cagno in an Itala. The Targa Florio quickly became one of the most prestigious events in international racing, celebrated for its danger and difficulty, and was run, with interruptions, until 1977. Florio also founded the Coppa Florio in 1905.

Later life

Florio's later years were marked by the decline of the family fortune, eroded by debt, war and the changing Italian economy. He lost control of much of the family business, but the Targa Florio endured as his monument, surviving him by nearly two decades. He died in Palermo in 1959. The Florio name remains prominent in Sicily, associated with the family's wine, its Liberty-style villas and the famous race.
