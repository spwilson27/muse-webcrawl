Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi

Born: 2 October 1869, Porbandar, Gujarat, British India

Died: 30 January 1948, New Delhi, India

Known for: Leadership of the Indian independence movement; philosophy of nonviolent resistance (satyagraha)

Spouse: Kasturba Gandhi

Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi (2 October 1869 – 30 January 1948) was an Indian lawyer, anti-colonial nationalist and political ethicist who employed nonviolent resistance to lead the successful campaign for India's independence from British rule. He inspired movements for civil rights and freedom across the world. The honorific Mahātmā (from Sanskrit, "great-souled" or "venerable") was first applied to him in South Africa in 1914 and is now used throughout the world.

Early life

Gandhi was born into a Gujarati Hindu family in Porbandar, a coastal town on the Kathiawar Peninsula. His father, Karamchand Gandhi, served as the dewan (chief minister) of Porbandar. In 1888 Gandhi travelled to London to study law at the Inner Temple, and he was called to the bar in 1891. After two unsuccessful years practising law in India, he moved to South Africa in 1893, where he spent 21 years developing his political views and his method of nonviolent protest in response to the discrimination faced by Indians there.

Leadership in India

Returning to India in 1915, Gandhi organised peasants, farmers and urban labourers to protest against excessive land taxes and discrimination. Assuming leadership of the Indian National Congress in 1921, he led nationwide campaigns for easing poverty, expanding women's rights, building religious and ethnic amity, ending untouchability, and above all achieving swaraj (self-rule). His most famous campaign was the Salt March of 1930, a 240-mile walk to the coastal village of Dandi to protest the British salt tax. He also launched the Quit India Movement in 1942, demanding an end to British rule.

Philosophy

Gandhi's philosophy rested on satyagraha ("truth-force"), the insistence that unjust laws be resisted through nonviolent civil disobedience, accepting suffering rather than inflicting it. He lived modestly, wore the traditional dhoti, fasted as a means of both self-purification and political protest, and promoted economic self-reliance through the spinning of khadi cloth on the charkha.

Assassination and legacy

India gained independence in August 1947, but Gandhi was devastated by the Partition of India and the communal violence that accompanied it. On 30 January 1948, he was assassinated in New Delhi by Nathuram Godse, a Hindu nationalist who believed Gandhi had been too accommodating toward Muslims. Gandhi's birthday, 2 October, is commemorated in India as Gandhi Jayanti, a national holiday, and worldwide as the International Day of Nonviolence. His methods profoundly influenced later leaders including civil rights movements abroad and figures such as Martin Luther King Jr. and Nelson Mandela.
