A chapel is a Christian place of prayer and worship that is usually relatively small. The term has several meanings. First, smaller spaces within a church that have an altar are often called chapels; the Lady chapel is a common example. Second, a chapel is a place of worship, sometimes non-denominational, that is part of a complex or larger structure such as a hospital, college, airport, or large church.

The word "chapel" derives from the Latin cappella, a diminutive of cappa ("cloak"), referring to the cloak of Saint Martin of Tours, which was preserved as a relic and carried into battle by Frankish kings, with the tent sheltering it giving rise to the term.

History

Chapels have been part of Christian worship since late antiquity. In the Middle Ages, cathedrals and large churches commonly included side chapels endowed for private prayer, chantries for the saying of masses for the dead, and chapels dedicated to particular saints. The Gothic period saw the construction of celebrated examples such as the Sainte-Chapelle in Paris, built in the 1240s to house relics of Christ's Passion.

Types of chapels

Chapels may be attached to hospitals, schools, universities, prisons, airports, military bases, and private estates. In Britain, the word "chapel" also came to denote places of worship of Nonconformist denominations, in contrast to the "church" of the established Church of England. Funeral chapels and wedding chapels are further specialised forms.
