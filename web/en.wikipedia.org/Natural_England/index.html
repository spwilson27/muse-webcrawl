Natural England

Agency overview

Formed: 1 October 2006

Parent department: Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs (Defra)

Natural England is a non-departmental public body in the United Kingdom sponsored by the Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs. It is responsible for ensuring that England's natural environment, including its land, flora and fauna, freshwater and marine environments, geology, and soils, is protected and improved. It also has a responsibility to help people enjoy, understand, and access the natural environment.

History

Natural England was established on 1 October 2006 under the Natural Environment and Rural Communities Act 2006, by merging English Nature, the landscape, access and recreation elements of the Countryside Agency, and the environmental land management functions of the Rural Development Service.

Responsibilities

The agency designates and advises on Sites of Special Scientific Interest (SSSIs) and National Nature Reserves, administers agri-environment schemes that pay farmers for environmental land management, issues licences for protected species, and promotes public access, including the development of the England Coast Path. It serves as the government's statutory adviser on the natural environment in England.
