Defense of the Ancients (DotA) is a multiplayer online battle arena (MOBA) mod for the video game Warcraft III: Reign of Chaos (2002) and its expansion, The Frozen Throne. The objective of the game is for each team to destroy their opponents' Ancient, a heavily guarded structure at the opposing corner of the map. Each player controls a powerful unit known as a "hero" with unique abilities and advantages; along the way, heroes may increase their experience and level, buy powerful equipment with gold, and cooperate with computer-controlled units known as creeps.

Gameplay

Defense of the Ancients pits two teams of players against each other: the Sentinel and the Scourge. Players on the Sentinel team are based at the southwestern corner of the map, and those on the Scourge team are based at the northeastern corner. Each base is defended by towers and waves of units which guard the main paths leading to their base. In the center of each base is the Ancient, a building that must be destroyed to win the game.

Each human player controls one hero, a powerful unit with unique abilities. In DotA, players on each side choose one of more than a hundred heroes, each with different abilities and tactical advantages over other heroes. The scenario is highly team-oriented; it is difficult for one player to carry the team to victory alone. Nevertheless, some heroes, given enough time, can become extremely powerful late in the game and can, by themselves, dominate the opposing team.

Development

The map was first released in 2003 by an anonymous creator known as Eul, based on the "Aeon of Strife" custom map from StarCraft. After the release of Warcraft III's expansion pack The Frozen Throne, competing versions proliferated; DotA: Allstars, developed by Steve Feak, became the dominant version. Development was later taken over by the pseudonymous IceFrog in 2005, who added many features and fixes over subsequent years.

Legacy

Defense of the Ancients was largely responsible for the creation of the MOBA genre. Its success spawned numerous standalone games, most notably League of Legends (2009) by Riot Games and Dota 2 (2013) by Valve, the latter developed with IceFrog's involvement. The mod maintained a dedicated player base for over a decade and was a staple of early professional esports competitions.
