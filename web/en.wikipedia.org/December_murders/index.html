December murders


Memorial in Paramaribo

The December murders (Dutch: Decembermoorden) were the murders of fifteen prominent Surinamese men on 7 and 8 December 1982 at Fort Zeelandia in Paramaribo, Suriname. The victims — journalists, lawyers, trade union leaders, academics, and military officers — had criticized the military regime of Dési Bouterse, who had seized power in the 1980 Sergeants' Coup.

Events

The men were arrested during the night, taken to Fort Zeelandia, the army's headquarters, and shot. The regime initially claimed they had been killed while trying to escape, an account widely rejected. The murders provoked international condemnation; the Netherlands suspended development aid to its former colony.

Trials

After a long legal process, a trial began in Suriname in 2007. In November 2019, a military court convicted Bouterse, by then president of Suriname, in absentia and sentenced him to 20 years in prison. The conviction was upheld on appeal in December 2023. Bouterse went into hiding in January 2024 and died in December 2024 without serving his sentence.
