Geheimschreiber

The Geheimschreiber ("secret writer"), designated the Siemens & Halske T52, was a cipher machine used by Nazi Germany during World War II to encrypt teleprinter (teletype) traffic. It belonged to the family of machines the British codebreakers at Bletchley Park collectively called "Fish".

Description

The T52 used ten pin wheels of varying sizes to generate a pseudorandom key stream that was combined with the plaintext by XOR in the Baudot code. Successive models (T52a through T52e) increased in complexity.

Codebreaking

Swedish mathematician Arne Beurling of the FRA broke an early version of the machine in 1940, allowing Sweden to read German traffic passing through Swedish cables. Bletchley Park also read some T52 traffic, though the more significant Fish system was the Lorenz SZ 40/42 ("Tunny").
