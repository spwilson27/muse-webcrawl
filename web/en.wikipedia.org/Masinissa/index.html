Masinissa

King of Numidia (c. 238 – 148 BC)

Masinissa (c. 238 BC – 148 BC) was the first king of Numidia, in present-day Algeria and Tunisia. Originally an ally of Carthage, he switched his allegiance to Rome during the Second Punic War, and his Numidian cavalry played a decisive role in Rome's victory at the Battle of Zama in 202 BC. He united the Massylii and Masaesyli peoples into a single kingdom and reigned for some sixty years.

Second Punic War

Masinissa, son of the Massylian chief Gaia, fought with distinction for Carthage in Spain. Betrothed to Sophonisba, daughter of the Carthaginian general Hasdrubal Gisco, he saw her married instead to his rival Syphax to secure a political alliance. Returning to Africa, Masinissa allied with the Roman commander Scipio Africanus, helped defeat Syphax at the Battle of Cirta in 203 BC, and at Zama his cavalry, after pursuing the Carthaginian horse, returned to strike Hannibal's infantry from the rear.

Kingdom

Rewarded with Syphax's lands, Masinissa built a prosperous kingdom with its capital at Cirta, encouraging settled agriculture — Roman sources credited him with turning Numidia into a granary — and promoting Hellenistic culture. His continual encroachments on Carthaginian territory, tacitly tolerated by Rome, helped provoke the Third Punic War, which began shortly after his death at about ninety years of age. He was succeeded by his son Micipsa.
