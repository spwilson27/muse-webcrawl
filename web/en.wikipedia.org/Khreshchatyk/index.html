Khreshchatyk

Khreshchatyk (Ukrainian: Хрещатик) is the main street of Kyiv, Ukraine. Roughly 1.3 kilometres long, it runs from European Square in the northeast through Maidan Nezalezhnosti (Independence Square) to Bessarabian Square in the southwest. Lined with chestnut trees and grand administrative, commercial and residential buildings, it is the city's principal ceremonial thoroughfare and a centre of public gatherings.

History

The street follows the bottom of the Khreshchata valley, its name derived from the Ukrainian word khreshchennia (baptism), associated in local tradition with the Baptism of Rus' in 988. It was laid out as a built street in the late 18th and early 19th centuries and developed rapidly after the 1830s, becoming Kyiv's commercial heart, served by one of the Russian Empire's first tram lines.

Destruction and rebuilding

In September 1941, shortly after German forces occupied Kyiv, a series of radio-detonated mines planted by the retreating Red Army destroyed most of Khreshchatyk's buildings. After the war the street was rebuilt in the monumental Stalinist neoclassical style, widened, and replanted with the chestnut trees that became a symbol of the city.

Modern role

Khreshchatyk is closed to traffic on weekends and holidays and serves as Kyiv's main public stage: it hosted rallies of the Orange Revolution (2004) and Euromaidan (2013–14), centred on Maidan Nezalezhnosti at its midpoint. Landmarks along the street include the Kyiv City Council, the main post office, the Besarabsky Market and the Globus shopping centre beneath the square.
