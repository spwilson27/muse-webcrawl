Tea tree oil

Tea tree oil, also known as melaleuca oil, is an essential oil with a fresh, camphoraceous odour and a colour ranging from pale yellow to nearly colourless. It is distilled from the leaves of the Australian tea tree, Melaleuca alternifolia, native to the northeast coast of New South Wales and southeast Queensland. Despite its name it should not be confused with the tea plant used for black and green teas.

History and production

Aboriginal peoples of eastern Australia traditionally used crushed tea tree leaves for coughs, colds, and skin complaints. Commercial production began in the 1920s after chemist Arthur Penfold reported the oil's antiseptic properties, and it was issued to Australian soldiers in the Second World War as a general antiseptic. Modern production is centred on plantations in New South Wales; the oil is obtained by steam distillation, and its composition is standardised internationally (ISO 4730), with the antimicrobial component terpinen-4-ol required at specified levels and cineole limited.

Uses and evidence

Tea tree oil is widely used in cosmetics and topical products for acne, dandruff, athlete's foot, and nail fungus. Laboratory studies show antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory activity, and some clinical trials support modest benefit for acne and dandruff, but high-quality evidence for most claimed uses is limited. The oil is toxic if swallowed and can cause skin irritation or allergic reactions; it should not be ingested, and poisonings have been reported in children and pets.
