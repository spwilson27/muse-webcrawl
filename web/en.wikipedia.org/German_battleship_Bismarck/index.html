Bismarck was the first of two Bismarck-class battleships built for Nazi Germany's Kriegsmarine. Named after Chancellor Otto von Bismarck, she was the largest warship of the German navy and, with her sister Tirpitz, among the largest battleships ever built by a European power. Her career lasted a single eight-day operation in May 1941 that became one of the most famous naval episodes of the Second World War.

Operation Rheinübung

In May 1941 Bismarck, commanded by Captain Ernst Lindemann and flying the flag of Fleet Chief Günther Lütjens, sailed with the heavy cruiser Prinz Eugen on Operation Rheinübung, a commerce-raiding sortie into the Atlantic. On 24 May, in the Battle of the Denmark Strait, Bismarck sank the British battlecruiser HMS Hood — a blow to British morale, as Hood was a symbol of Royal Navy power — but was herself damaged by HMS Prince of Wales.

The chase and sinking

Pursued by a large British force, Bismarck headed for occupied France. On 26 May a torpedo from a Fairey Swordfish of HMS Ark Royal jammed her rudder, leaving her unable to steer. On the morning of 27 May she was engaged by the battleships King George V and Rodney and cruisers, including Dorsetshire, and went down; whether she sank from British fire and torpedoes or was scuttled by her crew is still debated. Of more than 2,200 men aboard, only about 114 survived.

Wreck

The wreck was located in June 1989 by Robert Ballard, lying upright at a depth of about 4,800 metres, roughly 650 km west of Brest. Subsequent expeditions have extensively surveyed the site.
