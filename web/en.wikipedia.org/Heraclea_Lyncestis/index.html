Heraclea Lyncestis

Heraclea Lyncestis, also transliterated Herakleia Lynkestis, was an ancient Greek city in Macedon, near the modern town of Bitola in North Macedonia. It was founded by Philip II of Macedon in the middle of the 4th century BC.

History and remains

The city was named after Heracles, the mythical hero and ancestor of the Macedonian royal house, and after the region of Lynkestis in which it lay. Situated on the Via Egnatia, it became an important strategic and trading centre, and flourished in Roman and early Byzantine times, when it was an episcopal see.

Excavations have uncovered a theatre built in the time of Hadrian and completed under Antoninus Pius, Roman baths, a basilica, and exceptionally well-preserved floor mosaics, including the mosaic of the Great Basilica, a masterpiece of early Christian art. Heraclea was sacked and abandoned after an earthquake in 518 AD.
