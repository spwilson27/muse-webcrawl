Leptin

Leptin is a hormone produced mainly by adipose (fat) tissue that helps regulate energy balance by inhibiting hunger. It acts on receptors in the hypothalamus of the brain to suppress appetite and increase energy expenditure.

Discovery

Leptin was identified in 1994 by Jeffrey Friedman and colleagues at Rockefeller University, through studies of the obese (ob/ob) mouse, which lacks a functional leptin gene and becomes severely obese. The name derives from the Greek leptos, meaning "thin".

Function

Circulating leptin levels rise and fall with fat mass, signalling the brain about long-term energy stores. High leptin reduces food intake, while falling leptin during fasting triggers hunger and energy conservation. Leptin also influences reproduction, immunity and bone metabolism.

Leptin resistance and medicine

Most people with common obesity have high leptin levels but respond poorly to it, a state called leptin resistance. Recombinant leptin (metreleptin) is used to treat rare conditions of true leptin deficiency and lipodystrophy.
