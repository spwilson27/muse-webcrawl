Godwin, Earl of Wessex

Godwin (Old English: Godwine; died 15 April 1053) was an Anglo-Saxon nobleman who rose to become Earl of Wessex and the most powerful magnate in England under King Cnut and his successors. He founded the House of Godwin, whose members dominated English politics in the decades before the Norman Conquest; his son Harold Godwinson became the last Anglo-Saxon king of England in 1066.

Rise to power

Godwin's origins are obscure; he appears to have come from a relatively modest Sussex family. He attached himself to the Danish conqueror Cnut, who made him an earl, first of part and then of all Wessex, and gave him in marriage Gytha, a kinswoman of the Danish royal house. Godwin survived the succession crises after Cnut's death in 1035, though he was implicated in the blinding and death of the ætheling Alfred in 1036, an episode that long stained his reputation.

Conflict with Edward the Confessor

Godwin's daughter Edith married King Edward the Confessor in 1045, yet relations between earl and king deteriorated. In 1051, after a brawl at Dover, Godwin refused the king's order to punish the town, and the confrontation ended with Godwin and his sons exiled. The following year Godwin returned by sea with a fleet raised in Flanders and along the south coast, forcing Edward to restore the family's earldoms. He died suddenly at Winchester in April 1053, reportedly collapsing at the king's table.

Legacy

Godwin's career exemplifies the power a great earl could wield in late Anglo-Saxon England: at his death his family's earldoms covered most of the kingdom. His sons Harold and Tostig inherited his position, and their rivalry — ending at the battles of Stamford Bridge and Hastings in 1066 — closed the Anglo-Saxon age.
