Zebra

Zebras are African equines with distinctive black-and-white striped coats. There are three living species: the plains zebra, the mountain zebra, and Grévy's zebra. Their stripes are unique to each individual, much like fingerprints.

Stripes

Many hypotheses have been proposed for the function of zebra stripes, including camouflage, confusion of predators, social bonding, thermoregulation, and deterring biting flies; experimental evidence most strongly supports protection against horseflies and other biting insects.

Behaviour

Zebras are highly social grazers. Plains zebra live in stable family groups (harems) that aggregate into large herds, while Grévy's zebra have a more fluid social system. They communicate with barks, brays, and facial expressions, and defend themselves by biting and kicking.

Conservation

Grévy's zebra is endangered and the mountain zebra vulnerable, threatened by habitat loss, hunting, and competition with livestock; the plains zebra remains widespread but is declining in parts of its range.
