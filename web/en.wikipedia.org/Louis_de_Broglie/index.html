Louis de Broglie

Louis Victor Pierre Raymond, 7th duc de Broglie (15 August 1892 – 19 March 1987) was a French physicist who made groundbreaking contributions to quantum theory. In his 1924 PhD thesis he postulated the wave nature of electrons, proposing that all matter exhibits wave–particle duality.

Wave–particle duality

De Broglie hypothesised that any moving particle has an associated wave, with wavelength λ = h/p, where h is Planck's constant and p is momentum. The idea, extending Einstein's and Planck's work on light, was confirmed experimentally by electron diffraction in 1927 and became a foundation of quantum mechanics and Schrödinger's wave mechanics.

Honours

He received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1929 "for his discovery of the wave nature of electrons", was elected to the Académie des sciences and the Académie française, and held a chair at the University of Paris.
