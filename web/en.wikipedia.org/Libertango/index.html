"Libertango" is a composition by Argentine tango composer Ástor Piazzolla, recorded and published in 1974 in Milan, Italy. Its title is a portmanteau merging libertad ("liberty") and "tango", symbolizing Piazzolla's break from classical, traditional tango to his new style, nuevo tango.

Composition and release

Written during Piazzolla's period in Italy, "Libertango" appeared on the album of the same name recorded with a group of Italian session musicians, including electric instruments unusual for tango at the time. The piece is built on an insistent, driving rhythm and a memorable melodic line often carried by the bandoneón.

Covers and adaptations

The composition became one of Piazzolla's most famous works. Lyrics were added by Horacio Ferrer in some versions, and the melody was adapted with words as Grace Jones's "I've Seen That Face Before (Libertango)" (1981). It has been recorded by performers including Yo-Yo Ma, Al Di Meola, and the duo of Sharon Shannon and others, and used widely in film and advertising, notably in Roman Polanski's Frantic (1988).
