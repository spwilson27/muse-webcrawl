Microsoft Research Asia (MSRA) is the research arm of Microsoft in the Asia-Pacific region, founded in Beijing in 1998. It is one of the largest and most productive labs in the Microsoft Research network and has been described as among the most significant industrial computer science laboratories in China, contributing fundamental research in artificial intelligence and computer systems as well as talent who went on to lead major technology ventures in China.

History

The lab was founded under founding director Kai-Fu Lee, who later led Google China; subsequent leadership included Hsiao-Wuen Hon as managing director. MSRA grew to employ several hundred researchers and engineers and hosted thousands of interns and visiting scholars from universities across Asia. In the 2010s and 2020s Microsoft shifted some sensitive AI work away from the Beijing lab amid U.S.–China technology tensions, encouraging some staff to relocate to other labs, though MSRA continued operating, including with a Shanghai presence.

Research and impact

MSRA researchers produced influential work including the ResNet deep residual network (2015), which won the ImageNet competition and became one of the most cited papers in computer vision; contributions to neural machine translation, speech recognition, distributed systems, and graphics; and technologies transferred into products such as Bing, Windows, Office, Kinect, and Microsoft's Chinese input methods and chatbots. Its alumni founded or led numerous Chinese AI companies, leading commentators to nickname the lab the "Whampoa Military Academy" of China's AI industry.
