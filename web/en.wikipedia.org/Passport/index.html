Passport

A passport is an official travel document issued by a government that contains a person's identity for international travel. A passport holder is normally entitled to travel in and out of their home country, and when travelling to other countries, to protection by their home country's diplomatic missions. Passports certify the personal identity and nationality of their holders, and most modern passports are biometric, containing a contactless chip with the holder's data.

Modern biometric passports, identified by the camera-like symbol on the cover.

History

Travel documents requesting safe passage are ancient: a letter attributed to the Persian king Artaxerxes I allowed Nehemiah safe passage in the 5th century BCE. The word derives from the French passeport, a document required to pass through the gate (porte) of a city wall. The modern passport system was standardized after the First World War, when the League of Nations conferences of 1920 and 1926 established common formats; today the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) sets the standards for machine-readable travel documents.

Types

States issue several classes of passport: ordinary passports for citizens, diplomatic passports for diplomats and senior officials, official or service passports for government employees travelling on duty, and special documents such as laissez-passer for refugees and stateless persons. The "power" of a passport — the number of destinations its holder can enter visa-free — varies greatly by nationality and is tracked by several published indices.
