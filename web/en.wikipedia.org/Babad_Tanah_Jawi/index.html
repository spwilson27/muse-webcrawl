Babad Tanah Jawi

Babad Tanah Jawi ("History of the Land of Java") is a generic title for a large number of manuscripts written in Javanese language, recounting the history of Java from mythical origins through the Islamic kingdoms up to the era of the Mataram Sultanate and beyond. The texts blend myth, legend, and genealogy with historical events and were composed and recopied over several centuries, with major versions taking shape from the 17th and 18th centuries.

Content

The chronicles typically begin with the legendary first kings of Java, continue through the Hindu-Buddhist kingdoms and the coming of Islam with the Wali Sanga (Nine Saints), and give detailed dynastic accounts of Demak, Pajang, and Mataram, including the reigns of Senopati and Sultan Agung. Different recensions vary considerably in length and detail; some extend into the 18th century and the period of Dutch influence.

Significance

As babad (court chronicles), the texts served to legitimise rulers by linking them to prophetic and divine ancestry. They remain essential — if critically handled — sources for Javanese history, literature, and court culture, and survive in numerous manuscripts preserved in Indonesian and Dutch collections.
