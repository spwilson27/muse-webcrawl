Guido van Rossum

Guido van Rossum (born 31 January 1956) is a Dutch programmer best known as the creator of the Python programming language. For many years he led Python's development as its "benevolent dictator for life" (BDFL), a role he stepped down from in 2018.

Career

Van Rossum was born in Haarlem, Netherlands, and studied mathematics and computer science at the University of Amsterdam. He worked at Dutch research institutes including CWI, where he worked on the ABC programming language — an important influence on Python — and contributed to the Amoeba distributed operating system.

Python

Van Rossum began writing Python during the Christmas holidays of 1989 as a successor to ABC, naming it after the comedy troupe Monty Python. Python grew into one of the world's most widely used programming languages, valued for its readability. Van Rossum worked on Python while at CNRI in Virginia, Zope Corporation, Google (2005–2012), Dropbox (2013–2019) and, from 2020, Microsoft, where he worked on making CPython faster. In 2018, after a contentious debate over a language proposal, he resigned as BDFL, and Python moved to a steering-council governance model.
