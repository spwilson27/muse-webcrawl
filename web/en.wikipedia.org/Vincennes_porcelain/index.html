Vincennes porcelain
LocationChâteau de Vincennes, France
Active1740–1756
TypeSoft-paste porcelain
SuccessorSèvres porcelain

Vincennes porcelain was manufactured at the Château de Vincennes, east of Paris, from about 1740 until 1756, when the factory moved to Sèvres and became the famous Sèvres manufactory. Supported by Louis XV and Madame de Pompadour, Vincennes pioneered French soft-paste porcelain and raised it to a level rivalling Meissen.

Foundation and production

The venture began when the Orry de Fulvy brothers obtained a privilege in 1745, drawing on the expertise of workers from Chantilly and the chemist Gilles Dubois. The factory developed a high-quality soft-paste body with a fine white glaze and became known for its exquisitely modelled porcelain flowers, used in bouquets and mounted on chandeliers and clocks.

Colours and wares

Vincennes introduced celebrated ground colours, including a dark lapis blue (bleu lapis), a turquoise (bleu céleste, from 1752) and a green, gilded and enamelled with flowers and birds. Its wares included table services for the court — notably the service presented to the Queen of Denmark — tea wares, vases and figures modelled after François Boucher.

Move to Sèvres

Financial difficulties and the desire for larger premises led to the factory's transfer in 1756 to purpose-built premises at Sèvres, closer to Madame de Pompadour's château at Bellevue. Production at Vincennes ceased, and the Sèvres factory went on to dominate European luxury porcelain for the rest of the century.
