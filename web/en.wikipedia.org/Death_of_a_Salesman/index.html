Death of a Salesman

PlaywrightArthur Miller
PremiereFebruary 10, 1949, Morosco Theatre, Broadway
CharactersWilly Loman, Linda Loman, Biff Loman, Happy Loman
AwardsPulitzer Prize for Drama; Tony Award for Best Play

Death of a Salesman is a 1949 stage play written by American playwright Arthur Miller. It premiered on Broadway in February 1949, directed by Elia Kazan and starring Lee J. Cobb as Willy Loman, and won the Pulitzer Prize for Drama and the Tony Award for Best Play. It is considered one of the greatest American plays of the twentieth century.

Plot

Traveling salesman Willy Loman, exhausted and losing his grip on reality, drifts between the present and memories of a happier past. His sons Biff and Happy have failed to meet his dreams of being "well liked" and successful. After Biff confronts his father's illusions, Willy kills himself in his car so the family can collect his life insurance, hoping it will finally give Biff a start.

Themes

The play is a critique of the American Dream, exploring identity, denial, and the pressure to achieve material success. Willy's line "attention must be paid" frames the tragedy of an ordinary man's failure to attain his ideals.

Legacy

Death of a Salesman has been revived on Broadway many times, adapted into several films and television versions, and staged worldwide, including a famous 1983 production in Beijing directed by Miller himself.
