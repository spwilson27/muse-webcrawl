Pontiac (c. 1720 – 20 April 1769), also spelled Obwaandi'eyaag, was an Odawa war chief known for his role in Pontiac's War (1763–1766), a widespread Native American resistance against British occupation of the Great Lakes region following the French and Indian War.

Pontiac's War

In May 1763 Pontiac led an attack on Fort Detroit, beginning a conflict in which a loose confederation of tribes captured eight British forts and besieged Detroit and Fort Pitt. Inspired partly by the teachings of the Lenape prophet Neolin, the uprising prompted the British to issue the Royal Proclamation of 1763 reserving lands west of the Appalachians for Native peoples.

Later life

Pontiac made peace with the British in 1766. His influence declined thereafter, and he was killed in 1769 near Cahokia by a Peoria warrior. The city of Pontiac, Michigan, and the Pontiac automobile brand were later named for him.
