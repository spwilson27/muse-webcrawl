Kansas

Kansas is a state in the Midwestern United States, bordered by Nebraska to the north, Missouri to the east, Oklahoma to the south and Colorado to the west. Its capital is Topeka and its largest city is Wichita; the Kansas City metropolitan area straddles the state line with Missouri. Kansas is named after the Kansas River, itself named for the Kansa (Kaw) Native American people. Nicknamed the Sunflower State, it has a population of about 2.9 million.

History

For millennia the region was home to Native American peoples including the Kaw, Osage, Pawnee and Wichita. After the Louisiana Purchase (1803), Kansas Territory was organised in 1854; the Kansas–Nebraska Act's doctrine of popular sovereignty triggered the violent "Bleeding Kansas" struggle between pro- and anti-slavery settlers. Kansas entered the Union as a free state on 29 January 1861. After the Civil War it became a byword for cattle towns such as Dodge City and Abilene on the Chisholm Trail, for populist politics, and in 1954 for the Topeka case at the heart of Brown v. Board of Education.

Geography and economy

The state lies on the Great Plains, rising gradually from the low east to the High Plains in the west; Mount Sunflower (1,231 m) is the highest point. Agriculture dominates: Kansas is a leading producer of wheat, sorghum, corn and cattle, underpinned by the Ogallala Aquifer. Aviation manufacturing in Wichita ("the Air Capital of the World"), telecommunications and energy are also significant.
