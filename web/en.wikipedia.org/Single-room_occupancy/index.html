Single-room occupancy

Single-room occupancy (SRO) is a form of housing in which a tenant rents one small room, typically without a private bathroom or kitchen, sharing those facilities with other residents of the building. SROs — often former hotels, known as residential hotels — have historically provided the cheapest rung of private housing in American cities, serving low-wage workers, pensioners, students, and people leaving homelessness or institutions.

History

SRO hotels proliferated in US cities in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, housing a large, largely male, transient workforce. From the 1950s onward, urban renewal, zoning changes and condominium conversion destroyed much of this stock: by some estimates New York City lost most of its SRO units between 1950 and the 1980s, and similar losses occurred in Chicago, San Francisco and Vancouver's Downtown Eastside. The disappearance of SROs is frequently cited as a contributing factor to the rise of modern mass homelessness in the 1980s.

Modern role

Remaining and newly built SROs are an important part of supportive and affordable housing strategies, often combined with on-site social services ("permanent supportive housing"). Regulations vary widely, and many cities now restrict conversion or demolition of SRO buildings to preserve the remaining stock.
