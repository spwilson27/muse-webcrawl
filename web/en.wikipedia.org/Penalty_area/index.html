Penalty area

The penalty area, colloquially "the box", is a rectangular area of an association football pitch in front of each goal. It is the area within which a foul by a defending player punishable by a direct free kick results instead in a penalty kick, and within which the goalkeeper is permitted to handle the ball.

Dimensions and markings

Under Law 1 of the Laws of the Game, lines are drawn 16.5 m (18 yd) from the inside of each goalpost and 16.5 m into the field, joined by a line parallel to the goal line, enclosing an area 40.32 m wide. Within it, the penalty spot is marked 11 m (12 yd) from the midpoint of the goal line, and an arc of 9.15 m radius outside the area marks the minimum distance players must keep at a penalty kick. The smaller six-yard box (goal area) lies inside it.

Functions

Besides penalty kicks and goalkeeper handling, the penalty area governs other restarts: goal kicks and defensive free kicks may be taken from anywhere within it, and the ball is in play as soon as it is kicked at a goal kick. Introduced in 1902, the penalty area replaced an earlier rule under which penalties could be awarded for fouls within 12 yards of the goal line anywhere across the pitch.
