Diu Fort

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Diu Fort, also known as Diu Fortress, is a Portuguese-built fortification located on the eastern tip of Diu Island in the union territory of Daman and Diu, India. The fort was built in 1535 after a defensive alliance between the Sultan of Gujarat, Bahadur Shah, and the Portuguese, who were seeking to control the sea lanes of western India.

History

Construction was completed under the command of the Portuguese governor Nuno da Cunha. The fort withstood a siege by the Ottoman fleet and Gujarati forces in 1538 and another major siege in 1546, events that made it a celebrated symbol of Portuguese military strength in Asia. Diu remained under Portuguese rule for over four centuries, until India annexed the Portuguese enclaves of Goa, Daman and Diu in December 1961.

Architecture

The fort is built on a headland at the mouth of the Chasi river, with massive outer walls, a deep moat (now dry) on the landward side, and bastions including the Sea Bastion and St. George Bastion. Within the walls stand a chapel (the Church of St. Francis of Assisi), a lighthouse (the highest point of the fort, offering views over the Arabian Sea) and old cannons. The fort is a popular tourist attraction and a protected monument.
