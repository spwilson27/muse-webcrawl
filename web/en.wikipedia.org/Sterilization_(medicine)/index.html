Sterilization (medicine)

Sterilization in medicine refers to surgical procedures that permanently prevent reproduction. The most common methods are vasectomy in males, in which the vasa deferentia are cut or sealed, and tubal ligation in females, in which the fallopian tubes are blocked, cut, or removed.

Methods

Vasectomy is an outpatient procedure performed under local anaesthesia and is considered simpler and safer than female sterilization. Tubal ligation may be performed laparoscopically, during caesarean delivery, or via salpingectomy (complete tube removal), which also reduces ovarian cancer risk. Historically, transcervical devices were used but were withdrawn from markets over safety concerns.

Effectiveness and reversibility

Sterilization is among the most effective contraceptive methods, with failure rates below 1%. Reversal surgery is possible but uncertain and expensive, so the procedures are treated as permanent. Counselling about regret — most common among those sterilized at younger ages — is standard practice.

History and ethics

The 20th century saw coercive sterilization programmes under eugenics laws in several countries, now widely condemned; modern practice requires informed consent, and international guidelines prohibit forced sterilization.
