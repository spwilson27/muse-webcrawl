Internet.org

Internet.org was a partnership launched in 2013 by Facebook (now Meta) together with technology companies including Samsung, Ericsson, MediaTek, Opera Software (now Opera), Nokia and Qualcomm. Its stated goal was to bring affordable Internet access to the two-thirds of the world's population who were not yet connected.

Free Basics

The initiative's best-known product was Free Basics, an application and web service launched first in Zambia in 2014 that gave users free access to a limited set of websites — including Facebook, news, weather, health information and job listings — through participating mobile carriers, without data charges. The service expanded to dozens of countries in Africa, Asia and Latin America.

Criticism

Free Basics drew strong criticism from net neutrality advocates, who argued that a curated, zero-rated platform turned Facebook into a gatekeeper for the poor. In India, a large public campaign led the Telecom Regulatory Authority of India to ban discriminatory data pricing in February 2016, effectively prohibiting Free Basics there. Egypt also shut the service down. Meta later de-emphasised the programme, rebranding it under Facebook Connectivity and quietly closing Free Basics in many markets by the early 2020s.
