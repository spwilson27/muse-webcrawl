Lithuanian language

Lithuanian (lietuvių kalba) is an Eastern Baltic language in the Indo-European family, spoken by about three million people, principally in Lithuania, where it is the official language.

Features

Lithuanian is often described as the most conservative living Indo-European language, retaining many archaic features of Proto-Indo-European lost elsewhere, including a complex system of pitch accent and a rich nominal inflection: nouns decline in seven cases, and there are three numbers including a vestigial dual. It is written in a Latin alphabet of 32 letters with diacritics such as ą, č, ę, ė, š, ų and ž.

History

The first printed Lithuanian book was Martynas Mažvydas's Catechism of 1547, printed in Königsberg. A ban on Lithuanian Latin-alphabet printing under the Russian Empire (1864–1904) was defied by book smugglers (knygnešiai). Standard Lithuanian is based on the Aukštaitian (Highland) dialects; the other main group is Samogitian.
