Karp's 21 NP-complete problems

Karp's 21 NP-complete problems
Published: 1972, "Reducibility Among Combinatorial Problems"
Author: Richard M. Karp
Field: Computational complexity theory

Karp's 21 NP-complete problems are a set of computational problems whose NP-completeness was demonstrated by Richard Karp in his landmark 1972 paper "Reducibility Among Combinatorial Problems". Building on the Cook–Levin theorem, which established that the Boolean satisfiability problem (SAT) is NP-complete, Karp showed that 21 diverse problems are likewise NP-complete by chains of polynomial-time reductions.

The problems

The list includes satisfiability variants such as 3-SAT, graph problems such as clique, vertex cover, Hamiltonian cycle, graph colouring and set cover, and scheduling and packing problems such as knapsack, bin packing, exact cover and Steiner tree.

Impact

The paper established NP-completeness as a central concept in computer science and provided the standard technique of proving hardness by reduction from a known NP-complete problem. See also NP-completeness.
