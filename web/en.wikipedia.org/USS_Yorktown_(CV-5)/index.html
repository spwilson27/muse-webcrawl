USS Yorktown (CV-5)

USS Yorktown (CV-5) was the lead ship of the Yorktown-class aircraft carriers of the United States Navy, commissioned in 1937. She spent her early career in the Atlantic and Pacific Fleets and, after the United States entered World War II, took part in early carrier raids against Japanese-held islands.

At the Battle of the Coral Sea in May 1942, Yorktown was damaged by a bomb hit. Rushed into dry dock at Pearl Harbor, she was patched in roughly 72 hours by some 1,400 workers so she could sail for Midway. Japanese intelligence, believing her sunk or out of action, did not expect her there.

At the Battle of Midway in June 1942, her aircraft helped sink the Japanese carrier Sōryū, but Yorktown was crippled by dive-bomber and torpedo attacks. While under tow on 7 June, she was torpedoed by the Japanese submarine I-168 and sank, together with the destroyer Hammann. Her wreck was located in 1998 by a team led by Robert Ballard.
