British Antarctic Survey

Halley VI, BAS's station on the Brunt Ice Shelf

The British Antarctic Survey (BAS) is the United Kingdom's national Antarctic research programme and one of the world's leading polar research institutes. Based in Cambridge, it operates research stations in Antarctica and the sub-Antarctic, the polar research vessel RRS Sir David Attenborough, and aircraft, and employs around 400 staff. It is a component of the Natural Environment Research Council (NERC), part of UK Research and Innovation.

History

BAS grew out of Operation Tabarin (1943–45), a wartime expedition that established permanent bases in the Antarctic Peninsula region. Renamed the Falkland Islands Dependencies Survey, it became the British Antarctic Survey in 1962. In 1985, BAS scientists Joseph Farman, Brian Gardiner, and Jonathan Shanklin published the discovery of the Antarctic ozone hole, one of the landmark environmental findings of the century.

Stations and logistics

BAS operates Halley VI on the Brunt Ice Shelf — a relocatable station on hydraulic legs and skis, moved inland in 2016–17 as cracks grew in the shelf — and Rothera Research Station on Adelaide Island, the main logistics hub, along with facilities at Signy Island and on South Georgia (King Edward Point and Bird Island). The ice-strengthened RRS Sir David Attenborough, which entered service in 2021, replaced the James Clark Ross and Ernest Shackleton; BAS also flies Twin Otter and Dash 7 aircraft in the polar regions.

Research

Programmes span ice-sheet dynamics and sea-level rise, atmospheric science, space weather (in partnership with the British Geological Survey's geomagnetism work), polar ecology, and the long-running ice-core record of past climate.
