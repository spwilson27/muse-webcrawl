Palm oil is an edible vegetable oil derived from the mesocarp (reddish pulp) of the fruit of the oil palm tree, principally the African oil palm Elaeis guineensis. It is the most widely consumed vegetable oil in the world, valued for its high yield per hectare — oil palms produce several times more oil per unit of land than other oil crops — and for its stability at high temperatures and long shelf life.

Production

Oil palms are grown in tropical regions within about ten degrees of the equator. Indonesia and Malaysia together account for the great majority of global production. Fresh fruit bunches are harvested and sterilised, the fruit is pressed to yield crude palm oil, and the kernels are crushed separately to produce palm kernel oil, a distinct product with a higher proportion of saturated fat. Crude palm oil is naturally reddish because of its high beta-carotene content; refining removes the colour and much of the carotene.

Uses

Palm oil is found in a large share of packaged foods — including biscuits, instant noodles, margarine, chocolate and ice cream — as well as in soaps, cosmetics, detergents and candles. It is also used as a feedstock for biodiesel, particularly in Indonesia under blending mandates.

Environmental and social issues

Expansion of oil palm plantations has been a major driver of deforestation in Southeast Asia, with associated losses of habitat for species such as the orangutan, emissions from drained peatlands, and conflicts over land rights with local and indigenous communities. In response, certification schemes such as the Roundtable on Sustainable Palm Oil (RSPO, founded 2004) have been established, though their effectiveness remains debated. Because palm oil is so land-efficient, some analysts argue that boycotting it could shift demand to lower-yield oils requiring more land overall.

Health

Palm oil is about 50 percent saturated fat (chiefly palmitic acid), a level between butter and most liquid vegetable oils. Health authorities generally advise limiting saturated fat intake, and palm oil consumption has been studied in relation to cardiovascular risk.
