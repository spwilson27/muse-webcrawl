Sunnah is the body of traditions, practices, and teachings of the Islamic prophet Muhammad that constitutes a model for Muslims to follow. In pre-Islamic Arabia the word meant ancestral precedent or customary practice; in Islam it came to denote specifically the example of Muhammad — what he said (qawl), what he did (fi'l), and what he silently approved (taqrir).

Role in Islamic thought

Together with the Quran, the Sunnah forms the basis of Sharia (Islamic law). Where the Quran lays down general principles, the Sunnah explains and operationalizes them: for example, the Quran commands prayer and almsgiving, while the Sunnah specifies how and when they are performed. The deeds and sayings of Muhammad were transmitted through the hadith — short reports, each with a chain of narrators (isnad) — which were collected and authenticated by scholars over the following centuries into canonical collections such as those of al-Bukhari and Muslim.

Development

The jurist al-Shafi'i (767–820) is generally credited with establishing the doctrine that the Sunnah of the Prophet is a binding, divinely sanctioned source of law second only to the Quran, superior to local custom, consensus, or the opinions of later authorities. Sunni schools of law (Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, and Hanbali) all accept the Sunnah, while differing in how particular reports are authenticated and applied. Shia Islam maintains its own hadith corpus, which includes the teachings of the Twelve Imams alongside those of Muhammad.

Sunnah in worship and daily life

The term is also used for recommended (but not obligatory) acts of worship modelled on Muhammad's practice, such as extra voluntary prayers performed before or after the five obligatory daily prayers, as distinct from fard (obligatory) duties.
