Mid-Autumn Festival

The Mid-Autumn Festival, also known as the Moon Festival or Mooncake Festival, is a harvest festival celebrated in Chinese culture. It is held on the 15th day of the 8th month of the Chinese lunisolar calendar with a full moon at night, corresponding to mid-September to early October of the Gregorian calendar. The festival is a time for family reunion, moon worship, and thanksgiving for the harvest.

Festival traditions include eating mooncakes — baked pastries often filled with lotus seed paste, red bean paste, or salted egg yolks — carrying and displaying brightly lit lanterns, lighting lanterns on towers, and performing dragon and lion dances in some regions. The festival is associated with the legend of Chang'e, the Moon goddess of immortality, who is said to live on the Moon with the Jade Rabbit.

Celebration

The Mid-Autumn Festival is one of the most important holidays in Chinese culture and is also celebrated in Vietnam (as Tết Trung Thu, a children's festival) and by communities in Korea (Chuseok, on the same day), Japan (Tsukimi), Malaysia, Singapore, and elsewhere in East and Southeast Asia. Public holidays are observed in China, Taiwan, Hong Kong, Macau, Vietnam and South Korea.
