Forlì

Forlì (Italian: [forˈli]; Romagnol: Furlè) is a city and comune in Emilia-Romagna, northern Italy, on the Via Emilia. It is the capital of the province of Forlì-Cesena and, together with Cesena, forms a twin-city administrative centre. The city has a population of around 117,000 and lies at the foot of the Apennines, about 30 kilometres from the Adriatic coast.

History

The city was founded by the Romans in 188 BC as Forum Livii, named after the consul Gaius Livius Salinator, and grew as a station on the Via Aemilia between Placentia and Ariminum (Rimini). After the fall of the Western Roman Empire it passed through Byzantine, Lombard and papal control. In the Middle Ages Forlì was a free commune, frequently allied with the Ghibellines, and in the 13th and 14th centuries it was ruled by the Ordelaffi family. In 1500 the city was conquered by Cesare Borgia for the Papal States. Caterina Sforza, the formidable countess of Forlì, famously defended the Rocca di Ravaldino against Borgia's forces before surrendering.

Main sights

The heart of the city is Piazza Aurelio Saffi, one of the largest squares in Italy, dominated by the Romanesque Abbey of San Mercuriale with its tall 12th-century bell tower, and by the Palazzo delle Poste, built in the 1930s. Other landmarks include the Cathedral of the Holy Cross (Duomo), the Rocca di Ravaldino fortress, and the San Domenico museum complex, which hosts major art exhibitions in the former Dominican convent near the city walls.

Economy and transport

Forlì's economy is based on agriculture, food processing, furniture manufacture and mechanical engineering. A campus of the University of Bologna, specialising in economics, political science and interpreting, is based in the city. Forlì Airport (Luigi Ridolfi Airport) serves the Romagna area, and the city sits on the Bologna–Ancona railway line.

Notable people

Forlì was the birthplace of the painter Melozzo da Forlì (c. 1438–1494), the historian Flavio Biondo (1392–1463), and Aurelio Saffi (1819–1890), a leading figure of the Italian Risorgimento. Benito Mussolini was born in nearby Predappio and is buried there.
