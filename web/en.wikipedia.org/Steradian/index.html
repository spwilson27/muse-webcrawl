The steradian (symbol: sr) is the SI unit of solid angle, the three-dimensional analogue of the radian. One steradian is the solid angle subtended at the centre of a sphere by a patch of its surface whose area equals the square of the sphere's radius (A = r²).

Definition

A solid angle Ω in steradians equals the intercepted surface area divided by the radius squared: Ω = A/r². A complete sphere subtends 4π steradians, just as a complete circle subtends 2π radians. The steradian is dimensionless, like the radian.

Uses

Steradians appear in photometry (luminous intensity is measured in candelas, lumens per steradian), radiometry, antenna gain, and astrophysics. Conversion to square degrees uses 1 sr = (180/π)² ≈ 3282.8 square degrees.
