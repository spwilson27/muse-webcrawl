Battle of Molodi

The Battle of Molodi was fought from 30 July to 2 August 1572 near Molodi, about 55 kilometres south of Moscow, between the forces of the Crimean Khanate under Khan Devlet I Giray and the army of the Tsardom of Russia commanded by Prince Mikhail Vorotynsky and Prince Dmitry Khvorostinin. It is regarded as one of the most important battles in Russian history, ending the threat of a major Crimean invasion of central Russia.

Background

In 1571 Devlet Giray had burned Moscow, devastating the capital and carrying off tens of thousands of captives. Determined to finish the conquest and subjugate Muscovy, the Khan returned in 1572 with a large army, often estimated at 40,000–60,000 men, supported by Ottoman janissaries and artillery. The Russians, having reorganised after the disaster, fielded roughly 20,000–25,000 troops behind field fortifications.

The battle

The Russians took position behind the gulyay-gorod, a mobile fortress of wooden shields mounted on wagons, armed with cannon and arquebusiers. Khvorostinin's regiment lured the Crimean vanguard into the killing ground and then, after several days of assaults, led a bold outflanking march that fell on the Khan's rear while Vorotynsky attacked from the fortifications. The Crimean army was routed; only a fraction of it escaped back to the steppe, and Devlet Giray's sons were killed.

Aftermath

The victory secured Moscow and effectively ended large-scale Crimean invasions of the Russian heartland, although raids on the southern frontier continued for two centuries. Vorotynsky was briefly celebrated, then arrested and died under torture in 1573 amid Ivan the Terrible's suspicions.
