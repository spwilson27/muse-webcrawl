Fulbright Program

The Fulbright Program, officially the Fulbright–Hays Program, is a United States cultural exchange program founded in 1946 by Senator J. William Fulbright. It is among the most prestigious scholarship programs in the world, providing grants for students, scholars, teachers, and professionals to study, teach, and conduct research abroad, and for foreign nationals to do the same in the United States.

History

The program was established by the Fulbright Act of 1946, signed by President Harry S. Truman. Initial funding came from the sale of surplus American war property abroad, with foreign governments accepting the proceeds as payment of debts. Fulbright envisioned the program as a vehicle for promoting peace and mutual understanding in the aftermath of the Second World War.

Components

Major components include the Fulbright U.S. Student Program, the Fulbright Foreign Student Program, the Fulbright Scholar Program for faculty and professionals, the Fulbright English Teaching Assistant program, and the Fulbright–Hays program for research in modern foreign languages and area studies.

Administration

The program is administered by the U.S. Department of State's Bureau of Educational and Cultural Affairs, working with binational Fulbright commissions in some 50 countries and with the Institute of International Education in the United States. The J. William Fulbright Foreign Scholarship Board, appointed by the U.S. president, oversees selection policies.

Notable alumni

Fulbright alumni include more than 60 Nobel Prize laureates, 40 heads of state or government, 39 MacArthur Foundation fellows, and numerous Pulitzer Prize winners.
