Battle of Lützen (1632)

The Battle of Lützen, fought on 16 November 1632 (6 November Old Style) near Lützen in Saxony, was one of the major battles of the Thirty Years' War. A Swedish army under King Gustavus Adolphus defeated the Imperial army of Albrecht von Wallenstein, but Gustavus Adolphus was killed in the fighting — one of the most consequential deaths of the war.

Background

Wallenstein, recalled to Imperial service, had taken up winter quarters around Lützen and sent Gottfried zu Pappenheim's corps toward Halle, temporarily weakening his army. Gustavus Adolphus advanced to attack before Wallenstein could concentrate, marching through morning mist that delayed the Swedish deployment until late morning.

The battle

As the fog lifted and returned in turns, the Swedes attacked the Imperial line along the Leipzig road. On the Swedish right, Gustavus Adolphus led a cavalry charge to relieve his hard-pressed infantry and was shot and killed in the murk; his riderless horse carried the news through the army. Command passed to Bernhard of Saxe-Weimar, who concealed the king's death and rallied the Swedes to a second assault. Pappenheim returned to the field, was mortally wounded, and by nightfall Wallenstein ordered a retreat, abandoning his artillery.

Aftermath

Both sides lost roughly a quarter of their men. The Swedes held the field and claimed victory, but the death of Gustavus Adolphus deprived the Protestant cause of its greatest commander and political leader; Swedish direction passed to Chancellor Axel Oxenstierna and the Heilbronn League, and the war dragged on for sixteen more years.
