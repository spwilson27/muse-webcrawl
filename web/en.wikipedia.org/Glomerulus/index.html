Glomerulus

A glomerulus (plural glomeruli) is a network of small blood vessels (capillaries) known as a tuft, located at the beginning of a nephron in the kidney. Each of the approximately one million glomeruli in a human kidney is supported by a basement membrane and surrounded by Bowman's capsule. The glomerulus performs the first step in filtering blood to form urine: water and small solutes pass into the capsule while cells and plasma proteins are retained in the blood.

Structure

Blood enters the glomerulus through the afferent arteriole and leaves through the efferent arteriole, which is narrower, maintaining the high pressure needed for filtration. The filtration barrier has three layers: the fenestrated endothelium of the capillaries, the glomerular basement membrane, and the slit diaphragms between the foot processes (pedicels) of podocytes, specialised epithelial cells that wrap around the capillaries. Mesangial cells lie between the capillaries and provide structural support, contractile regulation of surface area, and phagocytic clearance.

Function

The glomerular filtration rate (GFR) — about 180 litres per day in a healthy adult — depends on the balance of hydrostatic and oncotic pressures across the filtration barrier (Starling forces) and is regulated by the juxtaglomerular apparatus, which senses sodium chloride delivery at the macula densa and modulates renin release and arteriolar tone. Damage to the glomerulus causes glomerular diseases (glomerulonephritis and glomerulosclerosis), which may leak protein and blood into the urine and progress to kidney failure.

Other uses of the term

In neuroanatomy, a glomerulus is also a spherical structure in the olfactory bulb where axons of olfactory receptor neurons synapse with the dendrites of mitral and tufted cells.
