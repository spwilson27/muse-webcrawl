Erich Maria Remarque

Born: 22 June 1898, Osnabrück, Germany
Died: 25 September 1970, Locarno, Switzerland
Occupation: Novelist
Notable work: All Quiet on the Western Front

Erich Maria Remarque (born Erich Paul Remark; 22 June 1898 – 25 September 1970) was a German-born novelist. His landmark novel All Quiet on the Western Front (1928), about the German military experience of the First World War, was an international bestseller that created a new literary genre and was adapted into several films.

Life

Remarque was drafted into the German army at 18 and served on the Western Front, where he was wounded by shrapnel. After the war he worked as a teacher, journalist, and editor. The success of All Quiet on the Western Front made him famous, but when the Nazis came to power his books were banned and burned, and his citizenship was revoked. He emigrated, eventually settling in the United States and later in Switzerland.

Works

His later novels include Three Comrades (1936), Arch of Triumph (1945), and A Time to Love and a Time to Die (1954), many of them dealing with the experiences of German exiles and the aftermath of war.

Legacy

Remarque is regarded as one of the foremost war novelists of the twentieth century, and All Quiet on the Western Front remains one of the most widely read anti-war works ever written.
