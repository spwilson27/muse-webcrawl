Lake Tahoe

Lake Tahoe
Location: California–Nevada, US
Surface area: 496 km²
Max. depth: 501 m
Elevation: 1,897 m

Lake Tahoe is a large freshwater lake in the Sierra Nevada of the United States, straddling the state line between California and Nevada. It is the largest alpine lake in North America and, at 501 metres deep, the second-deepest lake in the United States after Crater Lake in Oregon.

Geography

The lake is about 35 kilometres long and 19 kilometres wide, with 116 kilometres of shoreline. Its only outlet is the Truckee River, which flows northeast to Pyramid Lake in Nevada. Roughly two-thirds of the shoreline lies in California. The lake is renowned for the clarity of its water and the panorama of surrounding mountains.

History

The Washoe people inhabited the Tahoe Basin for thousands of years; "Tahoe" derives from the Washoe word dáʔaw, meaning "the lake". John C. Frémont's expedition sighted the lake in 1844. The Comstock Lode silver boom drove logging of the basin's forests in the 19th century, and the lake later became a major resort destination, hosting the 1960 Winter Olympics at nearby Squaw Valley (now Palisades Tahoe).

Tourism and environment

Lake Tahoe is a year-round destination for skiing, boating, and hiking. Environmental programmes work to protect the lake's famous clarity, which has been threatened by development, road runoff, and invasive species.
