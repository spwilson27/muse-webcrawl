Marine Helicopter Squadron One (HMX-1), nicknamed the "Nighthawks", is a United States Marine Corps helicopter squadron based at Marine Corps Air Facility Quantico, Virginia. Founded in 1947, it is responsible for the helicopter transport of the President of the United States — its aircraft carry the call sign "Marine One" whenever the President is aboard — as well as the Vice President, cabinet officials and visiting heads of state.

Missions

The squadron's "Green Side" performs operational test and evaluation of new helicopters and flies support missions with MV-22B Ospreys, while the "White Side" operates the white-topped presidential fleet. Presidential helicopters have included the VH-3D Sea King (in service since the 1960s) and the VH-60N White Hawk; the Sikorsky VH-92A Patriot began replacing them in the 2020s after the cancelled VH-71 programme.

History

HMX-1 began presidential transport in 1957, when Dwight D. Eisenhower used an H-13 Sioux from the squadron. Every president since has relied on HMX-1 for short-haul travel from the White House South Lawn. The squadron maintains exacting standards: its Marines are hand-selected and hold Yankee White security clearances.
