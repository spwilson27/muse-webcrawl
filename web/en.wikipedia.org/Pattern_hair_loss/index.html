Pattern hair loss

SynonymsAndrogenetic alopecia, androgenic alopecia, male-pattern baldness, female-pattern hair loss
SpecialtyDermatology
CausesGenetic sensitivity of hair follicles to dihydrotestosterone (DHT)
TreatmentMinoxidil, finasteride, hair transplantation

Pattern hair loss (also known as androgenetic alopecia or androgenic alopecia) is a hair loss condition that primarily affects the top and front of the scalp. In males it is called male-pattern hair loss and typically presents as a receding hairline at the temples and thinning at the crown (vertex), often progressing to partial or complete baldness. In females, female-pattern hair loss usually appears as diffuse thinning over the crown with the frontal hairline retained.

Pattern hair loss is the most common cause of hair loss in humans. By age 50, roughly half of men and a significant proportion of women are affected to some degree, and prevalence increases with age.

Causes

The condition results from a genetically determined sensitivity of scalp hair follicles to dihydrotestosterone (DHT), a metabolite of testosterone produced by the enzyme 5-alpha-reductase. In susceptible follicles, DHT progressively shortens the growth (anagen) phase of the hair cycle and miniaturizes the follicle, so successive hairs become finer, shorter, and less pigmented until growth ceases. Male-pattern loss is polygenic, with the androgen receptor gene on the X chromosome among the best-established contributors. Female-pattern loss follows a less clearly understood, partly similar mechanism.

Diagnosis and classification

Male-pattern loss is commonly graded on the Norwood scale (types I–VII), while female-pattern loss is graded on the Ludwig scale (grades I–III). Diagnosis is usually clinical, based on the distribution of loss; dermoscopy showing hair-shaft diameter diversity supports the diagnosis, and biopsy is occasionally used to exclude other causes such as alopecia areata or scarring alopecias.

Treatment

Two medications have the strongest evidence base. Topical minoxidil, a vasodilator originally developed for hypertension, prolongs the anagen phase and is used by both sexes. Oral finasteride, a 5-alpha-reductase inhibitor, lowers DHT levels and is approved for men; it can slow progression and partially regrow hair, with benefits lost after discontinuation. Dutasteride is used off-label in some countries. Surgical options include follicular unit hair transplantation and scalp reduction. Low-level laser therapy and platelet-rich plasma are also marketed, with more limited evidence. Camouflage methods include hairstyling, wigs, and scalp micropigmentation.

Society and culture

Although medically benign, pattern hair loss can cause significant psychological distress, affecting self-image and quality of life. It has been a subject of remedies since antiquity—Hippocrates noted the association with eunuchs, who do not develop the condition, an early hint of its hormonal basis.
