Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme

The Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme (IGMDP) was an Indian Ministry of Defence programme for the research and development of a comprehensive range of missiles. It was launched in 1983 under the leadership of Dr A. P. J. Abdul Kalam, who had previously directed the SLV-3 satellite launch vehicle project, and was managed by the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) together with Ordnance Factories and academic partners.

The five missiles

The programme set out to develop five missile systems:

  • Prithvi — a short-range surface-to-surface ballistic missile.
  • Agni — a medium/intermediate-range ballistic missile, originally conceived as a technology demonstrator for re-entry.
  • Akash — a medium-range surface-to-air missile system.
  • Trishul — a short-range surface-to-air missile.
  • Nag — a third-generation "fire-and-forget" anti-tank guided missile.

Development and outcome

The first missile to be tested was Prithvi in 1988, followed by the Agni technology demonstrator in 1989. The programme built key technologies — re-entry vehicles, composite materials, ring laser gyroscopes, phased-array radar (Rajendra) for Akash — and involved some 78 laboratories, universities and industrial partners. Trishul was eventually closed as a technology demonstrator in 2008 after persistent delays. The IGMDP was formally concluded in January 2008, after the successful induction of Prithvi, Agni and Akash and the maturation of Nag; the Agni series continued under separate strategic programmes, producing longer-range variants including the intercontinental-range Agni-V.

Legacy

The programme is widely credited with establishing India's indigenous missile development base and nurturing a generation of aerospace scientists; Kalam became known as the "Missile Man of India" and later served as President of India (2002–2007).
