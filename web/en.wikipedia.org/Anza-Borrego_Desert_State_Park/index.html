Anza-Borrego Desert State Park

Location: San Diego, Imperial and Riverside counties, California, US
Area: about 585,000 acres (237,000 ha)
Established: 1933

Anza-Borrego Desert State Park is a California state park in the Colorado Desert of Southern California, the largest state park in California and one of the largest in the United States, at roughly 585,000 acres. Its name combines that of the Spanish explorer Juan Bautista de Anza, who passed through in 1774, with borrego, Spanish for the bighorn sheep that inhabit its mountains.

Geography and nature

The park surrounds the town of Borrego Springs and ranges from eroded badlands and alluvial fans to rugged mountains over 6,000 feet. Habitats include creosote scrub, ocotillo flats, slot canyons such as The Slot, and native fan palm oases at Borrego Palm Canyon. Wildlife includes peninsular bighorn sheep, kit foxes, roadrunners, and golden eagles; after wet winters the desert floor erupts in celebrated wildflower blooms that draw large crowds. The park is also an International Dark-Sky Park, noted for astronomy.

History

Indigenous peoples, including the Cahuilla and Kumeyaay, lived in the region for millennia. Anza's expedition opened a land route from Sonora to Alta California. The park was established in 1933 and expanded repeatedly; it adjoins other protected lands in a vast desert conservation complex. The visitor centre at Borrego Springs interprets its geology, fossils — the area yields rich Pliocene and Pleistocene mammal remains — and ecology.
