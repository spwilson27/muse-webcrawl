Bauhaus

The Staatliches Bauhaus, commonly known as the Bauhaus (German for "building house"), was a German art school operational from 1919 to 1933 that combined crafts and the fine arts. Founded by architect Walter Gropius in Weimar, it was famous for its approach to design, which attempted to unify individual artistic vision with mass production and function. The Bauhaus is widely regarded as the most influential modernist art school of the 20th century, shaping modern architecture, graphic design, typography, and industrial design worldwide.

History

Weimar period (1919–1925)

Gropius founded the school by merging the Weimar Academy of Fine Arts with the Weimar School of Arts and Crafts. His 1919 manifesto called for a new unity of art and craft — "a new guild of craftsmen" — and pictured a cathedral of socialism on its cover. Students began with a preliminary course (Vorkurs), developed first by Johannes Itten, then by László Moholy-Nagy and Josef Albers, which taught materials, form, and colour theory (influenced by teachers such as Paul Klee and Wassily Kandinsky). Workshops covered metal, weaving, pottery, printing, theatre, and typography. From 1923 the slogan shifted to "Art and Technology — a new unity," embracing industrial production.

Dessau period (1925–1932)

Political pressure from the conservative Thuringian government forced the move to Dessau in 1925. Gropius designed the iconic Bauhaus Building there, with its glass curtain wall workshop wing, and the Masters' Houses. Hannes Meyer succeeded Gropius in 1928, emphasising social need and affordability; he was dismissed in 1930 and replaced by Ludwig Mies van der Rohe.

Berlin and dissolution (1932–1933)

The Nazi-dominated Dessau council closed the school in 1932. Mies reopened it as a private institution in Berlin, but after the Nazi seizure of power the Gestapo raided and closed it in April 1933; the masters dissolved the Bauhaus in July 1933.

Legacy

Bauhaus teachers and students emigrated worldwide, spreading its ideas: Albers to Black Mountain College, Gropius and Marcel Breuer to Harvard, Mies to Chicago's Armour Institute (later IIT), and Moholy-Nagy to found the New Bauhaus in Chicago. The Bauhaus buildings in Weimar and Dessau, and related sites in Bernau, were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1996 (extended 2017). The centenary in 2019 was marked by new museums in Weimar and Dessau.
