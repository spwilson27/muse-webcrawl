Matriarchs

The foremothers of Israel in the Hebrew Bible

The Matriarchs (Hebrew: Imahot, "the Mothers") are the four foremothers of the people of Israel in the Hebrew Bible: Sarah, wife of Abraham; Rebecca (Rivkah), wife of Isaac; and Leah and Rachel, the two wives of Jacob. Together with the three Patriarchs — Abraham, Isaac and Jacob — they stand at the head of the genealogies of Genesis and of Jewish tradition.

The four mothers

Sarah, long barren, miraculously bears Isaac in old age and is buried with Abraham in the Cave of Machpelah at Hebron. Rebecca, chosen as Isaac's wife in Mesopotamia, favours her younger son Jacob and engineers his receipt of the blessing. Jacob, fleeing to Haran, works fourteen years to marry Rachel, but is first tricked into marrying her elder sister Leah; between them and their maidservants Bilhah and Zilpah, they become the mothers of the twelve tribes of Israel. Rachel, dying in childbirth with Benjamin, was buried on the road to Bethlehem, where "Rachel's Tomb" remains a pilgrimage site.

In tradition

In rabbinic Judaism the Matriarchs are commemorated alongside the Patriarchs in liturgy — the first blessing of the Amidah invokes the "God of Abraham, God of Isaac and God of Jacob," and later prayer books and modern liturgies add the mothers by name. Traditional tombs of Sarah, Rebecca and Leah are located in the Cave of the Patriarchs in Hebron.
