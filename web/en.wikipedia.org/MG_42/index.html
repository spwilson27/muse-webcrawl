The MG 42 (shortened from German Maschinengewehr 42, "machine gun 42") is a German recoil-operated, air-cooled general-purpose machine gun chambered in 7.92×57mm Mauser, developed during the Second World War and introduced in 1942 to supplement and replace the earlier MG 34.

Design

The MG 42 was designed for cheap, rapid mass production from stamped and welded steel parts. It is famous for its very high cyclic rate — around 1,200 rounds per minute, among the fastest of any single-barrelled infantry weapon — which produced a distinctive tearing sound that led Allied soldiers to nickname it "Hitler's buzzsaw". A quick-change barrel system let the gunner swap an overheated barrel in seconds. It served as both a light machine gun on its bipod and a sustained-fire weapon on the Lafette tripod.

Legacy

Some 400,000 were built during the war. The design directly influenced post-war weapons: West Germany rechambered it as the MG1/MG3 in 7.62×51mm NATO, which remains in service, and the American M60 drew on its feed mechanism. Variants and copies were built in Yugoslavia, Spain, and elsewhere, and the MG 42 is still encountered in conflicts worldwide.
