Mount Kilimanjaro is a dormant volcano in Tanzania. It is the highest mountain in Africa and the highest single free-standing mountain above sea level in the world, rising to 5,895 metres (19,341 ft) at Uhuru Peak.

Geology

Kilimanjaro is a large dormant stratovolcano composed of three distinct cones: Kibo (the highest), Mawenzi (5,149 m) and Shira (4,005 m). Uhuru Peak is the highest summit on Kibo's crater rim. The mountain's glaciers have retreated markedly since the early 20th century.

Climbing

Kilimanjaro is a popular trekking destination; tens of thousands climb it each year via routes including the Marangu, Machame, Lemosho and Rongai routes. No technical climbing is required, but altitude sickness is a significant risk. The mountain lies within Kilimanjaro National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Climate and ecology

The mountain passes through five distinct ecological zones from cultivated foothills through montane forest, heath and moorland, alpine desert, to an arctic summit zone.
