Abelard and Héloïse

Peter Abelard: 1079–1142, French scholastic philosopher

Héloïse d'Argenteuil: c. 1100–1164, French abbess and writer

Peter Abelard and Héloïse d'Argenteuil were a celebrated medieval couple whose love affair, secret marriage and forced separation became one of the most famous stories of the twelfth century. Abelard was a leading scholastic philosopher and theologian in Paris; Héloïse, his student, was renowned for her learning in Latin, Greek and Hebrew.

The affair

Around 1115 Abelard was engaged by Canon Fulbert of Notre Dame to tutor his niece Héloïse. Teacher and pupil became lovers, and Héloïse bore a son, Astrolabe. They married secretly, but when the relationship became public, Fulbert's kinsmen attacked Abelard and castrated him. Abelard withdrew to become a monk at Saint-Denis, and Héloïse took the veil at the convent of Argenteuil.

Letters and later life

Years later the two exchanged a remarkable series of letters, beginning with Abelard's autobiographical Historia Calamitatum ("Story of My Misfortunes"). Héloïse became abbess of the Paraclete, an oratory Abelard had founded in Champagne, while Abelard continued to write influential works on logic, ethics and theology despite repeated condemnation for heresy, notably at the Council of Sens in 1141 at the instigation of Bernard of Clairvaux.

Legacy

Their correspondence is considered a foundation of French and European literature and an early monument of epistolary writing. The two are traditionally buried together at Père Lachaise Cemetery in Paris, a tomb that remains a pilgrimage site for lovers.
