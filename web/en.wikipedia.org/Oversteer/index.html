Oversteer

Oversteer is a handling condition in which a vehicle turns more sharply than the driver intends because the rear tires lose grip before the front tires, causing the rear of the car to swing outward. In vehicle dynamics terms, the rear axle's slip angle exceeds the front's. The opposite condition is understeer, in which the front tires lose grip first and the car runs wide.

Causes and control

Oversteer can be provoked by excessive speed for a corner, sudden lift-off of the throttle mid-corner (lift-off oversteer), braking while turning, or applying power in a rear-wheel-drive car. Rear-engined and mid-engined layouts, stiff rear suspension, and worn or over-inflated rear tires can also promote it. Correcting oversteer requires counter-steering — steering into the slide — while modulating the throttle; modern electronic stability control systems brake individual wheels to prevent spins. Deliberately induced oversteer is the basis of drifting in motorsport.
