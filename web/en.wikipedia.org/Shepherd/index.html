Shepherd

A shepherd is a person who tends, herds, feeds, and guards sheep. Shepherding is among humanity's oldest occupations, arising with the domestication of sheep around 10,000 years ago in western Asia.

Work

The shepherd moves the flock between pastures, protects it from predators — traditionally with the aid of herding dogs and livestock guardian dogs — assists at lambing, shears wool, and treats illness. In many regions shepherds practised transhumance, seasonal migration between lowland winter and mountain summer pastures, following ancient drove roads such as Spain's cañadas and Italy's tratturi.

Cultural significance

The shepherd is a potent religious and literary symbol: in the Hebrew Bible and New Testament, God and Jesus are figured as shepherds ("The Lord is my shepherd"; "the Good Shepherd"), and pastoral poetry from Theocritus and Virgil onward idealised shepherd life. The shepherd's crook became the emblem of bishops, and kings of ancient Near Eastern civilisations styled themselves shepherds of their people.
