Metamerism (color)

Metamerism
FieldColorimetry, colour science
Related conceptTrichromacy
Failure modeMetameric failure under changed lighting

In colorimetry, metamerism is the matched appearance of two colours with different spectral power distributions. Colours that match this way are called metamers. Because human vision reduces a spectrum to three signals from the cone cells in the retina, physically different spectra can produce identical responses and therefore look the same.

Causes and types

Metamerism arises from the way the visual system samples light. A match made under one illuminant may fail under another — illuminant metameric failure — because the spectra interact differently with the new light source. Observer metamerism occurs because individuals' colour-matching functions differ slightly, so a pair matched for one observer may mismatch for another. Geometric metamerism depends on viewing angle and surface texture.

Significance

Metamerism is fundamental to colour reproduction: printing, photography, and displays all work by producing metamers of original colours rather than reproducing their spectra. It is also a practical problem in industry — automotive paints, textiles, and dentistry must match under varied lighting — and is quantified by a metamerism index in colour quality control.
