The International Six Days Enduro (ISDE), formerly the International Six Days Trial (ISDT), is an annual FIM off-road motorcycle competition, the longest-running event on the FIM calendar and often described as the "Olympics of motorcycling". Riders from national teams compete over six days, covering long distances of off-road terrain with timed special tests, while having to maintain and repair their own machines under strict rules.

History

The first International Six Days Trial was held in 1913 in Carlisle, England, testing the reliability of motorcycles and the skill of riders. Originally centred in Europe, the event travelled further afield from the 1970s onwards, being held for the first time in the United States in 1973. In 1981 the FIM changed the name from "Trial" to "Enduro" to reflect the event's true nature. The event was not held during the world wars, and its hundredth anniversary edition era marked more than a century of competition.

Format

Teams of four riders contest the World Trophy; the Junior World Trophy is for riders under 23, and the Women's World Trophy began in 2007. Club teams and individual riders also compete for gold, silver and bronze medals awarded by performance relative to the class winner. Riders follow a set route each day with time checks and special tests, and overnight the motorcycles are held in a closed park (parc fermé), with limited working periods in which the riders themselves must do maintenance.
