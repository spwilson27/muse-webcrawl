Mystic chord

In music, the mystic chord or Prometheus chord is a six-note synthetic chord and the fundamental sonority of Alexander Scriabin's orchestral work Prometheus: The Poem of Fire (1910). It is a quartal hexachord: a chord built from superposed fourths.

In its canonical arrangement beginning on C, the chord consists of the notes C, F♯, B♭, E, A, and D — a series of fourths (augmented, diminished and perfect) stacked above the root.

Structure and analysis

The chord has been analysed in several ways: as a scale of whole tones with one note altered, as an altered dominant harmony (a dominant 13th chord with raised 11th and flat 9th resolutions in jazz-like voicings), and as the pitch content of six notes of the acoustic (Lydian dominant) scale. Scriabin derived both the harmony and much of the melodic material of Prometheus from this chord, using transpositions and rearrangements of it as the work's basis.

Usage

The mystic chord gained its nickname in English-language criticism reportedly via the theosophical associations of Scriabin's late music. Variations of the quartal voicing appear in later twentieth-century music and in jazz harmony, where similar stacked-fourth sonorities became standard.
