Cost-of-living index

A cost-of-living index is a theoretical price index that measures the relative cost of living over time or regions. It is an index that measures differences in the price of goods and services, and allows for substitutions to other items as prices change. Cost-of-living indices are used to compare the cost of maintaining a given standard of living in different places or periods.

Methodology

Indices are typically constructed from a basket of goods and services — food, housing, transport, healthcare and other categories — priced in each location or period. Theoretical cost-of-living indices, such as the Konüs index, define the minimum expenditure needed to maintain a fixed level of utility, while practical statistical indices such as consumer price indices approximate them using fixed baskets (Laspeyres) or current-period weights (Paasche).

Uses

Employers use geographic cost-of-living indices to set expatriate allowances and adjust salaries by location. Governments use related measures as cost-of-living adjustments (COLA) for social security benefits, pensions and tax thresholds. International surveys, such as those by Mercer and the Economist Intelligence Unit, rank cities worldwide by the cost of living for expatriates.

Limitations

Indices depend heavily on the basket chosen, the treatment of housing, and assumptions about substitution between goods. They may not reflect the experience of particular households, and comparisons can be distorted by quality changes and exchange-rate movements.
