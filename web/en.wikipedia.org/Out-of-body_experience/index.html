Out-of-body experience

An out-of-body experience (OBE) is an experience in which a person seems to perceive the world from a location outside their physical body. It is a form of autoscopy, distinct from the doppelgänger-like heautoscopy in that the viewpoint is felt to be outside the body, often above it looking down.

Causes

OBEs are reported in near-death experiences, during sleep paralysis and lucid dreaming, in migraine and epilepsy, and after taking dissociative drugs. Neurological research links them to disturbed multisensory integration at the temporoparietal junction; electrical stimulation there can induce OBE-like sensations.

Prevalence and study

Surveys suggest roughly 10% of people report at least one OBE in their lifetime. Parapsychological claims of veridical perception during OBEs have not been reliably replicated under controlled conditions, and mainstream science treats OBEs as hallucinatory experiences generated by the brain.
