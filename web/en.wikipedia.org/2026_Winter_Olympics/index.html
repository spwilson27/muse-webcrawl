The 2026 Winter Olympics, officially the XXV Olympic Winter Games and branded as Milano Cortina 2026, are an international winter multi-sport event scheduled to take place from 6 to 22 February 2026 across sites in Lombardy and north-eastern Italy, principally the cities of Milan and Cortina d'Ampezzo.

Host selection

The Italian bid defeated a joint Stockholm–Åre bid by 47 votes to 34 at the IOC Session in Lausanne on 24 June 2019. It will be Italy's third Winter Olympics, after Cortina d'Ampezzo in 1956 and Turin in 2006, and the first to be officially co-hosted by two cities. Milan will host the ice events, including figure skating, ice hockey and speed skating, while Cortina hosts the sliding events and women's alpine skiing; other clusters include Val di Fiemme (ski jumping and Nordic combined), Livigno and Bormio (freestyle skiing, snowboarding and men's alpine skiing) and Anterselva (biathlon).

Programme

The programme comprises 116 medal events in 16 disciplines — the largest Winter Games programme to date — including the Olympic debut of ski mountaineering, with sprint and mixed relay events. The opening ceremony is planned for the San Siro football stadium in Milan, and the closing ceremony for the Roman amphitheatre, the Verona Arena. The mascots, Tina and Milo, are a pair of stoat siblings named after Cortina and Milan.

Preparations

Preparations have involved the construction of a new sliding centre in Cortina — a point of contention with the IOC, which had advocated using an existing track abroad — and extensive transport upgrades across the Alpine venues. As with other recent Games, costs and the environmental impact of Alpine construction have been subjects of public debate in Italy.
