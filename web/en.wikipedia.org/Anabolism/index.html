Anabolism

Anabolism builds complex molecules from simpler precursors, powered by energy from catabolism.

Anabolism (from Greek: ἀνα-, "upward" and βάλλειν, "to throw") is the set of metabolic pathways that construct molecules from smaller units. These biosynthetic reactions require energy, usually in the form of consuming adenosine triphosphate (ATP). Anabolism is the building-up aspect of metabolism, whereas catabolism is the breaking-down aspect; together the two make up metabolism as a whole.

Stages

Large molecules such as polysaccharides, proteins, and nucleic acids are assembled in three broad stages: first, the production of precursors (amino acids, monosaccharides, nucleotides); second, their activation into reactive forms at the expense of ATP; and third, the assembly of the activated precursors into complex molecules. Examples of anabolic processes include photosynthesis, gluconeogenesis, protein synthesis, and the synthesis of fatty acids and glycogen.

Regulation

Anabolic hormones include insulin, growth hormone, and testosterone, which stimulate construction of tissue; anabolic steroids are synthetic derivatives of testosterone designed to promote muscle growth. Anabolism predominates in growing organisms and in states of nutritional abundance, whereas catabolism predominates during fasting and illness.
