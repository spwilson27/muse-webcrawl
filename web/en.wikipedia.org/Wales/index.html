Wales (Welsh: Cymru) is a country that is part of the United Kingdom. It is bordered by the Irish Sea to the north and west, England to the east, the Bristol Channel to the south, and the Celtic Sea to the south-west. As of the 2021 census it had a population of 3,107,500. It has a total area of 21,218 square kilometres (8,192 sq mi) and over 2,700 kilometres (1,680 mi) of coastline. It is largely mountainous with its higher peaks in the north and central areas, including Yr Wyddfa (Snowdon), its highest summit.

The modern Welsh identity emerged among the Celtic Britons after the Roman withdrawal from Britain in the 5th century, and Wales was briefly united under Gruffudd ap Llywelyn in 1057. Following the Edwardian conquest in the 13th century, Wales was annexed by England and incorporated into the English legal system under the Laws in Wales Acts 1535 and 1542. In the 19th century, distinctive Welsh politics developed, and Welsh Liberalism became a driving force. Following the establishment of the Welsh nationalist party Plaid Cymru in 1925 and the Welsh Language Society in 1962, the first Welsh devolution referendum in 1997 narrowly approved a Welsh Assembly, with greater legislative powers following the Government of Wales Act 2006.

Geography

Wales lies on the western side of central southern Great Britain. The Cambrian Mountains run through the centre of the country; Snowdonia (Eryri) in the north-west contains the highest ground. The Brecon Beacons (Bannau Brycheiniog) lie in the south, and the country has three national parks: Eryri, Bannau Brycheiniog and the Pembrokeshire Coast. Major rivers include the Dee, Wye, Severn (whose source is in Wales), Usk and Taff.

Demographics and language

The largest population centres are in South Wales: Cardiff, Swansea, Newport and the industrial South Wales Valleys. The Welsh language (Cymraeg), a Celtic language, was spoken by 17.8% of the population in the 2021 census, with the highest proportions in the north-west (Gwynedd and Anglesey). Since the Welsh Language (Wales) Measure 2011, Welsh has official status in Wales, and the Welsh Government targets one million Welsh speakers by 2050.

Government

Wales is represented in the UK Parliament at Westminster, and has its own devolved legislature, the Senedd (formerly the National Assembly for Wales), established in 1999 and based at the Senedd building in Cardiff Bay. The Welsh Government, led by the First Minister of Wales, is responsible for devolved matters including health, education, transport and agriculture.

Culture

Wales is known as the "land of song", with traditions of male voice choirs, eisteddfodau (festivals of literature and music, culminating in the National Eisteddfod), and hymn singing. Its symbols include the red dragon (Y Ddraig Goch) on its flag — one of the few national flags to feature a dragon — the daffodil and the leek. Rugby union is the national sport; the Welsh national team plays at the Principality Stadium in Cardiff.
