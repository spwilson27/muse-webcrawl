Toxic waste

Barrels of hazardous waste awaiting disposal

Toxic waste is any unwanted material that can cause death, injury or birth defects in living organisms, or that poisons the environment. It is a subset of hazardous waste and includes chemical wastes from industry and agriculture, radioactive waste, certain medical wastes, and contaminated materials such as solvents, pesticides, heavy metals and by-products of manufacturing.

Sources and effects

Major sources include chemical and petrochemical plants, metal processing, mining, electronics manufacturing (producing e-waste rich in lead, cadmium and mercury), hospitals and households. Exposure can occur through contaminated water, air and food chains, where substances such as PCBs and dioxins bioaccumulate. Notorious incidents include the Love Canal disaster in New York, the mercury poisoning at Minamata in Japan, and the Bhopal gas tragedy in India.

Management and regulation

Management follows a hierarchy of prevention, treatment (such as incineration, neutralisation or stabilisation), secure landfilling and long-term containment. In the United States, the Resource Conservation and Recovery Act (RCRA) regulates hazardous waste from "cradle to grave", while the Superfund programme (CERCLA) funds clean-up of abandoned sites. Internationally, the Basel Convention controls the transboundary movement and disposal of hazardous wastes.
