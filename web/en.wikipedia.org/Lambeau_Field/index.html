Lambeau Field

Lambeau Field is an American football stadium in Green Bay, Wisconsin, and the home field of the Green Bay Packers of the National Football League. Opened in 1957 as City Stadium, it was renamed in 1965 in memory of Packers founder, player, and long-time head coach Curly Lambeau, who had died earlier that year. It is the longest continually occupied stadium in the NFL.

History

The stadium opened on 29 September 1957 with a Packers victory over the Chicago Bears, attended by Vice President Richard Nixon. Its most famous game is the 1967 NFL Championship, the "Ice Bowl," played against the Dallas Cowboys at −13 °F (−25 °C), decided by Bart Starr's quarterback sneak in the final seconds. The Packers have sold out every game since 1960, and the waiting list for season tickets is famously decades long. The playing field is kept from freezing by an underground heating system installed by coach Vince Lombardi in 1967.

Features

Major renovations in 2003 and 2013–2015 expanded capacity to 81,441 — among the largest in the NFL — and added the Lambeau Field Atrium, the Packers Hall of Fame, and the Titletown District development to the west. The "Lambeau Leap," in which a scoring player jumps into the stands, was popularized by safety LeRoy Butler in 1993 and is now a signature celebration.
