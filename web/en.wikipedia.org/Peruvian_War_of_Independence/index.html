Peruvian War of Independence

The Peruvian War of Independence (1811–1826) was the struggle by which Peru broke from the Spanish Empire as part of the Spanish American wars of independence. Because Peru was the stronghold of Spanish power in South America, its liberation required campaigns by armies from outside: José de San Martín's expedition from Chile in the south, and Simón Bolívar's forces from the north.

Background

Early uprisings, including the great rebellion of Túpac Amaru II (1780–81) and the Cuzco rebellion of 1814, were suppressed, and the viceroyalty's elite largely remained loyalist. Independence momentum came from the liberation of neighbouring regions.

San Martín's expedition

After liberating Chile, San Martín sailed with the Chilean navy under Lord Cochrane, landed at Paracas in September 1820, and occupied Lima in July 1821 after the royalists withdrew to the highlands. He proclaimed Peruvian independence on 28 July 1821 and became Protector of the new state, but royalist armies remained undefeated inland.

Bolívar and Ayacucho

Following the Guayaquil Conference with Bolívar in 1822, San Martín resigned and left Peru. Bolívar arrived in 1823 with Colombian troops. In 1824 his lieutenant Antonio José de Sucre won the battles of Junín (6 August) and, decisively, Ayacucho (9 December 1824), where the main royalist army surrendered. The last royalist stronghold, the fortress of Callao, capitulated in January 1826.

Aftermath

Upper Peru became the separate republic of Bolivia in 1825. Peru itself entered independence burdened by debt, regional divisions and political instability that produced decades of caudillo rule.
