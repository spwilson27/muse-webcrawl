Truso

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Truso was an early medieval emporium and sea port on the southern Baltic coast, situated near the mouth of the Vistula on Lake Drużno, close to present-day Elbląg in northern Poland. It flourished from the late 8th to the 10th century as a trading place of the Old Prussians, frequented by Scandinavian and other merchants.

The account of Wulfstan

Truso is known chiefly from the voyage account of Wulfstan of Hedeby, an Anglo-Saxon traveller whose report of a seven-day sea journey from Hedeby in about AD 890 was inserted into the Old English Orosius. Wulfstan described the land of the Este (Prussians), their customs, and the port of Truso at the Vistula estuary.

Archaeology

Excavations at Janów Pomorski, beginning in the 1980s, identified Truso's remains: a harbour settlement of several hectares with workshops, ironworking and amber working, weights and coins (including Arabic dirhams), and imports from Scandinavia, the Slavic lands, and the Frankish world. Finds included a burial suggestive of Scandinavian presence and large numbers of merchant's balances, marking Truso as a node in the Baltic trade network linking Birka, Hedeby, and the rivers of eastern Europe. The settlement declined by the late 10th or early 11th century, possibly after a fire.
