Bernard Hinault

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Bernard Hinault (born 14 November 1954) is a French former professional road cyclist. With 147 professional victories, including five times the Tour de France, he is often named among the greatest cyclists of all time. Hinault is one of only seven cyclists to have won all three Grand Tours, and one of only two cyclists to have won each more than once.

Career

Hinault won the Tour de France in 1978, 1979, 1981, 1982 and 1985, the Giro d'Italia in 1980, 1982 and 1985, and the Vuelta a España in 1978 and 1983. In 1980 he achieved the rare Giro–World Championship double, winning the World Road Race in Sallanches. His aggressive, combative style earned him the nickname Le Blaireau ("The Badger"). In the 1986 Tour de France he rode in support of his teammate Greg LeMond, finishing second before retiring at the end of that season.

Legacy

Hinault is the most recent French winner of the Tour de France. After retirement he became a farmer in Brittany and worked for many years with the Tour de France organisation, presenting prizes on the podium. He remains a vocal commentator on the sport.
