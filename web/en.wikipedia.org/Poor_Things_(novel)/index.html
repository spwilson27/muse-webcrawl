Poor Things (novel)

Poor Things
AuthorAlasdair Gray
Published1992
GenrePostmodern novel

Poor Things is a novel by Scottish writer Alasdair Gray, published in 1992. It won the Whitbread Award and the Guardian Fiction Prize. A postmodern revision of Mary Shelley's Frankenstein, it is set largely in Victorian Glasgow.

Plot

The novel purports to be the memoir of Archibald McCandless, recounting how the surgeon Godwin Baxter created Bella Baxter by transplanting the brain of an unborn child into the body of a drowned woman. Bella journeys through Europe with the cad Duncan Wedderburn, gains education and socialist convictions, and eventually becomes a doctor. A later letter from Bella's counterpart "Victoria" disputes McCandless's whole account, leaving the reader to judge which version is true.

Adaptation

The novel was adapted into the 2023 film Poor Things, directed by Yorgos Lanthimos and starring Emma Stone, which won multiple Academy Awards.
