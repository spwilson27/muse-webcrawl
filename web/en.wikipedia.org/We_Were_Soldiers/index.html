We Were Soldiers

We Were Soldiers is a 2002 American war film written and directed by Randall Wallace and starring Mel Gibson. It dramatizes the Battle of Ia Drang in November 1965, the first major engagement between the United States Army and the People's Army of Vietnam during the Vietnam War. The film is based on the 1992 book We Were Soldiers Once… and Young by Lieutenant General Harold G. Moore and journalist Joseph L. Galloway.

Plot

Director: Randall Wallace
Starring: Mel Gibson, Madeleine Stowe, Greg Kinnear, Sam Elliott
Released: March 1, 2002
Budget: $75 million
Box office: $115.4 million

Lieutenant Colonel Hal Moore (Gibson) commands the 1st Battalion, 7th Cavalry Regiment, the first American unit to use helicopter air assault tactics at scale. On November 14, 1965, Moore and roughly 450 men are dropped into Landing Zone X-Ray in the Ia Drang Valley, where they are surrounded by a much larger North Vietnamese force. The film intercuts the three-day battle with the experiences of the soldiers' wives at Fort Benning, who receive the telegrams notifying families of deaths. It also gives unusual attention to the perspective of the North Vietnamese commander, Lieutenant Colonel Nguyen Huu An.

Reception

The film was a commercial success and received generally positive reviews for its battle sequences and its respectful treatment of soldiers on both sides, though some critics considered it conventional compared with other Vietnam films.
