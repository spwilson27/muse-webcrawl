Padlock

A padlock is a portable lock with a shackle that may be passed through an opening (such as a chain link, or hasp staple) to prevent use, theft, vandalism, sabotage, unauthorized access, entry, or harm. The term padlock originates from the Middle Ages, when such locks were used on paddocks for livestock. Padlocks are distinguished from built-in locks by being detachable and movable to the object to be secured.

History

Portable locks date to ancient times. The Romans and Egyptians used pin tumbler mechanisms in wooden locks over two thousand years ago, and padlocks with spring-tine mechanisms were known in Iron Age Europe. The modern padlock developed with the rise of iron and brass working; by the 19th century, makers such as Jeremiah Chubb, Joseph Bramah and Linus Yale Jr. had refined designs against picking and manipulation. Yale's pin tumbler padlock, patented in the 1860s, became the dominant pattern and remains the basis of most padlocks today.

Construction

A padlock consists of a body, a shackle, and a locking mechanism. The shackle is a U-shaped loop of metal, often hardened steel, which is released from the body when unlocked. Bodies are commonly made of brass, steel, aluminium, or laminated steel plates, chosen for cost, corrosion resistance, and strength. Locking mechanisms include pin tumblers, wafer tumblers, disc tumblers, lever tumblers, and combination (dial or wheel) mechanisms that require no key. Modern padlocks may also use electronic or biometric locking.

Grades and security

Padlocks are rated by standards such as CEN grades in Europe and by tests for shackle cutting resistance, body impact resistance, and lock picking resistance. High-security padlocks use boron alloy shackles, anti-drill plates, and restricted keyways. Combination padlocks are widely used for school and gym lockers but generally provide lower security than comparable keyed models.

Uses

Padlocks secure gates, lockers, bicycles, storage units, toolboxes, and luggage. They are also the traditional symbol of lockout-tagout safety procedures in industry, where each worker applies a personal padlock to an energy isolation point.
