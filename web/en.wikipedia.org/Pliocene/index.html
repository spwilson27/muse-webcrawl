The Pliocene Epoch is the second epoch of the Neogene Period in the Cenozoic Era, lasting from 5.333 million to 2.58 million years ago. It follows the Miocene and is followed by the Pleistocene. The epoch was named by Charles Lyell from Greek words meaning "more recent", referring to the proportion of its fossil molluscs with living representatives.

Climate and geography

The early Pliocene was warmer than today; during the mid-Pliocene warm period (about 3.3–3.0 million years ago), global temperatures were roughly 2–3 °C above pre-industrial levels with carbon dioxide near 400 ppm — often studied as an analogue for future climate. The Isthmus of Panama closed during the Pliocene, joining North and South America and triggering the Great American Interchange of fauna, while the Mediterranean refilled after the Messinian salinity crisis at the epoch's very start.

Life

Grasslands expanded as the climate cooled and dried; grazing mammals such as horses and antelope flourished. In Africa, early hominins including Australopithecus evolved and diversified. The Pliocene ended with the onset of the great Northern Hemisphere glaciations of the Pleistocene.
