American Indian Religious Freedom Act

The American Indian Religious Freedom Act (AIRFA), Public Law 95-341, 42 U.S.C. § 1996, is a United States federal law enacted in 1978 that declares it the policy of the United States to protect and preserve for American Indians their inherent right of freedom to believe, express, and exercise their traditional religions.

Enacted: 11 August 1978
Signed by: President Jimmy Carter

Background

For over a century federal policy had suppressed Indigenous ceremonies (e.g., the 1883 Code of Indian Offenses banning the Sun Dance and feasts), removed children to boarding schools, and restricted access to sacred sites and objects such as eagle feathers and peyote. AIRFA repudiated that history and directed federal agencies to review their policies for burdens on Native religion.

Scope and limits

The Act protects access to sacred sites, use and possession of sacred objects, and freedom of worship through ceremonials and traditional rites. However, in Lyng v. Northwest Indian Cemetery Protective Association (1988) the Supreme Court held AIRFA created no judicially enforceable individual rights. A 1994 amendment (42 U.S.C. § 1996a) legalised sacramental peyote use by members of federally recognised tribes, and related protections were advanced by the Native American Graves Protection and Repatriation Act (1990) and executive orders on sacred sites and eagle feathers.
