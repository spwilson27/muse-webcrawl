Fédération Internationale de Motocyclisme

The Fédération Internationale de Motocyclisme (FIM; English: International Motorcycling Federation) is the global governing and sanctioning body for motorcycle racing. It represents more than 110 national motorcycle federations grouped into six continental unions.

History

The FIM was founded in Paris in 1904 as the Fédération Internationale des Clubs Motocyclistes (FICM), largely through the efforts of the Motorcycle Club de France and the British Auto-Cycle Union. It adopted its present name in 1949, the same year the Road Racing World Championship — now MotoGP — was inaugurated.

Disciplines

The FIM sanctions world championships across many disciplines, including circuit racing (MotoGP, Superbike, Endurance), motocross, supercross, enduro, trials, speedway, and long track, as well as non-sporting activities in touring, leisure, rider safety, and environmental advocacy.

Role

The federation sets sporting and technical regulations, homologates circuits and equipment, issues international licences, maintains anti-doping rules, and coordinates the international racing calendar among its member federations.
