Dharmaśāstra

Dharmaśāstra (Sanskrit: धर्मशास्त्र) is a genre of Sanskrit texts on dharma — law, duty, conduct, and religious obligation — and a major source of the traditional legal tradition of Hinduism. The texts, composed over many centuries, prescribe the duties of individuals according to caste (varna) and stage of life (ashrama).

History

The earliest works of the genre are the Dharmasutras, aphoristic prose texts composed roughly between the 6th and 2nd centuries BCE, attributed to schools such as Āpastamba, Gautama, Baudhāyana, and Vāsiṣṭha. Later, verse treatises known as Smritis expanded on this material. The most famous is the Manusmriti (Laws of Manu), composed around the turn of the Common Era, followed by works such as the Yajnavalkya Smriti and Narada Smriti, and medieval commentaries and digests (nibandhas).

Contents

Dharmaśāstra texts treat the sources of dharma, the duties of kings, judicial procedure and evidence, property, inheritance, marriage, penances and expiations, dietary rules, and rites of passage. Alongside custom (acara) and the Veda, they form the traditional foundations of Hindu law.

Legacy

During British rule, administrators drew on Dharmaśāstra translations in codifying "Hindu law" for colonial courts. In modern India, most personal law has been reformed and codified by legislation such as the Hindu Code Bills of the 1950s, but the Dharmaśāstra remains a central object of study in Indology and legal history.
