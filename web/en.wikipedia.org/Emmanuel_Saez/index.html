Emmanuel Saez (born 26 November 1972) is a French-American economist and professor of economics at the University of California, Berkeley. His work, much of it with Thomas Piketty, studies income inequality using long-run historical tax records, and he is a leading figure in the modern field of public economics and optimal taxation.

Emmanuel Saez, economist of inequality and taxation

Education and career

Saez was born in Spain and raised in France. He studied at the École Normale Supérieure and the Paris School of Economics (then EHESS/DELTA), earning his PhD in economics from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology in 1999. He taught briefly at Harvard University before moving to UC Berkeley in 2002, where he later directed the Center for Equitable Growth.

Research on inequality

With Thomas Piketty, Saez constructed long time series of top income shares for the United States and many other countries from tax return data, work that transformed the empirical study of inequality. Their findings showed that the share of income held by the top 1 percent in the United States had returned to levels last seen in the 1920s. Their World Inequality Database has become a standard resource for researchers and policymakers.

Optimal taxation

Saez's theoretical work derives formulas for optimal tax rates based on empirically estimable elasticities — the "sufficient statistics" approach. He has contributed to the theory of optimal income taxation, capital taxation, tax evasion, and retirement policy, often with the aim of giving directly applicable guidance for policy design.

Honours

Saez received the John Bates Clark Medal in 2009, awarded to the most influential American economist under forty, and a MacArthur "Genius" Fellowship in 2010.
