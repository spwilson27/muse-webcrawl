Ho Chi Minh (Vietnamese: Hồ Chí Minh; 19 May 1890 – 2 September 1969), born Nguyễn Sinh Cung, was a Vietnamese revolutionary and politician. He served as prime minister of the Democratic Republic of Vietnam from 1945 to 1955 and as its president from 1945 until his death in 1969. Ideologically a Marxist–Leninist, he founded the Việt Minh independence movement, proclaimed the Democratic Republic of Vietnam in 1945, and led it through the First Indochina War against France and into the Vietnam War.

Early life and travels

Nguyễn Sinh Cung was born in 1890 in Kim Liên village, Nghệ An province, the son of a Confucian scholar and district magistrate. In 1911 he left Vietnam working as a kitchen helper aboard a French steamer, and over the following years lived in the United States, the United Kingdom and France, working jobs including baker, waiter and photo retoucher, while becoming active in anti-colonial politics. In Paris in 1919, under the name Nguyễn Ái Quốc ("Nguyễn the Patriot"), he petitioned the Versailles Peace Conference for civil rights for the Vietnamese people.

Communist organiser

A founding member of the French Communist Party in 1920, he travelled to Moscow and then to China, where in 1925 he organised Vietnamese exiles into a revolutionary youth league. In 1930 he founded the Communist Party of Vietnam (later the Indochinese Communist Party) in Hong Kong. After years abroad in the Soviet Union and China, he returned to Vietnam in 1941, adopting the name Hồ Chí Minh ("Bringer of Light") and founding the Việt Minh front to fight both Japanese occupation and French colonial rule.

Democratic Republic of Vietnam

Following the August Revolution of 1945, Ho proclaimed the Democratic Republic of Vietnam in Hanoi on 2 September 1945, quoting the American Declaration of Independence. France attempted to restore colonial control, precipitating the First Indochina War (1946–1954), which ended with the Việt Minh victory at Điện Biên Phủ and the Geneva Accords, which partitioned Vietnam at the 17th parallel with Ho's government in the North.

Later years and death

As chairman of the Workers' Party, Ho remained the symbolic leader of North Vietnam during the escalating war with South Vietnam and the United States, though day-to-day power increasingly passed to colleagues such as Lê Duẩn. He died of heart failure in Hanoi on 2 September 1969. His embalmed body is displayed in the Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum in Ba Đình Square. In 1976 the victorious communists renamed Saigon as Ho Chi Minh City in his honour.
