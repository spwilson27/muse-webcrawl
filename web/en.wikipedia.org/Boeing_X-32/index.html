The Boeing X-32A concept demonstrator

The Boeing X-32 was a concept demonstrator aircraft built for the Joint Strike Fighter competition. It competed against the Lockheed Martin X-35 to become the standard multirole fighter for the United States armed forces and allied nations. In October 2001, the X-35 was selected as the winner, and it went on to become the F-35 Lightning II.

Design

The X-32 featured a large chin-mounted intake feeding a Pratt & Whitney derivative of the F119 engine, and a one-piece composite delta wing. For short takeoff and vertical landing (STOVL), the X-32 used a direct-lift system similar to that of the Harrier, with vectoring nozzles deflecting engine thrust downward — in contrast to the X-35's shaft-driven lift fan. Two demonstrators were built: the X-32A for conventional and carrier testing, and the X-32B for STOVL testing.

Testing and cancellation

The X-32A first flew in September 2000 and the X-32B in March 2001. During testing, the X-32B's vertical landing configuration required parts to be swapped between flight modes, and the design's weight grew as requirements evolved; a proposed redesign with a conventional tail could not be demonstrated within the program's schedule. The Department of Defense judged the X-35 the lower-risk design, ending the X-32 program. Both X-32 airframes survive as museum exhibits.
