Fermilab

Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory
Established: 1967
Location: Batavia, Illinois, USA
Director (first): Robert R. Wilson
Budget: ~$700+ million/year
Type: Particle physics laboratory

Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory (Fermilab), located just outside Batavia, Illinois, near Chicago, is a United States Department of Energy national laboratory specializing in high-energy particle physics. Founded in 1967 as the National Accelerator Laboratory, it was renamed in 1974 in honour of Enrico Fermi.

History and discoveries

The laboratory's first director, Robert R. Wilson, oversaw construction of the 200 GeV Main Ring, completed ahead of schedule and under budget. Under his successor Leon Lederman, Fermilab built the Tevatron, a superconducting proton–antiproton collider about 6.3 km in circumference, which for many years was the highest-energy particle accelerator in the world. Landmark discoveries at Fermilab include the bottom quark (1977), via the Upsilon meson, and the top quark (1995), observed by the CDF and DØ experiments. The Tevatron shut down in 2011.

Current programme

Fermilab is now centred on neutrino physics: it hosts experiments such as NOvA and MicroBooNE, and is the lead laboratory for the international Deep Underground Neutrino Experiment (DUNE) and its Long-Baseline Neutrino Facility, which will send an intense neutrino beam 1,300 km to detectors in South Dakota. The laboratory also pursues muon physics (Muon g−2), accelerator science, astrophysics, and quantum information science. Its 2,600-hectare site includes restored prairie and a herd of American bison, and much of it is open to the public.
