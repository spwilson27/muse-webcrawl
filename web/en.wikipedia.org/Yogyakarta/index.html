Yogyakarta (often called Jogja) is the capital city of the Special Region of Yogyakarta in Indonesia, on the island of Java. It is renowned as a centre of classical Javanese fine arts and culture — including batik, ballet, drama, music, poetry, and puppetry — and as a major centre of education, home to Gadjah Mada University and many other institutions. Yogyakarta is the seat of the Yogyakarta Sultanate, whose sultan also serves as governor of the special region, a unique arrangement in Indonesia.

History

The city was founded in 1755–1756 by Sultan Hamengkubuwono I following the Treaty of Giyanti, which split the Mataram Sultanate; he built his palace, the Kraton, which remains the cultural heart of the city, laid out along a cosmological axis running from Mount Merapi to the southern sea. During the Indonesian National Revolution, Yogyakarta served as the capital of the Indonesian republic from 1946 to 1948 after Jakarta fell to returning Dutch forces, a role that earned the region its special autonomous status.

Culture and tourism

Yogyakarta is a gateway to the great temple complexes of Borobudur (Buddhist) and Prambanan (Hindu), both UNESCO World Heritage Sites lying within an hour of the city. Malioboro Street is its best-known shopping and street-food district. The city is celebrated for its batik workshops, wayang kulit shadow puppetry, gamelan music, and silverwork from the Kotagede quarter.

Geography

The city lies in south-central Java, about 25 km south of the highly active volcano Mount Merapi, whose 2010 eruption affected the region heavily. The metropolitan area extends into the surrounding regencies of Sleman and Bantul.
