Air mass (solar energy)

In solar energy and atmospheric optics, the air mass is a measure of the length of the path taken by sunlight through the Earth's atmosphere, relative to the shortest possible path (when the Sun is directly overhead). As sunlight passes through the atmosphere it is attenuated by scattering and absorption; the greater the air mass, the greater the attenuation and the more the spectrum shifts toward the red.

Definition

The (relative) air mass is approximately

AM ≈ 1 / cos(θ)

where θ is the solar zenith angle. At the zenith, AM = 1 ("AM1"); at a zenith angle of 60°, AM = 2. More refined formulas account for atmospheric curvature and refraction, which matter near the horizon, where the air mass approaches about 38. The absolute air mass corrects the relative value for altitude using local atmospheric pressure.

Standard spectra

  • AM0 – the extraterrestrial spectrum, outside the atmosphere (about 1366 W/m² total irradiance), relevant to spacecraft.
  • AM1.5 – the standard terrestrial reference spectrum, corresponding to a zenith angle of about 48.2° and a total irradiance of 1000 W/m². It is used worldwide for rating photovoltaic modules; the "G" variant (AM1.5G) includes diffuse light on a tilted surface, while "D" denotes direct beam only.

Applications

Air mass values are used in photovoltaic testing and performance modelling, solar simulator design, photobiology, and astronomical extinction corrections. Air pollution and aerosol measurements also depend on the optical air mass along the line of sight.
