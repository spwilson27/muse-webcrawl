Yield to maturity

Image placeholder: Bond price and yield curve chart

The yield to maturity (YTM), book yield or redemption yield of a fixed-interest security, such as a bond, is an estimate of the total return anticipated if the bond is held until it matures. It is the internal rate of return of the bond's cash flows.

Calculation

YTM is found by solving for the discount rate that equates the bond's current market price with the present value of all future coupon payments and the redemption of principal. It accounts for the coupon rate, the difference between price and face value, and the time to maturity.

Limitations

YTM assumes coupons are reinvested at the same yield and that the bond is held to maturity without default; the realized return can differ if those assumptions fail. It differs from the simpler current yield, which only divides annual coupons by price.
