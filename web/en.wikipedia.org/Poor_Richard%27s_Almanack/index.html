Poor Richard's Almanack

Poor Richard's Almanack was a yearly almanac published by Benjamin Franklin, who adopted the pseudonym of "Poor Richard" or "Richard Saunders" for this purpose. The publication appeared annually from 1732 to 1758 and was extremely popular in colonial America.

Contents

Alongside the usual almanac material—calendars, weather forecasts, astronomical information, and poems—the Almanack contained proverbs and aphorisms promoting industry and frugality, many of which became canonical American sayings, such as "Early to bed and early to rise, makes a man healthy, wealthy, and wise."

The Way to Wealth

In 1758 Franklin gathered many of the sayings into the preface to the final issue, published separately as The Way to Wealth, one of the most reprinted works of the colonial era. Print runs of the Almanack reached about 10,000 copies per year.
