RMS Queen Elizabeth

RMS Queen Elizabeth was an ocean liner operated by the Cunard Line. With Queen Mary she provided weekly luxury liner service between Southampton, Cherbourg, and New York City. She was launched on 27 September 1938 and named in honour of Queen Elizabeth, later the Queen Mother. At about 83,000 gross register tons and 1,031 feet (314 m) long, she was for many years the largest passenger liner ever built.

History

Built by John Brown & Company at Clydebank, Scotland, the ship was requisitioned as a troopship before her maiden passenger voyage; painted grey and fitted out in Singapore and Sydney, she carried troops, including Australian and American soldiers, throughout the Second World War, transporting over 750,000 troops in total. Her high speed made her able to outrun U-boats without escort. After the war she was refitted and entered transatlantic service in 1946, sailing in tandem with Queen Mary until jet airliners undermined the trade.

Later years

Withdrawn in 1968, she was sold several times and eventually bought by Hong Kong shipping magnate C. Y. Tung, who converted her into a floating university, renamed Seawise University. During final refit work in Hong Kong harbour on 9 January 1972, fires broke out, capsizing the ship. The wreck was later partially scrapped where it lay.
