Al-Shafi'i

Muhammad ibn Idris al-Shafi'i (767 – 820 CE) was an Arab Muslim jurist and theologian, the founder of the Shafi'i school (madhhab) of Sunni Islamic law and a foundational figure in the theory of Islamic jurisprudence (usul al-fiqh). His book Al-Risala ("The Epistle") is considered the first systematic work on the principles of Islamic law, establishing the Quran, the Sunnah of the Prophet Muhammad, consensus (ijma), and analogical reasoning (qiyas) as the ordered sources of law.

Life

Born in Gaza and raised in poverty in Mecca, al-Shafi'i memorized the Quran by age seven and studied under Malik ibn Anas in Medina, committing Malik's Muwatta to memory. He later studied in Iraq with Muhammad al-Shaybani, Abu Hanifa's leading disciple, exposing him to both the tradition-based and reason-based schools of law. Caught up in political turmoil in Yemen, he was taken to Baghdad in chains but released. He eventually settled in Egypt, where he revised his teaching — distinguishing his "old" and "new" opinions — and died in 820; his tomb in Cairo remains a major shrine.

Jurisprudence

Al-Shafi'i's great innovation was to systematize legal method, insisting that the Prophet's Sunnah, properly authenticated, outranked local custom and juristic preference, and disciplining the use of analogy. The Shafi'i school became predominant in Egypt, the Levant, Yemen, East Africa, and Southeast Asia, including Indonesia and Malaysia.
