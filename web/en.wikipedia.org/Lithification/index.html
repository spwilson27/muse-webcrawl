Lithification

Lithification (from the Greek word lithos, meaning "rock") is the process by which loose sediments are converted into solid sedimentary rock. It is a key part of diagenesis, the set of physical and chemical changes occurring during the burial of sediment.

Processes

Lithification involves two main processes:

  • Compaction — as sediment is buried under more material, the weight of the overburden squeezes grains closer together and expels water from pore spaces.
  • Cementation — dissolved minerals carried by groundwater precipitate in the pores between grains, binding them together. Common cements include calcite, quartz (silica) and iron oxides.

Examples

Sand lithifies into sandstone, mud into shale or mudstone, and accumulated shells and carbonate mud into limestone. The process can take place relatively quickly in geological terms, but complete lithification of thick sediment sequences commonly requires deep burial over millions of years.
