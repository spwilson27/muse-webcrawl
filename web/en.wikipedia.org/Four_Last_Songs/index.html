Four Last Songs

The Four Last Songs (Vier letzte Lieder), composed in 1948, are among Strauss's most beloved works.

The Four Last Songs (German: Vier letzte Lieder), Op. posth., AV 150, are four songs for soprano and orchestra composed in 1948 by Richard Strauss. They are settings of poems by Hermann Hesse and Joseph von Eichendorff and are regarded as the composer's valedictory work, suffused with a sense of acceptance and transfiguration in the face of death.

Composition and premiere

Strauss composed the songs in 1948, the year before his death, while living in Switzerland. The Eichendorff setting, "Im Abendrot" ("At Sunset"), was completed first, in May 1948; the three Hesse settings — "Frühling" ("Spring"), "September" and "Beim Schlafengehen" ("Going to Sleep") — followed over the summer. Strauss never heard the songs performed. The premiere took place at the Royal Albert Hall in London on 22 May 1950, with the soprano Kirsten Flagstad, the Philharmonia Orchestra and the conductor Wilhelm Furtwängler.

The title "Four Last Songs" was not Strauss's own: it was devised by his friend and publisher Ernst Roth, who also fixed the order in which the songs are usually performed. The songs were published posthumously as a set.

"Frühling" is a rapturous evocation of spring's return; "September" contemplates the end of summer and, by extension, of life. In "Beim Schlafengehen" the tired soul longs to take flight, a passage crowned by a celebrated violin solo. "Im Abendrot" depicts an elderly couple who have wandered together through joy and sorrow and now watch the sun set, asking "Is this perhaps death?" — to which Strauss quotes the transfiguration theme from his own tone poem Death and Transfiguration, written some sixty years earlier.

Style and legacy

The songs call for a large orchestra and are written in Strauss's late, lyrically opulent style, with long-breathed soprano lines floating above rich orchestral textures. Horns — the instrument of Strauss's father — feature prominently. Alongside the earlier songs, they form a cornerstone of the soprano repertoire, and numerous recordings have been made by singers including Elisabeth Schwarzkopf, Jessye Norman, Renée Fleming and Kiri Te Kanawa.
