Breastplate (Judaism)

The priestly breastplate or breastpiece (Hebrew: ḥōšen), also called the "breastplate of judgment", was a sacred garment worn by the High Priest of Israel, described in the Book of Exodus. It was set with twelve gemstones representing the twelve tribes of Israel and worn over the ephod.

Description

According to Exodus 28, the breastplate was made of gold, blue, purple, and scarlet yarn and fine linen, folded into a square of one span. Four rows of three stones each were mounted in gold settings; the stones are variously identified in translation, with the first row including carnelian, topaz, and emerald. Each stone was engraved with the name of a tribe.

Urim and Thummim

The breastplate contained a pocket holding the Urim and Thummim, objects used by the High Priest to discern the will of God. Their exact nature is unknown; they are mentioned in connection with oracular decisions in several biblical passages.

Symbolism

Bearing the names of the tribes "over his heart", the High Priest symbolically carried Israel before God when entering the sanctuary (Exodus 28:29). The garment is a frequent motif in later Jewish and Christian art and commentary.
