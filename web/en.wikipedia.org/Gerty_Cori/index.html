Gerty Theresa Cori (née Radnitz; 1896–1957) was a Czech-American biochemist who, with her husband Carl Ferdinand Cori, elucidated how glycogen is broken down and resynthesised in the body. In 1947 the Coris shared the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with Bernardo Houssay; Gerty Cori was the first woman to receive the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine and the third woman to win a Nobel Prize in science.

Life and career

Born in Prague, Cori earned her medical degree from the German University of Prague in 1920, the year she married Carl Cori. The couple emigrated to the United States in 1922 to work at the Roswell Park Institute in Buffalo, New York, and moved in 1931 to Washington University in St. Louis. Despite her achievements, Gerty Cori was for many years appointed only as a research associate at a fraction of her husband's salary; she became a full professor only in 1947, months before the Nobel announcement.

Scientific work

The Coris described the Cori cycle, the metabolic pathway by which lactate produced by muscle is converted back to glucose in the liver. They isolated glucose 1-phosphate (the "Cori ester") and discovered the enzyme phosphorylase, demonstrating how glycogen is enzymatically degraded and synthesised. Later work identified the enzyme defects underlying several glycogen storage diseases. Cori continued working in the laboratory until shortly before her death from myelofibrosis in 1957.
