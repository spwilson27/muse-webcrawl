One country, two systems

One country, two systems

Traditional Chinese: 一國兩制

Simplified Chinese: 一国两制

Meaning: "One country, two systems"

Proposed by: Deng Xiaoping

Applied to: Hong Kong (1997), Macau (1999)

One country, two systems is a constitutional principle of the People's Republic of China (PRC) describing the governance of the special administrative regions of Hong Kong and Macau. Developed in the early 1980s during negotiations with the United Kingdom over Hong Kong, the principle was first formulated by Deng Xiaoping, then the Paramount Leader of China, and was enshrined in the Sino-British Joint Declaration of 1984 and the Sino-Portuguese Joint Declaration of 1987 concerning Macau.

Overview

Under the principle, the two special administrative regions would retain their own capitalist economic and political systems for 50 years after their transfer to the PRC, while the rest of China continued under the socialist system. Each region would keep its own legal system, police force, monetary system, customs policy, immigration policy, and its own delegates to international organisations and events. The promise of a high degree of autonomy was codified in the Basic Law of Hong Kong and the Basic Law of Macau, which serve as mini-constitutions for the two regions.

Origins

The concept was originally devised with Taiwan in mind, as a framework for a possible peaceful reunification: Taiwan would be allowed to maintain its armed forces and government in exchange for accepting the sovereignty of Beijing. When the immediate question of Hong Kong's future arose after the lease of the New Territories approached its 1997 expiry, the formula was applied first to Hong Kong and Macau. Deng summarised the idea in the statement that there would be "one China" but two distinct systems could coexist within it.

Implementation in Hong Kong and Macau

Hong Kong was transferred from the United Kingdom to China on 1 July 1997, and Macau from Portugal on 20 December 1999. Both became Special Administrative Regions (SARs) under Article 31 of the Constitution of the People's Republic of China. Their Basic Laws guarantee that the socialist system and policies of the mainland shall not be practised in the regions and that their previous capitalist system and way of life shall remain unchanged for 50 years — until 2047 for Hong Kong and 2049 for Macau.

Debates and developments

The interpretation of "one country, two systems" has been a recurring subject of political debate, particularly in Hong Kong, where questions have arisen about the pace of democratic development, the autonomy of the judiciary, freedom of the press, and the role of the central government. Major events shaping the debate include the proposed Article 23 security legislation in 2003, the Umbrella Movement of 2014, the anti-extradition bill protests of 2019, and the imposition of the Hong Kong national security law in 2020. In Macau the principle has generally been regarded as operating with less friction.
