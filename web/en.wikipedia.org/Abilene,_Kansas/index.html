Abilene is a city in and the county seat of Dickinson County, Kansas, United States. It is best known as a Wild West cattle town and as the boyhood home of Dwight D. Eisenhower, the 34th president of the United States.

History

Abilene was founded in 1857 as a stagecoach stop. In 1867 the Kansas Pacific Railway reached the town and stockyards were built, making Abilene the first of the great Kansas cattle towns: the northern terminus where Texas longhorns driven up the Chisholm Trail were loaded onto trains for eastern markets. The boomtown years brought gunfighters and lawmen including Wild Bill Hickok, who served as marshal in 1871. The cattle trade moved west to Ellsworth and Dodge City by the mid-1870s.

Eisenhower

Dwight D. Eisenhower grew up in Abilene, and the city hosts the Dwight D. Eisenhower Presidential Library, Museum and Boyhood Home, as well as Eisenhower's grave. The Greyhound Hall of Fame, honoring the racing breed, is also located in Abilene, which for a time was a center of greyhound breeding.
