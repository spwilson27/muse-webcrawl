Pâté

Pâté is a paste, terrine, or forcemeat of ground meat and fat, originating in French cuisine. Common ingredients are pork, poultry, fish, or game, seasoned and often combined with liver; pâté may be served spreadable from a crock, sliced from a terrine, or baked in a crust as pâté en croûte.

Varieties

Pâté de foie gras, made from the fattened liver of a goose or duck, is the most famous and luxurious variety. Pâté de campagne (country pâté) is a coarse, rustic terrine of pork and liver. Other forms include smooth liver mousses, fish pâtés, and vegetable pâtés.

Preparation and serving

The mixture is ground or chopped, seasoned with herbs, spices, and often wine or brandy, then cooked — frequently in a water bath in a mould lined with fat or pastry. Pâté is typically served chilled or at room temperature with bread or toast, cornichons, and mustard.

Around the world

Related preparations include German Leberwurst, Scandinavian leverpostej, and the liver pâté essential to the Vietnamese bánh mì sandwich, a legacy of French colonial influence.
