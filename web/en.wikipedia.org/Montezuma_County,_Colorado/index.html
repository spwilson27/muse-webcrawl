Montezuma County is a county located in the southwest corner of the U.S. state of Colorado. As of the 2020 census, the population was 25,849. The county seat and most populous municipality is Cortez. The county is best known for Mesa Verde National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site containing some of the best-preserved Ancestral Puebloan cliff dwellings in the United States.

History

The county was created by the Colorado legislature in 1889 out of the northern portion of La Plata County. It was named indirectly for the Aztec emperor Moctezuma II, via the Montezuma Castle cliff dwellings believed (wrongly) at the time to be connected to the Aztecs. The area was inhabited by Ancestral Puebloans from about AD 600 to 1300.

Geography and communities

The county lies in the Four Corners region, bordering Utah, Arizona (at the Four Corners Monument, quadripoint with New Mexico), and New Mexico. Communities include Cortez, Mancos, Dolores, and Towaoc, the capital of the Ute Mountain Ute Tribe, whose reservation occupies the southwest part of the county. In addition to Mesa Verde, the county contains parts of Canyons of the Ancients National Monument and the Hovenweep National Monument.
