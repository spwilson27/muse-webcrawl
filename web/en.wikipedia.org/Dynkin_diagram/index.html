Dynkin diagram

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In the mathematical field of Lie theory, a Dynkin diagram, named for Eugene Dynkin, is a type of graph with some edges doubled or tripled (drawn as a double or triple line) and arrows on the multiple edges. Dynkin diagrams arise in the classification of semisimple Lie algebras over algebraically closed fields, in the classification of root systems, and in related subjects such as Coxeter groups and quiver representations.

Construction

Given a root system, choose a set of simple roots. The Dynkin diagram has one node for each simple root; pairs of nodes are joined by 0, 1, 2, or 3 edges according to the angle between the roots, and an arrow points toward the shorter root when roots joined by multiple edges have different lengths. The diagram encodes the same information as the Cartan matrix of the root system.

Classification

The connected finite Dynkin diagrams fall into four infinite families — An, Bn, Cn, and Dn — and five exceptional cases: E6, E7, E8, F4, and G2. These classify the irreducible reduced root systems and hence the simple Lie algebras over the complex numbers. The corresponding classification also appears for finite Coxeter groups (without arrows) and in the ADE classification of singularities and quivers of finite representation type.

Affine and extended diagrams

Allowing a null root gives the affine Dynkin diagrams, which classify affine Lie algebras. Variants include the extended and hyperbolic diagrams used in the study of Kac–Moody algebras.
