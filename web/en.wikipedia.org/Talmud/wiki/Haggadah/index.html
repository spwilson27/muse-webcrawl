Haggadah

The Haggadah (Hebrew: הַגָּדָה, "telling") is the Jewish text that sets forth the order of the Passover Seder. Reading the Haggadah fulfils the biblical commandment to tell one's children the story of the Exodus of the Israelites from slavery in ancient Egypt. It includes narrative, blessings, psalms, songs, and rabbinic commentary, and is among the most widely printed and illustrated books in Jewish tradition.

Origins and composition

The Haggadah's core took shape in the Mishnaic and Talmudic periods, incorporating such elements as the Four Questions (Mah Nishtana), the story of the Four Sons, and the recounting of the Ten Plagues. Its text was largely standardised by the geonic period, though communities developed their own rites and additions over the centuries.

Contents of the Seder

The Haggadah structures the evening around symbolic foods, four cups of wine, the eating of matzah and maror (bitter herbs), and the recitation of Hallel psalms. Well-known passages include "Dayenu" ("It would have been enough"), the invitation "Let all who are hungry come and eat," and the closing songs "Echad Mi Yodea" and "Chad Gadya." The evening concludes with the wish "Next year in Jerusalem."

Manuscripts and editions

Famous illuminated manuscripts include the Sarajevo Haggadah, the Golden Haggadah, and the Birds' Head Haggadah. Printed editions appeared from the early 16th century, and the Prague Haggadah of 1526 is a landmark of Hebrew printing. In modern times thousands of editions have appeared, including kibbutz, military, and thematic Haggadot.
