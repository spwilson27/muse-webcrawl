LocationLower Galilee, Israel
Known forAncient Jewish necropolis; seat of the Sanhedrin
StatusUNESCO World Heritage Site (2015)

Beit She'arim (Hebrew: בֵּית שְׁעָרִים, "House of Gates") was a Jewish village of the Roman and Byzantine eras in the Lower Galilee, best known for its extensive necropolis of rock-cut catacombs. In the 2nd century CE it became the seat of the Sanhedrin under Rabbi Judah the Prince (Yehudah HaNasi), the redactor of the Mishnah, who was buried there.

Necropolis

The burial caves contain sarcophagi and inscriptions in Hebrew, Aramaic, and Greek, reflecting the wide diaspora of Jews who sought burial near Judah the Prince. The site is frequently mentioned in the Talmud and later rabbinic literature in connection with the Sanhedrin and the Mishnah's compilation.

Excavations

Major excavations were conducted by Benjamin Mazar and Nahman Avigad in the 1930s and 1950s. The necropolis was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2015.
