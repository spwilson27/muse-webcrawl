Tannaim

The Tannaim (Aramaic: תנאים, singular Tanna, "teachers/repeaters") were the rabbinic sages whose teachings are recorded in the Mishnah and other Tannaitic works. Their period, the Mishnaic period, spans roughly the first two centuries CE, from the generation of Hillel and Shammai until the redaction of the Mishnah by Judah the Prince around 200 CE.

Role and works

The Tannaim transmitted and systematised the Oral Torah, debating points of law and interpretation that were eventually organised in the Mishnah, the Tosefta, and the halakhic midrashim (such as the Mekhilta, Sifra, and Sifre). Their statements, usually in concise Hebrew, form the foundation upon which the Amoraim of the Talmud later built.

Generations and major figures

Scholars divide the Tannaim into five or six generations. Leading figures include Hillel the Elder and Shammai, whose schools (Beit Hillel and Beit Shammai) disputed hundreds of questions; Rabban Yochanan ben Zakkai, who refounded the Sanhedrin at Yavneh after the destruction of the Temple; Rabbi Akiva, the great organiser of the Mishnah's system and supporter of the Bar Kokhba revolt; Rabbi Meir; and Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai. The final generation — Judah the Prince and his contemporaries — completed the Mishnah.

Historical setting

The Tannaitic age was marked by catastrophe: the destruction of the Second Temple (70 CE) and the failed Bar Kokhba revolt (132–135 CE). In response, the Tannaim reshaped Judaism around study, prayer, and law in place of the Temple cult, a transformation whose effects persist to the present.
