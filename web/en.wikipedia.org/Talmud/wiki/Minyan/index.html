Minyan

A minyan (Hebrew: מִנְיָן, "count, number") is the quorum of ten Jewish adults required for certain communal religious obligations in Judaism. In Orthodox Judaism the quorum consists of ten men aged thirteen or older; Conservative, Reform, and other egalitarian movements count women as well.

Source

The requirement is derived in the Talmud (Megillah 23b) through a chain of biblical interpretations linking the word "congregation" (edah) to the ten spies sent by Moses, establishing ten as the minimum public congregation.

Prayers requiring a minyan

Certain parts of the service may only be recited in the presence of a minyan, including the Barekhu, the Kedushah, the repetition of the Amidah, the Kaddish, and the public reading of the Torah and haftarah. A minyan is also traditionally required for the priestly blessing and for rituals such as the seven wedding blessings.

Practice

Synagogues organise daily minyanim for morning, afternoon, and evening services (Jewish prayer), and mourners rely on a minyan to recite the Mourner's Kaddish during the year of mourning. Smaller communities sometimes struggle to assemble a quorum, leading to the familiar appeal to passers-by to "make the minyan." Houses of mourning (shiva) also convene minyanim so mourners can pray at home.
