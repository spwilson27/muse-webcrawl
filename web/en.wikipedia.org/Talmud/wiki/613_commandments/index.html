613 commandments

The 613 commandments (Hebrew: תרי״ג מצוות, taryag mitzvot) are the traditional enumeration of the commandments (mitzvot) contained in the Torah. According to rabbinic tradition, they consist of 248 positive commandments ("do") and 365 negative commandments ("do not"), corresponding respectively to the parts of the human body and the days of the solar year.

Origin of the number

The figure of 613 derives from a teaching of Rabbi Simlai in the Talmud (Makkot 23b–24a), which states that Moses received 613 commandments on Mount Sinai. The passage goes on to describe how later prophets distilled the commandments into fewer principles, culminating in Habakkuk's single principle: "the righteous shall live by his faith."

Enumerations

Although the Talmud gives the total, it does not list the commandments. Medieval authorities compiled differing lists, the most influential being Maimonides' Sefer Hamitzvot ("Book of Commandments"), which sets out fourteen rules for determining which biblical statements count as commandments. Other classic enumerations include those of Nachmanides, the Sefer ha-Chinuch, and the Sefer Mitzvot Gadol.

Observance today

Many commandments presuppose the Temple in Jerusalem or conditions of Jewish sovereignty and cannot be performed today; authorities commonly count roughly 270 commandments applicable in the present day, depending on individual circumstances. The 613 commandments remain a foundational framework for Jewish law (halakha) and study.
