Judah ha-Nasi

Judah ha-Nasi (Hebrew: יהודה הנשיא, "Judah the Prince"; c. 135 – c. 217 CE), known simply as Rabbi in the Talmud, was the hereditary patriarch (nasi) of the Jewish community in Roman Palestine and the redactor of the Mishnah, the foundational code of rabbinic law. He is considered the last of the Tannaim.

Life

Judah was born, according to tradition, on the day Rabbi Akiva died a martyr (c. 135 CE). He was the son of Rabban Simeon ben Gamaliel II and a descendant of Hillel. Educated in the academies of the Galilee, he became patriarch around 165 CE and held the office for some fifty years, residing at Beit She'arim and later at Sepphoris. His great wealth and cordial relations with Roman authority — tradition speaks of friendship with an emperor called "Antoninus" — gave him the stability to undertake his great scholarly project.

The Mishnah

Fearing that the oral tradition would be lost amid persecution and dispersion, Judah collected and edited the teachings of the preceding generations into the Mishnah, completed around 200 CE. Written in a clear Mishnaic Hebrew, the work codified Jewish law in six orders and became the basis of both Talmuds and of all subsequent rabbinic Judaism.

Legacy

Judah is revered as a model of combined Torah scholarship and worldly leadership — "from the days of Moses until Rabbi, we find no one who united Torah and greatness in one place," the Talmud says. He was buried at Beit She'arim, whose necropolis became a favoured burial place for Jews from across the Roman world.
