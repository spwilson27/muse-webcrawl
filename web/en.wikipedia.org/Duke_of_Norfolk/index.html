Duke of Norfolk is a title in the Peerage of England, held by the Howard family. It is the premier dukedom in the English peerage, and the senior non-royal dukedom. The Duke of Norfolk also holds the hereditary office of Earl Marshal, responsible for organising state occasions such as coronations and state funerals.

History

The title was first created in 1397 for Thomas de Mowbray. After forfeitures and recreations, it was created a third time in 1483 for John Howard, a loyal supporter of Richard III, who was killed at the Battle of Bosworth in 1485. The title was restored to his son Thomas Howard, who commanded the English army at the victory over the Scots at Flodden in 1513.

The Howards remained leading figures through the Tudor period despite attainders and executions — the 4th Duke was executed in 1572 for the Ridolfi plot to place Mary, Queen of Scots, on the throne. The family is the foremost Roman Catholic family of the English aristocracy, and its seat is Arundel Castle in West Sussex. The dukedom descends with a large collection of subsidiary titles, including Earl of Arundel, the oldest earldom in England.

Earl Marshal

As Earl Marshal, the Duke of Norfolk is head of the College of Arms and organises major ceremonial state events. The office was made hereditary in the Howard family in the seventeenth century.
