Neurotrophic factors

Neurotrophic factors (NTFs) are a family of biomolecules — most of them peptides or small proteins — that support the growth, survival, and differentiation of both developing and mature neurons. They are also responsible for the maintenance of mature neurons and play roles in synaptic plasticity.

Families

The classic neurotrophin family includes nerve growth factor (NGF), brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF), neurotrophin-3 (NT-3) and neurotrophin-4 (NT-4), which act through Trk receptor tyrosine kinases and the p75 neurotrophin receptor. Other important families include the GDNF family ligands (glial cell line-derived neurotrophic factor, neurturin, artemin and persephin), which signal through Ret receptor complexes, and neuropoietic cytokines such as ciliary neurotrophic factor (CNTF).

Function

During development, target tissues secrete limited amounts of neurotrophic factors, and neurons compete for them; those that fail to obtain sufficient support undergo programmed cell death. This neurotrophic hypothesis, developed from the work of Rita Levi-Montalcini and colleagues who discovered NGF, explains how neuronal numbers are matched to their targets. In adults, NTFs continue to support neuronal survival and modulate learning and memory; reduced BDNF signalling has been implicated in depression and neurodegenerative disease.
