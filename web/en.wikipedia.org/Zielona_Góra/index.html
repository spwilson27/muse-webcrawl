Zielona Góra

Zielona Góra (Polish: [ʑɛˈlɔna ˈɡura]; German: Grünberg in Schlesien) is a city in western Poland, the largest city in Lubusz Voivodeship, and one of the two seats of its government, alongside Gorzów Wielkopolski. With a population of about 140,000, it is known as the centre of Poland's wine-growing region and for its annual wine festival, the Winobranie.

History

The settlement, whose name means "Green Mountain", grew around a Slavic stronghold and received town rights in the 13th century when it belonged to the Polish Piast Duchy of Głogów. It later passed to Bohemia, Austria, and Prussia, becoming the German town of Grünberg in Silesia, known for wine production and textiles. After the Second World War the city became part of Poland under the Potsdam Agreement and its German population was expelled and replaced by Poles, many displaced from Poland's former eastern territories.

Wine tradition

Viticulture around Zielona Góra dates to the Middle Ages; the city's wine festival each September celebrates the harvest with parades led by Bacchus. The Palm House on Wine Hill and its surrounding vineyards are among the city's best-known landmarks, and the region's vineyards have expanded considerably in recent decades.
