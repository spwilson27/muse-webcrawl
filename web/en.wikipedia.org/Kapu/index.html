Kapu

Kapu
Type: Traditional system of laws and taboos
Region: Ancient Hawaii
Abolished: 1819 (Ai Noa)

Kapu refers to the ancient Hawaiian code of conduct governing laws and regulations, derived from the Polynesian concept of tapu (taboo). The kapu system structured every aspect of traditional Hawaiian society, dictating rules about religion, resource use, social rank and daily behaviour.

The system

Kapu were proclaimed by chiefs and priests and enforced under penalty of death. Rules included the strict separation of men and women at meals (the ʻaikapu), restrictions on fishing seasons and sacred places, and protocols governing contact with high-ranking aliʻi.

Abolition

In 1819, shortly after the death of Kamehameha I, King Kamehameha II publicly broke the kapu by eating with women in the famous ʻAi Noa ("free eating"), and ordered the destruction of temples and images, ending the state religion shortly before the arrival of Christian missionaries.
