Fermented food

Fermented foods are foods and beverages produced through controlled microbial growth and the conversion of food components through enzymatic action. Fermentation has been used since the Neolithic period to preserve food, enhance flavour and improve nutritional qualities.

Examples

Well-known fermented foods include yoghurt, cheese, kefir, sauerkraut, kimchi, sourdough bread, miso, tempeh, soy sauce, pickles, beer, wine, cider and kombucha. Microorganisms involved include lactic acid bacteria, acetic acid bacteria, yeasts and moulds.

Health aspects

Fermentation can increase digestibility, produce vitamins and reduce antinutrients, and live-culture foods are studied for possible probiotic benefits. Conversely, some fermented products are high in salt or alcohol, and poorly controlled fermentations can allow spoilage or toxin-forming organisms.
