Skin cancer

Skin cancer is a cancer arising from the skin, most often caused by exposure to ultraviolet radiation from sunlight or tanning devices. It is the most common form of cancer worldwide. The three main types are basal-cell carcinoma (BCC), squamous-cell carcinoma (SCC) and melanoma.

Skin cancer

Specialty: Dermatology, oncology
Risk factors: UV exposure, fair skin, immunosuppression
Prevention: Sunscreen, sun avoidance
Types: BCC, SCC, melanoma

Types and outlook

BCC and SCC, collectively non-melanoma skin cancers, grow slowly and are rarely fatal when excised early. Melanoma is less common but far more dangerous because of its tendency to metastasise; early detection by monitoring changing moles (the ABCDE rule) is critical.

Prevention and treatment

Reducing UV exposure and using sunscreen lower risk; public campaigns in high-incidence countries such as Australia emphasise sun protection. Treatment is usually surgical excision, with Mohs surgery for delicate sites; advanced melanoma may be treated with immunotherapy and targeted drugs.
