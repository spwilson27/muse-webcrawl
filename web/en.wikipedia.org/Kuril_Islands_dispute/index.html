Kuril Islands dispute

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Kuril Islands dispute, known as the Northern Territories dispute in Japan, is a territorial dispute between Japan and Russia over the ownership of the four southernmost islands of the Kuril archipelago: Iturup (Etorofu), Kunashir (Kunashiri), Shikotan, and the Habomai group.

Background

The dispute arises from ambiguities in the treaties ending the Second World War. The Soviet Union occupied the islands in 1945, expelling the Japanese population of about 17,000 by 1949. The 1951 San Francisco Peace Treaty, which the Soviet Union did not sign, required Japan to renounce "all right, title and claim to the Kurile Islands" without specifying the beneficiary or whether the four southern islands count as part of the Kurils.

Positions

Japan maintains that the four islands are an inherent part of its territory that were never part of the Kuril chain ceded in 1951, and demands their return. Russia maintains that the islands were lawfully transferred to the Soviet Union as a result of the war and that Russian sovereignty is not in question. The dispute has prevented the two countries from signing a formal peace treaty since 1945.

In 1956 the USSR and Japan issued a joint declaration ending the state of war, in which the Soviet Union agreed to transfer Habomai and Shikotan after a peace treaty was concluded; no treaty followed. Negotiations continued intermittently for decades, including talks between Vladimir Putin and Shinzō Abe, but broke down further after Russia suspended peace-treaty talks in 2022 in response to Japanese sanctions over the invasion of Ukraine.
