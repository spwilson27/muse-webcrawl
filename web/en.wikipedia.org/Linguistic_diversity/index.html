Linguistic diversity

Linguistic diversity refers to the variety of languages spoken in a given region or in the world as a whole. Around 7,000 languages are spoken today, unevenly distributed: Papua New Guinea alone has more than 800.

Measurement

Diversity is measured by counts of languages, language families, and indices such as Greenberg's diversity index, which estimates the probability that two randomly chosen people in an area speak different mother tongues.

Distribution

Diversity is highest near the equator and in regions of rugged terrain and long human settlement, including New Guinea, the Caucasus, West Africa and the Amazon. A small number of large languages — Mandarin, Spanish, English, Hindi — account for a large share of the world's speakers.

Endangerment

Many languages are endangered as speakers shift to dominant languages; a language is estimated to fall silent every few weeks. Documentation and revitalisation efforts aim to record and sustain them.
