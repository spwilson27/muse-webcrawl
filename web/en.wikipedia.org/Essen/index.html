Essen

Essen, with the Zollverein colliery, a UNESCO World Heritage Site

Essen (German pronunciation: [ˈɛsn̩]) is a city in the state of North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany, in the centre of the Ruhr area on the river Ruhr. With a population of around 580,000, it is one of the largest cities in Germany and was historically one of the country's most important centres of coal and steel production.

History

Essen grew around a women's abbey founded about 845 by Altfrid, bishop of Hildesheim. From the nineteenth century the city became synonymous with the Krupp family and their steel and armaments empire; the Villa Hügel, the Krupp family residence, overlooks the Baldeneysee. Essen's coal mines and steelworks drew heavy Allied bombing in the Second World War. The Zollverein Coal Mine Industrial Complex, with its Bauhaus-influenced Shaft XII, closed in 1986 and was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2001.

Modern city

After the decline of heavy industry, Essen developed into a services and energy centre; major utilities such as RWE and E.ON have been headquartered there. The city was European Capital of Culture in 2010 on behalf of the whole Ruhr region (RUHR.2010) and was named European Green Capital in 2017. Sights include Essen Minster and its Golden Madonna — one of the oldest sculptures of the Virgin Mary in the West — the Museum Folkwang, the Aalto Opera House, and the Grugapark. The Spiel Essen fair held annually is the world's largest fair for board games.
