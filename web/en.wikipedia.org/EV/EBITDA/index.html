EV/EBITDA (enterprise value divided by earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation) is a valuation multiple used to compare the value of companies, including their debt, relative to their cash earnings. It is widely used in investment banking, private equity and equity research as a rough proxy for the number of years of operating cash earnings needed to pay for the whole business.

Interpretation

Enterprise value equals market capitalisation plus total debt and minority interests, minus cash and equivalents; it represents the theoretical cost of acquiring the entire firm. Because EV counts all capital providers and EBITDA is measured before interest and taxes, the ratio is neutral to capital structure and tax regime, unlike the price-to-earnings ratio. Lower multiples may indicate relative cheapness, though typical levels vary greatly by industry — capital-intensive sectors such as telecoms and utilities often trade at low multiples, while high-growth software firms trade at much higher ones.

Limitations

EBITDA ignores capital expenditure, working capital needs and differences in depreciation policy, so EV/EBITDA can flatter businesses with heavy ongoing investment requirements. Analysts therefore often use adjusted EBITDA and compare the multiple with EV/EBIT and free-cash-flow-based measures.
