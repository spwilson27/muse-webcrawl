Enterprise value

Enterprise value (EV), also called total enterprise value (TEV) or firm value, is an economic measure reflecting the market value of a business as a whole. It is commonly used in valuation, mergers and acquisitions, and financial modelling as a more comprehensive alternative to market capitalisation, because it takes into account a company's debt, cash and other claims on the business.

Formula

Enterprise value is calculated as:

EV = market capitalisation + total debt + minority interest + preferred equity − cash and cash equivalents

More complete versions include pension deficits and other debt-like items in the claims added, and other liquid assets among the items subtracted. Intuitively, EV approximates what an acquirer would effectively pay: the buyer must assume the company's debt but gains its cash, so a cash-rich, debt-free company may have an EV below its market capitalisation.

Use in multiples

EV is the numerator of common valuation multiples such as EV/EBITDA and EV/sales, which compare the value of the whole firm to earnings or revenue generated for all capital providers. These are often preferred to price/earnings ratios when comparing companies with different capital structures or tax regimes.

Limitations

EV is based on market prices for equity but often book values for debt and other items, and it can be distorted by complex structures, leases, pensions and minority interests. Analysts adjust the basic formula to suit the circumstances of the company being valued.
