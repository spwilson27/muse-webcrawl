Remotely operated underwater vehicle

A remotely operated underwater vehicle (ROV) is a free-swimming submersible craft used to perform tasks underwater, controlled in real time by an operator aboard a vessel or platform through an umbilical cable (tether) that carries power and data.

Design

ROVs carry cameras, lights, sonar, and thrusters, and many are fitted with manipulator arms and tooling for sampling, inspection, and intervention. Observation-class ROVs are small and used mainly for inspection, while work-class ROVs are large, powerful vehicles used in offshore construction and maintenance.

Uses

ROVs are extensively used by the offshore oil and gas industry to inspect and maintain subsea equipment, by scientists to explore and sample the deep ocean, by navies for mine countermeasures and salvage, and in the investigation of shipwrecks such as the Titanic.
