Trial of Jesus


Christ before the high priest

In the canonical gospels, the trial of Jesus refers to the proceedings in the Sanhedrin and before Pontius Pilate that preceded the crucifixion of Jesus. Following his arrest in the Garden of Gethsemane, Jesus was brought before the Jewish council, then before the Roman prefect Pilate, who ultimately sentenced him to death.

Before the Sanhedrin

According to the synoptic gospels, Jesus was tried at night before the high priest Caiaphas and the Sanhedrin, where witnesses testified against him and he was condemned for blasphemy after affirming that he was the Christ, the Son of God. Scholars debate the historicity and legal form of this proceeding, since some features appear inconsistent with later rabbinic descriptions of capital trials.

Before Pilate

Because the Sanhedrin lacked authority to carry out executions, Jesus was taken to Pontius Pilate, the Roman governor of Judaea. The gospels portray Pilate as finding no basis for a charge but yielding to pressure from the crowd, washing his hands of responsibility. Pilate ordered the crucifixion, carried out by Roman soldiers at Golgotha.

Historical analysis

Most historians accept that Jesus was crucified by order of Pilate, one of the best-attested facts of his life, while details of the trials remain subjects of scholarly debate.
