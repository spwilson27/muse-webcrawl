Soichiro Honda

Soichiro Honda (17 November 1906 – 5 August 1991) was a Japanese engineer and industrialist who founded the Honda Motor Company in 1948, building it from a small maker of motorized bicycles into the world's largest motorcycle manufacturer and a major global automobile producer.

Early life

Honda was born in Hamamatsu, the son of a blacksmith and bicycle repairman. Fascinated by machinery from childhood, he left school at fifteen to apprentice as a mechanic in Tokyo. In 1937 he founded Tōkai Seiki to manufacture piston rings, winning a contract with Toyota only after his first rings failed inspection and he educated himself in metallurgy.

Founding Honda

After his factory was destroyed by bombing and the 1945 Mikawa earthquake, Honda sold the remnants and in 1946 established the Honda Technical Research Institute. Attaching surplus generator engines to bicycles, he produced a hit motorized bicycle; in 1948 he incorporated Honda Motor Co., Ltd. with partner Takeo Fujisawa. The 1949 Dream D-Type and the 1958 Super Cub — eventually the best-selling motor vehicle in history — made Honda dominant in motorcycles.

Automobiles and later years

Despite government discouragement, Honda entered automobile production in 1963 and the United States market successfully; its CVCC engine met stringent 1970s American emissions standards without a catalytic converter. Honda retired as president in 1973 but remained a restless presence, travelling among factories. He died in Tokyo in 1991.

Legacy

Honda is remembered for hands-on engineering, contempt for bureaucracy, and aphorisms such as "success represents the 1% of your work that results from the 99% that is called failure." He was inducted into the Automotive Hall of Fame and remains one of Japan's most admired entrepreneurs.
