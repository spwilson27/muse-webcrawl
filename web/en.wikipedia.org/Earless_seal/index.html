Earless seal

The earless seals, phocids, or true seals are one of the three main groups of mammals within the seal lineage, Pinnipedia. All true seals are members of the family Phocidae. They are sometimes called crawling seals to distinguish them from the fur seals and sea lions of the family Otariidae. Seals live in the oceans of both hemispheres and are mostly confined to polar, subpolar, and temperate climates, with the exception of the more widespread monk seals.

Characteristics

Phocids lack external ear flaps (pinnae), having only small openings for the ears, and cannot flex their hind flippers forward under the body, which makes them clumsy on land: they move by wriggling or "galumphing". In compensation, they are more streamlined swimmers than otariids, propelling themselves mainly with their hind flippers and side-to-side movements of the rear body. Their foreflippers are short, clawed and furred, used chiefly for steering.

Diversity

The family contains 19 living species, including the harbour seal, grey seal, harp seal, leopard seal, Weddell seal, the huge southern elephant seal — the largest pinniped and largest living carnivoran — and the critically endangered Mediterranean and Hawaiian monk seals. True seals generally feed on fish, squid and krill, though the leopard seal also preys on penguins and other seals.
