Arroyo (watercourse)

An arroyo is a dry watercourse that temporarily or seasonally fills and flows after sufficient rain. Arroyos are characteristic of arid and semi-arid regions, such as the southwestern United States and northern Mexico, where the term comes from Spanish.

Formation and hazards

Infrequent but intense rainfall runs off quickly across sparsely vegetated ground, cutting steep-sided channels with flat floors. Because they can fill rapidly during storms upstream, dry arroyos pose a flash-flood hazard to people and livestock.

Ecology and human use

Although dry most of the year, arroyos concentrate moisture and support ribbons of vegetation such as mesquite and cottonwood, forming important wildlife corridors in desert landscapes. Similar landforms elsewhere are called wadis in North Africa and Arabia and nullahs in India.
