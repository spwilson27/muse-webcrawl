Canadian Centennial

The Canadian Centennial was a yearlong celebration held in 1967 to celebrate the 100th anniversary of Canadian Confederation. Celebrations in Canada occurred throughout the year but culminated on Dominion Day, July 1. Commemorative coins were minted, numerous special events were held, and the year is remembered as a high point of Canadian nationalism and optimism.

Preparations

The federal government established the Centennial Commission to plan events and funded projects across the country. Many communities built "centennial projects" — arenas, libraries, community halls, and arts centres — with cost-sharing support from Ottawa and the provinces. The Centennial Flame was lit on Parliament Hill by Prime Minister Lester B. Pearson on 31 December 1966.

Events

The centrepiece of the year was Expo 67, the world's fair in Montreal, which drew more than 50 million visitors and is widely regarded as one of the most successful exhibitions of the twentieth century. Other events included the Centennial Voyageur Canoe Pageant, Confederation trains travelling the country, and visits by Queen Elizabeth II, who attended the July 1 celebrations in Ottawa.

Legacy

The centennial left a lasting legacy of cultural institutions, including arts centres and the expanded CBC coverage of national events. Bobby Gimby's song "Canada" became the unofficial anthem of the year. Many historians view 1967 as the last unambiguously celebratory moment of Canadian unity before the rise of the Quebec sovereignty movement and other regional tensions in the following decades.
