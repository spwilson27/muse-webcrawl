Gallaudet University is a private federally chartered university in Washington, D.C., for the education of deaf and hard of hearing students. It was founded in 1864 by a charter signed by President Abraham Lincoln and is the first school for the advanced education of the deaf and hard of hearing in the world, and remains the only higher education institution in which all programs and services are specifically designed to accommodate deaf and hard of hearing students.

History

The university began as a grammar school for deaf and blind children, founded with support from philanthropist Amos Kendall on land he donated. Edward Miner Gallaudet, son of Thomas Hopkins Gallaudet, co-founder of the first permanent school for the deaf in the United States, served as its first president for 46 years. The institution was named in honor of Thomas Hopkins Gallaudet.

Deaf President Now

In March 1988, student protests known as "Deaf President Now" demanded that the board of trustees appoint a deaf president. The movement succeeded, and I. King Jordan became the university's first deaf president, a landmark moment in deaf civil rights.

Academics and language

Instruction is bilingual, in American Sign Language (ASL) and English. Hearing students are admitted to some programs, and all students are expected to use ASL on campus. Gallaudet is a major center for research on deaf culture, linguistics, and education.
