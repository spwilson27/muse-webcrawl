Torres Strait Islanders are the Indigenous Melanesian people of the Torres Strait Islands, a group of islands at the northernmost tip of Queensland, Australia, in the strait separating Cape York Peninsula from New Guinea. Together with Aboriginal peoples, they are the two distinct Indigenous groups of Australia, collectively referred to as Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples. Torres Strait Islander culture is distinct from that of Aboriginal Australians, with strong maritime traditions and cultural and linguistic links to the peoples of the New Guinea coast.

History

The islands have been inhabited for thousands of years. Sustained European contact began in the 1860s with the arrival of the bêche-de-mer and pearling industries, and in 1871 London Missionary Society missionaries arrived at Erub (Darnley Island), an event commemorated annually as the "Coming of the Light". Queensland annexed the islands in the 1870s. Islanders were largely excluded from the harsher controls applied to Aboriginal people under Queensland law and were recognised as a separate people, though they experienced discrimination and, in the Second World War, served in the Torres Strait Light Infantry Battalion. After the war large numbers migrated to mainland Australia for work.

Culture and languages

Islander society is organised into five island clusters — the western, central, eastern, top western and inner island groups — each with its own traditions, dances and clan and totem (augadh) systems. Two traditional languages are spoken: Kalaw Lagaw Ya in the western and central islands, and Meriam Mir in the eastern islands, alongside the widely spoken creole Yumplatok (Torres Strait Creole). Dance, song, storytelling, elaborate headdresses (dhari), pearl-shell and turtle-shell carving, and seafaring in outrigger canoes are central cultural expressions. The Torres Strait Islander flag, designed by Bernard Namok and adopted in 1992, features the dhari and a five-pointed star on green, blue and black panels.

Native title and recognition

The landmark Mabo case — brought by Meriam man Eddie Koiki Mabo and fellow plaintiffs from Mer (Murray Island) — produced the High Court of Australia's 1992 decision in Mabo v Queensland (No 2), which rejected the doctrine of terra nullius and recognised native title in Australian law. Sea country rights were further affirmed by the High Court's 2013 Torres Strait sea claim determination, which recognised native title over most of the region's waters.
