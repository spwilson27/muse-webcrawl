Capacitance

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Capacitance is the capability of a material object or device to store electric charge. It is measured by the charge in response to a difference in electric potential, expressed as the ratio of those quantities. Capacitance is defined as C = Q/V, where Q is the charge stored and V is the voltage across the device.

Units

The SI unit of capacitance is the farad (F), named after Michael Faraday: one farad is one coulomb per volt. Practical capacitors range from picofarads (pF) to thousands of farads in supercapacitors.

Parallel-plate capacitor

For two parallel conducting plates of area A separated by a distance d with a dielectric of permittivity ε between them, the capacitance is approximately C = εA/d. Capacitance therefore increases with plate area and dielectric permittivity, and decreases with separation.

Energy storage

The energy stored in a charged capacitor is U = ½CV². Capacitors exploit capacitance in electronic circuits for filtering, timing, coupling, and energy storage.
