Moon landing

Moon landing

First landing: Apollo 11, July 20, 1969
First person on the Moon: Neil Armstrong
Crewed landings: 6 (Apollo 11–17, 1969–1972)
Uncrewed landings: numerous (US, USSR, China, India, Japan, others)

A Moon landing is the arrival of a spacecraft on the surface of the Moon, including both crewed and robotic missions. The first human-made object to reach the Moon's surface was the Soviet Union's Luna 2 probe on September 13, 1959. The first crewed landing was achieved by the United States' Apollo 11 mission on July 20, 1969.

Crewed landings

Between 1969 and 1972, six Apollo missions landed on the Moon: Apollo 11, 12, 14, 15, 16, and 17. Twelve astronauts walked on the lunar surface, beginning with Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin, who landed in the Sea of Tranquility aboard the Lunar Module Eagle while Michael Collins remained in orbit. Armstrong's first step was accompanied by the words, "That's one small step for [a] man, one giant leap for mankind." The last crewed landing, Apollo 17 in December 1972, carried the only scientist to walk on the Moon, geologist Harrison Schmitt.

Uncrewed landings

The Soviet Luna programme achieved the first soft landing (Luna 9, 1966) and returned samples robotically. The US Surveyor programme also soft-landed probes in the 1960s. In the 21st century, China landed Chang'e 3 (2013), Chang'e 4 on the far side (2019), and sample-return missions Chang'e 5 (2020) and Chang'e 6 (2024). India's Chandrayaan-3 landed near the lunar south pole in 2023, Japan's SLIM landed in 2024, and commercial landers such as Intuitive Machines' Odysseus followed.

Significance

The Apollo landings were a pivotal achievement of the Space Race and remain the only occasions on which humans have travelled beyond low Earth orbit. Returned samples (382 kg from Apollo) transformed understanding of the Moon's origin and geology. Moon landing conspiracy theories alleging the landings were faked have been widely refuted by independent evidence.
