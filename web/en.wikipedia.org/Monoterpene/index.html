Monoterpene

Monoterpenes are a class of terpenes consisting of two isoprene units, giving them the molecular formula C10H16. They are major components of the essential oils of many plants and are responsible for much of the scent of flowers, herbs, and conifer resins.

Structure and examples

Monoterpenes may be acyclic (such as myrcene and geraniol), monocyclic (limonene, menthol), or bicyclic (pinene, camphor, borneol). Oxygenated derivatives, often called monoterpenoids, include linalool and citral.

Roles and uses

In plants they act in defence against herbivores and pathogens and in attracting pollinators. Industrially they are used as fragrances, flavourings, and solvents; α-pinene is the principal component of turpentine.
