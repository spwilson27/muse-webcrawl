Norilsk

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Norilsk (Russian: Норильск) is a city in Krasnoyarsk Krai, Russia, located north of the Arctic Circle in Siberia, east of the Yenisei River. With a population of roughly 175,000, it is one of the northernmost cities in the world with more than 100,000 inhabitants. It has no road or rail connection to the rest of Russia open to ordinary travellers and is reached by air or by river and sea via the port of Dudinka.

History

The city was founded in the 1930s to exploit the region's immense deposits of nickel, copper, and palladium, among the richest in the world. Much of the early construction was carried out by prisoners of the Norillag Gulag camp. After the camp closed in 1956 the city continued to grow around the mining and metallurgical combine, now operated by Nornickel.

Environment

Norilsk is notorious for severe industrial pollution: smelters have emitted millions of tonnes of sulphur dioxide, and the surrounding tundra is damaged for many kilometres. A major diesel spill in 2020 released about 17,500 tonnes of fuel into local rivers. The climate is subarctic, with long, extremely cold winters, a polar night lasting about six weeks, and buildings constructed on piles driven into permafrost.
