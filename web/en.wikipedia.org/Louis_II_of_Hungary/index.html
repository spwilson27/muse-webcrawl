Louis II (1 July 1506 – 29 August 1526) was King of Hungary, Croatia and Bohemia from 1516 to 1526. He was killed at the Battle of Mohács fighting the Ottoman Empire, an event that ended medieval Hungary and opened the way to 150 years of Ottoman rule over much of the country.

Reign

Louis was born prematurely at Buda and was kept alive, according to tradition, in the carcass of a freshly slaughtered animal used as an incubator. He succeeded his father Vladislaus II at the age of nine and was crowned in both Hungary and Bohemia. His court was extravagant while the kingdom's finances and defences decayed; the southern border fortresses fell one by one to Sultan Suleiman the Magnificent, Belgrade in 1521.

Battle of Mohács and death

On 29 August 1526 the Hungarian army met Suleiman's much larger force on the plain of Mohács and was annihilated in about two hours. Louis fled the field and drowned when his horse fell in a marshy stream. Dying childless, he was succeeded in Bohemia and western Hungary by his brother-in-law Ferdinand of Austria, beginning Habsburg rule.
