Italian football league system

The pyramid of Italian football

The Italian football league system, also known as the piramide calcistica (football pyramid), is the hierarchical structure of league competitions for association football clubs in Italy, administered by the Italian Football Federation (FIGC) and its leagues. It comprises over 9,000 teams in hundreds of divisions, connected by promotion and relegation.

Structure

LevelLeague
1Serie A (20 clubs)
2Serie B (20 clubs)
3Serie C (60 clubs in three groups)
4Serie D (semi-professional/amateur, nine groups)
5–9Eccellenza, Promozione, Prima Categoria, Seconda Categoria, Terza Categoria (regional)

The top three levels are fully professional. Serie A and Serie B are run by their own leagues, while Serie C is run by the Lega Pro. Below Serie C, the system is administered by the Lega Nazionale Dilettanti (National Amateur League).

History

A national round-robin top flight (Serie A) dates from 1929, replacing earlier regional group formats. The pyramid took broadly its modern shape with post-war reforms, and Serie C was split and remerged several times before settling on a three-group format of 60 clubs.

Cup competitions

Clubs also compete in the Coppa Italia (open to professional clubs), the Supercoppa Italiana, and amateur cups at lower levels, alongside UEFA competitions for top Serie A finishers.
