Lord Warden of the Cinque Ports

The Lord Warden of the Cinque Ports is a ceremonial official in the United Kingdom, once one of the most powerful appointments in medieval England. The office oversees the confederation of the Cinque Ports — originally Hastings, New Romney, Hythe, Dover, and Sandwich — coastal towns in Kent and Sussex that provided ships and men for the defence of the English Channel in return for privileges.

History

The office dates to the twelfth century; the Lord Warden combined it with the constableship of Dover Castle and wielded extensive military, judicial, and admiralty powers along the Channel coast, including authority over shipwrecks and, as Admiral of the Cinque Ports, a prize jurisdiction. As the Royal Navy grew, the office's practical power waned and it became a mark of honour bestowed on distinguished figures.

Notable holders

Famous Lord Wardens have included the Duke of Wellington, Winston Churchill (appointed 1941), and Queen Elizabeth The Queen Mother (1978–2002). The official residence is Walmer Castle, where the Duke of Wellington died in 1852. The modern office is purely ceremonial, and the holder is appointed by the monarch.
