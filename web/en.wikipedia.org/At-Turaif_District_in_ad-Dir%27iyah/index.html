At-Turaif District in ad-Dir'iyah

At-Turaif
Location: Diriyah, Riyadh Province, Saudi Arabia
Founded: 15th century; developed 18th century
UNESCO World Heritage Site: inscribed 2010

At-Turaif is a historic district in Diriyah, northwest of Riyadh, Saudi Arabia. Founded in the 15th century, it was the original home of the Saudi royal family and the capital of the Emirate of Diriyah, the first Saudi state, from 1727 until its destruction by Ottoman-Egyptian forces in 1818.

Description

The district bears the distinctive Najdi architectural style, built of mudbrick and characterised by ornamental geometric patterns and large palaces along the edge of Wadi Hanifa. The most notable structures include the Salwa Palace, a sprawling palace complex that served as the seat of government, and the Imam Muhammad bin Saud Mosque.

World Heritage status

At-Turaif was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2010 as an outstanding example of the Najdi architectural style and of the urban development of the first Saudi capital. After years of restoration, the district opened to visitors as part of the Diriyah Gate development, including the Bujairi Terrace dining district across the wadi.
