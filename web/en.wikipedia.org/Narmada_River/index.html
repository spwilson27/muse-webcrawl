Narmada River

The Narmada River is the fifth-longest river in India and the longest river flowing entirely within the country to flow westwards. Rising at Amarkantak in Madhya Pradesh, it flows about 1,312 km west through a rift valley between the Vindhya and Satpura ranges, through Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra and Gujarat, before emptying into the Gulf of Khambhat in the Arabian Sea.

Course and significance

Unlike most Indian rivers, the Narmada flows west through a graben, forming the traditional boundary between North and South India. It is one of the most sacred rivers of Hinduism — regarded as a goddess and the daughter of Shiva — and pilgrims perform the Narmada Parikrama, a circumambulation of the entire river. Noted sites include the marble gorge at Bhedaghat near Jabalpur, the temple town of Omkareshwar, and the pilgrimage city of Maheshwar.

Dams and controversy

The river basin is the site of some 30 major dams, most controversially the Sardar Sarovar Dam in Gujarat, whose height increases displaced hundreds of thousands of people and prompted the Narmada Bachao Andolan ("Save Narmada") movement led by Medha Patkar. Other dams include Indira Sagar and Omkareshwar.
