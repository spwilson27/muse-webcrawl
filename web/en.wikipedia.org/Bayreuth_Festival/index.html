The Bayreuth Festival (German: Bayreuther Festspiele) is a music festival held annually in Bayreuth, Germany, devoted exclusively to staged performances of the operas of Richard Wagner. Founded by the composer himself, it takes place in the Bayreuth Festspielhaus, a theatre Wagner designed specifically for his works, and it remains under the direction of his descendants.

History

Wagner conceived a festival presenting his works under ideal conditions, free of the compromises of ordinary opera houses. With financial support from King Ludwig II of Bavaria and subscribers ("Patronat" patrons), the Festspielhaus was built on the "Green Hill" in Bayreuth. The first festival opened in August 1876 with the first complete performances of the Ring des Nibelungen cycle, attended by Emperor Wilhelm I, Tchaikovsky, Grieg, and other notables. Wagner directed the festival until his death in 1883.

The Festspielhaus

The theatre embodies Wagner's ideas: a fan-shaped, steeply raked auditorium with continental seating, no boxes, and a deep, covered orchestra pit — the "mystic abyss" — that hides the orchestra from view and blends its sound. Its acoustics are widely considered exceptional for Wagner's music.

Modern festival

After the Second World War the festival was revived in 1951 by Wagner's grandsons Wieland and Wolfgang Wagner, whose austere "New Bayreuth" productions stripped away traditional staging. The festival presents the ten mature Wagner operas, from Der fliegende Holländer to Parsifal; a complete Ring cycle is performed most years. Demand for tickets vastly exceeds supply, and waiting lists have historically stretched to a decade or more.
