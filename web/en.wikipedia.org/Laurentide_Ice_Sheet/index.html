Laurentide Ice Sheet

The Laurentide Ice Sheet was the massive sheet of glacial ice that covered much of Canada and the northern United States during the Quaternary ice ages, most recently from about 95,000 to 20,000 years ago.

Extent and effects

At its maximum it covered some 13 million km² and reached 3–4 km thick over Hudson Bay, depressing the crust — which is still rebounding today. Meltwater from its retreat formed the Great Lakes and proglacial lakes such as Lake Agassiz, whose sudden drainages (notably the 8.2 ka event) affected global climate. The last remnants, over Hudson Bay and the Keewatin, melted by about 6,000 years ago.
