Furnace Creek, California

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Furnace Creek is a census-designated place in Inyo County, California, United States, within Death Valley National Park. It is the site of the park's headquarters and visitor center, and it holds the record for the highest reliably measured air temperature on Earth: 56.7 °C (134 °F), recorded on July 10, 1913. The settlement lies 58 metres (190 feet) below sea level.

History

The area was home to the Timbisha Shoshone, who called the valley Tümpisa. Borax mining in the 1880s, centred on the Harmony Borax Works nearby, brought the famous twenty-mule teams through the valley. The name Furnace Creek derives from a smelter furnace used in early mining. The Furnace Creek Ranch was established as a working ranch and later became a resort; the Furnace Creek Inn opened in 1927. Death Valley was proclaimed a national monument in 1933 and a national park in 1994.

Climate

Furnace Creek has a subtropical hot desert climate. It is one of the hottest and driest places in North America: annual rainfall is about 60 mm, and summer daytime highs routinely exceed 45 °C. The extreme heat record of 56.7 °C was measured at Greenland Ranch (now Furnace Creek) on 10 July 1913 and is recognised by the World Meteorological Organization as the highest air temperature ever reliably recorded on Earth. July 2018 was among the hottest months ever recorded anywhere, with an average temperature of 42.3 °C.

Community

Residents are largely National Park Service employees and resort staff. The Furnace Creek Golf Course, at 65 metres below sea level, is the world's lowest. Furnace Creek Airport serves general aviation.
