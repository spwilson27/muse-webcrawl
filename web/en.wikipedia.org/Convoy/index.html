Convoy

A convoy is a group of vehicles or ships travelling together for mutual support and protection, most famously merchant ships sailing under naval escort in wartime. Grouping vessels allows a limited number of warships to defend many merchantmen at once, concentrates defensive firepower and anti-submarine capability, and reduces the chance that an enemy raider will find targets at all.

Naval convoys

Convoying is an ancient practice, used by the Venetian Republic for its merchant fleets and by Spain's treasure fleets crossing the Atlantic. Britain's Royal Navy employed convoys extensively during the Napoleonic Wars. In the First World War, initial Admiralty reluctance gave way in 1917 to a general convoy system that sharply reduced losses to German U-boats.

In the Second World War the Battle of the Atlantic turned on the convoy system: escorted groups, designated by codes such as HX (Halifax to Liverpool) and PQ (to North Russia), were defended by destroyers, corvettes and escort carriers, aided by radar, sonar and signals intelligence. Although individual convoys suffered heavy attacks, losses per ship were far lower than for vessels sailing independently.

Land convoys

On land, convoys of military trucks move supplies under guard, and in civilian life the term covers organised groups of vehicles travelling together, from truck convoys coordinated by radio to protest convoys and aid convoys delivering humanitarian relief in conflict zones.
