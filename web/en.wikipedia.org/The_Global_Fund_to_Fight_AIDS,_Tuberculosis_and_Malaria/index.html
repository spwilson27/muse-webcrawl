The Global Fund to Fight AIDS, Tuberculosis and Malaria

Formation2002
TypeInternational financing institution / partnership
HeadquartersGeneva, Switzerland
PurposeFunding programmes against HIV/AIDS, tuberculosis and malaria

The Global Fund to Fight AIDS, Tuberculosis and Malaria (often simply the Global Fund) is an international financing and partnership organisation based in Geneva, Switzerland. Created in 2002, it raises and invests money to support locally led programmes against the three diseases in more than 100 countries.

History

The Fund was established following calls at the 2001 UN General Assembly Special Session on HIV/AIDS and the G8 summit in Genoa, with the first grants awarded in 2003. It operates as a partnership between governments, civil society, the private sector, and affected communities, rather than implementing programmes itself.

Impact

According to the Fund, health programmes it supports have saved tens of millions of lives since 2002, and mortality from AIDS, tuberculosis, and malaria in countries where it invests has fallen substantially. Funding is raised in multi-year replenishment rounds from donor governments and private contributors.
