Bonn is a city in western Germany on the banks of the Rhine, in the state of North Rhine-Westphalia, about 24 kilometres (15 mi) south-southeast of Cologne. Founded in Roman times, it was the residence of the Electors of Cologne for centuries and is the birthplace of Ludwig van Beethoven. From 1949 to 1990, Bonn was the seat of government — and effectively the capital — of West Germany, and it remained the seat of the federal government until 1999 after German reunification.

History

The Romans established a fort, Castra Bonnensia, on the site around the 1st century CE, making Bonn one of Germany's oldest cities. In the medieval and early modern periods it served as the residence of the Prince-Electors of Cologne. It passed to Prussia in 1815, and the University of Bonn, founded in 1818 (with antecedents from 1777), became one of Germany's leading universities.

Capital era

In 1949 the Parliamentary Council met in Bonn and drafted the Basic Law for the Federal Republic of Germany. Bonn was chosen over Frankfurt as the provisional capital of West Germany, a status it held until reunification in 1990. The 1991 Hauptstadtbeschluss designated Berlin the capital, and the government moved in 1999–2000; however, the Berlin/Bonn Act guarantees that Bonn retains several federal ministries' primary seats, a large share of federal jobs, and the official title of Federal City (Bundesstadt).

Present day

Bonn hosts numerous United Nations institutions, including the UN Climate Change Secretariat and the campus of UN organizations focused on sustainability, and is the headquarters of Deutsche Post/DHL and Deutsche Telekom. Sights include the Beethoven House museum, the baroque Poppelsdorf Palace, the Bonn Minster, and the cherry-blossom-lined streets of the Altstadt that draw visitors each spring.
