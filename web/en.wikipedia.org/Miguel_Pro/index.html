Miguel Pro

Miguel Agustín Pro Juárez (January 13, 1891 – November 23, 1927), also known as Blessed Miguel Pro, was a Mexican Jesuit priest executed during the persecution of the Catholic Church under President Plutarco Elías Calles and the Cristero War. Known for his wit, his use of disguises to carry out clandestine ministry in Mexico City, and his charitable work with the poor, he became one of the best-known martyrs of the period.

Pro was arrested on a charge of involvement in an assassination attempt against former president Álvaro Obregón, a charge widely regarded as baseless; he was executed by firing squad without trial on November 23, 1927. Photographs of his execution, ordered published by the government to intimidate the Cristeros, instead became icons of devotion. He reportedly forgave his executioners and died holding a crucifix and rosary, crying "¡Viva Cristo Rey!" ("Long live Christ the King!").

Veneration

Pope John Paul II beatified Miguel Pro on September 25, 1988. His feast day is November 23, and he is venerated especially in Mexico and among Mexican-American Catholics.
