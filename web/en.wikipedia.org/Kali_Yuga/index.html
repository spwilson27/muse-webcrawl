Kali Yuga

Kali Yuga (Sanskrit: कलियुग, "the age of Kali") is the fourth and worst of the four yugas (world ages) in Hindu cosmology, characterised by strife, discord (kali) and moral decline. It follows the Dvapara Yuga and is the age in which humanity is traditionally understood to be living at present.

Duration and chronology

According to Puranic chronology, Kali Yuga lasts 432,000 human years. Its traditional starting point is 3102 BCE, associated with the departure of Krishna and the end of the events of the Mahabharata. Hindu scriptures describe dharma as standing on only one of its four legs during this age.

Characteristics

The Vishnu Purana and other texts describe the signs of the age: rulers become corrupt, wealth and appetite govern conduct, lifespans shorten, and spiritual practice declines. At the end of the age, the avatar Kalki is prophesied to appear, destroy the wicked, and inaugurate a new Satya Yuga, beginning the cycle of yugas anew.

Interpretations

Some modern teachers, notably Sri Yukteswar in The Holy Science (1894), reinterpreted the yuga cycle as a much shorter, ascending and descending cycle of about 24,000 years, placing the present era in an ascending Dvapara Yuga rather than in Kali Yuga. Astronomical and astrological traditions also associate Kali Yuga with the demon Kali, a personification of conflict, not to be confused with the goddess Kālī.
