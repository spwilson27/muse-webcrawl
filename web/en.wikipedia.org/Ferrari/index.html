Ferrari N.V. is an Italian luxury sports car manufacturer based in Maranello, Italy. Founded by Enzo Ferrari in 1939 from the Alfa Romeo racing division as Auto Avio Costruzioni, the company built its first car in 1940 under the Ferrari name in 1947. Its cars are generally seen as a symbol of speed, luxury and wealth, and the Ferrari brand is among the world's most valuable.

History

Enzo Ferrari, a former racing driver, ran Alfa Romeo's racing team (Scuderia Ferrari) in the 1930s before founding his own marque. The first Ferrari-badged car, the 125 S, debuted in 1947 with a 1.5-litre V12 — the V12 layout becoming a marque hallmark. Fiat bought 50% of the company in 1969; after Enzo's death in 1988 Fiat raised its stake to 90%. Ferrari was spun off from Fiat Chrysler Automobiles with an IPO in 2015–16.

Motorsport

Scuderia Ferrari is the oldest surviving and most successful team in Formula One, competing since the championship's first season in 1950, with a record tally of Constructors' Championships and drivers' titles won by greats including Juan Manuel Fangio, Niki Lauda and Michael Schumacher, who won five consecutive titles in 2000–2004. Ferrari has also won the 24 Hours of Le Mans outright, including victories in 2023 and 2024 with the 499P hypercar.

Road cars

Landmark models include the 250 GTO (1962), the F40 (1987), the Enzo (2002) and the hybrid LaFerrari (2013). Production remains deliberately limited to preserve exclusivity, though it has grown to roughly 13–14 thousand cars a year.
