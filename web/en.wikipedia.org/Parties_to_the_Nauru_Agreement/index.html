Parties to the Nauru Agreement

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The eight Pacific island states that are parties to the Nauru Agreement

The Parties to the Nauru Agreement (PNA) are eight Pacific Island nations that collectively control much of the world's largest sustainable tuna purse seine fishery. The agreement, signed in February 1982 on the island of Nauru, provides a framework for the member states to coordinate the management of their exclusive economic zones (EEZs) and to maximise the economic returns from their shared tuna resources.

Members

The eight parties are the Federated States of Micronesia, Kiribati, the Marshall Islands, Nauru, Palau, Papua New Guinea, Solomon Islands and Tuvalu. Together, the waters of the PNA members supply a large share of the global catch of skipjack tuna, the species most commonly used for canned tuna, as well as significant quantities of yellowfin and bigeye tuna.

History

The Nauru Agreement Concerning Cooperation in the Management of Fisheries of Common Interest was concluded in 1982, shortly after the adoption of the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea established 200-nautical-mile exclusive economic zones. The island states, whose land areas are tiny but whose ocean territories are vast, recognised that by acting together they could exercise far greater leverage over distant-water fishing nations than any of them could alone.

Vessel Day Scheme

The PNA's best-known management tool is the Purse Seine Vessel Day Scheme (VDS), introduced in 2007. Under the scheme, the total number of fishing days available in PNA waters is capped, and days are allocated among the parties, which may trade them. By limiting supply, the scheme sharply increased the price of a fishing day and multiplied the access revenues earned by member states, which rose from tens of millions of US dollars per year to several hundred million.

Conservation measures

The PNA has adopted measures that go beyond those of the wider Western and Central Pacific Fisheries Commission, including a ban on fishing in certain high-seas pockets, restrictions on fish aggregating devices (FADs), 100 percent observer coverage on purse seiners, and the requirement that catches be retained in full rather than discarded. In 2022 the PNA Office, headquartered in Majuro in the Marshall Islands, oversaw the certification of the PNA fishery as sustainable by the Marine Stewardship Council.
