Logging

Logging is the process of cutting, processing and moving trees to a location for transport. It may include felling, skidding, on-site processing and loading of trees or logs onto trucks or rail cars. Logging is the first stage of the timber supply chain that feeds sawmills, pulp and paper mills and other forest-products industries.

Methods

In clear-cutting, most or all trees in an area are felled at once, a method suited to even-aged stands and shade-intolerant species. Selective logging removes only certain trees, leaving the stand's structure partly intact. Shelterwood and seed-tree systems stage removal over several entries to encourage natural regeneration.

Equipment

Modern mechanised logging uses feller bunchers, harvesters that fell, delimb and cut stems to length in one operation, forwarders, skidders and processors. In steep terrain, cable yarding systems move logs by overhead lines. Traditional operations relied on axes, crosscut saws and animal or river transport — log drives floated timber downstream to mills.

Environmental effects

Poorly managed logging can cause deforestation, habitat fragmentation, soil compaction and erosion, and damage to streams. Certification schemes such as the Forest Stewardship Council promote sustainable forest management. Illegal logging remains a major driver of forest loss in many tropical countries.
