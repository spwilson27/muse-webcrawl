Trope (literature)

A trope is a recurring literary device, motif or figure of speech.

A literary trope is the use of figurative language, via word, phrase or an image, for artistic effect such as using a figure of speech. The word trope has also come to be used for describing commonly recurring literary and rhetorical devices, motifs or clichés in creative works.

History of the term

The term derives from the Greek tropos (τρόπος), "a turn, a change," and entered rhetoric through Latin tropus. Classical and medieval rhetoricians catalogued tropes such as metaphor, metonymy, synecdoche and irony as "turns" of language away from literal meaning—Quintilian treated them systematically in the Institutio Oratoria. Renaissance handbooks of rhetoric expanded the lists, and the figures of speech remained central to literary education for centuries.

Modern usage

In contemporary criticism and popular culture, "trope" more often denotes a recognisable plot device, character type or narrative convention—for example the damsel in distress, the mentor figure, or the chosen one—that audiences identify across many works. Websites such as TV Tropes popularised this cataloguing usage. Tropes in this sense are not inherently good or bad: they are economical storytelling tools that can be played straight, subverted, deconstructed or recombined.
