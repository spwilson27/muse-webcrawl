Robin Sage

Robin Sage is a training exercise conducted by the United States Army as the final test for soldiers training to become Green Berets in the U.S. Army Special Forces. The exercise takes place across a large area of rural North Carolina known within the training scenario as "Pineland," a fictional country occupied by a hostile regime.

Overview

Robin Sage has been run in various forms since the 1950s and is the culmination of the Special Forces Qualification Course ("Q Course") based at Fort Bragg (now Fort Liberty). Student teams are inserted into the fictional Pineland, where they must train, equip, and organise a guerrilla force of local role-players to wage unconventional warfare against a conventional occupying army played by other soldiers.

The scenario

The exercise spans thousands of square miles of North Carolina countryside, involving civilian volunteers from local communities who act as guerrillas, auxiliary supporters, and government forces. Trainees practise liaison, negotiation, sabotage, ambushes, and civic action under the supervision of cadre. Because live scenarios unfold in public areas, the exercise has occasionally alarmed residents unfamiliar with it; a 2002 incident in which a trainee was shot by a local police officer who mistook the exercise for criminal activity led to tightened coordination with local authorities.

Significance

Successful completion of Robin Sage is the gateway to assignment to a Special Forces Group. The exercise is regarded as one of the most realistic large-scale unconventional warfare rehearsals in the world and embodies the Green Berets' motto, De oppresso liber — "to free the oppressed."
