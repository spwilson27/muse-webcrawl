Attic calendar

The Attic calendar or Athenian calendar is the lunisolar calendar that was in use in ancient Attica, the territory of ancient Athens. It is the best known of the many ancient Greek calendars, because Athens left abundant literary and inscriptional evidence. The year began in midsummer, around the first new moon after the summer solstice.

The Acropolis; the calendar organised the religious life of the Athenian polis.

Months

The year had twelve lunar months of 29 or 30 days, alternating "hollow" and "full" months: Hekatombaion, Metageitnion, Boedromion, Pyanepsion, Maimakterion, Poseideon, Gamelion, Anthesterion, Elaphebolion, Mounichion, Thargelion and Skirophorion. The total of about 354 days fell short of the solar year, so an extra month — a second Poseideon — was intercalated from time to time, in principle following the 19-year Metonic cycle, though in practice intercalation was irregular and politically influenced.

Days of the month

Months were divided into three ten-day periods associated with the phases of the moon. Days were counted forward in the first decade, forward with an added phrase in the second, and backward from the end of the month in the third.

Festivals

The calendar's primary purpose was religious: it scheduled the great festivals of the city, including the Panathenaea in Hekatombaion, the Eleusinian Mysteries in Boedromion, the Anthesteria in Anthesterion and the City Dionysia in Elaphebolion. Alongside the festival calendar ran a separate political calendar of ten prytanies used by the Council of Five Hundred.
