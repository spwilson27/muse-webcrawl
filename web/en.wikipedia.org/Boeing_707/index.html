The Boeing 707 is an early American long-range narrow-body airliner, the first jetliner developed and produced by Boeing Commercial Airplanes. Developed from the Boeing 367-80 prototype — first flown in 1954 — the 707 has a four-engine configuration and a swept wing. Its first flight took place on December 20, 1957, and it entered commercial service with Pan Am on October 26, 1958, inaugurating regular transatlantic jet service. Although it was not the first jetliner in service (the de Havilland Comet preceded it), the 707 was the first to be commercially successful and is often credited with ushering in the Jet Age.

Development

Boeing committed its own funds to the secret 367-80 project, betting that both the U.S. Air Force (as a tanker) and airlines would adopt jet transports. Launch airline customer Pan Am ordered 20 aircraft in October 1955. The passenger versions seated typically 140–189 passengers; variants included the shortened 720, the stretched -320 Intercontinental, and convertible freighter models.

Service history

The 707 dominated passenger air transport in the 1960s and remained in common use through the 1970s. It also served extensively in military roles: the VC-137 served as Air Force One, the E-3 Sentry performs airborne warning and control (AWACS), and the E-8 and RC-135 families derive from the related C-135 line. Most airlines retired passenger 707s by the 1980s–90s as fuel costs and noise rules rose, though cargo and government examples soldiered on; actor John Travolta famously owned a former Qantas 707.
