Supply chain management

Supply chain management (SCM) is the management of the flow of goods, data and finances related to a product or service, from the procurement of raw materials to the delivery of the product at its final destination. It encompasses the planning and management of sourcing, procurement, production, inventory, logistics and returns across a network of suppliers, manufacturers, distributors and customers.

Overview

A supply chain is the network of organisations, people, activities, information and resources involved in supplying a product or service. SCM seeks to coordinate the members of that network so that products are produced and distributed in the right quantities, to the right places, at the right time, while minimising cost and maximising service. The term was popularised by consultant Keith Oliver in 1982.

Key processes

Core processes include demand forecasting and planning, supplier selection and procurement, production scheduling, inventory management, warehousing, transportation and distribution, order fulfilment and reverse logistics for returns and recycling. The SCOR (Supply Chain Operations Reference) model organises these into Plan, Source, Make, Deliver and Return.

Concepts and challenges

Well-known SCM concepts include just-in-time production, pioneered by Toyota; lean manufacturing; vendor-managed inventory; and the bullwhip effect, in which small fluctuations in consumer demand amplify into large swings upstream. Modern supply chains are global and vulnerable to disruption from natural disasters, geopolitics and pandemics, which has spurred interest in resilience, nearshoring and digital tools such as RFID tracking and AI-driven forecasting.
