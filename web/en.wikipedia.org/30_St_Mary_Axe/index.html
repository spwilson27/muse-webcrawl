30 St Mary Axe

30 St Mary Axe, previously known as the Swiss Re Building and informally as the Gherkin, is a commercial skyscraper in London's primary financial district, the City of London. It was completed in December 2003 and opened in April 2004. With 41 floors, it stands 180 metres (591 feet) tall and is one of the most recognisable landmarks of the London skyline.

Design and construction

The tower was designed by Norman Foster and the engineering firm Arup, and built by Skanska. It stands on the site of the former Baltic Exchange building, which had been severely damaged by a Provisional IRA bomb in 1992. The building's distinctive tapering, bullet-like form is generated by a diagrid structural system: its circular plan widens in profile towards the middle and narrows towards the top, reducing wind deflections and allowing natural light to penetrate deep into the floor plates. Spiral light wells, formed by rotating each floor plate slightly relative to the one below, assist natural ventilation and reduce the building's energy consumption.

History

The building was commissioned by the reinsurance company Swiss Re, which sold it in 2007. After its owners defaulted on loans, it was bought out of receivership in 2014 by the Brazilian Safra Group for a reported sum in excess of £700 million. The top floor contains a private dining club with panoramic views over London, and the base is surrounded by a public plaza with shops and restaurants.

The Gherkin has won numerous architectural awards, including the RIBA Stirling Prize in 2004, and appears frequently in films and television series set in London.
