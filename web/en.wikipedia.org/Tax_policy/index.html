Tax policy

Tax policy refers to the choices a government makes about which taxes to levy, at what rates, on whom, and how they are administered. It is a central instrument of fiscal policy, used to raise revenue, redistribute income, and influence economic behaviour.

Objectives and trade-offs

Analysts commonly evaluate tax systems against criteria of efficiency (minimising economic distortion and deadweight loss), equity (horizontal and vertical fairness), simplicity and administrability, and revenue adequacy. Policies such as Pigouvian taxes aim to correct negative externalities, while tax incentives and credits are used to encourage activities such as research, investment or home ownership. The study of optimal taxation, associated with economists such as Frank Ramsey and James Mirrlees, formalises the trade-off between equity and efficiency.
