The Māori are the indigenous Polynesian people of mainland New Zealand (Aotearoa). Māori originated with settlers from East Polynesia, who arrived in New Zealand in several waves of canoe (waka) voyages roughly between 1250 and 1300 CE. Over several centuries in isolation, these settlers developed their own distinctive culture.

History

Māori society was organised into iwi (tribes) and hapū (sub-tribes). European contact began with Abel Tasman in 1642 and intensified after James Cook's voyages from 1769. The Treaty of Waitangi, signed in 1840, founded British sovereignty; disputes over its interpretation, land confiscation, and the New Zealand Wars of the 19th century caused severe loss of Māori land and population.

Culture

Māori culture is known for the haka war dance, intricate wood carving (whakairo), tattooing (tā moko), and the Māori language (te reo Māori), one of New Zealand's official languages. The meeting house (wharenui) and marae remain centres of community life.

Modern era

Since the 1970s a Māori cultural and political renaissance has revived the language and arts, and the Waitangi Tribunal has investigated historical treaty claims, leading to settlements with many iwi.
