Metallica is an American heavy metal band formed in Los Angeles in 1981 by vocalist and guitarist James Hetfield and drummer Lars Ulrich, and has been based in San Francisco for most of its career. The band's fast tempos, instrumentals, and aggressive musicianship made them one of the founding "big four" bands of thrash metal, alongside Megadeth, Anthrax, and Slayer.

History

Metallica's early releases, including Kill 'Em All (1983) and Ride the Lightning (1984), were influential in the underground metal scene. Master of Puppets (1986) is widely regarded as one of the greatest heavy metal albums of all time. Bassist Cliff Burton died in a bus accident in Sweden in 1986 and was replaced by Jason Newsted. The band achieved mainstream commercial success with its fifth album, Metallica (1991), commonly known as "The Black Album", which debuted at number one on the Billboard 200 and produced hits such as "Enter Sandman" and "Nothing Else Matters".

Later work

After the albums Load (1996), Reload (1997), and the controversial St. Anger (2003), bassist Robert Trujillo joined in 2003. Subsequent albums include Death Magnetic (2008), Hardwired... to Self-Destruct (2016), and 72 Seasons (2023). Metallica has released eleven studio albums and is among the best-selling music artists of all time, with over 125 million albums sold worldwide. The band was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2009 and has won nine Grammy Awards.

Legacy

Metallica is considered one of the most commercially successful and influential metal bands in history. Their dispute with the file-sharing service Napster in 2000 also made them a central figure in debates over music piracy and artists' rights in the digital era.
