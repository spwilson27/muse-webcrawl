Rocinha

Rocinha
Country: Brazil
State: Rio de Janeiro
City: Rio de Janeiro
Population (2010 census): 69,161

Rocinha (Portuguese for "little farm" or "little ranch") is the largest favela in Brazil, located in Rio de Janeiro's South Zone between the districts of São Conrado and Gávea. It is built on a steep hillside overlooking the Atlantic Ocean and some of the wealthiest neighborhoods of the city.

Although Rocinha is officially classified as a neighborhood (bairro), many still refer to it as a favela. It developed from a shantytown into an urbanized slum. Today, almost all the single houses in Rocinha are made from concrete and brick. Some buildings are three and four stories tall and almost all houses have basic sanitation, plumbing and electricity. Compared to other favelas, Rocinha has a better developed infrastructure with hundreds of businesses such as banks, medicine stores, bus routes, cable television, and a McDonald's franchise.

History

Rocinha's origins date to the 1930s, when the land—once part of a farm and a racing circuit built by the Jockey Club—was divided and occupied by squatters. Its population grew rapidly in the 1950s and 1960s with migration from Brazil's Northeast. The name is said to come from the expression "minha rocinha" ("my little farm"), used by residents who grew vegetables on the plots.

Population and services

The 2010 Brazilian census counted 69,161 residents, though community estimates are far higher, often exceeding 150,000 or 200,000. Rocinha contains schools, health clinics, a public library, and the sports complex used since the 2016 Olympic period. In November 2011, Rocinha was occupied by police as part of Rio's pacifying police unit (UPP) program ahead of the 2014 FIFA World Cup and 2016 Olympics.
