ISO 9000


Quality management systems under ISO 9000 emphasize documented processes and continual improvement.

The ISO 9000 family is a set of international standards for quality management systems (QMS), maintained by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO). The standards provide guidance and tools for companies and organizations who want to ensure that their products and services consistently meet customers' requirements, and that quality is continually improved.

Standards in the family

  • ISO 9000 — Quality management systems: fundamentals and vocabulary.
  • ISO 9001 — Quality management systems: requirements. The only standard in the family against which an organisation can be certified.
  • ISO 9004 — Managing for the sustained success of an organization (guidance).
  • ISO 19011 — Guidelines for auditing management systems.

History

The first ISO 9000 standards were published in 1987, drawing on the British standard BS 5750 and earlier military quality standards such as MIL-Q-9858. Major revisions followed in 1994, 2000 (which introduced the process approach and replaced the old ISO 9001/9002/9003 trio with a single requirements standard), 2008, and 2015. ISO 9001:2015 adopted the Annex SL structure, stronger emphasis on risk-based thinking, organizational context, and leadership.

Quality management principles

The family is based on seven quality management principles: customer focus; leadership; engagement of people; process approach; improvement; evidence-based decision making; and relationship management.

Certification and adoption

ISO 9001 certification by accredited third parties is widespread in manufacturing, services, and the public sector; over one million certificates have been in force worldwide. Certification requires periodic surveillance audits and full recertification, typically every three years.
