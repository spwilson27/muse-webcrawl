Herpes simplex

SpecialtyInfectious disease
CauseHerpes simplex virus types 1 and 2 (HSV-1, HSV-2)
SymptomsBlistering sores of the mouth or genitals; often asymptomatic
TreatmentAntivirals such as aciclovir (no cure)

Herpes simplex is a viral infection caused by the herpes simplex viruses, HSV-1 and HSV-2. HSV-1 is transmitted mainly by oral contact and typically causes cold sores around the mouth, while HSV-2 is usually sexually transmitted and causes genital herpes — though each type can infect either site. Most infections are unrecognized, yet the viruses persist in the body for life.

Course of infection

After primary infection, the virus travels along sensory nerves to nerve ganglia, where it becomes latent. Periodically it reactivates, travelling back to the skin to cause recurrent blistering lesions or asymptomatic shedding, which can transmit the infection. Triggers include illness, stress, sunlight, and immunosuppression.

Complications

Most disease is mild, but herpes simplex can cause serious illness in newborns (neonatal herpes), keratitis threatening sight, herpetic whitlow, eczema herpeticum, and encephalitis — the commonest cause of sporadic fatal viral encephalitis in adults. People with weakened immune systems may suffer severe, prolonged outbreaks.

Treatment

Antiviral drugs such as aciclovir, valaciclovir, and famciclovir shorten outbreaks and, taken daily, suppress recurrences and reduce transmission, but they do not eradicate latent virus. Billions of people worldwide carry HSV-1, and several hundred million carry HSV-2, making herpes simplex among the most prevalent of all human infections.
