Richard Hamilton (mathematician)

Richard Streit Hamilton (19 December 1943 – 29 September 2024) was an American mathematician who was a professor at Columbia University. He is best known for introducing the Ricci flow in 1982 and developing the programme that Grigori Perelman used to prove the Poincaré conjecture and Thurston's geometrization conjecture.

Career

Hamilton received his PhD from Princeton University in 1966 under Robert Gunning. He taught at UC Irvine, UC San Diego, Cornell University, and from 1999 at Columbia University, where he was the Davies Professor of Mathematics.

Ricci flow

In his landmark 1982 paper "Three-manifolds with positive Ricci curvature", Hamilton introduced a heat-type equation that deforms a Riemannian metric in the direction of its Ricci curvature, smoothing the geometry of a manifold. He subsequently developed techniques — including singularity analysis and surgery ideas — that became the foundation of Perelman's 2002–03 proof of the geometrization conjecture.

Awards

Hamilton received the Oswald Veblen Prize (1996), the Clay Research Award (2003), the Shaw Prize in Mathematical Sciences (2011, shared with Demetrios Christodoulou), and was elected to the National Academy of Sciences.
