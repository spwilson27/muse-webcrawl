Vilnius University

Vilnius University (Lithuanian: Vilniaus universitetas) is a public research university in Vilnius, Lithuania. Founded in 1579 by Stephen Báthory as a Jesuit academy, it is one of the oldest universities in Eastern and Central Europe and the largest and most prestigious university in Lithuania, with about 24,000 students and a campus set in Vilnius's Old Town.

History

The university began as a Jesuit college in 1570 and was raised to academy status by a privilege of King Stephen Báthory in 1579, confirmed by Pope Gregory XIII. For two centuries it was the foremost centre of learning in the Grand Duchy of Lithuania within the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, teaching theology, philosophy, and law in Latin. After the suppression of the Jesuits in 1773, it passed to the Commission of National Education — often described as Europe's first ministry of education. Following the November Uprising, Tsar Nicholas I closed the university in 1832; it was refounded in the interwar period as Stefan Batory University (1919–1939) and re-established as a Lithuanian institution during and after the Second World War. Under Soviet rule it was renamed for Vincas Kapsukas, and it recovered its historic name and autonomy in 1990.

Campus and library

The central campus occupies a complex of Gothic, Renaissance, Baroque, and Classical courtyards in the Old Town, including the Church of St. Johns and its tall bell tower. The university library, founded in 1570, is the oldest in Lithuania and holds some five million items, including rare manuscripts and early printed books. The university also operates the modern Saulėtekis campus for the sciences and is noted for research in laser physics, biotechnology, and life sciences.

Notable alumni

Alumni include the poets Adam Mickiewicz and Czesław Miłosz (Nobel laureate in Literature, 1980), the historian Joachim Lelewel, and numerous Lithuanian statesmen, scientists, and writers.
