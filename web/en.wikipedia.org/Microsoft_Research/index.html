Microsoft Research (MSR) is the research and development division of Microsoft, founded in 1991 by Richard Rashid. It employs more than a thousand computer scientists and researchers at laboratories around the world, and is one of the world's largest computer science research organisations, working across areas from machine learning and systems to programming languages, human–computer interaction, cryptography and quantum computing.

History and labs

The first laboratory opened in Redmond, Washington, followed by labs in Silicon Valley (closed in 2014), Cambridge (UK, 1997), Beijing (Microsoft Research Asia, 1998), Bangalore (India, 2005), New England, New York City, Montreal and others. The Cambridge and Beijing labs became central to Microsoft's global research output and trained generations of researchers.

Notable contributions

MSR research has contributed to products including Kinect, Bing, Cortana and Azure services, and to programming languages such as F#. Its scientists include Turing Award winners: Leslie Lamport (2013), for work on distributed systems; and earlier, Jim Gray (1998) for database research, Butler Lampson (1992) and Charles Thacker (2009) worked at Microsoft Research's Silicon Valley lab. The lab is also known for Station Q and Microsoft Quantum, pursuing topological quantum computing, and in recent years for research underpinning Microsoft's partnership with OpenAI and large language models.

Academic engagement

MSR maintains an academic, publish-first culture unusual in industry, with extensive university collaborations, fellowships and open publications.
