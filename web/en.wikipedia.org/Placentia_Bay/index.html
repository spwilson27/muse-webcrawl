Placentia Bay

Location: Southeastern Newfoundland, Canada
Separates: Burin and Avalon peninsulas
Length: approx. 100 km
Settlements: Placentia, Argentia, Marystown

Placentia Bay is a large bay on the southeast coast of the island of Newfoundland in the Canadian province of Newfoundland and Labrador. Formed in part by the Burin Peninsula to the west and the Avalon Peninsula to the east, it extends about 100 km inland and contains numerous islands and deep, sheltered inlets long used by fishing communities.

History

The bay's name comes from Plaisance, the French colony founded at Placentia in 1662, which served as France's base on Newfoundland until the Treaty of Utrecht in 1713. In August 1941, the bay hosted the Atlantic Charter conference, where Winston Churchill and Franklin D. Roosevelt met aboard warships anchored near Argentia to set out their postwar aims. During the Second World War the United States built Naval Station Argentia on the bay's shore under the destroyers-for-bases agreement.

Industry

Placentia Bay supports fisheries and aquaculture, a shipyard at Marystown, and one of Canada's busiest oil-handling routes: tankers serving the refinery at Come By Chance and the transshipment terminal at Whiffen Head must transit the bay, making it a focus of marine safety and oil-spill preparedness planning.
