Mettā

Mettā (Pali) or maitrī (Sanskrit) means benevolence, loving-kindness, friendliness, amity, good will and active interest in others. It is the first of the four sublime states (brahmavihāras) in Buddhism and one of the ten pāramīs of the Theravāda tradition.

Practice

Mettā meditation, often called loving-kindness meditation, cultivates an attitude of unconditional friendliness, traditionally beginning with goodwill toward oneself and progressively extending to a benefactor, a neutral person, a difficult person and finally all beings. The practice is described in the Mettā Sutta of the Pali Canon, which likens the cultivated mind to a mother's love for her only child.

Effects

Mettā is regarded as the antidote to anger and ill will (vyāpāda), and modern psychological research has associated regular loving-kindness practice with reduced stress and increased positive emotion.
