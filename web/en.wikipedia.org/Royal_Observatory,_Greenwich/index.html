Royal Observatory, Greenwich

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Royal Observatory, Greenwich (ROG) is an observatory in Greenwich Park, London, founded by Charles II in 1675 "so as to rectify the tables of the motions of the heavens, and the places of the fixed stars, in order to perfect the art of navigation". It played a defining role in the history of astronomy and navigation: the Prime Meridian passes through its courtyard, and the observatory gave its name to Greenwich Mean Time. It is now a museum, part of Royal Museums Greenwich.

History

Flamsteed House, designed by Sir Christopher Wren, was the first purpose-built scientific research facility in Britain. Successive Astronomers Royal — Flamsteed, Halley, Bradley, Maskelyne, Airy — produced star catalogues and the Nautical Almanac, essential to determining longitude at sea. In 1884 the International Meridian Conference adopted the Greenwich meridian as the world's Prime Meridian. Airy's transit circle defines the historic meridian line, though the modern IERS reference meridian lies about 102 metres to the east.

Time

The observatory distributed time first by the famous red time ball (dropped daily at 13:00 since 1833), then by telegraph signals, establishing Greenwich Mean Time as Britain's legal time and the basis of the world time-zone system. Observing work moved to Herstmonceux and then La Palma in the 20th century as London's light pollution grew; the Greenwich site became a museum in 1960.
