Marine Mammal Protection Act

Marine Mammal Protection Act of 1972
Enacted: 21 October 1972
Signed by: President Richard Nixon
Codification: 16 U.S.C. §§ 1361–1423h
Administered by: NMFS, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service

The Marine Mammal Protection Act (MMPA) is an Act of the United States Congress passed on 21 October 1972. It was the first legislation anywhere to adopt an ecosystem-based approach to marine resource management, and it protects all marine mammals within United States waters.

Provisions

The Act establishes a general moratorium on the "taking" — hunting, harassing, capturing or killing — and importation of marine mammals and marine mammal products, with exceptions for scientific research, public display, subsistence hunting by Alaska Natives, and incidental take in commercial fisheries under permit. Responsibility is split between the National Marine Fisheries Service (for whales, dolphins, seals and sea lions) and the Fish and Wildlife Service (for manatees, sea otters, walruses and polar bears). The Act introduced the concept of "optimum sustainable population" and requires stock assessments of marine mammal populations.

Amendments and effects

Major amendments in 1988 and 1994 created regimes for managing fishery interactions, including take reduction teams, after controversies over dolphin deaths in tuna purse-seine fisheries. The Act is credited with the recovery of many populations, including humpback whales, northern elephant seals and sea otters, and its import provisions have been used to pressure other nations to adopt comparable protections.
