Darley Arabian

The Darley Arabian (1700–1730), foundation sire of the Thoroughbred

The Darley Arabian (1700–1730) was one of three foundation sires of the Thoroughbred horse breed, along with the Godolphin Arabian and the Byerley Turk. A bay Arabian stallion imported to England from Syria in 1704, he stood at Aldby Park in Yorkshire, and his bloodline went on to dominate the breed: the overwhelming majority of modern Thoroughbreds descend from him in the direct male line, chiefly through his great-great-grandson Eclipse.

Life

The horse was purchased in Aleppo by Thomas Darley, a merchant and Levant Company agent, and shipped to England as a gift for his brother. At Aldby Park he covered relatively few mares, mostly of ordinary breeding, but among his offspring were Flying Childers, regarded as the first truly great racehorse, and his full brother Bartlet's Childers, who never raced but became a highly influential sire.

Legacy

Through Bartlet's Childers the line descends to Squirt, Marske and then Eclipse (1764–1789), the unbeaten champion whose name is still used for top-class races. Genetic studies of the Thoroughbred Y chromosome have confirmed the extraordinary reach of the Darley Arabian's sire line, which accounts for roughly 95 per cent of modern Thoroughbred male lines.
