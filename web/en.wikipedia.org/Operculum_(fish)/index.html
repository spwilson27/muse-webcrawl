Operculum (fish)

The operculum is the bony flap covering and protecting the gills of bony fish.

The operculum is a series of bones found in bony fishes and chimaeras that serves as a facial support structure below the mandible and as a protective cover for the gills. It is also used for respiration and feeding.

Anatomy

The operculum is composed of four bones: the opercle, preopercle, interopercle, and subopercle. The posterior margin of the operculum is equipped with a flexible, ribbed structure called the branchiostegal membrane, which plays an important role in respiration by helping to pump water over the gills.

Function

During respiration, the operculum opens and closes to move water across the gills, allowing the fish to extract dissolved oxygen. Many fish can ventilate their gills by buccal pumping with the opercula even while stationary. The opercular series also protects the delicate gill filaments from damage.

Development

The opercular series begins to appear in fish development at a similar time to the formation of the pectoral girdle. In some species the shape and markings of the operculum are used by biologists to determine age, since growth rings (annuli) may form on the opercular bone.
