Motorcycle racing

Motorcycle racing (also called moto racing and motorbike racing) is the motorcycle sport of racing motorcycles. Major varieties include motorcycle road racing and off-road racing, both either on circuits or open courses, and track racing. Other categories include hill climbs, drag racing and land speed record trials.

Road racing

Road racing takes place on paved surfaces. It includes Grand Prix racing, in which purpose-built prototype machines compete in the MotoGP world championship; production-based superbike and supersport racing; and traditional races on closed public roads such as the Isle of Man TT and the North West 200. Road racing on public-road circuits is among the most dangerous forms of motorsport but retains a devoted following.

Motocross and off-road

Motocross is raced on enclosed off-road circuits built on terrain with hills, jumps and sharp turns. Related disciplines include supercross (staged in stadiums), enduro (long-distance timed stages over rough terrain), trials (precision riding over obstacles without putting a foot down), rally raid events such as the Dakar Rally, and flat track racing on oval dirt circuits.

Track racing

Speedway is raced on short oval dirt tracks with bikes that have no brakes and a single gear. Variants include ice speedway, long track and grasstrack racing, popular in northern and central Europe and Australia.

History

The first motorcycle race is often traced to 1897, and organized competition grew quickly in the early 20th century. The Isle of Man TT was first held in 1907. After the Second World War the FIM established the Grand Prix world championship in 1949, which evolved into the modern MotoGP series.

Safety

Modern racing employs full-face helmets, leather or textile suits with armour, airbag systems, and circuit safety features such as air fences and gravel traps. Governing bodies including the FIM homologate circuits and equipment.
