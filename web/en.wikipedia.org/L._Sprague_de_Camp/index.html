Lyon Sprague de Camp (27 November 1907 – 6 November 2000) was an American author of science fiction, fantasy and non-fiction, one of the most prolific and influential writers of the pulp and early paperback eras. Over a career of more than sixty years he wrote over one hundred books, including novels, short stories, histories of technology and Atlantis, and biographies of fellow writers.

Fiction

De Camp's best-known novel is Lest Darkness Fall (1939), in which a modern American is transported to Ostrogothic Rome and tries to prevent the onset of the Dark Ages — a landmark of alternate-history and time-travel fiction. With Fletcher Pratt he wrote the humorous Harold Shea ("Incompleat Enchanter") stories, in which a psychologist travels into mythological worlds. He also wrote the Pusadian and Novarian fantasy series and numerous science-fiction works.

Conan and editorial work

De Camp played a central role in reviving Robert E. Howard's Conan the Barbarian in the 1950s and 1960s, editing Howard's stories for publication, completing fragments, and writing new Conan novels and pastiches, some with Lin Carter. His editorial revisions and additions were influential but later controversial among Howard purists. He also edited anthologies of fantasy and wrote The Ancient Engineers and other popular histories.

Biographies and recognition

His biography Lovecraft: A Biography (1975) was the first major life of H. P. Lovecraft, and with his wife Catherine he wrote further biographical and historical works. De Camp was named a Grand Master by the Science Fiction Writers of America in 1978 and received a World Fantasy Award for life achievement. His papers are held by research collections in the United States.
