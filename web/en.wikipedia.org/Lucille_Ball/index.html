Lucille Désirée Ball (6 August 1911 – 26 April 1989) was an American actress, comedian, producer and studio executive. She is widely regarded as the definitive star of the situation comedy and one of the most influential figures in television history. As Lucy Ricardo in I Love Lucy (1951–1957), she became TV's first major female star; as head of Desilu Productions she became the first woman to run a major television studio, green-lighting series including Star Trek and Mission: Impossible.

Early life and career

Ball was born in Jamestown, New York, and raised in nearby Celoron. After her father's death from typhoid fever in 1915, the family struggled financially. Determined to perform, she enrolled in drama school in New York (where Bette Davis was a classmate) and worked as a model and chorus girl before going to Hollywood in 1933. At RKO she became the "Queen of the B's", appearing in dozens of films such as Stage Door (1937) and working with the Marx Brothers, Katharine Hepburn and Ginger Rogers. On the set of Too Many Girls (1940) she met Cuban bandleader Desi Arnaz; they eloped that year. Radio success on My Favorite Husband (1948–1951) led CBS to develop the show for television.

I Love Lucy and Desilu

Ball insisted that Arnaz play her husband despite network doubts about a "mixed" marriage, and the couple formed Desilu Productions to own the show. I Love Lucy pioneered the three-camera studio-audience format shot on 35 mm film, the syndication rerun model, and high standards of ensemble writing; it was the most-watched show in America for four of its six seasons, and Ball won two Emmy Awards for the role. Episodes such as "Lucy Does a TV Commercial" (the Vitameatavegamin sketch) and the chocolate-factory scene in "Job Switching" became canonical television comedy.

After The Lucy–Desi Comedy Hour (1957–1960) and her divorce from Arnaz in 1960, Ball bought out his share and ran Desilu herself — the first woman to head a major studio. Desilu produced The Andy Griffith Show, The Dick Van Dyke Show, Mission: Impossible and Star Trek, whose survival Ball personally backed. She sold Desilu to Gulf+Western (Paramount) in 1967 for $17 million. Her later series The Lucy Show (1962–1968) and Here's Lucy (1968–1974) kept her a primetime fixture for over two decades.

Later life and legacy

Ball's final years included the acclaimed drama Stone Pillow (1985) and the short-lived Life with Lucy (1986). She died in 1989 following heart surgery. In 1989 she posthumously received the Presidential Medal of Freedom. The Lucille Ball Desi Arnaz Museum stands in her hometown of Jamestown, which also hosts an annual comedy festival. I Love Lucy has never been out of syndication, and Ball's physical comedy, business acumen and role in inventing the modern sitcom format remain foundational to American popular culture.
