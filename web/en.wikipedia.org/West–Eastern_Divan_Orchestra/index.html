West–Eastern Divan Orchestra

Founded: 1999
Founders: Daniel Barenboim, Edward Said
Principal conductor: Daniel Barenboim
Based: Seville, Spain

The orchestra brings together young musicians from Israel, Palestine, and other Arab countries.

The West–Eastern Divan Orchestra is an orchestra based in Seville, Spain, consisting of musicians from countries in the Middle East, of Egyptian, Iranian, Israeli, Jordanian, Lebanese, Palestinian, Syrian, and Spanish background. It was founded in 1999 by conductor Daniel Barenboim and the Palestinian-American scholar and activist Edward Said, who named the ensemble after the West–östlicher Divan, a collection of poems by Johann Wolfgang von Goethe.

Purpose and activities

The orchestra was conceived as a project against ignorance, intended to enable intercultural dialogue and to promote, through music-making, a shared creative experience between Israelis and Arabs. Each summer the musicians gather for rehearsals and discussions, followed by an international concert tour. The orchestra has performed at major venues and festivals including the BBC Proms, the Salzburg Festival, and Carnegie Hall, and appeared at the United Nations.

Barenboim–Said Academy

The project inspired the Barenboim–Said Academy in Berlin, opened in 2016, which trains young musicians from the Middle East and North Africa and houses the Pierre Boulez Saal concert hall. Barenboim and Said received the Prince of Asturias Award for Concord in 2002; their conversations were published as Parallels and Paradoxes.
