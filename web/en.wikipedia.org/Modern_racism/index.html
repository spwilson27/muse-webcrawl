Modern racism

Modern racism is a term in social psychology describing a form of prejudice that emerged in the United States following the civil rights movement. Unlike the overt "old-fashioned" racism of the Jim Crow era, modern racism is expressed through beliefs that discrimination is a thing of the past, that Black people push too hard for social change, and that their gains are undeserved.

Related concepts

The concept is closely related to symbolic racism, aversive racism, and laissez-faire racism, all of which describe how racial resentment can be expressed through ostensibly non-racial values such as individualism and opposition to government intervention. The Modern Racism Scale, developed by John McConahay in the 1980s, was designed to measure these attitudes in survey research.
