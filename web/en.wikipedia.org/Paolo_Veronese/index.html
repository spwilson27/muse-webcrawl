Paolo Veronese

Paolo Veronese Born: 1528, Verona, Republic of Venice
Died: 19 April 1588, Venice
Occupation: Painter
Movement: Renaissance, Mannerism
Known for: Large-scale history and feast paintings

Paolo Caliari (1528 – 19 April 1588), known as Paolo Veronese, was an Italian Renaissance painter based in Venice, known for extremely large history paintings of religion and mythology, such as The Wedding at Cana (1563) and The Feast in the House of Levi (1573). Included with Titian, a generation older, and Tintoretto, a decade senior, Veronese is one of the "great trio that dominated Venetian painting of the Cinquecento" of the late Renaissance in the 16th century.

Biography

Veronese was born in Verona, then the largest mainland possession of the Republic of Venice, the son of a stonecutter. He took his customary nickname from his birthplace. He trained locally under Antonio Badile and Giovanni Francesco Caroto, and by 1551 was receiving commissions in Venice, including work in the Doge's Palace (1553–1555) and the ceiling of the church of San Sebastiano, which he decorated over succeeding decades. In 1562 he was commissioned to paint The Wedding at Cana for the refectory of the Benedictine monastery of San Giorgio Maggiore; the result, completed in 1563, is one of the largest canvases of the Venetian Renaissance — some 6.77 × 9.94 metres, populated by about 130 figures — and now hangs in the Louvre opposite the Mona Lisa.

The Feast in the House of Levi and the Inquisition

In 1573 Veronese was summoned before the Venetian Inquisition to explain the inclusion of a German halberdier, a man with a nosebleed, buffoons, and other contemporary figures in a vast painting intended as a Last Supper for the Dominican convent of Santi Giovanni e Paolo. Rather than repaint the canvas, he simply retitled it The Feast in the House of Levi, a subject for which the festive crowd was theologically more acceptable. The transcript of his trial is a famous document of Renaissance artistic practice; Veronese argued that "we painters take the same liberties as poets and madmen."

Style

Veronese's style is distinguished by luminous, silvery colour, sumptuous contemporary Venetian dress and architecture presented as the setting for biblical and mythological scenes, and grand classical architectural perspectives. His huge banquet scenes, set amid marble colonnades, are among the most celebrated achievements of Venetian painting. He ran a large and productive workshop with his brother Benedetto and later his sons, who continued under the name "Haeredes Pauli" ("heirs of Paolo") after his death in Venice on 19 April 1588.
