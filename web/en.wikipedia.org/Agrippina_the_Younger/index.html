Julia Agrippina (6 November AD 15 – 23 March AD 59), known as Agrippina the Younger or Agrippina Minor, was a Roman empress and one of the most prominent women of the Julio-Claudian dynasty. She was the daughter of Germanicus and Agrippina the Elder, sister of the emperor Caligula, wife of the emperor Claudius, and mother of the emperor Nero.

Early life

Agrippina was born at Oppidum Ubiorum, the Rhine settlement that she later raised to a Roman colony named in her honour, Colonia Claudia Ara Agrippinensium — modern Cologne. Her first husband, Gnaeus Domitius Ahenobarbus, fathered her only child, the future emperor Nero, in AD 37. Under Caligula she was exiled in AD 39 for alleged involvement in a conspiracy, returning after his assassination in 41.

Marriage to Claudius and rise to power

In AD 49 Agrippina married her uncle, the emperor Claudius, after the fall of his previous wife Messalina. She secured the adoption of her son Nero by Claudius, placing him ahead of Claudius's own son Britannicus in the succession, and arranged Nero's marriage to Claudius's daughter Octavia. When Claudius died in AD 54 — ancient sources widely allege Agrippina poisoned him — Nero became emperor at seventeen, with Agrippina initially acting as the power behind the throne.

Conflict with Nero and death

As Nero matured he increasingly resisted his mother's influence, removing her allies and excluding her from power. In March AD 59, after a failed attempt to kill her with a collapsible boat at Baiae, Nero had her murdered at her villa. Ancient historians such as Tacitus, Suetonius, and Cassius Dio portray her as ruthless and ambitious, and their accounts, though often hostile, make her one of the best-documented women of the early Roman Empire.
