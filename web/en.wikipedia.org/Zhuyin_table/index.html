Zhuyin table

The Zhuyin table arranges the symbols of Zhuyin (注音), commonly known as Bopomofo, the phonetic transcription system used to teach the pronunciation of Chinese characters in Taiwan and formerly in mainland China. The system's name comes from its first four symbols: ㄅ (b), ㄆ (p), ㄇ (m) and ㄈ (f).

The system

Zhuyin was developed by the Commission on the Unification of Pronunciation and promulgated by the Republic of China in 1918; its symbols are derived from simplified forms of Chinese characters. The standard inventory comprises 37 symbols — 21 initials (consonants) and 16 finals (rhymes) — plus tone marks. Unlike pinyin, it uses no letters of the Latin alphabet.

Usage

In Taiwan, Zhuyin remains the standard tool for teaching children to read, for dictionary pronunciation guides, and as a text input method on phones and computers. In mainland China it was superseded by pinyin in the 1950s.
