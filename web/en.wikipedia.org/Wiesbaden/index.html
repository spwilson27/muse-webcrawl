Wiesbaden is the capital of the German state of Hesse and, with about 280,000 inhabitants, its second-largest city after Frankfurt am Main. It lies on the right bank of the Rhine, opposite Mainz, at the southern foot of the Taunus mountains, and is one of Europe's oldest spa towns.

History

The Romans knew the settlement as Aquae Mattiacorum, after the local Mattiaci tribe, and prized its hot springs — 26 thermal springs still flow today. In the Middle Ages Wiesbaden passed to the counts of Nassau and became the seat of the Duchy of Nassau in 1806. In the 19th century it flourished as an elegant international resort: emperors, Russian aristocrats, and writers such as Dostoevsky — who gambled heavily at its casino and drew on the experience in The Gambler — frequented its Kurhaus and parks. Largely spared bombing in the Second World War, the city retains an unusually complete stock of historicist villas and grand hotels.

Today

Wiesbaden hosts federal institutions including the Federal Criminal Police Office (BKA) and the Federal Statistical Office, a large US Army garrison, and the Hessian state government. Its landmarks include the Kurhaus with its casino, the neo-Gothic Marktkirche, the Schloss Biebrich on the Rhine, and the Nerobergbahn, a water-powered funicular opened in 1888.
