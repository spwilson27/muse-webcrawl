Web Ontology Language

The Web Ontology Language (OWL) is a family of knowledge representation languages for authoring ontologies, standardised by the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C) for the Semantic Web. OWL documents describe classes, properties, individuals and their relationships with formal semantics based on description logics, enabling automated reasoners to check consistency and infer new facts. The acronym was chosen as "OWL" rather than "WOL" as an easy-to-remember word and a nod to the owl of wisdom (and to Winnie-the-Pooh's "Wol").

OWL 1 and sublanguages

OWL 1 (2004) built on the earlier DAML+OIL language and defined three sublanguages of increasing expressiveness: OWL Lite, OWL DL (named for description logic, and decidable), and OWL Full, which is undecidable but fully compatible with RDF. OWL extends the vocabulary of RDF Schema with constructs such as class disjointness, cardinality restrictions, property characteristics (transitive, symmetric, functional), and enumerated classes.

OWL 2

OWL 2 (2009) extended OWL 1 with property chains, qualified cardinality restrictions, keys, and richer datatypes, and defined three tractable profiles tuned for different applications: OWL EL (large class hierarchies, polynomial reasoning — used for the SNOMED CT medical terminology), OWL QL (query answering over large datasets) and OWL RL (rule-based implementation). Its structural specification is supplemented by serialisations including RDF/XML, Turtle, OWL/XML and the readable Manchester syntax. Editing tools such as Protégé and reasoners such as HermiT and Pellet support practical ontology engineering, and OWL remains used in biomedical informatics, data integration and knowledge graphs.
