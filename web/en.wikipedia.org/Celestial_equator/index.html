Celestial equator

DefinitionProjection of Earth's equator onto the celestial sphere
Declination0° by definition
Inclination to eclipticAbout 23.44°
Crossings with eclipticThe two equinoxes

The celestial equator is the great circle on the imaginary celestial sphere in the same plane as the Equator of Earth. By extension, it is likewise a plane of reference — the projection of the terrestrial equatorial plane out into space. As a result of Earth's axial tilt, the celestial equator is inclined by about 23.44° with respect to the ecliptic plane.

Observing

An observer at Earth's equator sees the celestial equator pass through the zenith; at the poles it coincides with the horizon. Elsewhere it arcs across the sky, crossing the meridian at an altitude equal to 90° minus the observer's latitude. Stars on the celestial equator rise due east and set due west and are visible from everywhere on Earth during the year. Objects near it — the constellations Orion, Virgo, Aquila and others astride the equator — are the most universally visible in the sky.

Role in coordinates

The celestial equator is the fundamental plane of the equatorial coordinate system: declination is measured north or south of it, and right ascension is measured eastward from the point where the ecliptic crosses it at the March equinox. Because of precession, the equator and equinoxes move slowly, so coordinates tied to them are specified by epoch.
