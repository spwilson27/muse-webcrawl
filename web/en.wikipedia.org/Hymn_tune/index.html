Hymn tune

A hymn tune is a melody used for the singing of a hymn. Hymn writers frequently compose texts to fit a particular existing tune, and church musicians in turn compose new tunes for texts. Because many texts share the same metre, a hymn text can be sung to any tune of the matching metrical pattern; hymnals therefore carry a metrical index of tunes.

Hymn tunes are traditionally given names, often derived from places, people, or events associated with the composer or the tune's first use. Famous examples include Dix ("As with Gladness Men of Old"), Nicaea ("Holy, Holy, Holy"), Eventide ("Abide with Me"), Slane ("Be Thou My Vision"), and Hyfrydol. Ralph Vaughan Williams composed and arranged many tunes and edited The English Hymnal (1906).

Metre

Tunes are classified by the number of syllables in each line of the stanza: Common Metre (C.M., 86.86), Long Metre (L.M., 88.88), Short Metre (S.M., 66.86), and irregular patterns such as 87.87 D (trochaic, doubled). A tune in a given metre can serve any text of the same metre, which is why "Amazing Grace" can famously be sung to many different melodies.

Chorale tunes

In the Lutheran tradition, hymn tunes form the basis of the chorale, harmonised in four parts and used by composers such as J. S. Bach as the foundation of cantatas, motets and organ works.
