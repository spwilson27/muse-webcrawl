Professional association

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


An annual meeting of a professional association

A professional association (also called a professional body, professional organisation, or professional society) is an organisation, usually a non-profit, which seeks to further a particular profession, the interests of individuals and organisations engaged in that profession, and the public interest. In the United States, such an association is typically a non-profit business league.

Roles

The roles of professional associations have been variously defined: "a group of people in a learned occupation who are entrusted with maintaining control or oversight of the legitimate practice of the occupation;" also a body acting "to safeguard the public interest;" and an organisation "representing the interest of professional practitioners," acting as "an authority which benefits not only its members but also the public." Typical functions include setting educational and examination standards for entry, maintaining registers of qualified practitioners, publishing codes of ethics and disciplinary procedures, providing continuing professional development, publishing journals, organising conferences, accrediting university programmes, and representing the profession to government and the public.

Regulation and licensing

In many fields — medicine, law, engineering, accountancy — a professional body also acts as, or works alongside, a statutory regulator that licenses practitioners; in some jurisdictions membership of the association is legally required to practise. Professional associations differ from trade unions in that they focus on standards and professional interests rather than collective bargaining over pay and conditions, although some bodies perform both roles.
