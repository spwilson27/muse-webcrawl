Black Maria

Black Maria is a slang term for a police vehicle used to transport prisoners — historically a closed van or carriage, sometimes called a paddy wagon. The term was also applied to the enclosed vans used to carry prisoners and, in some usages, to hearses and ambulances.

History and etymology

The phrase is first recorded in the United States in the 1830s–40s. One popular account traces it to Maria Lee, a large and formidable Black woman in Boston who kept a sailors' boarding house and was said to help police so effectively that unruly prisoners were threatened with being "sent for Black Maria". Whatever its origin, the term spread through Britain and the Commonwealth, becoming standard slang for the windowless vans seen outside courthouses and police stations.

Other uses

"Black Maria" has also been the name of several racehorses, a card game term (the queen of spades in Hearts), Edison's first film studio — a tar-paper building at West Orange, New Jersey, nicknamed for its resemblance to a police van — and various works of fiction.
