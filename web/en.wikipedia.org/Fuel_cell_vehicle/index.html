Fuel cell vehicle

A fuel cell vehicle (FCV) or fuel cell electric vehicle (FCEV) is an electric vehicle that uses a fuel cell, usually fed with compressed hydrogen, to generate electricity on board to power its electric motors. Unlike battery electric vehicles, FCEVs carry their fuel in a tank and can be refuelled in minutes; the fuel cell combines hydrogen with oxygen from the air, producing electricity and water as the only exhaust product.

Technology

Most automotive fuel cell vehicles use a proton-exchange membrane (PEM) fuel cell stack, high-pressure carbon-fibre tanks storing hydrogen at 350 or 700 bar, a small buffer battery to handle acceleration and regenerative braking, and electric traction motors. Production models have included the Toyota Mirai (launched 2014), the Hyundai Nexo, and the earlier Honda Clarity Fuel Cell.

Advantages and challenges

Advantages include long range, fast refuelling, and zero tailpipe emissions other than water vapour. Challenges include the cost of fuel cell stacks and hydrogen storage, the scarcity of hydrogen refuelling infrastructure, and the efficiency and carbon footprint of hydrogen production, which today comes mostly from steam reforming of natural gas rather than electrolysis using renewable electricity. Deployment has therefore remained small compared with battery electric vehicles, with fleets concentrated in California, Japan, South Korea, and parts of Europe; fuel cells are also being pursued for buses, trucks, and trains.
