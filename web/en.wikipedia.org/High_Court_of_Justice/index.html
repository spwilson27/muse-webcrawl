High Court of Justice

The High Court of Justice in London, known properly as His Majesty's High Court of Justice in England, together with the Court of Appeal and the Crown Court, is one of the Senior Courts of England and Wales. It is based at the Royal Courts of Justice on the Strand in London and in district registries across England and Wales.

History

The High Court was created by the Judicature Acts of 1873–1875, which fused the administration of common law and equity by merging the historic courts of Queen's Bench, Chancery, Exchequer, Common Pleas, Probate, Divorce and Matrimonial Causes, and Admiralty into a single Supreme Court of Judicature. Appeals from it lay originally to the House of Lords and, since 2009, lie to the Supreme Court of the United Kingdom.

Divisions

The court is organised into three divisions. The King's Bench Division handles contract and tort claims, judicial review, and specialist lists including the Commercial Court, Admiralty Court, and Technology and Construction Court. The Chancery Division deals with business and property disputes, trusts, probate, insolvency, and intellectual property, incorporating the Patents Court. The Family Division hears matrimonial, children, and Court of Protection matters.

Judges

High Court judges are formally styled Mr/Mrs Justice (surname) and sit either in London or on circuit. They are appointed by the Crown on the advice of the Lord Chancellor following selection by the Judicial Appointments Commission, and are typically drawn from senior barristers (King's Counsel) or circuit judges. Cases are generally heard by a single judge, with or without a jury in limited civil contexts such as defamation and fraud.
