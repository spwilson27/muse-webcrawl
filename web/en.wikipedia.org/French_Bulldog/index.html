French Bulldog

French Bulldog (Frenchie)
Origin: France (developed from English toy bulldogs)
Height: about 28–33 cm (11–13 in)
Weight: under 12.7 kg (28 lb)
Coat: Smooth, short, fine
Traits: Bat ears, brachycephalic, companion breed

The French Bulldog (French: Bouledogue Français) is a French breed of companion dog. A cross between toy bulldogs imported from England and local ratters in Paris in the 1850s, it is a stocky, compact dog with a smooth coat, short face, and trademark "bat ears".

History

When English lacemakers from Nottingham emigrated to France during the Industrial Revolution, they brought small toy bulldogs, which were crossed with local dogs in Paris to produce the breed. French Bulldogs became fashionable companions in Parisian society and were brought to the United States, where the breed standard — insisting on erect bat ears rather than the rose ears of English types — was settled in the late 19th century. In 2022 the breed became the most popular in the United States according to American Kennel Club registrations, overtaking the Labrador Retriever, and it has also topped registrations in the United Kingdom.

Health

As a brachycephalic (flat-faced) breed, French Bulldogs are prone to brachycephalic obstructive airway syndrome, which causes breathing difficulties and poor tolerance of heat and exercise; many airlines restrict them in cargo holds. They are also predisposed to skin fold dermatitis, spinal malformations such as hemivertebrae, hip dysplasia, and eye problems. Their large heads and narrow hips mean most litters must be delivered by caesarean section. Average lifespan is around 10–12 years.

Temperament

French Bulldogs are bred as companion dogs: affectionate, playful, and adaptable to apartment living, with modest exercise needs. Their popularity has also made them targets of theft and of intensive, sometimes unscrupulous, breeding for fashionable colours.
