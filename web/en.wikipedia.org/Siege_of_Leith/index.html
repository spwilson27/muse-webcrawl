Siege of Leith

Date: April–July 1560
Location: Leith, Scotland
Result: Treaty of Edinburgh

The siege of Leith in 1560 was the culmination of the Scottish Reformation crisis, when English and Scottish Protestant forces besieged French troops occupying the port of Leith, Edinburgh's harbour.

Background

Mary of Guise, regent for her daughter Mary, Queen of Scots, relied on French soldiers, prompting the Protestant Lords of the Congregation to seek English help. By the Treaty of Berwick (1560), Elizabeth I sent an army and fleet north.

Siege and settlement

Assaults on Leith's new fortifications in the spring of 1560 were costly and inconclusive, but blockade and diplomacy prevailed. The Treaty of Edinburgh (July 1560) provided for the withdrawal of French and English forces, effectively ending the Auld Alliance and securing the Protestant settlement in Scotland.
