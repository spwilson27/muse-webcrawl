Begum

Begum (also Begüm, Begam, Baigum or Beygum) is a female royal and aristocratic title from Central and South Asia. It is the feminine equivalent of the title beg or bey, which in Turkish means "higher official", and originally designated a local chief. Thus, in principle, a begum is the female equivalent of a ruler, and in practice often denotes the wife or daughter of a beg or bey.

Usage

The title is common in Pakistan, Bangladesh, India and Central and Southeast Asian countries. It has lately been adopted in the West as a polite form of address for Muslim women of South Asian descent, akin to the use of "Mrs." In the former princely states of British India, the title was borne by the consorts of rulers; the Begum of Bhopal, a line of female rulers of the princely state of Bhopal, is a notable example.

Notable bearers

The Begums of Bhopal ruled the state from 1819 to 1926 and included Qudsia Begum and Sultan Jahan Begum, who were noted patrons of education. In Bangladesh and Pakistan, the title is still widely used as an honorific, for example by Khaleda Zia, commonly known as Begum Khaleda Zia.
