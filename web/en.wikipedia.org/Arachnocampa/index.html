Arachnocampa is a genus of nine fungus gnat species which have a bioluminescent larval stage, akin to the larval stage of glowworms. The genus is native to New Zealand and Australia. The name means "spider-worm," reflecting the larvae's habit of producing silken threads — like a spider's web — hung with sticky droplets to snare prey.

Glowworm threads and larvae in a New Zealand cave

Life cycle and behavior

Larvae live in caves, grottoes, and sheltered forest banks. Each larva builds a horizontal silk tube on a ceiling or bank and suspends dozens of vertical "fishing lines" of silk beaded with sticky mucus. The larva's blue-green light, produced by a modified excretory organ (a luciferase-based reaction distinct from that of fireflies), attracts flying insects, which become stuck on the lines; the larva then reels the line in and eats the prey. The larval stage lasts six to twelve months. Pupae and adult females are also faintly luminescent; adults live only a few days and do not feed.

Species and tourism

The best-known species is Arachnocampa luminosa of New Zealand, famous from the Waitomo Glowworm Caves, where colonies of thousands of larvae have drawn tourists since the late 19th century. Australian species such as A. tasmaniensis (Tasmania) and A. richardsae (New South Wales) inhabit caves and rainforest gullies. Populations are sensitive to disturbance, desiccation, and light pollution.
