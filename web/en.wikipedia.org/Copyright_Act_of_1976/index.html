Copyright Act of 1976

The Copyright Act of 1976 is a United States copyright law and remains the primary basis of copyright law in the United States today. It took effect on 1 January 1978, superseding the Copyright Act of 1909.

Key provisions

The Act, codified at Title 17 of the U.S. Code, grants authors exclusive rights to reproduce, distribute, perform, display, and create derivative works from original works of authorship fixed in a tangible medium—extending protection to unpublished works and abolishing most common-law copyright. Section 102 lists categories of protectable subject matter, while Section 107 codifies the fair use doctrine with four factors for courts to weigh. Section 108 provides exceptions for libraries and archives.

Duration

As enacted, copyright lasted for the life of the author plus 50 years, or 75 years for works made for hire; the Sonny Bono Copyright Term Extension Act of 1998 later extended these terms by 20 years.
