Saigō Takamori

Died24 September 1877, Shiroyama, Kagoshima
AllegianceSatsuma Domain; Empire of Japan

Saigō Takamori (1828–1877) was a Japanese samurai and statesman of the Satsuma Domain, one of the leading figures of the Meiji Restoration and often remembered as the "last true samurai."

Restoration

Saigō helped forge the Satsuma–Chōshū alliance that overthrew the Tokugawa shogunate in the Boshin War (1868–69), and he negotiated the bloodless surrender of Edo Castle. In the new Meiji government he became a senior councillor and a leader of the Imperial Japanese Army.

Satsuma Rebellion

Saigō resigned in 1873 after losing the debate over invading Korea (the Seikanron). In 1877 discontented Satsuma samurai rose in the Satsuma Rebellion with Saigō as their reluctant leader. The rebellion was crushed by the imperial conscript army; Saigō died at the Battle of Shiroyama. He was posthumously pardoned and is honoured by a famous statue in Ueno Park, Tokyo.
