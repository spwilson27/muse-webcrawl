Cyclotron radiation

Cyclotron radiation is electromagnetic radiation emitted by non-relativistic charged particles deflected by a magnetic field. Because the Lorentz force acts perpendicular to both the field and the particle's velocity, the particle spirals along the field lines and radiates at its gyrofrequency (cyclotron frequency) and its harmonics.

Properties

For an electron in a magnetic field B, the cyclotron frequency is proportional to B (about 28 GHz per tesla). The radiated power is given by the Larmor formula applied to the centripetal acceleration. When the particles become relativistic, the radiation is beamed and shifted to higher harmonics and is called synchrotron radiation.

Applications and occurrence

Cyclotron emission is an important energy-loss and diagnostic mechanism in magnetically confined fusion plasmas, where electron cyclotron emission is used to measure plasma temperature. Astrophysically, cyclotron lines are observed in the X-ray spectra of accreting neutron stars, giving direct measurements of their magnetic fields, and cyclotron maser emission drives auroral radio emissions at Earth and Jupiter.
