Non-directional beacon

A non-directional beacon (NDB) is a radio transmitter at a known location used as an aviation or marine navigational aid. It broadcasts a simple omnidirectional signal, typically a Morse code identifier, in the low-to-medium frequency band (190–535 kHz), which aircraft equipped with an automatic direction finder (ADF) can home to or track from.

Use

NDBs were among the earliest radio navigation aids and were widely used for en-route navigation and non-precision instrument approaches. The ADF needle points toward the station, allowing pilots to fly headings relative to the beacon. Signals are subject to errors including night effect, terrain and coastal refraction, and interference from thunderstorms.

Decline

With the spread of VOR, DME, and satellite-based GPS navigation, NDBs have been progressively decommissioned in many countries, though they remain in service in remote regions and as backups elsewhere.
