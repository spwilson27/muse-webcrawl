Biomaterial

A biomaterial is a substance that has been engineered to interact with biological systems for a medical purpose — either a therapeutic purpose (to treat, augment, repair or replace a tissue function of the body) or a diagnostic one. The study of biomaterials is called biomaterials science or biomaterials engineering.

Materials

Biomaterials include metals such as titanium and stainless steel used in joint replacements and dental implants; ceramics such as alumina and hydroxyapatite; polymers including silicone, polyethylene and biodegradable polyesters used in sutures and drug delivery; and natural materials such as collagen and decellularised tissues.

Biocompatibility

A central requirement is biocompatibility: the material must perform its function without eliciting an undesirable local or systemic response. The body's reaction to an implant, including protein adsorption, inflammation and fibrous encapsulation, is studied under the heading of the host response.

Applications

Applications range from heart valves, vascular grafts and pacemakers to contact lenses, wound dressings, tissue-engineering scaffolds and biosensors.
