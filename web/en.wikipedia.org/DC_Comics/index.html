DC Comics

DC Comics, Inc. is an American comic book publisher and the flagship unit of DC Entertainment, a subsidiary of Warner Bros. Discovery. One of the oldest and largest comic book companies, DC is home to Superman, Batman, Wonder Woman, the Flash, Green Lantern, Aquaman, and the Justice League. The initials "DC" come from the company's popular series Detective Comics, which debuted in 1937 and introduced Batman in issue #27 (1939).

History

Malcolm Wheeler-Nicholson founded National Allied Publications in 1934 and launched New Fun, the first comic book of entirely original material, in 1935. Action Comics #1 (1938) introduced Superman, created by Jerry Siegel and Joe Shuster, launching the superhero genre. Batman followed in 1939, and Wonder Woman in 1941. The company consolidated as National Comics Publications and was commonly known by the "DC" bullet logo long before formally adopting the name DC Comics in 1977.

Silver Age and beyond

Editor Julius Schwartz revived superheroes in the 1950s–60s with new versions of the Flash (1956) and Green Lantern, leading to the Justice League of America (1960) and friendly rivalry with Marvel Comics. The 1985–86 crossover Crisis on Infinite Earths rebooted DC's continuity; later relaunches include the New 52 (2011) and DC Rebirth (2016). Vertigo (1993–2020) published acclaimed adult titles such as Sandman and Watchmen was published by DC in 1986–87.

Media

DC characters anchor films, television, and animation, from the 1978 Superman film through Christopher Nolan's Dark Knight trilogy and the DC Extended Universe, as well as a DC Universe film slate overseen by DC Studios from 2023.
