Indoor arena

An indoor arena is an indoor space designed for sports events, concerts, and other large public gatherings, typically consisting of a large central floor area surrounded by tiered seating for spectators. Unlike stadiums, arenas are fully enclosed and climate-controlled, and they are generally smaller, with capacities commonly ranging from a few thousand to about 20,000 for basketball or ice hockey. Most modern arenas are multipurpose, converting between ice, court, and concert configurations.

History

The modern arena descends from 19th-century venues built for boxing, cycling, and ice skating. In North America, arenas developed alongside professional ice hockey and basketball; landmarks of the form include the original Madison Square Garden buildings in New York and Maple Leaf Gardens in Toronto (1931). Post-war venues such as the Spectrum in Philadelphia and the Capital Centre pioneered modern amenities, while late-20th-century designs added luxury suites, club seating, and vast video scoreboards ("jumbotrons").

Design

Arena design must balance sightlines for different sports: hockey rinks (200 ft × 85 ft) are larger than basketball courts, so hockey-optimized buildings have steeper, closer seating. Acoustics, rigging capacity for concert lighting, ice-making plant, and retractable seating are central engineering concerns. Many arenas anchor downtown entertainment districts and are financed partly with public funds, a practice that remains economically controversial.

Uses

Beyond hockey and basketball, indoor arenas host indoor soccer, arena football, lacrosse, wrestling, mixed martial arts, rodeos, circuses, political conventions, graduations, and concerts. The largest indoor arenas in the world include the Philippine Arena (capacity about 55,000), built primarily for church gatherings and events.
