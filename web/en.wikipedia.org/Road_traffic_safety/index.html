Road traffic safety

Traffic calming and marked crossings are core safety measures

Road traffic safety refers to the methods and measures used to prevent road users from being killed or seriously injured. Typical road users include pedestrians, cyclists, motorists, vehicle passengers, horse riders, and passengers of on-road public transport (mainly buses and trams).

According to the World Health Organization, approximately 1.19 million people died in road traffic crashes in 2021, and between 20 and 50 million more suffer non-fatal injuries each year. Road injuries are the leading cause of death for children and young adults aged 5–29 years.

Risk factors

Key behavioural risk factors include speeding, drink-driving, non-use of helmets, seat-belts and child restraints, and distracted driving. Infrastructure risk factors include high-speed roads mixing with vulnerable users, inadequate crossings and poor lighting.

Interventions

The "Safe System" approach, pioneered in Sweden as Vision Zero and in the Netherlands as Sustainable Safety, accepts that humans make mistakes and designs the road system so that crashes do not result in death. Proven measures include speed management, vehicle safety standards such as crumple zones and airbags, graduated driver licensing and automated enforcement.

Statistics

More than half of all road traffic deaths are among vulnerable road users — pedestrians, cyclists and motorcyclists. Low- and middle-income countries account for over 90% of fatalities despite having around 60% of the world's vehicles.
