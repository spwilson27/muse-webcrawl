Gotthard Tunnel

Location: Lepontine Alps, Switzerland

Opened: 22 May 1882

Length: 15.0 km (9.3 mi)

Route: Gotthard railway line (Göschenen–Airolo)

The Gotthard Tunnel (German: Gotthardtunnel, Italian: Galleria del San Gottardo) is a 15-kilometre-long railway tunnel that forms the summit of the Gotthard railway line in Switzerland. It connects Göschenen in the canton of Uri with Airolo in Ticino, passing beneath the Saint-Gotthard Massif. When it opened in 1882, it was the longest tunnel in the world.

Construction

Construction began in 1872 under the direction of the engineer Louis Favre, whose company contracted to complete the work in eight years. The tunnel was driven from both ends using compressed-air drills and dynamite. Conditions were brutal: temperatures inside reached 33 °C, and about 200 workers died, from accidents, rockfalls, and disease including hookworm infection. Favre himself died of a heart attack inside the tunnel in 1879. The two headings met on 29 February 1880, and the tunnel opened to traffic in 1882.

Significance

The Gotthard line created the first direct rail link between northern Europe and Italy across the central Alps and remains a major freight and passenger corridor. The name is also used for the Gotthard Road Tunnel (opened 1980, 16.9 km) and the Gotthard Base Tunnel (opened 2016, 57.1 km), the longest railway tunnel in the world, which bypasses the original summit tunnel for most through traffic.
