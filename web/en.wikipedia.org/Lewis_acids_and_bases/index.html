Lewis acids and bases

A Lewis acid is a chemical species that contains an empty orbital capable of accepting an electron pair from a Lewis base to form a Lewis adduct. A Lewis base is any species with a filled orbital containing an electron pair not involved in bonding that can be donated. The definitions were proposed by Gilbert N. Lewis in 1923.

Scope

The Lewis theory generalises the Brønsted–Lowry theory: every Brønsted acid–base reaction is also a Lewis reaction, but Lewis theory also covers reactions with no proton transfer, such as the reaction of boron trifluoride (BF3) with ammonia (NH3) to form the adduct H3N–BF3.

Applications

Lewis acids such as aluminium chloride are important catalysts, notably in Friedel–Crafts reactions. Concepts such as hard and soft acids and bases, frontier orbital descriptions, and the Gutmann donor number are used to rationalise Lewis acid–base strength and selectivity.
