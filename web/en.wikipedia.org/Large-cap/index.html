Large-cap

Definition: Companies with market capitalization typically above US$10 billion
Related: Mid-cap, small-cap, mega-cap

Large-cap (short for large market capitalization) refers to publicly traded companies with a large market capitalization, commonly defined as US$10 billion or more. Market capitalization is calculated by multiplying the share price by the number of shares outstanding.

Characteristics

Large-cap companies are usually well-established firms with strong market positions, stable earnings and a history of paying dividends. They are generally considered less volatile and less risky than small-cap stocks, though they may offer lower growth potential.

Indices

Many stock market indices track large-cap companies, including the S&P 500 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average in the United States. Companies with market capitalizations above roughly US$200 billion are sometimes called mega-cap.
