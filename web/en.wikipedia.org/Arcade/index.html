Arcade

Arcade may refer to several distinct concepts, most prominently in architecture, a succession of contiguous arches, supported by columns or piers, either freestanding or attached to a wall; and in entertainment, an amusement arcade housing coin-operated games. This article surveys the principal senses of the term.

Architecture

In architecture, an arcade is a series of arches carried on columns or piers. Arcades appear in Roman aqueducts and bridges, in the cloisters and naves of medieval churches (where the nave arcade separates the central vessel from the aisles), and in covered shopping streets such as the Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II in Milan. A blind arcade is applied decoratively to a wall surface, while an intersecting arcade is characteristic of Norman and Romanesque ornament.

Amusement arcade

An amusement arcade (also called a video arcade or games arcade) is a venue housing coin-operated machines — video games, pinball machines, redemption games, and electromechanical games. Video arcades flourished during the golden age of arcade video games in the late 1970s and early 1980s and remain culturally prominent in Japan, where they are known as game centers.

Other uses

"Arcade" is also the name of a literary journal, of the Arcade platform in geography software (an expression language in ArcGIS), and appears in music, place names, and the titles of works such as the band Arcade Fire.
