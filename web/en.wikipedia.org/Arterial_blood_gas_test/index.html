Arterial blood gas test

An arterial blood gas (ABG) test, or arterial blood gas analysis, measures the amounts of arterial gases, such as oxygen and carbon dioxide, together with blood pH and bicarbonate. Blood is usually drawn from an artery — most commonly the radial artery at the wrist — into a heparinised syringe and analysed promptly on a blood gas analyser.

Parameters

ParameterTypical reference range
pH7.35–7.45
PaO₂ (partial pressure of oxygen)75–100 mmHg
PaCO₂ (partial pressure of carbon dioxide)35–45 mmHg
HCO₃⁻ (bicarbonate)22–26 mmol/L
Oxygen saturation (SaO₂)95–100%
Base excess−2 to +2 mmol/L

Clinical use

The ABG test is used to evaluate gas exchange in the lungs and the body's acid–base balance. It helps diagnose and manage respiratory failure, metabolic disorders, diabetic ketoacidosis, sepsis, renal failure and poisoning, and is routinely used to guide mechanical ventilation in critical care.

Interpretation

Analysis typically proceeds by checking the pH (acidaemia or alkalaemia), then the respiratory component (PaCO₂) and metabolic component (bicarbonate) to determine whether the primary disturbance is respiratory or metabolic, and whether compensation has occurred. Disorders are classified as respiratory acidosis or alkalosis and metabolic acidosis or alkalosis, which may coexist as mixed disorders.
