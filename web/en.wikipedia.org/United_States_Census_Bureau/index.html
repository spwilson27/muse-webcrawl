United States Census Bureau

The United States Census Bureau is a principal agency of the U.S. Federal Statistical System, part of the Department of Commerce, responsible for producing data about the American people and economy. Its best-known task is the decennial census of population mandated by the U.S. Constitution.

Decennial census

Every ten years the Bureau conducts the census that determines apportionment of seats in the House of Representatives and the distribution of federal funds. The first census was taken in 1790; the most recent was the 2020 census.

Other programs

Between censuses the Bureau runs the American Community Survey, the Economic Census every five years, population estimates, and surveys on income, poverty, housing and health insurance that are used across government and research.

Confidentiality

Individual responses are protected by Title 13 of the U.S. Code and may not be disclosed in identifiable form; decennial records are released to the public after 72 years.
