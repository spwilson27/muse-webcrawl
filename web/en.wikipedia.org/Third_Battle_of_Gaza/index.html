The Third Battle of Gaza was fought on the night of 1–2 November 1917 and in the days that followed, during the Sinai and Palestine Campaign of the First World War. Forces of the British Egyptian Expeditionary Force under General Edmund Allenby broke the Ottoman defensive line running from Gaza on the Mediterranean coast inland to Beersheba.

Background

Two earlier attacks on Gaza in March and April 1917 had failed with heavy losses. Allenby, who replaced Archibald Murray in June, rebuilt his force and deceived the Ottoman command into expecting another frontal assault on Gaza, while his main blow fell inland at Beersheba, captured on 31 October by a celebrated charge of the Australian Light Horse.

Battle

On the night of 1–2 November, British infantry attacked the Gaza defences to pin Ottoman reserves, while the mounted corps exploited the Beersheba breakthrough and cut the line from the east. Gaza was evacuated by its garrison on the night of 6–7 November, and the Ottoman Seventh and Eighth Armies retreated northward.

Aftermath

The victory opened the way for the advance on Jerusalem, which fell in December 1917, and marked the turning point of the Palestine campaign.
