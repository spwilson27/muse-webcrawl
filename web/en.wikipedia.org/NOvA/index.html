NOvA


Labelled image: the NOvA far detector hall

NOvA (NuMI Off-Axis νe Appearance) is a long-baseline neutrino oscillation experiment based at Fermilab in Illinois, United States. It studies the oscillations of muon neutrinos and antineutrinos into electron neutrinos and antineutrinos over a baseline of 810 kilometres, using the NuMI beam produced at Fermilab and two detectors: a near detector at Fermilab and a 14-kiloton far detector at Ash River, Minnesota.

Design

Both detectors are built from extruded PVC cells filled with liquid scintillator and read out by avalanche photodiodes via wavelength-shifting fiber; the far detector consists of some 344,000 cells and sits slightly off the beam axis, which narrows the neutrino energy spectrum around 2 GeV, the peak of the oscillation probability for its baseline. Comparing the electron-neutrino appearance rate at the two detectors constrains the mixing angle θ23, the mass ordering of the neutrinos, and the CP-violating phase δCP.

Results

NOvA began taking data with the far detector in 2014 and alternates neutrino and antineutrino beam modes. Its measurements, often combined with those of Japan's T2K experiment, have favored the normal mass ordering and particular values of the CP phase, though with continuing statistical uncertainty; joint NOvA–T2K analyses have been used to reduce degeneracies between the two experiments' different baselines and energies. The experiment is expected to be succeeded in the United States by the more ambitious DUNE experiment.
