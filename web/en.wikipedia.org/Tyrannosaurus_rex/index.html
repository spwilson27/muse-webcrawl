Tyrannosaurus rex is a species of large theropod dinosaur in the genus Tyrannosaurus, which lived in western North America during the latest Cretaceous period, about 68 to 66 million years ago. It was among the last non-avian dinosaurs, extinguished in the Cretaceous–Palaeogene extinction event. The species epithet rex means "king" in Latin, and the animal — the "tyrant lizard king" — has become the most famous of all dinosaurs.

Description

The largest complete specimens measure around 12.3–12.4 metres in length and are estimated to have weighed between 8 and 10 tonnes. T. rex had a massive skull up to about 1.5 metres long, balanced by a long, heavy tail, and hind limbs among the largest of any theropod. Its forelimbs, by contrast, were strikingly small, ending in two clawed digits. Bone-crushing bite forces — the strongest of any known terrestrial animal — allowed it to exploit carcasses as well as live prey such as hadrosaurs and ceratopsians.

Discovery and research

The first specimens were described in the 1870s–90s under other names; Henry Fairfield Osborn coined Tyrannosaurus rex in 1905. Famous skeletons include "Sue" (FMNH PR 2081), found in South Dakota in 1990 and among the most complete; "Stan" (BHI 3033); and "Scotty" of Saskatchewan, among the largest known. Ongoing debates concern its top speed (commonly estimated at modest running or fast walking pace), whether it was primarily predator or scavenger, growth rates (a teenage growth spurt is well supported), and whether adults bore feathers or were fully scaled.
