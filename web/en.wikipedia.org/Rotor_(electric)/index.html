Rotor (electric)

The rotor is the rotating component of an electric motor, generator, alternator, or other electromagnetic machine, turning within the stationary stator. It typically consists of windings or permanent magnets mounted on a shaft supported by bearings.

Types

In induction motors the rotor is commonly a "squirrel-cage" of aluminium or copper bars shorted by end rings, or a wound rotor with slip rings. In synchronous machines the rotor carries field windings fed with direct current, or permanent magnets. DC machines carry the armature windings on the rotor, connected through a commutator.

Operation

Torque arises from the interaction of magnetic fields in the rotor and stator. The difference between rotor speed and the synchronous speed of the stator field in an induction motor is called slip.
