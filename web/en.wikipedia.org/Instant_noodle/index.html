Instant noodles, or instant ramen, are a type of food consisting of noodles sold in a precooked and dried block with flavouring powder and/or seasoning oil. The dried noodle block was originally created by flash-frying cooked noodles, which is still the main method used in Asian countries; air-dried noodle blocks are favoured in Western countries. They are designed for quick preparation in hot water, typically within three to five minutes.

History

Instant noodles were invented by Momofuku Ando of Nissin Foods in Japan. They were launched in 1958 under the brand name Chikin Ramen. In 1971, Nissin introduced Cup Noodles, the first cup noodle product, with noodles in a polystyrene cup to which boiling water is added. The product became a global staple food, with more than 100 billion servings consumed annually in recent years, according to industry estimates.

Production and consumption

Manufacture involves steaming noodle dough, shaping, and dehydrating by frying or hot air. The largest markets are China, Indonesia, Vietnam, India, and Japan. Instant noodles are inexpensive and shelf-stable, but are frequently criticised for high sodium, saturated fat, and low fibre and micronutrient content.

Cultural impact

In a Japanese poll in 2000, instant noodles were voted Japan's most important invention of the twentieth century. They have been eaten in space, are a staple of convenience stores and student diets worldwide, and inspired the Momofuku Ando Instant Ramen Museum in Osaka.
