Robert Pickton

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Robert William Pickton (24 October 1949 – 31 May 2024) was a Canadian serial killer and pig farmer from Port Coquitlam, British Columbia. He was convicted in 2007 of the second-degree murders of six women and was charged in the deaths of twenty more, most of them sex workers who had disappeared from Vancouver's Downtown Eastside. He is believed to be one of the most prolific serial killers in Canadian history.

Investigation and trial

Between the late 1970s and 2001, more than sixty women went missing from the Downtown Eastside. In February 2002, police searching Pickton's farm for illegal firearms found personal effects of missing women; the farm became the largest crime scene investigation in Canadian history, and DNA of 33 women was identified there. Pickton was tried for six murders in 2007 and convicted on all counts, receiving the maximum sentence of life with no parole eligibility for 25 years. Charges concerning twenty other victims were stayed after his conviction. In a recorded conversation with an undercover officer, he claimed to have killed 49 women.

Aftermath

The case prompted the British Columbia Missing Women Commission of Inquiry (the Oppal Inquiry), which criticized the police response and the failures that allowed the murders to continue. Pickton died in May 2024 after being attacked by another inmate in a Quebec prison. The case remains a focal point in public discussion of the safety of Indigenous women and sex workers in Canada.
