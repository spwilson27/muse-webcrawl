Ariane 5

Ariane 5 is a European heavy-lift space launch vehicle developed and operated by Arianespace for the European Space Agency (ESA). It was launched from the Guiana Space Centre (CSG) in French Guiana and was designed to deliver payloads into geostationary transfer orbit (GTO) and other orbits. Ariane 5 succeeded the Ariane 4 family and flew from 1996 until its retirement in 2023, when it was succeeded by Ariane 6.

Design

The Ariane 5 core consists of a large cryogenic main stage, the Étage Principal Cryotechnique (EPC), powered by a single Vulcain engine burning liquid hydrogen and liquid oxygen. Two large solid rocket boosters, the Étages d'Accélération à Poudre (EAP), flank the core and provide the bulk of the thrust at liftoff. Upper stages included the storable-propellant EPS and, on the ES and ECA variants, the cryogenic ESC-A powered by an HM7B engine.

Variants

The original Ariane 5G (Generic) was followed by the improved 5G+, the 5GS, the heavy 5 ECA capable of lifting about 10 tonnes to GTO, and the 5 ES used to launch the Automated Transfer Vehicle to the International Space Station. A planned ME (Mid-life Evolution) variant was cancelled in favour of Ariane 6.

History

The maiden flight on 4 June 1996 failed after the rocket veered off course and was destroyed, an accident traced to a software exception in the inertial reference system inherited from Ariane 4. The second qualification flight in 1997 was partially successful, and the third, in October 1998, was a full success. Commercial service began in 1999. Over its career Ariane 5 launched many landmark payloads, including the Rosetta comet mission, the Herschel and Planck observatories, Galileo navigation satellites, the BepiColombo Mercury mission, and the James Webb Space Telescope on 25 December 2021. The final flight, VA261, took place on 5 July 2023.
