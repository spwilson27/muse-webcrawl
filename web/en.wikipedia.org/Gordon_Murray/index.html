Ian Gordon Murray (born 18 June 1946) is a South African-British designer of Formula One racing cars and the McLaren F1 road car. He is regarded as one of the most innovative engineers in motor racing history, noted for lightweight design, radical aerodynamics, and packaging.

[ Image placeholder: Gordon Murray beside the Gordon Murray Automotive T.50 ]

Born in Durban, South Africa, Murray built and raced his own car, the IGM Ford, before moving to England in 1969 to join Brabham. As Brabham's chief designer he produced cars including the BT46 "fan car" (which won its only race, the 1978 Swedish Grand Prix, before being withdrawn), and the BT49 and BT52, which carried Nelson Piquet to the 1981 and 1983 World Championships.

McLaren

Joining McLaren in 1987, Murray designed the MP4/4 of 1988, which won 15 of 16 races with Ayrton Senna and Alain Prost — statistically one of the most dominant cars in Formula One history. As technical director he oversaw further titles before moving to McLaren Cars to design the McLaren F1 (1992), a naturally aspirated, gold-lined-engine-bay supercar with a central driving position that remains one of the fastest naturally aspirated production cars ever built; a racing version won the 24 Hours of Le Mans in 1995.

Later work

Murray founded Gordon Murray Design in 2007, developing the iStream carbon-fibre manufacturing process, and Gordon Murray Automotive, whose T.50 supercar (2020) is presented as the F1's spiritual successor: a lightweight, V12-powered three-seater with a rear-mounted downforce fan, followed by the T.33. He was appointed CBE in 2019.
