Seanad Éireann

Seanad Éireann (Irish for "Senate of Ireland") is the upper house of the Oireachtas, the legislature of Ireland. It consists of 60 senators and sits in Leinster House in Dublin, alongside the lower house, Dáil Éireann. The Seanad is less powerful than the Dáil: it can delay and amend most legislation but cannot ultimately block money bills or indefinitely prevent a bill becoming law.

Composition

Eleven senators are nominated by the Taoiseach; six are elected by graduates of Irish universities; and 43 are elected by serving politicians from five vocational panels representing areas such as culture and education, agriculture, labour, industry and commerce, and public administration. This indirect system has been widely criticised, and a 2013 referendum proposing the Seanad's abolition was narrowly defeated by voters.

History

The first Seanad served the Irish Free State from 1922 until its abolition in 1936; the present Seanad was established by the Constitution of Ireland in 1937.
