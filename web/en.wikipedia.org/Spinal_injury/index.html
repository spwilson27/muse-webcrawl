Spinal cord injury

A spinal cord injury (SCI) is damage to the spinal cord that causes temporary or permanent changes in its function. Symptoms may include loss of muscle function, sensation, or autonomic function in parts of the body below the level of injury.

Causes

Most spinal cord injuries result from trauma such as road traffic collisions, falls, violence, and sports injuries; non-traumatic causes include tumours, infections, and degenerative diseases. Damage may be complete (no function below the level) or incomplete.

Management

Emergency care focuses on immobilising the spine and maintaining breathing and circulation; surgery may relieve pressure on the cord. Rehabilitation, assistive devices, and treatment of complications such as pressure sores and spasticity are central to long-term care.
