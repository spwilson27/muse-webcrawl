Stapler

A typical desktop stapler used for binding paper.

A stapler is a mechanical device that joins pages of paper or similar material by driving a thin metal staple through the sheets and folding the ends. Staplers are widely used in offices, schools, and homes, and come in manual, electric, and heavy-duty forms. Surgical staplers are used in medicine to close wounds and connect tissue.

History

Fastening paper with metal fasteners dates back centuries; King Louis XV of France is said to have owned an ornate stapler in the 18th century. The first commercially successful staplers appeared in the 19th century, with patents issued to George McGill in the United States in the 1860s and 1870s. The modern staple-strip magazine design was developed in the early 20th century, and the familiar standard staple size No. 26/6 became widespread.

Mechanism

A typical stapler holds a strip of staples in a spring-loaded magazine. Pressing down drives a blade that separates one staple and pushes its legs through the paper; the legs strike a crimping plate (anvil) that folds them flat against the back of the sheets, binding them together. Staple removers use a claw to pull the folded legs straight and lift the staple out.
