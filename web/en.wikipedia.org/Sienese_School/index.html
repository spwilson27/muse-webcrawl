Sienese School

Location: Siena, Italy
Period: 13th–15th centuries
Key artists: Duccio, Simone Martini, Lorenzetti brothers

The Sienese School of painting flourished in Siena, Italy, between the 13th and 15th centuries, rivaling Florence as a centre of Italian art while retaining a more conservative, decorative and Byzantine-influenced style.

Characteristics

Sienese painting is noted for luminous gold grounds, elegant sinuous line, rich colour and a mystical, courtly mood, in contrast to the more naturalistic and sculptural Florentine style of Giotto and Masaccio.

Artists

Founders include Duccio di Buoninsegna, whose Maestà (1311) for Siena Cathedral is a masterpiece of the school. Other leading figures were Simone Martini, Pietro and Ambrogio Lorenzetti, Bartolo di Fredi, and later Sassetta and Giovanni di Paolo. The school's influence spread to Naples and Avignon through Simone Martini.
