SCN9A

GeneSCN9A (sodium voltage-gated channel alpha subunit 9)
Chromosome2q24.3 (human)
ProteinNav1.7 sodium channel
ExpressionPeripheral nervous system, especially dorsal root ganglion nociceptors
Associated conditionsErythromelalgia, paroxysmal extreme pain disorder, congenital insensitivity to pain, small fiber neuropathy

SCN9A is a human gene encoding the alpha subunit of the voltage-gated sodium channel Nav1.7. The channel is expressed predominantly in peripheral neurons, especially in the nociceptors of the dorsal root ganglia, where it acts as a "threshold channel" that amplifies small depolarisations and sets the gain of pain signalling. Because of this role, SCN9A has been intensively studied in human pain genetics and as a drug target for analgesics.

Clinical significance

Gain-of-function mutations in SCN9A cause inherited pain syndromes: primary erythromelalgia, characterised by burning pain and redness of the extremities triggered by warmth, and paroxysmal extreme pain disorder, with severe rectal, ocular and submaxillary pain attacks. Conversely, biallelic loss-of-function mutations cause congenital insensitivity to pain (channelopathy-associated insensitivity to pain): affected individuals never feel pain, commonly suffer painless fractures, burns and self-mutilation of the lips and tongue, and typically also have anosmia (inability to smell). Certain other variants are associated with small fiber neuropathy and with altered pain sensitivity in the general population.

Therapeutic interest

People lacking Nav1.7 are otherwise essentially normal, which made the channel an attractive target for a new class of non-opioid painkillers. Selective Nav1.7 blockers have been pursued by many pharmaceutical companies, though clinical results have been mixed, partly because near-complete block appears necessary to reproduce the congenital phenotype. Research into SCN9A also informs the broader study of the other peripheral sodium channels, Nav1.8 (SCN10A) and Nav1.9 (SCN11A); a Nav1.8 inhibitor, suzetrigine, was approved in the United States in 2025 as the first of this new analgesic class.
