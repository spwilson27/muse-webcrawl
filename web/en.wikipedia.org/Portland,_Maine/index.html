Portland, Maine

Portland is the most populous city in the U.S. state of Maine and the county seat of Cumberland County. Its population was about 68,000 at the 2020 census, with roughly half a million people in its metropolitan area — the largest in northern New England. The city sits on a peninsula jutting into Casco Bay on the Atlantic coast.

History

English settlers first established a trading post on the peninsula, known to Indigenous Wabanaki peoples as Machigonne, in the 1630s; the settlement, called Casco and later Falmouth, was repeatedly destroyed in colonial wars. It was incorporated as Portland in 1786, named for the Isle of Portland in England. Portland served as Maine's capital from statehood in 1820 until 1832, when Augusta took over. On 4 July 1866 a great fire destroyed much of the commercial district; the city was rebuilt in brick and stone, giving the Old Port its characteristic Victorian architecture. The poet Henry Wadsworth Longfellow was born in Portland and called it "the beautiful town that is seated by the sea."

Economy and culture

Portland's economy rests on its working waterfront, fishing and seafood industries, shipping, finance, healthcare, and a nationally recognised restaurant and craft-beer scene. The Old Port district and the Arts District along Congress Street are centres of tourism and culture. The city is served by Portland International Jetport and Amtrak's Downeaster train.
