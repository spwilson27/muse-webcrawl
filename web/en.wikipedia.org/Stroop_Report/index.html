Stroop Report

The Stroop Report is a 125-page official report, with a photographic record, prepared by SS-Gruppenführer Jürgen Stroop for Heinrich Himmler documenting the suppression of the Warsaw Ghetto Uprising in April and May 1943. Titled The Jewish Quarter of Warsaw Is No More! (Es gibt keinen jüdischen Wohnbezirk in Warschau mehr!), it chronicles, in the language of its perpetrators, the destruction of the Warsaw Ghetto and the murder or deportation of its inhabitants. It was entered into evidence at the Nuremberg trials.

Contents

The bound album consists of a summary, daily operational reports, statistics of Jews killed, captured, or deported to the Treblinka extermination camp, a tally of captured weapons and destroyed bunkers, and 52 black-and-white photographs taken by German personnel. Stroop boasted that 56,065 Jews had been captured, of whom some 7,000 were shot during the operation and roughly 7,000 more sent to Treblinka; the ghetto itself was systematically burned and demolished building by building. Its photographs — including the iconic image of a small boy with his hands raised — became some of the most reproduced documents of the Holocaust.

Aftermath

Stroop was captured by American forces, convicted by a US military court at Dachau for the murder of Allied airmen, and then extradited to Poland, where he was tried in Warsaw and hanged in 1952 near the site of the ghetto. Two copies of the report survived the war, one used at Nuremberg and one held in Polish archives — among the most important surviving perpetrator records of the Holocaust. The original is held by the Institute of National Remembrance in Warsaw, and copies are on the UNESCO Memory of the World register.
