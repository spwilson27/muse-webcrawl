Hong Kong dollar

Hong Kong dollarISO code: HKD
Symbol: HK$
Authority: Hong Kong Monetary Authority
Regime: linked exchange rate (pegged to USD since 1983)

The Hong Kong dollar (sign: HK$; code: HKD) is the official currency of Hong Kong. It is one of the most traded currencies in the world and is issued under a currency board system overseen by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA). Banknotes are printed by three commercial banks — HSBC, Standard Chartered and Bank of China (Hong Kong) — while coins and ten-dollar notes are issued by the government.

Linked exchange rate system

Since October 1983 the Hong Kong dollar has been pegged to the United States dollar. Since 2005 the HKMA has maintained a convertibility zone of HK$7.75–7.85 per US dollar, intervening to keep the rate within the band. The peg survived the Asian financial crisis of 1997–98 despite intense speculative pressure.

History

Hong Kong adopted the silver dollar standard in the nineteenth century; the Hong Kong dollar was established as a distinct unit in 1935 when China abandoned silver. It was pegged to sterling before floating in 1974, then re-pegged to the US dollar in 1983 amid uncertainty over the Sino-British negotiations on Hong Kong's future.
