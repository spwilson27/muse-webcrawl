Hibakusha

Meaning: "Explosion-affected people"
Origin: Japanese (被爆者)
Recognised: Atomic Bomb Survivors' Relief Law (1957, 1994)

Hibakusha (Japanese: 被爆者 or 被曝者, pronounced [çibaꜜkɯ̥ɕa]) is a Japanese word meaning "explosion-affected people" and is used for the survivors of the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki in August 1945. As of recent years, the number of officially recognised living hibakusha has fallen below 100,000, with an average age above 85. The category formally includes those within a designated distance of the hypocentres, those exposed to fallout (including the "black rain"), relief workers who entered the zones, and children exposed in utero.

Discrimination and health

Hibakusha and their children (hibakusha nisei) faced social discrimination in marriage and employment due to fears — scientifically unfounded for hereditary effects — about radiation and disease, and many concealed their status. Long-term health studies by the Radiation Effects Research Foundation have documented elevated risks of leukemia and solid cancers; the survivors receive special medical allowances and health handbooks under Japanese relief laws of 1957 and 1994.

Activism

Hibakusha have been central to the global movement against nuclear weapons, testifying at the United Nations and in schools worldwide. Their umbrella organisation, Nihon Hidankyo (the Japan Confederation of A- and H-Bomb Sufferers Organizations), founded in 1956, received the Nobel Peace Prize in 2024 "for its efforts to achieve a world free of nuclear weapons."

In culture

Survivor testimony (genbaku bungaku, "atomic bomb literature") includes works by Ōta Yōko, Hara Tamiki, and the manga Barefoot Gen by Nakazawa Keiji. Tsutomu Yamaguchi, who survived both bombings, was officially recognised as a double hibakusha (nijū hibakusha).
