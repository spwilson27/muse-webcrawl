Jean Ritchie

Jean Ruth Ritchie (December 8, 1922 – June 1, 2015) was an American folk singer, songwriter, and Appalachian dulcimer player, called by some the "Mother of Folk". In her youth she learned hundreds of folk songs in the traditional way (orally, from her family and community), many of which were Appalachian variants of centuries-old British and Irish songs.

Life and career

Ritchie was born in Viper, Kentucky, the youngest of fourteen children in a family of traditional ballad singers. She was encouraged by folklorist Alan Lomax, for whom she recorded extensively. In 1952–53, on a Fulbright scholarship, she travelled to Britain and Ireland to trace the origins of her family's songs, recording singers there and building lasting links between British and Appalachian traditions.

Ritchie popularized the Appalachian (mountain) dulcimer and recorded more than 35 albums. Her songs, including "My Dear Companion" and the anti-strip-mining song "Black Waters", have been recorded by many artists. She was also a writer, author of the memoir Singing Family of the Cumberlands (1955).

Legacy

Ritchie was inducted into the Long Island Music Hall of Fame in 2006 and received the Bess Lomax Hawes Award from the National Endowment for the Arts. Her field recordings and writings remain a major resource for the study of Anglo-American folk song.
