Mark Rothko (born Markus Yakovlevich Rothkowitz, 25 September 1903 – 25 February 1970) was an American abstract painter, best known for his colour field paintings: large canvases of soft-edged, hovering rectangles of colour.

Early life

Born in Dvinsk in the Russian Empire, Rothko emigrated with his family to Portland, Oregon, in 1913. He attended Yale University, left without a degree, and studied in New York under Max Weber, moving through figurative and surrealist phases in the 1930s and 1940s.

Mature work

By 1949 Rothko had arrived at his signature format: two or three luminous rectangles stacked on a coloured ground, painted in thin, layered washes. He insisted the works were dramas meant to envelop the viewer emotionally, not decorations. Major commissions include the Seagram Murals (1958–59, partly at Tate) and the Rothko Chapel in Houston (dedicated 1971), a non-denominational space containing fourteen dark paintings.

Death and legacy

Rothko died by suicide in 1970 amid ill health and a legal battle over his estate that became a landmark art-world scandal. His paintings command some of the highest prices in modern art; Orange, Red, Yellow (1961) sold for $86.9 million in 2012.
