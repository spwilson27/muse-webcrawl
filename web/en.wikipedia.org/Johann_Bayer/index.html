Johann Bayer

[Image placeholder: star chart from Uranometria]

Johann Bayer (1572 – 7 March 1625) was a German lawyer and uranographer (celestial cartographer). He was born in Rain, Lower Bavaria, and practised law in Augsburg. Bayer is best known for his star atlas Uranometria, published in Augsburg in 1603, the first atlas to cover the entire celestial sphere.

Uranometria

Uranometria contained 51 star charts: one for each of the 48 Ptolemaic constellations, one for the southern sky, and two planispheres. For the southern stars Bayer drew on the observations of Dutch navigators such as Pieter Dirkszoon Keyser and Frederick de Houtman, and he introduced twelve new southern constellations. The engravings, by Alexander Mair, remained influential for over a century.

Bayer designations

Bayer introduced the system of naming stars with Greek and Latin letters — the Bayer designations — that is still in use today. In most constellations he assigned the Greek letter alpha (α) to the brightest star, beta (β) to the second brightest, and so on, though in practice he often lettered stars in other orders, such as by position in the figure. Famous examples include α Centauri and β Pictoris. When the 24 Greek letters were exhausted, he continued with Latin letters.

The lunar crater Bayer is named in his honour.
