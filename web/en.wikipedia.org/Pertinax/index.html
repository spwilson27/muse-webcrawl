Pertinax

Publius Helvius Pertinax (1 August 126 – 28 March 193) was Roman emperor for the first three months of 193. He succeeded Commodus, whose assassination he may not have foreseen, and became the first claimant to the throne in the tumultuous Year of the Five Emperors. A senator of modest origins — the son of a freedman — Pertinax had risen through a distinguished military and civil career, serving as proconsul of Africa and prefect of Rome.

Reign

Proclaimed emperor by the Praetorian Guard on the night of Commodus's murder, Pertinax attempted to restore discipline and solvency after his predecessor's extravagance. He reformed the currency by increasing the silver purity of the denarius, cut court expenses, sold off Commodus's luxuries, and tried to enforce military discipline. His reforms, however, alienated the Praetorians, who resented his strictness and the modest donative they had received compared with what they had been promised.

Assassination

After several plots, on 28 March 193 a party of soldiers burst into the palace and murdered Pertinax, who, according to Cassius Dio, faced them bravely and attempted to reason with them before being struck down. He had reigned only 86 days. The Guard then notoriously auctioned the empire to the highest bidder, Didius Julianus. Pertinax was deified by Septimius Severus, who avenged his death and took the name Pertinax for himself.
