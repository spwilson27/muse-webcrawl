Liturgical drama

Liturgical drama refers to plays performed within or alongside the Christian liturgy in medieval Europe, growing out of sung embellishments of the Mass and Divine Office from about the tenth century.

Origins

The famous starting point is the Quem quaeritis trope — a short dialogue at Easter Matins in which angels at Christ's empty tomb answer the three Marys — preserved in manuscripts such as the Winchester Troper. Expanded with processions, costumes and additional scenes, such pieces became the Easter and Christmas plays performed in churches, in Latin, by clergy.

Development

As the dramas grew longer and more popular they moved outside the church, passed to lay guild performers, and shifted into vernacular languages, developing into the great mystery and miracle play cycles. Hildegard of Bingen's Ordo Virtutum (c. 1151) is an early sung morality play by a known author.
