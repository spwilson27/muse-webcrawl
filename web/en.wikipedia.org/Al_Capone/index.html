Al Capone

Al Capone
Born: 17 January 1899, Brooklyn, New York
Died: 25 January 1947, Miami Beach, Florida
Occupation: Gangster, crime boss
Conviction: Tax evasion (1931)

Alphonse Gabriel Capone (17 January 1899 – 25 January 1947), sometimes known by the nickname "Scarface", was an American gangster and businessman who attained notoriety during the Prohibition era as the co-founder and boss of the Chicago Outfit from 1925 to 1931.

Rise to power

Born in Brooklyn to Italian immigrant parents, Capone moved to Chicago and rose through the organisation of Johnny Torrio, inheriting the Outfit when Torrio retired in 1925. His empire of bootlegging, gambling, and prostitution generated enormous profits and was enforced with violence, most notoriously in the Saint Valentine's Day Massacre of 1929 against Bugs Moran's North Side Gang.

Downfall

Federal authorities, unable to convict Capone of his violent crimes, prosecuted him for income tax evasion. In 1931 he was convicted and sentenced to eleven years in federal prison, serving time in Atlanta and at Alcatraz. Released in 1939 in poor health from neurosyphilis, he lived in seclusion in Florida until his death in 1947.

Legacy

Capone became the archetypal American mobster in popular culture, the subject of numerous films, books, and television series.
