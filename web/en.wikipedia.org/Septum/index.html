Septum

In biology, a septum (Latin for "enclosure"; plural septa) is a wall dividing a cavity or structure into smaller parts. Septa occur throughout the body plans of animals, fungi, and other organisms.

Examples in human anatomy

  • The nasal septum separates the left and right nasal cavities.
  • The interatrial septum and interventricular septum separate the chambers of the heart.
  • The orbital septum is a membranous sheet acting as the anterior boundary of the orbit.
  • The septum pellucidum is a thin membrane between the lateral ventricles of the brain.

In other organisms

In fungi, septa are cross-walls that divide hyphae into cells, often with pores permitting cytoplasmic flow. In molluscs such as the nautilus, septa divide the shell into chambers. Many annelid worms have internal septa separating their coelomic segments. In plants, the septum of a fruit separates the locules of an ovary.
