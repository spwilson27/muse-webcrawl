ExxonMobil

Exxon Mobil Corporation, doing business as ExxonMobil, is an American multinational oil and gas corporation headquartered in Spring, Texas, near Houston. It was formed on November 30, 1999, by the merger of Exxon and Mobil, both descendants of John D. Rockefeller's Standard Oil, which was broken up by the U.S. Supreme Court in 1911. ExxonMobil is among the world's largest companies by revenue and one of the "supermajor" integrated oil companies, active in exploration and production, refining, chemicals, and fuel marketing under brands including Exxon, Mobil, and Esso.

Operations

The company explores for and produces crude oil and natural gas on several continents, with major positions in U.S. shale (expanded by its 2024 acquisition of Pioneer Natural Resources), Guyana's offshore fields, liquefied natural gas, and deepwater projects. Its refining and chemical segments are among the largest in the world, and it markets fuels and lubricants — notably Mobil 1 — in more than a hundred countries.

Criticism and controversy

ExxonMobil has been a central figure in controversies over climate change. The 1989 Exxon Valdez oil spill in Alaska's Prince William Sound, one of the worst in U.S. history, led to years of litigation and the Oil Pollution Act of 1990. Internal company research from the 1970s and 1980s that modelled global warming accurately, contrasted with later public emphasis on uncertainty, has been documented by journalists and scholars and examined in government investigations and lawsuits. The company states that it supports the Paris Agreement framework and is investing in lower-emission businesses including carbon capture, hydrogen, and lithium production.
