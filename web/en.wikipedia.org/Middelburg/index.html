Middelburg

Middelburg is a city and municipality in the south-western Netherlands, serving as the capital of the province of Zeeland. It is situated on the Walcheren peninsula, near the North Sea coast. The city has a population of roughly 49,000 and is known for its well-preserved historic centre, its abbey complex, and its role in the Dutch Golden Age as a major trading port.

History

Middelburg began as one of three circular defensive burgs built on Walcheren around the late 9th century, probably against Viking raids — its name means "middle fort". It received city rights in 1217. In the 17th century it became one of the most important chamber cities of the Dutch East India Company (VOC) and a significant centre of the Dutch West India Company, shipbuilding and the slave trade; for a time it rivalled Amsterdam in trade. The city was heavily bombed in May 1940, and much of the centre was rebuilt in traditional style after the Second World War. Inundation of Walcheren in 1944 also caused great damage.

Sights

  • The late-Gothic town hall (Stadhuis), one of the finest in the Netherlands
  • The abbey complex (Onze-Lieve-Vrouwe Abdij) with the 90.5 m Lange Jan tower
  • The Oostkerk, a domed Baroque church of 1667
  • The Zeeuws Museum, housed in the abbey

Education

Middelburg hosts University College Roosevelt, a liberal arts college affiliated with Utrecht University, founded in 2004.
