Zero copula

Zero copula is a linguistic phenomenon in which the copula — the verb "to be" or its equivalent — is omitted where a language would normally require it. It is a regular feature of many languages, including Russian, Arabic, Hebrew, Hungarian, Turkish, and many creole languages.

Examples

In Russian, the present-tense copula is normally absent: a sentence literally structured "he doctor" means "he is a doctor". In Arabic, nominal sentences in the present tense likewise lack a copula, though a form of the verb kāna appears in past and future tenses. Hungarian omits the copula in third-person present-tense nominal and adjectival sentences but retains it elsewhere.

In English varieties

African American Vernacular English permits copula deletion in many contexts where contraction is possible in standard English ("he tall" alongside "he's tall"), a pattern analysed by William Labov as rule-governed rather than careless speech.
