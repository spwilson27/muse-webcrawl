Oriental Orthodox Churches

The rock churches of Lalibela, Ethiopia — heartland of the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church.

The Oriental Orthodox Churches are a communion of Eastern Christian churches that recognise the first three ecumenical councils — Nicaea (325), Constantinople (381), and Ephesus (431) — but reject the Christological definition of the Council of Chalcedon (451). They are distinct from the Eastern Orthodox Church, with which they are not in full communion.

Member churches

The communion comprises six autocephalous churches: the Coptic Orthodox Church of Alexandria, the Syriac Orthodox Church of Antioch, the Armenian Apostolic Church, the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, the Eritrean Orthodox Tewahedo Church, and the Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church of India. Together they count tens of millions of adherents, concentrated in Egypt, Syria, Armenia, Ethiopia, Eritrea, and India, with large diasporas worldwide.

Theology and history

The division arose from disagreement over how to express the union of divinity and humanity in Christ. Oriental Orthodox theology follows Cyril of Alexandria's formula "one incarnate nature of God the Word" (miaphysitism), which its adherents insist is not the monophysitism condemned at Chalcedon. Centuries of separation were deepened by political conflict within and beyond the Byzantine Empire. Modern ecumenical dialogue, notably in the later twentieth century, produced agreed statements recognising that the historic dispute was substantially terminological, though full communion has not been restored.
