Medieval literature

Medieval literature is a broad term for literature written in the Middle Ages, roughly from the fall of the Western Roman Empire in the 5th century to the late 15th century. It encompasses works in Latin and in the vernacular languages of Europe and beyond.

European literature

Early medieval writing was dominated by religious works in Latin: hagiography, hymns, biblical commentary and chronicles by authors such as Bede and Gregory of Tours. Heroic epics, including Beowulf, the Chanson de Roland and the Nibelungenlied, preserve heroic oral traditions. From the 12th century, courtly romance flourished with writers such as Chrétien de Troyes and the troubadours, exploring themes of chivalry and courtly love.

Later Middle Ages

The later medieval period produced Dante Alighieri's Divine Comedy, Geoffrey Chaucer's Canterbury Tales, the Arthurian prose cycles, mystery and morality plays, and writers such as Christine de Pizan and Julian of Norwich. Medieval literature also includes the Icelandic sagas, Welsh and Irish tales, Byzantine works, and the rich traditions of Arabic, Persian, Hebrew and other literatures of the period.
