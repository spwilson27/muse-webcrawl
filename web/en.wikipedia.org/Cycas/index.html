Cycas

FamilyCycadaceae
Species~115 accepted
RangeOld World tropics and subtropics

Cycas is a genus of cycads, the only genus in the family Cycadaceae. About 115 species are accepted, distributed across Asia, Australia, Africa, and Pacific islands. The best-known species is Cycas revoluta, the sago palm, widely grown as an ornamental.

Description

Plants typically have a stout, unbranched trunk crowned with a rosette of large, pinnate, palm-like leaves. Cycas are dioecious: male plants produce pollen cones, while females bear loose clusters of megasporophylls rather than true cones — a feature distinguishing Cycas from other cycad genera. The seeds are large and often brightly coloured.

Uses and toxicity

Starch extracted from the stems and seeds of some species is eaten as sago in parts of Asia and the Pacific, but the tissues contain toxins including cycasin and the neurotoxic amino acid BMAA, requiring careful processing to make them safe. Many species are threatened by habitat loss and collecting.
