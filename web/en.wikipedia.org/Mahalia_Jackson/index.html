Mahalia Jackson (October 26, 1911 – January 27, 1972) was an American gospel singer, widely considered one of the most influential vocalists of the 20th century. With a career spanning 40 years, Jackson was integral to the development and spread of gospel blues in Black churches throughout the United States, and she became known as the "Queen of Gospel."

Mahalia Jackson performing.

Career

Born into a poor family in New Orleans, Jackson moved to Chicago in 1927, where she sang in church choirs and with the Johnson Gospel Singers before a solo career. Her 1947 recording of "Move On Up a Little Higher" sold millions of copies and made her internationally famous. Signed to Columbia Records in 1954, she appeared at Carnegie Hall, at the Newport Jazz Festival, and on national television, bringing gospel music to secular audiences while refusing throughout her life to sing secular songs. Her powerful contralto voice influenced singers from Aretha Franklin onward.

Civil rights

Jackson was a prominent supporter of the civil rights movement, a friend and confidante of Martin Luther King Jr., and performed at the 1963 March on Washington, where she sang "I've Been 'Buked and I've Been Scorned"; it was Jackson who called out "Tell them about the dream, Martin!" during King's address, prompting the improvised "I Have a Dream" passage. She also sang at the inauguration of John F. Kennedy and at King's funeral in 1968.
