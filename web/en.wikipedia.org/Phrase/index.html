Phrase

In grammar, a phrase is a group of words that functions as a constituent in the syntax of a sentence, headed by a word of a particular category. Common types include noun phrases, verb phrases, adjective phrases, adverb phrases and prepositional phrases.

Structure

In phrase structure grammar, a phrase is built around a head: a noun phrase is headed by a noun, a prepositional phrase by a preposition, and so on. The head may be accompanied by determiners, modifiers, complements and specifiers. For example, in "the very old house by the river", the noun phrase is headed by "house" and includes a determiner, adjectives and a prepositional phrase.

Phrase versus clause

A phrase is distinguished from a clause in that a clause contains a subject and a predicate (typically a finite verb), whereas a traditional phrase does not. Modern linguistics also recognises phrases built on non-finite or verbless structures, and some frameworks treat all major constituents, including clauses, as phrases of a special kind (complementiser phrases, tense phrases).

Everyday usage

In common usage, "phrase" can also mean any short, fixed expression or idiom, as in "turn of phrase".
