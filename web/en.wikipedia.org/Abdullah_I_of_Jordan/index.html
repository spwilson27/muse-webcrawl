Abdullah I of Jordan

Emir of Transjordan1921–1946
Born1882, Mecca

Abdullah I (1882–1951) was the ruler of Jordan and its predecessor state, Transjordan, from 1921 until his assassination in 1951. A son of Hussein bin Ali, Sharif of Mecca, he played a leading role in the Arab Revolt against the Ottoman Empire during the First World War.

Reign

After his brother Faisal was expelled from Syria by the French in 1920, Abdullah moved into Transjordan, and Britain recognized him as Emir under its mandate in 1921. Transjordan gained independence in 1946 and Abdullah became the first king of the Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan. In the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, Jordan's Arab Legion occupied the West Bank and East Jerusalem, which Abdullah formally annexed in 1950, a move recognized by few other states.

Assassination

Abdullah was assassinated on 20 July 1951 in Jerusalem while attending Friday prayers at the al-Aqsa Mosque, by a Palestinian gunman opposed to his policies and his reported contacts with Israel. He was succeeded by his son Talal, and the following year by his grandson Hussein.
