Cassiopeia A

Cassiopeia A imaged by the Hubble, Spitzer, and Chandra observatories

Cassiopeia A (Cas A) is a supernova remnant in the constellation Cassiopeia and the brightest extrasolar radio source in the sky at frequencies above 1 GHz. The supernova that created it occurred about 11,000 light-years away in the Milky Way; the expanding shell of debris is observed to be about 10 light-years across. The light of the explosion is thought to have first reached Earth around 1690, yet no clearly confirmed historical sighting was recorded, suggesting the explosion was unusually faint in visible light, possibly dimmed by interstellar dust or by an asymmetric, largely invisible outflow.

Discovery and observations

Cas A was discovered in 1947–1948 as a strong radio source, one of the first identified by early radio astronomy. The optical remnant, a field of fast-moving knots of ejecta, was identified shortly after. Cas A is the youngest known supernova remnant in the Milky Way and one of the most intensively studied objects in astronomy; the Chandra X-ray Observatory's "first light" image was of Cas A, revealing a point source — a neutron star — near its centre, the first central compact object found at the site of a supernova remnant.

Spectroscopic analysis shows the progenitor was likely a red supergiant that had lost most of its hydrogen envelope before exploding as a Type IIb supernova. Light echoes from the explosion have been observed reflecting off interstellar dust clouds, allowing astronomers to examine the original outburst spectroscopically centuries later.
