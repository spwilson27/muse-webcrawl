The Second Sino-Japanese War (1937–1945) was fought between the Republic of China and the Empire of Japan. It began with the Marco Polo Bridge Incident of 7 July 1937 and escalated into a full-scale war that became the largest in Asia in the twentieth century; after 1941 it formed the Chinese theatre of the Second World War.

Course of the war

Japanese forces rapidly seized Beiping, Shanghai (after a three-month battle) and the Chinese capital Nanjing, where the ensuing massacre of civilians became one of the war's defining atrocities. The Nationalist government under Chiang Kai-shek withdrew up the Yangtze to Chongqing, trading space for time, while Communist forces waged guerrilla warfare behind Japanese lines. China tied down large Japanese armies but suffered enormous losses; the deliberate breaching of the Yellow River dikes in 1938 and the scorched-earth campaigns compounded the civilian catastrophe.

Outcome

Following Japan's surrender in September 1945, China recovered Manchuria, Taiwan and all occupied territory and emerged as one of the Allied victors, taking a permanent seat on the new United Nations Security Council. The war's devastation, and the Communists' expansion during it, set the stage for the resumption of the Chinese Civil War.
