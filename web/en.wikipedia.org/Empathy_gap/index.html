Empathy gap

The empathy gap, sometimes called the hot–cold empathy gap, is a cognitive bias in which people underestimate the influence of visceral states — such as hunger, pain, anger, sexual arousal or craving — on their own behaviour and preferences, and fail to anticipate how they will feel and act when in a different state.

The concept was developed by psychologist George Loewenstein. In a "cold" state, people mispredict how strongly a "hot" state will affect them: a satiated person underestimates how tempting food will be when hungry; addicts in a calm state underestimate the power of future cravings; and people in pain have difficulty empathising with their pain-free future selves.

The gap also operates interpersonally: people in a hot state have trouble adopting the perspective of those in a cold state, and vice versa. The bias has implications for self-control, medical decision-making, negotiation and the prediction of one's own future behaviour.
