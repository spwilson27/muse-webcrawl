Skype

Skype was a telecommunications application that provided voice and video calling, instant messaging, and file sharing over the Internet. Launched in 2003, it pioneered consumer voice over IP (VoIP) and at its peak had hundreds of millions of users. Microsoft retired the service on May 5, 2025, directing users to Microsoft Teams.

History

Skype was created by Niklas Zennström and Janus Friis, with software written by Estonian developers Ahti Heinla, Priit Kasesalu, and Jaan Tallinn — the team behind the file-sharing program Kazaa. It used a peer-to-peer architecture with “supernodes.” eBay acquired Skype in 2005 for $2.6 billion; after selling most of its stake, Microsoft acquired Skype in 2011 for $8.5 billion, its largest purchase at the time, and folded it into its product line, replacing Windows Live Messenger.

Features and decline

Skype offered free Skype-to-Skype calls, group video chat, screen sharing, and paid calls to landlines and mobiles (SkypeOut). Its name became a verb for video calling. In the 2010s it lost ground to mobile-first rivals such as WhatsApp, FaceTime, and Zoom, and Microsoft prioritized Teams, especially during the remote-work boom of 2020. Skype for Business was retired in 2021, and the consumer service followed in 2025.
