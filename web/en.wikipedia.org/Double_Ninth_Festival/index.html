Double Ninth Festival

Chrysanthemums are drunk as tea and worn during the festival.

The Double Ninth Festival (Chinese: 重Ɓ阳节; pinyin: Chóngyángjié) is a traditional Chinese holiday observed on the ninth day of the ninth month of the Chinese calendar — "double nine". In Chinese numerology nine is the largest single-digit yang number, so the doubled date is considered especially auspicious but also potentially dangerous, an excess of yang. It is also called the Chrysanthemum Festival and, in modern China, Seniors' Day.

Origins

The festival is associated with a legend recorded in the Han dynasty: Huan Jing, warned by his teacher Fei Changfang of a disaster on the ninth day of the ninth month, led his family to high ground carrying dogwood and drinking chrysanthemum wine; on returning they found their animals dead in their place. Climbing mountains (denggao) on the day remains the central custom.

Customs

Families climb hills or mountains, wear sprigs of dogwood (zhuyu), drink chrysanthemum wine or tea, eat double-ninth cakes, and visit ancestral graves to clean and repair them — a custom especially prominent in Hong Kong, where the day is a public holiday and thousands climb the hills. Since 1989 the People's Republic of China has also designated the day as a festival honouring the elderly.

Elsewhere in East Asia

The festival is observed in Japan as Chōyō, one of the five seasonal festivals (gosekku), and in Korea as Junggu, where chrysanthemum wine and mountain climbing are likewise customary.
