Margarita

A margarita is a cocktail consisting of tequila, orange liqueur (such as triple sec or Cointreau) and lime juice, often served with salt on the rim of the glass. It is shaken with ice, blended with ice (a "frozen margarita"), or served straight up without ice. The drink is generally served in a margarita glass, a stepped-diameter variant of a cocktail glass or champagne coupe.

Origins

The margarita's origin is disputed, with several claims dating from the 1930s and 1940s in Mexico and the US border region. One story credits Carlos "Danny" Herrera of Tijuana in 1938; another attributes it to Dallas socialite Margarita Sames, who said she created it in Acapulco in 1948. The drink is closely related to the earlier Prohibition-era "daisy" (margarita is Spanish for "daisy") and to the tequila daisy in particular. Frozen margaritas were popularised after Dallas restaurateur Mariano Martinez adapted a soft-serve ice cream machine into the first frozen margarita machine in 1971; that machine is now in the Smithsonian Institution.

Variations

A standard ratio is often given as 2:1:1 — two parts tequila, one part orange liqueur, one part lime juice. Variations substitute mezcal, add flavours such as mango, strawberry or tamarind, or replace the liqueur with agave syrup (a "Tommy's margarita", created in San Francisco). The margarita is one of the most popular cocktails in the United States, and National Margarita Day is observed on 22 February.
