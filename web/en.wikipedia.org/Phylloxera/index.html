Phylloxera

Phylloxera (Daktulosphaira vitifoliae, commonly known as the grape phylloxera) is a tiny aphid-like insect, native to eastern North America, that feeds on the roots and leaves of grapevines. It is infamous for the devastation it caused to European vineyards in the late 19th century.

The phylloxera crisis

The pest was accidentally introduced into Europe in the 1860s, likely on imported American vine cuttings. European Vitis vinifera vines had no resistance, and the insect spread across the continent, destroying a large majority of vineyards in France and elsewhere — an event often called the Great Wine Blight. The crisis was eventually resolved by grafting European scions onto resistant American rootstocks (Vitis riparia, V. rupestris, V. berlandieri hybrids), a practice still universal in most wine regions.

Biology

Phylloxera has a complex life cycle with root-feeding (radicicola) and leaf-galling (gallicola) forms. Root feeding produces galls and secondary infections that kill the vine. American grape species coevolved with the insect and tolerate it; sandy soils also impede its spread, and a few ungrafted vineyards survive in such soils.
