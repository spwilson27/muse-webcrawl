East African Rift

The East African Rift (EAR) is an active continental rift zone in East Africa. The rift developed around 25–30 million years ago as the African Plate began splitting into the Somali Plate and the Nubian Plate, and it is a rare on-land example of a continent in the early stages of breakup.

Structure

The rift extends from the Afar Triple Junction, where it meets the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden rifts, southward through Ethiopia, Kenya and Tanzania. The Eastern Rift is volcanically active and relatively arid; the Western Rift contains the deep African Great Lakes, including Tanganyika and Malawi, and the snow-capped Rwenzori Mountains.

Volcanism and lakes

Rift volcanism produced Kilimanjaro, Mount Kenya and the active volcanoes Erta Ale and Ol Doinyo Lengai. Fault-bounded basins host soda lakes such as Nakuru and Natron, famous for flamingo flocks, alongside freshwater lakes such as Turkana and Victoria (which sits between the two branches).

Palaeoanthropology

Rift sediments preserve an unmatched record of human evolution, from Sahelanthropus and Australopithecus to early Homo; sites include Olduvai Gorge, Koobi Fora and the Afar deposits.
