Crime prevention through environmental design

Crime prevention through environmental design (CPTED) is a multidisciplinary approach to deterring crime by designing the physical environment to influence offender decisions. The term was coined by criminologist C. Ray Jeffery in 1971 and was popularized through Oscar Newman's concept of "defensible space" (1972).

Principles

Core principles include natural surveillance (designing spaces so occupants can observe their surroundings), natural access control (using physical elements to guide movement and deny access to targets), territorial reinforcement (features that express ownership and discourage intrusion), and maintenance (consistent with the broken windows theory, kept-up spaces signal oversight).

Application

CPTED is applied in urban planning, architecture, and facility management, influencing the design of housing developments, schools, parking facilities, and public transport. Second-generation CPTED adds social cohesion and community culture to the physical strategies.
