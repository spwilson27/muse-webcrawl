Anomalous X-ray pulsar

Anomalous X-ray pulsars (AXPs) are a class of X-ray pulsars whose luminosity in X-rays far exceeds the power available from the loss of rotational kinetic energy, and which show no evidence of a binary companion. Their periods are clustered in the range of about 2 to 12 seconds, and their spin-down is steady and rapid compared with ordinary rotation-powered pulsars.

Magnetar model

The most widely accepted explanation is that AXPs are magnetars: isolated neutron stars with extremely powerful magnetic fields, on the order of 1014–1015 gauss, whose X-ray emission is powered by the decay of the magnetic field. AXPs are closely related to soft gamma repeaters (SGRs), and several sources have shown behaviour of both classes, supporting a unified magnetar picture.

Properties

AXPs show persistent X-ray emission punctuated by bursts and occasional large outbursts, after which the flux decays over months. Their spectra are typically described by a thermal blackbody component from the neutron star surface plus a hard power-law tail. Long-term timing shows significant timing noise and occasional glitches.
