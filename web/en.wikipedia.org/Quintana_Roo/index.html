Quintana Roo

Quintana Roo
Country: Mexico
Capital: Chetumal
Largest city: Cancún
Statehood: 8 October 1974
Named for: Andrés Quintana Roo

Quintana Roo is one of the 31 states of Mexico, located on the eastern part of the Yucatán Peninsula, bordering the Caribbean Sea. Its capital is Chetumal, while its largest and best-known city is Cancún. The state is Mexico's foremost tourist destination, home to the resort corridor known as the Riviera Maya, including Playa del Carmen and Tulum.

History

The territory was named after Andrés Quintana Roo, a patriot of Mexican independence. In the pre-Hispanic era the region was part of the Maya world, and it preserves important archaeological sites including Tulum, Cobá and Kohunlich. During the colonial period and the nineteenth century the area was sparsely administered from Yucatán; Maya resistance in the Caste War made much of the interior effectively independent for decades.

President Porfirio Díaz created the federal territory of Quintana Roo in 1902. It was admitted as a state of the federation on 8 October 1974, largely in anticipation of the tourism development that followed the founding of Cancún as a planned resort in 1970.

Geography and tourism

The state is low and flat, covered in tropical forest, with a coastline of white-sand beaches and the Mesoamerican Barrier Reef offshore. Its economy is dominated by tourism; other sites include the island of Cozumel, the Sian Ka'an biosphere reserve and the cenotes (natural limestone sinkholes) of the interior.
