Arsenic acid

Arsenic acid, or arsoric acid, is the chemical compound with the formula H3AsO4. It is a colourless, highly toxic acid, analogous to phosphoric acid, in which arsenic is in the +5 oxidation state. Its salts are called arsenates. Arsenic acid is usually handled as aqueous solutions; the crystalline hemihydrate H3AsO4·½H2O can be obtained.

Properties and preparation

Arsenic acid is a triprotic acid of moderate strength, slightly weaker than phosphoric acid. It can be prepared by treating arsenic trioxide with concentrated nitric acid, or by dissolving arsenic pentoxide in water (a slow process). It is a mild oxidizing agent, reduced to arsenous acid by sulfur dioxide.

Uses

Arsenic acid was historically used in wood preservatives — notably in chromated copper arsenate (CCA) — and in herbicides, insecticides and desiccants, though these uses have been sharply curtailed because of toxicity and environmental persistence. Arsenates interfere with phosphate metabolism, which underlies much of their biocidal action and human toxicity; see arsenic poisoning.
