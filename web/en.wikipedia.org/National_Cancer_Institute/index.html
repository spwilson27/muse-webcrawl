National Cancer Institute

Formed: 6 August 1937

Headquarters: Bethesda, Maryland, U.S.

Parent agency: National Institutes of Health (NIH)

Abbreviation: NCI

The National Cancer Institute (NCI) is the United States federal government's principal agency for cancer research and training. It is one of the 27 institutes and centres of the National Institutes of Health (NIH), which is part of the Department of Health and Human Services. The NCI coordinates the National Cancer Program, which conducts and supports research, training, health information dissemination, and other programmes related to the cause, diagnosis, prevention and treatment of cancer, rehabilitation from cancer, and the continuing care of cancer patients and their families.

History

The NCI was established by the National Cancer Institute Act of 1937, signed by President Franklin D. Roosevelt. Its role was greatly expanded by the National Cancer Act of 1971, which launched what President Richard Nixon called the "war on cancer," giving the institute special budgetary authority and the ability to appoint its director by the President. The NCI's main campus is in Bethesda, Maryland, with major facilities at the Frederick National Laboratory for Cancer Research in Frederick, Maryland.

Research and programmes

The NCI conducts intramural research at its Center for Cancer Research and funds extramural research at universities and medical centres across the United States and abroad. It supports a network of NCI-designated cancer centres, runs clinical trials through the National Clinical Trials Network, maintains the PDQ database of cancer information summaries, and publishes the Journal of the National Cancer Institute. Its annual budget is over US$7 billion, making it one of the largest funders of cancer research in the world.
