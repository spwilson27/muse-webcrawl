Pillai conjecture

The Pillai conjecture is an unsolved problem in number theory proposed by the Indian mathematician S. S. Pillai. It is a far-reaching generalisation of Catalan's conjecture (Mihăilescu's theorem) concerning the differences between perfect powers.

Pillai conjecture
Field: Number theory
Proposed by: S. S. Pillai
Related to: Catalan's conjecture, abc conjecture

Statement

Pillai conjectured that for any positive integer k, there are only finitely many pairs of perfect powers (numbers of the form am with m ≥ 2) whose difference equals k. Equivalently, the gaps between consecutive perfect powers grow without bound in an appropriate sense, and the set of perfect powers has density zero relative to such fixed differences.

Relation to other problems

The case k = 1 is exactly Catalan's conjecture, proved by Preda Mihăilescu in 2002: the only consecutive perfect powers are 8 and 9. The general conjecture would follow from the abc conjecture, but remains open in full generality. Erdős conjectured related results on the number of representations of integers as differences of powers.

Examples

Small differences occur occasionally — for example 3² − 2³ = 1, 25 − 16 = 9, and 27 − 25 = 2 — but Pillai's conjecture asserts that for each fixed difference such coincidences eventually stop.
