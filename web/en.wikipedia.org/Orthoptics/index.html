Orthoptics

Orthoptics is a profession allied to medicine relating to the diagnosis and management of disorders of eye movements and binocular vision. Orthoptists are health professionals who specialise in problems such as strabismus (squint), amblyopia (lazy eye), and diplopia (double vision), working mainly alongside ophthalmologists in hospital eye services. The name derives from the Greek orthos (straight) and optikos (relating to sight)—literally, "straightening the eyes".

Practice

Orthoptists assess visual acuity in children and adults, measure the angle of squint using prism cover tests, evaluate binocular single vision and stereopsis, and investigate palsies of the cranial nerves controlling the extraocular muscles. Treatments include occlusion (patching) therapy for amblyopia, prism glasses, and eye exercises for convergence insufficiency. Orthoptists also play a central role in children's vision screening programmes and in stroke and neurology clinics where eye-movement disorders are common.

History and training

The profession developed in France in the first decades of the 20th century; Mary Maddox, daughter of ophthalmologist Ernest Maddox, established the first orthoptic clinic in London in 1928. Training is now at degree level in countries such as the United Kingdom, Australia, and Canada, and orthoptists are represented internationally by the International Orthoptic Association, founded in 1974.
