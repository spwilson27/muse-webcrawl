2024 Florida Amendment 4

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Florida Amendment 4 was a proposed amendment to the Constitution of Florida that appeared on the November 5, 2024 general election ballot. Titled the Amendment to Limit Government Interference with Abortion, it would have provided that no law shall prohibit, penalize, delay, or restrict abortion before fetal viability or when necessary to protect the patient's health, as determined by the patient's healthcare provider, while preserving the legislature's authority to require parental notification for minors.

Background

The measure was placed on the ballot by citizen initiative, sponsored by Floridians Protecting Freedom, after the Florida Supreme Court in April 2024 upheld the state's 15-week abortion ban and allowed a six-week ban (signed in 2023) to take effect, while also approving Amendment 4's ballot language. The six-week law, with limited exceptions, had sharply restricted abortion access in the Southeast, where Florida had previously been a destination for patients from neighbouring states.

Campaign

The campaign was among the most expensive in the 2024 cycle. Supporters framed the amendment as ending government interference in private medical decisions; opponents, including Governor Ron DeSantis and state agencies, argued the language was dangerously vague and campaigned against it with state resources, a strategy that drew legal challenges and national controversy.

Result

Amendment 4 received about 57 per cent of the vote — a clear majority, but short of the 60 per cent supermajority Florida requires to amend its constitution — and therefore failed. Florida and Nebraska (which passed a competing restrictive measure the same day) were the first states where abortion-rights ballot measures failed after the U.S. Supreme Court's 2022 Dobbs decision overturned Roe v. Wade; measures in seven other states passed in 2024.
