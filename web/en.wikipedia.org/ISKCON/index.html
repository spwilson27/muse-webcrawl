ISKCON

The International Society for Krishna Consciousness (ISKCON), known colloquially as the Hare Krishna movement, is a Gaudiya Vaishnava religious organisation. It was founded in 1966 in New York City by A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada, who arrived in the United States from India in 1965 at the age of 69 and began teaching bhakti-yoga centred on devotion to Krishna.

Beliefs and practices

ISKCON's core beliefs include the chanting of the Hare Krishna maha-mantra, study of scriptures such as the Bhagavad Gita and Srimad Bhagavatam, deity worship (puja), and adherence to four regulative principles: no meat-eating, no intoxication, no gambling and no illicit sex. Its theology follows the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition descending from the 16th-century saint Chaitanya Mahaprabhu.

Organisation and growth

Since its founding, the movement has grown to a worldwide confederation of more than 550 centres, including temples, rural communities, schools and restaurants. Major centres include the Krishna Balaram Mandir in Vrindavan, the temple at Mayapur in West Bengal, and Bhaktivedanta Manor in England. The organisation is governed by the Governing Body Commission (GBC), established by its founder in 1970.

Activities

ISKCON is known for its Ratha Yatra chariot festivals, book distribution, vegetarian restaurants (Govinda's), and charitable initiatives such as Food for Life, which distributes sanctified vegetarian meals. The movement attracted notable public interest in the 1960s and 1970s, including from members of the Beatles — George Harrison donated Bhaktivedanta Manor and recorded with devotees.
