Grand Duchy of Luxembourg

The Grand Duchy of Luxembourg is the formal name of Luxembourg, a landlocked country in Western Europe bordered by Belgium, France, and Germany. It is the world's only remaining grand duchy — a state headed by a grand duke. With an area of 2,586 km² and a population of about 670,000, it is one of Europe's smallest sovereign states, yet it has one of the highest GDPs per capita in the world.

Official name: Grand Duchy of Luxembourg

Capital: Luxembourg City

Head of state: Grand Duke (Henri until 2025; Guillaume from October 2025)

Government: Unitary parliamentary constitutional monarchy

Official languages: Luxembourgish, French, German

Currency: Euro (€)

History

Luxembourg began as a castle founded in 963 by Count Siegfried. It became a duchy in 1354 and, after centuries under Burgundian, Spanish, French, and Austrian rule, was raised to a grand duchy at the Congress of Vienna in 1815 and given to the King of the Netherlands in personal union. The Treaty of London (1839) partitioned it, granting the western part to Belgium; the remaining grand duchy became fully independent in 1890 when the personal union with the Netherlands ended. Occupied by Germany in both world wars, Luxembourg after 1945 became a founding member of the Benelux union, the European Coal and Steel Community, NATO, and the European Union.

Politics and economy

The Grand Duke is head of state; executive power rests with the prime minister and cabinet, accountable to the unicameral Chamber of Deputies. Luxembourg is a major financial centre, the world's second-largest investment fund centre after the United States, and hosts EU institutions including the Court of Justice of the European Union. Steel (ArcelorMittal has its headquarters there) was historically dominant; finance, technology, and satellite services (SES) now lead.

Society

Luxembourg is trilingual, with Luxembourgish as the national language, and has one of the highest proportions of foreign residents (nearly half the population) and cross-border workers in Europe. Grand Duke Henri abdicated in October 2025 in favour of his son, Grand Duke Guillaume.
