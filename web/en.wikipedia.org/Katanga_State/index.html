The State of Katanga was a breakaway state that proclaimed its independence from Congo-Léopoldville on 11 July 1960 and existed until its forcible integration into the Congolese state in January 1963. Under Moïse Tshombe, leader of the CONAKAT party, the mineral-rich southern province seceded eleven days after Congolese independence, with the support of Belgian mining interests, notably the Union Minière du Haut-Katanga, and Belgian officers who led its gendarmerie.

Congo Crisis

The secession was a central episode of the Congo Crisis. Katanga's copper and cobalt revenues made it economically vital, and its government was sustained by foreign mercenaries and Belgian technical advisers despite being recognised by no other state. The United Nations Operation in the Congo (ONUC) was mandated to end the secession; fighting between UN forces (including Irish and Swedish troops, and the besieged Irish company at Jadotville) and Katangese forces escalated through 1961. UN Secretary-General Dag Hammarskjöld died in a plane crash at Ndola in September 1961 while travelling to negotiate a ceasefire.

End

In December 1962 UN forces launched Operation Grandslam, capturing Élisabethville; Tshombe fled abroad and formally ended the secession on 21 January 1963. Remarkably, Tshombe returned in 1964 as prime minister of the whole Congo.
