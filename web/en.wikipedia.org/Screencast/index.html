Screencast

A screencast is a digital recording of a computer screen, often accompanied by audio narration. Screencasts are widely used for software demonstrations, tutorials, training, bug reports and online lectures. The term was popularised in 2004 by columnist Jon Udell, who invited suggestions for a name for the genre.

Uses

Educators use screencasts to create flipped-classroom lessons; software companies use them for product walkthroughs and customer support; developers use them to demonstrate bugs. Screencasting is closely related to the screenshot (a still image of a screen) and to live streaming, in which screen content is broadcast in real time, as in video-game streaming.

Software

Common screencasting tools have included Camtasia, OBS Studio, QuickTime Player, ScreenFlow and built-in game-bar recorders in operating systems. Recordings are typically encoded in standard video formats such as MP4 and distributed via video-sharing platforms.
