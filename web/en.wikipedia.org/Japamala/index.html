Japamala

A japamala (Sanskrit: जपमाला, from japa, "repetition [of a mantra]," and mālā, "garland") is a string of prayer beads used in Hinduism, Jainism, Sikhism and Buddhism to keep count while reciting, chanting or mentally repeating a mantra or the name of a deity — a practice known as japa.

Form

A full mala traditionally has 108 beads, plus a larger "guru bead" (meru) marking the start and finish. The number 108 is considered sacred in Dharmic traditions, and shorter malas of 54 or 27 beads are also used. Beads may be made of tulsi (holy basil) wood, favoured by Vaishnavas; rudraksha seeds, favoured by Shaivites; sandalwood; lotus seeds; crystal; or gemstones. The beads are counted with the thumb and middle finger of the right hand, traditionally avoiding the index finger; on reaching the guru bead the practitioner reverses direction rather than crossing it.

Use across traditions

Jains use malas in counting prayers such as the Namokar Mantra; Sikhs recite simran with them; and Buddhist versions of the mala — often of bodhi seed or sandalwood — travelled with Buddhism across Asia, becoming the Chinese shu zhu and Japanese juzu. The mala is also worn as a necklace or wrist wrap when not in use.
