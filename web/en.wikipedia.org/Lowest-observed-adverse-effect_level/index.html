Lowest-observed-adverse-effect level

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The lowest-observed-adverse-effect level (LOAEL), or lowest-observed-adverse-effect concentration (LOAEC), is the lowest dose (or exposure concentration) of a substance at which a statistically or biologically significant adverse effect is observed in a study population, relative to a control group. It is a standard output of toxicological dose–response studies and a key input to regulatory risk assessment.

Relationship to NOAEL

The LOAEL is the complement of the no-observed-adverse-effect level (NOAEL), the highest dose at which no adverse effect is found. In a typical dose-ranging experiment, tested doses that produce no significant adverse effect lie at or below the NOAEL, and doses that do produce such effects lie at or above the LOAEL; the true threshold of toxicity is presumed to lie between them. Both values are constrained by the doses actually tested: neither is a directly measured biological threshold.

Use in risk assessment

Regulators use the NOAEL — or, when no NOAEL has been established, the LOAEL — as a point of departure for deriving health-based guidance values such as the acceptable daily intake (ADI), tolerable daily intake (TDI) or reference dose (RfD). Uncertainty (safety) factors are applied to account for extrapolation from animals to humans, variation within human populations, use of a LOAEL instead of a NOAEL (commonly an extra factor of up to 10), and study duration. Agencies that use LOAELs include the United States Environmental Protection Agency, the Food and Drug Administration, and the European Chemicals Agency and European Food Safety Authority.

Limitations

Because the LOAEL depends on dose spacing, group size and statistical power, a poorly designed study can paradoxically yield a higher (apparently "safer") LOAEL. These shortcomings have motivated the benchmark dose (BMD) approach, which fits models to the full dose–response curve and derives a lower confidence limit (BMDL) as an alternative point of departure, largely independent of the particular doses chosen.
