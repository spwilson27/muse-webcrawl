Acute stress disorder

Acute stress disorder (ASD) is a psychological response that develops in some individuals after experiencing or witnessing a traumatic event. Symptoms last from three days to one month following the event and include intrusion symptoms (flashbacks, distressing dreams), negative mood, dissociative symptoms, avoidance, and arousal symptoms such as hypervigilance and sleep disturbance.

Diagnosis

The DSM-5 requires exposure to actual or threatened death, serious injury, or sexual violence, plus nine or more symptoms across five categories, causing significant distress or impairment. If symptoms persist beyond one month, the diagnosis may instead become post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD). A substantial proportion of people with ASD later develop PTSD, though PTSD can also arise without preceding ASD.

Treatment

Trauma-focused cognitive behavioral therapy delivered in the weeks after trauma is the best-supported early treatment and reduces progression to chronic PTSD. Psychological debriefing in a single session is not recommended. Medication is generally reserved for specific symptoms.

History

The diagnosis was introduced in the DSM-IV in 1994 to identify, in the immediate aftermath of trauma, individuals at risk of developing PTSD.
