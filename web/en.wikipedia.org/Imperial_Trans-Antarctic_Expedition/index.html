Imperial Trans-Antarctic Expedition

The Imperial Trans-Antarctic Expedition (1914–1917) was an attempt led by Sir Ernest Shackleton to make the first land crossing of the Antarctic continent. The expedition is remembered as an epic of survival: after its ship, the Endurance, was crushed by ice in the Weddell Sea, Shackleton and his crew endured nearly two years in the Antarctic — and every member of the Weddell Sea party survived.

Plan

Shackleton intended to sail to the Weddell Sea, march from Vahsel Bay to the South Pole, and continue to the Ross Sea, where a second party aboard the Aurora would lay supply depots. The crossing was never begun.

Loss of the Endurance

Endurance became beset in January 1915 and drifted with the ice for months before being crushed and sinking on November 21, 1915. The crew camped on the ice floes until they broke up, then took to the lifeboats, reaching Elephant Island in April 1916. Shackleton and five companions then made the celebrated 800-nautical-mile open-boat voyage in the James Caird to South Georgia, crossed its uncharted interior on foot, and organized a rescue. The Elephant Island party was recovered on August 30, 1916, all alive.

Ross Sea party

The Ross Sea party, tasked with laying depots for a crossing that never happened, suffered three deaths but completed its mission. The wreck of the Endurance was located by the Endurance22 expedition in March 2022, remarkably preserved at a depth of about 3,000 metres.
