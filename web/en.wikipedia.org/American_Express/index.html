American Express

American Express Company (Amex) is an American multinational financial services corporation headquartered at 200 Vesey Street in New York City. Founded in 1850 as an express mail business in Buffalo, New York, it is one of the 30 components of the Dow Jones Industrial Average and is best known for its charge card, credit card, and traveller's cheque businesses.

History

American Express began as an express shipment company formed by the merger of firms owned by Henry Wells, William Fargo, and John Warren Butterfield. It introduced money orders in 1882 and traveller's cheques in 1891, which became a mainstay of international travel. The company issued its first charge card in 1958 and the prestige Gold Card in 1966, the Platinum Card in 1984, and the invitation-only black Centurion Card in 1999. Its advertising campaigns include "Don't leave home without them" (1975) and "Membership has its privileges" (1987).

Business

Unlike Visa and Mastercard, American Express operates a closed-loop network, acting as both card issuer and payment processor and earning a discount fee from merchants, historically higher than rivals' interchange fees. In return it targets affluent cardholders and corporate travel-and-expense accounts. It is consistently ranked among the world's most valuable brands and a regular presence on Fortune's list of most admired companies.
