MOSE (Italian: Modulo Sperimentale Elettromeccanico, Experimental Electromechanical Module) is a project intended to protect the city of Venice, Italy, and the Venetian Lagoon from flooding. The project is an integrated system consisting of rows of mobile gates installed at the Lido, Malamocco, and Chioggia inlets that are able to temporarily isolate the lagoon from the Adriatic Sea during high tides (acqua alta).

MOSE barriers raised at the Lido inlet during a high-tide event.

Design

The system comprises 78 hollow steel gates hinged to concrete caissons on the seabed. Normally filled with water, the gates lie flat and invisible; when a tide above 110 cm is forecast, compressed air forces the water out, causing the gates to rise and pivot upward, forming a barrier that separates the lagoon from the sea for the duration of the tide.

History

The catastrophic flood of 4 November 1966 prompted decades of study. Construction began in 2003 under the Consorzio Venezia Nuova. The project was delayed by rising costs and a major corruption scandal in 2014. The barriers were raised together for the first time in a test on 10 July 2020, and on 3 October 2020 MOSE successfully protected Venice from a 135 cm tide that would otherwise have flooded much of the city.

Criticism

Critics have raised concerns over the project's cost, maintenance demands in a corrosive marine environment, and effects on the lagoon ecosystem and port traffic.
