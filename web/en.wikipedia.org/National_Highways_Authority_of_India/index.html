National Highways Authority of India

Founded: 1988 (Act); operational 1995

Headquarters: Dwarka, New Delhi, India

Parent: Ministry of Road Transport and Highways

Abbreviation: NHAI

The National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) is an autonomous agency of the Government of India, set up by an Act of Parliament in 1988 and made operational in 1995. It is responsible for the development, maintenance and management of the national highways entrusted to it by the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways. India's national highway network exceeds 146,000 kilometres and, although it forms only about 2 percent of the country's road length, carries roughly 40 percent of its road traffic.

Programmes

The NHAI implemented the National Highways Development Project (NHDP), launched in 1998, whose early phases included the Golden Quadrilateral connecting Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai and Kolkata, and the North–South and East–West corridors. In 2017 the NHDP was subsumed into the Bharatmala Pariyojana programme, which plans tens of thousands of kilometres of new highways, economic corridors and expressways. The authority finances construction through budgetary support, toll revenues, market borrowing and models such as Build–Operate–Transfer (BOT), the Hybrid Annuity Model and Toll–Operate–Transfer asset monetisation.

Technology and tolling

The NHAI rolled out FASTag, an RFID-based electronic toll collection system made mandatory on national highways in 2021, greatly reducing toll plaza congestion. It also maintains the 1033 highway emergency helpline and publishes road condition and project data through its digital platforms.
