Arrest warrant

An arrest warrant is a warrant issued by a judge or magistrate on behalf of the state that authorises the arrest and detention of an individual, or the search and seizure of an individual's property. Arrest warrants are normally issued where there is probable cause to believe the named person has committed an offence, and they are executed by police officers or other law-enforcement agents.

Types

A warrant may be issued for a suspect named in a criminal complaint or indictment, or as a bench warrant when a person fails to appear in court as required. Warrants generally identify the person to be arrested, the offence charged and, in some jurisdictions, the amount of bail. Outstanding warrants are recorded in law-enforcement databases and can lead to arrest during unrelated police encounters. International arrest may be sought through Interpol Red Notices, which request the location and arrest of a person pending extradition, though they are not themselves international warrants.

Legal requirements

In the United States, the Fourth Amendment requires warrants to be supported by probable cause and to particularly describe the person or place concerned; similar protections exist in other common-law and civil-law systems. A person arrested on a warrant is ordinarily entitled to be brought promptly before a court. Warrants that are defective, stale or issued without jurisdiction may be challenged and quashed.
