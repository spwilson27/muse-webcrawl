Kynurenine

L-Kynurenine is an amino acid metabolite of L-tryptophan, produced in the first and rate-limiting step of the kynurenine pathway, the route by which most dietary tryptophan is catabolised in mammals. The enzyme tryptophan 2,3-dioxygenase or indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase opens the indole ring to form N-formylkynurenine, which is converted to kynurenine.

Downstream metabolites

Kynurenine is a branch point: it can be transaminated to kynurenic acid (a neuroprotective antagonist at glutamate receptors), hydroxylated toward 3-hydroxykynurenine and quinolinic acid (an NMDA-receptor agonist), and ultimately used to synthesise NAD+. Dysregulation of the pathway is implicated in inflammation, depression, schizophrenia, and neurodegenerative disease, and in immune tolerance in cancer and pregnancy.
