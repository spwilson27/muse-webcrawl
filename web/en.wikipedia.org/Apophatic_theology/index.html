Apophatic theology

Apophatic theology, also called negative theology or the via negativa, is an approach to theology that attempts to describe God by negation — by speaking of what God is not, rather than what God is. It rests on the conviction that the divine nature transcends human concepts and language, so positive descriptions are inadequate or misleading.

History

Roots of the approach appear in Plato's Parmenides and in the Middle Platonist and Neoplatonist traditions. It entered Christian thought through writers such as Gregory of Nyssa and, most influentially, Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite, whose Mystical Theology describes an ascent into "darkness" beyond affirmation and denial. Apophatic themes run through Eastern Orthodox theology, the medieval mysticism of Meister Eckhart, and The Cloud of Unknowing, as well as in Jewish philosophy (Maimonides) and Islamic thought.

Relation to cataphatic theology

Apophatic theology is traditionally balanced by cataphatic (positive) theology, which makes affirmative statements about God. Many theologians hold the two together: God may be spoken of positively, but all such speech must be qualified by the recognition that God exceeds it.
