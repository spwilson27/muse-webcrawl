Parody film

A parody film or spoof film is a subgenre of comedy film that parodies other film genres or films as pastiches, works created by imitation of the style of many different films reassembled together. Although the subgenre is often overlooked by critics, parody films are commonly profitable at the box office.

Characteristics

Parody films typically mock recognizable conventions, scenes, and characters of their source material, exaggerating them for comic effect. Humour often depends on the audience's familiarity with the works being spoofed, and jokes are frequently dense, rapid-fire, and intertextual.

History

Early examples drew on silent slapstick and the work of comedians such as Buster Keaton and the Marx Brothers. The genre flourished in the 1970s and 1980s with films by Mel Brooks, such as Blazing Saddles and Young Frankenstein, and the Zucker–Abrahams–Zucker team behind Airplane! and the Naked Gun series. Later entries include the Scary Movie and Austin Powers franchises.

Notable examples

Monty Python and the Holy Grail, This Is Spinal Tap, Galaxy Quest, and Hot Fuzz are frequently cited among the most acclaimed parody films.
