Serie B, known as Serie BKT for sponsorship reasons, is the second-highest division in the Italian football league system after Serie A. It has been organised by the Lega Serie B since 2010 (previously by the Lega Calcio). The championship is nicknamed il campionato cadetto ("the cadet championship") and its winner receives the Ali della Vittoria trophy.

Format

Since the 2003–04 season the league has generally consisted of 20 to 24 teams (20 since 2019–20) playing a double round-robin. The top two teams are promoted directly to Serie A, and a third promotion place is decided by play-offs contested by the teams finishing third to eighth (subject to a points-gap rule that can cancel the play-offs). The bottom three teams are relegated directly to Serie C, with a fourth relegation place decided by a play-out between the 16th- and 17th-placed teams unless separated by more than four points.

History

Serie B was founded in 1929, when the Italian championship was reorganised into single-table national divisions. Its first champion was Casale. Over the decades, famous clubs have passed through the division, including Juventus (in 2006–07, following the Calciopoli scandal), AC Milan (twice relegated in the 1980s), Napoli, Roma, Lazio and Torino. Genoa and Brescia are among the clubs with the most Serie B titles and seasons played.

Notable facts

The league is noted for its competitiveness and unpredictability: promotion races and play-offs regularly go to the final matchdays, and smaller clubs such as Frosinone, Benevento, Empoli and Cittadella have reached Serie A through it. The all-time top scorer in Serie B is Stefan Schwoch, with 135 goals.
