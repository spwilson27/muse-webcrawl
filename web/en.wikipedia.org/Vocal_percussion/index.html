Vocal percussion

Vocal percussion
Related formsBeatboxing, a cappella
Sounds imitatedKick drum, snare, hi-hat, cymbals, scratching

Vocal percussion is the art of creating sounds with one's mouth that approximate, imitate, or otherwise serve the same purpose as a percussion instrument. It is a common feature of a cappella music, where a designated vocal percussionist provides the rhythmic backbone of the arrangement, and it reaches an extended form in beatboxing within hip hop culture.

Technique

Performers produce drum sounds with plosives and fricatives: a "p" or "b" sound for the kick drum, "tss" or "ch" for hi-hats, and "pf" or "kch" for the snare. More advanced techniques include inhaled sounds for continuous rhythm, lip oscillations, throat bass, and simultaneous humming to add a bassline or melody. In a cappella arrangements, vocal percussion parts are often written in score with phonetic syllables.

History

Traditions of vocal imitation of instruments are old and widespread, from Indian konnakol and bol to Scottish puirt à beul. In Western popular music, scat singing and the vocal group tradition preceded the beatboxing pioneered in 1980s New York hip hop by artists such as Doug E. Fresh and Biz Markie. The rise of collegiate a cappella in the 1990s, popularized by groups such as Rockapella and later by the film Pitch Perfect (2012) and the group Pentatonix, brought dedicated vocal percussionists into the mainstream.
