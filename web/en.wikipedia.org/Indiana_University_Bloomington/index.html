Indiana University Bloomington (IU Bloomington, IU, or simply Indiana) is a public research university in Bloomington, Indiana, United States. It is the flagship campus of Indiana University and, with over 40,000 students, its largest. Founded in 1820 as the State Seminary School, it became Indiana College in 1828 and Indiana University in 1838.

The university is a member of the Association of American Universities and is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities — Very high research activity". Its schools include the Kelley School of Business, the Maurer School of Law, the Jacobs School of Music — one of the most acclaimed music schools in the United States — and the O'Neill School of Public and Environmental Affairs.

Campus

The roughly 1,900-acre campus is noted for its limestone buildings drawn from Indiana quarries and for the wooded Dunn's Woods at its centre. Landmarks include the Sample Gates, the Indiana Memorial Union, the Lilly Library of rare books, and Assembly Hall.

Student life and athletics

IU fields the Indiana Hoosiers in NCAA Division I as part of the Big Ten Conference. The men's basketball programme became famous under coach Bob Knight, winning national championships in 1976, 1981, and 1987; the 1976 team remains the last undefeated national champion in men's college basketball. The university also hosts the annual Little 500 bicycle race, immortalised in the 1979 film Breaking Away.
