Battle of Bornhöved (1227)

Battle of Bornhöved
Date: 22 July 1227
Location: Bornhöved, Holstein (modern Germany)
Result: Coalition victory; end of Danish supremacy in northern Germany

The Battle of Bornhöved was fought on 22 July 1227 at Bornhöved in Holstein between the forces of King Valdemar II of Denmark and a coalition of north German princes and cities, including Count Adolf IV of Holstein, the Archbishop of Bremen, the Duke of Saxony, and the free cities of Lübeck and Hamburg.

Background

Valdemar the Victorious had built a Danish empire around the Baltic, conquering Holstein, Mecklenburg, and Estonia. Captured by his vassal Count Henry of Schwerin in 1223, he was released only after renouncing his German conquests south of the Eider; Pope Honorius III later absolved him from the oath, and Valdemar invaded Holstein again in 1226–27 to recover his lost dominions.

The battle

The armies met on the heath of Bornhöved on the feast day of Mary Magdalene. The hard-fought battle turned when the Ditmarscher troops fighting in the Danish ranks changed sides and attacked their Danish neighbours, in accordance with a prior agreement with Lübeck. The Danish army collapsed; Valdemar, wounded and nearly captured, was carried from the field. Adolf IV is said to have vowed during the battle to build a Franciscan monastery in Kiel if victorious.

Significance

Bornhöved ended Danish expansion into northern Germany and confirmed the independence of Lübeck, which received imperial immediacy, opening the way for the rise of the Hanseatic League. Denmark thereafter concentrated on its Baltic and Scandinavian position.
