Adventure is an action-adventure video game developed by Warren Robinett for the Atari Video Computer System (later renamed the Atari 2600) and released in 1980 by Atari, Inc. The player controls a square avatar through a multi-room maze, searching for an enchanted chalice stolen by an evil magician and hidden in the kingdom, while avoiding or slaying dragons. It was inspired by the text adventure game Colossal Cave Adventure.

Gameplay

The world consists of some thirty rooms and three castles (gold, black and white) opened by matching keys. Items include a sword, a bridge for crossing barriers, and a magnet that attracts objects. Three dragons — Yorgle (yellow), Grundle (green) and the fast, fierce Rhindle (red) — guard treasures and can swallow the player, who can be resurrected after death. A bat randomly steals and swaps items, adding chaos. Three difficulty levels change the layout and complexity.

Legacy

Adventure sold about a million copies and is considered a foundational action-adventure game, establishing the genre of graphical adventure on home consoles. It is most famous for containing one of the first widely known video game Easter eggs: because Atari did not credit its programmers, Robinett hid a secret room displaying the message "Created by Warren Robinett". The game figures prominently in Ernest Cline's novel Ready Player One and its film adaptation.
