Loop of Henle

In the kidney, the loop of Henle (also called Henle's loop or the nephronic loop) is the U-shaped portion of the nephron tubule that extends from the proximal convoluted tubule into the renal medulla and back to the distal convoluted tubule. It is named after the German anatomist Friedrich Gustav Jakob Henle, who described it in the 1860s.

Structure

The loop consists of a descending limb and an ascending limb. The thin descending limb is permeable to water, while the ascending limb — with its thin and thick segments — is impermeable to water but actively transports sodium, potassium, and chloride ions out of the tubule, principally via the Na-K-2Cl cotransporter in the thick ascending limb.

Function

Together with the vasa recta capillaries, the loop of Henle operates as a countercurrent multiplier: as filtrate descends, water leaves and the fluid becomes concentrated; as it ascends, salts are pumped out, building the osmotic gradient of the medulla. This gradient allows the collecting duct, under the influence of antidiuretic hormone, to concentrate urine and conserve water. Loop diuretics such as furosemide act on the thick ascending limb.
