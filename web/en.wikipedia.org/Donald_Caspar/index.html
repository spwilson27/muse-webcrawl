Donald Caspar

Donald L. D. Caspar
Born: 8 January 1927, Ithaca, New York, U.S.
Died: 27 November 2021
Field: Structural biology, biophysics
Known for: Caspar–Klug theory of virus structure

Donald L. D. Caspar (1927–2021) was an American structural biologist known for his foundational work on the architecture of viruses. Together with Aaron Klug, he developed the Caspar–Klug theory (1962), which explained how identical protein subunits assemble into icosahedral virus capsids arranged in quasi-equivalent positions on triangulated lattices.

Research

Caspar's early work at Yale and later at the Children's Hospital and Harvard Medical School used X-ray diffraction to study virus structure. His work on tomato bushy stunt virus produced the first detailed picture of a virus capsid and demonstrated how conformational flexibility lets identical proteins occupy slightly different environments — the principle of quasi-equivalence.

Career

He later joined Florida State University, where he was a professor of biological science and worked at the Institute of Molecular Biophysics. He was elected to the National Academy of Sciences in 1994. Caspar died in 2021 at the age of 94.
