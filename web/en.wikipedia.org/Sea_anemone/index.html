Sea anemone

Sea anemones are predatory marine invertebrates of the order Actiniaria, named after the terrestrial anemone flower. They are cnidarians, related to corals and jellyfish, consisting of a soft cylindrical body attached to a surface by an adhesive foot, topped by an oral disc ringed with stinging tentacles.

Biology

The tentacles are armed with nematocysts — microscopic harpoon-like stinging cells — used to capture small fish and invertebrates, which are drawn to the central mouth. Most anemones are sessile, though some can creep, burrow or even swim by flexing the body. They range from a centimetre across to over a metre in tropical species.

Symbiosis

Many anemones form famous mutualistic relationships: clownfish live unharmed among the stinging tentacles, gaining protection in exchange for food scraps and defence, while some hermit crabs carry anemones on their shells and many harbour photosynthetic algae in their tissues.
