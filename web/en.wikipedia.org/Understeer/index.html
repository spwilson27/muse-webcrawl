Understeer

Understeer is a vehicle dynamics term used to describe the sensitivity of a vehicle to steering inputs. A vehicle is said to understeer when the front tyres lose grip before the rear tyres in a corner, so the car turns less sharply than the steering angle demands and follows a wider path — colloquially, the front end "pushes" or "ploughs" towards the outside of the turn. Its opposite is oversteer, in which the rear tyres lose grip first and the car turns more than intended.

Causes and behaviour

Understeer arises from the balance of cornering stiffness between front and rear axles, weight distribution, suspension geometry, tyre sizes and aerodynamic load. Front-wheel-drive cars typically exhibit mild understeer, which manufacturers regard as safer for average drivers because the natural correction — easing off the throttle and unwinding steering — restores grip. Severe understeer at the limit can carry a car off the road on the outside of a bend.

Measurement

Engineers quantify steady-state understeer by the understeer gradient, measured in constant-radius tests: the extra steering angle required per unit of lateral acceleration. A positive gradient indicates understeer; racing drivers tune it away or towards neutrality depending on style and circuit.
