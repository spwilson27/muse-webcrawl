The Garabit Viaduct (French: Viaduc de Garabit) is a railway arch bridge spanning the Truyère river gorge near Ruynes-en-Margeride in the Cantal department of southern France. Designed by Gustave Eiffel and built between 1880 and 1884, it was at the time of its completion among the longest and highest arch bridges in the world.

Design and construction

The viaduct consists of a two-hinged wrought-iron parabolic arch of 165 metres span, carrying a lattice truss deck supported on masonry piers at either end. The total length is nearly 565 metres and the deck stands about 122 metres above the river. Eiffel's company fabricated the ironwork and erected the bridge using cable-supported cantilevering from both banks, techniques refined shortly afterwards on the Eiffel Tower in Paris.

History

The bridge opened to traffic in 1885, completing the railway link between Béziers and Neussargues through the Massif Central. It was classified as a historic monument in 1965. The bridge gained wider fame when it appeared in the 1976 film The Cassandra Crossing. Its distinctive red colour dates from a renovation completed in 1992. Today it carries regional trains and is a protected landmark; heavy freight traffic has long been restricted because of the structure's age.
