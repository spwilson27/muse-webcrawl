Overfishing

Overfishing is the removal of fish and other aquatic species from a body of water at a rate greater than the population can replenish itself naturally, resulting in declining stocks and, in severe cases, fishery collapse. The Food and Agriculture Organization estimates that more than a third of assessed marine fish stocks are fished at biologically unsustainable levels.

Causes

Causes include industrial fishing technology (sonar, factory trawlers, fish aggregating devices), government subsidies that keep excess fleet capacity at sea, illegal, unreported and unregulated fishing, open-access "tragedy of the commons" incentives, and growing global demand for seafood and fishmeal.

Effects

Overfishing removes top predators and shifts ecosystems toward smaller, faster-breeding species ("fishing down the food web"). Famous collapses include the Newfoundland northern cod fishery, closed in 1992 with the loss of tens of thousands of jobs, and severely depleted stocks of Atlantic bluefin tuna and many sharks. Bycatch of juveniles, seabirds, turtles and marine mammals compounds the damage.

Mitigation

Remedies include science-based catch quotas, individual transferable quotas, marine protected areas, gear restrictions, seasonal closures and certification schemes such as the Marine Stewardship Council. Some stocks, notably in the United States and Norway, have recovered under such management.
