Circulation (physics)

Circulation is the line integral of a vector field around a closed curve.

In fluid dynamics, circulation is the line integral of the velocity field around a closed curve. In symbols, circulation Γ is defined as

Γ = ∮C V · dl

where dl is an infinitesimal element of the curve C, traversed in the positive (counterclockwise) direction. Circulation is a scalar quantity that measures the tendency of the fluid to rotate around the curve. It plays a central role in aerodynamics, where it is linked to lift through the Kutta–Joukowski theorem, and in meteorology and oceanography, where it describes large-scale rotating flows.

Relation to vorticity

By Stokes' theorem, the circulation around a closed curve equals the surface integral of the vorticity over any surface bounded by the curve:

Γ = &iint;A (∇ × V) · dA

Thus circulation per unit area, in the limit where the enclosed area shrinks to zero, is the component of vorticity normal to the surface. A flow with zero circulation around every closed curve is called irrotational.

Kutta–Joukowski theorem

In aerodynamics, the Kutta–Joukowski theorem states that the lift per unit span L′ generated by a two-dimensional body in a flow of density ρ and speed V is

L′ = ρ V Γ

This relation, derived independently by Martin Kutta and Nikolay Joukowski in the early 20th century, connects the abstract quantity of circulation to the measurable force of lift on an airfoil.

Kelvin's circulation theorem

Kelvin's circulation theorem, stated by Lord Kelvin in 1869, says that the circulation around a closed contour moving with an inviscid, barotropic fluid subject to conservative body forces remains constant in time. This conservation law underlies much of classical vortex dynamics, including the persistence of smoke rings and the behaviour of wingtip vortices.

Applications

Circulation is used in the analysis of pumps and turbines, in the definition of potential flow around airfoils, in ocean gyres and atmospheric cyclones, and in electromagnetism, where the circulation of the magnetic field around a closed loop is given by Ampère's circuital law.
