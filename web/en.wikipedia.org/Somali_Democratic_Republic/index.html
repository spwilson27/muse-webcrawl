Somali Democratic Republic


Flag of the Somali Democratic Republic (1969–1991)

The Somali Democratic Republic (Somali: Jamhuuriyadda Dimuqraadiga Soomaaliya; Arabic: الجمهورية الديمقراطية الصومالية) was the name of the Somali state from 1969 to 1991, under the military government of President Siad Barre. The regime came to power after a coup d'état on 21 October 1969, nine days after the assassination of President Abdirashid Shermarke, and ruled until its collapse in January 1991 at the outset of the Somali Civil War.

History

After the coup, the Supreme Revolutionary Council (SRC), led by Major General Siad Barre, dissolved parliament and the Supreme Court, suspended the constitution, and renamed the country the Somali Democratic Republic. The new regime embraced "scientific socialism", aligning itself initially with the Soviet Union. In 1976 the SRC founded the Somali Revolutionary Socialist Party, and Somalia became a one-party state.

Ogaden War

In 1977 Somalia invaded the Ogaden region of Ethiopia, seeking to unite ethnic Somalis in a "Greater Somalia". The Soviet Union switched its support to Ethiopia, and with Cuban assistance the Somali forces were driven out by March 1978. Somalia thereafter realigned with the United States and the Western bloc during the Cold War.

Decline and collapse

Defeat in the Ogaden War, economic deterioration, and growing clan-based opposition weakened the regime through the 1980s. Armed insurgencies by movements such as the Somali National Movement and the United Somali Congress escalated into full civil war. Barre fled Mogadishu on 26 January 1991, ending the Somali Democratic Republic; the country descended into fragmentation and the central government collapsed.
