Baal Hammon

Baal Hammon (Punic: Baʿl Ḥammon) was the chief god of ancient Carthage, a weather and fertility deity responsible for the growth of vegetation. He was typically depicted as a bearded elderly man seated on a throne, wearing a crown of feathers.

Cult

Baal Hammon was worshipped alongside the goddess Tanit in Carthaginian sanctuaries called tophets, where urns containing cremated remains were buried. Greek and Roman authors alleged that children were sacrificed to him as "Moloch"; modern scholars debate the scale and nature of the practice.

Identity

His name may mean "Lord of the Brazier" or derive from Mount Amanus (Hammon). The Romans identified him with Saturn, and his cult survived in North Africa into the Roman period as Saturnus Balcaranensis.
