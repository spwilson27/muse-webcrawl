Constantino Paul Castellano (1915–1985) was an American crime boss who succeeded Carlo Gambino as head of the Gambino crime family of New York City, then the largest of the Five Families of the American Mafia. Nicknamed "Big Paul" for his height and "the Howard Hughes of the Mob" for his reclusive, business-oriented style, he ran the family from 1976 until his assassination in 1985.

Rise to power

Castellano, a cousin and brother-in-law of Gambino, rose through the family's ranks in businesses including meat distribution and construction. Gambino's choice of Castellano over underboss Aniello Dellacroce caused lasting friction within the family. Castellano focused on white-collar rackets — bid rigging in the concrete industry, labor racketeering, and infiltration of legitimate businesses — and discouraged drug dealing by family members.

Downfall and death

By 1985 Castellano faced federal RICO indictments, and his authority was challenged by capo John Gotti, whose crew was involved in drug trafficking that Castellano had forbidden. On December 16, 1985, Castellano and his new underboss Thomas Bilotti were shot dead outside Sparks Steak House in Midtown Manhattan by gunmen acting on Gotti's orders. Gotti then seized control of the Gambino family. The unsanctioned hit and Gotti's flamboyant style marked a turning point in the decline of the New York Mafia.
