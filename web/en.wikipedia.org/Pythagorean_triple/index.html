Pythagorean triple

The 3–4–5 right triangle

A Pythagorean triple consists of three positive integers a, b, and c, such that a2 + b2 = c2. Such a triple is commonly written (a, b, c); a well-known example is (3, 4, 5). The name is taken from the Pythagorean theorem, since the three numbers can be the side lengths of a right triangle. A triple in which a, b and c are coprime is called a primitive Pythagorean triple.

Generating triples

Euclid's formula generates all primitive triples: for integers m > n > 0 with m and n coprime and not both odd, set a = m2 − n2, b = 2mn, c = m2 + n2. Every non-primitive triple is a multiple of a primitive one. Pythagorean triples were known in antiquity: the Babylonian tablet Plimpton 322, dated to about 1800 BC, lists fifteen of them.

Properties

In a primitive triple exactly one of a and b is even, the area (ab/2) is a congruent number, and c is the hypotenuse of a right triangle whose other two sides are integers. Sets of four integers satisfying the analogous equation for a rectangular box are called Pythagorean quadruples.
