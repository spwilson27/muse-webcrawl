World Bank

The World Bank is an international financial institution that provides loans and grants to the governments of low- and middle-income countries for capital projects. It comprises the International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (IBRD) and the International Development Association (IDA), and is part of the World Bank Group alongside IFC, MIGA and ICSID.

History

The Bank was created at the 1944 Bretton Woods Conference, initially to help finance the reconstruction of Europe after the Second World War. Its focus later shifted to development in the Global South, poverty reduction and infrastructure.

Activities

The Bank lends for agriculture, education, health, infrastructure, energy and climate projects, and publishes research and data including the World Development Indicators and the annual World Development Report. Its stated goals are ending extreme poverty and boosting shared prosperity.

Governance and criticism

The Bank is owned by 189 member countries and headquartered in Washington, D.C. By convention its president is a nominee of the United States, its largest shareholder. Critics have challenged its structural-adjustment policies, environmental record and governance imbalance.
