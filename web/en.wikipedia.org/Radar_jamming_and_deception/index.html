Radar jamming and deception

Radar jamming obscures real targets with noise or creates false returns to deceive an operator.

Radar jamming and deception is a form of electronic countermeasure (ECM) that intentionally sends out radio frequency signals to interfere with the operation of a radar by saturating its receiver with noise or false information. Concepts that blanket the radar with signals so its display cannot be read are normally known as jamming, while systems that produce confusing or misleading information are known as deception, but both fall under the general heading of radar jamming.

Mechanically steered jamming

Early countermeasures were passive. Chaff — clouds of small strips of metal foil cut to lengths resonant with enemy radar wavelengths — produces a mass of false echoes that hides aircraft within or behind it. It was used operationally from 1943, when the RAF dropped "Window" during the bombing of Hamburg, and remains in use today on aircraft, ships and as decoy rounds. Passive decoys and radar reflectors similarly generate attractive false targets.

Active jamming

Active jammers radiate their own signals. Noise jamming broadcasts powerful broadband or spot noise at the radar's frequency to raise the noise floor and mask real echoes; barrage jamming covers many frequencies at once at reduced power per channel. Deceptive jamming is subtler: a digital radio frequency memory (DRFM) repeater receives the radar's pulses and retransmits altered copies, producing false targets at false ranges and velocities, or employs techniques such as range-gate pull-off and velocity-gate pull-off to gradually "walk" a tracking radar off its real target. Self-protection jammers are carried by combat aircraft, while stand-off jammers operate from dedicated electronic-warfare aircraft positioned away from the target area.

Counter-countermeasures

Radar designers respond with electronic counter-countermeasures (ECCM): frequency agility, low sidelobes, monopulse tracking, pulse compression and burn-through processing that increase the power or sophistication a jammer must defeat, driving a continuing measure-and-countermeasure contest in modern air warfare.
