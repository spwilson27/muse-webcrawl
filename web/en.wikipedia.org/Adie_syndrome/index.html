Adie syndrome

Adie syndrome, also known as Holmes–Adie syndrome, is a neurological disorder characterised by a tonically dilated pupil that reacts slowly to light, together with diminished or absent deep tendon reflexes. It is named after the British neurologist William John Adie.

Signs and symptoms

The affected pupil is typically larger than normal and constricts slowly and incompletely, with better response to near effort than to light. Patients may notice blurred near vision and sensitivity to light. Loss of knee or ankle reflexes is common.

Cause

The condition results from damage to the postganglionic fibres of the parasympathetic innervation of the eye, usually attributed to a viral or bacterial infection affecting the ciliary ganglion. It most often affects young to middle-aged women and is usually unilateral at onset and benign.

Treatment

No treatment is usually required; reading glasses or dilute pilocarpine drops may help symptoms.
