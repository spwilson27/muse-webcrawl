Davao City

Davao City, the largest city of Mindanao

Davao City, officially the City of Davao, is a highly urbanised city in the Davao Region of the Philippines. It is the most populous city in Mindanao and the third most populous in the Philippines, with a population of about 1.85 million in the 2024 census. With a land area of 2,444 km², it is the largest city in the country by area. It lies on the island's southeastern coast, facing the Davao Gulf and the island of Samal.

History

The area was home to Lumad and Moro peoples, including the Bagobo, Maranao and Maguindanaon, before Spanish forces under Don José Oyanguren founded a settlement in 1848 after defeating the local ruler Datu Bago. Under American rule Davao became a centre of abacá (Manila hemp) production, attracting a large Japanese community. The city was chartered in 1937, occupied by Japan during the Second World War, and rebuilt afterward as Mindanao's principal port and commercial hub.

Geography and economy

Davao's territory stretches from the coast to the foothills of Mount Apo, the highest peak in the Philippines, and includes fruit plantations, forests, and protected watersheds. The city is a major producer and exporter of bananas, pineapples, mangoes and durian, the famously pungent fruit celebrated at the annual Kadayawan Festival. Its port and Francisco Bangoy International Airport link it to the rest of the Philippines and to Southeast Asia.

Government and culture

The city was governed for more than two decades by Rodrigo Duterte, whose tenure as mayor made Davao nationally prominent before he became President of the Philippines in 2016. Attractions include the Philippine Eagle Center, home of the endangered Philippine eagle, the Davao River, and Samal Island's resorts just offshore.
