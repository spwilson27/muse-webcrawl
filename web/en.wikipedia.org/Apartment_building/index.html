Apartment building

An apartment building, or block of flats, is a residential building containing multiple separate housing units — apartments or flats — within a single structure. Units may be rented from a landlord, owned individually as condominiums, or held cooperatively. Apartment buildings range from small walk-up blocks of a few units to high-rise towers containing hundreds of homes.

History

Multi-unit housing dates back to the insulae of ancient Rome. In the 19th century, industrial cities in Europe and North America saw rapid construction of tenements and purpose-built apartment houses, while Paris became known for its Haussmann-era apartment blocks. The invention of the elevator and steel-frame construction enabled the tall residential towers of the 20th century.

Terminology

In the United Kingdom and much of the Commonwealth, a unit is usually called a flat and the building a block of flats; in North America, apartment predominates. Terms such as walk-up, garden apartment, mid-rise and high-rise describe the building's form and height.

Facilities

Larger buildings often include shared amenities such as laundry rooms, parking, gyms, courtyards and concierge or security services. Apartment living is associated with urban density, shorter commutes, and lower per-unit land use compared with detached housing.
