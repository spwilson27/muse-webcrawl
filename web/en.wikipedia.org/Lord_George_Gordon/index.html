Lord George Gordon

Lord George Gordon
Born: 26 December 1751, London
Died: 1 November 1793, Newgate Prison, London
Known for: Gordon Riots of 1780

Lord George Gordon (1751–1793) was a British politician best known for his role in the Gordon Riots of 1780. A younger son of the 3rd Duke of Gordon, he entered Parliament for Ludgershall and became president of the Protestant Association, campaigning against the Papists Act 1778, which had relieved some legal restrictions on Roman Catholics.

Gordon Riots

On 2 June 1780 Gordon led a huge procession to Parliament to present a petition against the Act. The event spiralled into several days of anti-Catholic rioting in London, in which chapels, houses, prisons and the Bank of England were attacked and hundreds died. Gordon was tried for high treason and acquitted, defended by Thomas Erskine, on the basis that he had intended no violence.

Later life

In later years Gordon converted to Judaism. In 1788 he was convicted of defaming Marie Antoinette and the French ambassador and, after fleeing and being recaptured, spent the last five years of his life in Newgate Prison, where he died of typhoid fever in 1793.
