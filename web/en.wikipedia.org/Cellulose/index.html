Cellulose

Cellulose
Formula: (C6H10O5)n
Type: Polysaccharide
Main source: Plant cell walls, cotton

Cellulose is an organic compound, a polysaccharide of hundreds to thousands of β(1→4)-linked D-glucose units. It is the most abundant organic polymer on Earth and the main structural component of plant cell walls and vegetable fibres such as cotton, flax and wood.

Structure and properties

Straight chains pack into microfibrils held by hydrogen bonds, giving high tensile strength. Cellulose is insoluble in water and most organic solvents and is indigestible by humans, forming dietary fibre, though ruminants and termites digest it via symbiotic microbes.

Uses

Cellulose is used for paper, cardboard, textiles, cellophane, rayon and derivatives such as nitrocellulose and cellulose acetate. It is also a feedstock for biofuels research.
