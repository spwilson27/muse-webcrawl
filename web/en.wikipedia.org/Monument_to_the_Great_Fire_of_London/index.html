Monument to the Great Fire of London

LocationFish Street Hill, City of London, UK
Height61.5 m (202 ft)
Designed byChristopher Wren and Robert Hooke
Built1671–1677

The Monument to the Great Fire of London, more commonly known simply as the Monument, is a Doric column in London, England, situated near the northern end of London Bridge. Commemorating the Great Fire of London, it stands at the junction of Monument Street and Fish Street Hill, 202 feet (61.5 m) in height. It was built between 1671 and 1677 on the site of St Margaret, New Fish Street, the first church destroyed by the Great Fire.

Description

The column is of Portland stone, topped by a gilded urn of fire, and is designed by Christopher Wren and Robert Hooke. Its height marks the distance to the site of Thomas Farriner's bakery on Pudding Lane, where the fire began. A spiral staircase of 311 steps leads to a viewing platform — caged in the 19th century after a series of suicides — which offers panoramic views of the City. The base is decorated with an allegorical sculpted panel by Caius Gabriel Cibber and inscriptions in Latin describing the fire and the rebuilding of London.

Scientific use

Wren and Hooke also designed the Monument as a giant scientific instrument: its central shaft contains a zenith telescope and an underground laboratory for pendulum experiments and barometric measurements, though passing traffic vibrations made it unusable for that purpose.
