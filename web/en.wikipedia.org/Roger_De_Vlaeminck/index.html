Roger De Vlaeminck (born 24 August 1947) is a Belgian former professional racing cyclist. He was one of the most successful classics specialists of his era and one of the greatest cobbled-classics riders of all time. He is one of only three riders to have won all five of cycling's "Monuments" during his career, and he holds the joint record of four victories in Paris–Roubaix, earning him the nickname "Monsieur Paris–Roubaix".

Career

De Vlaeminck turned professional in 1969 and quickly established himself as a formidable rider in the spring classics, winning Omloop Het Volk in his first season. His career ran in parallel with that of Eddy Merckx, and the two Belgians were fierce rivals; although Merckx dominated the grand tours, De Vlaeminck was frequently his equal in one-day races.

His Monument victories were Milan–San Remo (three times, 1973, 1978, 1979), the Tour of Flanders (1977), Paris–Roubaix (four times: 1972, 1974, 1975, 1977), Liège–Bastogne–Liège (1970), and the Giro di Lombardia (twice, 1974 and 1976). He also won the 1975 Tour de Suisse, six stages of the Giro d'Italia, and the points classification at the 1979 Giro.

De Vlaeminck was also an outstanding cyclo-cross rider, winning the world championship in 1975, and he competed on the track as well. Remarkably versatile, he raced professionally until 1984.

Post-career

After retiring, De Vlaeminck worked in cycling media and briefly coached the Zimbabwe national cycling team ahead of the 2002 UCI Road World Championships. His Paris–Roubaix record of four wins was later equalled by Tom Boonen in 2012.
