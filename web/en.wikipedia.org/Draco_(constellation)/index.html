Draco (constellation)

The constellation Draco winding around the north celestial pole.

Draco is a constellation in the far northern sky. Its name is Latin for dragon, and it was one of the 48 constellations listed by the 2nd-century astronomer Ptolemy, remaining one of the 88 modern constellations. Draco winds around the north celestial pole, lying between Ursa Major and Ursa Minor.

Features

The dragon's head is marked by a quadrilateral of stars including Eltanin (Gamma Draconis), the constellation's brightest star, once famous for James Bradley's discovery of the aberration of starlight in 1728. Around 2700 BC the star Thuban (Alpha Draconis) was the pole star. Draco contains the Cat's Eye Nebula (NGC 6543), a well-known planetary nebula, and numerous distant galaxies.

Mythology

In Greek myth Draco is often identified with Ladon, the dragon that guarded the golden apples of the Hesperides and was slain by Heracles. The northern ecliptic pole lies within Draco, and the Quadrantid meteor shower radiates from near its border with Boötes.
