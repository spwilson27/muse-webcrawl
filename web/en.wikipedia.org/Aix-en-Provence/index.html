Aix-en-Provence, usually shortened to Aix, is a city in southern France, about 30 km north of Marseille. Founded by the Romans in 123 BC as Aquae Sextiae around its thermal springs, it was the capital of Provence in the Middle Ages and remains a judicial, university, and cultural center, noted for its fountains, elegant cours Mirabeau, and association with the painter Paul Cézanne.

History

At nearby Entremont stood the oppidum of the Salluvii, destroyed by the Romans who founded Aquae Sextiae after Gaius Sextius Calvinus's campaign; in 102 BC Gaius Marius defeated the Teutones at the Battle of Aquae Sextiae. Medieval Aix flourished under the counts of Provence; "Good King René" of Anjou held a brilliant court there in the 15th century. The Parliament of Provence sat in Aix from 1501 until the Revolution.

Culture

Aix is the birthplace of Paul Cézanne, whose studio (Atelier Cézanne) and the Montagne Sainte-Victoire he painted repeatedly dominate its artistic identity; his school friend Émile Zola also grew up there. The city hosts the Festival d'Aix-en-Provence, an internationally renowned opera festival founded in 1948, and campuses of Aix-Marseille University. Its many squares and fountains — including the Rotonde and the fountains of the Cours Mirabeau — give it the nickname "city of a thousand fountains."
