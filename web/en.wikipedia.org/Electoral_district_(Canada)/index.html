Electoral district (Canada)

An electoral district in Canada, commonly known as a riding, is a geographical constituency upon which Canada's representative democracy is based. Each federal electoral district returns one member of Parliament to the House of Commons; provincial and territorial legislatures likewise divide their jurisdictions into electoral districts, each electing one member.

Federal districts

Following the 2022 redistribution, there are 343 federal electoral districts. Boundaries are reviewed after each decennial census by independent commissions in each province, under the Electoral Boundaries Readjustment Act, with representation allocated to provinces according to constitutional rules administered by Elections Canada. Territories each form a single district.

Names and character

Ridings are usually named for their geographical location, sometimes combined with a community or direction, and vary enormously in area and population — from compact urban districts in Toronto or Montreal to vast northern districts such as Nunavut, which covers over two million square kilometres. A member of Parliament is commonly described as representing their riding.
