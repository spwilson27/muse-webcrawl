The Paris Agreement (French: Accord de Paris) is a legally binding international treaty on climate change adopted by 196 parties at the UN Climate Change Conference (COP21) in Paris, France, on 12 December 2015. It entered into force on 4 November 2016. Its overarching goal is to hold "the increase in the global average temperature to well below 2 °C above pre-industrial levels" and to pursue efforts "to limit the temperature increase to 1.5 °C above pre-industrial levels."

Structure

Unlike the top-down Kyoto Protocol, the Paris Agreement works through nationally determined contributions (NDCs): each party prepares, communicates, and maintains successive pledges of emissions reductions and adaptation measures, updating them every five years with an expectation of progression. A global stocktake, held every five years (the first concluded at COP28 in Dubai in 2023), assesses collective progress. The agreement also addresses adaptation, climate finance — including the goal of mobilising US$100 billion per year for developing countries — and loss and damage, for which a dedicated fund was agreed at COP27 in 2022.

Implementation and adequacy

Because NDCs are nationally set and the agreement's compliance mechanism is facilitative rather than punitive, the treaty's effect depends on domestic politics. Analyses by the UN Environment Programme and others find that current pledges, even if fully implemented, put the world on a path to roughly 2.4–2.8 °C of warming by 2100 — better than pre-Paris trajectories but short of the 1.5 °C goal. Carbon Brief and the IPCC note that holding 1.5 °C requires global emissions to peak before 2025 and fall by roughly half by 2030. The COP28 stocktake for the first time called on parties to transition away from fossil fuels in energy systems.

United States participation

The United States signed the agreement in 2016 under President Barack Obama. President Donald Trump announced withdrawal in 2017, effective 4 November 2020; President Joe Biden rejoined on 19 February 2021. In January 2025, on his return to office, Trump again signed an executive order withdrawing the United States, effective one year later under the treaty's terms.
