Vellus hair

Vellus hair is short, thin, light-coloured, and barely noticeable hair that develops on most of a person's body during childhood. It differs from the coarser, longer, and more pigmented terminal hair that grows on the scalp and, after puberty, in the pubic region, underarms, and (in males) the face and chest.

Development

Vellus hair develops from the hair follicles that in the womb produce lanugo, the fine down covering a fetus. After birth, most body follicles produce vellus hair. During puberty, increased androgen levels cause vellus follicles in certain regions to transform and produce terminal hair instead. Each vellus hair is usually less than 2 mm long, and its follicle lacks an arrector pili muscle, so vellus hair does not stand on end during goose bumps.

Function

Vellus hair provides a measure of thermal insulation and helps regulate body temperature by facilitating the evaporation of perspiration. It also enhances the sense of touch: the follicles act as levers amplifying the movement of the hair shaft, making the skin sensitive to light contact and air movement.

Clinical significance

Excessive growth of vellus hair, or its conversion to terminal hair in atypical patterns, can occur in conditions such as hirsutism and hypertrichosis. In anorexia nervosa, a soft downy growth resembling lanugo may appear on the body. Changes in vellus hair are also examined in forensic and dermatological contexts.
