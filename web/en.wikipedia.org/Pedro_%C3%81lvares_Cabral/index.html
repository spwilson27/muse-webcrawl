Pedro Álvares Cabral

Pedro Álvares Cabral (c. 1467 – c. 1520) was a Portuguese nobleman, military commander, navigator and explorer regarded as the European discoverer of Brazil. In 1500 Cabral conducted the first substantial exploration of the northeast coast of South America and claimed it for Portugal.

Pedro Álvares Cabral
Born: c. 1467, Belmonte, Portugal
Died: c. 1520, Santarém, Portugal
Known for: Discovery of Brazil (1500)

Voyage of 1500

Commanding a fleet of 13 ships bound for India by Vasco da Gama's new route, Cabral swung far west into the Atlantic—whether by accident or design remains debated—and landed in April 1500 on the coast of present-day Brazil, claiming it under the Treaty of Tordesillas. Continuing to India, he established trade at Calicut and Cochin amid conflict, and returned to Lisbon in 1501 with a valuable cargo of spices.

Later life

Passed over for subsequent India commands, Cabral retired to his estates at Santarém. His grave was rediscovered in the 19th century; he is commemorated as a national hero in both Portugal and Brazil.
