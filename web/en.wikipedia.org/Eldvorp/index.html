Eldvörp

Eldvörp is a row of volcanic craters on the Reykjanes Peninsula in southwestern Iceland, west of Grindavík and near the Svartsengi geothermal power plant.

The crater row is about 10 kilometres long and was formed in a fissure eruption during the Reykjanes Fires, a period of volcanic activity in the 13th century, around 1210–1240. The eruptions produced extensive lava fields that now cover much of the surrounding area.

Geology

Eldvörp belongs to the Svartsengi volcanic system. The craters are scoria and spatter cones aligned along a northeast–southwest trending fissure, characteristic of the rift volcanism of the Mid-Atlantic Ridge where it rises above sea level in Iceland.

Steam vents and geothermal features are present near the crater row, associated with the Svartsengi high-temperature geothermal field used for power generation and the nearby Blue Lagoon.
