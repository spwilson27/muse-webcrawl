The war in Donbas was an armed conflict in the Donbas region of Ukraine, part of the broader Russo-Ukrainian War, that began in April 2014 when Russian-backed separatists seized government buildings in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts following Russia's annexation of Crimea and the Euromaidan revolution.

Course of the war

Ukraine launched an "Anti-Terrorist Operation" to retake the seized territory. Separatist forces, supported by Russian troops and equipment — decisively at Ilovaisk in August 2014 and Debaltseve in early 2015 — held parts of both oblasts. On 17 July 2014, Malaysia Airlines Flight 17 was shot down over the conflict zone, killing all 298 people aboard; investigators attributed the shootdown to a Buk missile supplied from Russia. The Minsk agreements of September 2014 and February 2015 froze the front line but never produced a lasting settlement, and low-intensity fighting continued for years.

Escalation

On 21 February 2022 Russia recognised the self-proclaimed Donetsk and Luhansk people's republics, and on 24 February 2022 launched a full-scale invasion of Ukraine, subsuming the Donbas war into a much larger conflict. The leadership in Kyiv under President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and support decisions in Washington, including at the White House, became central to the war's international dimension.
