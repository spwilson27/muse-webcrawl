Bonn Agreement (Afghanistan)

The Bonn Agreement, formally the Agreement on Provisional Arrangements in Afghanistan Pending the Re-Establishment of Permanent Government Institutions, was signed on 5 December 2001 in Bonn, Germany. Negotiated under United Nations auspices after the fall of the Taliban regime in late 2001, it set out a roadmap for rebuilding the Afghan state.

Provisions

The agreement established an Interim Administration led by a chairman — Hamid Karzai was chosen — which took power in Kabul on 22 December 2001. It provided for an Emergency Loya Jirga to be held within six months to install a Transitional Administration, followed by a Constitutional Loya Jirga and, ultimately, democratic elections. It also requested the deployment of a UN-mandated international security force, which became the International Security Assistance Force (ISAF).

Participants and aftermath

Four Afghan delegations took part: the Northern Alliance (United Front), the Rome group associated with former king Mohammed Zahir Shah, the Cyprus group, and the Peshawar group. The Taliban were not represented. The process culminated in the 2004 constitution and the presidential election won by Karzai. Critics have argued that the agreement's power-sharing, which rewarded armed faction leaders, entrenched warlordism, and that excluding the Taliban contributed to the later insurgency.
