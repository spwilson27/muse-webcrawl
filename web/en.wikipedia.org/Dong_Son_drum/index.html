Dong Son drum
Origin: Dong Son culture, northern Vietnam
Period: c. 600 BCE – 1st century CE
Material: Bronze
Also called: Heger type I drum

Dong Son drums are bronze drums fabricated by the Dong Son culture of the Red River Delta in what is now northern Vietnam. They are famous for their sophisticated casting and for the intricate decoration of their tympana, typically featuring a central star surrounded by concentric bands of geometric patterns, animals, and scenes of daily life, warfare, and ritual.

Distribution and significance

First identified systematically by Franz Heger in 1902 (his "type I" drum), Dong Son drums have been found across mainland and island Southeast Asia, from southern China to Indonesia, indicating extensive trade or exchange networks. Large examples such as the Ngoc Lu drum are regarded as national treasures of Vietnam. The drums likely served ceremonial purposes, possibly in rain or fertility rituals, and as symbols of chiefly wealth and power.
