The Brahmaputra is a trans-boundary river which flows through southwestern China, northeastern India and Bangladesh. At about 3,969 km long, it is the ninth largest river in the world by discharge and one of the longest. Its name, from Sanskrit, means "son of Brahma".

Course

It rises in Tibet as the Yarlung Tsangpo, flowing east for some 1,600 km north of the Himalayas, then plunges through the Yarlung Tsangpo Grand Canyon — among the deepest gorges on Earth — near Namcha Barwa and turns south into Arunachal Pradesh as the Siang/Dihang. Entering Assam, it becomes the broad, braided Brahmaputra, then crosses into Bangladesh as the Jamuna and joins the Ganges (Padma) and Meghna to form the world's largest delta.

Significance

The river sustains the agriculture and fisheries of Assam and Bangladesh, supports the one-horned rhinoceros habitats of Kaziranga, and carries enormous sediment loads. Its floods are both life-giving and destructive. Hydropower and water-sharing among China, India and Bangladesh make the basin strategically sensitive; no comprehensive basin treaty exists.
