Matching (graph theory)

FieldGraph theory
ProblemMaximum matching
Key algorithmEdmonds' blossom algorithm

In graph theory, a matching in a graph is a set of edges with no shared endpoints — that is, each vertex is incident to at most one edge in the set. A matching is maximum if no other matching has more edges, and perfect if it covers every vertex of the graph.

Theory

Matching theory is a central part of combinatorics, with classical results such as Hall's marriage theorem for bipartite graphs and Tutte's theorem for general graphs. Jack Edmonds' blossom algorithm (1965) finds maximum matchings in polynomial time.

Applications

Matchings model assignments such as matching job applicants to jobs, kidney donors to recipients, and riders to drivers in ride-hailing systems.
