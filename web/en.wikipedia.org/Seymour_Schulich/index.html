Seymour Schulich

Seymour Schulich (born January 6, 1940) is a Canadian businessman and philanthropist, best known for his work in the mining industry.

Seymour Schulich CM (born January 6, 1940) is a Canadian businessman, investor and philanthropist. He co-founded the Franco-Nevada mining royalty company and is among Canada's most prominent donors to higher education.

Early life and education

Schulich was born in Montreal, Quebec. He studied at McGill University, earning a Bachelor of Science degree in 1961 and an MBA in 1965; he also earned a Chartered Financial Analyst designation. He began his career at Shell Oil and then joined the investment firm Beutel, Goodman & Company, where he became a vice-president and developed expertise in the natural resource sector, including pioneering work in oil and gas royalty trusts and precious-metals royalty arrangements.

Career

In 1982, Schulich co-founded Franco-Nevada Corporation with Pierre Lassonde. Originally a gold royalty company, Franco-Nevada grew into one of the world's largest royalty and streaming companies and was an early innovator of the royalty model in mining finance. The company merged with Newmont Mining in 2002 in a transaction valued in the billions of dollars.

Philanthropy

Schulich has donated hundreds of millions of dollars to Canadian universities and hospitals. The business school at York University was renamed the Schulich School of Business following his landmark gift, and his name is attached to the Schulich School of Medicine & Dentistry at Western University, the Schulich School of Engineering at the University of Calgary, the Schulich Faculty of Chemistry at the Technion, and the Schulich School of Music at McGill. In 2012 he established the Schulich Leader Scholarships, a large undergraduate STEM scholarship program in Canada and Israel. He was appointed a Member of the Order of Canada.
