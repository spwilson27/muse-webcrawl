Diatom

Diatoms are a major group of algae, specifically microalgae, found in the oceans, waterways, and soils of the world. They are unicellular organisms encased in intricate cell walls made of silica, called frustules, which display a wide variety of symmetrical patterns.

Ecology

Diatoms are among the most important primary producers on Earth, generating an estimated 20–50 percent of the oxygen produced on the planet each year. They form a major part of phytoplankton and sit at the base of many aquatic food webs. Because their silica shells sink after death, diatoms play a major role in the ocean's biological carbon pump.

Frustules and fossils

The glassy frustule consists of two overlapping halves. Accumulated deposits of fossil diatom shells form diatomaceous earth, used industrially as a filter aid, mild abrasive, and natural insecticide. Diatom assemblages in sediments are used to reconstruct past climates and environments.

Diversity

Estimates of the number of diatom species range from 20,000 to over 100,000. They are classified principally into centric diatoms, with radial symmetry, and pennate diatoms, with bilateral symmetry.
