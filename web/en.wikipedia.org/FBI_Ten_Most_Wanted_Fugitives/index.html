FBI Ten Most Wanted Fugitives

The FBI Ten Most Wanted Fugitives is a most wanted list maintained by the United States Federal Bureau of Investigation. It originated in 1949, when an FBI official discussed with a journalist the "toughest guys" the bureau was pursuing, and the resulting article generated such publicity that director J. Edgar Hoover formally established the programme on March 14, 1950.

A Ten Most Wanted Fugitives poster

Operation

Fugitives are added to the list through a selection process involving the FBI's Criminal Investigative Division, field offices, and the Office of Public Affairs, and are removed when captured, killed, or when the charges against them are dropped or they no longer meet the criteria. The FBI offers a reward of at least $250,000 for information leading directly to an arrest of a listed fugitive. As of recent counts, more than 530 fugitives have appeared on the list, and the great majority have been located, many as a result of citizen recognition of the widely distributed posters and media coverage.

Notable fugitives

Well-known figures who have appeared on the list include bank robber Willie Sutton, James Earl Ray, serial killer Ted Bundy, Boston crime boss James "Whitey" Bulger, and — after the September 11 attacks — al-Qaeda leader Osama bin Laden, on whose head a $25 million reward was offered.
