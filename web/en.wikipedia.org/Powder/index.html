Powder

A powder is a dry bulk solid composed of many very fine particles that may flow freely when shaken or tilted. Powders are a class of granular material, distinguished by their small particle size, which makes interparticle forces — cohesion, friction, and electrostatic attraction — significant compared with gravity, so powders can clump, cake, or behave in some ways like liquids or even gases.

Properties

Important powder properties include particle size and shape distribution, bulk and tapped density, flowability, moisture content, and angle of repose. Powders tend to compress under their own weight, so a powder's bulk density can change with handling. Fine powders present inhalation hazards and, when dispersed in air at sufficient concentration, can form explosive dust clouds (dust explosions), a serious industrial risk in grain elevators, coal mines, and mills.

Production and uses

Powders are produced by crushing, grinding, milling, precipitation, atomisation, and spray drying. They are ubiquitous in industry and daily life: flour, cement, powdered milk, pharmaceuticals and powdered medicines, cosmetics (face powder, talc), toner, metal powders for powder metallurgy and additive manufacturing, gunpowder and other propellants, detergent, spices, and powder coatings. Powder technology (powder engineering) studies their storage in silos and hoppers, conveying, mixing, and compaction into tablets.
