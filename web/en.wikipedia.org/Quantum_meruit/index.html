Quantum meruit

Quantum meruit is a Latin phrase meaning "what one has earned". In the context of contract law, it means something along the lines of "reasonable value of services". In the United States, quantum meruit is a doctrine under which a person who has conferred a benefit on another may recover the reasonable value of that benefit in order to prevent the recipient's unjust enrichment, even in the absence of an enforceable contract.

Doctrine

A claim in quantum meruit typically arises where:

  • valuable services were rendered or materials furnished;
  • to the person sought to be charged;
  • which were accepted by that person and used and enjoyed by them;
  • under circumstances which reasonably notified them that the person rendering the services expected to be paid.

Quantum meruit claims often appear where an express contract exists but is unenforceable, where a contract has been partially performed before termination, or where work was requested but the parties failed to agree a price. The measure of recovery is the reasonable value of the services, rather than any contract price.

Relationship to other doctrines

Quantum meruit is closely related to quantum valebant ("as much as they are worth", for goods supplied) and to the broader law of restitution and unjust enrichment. In English law after British Steel Corp v Cleveland Bridge and Engineering Co Ltd and subsequent cases, restitutionary claims of this kind are analysed through the unjust enrichment framework. In construction law, quantum meruit is a common basis for a contractor's claim where work is done under a letter of intent or a contract that never materialised.
