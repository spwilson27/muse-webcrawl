Cochin Port or Kochi Port is a major port on the Arabian Sea – Laccadive Sea – Indian Ocean sea route in the city of Kochi, Kerala, India. It is one of the largest ports in India and is administered by the Cochin Port Authority, an autonomous corporation wholly owned by the Government of India. The port lies on two islands in the Lake of Kochi: Willingdon Island and Vallarpadam, towards the Fort Kochi river mouth opening onto the Laccadive Sea.

History

The modern port was constructed between 1926 and 1928 under the supervision of harbour engineer Sir Robert Bristow, who developed Willingdon Island by dredging material from the harbour channel. The port was declared a major port in 1936. Kochi had been an important spice trading port along the Malabar Coast since the 14th century, frequented by Portuguese, Dutch, and British merchants; the Portuguese explorer Vasco da Gama's fleet reached nearby Calicut in 1498, and the Portuguese established themselves at Cochin in the early 1500s.

Facilities

The port's facilities include the International Container Transshipment Terminal (ICTT) at Vallarpadam, commissioned in 2011 and operated by DP World, which was intended to make Kochi a transshipment hub reducing India's dependence on foreign ports such as Colombo. Other facilities include an LNG regasification terminal at Puthuvypeen, oil berths serving the Kochi Refineries (BPCL), a cruise terminal, and the nearby Cochin Shipyard, one of India's largest shipbuilding and maintenance facilities.
