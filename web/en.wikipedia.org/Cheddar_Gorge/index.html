Overview

Cheddar Gorge is a limestone gorge in the Mendip Hills, near the village of Cheddar, Somerset, England. The gorge, with cliffs rising up to 137 metres (449 ft), is the largest in the United Kingdom and forms part of a Site of Special Scientific Interest. A road, the B3135, winds through its floor, and the site attracts around 350,000 visitors a year.

Caves and prehistory

The gorge's caves were cut by an underground river and later occupied by humans. Gough's Cave, the largest show cave, yielded Cheddar Man, Britain's oldest near-complete human skeleton, dated to around 7150 BC; DNA analysis of the remains attracted wide attention in 2018. Finds from the caves also demonstrate cannibalism among the gorge's Magdalenian inhabitants some 15,000 years ago. Cox's Cave, with its colourful calcite formations, is the other principal show cave.

Geology and wildlife

The gorge was formed by meltwater floods during successive periglacial episodes over the past 1.2 million years, cutting into Carboniferous limestone; in interglacial periods the water drained underground, leaving the gorge dry. Its south-facing cliffs host rare plants including the Cheddar pink, and support populations of feral goats, Soay sheep, peregrine falcons and greater and lesser horseshoe bats. Cheddar cheese takes its name from the village, where the caves' constant temperature was traditionally used to mature it.
