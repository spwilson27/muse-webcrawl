An amusement park is a park that features various attractions, such as rides and games, as well as other events for entertainment purposes. A theme park is a type of amusement park that bases its structures and attractions around a central theme, often featuring multiple lands with different themes. Unlike temporary fairs and travelling carnivals, amusement parks are stationary and built for long-lasting operation.

History

Amusement parks evolved from European pleasure gardens, fairs, and picnic groves. Bakken near Copenhagen, opened in 1583, is the world's oldest operating amusement park, and Vienna's Prater (1766) became famous for its giant Ferris wheel. In the United States, trolley companies built "trolley parks" at the ends of their lines to generate weekend ridership; Coney Island in New York became the emblematic amusement district of the early 20th century. The opening of Disneyland in California in 1955, created by Walt Disney, transformed the industry with the fully themed park, and was followed by large chains such as Six Flags (founded 1961) and Cedar Fair.

Attractions

Common attractions include roller coasters — wooden and steel — Ferris wheels, carousels, log flumes and other water rides, dark rides, flat rides such as drop towers, and live shows and parades. Parks typically charge either per-ride fees or a single admission covering all rides, and are usually divided into themed areas.

Attendance and industry

The most visited amusement park in the world is Magic Kingdom at Walt Disney World in Florida, attracting roughly 17 million visitors annually. The industry is tracked by the TEA/AECOM Theme Index and represented by the International Association of Amusement Parks and Attractions (IAAPA), founded in 1918.
