Hare Krishna (mantra)

TraditionGaudiya Vaishnavism
LanguageSanskrit
SourceKali-Saṇṭāraṇa Upaniṣad
Also calledMahāmantra ("great mantra")

The Hare Krishna mantra, also called the Mahāmantra ("Great Mantra"), is a sixteen-word Vaishnava mantra in Sanskrit composed of the names Hare, Krishna, and Rama: "Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare; Hare Rama, Hare Rama, Rama Rama, Hare Hare."

Origins

The mantra appears in the Kali-Saṇṭāraṇa Upaniṣad and was popularised in the sixteenth century by the saint Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, who taught congregational chanting (sankirtana) as the principal spiritual practice for the age of Kali.

ISKCON and the West

In 1965 A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada brought the chanting to New York and founded the International Society for Krishna Consciousness (ISKCON). Public chanting by saffron-robed devotees became a familiar sight in Western cities in the late 1960s and 1970s.

Cultural influence

The mantra entered popular culture through George Harrison's hit song "My Sweet Lord" (1970), the musical Hair, and Allen Ginsberg's public chanting. Adherents typically chant the mantra on japa beads for a prescribed number of rounds daily.
