Schleswig (Danish: Slesvig; Low German: Sleswig) is a town in the northeastern part of Schleswig-Holstein, Germany, at the western end of the Schlei, an inlet of the Baltic Sea. It is the capital of the district of Schleswig-Flensburg and gave its name to the surrounding region, the Duchy of Schleswig.

History

The area is closely associated with the Viking Age trading port of Hedeby (Haithabu), founded around 770 on the opposite shore of the Schlei and destroyed in the 11th century. Schleswig itself grew around its cathedral, first mentioned in 947, and became an important medieval trading town and bishop's seat. Control of the town and duchy was contested for centuries between Denmark and German rulers, a struggle known as the Schleswig-Holstein Question. After the Second Schleswig War of 1864 the town passed to Prussia; a 1920 plebiscite divided the wider region between Germany and Denmark.

Sights

Schleswig Cathedral (St. Peter's) contains the famous Bordesholm Altar by Hans Brüggemann. Gottorf Castle, former residence of the dukes of Holstein-Gottorp, now houses state museums, including the archaeological collections from Hedeby and the Nydam boat. The old fishing quarter of Holm and the reconstructed Viking museum at Haithabu are popular attractions.
