Watchmen

Publisher: DC Comics
Dates: 1986–1987 (12 issues)
Creators: Alan Moore (writer), Dave Gibbons (artist), John Higgins (colorist)

Watchmen is a comic book limited series by writer Alan Moore, artist Dave Gibbons, and colorist John Higgins, published by DC Comics in 1986 and 1987. Set in an alternate history in which superheroes emerged in the 1940s and 1960s and the United States won the Vietnam War, the story follows aging, outlawed costumed vigilantes drawn into investigating the murder of one of their own as the world teeters on the brink of nuclear war between the United States and the Soviet Union.

Themes and significance

The series is a deconstruction of the superhero genre, examining the psychology of its characters — including Rorschach, Doctor Manhattan, Nite Owl, Silk Spectre, the Comedian, and Ozymandias — and exploring themes of power, morality, time, and Cold War anxiety. Its complex nine-panel grid structure, literary allusions, and supplementary prose material were widely praised.

Legacy

Watchmen is frequently cited as one of the greatest graphic novels ever produced; it won a Hugo Award in 1988 and appeared on Time magazine's list of the 100 best English-language novels published since 1923. It was adapted as a feature film in 2009 directed by Zack Snyder, and an acclaimed HBO television sequel series aired in 2019.
