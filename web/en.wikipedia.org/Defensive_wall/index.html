Defensive wall

A defensive wall is a fortification built to protect a city, settlement, or territory from potential aggressors. Defensive walls range from simple earthen ramparts and wooden palisades to massive masonry structures with towers, gates, and moats. In some places, such as China, walls were built on an enormous scale to defend entire frontiers, while in medieval Europe they typically encircled individual towns.

History

Some of the oldest known defensive walls date to the Neolithic period; the wall of Jericho, parts of which survive from around 8000 BCE, is among the earliest examples. Ancient Mesopotamian, Egyptian, Greek, and Roman cities all built substantial circuit walls. The Roman Empire constructed frontier works such as Hadrian's Wall in northern Britain and the Limes along the Rhine and Danube. In China, successive dynasties built rammed-earth and later stone-faced walls that were unified and extended under the Ming dynasty into the structure now known as the Great Wall of China, stretching thousands of kilometres.

Design and construction

Defensive walls were designed to delay and deter attackers and to give defenders elevated firing positions. Typical features include a thick curtain wall topped by a parapet and wall walk, projecting towers that allowed flanking fire along the face of the wall, fortified gatehouses at the points where roads entered, and external obstacles such as ditches and moats. Materials varied with local resources: stone, brick, timber, and packed earth were all used. With the spread of gunpowder artillery in the late medieval and early modern periods, high thin walls gave way to lower, thicker bastioned fortifications, and many obsolete city walls were demolished in the 18th and 19th centuries as cities expanded.

Modern examples

Defensive walls have also been built in the modern era, including separation barriers and border walls intended to control movement as much as to resist attack. Well-known historical examples of the 20th century include the Berlin Wall and the fortified lines of the two World Wars, such as the Maginot Line and the Atlantic Wall.
