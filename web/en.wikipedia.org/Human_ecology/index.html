Human ecology is an interdisciplinary field that studies the relationship between humans and their natural, social and built environments. It applies ecological principles — adaptation, competition, succession and interdependence — to human communities and populations, and draws on geography, sociology, anthropology, psychology and public health.

History

Ellen Swallow Richards used "human ecology" for the study of home environments in the 1890s, and the term was established in sociology by Robert E. Park and the Chicago School in the 1920s, who mapped urban social patterns ("natural areas") using ecological concepts. Amos Hawley's Human Ecology (1950) recast the field around population, organisation, environment and technology. Later strands include cultural ecology (Julian Steward), political ecology, and the ecosystem approaches of Eugene Odum and others.

Scope

Topics include settlement and urbanisation, migration, human adaptation to environments from the Arctic to the tropics, resource use and sustainability, disease ecology, and the ecological footprint of societies. Academic programmes (such as the College of the Atlantic's single-major model) and journals like Human Ecology (founded 1972) institutionalised the field.
