West Antarctic Ice Sheet

Location: West Antarctica
Sea-level equivalent: ~3.3 m
Major outlets: Thwaites Glacier, Pine Island Glacier
Status: Losing mass

The West Antarctic Ice Sheet rests largely on bedrock below sea level.

The West Antarctic Ice Sheet (WAIS) is the segment of the continental ice sheet that contains the ice over (and under) West Antarctica, the portion of Antarctica on the side of the Transantarctic Mountains that lies in the Western Hemisphere. The WAIS is classified as a marine-based ice sheet, meaning that its bed lies well below sea level and its margins form floating ice shelves.

Characteristics

Much of the WAIS is grounded on a bed that declines inland below sea level, a configuration vulnerable to marine ice sheet instability: retreat of the grounding line into deeper water can become self-sustaining. The ice sheet contains enough ice to raise global sea levels by roughly 3.3 metres if it melted entirely. Key drainage basins include the Amundsen Sea sector, where Thwaites Glacier and Pine Island Glacier have accelerated and thinned in recent decades, and the Ross and Filchner-Ronne ice shelves.

Warming and collapse risk

Satellite gravimetry and altimetry show the WAIS losing mass, contributing measurably to sea-level rise. Studies since the 1970s (notably by John Mercer) have warned that anthropogenic warming could trigger collapse of the ice sheet over centuries to millennia. The IPCC has assessed low-likelihood, high-impact storylines in which deep uncertainty in ice-sheet processes leads to substantially greater sea-level rise by 2100 and beyond.
