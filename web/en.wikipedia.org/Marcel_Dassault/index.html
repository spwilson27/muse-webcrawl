Marcel Dassault

Marcel Dassault
Born: 22 January 1892, Paris, France
Died: 27 April 1986 (aged 94), Neuilly-sur-Seine
Occupation: Aeronautical engineer, industrialist, politician
Known for: Founder of Dassault Aviation

Marcel Dassault (born Marcel Bloch; 22 January 1892 – 27 April 1986) was a French engineer and industrialist who spent his career in aircraft design and manufacturing. He was the founder of what became Dassault Aviation, the company behind the Mirage fighter jets and the Falcon business jets.

Early life and First World War

Born in Paris to a Jewish family of Alsatian origin, Marcel Bloch studied electrical engineering and then aeronautics. During the First World War he designed a highly efficient wooden propeller, the Éclair, fitted to many French aircraft. With Henry Potez he co-founded an aircraft company that built the SEA IV fighter.

Interwar years

In 1929 he founded the Société des Avions Marcel Bloch, producing mail planes, transports and the MB.200 bomber series used by the French Air Force in the Second World War.

Second World War and deportation

After the 1940 armistice, Bloch refused to collaborate with the German occupiers. He was arrested by the Vichy authorities and later deported by the Germans to Buchenwald concentration camp in August 1944. He survived, gravely ill, until the camp's liberation in April 1945.

Postwar career

On his return he converted to Catholicism and in 1949 changed his surname to Dassault, from the codename "Char d'assaut" (assault tank) used by his brother, General Darius Paul Bloch, in the Resistance. His postwar company built France's first jet fighters — the Ouragan, Mystère and the delta-winged Mirage III — making France a major exporter of combat aircraft.

Politics and later life

Dassault served for decades as a deputy in the National Assembly for the Oise department and was for a time the assembly's oldest member. He was embroiled in financial controversies late in life, including a major tax case. He died in 1986, one of France's richest men, and his company passed to his son Serge Dassault.
