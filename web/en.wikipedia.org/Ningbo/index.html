Ningbo

Ningbo (Chinese: 宁波; pinyin: Níngbō) is a sub-provincial city in northeastern Zhejiang province, China, on the south shore of Hangzhou Bay. Its metropolitan area is home to roughly 9.6 million people. Ningbo is one of China's oldest cities, with a history dating to the Hemudu culture of around 4800 BCE, and has been an important seaport for over a thousand years.

History

Known in the Tang and Song dynasties as Mingzhou, the city was a major port for trade with Japan, Korea, and Southeast Asia. After the First Opium War, Ningbo was opened to foreign trade as one of the five treaty ports under the Treaty of Nanking (1842). The Tianyi Pavilion, founded in 1561, is the oldest existing private library in China.

Economy

The Port of Ningbo-Zhoushan, formed by merging the Ningbo and Zhoushan ports, has been the world's busiest port by cargo tonnage since 2009 and among the busiest by container traffic. The city is a manufacturing hub for textiles, petrochemicals, machinery, and consumer goods. The 36-kilometre Hangzhou Bay Bridge, opened in 2008, links Ningbo directly with Shanghai.
