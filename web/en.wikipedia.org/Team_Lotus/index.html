Team Lotus

Team Lotus won seven Constructors' Championships between 1963 and 1978.

Team Lotus was the motorsport sister company of English sports-car manufacturer Lotus Cars and one of the most successful racing teams of all time. Active in Formula One from 1958 to 1994 under founder Colin Chapman, Lotus won seven Constructors' Championships and six Drivers' Championships and pioneered many innovations that reshaped the sport.

Innovation

Under Chapman's engineering leadership Lotus introduced the first monocoque chassis in F1 (Lotus 25, 1962), moved the engine behind the driver as standard practice, popularised wings and aerodynamic downforce, introduced commercial sponsorship liveries (Gold Leaf in 1968 and the iconic black-and-gold John Player Special scheme), the wedge-shaped Lotus 72 — one of the most successful F1 cars ever — and the ground-effect Lotus 79 of 1978.

Drivers

Jim Clark, regarded as among the greatest drivers ever, won two world championships with Lotus before his death at Hockenheim in 1968. Other Lotus champions were Graham Hill, Jochen Rindt (the sport's only posthumous champion, 1970), Emerson Fittipaldi, and Mario Andretti. In the 1980s the team fielded Nigel Mansell, Elio de Angelis, and a young Ayrton Senna, who took his first Grand Prix wins in a Lotus in 1985–1987.

Decline

After Chapman's sudden death in 1982 the team declined financially and competitively despite a final win at Detroit in 1987. Team Lotus folded at the end of the 1994 season. The Lotus name later returned to F1 with unrelated teams in 2010–2015.
