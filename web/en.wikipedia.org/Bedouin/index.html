Bedouin

RegionsArabian Peninsula, Levant, North Africa
Traditional livelihoodPastoral nomadism (camels, sheep, goats)
LanguageBedouin Arabic dialects

The Bedouin (from Arabic badawī, "desert dweller") are Arab pastoral nomads who have historically inhabited the desert regions of the Arabian Peninsula, the Levant, Mesopotamia, and North Africa.

Society

Bedouin society is organised in kinship-based tribes and clans, with strong traditions of hospitality, honour, oral poetry, and customary law. Herding groups traditionally moved seasonally between pastures, living in black tents woven from goat hair, while others combined herding with oasis agriculture or trade.

Modern era

Through the twentieth and twenty-first centuries, most Bedouin communities have settled, through both choice and government policy, in countries including Jordan, Israel (notably in the Negev), Saudi Arabia, Egypt (Sinai), and Syria, though nomadic and semi-nomadic traditions persist.
