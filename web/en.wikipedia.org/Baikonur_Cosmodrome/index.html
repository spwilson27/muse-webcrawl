The Baikonur Cosmodrome is a spaceport operated by Russia within Kazakhstan. Located in the Kazakh steppe about 200 km east of the Aral Sea and north of the Syr Darya river, it is the world's first spaceport and the largest operational launch facility. It was built by the Soviet Union in 1955 as a test range for intercontinental ballistic missiles and took the name "Baikonur" — borrowed from a mining town hundreds of kilometres away — as a deception to conceal its true location.

Historic firsts

From Baikonur were launched Sputnik 1, the first artificial satellite (4 October 1957), and Vostok 1 carrying Yuri Gagarin, the first human in space (12 April 1961) — the pad he used is still in service as "Gagarin's Start". Valentina Tereshkova, the first woman in space, also launched from Baikonur in 1963, as did the probes of the Luna, Venera and Mars programmes and the Buran shuttle's single flight in 1988.

Post-Soviet era

After the dissolution of the USSR in 1991 the cosmodrome found itself in independent Kazakhstan. Russia leases the site and the adjoining city of Baikonur under an agreement currently extending to 2050. Baikonur remained for a decade after the Space Shuttle's retirement (2011–2020) the only launch site for crewed flights to the International Space Station, using Soyuz rockets and spacecraft; Proton rockets also fly from Baikonur, with the joint Kazakh-Russian Baiterek complex planned for newer vehicles. Russia's newer Vostochny Cosmodrome in the Far East is intended to reduce dependence on Baikonur for some missions.
