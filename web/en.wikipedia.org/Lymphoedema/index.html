Lymphoedema

Lymphoedema
Also spelled: lymphedema
Cause: impaired lymphatic drainage
Types: primary (congenital) and secondary (acquired)
Treatment: compression, manual lymphatic drainage, exercise

Lymphoedema is a long-term condition in which excess lymphatic fluid collects in tissues, causing swelling, most often in an arm or leg. It results from damage to, or malformation of, the lymphatic system.

Causes

Primary lymphoedema is caused by developmental abnormalities of the lymph vessels and is rare. Secondary lymphoedema is much more common and follows cancer treatment involving lymph-node removal or radiotherapy, infection, or — worldwide most frequently — filariasis, a parasitic disease.

Management

There is no cure, but symptoms are managed with complete decongestive therapy: manual lymphatic drainage, multilayer compression bandaging and garments, skin care and remedial exercise. Untreated lymphoedema predisposes to recurrent cellulitis and progressive tissue change.
