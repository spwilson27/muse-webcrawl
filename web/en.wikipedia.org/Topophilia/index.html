Topophilia

Topophilia (from Greek topos, "place", and -philia, "love of") is a strong sense of place, attachment and identity bound up with particular environments and landscapes. The term was used by W. H. Auden and popularised by the geographer Yi-Fu Tuan in his 1974 book Topophilia: A Study of Environmental Perception, Attitudes, and Values.

Concept

Tuan explored how culture, memory, livelihood and the senses create affection for places, contrasting topophilia with topophobia, aversion to places. The concept has influenced human geography, environmental psychology, heritage studies and architecture, and relates to ideas such as sense of place, biophilia and the emotional bonds people form with homes, homelands and sacred landscapes.
