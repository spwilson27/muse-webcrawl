African American music


Jazz emerged from African American communities in New Orleans

African American music is an umbrella term covering a diverse range of musical genres largely developed by Black Americans and traces its origins in West Africa, where musical traditions were brought to the United States through the transatlantic slave trade. Many of the most influential popular music genres of the modern era — including spirituals, gospel, blues, jazz, rhythm and blues, rock and roll, soul, funk and hip hop — originated in African American communities.

History

Enslaved Africans preserved musical traditions through work songs, field hollers and spirituals that combined African rhythms and call-and-response patterns with European hymnody. Following emancipation, ragtime, blues and jazz flourished in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The Great Migration carried these styles to northern cities, where they evolved into swing, bebop, R&B and soul. In the 1970s funk and disco dominated dance music, and hip hop, born in the Bronx in 1973, grew to become the dominant global popular genre by the early 21st century.

Influence

African American music has profoundly shaped popular music worldwide, underpinning rock, pop, country, electronic dance music and much contemporary commercial songwriting and production.
