Falaise pocket

The Falaise pocket or battle of the Falaise pocket (German: Kessel von Falaise) (12–21 August 1944) was the decisive engagement of the Battle of Normandy in the Second World War. Allied forces formed a pocket around the German 7th Army and 5th Panzer Army south of the Norman town of Falaise, trapping some 50,000 German troops. Much of the encircled force was killed or captured, though tens of thousands escaped eastward before the pocket was fully closed.

Background

After the American breakout in Operation Cobra and the failed German counterattack at Mortain (Operation Lüttich), the German armies in Normandy risked encirclement between the advancing Americans to the south and British-Canadian forces pressing from the north around Falaise.

The battle

Polish armoured forces seized the strategic height of Mont Ormel, closing the pocket's last escape route on 19–20 August and holding it against desperate German breakout attempts. The fighting left the roads of the pocket choked with wrecked vehicles; General Eisenhower, touring the area, described walking "on nothing but dead and rotting flesh".

Aftermath

The destruction of the German forces west of the Seine allowed the Allies to advance rapidly and liberate Paris on 25 August 1944.
