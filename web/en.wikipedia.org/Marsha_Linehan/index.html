Marsha M. Linehan (born 5 May 1943) is an American psychologist and author. She is the creator of dialectical behavior therapy (DBT), a type of psychotherapy that combines cognitive-behavioral techniques with concepts of acceptance and mindfulness, originally developed to treat people with borderline personality disorder and chronic suicidal behaviour.

Career

Linehan earned her PhD in social and personality psychology from Loyola University Chicago in 1971. She spent most of her career at the University of Washington in Seattle, where she directed the Behavioral Research and Therapy Clinics and conducted the trials that established DBT's effectiveness.

Personal disclosure

In 2011, in a lecture at the Institute of Living in Hartford — where she had herself been hospitalised as a teenager — Linehan publicly disclosed her own history of severe mental illness and self-harm, explaining that her experience motivated her work with patients others considered untreatable. She told her story further in her 2020 memoir Building a Life Worth Living.
