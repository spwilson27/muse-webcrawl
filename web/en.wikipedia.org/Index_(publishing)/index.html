Index (publishing)

An index (plural: usually indexes, more rarely indices) is a list of words or phrases ("headings") and associated pointers ("locators") to where useful material relating to that heading can be found in a document or collection of documents. In a book, the index appears at the end and directs readers to pages where topics, names and concepts are discussed.

Structure

Entries are arranged alphabetically and may include subentries for narrower aspects of a topic, cross-references ("see" and "see also"), and locators that are page numbers, section numbers or paragraph identifiers.

Indexing

Good indexing is an intellectual, not merely mechanical, task: the indexer anticipates the terms readers will look up and analyses the text for concepts, which may be discussed without being named. Professional indexers are supported by specialist societies and software, though automatically generated word lists are generally considered inferior to human-made indexes.

History

Alphabetical subject indexes developed alongside printing in the early modern period and became a standard feature of scholarly and reference books.
