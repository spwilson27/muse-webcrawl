Hot-air balloon

A hot-air balloon is a lighter-than-air aircraft consisting of a bag, called an envelope, which contains heated air. Suspended beneath is a gondola or wicker basket, which carries passengers and a source of heat, in most cases a burner with an open flame. The heated air inside the envelope makes it buoyant because it has a lower density than the colder air outside. As with all aircraft, hot-air balloons cannot fly beyond the atmosphere; the envelope does not have to be sealed, as the pressure inside is the same as outside, but lift is generated by the temperature difference.

History

Unmanned hot-air balloons were used in China as early as the Three Kingdoms era (Kongming lanterns). The first verified manned flight took place in Paris on 21 November 1783, when Jean-François Pilâtre de Rozier and François Laurent d'Arlandes flew in a balloon built by the Montgolfier brothers, Joseph-Michel and Jacques-Étienne. Ballooning became a popular spectacle and technology of the 19th century, used for observation in warfare from the American Civil War to the First World War. The modern sport balloon was revived in the 1960s by Ed Yost, who introduced the propane burner and synthetic envelope fabrics.

Flight technique

The pilot controls altitude by heating the air in the envelope to climb and allowing it to cool—or venting it through a parachute valve at the top—to descend. Because the balloon moves with the wind, steering is achieved by changing altitude to find winds blowing in different directions at different heights. Flights usually take place in the calm conditions of early morning or late afternoon.

Records and festivals

Notable achievements include the first non-stop circumnavigation of the Earth by balloon, completed by Bertrand Piccard and Brian Jones in Breitling Orbiter 3 in 1999, and Fedor Konyukhov's solo circumnavigation in 2016. Large gatherings such as the Albuquerque International Balloon Fiesta in New Mexico draw hundreds of balloons and spectators.
