Wireless broadband

Wireless broadband is the delivery of high-speed internet access over a wireless connection rather than by wire or cable. It includes mobile broadband over cellular networks (4G LTE and 5G), fixed wireless access, satellite internet and point-to-point microwave links.

Forms

In fixed wireless access, a provider beams service from a tower or base station to an antenna at the customer's premises, common in rural areas where laying cable is expensive. Mobile broadband serves phones and hotspots from cellular networks. Satellite systems — geostationary services and low-Earth-orbit constellations such as Starlink — reach the most remote locations at the cost of higher latency or specialised terminals.

Considerations

Wireless broadband avoids trenching and can be deployed quickly, but capacity per user is shared, performance varies with signal conditions and congestion, and spectrum is a limited resource regulated by national authorities.
