Miss Marple, full name Jane Marple, is a fictional character created by the English writer Agatha Christie. She appears in twelve novels and twenty short stories. An elderly spinster living in the village of St Mary Mead, Miss Marple solves crimes that baffle the police through her shrewd knowledge of human nature, acquired — as she often explains — from a lifetime of observing village life and drawing parallels between suspects and people she has known.

Miss Marple first appeared in the novel The Murder at the Vicarage (1930) and last in Sleeping Murder, written during the Second World War but published in 1976 after Christie's death. Unlike Christie's other great detective, Hercule Poirot, she relies on intuition and analogy rather than forensic method, and her fluffy, apparently scatter-brained manner leads others to underestimate her formidable mind.

Adaptations

The character has been played on screen by Margaret Rutherford in a series of 1960s films, by Joan Hickson in the acclaimed BBC adaptations of all twelve novels, and by Geraldine McEwan and Julia McKenzie in ITV's Marple. She remains, with Poirot, one of the most famous detectives in crime fiction.
