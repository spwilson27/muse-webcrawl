Classics, also known as classical studies, is the branch of the humanities devoted to the study of the languages, literature, history, art and philosophy of ancient Greece and Rome. The discipline traditionally centres on the Greek and Latin languages and the texts written in them, from Homer and the earliest Greek inscriptions through to late antiquity. In the Western educational tradition, classics formed for centuries the core of higher learning.

Scope

Classical philology, the study of the texts, involves editing manuscripts, reconstructing fragmentary works, and analysing language and metre. Classical historians study the political and social history of the Greco-Roman world using literary sources, inscriptions and material evidence. Classical archaeology investigates sites and artefacts from Bronze Age Greece to the Roman Empire. The discipline also embraces ancient philosophy, the history of art and architecture, and the reception of antiquity in later culture.

History of the discipline

The study of classical texts was revived by Renaissance humanists such as Petrarch, who sought to recover and imitate ancient Latin style. In the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, German scholars — notably at Göttingen and Berlin — refashioned classics as a systematic science (Altertumswissenschaft), with figures such as F. A. Wolf and Ulrich von Wilamowitz-Moellendorff setting new standards of scholarship. Classics dominated elite education in Britain and its empire, and entrance to Oxford and Cambridge long required Latin and Greek.

Modern classics

Since the mid-twentieth century the field has broadened: many courses now teach ancient texts in translation, and scholarship has expanded toward social history, slavery, women and gender, and the cultures on the margins of the Greek and Roman world. Reception studies examine how later ages, down to modern film and politics, have remade the classical past. Although student numbers are smaller than at the discipline's height, classics continues as a research field worldwide.
