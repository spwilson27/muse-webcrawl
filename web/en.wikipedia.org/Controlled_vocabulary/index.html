Controlled vocabulary

Controlled vocabularies provide a way to organize knowledge for subsequent retrieval. They are used in subject indexing schemes, subject headings, thesauri, taxonomies and other knowledge organization systems. Controlled vocabulary schemes mandate the use of predefined, authorised terms that have been preselected by the designers of the schemes, in contrast to natural language vocabularies, where there is no restriction on the vocabulary.

Purpose

By prescribing a single preferred term for each concept, a controlled vocabulary overcomes problems of synonymy, where different words describe the same thing, and homography, where the same word describes different things. Indexers and searchers use the same authorised terms, improving both the precision and recall of information retrieval.

Examples

Well-known controlled vocabularies include the Library of Congress Subject Headings, the Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) used for indexing biomedical literature, and the Art & Architecture Thesaurus. In digital systems, controlled vocabularies underpin faceted search, metadata standards, and ontology development.

Types

Controlled vocabularies range in complexity from simple term lists and authority files through taxonomies with hierarchical relationships to fully fledged thesauri that also encode equivalence and associative relationships between terms. See also thesaurus and taxonomy.
