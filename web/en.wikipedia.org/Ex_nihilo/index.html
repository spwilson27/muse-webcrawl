Ex nihilo

Ex nihilo
LanguageLatin
Literal meaning"Out of nothing"

Ex nihilo is a Latin phrase meaning "out of nothing". It most often refers to the doctrine of creatio ex nihilo, "creation out of nothing", the belief that God created the universe from no pre-existing material. The phrase also appears in other contexts, such as law and philosophy.

Creatio ex nihilo

The doctrine that the world was created out of nothing became a central teaching of Judaism, Christianity and Islam. It contrasts both with creation from pre-existing chaotic matter, as in many ancient cosmogonies, and with the Greek philosophical maxim that nothing can come from nothing. The doctrine was articulated by early Christian writers such as Theophilus of Antioch and Irenaeus in the second century AD.

Other uses

In constitutional law, courts are sometimes said to create rights or doctrines "ex nihilo" when critics believe a ruling lacks grounding in the legal text. In discussions of value and property, creation ex nihilo is invoked in debates about intellectual property and wealth creation.
