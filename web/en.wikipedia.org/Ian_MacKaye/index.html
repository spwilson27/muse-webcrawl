Ian Thomas Garner MacKaye (born 16 April 1962) is an American singer, musician, and producer. He is best known as the frontman of the hardcore punk band Minor Threat and the post-hardcore band Fugazi, and as the founder of the independent label Dischord Records, which he has run from Washington, D.C. since 1980.

Career

MacKaye began playing in the Teen Idles and co-founded Dischord Records to release their record. With Minor Threat (1980–1983) he wrote "Straight Edge," whose title became the name of the straight edge movement advocating abstinence from alcohol, drugs, and promiscuous sex — a label MacKaye himself has treated ambivalently. After the short-lived Embrace, he formed Fugazi in 1987 with Guy Picciotto, Joe Lally, and Brendan Canty; the band became famous for its ethics, including low ticket prices and all-ages shows, and has been on hiatus since 2003. MacKaye later performed with the Evens and Coriky.

Dischord Records and ethos

Dischord has documented the Washington, D.C. punk scene for decades while keeping prices low and refusing major-label distribution practices. MacKaye is widely associated with the DIY ethic in independent music, favoring all-ages venues and resisting merchandising and corporate sponsorship.
