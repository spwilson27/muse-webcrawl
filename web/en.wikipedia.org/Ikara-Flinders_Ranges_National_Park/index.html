Ikara-Flinders Ranges National Park is a protected area in the Flinders Ranges of South Australia, located approximately 400 kilometres north of Adelaide. The park is renowned for its rugged mountain ranges, spectacular gorges, and abundant wildlife, and is centred on the natural amphitheatre of Wilpena Pound.

History

The area has been home to the Adnyamathanha people for tens of thousands of years. The name "Ikara", an Adnyamathanha word meaning "meeting place", was added to the park's name in 2016 in recognition of the traditional owners. The park was first proclaimed in 1945 as the Flinders Ranges National Park.

Geography

The park covers the central Flinders Ranges, including Wilpena Pound, a large natural amphitheatre formed by the edges of an ancient mountain range. Other features include Brachina Gorge, Bunyeroo Gorge, and the Heysen Range, named after the artist Hans Heysen.

Flora and fauna

Vegetation includes river red gums, native pines, and wildflower displays in spring. The park supports yellow-footed rock wallabies, red kangaroos, emus, and the wedge-tailed eagle. The ancient geology of the region includes fossils of the Ediacaran biota.
