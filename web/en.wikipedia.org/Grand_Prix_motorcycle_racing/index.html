Grand Prix motorcycle racing is the highest class of motorcycle road racing, sanctioned by the Fédération Internationale de Motocyclisme (FIM) since the inaugural world championship in 1949. The championship comprises three racing classes — MotoGP, Moto2 and Moto3 — contested on purpose-built prototype machines at circuits around the world.

Classes

MotoGP is the premier class, using 1000 cc four-stroke prototype motorcycles built by manufacturers such as Ducati, Honda, Yamaha, Aprilia and KTM. Moto2 uses a standardised 765 cc Triumph engine in prototype chassis, while Moto3 features 250 cc single-cylinder machines and typically the closest racing. The electric MotoE World Cup ran alongside the championship from 2019.

History

The post-war championship began with classes from 125 cc to 500 cc. The 500 cc class became MotoGP in 2002 with the switch from two-stroke to four-stroke engines. Riders such as Giacomo Agostini, Mike Hailwood, Valentino Rossi and Marc Márquez have defined eras of the sport; Agostini holds the record with 15 world titles across classes.

Race weekend

Events run over three days: practice and qualifying determine the grid, and since 2023 MotoGP weekends include a half-distance sprint race on Saturday in addition to the main Grand Prix on Sunday. Points are awarded to the top fifteen finishers.
