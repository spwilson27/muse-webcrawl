Daniel Inouye

Daniel Ken Inouye
Born: 7 September 1924, Honolulu, Hawaii, U.S.
Died: 17 December 2012, Washington, D.C., U.S.
Office: United States Senator from Hawaii (1963–2012)
Decorations: Medal of Honor, Presidential Medal of Freedom (posthumous)

Daniel Ken Inouye (September 7, 1924 – December 17, 2012) was an American politician and attorney who represented Hawaii in the United States Congress from statehood in 1959 until his death — first as the state's first U.S. representative, then from 1963 as a U.S. senator. He was the second-longest-serving senator in U.S. history at the time of his death and served as President pro tempore of the Senate from 2010 to 2012.

Military service

Inouye served in the U.S. Army's 442nd Regimental Combat Team, the famed Japanese-American unit, during the Second World War. In April 1945 in Italy, he destroyed multiple German positions despite severe wounds, losing his right arm to a grenade; his actions were initially awarded the Distinguished Service Cross and were upgraded to the Medal of Honor in 2000. The injury ended his ambition to become a surgeon and redirected him to law and politics.

Political career

A Democrat, Inouye rose to the chairmanship of the Senate Appropriations Committee and played prominent national roles on the Senate Watergate Committee in 1973 and as chairman of the Senate select committee investigating the Iran-Contra affair in 1987. He was posthumously awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2013, and Honolulu's international airport was renamed Daniel K. Inouye International Airport in his honour in 2017.
