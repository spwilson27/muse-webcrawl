Swing state

Image placeholder: swing states in a recent US presidential election.

In United States politics, a swing state (also known as a battleground state or purple state) is a state that can reasonably be won by either the Democratic Party or the Republican Party candidate in a statewide election, most often referring to presidential elections. Because of the Electoral College system, candidates concentrate their campaigning, advertising and visits in these competitive states rather than in states considered safe for either party.

Characteristics

Swing states are identified by close election margins and opinion polling. States such as Pennsylvania, Michigan, Wisconsin, Arizona, Georgia, Nevada and North Carolina have been central battlegrounds in recent cycles, while former swing states such as Ohio and Florida have trended more reliably Republican and Colorado and Virginia more reliably Democratic.

Role in campaigns

The winner-take-all allocation of electoral votes in nearly all states means a few thousand votes in a swing state can decide the presidency, as happened in 2000 and 2016. Campaigns tailor policy messages, field operations and advertising spending to these states, and voters there receive disproportionate attention. Related concepts include the undecided swing vote.
