Coxsackievirus

Coxsackievirus
Family: Picornaviridae
Genus: Enterovirus
Groups: A (23 serotypes), B (6 serotypes)
Genome: Positive-sense single-stranded RNA
Named after: Coxsackie, New York

Coxsackieviruses are a few related enteroviruses that belong to the family Picornaviridae of nonenveloped, positive-sense single-stranded RNA viruses. They are divided into group A (23 serotypes) and group B (6 serotypes), originally distinguished by the patterns of disease they caused in suckling mice. The viruses are named after the town of Coxsackie, New York, where Gilbert Dalldorf and Grace Sickles first isolated them from fecal samples in 1948 while investigating a polio outbreak.

Diseases

Coxsackie A viruses are best known for hand, foot and mouth disease (mainly A16 and A6) and herpangina, as well as viral conjunctivitis. Coxsackie B viruses cause epidemic pleurodynia (Bornholm disease) and, more seriously, myocarditis and pericarditis, which can be fatal in newborns. Both groups can cause aseptic meningitis and nonspecific febrile illnesses, especially in summer and early autumn. Coxsackie B infection has long been investigated — though not proven — as a possible environmental trigger of type 1 diabetes.

Transmission and treatment

The viruses spread by the fecal–oral route and by respiratory secretions, survive well in the environment, and disproportionately infect children. There is no specific antiviral treatment or vaccine for coxsackieviruses in general; care is supportive, and prevention relies on hand hygiene and disinfection. (A vaccine against the related enterovirus A71, a major cause of severe hand, foot and mouth disease in Asia, is used in China but does not cover coxsackieviruses.)
