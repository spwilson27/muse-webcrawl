Marek Edelman

Marek Edelman (1 January 1922 – 2 October 2009) was a Polish political and social activist and cardiologist. He was the last surviving leader of the Warsaw Ghetto Uprising of 1943, in which he served as deputy commander of the Jewish Combat Organization (ŻOB). After the war he became a prominent cardiologist in Łódź and a leading figure in the Solidarity movement and Poland's democratic opposition.

Early life and the ghetto

Edelman was born in Homel (in present-day Belarus) and raised in Warsaw, where his parents were activists in the Jewish socialist Bund. After the German invasion of Poland he worked as a messenger at the ghetto hospital. In 1942 he co-founded the ŻOB, and during the uprising of April–May 1943 he led fighters in the brushmakers' area. After the uprising was crushed he escaped through the sewers. He later fought in the Warsaw Uprising of 1944 with the Polish People's Army.

Postwar life

Unlike most survivors, Edelman remained in Poland. He studied medicine and became a cardiologist at the Pirogov Hospital in Łódź, where he developed innovative approaches to treating heart failure. In 1976 he co-founded the Workers' Defence Committee (KOR) and later advised Solidarity; he was interned after martial law was declared in 1981. In 1989 he took part in the Round Table talks and subsequently served in the Sejm.

Legacy

Every year on the anniversary of the ghetto uprising, Edelman placed daffodils at the Monument to the Ghetto Heroes in Warsaw — a custom continued in his memory by volunteers across Poland. He received Poland's highest honour, the Order of the White Eagle, and France's Legion of Honour. He died in Warsaw in 2009.
