Upper Peninsula of Michigan

The Upper Peninsula of Michigan, commonly called the U.P., is the northern of Michigan's two peninsulas, bordered by Lake Superior, Lake Michigan and Lake Huron. It is sparsely populated, heavily forested and known for mining, logging and winter sports.

Geography

Major features include the Keweenaw Peninsula, Porcupine Mountains, Pictured Rocks National Lakeshore and Isle Royale ferry access points. Marquette is the largest city.

History

The region was awarded to Michigan after the Toledo War compromise. Copper and iron mining drove settlement by Cornish, Finnish and other immigrants.

Culture

Residents are called Yoopers. Pasty shops, snowmobiling, and the Mackinac Bridge linking the peninsulas to St. Ignace are regional icons.
