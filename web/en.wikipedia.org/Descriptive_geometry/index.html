Descriptive geometry

Descriptive geometry is the branch of geometry concerned with representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions by means of standardised projection methods, so that spatial problems can be solved graphically on the drawing. It was systematised by the French mathematician Gaspard Monge in the late 18th century and became a foundation of engineering drawing.

Monge's method

Monge's method uses two perpendicular projection planes (horizontal and vertical) onto which a point or object is orthogonally projected; one plane is then rotated into the other, giving a pair of linked views from which true lengths, angles and intersections of solids can be constructed.

Applications

Descriptive geometry underlies technical drawing, architecture, and stereotomy (stonecutting), and historically was taught in engineering schools worldwide. Its concepts survive in modern CAD software, which automates the projections that drafters once constructed by hand.
