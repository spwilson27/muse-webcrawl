Guildford

Guildford is a town in west Surrey, England, around 27 miles (43 km) southwest of central London. As the county town of Surrey, it is the seat of the borough of Guildford and the administrative headquarters of Surrey County Council.

History

Guildford has Saxon origins and a medieval castle, Guildford Castle, whose keep and gardens survive. The town grew prosperous on the wool trade and as a coaching stop on the London–Portsmouth road, and expanded further with the coming of the railway in 1845.

Landmarks and institutions

The High Street is paved with setts and lined by the Guildhall with its prominent clock. Guildford Cathedral, consecrated in 1961, stands on Stag Hill, also home to the University of Surrey. The River Wey runs through the town, which is a notable retail and commuter centre.
