The 3. Liga is the third division of professional football in Germany and the lowest division administered nationwide by the German Football Association (DFB). It was introduced for the 2008–09 season, replacing the two regional Regionalligas as the third tier, and sits below the Bundesliga and the 2. Bundesliga in the German football league system.

History

The DFB created the single-division 3. Liga to close the gap — financial and sporting — between the fully professional second tier and the semi-professional regional leagues, and to give the reserve teams of professional clubs a defined place in the pyramid. The founding season assembled qualifiers from the old Regionalliga Nord and Süd. Union Berlin won the inaugural championship, and the division quickly produced future Bundesliga clubs, including RB Leipzig and SC Paderborn, who passed through on their way up.

Format

Twenty clubs play each other twice, home and away, for a 38-match season. The top two teams are promoted automatically to the 2. Bundesliga; the third-placed team contests a two-legged play-off against the 16th-placed team of the 2. Bundesliga. The bottom four teams are relegated to the Regionalliga. Reserve ("II") teams of Bundesliga clubs are permitted but cannot be promoted to the 2. Bundesliga. The champions of the era include Dynamo Dresden, Arminia Bielefeld, VfL Osnabrück and 1860 Munich, clubs whose large fanbases give the league unusually strong attendances for a third tier — regularly averaging around 8,000–10,000 spectators per match, among the highest third-division figures in world football.

Notable features

The league is known for its competitiveness and its mix of former Bundesliga names, ambitious regional clubs and professional reserve sides. Several of its players and coaches have progressed to the top flight, and its play-off has produced a series of dramatic finals, reinforcing the 3. Liga's reputation as the strongest fully professional third division in Europe.
