Drug cartel

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A drug cartel is a criminal organization composed of independent drug lords who collude with each other in order to improve their profits and dominate the illegal drug trade. Drug cartels are not technically cartels in the economic sense — holding and controlling an illegal drug market is not the same as a legal cartel arrangement — but the term has stuck because of the loose alliances and price coordination that some groups have practiced.

History

The term became prominent in the 1980s with Colombia's Medellín Cartel and Cali Cartel, which dominated the international cocaine trade. After the dismantling of the large Colombian organizations in the 1990s, Mexican trafficking organizations — including the Sinaloa, Gulf, Juárez, and Tijuana cartels — grew in power, controlling smuggling routes into the United States. Fragmentation of these groups under enforcement pressure has produced numerous successor factions and heightened violence in Mexico since the mid-2000s.

Structure

Cartels typically consist of cells handling distinct functions: production, transport, distribution, enforcement ("sicarios"), and money laundering. Leadership structures vary from centralized hierarchies to loose federations of allied trafficking families. Corruption of officials and infiltration of legitimate businesses are common supporting activities.

Countermeasures

Governments respond with domestic law enforcement, military operations, intelligence sharing, extradition, asset forfeiture, and international agreements. In the United States, the Drug Enforcement Administration leads federal anti-cartel efforts; several cartels have been designated as foreign terrorist organizations by the U.S. government in 2025.
