Love and Rockets are an English alternative rock band formed in 1985 by former Bauhaus members Daniel Ash (vocals, guitar), David J (bass, vocals) and Kevin Haskins (drums). Named after the comic book series Love and Rockets by the Hernandez brothers, the band moved away from Bauhaus's gothic rock toward psychedelic, folk and electronic influences, scoring an international hit with "So Alive" in 1989.

History

Formation and early albums (1985–1987)

After Bauhaus split in 1983, Ash and Haskins recorded as Tones on Tail, while David J pursued a solo career. The three reunited in 1985 — without Bauhaus singer Peter Murphy — as Love and Rockets, debuting with a cover of the Temptations' "Ball of Confusion". Their debut album Seventh Dream of Teenage Heaven (1985) was a psychedelic work; Express (1986) yielded the US college-radio hit "All in My Mind", and Earth, Sun, Moon (1987) featured the single "No New Tale to Tell".

Mainstream success (1988–1990)

The self-titled Love and Rockets album (1989), recorded after a fire destroyed their studio equipment and pushed them toward a leaner rock and electronic sound, produced "So Alive", which reached number 3 on the US Billboard Hot 100 and topped the Modern Rock Tracks chart. The album went gold in the United States.

Electronic turn and dissolution (1990–1999)

The band embraced dance and electronic music on Hot Trip to Heaven (1994) and Sweet F.A. (1996). Lift (1998) was their final studio album; the group disbanded in 1999 as the members turned to solo work and a Bauhaus reunion.

Reunions

Love and Rockets re-formed for festival appearances in 2007–2009, including Coachella, and again in 2023 for an extensive North American tour beginning at the Cruel World festival in Pasadena, California — their first full tour in decades.
