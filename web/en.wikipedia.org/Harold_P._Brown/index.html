Harold P. Brown

Harold Pitney Brown (1869–1932) was an American electrical engineer and inventor best known for designing the first electric chair, used for executions in New York State from 1890. Working in the context of the "war of the currents," he campaigned to demonstrate the dangers of alternating current (AC) electricity.

The electric chair

In 1888, following New York's decision to replace hanging with electrocution, Brown conducted public experiments in which animals were killed with alternating current, seeking to show that AC was more dangerous than the direct current promoted by Thomas Edison. He was commissioned to design an execution device, and the first electric chair was built at Auburn Prison. It was first used on William Kemmler in August 1890.

Controversy

Brown's demonstrations, some conducted with Edison's tacit support, were part of a public-relations struggle between Edison's DC system and the AC system of George Westinghouse. Historians debate the extent of Edison's direct involvement in Brown's work.
