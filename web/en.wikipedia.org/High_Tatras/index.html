High Tatras

The High Tatras or High Tatra Mountains (Slovak: Vysoké Tatry; Polish: Tatry Wysokie) are a mountain range along the border between northern Slovakia and southern Poland. They are part of the Eastern Tatras within the Carpathian Mountains and are the highest range in the entire Carpathian arc.

Geography

The range extends about 26 km along the frontier, with more than two dozen peaks exceeding 2,500 metres. The highest is Gerlachovský štít (2,655 m), the highest point in Slovakia and in the Carpathians; other major summits include Lomnický štít (2,634 m) and Rysy (2,503 m), the highest peak in Poland. Glacial valleys hold more than a hundred mountain lakes (tarns), such as Štrbské pleso and Morskie Oko.

Protection and tourism

The Slovak side has been protected as Tatra National Park (TANAP) since 1949 and the Polish side as Tatrzański Park Narodowy since 1954; together they form a UNESCO transboundary biosphere reserve. The area is a major centre for hiking, climbing, and skiing. Resorts such as Tatranská Lomnica, Starý Smokovec, and Štrbské Pleso are linked by the Tatra Electric Railway. On 19 November 2004, a catastrophic windstorm (the Alžbeta bora) felled trees across some 12,000 hectares of the Slovak Tatras, reshaping the forest landscape for decades.

Ecology

The range is home to the Tatra chamois, marmots, lynx, wolves, and brown bears, and preserves endemic plant species adapted to its alpine environment. The region's resorts on the Slovak side are administered by the town of Vysoké Tatry, formed from merged settlements.
