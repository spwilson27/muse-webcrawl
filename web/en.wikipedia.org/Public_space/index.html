Public space

A public space is a place that is generally open and accessible to people. Roads (including the pavement), public squares, parks, and beaches are typically considered public space. Government buildings which are open to the public, such as public libraries, are also public space, though they generally have restricted areas and greater limits upon use.

Types

Public spaces include streets, squares and plazas, parks, playgrounds, markets and waterfronts. Privately owned public spaces, sometimes called POPS, are spaces owned privately but legally required to be open to the public, common in cities such as New York.

Rights in public space

Public spaces have historically been venues for political protest, free speech and assembly. Debates continue over behaviors permitted in public space, the exclusion of homeless people through hostile architecture and anti-homeless ordinances, and the increasing surveillance of public areas.

Design

Urban designers such as Jan Gehl and William H. Whyte studied how people use public space and advocated design that encourages social life, lingering and informal activity.
