Boaz

Boaz (Hebrew: בֹּעַז, Bōʿaz) is a biblical figure appearing in the Book of Ruth in the Hebrew Bible. A wealthy landowner of Bethlehem belonging to the tribe of Judah, he is portrayed as a man of standing and kindness who acts as a "kinsman-redeemer" for the widowed Ruth and her mother-in-law Naomi.

Narrative

In the story, the Moabite widow Ruth comes to glean in Boaz's fields during the barley harvest. Boaz, a relative of Ruth's late husband, instructs his workers to leave extra grain for her and to protect her. At Naomi's prompting, Ruth appeals to him at the threshing floor; Boaz, after first ensuring that a closer relative declines the duty, publicly redeems Naomi's family land and marries Ruth. Their son Obed becomes the grandfather of King David, placing Boaz and Ruth in the genealogy of David and, in the New Testament, of Jesus.

Other references

The name Boaz is also given to the left-hand of the two bronze pillars that stood at the entrance of Solomon's Temple in Jerusalem, its counterpart being Jachin. In rabbinic tradition Boaz is identified with the judge Ibzan and is held up as a model of generosity and righteousness.
