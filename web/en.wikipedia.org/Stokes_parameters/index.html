Stokes parameters

The Stokes parameters are a set of four values that describe the polarization state of electromagnetic radiation. They were introduced by George Gabriel Stokes in 1852. Unlike descriptions based on the electric field's amplitude and phase, the Stokes parameters are defined in terms of measurable intensities, and they can describe partially polarized and unpolarized light as well as fully polarized light.

Definition

For a quasi-monochromatic beam, the parameters are conventionally written I, Q, U and V. I is the total intensity. Q describes the preponderance of linear polarization in the horizontal over the vertical direction; U describes linear polarization at +45° over −45°; and V describes the preponderance of right- over left-handed circular polarization. For fully polarized light, Q² + U² + V² = I²; for partial polarization the sum of squares is less than I², and the degree of polarization is √(Q² + U² + V²)/I.

Poincaré sphere and Mueller calculus

The normalized parameters (Q/I, U/I, V/I) can be plotted as a point on or inside the Poincaré sphere, with linear polarizations on the equator and circular polarizations at the poles. The effect of an optical element on the Stokes vector is represented by a 4×4 real Mueller matrix, making the formalism the standard tool of polarimetry in astronomy, remote sensing and optics.
