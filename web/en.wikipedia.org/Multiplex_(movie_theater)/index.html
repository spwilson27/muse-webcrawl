Multiplex (movie theater)

A multiplex is a movie theater complex with multiple screens within a single complex. They are usually housed in specially designed buildings — often in shopping malls or entertainment centres — with shared lobbies, box offices, concession stands and restrooms serving all auditoriums. Complexes with a very large number of screens, conventionally twenty or more, are known as megaplexes.

History

The concept developed as exhibitors divided older picture palaces and built twin theatres in the 1960s; Stanley Durwood of American Multi-Cinema (AMC) is often credited with pioneering the modern multiplex in Kansas City in 1963 with Parkway Twin. Multiplexes spread rapidly in the 1970s–1990s as suburban shopping centres grew, reaching very large formats such as Ontario's Colossus and the 30-screen megaplexes of the 1990s. The format offered audiences wide film choice and staggered showtimes while letting operators share staff and projection equipment.

Characteristics

Modern multiplexes feature stadium seating, digital projection, premium large formats (such as IMAX-within-multiplex screens), reserved recliner seating, expanded food service and, in the streaming era, event cinema such as concerts and sports broadcasts. The multiplex model reshaped film distribution, favouring wide opening weekends for blockbuster titles.
