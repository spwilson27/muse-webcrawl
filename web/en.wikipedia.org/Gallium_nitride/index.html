Gallium nitride

Gallium nitride (GaN) is a binary III/V direct bandgap semiconductor with a wide bandgap of about 3.4 eV. It crystallizes in the wurtzite structure and is noted for its hardness, high thermal conductivity relative to other semiconductors, and ability to operate at high temperatures, voltages, and frequencies.

Optoelectronics

GaN and its alloys with indium and aluminium (InGaN, AlGaN) made possible efficient blue and ultraviolet light-emitting diodes and laser diodes. The development of bright blue LEDs by Isamu Akasaki, Hiroshi Amano, and Shuji Nakamura — recognized by the 2014 Nobel Prize in Physics — completed the set of primary colors needed for full-color displays and white LED lighting, since earlier LEDs could not produce blue light efficiently.

Power and radio-frequency electronics

Because of its high electron mobility and breakdown field, GaN is used in high-electron-mobility transistors (HEMTs) for radar, satellite communications, and 5G base stations. In power electronics, GaN transistors switch faster and with lower losses than silicon devices, enabling smaller, more efficient chargers and power converters — one reason compact fast phone chargers based on GaN became common in the late 2010s.

Challenges

Growing large, low-defect GaN crystals is difficult; most devices are built by epitaxy on sapphire, silicon carbide, or silicon substrates, and producing reliable p-type doping was a key historical obstacle overcome in the late 1980s.
