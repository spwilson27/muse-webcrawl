The HAL Rudra, also known as the ALH-WSI (Advanced Light Helicopter – Weapon System Integrated), is the armed version of the HAL Dhruv utility helicopter, designed and manufactured by India's Hindustan Aeronautics Limited. The Rudra is distinguished from the dedicated HAL Prachand light combat helicopter: it retains the Dhruv's utility cabin and side-by-side cockpit while adding integrated weapons, sensors and self-protection systems.

Design

The Rudra (ALH Mk IV) is fitted with a chin-mounted 20 mm Nexter turret gun, stub wings carrying 70 mm rocket pods, Mistral air-to-air missiles and the Dhruvastra/Helina anti-tank missile. It carries an electro-optical targeting pod, helmet-mounted sight, electronic warfare suite with radar and missile warning receivers, and armoured seats for the crew. Power comes from two Shakti engines.

Service

The first Rudras were delivered to the Indian Army in 2013, and the type equips Army Aviation attack squadrons; the Indian Air Force also operates the type. Rudras have been deployed in high-altitude areas and in support of ground forces in counter-insurgency roles. The Indian Army has planned for several squadrons totalling around 60–70 aircraft.

Relationship to the Dhruv family

The Rudra shares engines, transmission and rotor systems with the Dhruv, simplifying logistics. Its development ran in parallel with the purpose-built Prachand, with the Rudra filling the armed-escort and armed-reconnaissance roles while the Prachand specialises in attack missions.
