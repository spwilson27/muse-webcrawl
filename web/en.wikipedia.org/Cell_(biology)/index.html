Cell (biology)

The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of all known living organisms. It is the smallest unit of life. Cells are often called the "building blocks of life". The study of cells is called cell biology, cellular biology, or cytology.

Types

Cells are of two main types: prokaryotic cells, in bacteria and archaea, which lack a membrane-bound nucleus; and eukaryotic cells, in animals, plants, fungi, and protists, which contain a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles such as mitochondria, chloroplasts (in plants), and the endomembrane system.

Structure and function

All cells are bounded by a cell membrane and contain cytoplasm, ribosomes for protein synthesis, and genetic material (DNA). Eukaryotic cells regulate their activities through the cell cycle, divide by mitosis or meiosis, obtain energy by metabolism, and respond to their environment.

History

Cells were first observed by Robert Hooke in 1665, who named them for their resemblance to monks' cells. Cell theory, developed by Schleiden, Schwann, and Virchow in the 19th century, states that all organisms are composed of cells, that the cell is the basic unit of life, and that all cells arise from pre-existing cells.
