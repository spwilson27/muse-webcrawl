Harvard Business Review (HBR) is a general management magazine published by Harvard Business Publishing, a wholly owned subsidiary of Harvard University. Founded in 1922 by Harvard Business School, it is one of the most influential business publications in the world, aimed at senior managers, executives, and management scholars.

History

The magazine was launched in 1922, shortly after the founding of Harvard Business School, with the goal of giving business practitioners access to the school's research and ideas. Over the decades it became a primary channel through which management concepts — from scientific management and human relations to strategy, leadership, and innovation — reached practicing managers. Landmark articles published in HBR include Peter Drucker's writings on management practice, Michael Porter's work on competitive strategy ("What Is Strategy?"), and Clayton Christensen's introduction of disruptive innovation ("Disruptive Technologies: Catching the Wave", 1995).

Content and influence

HBR publishes articles by academics, consultants, and business leaders, alongside case studies, interviews, and the magazine's well-known fictional case feature. It appears in print several times a year and maintains a large digital presence at hbr.org, with podcasts, webinars, and data interactives. Editions are licensed in many languages and countries.

The magazine has been criticized at times for promoting management fads and for the tension between scholarly rigor and readability, but its role in popularizing concepts such as core competence, the balanced scorecard, emotional intelligence in leadership, and blue ocean strategy is widely acknowledged.

Harvard Business Publishing

Harvard Business Publishing also distributes Harvard Business School case studies, books through Harvard Business Review Press, and corporate learning programs. The magazine's best-known book titles include HBR's 10 Must Reads series.
