Edward II of England

Edward II (25 April 1284 – 21 September 1327), also called Edward of Caernarfon, was King of England from 1307 until his deposition in January 1327 — the first English monarch to be formally deposed. His reign was marked by military failure in Scotland, bitter conflict with his barons over his favourites, and eventual overthrow by his wife, Isabella of France, and her ally Roger Mortimer.

Edward II's tomb effigy at Gloucester Cathedral

Early life

The youngest surviving son of Edward I, he was born at Caernarfon Castle during his father's conquest of Wales and in 1301 became the first English heir apparent created Prince of Wales. His close friendship with the Gascon knight Piers Gaveston alarmed his father, who temporarily exiled Gaveston.

Reign

On succeeding in 1307, Edward recalled Gaveston and made him Earl of Cornwall, provoking the barons who issued the Ordinances of 1311 restricting royal power; Gaveston was captured and executed by them in 1312. In 1314 Edward suffered a catastrophic defeat by Robert the Bruce at the Battle of Bannockburn, ending hopes of subduing Scotland. A second favourite, Hugh Despenser the Younger, accumulated land and power in the 1320s, driving the king's opponents into rebellion and civil war.

Deposition and death

In 1326 Isabella and Mortimer invaded from France, Despenser was executed, and Edward was captured in Wales. In January 1327 Parliament declared him deposed in favour of his son, Edward III. He died at Berkeley Castle that September, probably murdered; a famous later legend about the manner of his killing is now doubted by historians. His tomb at Gloucester Cathedral became a pilgrimage site.
