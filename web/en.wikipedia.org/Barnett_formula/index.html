Barnett formula

The Barnett formula is a mechanism used by the Treasury in the United Kingdom to automatically adjust the amounts of public expenditure allocated to Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland to reflect changes in spending levels allocated to public services in England, England and Wales, or Great Britain, as appropriate.

Operation and history

The formula was devised in 1978 by Joel Barnett, then Chief Secretary to the Treasury, as a temporary measure ahead of planned devolution. It allocates changes in funding in proportion to population: when spending on a comparable ("Barnettised") service in England rises or falls, each devolved administration receives a population-based share of the change. Crucially, it applies only to changes, not to the underlying baseline, which derives from historic spending levels. From 1999 it was applied to the newly devolved Scottish Parliament, Welsh Assembly (now Senedd), and Northern Ireland Assembly.

Criticism

The formula is controversial: critics argue it perpetuates higher per-capita spending in Scotland unrelated to need, produces the "Barnett squeeze" (convergence of per-capita spending over time), and lacks any statutory basis or needs assessment. Successive governments have retained it, though funding floors for Wales were introduced following the Holtham Commission.
