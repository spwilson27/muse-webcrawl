Noise rock

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Noise rock (sometimes called noise punk) is a noise-oriented style of experimental rock that first emerged in the 1980s, fusing punk rock with the abrasive textures of industrial music and noise. Bands make use of extreme distortion, feedback, dissonance, atonality and unconventional song structures, often rejecting conventional melody altogether — in contrast to the related, more melodic noise pop.

History

The genre's roots lie in the Velvet Underground's feedback experiments, the Stooges, free jazz, and the no wave scene of late-1970s New York, where bands such as DNA and Mars pushed rock towards noise. Sonic Youth, Swans and Big Black defined the 1980s American noise rock sound, alongside Butthole Surfers, The Jesus Lizard, Scratch Acid and Pussy Galore. Glenn Branca's guitar symphonies and Rhys Chatham's massed-guitar works bridged the genre with avant-garde composition.

Later developments

In the 1990s and 2000s, bands such as Lightning Bolt, Boredoms, Melt-Banana, Deerhoof, HEALTH, Metz and Daughters carried the style forward, while labels including Touch and Go, Amphetamine Reptile and Skin Graft became synonymous with it. Noise rock strongly influenced grunge, industrial rock, math rock and shoegaze. See also no wave, industrial music and post-punk.
