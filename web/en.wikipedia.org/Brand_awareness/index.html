Brand awareness

Brand awareness is the extent to which consumers are able to recall or recognise a brand under different conditions. It is one of the foundational concepts of marketing and brand management, reflecting how familiar a target audience is with a brand and its products or services.

Levels of awareness

Researchers commonly distinguish a hierarchy: at the lowest level, a consumer simply recognises the brand when shown its name or logo (aided awareness or brand recognition); higher up, the consumer can recall the brand unaided when given the product category (brand recall); and at the top, the brand is the first one that comes to mind — "top-of-mind awareness." David Aaker's brand-equity framework treats awareness as a core asset alongside loyalty, perceived quality and associations.

Measurement and strategy

Awareness is measured through surveys asking for unaided and aided recall, and through metrics such as share of search and social-media mentions. Firms build awareness with advertising reach and frequency, distinctive brand assets (colours, logos, jingles, mascots), sponsorships, public relations and consistent packaging — ideally so that the brand comes to mind at the moment of purchase in its category.

Importance

Familiar brands benefit from the mere-exposure effect: consumers tend to prefer products they recognise, and awareness is usually a prerequisite for consideration and purchase, especially for low-involvement goods.
