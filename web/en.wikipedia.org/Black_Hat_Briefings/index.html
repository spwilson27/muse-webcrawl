Black Hat Briefings

The Black Hat Briefings (commonly referred to as Black Hat) are a series of computer security conferences that bring together researchers, security professionals, and representatives of government agencies and industry. Founded in 1997 by Jeff Moss, who also founded DEF CON, Black Hat is best known for its flagship event held each summer in Las Vegas, Nevada, immediately before DEF CON.

Conference format

The conference consists of paid training sessions taught by security practitioners, followed by the Briefings themselves, at which researchers present new vulnerabilities, attack techniques, and defensive methods, often accompanied by live demonstrations and tool releases. Presentations are selected through a call for papers reviewed by an independent review board. Other events are held in locations including Abu Dhabi (Black Hat MEA), London (Black Hat Europe), Singapore (Black Hat Asia), and Riyadh.

Notable presentations

Black Hat talks have disclosed widely publicized vulnerabilities, including flaws in industrial control systems, medical devices, voting machines, automobiles, and mobile platforms. Some presentations have been controversial, occasionally being withdrawn under legal or corporate pressure, which has itself drawn attention to the research.

Ownership

Black Hat was acquired by CMP Media (later part of Informa) in 2005. The event has grown from a single gathering of the hacker underground into one of the largest professional information-security conferences in the world, attended by tens of thousands of participants.
