Querétaro

Querétaro, officially the Free and Sovereign State of Querétaro (Spanish: Estado Libre y Soberano de Querétaro), is one of the 32 federal entities of Mexico. It is divided into 18 municipalities. Its capital city is Santiago de Querétaro, located about 213 kilometres (132 mi) northwest of Mexico City. The state is known for its colonial history, aqueduct, aerospace and automotive industries, and its dramatic Sierra Gorda mountains.

History

The region was inhabited in pre-Hispanic times by Otomi and Chichimeca peoples; the name derives from the Purépecha K'erhiretarhu, "place of the ball game". Santiago de Querétaro was founded in 1531 and grew wealthy on mining and agriculture, becoming one of New Spain's most important colonial cities, famed for its baroque churches and the great aqueduct built in the 1730s under the Marqués de la Villa del Villar del Águila.

Querétaro played central roles in Mexican history: the conspiracy that launched the independence movement was organised there in 1810; the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo ending the Mexican–American War was signed there in 1848; Emperor Maximilian I was executed on the Cerro de las Campanas in 1867 after the siege of Querétaro; and the Mexican Constitution of 1917 was promulgated at the Teatro de la República in the city.

Geography and economy

The state covers about 11,684 km² in Mexico's Bajío region, bordering San Luis Potosí, Guanajuato, Hidalgo, the State of Mexico and Michoacán. The north is dominated by the Sierra Gorda, a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve with five 18th-century Franciscan mission churches that are World Heritage Sites. Since the 1990s Querétaro has been one of Mexico's fastest-growing states economically, attracting aerospace manufacturing (anchored by Bombardier and later Airbus suppliers), automotive plants, data centres and wine production in its southern valleys.

Demographics

The population is roughly 2.4 million (2020 census), concentrated in the Querétaro metropolitan area. The state capital's historic centre is a UNESCO World Heritage Site (1996), recognised for its well-preserved colonial street plan and architecture.
