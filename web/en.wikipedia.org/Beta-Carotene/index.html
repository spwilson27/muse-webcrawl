β-Carotene (beta-carotene) is an organic, strongly coloured red-orange pigment abundant in fungi, plants, and fruits. It is a member of the carotenes, which are terpenoids (isoprenoids), synthesized biochemically from eight isoprene units and thus having 40 carbons.

Provitamin A activity

Among the carotenes, β-carotene is distinguished by having beta-rings at both ends of the molecule. In the body it is cleaved by the enzyme beta-carotene 15,15'-dioxygenase in the small intestine to produce retinal, a form of vitamin A. β-Carotene is the most common provitamin A carotenoid in the diet, and is converted to vitamin A on demand, so excess intake does not cause vitamin A toxicity, though it can turn the skin yellow-orange (carotenemia).

Sources

Rich sources include carrots, sweet potatoes, spinach, kale, cantaloupe, and butternut squash. The colour of β-carotene is masked by chlorophyll in green leaves. It is also responsible for the orange colour of dried and ripe fruit such as mango and papaya.

Uses

β-Carotene is used as a food colouring (E160a) and as a dietary supplement. Large clinical trials of high-dose β-carotene supplements in smokers found an increased risk of lung cancer, so supplementation is generally recommended only for those with inadequate dietary intake.

Chemistry

The molecule was first crystallised from carrot roots in 1831 by Heinrich Wackenroder, and its structure was determined by Paul Karrer in 1930. Commercial production is by chemical synthesis or extraction from algae such as Dunaliella salina and from fungi.
