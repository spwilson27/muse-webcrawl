Adolf "Adi" Dassler (3 November 1900 – 6 September 1978) was a German cobbler, inventor, and entrepreneur who founded the sportswear company Adidas. Trained as a baker but drawn to shoemaking, he began producing sports shoes in his mother's washroom in Herzogenaurach after the First World War, in which he had served.

Gebrüder Dassler and the split

In 1924 his brother Rudolf joined the business, formally registered in 1925 as the Dassler Brothers Shoe Factory (Gebrüder Dassler Schuhfabrik). Their lightweight spikes and football boots gained fame when athletes such as Jesse Owens — in a famous, much-debated account — wore Dassler shoes at the 1936 Berlin Olympics. Relations between the brothers deteriorated during and after the Second World War; in 1948 they split the company. Rudolf founded the firm that became Puma, and in 1949 Adolf registered "Adidas," a contraction of "Adi Dassler," distinguished by its three stripes.

Adidas

Under Adolf Dassler, Adidas became the dominant supplier of football boots — West Germany's 1954 World Cup victory in Adidas screw-in studs was a breakthrough — and expanded into apparel and equipment worldwide. He remained personally involved in product development until his death in 1978, after which his wife Käthe and son Horst led the company. The rivalry between Adidas and Puma famously divided their hometown of Herzogenaurach for decades.
