Festival Internacional Cervantino

The Festival Internacional Cervantino (FIC), popularly known as El Cervantino, is a festival of performing arts held each autumn in the city of Guanajuato, Mexico. Founded in 1972, it is the most important artistic and cultural festival in Mexico and Latin America, and one of the largest of its kind in the world. It is named in honour of Miguel de Cervantes, author of Don Quixote.

Origins

The festival grew out of performances of Cervantes's short comic plays, the entremeses, staged from the 1950s in Guanajuato's plazas by the university theatre under director Enrique Ruelas. In 1972 the Mexican federal government formalised the event as an international festival, with the first edition featuring artists from fourteen countries.

Programming

Each edition lasts roughly three weeks in October and presents opera, symphonic and chamber music, theatre, dance, visual arts, film, and academic events. Each year a guest country and a guest Mexican state are honoured. Performances take place in historic venues such as the Teatro Juárez, the Teatro Principal, and the Alhóndiga de Granaditas esplanade, as well as in streets and plazas throughout the colonial city, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Significance

The festival draws hundreds of thousands of visitors and thousands of artists from dozens of countries. It has helped make Guanajuato synonymous with Cervantes: the city also hosts the Museo Iconográfico del Quijote and celebrates its identity as the "Cervantine capital of America".
