International Internet Preservation Consortium

The International Internet Preservation Consortium (IIPC) is a consortium of national libraries, archives, research institutions and other organisations dedicated to preserving content from the World Wide Web and advancing web archiving. It was founded in 2003 under the leadership of the Bibliothèque nationale de France, with founding members including the national libraries of Australia, Canada, Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Italy, Norway, Sweden and the United Kingdom, the Library of Congress, and the Internet Archive.

Activities

The IIPC develops open-source tools and standards for web archiving. Its best-known product is the Heritrix web crawler, originally developed for the Internet Archive, which is used to harvest websites at scale. It also maintains resources related to the WARC (Web ARChive) file format, standardised as ISO 28500, and the Wayback Machine replay software OpenWayback. Members coordinate collaborative collections archiving websites around major events — such as Olympic Games, elections and natural disasters — and conduct research on topics including quality assurance, access and legal issues in web archiving.

The consortium is organised into a steering committee, working groups (including groups on access, content, and tools and technology), and a programme officer. Member institutions include national libraries running web archiving programmes under legal deposit laws, university libraries and the Internet Archive.

Web archiving

Web archiving — the process of collecting portions of the World Wide Web and preserving them in archive formats for future researchers, historians and the public — grew rapidly from the mid-1990s, when the Internet Archive began its crawls in 1996. National programmes such as the National Library of Australia's PANDORA archive (begun 1996) and Sweden's Kulturarw3 project were among the earliest. The IIPC was formed to let these programmes share tools, standards and experience rather than duplicating effort.
