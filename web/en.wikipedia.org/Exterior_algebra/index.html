Exterior algebra

In mathematics, the exterior algebra of a vector space V is an algebra constructed from V using the exterior (wedge) product, an alternating bilinear operation. Its elements, called multivectors, generalize scalars, vectors, and the familiar cross product of three-dimensional space.

Exterior algebra
Operation: wedge product ∧
Introduced by: Hermann Grassmann (1844)

Definition

The exterior algebra Λ(V) is the quotient of the tensor algebra by the ideal generated by elements of the form v ⊗ v. The wedge product is associative and satisfies v ∧ v = 0, hence u ∧ v = −v ∧ u.

Applications

Exterior algebra underlies differential forms, determinants, and the geometry of oriented areas and volumes, and appears in physics in the formulation of electromagnetism and fermionic systems.
