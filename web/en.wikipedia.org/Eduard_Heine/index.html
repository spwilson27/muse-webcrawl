Heinrich Eduard Heine (15 March 1821 – 21 October 1881) was a German mathematician. He is best known for results in mathematical analysis, including the Heine–Borel theorem and the Heine–Cantor theorem on uniform continuity.

Biography

Heine was born in Berlin, the son of the banker Karl Heine and Henriette Märtens. He studied mathematics at the University of Göttingen and the University of Berlin, where his teachers included Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet. He received his doctorate in 1842 and became a professor at the University of Bonn and later at the University of Halle, where he spent most of his career.

Work

Heine worked on Legendre polynomials, Lamé functions, Bessel functions, potential theory, and basic concepts of real analysis. His 1872 paper Die Elemente der Functionenlehre introduced a definition of continuity in terms of sequences — the "Heine definition" — which is equivalent to the epsilon-delta definition of Cauchy. He proved that a continuous function on a closed bounded interval is uniformly continuous (the Heine–Cantor theorem) and stated the covering property later associated with Émile Borel (the Heine–Borel theorem). He also introduced the concept of uniform convergence into German analysis teaching.

Legacy

Heine died in Halle in 1881. His collected approach to real analysis influenced Georg Cantor, who was his colleague and friend at Halle.
