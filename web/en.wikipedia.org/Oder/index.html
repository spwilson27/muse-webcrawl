Oder

The Oder (German: Oder; Polish and Czech: Odra) is a river in Central Europe rising in the Czech Republic and flowing through western Poland, forming much of the modern border between Poland and Germany, before emptying into the Szczecin Lagoon and the Baltic Sea. At about 854 km it is Poland's second-longest river after the Vistula.

Course

The river rises in the Oder Mountains of Moravia, passes Ostrava, enters Poland, and flows northwest through Silesia — past Opole and Wrocław — before turning north along the German border from the confluence with the Lusatian Neisse. Below Schwedt it divides into eastern and western arms (the Odra and West Oder) around the Międzyodrze wetlands, reaching the Szczecin Lagoon and the Baltic through the Świna, Dziwna, and Peene channels.

History

Following the Second World War, the Potsdam Conference set Poland's western frontier along the Oder–Neisse line, a border formally recognised by East Germany in 1950 and by reunified Germany in 1990. Major floods struck the basin in 1997 (the "Millennium Flood") and 2010. In 2022 a mass fish die-off, linked to golden algae (Prymnesium parvum) blooms in warm, saline water, killed hundreds of tonnes of fish.

Navigation

The Oder is navigable for much of its length and is linked by canals to the Vistula (via the Bydgoszcz Canal) and to German waterways (via the Oder–Spree and Oder–Havel canals).
