Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment Directive

The Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment Directive (WEEE Directive, 2012/19/EU) is a European Union directive that addresses the disposal and recycling of electrical and electronic equipment waste (e-waste). Together with the Restriction of Hazardous Substances Directive (RoHS), it became European law in February 2003, and was recast in 2012.

Aims and scope

The directive aims to reduce the amount of electrical and electronic waste going to landfill, to improve the environmental performance of all operators involved in the life cycle of such equipment, and to make producers responsible for financing the collection, treatment and recovery of their products — an application of extended producer responsibility. It covers ten broad categories of equipment, from large household appliances and IT equipment to toys, medical devices and automatic dispensers.

Requirements

Member states must set up collection systems allowing consumers to return WEEE free of charge, with collection targets expressed initially as a minimum of 4 kilograms per inhabitant per year, and under the recast directive rising to 65% of the average weight of equipment placed on the market (or 85% of WEEE generated). Equipment must be marked with the crossed-out wheeled-bin symbol. Producers register and report in each member state where they sell.

Effects and criticism

The WEEE system greatly expanded formal e-waste collection in Europe, but critics note that a large share of European e-waste is still exported, sometimes illegally, to West Africa and Asia, or scavenged for valuable metals before entering official channels. The directive has influenced similar producer-responsibility laws in other jurisdictions, including China’s WEEE regulation.
