Harland and Wolff

Founded: 1861, Belfast, Ireland

Founders: Edward Harland and Gustav Wolff

Industry: Shipbuilding, marine engineering

Headquarters: Belfast, Northern Ireland

Harland & Wolff is a British shipbuilding and fabrication company headquartered in Belfast, Northern Ireland. Founded in 1861 by Edward Harland and Gustav Wilhelm Wolff, the yard became one of the largest shipbuilders in the world and is best known for constructing RMS Titanic and her sister ships Olympic and Britannic for the White Star Line.

History

Harland bought the Queen's Island yard in 1858 and formed the partnership with Wolff in 1861. Under long-serving chairman Lord Pirrie, the company built hundreds of liners and warships, including vessels for the Royal Navy during both world wars, and at its peak employed tens of thousands of workers in Belfast. The yard's twin gantry cranes, Samson and Goliath, erected in the 1970s, remain landmarks of the city.

Later years

Post-war decline in British shipbuilding brought nationalisation in 1977 and privatisation in 1989. The company diversified into offshore energy, ship repair, and fabrication, and in the 2020s won naval contracts including work on the Royal Navy's Fleet Solid Support ships. The historic Titanic Quarter in Belfast, including the Titanic Belfast visitor attraction, stands on the former shipyard site.
