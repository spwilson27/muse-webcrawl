Tang poetry

Tang poetry (唐詩) — poetry written in or characteristic of the Tang dynasty (618–907), widely regarded as the golden age of Chinese poetry. Major poets: Li Bai, Du Fu, Wang Wei, Bai Juyi. The anthology Complete Tang Poems preserves ~49,000 poems by ~2,200 poets.

Tang poetry (traditional Chinese: 唐詩; simplified Chinese: 唐诗; pinyin: Tángshī) refers to poetry written in or characteristic of the Tang dynasty (618–907), often considered the golden age of Chinese poetry. More than fifty thousand Tang poems have survived, the largest collection being the imperial Complete Tang Poems (Quan Tangshi), compiled in the early Qing dynasty.

Forms

Tang poets perfected both "old style" verse (gushi) and the formally strict "recent style" (jintishi): regulated verse (lüshi) of eight lines with tonal patterns and parallel couplets, and quatrains (jueju) of four lines. Poetry composition was tested in the imperial examinations, making verse a career skill for officials.

Major poets

The High Tang produced Li Bai (Li Po), famed for romantic, Daoist-inflected spontaneity; Du Fu, the "poet-sage" whose work records the An Lushan rebellion's devastation with Confucian moral depth; Wang Wei, painter-poet of serene Buddhist landscapes; and Meng Haoran. The Mid Tang saw Bai Juyi's plain-language social verse and the strange, dense styles of Li He. The era's poetry remains a cornerstone of education and quotation across East Asia.
