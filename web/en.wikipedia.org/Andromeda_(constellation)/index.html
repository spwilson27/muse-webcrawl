Andromeda (constellation)

Andromeda
Genitive: Andromedae
Brightest star: Alpheratz (α And)
Area: 722 sq. deg. (19th)
Notable object: Andromeda Galaxy (M31)

Andromeda is one of the 48 constellations listed by the 2nd-century astronomer Ptolemy, and one of the 88 modern constellations. Located in the northern sky near Pegasus, it is named for Andromeda, the princess of Greek myth who was chained to a rock as a sacrifice to the sea monster Cetus and rescued by Perseus.

Stars

Its brightest star, Alpheratz (Alpha Andromedae), is shared historically with Pegasus and forms a corner of the Great Square. Beta Andromedae (Mirach) and Gamma Andromedae (Almach)—a colourful double star popular with amateur observers—trace the princess's body, while Upsilon Andromedae was among the first sun-like stars found to host a multi-planet system.

Deep-sky objects

Andromeda's glory is the Andromeda Galaxy (Messier 31), the nearest large spiral galaxy to the Milky Way at about 2.5 million light-years, visible to the naked eye as a faint smudge and attended by its satellite galaxies M32 and M110. The constellation also contains the open cluster NGC 752 and the planetary nebula NGC 7662, the Blue Snowball.

Mythology

Andromeda was the daughter of Cepheus and Cassiopeia, rulers of Aethiopia. Her mother's boast that she outshone the Nereids provoked Poseidon to send floods and the monster Cetus; an oracle demanded Andromeda's sacrifice, but Perseus, returning with the head of Medusa, rescued and married her. The whole family group—Cepheus, Cassiopeia, Perseus, Pegasus, and Cetus—lies together in the sky.
