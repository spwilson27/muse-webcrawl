Epicyclic gearing

Epicyclic gearing, also called planetary gearing, is a gear system in which one or more planet gears rotate around a central sun gear while meshing with an outer ring gear. A carrier connects the planet gears and can itself rotate, producing compact speed reduction or multiplication.

Operation

The gear ratio depends on which member—sun, ring, or carrier—is held fixed and which are input and output. Holding different members yields different ratios or reverse motion, which makes epicyclic trains useful in automatic transmissions where clutches and brakes select combinations.

Advantages and uses

Planetary gears share load among several planets, giving high torque capacity for their size and allowing coaxial input and output shafts. They appear in automatic gearboxes, hybrid vehicle power-split devices, hub gears for bicycles, wind turbines, robotics, and precision instruments.

Analysis

Because relative motion is central to the mechanism, engineers often analyze epicyclic gears with the Willis equation or tabular methods that first lock the carrier and then add carrier rotation. See also gear train, automatic transmission, and sun and planet gear.
