Early music

Early music generally comprises Medieval music (500–1400) and Renaissance music (1400–1600). It can also include Baroque music (1600–1750). Early music is a broad musical era for the beginning of Western classical music.

Performance practice

The term is closely associated with the historically informed performance movement that grew after the Second World War: performing early repertoire on period instruments (or faithful copies) and using techniques, pitches and temperaments documented in historical treatises. Pioneers included Arnold Dolmetsch, Wanda Landowska, and later Nikolaus Harnoncourt, Gustav Leonhardt, Christopher Hogwood and Jordi Savall.

Repertoire

The repertoire includes plainchant and the polyphony of the Notre-Dame school, troubadour and trouvère song, Machaut, Dufay, Josquin des Prez, the English madrigalists, Monteverdi, and the Baroque masters Bach, Handel and Vivaldi. The revival transformed mainstream performance too: modern orchestras now commonly adopt faster tempi, lighter articulation and reduced vibrato in pre-Classical works.

The journal Early Music, founded in 1973, and festivals such as the Boston Early Music Festival and the Utrecht Early Music Festival are central institutions of the field.
