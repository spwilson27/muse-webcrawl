Battle of Chaeronea (338 BC)

Date2 August 338 BC (traditional date)
LocationChaeronea, Boeotia, Greece
ResultDecisive Macedonian victory
BelligerentsMacedon vs. Athens, Thebes and allies
CommandersPhilip II of Macedon, Alexander the Great; Chares and Lysicles (Athens), Theagenes (Thebes)

The Battle of Chaeronea was fought in 338 BC, near the city of Chaeronea in Boeotia, between the Macedonians led by Philip II and an alliance of Greek city-states including Athens and Thebes. The Macedonian victory effectively ended Greek resistance and established Macedonian hegemony over the Greek mainland, paving the way for the League of Corinth and the campaigns of Alexander the Great.

Background

After two decades of diplomacy and warfare — the so-called rise of Macedon — Philip controlled Thessaly, Thrace, and much of northern Greece. Alarmed by his seizure of Elatea in 339 BC, which threatened the road into Boeotia and Attica, Athens and Thebes, long-standing rivals, formed an alliance urged by the orator Demosthenes. The combined Greek army took position at Chaeronea to block Philip's advance into southern Greece.

The battle

Philip commanded about 30,000 infantry and 2,000 cavalry; the allies fielded comparable numbers. According to later sources, Philip's right wing executed a controlled feigned retreat that drew the Athenians forward and opened a gap in the allied line, while the eighteen-year-old Alexander, commanding the Companion cavalry on the left, broke the Theban line. The Sacred Band of Thebes — an elite unit of 300 — refused to retreat and was annihilated where it stood; its mass grave, marked by the great stone Lion of Chaeronea, was excavated in the 19th century with the skeletons of 254 men laid in rows. Greek losses were heavy: roughly 1,000 Athenians killed and 2,000 captured, with still greater Theban losses.

Aftermath

Philip treated Athens leniently but garrisoned Thebes and dissolved the Boeotian League. In 337 BC he convened the Greek states at Corinth, forming a league under Macedonian leadership that declared war on Persia — the project his son Alexander would carry out. A second battle at Chaeronea was fought at the same site in 86 BC.
