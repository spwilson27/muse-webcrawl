Roméo et Juliette

Roméo et Juliette (Romeo and Juliet) is an opera in five acts by Charles Gounod to a French libretto by Jules Barbier and Michel Carré, based on William Shakespeare's play Romeo and Juliet.

Composition and performance history

Gounod's lifelong fascination with Shakespeare led him to compose the opera in 1867, with a libretto that closely follows the play, condensing its action and placing emphasis on the lovers. The opera premiered at the Théâtre Lyrique in Paris on 27 April 1867 and was an immediate success; the celebrated soprano Marie Miolan-Carvalho created the role of Juliette. The work was later revised for the Opéra-Comique (1873) and for the Paris Opéra (1888), where it entered the repertory.

Synopsis

In Verona, the Capulet and Montague families are feuding. Roméo, a Montague, meets Juliette at a Capulet ball and they fall in love. They are secretly married by Friar Laurent. After Roméo kills Juliette's cousin Tybalt in a duel he is banished; Juliette takes a potion that simulates death to avoid a forced marriage to Paris. Roméo, believing her truly dead, poisons himself at her tomb; Juliette awakens, and finding him dead, stabs herself.

Music

The opera is noted for its four duets for the lovers, Juliette's coloratura waltz-song "Je veux vivre", the balcony scene, and the Queen Mab ballad for Mercutio. It remains Gounod's most frequently performed opera after Faust.
