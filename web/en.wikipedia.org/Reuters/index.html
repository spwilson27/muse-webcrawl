Reuters is an international news agency owned by Thomson Reuters. Founded in London in 1851 by the German-born Paul Julius Reuter, it is one of the largest news agencies in the world, employing around 2,500 journalists and 600 photojournalists in about 200 locations worldwide.

History

Paul Reuter arrived in London from Aachen in 1851 and established a news service transmitting stock market quotations between London and Paris via the new Calais-Dover telegraph cable, having previously used carrier pigeons to bridge a gap in the telegraph line between Aachen and Brussels. The agency expanded to cover general news and became renowned for speed and impartiality, reporting the assassination of Abraham Lincoln to European readers, among other scoops.

Modern era

In 2008 the Thomson Corporation merged with Reuters Group to form Thomson Reuters. The agency supplies text, photographs, video and graphics to newspapers, television and radio broadcasters, and digital publishers. In 2018, a 30 percent stake in its financial data business was sold to Blackstone, creating Refinitiv, later acquired by the London Stock Exchange Group.

Editorial principles

Reuters operates under trust principles established in 1941 that commit it to independence, integrity and freedom from bias in the supply of news.
