Cannabidiol

Cannabidiol (CBD) is a phytocannabinoid discovered in 1940. It is one of 113 identified cannabinoids in cannabis plants, along with tetrahydrocannabinol (THC), and accounts for up to 40% of the plant's extract. Unlike THC, CBD is not intoxicating and does not produce the "high" associated with cannabis use.

FormulaC21H30O2
Molar mass314.47 g/mol
SourceCannabis sativa
Approved drugEpidiolex (for certain severe epilepsies)

Medical use

As of recent evidence reviews, the only well-supported medical use of CBD is as an anti-seizure medication: the purified CBD oral solution Epidiolex was approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration in 2018 for seizures associated with Lennox–Gastaut syndrome, Dravet syndrome, and tuberous sclerosis complex. Evidence is insufficient for the many other conditions for which CBD products are marketed, including pain, anxiety, and inflammation, and regulators have warned companies against making unproven therapeutic claims.

Legality and products

Hemp-derived CBD was removed from the U.S. Controlled Substances Act framework by the 2018 Farm Bill, though the FDA continues to regulate CBD in foods, supplements, and drugs, and its legal status varies widely by country. CBD is sold as oils, capsules, edibles, and topical products; side effects can include dry mouth, diarrhoea, reduced appetite, drowsiness, and interactions with other medications.
