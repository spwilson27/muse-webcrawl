Carl Icahn

Carl Celian Icahn (born 16 February 1936) is an American businessman and investor, founder of Icahn Enterprises. One of the most prominent activist investors on Wall Street, he became famous in the 1980s as a "corporate raider" and later recast himself as a champion of shareholder value.

Early life and career

Icahn was born in Queens, New York, studied philosophy at Princeton, and briefly attended medical school before joining the army. He began on Wall Street in 1961 at Dreyfus Corporation, made money in options arbitrage, and in 1968 founded his own firm, Icahn & Co., with backing from his uncle.

Activist investing

Icahn's method is to accumulate a stake in an undervalued or badly managed company, then publicly pressure its board — through letters, proxy fights, and media — to spin off divisions, buy back shares, or replace management. Famous campaigns include his hostile takeover of TWA in 1985, battles over RJR Nabisco, Texaco, Blockbuster, Netflix, eBay, Herbalife (opposing Bill Ackman's short position), and positions in Apple, where he pressed for larger buybacks. He held a controlling stake in the Trump Taj Mahal casino in Atlantic City and owned the shuttered Fontainebleau Las Vegas project for a period.

Public life

Icahn supported Donald Trump's 2016 presidential campaign and briefly served as a special adviser on regulatory reform in 2017, resigning amid conflict-of-interest questions. His philanthropy includes major gifts to the Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai and to charter schools in the Bronx.
