Babri Masjid

The Babri Masjid was a mosque in Ayodhya, Uttar Pradesh, India. It was constructed in 1528–29 on the orders of Mir Baqi, a commander of the Mughal emperor Babur, after whom it was named. The mosque was demolished on 6 December 1992 by a large crowd of Hindu nationalist activists.

History

Many Hindus believe the site to be Ram Janmabhoomi, the birthplace of the deity Rama, and a long-running dispute over the land developed from the nineteenth century onward. In 1949 idols of Rama were placed inside the mosque, after which it was locked by court order and remained in litigation for decades.

Demolition

On 6 December 1992 a rally organised by Hindu nationalist groups turned into an attack that destroyed the mosque within hours, despite assurances to the Supreme Court of India. The demolition triggered communal riots across India in which around 2,000 people were killed.

Legal resolution

In 2019 the Supreme Court awarded the disputed site for the construction of a Hindu temple, while directing that land be allotted elsewhere in Ayodhya for a mosque. The Ram Mandir was consecrated on the site in January 2024. See also Ayodhya dispute.
