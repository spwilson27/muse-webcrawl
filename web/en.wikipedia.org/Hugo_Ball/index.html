Hugo Ball (22 February 1886 – 14 September 1927) was a German author, poet, and artist who is considered a founder of the Dada movement in European art. With his partner Emmy Hennings he founded the Cabaret Voltaire in Zürich in 1916, the birthplace of Dada, and wrote the movement's first manifesto.

[Image: Hugo Ball performing his sound poem Karawane at the Cabaret Voltaire, 1916]

Early life

Ball grew up in a Catholic family in Pirmasens and studied sociology and philosophy at Munich and Heidelberg, writing a dissertation on Nietzsche. He worked in theatre in Berlin and Munich before emigrating to Switzerland with Hennings in 1915, disillusioned by the First World War.

Dada

At the Cabaret Voltaire, Ball performed his pioneering sound poems — most famously Karawane (1916), recited in a stiff cardboard "bishop" costume — which abandoned conventional meaning in favour of rhythm, sound, and nonsense syllables. Although he withdrew from Dada within about a year, his experiments with phonetic poetry had lasting influence on avant-garde and performance art.

Later life

Ball became a devout Catholic and wrote a biography of Hermann Hesse, a life of the anarchist Bakunin, and the diary Flight Out of Time (1927), one of the key documents of Dada. He died in Switzerland in 1927.
