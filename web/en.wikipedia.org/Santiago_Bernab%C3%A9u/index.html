Santiago Bernabéu de Yeste (8 June 1895 – 2 June 1978) was a Spanish footballer and executive who was president of Real Madrid from 1943 until his death in 1978. He is widely regarded as the most important figure in the history of the club, transforming it into one of the most successful sporting institutions in the world. Real Madrid's home stadium is named in his honour.

Life and career

Bernabéu joined Real Madrid as a player in his youth, serving as a forward and later as captain, coach, and director. After the Spanish Civil War, in which he fought on the Nationalist side, he helped rebuild a club whose infrastructure and membership had been devastated. Elected president in 1943, he pursued an ambitious strategy: constructing a vast new stadium (opened in 1947 and renamed for him in 1955), professionalising the club's structure, and signing world stars such as Alfredo Di Stéfano, Ferenc Puskás, and Paco Gento.

Legacy

Under Bernabéu's presidency Real Madrid won six European Cups, including the first five in succession (1956–1960), as well as numerous domestic titles, and he was a driving force behind the creation of the European Cup itself. He also built up the club's basketball section into a European power. Bernabéu died during the 1978 World Cup; FIFA ordered mourning tributes, and he was posthumously honoured as one of the game's great administrators. The annual Trofeo Santiago Bernabéu friendly tournament also bears his name.
