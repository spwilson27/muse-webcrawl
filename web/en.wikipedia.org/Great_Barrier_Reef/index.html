Great Barrier Reef

LocationCoral Sea, off Queensland, Australia
Lengthover 2,300 km (1,400 mi)
Areaabout 344,400 km²
UNESCOWorld Heritage Site (inscribed 1981)

The Great Barrier Reef is the world's largest coral reef system, stretching for over 2,300 kilometres along the coast of Queensland in north-eastern Australia. Composed of around 2,900 individual reefs and 900 islands, it is the largest living structure on Earth and can be seen from outer space.

Biodiversity

The reef supports an extraordinary diversity of life, including some 1,500 species of fish, more than 400 species of hard coral, a third of the world's soft corals, 134 species of sharks and rays, six species of sea turtle that breed there, and more than 30 species of whales and dolphins. It is also a critical habitat for dugongs and a breeding ground for humpback whales.

Formation and history

The present reef structure began forming on an older platform as sea levels rose after the last glacial period, roughly 20,000 years ago. Aboriginal Australians and Torres Strait Islanders have known and used the reef for tens of thousands of years. Europeans first recorded it when Captain James Cook ran aground on it in 1770.

Threats and protection

The reef is under pressure from climate change — mass coral bleaching events driven by marine heatwaves occurred in 1998, 2002, 2016, 2017, 2020 and 2022 — as well as crown-of-thorns starfish outbreaks, agricultural runoff and coastal development. Most of the reef lies within the Great Barrier Reef Marine Park, established in 1975, and it was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1981.
