WASH

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

WASH (or Watsan) is an acronym used in international development for "water, sanitation and hygiene". Access to safe water, adequate sanitation, and hygiene education is a major focus of public health and development work, because diarrhoeal disease and other WASH-related illnesses remain leading causes of death among children under five in low-income countries.

Components

Water work covers drinking-water supply, water quality, and resource management. Sanitation concerns the safe management of human excreta — toilets, sewerage, and treatment — and increasingly menstrual hygiene. Hygiene promotion centres on hand washing with soap, which studies rank among the most cost-effective health interventions available.

Global goals

The United Nations recognised the human right to water and sanitation in 2010. Sustainable Development Goal 6 commits the world to clean water and sanitation for all by 2030, monitored by the WHO/UNICEF Joint Monitoring Programme. Despite progress, in the early 2020s roughly two billion people still lacked safely managed drinking water and over four billion lacked safely managed sanitation.

Approaches

Major approaches include community-led total sanitation (CLTS), sanitation marketing, WASH in schools and healthcare facilities, and humanitarian WASH in emergencies, coordinated internationally by organisations such as UNICEF, WaterAid, and the Global Water Security and Sanitation Partnership.
