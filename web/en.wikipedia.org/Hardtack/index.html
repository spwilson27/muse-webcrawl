Hardtack

TypeBiscuit / cracker
Main ingredientsFlour, water, sometimes salt
Other namesShip's biscuit, pilot bread, sea biscuit
Used asMilitary and naval rations; survival food

Hardtack (or hard tack) is a simple, dense biscuit or cracker made from flour, water, and sometimes salt, baked repeatedly to remove moisture. Because it is inexpensive and keeps almost indefinitely when dry, hardtack was for centuries a staple ration for sailors and soldiers; at sea it was known as ship's biscuit, and a modern commercial form survives as pilot bread, still popular in Alaska and Hawaii.

History

Dried bread rations go back to antiquity, but hardtack became emblematic of the age of sail and of nineteenth-century armies. During the American Civil War, soldiers joked grimly about biscuits so hard they had to be softened in coffee, fried in pork fat, or broken with rifle butts, and so prone to weevil infestation that the pieces were nicknamed "worm castles". Specimens issued in the nineteenth century remain on display in museums, still essentially intact.

Preparation and use

Traditional hardtack is rolled, docked with holes, and baked two or more times until rock hard. Before eating it is usually soaked in water, coffee, or stew. It remains in production as an emergency and survival ration because of its calorie density and extraordinary shelf life. See also ship's biscuit and field ration.
