Secularization

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Declining church attendance and the conversion of churches to secular uses are common indicators of secularization.

Secularization is the process by which a society moves from close identification with religious values and institutions toward nonreligious or irreligious values and secular institutions. In sociology, the term refers to the historical process in which religion loses social and cultural significance — through the separation of religious institutions from the state, the decline of religious belief and practice, and the transfer of functions such as education and welfare from religious to secular bodies.

The secularization thesis

Classical sociologists including Karl Marx, Émile Durkheim, and Max Weber expected modernization to bring religious decline; Weber described the rationalization and "disenchantment" of the world. Twentieth-century sociologists formalized this as the secularization thesis, predicting that industrialization, urbanization, science, and pluralism would steadily erode religion. The thesis is strongly supported in much of Europe, where church attendance and stated belief have fallen dramatically.

Debate and revision

From the late 20th century, scholars such as Peter Berger — who later recanted his earlier support — José Casanova, and Pippa Norris and Ronald Inglehart debated the thesis. Critics noted the persistent religiosity of the United States, the global growth of Pentecostalism, and the public resurgence of religion. Alternative views hold that secularization proceeds unevenly: existential security, rather than modernization as such, best predicts religious decline, and religion may be transformed or privatized rather than simply disappear. Sociologists now commonly distinguish societal secularization, organizational secularization, and individual secularization.

Legal and historical uses

The term also has a legal-historical sense: the confiscation or transfer of church property to secular owners, as in the German mediatisation of 1803, the dissolution of the monasteries under Henry VIII, or revolutionary France's nationalization of church lands.
