The Yale Law Journal is a student-run law review affiliated with Yale Law School. Founded in 1891, it is one of the most widely read and influential legal periodicals in the United States and a flagship of the American law-school law review tradition, publishing scholarship by academics, judges and practitioners as well as student notes.

History and significance

The Journal publishes eight issues annually, covering constitutional law, jurisprudence, legal history, and empirical legal studies, among other fields. Many articles published in the Yale Law Journal have shaped American law and legal theory; the Journal is regularly ranked among the most-cited law reviews in the country, frequently exchanging the top position with the Harvard Law Review.

Notable alumni and articles

Editors and authors have included numerous future Supreme Court justices, scholars and public officials. A famous contribution is Charles Reich's 1964 article "The New Property", which argued for procedural protections for government benefits. The Journal also published influential work by scholars such as Akhil Amar, Guido Calabresi and Robert Bork — whose 1971 article "Neutral Principles and Some First Amendment Problems" appeared while he was on the Yale faculty.
