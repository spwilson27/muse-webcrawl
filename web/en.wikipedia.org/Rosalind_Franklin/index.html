Rosalind Franklin

Rosalind Elsie Franklin (25 July 1920 – 16 April 1958) was an English chemist and X-ray crystallographer whose work was central to understanding the molecular structures of DNA, RNA, viruses, coal and graphite.

Born: 25 July 1920, London
Died: 16 April 1958 (aged 37), London
Alma mater: Newnham College, Cambridge
Known for: Structure of DNA; fine structures of coal and viruses

DNA work

At King's College London from 1951, Franklin and her student Raymond Gosling produced the X-ray diffraction image known as Photo 51, showing DNA's helical structure, and established that DNA exists in two forms (A and B). Her unpublished reports underpinned the 1953 double-helix model of Watson, Crick and Wilkins; the extent and ethics of the data's use remain debated.

Later work and legacy

At Birkbeck College she led pioneering studies of the tobacco mosaic virus and poliovirus. She died of ovarian cancer at 37, four years before the 1962 Nobel Prize for the DNA structure was awarded to Watson, Crick and Wilkins — Nobel rules barred a posthumous award. Her role is now widely commemorated in institutes, prizes and the ExoMars rover named for her. See also DNA and X-ray crystallography.
