The Julian Alps (Slovene: Julijske Alpe; Italian: Alpi Giulie) are a mountain range of the Southern Limestone Alps extending from northeastern Italy into northwestern Slovenia. They are named after Julius Caesar, who founded the nearby municipium of Forum Julii (modern Cividale del Friuli). The range covers about 4,400 km² and is dominated by limestone peaks, deep glacial valleys and karst plateaus.

Geography

The range's highest summit is Mount Triglav (2,864 m), Slovenia's highest mountain and national symbol, surrounded by Triglav National Park, one of Europe's oldest protected areas. Other notable peaks include Škrlatica (2,740 m), Mangart, Jalovec, Razor and Prisojnik; on the Italian side are Jôf di Montasio and Jôf Fuart. The Sava and Soča (Isonzo) rivers rise in the range; Lake Bohinj and Lake Bled lie at its eastern and southeastern margins. The Vršič Pass (1,611 m), built by Russian prisoners of war in the First World War, is Slovenia's highest road pass.

History and recreation

The Soča valley was the scene of the Isonzo Front in the First World War, including the Battle of Caporetto (1917). Today the Julian Alps are a centre for mountaineering, hiking, skiing (Kranjska Gora, Vogel, Kanin) and white-water sports on the emerald Soča River.
