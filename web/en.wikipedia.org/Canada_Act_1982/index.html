Canada Act 1982

The Canada Act 1982 is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that was passed at the request of the Parliament of Canada to "patriate" Canada's constitution, ending the power of the British Parliament to amend the Constitution of Canada. The Act also enacted the Constitution Act, 1982, which contained the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms and an amending formula for the constitution.

Background

Canada's constitution was originally the British North America Act, 1867, an Act of the British Parliament. Amendments therefore required action in London. After the Statute of Westminster 1931 granted legislative independence to the dominions, Canada's federal and provincial governments could not agree on a domestic amending formula, so the power to amend the core constitutional documents remained with Westminster at Canada's request.

Enactment

Following negotiations between Prime Minister Pierre Trudeau's federal government and the provinces — culminating in the November 1981 agreement often associated with the "Kitchen Accord" — Canada requested the Act. Queen Elizabeth II gave royal assent on 29 March 1982, and the Queen proclaimed the Constitution Act, 1982 in force at a ceremony on Parliament Hill in Ottawa on 17 April 1982.

Provisions

Section 2 of the Canada Act states that no Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom passed after the Constitution Act, 1982 comes into force shall extend to Canada as part of its law. The Act itself is unusual in being partly written in French; its French version is set out in a schedule, since it was enacted before Parliament routinely produced bilingual statutes.
