Assassination of William McKinley

DateSeptember 6, 1901 (shot); died September 14, 1901
LocationTemple of Music, Pan-American Exposition, Buffalo, New York
TargetWilliam McKinley, 25th president of the United States
PerpetratorLeon Czolgosz

The assassination of William McKinley took place on September 6, 1901, at the Pan-American Exposition in Buffalo, New York. McKinley, the 25th president of the United States, was shot twice at close range while greeting the public at a reception in the exposition's Temple of Music, and died of his wounds eight days later. He was the third American president to be assassinated.

The shooting

The assassin, Leon Czolgosz, was an unemployed former steelworker influenced by anarchist ideas. He concealed a revolver in a handkerchief wrapped around his hand, and as McKinley reached to shake hands in the receiving line, Czolgosz fired two shots into the president's abdomen. Czolgosz was immediately seized by the crowd and Secret Service agents and nearly lynched before police intervened.

Death and aftermath

One bullet was recovered, but the second lodged in the stomach wall and could not be found. McKinley initially seemed to recover but developed gangrene around the wound and died on September 14. Vice President Theodore Roosevelt was sworn in as president the same day. Czolgosz was tried, convicted, and executed in the electric chair on October 29, 1901. The assassination prompted Congress to formally assign the Secret Service the duty of protecting the president.
