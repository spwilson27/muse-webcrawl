Athens (Greek: Αθήνα, Athína) is the capital and largest city of Greece. One of the world's oldest cities, with a recorded history spanning more than 3,400 years, Athens was the heart of Ancient Greece — a powerful city-state that gave birth to democracy, Western philosophy, theatre and much of classical art and architecture. Its archaeological heritage, crowned by the Acropolis and the Parthenon, is recognised as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

Athens has been continuously inhabited for more than 5,000 years. By the late Bronze Age it was a Mycenaean stronghold. In 508 BC, under Cleisthenes, Athens established the world's first known democratic system of government. In the fifth century BC — the "Golden Age" of Pericles — Athens led the Delian League, defeated Persia, and built the temples of the Acropolis; Socrates, Plato and Aristotle taught there, and the tragedies of Aeschylus, Sophocles and Euripides were performed in its theatres. Defeated by Sparta in the Peloponnesian War (431–404 BC), Athens later passed under Macedonian, Roman, Byzantine and Ottoman rule before becoming the capital of the modern Greek state in 1834.

Geography

Athens lies in the central plain of Attica, surrounded by four mountains — Aigaleo, Parnitha, Penteli and Hymettus — and opens southwest to the Saronic Gulf, where the port of Piraeus serves the city. The climate is Mediterranean, with hot, dry summers and mild winters. The urban area is one of the most densely built in Europe.

Culture and landmarks

Beyond the Acropolis and its museum, Athens contains the Ancient Agora, the Temple of Olympian Zeus, the Panathenaic Stadium (site of the first modern Olympic Games in 1896), the National Archaeological Museum and lively districts such as Plaka and Monastiraki. Athens hosted the 2004 Summer Olympics, which spurred a major modernisation of transport, including a new airport and metro lines.

Economy and transport

Athens is Greece's economic, financial and maritime centre; the wider Attica region produces a large share of national GDP, and Piraeus is among Europe's busiest passenger and container ports. The city is served by Athens International Airport, three metro lines, trams and extensive bus and rail networks.
