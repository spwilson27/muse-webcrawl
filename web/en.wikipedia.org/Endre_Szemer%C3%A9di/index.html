Endre Szemerédi (born 21 August 1940) is a Hungarian-American mathematician and computer scientist, working in combinatorics and theoretical computer science. He is a professor at Rutgers University and a researcher at the Alfréd Rényi Institute of Mathematics in Budapest, and he received the Abel Prize in 2012.

Work

Szemerédi is best known for proving, in 1975, the celebrated conjecture of Erdős and Turán that any set of integers of positive upper density contains arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions — now called Szemerédi's theorem. His proof introduced ideas of lasting influence, including the Szemerédi regularity lemma, a fundamental tool in graph theory stating that every large dense graph can be approximated by a bounded number of random-like bipartite pieces.

Other contributions

With Miklós Ajtai and János Komlós he developed the AKS sorting network, a landmark in parallel algorithms. He also contributed to additive number theory and the theory of pseudorandomness, and with Ajtai and Komlós proved the ct2/log t upper bound for Ramsey numbers.

Honours

Besides the Abel Prize "for his fundamental contributions to discrete mathematics and theoretical computer science", Szemerédi received the Rolf Schock Prize (2008) and the Steele Prize (2008), and is a member of the Hungarian Academy of Sciences and the US National Academy of Sciences.
