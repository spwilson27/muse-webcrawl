Peer Gynt (Grieg)

Peer Gynt, Op. 23, is the incidental music composed by Edvard Grieg in 1875 for Henrik Ibsen's 1867 play of the same name. Grieg later extracted two orchestral suites from the score — Op. 46 (1888) and Op. 55 (1891) — which have become among the most popular works in the orchestral repertoire.

Music

The original score contained 26 movements accompanying Ibsen's verse drama about the feckless Norwegian adventurer Peer Gynt. The first suite includes "Morning Mood", "The Death of Åse", "Anitra's Dance", and "In the Hall of the Mountain King", the last a menacing, accelerating march depicting trolls that has become ubiquitous in popular culture. The second suite includes "The Abduction of the Bride", "Arabian Dance", "Peer Gynt's Homecoming", and "Solveig's Song", the lyrical theme of Peer's faithful beloved.

History

Ibsen commissioned the music for the play's first staged production, premiered in Christiania (Oslo) in 1876. Grieg, who found some of the commission restrictive, nevertheless produced a score that overshadowed the play internationally; the complete incidental music is now usually performed in concert with narration, while the suites dominate concert programmes.
