Housing association
Also called: Registered social landlord / registered provider
Sector: Non-profit social housing (United Kingdom)
Regulator: Regulator of Social Housing (England)
Trade body: National Housing Federation

Housing associations in the United Kingdom are independent, not-for-profit organisations that provide social housing for people in housing need. They are the main providers of new social rented housing and, following large-scale transfers of council housing stock, manage the majority of social homes in England. Equivalent providers exist in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, with separate regulators.

History

Housing associations trace their roots to medieval almshouses and to Victorian philanthropic trusts such as Peabody (founded 1862) and Guinness Trust, which built dwellings for the working poor. The modern sector took shape with the Housing Act 1974, which channelled public subsidy through the Housing Corporation. The Housing Act 1988 allowed associations to raise private finance against future rental income, greatly expanding building, and from the 1990s many councils transferred their housing stock to new or existing associations ("large-scale voluntary transfer").

Role and regulation

Associations provide general rented housing as well as shared ownership, supported housing, hostels and homes for older people. Rents for social housing are set under government formulae, and allocation is largely via local authority waiting lists. In England the Regulator of Social Housing sets economic and consumer standards; associations are typically registered societies or charitable companies whose surpluses are reinvested rather than distributed.
