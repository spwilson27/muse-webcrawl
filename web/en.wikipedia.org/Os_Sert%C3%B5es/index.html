Os Sertões

Os Sertões (translated as Rebellion in the Backlands) is a 1902 book by the Brazilian writer and engineer Euclides da Cunha, regarded as one of the greatest works of Brazilian literature. It recounts the War of Canudos (1896–97), in which the Brazilian army destroyed the settlement of Canudos in the arid backlands (sertão) of Bahia.

Structure

The book is divided into three parts: "The Land", a study of the geography and climate of the sertão; "The Man", an anthropological portrait of the sertanejo backlander; and "The Battle", Da Cunha's eyewitness account of the military campaigns against the followers of the religious leader Antônio Conselheiro.

Significance

Combining scientific analysis with literary narrative, the work exposed the young republic's violence against its own interior population and shaped Brazilian thinking about national identity.
