The Laplace transform converts a function of time into a function of a complex variable.

In mathematics, the Laplace transform, named after Pierre-Simon Laplace, is an integral transform that converts a function of a real variable (usually time, t) to a function of a complex variable s (frequency). It is defined by the integral F(s) = ∫0∞ e−st f(t) dt.

Applications

The transform is widely used in engineering and physics because it converts differential equations into algebraic equations, which are usually easier to solve. It is fundamental to control theory, signal processing, and the analysis of linear time-invariant systems. Convolution in the time domain becomes multiplication in the s-domain, and differentiation becomes multiplication by s.

Properties

Key properties include linearity, time shifting, frequency shifting, and the transform of derivatives and integrals. The inverse Laplace transform recovers the original function and can be computed using the Bromwich integral or by partial-fraction expansion and tables of known transform pairs.

History

Laplace introduced the transform in his work on probability theory in the 1780s. It was later developed and popularised for engineering by Oliver Heaviside, whose operational calculus provided an intuitive precursor to the modern rigorous treatment.
