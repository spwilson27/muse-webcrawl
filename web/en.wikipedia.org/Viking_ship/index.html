Viking ship

Viking ships were marine vessels of unique structure, used by the Norse peoples from the Viking Age throughout the Early Middle Ages. The boat types were quite varied, depending on what the ship was intended for, but they were generally characterised as being slender and flexible boats, with symmetrical ends and a true keel.

Construction

They were clinker built — the planks overlapped and were riveted together — which made them light, strong and flexible enough for ocean voyages yet shallow enough to beach or navigate rivers. Ships carried a single square sail and could also be rowed.

Types and famous finds

Types ranged from small faerings and knarr cargo ships to longships such as snekkja and skeid warships. Famous preserved examples include the Gokstad ship, the Oseberg ship and the Tune ship in Norway, and the Skuldelev ships in Denmark.
