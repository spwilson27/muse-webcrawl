A relic (from Latin reliquiae, "remains") is an object or article of religious significance, usually a physical part of a saint or other venerated person, or an object that such a person owned or touched, preserved for the purpose of veneration. Relics are an important aspect of many religions, including Christianity, Buddhism, Islam, Hinduism and shamanic traditions, and pilgrimage to shrines housing famous relics has shaped religious practice, art and trade for centuries.

Christianity

The veneration of relics in Christianity dates to the early church, when the remains of martyrs were gathered and honoured. By the Middle Ages, relics were central to devotional life: cathedrals and monasteries competed for prestigious relics, which drew pilgrims and revenue. The Catholic Church classifies relics into three classes: first-class relics are parts of a saint's body; second-class relics are items the saint owned or used; third-class relics are objects touched to a first- or second-class relic. Famous relics include fragments of the True Cross, the Shroud of Turin, and the Crown of Thorns housed at Notre-Dame de Paris. The Council of Trent reaffirmed their veneration, though the sale of relics (a form of simony) was condemned, and the medieval trade also produced many forgeries, criticised by figures such as John Calvin.

Buddhism

In Buddhism, relics of the Buddha (śarīra) — said to be pearl-like remains found in his ashes after cremation — were divided and enshrined in stupas across Asia. The Temple of the Tooth in Kandy, Sri Lanka, houses a relic of the Buddha's tooth and is a major pilgrimage site. Relics of revered monks are similarly enshrined and venerated.

Other traditions

In Islam, relics of the Prophet Muhammad, including hairs of his beard and his cloak, are preserved in places such as the Topkapı Palace in Istanbul, though veneration practices vary. Hindu temples preserve objects associated with saints, and in many indigenous traditions objects connected with ancestors or culture heroes are kept as sacred remains.

Reliquaries

Relics are typically kept in containers called reliquaries, often lavishly decorated with gold, gems and enamel. Medieval reliquaries are among the finest surviving works of goldsmith art. In Catholic practice, altars traditionally contained relics, and relics continue to be placed in new altars at their dedication.
