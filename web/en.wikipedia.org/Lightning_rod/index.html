Lightning rod


A lightning rod (or lightning conductor) is a metal rod mounted on a structure, connected by a cable to the ground, intended to protect the structure from a lightning strike.

A lightning rod or lightning conductor is a metal rod mounted on a structure and intended to protect the structure from a lightning strike. If lightning hits the structure, it will preferentially strike the rod and be safely conducted to the ground through a cable, instead of passing through the structure, where it could start a fire or cause electrocution.

History

The lightning rod was invented by Benjamin Franklin, who described his experiments in 1749 and installed rods on buildings in Philadelphia in the 1750s. A long debate over pointed versus blunt tips — Franklin favoured points — persisted into modern standards.

How it works

The rod and its down-conductor form part of a lightning protection system together with air terminals, bonding and a ground electrode, giving the strike a low-resistance path to earth and limiting dangerous voltage differences within the building.
