Perch

A yellow perch (Perca flavescens).

Perch is a common name for freshwater fish of the genus Perca, which belongs to the family Percidae of the large order Perciformes. The name is also widely, though not precisely, applied to various other distantly related fish. The type species for this genus is the European perch, Perca fluviatilis.

Species

The genus contains three species:

  • Perca fluviatilis — the European perch, found throughout Europe and northern Asia
  • Perca flavescens — the yellow perch, native to North America
  • Perca schrenkii — the Balkhash perch, found in Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, and China

Description

Perch have a long and narrow body. Their scales are ctenoid, giving the fish a rough texture. They possess two dorsal fins: the first is spiny and the second is soft-rayed. The anal fin also contains spines. Most perch are greenish in color with dark vertical bars on their sides, and many have reddish or orange lower fins. The typical size is around 15–30 cm (6–12 in), though European perch can exceed 50 cm (20 in).

Habitat and diet

Perch inhabit lakes, ponds, slow-moving rivers, and streams. They are carnivorous, feeding on smaller fish, insect larvae, zooplankton, and other invertebrates. They are popular with anglers as a sport fish and are valued as a food fish, with firm, white, mild-flavored flesh.

Fishing

Perch fishing is a popular recreational activity in both Europe and North America. Yellow perch are a staple catch through the ice in the Great Lakes region during winter, and European perch are widely targeted across Eurasian waters.
