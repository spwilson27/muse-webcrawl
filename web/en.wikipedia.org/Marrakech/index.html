Marrakech

Marrakech (also Marrakesh) is the fourth-largest city in Morocco, located west of the foothills of the Atlas Mountains. Founded c. 1070 by Abu Bakr ibn Umar as capital of the Almoravid Empire, it is known as the "Red City" for its buildings of red sandstone.

History

Marrakech served as imperial capital under the Almoravids, Almohads, and Saadians, who built palaces, gardens, and the Saadian Tombs. The Koutoubia Mosque's 77-metre minaret dates to the 12th century. Under French protectorate from 1912, the new town (Guéliz) was built outside the medina walls.

Sights

The medina, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, centres on Jemaa el-Fnaa square, famous for food stalls, storytellers, and performers. Landmarks include Bahia Palace, Ben Youssef Madrasa, Majorelle Garden, and the souks. In September 2023 a major earthquake damaged parts of the old city.

Economy and transport

Tourism is the main industry. Marrakech-Menara Airport and a railway to Casablanca serve the city; it hosted the COP22 climate conference in 2016.
