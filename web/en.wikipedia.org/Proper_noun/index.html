Proper noun

Proper nouns name unique entities, such as London or the Thames.

A proper noun is a noun that identifies a single unique entity, such as a person, place, or organization, distinguishing it from a class of entities. In English and many other languages, proper nouns are capitalized: examples include Mary, London, France, and Microsoft. The contrasting category is the common noun, which names a class (e.g. woman, city, country).

Characteristics

Proper nouns typically do not take articles or plurals in the same way as common nouns, though exceptions abound ("the Netherlands", "the Smiths"). A word or phrase that functions as a name for a specific entity is sometimes called a proper name, and a multi-word name such as New York City is a proper noun phrase whose individual components need not all be proper nouns.

Capitalization across languages

Conventions differ: German capitalizes all nouns, while many languages capitalize only proper nouns. Some proper nouns derive from common nouns that became associated with one entity, and trademarks are legally protected proper names that can risk genericization, as happened with aspirin and escalator.
