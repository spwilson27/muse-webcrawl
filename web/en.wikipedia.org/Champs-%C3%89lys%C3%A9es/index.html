The Avenue des Champs-Élysées is a famous avenue in the 8th arrondissement of Paris, France. Running 1.9 kilometres between the Place de la Concorde and the Arc de Triomphe, it is known for its theatres, cafés, and luxury shops, and is the route of the Bastille Day military parade and the finish of the Tour de France.

History

The avenue was laid out in 1670 by the gardener André Le Nôtre as an extension of the Tuileries Garden axis. It took its name from the Elysian Fields of Greek mythology. The lower gardens were landscaped in the 19th century, and the upper part became a fashionable shopping street lined with grand hotels and flagship stores.

Events

German troops marched down the avenue in 1940, and Allied forces celebrated the liberation of Paris there in 1944. Each 14 July the avenue hosts France's largest military parade, and since 1975 the final stage of the Tour de France has finished on its cobblestones.
