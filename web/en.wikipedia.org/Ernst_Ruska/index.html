Ernst Ruska

Born: 25 December 1906, Heidelberg, Germany
Died: 27 May 1988, West Berlin
Known for: Electron microscope
Awards: Nobel Prize in Physics (1986)

Ernst August Friedrich Ruska (25 December 1906 – 27 May 1988) was a German physicist who won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1986 for his work in electron optics, including the design of the first electron microscope.

The first electron microscope

Working with Max Knoll at the Technical University of Berlin, Ruska built the first transmission electron microscope in 1931 and demonstrated in 1933 an instrument that surpassed the resolution of the light microscope. He continued development at Siemens, where the first commercially produced electron microscope appeared in 1939.

Later career

After the Second World War Ruska directed an institute of the Fritz Haber Institute of the Max Planck Society in Berlin, advancing electron microscopy for materials science and biology. He shared half of the 1986 Nobel Prize; the other half went to Gerd Binnig and Heinrich Rohrer for the scanning tunnelling microscope.

Legacy

The electron microscope transformed structural biology, virology, and materials research, making viruses and macromolecular complexes visible for the first time.
