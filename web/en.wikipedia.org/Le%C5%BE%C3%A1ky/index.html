Ležáky
Country: Czech Republic (then Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia)
Destroyed: 24 June 1942
Population (1942): 47

Ležáky was a village in eastern Bohemia, in what is now the Czech Republic. It was destroyed by Nazi forces on 24 June 1942 as a reprisal for the assassination of Reich Protector Reinhard Heydrich, two weeks after the similar destruction of the village of Lidice.

Destruction

The hamlet was linked to the Silver A parachutist group, whose radio transmitter had been hidden there. After the Gestapo uncovered the connection, all 33 adult inhabitants were executed at Pardubice on the day of the destruction. Eleven children were sent to the Chełmno extermination camp and murdered; two young sisters were selected for Germanisation and survived the war.

Memorial

Unlike Lidice, Ležáky was never rebuilt. The site is preserved as a memorial with marked foundations of the destroyed houses.
