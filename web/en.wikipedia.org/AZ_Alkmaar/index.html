AZ Alkmaar, officially Alkmaar Zaanstreek and widely known as AZ, is a Dutch professional football club from the city of Alkmaar. Founded in 1967 by the merger of Alkmaar '54 and FC Zaanstreek, the club plays in the Eredivisie, the top division of Dutch football, and has been one of the most successful Dutch clubs outside the traditional "big three" of Ajax, PSV, and Feyenoord.

History

Backed in the 1970s by the brothers Cees and Klaas Molenaar, AZ rose to the elite, finishing consistently near the top and winning the Eredivisie in 1980–81 — losing only one match — along with KNVB Cups in 1978, 1981, and 1982. That side, featuring players such as Kees Kist and Johnny Metgod, also reached the 1981 UEFA Cup final, losing to Ipswich Town. After financial collapse and relegation in 1988, the club rebuilt under chairman Dirk Scheringa in the 1990s and 2000s, opened the new AFAS Stadion in 2006, and, under coach Louis van Gaal, won a second league title in 2008–09. AZ added a fourth KNVB Cup in 2013 and reached the UEFA Europa Conference League semi-finals in 2023. The club is renowned for its academy and data-informed recruitment.

Stadium

AZ played at the Alkmaarderhout until 2006, then moved to the purpose-built AFAS Stadion (formerly DSB Stadion). A partial roof collapse during a storm in 2019 led to extensive reconstruction of the roof, completed in 2021.

Honours

  • KNVB Cup: 1977–78, 1980–81, 1981–82, 2012–13
  • Johan Cruyff Shield: 2009
  • Eerste Divisie: 1995–96, 1997–98
  • UEFA Cup runners-up: 1980–81
