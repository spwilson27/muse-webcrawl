Aspergillosis

Specialty: Infectious disease

Cause: Aspergillus species, most often A. fumigatus

Risk factors: Immunosuppression, chronic lung disease, asthma

Treatment: Antifungals such as voriconazole; surgery in some cases

Aspergillosis is a group of diseases caused by fungi of the genus Aspergillus. The most common causative species is Aspergillus fumigatus. Most people breathe in Aspergillus spores every day without becoming ill; disease occurs mainly in people with weakened immune systems or underlying lung conditions.

Types

Allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis (ABPA) is an allergic reaction to the fungus seen in people with asthma or cystic fibrosis. An aspergilloma is a ball of fungal hyphae that forms in a pre-existing lung cavity. Chronic pulmonary aspergillosis develops slowly in damaged lungs, while invasive aspergillosis is a rapidly spreading, life-threatening infection in severely immunocompromised patients, such as those receiving chemotherapy or organ transplants. The fungus can also infect the sinuses, skin, and other sites.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis combines chest imaging, sputum or tissue culture, microscopy, and antigen tests such as galactomannan. First-line treatment of invasive disease is the antifungal voriconazole; alternatives include isavuconazole, amphotericin B, and echinocandins. Aspergillomas may be removed surgically, and reducing immunosuppression where possible is important to recovery.
