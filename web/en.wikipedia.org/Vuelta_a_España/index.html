Vuelta a España

The Vuelta a España (Spanish for "Tour of Spain") is an annual multi-stage bicycle race primarily held in Spain, though it occasionally passes through nearby countries. Along with the Tour de France and the Giro d'Italia, it constitutes one of cycling's prestigious three-week-long Grand Tours. The race was first organised in 1935 and has been held annually since 1955; since 1995 it has taken place in August–September.

History and course

Inspired by the success of the French and Italian tours, the newspaper Informaciones launched the Vuelta in 1935. The Spanish Civil War and World War II caused early interruptions. Traditionally an April–May race, it moved to late summer in 1995 to avoid clashing with the Giro. The route changes yearly, finishing in Madrid, and is known for steep mountain finishes such as the Alto de l'Angliru and Lagos de Covadonga.

Classifications

The general classification leader wears the red jersey (maillot rojo). Other competitions include the points classification (green jersey), the mountains classification (polka-dot jersey), and the young rider classification (white jersey). Roberto Heras holds the record with four overall victories; Primož Roglič also won four consecutive editions from 2021 counts among multiple winners including Tony Rominger and Alberto Contador (three each).
