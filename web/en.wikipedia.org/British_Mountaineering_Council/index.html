British Mountaineering Council

Climbing on gritstone in the Peak District, one of the BMC's core landscapes

The British Mountaineering Council (BMC) is the national representative body for climbers, hill walkers, and mountaineers in England and Wales. Founded in 1944, it has around 85,000 members, including individual members and members of some 500 affiliated clubs. Its headquarters are in West Didsbury, Manchester.

History

The BMC was established in 1944 after proposals by the Alpine Club and other bodies for a single organisation representing British mountaineering, with Geoffrey Winthrop Young among its early advocates. It took on national governing-body functions for competition climbing and, in the 2020s, responsibility for Team GB climbing squads was organised with GB Climbing, while Olympic sport climbing debuted at Tokyo 2020 under the BMC's umbrella.

Activities

The BMC campaigns for access to crags and open country — its access work, local area volunteers, and the Regional Access Database are central to protecting climbing sites; it contributed to campaigns around the Countryside and Rights of Way Act 2000. It provides member insurance, publishes guidebooks through volunteers, runs safety training and the Mountain Training awarding framework partnerships, and supports expedition grants and environmental work such as the Mend Our Mountains path-repair appeals. It also publishes Summit magazine for members.
