Motor neuron

A motor neuron (or motoneuron) is a neuron whose cell body is located in the central nervous system — in the motor cortex, brainstem or spinal cord — and whose axon projects out to control effector organs, principally muscles and glands. Motor neurons are the final common pathway by which the nervous system produces movement: a motor neuron and the muscle fibres it innervates form a motor unit.

Upper and lower motor neurons

Upper motor neurons originate in the primary motor cortex and brainstem and descend through tracts such as the corticospinal tract to synapse on lower motor neurons, whose axons leave the central nervous system in cranial and spinal nerves and end at the neuromuscular junction, releasing acetylcholine to trigger contraction. Damage to upper motor neurons causes spasticity and hyperreflexia, while damage to lower motor neurons causes flaccid paralysis, atrophy and fasciculations — a distinction central to neurological diagnosis.

Classes

Lower motor neurons include alpha motor neurons innervating extrafusal skeletal muscle fibres, gamma motor neurons innervating muscle spindles, and beta motor neurons innervating both. Visceral (general visceral efferent) motor neurons of the autonomic nervous system control smooth muscle, cardiac muscle and glands via a two-neuron pathway.

Disease

Motor neuron diseases include amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), spinal muscular atrophy, progressive bulbar palsy and primary lateral sclerosis. Poliomyelitis destroys lower motor neurons, and toxins such as botulinum toxin block transmission at their terminals.
