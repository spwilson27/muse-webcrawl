Yosa Buson

Yosa Buson (Japanese: 与謝 蕪村; 1716 – January 17, 1784), also known as Taniguchi Buson, was a Japanese poet and painter of the Edo period. Along with Matsuo Bashō and Kobayashi Issa, Buson is considered among the greatest poets of the Edo period, and one of the greatest haiku poets of all time. He is also considered one of the greatest Japanese artists, renowned for his haiga paintings combining poetry and visual art.

Life

Buson was born in the village of Kema in Settsu Province (now part of Osaka). His original family name was Taniguchi. Little is known of his early life; his father is thought to have been a wealthy farmer, and his mother came from Yosa in Tango Province, from which Buson later took his pen name. Orphaned at a young age, Buson moved to Edo (modern Tokyo) around 1737 to study poetry and painting. He studied haikai under Hayano Hajin and, after his master's death in 1742, travelled through northern Japan, following in the footsteps of Bashō, whose work he deeply admired. He recorded these travels in prose-and-poetry (haibun) form.

Around 1751 Buson settled in Kyoto, where he spent most of the rest of his life teaching poetry, painting, and working to revive the haiku tradition in the spirit of Bashō. In 1770 he took the haigō (pen name) Yosa. He died in Kyoto in 1784 and was buried at Konpuku-ji temple.

Poetry

Buson wrote thousands of haiku. His verse is often described as more pictorial, romantic, and sensuous than Bashō's, reflecting his training as a painter. Where Bashō's poetry tends toward spiritual austerity, Buson's is noted for its vivid imagery, elegant diction, and attention to the beauty of the visible world, including everyday and even erotic subjects. Among his best-known poems is the spring haiku: "The spring sea rising / and falling, rising and falling / all day long."

Together with his disciples, Buson led the movement known as the Bashō Revival, editing anthologies and establishing schools of haikai that restored Bashō's reputation while developing a distinct, lyrical style of their own.

Painting

As a painter, Buson worked in the nanga (literati painting) style influenced by Chinese models, producing landscapes and figure paintings of great refinement. He collaborated with the painter Ike no Taiga on the celebrated screen paintings Jūben Jūgi (Ten Conveniences and Ten Pleasures, 1771), depicting the ideal life of the scholar-recluse. Buson is credited with popularizing haiga, the simple, sketch-like painting accompanying a haiku, as an art form in its own right.

Legacy

Buson ranks with Bashō and Issa as one of the three great masters of classical haiku. His integration of poetry and painting, and his lush, visual style, influenced later Japanese poets and artists, and his work is widely translated and studied internationally.
