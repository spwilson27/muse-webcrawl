Surveillance

Surveillance cameras in a public area.

Surveillance is the monitoring of behavior, many activities, or information for the purpose of information gathering, influencing, managing or directing. This can include observation from a distance by means of electronic equipment, such as closed-circuit television (CCTV), or interception of electronically transmitted information like Internet traffic. It can also include simple technical methods, such as human intelligence gathering and postal interception.

Surveillance is used by governments for intelligence gathering, prevention of crime, the protection of a process, person, group or object, or the investigation of crime. It is also used by criminal organizations to plan and commit crimes, and by businesses to gather intelligence on criminals, their competitors, suppliers or customers. Religious organisations charged with detecting heresy and heterodoxy may also carry out surveillance.

History

The word surveillance comes from a French phrase for "watching over" (sur means "from above" and veiller means "to watch"). The civil liberty community typically notes that mass surveillance is a danger to privacy and civil liberties; supporters counter that surveillance protects public safety. The tension between security and privacy has deepened with the growth of digital communications.

Types

Common forms include computer surveillance, telephones and mobile phones, surveillance cameras, social network analysis, biometric surveillance, aerial surveillance, corporate surveillance, and data mining and profiling. Modern states engage in signals intelligence and communications interception on a large scale, a practice publicly debated after the global surveillance disclosures of 2013.

Privacy concerns

Privacy advocates argue that pervasive monitoring chills free expression and association, while proponents argue it deters crime and terrorism. Many jurisdictions regulate surveillance through data protection law, warrant requirements, and oversight bodies.
