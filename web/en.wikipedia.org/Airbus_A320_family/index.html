The Airbus A320 family is a series of narrow-body, twin-engine jet airliners developed and produced by Airbus. It comprises the shortened A318 and A319, the baseline A320, and the stretched A321. First flown in 1987 and introduced into service in 1988, the A320 pioneered digital fly-by-wire flight controls and side-stick controllers in a commercial airliner, and became one of the best-selling aircraft families in history, rivalled chiefly by the Boeing 737.

An Airbus A320, the founding member of the family, in flight.

Development

Airbus launched the A320 programme in March 1984 to compete in the 150-seat single-aisle market dominated by the Boeing 737 and McDonnell Douglas MD-80. The design combined a wider fuselage than its rivals — allowing a roomier cabin and larger cargo holds — with fly-by-wire controls, a glass cockpit and composite structures. The prototype first flew on 22 February 1987, and the A320 entered service with Air France in April 1988.

The family expanded with the stretched A321 (launched 1989), the shortened A319 (1993) and the further-shortened A318 (1999). Production centred on Toulouse and Hamburg, later joined by final assembly lines in Tianjin, China, and Mobile, Alabama.

A320neo family

In December 2010 Airbus launched the A320neo (new engine option) family with more efficient engines — the CFM International LEAP-1A or Pratt & Whitney PW1000G — and sharklet wingtip devices, delivering roughly 15–20% lower fuel burn. The neo entered service with Lufthansa in January 2016 and rapidly outsold the original "ceo" generation, while the long-range A321XLR extended the family's reach to transatlantic sectors.

Sales and operations

The A320 family has been ordered by hundreds of airlines and leasing companies worldwide; cumulative deliveries passed 10,000 aircraft in the 2020s, and in October 2019 the family surpassed the Boeing 737 to become the best-selling airliner family in history by orders. The aircraft are widely used on short- and medium-haul routes, from low-cost carriers to flag airlines.
