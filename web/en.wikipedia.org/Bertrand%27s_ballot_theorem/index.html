Bertrand's ballot theorem

Bertrand's ballot theorem is a result in combinatorics concerning elections. It was first published by Joseph Bertrand in 1887 and proved by Désiré André using the reflection method.

Statement

Suppose candidate A receives p votes and candidate B receives q votes, with p > q. The probability that A is strictly ahead of B throughout the counting of the votes is (p − q)/(p + q), regardless of the order in which the ballots are counted.

Proof ideas

The classical proof uses a reflection principle: sequences in which A fails to stay ahead can be placed in bijection with sequences beginning with a vote for B, allowing the favourable sequences to be counted directly. The theorem is closely related to the reflection principle for random walks and to Catalan numbers.

Applications

The ballot theorem has applications in probability theory, queueing theory and the analysis of random walks with absorbing barriers.
