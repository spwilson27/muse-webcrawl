Deirdre Wilson is a British linguist and cognitive scientist, best known as the co-developer, with the French anthropologist Dan Sperber, of relevance theory, an influential cognitive approach to pragmatics and human communication. She is an emeritus professor of linguistics at University College London and a research professor at the Centre for the Study of Mind in Nature at the University of Oslo.

Work

Wilson's work with Sperber, presented in the book Relevance: Communication and Cognition (1986; 2nd ed. 1995), proposes that human communication is guided by a search for relevance: hearers interpret utterances by following a path of least effort to derive cognitive effects, stopping when their expectations of relevance are satisfied. The theory has been widely applied in linguistics, literary studies, psychology and communication research.

Selected publications

Her publications include Meaning and Relevance (2012), a collection of her papers, and, with Robyn Carston, a series of influential articles on lexical pragmatics. She also wrote the novel The Summons earlier in her career before turning fully to linguistics.
