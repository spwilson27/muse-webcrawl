Large igneous province

A large igneous province (LIP) is an extremely large accumulation of igneous rocks—covering more than 100,000 km² with volumes over 100,000 km³—emplaced in a geologically short interval, often a few million years. LIPs include continental flood basalts, volcanic rifted margins, oceanic plateaus and giant dyke swarms.

Examples

Famous examples include the Siberian Traps (associated with the end-Permian mass extinction), the Deccan Traps (coinciding with the end-Cretaceous extinction), the Paraná–Etendeka province, the Karoo–Ferrar province and the Ontong Java Plateau.

Causes and effects

LIPs are commonly attributed to mantle plumes reaching the lithosphere. Their eruptions release vast amounts of CO₂, sulphur and halogens, and are implicated in global warming episodes, ocean anoxic events and mass extinctions.
