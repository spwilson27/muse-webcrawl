The World Geodetic System (WGS) is a standard used in cartography, geodesy, and satellite navigation including GPS. The current version, WGS 84, defines an Earth-centered, Earth-fixed coordinate system and a geodetic datum based on a reference ellipsoid.

The WGS 84 reference ellipsoid approximates the shape of the Earth.

History

The system originated with the United States Department of Defense in the 1950s, consolidating earlier datums such as WGS 60, WGS 66, and WGS 72. WGS 84 was published in 1984 and has been refined several times, most recently in alignment with the International Terrestrial Reference Frame.

Coordinates

Positions are given as latitude, longitude, and ellipsoidal height. The prime meridian of WGS 84 lies about 102 metres east of the Royal Observatory meridian at Greenwich because of the system's Earth-centered definition.
