Digital wallet

A digital wallet, also known as an e-wallet, is an electronic device, online service, or software program that allows one party to make electronic transactions with another party bartering digital currency units for goods and services. Money can be deposited in the digital wallet prior to any transactions, or a user's bank account or payment card can be linked to the wallet.

Digital wallets are composed of both a software component, which stores payment information securely (often using tokenization, replacing card numbers with device-specific tokens), and an information component holding user details. Mobile wallets such as Apple Pay, Google Pay, and Samsung Pay use near-field communication (NFC) for contactless payments in stores, while services such as PayPal, Alipay, and WeChat Pay dominate online and peer-to-peer payments.

History

Early digital wallets appeared in the 1990s for online shopping; PayPal, founded in 1998, became the dominant example. The launch of Apple Pay in 2014 brought NFC-based mobile payment to mass adoption in many markets. In China, Alipay (2004) and WeChat Pay (2013) grew into "super-app" ecosystems used daily by hundreds of millions.

Uses

Beyond payments, digital wallets store loyalty cards, transit passes, event tickets, boarding passes, and identity documents. Cryptocurrency wallets — software or hardware that stores the private keys controlling digital assets — are a related but distinct category.
