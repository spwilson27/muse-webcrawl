Culture of Scotland

The culture of Scotland encompasses the traditions, arts, literature, music and customs of Scotland and the Scottish people. Shaped by the country's distinct history within the United Kingdom, its Celtic and Norse inheritances, and its churches and educational institutions, Scottish culture includes internationally recognised symbols such as tartan, the kilt, bagpipes, whisky and the poetry of Robert Burns.

Language and literature

Scotland has three indigenous languages: English, Scots and Scottish Gaelic. Scottish literature includes the works of Burns, Walter Scott, Robert Louis Stevenson and a modern renaissance of writers in all three languages. Burns Night, celebrated on 25 January, features haggis, whisky and recitations of the poet's work.

Music and dress

Traditional music centres on instruments such as the great Highland bagpipe, fiddle and clarsach (harp), and social dances called ceilidhs. Highland dress — the kilt in clan tartans — is worn at formal occasions and Highland games, which feature events such as caber tossing.

Festivals

Edinburgh hosts the world's largest arts festival each August, including the Edinburgh Festival Fringe. Hogmanay (New Year) is celebrated with particular enthusiasm across the country.
