Cub Scouting is the foundational program of the Boy Scouts of America (now Scouting America) for children in kindergarten through fifth grade. Organised into community-based "packs" subdivided by grade into "dens", it uses games, crafts, outdoor activities and family participation to teach character, citizenship and fitness. It was officially launched in the United States in 1930, adapting Robert Baden-Powell's British Wolf Cub scheme, and since 2018 has fully included girls.

Structure and ranks

Each grade level has its own rank: Lion (kindergarten), Tiger (grade 1), Wolf (grade 2), Bear (grade 3), and Webelos (grades 4–5), culminating in the Arrow of Light, Cub Scouting's highest award, which prepares members to cross over into a Scouts BSA troop. Youth complete required and elective "adventures" — short, hands-on activities — to earn belt loops and pins.

Activities

Signature Cub Scout events include the Pinewood Derby, in which Scouts race small wooden cars they build with an adult partner; the Raingutter Regatta; pack campouts; and community service projects. Adult leaders are trained volunteers, and the program emphasises "do your best", echoed in the Cub Scout motto.
