Wage and Hour Division

The Wage and Hour Division enforces federal minimum wage and overtime law.

The Wage and Hour Division (WHD) is a division of the United States Department of Labor responsible for enforcing federal labour standards, most notably the minimum wage, overtime pay, recordkeeping, and child-labour provisions of the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) of 1938, the law under which the division was created.

Responsibilities

WHD investigators conduct workplace inspections, respond to worker complaints, and recover back wages for employees who were underpaid. In addition to the FLSA, the division enforces the Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA), the Migrant and Seasonal Agricultural Worker Protection Act, prevailing-wage requirements on federal contracts under the Davis–Bacon and Service Contract Acts, and wage provisions of immigration programmes such as H-1B and H-2A. Its standards cover more than 140 million workers in over seven million establishments.

History

The division was established in 1938 with William Rogers as its first administrator, following the Supreme Court's upholding of federal labour regulation. Elmer Andrews became its first prominent administrator in 1938–39. Over the decades the division's remit expanded as Congress extended FLSA coverage to retail, service, and public-sector workers and added new statutes such as the FMLA in 1993.
