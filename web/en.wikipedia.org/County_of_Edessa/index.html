County of Edessa

Capital: Edessa (1098–1144); Turbessel (1144–1150)

Established: 1098

Disestablished: 1150

First count: Baldwin I

The County of Edessa was one of the Crusader states in the 12th century. It was the first of the states to be established during and after the First Crusade, and the first to fall. Unlike the other Crusader states, it was largely landlocked and remote from the others, and much of its population was Armenian and Syriac Christian rather than Latin.

History

Baldwin of Boulogne, invited by the Armenian ruler Thoros of Edessa, took control of the city in 1098, becoming the first count. When Baldwin became king of Jerusalem in 1100, the county passed to his cousin Baldwin of Bourcq, and later to Joscelin I and Joscelin II of Courtenay. The county's exposed position east of the Euphrates made it vulnerable to the rising power of Zengi of Mosul.

Fall

In December 1144 Zengi besieged and captured Edessa — the first major Crusader city to fall — an event that shocked Christendom and triggered the Second Crusade. Joscelin II briefly retook the city in 1146, but it was recaptured and its Christian population massacred or enslaved. The remaining fortresses west of the Euphrates were sold to the Byzantine Empire and soon conquered by the Muslims; by 1150 the county had ceased to exist.
