Atomic, molecular, and optical physics

AMO physics
Field: Physics
Topics: atomic structure, molecular spectra, lasers, quantum optics, ultracold atoms

Atomic, molecular, and optical physics (AMO) is the study of matter–matter and light–matter interactions at the scale of one or a few atoms. It encompasses the structure and dynamics of atoms and molecules, their interaction with electromagnetic radiation, and the properties of light itself, particularly where quantum mechanics is essential.

Scope

Atomic physics treats the atom as an isolated system of electrons and nucleus; molecular physics extends this to molecules, their rotations, vibrations, and chemical bonds; optical physics studies the generation, control, and detection of light, including lasers and nonlinear optics. Quantum optics examines light at the level of individual photons and phenomena such as entanglement.

Modern directions

The advent of laser cooling and trapping enabled the creation of Bose–Einstein condensates (1995) and ultracold quantum gases, now used to simulate condensed-matter systems. AMO techniques underlie atomic clocks that define the second, precision tests of fundamental physics, and much of quantum information science.
