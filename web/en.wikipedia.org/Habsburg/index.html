The House of Habsburg (German: Haus Habsburg), also known as the House of Austria, is one of the most prominent and important dynasties in European history. Taking its name from Habsburg Castle in present-day Switzerland, the family rose from counts in the Aargau to dukes of Austria (1282), and from 1438 held the title of Holy Roman Emperor almost continuously until the empire's dissolution in 1806.

Rise and expansion

Through strategic marriages rather than conquest — summed up in the saying "Let others wage war; you, fortunate Austria, marry" — the Habsburgs acquired Burgundy, the Netherlands, Spain and its empire, Bohemia and Hungary. Under Charles V (r. 1519–1556), who was simultaneously Holy Roman Emperor, King of Spain, and ruler of the Netherlands and the Spanish Americas, the Habsburg dominions formed the first empire "on which the sun never sets". Charles's abdication split the house into Spanish and Austrian branches.

Later history

The Spanish branch ended with the death of Charles II in 1700, precipitating the War of the Spanish Succession. The Austrian branch continued as rulers of Austria, Hungary and Bohemia and heads of the Habsburg Monarchy, becoming emperors of Austria in 1804 and of Austria-Hungary in 1867. Defeat in the First World War ended Habsburg rule in 1918; the last emperor, Charles I, went into exile. The family's later reputation suffered from the inbreeding of the Spanish line, visible in the characteristic "Habsburg jaw". The present head of the house is Karl von Habsburg.
