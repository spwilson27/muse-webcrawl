University College, informally known as Castle, is the oldest college of Durham University in England. Founded in 1832, it is housed in Durham Castle, a Norman castle on Palace Green that forms part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site together with Durham Cathedral. Students resident in the castle dine in its medieval Great Hall.

History

Durham University was founded by Act of Parliament in 1832 under the initiative of Bishop William van Mildert; University College was its first and, for a time, only college, and the castle — formerly a palace of the Prince-Bishops of Durham — was given to the new university. The keep was rebuilt in the nineteenth century to provide student accommodation. Additional accommodation was built at Moatside Court in the twentieth century.

Student life

Castle is one of Durham's most oversubscribed colleges. Its Junior Common Room organises social events including the June Ball, held in the castle. Formals (gowned dinners) take place in the Great Hall several times a term. The college has a chapel choir, boat club and an annual arts festival. A longstanding rivalry exists with neighbouring Hatfield College.

Notable alumni

Alumni include the historian and broadcaster, former members of Parliament, senior clergy and academics. The college's master's office has been held by figures including the theologian David Jasper and historian David Held (as acting master).
