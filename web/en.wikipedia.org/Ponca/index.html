Ponca

The Ponca are a Native American people of the Dhegiha Siouan group, originally from the region around the confluence of the Niobrara and Missouri rivers in present-day Nebraska and South Dakota.

History

The Ponca were closely related in language and culture to the Omaha, Osage, Kansa, and Quapaw. In 1877 the United States government forcibly removed the Ponca from their Nebraska homeland to Indian Territory (present-day Oklahoma), a journey on which many died, including the son of Chief Standing Bear. In 1879 Standing Bear's habeas corpus case, Standing Bear v. Crook, established that Native Americans are persons under U.S. law. Part of the tribe remained in Oklahoma (the Ponca Tribe of Indians of Oklahoma), while those who returned to Nebraska became the Ponca Tribe of Nebraska, federally recognized in 1990.

Culture

Traditional Ponca life combined farming—corn, beans, and squash—with seasonal buffalo hunts. The Hethushka society dance is an antecedent of the modern powwow war dance.
