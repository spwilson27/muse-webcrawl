Harvard College is the undergraduate college of Harvard University, an Ivy League research university in Cambridge, Massachusetts. Founded in 1636, it is the oldest institution of higher learning in the United States and the original school from which the university grew. Its roughly 6,800 students pursue a liberal arts curriculum of concentrations (majors), general education and electives, and live from their second year in one of twelve residential Houses.

History

The Great and General Court of the Massachusetts Bay Colony voted in 1636 to establish a college at Newtowne (soon renamed Cambridge). In 1638 the young clergyman John Harvard bequeathed his library of some 400 books and half his estate, and the school took his name; its first president, Henry Dunster, modelled it on the English universities. Harvard College was incorporated in 1650 under the Harvard Charter, which still governs the university. Over the 19th century, under presidents including Charles William Eliot (1869–1909), the undergraduate curriculum moved from a fixed classical course to the elective system and then to concentration and distribution requirements.

Academics and admissions

Students choose from around 50 concentrations in the arts, humanities, social sciences, natural sciences and engineering, alongside a Programme in General Education. Admission is among the most selective in the world, with acceptance rates in the low single digits, and is need-blind with full-need financial aid for domestic and international students alike. Graduates receive the AB or SB degree at the Commencement exercises in Harvard Yard.

Student life and the House system

First-year students live in Harvard Yard; sophomores, juniors and seniors are assigned, largely by lottery ("Housing Day" is a celebrated rite), to twelve residential Houses — Adams, Cabot, Currier, Dunster, Eliot, Kirkland, Leverett, Lowell, Mather, Pforzheimer, Quincy and Winthrop — each with its own dining hall, library, faculty dean and traditions, a system established in the 1930s under President Abbott Lawrence Lowell with the benefaction of Edward Harkness. Student life includes more than 400 extracurricular organisations, the Harvard Crimson newspaper (1873), the Hasty Pudding theatricals, and varsity athletics, including the Harvard–Yale football rivalry known simply as The Game.
