Sigismund's Chapel

Sigismund's Chapel (Polish: Kaplica Zygmuntowska) is a royal chapel of the Wawel Cathedral in Kraków, Poland. Built between 1519 and 1533 under King Sigismund I the Old, it is considered one of the finest examples of Renaissance architecture north of the Alps and serves as the burial place of the last Jagiellonian kings of Poland.

History

King Sigismund I commissioned the chapel in 1517 following the death of his first wife, Barbara Zápolya. The Florentine architect Bartolomeo Berrecci designed and supervised the work, executed by Italian artists including Giovanni Cini and Bernardino de Gianotti. The chapel was consecrated in 1533 and became the mausoleum of Sigismund I, his son Sigismund II Augustus, and his daughter Anna Jagiellon.

Architecture

The chapel is a square structure topped by a dome resting on an octagonal drum, crowned with a lantern. Its interior is richly decorated with stucco, grotesque ornament, and carved sandstone. The royal tombs, carved in red Hungarian marble, show the kings in full armour in the pose of awakening, a motif linked to humanist ideas of fame and immortality.

Significance

Art historians regard the chapel as the purest work of the Italian Renaissance in Poland and a turning point in the introduction of Renaissance forms into Central and Eastern Europe.
