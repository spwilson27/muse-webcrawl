Balafon

The balafon is a West African percussion instrument, a type of xylophone with wooden keys mounted over calabash gourd resonators. It is central to the musical traditions of the Mandé and neighbouring peoples of Mali, Guinea, Burkina Faso, Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire and northern Ghana.

Construction

A balafon typically has between 16 and 27 keys of dense hardwood, often bene or rosewood, tuned to pentatonic or heptatonic scales that may differ between ethnic groups. Beneath each key hangs a gourd resonator; small holes in the gourds covered with thin membranes — traditionally spider egg-sac silk or thin plastic — produce the instrument's characteristic buzzing timbre. Players strike the keys with two mallets tipped with rubber.

Cultural role

The balafon is strongly associated with griot (jeli) musicians, who perform epics, praise songs and dance music, often in ensembles with two or three balafons playing interlocking parts. According to Mandé tradition, the most revered instrument, the Sosso Bala of Guinea, dates to the 13th-century Mali Empire of Sundiata Keita and is kept by the Kouyaté family; its cultural space was proclaimed by UNESCO as part of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
