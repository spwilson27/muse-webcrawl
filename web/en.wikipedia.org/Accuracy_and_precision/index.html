Accuracy and precision

Accuracy and precision
Field: Measurement science
Accuracy: Closeness to true value (bias)
Precision: Closeness of repeated results (variance)

Accuracy and precision are two measures of observational error. Accuracy is how close a given set of measurements is to their true value, while precision is how close the measurements are to each other.

Definitions

In measurement, accuracy reflects systematic error (bias), whereas precision reflects random error. A measurement system can be accurate but not precise, precise but not accurate, neither, or both. The combination corresponds to the distinction between trueness and precision in ISO 5725.

In statistics and classification

In information retrieval and machine learning, precision is the fraction of retrieved instances that are relevant, while recall is the fraction of relevant instances retrieved. Accuracy in binary classification is the proportion of correct predictions overall.

Target analogy

A common illustration uses darts on a target: tight grouping near the bullseye is both accurate and precise; tight grouping off-center is precise but inaccurate; scattered hits centered on the bullseye average to accurate but are imprecise.
