First fundamental form

In differential geometry, the first fundamental form is the inner product on the tangent space of a surface in three-dimensional Euclidean space, canonically induced from the inner product of R3. It allows the calculation of curvature and metric properties of the surface — arc length of curves, angles between curves, and areas of regions — without reference to the ambient space.

Definition

For a surface parametrised by x(u, v), the first fundamental form is the quadratic form I = E du² + 2F du dv + G dv², where E = xu·xu, F = xu·xv and G = xv·xv are built from the partial derivatives of the parametrisation. In modern notation it is simply the pullback of the Euclidean metric by the parametrisation, equivalently the restriction of the ambient inner product to each tangent plane.

Uses

The length of a curve on the surface and the area element √(EG − F²) du dv follow directly from the form. Quantities depending only on the first fundamental form are called intrinsic; Gauss's Theorema Egregium famously shows that Gaussian curvature, though defined using the second fundamental form, is in fact intrinsic.
