Perfume

Perfume is a mixture of fragrant essential oils or aroma compounds, fixatives and solvents, usually in liquid form, used to give the human body, animals, food, objects and living spaces a pleasant scent. The word derives from Latin per fumum, "through smoke".

Perfume types by concentration
Parfum: 20–30%
Eau de parfum: 15–20%
Eau de toilette: 5–15%
Eau de cologne: 2–4%

History

Perfumery began in ancient Mesopotamia, Egypt and the Indus Valley. The world's first recorded chemist, Tapputi, is mentioned on a cuneiform tablet from the 2nd millennium BCE. Perfume manufacture flourished in the Islamic world and in Renaissance Europe; Grasse in France became a centre of the industry. Modern perfumery began in the late 19th century with synthetic aroma chemicals such as coumarin and vanillin.

Composition

A fragrance is often described in notes: top notes perceived first, heart (middle) notes, and base notes that linger. Sources include flowers, fruits, spices, resins, leaves, woods and animal products, increasingly replaced by synthetics.
