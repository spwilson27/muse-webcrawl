Punjabi culture

Punjabi culture is the culture of the Punjabi people, natives of the Punjab region of the northern Indian subcontinent, now divided between India and Pakistan. It is shaped by the region's fertile agricultural plains, its position on historic trade and invasion routes, and the traditions of its Hindu, Muslim, and Sikh communities.

Language and literature

The Punjabi language is written in the Gurmukhi script in India and in the Shahmukhi (Perso-Arabic) script in Pakistan. Punjabi literature includes the Sufi poetry of Bulleh Shah and Waris Shah, whose epic Heer Ranjha is a classic of the language, as well as the hymns of the Guru Granth Sahib, the holy scripture of Sikhism.

Festivals

Major festivals include Vaisakhi, celebrating the spring harvest and the founding of the Khalsa; Lohri, a winter festival centred on bonfires; Diwali and Bandi Chhor Divas; and Eid celebrations among Punjabi Muslims. Weddings are elaborate multi-day affairs featuring music, dance, and feasting.

Music and dance

Bhangra, originally a folk dance of farmers celebrating the harvest, has become a globally popular music and dance form. Giddha is a traditional dance performed by women. Punjabi folk instruments include the dhol, tumbi, and algoza.

Cuisine

Punjabi cuisine is known for rich, hearty dishes such as butter chicken, sarson da saag with makki di roti, chole, dal makhani, and tandoori breads, along with dairy products like paneer and lassi.
