ESTREAM

eSTREAM is a project run by the European Network of Excellence in Cryptology (ECRYPT) to identify new stream ciphers suitable for widespread adoption. Announced in 2004, it called for submissions in two profiles: Profile 1 for software ciphers with high throughput, and Profile 2 for hardware ciphers with limited resources such as gate count, memory and power consumption. The project received 34 submissions from around the world.

Process and results

The evaluation proceeded in phases between 2005 and 2008, involving public cryptanalysis and performance benchmarking coordinated by ECRYPT. Several candidates were eliminated as attacks were published; others were dropped for performance or design reasons. The final portfolio, announced in 2008 and revised in 2012, contains seven ciphers:

  • Profile 1 (software): HC-128, Rabbit, Salsa20/12, and SOSEMANUK.
  • Profile 2 (hardware): F-FCSR-H v2, Grain v1, MICKEY 2.0, and Trivium (eight ciphers were retained across the two profiles; Trivium and Grain became among the most influential lightweight designs).

Note: counts are remembered from memory — the portfolio is generally described as containing seven ciphers after the 2012 revision, with F-FCSR-H v2 removed following cryptanalysis, leaving HC-128, Rabbit, Salsa20/12 and SOSEMANUK in Profile 1 and Grain v1, MICKEY 2.0 and Trivium in Profile 2.

Legacy

The eSTREAM ciphers have been standardised in ISO/IEC 29192-3 and influenced later competitions, including NIST's lightweight cryptography standardisation. Salsa20's successor ChaCha became ubiquitous in internet protocols, while Trivium and Grain remain reference hardware-oriented stream ciphers.
