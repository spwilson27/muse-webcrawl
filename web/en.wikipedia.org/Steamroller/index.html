Steamroller

A steamroller (or steam roller) is a form of road roller — a type of construction engineering vehicle used to compact soil, gravel, concrete, or asphalt in the construction of roads and foundations — propelled by the weight of the vehicle and by a steam engine. Similar compaction vehicles with a smooth roller were also originally called rollers. Although steam propulsion was superseded by diesel and other internal combustion in the middle of the 20th century, the term "steamroller" persists in common usage for road rollers generally.

History

Steam rollers were produced from the 1860s, notably by the British firm Aveling and Porter, whose engines became the archetype of the type. Unlike traction engines, steam rollers have smooth, wide front rolls designed to flatten surfaces without leaving tread marks, and they work by slow repeated passes that use the machine's weight to compact material.

Metaphorical use

From its literal sense derives the figurative use of "steamroller" for an irresistible force that crushes opposition — as in the Russian "steamroller" of the First World War — and the verb "to steamroll", meaning to overwhelm or to force something through by sheer weight of power.
