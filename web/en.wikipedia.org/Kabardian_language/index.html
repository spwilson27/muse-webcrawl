Kabardian language

Kabardian (къэбэрдеибзэ, Qabardeibza), also known as East Circassian, is a Northwest Caucasian language spoken by the Kabardian (East Circassian) people, mainly in the Kabardino-Balkaria and Karachay-Cherkessia republics of Russia, with large diaspora communities in Turkey and the Middle East.

Features

Kabardian forms a dialect continuum with Adyghe (West Circassian), and the literary standards are based on different dialects. The language is notable for a very large consonant inventory — including ejective and labialised consonants — contrasted with only two or three phonemic vowels, a typologically unusual combination. Grammar is ergative and highly polysynthetic, with verbs encoding subject, objects and many other categories.

History and writing

Kabardians were deported in large numbers to the Ottoman Empire after the Russian conquest of the Caucasus in 1864, leaving substantial speaker communities abroad. A written standard was developed in the Soviet period, first in Latin script (from 1923) and then in an adapted Cyrillic alphabet (from 1936) that uses digraphs and the letter Ӏ for ejectives and glottalised sounds. Kabardian is co-official in its home republics and is taught in schools and used in regional media.
