Amelia Fletcher (born 1 April 1966) is a British singer, songwriter, guitarist and economist. She is best known as the frontwoman of the indie pop bands Talulah Gosh and Heavenly, and has been a central figure in the twee pop and indiepop movements. In parallel with her music career, she has worked as a professional economist, including as chief economist at the UK Office of Fair Trading.

Music career

Fletcher formed Talulah Gosh in Oxford in 1986 with Elizabeth Price, Mathew Fletcher (her brother), Peter Momtchiloff and Chris Scott. One of the leading bands of the C86 and shambling scenes, Talulah Gosh released a handful of singles before splitting in 1988. Fletcher and Momtchiloff then formed Heavenly, whose four albums on Sarah Records and K Records — including The Decline and Fall of Heavenly (1994) — made them one of the defining acts of twee pop.

After drummer Mathew Fletcher's death in 1996, Heavenly disbanded; Amelia Fletcher continued with Marine Research, then Tender Trap, and later The Catenary Wires with Rob Pursey. In 2020 she and Pursey formed Swansea Sound, releasing the albums Live at the Rum Puncheon (2021) and Twentieth Century (2023).

Economics career

Fletcher studied economics at university and earned a DPhil at Oxford. She worked at the consultancy NERA and served as chief economist of the Office of Fair Trading from 2001 to 2013. She has subsequently been professor of competition policy at the University of East Anglia's Norwich Business School and has held roles advising on digital markets and competition regulation in the UK.

Legacy

Fletcher's songwriting — combining bright melodies with feminism and melancholy — has influenced generations of indie pop musicians, and she is regarded as one of the originators of the twee pop sound.
