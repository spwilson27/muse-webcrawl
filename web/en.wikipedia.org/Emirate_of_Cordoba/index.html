The Emirate of Córdoba (Arabic: إمارة قرطبة) was a Muslim kingdom in the Iberian Peninsula from 756 to 929, ruled by the Umayyad dynasty and centred on the city of Córdoba. It was established by Abd al-Rahman I, a survivor of the Umayyad dynasty who fled the Abbasid Revolution in Syria and seized control of al-Andalus.

History

Abd al-Rahman I landed in Iberia in 755 and defeated the existing governor at the Battle of Musarah in 756, proclaiming himself emir. Although nominally an emirate, it was independent of the Abbasid Caliphate in Baghdad. His successors consolidated Umayyad rule over most of Muslim Iberia, fighting the Christian kingdoms of the north and internal revolts such as the long rebellion of Umar ibn Hafsun.

Society and culture

Under the emirs, Córdoba grew into one of the largest and most sophisticated cities in Europe, with paved streets, irrigation works and the beginnings of the Great Mosque of Córdoba. The population was a mixture of Arab and Berber settlers, converted Iberians (Muwallads), Christians (Mozarabs) and Jews.

End of the emirate

In 929, Abd al-Rahman III, having restored internal unity, proclaimed himself caliph, transforming the emirate into the Caliphate of Córdoba, which rivalled the Abbasids and Fatimids in prestige.
