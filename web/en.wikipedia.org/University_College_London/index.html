University College London

Type: Public research university
Established: 1826
Location: London, England
Students: ~46,000

University College London (UCL) is a public research university in London, England, and a member institution of the federal University of London. Founded in 1826 as London University, it was the first university institution in England to admit students regardless of religion and the first to admit women on equal terms with men.

History

Inspired by the ideas of Jeremy Bentham — whose auto-icon is famously displayed at the college — UCL pioneered secular higher education in England. It merged with numerous medical schools and institutes, including the Institute of Neurology and the Royal Free Hospital school, and became a founding member of the Russell Group.

Academics

UCL is consistently ranked among the world's top ten universities. It has 11 faculties, including the Bartlett (architecture), the Slade School of Fine Art and a large medical school. Alumni and staff include 30 Nobel laureates, and discoveries associated with UCL include the inert gases and the structure of DNA's context through Rosalind Franklin's contemporaries and Maurice Wilkins's King's/UCL milieu.
