Supermajor

Supermajor is a term used in the petroleum industry for the largest publicly traded oil and gas companies. The group, an extension of the earlier "Seven Sisters", is conventionally taken to comprise BP, Chevron, ExxonMobil, Shell, TotalEnergies, and ConocoPhillips — with Eni sometimes included.

Usage

The term gained currency in the late 1990s and early 2000s as a wave of mergers — such as Exxon with Mobil, Chevron with Texaco, and BP with Amoco and ARCO — created integrated companies of unprecedented scale, active across exploration, production, refining, and marketing worldwide.

Context

Despite their size, the supermajors control a relatively small share of world oil and gas reserves compared with national oil companies such as Saudi Aramco, which hold the majority of global reserves.
