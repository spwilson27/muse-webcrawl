Gendarmerie

A gendarmerie is a military force with law-enforcement duties among the civilian population. The term derives from the Old French gens d'armes ("men-at-arms"). Gendarmes are soldiers — with military organisation, ranks and discipline — who police civilians, typically in rural areas and small towns, complementing civilian police forces that cover cities.

Characteristics

Gendarmerie forces generally combine police powers (patrol, criminal investigation, traffic, public order) with military status and capabilities. Their personnel are subject to military law, live in barracks in some countries, and can be deployed abroad on military or stability-policing missions. They often also guard borders, government buildings and critical infrastructure, and provide specialised units such as mountain rescue, maritime patrol and intervention squadrons.

History

The model originates in France: the medieval Maréchaussée policed the countryside and highways, and in 1791 it was renamed the Gendarmerie Nationale. Napoleon's armies spread the institution across Europe, and it was widely adopted in countries with French legal influence.

Examples

  • France: Gendarmerie Nationale, one of France's two national police forces, under the Ministry of the Interior since 2009 but retaining military status.
  • Italy: the Carabinieri, an armed force in its own right with general policing duties.
  • Spain: the Guardia Civil, founded 1844.
  • Netherlands: the Royal Marechaussee, a service of the armed forces with military police and border duties.
  • Turkey: the Gendarmerie General Command; Romania: the Jandarmeria; and similar forces in many francophone African states and Latin American countries.

Several European gendarmeries cooperate through the European Gendarmerie Force (EUROGENDFOR), formed in 2006 for crisis-management operations.
