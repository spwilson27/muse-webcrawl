The International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda (ICTR) was an international court established by the United Nations Security Council in November 1994 to prosecute persons responsible for the Rwandan genocide and other serious violations of international humanitarian law committed in Rwanda and neighbouring states between January and December 1994. It was seated in Arusha, Tanzania.

Mandate and work

The Tribunal had jurisdiction over genocide, crimes against humanity and war crimes. Over its lifetime it indicted 93 individuals, completing trials of political, military and media leaders. Among the convicted were Prime Minister Jean Kambanda — the first head of government convicted of genocide — and Théoneste Bagosora, a senior military officer regarded as a central organiser of the genocide.

Landmark judgments

The 1998 judgment in Prosecutor v. Akayesu was the first conviction for genocide by an international court and the first to recognise that rape and sexual violence could constitute acts of genocide. The "Media case" (Nahimana et al.) addressed the role of RTLM radio broadcasts in inciting the killings and set important precedents on hate speech and incitement.

Closure

The ICTR formally closed on 31 December 2015. Its remaining functions — including tracking fugitives, protecting witnesses and supervising sentences — passed to the International Residual Mechanism for Criminal Tribunals, which also succeeded the tribunal for the former Yugoslavia. Félicien Kabuga, long among the most wanted fugitives, was arrested in France in 2020 and transferred to the Mechanism.
