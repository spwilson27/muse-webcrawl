The Los Angeles County Board of Supervisors is the five-member elected governing body of Los Angeles County, California, the most populous county in the United States with roughly ten million residents. Because it exercises legislative, executive and quasi-judicial powers over such a large population and budget, its members are sometimes called the "five little kings".

Powers and responsibilities

The Board sets the county budget — over $40 billion annually in recent years — and oversees departments providing health care, public welfare, law enforcement through the Sheriff's Department, fire protection, public works and services to unincorporated areas. It also sits as the governing body of various special districts.

History

The Board was created in 1852, shortly after county government was organised in California. District boundaries are redrawn after each census; in 2024 voters approved Measure G, a charter amendment expanding the Board from five to nine members and creating an elected county chief executive, to be implemented over the following election cycles.
