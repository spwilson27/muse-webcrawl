Pokémon Gold and Silver

Pokémon Gold Version and Pokémon Silver Version are 1999 role-playing video games developed by Game Freak and published by Nintendo for the Game Boy Color. They are the first installments in the second generation of the Pokémon video game series, released in Japan on 21 November 1999, in North America in October 2000, and in Europe in April 2001. An enhanced version, Pokémon Crystal, followed in 2000. Remakes, Pokémon HeartGold and SoulSilver, were released for the Nintendo DS in 2009.

The Johto region, introduced in Gold and Silver.

Gameplay

Players control a young Trainer traveling the Johto region, catching and training Pokémon to battle the eight Gym Leaders and the Elite Four. The games introduced 100 new species of Pokémon, bringing the total to 251, along with two new types (Dark and Steel), Pokémon breeding, held items, a real-time day-and-night cycle driven by an internal clock, and the Pokégear device. After defeating the Johto League, players can travel to the Kanto region from Red and Blue and face its Gym Leaders, culminating in a battle against Red atop Mt. Silver.

Development

Directed by Satoshi Tajiri and produced by Junichi Masuda, the games were designed to conclude the series and squeezed both Johto and Kanto onto one cartridge with the help of Nintendo president Satoru Iwata, who optimized the code to fit the extra region. Shiny Pokémon, the Unown, and legendary Pokémon Ho-Oh, Lugia, Suicune, Raikou, Entei, and Celebi debuted here.

Reception

Gold and Silver were critical and commercial successes, selling over 23 million copies and becoming among the best-selling Game Boy games. Critics praised the new features, the day/night system, and the two-region structure; the games are frequently cited among the greatest video games of all time.
