Electronic Road Pricing

Electronic Road Pricing (ERP) is an electronic toll collection system used in Singapore to manage traffic by way of road pricing based on usage. It was the first electronic congestion charging system implemented in the world when it began operation in 1998.

System

Vehicles carry an in-vehicle unit holding a stored-value card. As a vehicle passes under a gantry at a charging point, the fee is deducted automatically. Charges vary by location and time of day, being highest during peak periods on congested expressways and in the central business district.

History

The ERP replaced the manual Area Licensing Scheme, in force since 1975. It is administered by the Land Transport Authority, which adjusts rates quarterly to keep traffic speeds within target ranges.

Effects

The system is credited with keeping traffic flowing in central Singapore and encouraging use of public transport. A satellite-based successor, using global navigation satellite system technology, has been developed to allow distance-based charging.
