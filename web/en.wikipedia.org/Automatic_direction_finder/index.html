Automatic direction finder

An automatic direction finder (ADF) is an aircraft radio-navigation instrument that automatically and continuously indicates the relative bearing from the aircraft to a suitable ground radio station, most commonly a non-directional beacon (NDB) transmitting in the low- and medium-frequency bands.

Operation

The ADF receiver combines a directional loop antenna with a non-directional sense antenna to resolve the bearing unambiguously. The cockpit indicator — either a fixed-card, rotatable-card or radio magnetic indicator (RMI) display — shows the bearing to the station, which the pilot combines with the aircraft heading to navigate. Because NDBs transmit their identifier in Morse code, pilots must listen to the ident to confirm the correct station. ADF suffers from effects including night effect, coastal refraction, terrain and thunderstorm interference, and signals can be reflected by the ionosphere at dawn and dusk.

Decline

ADF/NDB navigation was a mainstay of airways systems for much of the twentieth century, but has been widely superseded by VOR, DME and satellite navigation (GNSS/GPS); many air forces and regulators have decommissioned NDBs, though the equipment remains common in general aviation and as a backup in some regions.
