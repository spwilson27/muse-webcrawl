Misrata International Airport (IATA: MRA, ICAO: HLMS) is an international airport serving Misrata, a Mediterranean coastal city in northwestern Libya. It lies about 8 km southwest of the city centre and shares its site with an air base of the Libyan Air Force.

The airport originated as a military airfield constructed in the late 1930s during the Italian colonial period. Civilian development followed Libya's independence, and during the Gaddafi era the base hosted Soviet-built aircraft operated by the Libyan Air Force. In the 2011 Libyan Civil War, the airport was captured by anti-Gaddafi forces after the siege of Misrata, and NATO aircraft struck military installations at the site. It became the city's lifeline and principal gateway following the closure of Tripoli International Airport during later fighting in 2014.

Since then the airport has handled scheduled services by Libyan carriers such as Libyan Airlines, Afriqiyah Airways, Buraq Air, and Berniq Airways, mainly to Istanbul, Tunis, Alexandria, and other regional destinations, together with cargo and charter traffic connected with the Misrata free zone. Expansion projects, including a new passenger terminal, have been planned to raise capacity.
