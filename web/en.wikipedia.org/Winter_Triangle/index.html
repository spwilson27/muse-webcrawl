Winter Triangle

StarsBetelgeuse, Sirius, Procyon
ConstellationsOrion, Canis Major, Canis Minor
Best visibleWinter evenings (Northern Hemisphere)

The Winter Triangle is an astronomical asterism formed by three of the brightest stars in the northern winter sky: Sirius in Canis Major, Procyon in Canis Minor, and Betelgeuse in Orion. The three stars form a nearly equilateral triangle that is easy to locate on clear winter nights.

Members

Sirius, at magnitude −1.46, is the brightest star in the entire night sky; it lies at the southern vertex of the triangle. Procyon, the eighth-brightest star, marks the eastern vertex, while the red supergiant Betelgeuse — itself variable in brightness — marks the northwestern point near Orion's shoulder. The much larger Winter Hexagon shares Sirius and Procyon among its vertices.

Visibility

The asterism is prominent from mid-northern latitudes during the months around the December solstice and remains visible through early spring. From the Southern Hemisphere it appears during the summer months, inverted relative to its northern appearance.
