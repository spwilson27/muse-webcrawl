Massed pipes and drums on the castle esplanade

The Royal Edinburgh Military Tattoo is a series of military tattoos — performances of music and display by armed forces — held each August on the esplanade of Edinburgh Castle, Scotland, during the Edinburgh festivals. It is one of Scotland's most famous cultural events, watched by a total television audience estimated in the hundreds of millions.

History

The first Edinburgh Tattoo, titled "Something About a Soldier", was staged in 1949; the first formal Tattoo followed in 1950 with eight items and Lieutenant Colonel George Malcolm of Poltalloch as producer. Brigadier Norman Gillett and later producers expanded the format. Queen Elizabeth II granted the "Royal" title in 2010, the Tattoo's sixtieth year. Remarkably, no performance has ever been cancelled because of weather — the show famously goes on in all conditions.

The performance

Each performance centres on the Massed Pipes and Drums of the Royal Navy, Army and Royal Air Force, and the Massed Military Bands, framed by the castle's floodlit ramparts and projections on the castle wall. International contingents — from Norway's King's Guard to the Trinidad and Tobago Defence Force steel orchestra — feature alongside cultural acts, precision drill teams and fireworks. The show traditionally closes with everyone joining in "Scotland the Brave" and "Auld Lang Syne", and a lone piper playing a lament from the castle battlements in memory of fallen comrades.

Charitable work and touring

The Tattoo is run by a charitable company; since 1950 it has distributed over £12 million to military, veterans' and arts charities. International tours have taken official overseas versions of the show to Sydney, Melbourne, Oslo and other cities.
