North and South (novel)

North and South is a social novel published in 1854–55 by the English writer Elizabeth Gaskell. The story, first serialised in Charles Dickens's magazine Household Words, follows Margaret Hale, a clergyman's daughter from the rural south of England, who moves with her family to the industrial manufacturing town of Milton (based on Manchester) in the north. Through her conflict and eventual understanding with mill owner John Thornton, the novel explores the social and moral conflicts of the industrial revolution.

Plot

When her father leaves the Church of England over a crisis of conscience, Margaret Hale moves with her parents from the Hampshire village of Helstone to Milton, where Mr Hale works as a tutor. Margaret clashes with John Thornton, a self-made cotton manufacturer, over the treatment of workers. Thornton falls in love with her and proposes; she refuses. Margaret becomes involved in the world of the striking workers, befriending the Higgins family and shielding Thornton from a mob at personal risk. After the deaths of both her parents and a series of misunderstandings — including Thornton's mistaken belief that Margaret has a lover (her fugitive brother Frederick) — the two are reconciled, and Margaret, now an heiress, invests in Thornton's failing mill; they marry.

Themes

The novel contrasts the agricultural, tradition-bound South with the industrial, commercial North, and examines class conflict, trade unionism, and the mutual incomprehension of masters and men. Unlike many "Condition-of-England" novels, Gaskell — who knew Manchester intimately — gives both the manufacturer's and the workers' viewpoints considerable sympathy. Gender, religion, and the moral responsibilities of wealth are also central themes.

Adaptations

The novel was adapted by the BBC in 1975 and again in 2004; the four-part 2004 serial, starring Daniela Denby-Ashe as Margaret and Richard Armitage as Thornton, became a surprise popular success and greatly raised the novel's readership.
