Cnidocyte

A cnidocyte (also known as a cnidoblast or nematocyte) is an explosive cell containing one large secretory organelle called a cnidocyst or nematocyst. Cnidocytes are the defining feature of the phylum Cnidaria, which includes jellyfish, sea anemones, corals, and hydrae, and are used for prey capture and defense.

Structure and discharge

The cnidocyst consists of a capsule holding a coiled, hollow tubule, often barbed and connected to a venom sac. A hair-like trigger, the cnidocil, protrudes from the cell. When triggered mechanically or chemically, the capsule everts the tubule with enormous acceleration — among the fastest processes in biology — piercing prey and injecting venom.

Function

Cnidocytes are single-use cells; after discharge they are replaced by new cells migrating from interstitial stem cell populations. Their stings range from mild irritations to potentially lethal envenomations in humans, as with box jellyfish.
