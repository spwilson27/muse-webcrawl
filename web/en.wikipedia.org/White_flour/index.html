White flour

White flour is flour made from wheat after the bran and germ have been removed from the endosperm. Roller milling in the nineteenth century made fine white flour inexpensive and widely available, changing baking and bread consumption.

Production

After cleaning and tempering, wheat passes through rollers and sieves that separate the starchy endosperm from bran and germ. Some flours are bleached or treated to modify colour and baking qualities. Because milling removes fibre and several nutrients, many countries require enrichment with iron and B vitamins.

Uses and health

White flour is used for breads, cakes, pastries, noodles and sauces. Compared with wholemeal flour it generally has less fibre and micronutrient content, though its fine texture and neutral flavour suit many products.
