Petrel

FamiliesProcellariidae, Hydrobatidae, Oceanitidae, Pelecanoididae

Petrels are a varied group of tube-nosed seabirds in the order Procellariiformes. The name is used for many members of the family Procellariidae (the shearwaters and typical petrels), the storm petrels of the families Hydrobatidae and Oceanitidae, and the diving petrels. They range from the tiny storm petrels, among the smallest of all seabirds, to the giant petrels, which rival small albatrosses in size.

A northern fulmar, one of the most widespread petrels

Petrels spend most of their lives at sea, coming ashore only to breed, usually on remote islands. They have a characteristic tube-shaped nostril structure on the bill used in olfaction and salt excretion, and they feed on krill, squid, fish and offal, often following ships. Many species undertake enormous migrations; the sooty shearwater figures among the longest animal migrations recorded. The name "petrel" is traditionally linked to St Peter, because the birds appear to walk on water as they patter across the surface while taking off.

Groups

The Procellariidae include the gadfly petrels (Pterodroma), prions, shearwaters and fulmars. Storm petrels were long grouped together but are now split between northern (Hydrobatidae) and southern (Oceanitidae) families. Diving petrels of the Southern Ocean are small, auk-like birds that fly straight through wave crests. Many petrel species are threatened by introduced predators such as rats and cats on their breeding islands, and by longline fishing, making them a focus of international conservation agreements.
