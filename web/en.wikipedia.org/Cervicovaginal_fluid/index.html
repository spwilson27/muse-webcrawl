Cervicovaginal fluid

Cervicovaginal fluid (CVF) is a combination of secretions of the female reproductive tract: cervical mucus produced by glands of the cervix, transudate across the vaginal epithelium, exfoliated epithelial cells, secretions of the Bartholin's and Skene's glands, endometrial and oviductal fluids, and the products of the vaginal microbiota. Together these form the fluid environment of the cervix and vagina, of which visible discharge is sometimes a manifestation. Its volume, viscosity and composition vary markedly across the menstrual cycle under the influence of estrogen and progesterone, and it plays central roles in lubrication, defence against infection and reproduction.

Cervical mucus and the cycle

Under rising estrogen before ovulation, cervical mucus becomes abundant, clear, stretchy (spinnbarkeit) and alkaline — a state that supports sperm survival and passage; dried midcycle mucus shows a fern-like crystallisation pattern. After ovulation, progesterone makes the mucus scant, thick and impenetrable to sperm. These changes are the basis of fertility-awareness methods such as the Billings ovulation method, and of hormonal contraception: progestins thicken cervical mucus as one mechanism of action.

Composition and defence

CVF contains mucins (chiefly MUC5B from the cervix), immunoglobulins (IgG and secretory IgA), lysozyme, lactoferrin, defensins and other antimicrobial peptides, and — in most reproductive-age women — lactic acid produced by lactobacillus-dominated microbiota, which keeps vaginal pH acidic and inhibits pathogens. Disruption of this environment (bacterial vaginosis) alters CVF odour, colour and consistency, and increases susceptibility to sexually transmitted infections including HIV.

Clinical use

Cervicovaginal fluid is sampled in tests including Pap smears, nucleic-acid tests for chlamydia, gonorrhoea and HPV, vaginal pH and wet-mount microscopy, and tests for rupture of membranes in pregnancy (e.g. detection of placental alpha microglobulin-1 or IGFBP-1) and for risk of preterm birth (fetal fibronectin). Proteomic analysis of CVF is an active research field for biomarkers of infection, pregnancy complications and gynaecological cancer.

Arousal and lubrication

During sexual arousal, vasocongestion increases vaginal transudation ("sweating" of the vaginal wall), supplemented by Bartholin's gland secretion, rapidly increasing lubrication independent of cycle phase.
