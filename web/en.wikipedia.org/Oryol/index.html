Oryol (Russian: Орёл, IPA: [ɐˈrʲol], lit. "eagle"), alternatively romanised Orel, is a city and the administrative centre of Oryol Oblast, Russia, situated on the Oka River approximately 368 kilometres southwest of Moscow. It is known as a literary city, associated with writers including Ivan Turgenev and Leonid Andreyev.

History

Oryol was founded in 1566 on the orders of Ivan the Terrible as a fortress defending the southern frontier of the Tsardom of Russia against Crimean Tatar raids; its coat of arms depicts an eagle, reflecting the name. During the Russian Civil War it changed hands repeatedly and in October 1919 its capture by Denikin's White forces marked their closest advance on Moscow from the south. In the Second World War it was occupied by Nazi Germany from October 1941 until its liberation on 5 August 1943 during Operation Kutuzov; Moscow marked the occasion with one of the war's first artillery salutes, a practice that originated with Oryol and Belgorod's recapture.

Culture

The city preserves the Spasskoye-Lutovinovo estate nearby, the family home of Ivan Turgenev, and museums to Turgenev, Ivan Bunin and Leonid Andreyev. Its economy centres on food processing, textiles, electronics and machinery.
