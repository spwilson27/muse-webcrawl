Balance of payments

RecordsTransactions between residents and nonresidents

The balance of payments (BOP) of a country is a systematic record of all economic transactions between residents of that country and the rest of the world over a period of time, usually a quarter or a year.

Structure

The BOP consists of the current account (trade in goods and services, primary income and secondary income), the capital account (capital transfers and disposal of non-produced assets), and the financial account (direct investment, portfolio investment, other investment and reserve assets). By double-entry bookkeeping, the accounts sum to zero in principle.

Imbalances

A current account surplus means a country is a net lender to the rest of the world; a deficit means it is a net borrower. Persistent global imbalances, such as large United States deficits and Chinese surpluses in the 2000s, have been a focus of international economic policy debate.

History

Mercantilist writers treated trade surpluses as the goal of policy. Modern BOP statistics were standardized by the International Monetary Fund's Balance of Payments Manual, first published in 1948.
