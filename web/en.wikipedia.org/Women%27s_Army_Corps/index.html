Women's Army Corps

The Women's Army Corps (WAC) was the women's branch of the United States Army. It was created as an auxiliary unit, the Women's Army Auxiliary Corps (WAAC), on 15 May 1942, and converted to an active-duty branch in July 1943. Its first director was Oveta Culp Hobby. The WAC was disbanded in 1978, when women were integrated into the regular Army.

Second World War

By 1945 nearly 150,000 American women had served in the WAAC/WAC, working in more than 200 non-combatant jobs — as clerks, mechanics, radio operators, drivers, cartographers and intelligence analysts — and releasing men for combat duty. WACs served in North Africa, Europe and the Pacific, some attached to the Manhattan Project.

Later service

WACs served in the Korean and Vietnam Wars, and the corps included the 6888th Central Postal Directory Battalion, the only all-Black women's unit deployed overseas in the Second World War. The corps was discontinued on 20 October 1978.
