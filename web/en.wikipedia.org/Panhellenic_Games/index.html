Panhellenic Games

The Panhellenic Games were four separate sets of athletic festivals held in ancient Greece, open to competitors from all Greek city-states. Together they formed a circuit known as the periodos, and an athlete who won at all four was called a periodonikes. The games were held in honour of the gods at major sanctuaries and combined athletic and, in some cases, musical and equestrian competitions.

The four games

The Olympic Games, the most prestigious, were held every four years at Olympia in honour of Zeus; victory brought an olive wreath (kotinos). The Pythian Games were held at Delphi in honour of Apollo, every four years, two years after the Olympics, with a laurel wreath as prize and famous musical contests. The Nemean Games were held at Nemea in honour of Zeus (and in memory of the infant Opheltes), every two years, with a crown of wild celery. The Isthmian Games were held at Isthmia near Corinth in honour of Poseidon, every two years, with crowns of pine, later celery.

Organisation

The games were staged in alternating years so that a major festival occurred in most years. Sacred truces (ekecheiria) protected athletes and spectators travelling to the host sanctuary. Competitions included foot races, wrestling, boxing, the pankration, the pentathlon, and horse and chariot races; victors were celebrated with statues, odes by poets such as Pindar, and civic honours at home.

Decline

The festivals continued under Roman rule but faded in late antiquity; the Olympic Games were suppressed by the end of the 4th century AD under the Christian emperors. The modern Olympic Games, revived in 1896, took their inspiration from the ancient Panhellenic circuit.
