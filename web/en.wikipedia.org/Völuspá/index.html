Völuspá

Völuspá (Old Norse: Vǫluspá, "Prophecy of the Seeress"; also rendered Völuspa) is the best known poem of the Poetic Edda. It tells the story of the creation of the world and its coming end, Ragnarök, related to Odin by a völva (seeress) whom he has summoned from the dead.

Content

The poem begins with the völva addressing Odin and recounting the creation of the world from the void of Ginnungagap: the origin of the sea, earth, sky, and the sun and moon; the creation of the first humans, Ask and Embla; and the dwarfs. She describes the world tree Yggdrasil, the fate-weaving Norns, the first war (between the Æsir and Vanir), and the death of Baldr, the best of the gods.

She then prophesies Ragnarök: the binding and breaking of the wolf Fenrir, earthquakes, the sun turning black, the earth sinking into the sea, and the final battle in which Odin is devoured by Fenrir and Thor slays the serpent Jörmungandr before falling. Finally she foresees a renewed world rising green from the waters, with Baldr returned and a new generation of gods.

Manuscripts

The poem survives principally in the Codex Regius manuscript of the Poetic Edda (c. 1270) and in a shorter version in Hauksbók, and stanzas are quoted extensively in Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda. It is widely regarded as the most famous and influential of the Eddic poems.
