Greenfield land

Greenfield land is undeveloped land in a city or rural area, either used for agriculture or landscape design, or left to evolve naturally. The term contrasts with brownfield land, which has previously been developed and may carry contamination. "Greenfield" is also used metaphorically in business and software engineering for projects started from scratch with no constraints from prior work.

Development debates

Greenfield development is often cheaper than redeveloping brownfield sites because there are no demolition or remediation costs, but it is criticized for contributing to urban sprawl, loss of farmland and habitat, and increased car dependency. Planning policies in many countries, notably the United Kingdom, encourage prioritizing brownfield ("previously developed") land and protecting green belt land around cities.

Other uses of the term

In industry, a "greenfield investment" is a form of foreign direct investment in which a parent company builds new facilities from the ground up, as opposed to acquiring existing ones. In software, greenfield development describes building a system without legacy code constraints.
