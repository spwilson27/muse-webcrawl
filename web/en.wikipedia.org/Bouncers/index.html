Bouncer

A bouncer (also called a doorman or door supervisor) is a type of security guard employed by bars, nightclubs, music venues and other licensed premises to control admission, check identification and age, enforce house rules and remove unruly patrons.

Duties and regulation

Typical duties include checking ID at the door, monitoring crowd behaviour, searching bags where permitted, de-escalating conflicts and liaising with police. Many jurisdictions now regulate the occupation: in the United Kingdom door supervisors must hold a licence from the Security Industry Authority (SIA); various U.S. states and Australian states impose registration, training and background checks. Bouncers generally have no special police powers and rely on the same citizen's-arrest and property rights as other private staff, under principles of reasonable force.

History and culture

The name is often traced to the practice of "bouncing" — ejecting — troublemakers from 19th-century American saloons; the notorious New York bouncer of the 1880s inspired popular accounts of the role. Modern training emphasises conflict management and de-escalation over physical removal.
