Pier Giorgio Perotto

Pier Giorgio Perotto (24 December 1930 – 23 January 2002) was an Italian electrical engineer and inventor, best known as the designer of the Olivetti Programma 101, widely regarded as the first commercial desktop personal computer. Born in Turin, Perotto graduated in mechanical engineering from the Polytechnic University of Turin and worked on early electronic calculators at Olivetti under Adriano Olivetti's research division.

Programma 101

Leading a small team including Giovanni De Sandre and Gastone Garziera, Perotto designed the Programma 101, launched at the 1964 New York World's Fair. It was a programmable calculator the size of a typewriter, storing programs on magnetic cards, with conditional jumps and a printing unit—a true computer usable by non-specialists. It sold over 44,000 units, many in the United States, and was used by NASA in planning for the Apollo missions.

When Olivetti sold its electronics division to General Electric in 1964, Perotto's work was nearly lost; he later described his team's experience in the memoir Programma 101. L'invenzione del personal computer. Perotto went on to lead research at Olivetti and to work on later systems. His role in the prehistory of personal computing has been increasingly recognized, and his legacy is commemorated by awards for young innovators in Italy.
