Pope Adrian IV (Latin: Adrianus IV; born Nicholas Breakspear; c. 1100 – 1 September 1159) was head of the Catholic Church and ruler of the Papal States from 4 December 1154 until his death. He is the only Englishman ever to have been pope.

Early life

Nicholas Breakspear was born near St Albans in Hertfordshire. Denied entry to the abbey school there, he travelled to France, studied in Paris and Arles, and became a canon regular and then abbot of St Rufus near Avignon. His abilities brought him to the attention of Pope Eugenius III, who made him Cardinal Bishop of Albano in 1149 and sent him as legate to Scandinavia (1152–54), where he organised the Norwegian church province of Nidaros and helped establish the Swedish church.

Pontificate

Elected pope days after returning to Rome, Adrian confronted Arnold of Brescia's Roman commune, placing the city under interdict until Arnold was expelled and executed. He concluded the Treaty of Benevento (1156) with William I of Sicily, recognising the Norman kingdom in exchange for support, which angered Emperor Frederick Barbarossa; relations with the Empire deteriorated further after the Diet of Besançon (1157). Adrian is traditionally credited with the bull Laudabiliter, said to grant Henry II of England lordship over Ireland — a document whose authenticity and interpretation have long been debated. He died at Anagni amid a deepening conflict with Barbarossa, and his death triggered a disputed election and schism.
