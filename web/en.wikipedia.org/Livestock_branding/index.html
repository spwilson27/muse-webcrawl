Livestock branding

Livestock branding is the marking of livestock with a distinctive symbol to indicate ownership. The practice is ancient: Egyptian tomb paintings show cattle being branded, and the technique became central to open-range ranching in North America, Australia and South America, where animals of many owners grazed together and were sorted at roundups.

Methods

Traditional hot-iron branding burns a permanent scar with a heated iron. Freeze branding, using irons chilled in liquid nitrogen or dry ice and alcohol, destroys hair pigment cells so the mark grows back white and is considered less painful. Other identification methods include ear tags, tattoos, earmarks, microchips and paint brands.

Registration and law

In many western US states brands must be recorded with a state brand registry, and brand inspectors check animals at sale and slaughter; unregistered or altered brands ("brand blotting") are offences connected with cattle rustling. Brand designs — letters, numerals and symbols described in a specialised vocabulary ("lazy", "rocking", "walking") — are read aloud in a conventional order.
