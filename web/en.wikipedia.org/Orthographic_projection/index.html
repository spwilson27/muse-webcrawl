Orthographic projection

Orthographic projection is a means of representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions. It is a form of parallel projection in which all projection lines are perpendicular (orthogonal) to the projection plane, so objects appear without perspective foreshortening based on distance.

Engineering drawing

In technical drawing, multiview orthographic projection shows an object from several sides — typically front, top, and side views — arranged in a standard layout (first-angle in Europe, third-angle in North America). Because dimensions are true to scale, such drawings are used for manufacturing.

Cartography

In mapmaking, the orthographic projection depicts a hemisphere of the Earth as it would appear from deep space. It was known to the ancient Greeks; Hipparchus used it for star charts in the 2nd century BC.
