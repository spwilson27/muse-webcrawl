Canadian Rangers

The Canadian Rangers (French: Rangers canadiens) are a sub-component of the Canadian Army Reserve, comprising mostly Indigenous and other local residents of Canada's sparsely settled northern, coastal, and isolated communities. Formed in 1947, the Rangers provide a military presence in areas where regular forces cannot operate year-round.

History

The Rangers trace their lineage to the Pacific Coast Militia Rangers of the Second World War. The modern organization was established in 1947 as concerns grew about sovereignty in the Arctic during the early Cold War. Membership expanded steadily, and in 1996 the Junior Canadian Rangers program was created for youth aged 12 to 18.

Role and organization

Approximately 5,000 Rangers serve in more than 200 patrols grouped into five Canadian Ranger Patrol Groups. Their duties include reporting unusual activity, conducting sovereignty patrols (known as SOVPATs), assisting search and rescue, and supporting regular forces with local knowledge of terrain, weather, and wildlife. Rangers are recognizable by their red hoodies and ball caps.

Equipment

Rangers traditionally carried the .303-inch Lee-Enfield No. 4 rifle, which served for decades because of its reliability in extreme cold. Beginning in 2017 the Lee-Enfield was replaced by the C19, a Canadian-made variant of the Tikka T3 rifle produced by Colt Canada. Rangers provide their own snowmobiles, boats, and other local transport, for which they receive an equipment usage rate.

Significance

Often described as the "eyes and ears of the North," the Rangers embody Canada's claim to Arctic sovereignty and play an important cultural role in their communities, where patrols carry on Indigenous traditions of land stewardship and survival skills.
