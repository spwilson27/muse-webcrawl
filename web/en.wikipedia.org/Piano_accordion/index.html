Piano Accordion

A piano accordion is an accordion equipped with a right-hand treble keyboard modeled on a piano keyboard; the left hand operates buttons for bass notes and chords (typically a Stradella bass system) while working the bellows. It is a free-reed aerophone: air driven by the bellows vibrates metal reeds.

Design

Full-size instruments commonly have 41 treble keys and 120 bass buttons arranged in rows of counterbass, fundamental bass, major, minor, seventh, and diminished chords. Registers (couplers) switch combinations of reed banks to change timbre. Smaller accordions with fewer keys and basses are made for students and folk musicians.

Use

Developed in the late 19th century, the piano accordion became popular worldwide in folk, popular, and dance music — from French musette and tango to zydeco, norteño, and klezmer-influenced styles — and is also used in classical and contemporary compositions.
