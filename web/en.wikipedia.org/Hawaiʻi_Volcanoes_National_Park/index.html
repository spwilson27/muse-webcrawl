Location: Island of Hawaii, Hawaii, US
Area: 323,431 acres (1,308.9 km²)

Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park is an American national park located in the state of Hawaii on the island of Hawaii. The park encompasses two of the world's most active volcanoes: Kīlauea, one of Earth's most active volcanoes, and Mauna Loa, the world's largest shield volcano. The park was established on August 1, 1916, as Hawaii National Park (a single park then encompassing Haleakalā on Maui), and it delivers the dramatic volcanic landscapes of the Big Island: calderas, pit craters, lava tubes, steam vents, and recent lava flows.

Environment

The park ranges from sea level to the 13,681-foot (4,170 m) summit of Mauna Loa, protecting ecosystems from tropical rainforest at the windward coast to arid lava deserts and alpine tundra. It shelters endangered species including the nēnē (Hawaiian goose), the hawksbill sea turtle, and endemic honeycreeper birds. The park is both an International Biosphere Reserve and a UNESCO World Heritage Site, inscribed in 1987 for its volcanic features and the unique flora and fauna that evolve on young lava.

Volcanic activity

Kīlauea's Puʻu ʻŌʻō eruption lasted from 1983 to 2018 and buried communities including Kalapana and Royal Gardens. In 2018 a major lower East Rift Zone eruption destroyed more than 700 homes in the Puna district outside the park, while the summit caldera collapsed dramatically and Halemaʻumaʻu crater deepened; the park was closed for 134 days. A water lake appeared in Halemaʻumaʻu in 2019, then summit eruptions beginning in December 2020 boiled it away and repeatedly refilled the crater with lava lakes. Mauna Loa erupted in November–December 2022 for the first time in 38 years, with lava visible from the park's overlooks.

Sights

Main features include Crater Rim Drive and the Kīlauea Visitor Center, the Jaggar Museum and Hawaiian Volcano Observatory overlook (closed after 2018 damage), Thurston Lava Tube (Nāhuku), Chain of Craters Road leading 19 miles down to the coast where lava crossed the highway, the Hōlei Sea Arch, and the Puʻu Loa petroglyph field, one of the largest in Hawaii.
