Quetta (Pashto and Urdu: کوئٹہ) is the capital and largest city of the Pakistani province of Balochistan. Lying in a high valley near the border with Afghanistan, surrounded by mountains including Chiltan and Zarghoon, it is known as the "Fruit Garden of Pakistan" for its orchards and dried fruit trade. The name derives from the Pashto kwatta, "fort".

History

Quetta guarded strategic routes through the Bolan and Khojak passes between Central Asia and the Indus plain. It came under Mughal and then Durrani Afghan rule before the British occupied it during the First Anglo-Afghan War (1839) and formally acquired it by the Treaty of Gandamak (1879); it became a major garrison and administrative cantonment of British Baluchistan. The city was devastated by the 1935 Quetta earthquake, which killed tens of thousands, and was rebuilt to earthquake-resistant standards. Since 1947 it has been the capital of Pakistan's Balochistan province; a further major earthquake struck the region in 2008.

Geography and demography

At about 1,680 m elevation, Quetta has cold winters and mild summers by Pakistani standards. Its population is ethnically diverse — Pashtuns, Baloch, Brahui, Hazaras (many refugees from Afghanistan), Punjabis and others — with Pashto, Balochi, Brahui, Urdu and Persian (Dari/Hazaragi) widely spoken. The city hosts the Pakistan Army's Command and Staff College, the University of Balochistan, and the nearby Hanna Lake and Hazarganji-Chiltan National Park, home to the Chiltan ibex and markhor.
