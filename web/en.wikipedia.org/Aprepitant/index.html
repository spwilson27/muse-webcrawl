Aprepitant

Brand name: Emend
Class: NK1 receptor antagonist (antiemetic)
Approved: 2003 (US)

Aprepitant, sold under the brand name Emend among others, is a medication used to prevent nausea and vomiting caused by chemotherapy and after surgery. It was the first approved member of the neurokinin-1 (NK1) receptor antagonist class of antiemetics, developed by Merck & Co. and approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration in 2003.

Mechanism and use

Aprepitant blocks substance P from binding to NK1 receptors in the brain's vomiting centre. It is particularly effective against the delayed phase of chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting, and is typically given as a three-day oral course in combination with a 5-HT3 antagonist (such as ondansetron) and dexamethasone before highly or moderately emetogenic chemotherapy. An intravenous prodrug, fosaprepitant, is converted to aprepitant in the body. Aprepitant is metabolised mainly by CYP3A4 and itself inhibits that enzyme, giving it numerous interactions, including with warfarin, some chemotherapeutics, and hormonal contraceptives.

Side effects and history

Common adverse effects include fatigue, hiccups, constipation, and headache. Before aprepitant, NK1 antagonists had been investigated as antidepressants without success; the antiemetic application proved the clinically valuable one, changing prophylaxis guidelines internationally. Netupitant and rolapitant are later drugs in the same class.
