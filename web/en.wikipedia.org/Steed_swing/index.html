Steed swing

The steed swing is a basic aerial silk act and trick in circus arts, performed on two lengths of hanging fabric (silks). In the move, the performer climbs the silks, wraps the fabric around one leg and body, and swings the free leg — the "steed" leg — in a controlled arc while suspended, combining elements of wrap, drop preparation, and choreographed leg movement. It is among the beginner-to-intermediate skills taught in aerial arts curricula.

Technique

The performer ascends the silks, secures a single-leg wrap or foot lock, and leans back so the body is held by the wrapped fabric at the thigh and hip. The free leg is extended and swept in a swinging motion, often coordinated with an opposite arm reach, before the performer returns upright and unwraps. As with all aerial disciplines, the skill is taught under supervision with crash mats because it involves inversion and height.

Context

Aerial silks (also called aerial tissu) emerged as a modern circus discipline in the late 20th century and were popularised by companies such as Cirque du Soleil. Named fundamental moves — climbs, foot locks, hip keys, and swings like the steed swing — form the vocabulary from which choreographed routines are assembled.
