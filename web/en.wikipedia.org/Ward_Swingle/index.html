Ward Lamar Swingle (September 21, 1927 – January 19, 2015) was an American jazz musician, arranger, and vocalist best known as the founder of the Swingle Singers, the vocal group famous for performing the music of Johann Sebastian Bach and other classical composers with jazz scat singing and rhythm accompaniment.

Early life and career

Born in Mobile, Alabama, Swingle was a child prodigy on piano and played in big bands while still a teenager. He studied at the Cincinnati Conservatory of Music, graduating summa cum laude, then moved to France in 1951 to study piano with Walter Gieseking. In Paris he worked as a rehearsal pianist and session musician, became a founding member of the vocal group Les Double Six, and sang with the Blue Stars of France led by Blossom Dearie.

The Swingle Singers

In 1962 Swingle formed an octet of studio singers in Paris to record vocal arrangements of Bach keyboard works. The resulting album, Jazz Sébastien Bach (1963), in which singers performed the fugues of the Well-Tempered Clavier with scat syllables over bass and drums, became an international bestseller and won Grammy Awards, including Best New Artist for the group. The Swingle Singers went on to record music by Mozart, Beethoven, Vivaldi, and many others, as well as modern compositions written for them by Luciano Berio (whose Sinfonia featured the group). After the French group disbanded in 1973, Swingle re-formed the ensemble in London as Swingle II, later renamed The Swingles and eventually The Swingle Singers, which continues under new generations of singers.

Later work and honours

Swingle arranged widely for television, film, and other vocal groups, gave workshops in choral arranging, and published collections of his arrangements. He received several Grammy Awards across his career and was recognized for transforming perceptions of a cappella singing, inspiring ensembles such as The Manhattan Transfer and countless collegiate groups. He settled in Eastbourne, England, and died there on January 19, 2015, aged 87.
