Jiangxi

Jiangxi (江西, "west of the Yangtze") is a landlocked province of the People's Republic of China in the southeast of the country, spanning the basin of the Gan River and Poyang Lake. Its capital and largest city is Nanchang. Jiangxi borders Anhui and Zhejiang to the northeast and east, Fujian to the southeast, Guangdong to the south, Hunan to the west, and Hubei to the northwest. Historically a major rice-producing region and the heartland of China's porcelain industry at Jingdezhen, the province occupies a central place in modern Chinese history as the cradle of the Chinese Red Army: the Nanchang Uprising of 1927 and the Chinese Soviet Republic proclaimed at Ruijin in 1931 both took place here, and the Long March began from Jiangxi in 1934.

Geography

Jiangxi is dominated by the Gan River, which flows northward through the centre of the province to Poyang Lake — China's largest freshwater lake, an important wintering ground for Siberian cranes — and thence to the Yangtze at Jiujiang. Mountains ring the province: the Wuyi range in the east, the Luoxiao range in the west (including the Jinggang Mountains on the Hunan border), and the Mufu and Jiuling ranges in the north. Mount Lu (Lushan), near Jiujiang, is a famed summer retreat and UNESCO-listed cultural landscape; the granite peaks of Sanqingshan in the northeast are also a World Heritage Site. The climate is humid subtropical, with hot summers and a rice-growing season that supports two crops in much of the province.

History

Jiangxi was integrated into imperial China under the Qin and Han dynasties, with Yuzhang commandery centred near modern Nanchang. Its porcelain centre, Jingdezhen, supplied imperial kilns for a thousand years and gave the world "china"; blue-and-white porcelain was perfected there under the Yuan and Ming dynasties. The province prospered in the Song and Ming as a rice and tea region on trade routes to Canton, and produced notable Neo-Confucian scholars — the philosopher Lu Jiuyuan was born here, and Wang Yangming served as a Jiangxi governor. Its relative decline followed the nineteenth-century shift of trade to coastal ports and the devastation of the Taiping Rebellion.

In the twentieth century, Jiangxi became the cradle of the Chinese Communist revolution. On 1 August 1927, Communist forces under Zhou Enlai, Zhu De, and others staged the Nanchang Uprising — commemorated today as the founding of the People's Liberation Army. Mao Zedong and Zhu De established the Jinggangshan base on the Hunan border, and in November 1931 the Chinese Soviet Republic was proclaimed at Ruijin. Encirclement campaigns by Chiang Kai-shek's Nationalists forced the Communists to abandon Jiangxi in October 1934, beginning the Long March.

Economy and culture

Jiangxi remains one of China's less wealthy provinces, though its economy has grown quickly in recent decades around copper (the Dexing mine is among Asia's largest), rare earths in the Ganzhou region, rice, tea, citrus fruit, and manufacturing in Nanchang — including aviation, automobiles, and pharmaceuticals. The dominant language is Gan Chinese, with Hakka communities in the south around Ganzhou, a historic Hakka heartland known for its enclosed tulou-like weilongwu dwellings. Ganju opera, Jiangxi's tea-picking opera traditions, and the province's many historic academies — including the White Deer Grotto Academy on Mount Lu, one of China's oldest — reflect a deep scholarly heritage.
