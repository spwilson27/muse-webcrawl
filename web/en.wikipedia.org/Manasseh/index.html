Manasseh

Manasseh is a name borne by several figures in the Hebrew Bible. Most prominently, Manasseh was the firstborn son of Joseph and Asenath and the ancestor of one of the twelve tribes of Israel, the Tribe of Manasseh. The name is traditionally interpreted as "causing to forget" (Genesis 41:51).

Son of Joseph and the tribe

According to Genesis, Manasseh and his younger brother Ephraim were born in Egypt and adopted by their grandfather Jacob, who blessed Ephraim above the firstborn Manasseh despite Joseph's protest. The tribe of Manasseh received territory on both sides of the Jordan River — a western portion in the hill country and Samaria and an eastern portion (including Gilead and Bashan) — making it one of the largest tribal allotments.

King Manasseh of Judah

Manasseh was also a king of Judah (reigned 7th century BC), son of Hezekiah, remembered in 2 Kings as an idolatrous ruler whose sins provoked divine judgement, though 2 Chronicles adds a story of his captivity in Babylon and repentance; the Prayer of Manasseh is a later work attributed to him. Other biblical bearers include a grandfather of a Danite priest in Judges 18.
