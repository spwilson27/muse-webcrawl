Methanol economy
Proposed byGeorge A. Olah
Key textBeyond Oil and Gas: The Methanol Economy (2006)
Energy carrierMethanol (CH3OH)

The methanol economy is a proposed future economy in which methanol and dimethyl ether replace fossil fuels as a means of energy storage, ground transportation fuel, and raw material for synthetic hydrocarbons and their products. It offers an alternative to the proposed hydrogen economy. The concept was championed by Nobel laureate George Andrew Olah in his 2006 book Beyond Oil and Gas: The Methanol Economy, written with Alain Goeppert and G. K. Surya Prakash.

Proposal

Methanol is a liquid at ambient conditions, making it far easier to store, transport, and dispense than hydrogen gas using infrastructure similar to today's petroleum network. It can fuel internal combustion engines, turbines, and direct methanol fuel cells. Crucially, methanol can be produced by the chemical recycling of carbon dioxide — hydrogenating CO2 captured from industrial sources or the air with hydrogen made using renewable or nuclear energy — closing the carbon cycle in analogy to photosynthesis.

Developments and criticism

Iceland's Carbon Recycling International operates a plant producing renewable methanol from geothermal CO2 and hydrogen, and methanol has seen growing use as a marine fuel and in China as a motor-fuel blend. Critics note methanol's toxicity, lower energy density than gasoline, corrosiveness to some materials, and the current cost of green hydrogen for CO2-based production.
