Bad Robot Productions is an American film and television production company founded by filmmaker J. J. Abrams in 1999. It is best known for television series such as Alias, Lost, Fringe, Person of Interest and Westworld, and for films including Cloverfield (2008), Star Trek (2009), Super 8 (2011), and Star Wars: The Force Awakens (2015). The company's name and logo derive from a line of dialogue from the animated film The Iron Giant ("bad robot"), and its animated logo shows a red-domed robot walking through a field of tall grass. The company is based in Santa Monica, California, in a building nicknamed "The National Typewriter Company" as a nod to Abrams's "mystery box" storytelling philosophy.

History

Bad Robot was formed in association with Touchstone Television (later ABC Studios). Its first series was Felicity (created under Abrams's earlier banner) followed by the spy drama Alias (2001–2006) starring Jennifer Garner. In 2004 it launched Lost, co-created by Abrams, Damon Lindelof and Jeffrey Lieber, which became a major critical and commercial success and a landmark of 2000s television.

Film work

Bad Robot's first feature film was Joy Ride (2001). Under a deal with Paramount Pictures, the company produced the found-footage monster film Cloverfield (2008), notable for its secretive viral marketing campaign, and revived the Star Trek franchise with Star Trek (2009) and its sequels. Abrams directed Star Wars: The Force Awakens (2015) for Lucasfilm, which grossed over $2 billion worldwide, and returned for Star Wars: The Rise of Skywalker (2019). The company also produced entries in the Mission: Impossible series and the Cloverfield spin-off films 10 Cloverfield Lane (2016) and The Cloverfield Paradox (2018).

WarnerMedia deal

In 2019 Bad Robot signed a major overall deal with WarnerMedia (reported at around $250 million) covering film, television, games and digital content, expanding its activities through divisions such as Bad Robot Games. Katie McGrath serves as co-CEO alongside Abrams.
