Birka

Birka, located on the island of Björkö in Lake Mälaren, Sweden, was one of the most important trading centres in northern Europe during the Viking Age, active from about AD 750 to 975.

History

Birka is mentioned in the Vita Ansgarii, the account of the missionary Ansgar, who visited in 829 and 852. The town had a population of roughly 700–1,000 and was protected by a semi-circular rampart and a hillfort.

Archaeology

Around 3,000 graves have been identified, including richly furnished chamber graves containing goods from as far away as Arabia and China, attesting to Birka's far-reaching trade networks. Together with nearby Hovgården, Birka was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1993.
