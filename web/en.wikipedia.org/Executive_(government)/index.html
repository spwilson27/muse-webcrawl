Executive (government)

Branch ofGovernment
FunctionEnforcing and administering the law

The executive is the branch of government responsible for implementing and enforcing laws and for the day-to-day administration of the state. Under the doctrine of the separation of powers, it is distinguished from the legislature, which makes the laws, and the judiciary, which interprets them.

Composition

The executive typically comprises the head of state, the head of government, the cabinet and ministers, and the civil service and administrative agencies beneath them. In presidential systems, such as the United States, the president is both head of state and head of the executive. In parliamentary systems, executive power is exercised by a prime minister and cabinet drawn from, and accountable to, the legislature, while a monarch or president serves as ceremonial head of state.

Powers

Executive powers include commanding the armed forces, conducting foreign policy, appointing officials and judges, issuing decrees or executive orders, granting pardons, and preparing and executing the state budget. The scope of these powers is defined by each country's constitution and laws, and is subject to legislative oversight and judicial review.
