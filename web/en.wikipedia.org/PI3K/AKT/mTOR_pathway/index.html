PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway

PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway
TypeSignal transduction pathway
Key componentsPI3K, PIP3, PDK1, AKT, mTOR, PTEN
FunctionCell growth, proliferation, survival, metabolism, angiogenesis
Key inhibitorsRapamycin (mTOR), wortmannin (PI3K)
Clinical relevanceCancer, diabetes, ageing

The PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway is an intracellular signalling pathway important in regulating the cell cycle, cell growth, proliferation, survival, protein synthesis and metabolism. It is activated by growth factors and nutrients, and it is one of the most frequently dysregulated pathways in human cancer, with activating mutations found in a large fraction of tumours.

Mechanism

The pathway begins when extracellular signals such as insulin or growth factors bind receptor tyrosine kinases at the cell surface. This activates phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K), which phosphorylates the membrane lipid phosphatidylinositol (4,5)-bisphosphate (PIP2) to generate phosphatidylinositol (3,4,5)-trisphosphate (PIP3). PIP3 acts as a docking site that recruits proteins containing pleckstrin homology domains to the membrane, most importantly the serine/threonine kinase AKT (also called protein kinase B) and its activating kinase PDK1.

AKT is activated by phosphorylation at two sites: threonine 308 by PDK1 and serine 473 by the mTORC2 complex. Once active, AKT phosphorylates dozens of substrates, including the TSC1/TSC2 complex, whose inhibition allows the small GTPase Rheb to activate mTORC1, a central regulator of protein synthesis through targets such as S6 kinase and 4E-BP1. The tumour suppressor PTEN antagonises the pathway by dephosphorylating PIP3 back to PIP2, and loss of PTEN is a common event in cancer.

mTOR complexes

The mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) is a serine/threonine kinase that forms two distinct complexes. mTORC1 (containing Raptor) senses amino acids, growth factors and energy status, and promotes anabolic processes including protein and lipid synthesis while suppressing autophagy. mTORC2 (containing Rictor) phosphorylates AKT at serine 473 and regulates the actin cytoskeleton; unlike mTORC1 it is relatively insensitive to acute rapamycin treatment.

Clinical significance

Because the pathway controls cell growth and metabolism, its hyperactivation drives tumour growth, while its inhibition is a major strategy in oncology. Drugs targeting the pathway include the mTOR inhibitors rapamycin (sirolimus) and its analogues (rapalogs such as everolimus and temsirolimus), PI3K inhibitors such as alpelisib and idelalisib, and AKT inhibitors. The pathway also links insulin signalling to glucose homeostasis, and its dysfunction contributes to insulin resistance and type II diabetes.
