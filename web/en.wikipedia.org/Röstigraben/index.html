Röstigraben

Rösti, the Bernese potato dish from which the term Röstigraben derives.

Röstigraben (literally "rösti ditch" or "rösti trench") is a humorous Swiss German term for the cultural and political divide between the German-speaking and French-speaking parts of Switzerland. It takes its name from rösti, the grated-potato dish associated with the Bernese countryside, and the Graben — the trench or ditch — imagined to run along the language border, roughly following the Saane/Sarine river.

Meaning and usage

The term is invoked whenever federal votes reveal sharply different results between German-speaking Switzerland and the Romandy — for example, on European integration, social policy, or environmental questions, where French-speaking cantons often vote more progressively or more favorably toward the European Union. Commentators speak of the Röstigraben "opening" or "closing" depending on the size of the regional gap.

Background

Switzerland's multilingualism divides the country into German-, French-, Italian-, and Romansh-speaking regions, and language correlates imperfectly with differences in mentality, media consumption, and political outlook. A parallel term, Polentagraben, jokingly describes the divide with Italian-speaking Ticino. Despite the jokes, the Röstigraben is generally treated as a feature of Swiss federal identity rather than a serious threat to national unity.
