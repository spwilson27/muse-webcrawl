Art Ensemble of Chicago

Origin: Chicago, Illinois, United States

Genres: Avant-garde jazz, free jazz

Years active: 1969–present

Labels: Nessa, Delmark, BYG Actuel, ECM, Atlantic, DIW, Pi

Associated with: AACM

Key members: Lester Bowie, Roscoe Mitchell, Joseph Jarman, Malachi Favors, Famoudou Don Moye

The Art Ensemble of Chicago (AEC) is an avant-garde jazz group that grew out of the Association for the Advancement of Creative Musicians (AACM) in the late 1960s. The ensemble integrates many jazz styles and plays numerous instruments, including "little instruments": bells, bicycle horns, birthday party gifts, wind chimes, and various forms of percussion. The musicians would wear costumes and face paint in performance. These characteristics have led to the name of the ensemble and its motto, "Great Black Music: Ancient to the Future".

History

The group grew out of the Roscoe Mitchell Sextet and the Roscoe Mitchell Art Ensemble. In 1969, the ensemble — Roscoe Mitchell, Joseph Jarman, Lester Bowie, and Malachi Favors — travelled to Paris, where they recorded extensively for the BYG Actuel label and became known as the Art Ensemble of Chicago. Drummer Famoudou Don Moye joined in 1970, completing the group's classic quintet line-up.

Returning to the United States in the early 1970s, the AEC became one of the most acclaimed groups in avant-garde jazz, recording for Atlantic and ECM, including the albums Les Stances à Sophie (1970), Bap-Tizum (1972), Fanfare for the Warriors (1973), Nice Guys (1978), and Full Force (1980). Lester Bowie died in 1999 and Malachi Favors in 2004; the group continued in various configurations led by Mitchell and Moye, and celebrated its 50th anniversary in 2019 with the album We Are on the Edge.

Style

The Art Ensemble's music deliberately spans the full history of African-American music, from New Orleans jazz, blues and gospel to free improvisation, incorporating theatrical elements, spoken word, and hundreds of instruments on stage.
