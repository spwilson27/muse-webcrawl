Curragh incident

The Curragh incident of 20 March 1914, also known as the Curragh mutiny, occurred when British Army officers stationed at the Curragh Camp in Ireland indicated they would resign or accept dismissal rather than obey orders to move against Ulster unionists who opposed Irish Home Rule.

Curragh incident
Date: 20 March 1914
Location: Curragh Camp, County Kildare
Context: Home Rule Crisis
Key figures: Hubert Gough, Sir Henry Wilson, J. E. B. Seely

Background

The Third Home Rule Bill threatened to place Protestant-majority Ulster under a Dublin parliament. Unionists formed the Ulster Volunteer Force and threatened armed resistance. The government of H. H. Asquith ordered precautionary troop movements, and cavalry commander Brigadier-General Hubert Gough and dozens of his officers chose threatened dismissal over potentially coercing Ulster.

Consequences

The government gave a written assurance — the "peccant paragraphs" added by Secretary of State for War J. E. B. Seely — that the army would not be used to crush Ulster opposition. Seely resigned, and the incident gravely weakened the government's authority and emboldened both unionist and nationalist paramilitaries in the months before the First World War. Despite the name, no formal mutiny — collective disobedience of a direct order — actually took place.
