Lorentz force

In physics, the Lorentz force is the force exerted on a charged particle by electric and magnetic fields. For a particle of charge q moving with velocity v in an electric field E and magnetic field B, it is F = qE + qv × B.

The electric term acts along the field; the magnetic term is perpendicular to both velocity and field, so it curves trajectories without doing work — the basis of cyclotron motion, mass spectrometry, cathode-ray tubes and magnetohydrodynamics. The law is named after Dutch physicist Hendrik Antoon Lorentz, who derived expressions for the force in the 1890s building on work by Maxwell, Heaviside and others.

Combined with Maxwell's equations, the Lorentz force law forms the foundation of classical electromagnetism.
