Polynomial

In mathematics, a polynomial is a mathematical expression consisting of indeterminates (also called variables) and coefficients, that involves only the operations of addition, subtraction, multiplication, and positive-integer powers of variables. An example of a polynomial of a single indeterminate x is x² − 4x + 7. An example with three indeterminates is x³ + 2xyz² − yz + 1.

Definitions and terminology

Each part of a polynomial is a term: a coefficient multiplied by a power of the variable. The degree of a polynomial is the highest degree of its terms; polynomials of degree one, two, and three are called linear, quadratic, and cubic. Polynomials with one, two, or three terms are monomials, binomials, and trinomials. The set of polynomials in one variable over a coefficient ring forms a polynomial ring, denoted R[x].

Polynomial equations and functions

A polynomial equation sets a polynomial equal to zero; its solutions are called roots. The fundamental theorem of algebra states that every non-constant polynomial with complex coefficients has a complex root, and hence as many roots (counting multiplicity) as its degree. Closed-form solution formulas exist for degrees up to four (the quadratic formula being the best known), while the Abel–Ruffini theorem proves no general radical formula exists for degree five or higher. Polynomial functions are smooth, easy to evaluate (e.g. by Horner's method), and are used universally to approximate other functions, as in Taylor series and interpolation.
