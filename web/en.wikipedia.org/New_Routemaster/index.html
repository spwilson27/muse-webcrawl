The New Routemaster, originally referred to as the New Bus for London, is a hybrid diesel-electric double-decker bus operated in London, England. Designed by Heatherwick Studio and built by Wrightbus in Northern Ireland, it was developed under the mayoralty of Boris Johnson as a twenty-first-century successor to the iconic AEC Routemaster. The bus entered service in February 2012, and around 1,000 were built before production ended in 2017.

Design

The New Routemaster revives signature features of the original Routemaster, including the rounded front and rear styling and, in early operation, an open rear platform that passengers could use to hop on and off when a conductor was present. It has three doors and two staircases to speed boarding, a hybrid drivetrain pairing a 4.5-litre Cummins diesel engine with an electric drive system, and a capacity of up to 87 passengers in its three-door form. The design won praise for its appearance but criticism for stuffy interior temperatures before air cooling was retrofitted.

History

The project was a manifesto commitment of Boris Johnson's 2008 mayoral campaign. Prototypes were unveiled in 2011, and the first buses entered service on route 38 in February 2012. The open platform was discontinued in later years as services converted to driver-only operation and rear doors were kept closed between stops. Production ended at 1,000 buses after the succeeding mayor, Sadiq Khan, halted further orders, citing cost. The fleet nonetheless remains a prominent feature of central London routes.

Criticism

The programme was criticised for its high unit cost—around £355,000 per bus—relative to conventional double-deckers, and for early problems with battery failures and air conditioning. Supporters pointed to the bus's longevity, fuel efficiency compared with older diesel fleets, and its status as a symbol of London.
