Vertex form

In algebra, the vertex form of a quadratic function is a way of writing the function that displays the vertex of its graph — a parabola — directly. A quadratic in vertex form is written:

f(x) = a(x − h)2 + k

where (h, k) are the coordinates of the vertex, and a determines the direction and width of the parabola: if a > 0 the parabola opens upward and the vertex is a minimum; if a < 0 it opens downward and the vertex is a maximum. The vertical line x = h is the axis of symmetry.

Conversion from standard form

Any quadratic in standard form f(x) = ax2 + bx + c can be converted to vertex form by completing the square. The vertex coordinates are given by h = −b/(2a) and k = f(h) = c − b2/(4a). Conversely, expanding the squared term converts vertex form back to standard form.

Uses

Vertex form makes optimisation problems immediate, since the extreme value k and the point at which it occurs, h, are read off directly. It also describes parabolas as transformations of the parent function y = x2: a horizontal shift by h, a vertical shift by k, and a vertical stretch or reflection by a. Vertex form generalises to the standard equations of other conic sections, such as circles, ellipses, and hyperbolas written about a centre (h, k).
