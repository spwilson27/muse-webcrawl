The Satish Dhawan Space Centre (SDSC), formerly known as the Sriharikota Range (SHAR), is India's primary spaceport, operated by the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO). It is located on the barrier island of Sriharikota in Andhra Pradesh, between Pulicat Lake and the Bay of Bengal, and provides launch infrastructure for India's orbital rockets.

History

The site was selected in the late 1960s for its sparse population, equatorial proximity, and eastward access over the sea, which allows launch vehicles to take advantage of the Earth's rotation and drop spent stages into the ocean. The range became operational in 1971 with the launch of sounding rockets; the first orbital launch, of the Rohini satellite aboard SLV-3, took place in 1980. In 2002 the centre was renamed in honour of Satish Dhawan, the former chairman of ISRO.

Facilities

The centre has two orbital launch pads. The First Launch Pad, commissioned in 1993, supports PSLV and GSLV launches, while the Second Launch Pad, inaugurated in 2005, serves PSLV, GSLV, and the heavier LVM3 (GSLV Mk III) vehicles. Support facilities include solid propellant production plants, static test stands, a mission control centre, and tracking stations.

Launches

SDSC has launched India's major missions, including the Chandrayaan lunar probes, the Mars Orbiter Mission (Mangalyaan), and numerous communication and Earth-observation satellites. It is India's only orbital launch site; a second spaceport has been under development at Kulasekarapattinam in Tamil Nadu.
