Ernest Howard Shepard (10 December 1879 – 24 March 1976) was an English artist and book illustrator. He is best known for his illustrations of the animals and characters in Winnie-the-Pooh by A. A. Milne and the 1931 edition of The Wind in the Willows by Kenneth Grahame, images so definitive that they are inseparable from the books in the public imagination.

Life and career

Shepard studied at Heatherley School of Fine Art and the Royal Academy Schools, winning scholarships in both. He became a regular contributor to Punch magazine, joining its staff in 1921 and eventually becoming a senior cartoonist. During the First World War he served as an artillery officer, was awarded the Military Cross for his actions at the Battle of Passchendaele, and rose to the rank of major; his wartime sketches were published in Punch.

Winnie-the-Pooh

Milne, a colleague at Punch, was initially reluctant to use Shepard, but commissioned him for the verse collection When We Were Very Young (1924) and then for Winnie-the-Pooh (1926) and The House at Pooh Corner (1928). Shepard modelled Pooh not on Christopher Robin Milne's toy but on "Growler", a toy bear belonging to his own son Graham, and drew the Hundred Acre Wood from the Ashdown Forest landscape. Shepard's original drawings are held by the Victoria and Albert Museum, and the stuffed toys that inspired the stories are displayed at the New York Public Library.

Later years

Tragically, his son Graham died in the Second World War when his ship was sunk. Shepard wrote two volumes of autobiography, Drawn from Memory (1957) and Drawn from Life (1961), was appointed OBE in 1972, and continued drawing into his nineties, dying in 1976 at the age of 96.
