3200 Phaethon is an active Apollo asteroid with an orbit that brings it closer to the Sun than any other named asteroid, reaching a perihelion of just 0.14 astronomical units — less than half Mercury's distance. It was discovered in 1983 in data from the IRAS satellite, the first asteroid found by a spacecraft, and is named after Phaëthon, son of the Greek sun god Helios.

Parent of the Geminids

Phaethon is the parent body of the Geminid meteor shower, one of the richest annual showers. Unusually for a meteor stream, its parent is an asteroid rather than a comet, leading scientists to describe Phaethon as a "rock comet": near the Sun, its surface heats to roughly 1,000 K, cracking and shedding dust that feeds the stream. Observations by the STEREO spacecraft have caught Phaethon brightening and growing a small tail near perihelion.

Exploration

Phaethon made a relatively close approach to Earth in December 2017, when radar imaging from Arecibo revealed a roughly spherical body at least 5.8 km across with a large concavity and a dark polar spot. JAXA's DESTINY+ spacecraft is planned to fly by Phaethon in the late 2020s to study its dust environment and surface.
