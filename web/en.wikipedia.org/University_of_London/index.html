The University of London (UoL) is a federal public research university located in London, England, United Kingdom. Established by royal charter in 1836, it is a federation of 17 self-governing member institutions — including UCL, King's College London, and the London School of Economics — together with central academic bodies. It was the first university in the United Kingdom to admit women to degrees (1878) and pioneered distance learning through its external programmes.

History

The university was founded in 1836 as an examining and degree-awarding body for students of University College London and King's College London, without religious tests. In 1858 it opened its degrees to candidates worldwide regardless of where they studied, creating the External System — a forerunner of modern distance education. The Universities Tests Act and reforms of 1900 reconstituted it as a federal teaching university. Its headquarters, Senate House in Bloomsbury, opened in 1937 and is said to have inspired the Ministry of Truth in Orwell's Nineteen Eighty-Four.

Member institutions

Member institutions include UCL, King's College London, LSE, Queen Mary, Royal Holloway, SOAS, Birkbeck, Goldsmiths, City, the Royal Academy of Music, and specialist institutes. Most students are enrolled at these institutions, which award University of London degrees (some now award their own). The School of Advanced Study and University of London Worldwide serve central and distance-learning functions; tens of thousands of students study its programmes in over 190 countries.

Notable alumni

Graduates include Mahatma Gandhi, Nelson Mandela, and numerous heads of state and government, as well as at least 12 Nobel laureates in the federation's history. Nelson Mandela studied for a University of London law degree by correspondence while imprisoned on Robben Island.
