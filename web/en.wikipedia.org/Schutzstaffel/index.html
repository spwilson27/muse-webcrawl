Schutzstaffel

The SS runes insignia

The Schutzstaffel (SS; also stylized as ᛋᛋ with Armanen runes; literally "Protection Squadron") was a major paramilitary organization under Adolf Hitler and the Nazi Party in Nazi Germany, and later throughout German-occupied Europe during World War II. It began with a small guard unit known as the Saal-Schutz ("Hall Protection") made up of party volunteers to provide security for party meetings in Munich.

In 1929, Heinrich Himmler became Reichsführer-SS and transformed the organization: by the time the Nazis came to power in 1933, the SS had grown from 280 members to over 50,000. Himmler envisioned the SS as an elite, ideologically committed racial order—the "knighthood" of the Nazi movement—bound by personal loyalty to Hitler and by pseudo-chivalric rituals of its own invention.

Organization and role

The SS developed into a sprawling state-within-a-state. Its main branches included the Allgemeine SS (General SS), the SS-Totenkopfverbände, which ran the concentration camps, and the Waffen-SS, its armed military formations, which grew to nearly a million men and fought alongside (and sometimes in rivalry with) the regular Wehrmacht. Through the Reich Security Main Office (RSHA), the SS controlled the Gestapo, the criminal police, and the SD intelligence service; through its Race and Settlement Main Office and the Einsatzgruppen mobile killing squads, it organized and carried out the Holocaust—the genocide of six million Jews—as well as the murder of Roma, Poles, Soviet prisoners of war, disabled people, and other victims. The SS also ran vast economic enterprises exploiting concentration-camp slave labor.

Defeat and judgment

After Germany's defeat in 1945, the International Military Tribunal at Nuremberg declared the SS a criminal organization and convicted its leading surviving figures of war crimes and crimes against humanity. Himmler committed suicide in British custody in May 1945. The SS's combination of ideological fanaticism, police terror, military power, and bureaucratic organization of genocide has made it the enduring symbol of the Nazi regime's criminality.
