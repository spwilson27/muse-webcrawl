Cytidine deaminase

Cytidine deaminase
EC number3.5.4.5
Gene (human)CDA
ReactionCytidine + H₂O → uridine + NH₃
CofactorZinc

Cytidine deaminase is an enzyme (EC 3.5.4.5) that catalyses the hydrolytic deamination of cytidine to uridine, releasing ammonia. In humans it is encoded by the CDA gene. The enzyme participates in pyrimidine salvage pathways, recycling nucleosides for nucleic acid synthesis, and it also acts on deoxycytidine and on a number of synthetic cytidine analogues.

Structure

Human cytidine deaminase is a homotetramer built from subunits of 146 amino acids. Each active site contains a zinc ion coordinated by cysteine and histidine residues, which activates a water molecule for attack on the substrate. The enzyme is structurally related to the broader family of cytidine deaminases that includes the APOBEC proteins involved in RNA and DNA editing and innate antiviral defence.

Clinical significance

Cytidine deaminase inactivates several anticancer and antiviral nucleoside drugs, including cytarabine (ara-C) and gemcitabine, converting them to their inactive uridine counterparts. Levels of the enzyme in liver, plasma, and tumour tissue therefore influence drug efficacy and toxicity, and genetic polymorphisms in CDA have been associated with variable responses to these agents. Inhibitors of cytidine deaminase, such as tetrahydrouridine and cedazuridine, are used to protect drugs like decitabine from rapid deamination and improve their oral bioavailability.
