Mass

Mass is a quantity of matter, usually measured in kilograms in the International System of Units (SI). In physics, mass is not the same as weight: weight is a force depending on gravity, whereas mass is an intrinsic property of matter that resists changes in motion (inertia) and is a source of gravitational attraction.

Definitions

Several concepts are distinguished in physics. Inertial mass measures an object's resistance to acceleration when a force is applied (Newton's second law, F = ma). Gravitational mass measures the strength of an object's interaction with a gravitational field. Experiments, culminating in the Eötvös experiment and space-based tests, show inertial and gravitational mass to be equal to extremely high precision — a fact Einstein elevated into the equivalence principle of general relativity.

Mass and energy

In special relativity, mass and energy are related by E = mc². A system's mass includes its internal energy; rest mass is energy in invariant form. Particle physicists often use electronvolts as units of mass. Since the 2019 redefinition of the SI, the kilogram is defined by fixing the value of the Planck constant rather than by a physical artefact.

Measurement

Mass is measured by comparison against standards using balances, or by measuring acceleration under a known force. Mass differs from weight, which is measured in newtons and varies with local gravity — an object's weight on the Moon is about one-sixth of its weight on Earth, while its mass is unchanged.
