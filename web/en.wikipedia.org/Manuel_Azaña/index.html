Manuel Azaña

Born:10 January 1880, Alcalá de Henares
Died:3 November 1940, Montauban, France
Offices:Prime Minister of Spain (1931–1933, 1936); President of the Republic (1936–1939)

Manuel Azaña Díaz (10 January 1880 – 3 November 1940) was a Spanish politician and writer who served as Prime Minister of the Second Spanish Republic and, during the Spanish Civil War, as its President. A leading intellectual of his generation, he was the principal architect of the Republic's early reform programme.

Early life and career

Azaña was born in Alcalá de Henares, studied law in Madrid and Paris, and worked as a civil servant in the Directorate-General of Registries and Notaries. He became a prominent essayist, translator, and novelist — his works include the novel El jardín de los frailes and studies of Juan Valera — and edited the journal España. He founded the party Acción Republicana (Republican Action) in 1925, during the dictatorship of Primo de Rivera.

Second Republic

After the proclamation of the Republic in April 1931, Azaña became Minister of War and then Prime Minister (October 1931–September 1933). His government pursued military reform, agrarian reform, Catalan autonomy, secular education, and the separation of church and state — a programme that made him the central figure, and the chief target of conservative opposition, of the regime. He returned as Prime Minister in February 1936 after the Popular Front's electoral victory, and in May 1936 was elected President of the Republic, succeeding Niceto Alcalá-Zamora.

Civil War and exile

When civil war broke out in July 1936, Azaña remained head of state but with diminishing real power, increasingly isolated as the war radicalized politics. He appealed in vain for a negotiated settlement and foreign mediation, most famously in his 1938 speech calling for "peace, pity, and forgiveness" (paz, piedad y perdón). In February 1939 he crossed into France, resigned the presidency, and died in exile at Montauban in November 1940, shortly after the Vichy regime and the Franco government disputed custody of his remains.
