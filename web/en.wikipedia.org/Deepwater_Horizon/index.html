Deepwater Horizon


Deepwater Horizon ablaze on 21 April 2010

Deepwater Horizon was an ultra-deepwater, dynamically positioned, semi-submersible offshore drilling rig owned by Transocean and leased to BP. Built in 2001 by Hyundai Heavy Industries in South Korea, it could drill in waters up to about 2,400 metres deep to well depths of 9,100 metres.

Explosion and oil spill

On 20 April 2010, while drilling the Macondo Prospect in the Gulf of Mexico, a blowout caused an explosion and fire that killed eleven workers and injured seventeen. The rig sank two days later. The broken well discharged oil for 87 days until it was capped on 15 July 2010; an estimated 3.19 million barrels (about 134 million US gallons) flowed into the Gulf, making it the largest marine oil spill in history.

Aftermath

The disaster caused extensive damage to marine and wildlife habitats and to the fishing and tourism industries of the Gulf Coast. Investigations found failures in cementing, well monitoring, and the blowout preventer, and faulted management decisions by BP, Transocean, and Halliburton. In 2015 BP agreed to a settlement of over $20 billion with the United States and five Gulf states, the largest environmental settlement in US history; total costs to BP exceeded $65 billion. A dramatization, the film Deepwater Horizon, was released in 2016.
