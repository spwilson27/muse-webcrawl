Light cavalry


Light cavalry comprises lightly armed and lightly armoured cavalry troops mounted on fast horses, used for reconnaissance, screening, skirmishing and raiding.

Light cavalry comprises lightly armed and armored troops mounted on horses, as opposed to heavy cavalry, where the riders and sometimes horses are heavily armored. Light cavalry's tasks were reconnaissance, screening, skirmishing, raiding, and communications.

History

Light horsemen such as Numidian cavalry served in ancient armies. In early modern Europe, hussars, uhlans, chasseurs à cheval and light dragoons formed light cavalry regiments. Perhaps the most famous action was the Charge of the Light Brigade at Balaclava in 1854 during the Crimean War.

Decline

Mechanisation in the 20th century replaced horses with armoured cars and light tanks, but many modern units retain historic light cavalry titles and the reconnaissance role.
