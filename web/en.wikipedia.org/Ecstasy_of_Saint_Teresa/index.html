The Ecstasy of Saint Teresa (also known as Saint Teresa in Ecstasy) is a sculptural group in white marble by the Italian Baroque master Gian Lorenzo Bernini, set in an elevated aedicule in the Cornaro Chapel of the church of Santa Maria della Vittoria in Rome. It is widely considered a masterpiece of Baroque sculpture.

Subject

The work depicts Saint Teresa of Ávila, the Spanish Carmelite mystic, at the moment described in her autobiography when an angel pierced her heart with a golden spear — an experience of religious ecstasy she called her transverberation. Teresa swoons on a cloud, eyes closed and mouth open, while a smiling seraph holds the arrow above her. Natural light from a hidden window falls on the group, supplemented by gilded bronze rays, blurring the boundary between sculpture, architecture and divine illumination.

Commission and reception

The chapel was commissioned by Cardinal Federico Cornaro, whose family members appear as marble witnesses in theatre boxes on either side. Critics have long debated the work's sensuality: Teresa's expression has famously been compared to erotic rapture, a reading Bernini's contemporaries already sensed, though the artist — a devout Catholic — understood the piece as the representation of divine love. A popular legend that Bernini modelled the saint on a mistress is unfounded.
