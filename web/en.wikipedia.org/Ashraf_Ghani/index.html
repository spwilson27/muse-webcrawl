Mohammad Ashraf Ghani Ahmadzai (born 19 May 1949) is an Afghan anthropologist, economist and politician who served as the president of Afghanistan from September 2014 until August 2021, when he fled the country as the Taliban captured Kabul during their 2021 offensive, ending the Islamic Republic of Afghanistan.

Early life and career

Ghani was born in Logar Province into a prominent Pashtun family. He studied at the American University of Beirut and earned a PhD in anthropology from Columbia University. He taught at several universities before joining the World Bank in 1991, where he worked on development programs in East and South Asia. After the fall of the Taliban in 2001 he returned to Afghanistan, serving as finance minister (2002–2004) under Hamid Karzai, where he introduced reforms including a new currency. He later founded the Institute for State Effectiveness and co-authored the book Fixing Failed States (2008). He ran unsuccessfully for president in 2009.

Presidency

Ghani won the disputed 2014 presidential election and, after a US-brokered agreement, formed a national unity government with his rival Abdullah Abdullah as chief executive. He was re-elected in 2019, again amid dispute, and sworn in for a second term in March 2020. His presidency focused on anti-corruption, economic reform, regional connectivity and peace negotiations with the Taliban, but was hampered by insecurity, political fragmentation and dependence on foreign aid.

Fall of Kabul and exile

Following the withdrawal of US and NATO forces in 2021, the Taliban rapidly seized provincial capitals. On 15 August 2021, as Taliban fighters entered Kabul, Ghani fled Afghanistan, later surfacing in the United Arab Emirates. He stated he had left to prevent bloodshed in the capital. His departure prompted criticism at home and abroad, and he has denied reports that he took large sums of cash with him.
