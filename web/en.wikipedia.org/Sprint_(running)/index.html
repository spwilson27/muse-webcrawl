Sprint (running)

Sprinters at the start of a 100 metres race.

Sprinting is running over a short distance at the top-most speed of the body in a limited period of time. It is used in many sports that incorporate running, typically as a way of quickly reaching a target or goal, or avoiding or catching an opponent. Human physiology dictates that a runner's near-top speed cannot be maintained for more than 30–35 seconds because of the depletion of phosphocreatine stores in muscles, and perhaps secondarily to excessive metabolic acidosis as a result of anaerobic glycolysis.

The standard sprint distances contested at the Olympic Games and World Championships are the 100 metres, 200 metres, and 400 metres. The 100 metres is regarded as one of the blue ribbon events of athletics, and its world record holders are often called the "fastest man" or "fastest woman" in the world.

Technique

Sprint races are started from starting blocks, which allow athletes to begin with a powerful push against the track. A sprint typically progresses through phases: the block start, acceleration, transition, maximum velocity, and speed maintenance (or deceleration) toward the finish. Sprinters keep their bodies upright at top speed, strike the ground on the forefoot, and drive their arms powerfully in coordination with the legs.

Rules

Sprints are governed by the rules of World Athletics. Runners must stay within their assigned lanes for the 100 m, and for the 200 m and 400 m in major championships. A false start — leaving the blocks before the starting gun or within 0.1 seconds of it — results in immediate disqualification under rules adopted in 2010. Wind readings are taken for record purposes; a tailwind exceeding 2.0 metres per second invalidates a performance for record ratification.

History

The sprint was the original Olympic event: the stadion footrace of about 192 metres was the sole event of the ancient Olympic Games for the first thirteen Olympiads. The modern 100 metres became an Olympic event in 1896 for men and 1928 for women. Famous sprinters include Jesse Owens, Carl Lewis, Florence Griffith Joyner, and Usain Bolt, whose 9.58-second world record for the 100 metres was set in Berlin in 2009.
