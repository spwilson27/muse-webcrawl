People's commune

A people's commune (Chinese: 人民公社) was the highest of three administrative levels in rural China during the period 1958 to the early 1980s, combining government administration with collective ownership of land and organisation of agricultural production. Communes were established during the Great Leap Forward, merging agricultural cooperatives into units that often comprised thousands of households.

Organisation

Communes were subdivided into production brigades and production teams. They ran collective dining halls, nurseries, schools, and clinics, and during the Great Leap Forward organised mass labour for irrigation works and backyard steel furnaces. Private plots were abolished at the movement's height, and grain procurement based on inflated output reports contributed to the catastrophic famine of 1959–1961.

Dissolution

After the famine, production teams became the basic accounting unit and small private plots were restored. Following the reforms of Deng Xiaoping, the household responsibility system spread from 1978, and people's communes were formally abolished in the early 1980s, their governmental functions passing to township governments.
