Buffalo is a city in New York State and the seat of Erie County, located at the eastern end of Lake Erie at the head of the Niagara River, on the Canadian border opposite Fort Erie, Ontario. It is the second-largest city in the state, after New York City. Buffalo grew rapidly after the opening of the Erie Canal in 1825, becoming a major terminus for shipping between the Great Lakes and the Atlantic, and later a centre of grain milling, steel production and rail transport.

History

The village of Buffalo was burned by British forces during the War of 1812 and rebuilt, and the completion of the Erie Canal made it a booming port. At the start of the 20th century Buffalo was one of the largest cities in the United States and hosted the Pan-American Exposition of 1901, during which President William McKinley was assassinated in the city. The opening of the St. Lawrence Seaway in 1959 and the decline of heavy industry caused a long period of population loss, from a peak of about 580,000 in 1950.

Geography and culture

Buffalo is known for its lake-effect snow, its radial street and parkway system designed in part by Frederick Law Olmsted, and major works by architects Frank Lloyd Wright, Louis Sullivan and H. H. Richardson. It is the namesake of Buffalo wings, invented at the Anchor Bar in 1964, and home to the Buffalo Bills of the NFL and the Buffalo Sabres of the NHL, as well as the University at Buffalo. A medical and educational revival has driven renewed growth in the 21st century.
