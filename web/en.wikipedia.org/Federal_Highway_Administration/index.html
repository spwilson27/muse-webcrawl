Federal Highway Administration

The Federal Highway Administration (FHWA) is a division of the United States Department of Transportation responsible for administering the Federal-Aid Highway Program, the principal source of federal funding for highway construction and maintenance in the United States, in cooperation with state departments of transportation.

FormedOctober 15, 1966
HeadquartersWashington, D.C.
Parent agencyU.S. Department of Transportation
PredecessorBureau of Public Roads

History

The FHWA descends from the Office of Road Inquiry, created in 1893 in the Department of Agriculture, and from the Bureau of Public Roads, which administered the Federal-Aid Highway Acts and the construction of the Interstate Highway System begun in 1956. The agency was reorganised under the new Department of Transportation in 1966.

Role

The FHWA apportions federal highway funds to the states, sets design and safety standards for federal-aid projects, runs research programmes through the Turner-Fairbank Highway Research Center, and maintains an office in every state, Puerto Rico and the District of Columbia.
