Abkhaz–Georgian conflict

Date1989–present (main war 1992–1993)
LocationAbkhazia, north-western Georgia
StatusAbkhazia de facto independent; recognised by Russia and a few states

The Abkhaz–Georgian conflict is a political and military conflict between Georgia and the separatist authorities of Abkhazia, a region on the Black Sea coast in Georgia's north-west. It is one of the post-Soviet "frozen conflicts". Abkhazia functions as a de facto independent state but is internationally recognised as part of Georgia by most countries.

Background and war

Tensions between ethnic Abkhaz and Georgians have roots in the Soviet period, when Abkhazia's status within the Georgian SSR shifted and demographic changes reduced the Abkhaz share of the region's population. Riots broke out in Sukhumi in July 1989. In August 1992, after Abkhazia's Supreme Soviet declared sovereignty, Georgian forces entered the region, beginning a war marked by heavy fighting and ethnic cleansing on both sides. In September 1993 Abkhaz and allied forces captured Sukhumi; some 200,000–250,000 ethnic Georgians fled or were expelled, and thousands were killed.

Aftermath

A 1994 ceasefire, monitored by CIS (largely Russian) peacekeepers and UN observers (UNOMIG), froze the conflict. A brief resumption of fighting occurred in the Gali district in 1998. After the Russo-Georgian War of August 2008, Russia recognised Abkhazia as independent and stationed troops there; only a handful of states have followed suit. Negotiations in the Geneva International Discussions have not resolved the status question or the return of displaced Georgians.
