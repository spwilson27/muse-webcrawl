Applied mathematics is the application of mathematical methods by different fields such as physics, engineering, medicine, biology, finance, business, computer science and industry. Thus, applied mathematics is a combination of mathematical science and specialised knowledge. The term also describes the professional specialty in which mathematicians work on practical problems by formulating and studying mathematical models.

Historical orientation

Historically, applied mathematics consisted principally of applied analysis — especially differential equations — approximation theory and applied probability. Figures such as Isaac Newton, Leonhard Euler, Joseph-Louis Lagrange and Carl Friedrich Gauss developed mathematics in close connection with mechanics and astronomy. In the twentieth century, the needs of physics, engineering and wartime research drove the expansion of fields such as fluid dynamics, operations research and control theory, and the rise of the electronic computer transformed the discipline by making large-scale numerical computation routine.

Principal areas

Major areas of applied mathematics include numerical analysis (algorithms for solving equations, integration, and simulation), mathematical optimisation, statistics and probability, discrete mathematics and combinatorics as used in computer science, mathematical modelling of biological, physical and social systems, cryptography, information theory, game theory and financial mathematics. Computational mathematics and scientific computing are closely allied fields concerned with implementing these methods on computers.

Relation to pure mathematics

The distinction between applied and pure mathematics is one of outlook rather than strict subject matter: the same theory may be pursued for its own sake or for applications. Results developed as pure mathematics, such as number theory, have later proved essential to applications like cryptography. Professional societies such as the Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics (SIAM), founded in 1951, serve the applied community, and the field is often described along a spectrum from pure research to engineering practice.
