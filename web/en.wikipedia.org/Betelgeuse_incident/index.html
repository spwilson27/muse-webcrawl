Betelgeuse incident

Date2019–2020
SubjectAnomalous dimming of Betelgeuse
CauseDust cloud from surface mass ejection

The Betelgeuse incident, also known as the "Great Dimming", refers to the unexpected and dramatic fading of the red supergiant star Betelgeuse in the constellation Orion beginning in late 2019, when its brightness dropped to roughly a third of its usual value.

Observations

Because Betelgeuse is a variable star near the end of its life, the unusual dimming attracted widespread attention and speculation that the star might be about to explode as a supernova. Observations with the Hubble Space Telescope and the Very Large Telescope instead revealed a large dark patch and evidence of dust.

Explanation

Astronomers concluded that the dimming was caused by a surface mass ejection: a large outflow of material from the star's southern hemisphere cooled and condensed into a dust cloud that partly obscured the star. Betelgeuse recovered its normal brightness during 2020, though its pulsation behaviour was altered afterwards.
