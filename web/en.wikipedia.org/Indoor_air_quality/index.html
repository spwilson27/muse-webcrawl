Indoor air quality

Indoor air quality (IAQ) is the quality of air within and around buildings and structures, especially as it relates to the health and comfort of building occupants. People in industrialised countries spend most of their time indoors, so control of common pollutants — from combustion, building materials, radon, mould, and cleaning products — can greatly reduce health risks, including respiratory disease, allergies, and sick building syndrome.

Adequate ventilation is central to maintaining indoor air quality.

Common pollutants

Key indoor pollutants include carbon monoxide and nitrogen dioxide from fuel-burning appliances; volatile organic compounds (VOCs) such as formaldehyde from furnishings and paints; fine particulate matter (PM2.5) from cooking, candles, and outdoor infiltration; radon gas seeping from the ground; biological contaminants such as mould spores, dust mites, and Legionella bacteria; and environmental tobacco smoke. Carbon dioxide levels serve as a widely used proxy for ventilation adequacy.

Control

IAQ is managed by source control, ventilation (natural and mechanical, per standards such as ASHRAE 62.1 and 62.2), filtration and air cleaning (for example HEPA filters), and humidity control to suppress mould and mites. Globally, household air pollution from solid fuels used for cooking and heating remains a major cause of premature death, addressed through clean-fuel programmes promoted by the World Health Organization.
