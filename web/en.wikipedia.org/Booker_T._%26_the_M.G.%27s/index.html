Booker T. & the M.G.'s were an American instrumental R&B and soul band formed in Memphis, Tennessee, in 1962. As the house band of Stax Records, they played on hundreds of recordings by artists including Wilson Pickett, Otis Redding, Bill Withers, Sam & Dave, and Albert King, while also releasing successful instrumental records under their own name.

History

The group's line-up centred on organist Booker T. Jones, guitarist Steve Cropper, bassist Donald "Duck" Dunn, and drummer Al Jackson Jr. — one of the first racially integrated rock bands. Their debut single, "Green Onions" (1962), became a major hit and one of the most popular instrumental songs ever recorded. Follow-up hits included "Hip Hug-Her", "Soul Limbo", "Hang 'Em High", and "Time Is Tight".

Style and influence

The M.G.'s defined the Memphis soul sound with spare, groove-driven arrangements built around Jones's Hammond organ and Cropper's economical guitar parts. Their work as a backing band shaped the sound of Southern soul throughout the 1960s.

Legacy

Booker T. & the M.G.'s were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1992 and received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2007. "Green Onions" was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame and the Library of Congress's National Recording Registry.
