Richardson–Lucy deconvolution

Richardson–Lucy deconvolution is an iterative algorithm for recovering an image that has been blurred by a known point spread function (PSF). It was described by William H. Richardson in 1972 and Leon B. Lucy in 1974, building on Bayes' theorem and maximum-likelihood estimation under Poisson noise statistics.

Algorithm

Starting from an initial estimate (often the observed image itself), the algorithm repeatedly applies a multiplicative correction: the current estimate is convolved with the PSF, compared with the observed image, and the ratio is convolved with the flipped PSF and multiplied back into the estimate. Each iteration increases the likelihood of the estimate, and non-negativity of pixel values is preserved automatically.

Applications

The method became famous in astronomy, where it was used to sharpen images from the Hubble Space Telescope before and after its optics were corrected, and it remains widely used in microscopy, medical imaging, and photography. Its drawbacks include noise amplification with many iterations and the need for an accurate PSF.
