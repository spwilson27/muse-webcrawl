Meroë

Meroë

Location: Shendi area, Sudan

Region: Nubia

Period: Kingdom of Kush, c. 800 BC – AD 350

Status: UNESCO World Heritage Site (2011)

Meroë (also spelled Meroe) was an ancient city on the east bank of the Nile, about 230 km northeast of modern Khartoum, Sudan. It was the southern capital of the Kingdom of Kush for several centuries and is known for its more than two hundred pyramids.

History

Meroë rose to prominence after the Kushite capital moved south from Napata around 590 BC. The city was a major centre of ironworking and trade, sometimes called the "Birmingham of Africa" for its iron production. The kingdom flourished on trade in gold, ivory, ebony, and iron with Egypt, the Mediterranean world, and Arabia.

Pyramids

The royal cemeteries contain steep-sided Nubian pyramids, smaller and sharper-angled than the Egyptian pyramids, arranged in three main cemeteries (Begrawiya). In 1834 the Italian treasure hunter Giuseppe Ferlini destroyed the tops of several pyramids searching for gold.

Decline

Meroë weakened in the 3rd and 4th centuries AD and was abandoned after incursions associated with the rising Kingdom of Aksum around AD 350. The site, together with Naqa and Musawwarat es-Sufra, was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2011.
