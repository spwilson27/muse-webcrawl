Raga

Raga
Melodic framework in Indian classical music
Defined by scale, characteristic phrases and mood

A raga (or raag) is a melodic framework for improvisation in Indian classical music. The raga is a unique and central feature of the classical Indian music tradition; each raga is an array of melodic structures with musical motifs, considered in the Indian tradition to have the ability to "color the mind" and affect the emotions of the audience.

Structure

A raga specifies an ascending scale (aroha) and descending scale (avaroha), which may differ; a hierarchy of notes with a dominant (vadi) and subdominant (samvadi); characteristic phrases (pakad); and rules for approaching and ornamenting notes. Ragas may use five, six or seven notes drawn from the twelve-tone octave of shrutis. Hindustani (northern) and Carnatic (southern, ragam) systems classify ragas differently — the Carnatic melakarta system organizes 72 parent scales.

Time and mood

Tradition associates many ragas with a particular time of day or season and with a rasa (aesthetic mood) such as devotion, longing or heroism. Performances typically unfold from a slow, unmetered alap through increasingly rhythmic sections to a fast climax, accompanied by tabla and drone (tanpura).

Notable ragas

Widely performed ragas include Yaman, Bhairavi, Malkauns, Darbari Kanada and the Carnatic Mohanam and Kalyani.
