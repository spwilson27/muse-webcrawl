Consecutive interpreting

Consecutive interpreting is a mode of language interpretation in which the interpreter listens to a segment of speech — ranging from a few seconds to several minutes — and then renders it in the target language after the speaker pauses. It contrasts with simultaneous interpreting, in which the interpreter speaks while the source speech continues.

Technique

Consecutive interpreters rely on memory supported by a specialized system of note-taking: abbreviations, symbols, and vertical layout that capture the logic and links of the speech rather than every word. In "short consecutive," the interpreter renders brief segments with minimal notes, common in meetings and medical or legal settings; in "long consecutive," speeches of up to ten minutes or more are rendered after specialized training.

Use

Consecutive interpreting is used in business negotiations, press conferences, court proceedings, hospital encounters, and state visits. It requires no special equipment, but it roughly doubles the time needed for communication, which is why simultaneous interpreting predominates at large multilingual conferences.
