Vodun

Vodun (also spelled Vudun, Vodou, Voodoo, and in the diaspora developed into related traditions) is a religion practiced by the Aja, Ewe, and Fon peoples of Benin, Togo, Ghana, and Nigeria. It centres on the vodun — spirits that govern natural and human domains — within a monotheistic framework headed by a creator deity paired as Mawu-Lisa.

[Image: A Vodun shrine in Benin with offerings and carved figures of the spirits.]

Beliefs and practice

Each vodun is served by priests (vodunon), shrines, dances, drumming and offerings; worshippers may be mounted by spirits in possession rituals. Prominent vodun include Legba, guardian of crossroads; Sakpata, associated with the earth and smallpox; and Dan, the serpent of continuity. The Fa (Ifa) divination system guides major decisions.

History and diaspora

Vodun flourished in the kingdom of Dahomey and survived colonial suppression. Through the Atlantic slave trade it gave rise to Haitian Vodou, Louisiana Voodoo, and influenced Candomblé in Brazil. It is an officially recognized religion in Benin, where National Vodun Day is celebrated on 10 January, centred on the town of Ouidah.
