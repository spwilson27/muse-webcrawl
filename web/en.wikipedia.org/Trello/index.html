Trello is a web-based, Kanban-style list-making application developed by Atlassian. Originally created by Fog Creek Software in 2011, it was launched at an event by TechCrunch founder Michael Arrington in September of that year. It was spun out to form the basis of a company in 2014, operating as a subsidiary of Fog Creek, and was sold to Atlassian in January 2017 for US$425 million.

Operation

Trello operates using a freemium model, in which a basic version is offered free of charge and a premium service was launched in 2013. Users create task boards with columns and move cards between them, generally following a kanban board method. Boards can contain multiple lists representing task progress, and cards can be moved between lists as progress is made. Users create and edit cards within their own boards, and may invite others to the board for collaboration.

Features

Users can assign tasks to individual users or groups, attach due dates and timers, and check off sub-tasks from checklists. Trello integrates with many other platforms and supports extensions known as "Power-Ups". On 18 March 2021, Trello introduced changes to its business plan pricing, reducing the number of users that could be invited to a free board and introducing a new "Butler" automation tool based on the Butler add-on it had acquired in 2018.

Reception

In July 2012, the site surpassed 500,000 users. It surpassed 1,000,000 unique monthly users in December 2013 and 25 million in March 2017. By 2019 the service reported more than 35 million registered users, and in October 2019, 50 million.
