Strain (mechanics)

In continuum mechanics, strain is a measure of deformation representing the displacement between particles in a material body relative to a reference length. It is a dimensionless quantity, often expressed as a percentage or in microstrain. Strain quantifies how much a material stretches, compresses or shears under applied stress.

Engineering strain

The simplest measure, engineering (or nominal) normal strain, is the change in length divided by the original length: ε = ΔL / L₀. For small deformations this coincides with the more rigorous measures. Because it does not account for the changing cross-section and instantaneous length, engineers also use true strain, defined as the integral of the incremental elongation, εtrue = ln(L / L₀).

Strain tensor

In three dimensions, deformation is described by the strain tensor, whose diagonal components are normal strains in each direction and whose off-diagonal components are shear strains, describing changes in the angles between material lines. For finite deformations, formulations such as the Green–Lagrange and Euler–Almansi strain tensors are used.

Relation to stress

For elastic materials under small strain, stress is proportional to strain through Hooke's law, with the constant of proportionality given by elastic moduli such as Young's modulus. Strain is measured experimentally with strain gauges and extensometers, and is central to structural engineering, geology (where it describes rock deformation) and materials science.
