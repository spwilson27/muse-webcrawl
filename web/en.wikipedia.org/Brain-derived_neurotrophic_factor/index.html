Brain-derived neurotrophic factor

Brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) is a protein, encoded in humans by the BDNF gene, belonging to the neurotrophin family of growth factors. It is widely expressed in the brain, especially in the hippocampus, cortex, and basal forebrain, and acts on the TrkB receptor to support the survival of neurons and the growth and differentiation of new neurons and synapses.

BDNF is important for learning, memory, and synaptic plasticity, including long-term potentiation. Its expression is increased by physical exercise and is altered in depression, Alzheimer's disease, schizophrenia, and eating disorders. A common polymorphism, Val66Met, affects activity-dependent BDNF secretion and has been studied in relation to memory and mood disorders.
