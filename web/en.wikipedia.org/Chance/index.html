Chance

Chance
Related conceptsRandomness, probability, luck, uncertainty
DisciplinesPhilosophy, mathematics, statistics

Chance is the occurrence of events in the absence of any obvious intention or cause, or the possibility of something happening. Philosophers and scientists distinguish between chance as an expression of ignorance about underlying causes (epistemic chance) and chance as a genuine indeterminacy in nature (ontic chance), as suggested by quantum mechanics.

In philosophy

Aristotle analysed chance (tyche) as a cause incidental to purposeful action. Later thinkers debated whether chance is compatible with determinism: for Laplace, a perfect intelligence knowing all positions and forces would need no concept of chance, while modern physics treats some randomness as fundamental.

In mathematics

Probability theory, formalised by Pascal, Fermat, and Kolmogorov, quantifies chance using probability spaces, random variables, and distributions. Statistics applies these tools to inference under uncertainty, and games of chance provided the historical stimulus for the theory.

In everyday life

Chance is invoked in accounts of luck, coincidence, and fortune-telling, and chance events play celebrated roles in evolution (genetic drift and mutation) and in art, from Dada to John Cage's aleatoric music.
