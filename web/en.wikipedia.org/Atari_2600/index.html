Atari 2600

Manufacturer: Atari, Inc.

Released: September 1977 (as Atari VCS)

CPU: MOS Technology 6507 at 1.19 MHz

Media: ROM cartridge

Units sold: Approximately 30 million

The Atari 2600 is a home video game console developed and produced by Atari, Inc. Released in September 1977 as the Atari Video Computer System (VCS), it popularised the use of microprocessor-based hardware and interchangeable game cartridges containing ROM, a format first used with the Fairchild Channel F in 1976.

History

The console was bundled with two joystick controllers, a pair of paddle controllers, and a game cartridge — initially Combat, and later Pac-Man. Under Ray Kassar, Atari licensed Taito's Space Invaders in 1980, the first official licensing of an arcade game, which quadrupled sales and became the industry's first "killer app".

The 2600 was hugely successful through the early 1980s, but a flood of poorly received third-party games, including the commercial failures Pac-Man and E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial, contributed to the video game crash of 1983. Warner Communications sold the home console division in 1984, and the system was discontinued on January 1, 1992. Third-party developers, beginning with Activision in 1979, were an important part of the platform's library and legacy.

Hardware

The 2600's MOS 6507 processor and Television Interface Adaptor chip offered extremely limited graphics by later standards: programmers manipulated the display scanline by scanline, a technique nicknamed "racing the beam". Most early cartridges held only 2 or 4 KB of ROM.

Legacy

The 2600 is credited with launching the home console industry and is remembered for games such as Adventure (which contained one of the first known Easter eggs), Pitfall!, and Yars' Revenge. It was inducted into the National Toy Hall of Fame in 2007.
