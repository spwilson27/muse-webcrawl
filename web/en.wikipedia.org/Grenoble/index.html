Grenoble (French pronunciation: [ɡʁənɔbl]) is the prefecture and largest city of the Isère department in the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region of southeastern France. It lies where the river Drac joins the Isère, in the foothills of the French Alps, surrounded by the Chartreuse, Vercors, and Belledonne mountain ranges. The wider metropolitan area is home to more than 450,000 people. Because of its setting, Grenoble is often styled the "Capital of the Alps".

History

The site was first settled by the Gallic tribe of the Allobroges; under Rome it was known as Cularo and later renamed Gratianopolis after the emperor Gratian. In the Middle Ages it became the capital of the Dauphiné province, whose rulers, the Dauphins of Viennois, gave their title to the heirs of the French crown. Grenoble played an early role in the French Revolution: the "Day of the Tiles" on 7 June 1788, when townspeople hurled roof tiles at royal troops, is often cited as a precursor of the Revolution. The writer Stendhal, one of the city's most famous sons, called it a place where "every street corner is the scene of a memory."

The city hosted the 1968 Winter Olympics, which transformed its infrastructure and international profile. A distinctive feature is the Bastille, a fortress on the heights above the old town, reached since 1976 by a cable car whose spherical cabins — "les bulles" (the bubbles) — are an emblem of the city.

Science and economy

Grenoble is one of Europe's most important scientific centres. It hosts the European Synchrotron Radiation Facility (ESRF), the Institut Laue–Langevin neutron source, and major laboratories in microelectronics, nanotechnology, and computing. The Grenoble Alpes University and Grenoble INP educate tens of thousands of students, and the area around the city is sometimes nicknamed France's "Silicon Valley" of the mountains.
