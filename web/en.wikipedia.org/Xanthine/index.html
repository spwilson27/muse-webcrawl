Xanthine

Chemical formulaC5H4N4O2
Molar mass152.11 g/mol
ClassPurine base
Metabolized toUric acid

Xanthine (from Greek xanthos, "yellow", owing to the yellow colour of its oxidation products) is a purine base found in most tissues and fluids of the human body and in many other organisms. It is an intermediate in the degradation of purine nucleotides: adenosine monophosphate is deaminated and oxidized through hypoxanthine to xanthine, and guanosine monophosphate breakdown also yields xanthine. The enzyme xanthine oxidase then converts xanthine to uric acid, which is excreted in urine.

Derivatives

Methylated xanthines are a well-known family of mild stimulants: caffeine is 1,3,7-trimethylxanthine, while theobromine (in cocoa) and theophylline (used for asthma) are dimethylxanthines. These compounds act chiefly by antagonizing adenosine receptors and inhibiting phosphodiesterase.

Clinical significance

Deficiency of xanthine oxidase causes xanthinuria, a rare inherited disorder in which xanthine accumulates and may form kidney stones. Conversely, excessive production of uric acid from xanthine contributes to gout, which is treated in part with xanthine oxidase inhibitors such as allopurinol.
