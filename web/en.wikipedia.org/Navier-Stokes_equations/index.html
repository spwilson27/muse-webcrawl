Navier–Stokes equations

The Navier–Stokes equations are partial differential equations that describe the motion of viscous fluid substances. Named after Claude-Louis Navier and George Gabriel Stokes, they express conservation of momentum for a fluid, together with conservation of mass (the continuity equation).

Formulation

For an incompressible fluid, the momentum equation states that the acceleration of the fluid equals the sum of pressure forces, viscous forces and external body forces per unit mass. The equations are nonlinear because of the convective acceleration term, which is responsible for much of their complexity.

Applications and open problems

The equations underpin aerodynamics, weather modelling, oceanography and many branches of engineering. Whether smooth solutions always exist in three dimensions is one of the Millennium Prize Problems of the Clay Mathematics Institute, carrying a prize of one million US dollars.
