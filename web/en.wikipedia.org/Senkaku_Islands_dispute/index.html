Senkaku Islands dispute

The neutrality of this article's presentation of competing claims reflects the unresolved status of the dispute.


Location of the disputed islands in the East China Sea.

The Senkaku Islands dispute, or Diaoyu Islands dispute, is a territorial dispute over a group of uninhabited islands in the East China Sea known in Japanese as the Senkaku Islands, in Mandarin Chinese as the Diaoyu Islands, and in English sometimes as the Pinnacle Islands. The islands are administered by Japan, which has controlled them since 1895 (except for a period of United States administration from 1945 to 1972), but are claimed by both the People's Republic of China and the Republic of China (Taiwan).

Background

The island group consists of five uninhabited islets and three barren rocks northwest of the Yaeyama Islands and northeast of Taiwan. Japan incorporated the islands in January 1895 during the First Sino-Japanese War, asserting that surveys since 1885 had shown them to be terra nullius (land belonging to no one). Under the 1951 San Francisco Peace Treaty the islands were placed under the administration of the United States as part of the Ryukyu Islands, and administrative rights reverted to Japan in 1972 under the Okinawa Reversion Agreement.

Competing claims

China maintains that the islands have been part of Chinese territory since ancient times, recorded in Ming dynasty documents, and that they were seized by Japan in 1895 along with Taiwan under the Treaty of Shimonoseki, so should have been returned after the Second World War under the Cairo and Potsdam declarations. Japan's official position is that there exists no issue of territorial sovereignty to be resolved, and that Chinese claims emerged only in the 1970s after a 1968 survey suggested possible oil and gas reserves in the surrounding seabed.

Escalations

The dispute has repeatedly flared: in 2010 a collision between a Chinese fishing trawler and Japan Coast Guard vessels near the islands, followed by the detention of the Chinese captain, caused a major diplomatic row. In 2012 the Japanese government's purchase of three of the islands from their private Japanese owner triggered widespread protests in China and a sharp deterioration in relations, after which Chinese coast guard vessels began regular patrols in the islands' territorial waters. Chinese and Japanese patrol vessels and aircraft continue to operate around the islands, and the area is covered by the United States' security commitment to Japan.
