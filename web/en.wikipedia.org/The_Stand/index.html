The Stand

The Stand is an epic post-apocalyptic fantasy novel by American author Stephen King, first published in 1978. One of King's best-selling and most highly regarded works, the novel was reissued in 1990 in an expanded edition, The Stand: The Complete and Uncut Edition, restoring material cut from the original publication. It has been adapted twice as a television miniseries, in 1994 and 2020.

Plot

The story begins with the accidental release of a government-engineered superflu, nicknamed "Captain Trips", which kills more than 99 percent of the world's population. The survivors are drawn by dreams toward one of two figures: Mother Abagail, a 108-year-old woman living in Nebraska who represents good, or Randall Flagg, the sinister "Dark Man" who establishes a tyrannical regime in Las Vegas. The conflict between the two camps builds toward a final confrontation over the future of humanity.

Themes and reception

King described the novel as his attempt to write an American epic comparable to The Lord of the Rings, relocated to the United States. Recurring themes include the struggle between good and evil, the fragility of civilization, and the cyclical nature of human society. The expanded 1990 edition became one of the best-selling novels of that year.

Adaptations

The 1994 ABC miniseries starred Gary Sinise as Stu Redman and Molly Ringwald as Frannie Goldsmith. A second adaptation aired on CBS All Access (later Paramount+) in 2020–2021, with James Marsden and Odessa Young in the lead roles. See also Stephen King and The Dark Tower.
