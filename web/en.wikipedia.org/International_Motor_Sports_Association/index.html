The International Motor Sports Association (IMSA) is a North American auto racing sanctioning body based in Daytona Beach, Florida, United States. It was started by John Bishop, a former employee of SCCA (Sports Car Club of America), and his wife Peggy in 1969 with help from Bill France Sr. of NASCAR. IMSA is the sanctioning body of the IMSA SportsCar Championship, the premier series it sanctions, which includes the endurance classics of the 24 Hours of Daytona, the 12 Hours of Sebring and Petit Le Mans.

History

IMSA organised its first races in 1969 and launched the IMSA GT Championship in 1971, which grew into a leading North American series with Camel GT sponsorship and, later, the spectacular GTP prototypes of the 1980s. After financial troubles and changes of ownership in the 1990s, IMSA sanctioned the American Le Mans Series from 1999. In 2012 NASCAR Holdings acquired IMSA, and in 2014 the Grand-Am Rolex Sports Car Series and American Le Mans Series merged to form the United SportsCar Championship, now the IMSA SportsCar Championship (WeatherTech Championship).

Series

In addition to the SportsCar Championship—with classes for GTP hybrid prototypes, LMP2, and GTD production-based cars—IMSA sanctions support series including the Michelin Pilot Challenge, IMSA VP Racing SportsCar Challenge, and single-make series such as Mazda MX-5 Cup, Porsche Carrera Cup North America and Lamborghini Super Trofeo.
