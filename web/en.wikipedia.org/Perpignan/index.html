Perpignan (French: [pɛʁpiɲā]; Catalan: Perpinyà) is the prefecture of the Pyrénées-Orientales department in southern France, in the heart of the plain of Roussillon, at the foot of the Pyrenees some 13 km west of the Mediterranean coast. Historically the capital of the County of Roussillon and, in the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries, the continental capital of the Kingdom of Majorca, it retains a strong Catalan identity.

History

Roman Ruscino preceded the medieval town, whose name is attested in 927. Perpignan passed between the counts of Roussillon, the kings of Aragon and Majorca, and the French crown; the Palace of the Kings of Majorca still dominates the city. Roussillon was definitively ceded by Spain to France by the Treaty of the Pyrenees in 1659. In the twentieth century the city received Spanish Republican refugees after 1939 and repatriated settlers from Algeria after 1962.

Sights and culture

Landmarks include the red-brick Castillet (the city's emblem), Perpignan Cathedral of Saint John the Baptist, the Palace of the Kings of Majorca, and the Campo Santo cloister. Salvador Dalí famously called Perpignan's railway station the "centre of the universe". The city hosts the Visa pour l'Image photojournalism festival and is home to the USAP rugby union and Catalan Dragons rugby league teams; rugby and Catalan festivals such as the Sant Joan bonfires remain central to local culture.
