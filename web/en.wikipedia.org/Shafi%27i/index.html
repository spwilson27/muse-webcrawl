Shafi'i

The Shafi'i school (Arabic: شافعي, Shāfiʿī) is one of the four major schools (madhhabs) of Islamic jurisprudence within Sunni Islam. It was founded by the Arab scholar Muhammad ibn Idris al-Shafi'i (767–820 CE), a student of Malik ibn Anas and a teacher of Ahmad ibn Hanbal.

Principles

Al-Shafi'i systematized the sources of law in his treatise Al-Risala: the Qur'an, the Sunnah of the Prophet Muhammad, consensus (ijma), and analogical reasoning (qiyas). He is credited with founding the discipline of usul al-fiqh (principles of jurisprudence), mediating between the traditionalist and rationalist tendencies of his time.

Distribution

The Shafi'i school predominates in Egypt, the Levant (in part), Yemen, the Swahili coast of East Africa, Kurdistan, the Caucasus, and Southeast Asia, including Indonesia, Malaysia, and Brunei — making it one of the most widely followed schools in the Muslim world.
