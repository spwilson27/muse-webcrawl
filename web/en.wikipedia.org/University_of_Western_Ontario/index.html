The University of Western Ontario (UWO), branded as Western University since 2012, is a public research university in London, Ontario, Canada. Founded in 1878 by the Anglican Diocese of Huron, it became non-denominational in 1908 and grew into one of Canada's largest universities, known for the Ivey Business School and its medical school.

University College, the landmark Gothic Revival tower of Western's campus.

History

The university was founded by Bishop Isaac Hellmuth and incorporated in 1878 as the Western University of London, Ontario, with faculties of arts and medicine (the latter dating from 1882). Early benefactors included the politician and businessman who helped secure its campus lands along the Thames River. In 1916 the King family donated the present campus site. Western expanded rapidly after the Second World War, adding engineering, law (1959), business (the Richard Ivey School of Business, founded 1922 as a department), and graduate studies. In 2012 it adopted the brand name "Western University" while retaining its legal name.

Academics

Western comprises faculties of Arts and Humanities; Science; Social Science; the Schulich School of Medicine and Dentistry; the Ivey Business School; Law; Education; Engineering; Health Sciences; Information and Media Studies; and the Don Wright Faculty of Music, plus three affiliated university colleges: Huron, King's and Brescia. The Ivey Business School is consistently ranked among Canada's top business schools and is noted for its case-method teaching. Western is a member of the U15 group of Canadian research universities.

Campus and student life

The main campus is noted for its Collegiate Gothic architecture, including University College (1923) and Middlesex College. The Western Mustangs compete in U Sports; the football team has won multiple Vanier Cups. The university's Orientation Week and homecoming are major student traditions, and the student newspaper, the Gazette, dates to 1906.

Notable alumni

Alumni include former prime minister John Turner (briefly), Nobel laureate Frederick Banting lectured at Western in the 1920s, novelist Alice Munro attended Western, and astronaut Roberta Bondar and businessman and philanthropist Richard Ivey are associated with the institution.
