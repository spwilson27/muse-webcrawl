Twelve Nidānas

The twelve nidānas are traditionally depicted around the rim of the Bhavacakra (wheel of life).

The Twelve Nidānas (Sanskrit: dvādaśa nidāna; Pali: dvādasa nidāna) are the twelve links of dependent origination (pratītyasamutpāda) in Buddhism: a chain of conditioned factors that describes how ignorance gives rise, link by link, to birth, ageing, death and suffering (dukkha), and how breaking the chain brings liberation. A nidāna is a link or cause. The twelvefold chain is presented in the Pali Canon's Nidāna Saṃyutta and in Sanskrit Abhidharma and Mahāyāna literature, and became one of the most analysed teachings in Buddhist philosophy.

Interpretation

The chain is taught in forward order (how suffering arises) and in reverse order (how it ceases: with the cessation of ignorance, formations cease, and so on to the cessation of the whole mass of suffering). One influential interpretation, systematised in the commentaries, distributes the links over three lifetimes — a past life (links 1–2), the present life (3–10) and a future life (11–12) — with craving, clinging and becoming as the karmic engine between them. Other readings treat the chain as describing processes within a single lifetime or even moment-to-moment experience. In Tibetan Buddhism the twelve links are iconographically rendered as the outer ring of the Bhavacakra, each link with a conventional image (a blind man for ignorance, a potter for formations, a monkey for consciousness, and so on).
