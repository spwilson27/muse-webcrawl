Smallholding

A smallholding combining a dwelling, gardens and a few livestock

A smallholding or smallholder farm is a small farm operating under a small-scale agriculture model. Definitions vary by country and context: in the United Kingdom a smallholding is usually a holding of a few acres — often under 50 acres (20 hectares) — while worldwide, smallholders, many of them subsistence farmers in developing countries, cultivate the majority of the world's farms, most of them under two hectares.

Characteristics

Smallholdings are typically mixed enterprises combining some livestock (poultry, pigs, sheep, goats) with vegetable gardens, orchards or cash crops, and often include a dwelling on the land. Unlike hobby farms, smallholdings are generally intended to be commercially productive or to provide a substantial share of the household's income or food, though many smallholders also work off-farm jobs.

Significance

Smallholder farmers produce a large share of the world's food — commonly estimated at around a third to 70% depending on definitions — and smallholdings play roles in rural livelihoods, food security, landscape management and the preservation of rare livestock breeds and heritage crop varieties. In Britain, county council smallholdings were created from the late 19th century to give agricultural workers and ex-servicemen access to land.
