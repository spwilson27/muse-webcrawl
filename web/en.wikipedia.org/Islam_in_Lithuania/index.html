Islam in Lithuania

Islam in Lithuania, unlike in much of Central and Western Europe, has a history spanning more than 600 years. It arrived in the 14th century with the Lipka Tatars, Turkic Muslim soldiers settled in the Grand Duchy of Lithuania by Grand Duke Vytautas in return for military service.

History

The Tatars retained their Sunni (Hanafi) faith while gradually losing their language in favour of Lithuanian, Polish, and Belarusian; they formed a respected military estate, serving in Lithuanian armies for centuries, including at the Battle of Grunwald in 1410. Tatar villages such as Raižiai, Nemėžis, and Keturiasdešimt Totorių ("Forty Tatars") preserve wooden mosques unique in the region.

Modern community

Today Muslims number only a few thousand — the 2021 census recorded about 2,700 Sunni Muslims, including the Tatar remnant and newer arrivals. The Kaunas mosque, a brick mosque built in 1930–1933, is one of the few purpose-built mosques in the Baltic states; the community is led by the Muftiate of Lithuanian Muslims, headquartered in Vilnius.
