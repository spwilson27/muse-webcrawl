Buenos Aires is the capital and largest city of Argentina, located on the western shore of the Río de la Plata estuary. Its metropolitan area, Greater Buenos Aires, is one of the largest in South America. The city is an autonomous district, formally the Autonomous City of Buenos Aires, and is the country's political, economic and cultural centre. Its residents are known as porteños.

History

The city was first founded in 1536 by Pedro de Mendoza but was abandoned after attacks by indigenous peoples; it was refounded permanently in 1580 by Juan de Garay. Long a modest colonial port dependent on trade through Lima, Buenos Aires grew in importance in the 18th century, becoming capital of the Viceroyalty of the Río de la Plata in 1776. It was a focal point of the May Revolution of 1810 that began Argentina's independence. Mass European immigration in the late 19th and early 20th centuries transformed it into a cosmopolitan metropolis.

Geography and layout

Buenos Aires stands on flat pampas terrain on the southwestern bank of the Río de la Plata, the widest river estuary in the world. The historic core centres on the Plaza de Mayo, flanked by the Casa Rosada presidential palace and the Metropolitan Cathedral. The Avenida 9 de Julio is among the widest avenues in the world, and the city is divided into 48 barrios (neighbourhoods), including La Boca, San Telmo, Recoleta and Palermo.

Culture

The city is the birthplace of tango, which emerged in its port districts in the late 19th century. Buenos Aires has a rich theatre scene centred on Avenida Corrientes, numerous bookshops, and the Teatro Colón, one of the world's great opera houses. Football is a passion, with the Superclásico rivalry between Boca Juniors and River Plate among the most famous in world sport.
