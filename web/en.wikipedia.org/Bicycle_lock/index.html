Bicycle lock

A bicycle lock is a security device used to deter bicycle theft. It is generally used to attach the bicycle to a fixed object, such as a bicycle stand, though locks may also be used simply to immobilise a wheel.

Types

The most secure common type is the U-lock (or D-lock), a rigid hardened-steel shackle in the shape of a U closed by a crossbar, popularised by the Kryptonite company in the 1970s. Chain locks use hardened links and offer flexible locking at greater weight. Cable locks are light but easily cut and are recommended only for low-risk situations. Folding locks use linked steel plates, and frame locks (ring locks) are fixed to the frame and immobilise the rear wheel.

Locking technique

Security advice generally recommends locking the frame and at least one wheel to an immovable object, minimising slack and gaps in a U-lock shackle, and using two different types of lock to force a thief to carry multiple tools. Quick-release wheels and accessories are common targets where not secured.

Theft methods

Locks can be defeated by cutting with bolt croppers or angle grinders, by picking or impressioning the cylinder, or by attacks such as freezing. Independent ratings, such as those from Sold Secure, grade locks by resistance. In 2004 it was widely reported that some tubular-key locks could be opened with a plastic pen barrel, prompting redesigns.
