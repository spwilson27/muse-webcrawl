Primary care

Primary care is the day-to-day healthcare given by a health care provider—typically the first point of contact and the main point of continuing care for patients within a healthcare system. Primary care practitioners include family physicians, general internists, paediatricians, nurse practitioners, and general practitioners, who diagnose and treat a wide range of conditions, provide preventive care and health education, and coordinate referrals to specialists.

Characteristics

The defining attributes of primary care are commonly summarized as first contact, accessibility, comprehensiveness, continuity, and coordination, a framework articulated by Barbara Starfield. Primary care emphasizes prevention—vaccination, screening, and lifestyle counselling—alongside management of chronic diseases such as diabetes, hypertension, and asthma, and the treatment of common acute illnesses and injuries.

Evidence and policy

Studies across countries associate strong primary care systems with better population health outcomes, lower costs, and reduced health inequalities. The 1978 Alma Ata Declaration identified primary health care as the key to attaining "health for all", and strengthening primary care remains a central goal of health policy worldwide, though many systems face shortages of primary care providers.
