Scottish National Antarctic Expedition

The Scottish National Antarctic Expedition (SNAE), 1902–1904, was organised and led by William Speirs Bruce, a natural scientist and former medical student from the University of Edinburgh. Unlike the contemporaneous Discovery Expedition led by Robert Falcon Scott, the SNAE was a purely scientific undertaking, funded largely by Scottish private patrons including the Coats family of Paisley.

Voyage of the Scotia

The expedition travelled in the converted Norwegian whaler Scotia, commanded by Captain Thomas Robertson. Reaching the Weddell Sea in 1903, the ship was beset by ice at Laurie Island in the South Orkney Islands, where the party established a shore station, Omond House.

Scientific work

The expedition made major contributions to oceanography, meteorology, and marine biology, including extensive deep-sea trawling and the first systematic meteorological observations in the Antarctic region. In March 1904 the Scotia reached 74°01'S in the Weddell Sea and discovered new land, which Bruce named Coats Land after the expedition's sponsors.

Omond House and Orcadas Station

In 1904 Bruce handed the Laurie Island station to the Argentine government, which has maintained it continuously ever since as Orcadas Base — the oldest continuously occupied research station in Antarctica.

Legacy

Though overshadowed in popular memory by the heroic-age expeditions to the geographic South Pole, the SNAE is now regarded as one of the most scientifically productive Antarctic expeditions of its era, and it led Bruce to found the Scottish Oceanographical Laboratory in Edinburgh.
