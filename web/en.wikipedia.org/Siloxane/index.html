Siloxane

A siloxane is a functional group in organosilicon chemistry with the Si−O−Si linkage. The parent siloxanes include the oligomeric and polymeric hydrides with the formulae H(OSiH2)nOH and (OSiH2)n. Siloxanes also include branched compounds, the defining feature of which is the Si−O−Si linkage. Siloxanes are manmade and have many commercial and industrial applications because of the compounds' hydrophobicity, low thermal conductivity, and high flexibility.

Structure and synthesis

Siloxanes generally adopt structures expected for linked tetrahedral centers; the Si−O bond length is about 1.64 Å and the Si−O−Si angle is rather open, about 144°, which accounts for the exceptional flexibility and low glass-transition temperatures of siloxane polymers. They are typically prepared by the hydrolysis of silicon chlorides—industrially, the direct process gives dimethyldichlorosilane, whose hydrolysis yields a mixture of cyclic siloxanes (such as octamethylcyclotetrasiloxane, D4) and linear polymers that can be converted into high-molecular-weight silicones by ring-opening polymerization.

Applications

Polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) is the most widely used silicon-based organic polymer, found in lubricants, sealants, caulks, medical devices, cosmetics, and food additives (as an antifoaming agent). Volatile cyclic siloxanes (D4, D5) are used as solvents and ingredients in personal care products; their environmental persistence has drawn regulatory attention in the European Union.

Nomenclature

The word siloxane is derived from the words silicon, oxygen, and alkane. In polymer science, siloxane repeat units are designated by letters—M, D, T, and Q—according to the number of oxygen atoms attached to silicon.
