Operation Flash (Croatian: Operacija Bljesak) was a brief offensive conducted by the Croatian Army (HV) in May 1995 during the Croatian War of Independence. In three days, Croatian forces retook the territory of western Slavonia held by the self-proclaimed Republic of Serbian Krajina (RSK), the first major Croatian success in restoring control over occupied territory.

Croatian forces retook the Okučani pocket in May 1995.

Background

Western Slavonia, centred on Okučani and Pakrac, had been held by Serb forces since 1991 and was designated a UN Protected Area. In early 1995 Croatia declined to extend the mandate of the UN force (UNPROFOR), and tensions rose over the Zagreb–Belgrade motorway segment running through the enclave, which reopened under the 1994 ceasefire agreement. Incidents at the motorway in late April provided the immediate trigger.

The operation

Some 7,200 Croatian troops attacked on 1 May 1995 along a 31-kilometre front. By the following day Okučani had fallen; remaining RSK forces either surrendered or withdrew across the Sava River into Bosnia by 3 May. The RSK leadership responded with rocket attacks on Zagreb that killed seven civilians and wounded more than 200 — an event prosecuted as a war crime. Much of the local Serb population fled; estimates of Serb civilian and military deaths vary, and human rights organisations reported abuses during and after the operation.

Significance

Flash restored Croatian control over the main east–west motorway, demonstrated the growing capability of the HV, and set the stage for the much larger Operation Storm three months later, which ended the war in Croatia.
