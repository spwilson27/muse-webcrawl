Progestogen

Progestogens, also sometimes spelled progestogens or gestagens, are a class of natural or synthetic steroid hormones that bind to and activate the progesterone receptor (PR). Progesterone is the major and only naturally occurring human progestogen. Synthetic progestogens used in medicine are termed progestins.

ClassSteroid hormones
Endogenous memberProgesterone
ReceptorProgesterone receptor (PR)
Synthetic formsProgestins (e.g. norethisterone, levonorgestrel, medroxyprogesterone acetate)

Biological functions

Progesterone is produced mainly by the corpus luteum of the ovary after ovulation and by the placenta during pregnancy. Progestogens prepare the endometrium for implantation of a fertilised egg, maintain pregnancy, suppress ovulation during pregnancy, and play roles in mammary gland development in preparation for lactation. They also have effects on the central nervous system, metabolism, and other tissues through progesterone receptors.

Medical uses

Synthetic progestins are widely used in hormonal contraception (alone or combined with an estrogen), in hormone replacement therapy to protect the endometrium against unopposed estrogen, in the treatment of endometriosis, abnormal uterine bleeding, and secondary amenorrhea, and in some cases for the prevention of preterm birth and as part of treatment for certain hormone-sensitive cancers.

Terminology

The terms are used slightly differently by region and source: "progestogen" is the generic class name, "progesterone" refers to the specific natural hormone, and "progestin" usually refers to synthetic compounds with progesterone-like activity.
