Millet

Common typesPearl, foxtail, proso, finger millet
FamilyPoaceae (grasses)
Major producersIndia, Niger, China

Millets are a group of highly variable small-seeded grasses, widely grown around the world as cereal crops or grains for fodder and human food. The most widely grown species is pearl millet, followed by foxtail millet, proso millet, and finger millet (ragi). They are important food staples in the semi-arid tropics of Asia and Africa, where their short growing seasons and drought tolerance suit them to harsh environments.

Millets were among the earliest domesticated crops: foxtail and proso millet were cultivated in northern China by around 7000 BC, and pearl millet in West Africa several thousand years ago. Millet grains are gluten-free, rich in fiber and minerals, and are cooked as porridge, ground into flour for flatbreads such as roti and injera-like preparations, or fermented into beverages.

Production and uses

India is the world's largest producer of millet, and the United Nations declared 2023 the International Year of Millets to promote their nutritional and climate-resilience benefits. In developed countries millets are grown mainly for birdseed, livestock feed, and specialty health foods. Teff and sorghum are sometimes loosely grouped with the millets though they are distinct grains.
