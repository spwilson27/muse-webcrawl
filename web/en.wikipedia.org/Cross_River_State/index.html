Cross River State

Cross River State is a state in the South-South geopolitical zone of Nigeria, named after the Cross River which passes through it. Its capital is Calabar, a historic port city. Bordering Cameroon to the east and the Atlantic Ocean at the Bight of Biafra to the south, the state is known for its rainforest, the Cross River National Park, and the Calabar Carnival.

History

The state was created in 1967 as the South-Eastern State and renamed Cross River in 1976; in 1987 its southwestern portion was split off to form Akwa Ibom State. Calabar was an important port in the era of the palm oil trade and earlier in the transatlantic slave trade.

Geography and economy

The state contains some of Nigeria's last remaining tropical rainforest, home to the critically endangered Cross River gorilla. Agriculture (cocoa, rubber, oil palm), fishing and tourism — including the Obudu Mountain Resort on the Obudu Plateau — support the economy. Calabar hosts a free trade zone and the annual Calabar Carnival, billed as Africa's biggest street party.
