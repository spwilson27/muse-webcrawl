The Maritimes

The Maritimes, also called the Maritime provinces, is a region of Eastern Canada consisting of the provinces of New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, and Prince Edward Island. Together with Newfoundland and Labrador they form Atlantic Canada. The Maritimes had a population of roughly 1.9 million as of the early 2020s.

Geography

The region borders the Gulf of St. Lawrence, the Atlantic Ocean, and the Bay of Fundy, famous for the highest tides in the world. Its landscape of rolling hills, river valleys, and rugged coastline was shaped by the Appalachian mountain system and glaciation. Major cities include Halifax, Moncton, Saint John, Fredericton, Charlottetown, and Sydney.

History

The Maritimes are the traditional homeland of the Mi'kmaq and other Indigenous peoples. French Acadian settlements began in the early 1600s, and the region was contested between France and Britain before coming under British control. Nova Scotia and New Brunswick were among the founding provinces of Canadian Confederation in 1867; Prince Edward Island joined in 1873. Fishing, shipbuilding, forestry, and agriculture long anchored the economy, which today also includes tourism, energy, and services.

Culture

Maritime culture is known for its Celtic and Acadian musical traditions, seafood cuisine, and a reputation for hospitality. English and French are both widely spoken, and New Brunswick is Canada's only officially bilingual province.
