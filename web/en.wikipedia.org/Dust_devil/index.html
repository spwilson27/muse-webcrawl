Dust devil

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A dust devil (also known regionally as a dirt devil) is a strong, well-formed, and relatively short-lived whirlwind. Its size ranges from small (18 in / half a metre wide and a few yards tall) to large (more than 30 ft / 10 metres wide and more than 300 ft / 100 metres tall). The primary vertical motion is upward. Dust devils are usually harmless, but on rare occasions can grow large enough to pose a threat to both people and property.

Formation

Dust devils form when a pocket of hot air near the surface rises quickly through cooler air above it, creating an updraft. If a horizontal wind shear or existing rotation is present, the rising column can begin to rotate and stretch vertically, intensifying the spin — similar to the way a figure skater spins faster by pulling in their arms. They are most common in arid regions under clear skies with light winds, around midday when surface heating peaks.

Comparison with tornadoes

Unlike tornadoes, dust devils form from the ground up under fair weather and are not connected to a parent thunderstorm cloud. Their wind speeds usually do not exceed those of a weak tornado, though the strongest recorded dust devils have caused damage comparable to an EF1 tornado.

On Mars

Dust devils are common on Mars, where they have been observed by orbiters and rovers and can grow to kilometres in height. Martian dust devils help clear dust from solar panels on rovers and play a major role in moving dust in the thin atmosphere.
