Common fibular nerve

The common fibular nerve (also called the common peroneal nerve) is a nerve of the lower limb derived from the dorsal branches of the fourth and fifth lumbar and first and second sacral nerves (L4–S2). It is the smaller terminal branch of the sciatic nerve, the larger being the tibial nerve.

Course and branches

The nerve descends along the medial border of the biceps femoris, winds around the neck of the fibula — where it is superficial and vulnerable to injury — and divides into the superficial fibular nerve, supplying the lateral compartment of the leg, and the deep fibular nerve, supplying the anterior compartment and dorsum of the foot.

Clinical significance

Damage at the fibular neck, from fractures, compression or prolonged leg-crossing, paralyses the dorsiflexors of the foot, producing foot drop and a characteristic high-stepping gait, with sensory loss over the dorsum of the foot.
