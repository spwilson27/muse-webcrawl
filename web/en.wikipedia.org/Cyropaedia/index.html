Cyropaedia

The Cyropaedia, sometimes spelled Cyropedia, is a partly fictional biography of Cyrus the Great, founder of the Achaemenid Empire, written by the Athenian historian and soldier Xenophon in the early 4th century BC. Its title means "The Education of Cyrus".

Content

In eight books, Xenophon traces Cyrus from his boyhood education at the Persian court through his conquests — treated largely as vehicles for lessons in leadership, discipline, logistics, and the management of men — to his organisation of the empire and his death. The work is less a reliable history than a didactic portrait of the ideal ruler, blending biography, political philosophy, and military manual; a romantic subplot concerning Panthea and Abradatas is among the earliest prose romances.

Influence

The Cyropaedia was a standard school text in antiquity and the Renaissance, admired by Alexander the Great, Julius Caesar, and Scipio Africanus according to tradition, and it shaped the later "mirror for princes" genre. Machiavelli and other political writers drew on its model of the virtuous, pragmatic ruler.
