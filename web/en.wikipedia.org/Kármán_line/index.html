Kármán line

The Kármán line is a conventional boundary between Earth's atmosphere and outer space, set at an altitude of 100 kilometres (62 miles) above mean sea level. It is named after the Hungarian-American engineer and physicist Theodore von Kármán, who calculated that around this altitude the atmosphere becomes too thin for conventional aeronautical flight — a vehicle would need to travel faster than orbital velocity to derive sufficient aerodynamic lift.

Use

The 100 km definition is adopted by the Fédération Aéronautique Internationale (FAI) for astronautics records; crossing it is commonly taken as the beginning of spaceflight. The United States has historically used a lower boundary of 50 miles (about 80 km) for awarding astronaut wings, and the exact legal boundary of outer space remains undefined in international law.
