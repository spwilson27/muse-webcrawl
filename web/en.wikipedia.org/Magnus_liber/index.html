Magnus liber

The Magnus liber organi ("Great Book of Organum") is a large collection of organum — early medieval polyphonic music — associated with the Notre Dame school in Paris and traditionally attributed to the composers Léonin (Leoninus) and Pérotin (Perotinus). It is a foundational repertory of Western polyphony.

Contents

The collection comprises over a hundred pieces for the liturgical year, including two-voice organa by Léonin and three- and four-voice organa and conductus attributed to Pérotin, among them the celebrated Viderunt omnes and Sederunt principes. The music survives in several manuscripts, principally the Florence and Wolfenbüttel codices.

Significance

The Magnus liber documents the emergence of rhythmic modes and measured polyphony in the 12th and early 13th centuries, practices described by the theorist known as Anonymous IV.
