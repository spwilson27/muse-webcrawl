Soft-body dynamics


A cloth simulation, a classic soft-body problem

Soft-body dynamics is a field of computer graphics that focuses on visually realistic physical simulations of the motion and properties of deformable objects (or soft bodies). In contrast to rigid body dynamics, in which objects are assumed not to change shape, soft bodies deform under applied forces, changing their geometry as they move, collide, and interact with the environment. Applications include simulating cloth, hair, flesh, rubber, plants, and other pliable materials in films, video games, and engineering visualisation.

Approaches

The most common techniques model a soft body as a system of particles connected by constraints. Mass–spring systems link point masses with virtual springs and dampers whose stiffness approximates the material. Position-based dynamics, popular in games, directly manipulates particle positions to satisfy distance and volume constraints, offering stability and speed at the cost of physical exactness. More accurate engineering approaches discretise the body with the finite element method (FEM), solving continuum mechanics equations for stress and strain, as in surgical simulation.

Cloth is usually simulated as a two-dimensional grid or triangle mesh of particles with structural, shear, and bend constraints. Volume preservation, pressure models for balloons and muscles, and coupling to fluids add further effects.

Software

Soft-body simulation is implemented in physics engines such as Bullet, NVIDIA PhysX, and Havok, in 3D packages including Blender, Houdini, and Maya (nCloth), and in specialised systems for medical simulation. Real-time constraints require trade-offs between mesh resolution, timestep, and accuracy.
