Churn rate

The churn rate, also known as the rate of attrition, is the percentage of subscribers or customers who discontinue their subscriptions within a given time period. It is a key metric in subscription businesses such as telecommunications, pay television, software as a service (SaaS), banking, and insurance.

Calculation

A basic monthly churn rate is calculated as the number of customers lost during the month divided by the number of customers at the start of the month. A business with 10,000 subscribers that loses 250 in a month has a monthly churn rate of 2.5%. Revenue churn is computed analogously using recurring revenue lost. Churn may be voluntary (customers choose to leave) or involuntary (for example, failed payments or service termination).

Significance

For a company to grow its customer base, its new-customer growth rate must exceed its churn rate. Because acquiring a new customer typically costs several times more than retaining an existing one, churn has a large effect on profitability and on customer lifetime value. Businesses analyse churn with cohort analysis, survival models, and predictive analytics to identify at-risk customers, and they combat it through customer success programmes, loyalty offers, exit surveys, and win-back campaigns.

Employee churn

The term is also used for the rate at which employees leave an organisation over a period, equivalent to staff turnover or attrition.
