Gautama Buddha (born Siddhartha Gautama), also known as Shakyamuni ("sage of the Shakyas"), was an ascetic and spiritual teacher of ancient India who founded Buddhism. According to tradition he lived in the 6th to 5th or 5th to 4th century BC. After attaining enlightenment he spent the remaining 45 years of his life teaching across the Gangetic plain.

Life

Traditional accounts relate that Siddhartha was born at Lumbini to King Śuddhodana and Queen Maya of the Shakya clan, and was sheltered in the palace at Kapilavastu from the sight of suffering. On excursions outside he encountered the "four sights" — an old man, a sick man, a corpse and an ascetic — and at 29 renounced his princely life, leaving his wife Yaśodharā and infant son Rāhula in the Great Departure. After six years of severe austerities he adopted the Middle Way between indulgence and self-mortification, and, meditating beneath the Bodhi tree at Bodh Gaya, attained enlightenment (bodhi), becoming the Buddha, "the Awakened One". His first sermon at Sarnath — setting in motion the Wheel of Dharma — taught the Four Noble Truths. He died at Kushinagar, attaining parinirvana.

Teachings

The Buddha taught that suffering (dukkha) arises from craving and ignorance, that it can cease, and that the path to cessation is the Noble Eightfold Path of right view, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness and concentration. Key doctrines include impermanence (anicca), non-self (anatta), dependent origination (pratitya-samutpada) and karma and rebirth within samsara, from which liberation is nirvana. His disciples formed the sangha, and his teachings were preserved orally before being written down in the Pali Canon and other canons.

Legacy

Buddhism spread across Asia under patrons such as the Mauryan emperor Ashoka and developed into Theravada, Mahayana and Vajrayana traditions; today it has roughly 500 million adherents worldwide. In Hinduism the Buddha is sometimes regarded as the ninth avatar of Vishnu.
