Campaign for Real Ale

The Campaign for Real Ale (CAMRA) is an independent voluntary consumer organisation headquartered in St Albans, England, which promotes real ale, real cider and real perry, and traditional British pubs and clubs. It is one of the most successful consumer organisations in Europe, with around 150,000 members.

History

CAMRA was founded in 1971 in Kruger's Bar, Dunquin, County Kerry, Ireland, by four men — Michael Hardman, Graham Lees, Jim Makin and Bill Mellor — concerned at the domination of the British brewing industry by a handful of large companies pushing processed, poorly flavoured keg beer.

Campaigns and publications

CAMRA publishes the annual Good Beer Guide, campaigns against pub closures and punitive beer duty, and organises hundreds of beer festivals, including the Great British Beer Festival. Its definition of "real ale" — beer brewed from traditional ingredients, matured by secondary fermentation in the container from which it is dispensed, and served without extraneous carbon dioxide — helped revive cask-conditioned ale in Britain.
