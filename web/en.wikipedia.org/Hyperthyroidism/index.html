Hyperthyroidism

Hyperthyroidism
Field: Endocrinology
Cause: Excess production of thyroid hormones (T3/T4)
Most common cause: Graves' disease
Opposite condition: Hypothyroidism

Hyperthyroidism is the condition that occurs due to excessive production of thyroid hormones by the thyroid gland. Thyrotoxicosis is the clinical syndrome of excess circulating thyroid hormones, whatever the source. Hyperthyroidism speeds up the body's metabolism, causing unintentional weight loss and a rapid or irregular heartbeat.

Signs and symptoms

Common features include weight loss despite normal or increased appetite, heat intolerance, sweating, tremor, palpitations, anxiety, irritability, frequent bowel movements, and menstrual irregularities. In Graves' disease, an autoimmune cause, the eyes may bulge (exophthalmos) and the thyroid is diffusely enlarged (goitre).

Causes

Graves' disease accounts for the majority of cases. Other causes include toxic multinodular goitre, toxic adenoma, thyroiditis, excessive iodine intake, and pituitary tumours secreting thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH).

Diagnosis and treatment

Blood tests typically show suppressed TSH with elevated free thyroxine (T4) and triiodothyronine (T3). Treatments include antithyroid drugs such as carbimazole or propylthiouracil, radioactive iodine ablation, and thyroid surgery; beta blockers control adrenergic symptoms. Untreated, hyperthyroidism can cause atrial fibrillation, heart failure, osteoporosis, and the rare crisis of thyroid storm. See also Thyroid and Hypothyroidism.
