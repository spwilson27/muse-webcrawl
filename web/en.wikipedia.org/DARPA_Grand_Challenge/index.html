DARPA Grand Challenge

The DARPA Grand Challenge was a series of prize competitions for autonomous vehicles sponsored by the US Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency (DARPA). Intended to spur the development of self-driving technology for military use, the challenges are credited with kick-starting the modern autonomous vehicle industry.

The 2004 challenge

The first event, held in March 2004 in the Mojave Desert, offered $1 million to any team whose vehicle could autonomously complete a roughly 240 km (150-mile) route. None of the fifteen entrants came close: the best-performing vehicle, Carnegie Mellon's Sandstorm, travelled only about 11.8 km before becoming stuck.

The 2005 challenge

In October 2005 the prize was doubled to $2 million. Twenty-three finalists attempted a 212 km desert course, and five finished. Stanford University's Volkswagen Touareg "Stanley", led by Sebastian Thrun, won with a time of just under seven hours. The result demonstrated that machine learning and better sensors had transformed the state of the art in eighteen months.

The Urban Challenge

The third event, in November 2007, moved to a mock urban environment at a former air base in California, where vehicles had to obey traffic laws and interact with other moving traffic. Carnegie Mellon's "Boss" won the $2 million first prize.

Legacy

Many challenge participants went on to lead self-driving car programmes at Google (later Waymo), Uber and major carmakers. DARPA later applied the same competition model to robotics, notably in the DARPA Robotics Challenge of 2012–2015.
