Almanac

An almanac (also spelled almanack and almanach) is a regularly published listing of a set of current information about one or multiple subjects. It includes information like weather forecasts, farmers' planting dates, sea tide tables and other tabular data often arranged according to the calendar. Celestial figures and various statistics are found in almanacs, such as the rising and setting times of the Sun and Moon, dates of eclipses, hours of high and low tides, and religious festivals.

History

Almanacs descend from Babylonian and Greek astronomical tables. The word's origin is uncertain; it appears in medieval Latin as almanachus and may derive from an Arabic term for a calendar or astronomical handbook. In early modern Europe, almanacs became among the most widely printed books after the Bible. Benjamin Franklin's Poor Richard's Almanack (1732–1758) made the genre famous in colonial America, mixing calendars and astronomical data with proverbs and practical advice.

Modern almanacs

Famous continuing examples include The Old Farmer's Almanac (published since 1792 in North America), the Farmers' Almanac, Whitaker's Almanack in the United Kingdom, and The World Almanac and Book of Facts, a general reference annual first published in 1868. Nautical almanacs remain essential official publications for celestial navigation at sea.
