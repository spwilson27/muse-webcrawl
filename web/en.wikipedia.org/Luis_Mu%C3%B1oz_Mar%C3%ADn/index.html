Luis Muñoz Marín

Luis Muñoz Marín
Governor of Puerto Rico: 1949–1965 (first elected governor)
Born: 18 February 1898, San Juan, Puerto Rico
Died: 30 April 1980, San Juan

José Luis Alberto Muñoz Marín (18 February 1898 – 30 April 1980) was a Puerto Rican journalist, politician and statesman, regarded as the architect of modern Puerto Rico. He was the first democratically elected governor of Puerto Rico, serving from 1949 to 1965, and the founder of the Popular Democratic Party (PPD).

Career

Son of Luis Muñoz Rivera, a leading advocate of Puerto Rican autonomy, Muñoz Marín began as a poet and journalist. In the 1930s he moved from supporting independence towards an autonomist programme; he founded the PPD in 1938 under the slogan "Bread, Land and Liberty", became president of the Puerto Rican Senate in 1941, and worked closely with appointed governor Rexford Tugwell on New Deal-style reforms.

Governorship

Elected governor in 1948, he oversaw Operation Bootstrap (Operación Manos a la Obra), an industrialisation programme that transformed Puerto Rico from an agricultural economy of sugar plantations into an industrial one, sharply raising living standards while prompting large-scale emigration to the mainland United States. Politically he guided the island to its current status: the Constitution of the Commonwealth (Estado Libre Asociado) of Puerto Rico was approved in 1952. His governments also suppressed the Puerto Rican independence movement, including under the 1948 "Gag Law" (Ley de la Mordaza), a record that remains controversial. He was succeeded by Roberto Sánchez Vilella in 1965.
