Marine mammal

Groups: Cetaceans, pinnipeds, sirenians, sea otters, polar bears
Species: ~130 recognised
Adaptations: Blubber, streamlined bodies, breath-holding, echolocation (odontocetes)

Marine mammals are mammals that rely on marine ecosystems for their existence. They include whales, dolphins and porpoises (cetaceans); seals, sea lions and walruses (pinnipeds); manatees and dugongs (sirenians); sea otters; and polar bears.

Evolution and adaptations

Each group evolved independently from land ancestors. Adaptations include insulating blubber or dense fur, flippers, reduced hind limbs in cetaceans and sirenians, and physiological tolerance for deep diving.

Threats

Historically hunted for oil, fur and meat, many populations were depleted; modern threats include bycatch, ship strikes, noise, plastic pollution and climate change. Protections include the US Marine Mammal Protection Act (1972) and the International Whaling Commission moratorium.
