Air safety

Air safety is the field concerned with the prevention of aviation accidents and incidents. Commercial aviation is among the safest modes of transport: the fatal-accident rate for large passenger jets has fallen steadily for decades, driven by regulation, investigation of accidents, improved aircraft design, and better training. Safety work spans aircraft certification, airline operations, air traffic control, maintenance, and airport design.

Statistics

Measured per passenger-kilometre, flying is far safer than road travel. In recent years there have been entire calendar years with no fatal accidents involving large commercial passenger jets worldwide, even as annual passenger numbers exceeded four billion. Most accidents occur during takeoff, approach, and landing.

Investigation and regulation

Independent bodies such as the U.S. National Transportation Safety Board investigate accidents and issue recommendations; regulators including the FAA and EASA certify aircraft and license operators. International standards are set by the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO). Accident investigation focuses on systemic causes rather than blame, and findings have produced innovations from ground-proximity warning systems to crew resource management.

Human factors

A large share of accidents involve human error, addressed through standard operating procedures, checklists, fatigue rules, and simulator training. Safety management systems and confidential reporting programmes encourage reporting of hazards before they cause harm.
