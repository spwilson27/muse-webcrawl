Luís Vaz de Camões (c. 1524 – 10 June 1580) is considered Portugal's greatest poet and one of the master writers of the Portuguese language. His epic poem Os Lusíadas (The Lusiads, 1572), celebrating Vasco da Gama's voyage to India, made him the national poet of Portugal; his death date, 10 June, is Portugal's national day.

Life

The details of Camões's life are shadowy and heavily embroidered by legend. He was probably born in Lisbon or Coimbra, educated at Coimbra, and served as a soldier in North Africa, where he is said to have lost his right eye at Ceuta. After a brawl in Lisbon he was pardoned on condition of serving in India; he spent some seventeen years in the East — Goa, Macau and Mozambique — surviving shipwreck off the Mekong, supposedly swimming ashore holding his manuscript above the water. He returned to Lisbon in 1570, poor, and published Os Lusíadas in 1572 with a small royal pension.

Works

Besides the epic, Camões wrote lyric poetry — sonnets, odes, elegies and songs collected posthumously as the Rimas — and several plays. His sonnet sequence is regarded as the summit of Portuguese lyric verse.
