Workplace harassment

Workplace harassment is belittling, threatening or offensive conduct directed at a worker or group of workers that creates an intimidating, hostile or humiliating working environment. It includes verbal abuse, insults, sabotage, exclusion, unwanted sexual conduct, and online abuse, and may be perpetrated by managers, colleagues, subordinates or third parties such as customers.

Forms and effects

Sexual harassment — unwelcome sexual advances, requests for sexual favours and other verbal or physical conduct of a sexual nature — is the most widely legislated form. Discriminatory harassment targets protected characteristics such as race, sex, age, disability, religion or sexual orientation. Workplace bullying, typically repeated psychological mistreatment by one or more people, overlaps with harassment but need not involve a protected characteristic. Victims commonly report anxiety, depression, sleep disturbance and physical symptoms, and organisations face reduced productivity, higher absence and turnover, and reputational damage.

Legal frameworks

In the United States, harassment based on protected characteristics violates Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and related statutes when it is severe or pervasive enough to create a hostile work environment, and employers can be liable for harassment by supervisors. The Equality Act 2010 covers harassment in the United Kingdom, and European Union directives require member states to prohibit harassment related to protected grounds. Effective responses typically combine clear policies, training, accessible reporting channels, prompt investigation and protection against retaliation. See also work–life balance.
