Opera house

An opera house is a theatre building used for performances of opera. Such a building typically contains a stage, an orchestra pit, audience seating and backstage facilities for costumes and scenery. The term is often used for large performing arts centres hosting ballet and concerts as well.

The first public opera house was the Teatro San Cassiano in Venice, opened in 1637. Early houses were intimate; 19th-century houses such as La Scala in Milan (1778), the Palais Garnier in Paris (1875) and the Vienna State Opera became grand civic monuments with horseshoe auditoriums and tiers of boxes optimised for voice and social display. Twentieth-century icons include the Sydney Opera House (1973), with its shell-like roofs designed by Jørn Utzon.

Acoustics and facilities

Design balances reverberation for singers and orchestra, sightlines, fly towers, scene docks, rehearsal rooms and workshops. Many companies are resident ensembles with their own orchestra, chorus and ballet.
