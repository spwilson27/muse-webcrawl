Barbershop Harmony Society

The Barbershop Harmony Society, legally the Society for the Preservation and Encouragement of Barber Shop Quartet Singing in America, Inc. (SPEBSQSA), is an organisation of singers dedicated to a cappella barbershop music. Founded in Tulsa, Oklahoma, in 1938 by Owen C. Cash and Rupert I. Hall, it is the largest all-male singing organisation of its kind, with tens of thousands of members in chapters across North America organised into districts.

Activities

The Society organises annual international conventions and contests for choruses and quartets, judged in categories including music, performance, and singing. Its headquarters are in Nashville, Tennessee. It publishes the magazine The Harmonizer and operates extensive youth outreach and music education programmes. In 2018 the Society announced that women would be permitted to join as full members, ending its all-male membership policy. Sister organisations include Sweet Adelines International and Harmony, Inc., which historically served women barbershop singers.

Musical style

Barbershop harmony is four-part a cappella singing — lead, tenor, baritone, and bass — characterised by close harmony, dominant seventh ("barbershop seventh") chords, and ringing overtones produced by precise tuning.
