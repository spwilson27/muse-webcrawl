Regina, Saskatchewan

Regina is the capital city of the Canadian province of Saskatchewan and its second-largest city after Saskatoon. It is a commercial and governmental centre for the southern part of the province and sits on the flat, fertile plains of the northern Great Plains, about 160 km north of the United States border.

History

The site was originally known by the Cree name Wascana ("buffalo bone pile"), from the piles of bison bones gathered there. Regina was founded in 1882 when the Canadian Pacific Railway route was fixed, and was named in honour of Queen Victoria — Regina being Latin for "queen" — by her daughter Princess Louise, wife of the governor general, the Marquis of Lorne. Regina became the capital of the North-West Territories, and in 1883 the headquarters of the North-West Mounted Police; its successor training depot of the Royal Canadian Mounted Police remains in the city, along with the RCMP Heritage Centre. Regina was the site of the trial and hanging of Louis Riel in 1885, of the Regina Cyclone of 1912, and of the Regina Riot of 1935 during the On-to-Ottawa Trek. It became the provincial capital when Saskatchewan was created in 1905.

Geography and life

The city is notable for Wascana Centre, a large urban park of about 930 hectares built around the artificial Wascana Lake, on whose shore stands the Saskatchewan Legislative Building (completed 1912). Because the treeless plain offered no natural shelter, virtually all of Regina's urban forest was hand-planted. The city's economy rests on provincial government, Crown corporations, agriculture and its services, energy, and the Regina-based Saskatchewan Roughriders of the Canadian Football League, whose passionate fan base is famous across Canada.
