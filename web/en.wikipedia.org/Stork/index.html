Stork

Family Ciconiidae
Order Ciconiiformes
Genera 6

A white stork (Ciconia ciconia)

Storks are large, long-legged, long-necked wading birds with long, stout bills. They belong to the family Ciconiidae, and make up the order Ciconiiformes. Ciconiiformes previously included a number of other families, such as herons and ibises, but those families have been moved to other orders.

Distribution and habitat

Storks occur on all continents except Antarctica, favouring wetlands, grasslands, and savannas. Many species, notably the white stork, are long-distance migrants that soar on thermals between breeding grounds in Europe and wintering grounds in Africa.

Biology

Storks are carnivorous, eating frogs, fish, insects, earthworms, and small birds and mammals. They are largely voiceless, communicating by clattering their bills. They build large stick nests in trees, on rocks, or on buildings and chimneys, which they may reuse for many years. Most species are monogamous for a season.

In folklore

Storks are symbols of good luck and fertility in many cultures; the widespread European folklore that storks deliver babies derives from Germanic and Dutch traditions.
