Southwest Airlines Co. is a major American low-cost airline headquartered in Dallas, Texas. It is the world's largest low-cost carrier and carries more domestic passengers within the United States than any other airline. Southwest is famous for pioneering the low-cost business model studied worldwide as the "Southwest Effect".

History

The airline was founded in 1967 by Herb Kelleher and Rollin King and began service in 1971, flying between Dallas, Houston and San Antonio after lengthy legal battles with incumbent carriers. Deregulation in 1978 allowed rapid expansion beyond Texas. Southwest acquired AirTran Airways in 2011 and began international services in 2014; it later added service to Hawaii in 2019.

Business model

Southwest operates a single aircraft type, the Boeing 737, to reduce training and maintenance costs, and historically relied on point-to-point routes, quick turnarounds, free checked baggage ("Bags Fly Free"), no change fees, open seating and a single-class cabin. Its fuel hedging and consistently profitable record — decades of consecutive annual profits until the COVID-19 pandemic — made it an industry outlier. In 2024 the company announced major changes, including assigned seating, extra-legroom rows and its first red-eye flights, and in 2025 it ended the free checked baggage policy for most fares.
