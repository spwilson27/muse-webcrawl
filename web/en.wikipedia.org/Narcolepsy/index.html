Narcolepsy

Narcolepsy is a chronic neurological disorder that affects the brain's ability to regulate sleep–wake cycles. People with narcolepsy experience excessive daytime sleepiness and may fall asleep suddenly at inappropriate times.

Symptoms

The classic tetrad of symptoms comprises excessive daytime sleepiness, cataplexy (sudden loss of muscle tone triggered by strong emotion), sleep paralysis, and hypnagogic hallucinations. Night-time sleep is often fragmented.

Cause

Narcolepsy type 1 is associated with a severe loss of neurons producing hypocretin (orexin), a neuropeptide that promotes wakefulness. The loss is thought to be autoimmune in origin and is strongly associated with the HLA-DQB1*06:02 allele. Onset is usually in adolescence or young adulthood.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis uses polysomnography and the multiple sleep latency test. There is no cure, but symptoms are managed with scheduled naps, stimulant medications such as modafinil, and sodium oxybate for cataplexy and disturbed night sleep.
