John Rolfe

Born: 6 May 1585, Heacham, Norfolk, England
Died: 1622 (aged 36–37), Virginia Colony
Occupation: Explorer, tobacco planter
Known for: First successful cultivation of tobacco as an export crop in Virginia; marriage to Pocahontas
Spouse: Pocahontas (Rebecca Rolfe)

John Rolfe (1585 – 1622) was one of the early English settlers of North America. He is credited with the first successful cultivation of tobacco as an export crop in the Colony of Virginia, and is known as the husband of Pocahontas, daughter of the chief of the Powhatan Confederacy.

Voyage and shipwreck

Sailing for Virginia in 1609 aboard the Sea Venture, Rolfe was shipwrecked on Bermuda, where his first wife died. The castaways built two small ships and reached Jamestown in May 1610, finding the colony in ruins after the "Starving Time".

Tobacco

The native Virginia tobacco was unpalatable to English smokers. Rolfe experimented with seeds of the sweeter West Indian variety and by 1612 had produced a marketable crop; the first shipment reached London in 1614. Tobacco exports boomed and saved the colony economically.

Marriage to Pocahontas

In April 1614 Rolfe married Pocahontas, who had been held hostage by the English and converted to Christianity, taking the name Rebecca. The marriage brought eight years of peace between the settlers and the Powhatan. The couple travelled to England in 1616, where Pocahontas was presented at court; she died at Gravesend in 1617 as they prepared to return.

Death

Rolfe served on Virginia's council and as secretary and recorder of the colony. He died in 1622, possibly in the great Powhatan attack of that year, though the cause is uncertain. His son Thomas Rolfe became a prominent Virginia planter.
