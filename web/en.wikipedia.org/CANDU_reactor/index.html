The CANDU (CANada Deuterium Uranium) is a Canadian pressurised heavy-water reactor design used for generating electric power. Developed by Atomic Energy of Canada Limited (AECL), it uses deuterium oxide (heavy water) as moderator and coolant and natural (unenriched) uranium as fuel.

Design

The reactor core, called a calandria, is a horizontal cylinder containing hundreds of pressure tubes through which fuel bundles and hot pressurised heavy-water coolant pass. Because the moderator is separate and near ambient pressure, the design permits on-power refuelling: individual channels can be refuelled without shutting down the reactor. The use of natural uranium avoids the need for enrichment facilities.

Deployment

CANDU reactors operate in Canada (notably in Ontario and New Brunswick), Argentina, China, India, Pakistan, Romania and South Korea, where derivative designs are known as PHWRs. Export models include the CANDU 6 and the enhanced EC6; the Advanced CANDU Reactor was a later, unbuilt development.
