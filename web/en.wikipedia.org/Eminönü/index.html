Eminönü (Turkish pronunciation: [eˈminœny]) is a quarter and former district in Istanbul, Turkey, lying at the confluence of the Golden Horn and the Bosphorus on the historic peninsula of the old city. Eminönü has long been one of Istanbul's principal commercial and transport hubs, a meeting point for the city's ferries, trams and pedestrians.

History

From Byzantine times, natural harbours on the Golden Horn made the Eminönü area the city's busiest port and trading quarter. After the Ottoman conquest of 1453 it remained the centre of commerce and, with the building of the Yeni Mosque (New Mosque, 1597–1663) and the Egyptian Spice Bazaar (1664), a focus of imperial piety and guild life. In the 19th century, industrialisation and new bridges — notably the Galata Bridge (first built 1845, replaced several times) — integrated the quarter into the modern metropolis, and the Sirkeci station (1890) served as the terminus of the Orient Express.

Landmarks and transport

Landmarks in and around Eminönü include the New Mosque, the Egyptian (Spice) Bazaar with its L-shaped plan of aromatic shops, the Rüstem Pasha Mosque, the Büyük Postane (Grand Post Office), the ferry piers from which boats depart for Üsküdar, Kadıköy and the Bosphorus, and the Golden Horn Metro Bridge nearby. It is a principal interchange for the T1 tram line, buses and the Marmaray commuter railway.
