Mince pie

CourseDessert
RegionUnited Kingdom
Serving temp.Warm or cold
Main ingredientsMincemeat, shortcrust pastry

A mince pie is a sweet British pie of medieval origin, traditionally served during the Christmas season. Its filling, called mincemeat, is a mixture of dried fruits (raisins, currants, sultanas), chopped apples, suet, brown sugar, spices such as cinnamon, nutmeg, and cloves, and often a splash of brandy or rum, encased in shortcrust or puff pastry.

Early mince pies were large and genuinely meaty, combining minced meat with fruit and spices brought back from the Crusades — a blend of sweet and savoury typical of medieval cookery. Over the centuries the meat content diminished until by the Victorian era the pies had become the small, meatless sweet treats known today, though suet remains a traditional ingredient. Folklore holds that eating a mince pie on each of the twelve days of Christmas brings luck.

Traditions

Mince pies are traditionally left out for Father Christmas on Christmas Eve, alongside a glass of sherry or milk. They are served warm, often with brandy butter, cream, or custard. In 1644 and during the Commonwealth period, Christmas foods including mince pies were caught up in Puritan prohibitions — giving rise to the persistent (though exaggerated) myth that mince pies were long illegal in England.
