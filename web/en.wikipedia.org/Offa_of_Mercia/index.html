Offa of Mercia

Offa (died 29 July 796) was King of Mercia, a kingdom of Anglo-Saxon England, from 757 until his death. The son of Thingfrith and a descendant of Eowa, Offa came to the throne after a period of civil war following the assassination of Æthelbald. By the end of his reign he controlled much of England south of the Humber and was overlord of kingdoms including Kent, Sussex, and East Anglia.

A penny of Offa, whose coinage reforms standardised English silver currency.

Reign

Offa defeated the Welsh at Hereford in 760 and campaigned repeatedly along the Welsh border. The great linear earthwork known as Offa's Dyke, running along the frontier between Mercia and the Welsh kingdoms, is traditionally attributed to him. He corresponded with Charlemagne as a near-equal, quarrelled and reconciled with the papacy, and secured the elevation of Lichfield to an archbishopric in 787.

Coinage and legacy

Offa's reformed silver pennies, struck in large numbers with fine portrait and non-portrait types, set the standard for English coinage for centuries. His army, like those of earlier kings such as Octavian's Roman predecessors in Britain, depended on a military household led by an experienced officer class of thegns.
