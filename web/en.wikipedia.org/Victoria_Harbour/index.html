Victoria Harbour is a natural landform harbour in Hong Kong separating Hong Kong Island in the south from the Kowloon Peninsula to the north. Its deep, sheltered waters were a principal reason for the founding of Hong Kong as a British colony in 1841, and the harbour has since been central to the territory's development as one of the world's busiest ports.

Geography

The harbour stretches from the Lei Yue Mun strait in the east to waters west of Hong Kong Island. Successive land reclamation projects on both shores have greatly narrowed it: reclamation since the late 19th century has expanded Hong Kong's urban land but reduced the harbour's area from about 60 km² historically. The filling of the harbour has been controversial, and since 1997 reclamation has been restricted under the Protection of the Harbour Ordinance.

Transport

The harbour is crossed by no bridge, owing to height requirements for shipping, but three road tunnels — the Cross-Harbour Tunnel (1972), the Eastern Harbour Crossing (1989) and the Western Harbour Crossing (1997) — link the island with Kowloon, as do several MTR railway tunnels. The Star Ferry, operating since the late 19th century, carries passengers between Central, Wan Chai and Tsim Sha Tsui and remains an iconic service alongside other ferry routes and harbour cruises.

Tourism and events

The harbourfront is one of Hong Kong's defining sights. The nightly "A Symphony of Lights" show illuminates the skylines on both sides, and annual events include fireworks displays over the harbour for Lunar New Year and National Day, the New World Harbour Race swimming event, and dragon boat races. The Avenue of Stars and the Tsim Sha Tsui promenade overlook the water from Kowloon, while the Central harbourfront and Hong Kong Observation Wheel face it from the island side.
