London, Ontario

London is a city in southwestern Ontario, Canada, along the Quebec City–Windsor Corridor. The city had a population of about 422,000 in the 2021 census, with a metropolitan population of roughly 543,000, making it the sixth-largest city in Ontario.

History

The site at the forks of the Thames River was selected in 1793 by Lieutenant-Governor John Graves Simcoe as a potential capital of Upper Canada, and named for London, England. The village was founded in 1826 and incorporated as a city in 1855. It grew as a regional centre for agriculture, manufacturing and administration in Middlesex County.

Geography and economy

London lies between Lake Huron and Lake Erie, roughly midway between Toronto and Detroit. Its economy is based on education, health care, insurance, information technology and manufacturing. It is home to Western University, Fanshawe College, and major teaching hospitals including Victoria Hospital and University Hospital, earning it a reputation as a centre for medical research.

Culture

Nicknamed the "Forest City", London has extensive parks and tree-lined streets. Attractions include the Museum London, Budweiser Gardens, Eldon House, Victoria Park festivals, and the London Knights hockey team. Notable Londoners include actor Ryan Gosling and Nobel laureate Frederick Banting, whose house is a national historic site.
