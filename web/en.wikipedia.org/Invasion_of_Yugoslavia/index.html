Invasion of Yugoslavia

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The invasion of Yugoslavia, also known as the April War or Operation 25, was the Axis attack on the Kingdom of Yugoslavia during the Second World War. It began on 6 April 1941 with a massive bombing of Belgrade and ended with the unconditional surrender of the Royal Yugoslav Army on 17 April 1941. Germany, Italy, Hungary and Bulgaria participated, and Yugoslavia was partitioned among the victors; the occupation that followed was met by one of Europe's largest resistance movements.

Background

Yugoslavia had sought neutrality, but on 25 March 1941 the government of Prince Paul signed the Tripartite Pact with the Axis. Two days later a military coup d'état deposed the regency and repudiated the pact, delighting public opinion but enraging Hitler, who ordered Yugoslavia's destruction as a state. The invasion was coordinated with the simultaneous German invasion of Greece (Operation Marita).

The campaign

The Luftwaffe opened the attack with Operation Retribution, the bombing of Belgrade, killing thousands of civilians. German armies struck from Bulgaria, Romania, Hungary and Austria, while Italian forces advanced from Albania and the Julian March and Hungarian troops entered the Bácska. The Yugoslav high command's plan to defend the whole frontier collapsed; mobilisation was incomplete, equipment obsolete and ethnic tensions within the army severe. Zagreb fell on 10 April, where the Independent State of Croatia was proclaimed; Belgrade was occupied on 13 April, and an armistice was signed on 17 April effective the next day. The king and government fled into exile; German casualties were remarkably light, fewer than 200 dead.

Occupation and aftermath

Yugoslavia was dismembered: Germany, Italy, Hungary and Bulgaria annexed border territories; Serbia came under German military administration, and Croatia became an Axis client state under the Ustaše. Occupation and civil war followed, with the Partisan movement under Josip Broz Tito and the royalist Chetniks fighting the occupiers and each other, until the Partisans liberated the country in 1944–45 and founded socialist Yugoslavia.
