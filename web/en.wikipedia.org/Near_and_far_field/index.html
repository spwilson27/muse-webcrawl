Near and far field

The near field and far field are regions of the electromagnetic field around an object, such as a transmitting antenna. The near field lies close to the source, where the electric and magnetic fields do not behave as simple plane waves and may store energy rather than radiate it. The far field lies at greater distances, where radiation dominates and field strength falls with distance in a predictable pattern.

Regions

The near field is often divided into the reactive near field, immediately around the antenna, and the radiating near field (Fresnel region). The far field (Fraunhofer region) begins at a distance of roughly 2D²/λ, where D is the largest dimension of the antenna and λ is the wavelength.

Applications

The distinction matters in antenna measurements, wireless power transfer, near-field communication (NFC) and RFID, which deliberately exploit near-field inductive coupling.
