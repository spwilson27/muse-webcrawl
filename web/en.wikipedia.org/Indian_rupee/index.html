The Indian rupee (symbol: ₹; code: INR) is the official currency of India. It is issued and controlled by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI). The rupee is subdivided into 100 paise, though paisa coins are now rarely used. The name derives from the Sanskrit rūpya ("wrought silver") and from the rupiya introduced by Sher Shah Suri in the 16th century.

History

India was among the earliest issuers of coins, with punch-marked coins circulating by the 6th century BCE. The Mughal Empire and later the British East India Company standardized silver rupees; the Coinage Act of 1835 made the silver rupee the uniform currency of British India. After independence in 1947, India retained the rupee, and in 1957 it was decimalized, replacing the older system of annas and pies. The currency was pegged to sterling until 1975 and subsequently moved toward a managed float.

Symbol

The distinctive ₹ symbol, designed by Udaya Kumar Dharmalingam, was adopted by the Government of India on 15 July 2010. It combines the Devanagari letter र (ra) with two parallel horizontal lines representing the national flag and an equals sign.

Demonetisation of 2016

On 8 November 2016, the government announced the demonetisation of all ₹500 and ₹1,000 banknotes of the Mahatma Gandhi Series, stating aims of curbing black money, counterfeiting, and corruption. New ₹500 and ₹2,000 notes were introduced; the ₹2,000 note was withdrawn from circulation in 2023.

Exchange rate

The rupee is a freely floating currency subject to RBI intervention to curb excessive volatility. It traded in the range of roughly 60–85 per US dollar during the 2010s and early 2020s, gradually depreciating over time.
