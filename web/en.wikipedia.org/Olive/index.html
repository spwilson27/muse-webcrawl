The olive (Olea europaea) is a small evergreen tree in the family Oleaceae, native to the Mediterranean basin. Its fruit, also called the olive, is of major agricultural importance as the source of olive oil and is eaten whole as a table olive. The olive has been cultivated since ancient times; fossil evidence and archaeological finds indicate olives were grown in the eastern Mediterranean by the Neolithic, and domestication was well established by the Bronze Age.

Description

The olive tree is evergreen, typically 8–15 metres tall, with a gnarled, often twisted trunk and silvery-green lance-shaped leaves, dark green above and pale silvery beneath. Small white fragrant flowers appear in spring, and the fruit is a small drupe starting green and ripening blackish-purple. Wild olives (oleasters) produce smaller fruit than cultivated forms.

Cultivation and uses

Olives are propagated mainly by cuttings and grafting, preserving the characteristics of named cultivars such as Arbequina, Frantoio, Koroneiki, and Kalamata. Fresh olives are bitter because of the compound oleuropein and must be cured — in brine, water, lye, or oil — before eating. Most of the harvest is pressed for oil: olive oil is a cornerstone of Mediterranean cuisine and is graded, with extra virgin the highest quality. Spain is the world's largest producer, followed by Italy and Greece.

History and symbolism

The olive was foundational to ancient Greek and Roman civilization; Athena's gift of the olive tree won her patronage of Athens in myth, and Olympic victors were crowned with olive wreaths. The olive branch became a widespread symbol of peace — a dove brought Noah an olive leaf in Genesis — and appears on the flag of the United Nations. Ancient olive trees, some claimed to be more than 2,000 years old, still grow around the Mediterranean; the trees regenerate from their rootstock even when the trunk dies back.

Pests and diseases

Major threats include the olive fruit fly and the bacterial disease caused by Xylella fastidiosa, which has devastated groves in Italy's Apulia region since 2013.
