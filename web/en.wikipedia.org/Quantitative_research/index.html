Quantitative research

Quantitative research is a research strategy that focuses on quantifying the collection and analysis of data. It is formed from a deductive approach where emphasis is placed on the testing of theory, shaped by empiricist and positivist philosophies. Quantitative research is generally contrasted with qualitative research, which examines phenomena through non-numerical data such as interviews and texts.

Overview

Quantitative methods emphasise objective measurements and the statistical, mathematical, or numerical analysis of data collected through polls, questionnaires, and surveys, or by manipulating pre-existing statistical data using computational techniques. A central aim is to establish generalisable facts about a phenomenon by testing hypotheses derived from theory, measuring variables on representative samples, and analysing numerical results with procedures such as correlation, regression and analysis of variance.

Methods

  • Surveys and questionnaires with structured, closed-ended questions
  • Experiments and quasi-experiments with controlled manipulation of variables
  • Observational and longitudinal studies using systematic measurement
  • Secondary data analysis of census, administrative or archival datasets

Measurement and statistics

Quantitative research relies on operationalising concepts into measurable variables at nominal, ordinal, interval or ratio levels, and on assessing the reliability and validity of instruments. Statistical inference — including significance testing and confidence intervals — is used to generalise from samples to populations, though over-reliance on p-values and publication bias are recurring criticisms, often addressed through mixed-methods designs combining quantitative and qualitative strands.
