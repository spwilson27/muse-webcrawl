The App Store is a digital distribution platform operated by Apple Inc. for applications ("apps") for its operating systems. Launched on 10 July 2008 with the release of iPhone OS 2 and the iPhone 3G, it was the first major app storefront for a smartphone platform and established the model — centralised review, one-tap installation, and a revenue share with developers — that came to dominate mobile software distribution. Apps are sold, or offered free with in-app purchases or subscriptions, and Apple historically retained a 30 percent commission on paid transactions.

History

The App Store opened with about 500 applications and recorded 10 million downloads in its first weekend. Apple announced 100,000 apps by late 2009, one million by 2013, and two million in 2016; the catalogue later stabilised at around 1.8 million as Apple removed outdated and low-quality titles. The store popularised the phrase "there's an app for that" (later trademarked by Apple), and milestones include the 10 billionth download in 2011 and 50 billionth in 2013. In 2017 Apple redesigned the store around editorial curation and a "Today" tab; Apple Arcade, a game subscription service, followed in 2019.

Developer model and review

Developers join the Apple Developer Program for an annual fee, submit apps through App Store Connect, and must pass App Review against Apple's guidelines covering content, security, and use of private APIs. The standard commission is 30 percent, reduced to 15 percent for subscriptions after the first year and, from 2021, for developers earning under $1 million annually under the App Store Small Business Program. Apple reports paying out hundreds of billions of dollars to developers cumulatively.

Controversies and regulation

The store's control over distribution and payments has drawn sustained criticism and legal challenge. In Epic Games v. Apple (2021), a US court found that Apple's anti-steering rules violated California law, though it upheld most of the model. In the European Union, the Digital Markets Act obliged Apple from 2024 to permit alternative app marketplaces and payment options on iOS in the EU. Regulators in South Korea, the Netherlands, Japan, and elsewhere have imposed similar requirements, and disputes over commissions and review decisions remain central to the global debate over platform power.
