University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign

Type: Public land-grant research university
Established: 1867
Location: Champaign–Urbana, Illinois, U.S.
Students: ~56,000

The University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign (UIUC) is a public land-grant research university in the twin cities of Champaign and Urbana, Illinois. Founded in 1867 as Illinois Industrial University, it is the flagship campus of the University of Illinois system and is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity."

Research and innovation

UIUC's Grainger College of Engineering is among the world's top engineering schools. The campus was home to the PLATO computer-based education system, the ILLIAC computers, the National Center for Supercomputing Applications (where the Mosaic web browser was created by Marc Andreessen and Eric Bina), and the transistor-era physics of John Bardeen, the only two-time Nobel laureate in Physics. The Morrill plots are the oldest continuously used experimental cornfields in the United States.

Campus life

The university has one of the largest Greek systems in the US and one of the biggest public university libraries. Its teams, the Fighting Illini, compete in the Big Ten Conference.
