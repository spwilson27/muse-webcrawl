The Giving Pledge is a campaign to encourage extremely wealthy people to contribute a majority of their wealth to philanthropic causes. It was announced in August 2010 by Bill Gates, Melinda French Gates, and Warren Buffett. Signatories make a public, morally binding — but not legally enforceable — commitment, usually accompanied by a personal letter explaining their reasons for giving.

History

The pledge grew out of dinners and conversations hosted by the founders with fellow billionaires beginning in 2009. Forty American billionaires signed at the public launch. The campaign went international in 2013, with signatories from countries including India, Russia, South Africa, Ukraine, and the United Kingdom, and has continued to add members annually.

Signatories and practice

Signatories have included Mark Zuckerberg and Priscilla Chan, Elon Musk, MacKenzie Scott, Richard Branson, Michael Bloomberg, and Larry Ellison. The pledge sets no rules about which causes to support or when gifts must be made; signatories may give during their lifetimes or through their estates. Gatherings of pledgers are held to discuss effective philanthropy.

Criticism

Critics have observed that the pledge is unenforceable, that some signatories' fortunes have grown faster than their giving, and that large-scale billionaire philanthropy can exert outsized, undemocratic influence over public priorities. Supporters respond that the campaign has shifted social norms around great wealth and prompted billions of dollars in additional charitable commitments.
