J'Accuse...!

"J'Accuse...!" ("I Accuse...!") is an open letter by the French novelist Émile Zola, published on the front page of the newspaper L'Aurore on 13 January 1898. Addressed to President Félix Faure, it accused the French government and army of antisemitism and of wrongfully convicting the artillery officer Alfred Dreyfus of treason, and it became the pivotal intervention of the Dreyfus affair.

Background

Captain Alfred Dreyfus, a Jewish officer, had been convicted in 1894 of passing secrets to Germany on the basis of a torn-up memorandum (the bordereau) and dubious handwriting evidence, and was imprisoned on Devil's Island. Evidence emerged that the real culprit was Major Ferdinand Walsin Esterhazy, but a military court acquitted Esterhazy in January 1898 and the army closed ranks.

The letter

Zola's letter, its headline coined by editor Georges Clemenceau, named the officers and officials responsible for the miscarriage of justice, repeating the anaphora "J'accuse" against each: the war ministry, the general staff, the handwriting experts, and the courts-martial. It argued the affair rested on antisemitic prejudice and a forged dossier.

Aftermath

Zola was prosecuted for libel, convicted, and fled to England for a year. The affair split France between Dreyfusards and anti-Dreyfusards. Dreyfus was retried in 1899, pardoned, and finally fully exonerated in 1906. "J'accuse" has since become a generic expression for a forceful public denunciation of injustice.
