"The Eraser" most famously refers to the subject of Robert Rauschenberg's 1953 conceptual work Erased de Kooning Drawing, in which Rauschenberg obtained a drawing from the artist Willem de Kooning and spent weeks erasing it almost entirely, presenting the faint remainder in a gold frame with a label by Jasper Johns.

Background

Rauschenberg, then a young artist working on monochrome White Paintings, wanted a drawing that de Kooning would miss — and that was difficult to erase. De Kooning reluctantly selected a heavily worked drawing in ink and crayon. The gesture, both homage and challenge to Abstract Expressionism, became a landmark of conceptual art and a forerunner of discussions about authorship and negation in art. The work is held by the San Francisco Museum of Modern Art, where modern imaging has revealed ghostly traces of the original drawing beneath the surface.
