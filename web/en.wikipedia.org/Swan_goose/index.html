Swan goose

The swan goose (Anser cygnoid) breeds in inland Asia.

The swan goose (Anser cygnoid) is a large goose in the waterfowl family Anatidae. It breeds in inland Mongolia, northernmost China, and the Russian Far East and is migratory, wintering mainly in central and eastern China. It is vagrant in Japan and Korea. It is classified as Vulnerable on the IUCN Red List because of habitat loss and hunting.

Description

The swan goose is large and long-necked for its genus, weighing about 2.8–3.5 kg (6.2–7.7 lb). The sexes are similar, though the male is larger and has a proportionally longer bill and neck. The plumage is greyish-brown with a dark stripe running down the back of the neck, and a chestnut-brown cap; the bill is long, mostly black, and in males bears a prominent basal knob.

Domestic form

The domestic Chinese goose (sometimes called Anser cygnoides domesticus) was domesticated from the swan goose in China more than 3,000 years ago. Domestic birds are heavily built, with an enlarged bill knob, an upright posture, and often a fatty dewlap, and are kept for meat, eggs, guarding, and weed control.
