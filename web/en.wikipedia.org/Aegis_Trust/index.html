Aegis Trust

The Aegis Trust is a British non-governmental organisation dedicated to preventing genocide and mass atrocities worldwide. It was founded in 2000 by brothers James and Stephen Smith, who had earlier established the Beth Shalom Holocaust Centre (now the National Holocaust Centre and Museum) in Nottinghamshire in 1995.

Work

Aegis campaigns for the prevention of crimes against humanity, advocates for the Responsibility to Protect doctrine, and runs education and peacebuilding programmes. In Rwanda it built and operates the Kigali Genocide Memorial, opened in 2004 as the final resting place of over 250,000 victims of the 1994 genocide against the Tutsi, and has developed peace education curricula used in Rwandan schools. Aegis also supports the preservation of survivor testimony and documentation, including the Genocide Archive of Rwanda.

Recognition

The organisation's founders and staff have been recognised for their contributions to Holocaust and genocide education. Aegis works with governments, the African Union, and civil-society partners on early-warning and atrocity-prevention initiatives.
