Milford Sound (Māori: Piopiotahi) is a fiord in the south-west of New Zealand's South Island, within Fiordland National Park and the Te Wahipounamu World Heritage Site. Despite its name it is a fiord, carved by glaciers, not a sound. It is New Zealand's most famous tourist destination, praised by Rudyard Kipling as the "eighth Wonder of the World."

Geography

The fiord runs 15 km inland from the Tasman Sea. Its most photographed feature is Mitre Peak, rising 1,690 m almost sheer from the water. Heavy rainfall — among the highest in the world at roughly 6,400 mm per year — creates countless temporary waterfalls cascading down the cliffs, and a permanent dark freshwater layer staining the surface of the fiord.

History and access

Māori visited the area to gather pounamu (greenstone). Welsh sealer John Grono named it Milford Sound after Milford Haven in Wales in the early 19th century. Access was transformed by the Milford Track (opened 1889) and by the Homer Tunnel road, completed in 1953. Today boat cruises, kayaking and the small settlement at the head of the fiord serve close to a million visitors in peak years.

Wildlife

The fiord hosts bottlenose dolphins, fur seals, Fiordland crested penguins and black coral trees growing in its dark surface layer.
