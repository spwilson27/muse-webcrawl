Cadence

In Western musical theory, a cadence is a melodic or harmonic configuration that creates a sense of resolution or finality, marking the end of a phrase, section or piece of music. Cadences are the punctuation marks of tonal music.

Types

The perfect authentic cadence (V–I in major keys) provides the strongest closure. A half cadence ends on the dominant, leaving the phrase open. The plagal cadence (IV–I) is known as the "Amen" cadence from its use in hymns. A deceptive cadence substitutes an unexpected chord, typically vi, for the expected tonic, prolonging the music, while the imperfect and interrupted cadences describe weaker or evaded forms of closure.

Role

Cadential patterns define formal structure in music of the common practice period, articulating phrases and confirming key centres. Composers often delay or evade cadences to build tension, and expanded cadential progressions mark the climaxes of sonata-form movements.
