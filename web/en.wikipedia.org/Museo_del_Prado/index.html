Museo del Prado


The Villanueva building, the Prado's main seat on the Paseo del Prado.

The Museo del Prado, officially the Museo Nacional del Prado, is the main Spanish national art museum, located in central Madrid on the Paseo del Prado. Widely considered to hold one of the world's finest collections of European art, dating from the 12th century to the early 20th century, it was founded as a museum of paintings and sculpture in 1819. Together with the nearby Thyssen-Bornemisza Museum and the Museo Reina Sofía, it forms Madrid's "Golden Triangle of Art."

History

The core building was designed in 1785 by architect Juan de Villanueva on the orders of Charles III, originally to house a natural history cabinet. Ferdinand VII, encouraged by his wife Isabel of Braganza, repurposed it for a royal museum of paintings, and it opened in November 1819 with some 311 paintings from the Spanish royal collection. The collection grew through the incorporation of works from suppressed monasteries, state purchases, and bequests; in 1868 the museum was nationalised. A major expansion designed by Rafael Moneo, incorporating the cloister of the Jerónimos church, opened in 2007.

Collection

The Prado holds around 8,200 drawings, 7,600 paintings, 4,800 prints, and about 1,000 sculptures, of which roughly 1,300 works are on display at any time. Its strengths are the Spanish Golden Age and the court painters of the Habsburg and Bourbon monarchies: Diego Velázquez — whose Las Meninas is the museum's most famous painting — Francisco de Goya, including the harrowing Black Paintings and The Third of May 1808, El Greco, Zurbarán, Murillo, and Ribera. International holdings include Hieronymus Bosch's The Garden of Earthly Delights, Rogier van der Weyden's The Descent from the Cross, major works by Titian, Rubens, Raphael, Dürer, and Rembrandt.

The museum today

The Prado is among the most visited art museums in the world, receiving around three million visitors in a typical pre-pandemic year. In 2019 it celebrated its bicentenary. Its research library, restoration workshops, and the Casón del Buen Retiro study centre make it a leading institution for the study of Spanish painting.
