Prefix

A prefix is an affix which is placed before the stem of a word. Particularly in the study of languages, a prefix is also called a preformative, because it alters the form of the word to which it is affixed. Prefixes, like other affixes, can be either inflectional, creating a new form of the word with the same basic meaning, or derivational, creating a new word with a new semantic meaning.

English prefixes

English has many derivational prefixes, largely inherited from Germanic or borrowed from Latin and Greek. Examples include un- (unhappy), re- (rewrite), pre- (preview), dis- (disagree), anti- (antibiotic), sub- (submarine), hyper- (hyperactive) and counter- (counterattack). There is no general rule for hyphenation: usage varies and often depends on whether the result is an established word, whether vowels collide, or whether the stem is a proper noun (as in anti-American).

In other languages

Prefixes are widespread. German uses separable prefixes such as auf- in aufstehen; Bantu languages organise nouns into classes by prefixes; Hebrew, Arabic and other Semitic languages use prefixes in verb conjugation; Japanese and Korean employ honorific prefixes. In Russian and other Slavic languages, verbal prefixes systematically mark aspect.

Other senses

By extension, "prefix" denotes anything placed at the beginning of a sequence: SI prefixes (kilo-, mega-, giga-), telephone dialling prefixes and country codes, negative-number prefixes, and prefixes in computing such as file paths, IP network prefixes and binary prefixes (kibi-, mebi-).
