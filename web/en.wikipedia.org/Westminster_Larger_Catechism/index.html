Westminster Larger Catechism

The Westminster Larger Catechism, completed in 1648

The Westminster Larger Catechism is a catechism — a summary of Christian doctrine in question-and-answer form — produced by the Westminster Assembly of Divines during the English Civil War. Together with the Westminster Shorter Catechism, it remains a doctrinal standard of Presbyterian and Reformed churches throughout the world.

The Assembly, convened by the Long Parliament in 1643 to reform the Church of England, was instructed to prepare two catechisms. The Larger Catechism, intended primarily for the pulpit and for instructing adults, was completed on 14 April 1648 and approved by the General Assembly of the Church of Scotland in July 1648. It contains 196 questions and answers.

Content

The first question and answer set the pattern of the catechism:

Q. 1. What is the chief and highest end of man?
A. Man's chief and highest end is to glorify God, and fully to enjoy him forever.

The catechism proceeds through the doctrine of God and his decrees, creation and providence, the fall of mankind, the covenant of grace, the person and offices of Christ, the application of redemption, the benefits of Christ's mediation, and an extensive exposition of the Ten Commandments (questions 91–148), followed by teaching on the means of grace: the Word, the sacraments, and prayer, concluding with an exposition of the Lord's Prayer.

Use

The Larger Catechism was designed to be expounded from the pulpit, while the Shorter Catechism — compiled "for such as are of weaker capacity" — became the version memorised by children and new converts. Both are subordinate standards of the Church of Scotland and of Presbyterian churches worldwide, subordinate only to Scripture, and together with the Westminster Confession of Faith form the Westminster Standards.
