The Confederation of African Football (CAF) is the administrative and controlling body for association football, beach soccer, and futsal in Africa. Founded in 1957 in Khartoum by the national associations of Egypt, Sudan, Ethiopia, and South Africa, CAF is one of FIFA's six continental confederations and, with 54 member associations, its largest. Its headquarters are in 6th of October City, near Cairo, Egypt.

Competitions

CAF organises the Africa Cup of Nations (AFCON), first held in 1957 and its flagship tournament, as well as the African Nations Championship for home-based players, the Women's Africa Cup of Nations, youth and futsal championships, and the African qualifiers for the FIFA World Cup. At club level it runs the CAF Champions League, the CAF Confederation Cup, and the CAF Super Cup. The confederation also presents annual awards, including African Footballer of the Year.

History

CAF grew with African decolonisation, and its members played a key role in expanding World Cup places for Africa and in the boycott movements over apartheid South Africa, which was expelled and later readmitted after the end of apartheid. Long-serving presidents include Abdel Halim Muhammad, Ydnekatchew Tessema, and Issa Hayatou, who led CAF from 1988 to 2017. Ahmad Ahmad succeeded Hayatou before being banned by FIFA; South African businessman Patrice Motsepe became president in 2021.

Member associations

CAF's members are organised into five regional zones—North, West, Central, Central-East, and Southern Africa—several of which have their own regional federations and tournaments.
