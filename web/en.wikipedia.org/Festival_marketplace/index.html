Festival marketplace

A festival marketplace is a shopping and leisure development concept, popularised in the United States from the 1970s by developer James W. Rouse and his company, which transformed historic or waterfront districts into centres of shops, restaurants and entertainment aimed at tourists and suburban visitors.

Notable examples include Faneuil Hall Marketplace in Boston (opened 1976), Harborplace in Baltimore (1980), South Street Seaport in New York, and Union Station in Washington, D.C. The concept influenced urban regeneration worldwide, though many festival marketplaces later struggled as retail patterns changed, and some were converted to other uses or redeveloped into more conventional mixed-use schemes.
