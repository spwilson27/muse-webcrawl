Wakayama Prefecture

Wakayama Prefecture (和歌山県)
Country: Japan
Region: Kansai
Island: Honshu
Capital: Wakayama City
Area: approx. 4,725 km²
Population: approx. 920,000 (2020s)

Wakayama Prefecture (Japanese: 和歌山県, Wakayama-ken) is a prefecture of Japan occupying the southwestern part of the Kii Peninsula in the Kansai region of Honshu. Its capital is the city of Wakayama. Mountainous and forested, the prefecture is known for its hot springs, mandarin oranges, and some of Japan's most sacred religious sites.

Geography

Wakayama borders Osaka, Nara, and Mie prefectures and faces the Pacific Ocean and the Kii Channel. Most of the population lives along the northern coast; the interior is dominated by the Kii Mountains, deep river valleys, and waterfalls, including Nachi Falls, one of Japan's tallest. The climate is mild, favouring the cultivation of mikan (satsuma mandarins), persimmons, and ume plums — the prefecture is Japan's leading producer of umeboshi.

History and culture

The region corresponds largely to the old Kii Province. In the Edo period it was ruled by the Kishū branch of the Tokugawa clan from Wakayama Castle; the eighth shogun, Tokugawa Yoshimune, came from this domain. Wakayama is the spiritual heartland of the Kumano faith: the Kumano Kodō pilgrimage routes and the shrines of Kumano Hongū, Hayatama, and Nachi, together with the Shingon Buddhist mountain complex of Mount Kōya (Kōyasan), founded by Kūkai in 816, form the UNESCO World Heritage Site "Sacred Sites and Pilgrimage Routes in the Kii Mountain Range" (inscribed 2004).

Other attractions

The coastal resort of Shirahama offers white-sand beaches and open-air hot springs, while Taiji and Nachikatsuura are historic whaling and tuna ports. Kannonyama and other sites preserve the prefecture's agricultural heritage.
