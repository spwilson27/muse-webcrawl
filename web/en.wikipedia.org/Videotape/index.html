Videotape

Videotape is magnetic tape used for storing video and usually sound in addition. Information stored can be in the form of either an analog or digital signal. Videotape is used in both video tape recorders (VTRs) and, more commonly, videocassette recorders (VCRs) and camcorders.

Practical videotape recording began with Ampex's Quadruplex system in 1956, which used two-inch-wide tape and revolutionized television production by allowing programs to be recorded, edited, and rebroadcast. Later professional formats included 1-inch Type C, Betacam, and digital formats such as D1 and DV. Consumer formats were dominated by the format war between Betamax and VHS, won by VHS in the 1980s; cassettes then enabled the home-video rental industry.

Decline

From the late 1990s, videotape was displaced by optical discs (DVD and Blu-ray), hard-disk and flash recording, and eventually streaming media. The last major manufacturer of VCRs ceased production in 2016. Magnetic tape survives in data-storage applications (such as LTO) and in archives, though aging videotapes pose a significant preservation challenge because of binder degradation and the scarcity of working playback equipment.
