Beverage can

A beverage can is a metal container designed to hold a fixed portion of liquid such as soft drinks, beer, juice or energy drinks. Most modern beverage cans are made of aluminium, drawn and ironed into a seamless body with a separate top; some are made of tin-plated steel. The standard can holds 330 ml in much of the world and 355 ml (12 US fl oz) in North America.

History

Canned beer was first sold in 1935 by the Krueger's brewery in the United States. Early cans required a separate opener ("church key") to pierce the top. The pull-tab, invented by Ermal Fraze in 1959, was widely adopted in the 1960s but created litter and wildlife hazards; it was superseded from the 1970s by the stay-on tab, designed by Daniel Cudzik, which remains standard. Aluminium cans were introduced by Coors in 1959 and promoted recycling with deposit schemes.

Manufacture and recycling

Can bodies are formed by drawing and wall-ironing a metal disc, then printed, filled and seamed to the lid. An internal lacquer coating prevents contact between the drink and the metal. Aluminium cans are among the most recycled packaging formats in the world; recycling a can uses about 5% of the energy of producing primary aluminium.
