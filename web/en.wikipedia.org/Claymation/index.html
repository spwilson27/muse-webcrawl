Claymation

Claymation
Type: Stop-motion animation
Material: Plasticine and similar modelling compounds
Trademark: Registered term of Will Vinton Studios
Notable examples: California Raisins, Gumby (clay animation), Wallace and Gromit (clay animation)

Claymation is a form of stop-motion animation in which characters and sets are sculpted from a malleable material such as Plasticine and photographed frame by frame, with the models slightly repositioned between exposures to create the illusion of movement. The word is a portmanteau of "clay" and "animation" and is a registered trademark of Will Vinton Studios (later Laika) in the United States; the technique itself is generically called clay animation.

History

Clay animation dates to the early 20th century, with works such as The Sculptor's Welsh Rarebit Dream (1908) and the films of Helena Smith Dayton. Art Clokey popularized it in the 1950s with Gumbasia and the television series Gumby. Will Vinton and Bob Gardiner won an Academy Award in 1974 for Closed Mondays, and Vinton's studio later produced the California Raisins commercials of the 1980s. In Britain, Aardman Animations became famous for its Plasticine characters: Morph (1977), the Oscar-winning Creature Comforts (1989), and the Wallace and Gromit films beginning with A Grand Day Out (1989), followed by the feature Chicken Run (2000).

Technique

Animators use oil-based plasticine over wire armatures so figures can hold poses. Shooting on twos (12 poses per second) is common. Because the material deforms under repeated handling, characters are often rebuilt or use replacement parts; heat from studio lights and the sheer labour involved make clay features time-consuming and comparatively rare.
