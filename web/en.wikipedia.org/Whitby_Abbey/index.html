Whitby Abbey

Whitby Abbey is a ruined Benedictine abbey overlooking the North Sea at Whitby in North Yorkshire, England. The first monastery on the site was founded in the seventh century and became famous as the setting for the Synod of Whitby in 664.

History

The Anglo-Saxon monastery, associated with Abbess Hild, was destroyed in the Viking age. A Benedictine house was refounded after the Norman Conquest, and the surviving Gothic ruins largely date from the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries. The abbey was dissolved under Henry VIII in the sixteenth century.

Culture

The dramatic ruins inspired Bram Stoker, who set part of Dracula in Whitby. The site is managed as a historic monument and remains one of the most recognisable monastic ruins in England.
