The 2005 Dutch European Constitution referendum was a consultative referendum held in the Netherlands on 1 June 2005, asking voters whether the country should approve the Treaty establishing a Constitution for Europe. It was the first national referendum in the Netherlands in more than two centuries, and voters rejected the treaty decisively, by 61.5% to 38.5%, on a turnout of 63.3%.

Background

The referendum was initiated not by the government but by parliamentarians from the Labour Party, Democrats 66, and GroenLinks, who passed a special referendum law over the objections of parts of the governing coalition. Most major parties — the governing CDA, VVD, and D66, plus the opposition PvdA — campaigned for a Yes vote. The Socialist Party, Pim Fortuyn List, ChristianUnion, and Geert Wilders campaigned for No.

Campaign and result

The No campaign successfully framed the treaty as a step toward a European superstate, tapping dissatisfaction with the euro changeover, the pace of enlargement, and the government's domestic unpopularity. Coming just three days after French voters rejected the same treaty in their referendum, the Dutch No made the Constitution's ratification effectively impossible.

Aftermath

The European Union entered a "period of reflection," and the Constitution was abandoned. Many of its institutional provisions were later incorporated into the Treaty of Lisbon (2007), which the Netherlands ratified by parliament without a referendum. The 2005 vote marked a turning point in Dutch politics, revealing a durable gap between the political establishment and voters on European integration.
