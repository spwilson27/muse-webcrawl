Perspectivism

Perspectivism is the philosophical view that knowledge and truth are always from a perspective — shaped by the knower's standpoint, interests, concepts and embodied drives — rather than available from a "view from nowhere". It is most closely associated with Friedrich Nietzsche.

Nietzsche

Nietzsche argued, notably in On the Genealogy of Morals (1887), that objectivity is not disinterested contemplation but the ability to multiply perspectives: "the more affects we allow to speak about a thing, the more complete will our 'concept' of this thing be". His perspectivism underlies his critiques of morality, truth and metaphysics, and influenced postmodern and interpretive thought.

Related views

Precursors include Leibniz's monads, each mirroring the universe from a point of view. In anthropology, "perspectivism" also names Eduardo Viveiros de Castro's account of Amerindian cosmologies in which different beings perceive themselves as human and the world through their own perspective.
