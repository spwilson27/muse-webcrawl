Konstantin Tsiolkovsky

Konstantin Eduardovich Tsiolkovsky (1857–1935) was a Russian and Soviet rocket scientist and pioneer of astronautics. A largely self-educated schoolteacher from Kaluga, he is regarded, alongside Robert Goddard and Hermann Oberth, as one of the founding figures of rocketry and spaceflight.

Work

In 1903 he published The Exploration of Cosmic Space by Means of Reaction Devices, which derived the rocket equation relating a rocket's change in velocity to exhaust velocity and mass ratio. He calculated that liquid hydrogen and oxygen would be efficient propellants, envisioned multi-stage rockets, space stations, airlocks and colonisation of space, and wrote extensively — over 400 works — including science fiction.

Legacy

His work, little known outside Russia during his lifetime, strongly influenced Soviet space pioneers such as Sergei Korolev. The Tsiolkovsky crater on the far side of the Moon is named for him, and his aphorism "Earth is the cradle of humanity, but one cannot live in a cradle forever" is widely quoted.
