Hypnos

Abode: Underworld / Lemnos
Symbol: Poppy, inverted torch
Parents: Nyx and Erebus
Siblings: Thanatos, Moros, Nemesis
Roman equivalent: Somnus

Hypnos (Greek: Ὕπνος, "sleep") is the personification of sleep in Greek mythology. He is the son of Nyx, goddess of night, and Erebus, god of darkness, and the twin brother of Thanatos, personification of death.

Mythology

Hypnos lived in a cave in the Underworld, or on the island of Lemnos, through which flowed the river Lethe, whose waters induced forgetfulness. His bed was of ebony, and poppies and other soporific plants grew at the cave's entrance. No light or sound penetrated his dwelling.

In the Iliad, Hera persuades Hypnos to put Zeus to sleep so that Poseidon can aid the Greeks against Zeus's will. Hypnos initially refuses, recalling that on a previous occasion Zeus nearly hurled him into the sea, and he was saved only by his mother Nyx, whom Zeus feared. Hera bribes him with marriage to Pasithea, youngest of the Graces, and Hypnos succeeds.

Family and offspring

With Pasithea, Hypnos was father of the Oneiroi, the dreams, including Morpheus, Phobetor (Icelus), and Phantasos. Morpheus, the chief dream god, could take human form in dreams.

Legacy

The English words hypnosis, hypnotism, and hypnotic derive from Hypnos. The Roman counterpart was Somnus, source of words such as somnolent and insomnia. Hypnos appears frequently in classical art reclining with wings on his head or shoulders, carrying a poppy stem or an inverted torch.
