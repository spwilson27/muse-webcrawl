Sefirot

Sefirot (Hebrew: סְפִירוֹת, singular sefirah) are the ten emanations in Kabbalah through which the infinite God (Ein Sof) reveals Himself and continuously creates both the physical realm and the chain of higher metaphysical realms. They are often depicted as an interconnected "Tree of Life."

The ten sefirot

The ten sefirot are Keter (Crown), Chokhmah (Wisdom), Binah (Understanding), Chesed (Kindness), Gevurah (Judgment), Tiferet (Beauty), Netzach (Eternity), Hod (Splendor), Yesod (Foundation), and Malkuth (Kingdom). A hidden eleventh, Da'at (Knowledge), is sometimes counted in place of Keter.

Structure

The sefirot are arranged in three columns — right (expansion), left (restriction), and middle (balance) — and channel divine light (shefa) through vessels (kelim). In Lurianic Kabbalah, the breaking of the vessels (shevirat ha-kelim) and their repair (tikkun) explain the presence of evil and the purpose of human action.

Sources

The concept develops from the early text Sefer Yetzirah and receives its classic exposition in the Zohar and later Kabbalistic literature.
