National Association of Realtors (NAR)
Founded: 1908 (as National Association of Real Estate Exchanges)
Headquarters: Chicago, Illinois
Membership: ~1.5 million (peak early 2020s)
Type: Trade association

The National Association of Realtors (NAR) is an American trade association for those involved in the real estate industry. Founded in 1908, it is one of the largest trade groups in the United States, with around 1.4–1.5 million members including residential and commercial brokers, salespeople, property managers, appraisers and counsellors. Members who belong to the association may use the trademarked term Realtor.

Activities

NAR maintains a code of ethics (first adopted in 1913), operates lobbying and political action programmes at federal, state and local level, publishes research and housing market statistics, and is linked to the system of multiple listing services (MLS) through local associations. Membership follows a "three-way agreement" tying local, state and national associations together.

Antitrust litigation

NAR's rules on broker commissions were challenged in major antitrust lawsuits, notably Sitzer/Burnett, in which a Missouri jury in October 2023 found NAR and several brokerages liable for conspiring to inflate commissions and awarded $1.78 billion in damages. In March 2024 NAR agreed to a nationwide settlement of about $418 million and to rule changes ending offers of buyer-broker compensation on the MLS, changes that took effect in August 2024.
