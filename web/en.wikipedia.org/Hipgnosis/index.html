Hipgnosis

Hipgnosis was a British art design group based in London that specialised in creating album cover artwork for rock musicians and bands. Founded in 1968 by Storm Thorgerson and Aubrey Powell, with Peter Christopherson joining later, the group became famous for its surreal, elaborately staged photographic covers, made largely without digital manipulation.

History

Thorgerson and Powell, students at the Royal College of Art, took their name from a word scrawled on a door by an acquaintance — a pun on "hypnosis" and "hip". Their breakthrough came with work for Pink Floyd, beginning with A Saucerful of Secrets (1968). Hipgnosis designed many of the most recognisable covers of the 1970s, including Pink Floyd's The Dark Side of the Moon (1973, with George Hardie), whose prism design became iconic, Atom Heart Mother, and Wish You Were Here, as well as covers for Led Zeppelin (Houses of the Holy), Peter Gabriel, Genesis, Yes, and 10cc. The group dissolved in 1983.

Style

Hipgnosis covers favoured witty, surreal photographic tableaux — inflatable pigs over Battersea Power Station, a man on fire shaking hands in a Los Angeles studio lot — often built as full-scale physical sets and shot on location. The designs frequently omitted the band's name and the album title entirely.
