Tapti River

The Tapti River (also spelled Tapi) is one of the major rivers of peninsular India, flowing westward for about 724 kilometres (450 miles) through the states of Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, and Gujarat before emptying into the Gulf of Khambhat of the Arabian Sea near Surat. It is one of only three major Indian rivers — with the Narmada and the Mahi — that flow from east to west.

Course

The Tapti rises in the Satpura Range near Multai in the Betul district of Madhya Pradesh, at an elevation of roughly 752 metres. It flows west through the Khandesh region of Maharashtra, between the Satpura Range to the north and the Ajanta hills to the south, passes the historic city of Burhanpur, and enters the Gujarat plain before reaching Surat and the sea.

Basin and tributaries

The Tapti basin covers about 65,000 square kilometres. Its principal tributary is the Purna River, joining from the south; other tributaries include the Girna, Waghur, and Bori. The Ukai Dam, completed in 1972, is the largest reservoir project on the river and supplies irrigation, flood control, and hydroelectric power.

Significance

In Hindu tradition the Tapti (Tapati) is revered as a daughter of the sun god Surya. The river is historically important as the corridor of the ancient trade and invasion route through Burhanpur into the Deccan. Surat, at its mouth, was once the Mughal Empire's principal port for trade with Europe and the embarkation point for pilgrims to Mecca.
