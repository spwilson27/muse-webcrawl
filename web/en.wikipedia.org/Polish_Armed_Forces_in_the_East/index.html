Polish Armed Forces in the East

Active1943–1945
CountryPoland (aligned with the USSR)
AllegianceSoviet Union / Union of Polish Patriots
SizeAbout 400,000 by 1945 (First and Second Armies)

The Polish Armed Forces in the East (Polish: Polskie Siły Zbrojne na Wschodzie) were the Polish military formations raised in the Soviet Union during the Second World War after 1943, distinct from both the Polish Armed Forces in the West and the earlier Anders Army, which had left the USSR in 1942.

Formation

In May 1943 the 1st Tadeusz Kościuszko Infantry Division was formed at Sielce under Colonel Zygmunt Berling, sponsored by the Soviet-backed Union of Polish Patriots. It first saw action at the Battle of Lenino in October 1943. The force grew into the Polish First Army and later the Second Army, fighting alongside the Red Army through Belarus and Poland.

Campaigns

Units fought in the Lublin–Brest Offensive, reached the Vistula during the Warsaw Uprising (making limited crossings at Czerniaków), liberated Warsaw in January 1945, broke the Pomeranian Wall, and took part in the Battle of Berlin. By war's end they formed the core of the armed forces of the emerging Polish People's Republic.
