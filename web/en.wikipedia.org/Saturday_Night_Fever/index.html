Saturday Night Fever is a 1977 American dance drama film directed by John Badham and starring John Travolta as Tony Manero, a young Italian-American from Brooklyn who spends his weekends dancing at a local discotheque. The screenplay by Norman Wexler was adapted from Nik Cohn's 1976 New York magazine article "Tribal Rites of the New Saturday Night," which Cohn later admitted was largely fabricated.

Plot

Tony Manero, a 19-year-old paint-store clerk, finds escape from his working-class life and family tensions as the celebrated king of the dance floor at the 2001 Odyssey disco in Bay Ridge. He partners with Stephanie Mangano, an older, ambitious dancer who plans to move to Manhattan, for a dance competition. After a series of disillusioning events—including the death of his friend Bobby C.—Tony wins the contest but rejects the prize, recognizing it was awarded unfairly, and resolves to leave Brooklyn and start a new life in Manhattan.

Music

The film's soundtrack, featuring the Bee Gees songs "Stayin' Alive," "How Deep Is Your Love," "More Than a Woman," "Night Fever," and "You Should Be Dancing," became one of the best-selling soundtrack albums of all time, with sales estimated at over 40 million copies worldwide. It won the Grammy Award for Album of the Year and was added to the National Recording Registry in 2012.

Reception and legacy

Saturday Night Fever was a major commercial success, grossing about $237 million worldwide on a small budget, and it popularized disco music and fashion around the world. Travolta was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Actor. The film was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry in 2010 as culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant. A sequel, Staying Alive (1983), directed by Sylvester Stallone, was a commercial success but poorly received by critics.
