Papal election, 1268–1271

The papal election of 1268–1271, following the death of Pope Clement IV, was the longest papal election in the history of the Roman Catholic Church, lasting two years and nine months. It ultimately elected Teobaldo Visconti as Pope Gregory X, and the deadlock that produced it led directly to the formal institution of the papal conclave.

Background

Clement IV died at Viterbo on 29 November 1268. The cardinals — about twenty, divided roughly evenly between French and Italian factions — met in the episcopal palace at Viterbo but could not agree on a candidate, as the French party favoured a pope sympathetic to Charles of Anjou and the Italians opposed him.

The deadlock and the citizens of Viterbo

Frustrated by nearly two years of vacancy, the magistrates and people of Viterbo took drastic measures: they locked the cardinals into the palace (the origin of the term conclave, "with a key"), reduced their food to bread and water, and even removed the roof of the palace to expose them to the elements. Under this pressure the cardinals delegated the choice to a committee of six, which selected Teobaldo Visconti — an Italian not then a cardinal, who was away in the Holy Land on crusade. He accepted, took the name Gregory X, and arrived in Rome in 1272.

Consequences

Determined that the scandal never recur, Gregory X issued the constitution Ubi periculum at the Second Council of Lyon in 1274, requiring cardinals to be sequestered in conclave, progressively deprived of food and income until they reached a decision — rules that, with later modifications, still govern papal elections today.
