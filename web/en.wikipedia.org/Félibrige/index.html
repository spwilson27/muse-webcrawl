The Félibrige is a literary and cultural association founded in 1854 by the Provençal poets Frédéric Mistral, Joseph Roumanille, Théodore Aubanel and four companions, dedicated to restoring and promoting the Provençal language — a variety of Occitan — and the culture of the south of France. Its name derives from the Provençal word félibre, whose origin is uncertain and which Mistral associated with the "doctors of the law" in an old legend; the members are called félibres (feminine félibresso).

Founding

The association was founded on 21 May 1854 at the Château de Font-Ségugne, near Châteauneuf-de-Gadagne in the Vaucluse, by seven young poets: Frédéric Mistral, Joseph Roumanille, Théodore Aubanel, Jean Brunet, Paul Giéra, Anselme Mathieu and Alphonse Tavan. The founding coincided with the feast of Saint Estelle (Santa Estello), who became the patron saint of the movement, and whose seven-pointed star became its emblem.

Aims and activity

The Félibrige sought to give Provençal a modern literary orthography, to publish poetry, prose and an annual almanac (the Armana Prouvençau, first issued for 1855), and to defend the language in public life. Mistral's epic poem Mirèio (1859) became the movement's flagship work; his later dictionary, Lou Tresor dóu Felibrige (1878–1886), codified the language. The association spread beyond Provence to other Occitan-speaking regions and inspired similar movements, including in Catalonia, with which it maintained close ties.

Organisation

The Félibrige is headed by a president called the capoulié. It is organised into regional sections (maintenances) covering Provence, Languedoc, Aquitaine, Limousin, Auvergne, Roussillon and Catalonia. A general assembly, the consistoire, elects fifty majorals (majorau), who hold seats named after Provençal symbols. The movement's festival of Sainte-Estelle is held each year in a different town of the pays d'oc.

Legacy

Although French centralisation and the decline of everyday Occitan limited its mass influence, the Félibrige remains the oldest and most prestigious body devoted to Provençal. Its headquarters and museum, the Museon Arlaten in Arles, founded by Mistral with the money from his 1904 Nobel Prize in Literature, preserve Provençal ethnography and the movement's archives. Debates between the Félibrige's "Mistralian" spelling and the classical Occitan norm continue within the wider Occitan movement.
