Heike Kamerlingh Onnes


Illustration: Heike Kamerlingh Onnes

Heike Kamerlingh Onnes (21 September 1853 – 21 February 1926) was a Dutch physicist and Nobel laureate. He won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1913 for his investigations of the properties of matter at low temperatures, which led to the production of liquid helium.

Cryogenics

At Leiden University, Onnes built a world-leading cryogenics laboratory. In 1908 he became the first to liquefy helium, reaching temperatures within a degree of absolute zero — a field pioneered earlier by Guillaume Amontons's concept of absolute zero.

Superconductivity

In 1911 Onnes discovered superconductivity: the electrical resistance of mercury vanished abruptly at 4.2 K. The phenomenon, observed in helium-4-cooled experiments, opened an entirely new branch of physics.
