Instrument approach

An instrument approach or instrument approach procedure (IAP) is a series of predetermined manoeuvres by reference to flight instruments, with specified protection from obstacles, from the initial approach fix — or the end of an arrival route — to a point from which a landing can be completed and, if not, to a position at which holding or en-route obstacle-clearance criteria apply. Instrument approaches allow aircraft to descend and land safely in instrument meteorological conditions (cloud, fog, darkness) when the runway cannot be seen from altitude. They are published as charts ("approach plates") for individual runways and are flown under instrument flight rules (IFR).

Segments

Approaches are built from standard segments: the arrival segment from the en-route structure; the initial segment from the initial approach fix (IAF) to the intermediate fix (IF); the intermediate segment, in which the aircraft is configured and aligned; the final segment, beginning at the final approach fix (FAF) or point (FAP), in which the aircraft descends on the final approach course to the decision altitude/height (DA/DH) or minimum descent altitude (MDA); and the missed approach segment, which the pilot flies if the required visual reference is not obtained — climbing away to a holding fix to try again or divert.

Types

  • Precision approaches (PA) provide both lateral and vertical guidance to a decision height: the Instrument Landing System (ILS), microwave landing system (MLS), and satellite-based GLS (GBAS landing system), plus PAR radar approaches.
  • Approaches with vertical guidance (APV) — satellite approaches (LPV, LNAV/VNAV) flown to a decision altitude but not classified as precision approaches.
  • Non-precision approaches (NPA) provide lateral guidance only, to an MDA: VOR, NDB, localiser-only and LNAV approaches.

The lowest minima are achieved by ILS: Category I typically permits descent to 200 ft with runway visual range of 550 m; Categories II and III (IIIA/B/C) allow progressively lower decision heights and visibilities, with Category IIIC in principle permitting landing in zero visibility, subject to autoland equipment, crew qualification and airport facilities.

Procedure design

Approach procedures are designed to ICAO's PANS-OPS (or the FAA's TERPS in the United States) criteria, which guarantee obstacle clearance in each segment — for example, at least 1,000 ft in the initial segment, tapering to a few hundred feet on final. Modern procedures are increasingly based on area navigation (RNAV/RNP), allowing satellite-guided paths independent of ground beacons.
