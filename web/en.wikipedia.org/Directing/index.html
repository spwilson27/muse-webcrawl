Directing

Directing is the craft of guiding the artistic and technical elements of a dramatic production — in film, theatre, television, or radio — so that the finished work realises a unified vision. The person who directs is the director.

In film

Film directing begins in pre-production, with interpreting the screenplay, casting, and planning the visual style, continues through production, where the director works with actors on performance and with the cinematographer on framing and camera movement, and concludes in post-production, supervising editing, sound, and music. The notion that the director is the film's principal author is the basis of auteur theory.

In theatre

The modern theatre director emerged in the late nineteenth century, with figures such as the Duke of Saxe-Meiningen and later Konstantin Stanislavski, who unified staging, design, and acting into a coherent interpretation of the play. Before this, productions were typically mounted by actor-managers or the playwright.

Responsibilities

Across media, directing involves analysing the text, shaping performances, composing the stage or screen picture, controlling pace and rhythm, and coordinating the work of designers, technicians, and performers.
