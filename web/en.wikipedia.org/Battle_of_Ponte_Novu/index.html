Battle of Ponte Novu
Date8–9 May 1769
LocationPonte Novu, near Lento, Corsica
ResultDecisive French victory
BelligerentsFrance vs. Corsican Republic

The Battle of Ponte Novu took place on 8 and 9 May 1769 between royal French forces under the Comte de Vaux and the troops of the Corsican Republic under Pasquale Paoli, at the bridge of Ponte Novu over the Golo River in northern Corsica. The French victory effectively ended Corsican independence.

Background

Under the Treaty of Versailles of 1768, the Republic of Genoa ceded its claims on Corsica to France, which invaded to take possession. Paoli's Corsicans, who had governed an independent republic since 1755, resisted, but after the defeat at Borgo in October 1768 the French advanced into the interior in the spring of 1769.

The battle

Paoli positioned his forces to block the French at the Golo crossing at Ponte Novu. After two days of fighting, French regulars forced the bridge, Corsican militia broke, and confusion — including fire from Prussian mercenaries in Corsican service against Corsican troops — turned the retreat into a rout.

Aftermath

Paoli fled into exile in England, and Corsica was incorporated into France. Ponte Novu remains a symbol of Corsican nationalism, commemorated every 8 May. Napoleon Bonaparte was born in Ajaccio three months after the battle, a French citizen by conquest.
