Sidereus Nuncius (Latin for "Starry Messenger" or "Starry Message") is a short astronomical treatise published in 1610 by the Italian astronomer Galileo Galilei. Written in New Latin, it was the first scientific work based on observations made through a telescope, and it announced discoveries that transformed astronomy.

Contents

Galileo reported that the surface of the Moon is not smooth, as Aristotelian cosmology held, but rough and uneven, with mountains and craters, whose heights he estimated from their shadows. He observed vastly more stars than are visible to the naked eye, and described the Milky Way as a multitude of individual stars. Most sensationally, he reported four "stars" orbiting Jupiter — the moons now known as Io, Europa, Ganymede, and Callisto — which he named the "Medicean stars" in honour of his patrons, the Medici.

Impact

The moons of Jupiter provided powerful evidence against the geocentric model, since they clearly orbited a body other than Earth. The book made Galileo famous across Europe; he dedicated it to Cosimo II de' Medici and was rewarded with the post of court mathematician and philosopher in Florence. Johannes Kepler responded with his Conversation with the Starry Messenger the same year.
