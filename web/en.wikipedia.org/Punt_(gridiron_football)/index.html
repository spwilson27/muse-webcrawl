Punt (gridiron football)

A punt in gridiron football (American and Canadian football) is a kick performed by dropping the ball from the hands and kicking it before it touches the ground. It is typically executed on fourth down, when the offensive team surrenders possession to the opposing team while attempting to push them as far back toward their own end zone as possible.

Technique

The punter receives the snap from the long snapper, usually about 14 to 16 yards behind the line of scrimmage, drops the ball with its long axis nearly vertical, and strikes it with the instep of the foot. A well-struck punt travels in a tight spiral, maximising distance and "hang time" — the time the ball spends in the air, which allows the coverage team to run downfield.

Outcomes

The receiving team may catch the punt and attempt a return, signal for a fair catch, or let the ball bounce and roll. A punt that enters the end zone results in a touchback. Punts can be blocked by the defence, and teams may fake a punt to attempt a first-down conversion. The statistic most often used to evaluate punters is gross and net punting average.

Strategy

Punting is a field-position play: coaches weigh the probability of converting a fourth down against the benefit of pinning the opponent deep. Directional and "coffin corner" punts aim to place the ball near the opponent's goal line and out of bounds.
