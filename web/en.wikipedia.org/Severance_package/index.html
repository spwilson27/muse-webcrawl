Severance package

A severance package is the pay and benefits that employees receive when they leave employment at a company unwillfully. In addition to their remaining regular pay, it may include an additional payment based on years of service, payment for unused vacation and sick leave, and extended health insurance coverage.

Components

Typical components include severance pay (often calculated as a number of weeks of salary per year of service), continuation of benefits such as COBRA health coverage in the United States, outplacement assistance, and accelerated vesting of stock options. In exchange, departing employees are commonly asked to sign a general release of claims against the employer.

Legal considerations

In the United States, there is no federal requirement that employers provide severance pay, except in certain mass layoff situations governed by the WARN Act, which requires advance notice. Severance is frequently governed by contract, collective bargaining agreements, or company policy. "Golden parachutes" are generous severance arrangements for senior executives, often triggered by a change of control of the company.
