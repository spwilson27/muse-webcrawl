Cuttack

Cuttack is a city in the eastern Indian state of Odisha, situated at the apex of the Mahanadi River delta where the Mahanadi splits from its branch, the Kathajodi. One of the oldest cities in Odisha, with a history of over a thousand years, it served as the capital of the region for centuries and remains a major commercial and cultural centre, paired with the modern capital Bhubaneswar as a twin city.

History

Cuttack was founded in the late 10th century as a military cantonment under the Keshari dynasty; its name derives from the Sanskrit Kataka, meaning a military camp or fort. The Barabati Fort, built by the Ganga dynasty in the 13th–14th centuries, guarded the city. Cuttack later passed under Afghan, Mughal, Maratha and, from 1803, British rule, and was the capital of Orissa until Bhubaneswar took that role in 1948.

Economy and culture

The city is famous for its silver filigree work (tarakasi), horn and brass crafts, and textiles. The Bali Jatra festival, held on the Mahanadi riverbank, commemorates the ancient maritime trade between Kalinga and Southeast Asia. Cuttack hosts the Barabati Stadium, an international cricket venue, and is the birthplace of the nationalist leader Subhas Chandra Bose.
