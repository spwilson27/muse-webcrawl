A feedlot or feed yard is a type of animal feeding operation used in intensive animal farming, notably beef cattle, but also swine, horses, sheep and poultry, for the rapid feeding of animals before slaughter. The United States Department of Agriculture defines large beef feedlots as concentrated animal feeding operations (CAFOs) holding 1,000 or more animal units.

Operation

Cattle typically arrive at a feedlot as yearlings after grazing and spend several months being "finished" on high-energy rations of grain — chiefly corn — supplemented with silage, hay, protein meals and minerals. Feed is delivered by truck along feed bunks, and animals are grouped in open-air pens. Feed conversion, daily weight gain and health are closely monitored; antibiotics and growth-promoting implants have historically been common, though their use is increasingly restricted in some jurisdictions.

Issues

Feedlots are criticised for animal welfare concerns, the environmental burden of manure management and water use, greenhouse gas emissions, and their role in the spread of food-borne pathogens such as E. coli O157:H7. Defenders argue that grain-finishing produces beef efficiently and consistently, and that concentrated operations simplify waste management relative to dispersed grazing.
