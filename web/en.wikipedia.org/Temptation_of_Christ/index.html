Temptation of Christ

The temptation of Christ is a biblical narrative detailed in the Gospels of Matthew, Mark and Luke. After being baptized by John the Baptist, Jesus was led by the Spirit into the wilderness to fast for forty days and forty nights, during which Satan appeared to him and attempted to tempt him. Jesus refused each temptation, quoting Scripture, and the Devil departed; angels then came and ministered to him.

The three temptations

In Matthew's and Luke's accounts (in slightly different orders), Satan first urges Jesus to turn stones into bread to relieve his hunger; then to throw himself from the pinnacle of the Temple in Jerusalem, testing whether angels would save him; and finally, from a high mountain showing all the kingdoms of the world, offers dominion over them in exchange for worship. Jesus answers each with quotations from Deuteronomy: "Man shall not live by bread alone", "Thou shalt not tempt the Lord thy God", and "Thou shalt worship the Lord thy God, and him only shalt thou serve."

Interpretation and art

The episode has been read as a recapitulation of Israel's forty years in the wilderness and of Adam's fall, and as a model of resistance to temptation during Lent, which commemorates the forty days. It has been a frequent subject in Christian art, literature and film, from Byzantine mosaics to Botticelli's fresco in the Sistine Chapel and modern treatments such as The Last Temptation of Christ.
