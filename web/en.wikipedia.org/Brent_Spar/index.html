Brent Spar

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The Brent Spar buoy

Brent Spar, or Brent E, was an oil storage and tanker loading buoy in the Brent oilfield, operated by Shell UK. Installed in 1976 in the East Shetland Basin of the North Sea, it stood about 30 metres tall above water with most of its 140-metre structure below the surface, and could store roughly 300,000 barrels of oil. It became internationally famous in 1995 as the focus of a Greenpeace campaign against Shell's plan to dispose of it in the deep Atlantic.

The disposal controversy

After the buoy was decommissioned in 1991, Shell studied disposal options and obtained UK government approval to sink it in deep water at the North Feni Ridge, judging this the "best practicable environmental option". In April 1995 Greenpeace activists occupied the Spar and campaigned against ocean dumping. A consumer boycott, particularly strong in Germany, together with political pressure from European governments, led Shell to abandon the sinking plan in June 1995. Greenpeace later acknowledged that its estimate of the oil remaining aboard (5,500 tonnes) had been badly overstated; the true figure was closer to 50–100 tonnes.

Outcome

The Spar was moored in a Norwegian fjord while options were reviewed, and in 1998 Shell announced it would be dismantled onshore and its hull reused as the foundation for a new quay at Mekjarvik near Stavanger, Norway. The affair led to an OSPAR decision in 1998 effectively banning the sea disposal of large steel offshore installations in the North-East Atlantic, and it remains a landmark case in environmental campaigning and corporate reputation.
