Clinical neuropsychology

Clinical neuropsychology is a sub-field of psychology concerned with the applied science of brain–behaviour relationships. Clinical neuropsychologists use this knowledge in the assessment, diagnosis, treatment and rehabilitation of patients with neurological, medical, neurodevelopmental and psychiatric conditions, as well as other cognitive and learning disorders.

Assessment

The core clinical task is the neuropsychological assessment: standardised tests measure domains such as intelligence, memory, attention, language, executive function, visuospatial ability and motor skills. Patterns of strengths and deficits help localise dysfunction, distinguish dementia types, evaluate the effects of stroke, traumatic brain injury, epilepsy or tumours, and establish baselines before and after neurosurgery. Test results are interpreted together with medical history, neuroimaging and behavioural observation.

History

The field's roots lie in 19th-century studies of aphasia by Broca and Wernicke and in the lesion studies of wartime neurologists; Alexander Luria's work in the Soviet Union and the Halstead–Reitan battery in the United States shaped modern practice. Professional organisations and board certification emerged in the late 20th century as training standardised around the Houston Conference model.

Intervention

Beyond diagnosis, neuropsychologists design cognitive rehabilitation programmes, advise families and schools, provide psychotherapy adapted to cognitive impairment, and testify as expert witnesses on brain injury.
