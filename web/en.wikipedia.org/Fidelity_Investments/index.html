Fidelity Investments Inc., commonly referred to as Fidelity, is an American multinational financial services corporation based in Boston, Massachusetts. Founded in 1946 as Fidelity Management & Research by Edward Crosby "Ned" Johnson II, it is one of the largest asset managers in the world, administering trillions of dollars in customer assets and serving tens of millions of individual investors, employers, and institutions.

History

The Fidelity Fund itself dated to 1930; Johnson took it over in 1946 and built the firm into a mutual fund powerhouse. His son, Edward C. "Ned" Johnson III, ran the company from 1977, expanding aggressively into discount brokerage, retirement plans, and technology-driven customer service. Fidelity's Magellan Fund, managed by Peter Lynch from 1977 to 1990, became the era's most famous mutual fund, compounding at extraordinary rates and popularizing growth-stock investing. In 2014 Abigail Johnson, the founder's granddaughter, became chief executive and later chairman.

Business

Fidelity operates retail brokerage, mutual funds and ETFs, 401(k) and workplace retirement plan recordkeeping, wealth management, clearing and custody for other firms, and a digital-assets business that was among the first major financial institutions to offer bitcoin custody and trading. Because it is privately held by the Johnson family and employees, it does not report the same public financials as listed rivals such as BlackRock and Vanguard, its principal competitors in scale.
