Germplasm

Germplasm is the living genetic material of plants and animals — seeds, tissues, cells, sperm, eggs, or whole organisms — that is preserved for breeding, research, and conservation. The term is used mainly in agriculture and biology to describe collections of genetic resources that carry the hereditary information of a species or population.

The concept grew out of the work of plant collectors and geneticists in the early twentieth century, notably the Russian botanist Nikolai Vavilov, who assembled one of the world's first large seed collections and identified centres of origin of cultivated plants.

Germplasm collections

Germplasm is conserved in specialised facilities known as gene banks or seed banks. Seeds are dried and stored at low temperatures, often around −18 °C, to slow ageing and preserve viability for decades. Collections that cannot be stored as seed — such as clonally propagated crops like potato and banana — are maintained as living plants in field gene banks, as tissue cultures in vitro, or by cryopreservation in liquid nitrogen.

Major collections include the Svalbard Global Seed Vault in Norway, which holds backup samples from gene banks worldwide, and national collections such as those of the United States Department of Agriculture.

Uses

Plant breeders use germplasm to introduce traits such as disease resistance, drought tolerance, and improved yield into modern crop varieties. Wild relatives of crops are an especially valuable source of genetic diversity, because commercial varieties often descend from a narrow genetic base. Germplasm is also used in evolutionary biology, taxonomy, and the restoration of endangered species.

Conservation

Conservation strategies are divided into ex situ methods, in which material is stored away from its natural habitat in gene banks and botanical gardens, and in situ methods, in which populations are protected in the wild or maintained by farmers in traditional agricultural systems. International exchange of germplasm is governed by treaties such as the International Treaty on Plant Genetic Resources for Food and Agriculture.
