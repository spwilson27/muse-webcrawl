Hyper-Kamiokande

Hyper-Kamiokande (also called Hyper-K or HK) is a neutrino observatory and experiment under construction in Japan. It is the successor to the Super-Kamiokande experiment and will be the largest water Cherenkov detector ever built. The detector is being constructed in the Tochibora mine near Hida, Gifu Prefecture, close to the Kamioka Observatory. Construction began in 2020 and data taking is expected to begin around 2027–2028.

Design

The main detector is a cylindrical tank about 68 metres in diameter and 71 metres tall, holding approximately 260,000 tonnes of ultra-pure water — roughly eight times the volume of Super-Kamiokande. Its inner detector will be viewed by about 40,000 photomultiplier tubes that detect the faint Cherenkov light produced when a neutrino interaction creates a charged particle moving faster than the speed of light in water.

Physics goals

Hyper-Kamiokande's programme includes a precise measurement of CP violation in neutrino oscillations using the upgraded J-PARC neutrino beam, searches for proton decay (a direct test of grand unified theories), observation of neutrinos from supernovae — including the diffuse supernova neutrino background — and studies of solar and atmospheric neutrinos. As a far detector for the T2HK long-baseline experiment, it will work with beam neutrinos produced 295 km away at J-PARC in Tokai.
