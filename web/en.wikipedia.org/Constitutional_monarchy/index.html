Constitutional monarchy

A constitutional monarchy, also known as a limited monarchy, parliamentary monarchy, or democratic monarchy, is a form of monarchy in which the monarch exercises their authority in accordance with a constitution and is not alone in making decisions. It differs from absolute monarchy in that an independent body — typically a constitution — limits the monarch's powers. In most modern constitutional monarchies the monarch is a ceremonial head of state while an elected parliament and a prime minister exercise governing power; in some, the monarch retains substantial executive authority.

History

The oldest constitutional monarchy with a written constitution is often traced to England's settlement after the Glorious Revolution of 1688 and the Bill of Rights 1689, which subordinated the Crown to Parliament. The nineteenth century saw constitutional monarchies spread across Europe — France's July Monarchy, Belgium (1831), and the German Empire among them — often as compromises between dynastic legitimacy and liberal demands. Japan adopted a constitutional monarchy with the Meiji Constitution of 1889; the 1947 constitution redefined the emperor as a symbol of the state.

Forms

In ceremonial (parliamentary) monarchies such as Sweden, the monarch performs no formal executive role. In others — the United Kingdom, Spain, the Netherlands, Denmark, and the Commonwealth realms — the monarch retains formal powers (appointing the prime minister, assenting to legislation, dissolving parliament) exercised by convention on ministerial advice. "Executive constitutional monarchies", such as Jordan, Morocco, and Liechtenstein, grant the monarch real governing authority within a constitutional framework.

Debate

Supporters argue constitutional monarchy provides continuity, a non-partisan head of state above politics, and a symbol of national unity; critics object to hereditary privilege and prefer an elected republic. Several countries have settled the question by referendum, including Australia in 1999 (which retained the monarchy).
