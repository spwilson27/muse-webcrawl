Battle of Rocroi

The field of Rocroi, 19 May 1643, where the young Duke of Enghien destroyed the Spanish tercios.

Date19 May 1643
LocationRocroi, France
ResultDecisive French victory
CommandersLouis, Duke of Enghien vs. Francisco de Melo

The Battle of Rocroi was fought on 19 May 1643, during the Thirty Years' War, between a French army commanded by the 21-year-old Louis, Duke of Enghien (later the Great Condé), and a Spanish army under Francisco de Melo that was besieging the fortress of Rocroi in the Ardennes.

Background

Days after the death of Louis XIII left France with a child king, the Spanish Army of Flanders advanced into northern France and invested Rocroi, hoping to repeat the invasion of 1636. Enghien, commanding the French Army of Picardy, resolved — against cautious advice from Paris — to relieve the fortress by battle.

The battle

At dawn on 19 May, Enghien attacked. On the French right his cavalry routed the Spanish horse opposite; on the left, the French cavalry was itself driven back and the French centre, including its reserve, was badly mauled by the veteran Spanish tercios. Enghien then led his victorious right wing in a great sweep behind the Spanish army, destroyed the remaining cavalry and artillery, and fell upon the isolated infantry squares. The Italian and Walloon tercios broke or surrendered; the Spanish tercios stood in formation, twice repelling attacks, until French artillery and infantry overwhelmed them after they had been refused quarter in confusion. Spanish losses were some 7,000–8,000 killed and 7,000 captured, with all their guns; French losses were about 4,500.

Significance

Rocroi has traditionally been seen as the battle that ended the era of Spanish infantry supremacy begun a century earlier and announced the rise of French military dominance in Europe. Modern historians qualify the symbolism — Spanish armies remained formidable for decades — but the scale of the victory, won in the first days of a new reign, gave the regency of Anne of Austria and Cardinal Mazarin a priceless boost in prestige.
