Saag

Saag (Hindustani: साग) is a leafy vegetable dish from the Indian subcontinent, made from greens such as spinach, mustard leaves, fenugreek or bathua, cooked with spices, onion, ginger and garlic, and often finished with ghee or cream. The word saag generically denotes cooked leafy greens in several North Indian languages.

Varieties

The best-known forms include sarson da saag, made from mustard greens and traditionally eaten in Punjab with makki di roti (cornmeal flatbread) and a knob of white butter; palak dishes made with spinach; and combined dishes such as saag paneer (with fresh cheese) and saag aloo (with potatoes). Saag gosht uses meat, typically lamb.

Preparation

The leaves are washed, chopped or blanched, then simmered and pounded or blended to a coarse purée before being tempered with spices. Regional versions vary in texture from soupy to dry.
