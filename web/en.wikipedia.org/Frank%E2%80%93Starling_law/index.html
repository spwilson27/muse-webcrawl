Frank–Starling law

Frank–Starling law
FieldCardiovascular physiology
Named afterOtto Frank, Ernest Starling
StatementStroke volume increases with end-diastolic volume

The Frank–Starling law of the heart represents the relationship between stroke volume and end diastolic volume. The law states that the stroke volume of the left ventricle will increase as the left ventricular volume increases due to the myocyte stretch causing a more forceful systolic contraction.

Mechanism

Stretch of cardiac muscle fibers increases their sensitivity to calcium and the overlap of actin and myosin filaments, so a ventricle filled with more blood contracts more strongly and ejects more blood — automatically matching output to venous return.

History

The principle was described by Otto Frank in 1895 and developed by Ernest Starling, whose experiments with the heart–lung preparation in the 1910s established the "law of the heart".

Clinical significance

The Frank–Starling mechanism keeps the outputs of the right and left ventricles balanced. In heart failure the curve flattens and shifts downward, so increased filling no longer yields a proportional increase in output.
