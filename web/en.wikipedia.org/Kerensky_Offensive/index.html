Kerensky Offensive

The Kerensky Offensive (Russian: Наступление Керенского), also called the June Offensive or July Offensive, was the last Russian offensive of the First World War, launched by the Russian Provisional Government in July 1917. It failed disastrously, breaking the morale of the Russian Army and fatally weakening the government of Alexander Kerensky in the months before the October Revolution.

Background

After the February Revolution, the Provisional Government pledged to honour Russia's alliance commitments. War Minister Alexander Kerensky and commander-in-chief Aleksei Brusilov planned a major attack in Galicia, coordinated with Allied operations in the west, hoping a victory would restore army discipline and the government's authority. Soldiers' committees and Bolshevik agitation opposed the offensive, and many units voted on whether to advance at all.

The offensive

Following a two-day artillery bombardment, Russian armies attacked on 1 July 1917 toward Lemberg (Lviv). Initial advances against Austro-Hungarian positions gained ground — Brzezany and Kalusz fell — and the Czechoslovak Rifle Brigade distinguished itself at Zborov (2 July). But reserves refused to move up, attacks elsewhere stalled, and on 19 July German and Austro-Hungarian forces counterattacked near Tarnopol. The Russian front collapsed; retreat became rout, with looting and desertion on a massive scale.

Consequences

The failure destroyed what remained of the army's fighting power. Kerensky replaced Brusilov with Lavr Kornilov, reintroduced the death penalty at the front, and became head of government in the July reshuffle, but the Provisional Government never recovered its authority. The disaster fed directly into the July Days crisis in Petrograd and set the stage for the Kornilov affair and the Bolshevik seizure of power that autumn.
