Unbalanced circuit

An unbalanced circuit is an electrical circuit in which the impedances of the two legs are not equal with respect to ground. In an unbalanced signalling system — also called single-ended signalling — the signal is carried on one conductor and referenced to a ground that carries the return current, as in ordinary coaxial cable connections.

Characteristics

Unbalanced connections are simple and cheap, and dominate consumer audio and short-distance digital links (for example RCA phono connectors and TS instrument cables). Their weakness is susceptibility to interference: noise induced equally in a balanced pair is rejected by a differential receiver, but in an unbalanced line any noise picked up on the conductor, and any potential difference between the grounds at each end (a ground loop), appears directly in the signal as hum or buzz.

Comparison with balanced circuits

A balanced circuit uses two conductors with equal impedance to ground carrying equal and opposite signals, so common-mode noise cancels at the receiver. Professional audio and long cable runs therefore use balanced lines, while unbalanced connections are preferred for short runs where cost and simplicity matter. Devices such as a balun or a DI box convert between the two forms.
