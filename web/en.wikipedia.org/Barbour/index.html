Barbour

J. Barbour & Sons Ltd, commonly known simply as Barbour, is a British luxury and lifestyle brand founded in 1894 by John Barbour in South Shields, England. The company is best known for its waxed cotton jackets, originally designed for sailors, fishermen and dock workers on the north-east coast of England.

History

John Barbour, a Scotsman from Galloway, began importing oilskins in 1894. The business grew supplying protective clothing to the local maritime community, and later to motorcyclists, aviators and the military. During both World Wars Barbour supplied the British armed forces, including Ursula submarine suits.

Products

Barbour's most famous garments include the Bedale, Beaufort and Border waxed jackets. The brand holds royal warrants and became a fashion staple of British country clothing. The company remains family-owned, run by the fifth generation of the Barbour family, and its jackets are still largely made at its factory in Simonside, South Shields.
