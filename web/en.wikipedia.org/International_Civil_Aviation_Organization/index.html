The International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) is a specialized agency of the United Nations that coordinates the principles and techniques of international air navigation and fosters the planning and development of international air transport to ensure safe and orderly growth. Its headquarters are in the Quartier International of Montreal, Canada.

History

ICAO was established by the Convention on International Civil Aviation (the Chicago Convention), signed on 7 December 1944 by 52 states. The organization came into being on 4 April 1947, thirty days after the convention's ratification by the required 26 states, and became a UN specialized agency later that year.

Functions

ICAO adopts Standards and Recommended Practices (SARPs) covering air navigation, flight operations, airworthiness, personnel licensing, air traffic services, accident investigation and the facilitation of border formalities. These are published in the 19 Annexes to the Chicago Convention. ICAO also assigns the four-letter airport location indicators and three-letter airline designators used worldwide, sets the standard for machine-readable passports, and audits member states' safety oversight through its Universal Safety Oversight Audit Programme. Its Carbon Offsetting and Reduction Scheme for International Aviation (CORSIA), adopted in 2016, addresses the climate impact of international flights.

Governance

The Assembly, comprising all member states, meets at least every three years. It elects a 36-member Council, the governing body, which directs the work of the Secretariat headed by the Secretary General. ICAO's working languages include English, French, Spanish, Russian, Arabic and Chinese.
