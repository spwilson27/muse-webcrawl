Phenology

SubjectTiming of recurring biological events
ExamplesBudburst, flowering, migration, leaf fall
Term coinedCharles Morren (c. 1849)

Phenology is the scientific study of the timing of recurring biological events and the causes of that timing, including biotic and abiotic forces and the interrelation among phases of the same or different species. Familiar phenological events include the budding and flowering of plants in spring, leaf colouring and fall in autumn, the arrival of migratory birds, and the emergence of insects.

Phenology tracks events such as budburst, flowering and leaf senescence

The word derives from the Greek phainō ("to show, to bring to light") and was coined by the Belgian botanist Charles Morren around 1849. Records of plant phenology extend back centuries — Japanese cherry-blossom festival records from Kyoto reach back to the ninth century — and systematic observation networks were established in several countries in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. Robert Marsham's "Indications of Spring", begun in Norfolk in 1736, is a landmark of British phenology.

Phenology and climate change

Because phenological events are sensitive to temperature, long records are powerful indicators of climate change. Studies have documented advances in spring events — earlier flowering, leafing and insect emergence — across the temperate world, with the risk of "phenological mismatch" when interacting species, such as caterpillars and the birds that feed on them, shift their timing by different amounts. Modern phenology combines ground observation networks, citizen science, and satellite monitoring of land-surface green-up.
