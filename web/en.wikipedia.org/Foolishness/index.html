Foolishness

Foolishness is the unawareness or lack of social norms which causes offence, embarrassment, or annoyance, displayed by someone who, despite being intelligent or well-intentioned, acts in an inappropriate or immature fashion. Foolishness differs from stupidity, which is a lack of intelligence; a foolish act may be committed by a person of high intelligence who disregards judgement, experience or propriety.

In literature and culture

The figure of the fool is a longstanding literary and theatrical archetype, from the jesters of medieval courts and Shakespeare's fools — who often speak truth to power — to Erasmus's In Praise of Folly (1509), in which Folly herself delivers the oration. The biblical book of Proverbs contrasts the fool with the wise person. Festivals such as April Fools' Day institutionalise brief, socially permitted foolishness.

Philosophical treatment

Philosophers and moralists have debated whether foolishness is primarily a failure of knowledge, of attention or of self-control. In modern psychology, related constructs include impulsivity and poor risk judgement, and foolish behaviour by experts in narrow domains is sometimes discussed under the label of the "foolishness of the learned".
