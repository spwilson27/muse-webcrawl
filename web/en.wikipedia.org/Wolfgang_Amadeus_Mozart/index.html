Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart (27 January 1756 – 5 December 1791) was a prolific and influential composer of the Classical period. Born in Salzburg, he showed prodigious ability from his earliest childhood, composing from the age of five and performing before European royalty.

Early life

Mozart was the son of Leopold Mozart, a composer and music teacher in the court of the Prince-Archbishop of Salzburg. Leopold toured Wolfgang and his sister Maria Anna ("Nannerl") around the courts of Europe as child prodigies. Mozart was employed as a court musician in Salzburg but grew restless and travelled in search of a better position.

Vienna years

In 1781 Mozart settled in Vienna, where he achieved fame as a pianist and composer but struggled financially. His mature works include the operas The Marriage of Figaro, Don Giovanni and The Magic Flute; the last three symphonies (Nos. 39–41, including the "Jupiter"); the Clarinet Concerto; and numerous piano concertos, chamber works and masses. His catalogue runs to more than 800 works, catalogued by Ludwig von Köchel (the "K." numbers).

Death and legacy

Mozart died in Vienna in 1791 at the age of 35, leaving his Requiem in D minor unfinished. Despite his short life, his rapid pace of composition produced works spanning every major genre of his time. He is widely regarded as one of the greatest composers in Western music history, and his music remains central to the concert repertoire.
