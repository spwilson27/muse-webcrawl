Winter guard

Winter guard (also called winterguard) is a performing art and competitive activity similar to color guard, performed indoors during the winter and spring seasons. Like color guard, which performs alongside marching bands in the autumn, winter guard ensembles perform choreographed routines using flags, sabres, mock rifles and other equipment, combined with dance and interpretive movement, usually on a gymnasium floor.

Performance and competition

Winter guard performances are set to recorded music and are typically between three and eight minutes long. Performers perform on a protective floor covering, often a decorated vinyl tarp. Ensembles are judged on equipment skill, movement, design and general effect.

The principal governing body is Winter Guard International (WGI), founded in 1977, which organizes regional competitions and the WGI World Championships held each spring in Dayton, Ohio. Other circuits, such as local state and regional associations, also run winter guard competitions. Ensembles are divided into classes such as Scholastic (school-based) and Independent (community-based), and by skill levels including A, Open and World class.

History

Indoor color guard developed in the 1960s and 1970s as color guards sought to continue performing after the marching season ended. Over time the activity moved away from its military inspection roots toward dance, theatre and modern interpretive performance.
