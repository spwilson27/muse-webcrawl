Ticagrelor

Trade namesBrilinta, Brilique
ClassP2Y12 platelet inhibitor
RoutesBy mouth
Legal statusPrescription only

Ticagrelor, sold under the brand names Brilinta and Brilique among others, is a medication used for the prevention of stroke, heart attack and other events in people with acute coronary syndrome. It acts as a platelet aggregation inhibitor by reversibly blocking the P2Y12 receptor on platelets.

Medical uses

Ticagrelor is used together with low-dose aspirin to reduce the risk of cardiovascular death, myocardial infarction and stent thrombosis after acute coronary syndrome or percutaneous coronary intervention. It is taken twice daily, and unlike clopidogrel it does not require metabolic activation in the liver.

Side effects

Common adverse effects include bleeding, shortness of breath (dyspnoea) and bradycardia. The dyspnoea is usually mild and transient. Ticagrelor should not be used in people with active bleeding or a history of intracranial haemorrhage.

History

Developed by AstraZeneca, ticagrelor was approved for medical use in the European Union in 2010 and in the United States in 2011. The PLATO trial showed it reduced the composite of cardiovascular death, heart attack and stroke compared with clopidogrel.
