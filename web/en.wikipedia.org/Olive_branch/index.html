Olive branch

The olive branch is a symbol of peace and victory originating in the cultures of the ancient Mediterranean. To "extend an olive branch" is to make an offer of peace or reconciliation. The symbolism combines the olive tree's associations with abundance and longevity and the practice of suppliants carrying olive branches to plead for mercy or to signal peaceful intent.

Ancient origins

In Greek mythology the olive was sacred to Athena, who won the patronage of Athens by offering its citizens the first olive tree. Suppliants in Greece carried branches wrapped in wool, and victors at the ancient Olympic Games were crowned with wreaths of wild olive. In Rome the olive branch accompanied envoys and appeared on coins as an attribute of Pax, goddess of peace. Virgil's Aeneid depicts Aeneas holding out an olive branch in parley.

Biblical and later use

In the Book of Genesis, a dove returns to Noah's Ark carrying a freshly plucked olive leaf, signalling that the Flood waters have receded and God's anger has abated. Olive branches became common in early Christian art as emblems of peace. The motif appears on the flags and emblems of several nations and organisations: the flag of Cyprus shows a map of the island above two crossed olive branches, the emblem of the United Nations encircles the world map with olive wreaths, and the Great Seal of the United States shows the American bald eagle holding an olive branch in one talon and arrows in the other — peace preferred, war prepared for.

In modern language

The phrase "to offer an olive branch" remains a standard metaphor in diplomacy and everyday speech for any conciliatory gesture made after conflict.
