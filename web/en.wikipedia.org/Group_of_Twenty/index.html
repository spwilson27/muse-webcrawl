Group of Twenty

The Group of Twenty (G20) is an intergovernmental forum comprising 19 sovereign countries, the European Union (EU) and the African Union (AU). It works to address major issues related to the global economy, such as international financial stability, climate change mitigation and sustainable development.

Membership

The member countries are Argentina, Australia, Brazil, Canada, China, France, Germany, India, Indonesia, Italy, Japan, Mexico, Russia, Saudi Arabia, South Africa, South Korea, Turkey, the United Kingdom and the United States, together with the European Union. The African Union became a permanent member in 2023. Together the members represent about 85 per cent of global GDP, over 75 per cent of global trade and roughly two-thirds of the world's population.

History

The G20 was founded in 1999 as a meeting of finance ministers and central bank governors in response to the Asian financial crisis. During the global financial crisis of 2008 it was elevated to a leaders' summit forum, and the 2009 Pittsburgh summit declared it the premier forum for international economic cooperation.

Structure and summits

The presidency rotates annually among members, and leaders meet at an annual summit. Work proceeds through a finance track and a Sherpa track, with engagement groups drawn from business, labour, youth and civil society.
