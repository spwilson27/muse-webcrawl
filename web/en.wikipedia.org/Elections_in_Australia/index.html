Elections in Australia

Elections in Australia take place at federal, state and territory, and local levels. Federal elections choose members of the House of Representatives and the Senate of the Parliament of Australia.

Voting is compulsory for enrolled citizens aged 18 and over, and elections are administered by the Australian Electoral Commission. Federal elections are usually held every three years on a Saturday.

Systems

The House of Representatives is elected by instant-runoff voting (preferential voting) in single-member divisions. The Senate is elected by the single transferable vote with proportional representation, each state electing 12 senators and the mainland territories two each. Preferential voting, introduced in 1918, allows voters to rank candidates.

Parties

Major parties include the Australian Labor Party, the Liberal Party and its Coalition partner the National Party, and the Australian Greens.
