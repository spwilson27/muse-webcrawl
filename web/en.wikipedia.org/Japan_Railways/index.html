The Japan Railways Group, commonly known as the JR Group, is a group of companies that controls Japan's former national railway network. It was created on 1 April 1987 when the state-owned Japanese National Railways (JNR) was privatised and divided into six regional passenger operators — JR Hokkaido, JR East, JR Central, JR West, JR Shikoku and JR Kyushu — plus the nationwide JR Freight and several supporting organisations.

History

JNR had accumulated enormous debts by the 1980s. The Nakasone government's reform split and privatised it, writing off much of the debt through a settlement corporation. The three Honshu companies later listed on the stock exchange and became fully private; the island companies of Hokkaido, Shikoku and Kyushu initially remained state-held, with Kyushu listing in 2016.

Operations

The JR companies operate the Shinkansen high-speed network, dense urban commuter services in Tokyo, Osaka and other cities, and rural local lines. JR East is one of the world's largest railway companies by passengers carried. The companies cooperate on through ticketing, the Japan Rail Pass for foreign visitors, and interoperable IC cards such as Suica and ICOCA.
