Kopaonik (Serbian: Копаоник) is a mountain range in central Serbia and one of the country's largest. Its highest point, Pančić's Peak, rises to 2,017 metres above sea level. Kopaonik is the site of Serbia's largest ski resort and was declared a national park in 1981, covering an area of about 118 square kilometres.

Geography

The range extends roughly 75 kilometres from north to south. Its name derives from the Serbian word kopati, "to dig", reflecting the area's long history of mining; silver, iron, lead and zinc were extracted here from Roman and medieval times. The mountain is known for its sunny weather, with around 200 sunny days a year, earning it the nickname "the sunny mountain".

National park

Kopaonik National Park protects extensive forests of beech, oak, spruce and fir, as well as endemic plant species and fauna including wildcats and golden eagles. Remnants of medieval mining settlements and churches, such as the monastery of Studenica in the wider region, add cultural interest.

Tourism

The ski centre offers dozens of kilometres of pistes served by modern lifts and is Serbia's premier winter sports destination. In summer the mountain attracts hikers and mountain bikers. The resort town lies at about 1,770 metres elevation.
