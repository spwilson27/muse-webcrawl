The Supremes were an American girl group and a premier act of Motown Records during the 1960s. Founded as the Primettes in Detroit, Michigan, in 1959, the Supremes were the most commercially successful of Motown's acts and the most successful American vocal group, with 12 number-one singles on the Billboard Hot 100. Most of these hits were written and produced by Motown's main songwriting and production team, Holland–Dozier–Holland.

History

The group was formed by Florence Ballard, Mary Wilson, Diana Ross, and Betty McGlown, who grew up in Detroit's Brewster-Douglass housing projects. Signed to Motown by Berry Gordy in 1961, they struggled for several years before breaking through in 1964 with "Where Did Our Love Go", the first of five consecutive number-one singles, followed by "Baby Love", "Come See About Me", "Stop! In the Name of Love", and "Back in My Arms Again".

At their peak in the mid-1960s, the Supremes rivaled the Beatles in worldwide popularity, and their success made it possible for future African-American R&B and soul musicians to find mainstream success. Ballard left the group in 1967 and was replaced by Cindy Birdsong; Ross departed for a solo career in 1970, replaced by Jean Terrell. The group continued in changing lineups until disbanding in 1977.

Legacy

The Supremes were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1988. Their story inspired the Broadway musical Dreamgirls (1981) and its 2006 film adaptation. In 2023, Rolling Stone ranked the Supremes among the greatest artists of all time, and two of their songs are in the Grammy Hall of Fame.

Discography highlights

Studio albums include Where Did Our Love Go (1964), More Hits by The Supremes (1965), I Hear a Symphony (1966), The Supremes A' Go-Go (1966), and Diana Ross & the Supremes: Reflections (1968).
