Carl von Ossietzky

Carl von Ossietzky
Born: 3 October 1889, Hamburg, German Empire
Died: 4 May 1938, Berlin, Nazi Germany
Occupation: Journalist, pacifist
Award: Nobel Peace Prize (1935)

Carl von Ossietzky (3 October 1889 – 4 May 1938) was a German journalist and pacifist. He was the recipient of the 1935 Nobel Peace Prize for his work in exposing the clandestine German rearmament programme.

Journalism

Ossietzky became editor-in-chief of the weekly magazine Die Weltbühne in 1927. In 1931 he was convicted of treason and espionage for an article revealing that Germany was secretly rebuilding an air force in violation of the Treaty of Versailles, and was sentenced to eighteen months in prison.

Persecution and Nobel Prize

Released under an amnesty in late 1932, he was rearrested on the morning after the Reichstag fire in February 1933 and held in concentration camps, including Esterwegen, where his health was destroyed by mistreatment and tuberculosis. In 1936 the Nobel Committee announced that he had been awarded the 1935 Peace Prize; the Nazi regime forbade him to travel to Oslo and barred all Germans from accepting Nobel Prizes thereafter. Ossietzky died in a Berlin hospital under police guard in 1938.

Legacy

The Carl von Ossietzky University of Oldenburg and the International League for Human Rights' Carl von Ossietzky Medal commemorate him. His case remains a symbol of resistance to militarism and of the defence of press freedom.
