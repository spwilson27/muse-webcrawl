Kiang

Kiang (Equus kiang)
Family: Equidae
Habitat: Tibetan Plateau alpine steppe, 2,700–5,400 m
Conservation status: Least Concern (IUCN)

The kiang (Equus kiang) is the largest of the Asian wild asses and is native to the Tibetan Plateau, where it inhabits high alpine grasslands and steppe between 2,700 and 5,400 metres in elevation. Closely related to the onager and the African wild ass, it is distinguished by its rich chestnut coat, white underparts and a dark dorsal stripe.

Description and behaviour

Kiangs stand 132–142 cm at the shoulder and weigh 250–400 kg. They live in herds led by an older mare, grazing on grasses and sedges; in winter they dig through snow to reach vegetation. Males are largely solitary outside the summer rutting season. The species is a strong swimmer and crosses rivers readily. Predators include wolves; foals may also be taken by snow leopards.

Distribution and subspecies

Three subspecies are recognised: the western kiang (E. k. kiang) in Ladakh and western Tibet, the eastern kiang (E. k. holdereri) in Qinghai, and the southern kiang (E. k. polyodon) in southern Tibet and adjacent Nepal and Bhutan. Population estimates for China suggest tens of thousands of animals, and numbers recovered after protection measures curbed poaching, though competition with domestic livestock and fencing of pastures remain concerns.
