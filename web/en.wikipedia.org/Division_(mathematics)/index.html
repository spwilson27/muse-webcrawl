Division (mathematics)

Division

Symbols: ÷, /, fraction bar

Operation: Inverse of multiplication

Terms: Dividend ÷ divisor = quotient

Undefined case: Division by zero

Division is one of the four basic operations of arithmetic, the others being addition, subtraction, and multiplication. It is the inverse operation of multiplication: if a ÷ b = c, then c × b = a. In the expression a ÷ b, a is the dividend, b is the divisor, and the result is the quotient.

Interpretations

Division can be understood in two main ways. In the partitive (sharing) interpretation, 20 divided by 4 asks how large each share is when 20 objects are split equally among 4 groups: the answer is 5. In the quotitive (measurement) interpretation, it asks how many groups of 4 can be made from 20: again 5, but the reasoning differs. When the dividend is not a multiple of the divisor, division may yield a remainder (as in Euclidean division) or a fraction or decimal.

Notation

Division is written with the obelus (÷), introduced by Johann Heinrich Rahn in 1659, with the slash (a/b) common in algebra and computing, or as a fraction with a horizontal vinculum. The obelus is largely confined to elementary education in English-speaking countries.

Division by zero

Division by zero is undefined in ordinary arithmetic: no number multiplied by zero gives a non-zero dividend, and allowing it would collapse the rules of algebra. Computers typically return an error or, in floating-point arithmetic, infinity or NaN.

Abstract algebra

Beyond numbers, division generalises to any structure in which elements have multiplicative inverses. In a field such as the rational, real, or complex numbers, every non-zero element can be divided by. In rings without inverses, divisibility becomes a property to study: one integer divides another if the quotient is an integer, a notion central to number theory.
