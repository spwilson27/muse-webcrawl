Washington Accord (credentials)

Washington Accord
Signed1989
TypeMutual recognition agreement for engineering qualifications
Administered byInternational Engineering Alliance

The Washington Accord is an international agreement between bodies responsible for accrediting engineering degree programmes. Signed in 1989, it recognises the substantial equivalence of accredited engineering degrees in signatory countries and recommends that graduates of such programmes be recognised as meeting the academic requirements for entry to engineering practice in the other signatory jurisdictions.

History

The original signatories in 1989 were the accreditation bodies of Australia, Canada, Ireland, New Zealand, the United Kingdom and the United States. Membership has since expanded considerably, with signatories representing countries and economies including South Africa, Japan, Singapore, Hong Kong, South Korea, Malaysia, India, China, Russia, Turkey, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, Indonesia, the Philippines and others, alongside provisional members. The accord is administered through the International Engineering Alliance, which also oversees sister agreements such as the Sydney Accord (for engineering technologists) and the Dublin Accord (for engineering technicians).

Scope

The Washington Accord covers professional engineering degrees — typically four- or five-year bachelor-level programmes — accredited within the signatory's own jurisdiction. It addresses only the academic qualification, not professional licensure itself: registration or chartered status may still require assessment of experience and competence by the host jurisdiction. Equivalence is expressed through graduate attributes and professional competencies agreed by the signatories, against which each signatory's accreditation system is periodically reviewed by the others.

Significance

The accord underpins the international mobility of engineers, allowing employers and licensing bodies in signatory countries to accept accredited foreign degrees without re-examining curricula in detail. It has also driven convergence in outcomes-based accreditation worldwide, since aspiring signatories must demonstrate that their accreditation processes and graduate standards are substantially equivalent to those of existing members.
