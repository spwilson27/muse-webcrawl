Acanthaceae

Acanthaceae
Order: Lamiales
Genera: ~229
Species: ~4,300
Common name: Acanthus family

Acanthaceae is a family of dicotyledonous flowering plants containing almost 250 genera and about 4,300 species. Most are tropical herbs, shrubs, or twining vines; some are epiphytes. Only a few species are distributed in temperate regions.

Description

Plants in the family typically have simple, opposite leaves, often with cystoliths visible as streaks. Flowers are frequently showy, two-lipped, and borne with prominent bracts. The fruit is usually a loculicidal capsule whose seeds are flung out by hooked retinacula when the capsule splits.

Distribution and uses

The family is distributed mainly in Indonesia, Malaysia, Africa, Brazil, and Central America. Familiar members include Acanthus, Justicia, Thunbergia, and Avicennia mangroves. Several species are grown as ornamentals, and some have traditional medicinal uses.

Taxonomy

Acanthaceae belongs to the order Lamiales. Molecular studies divide it into subfamilies including Acanthoideae, Avicennioideae, Nelsonioideae, and Thunbergioideae.
