Grand Duchess Xenia Alexandrovna of Russia (Russian: Ксения Александровна; 6 April [O.S. 25 March] 1875 – 20 April 1960) was the eldest daughter of Emperor Alexander III of Russia and Empress Maria Feodorovna, and the sister of the last Russian Emperor, Nicholas II.

Early life

Xenia was born at the Anichkov Palace in Saint Petersburg. She grew up at Gatchina Palace outside the capital, in the comparatively simple and secure environment her father preferred for his children. She received a careful education from private tutors, with an emphasis on foreign languages, drawing and music.

Marriage and family

In 1894 she married her first cousin once removed, Grand Duke Alexander Mikhailovich, known in the family as "Sandro". The couple had seven children: Princess Irina Alexandrovna, Prince Andrei Alexandrovich, Prince Feodor Alexandrovich, Prince Nikita Alexandrovich, Prince Dmitri Alexandrovich, Prince Rostislav Alexandrovich and Prince Vasili Alexandrovich. Her daughter Irina married Felix Yusupov, one of the men involved in the murder of Grigori Rasputin.

Revolution and exile

After the February Revolution of 1917, Xenia left Saint Petersburg for the Crimea, where she was held under house arrest at Dulber and later at Kharax together with her mother, the Dowager Empress Maria Feodorovna, and other Romanov relatives. In April 1919 she escaped Russia aboard the British battleship HMS Marlborough, sent by King George V to rescue the Dowager Empress.

Later life

Xenia settled in England, living first at Frogmore Cottage and later in a grace-and-favour house, Wilderness House, at Hampton Court Palace. She died there on 20 April 1960 and was buried at Roquebrune-Cap-Martin in the south of France, beside her husband.
