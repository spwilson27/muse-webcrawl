The 1970 Huascarán earthquake, also known as the Ancash earthquake or the Great Peruvian earthquake, occurred on 31 May 1970 off the coast of Peru in the Pacific Ocean, near Chimbote. With a moment magnitude of 7.9, it is the most catastrophic natural disaster in the recorded history of Peru, killing approximately 70,000 people and leaving some 800,000 homeless.

The Yungay landslide

The shaking destabilised the steep north wall of Huascarán, Peru's highest peak, triggering an enormous avalanche of ice, rock and mud that broke free from the mountain's north summit. An estimated 50–100 million cubic metres of debris roared down the valley at speeds exceeding 280 km/h, completely burying the town of Yungay and killing more than 20,000 of its inhabitants within minutes; only those who reached the elevated cemetery hill and a few other high points survived — roughly 400 people. The neighbouring village of Ranrahirca was also destroyed, having previously been hit by the 1962 Huascarán avalanche.

Damage

The Callejón de Huaylas valley was devastated: Huaraz, Caraz and other towns suffered heavy destruction, with some 80–90 per cent of buildings in the worst-hit areas collapsing. Coastal cities such as Chimbote and Casma were also badly damaged. The earthquake occurred on a Sunday afternoon during a football World Cup broadcast, when many people were indoors.

Aftermath

Yungay was never rebuilt on its original site; the buried city was declared a national cemetery, marked by a memorial and the surviving palm trees of its plaza, and a new Yungay was founded nearby. The disaster prompted major international relief efforts and long-term changes to Peruvian civil defence and disaster planning. A Czechoslovak mountaineering expedition of 14 climbers on Huascarán was killed by the avalanche.
