Die (integrated circuit)

A die, in the context of integrated circuits, is a small block of semiconducting material on which a given functional circuit is fabricated. Typically, many dies are produced together on a single wafer of electronic-grade silicon through processes such as photolithography, and the wafer is later cut ("diced") into individual dies.

Manufacture

Each die on a wafer is a copy of the same circuit design. After fabrication, the wafer is tested, and working dies are separated by sawing along scribe lines, then packaged in protective housings with external pins or pads. Unpackaged dies may be used directly in chip-on-board assembly or in multi-chip modules.

Terminology

The plural of die is variously given as dies, dice, or die. Die size strongly affects manufacturing cost and yield: larger dies are more likely to contain a defect, so modern processors often adopt chiplet designs combining several smaller dies in one package.
