Colorado is a landlocked state in the Mountain West subregion of the Western United States. It is bordered by Wyoming to the north, Nebraska to the northeast, Kansas to the east, Oklahoma to the southeast, New Mexico to the south, Utah to the west, and touches Arizona to the southwest at the Four Corners. Colorado is noted for its landscape of mountains, forests, high plains, mesas, canyons, plateaus, rivers, and desert lands.

Colorado has a population of over 5.8 million as of 2023 estimates, making it the 21st most populous state. Denver is the capital, the most populous city, and the centre of the Front Range Urban Corridor.

Geography

The state encompasses most of the Southern Rocky Mountains as well as the northeastern portion of the Colorado Plateau and the western edge of the Great Plains. Colorado is the only U.S. state lying entirely above 1,000 metres elevation; it contains 58 named mountain peaks over 14,000 feet ("fourteeners"), more than any other state, including Mount Elbert, the highest summit of the Rocky Mountains. The Colorado River, the Rio Grande, and the Arkansas River all rise in the state.

History

Indigenous peoples, including Ancestral Puebloans and later Ute, Arapaho, and Cheyenne nations, inhabited the region for thousands of years; Mesa Verde's cliff dwellings are a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Parts of the territory were acquired by the United States in the Louisiana Purchase (1803) and the Mexican Cession (1848). The Pike's Peak Gold Rush of 1858–1861 drew tens of thousands of settlers; Colorado Territory was organised in 1861, and statehood followed on August 1, 1876 — earning it the name "Centennial State".

Economy and recreation

Colorado's economy includes mining, agriculture, technology, defence and aerospace installations (including NORAD and the U.S. Air Force Academy), and a large tourism sector built around ski resorts such as Aspen, Vail, and Breckenridge and four national parks: Rocky Mountain, Mesa Verde, Great Sand Dunes, and Black Canyon of the Gunnison.
