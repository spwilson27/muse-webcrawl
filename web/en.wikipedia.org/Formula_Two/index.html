Formula Two (abbreviated to F2) is a type of open-wheel formula racing category first codified in 1948. It was replaced in 1985 by Formula 3000, created as a more affordable step below Formula One. The name returned in 2009 with a short-lived FIA Formula Two Championship, and again in 2017 when the GP2 Series was rebranded as the FIA Formula 2 Championship, the principal feeder series to Formula One.

Modern Formula 2 is a spec series: all teams use an identical Dallara chassis and a Mecachrome V6 turbocharged engine, keeping costs down and emphasizing driver ability. Each round supports a Formula One Grand Prix and comprises a sprint race — with a partially reversed grid — and a longer feature race with a mandatory pit stop.

History

In the immediate post-war years, Formula Two (2-litre unsupercharged cars) provided inexpensive racing, and in 1952–53 the World Championship itself was run to F2 rules, both titles going to Alberto Ascari. Through the 1960s and 1970s the European Formula Two Championship was the finishing school for future Grand Prix stars, its champions and race winners including Jacky Ickx, Clay Regazzoni, and Ronnie Peterson. Brian Henton, Mike Thackwell, and Corrado Fabi were among the last European F2 champions before Formula 3000 replaced the category in 1985.

As a feeder series

The rebranded FIA Formula 2 Championship has graduated Charles Leclerc (2017 champion), George Russell (2018), Lando Norris, Oscar Piastri (2021), and others to Formula One seats. It sits above the FIA Formula 3 Championship on the FIA Global Pathway and awards the largest allocation of Super Licence points of any junior series.
