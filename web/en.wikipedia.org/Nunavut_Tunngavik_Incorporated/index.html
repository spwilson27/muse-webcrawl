Nunavut Tunngavik Incorporated (NTI; Inuktitut: ᓄᓇᕗᑦ ᑐᓐᖓᕕᒃ) is the organisation that represents Inuit under the Nunavut Land Claims Agreement (NLCA) of 1993. Its name means "foundation of Nunavut". NTI is responsible for ensuring that the federal and territorial governments fulfil their obligations under the Agreement, and for managing Inuit-owned lands and the capital transferred under the settlement for the benefit of all enrolled Nunavut Inuit.

History

NTI descends from the Tunngavik Federation of Nunavut, the body that negotiated the land claim on behalf of Inuit. When the NLCA was signed in 1993, NTI was created to receive the settlement's lands and funds and to act as the "designated Inuit organisation" for many purposes under the Agreement.

Functions

NTI holds title to Inuit Owned Lands (about 350,000 km², including subsurface mineral rights on roughly 38,000 km²), coordinates with the three regional Inuit associations (Kitikmeot, Kivalliq, and Qikiqtani), appoints Inuit members to co-management boards for wildlife, land, water, and environmental assessment, and administers programmes in areas such as Inuit employment, language, and wellness. In 2006 NTI sued the Government of Canada for breach of the Agreement's implementation obligations; the dispute was settled in 2015 with a payment of $255.5 million and renewed implementation commitments.

Governance

NTI is governed by an elected board and executive drawn from Nunavut Inuit, with a president elected territory-wide. It works alongside, but independently of, the Government of Nunavut, which is a public government serving all residents.
