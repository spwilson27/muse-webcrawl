Gallica

Gallica is the digital library of the Bibliothèque nationale de France (BnF) and its partners. Launched in 1997, it gives free online access to millions of digitised documents, including books, periodicals, newspapers, manuscripts, maps, sound recordings, images and sheet music.

History

Gallica went online in October 1997 with an initial collection of texts and images from the nineteenth century. The project grew out of the BnF's digitisation programme begun in the early 1990s, initially focused on building a virtual library of French-language heritage works. Over the following decades the collection expanded to include medieval manuscripts, press titles, prints and photographs, and documents contributed by partner libraries across France.

Major milestones included the integration of large-scale newspaper digitisation, the addition of legal-deposit ebooks, and partnerships with regional and university libraries that broadened the corpus beyond the BnF's own holdings. By the 2020s Gallica held more than ten million documents, with new material added continuously.

Collections

The library's holdings span printed books from the fifteenth century onward, illuminated manuscripts, maps and atlases, periodicals and daily newspapers, posters, prints, photographs, sound recordings and scores. A substantial share of the material is in the public domain and can be downloaded freely, often as PDF files or plain text derived from optical character recognition (OCR).

Access and reuse

Documents in the public domain are freely reusable; each document page provides a persistent identifier (ARK) used for citation. Interfaces such as Gallica's APIs and its integration with the Europeana aggregator allow researchers and developers to build on the corpus. Thematic selections and curated exhibitions highlight parts of the collection, and Gallica's blog showcases notable items.
