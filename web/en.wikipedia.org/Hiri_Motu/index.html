Hiri Motu

RegionPapua New Guinea, Central and Gulf provinces
Language familyAustronesian (based on Motu), pidgin/creole
Official statusOne of the official languages of Papua New Guinea

Hiri Motu, also known as Police Motu or Pidgin Motu, is a simplified version of the Motu language of Papua New Guinea. Together with Tok Pisin and English, it is one of the official languages of Papua New Guinea. It is spoken widely in the southern coastal regions, particularly around the capital, Port Moresby.

History

Hiri Motu developed from the language used in the hiri trading voyages, in which Motu-speaking sailors exchanged clay pots for sago with the peoples of the Gulf of Papua. During the colonial period it was spread by the native police force, giving it the name Police Motu, and became a lingua franca of the former Territory of Papua.

Features

The language has a reduced vocabulary and simplified grammar compared with pure Motu. Two main varieties are distinguished: the Central Province variety and the more widely used lingua-franca variety. Although its use has declined in favour of Tok Pisin and English, it remains important in its home region.
