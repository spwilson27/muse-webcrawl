Wisconsin is a state in the Upper Midwestern United States, bordered by Lake Michigan to the east, Lake Superior to the north, Minnesota, Iowa, Illinois, and Michigan. It became the 30th state in 1848. Its capital is Madison, home to the University of Wisconsin–Madison, and its largest city is Milwaukee.

History

The region was inhabited by Indigenous peoples for millennia before French explorers such as Jean Nicolet arrived in 1634. After periods of French and British control, it passed to the United States and became a territory in 1836. Waves of German, Scandinavian, and other European immigrants settled in the nineteenth century, shaping the state's culture. Wisconsin was a centre of progressive politics under Governor Robert M. La Follette in the early twentieth century.

Geography and economy

The landscape was sculpted by glaciers, leaving thousands of lakes and the distinctive Driftless Area in the southwest. Wisconsin leads the United States in cheese production and is a major producer of milk, cranberries, and ginseng; manufacturing — including machinery, paper products, and Harley-Davidson motorcycles — and tourism are also central to the economy.

Culture

The state is known for cheese curds, bratwurst, beer brewing, and passionate support for the Green Bay Packers, the only publicly owned major-league sports franchise in the United States.
