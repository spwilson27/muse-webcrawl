Diocletian's Palace (Croatian: Dioklecijanova palača) is an ancient palace built for the Roman emperor Diocletian at the end of the third century AD, which today forms about half of the old town of Split, Croatia. The "palace" was in fact a large fortified complex, part imperial residence and part military garrison, intended as Diocletian's retirement home after his abdication in 305 AD.

History

Diocletian, born in the Roman province of Dalmatia, ordered construction of the palace around 295 AD on the shore of the Adriatic Sea, near the town of Salona. After abdicating the throne on 1 May 305 — the first Roman emperor to voluntarily retire — he lived in the palace until his death around 311–316 AD, tending, according to legend, his famous cabbages.

After the fall of Salona in the 7th century, refugees from the destroyed city settled within the palace walls, transforming the imperial complex into a fortified town. Over the centuries the palace's fabric was absorbed into the growing city of Split; today some 3,000 people live within its walls, and its basements, gates, and peristyle remain remarkably preserved.

Architecture

The palace is roughly rectangular, measuring about 215 m by 175 m, enclosed by walls up to 24 m high with towers and four gates, of which the Golden Gate (Porta Aurea) on the north is the most monumental. The complex is divided by two main streets: the cardo and the decumanus. The southern half contained the emperor's apartments, the Temple of Jupiter, Diocletian's mausoleum (now Split Cathedral), and a grand peristyle courtyard. White limestone came from the island of Brač, while columns and sphinxes were imported from Egypt and the eastern Mediterranean.

Legacy

Diocletian's Palace is among the best-preserved monuments of Roman architecture in the world. It was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1979 as part of the Historical Complex of Split. The palace peristyle and cellars have also served as filming locations, notably for the television series Game of Thrones.
