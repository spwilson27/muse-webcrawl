Surrogate alcohol

Surrogate alcohol is a term for non-beverage substances containing ethanol — or, in the most dangerous cases, other alcohols — that people consume as a substitute for alcoholic drinks. Examples include mouthwash, hand sanitizer, aftershave, perfumes, medicinal tinctures, and industrial spirits.

Health risks

Consumption of surrogates is associated with poverty and severe alcohol dependence, and is a significant public-health problem in some countries, particularly where beverage alcohol is expensive or restricted. Products may contain denaturants or toxic alcohols such as methanol, whose consumption can cause blindness, organ failure, and death; mass methanol poisonings linked to surrogate or illicit alcohol recur in many regions.

Policy

Responses include adding bittering agents and taxes to non-beverage alcohol, restricting the size and strength of medicinal alcohol containers, and treatment programs for dependent drinkers.
