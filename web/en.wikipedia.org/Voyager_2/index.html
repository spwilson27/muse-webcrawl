Voyager 2 is a space probe launched by NASA on 20 August 1977, as part of the Voyager program. It is the only spacecraft to have visited all four giant outer planets — Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune — exploiting a rare planetary alignment that occurs once every 176 years. In November 2018 it became the second human-made object to enter interstellar space, following its twin Voyager 1.

History

The Voyager probes grew out of the "Grand Tour" concept of the late 1960s. Voyager 2 was launched first, on a slower trajectory than Voyager 1, which allowed it to be redirected toward Uranus and Neptune after its Saturn encounter. Its Jupiter flyby occurred in July 1979 and its Saturn flyby in August 1981.

Uranus and Neptune encounters

Voyager 2 reached Uranus in January 1986, discovering ten new moons and studying the planet's tilted magnetosphere, and Neptune in August 1989, where it observed the Great Dark Spot storm system and imaged the moon Triton, revealing active geysers on its frozen surface. No spacecraft has visited either planet since.

Interstellar mission

After the planetary encounters, the probe began its interstellar mission. It crossed the termination shock in 2007 and passed through the heliopause into interstellar space on 5 November 2018, at a distance of about 119 AU from the Sun. It carries the Golden Record, a phonograph record containing sounds and images of Earth. NASA expects the probe to continue operating some instruments into the mid-2020s before its radioisotope power source runs too low.
