Vatican City

Vatican City, officially the Vatican City State, is a landlocked sovereign city-state and microstate surrounded by Rome, Italy. With an area of 49 hectares (121 acres) and a population of roughly 800, it is the smallest state in the world both by area and population. It is governed by the Pope, who is the head of the worldwide Catholic Church, and functions as the spiritual and administrative centre of the Church.

History

The state came into existence with the Lateran Treaty of 1929 between the Holy See and Italy, resolving the "Roman Question" that had persisted since Italian unification absorbed the Papal States in 1870. It is distinct from the Holy See, which dates to early Christianity and is the entity maintaining diplomatic relations and memberships in international organisations.

Governance and defence

The Pope exercises absolute legislative, executive, and judicial power; day-to-day administration is handled by the Pontifical Commission and Governorate. Defence is provided de facto by Italy, while ceremonial and security duties fall to the Pontifical Swiss Guard, founded in 1506, and the Gendarmerie Corps.

Sites

The territory contains St Peter's Basilica — built over the tomb traditionally attributed to Saint Peter and crowned by Michelangelo's dome — St Peter's Square, the Sistine Chapel with Michelangelo's frescoes, and the Vatican Museums, among the most visited art collections in the world. The whole state is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, inscribed in 1984.
