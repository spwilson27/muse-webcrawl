Working animal

A working animal is an animal, usually domesticated, that is kept by humans and trained to perform tasks instead of being harvested for food, fibre or other animal products. Working animals include draft animals such as horses, oxen, mules, donkeys and water buffalo used for ploughing and hauling; pack animals carrying loads; riding animals; and service animals such as guide dogs, police dogs, herding dogs and detection dogs.

History

Animals have been put to work since the Neolithic Revolution. Dogs were used for hunting and herding, cattle for ploughing from around 4000 BCE, and horses for transport and war. Mechanisation in the 19th and 20th centuries greatly reduced draft-animal use in industrialised countries, but working animals remain essential in much of the developing world.

Types

Draft animals pull ploughs, carts and sledges. Pack animals carry freight on their backs. Riding and driving animals carry or pull people. Working dogs herd livestock, guard property, assist people with disabilities, and detect explosives, drugs and missing persons. Other working animals include carrier pigeons, hunting falcons, mine-clearing rats and elephants used in forestry.

Welfare

The welfare of working animals is a growing concern, addressed by charities and by standards limiting loads, working hours and requiring rest, water and veterinary care.
