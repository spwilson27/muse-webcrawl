The Coup of 18 Brumaire brought General Napoleon Bonaparte to power as First Consul of France and is generally regarded as the end of the French Revolution. In the French Republican calendar the coup took place on 18 Brumaire, Year VIII (9 November 1799).

Background

By 1799 the Directory, France's five-man executive, was widely seen as corrupt, bankrupt and unable to win the War of the Second Coalition or to govern effectively. The Abbé Sieyès, a Director, plotted a constitutional coup and recruited the popular general Napoleon, recently returned from Egypt, to provide military backing.

Events

On 18 Brumaire the Council of Ancients, citing a fictitious Jacobin plot, voted to move the legislature to Saint-Cloud and put Napoleon in command of the troops in Paris; the Directors resigned or were detained. The next day (19 Brumaire), when the Council of Five Hundred resisted and deputies shouted for Napoleon to be outlawed, his brother Lucien, the Council's president, persuaded the troops that the deputies were traitors, and grenadiers cleared the hall.

Outcome

A rump legislature dissolved the Directory and appointed Napoleon, Sieyès and Roger Ducos as provisional consuls. The Constitution of the Year VIII made Napoleon First Consul, concentrating power in his hands; the coup is often described as the point at which the Revolution gave way to Napoleon's personal rule.
