Locavore

A farmers market, a common venue for locavore food sourcing

A locavore is a person whose diet consists only or principally of food that is grown or produced locally, typically within a limited radius of their home — commonly defined as within 100 miles (160 km), though definitions range from 50 to 400 miles. The term combines the Latin locus ("place") with vorare ("to devour"), on the model of "carnivore" and "herbivore."

The word was coined in 2005 by Jessica Prentice, a San Francisco chef and food writer, for World Environment Day, when she challenged Bay Area residents to eat only food grown within 100 miles of San Francisco for a month. "Locavore" was named the New Oxford American Dictionary's Word of the Year in 2007.

Motivations

Locavores cite several reasons for eating locally: supporting local farmers and economies; reducing the environmental cost of long-distance food transport ("food miles"); obtaining fresher, more seasonal produce; and preserving regional food traditions and crop varieties. The movement is closely associated with farmers markets, community-supported agriculture (CSA) schemes, and the broader Slow Food movement.

Criticism

Critics argue that food miles are a poor proxy for environmental impact, since transportation accounts for a relatively small share of food's total carbon footprint compared with production methods; locally produced food grown in heated greenhouses, for example, may have a larger footprint than imported field-grown produce. Others note that strict localism is impractical in many climates and can conflict with food security and international development.
