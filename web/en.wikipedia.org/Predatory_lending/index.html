Predatory lending

High-cost lending storefronts are often associated with predatory lending practices.

Predatory lending refers to unethical or abusive practices by lenders that impose unfair or deceptive loan terms on borrowers, typically for the lender's benefit and often targeting vulnerable groups such as the elderly, minorities, or people with low incomes or poor credit. While definitions vary and there is no single legal definition in the United States, consumer advocates commonly cite practices such as excessive fees and interest rates far above the risk-based price, repeated refinancing that strips home equity ("loan flipping"), balloon payments, prepayment penalties, and steering borrowers into costlier loans than they qualify for.

Forms and effects

Predatory lending is associated with subprime mortgage lending, payday loans, car-title loans and some rent-to-own arrangements. Abusive mortgage lending was widely cited as a contributing factor to the foreclosure wave of the late 2000s and the 2007–2008 financial crisis, disproportionately eroding wealth in minority communities.

Regulation

In the United States, the Home Ownership and Equity Protection Act (HOEPA, 1994) imposed special rules on high-cost mortgages, the Military Lending Act caps rates for service members, and the Dodd–Frank Act (2010) created the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau and ability-to-repay rules for mortgages. Many states have enacted their own anti-predatory-lending statutes.
