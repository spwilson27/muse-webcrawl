Minitel

TypeVideotex online service
CountryFrance
Launched1982
Closed30 June 2012
OperatorFrance Télécom

Minitel was a French videotex online service accessible through telephone lines, launched commercially in 1982 by the state telecommunications administration (later France Télécom). It was one of the world's first and most successful pre-World Wide Web online services, giving millions of households access to electronic directories, banking, reservations, messaging and chat services years before the web became widespread.

Terminals were distributed free to telephone subscribers as a replacement for printed phone books. The system used a simple text-and-block-graphics display (Videotex standard), a fold-down AZERTY keyboard on later models, and a built-in modem. Services were billed through the telephone bill using the famous "kiosque" micro-payment system, identified by short codes such as 3611 for the directory and 3615 for commercial services.

Decline and closure

At its peak in the 1990s, Minitel had around nine million terminals in use and tens of thousands of services. The rise of the Internet gradually eroded its user base, though it remained popular with some businesses, farmers checking market prices, and older users. France Télécom switched the service off on 30 June 2012, ending a thirty-year run that remains a landmark in the history of online services and a frequently cited example of a successful national technology programme.
