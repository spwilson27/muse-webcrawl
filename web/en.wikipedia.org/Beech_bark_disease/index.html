Beech bark disease

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Beech bark disease is a disease complex affecting American beech (Fagus grandifolia) and European beech (Fagus sylvatica), caused by the interaction of the sap-feeding beech scale insect (Cryptococcus fagisuga) with fungi of the genus Neonectria.

Mechanism

The scale insect, introduced to Nova Scotia from Europe around 1890, pierces the bark to feed, creating wounds through which Neonectria faginata and Neonectria ditissima infect the tree. Cankers, bark cracking and "tarry spots" develop; infected trees often snap in wind (beech snap) and many die, though a small percentage show genetic resistance.

Spread and impact

The disease has spread through the northeastern United States and eastern Canada into the Great Lakes region, killing large numbers of mature beech and altering forest composition: root sprouting produces dense thickets of susceptible beech ("aftermath forests"). Management relies on retaining and propagating resistant trees.
