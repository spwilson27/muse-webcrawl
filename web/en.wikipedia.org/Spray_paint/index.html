Spray paint

Aerosol cans with different nozzle caps for varied line widths

Spray paint (also aerosol paint) is paint supplied in a sealed pressurised container and released as an aerosol spray through a valve when a button is depressed. Spray painting leaves a smooth, even surface on which brush strokes are invisible, and it is used for everything from industrial coating and furniture refinishing to graffiti and street art.

History

The aerosol can concept dates to the 1920s; the modern spray-paint can was developed in 1949 by Edward Seymour of Sycamore, Illinois, who added paint to an aluminium aerosol can at his wife's suggestion and founded Seymour of Sycamore, Inc. to manufacture it. Later manufacturers such as Krylon and Rust-Oleum popularised consumer spray paint in a wide range of colours and finishes.

Composition and use

A spray-paint can contains paint, solvent, and a propellant (historically chlorofluorocarbons, later replaced by propane-butane blends and other gases after CFCs were phased out for damaging the ozone layer), together with a mixing ball that rattles when the can is shaken. Interchangeable caps of different apertures let artists control line width from thin outlines to broad fills. Spray enamel, lacquer, primer, and specialty coatings (metallic, textured, heat-resistant) are widely available.

Graffiti and regulation

Spray paint became the defining medium of modern graffiti writing from the 1970s in New York and Philadelphia. Because of its association with vandalism, many jurisdictions restrict its sale to minors, require locked display, or mandate its removal from public property. Health concerns include solvent inhalation and volatile organic compound emissions; water-based and low-VOC formulations have been developed in response.
