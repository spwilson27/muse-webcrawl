The University of British Columbia (UBC) is a public research university with campuses in Vancouver and Kelowna (Okanagan), British Columbia, Canada. Established in 1908, it is British Columbia's oldest and largest university, enrolling about 70,000 students, and consistently ranks among the top three universities in Canada and the top public universities worldwide.

Campuses

The Vancouver campus sits at Point Grey, about 10 km west of downtown, on the traditional territory of the Musqueam people. It is home to the Museum of Anthropology, designed by Arthur Erickson and famed for its Northwest Coast Indigenous collections, the Beaty Biodiversity Museum, and TRIUMF, Canada's national particle accelerator centre. The Okanagan campus in Kelowna opened in 2005 on the site of Okanagan University College.

Academics

UBC is a member of the U15 group of Canadian research universities. Its research output is among the highest in Canada, and its alumni and faculty include eight Nobel laureates — among them Michael Smith, who won the 1993 chemistry prize for site-directed mutagenesis — and three prime ministers of Canada, including Justin Trudeau. The university is known for the Sauder School of Business, the Peter A. Allard School of Law, and strong programs in forestry, oceanography, and medicine.

Athletics

The Vancouver teams are the Thunderbirds and the Okanagan teams the Heat. UBC has won numerous national championships, notably in football (Vanier Cup), soccer, and swimming.
