Chancel

In church architecture, the chancel is the space around the altar, including the choir and the sanctuary, at the liturgical east end of a traditional Christian church building. It may terminate in an apse. The chancel is typically raised above the level of the nave and separated from it by steps, a rood screen, or a communion rail.

History

The word derives from the Latin cancelli, meaning a lattice or screen, referring to the barrier that once separated the clergy from the congregation. In early churches the area around the altar was reserved for the clergy and choir. During the Middle Ages the chancel became a distinct architectural element, often with choir stalls for the singing of the Divine Office.

Use

Responsibility for maintaining the chancel traditionally rested with the rector rather than the parishioners, which in England gave rise to the legal concept of chancel repair liability. In many modern churches the distinction between chancel and nave has softened, reflecting liturgical reforms that encourage congregational participation.
