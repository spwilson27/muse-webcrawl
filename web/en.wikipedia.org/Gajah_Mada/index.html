Gajah Mada (c. 1290 – c. 1364) was a powerful military leader and mahapatih (the approximate equivalent of a governing prime minister) of the Javanese empire of Majapahit during its 14th-century golden age. He is credited in Old Javanese manuscripts, poems and inscriptions with bringing the empire to its peak of glory, extending its influence throughout much of the Maritime Southeast Asian archipelago.

Career

Little is known of Gajah Mada's origins; later accounts suggest humble birth. He rose through service in the royal guard and distinguished himself by suppressing the rebellion of Ra Kuti in 1319, saving King Jayanegara. He became mahapatih under Queen Tribhuwana Wijayatunggadewi around 1331, and served her son Hayam Wuruk from 1350.

The Palapa oath

According to the Pararaton, at his inauguration Gajah Mada swore the Palapa oath (Sumpah Palapa): he would not taste "palapa" — interpreted as spice, salt, or worldly pleasure — until he had subdued the whole Nusantara archipelago under Majapahit. Under his leadership, Majapahit extended its power over Sumatra, the Malay Peninsula, parts of Borneo, Sulawesi, the Lesser Sunda Islands and beyond, though scholars debate how direct this control was. His career ended after the Bubat tragedy of 1357, in which a Sundanese royal wedding party was killed; he fell from favour and died around 1364.

Legacy

Gajah Mada is regarded as a national hero in Indonesia. Gadjah Mada University in Yogyakarta is named after him, as are streets throughout the country, and the Indonesian satellite Palapa was named for his oath.
