Ace Cafe

Ace Cafe London
Founded1938 (original); reopened 1997
LocationAce Corner, North Circular Road, Stonebridge, London NW10
TypeTransport café; biker and rocker venue

The Ace Cafe is a historic transport café on the North Circular Road in Stonebridge, north-west London. Originally opened in 1938 to serve travellers on the newly built arterial road, it became famous in the 1950s and 1960s as a gathering place for young motorcyclists — the "ton-up boys" and rockers — who raced between cafés and developed the café racer motorcycle style. Its jukeboxes played the rock and roll music banned or restricted on BBC radio.

History

The café operated around the clock and became a focal point of post-war youth culture; it was also a base for the Christian motorcyclists of the 59 Club, founded by Father Bill Shergold, who held services for rockers. As car ownership rose and the original building was damaged and eventually demolished, the Ace closed in 1969 and the site was used as a tyre depot.

Reopening

Beginning with a reunion event in 1994 organised by Mark Wilsmore, the café was restored and fully reopened in 1997, with the rest of the original building work completed by 2001. It now hosts regular bike and car meets, live music and charity runs, and the Ace Cafe name has been licensed internationally, with branches opened in places including Orlando, Florida, and Beijing.
