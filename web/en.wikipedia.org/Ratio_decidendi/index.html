Ratio decidendi

Ratio decidendi (Latin plural rationes decidendi) is a Latin phrase meaning "the reason" or "the rationale for the decision". In common law, the ratio decidendi of a case is the point in the case that determines the judgement — the principle of law on which the court's decision is founded. It is the binding part of a judicial decision under the doctrine of precedent (stare decisis).

Distinction from obiter dicta

Statements in a judgment that are not essential to the outcome are called obiter dicta ("things said by the way"). Obiter remarks may be persuasive but do not bind later courts, whereas the ratio is binding on lower courts and, depending on the system, on courts of equal standing. Identifying the ratio within often lengthy judgments is a central skill of legal analysis; it is usually formulated by reference to the material facts of the case and the legal rule applied to them.

Interpretation

Different tests have been proposed for extracting the ratio, including the "material facts" approach and Goodhart's method. Later courts may distinguish a precedent by finding its material facts differ, thereby narrowing the ratio's reach, or treat broad statements of principle as the ratio. In systems with multiple concurring judgments, determining a single ratio can be genuinely difficult.
