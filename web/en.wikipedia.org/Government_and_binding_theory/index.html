Government and binding theory

Government and binding theory (GB), also known as government-binding theory, is a framework of syntax and a theory of grammar in linguistics, proposed by Noam Chomsky in his Lectures on Government and Binding (1981). It was the dominant form of generative grammar through the 1980s and into the 1990s, before being largely superseded by the Minimalist Program.

Framework

GB replaced earlier transformational models with a modular system in which several autonomous sub-theories interact: X-bar theory (phrase structure), case theory (licensing of noun phrases), theta theory (assignment of semantic roles), control theory, bounding theory (constraints on movement), government (a structural relation between a head and its dependents), and binding theory (the distribution of anaphors, pronouns, and referring expressions).

Binding theory

The theory's best-known component, binding theory, classifies nominal expressions into anaphors (such as himself), pronouns (such as him), and R-expressions (such as John), governed by three principles: Principle A requires an anaphor to be bound in its local domain; Principle B requires a pronoun to be free in its local domain; Principle C requires an R-expression to be free everywhere.

Legacy

Although Minimalism abandoned much of GB's machinery, concepts such as c-command, binding domains, and the EPP remain influential in syntactic research, and GB remains a standard part of linguistics curricula.
