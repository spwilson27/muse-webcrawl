Electrodiagnosis

Electrodiagnosis (EDX) is a branch of medicine that uses the electrical properties of the body to diagnose disorders of nerves and muscles. It is closely associated with neurology and physical medicine and rehabilitation.

Techniques

The main techniques are nerve conduction studies (NCS), which measure how fast and how well peripheral nerves transmit impulses, and needle electromyography (EMG), which records the electrical activity of muscles at rest and during contraction. Evoked potentials, which measure responses of the brain and spinal cord to stimulation, are a related method.

Uses

Electrodiagnostic testing helps diagnose carpal tunnel syndrome and other entrapment neuropathies, radiculopathy, peripheral polyneuropathy, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, myasthenia gravis, and muscle diseases, and helps localize and grade nerve injuries.
