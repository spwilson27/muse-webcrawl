A road train, also known in Australia as a land train or large combination vehicle, is a truck consisting of a prime mover towing two or more trailers or semi-trailers. Road trains are used most extensively in remote areas of Australia to transport freight efficiently over long distances, and are also found in parts of Canada, the United States, Argentina and Europe in more restricted forms.

Australia

Australian road trains can exceed 50 metres in length, with the longest configurations — triples and quads — restricted to outback highways in Western Australia, the Northern Territory, South Australia and Queensland. They haul livestock, fuel, ore and general freight to isolated communities and mines. Because of their size and mass, they require specialised licences, regulated routes and speed limits, and feature prominently in outback road-safety campaigns urging other drivers to allow generous overtaking room.
