Sagarmala

CountryIndia
Launched2015 (approved by the Union Cabinet)
MinistryMinistry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways
AimPort-led development; reduce logistics costs

Sagarmala (Hindi for "garland of the sea") is a programme of the Government of India to promote port-led development along India's roughly 7,500 km coastline and 14,500 km of potentially navigable waterways. Approved in 2015, it seeks to modernise India's ports, improve their connectivity to the hinterland, develop coastal economic zones and support coastal communities, with the goal of cutting the cost of logistics for domestic and EXIM trade.

Pillars

The programme rests on four pillars:

Port modernisation and new port development — mechanisation, capacity expansion and technology upgrades at the twelve major ports, and development of new greenfield ports, including Vadhavan in Maharashtra and Galathea Bay in Great Nicobar.

Port connectivity enhancement — rail, road, inland waterway and pipeline links between ports and production and consumption centres, including last-mile rail connectivity projects.

Port-linked industrialisation — coastal economic zones and clusters in sectors such as energy, materials and discrete manufacturing, located near ports to cut transport costs.

Coastal community development — skill development, fisheries infrastructure and livelihood projects for communities in coastal districts.

Implementation

Several hundred projects, with an estimated investment of several lakh crore rupees, were identified under Sagarmala for phased execution by central ministries, state governments, port authorities and private partners. Implementation is coordinated by the Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways, and the Sagarmala Development Company Limited was set up to assist with project development and financing. The programme is frequently discussed alongside Bharatmala (highways) and the Dedicated Freight Corridors as part of India's wider logistics strategy, and has been folded into the framework of the PM Gati Shakti National Master Plan.

Criticism

Commentators have noted slow execution, land acquisition difficulties, environmental concerns around new ports in ecologically sensitive areas, and the risk of underused capacity; fisherfolk organisations have raised concerns about displacement and access to the coast.
