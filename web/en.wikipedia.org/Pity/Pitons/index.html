Pitons

Pitons
Location: Saint Lucia, Caribbean
Elevation: Gros Piton 798 m; Petit Piton 743 m
Designated: UNESCO World Heritage Site (2004)

The Pitons are two mountainous volcanic plugs and spires located in Saint Lucia. The larger of the two, Gros Piton, is 798.25 metres (2,619 feet) high; the other, Petit Piton, is 743 metres (2,438 feet) high.

The Pitons are linked by the Piton Mitan ridge. They are located near the town of Soufrière on the southwest coast of the island, within the Soufrière Marine Management Area. The Pitons are a World Heritage Site, designated in 2004, covering 2,909 hectares (7,190 acres) and including a marine area of 875 hectares.

Geography and ecology

The Pitons are volcanic plugs formed from the cooled lava of an eroded volcanic complex. The surrounding area is rich in geothermal activity, including the Sulphur Springs, and supports tropical rainforest habitat with a wide variety of plant species, including endemic species. Coral reefs cover almost 60% of the site's marine area, hosting a diverse array of fish and other marine life.

Tourism

Gros Piton can be climbed without ropes or mountaineering experience, with a typical round trip taking about four hours when accompanied by a local guide. Petit Piton is steeper and more dangerous and is generally not recommended for climbing. The Pitons appear on the flag of Saint Lucia and are the namesake of the local Piton beer brewed on the island.
