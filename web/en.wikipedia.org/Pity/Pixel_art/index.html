Pixel art

Pixel art is a form of digital art drawn with graphical software where images are built using pixels as the only building block. It is widely associated with the low-resolution graphics of 1980s and 1990s video games, in which it served as the standard visual style because of the technical limitations of the hardware of the time. In much the same way, similar aesthetics were used in embroidery and mosaics, with patterns constructed from small coloured units, long before the digital era.

The aesthetic of pixel art comes from the limitations of early computing hardware: limited colour palettes, low screen resolutions, and limited storage space forced artists to work economically, choosing every pixel deliberately. Many pixel artists restrict themselves to the palette and canvas size of a target retro system even when working on modern hardware.

History

Early computer graphics in the 1970s and 1980s were necessarily pixel based. Arcade games and home computers such as the Commodore 64 and the Nintendo Entertainment System produced distinctive looks that later generations of artists emulated deliberately. With the move to 3D graphics and high-resolution displays in the 1990s, pixel art declined in mainstream commercial games but survived on handheld consoles, and later enjoyed a revival in independent game development.

Techniques

Common techniques include careful colour palette selection, anti-aliasing done manually pixel by pixel, and dithering — arranging pixels of two colours in patterns to suggest an intermediate colour or gradient. Pixel art is typically created in software ranging from general-purpose editors with grid tools to specialised programs designed for sprites and tiles.

Uses

Pixel art is most commonly used in video games, especially indie and retro-styled titles, as well as in icons, avatars, and digital illustrations. Related styles include voxel art, which applies a similar blocky aesthetic in three dimensions.
