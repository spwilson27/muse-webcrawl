Volcanic plug

A volcanic plug, also called a volcanic neck or lava neck, is a geological landform created when magma hardens within the vent of an active volcano. When erosion removes the softer surrounding rock, the resistant plug is left standing as a steep-sided tower or spire.

Examples

Famous examples include Devils Tower in Wyoming, USA — the first United States National Monument — Shiprock in New Mexico, and the Pitons of Saint Lucia, a pair of plugs rising from the Caribbean Sea that form a UNESCO World Heritage Site. In Scotland, Castle Rock in Edinburgh and North Berwick Law are eroded plugs.

Formation

Plugs form when viscous magma solidifies in the conduit of a volcano and the edifice around it is stripped away over millions of years. Because the plug rock is often harder and more fractured than the host rock, plugs can be hazardous sources of rockfall, and they may preserve valuable evidence of a volcano's internal plumbing.
