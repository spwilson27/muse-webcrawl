Sprite (computer graphics)

In computer graphics, a sprite is a two-dimensional bitmap that is integrated into a larger scene, most often in a 2D video game. Originally, the term referred to hardware objects — such as those generated by chips in the Atari 2600, Commodore 64 and Nintendo Entertainment System — that could be moved independently of the background.

Hardware sprites

Early game consoles and home computers provided dedicated sprite hardware that composited small images over the playfield during scan-out, freeing the CPU. The number, size and colours per sprite were strictly limited, shaping the look of 8-bit and 16-bit games. Sprite multiplexing and other programming tricks pushed beyond nominal limits.

Modern usage

Today sprites are usually textured quads drawn by a GPU, but the concept survives in sprite sheets, CSS sprites that combine interface images into one file, and 2D game engines such as Unity and Godot, where "sprite" remains the standard term for a movable 2D image.
