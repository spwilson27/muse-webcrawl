Raster graphics

In computer graphics, raster graphics (also called bitmap graphics) represent an image as a rectangular grid of pixels, each with its own colour value. Raster formats are resolution-dependent: enlarging an image beyond its native resolution produces visible pixelation. Common raster formats include JPEG, PNG, GIF, BMP and TIFF.

Comparison with vector graphics

Unlike vector graphics, which describe images with geometric primitives that scale cleanly, raster graphics are ideal for photographs and complex colour gradients. Most images captured by cameras and scanners are raster images, and most displays are raster devices that ultimately render vectors into pixels.

Colour depth

Each pixel stores a fixed number of bits — 1-bit for monochrome, 8-bit for greyscale or indexed colour, and 24-bit "true colour" with 8 bits per channel. Higher bit depths, such as 10-bit per channel, reduce banding in gradients and are standard in professional imaging.
