Soufrière, Saint Lucia

Soufrière is a town on the west coast of Saint Lucia in the Caribbean. Founded by the French in 1746, it was the island's original capital before Castries. The town sits in the caldera of the dormant Qualibou volcano, near the famous Sulphur Springs — marketed as the world's only "drive-in volcano" — and beneath the twin peaks of the Pitons.

Attractions

Visitors come for the Sulphur Springs mud baths, the Diamond Botanical Gardens and waterfall, historic sugar estates such as Fond Doux, and snorkelling at Anse Chastanet. The nearby Pitons Management Area is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Josephine Bonaparte, later Empress of France, spent part of her childhood on a plantation near Soufrière.

1993 and later

The town's economy shifted from sugar and lime production to tourism in the later 20th century. Soufrière remains a fishing community and the gateway to Saint Lucia's most visited natural attractions.
