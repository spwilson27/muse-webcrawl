Pixel

In digital imaging, a pixel (short for picture element) is the smallest addressable element in a raster image, or the smallest point in an all-points-addressable display device. Each pixel is a sample of an original image; more samples typically provide a more accurate representation. The intensity of each pixel is variable, and in colour systems each pixel is usually composed of red, green and blue subpixels.

Terminology

A megapixel is one million pixels, a common measure for digital camera sensors. Related concepts include the texel (texture element), voxel (volume element) and surfel. On displays, pixels are governed by standards of resolution expressed as width × height, such as 1920 × 1080.

Subpixels and geometry

LCD and OLED screens render each pixel from separate red, green and blue subpixels, often in a stripe or PenTile arrangement. The geometry of pixels — square on most displays, rectangular in some video standards — affects how images are scaled and anti-aliased.
