Saint Lucia is an island country in the eastern Caribbean, part of the Windward Islands chain of the Lesser Antilles. Its dramatic volcanic landscape is dominated by the Pitons, two towering spires on the southwest coast that are a UNESCO World Heritage Site and a national symbol appearing on the country's flag imagery and beer brand.

History

The island was first settled by Arawak and later Carib peoples. France and Britain fought over Saint Lucia so often — it changed hands fourteen times — that it was called the "Helen of the West Indies". Britain gained definitive control in 1814, and Saint Lucia became independent in 1979 while remaining a Commonwealth realm.

Economy and culture

Tourism, bananas and offshore services drive the economy. Saint Lucia has produced two Nobel laureates — economist W. Arthur Lewis and poet Derek Walcott — the most per capita of any country. The annual Saint Lucia Jazz Festival and Carnival are major cultural events.
