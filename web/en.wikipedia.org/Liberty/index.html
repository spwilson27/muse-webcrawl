Liberty

Liberty is the state of being free within society from oppressive restrictions imposed by authority on one's way of life, behaviour or political views. The concept is central to political philosophy, law and democratic theory, though its meaning is contested.

Concepts

Isaiah Berlin's 1958 lecture "Two Concepts of Liberty" distinguished negative liberty — freedom from interference by others — from positive liberty — freedom to act on one's will and realise one's potential. Republican traditions define liberty as non-domination, freedom from arbitrary power. Philosophers also debate the relation between liberty and equality, property, and the rule of law.

History

Ideas of liberty developed from ancient notions of the free citizen, through Magna Carta and early modern natural rights theory, to the American and French Revolutions, abolitionism, and modern civil liberties movements. Constitutional systems protect specific liberties such as speech, religion, assembly and movement.
