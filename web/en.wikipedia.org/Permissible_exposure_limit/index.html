Permissible exposure limit

The permissible exposure limit (PEL or OSHA PEL) is a legal limit in the United States for exposure of an employee to a chemical substance or physical agent such as noise. Permissible exposure limits are established by the Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA).

Definitions

Most PELs are expressed as an 8-hour time-weighted average (TWA) concentration in air, measured in parts per million or milligrams per cubic metre. Some substances also have short-term exposure limits (STELs) over 15 minutes and ceiling limits that must never be exceeded. OSHA's PELs cover roughly 500 substances and are published in 29 CFR 1910.1000 (Tables Z-1, Z-2 and Z-3).

Comparison with other limits

Many OSHA PELs date from 1970 and are considered outdated relative to the recommended exposure limits (RELs) of NIOSH and the threshold limit values (TLVs) of the ACGIH, which are updated more frequently but are not legally binding. PELs also exist for noise (90 dBA TWA) and other physical agents.
