Thermistor

A thermistor is a type of resistor whose electrical resistance varies strongly with temperature, used widely as a temperature sensor. The word combines "thermal" and "resistor". Thermistors are typically made from ceramic or polymer semiconductors such as sintered metal oxides.

Types

NTC (negative temperature coefficient) thermistors decrease in resistance as temperature rises and are common in thermometers and inrush-current limiters. PTC thermistors increase sharply in resistance above a transition temperature and are used as self-resetting fuses and heaters.

Applications

Their resistance–temperature relationship is described approximately by the Steinhart–Hart equation. Thermistors are cheap and precise over narrow ranges but nonlinear compared with platinum resistance thermometers.
