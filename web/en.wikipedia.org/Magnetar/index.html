Magnetar

A magnetar is a type of neutron star with an extremely powerful magnetic field, typically 1014 to 1015 gauss — a thousand times stronger than an ordinary pulsar's and quadrillions of times Earth's field. Decay of the field powers emission of X-rays and gamma rays.

Characteristics

Magnetars are about 20 km across, rotate slowly (periods of 2–12 seconds), and include soft gamma repeaters (SGRs) and anomalous X-ray pulsars (AXPs). Starquakes in their solid crust and magnetic reconnection produce bursts; giant flares, such as the 27 December 2004 flare from SGR 1806−20, are among the most energetic events known.

Formation and theory

The magnetar model was developed by Robert Duncan and Christopher Thompson in 1992. A dynamo in a rapidly spinning newborn neutron star may amplify its field; alternatively fields may be inherited and compressed from the progenitor star. Magnetars are candidate sources of some fast radio bursts, confirmed when SGR 1935+2154 produced an FRB-like burst in 2020.

Known examples

About 30 magnetars are catalogued in the Milky Way and Magellanic Clouds, including SGR 1806−20, 1E 1048.1−5937, and the central source in supernova remnant RCW 103.
