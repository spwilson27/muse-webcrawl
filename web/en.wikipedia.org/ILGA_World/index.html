ILGA World

The International Lesbian, Gay, Bisexual, Trans and Intersex Association (ILGA World) is an international organization bringing together more than 1,900 LGBTI groups from over 160 countries and territories. It campaigns for lesbian, gay, bisexual, trans and intersex human rights on the international level, and provides research, advocacy and capacity-building for its member organisations. Its headquarters are in Geneva, Switzerland.

History

Founded in Coventry, England, in 1978 as the International Gay Association (IGA), the organisation adopted the name International Lesbian and Gay Association in 1986 and progressively broadened its remit to include bisexual, trans and intersex people. ILGA gained ECOSOC consultative status at the United Nations in 1993, lost it in 1994 amid controversy, and regained it in 2011. In 2019 it rebranded its public name as ILGA World. Regional bodies include ILGA-Europe, Pan Africa ILGA, ILGA Asia, ILGA-LAC, ILGA North America and ILGA Oceania.

Research and reports

ILGA World is known for its State-Sponsored Homophobia report (published since 2006), a global survey of laws affecting people on the basis of sexual orientation, and its Trans Legal Mapping Report and Sexual Orientation Laws in the World maps and databases. Its research tracks criminalisation of consensual same-sex activity, legal protections, recognition of relationships and gender identity, and restrictions on civil society.

Conferences and advocacy

ILGA World holds periodic world conferences — recent editions in Long Beach (2022) and Cape Town (2024) — which elect its executive board and co-secretaries-general and set the federation's direction. At the United Nations it advocates before the Human Rights Council, treaty bodies and the Universal Periodic Review, and supports activists from countries where LGBTI people face criminalisation and violence.
