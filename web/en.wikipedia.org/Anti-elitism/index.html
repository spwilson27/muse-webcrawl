Anti-elitism

Anti-elitism is a belief or attitude opposed to elites — the small groups held to exercise disproportionate power, wealth or cultural authority in a society. It holds that elites are corrupt, self-serving or out of touch, and that political and cultural authority should rest with ordinary people instead. Anti-elitism is a core ingredient of populism, across both left-wing and right-wing varieties.

Forms

Anti-elitism may target political establishments, economic elites such as bankers and large corporations, or cultural and intellectual elites including academics, experts and mainstream media. In some forms it shades into anti-intellectualism — suspicion of expertise itself — while in others it is a critique of unaccountable privilege that coexists with respect for knowledge. Historically, movements from the Levellers and Jacksonian democracy to modern populist parties have mobilised anti-elite sentiment.

Criticism

Critics argue that anti-elitism can be manipulated by wealthy or powerful figures who present themselves as outsiders, that it can undermine trust in necessary institutions and expertise, and that "the elite" is often defined so loosely that the charge becomes unfalsifiable. Defenders reply that concentrated, unaccountable power is a genuine democratic problem.
