George Mortimer Pullman (March 3, 1831 – October 19, 1897) was an American engineer and industrialist. He designed and manufactured the Pullman sleeping car, which transformed long-distance rail travel, and founded the company town of Pullman, Illinois.

Sleeping car business

After working as a building mover in Chicago, Pullman turned to railroad cars. His luxurious "Pioneer" sleeping car of 1864 gained national attention, and the Pullman Palace Car Company, founded in 1867, came to dominate the market, leasing cars and crews — including the famous Pullman porters — to railroads.

Company town and strike

In 1880 Pullman built a model town for his workers south of Chicago, controlling housing, shops and services. During the depression of 1893 he cut wages but not rents, provoking the Pullman Strike of 1894, a nationwide boycott led by Eugene V. Debs that was broken by federal troops and court injunctions.

Death and legacy

Pullman died in 1897 and was buried in Graceland Cemetery under a reinforced concrete vault. The Pullman district of Chicago is now a national monument.
