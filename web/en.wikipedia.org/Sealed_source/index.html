Sealed source

A sealed source is a radioactive material that is permanently encapsulated in a capsule or closely bonded covering designed to prevent leakage or escape of the material under normal conditions of use and foreseeable accidental conditions.

Uses

Sealed sources are used in industrial radiography, medical radiotherapy and brachytherapy, well logging, thickness and level gauges, irradiators, and calibration instruments. Common radionuclides include cobalt-60, caesium-137, iridium-192, and americium-241.

Safety and regulation

Because a damaged or abandoned source can pose a serious hazard, sealed sources are licensed and tracked by regulators; the IAEA categorises sources by activity from Category 1 (most dangerous) to Category 5. An "orphan source" is one that has fallen outside regulatory control.
