Isobar (meteorology)

DefinitionLine joining points of equal atmospheric pressure
Used onSurface weather charts
Typical interval4 hPa
Related termsIsotherm, isohyet, contour line

An isobar (from Greek isos, equal, and baros, weight) is a line on a map connecting points having the same atmospheric pressure at a given time or on average over a given period. In meteorology, isobars drawn on surface weather charts (usually at intervals of four hectopascals) are fundamental tools for analysing weather systems.

Interpretation

Closed isobars around a centre of low pressure mark a cyclone (marked "L"), and around high pressure an anticyclone ("H"). The spacing of the isobars indicates the pressure gradient: closely packed isobars mean a steep gradient and strong winds, while widely spaced isobars indicate light winds. Because of the Coriolis effect, winds in the Northern Hemisphere blow roughly parallel to the isobars — clockwise around high pressure and anticlockwise around low pressure (Buys Ballot's law) — with a slight inward component near the surface due to friction.

Use in forecasting

Analysing isobar patterns reveals fronts, troughs, ridges and cols, and the movement of isobars over time shows how weather systems are developing and travelling. Isobars are usually reduced to mean sea level so that values can be compared between stations at different elevations.
