Jay Ward

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Joseph Ward Cohen Jr. (September 20, 1920 – October 12, 1989), better known as Jay Ward, was an American creator and producer of animated TV cartoon shows. He produced animated series based on such characters as Crusader Rabbit, Rocky and Bullwinkle, Dudley Do-Right, Peabody and Sherman, Hoppity Hooper, George of the Jungle, Tom Slick, and Super Chicken. His own company, Jay Ward Productions, designed the trademark characters on the Cap'n Crunch, Quisp, and Quake breakfast cereals and made TV spots for businesses such as Cheerios, Cocoa Puffs, and Trix.

Early life and career

Ward was born in San Francisco, California, and grew up in Berkeley. He studied at the University of California, Berkeley, and Harvard Business School. After serving in the Army, he went into real estate, but a car accident that seriously injured him helped turn his attention to animation. With his childhood friend Alex Anderson, he pitched an idea for a cartoon made for television.

Crusader Rabbit and Rocky and Bullwinkle

The result was Crusader Rabbit (1949–1952), the first animated series produced specifically for television. Ward later partnered with writer Bill Scott to create Rocky and His Friends (1959–1961) and The Bullwinkle Show (1961–1964), featuring Rocket "Rocky" J. Squirrel and Bullwinkle J. Moose in satirical adventures against the Cold War spies Boris Badenov and Natasha Fatale. The shows were noted for witty scripts, puns, and humour aimed at adults as well as children, with animation outsourced to a studio in Mexico City to save costs.

Later work and legacy

Ward's later productions included Hoppity Hooper (1964) and George of the Jungle (1967). His studio's advertising work created cereal mascots that remained in use for decades. Ward died of lymphoma in West Hollywood, California, in 1989. A star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame honours him, and his company's legacy continued through later film adaptations of his characters.
