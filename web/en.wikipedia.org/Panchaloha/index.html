Panchaloha

Panchaloha (Sanskrit: पञ्चलोह, "five metals") is a traditional alloy of five metals — gold, silver, copper, zinc (or brass), and iron (in some traditions lead replaces zinc) — used in South India for casting sacred images (murtis) and temple objects in accordance with the Shilpa Shastras, the ancient texts governing sculpture and iconography.

Composition and symbolism

The five metals are considered sacred and are associated with cosmic elements and deities; their combination is believed to give icons spiritual potency and durability. Traditional practice prescribes specific proportions described in the agamas, though in practice precious metals (gold and silver) are used in small quantities. Related multi-metal alloys include ashtadhatu (eight metals) used in northern India for Jain and Hindu images.

Use

Panchaloha icons are made by the lost-wax (cire perdue) casting method, notably by the hereditary sthapati (sculptor) workshops of Swamimalai in Tamil Nadu, famous for Chola-style bronzes. Panchaloha processional images (utsava murtis) are carried in temple festivals, and smaller panchaloha idols are widely sold for home worship. Because of their metal value and antiquity, historic panchaloha and bronze icons have frequently been targets of art theft and smuggling, prompting international restitution efforts.
