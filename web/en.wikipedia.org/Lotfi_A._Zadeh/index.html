Lotfi Aliasker Zadeh (February 4, 1921 – September 6, 2017) was a mathematician, computer scientist, electrical engineer, artificial intelligence researcher, and professor of computer science at the University of California, Berkeley. He is best known for proposing fuzzy mathematics, consisting of several related concepts: fuzzy sets, fuzzy logic, fuzzy systems, and fuzzy control.

Early life and education

Zadeh was born in Baku, then part of the Azerbaijan Soviet Socialist Republic, to an Azerbaijani mother who was a pediatrician and an Iranian-Azerbaijani father who was a journalist. In 1931 the family moved to Tehran, Iran, where Zadeh attended Alborz College and later the University of Tehran, graduating in electrical engineering in 1942. He emigrated to the United States in 1944, earning a master's degree from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology in 1946 and a Ph.D. from Columbia University in 1949.

Career

Zadeh taught at Columbia University before joining the faculty of the University of California, Berkeley in 1959, where he served as chair of the electrical engineering department from 1963 to 1968. With Charles Desoer he co-authored Linear System Theory: The State Space Approach (1963). His earlier work, with John Ragazzini, introduced the z-transform method of discrete-time signal processing.

Fuzzy sets and fuzzy logic

In his landmark 1965 paper "Fuzzy Sets," published in Information and Control, Zadeh proposed a generalization of classical set theory in which an element's membership in a set is a matter of degree, expressed by a membership function taking values in the interval [0, 1]. The theory initially met skepticism from parts of the mathematics and engineering establishment, but from the 1980s onward fuzzy logic found extensive commercial application, especially in Japan, in control systems for appliances, cameras, elevators, and the Sendai subway.

Zadeh later developed related frameworks including "computing with words" and generalized theory of uncertainty, arguing that human reasoning deals primarily with perceptions rather than precise measurements.

Honors

Zadeh was a member of the National Academy of Engineering and received numerous awards, including the IEEE Medal of Honor (1995) and the Benjamin Franklin Medal in Electrical Engineering (2009). He died at his home in Berkeley in 2017 and was buried in Baku, Azerbaijan.
