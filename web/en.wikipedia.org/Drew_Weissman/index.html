Drew Weissman (born 1959) is an American immunologist and professor of medicine at the Perelman School of Medicine at the University of Pennsylvania. He is best known for his work, together with Katalin Karikó, on modifying messenger RNA (mRNA) so that it can be used safely in vaccines and therapies.

Work on mRNA

In 2005, Weissman and Karikó published their landmark finding that replacing the nucleoside uridine in synthetic mRNA with pseudouridine prevented the mRNA from provoking a strong inflammatory immune response and greatly increased protein production. This nucleoside-modified mRNA technology became the basis of the Pfizer–BioNTech and Moderna COVID-19 vaccines.

Honours

Weissman and Karikó received numerous awards for this work, including the Lasker–DeBakey Clinical Medical Research Award (2021), the Breakthrough Prize in Life Sciences (2022), and the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 2023, "for their discoveries concerning nucleoside base modifications that enabled the development of effective mRNA vaccines against COVID-19". Weissman has continued research into mRNA vaccines for influenza, HIV, and other diseases, and serves as director of the Penn Institute for RNA Innovation.
