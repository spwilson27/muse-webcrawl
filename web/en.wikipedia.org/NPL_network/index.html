NPL network

The NPL network, or NPL Data Communications Network, was a local area computer network operated by a team from the National Physical Laboratory (NPL) in London, England, that pioneered the concept of packet switching. Along with the ARPANET in the United States, it was one of the first two computer networks in the world to implement packet switching.

History

Donald Davies coined the term "packet" and developed the design at the NPL from 1965, publishing the proposal in 1967. A local network, the Mark I, came into service in 1970 to serve NPL's own computing needs; the more advanced Mark II operated from 1973 until 1986. The NPL team — including Derek Barber, Roger Scantlebury, and Peter Wilkinson — influenced the design of the ARPANET and later European networking research.

Design

The network connected terminals and computers via interface computers handling fixed-size packets, early work on flow control, and high-speed local links running initially at 768 kbit/s and later at several Mbit/s — unusually fast for the era. The NPL's mathematical modelling of packet networks also contributed foundational results in queuing and congestion studies.
