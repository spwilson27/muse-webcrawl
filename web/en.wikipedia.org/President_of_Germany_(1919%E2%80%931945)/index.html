President of Germany (1919–1945)

The President of Germany (German: Reichspräsident) was the head of state of the German Reich from 1919 to 1945, under the Weimar Constitution and into the early Nazi period. The office was held by Friedrich Ebert (1919–1925) and Paul von Hindenburg (1925–1934).

Role

The Reichspräsident was elected by popular vote for a seven-year term. He appointed and dismissed the Chancellor and government, could dissolve the Reichstag, and under Article 48 of the constitution could rule by emergency decree — powers that proved fateful in the republic's final years.

End of the office

After Hindenburg appointed Adolf Hitler as Chancellor in January 1933, presidential authority was progressively hollowed out. On Hindenburg's death on 2 August 1934, Hitler merged the presidency with the chancellorship and took the title Führer und Reichskanzler. The office was briefly revived in 1945 under Karl Dönitz as Reichspräsident of the Flensburg Government.
