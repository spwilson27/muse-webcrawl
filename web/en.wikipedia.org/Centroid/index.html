Centroid

In mathematics and physics, the centroid, also known as the geometric center or center of figure, of a plane figure or solid figure is the arithmetic mean position of all the points in the surface or volume of the figure. Informally, it is the point at which a cutout of the shape (with uniformly distributed mass) could be balanced on the tip of a pin.

Properties

The centroid of a finite set of points is simply the average of their coordinates. For a triangle, the centroid is the intersection of the three medians and divides each median in a 2:1 ratio; it coincides with the triangle's center of mass if the triangle has uniform density. For compound shapes, centroids can be computed by decomposing the shape into simpler pieces and weighting their centroids by area or volume. In physics, the centroid of a uniform-density body equals its center of mass.

Applications

Centroids are used in engineering for stress and load calculations, in geography for population centers, and in cluster analysis, where the k-means algorithm repeatedly assigns points to the nearest centroid.
