Scratching


Image placeholder: Scratching

Scratching, sometimes referred to as scrubbing, is a DJ technique of moving a vinyl record back and forth on a turntable to produce percussive or melodic sounds. It is a defining element of hip-hop music and turntablism, and is also used in other genres of popular music. A crossfader on a DJ mixer is typically used to cut the sound in and out in coordination with the hand manipulating the record.

History

The technique was pioneered in the Bronx in the mid-1970s. Grand Wizard Theodore is credited with accidentally discovering the sound in 1975 while holding a record still as his mother spoke to him, then developing it into a deliberate performance technique. Grandmaster Flash refined scratching and related techniques such as cutting and the "backspin," and Afrika Bambaataa popularized them. Herbie Hancock's 1983 single "Rockit," featuring scratching by GrandMixer DXT, brought the sound to a mainstream audience. Turntablism developed into a competitive discipline with battles such as the DMC World DJ Championships.

Techniques

Named scratches include the baby scratch (the simplest back-and-forth motion), the chirp, the flare (developed by DJ Flare), the crab, the transform, and the orbit. Digital vinyl systems and controllers later allowed the technique to be applied to digital audio files while retaining tactile record control.
