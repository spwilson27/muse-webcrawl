1984 Rajneeshee bioterror attack

The 1984 Rajneeshee bioterror attack was a food poisoning attack in The Dalles, Oregon, in which followers of the Indian guru Bhagwan Shree Rajneesh (later known as Osho) deliberately contaminated salad bars at ten local restaurants with Salmonella typhimurium. A total of 751 people were infected, 45 of them hospitalised; there were no deaths. It was the first, and remains one of the largest, bioterrorist attacks in the history of the United States.

Background

In 1981 Rajneesh's followers founded the commune of Rajneeshpuram on a ranch in rural Wasco County and soon clashed with local residents and county authorities over land use. Seeking political control, commune leaders — chiefly Rajneesh's secretary Ma Anand Sheela — planned to suppress voter turnout among non-Rajneeshee residents in the November 1984 county election so that commune candidates could win seats on the Wasco County Court. The salad-bar poisonings in September and October 1984 were a trial run for incapacitating voters on election day.

The attack

Members of the commune's medical organisation cultured Salmonella in the Rajneeshpuram laboratory and sprayed or poured the bacteria over salad-bar items — salad dressings, produce, and coffee creamers — at restaurants in The Dalles, the county seat. Public-health investigators initially considered the outbreak a case of poor food handling, and the commune's role was not confirmed until Rajneesh himself publicly accused Sheela's group in September 1985, after she fled to Europe.

Investigation and prosecutions

Investigators matched the outbreak strain to a sample found in the Rajneeshpuram laboratory. Sheela and Ma Anand Puja, who ran the commune's medical arm, pleaded guilty to charges including attempted murder and product tampering and served time in federal prison. Rajneesh pleaded guilty to immigration violations and was deported from the United States in 1985; the commune collapsed soon after.

Legacy

The attack is frequently cited in biodefence literature as a demonstration that a small group with modest laboratory resources could poison hundreds of people, and it influenced later American policies on food defence and bioterrorism preparedness. The broader Rajneeshpuram episode was documented in the 2018 Netflix series Wild Wild Country.
