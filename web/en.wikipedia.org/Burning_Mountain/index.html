Burning Mountain, also known as Mount Wingen, is a hill near Wingen in the Upper Hunter region of New South Wales, Australia. It is famous for an underground coal-seam fire that has been burning for thousands of years — believed to be the oldest known coal fire on Earth.

The fire

The fire burns in a coal seam about 30 metres below the surface and advances southward at roughly one metre per year. Scientific estimates based on the distance it has travelled (some 6.5 kilometres from its probable point of ignition, likely started by a lightning strike or Aboriginal burning) suggest it has burned for at least 5,500–6,000 years. At the surface, the fire produces a small vent of smoke, sulphur-encrusted ground too hot to stand on comfortably, and an area of baked, discoloured earth surrounded by otherwise normal bushland.

History

Early European explorers and settlers mistook the smoke for volcanic activity — Mount Wingen was long thought to be a volcano. The site is within Burning Mountain Nature Reserve and is significant to the local Wonaruah people. A walking track from the Pacific Highway leads visitors to an information platform overlooking the vent.
