Aasiaat (Greenlandic pronunciation: [aːsiɑt]; Danish: formerly Egedesminde) is a town in the Qeqertalik municipality, on the southern shore of Disko Bay in western Greenland. With roughly 3,000 inhabitants, it is one of the largest towns in Greenland and a regional center for education, fishing, and transport in the Disko Bay area. The name means "the spiders" in Greenlandic.

History

The area was inhabited by Inuit long before colonization; the Saqqaq and Dorset cultures and later the Thule people used the archipelago's rich hunting grounds. The Danish colony of Egedesminde was founded in 1759 by Niels Egede, son of missionary Hans Egede, and was moved to its present site on Aasiaat Island in 1763. The settlement grew around trade in seal and whale products, and later around shrimp and fish processing. During the Second World War and the Cold War, the town lay within the American-supplied weather-reporting network in Greenland.

Geography and climate

Aasiaat sits on an island at the mouth of Disko Bay amid a skerry archipelago, with the Ilulissat Icefjord to the northeast. It has a polar (tundra) climate moderated slightly by the sea: winters are long and cold (around −13 °C in the coldest months) and summers cool (around 8 °C). The sea freezes in winter, when travel is by dog sled and snowmobile, while summer brings the midnight sun and open-water shipping.

Economy and transport

Fishing — particularly shrimp, Greenland halibut, and cod — and the Royal Greenland fish factory anchor the economy, together with public services, a regional hospital, and secondary schools (including a gymnasium and a special-needs school) that draw students from settlements across the region. Aasiaat Airport (opened 1998) provides Air Greenland flights to Ilulissat and Kangerlussuaq, and the town is served by Disko Line ferries and the Arctic Umiaq Line coastal ship, as well as a heliport linking nearby settlements such as Kitsissuarsuit and Akunnaaq.

Culture

The town hosts the annual Nipiaa Rock Festival, one of Greenland's notable music events, and the Aasiaat Museum displays local Inuit and colonial history. Its painted houses, harbor, and surrounding skerries make it a stop on Greenlandic summer cruise itineraries.
