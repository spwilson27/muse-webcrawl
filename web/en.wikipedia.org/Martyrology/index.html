Martyrology

A martyrology is a catalogue or list of martyrs and other saints, arranged in order of their feast days. The term is used especially of the official list of saints recognized by the Roman Catholic Church, the Roman Martyrology.

History

Early Christian communities kept local calendars of the anniversaries (dies natalis) of their martyrs' deaths. These merged into regional lists, and in the Middle Ages compilations such as the Martyrologium Hieronymianum (attributed to Jerome) and the martyrologies of Bede, Florus of Lyons, and Usuard circulated widely and were read aloud daily in chapter houses and refectories.

Roman Martyrology

The first official Roman Martyrology was published in 1583 under Pope Gregory XIII in connection with the calendar reform, with a revised edition under Cardinal Baronius in 1584. It gives, for each day, brief notices of the saints commemorated, with the place and manner of their deaths. A thoroughly revised edition appeared in 2001 following the Second Vatican Council. Eastern traditions maintain analogous works, such as the Byzantine Synaxarion and the Coptic Synaxarium.
