Elliott Cook Carter Jr. (11 December 1908 – 5 November 2012) was an American modernist composer. One of the most respected composers of the later twentieth century, he combined European modernist influences with his own experiments in rhythm and form, notably the technique of metric modulation, in which simultaneous contrasting tempos interact. He composed prolifically into his second century, producing significant new works after the age of 100.

Life

Carter was born in Manhattan and was encouraged as a teenager by Charles Ives. He studied at Harvard University and then in Paris with Nadia Boulanger. Returning to the United States in the 1930s, he initially wrote in an accessible neoclassical style — the ballet Pocahontas and the Symphony No. 1 — before turning decisively toward a more complex musical language after the Second World War.

Music

The Cello Sonata (1948) and the First String Quartet (1950–51) established his mature manner: independent instrumental layers moving at different speeds, linked by carefully calculated tempo changes. He twice won the Pulitzer Prize for Music, for his Second and Third String Quartets. Major later works include the Double Concerto for harpsichord and piano (1961), the Piano Concerto (1965), A Symphony of Three Orchestras (1976), the opera What Next? (1997–98, premiered when he was 90), and a long series of concertos and chamber pieces written in his nineties and beyond.

Legacy

Carter is regarded as a central figure of post-war modernism. His music's rhythmic complexity and intellectual rigour long confined it to specialist audiences, but performances and recordings increased markedly around his centenary in 2008, celebrated by orchestras and festivals worldwide.
