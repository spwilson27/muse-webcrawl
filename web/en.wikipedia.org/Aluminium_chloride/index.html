Aluminium chloride

Aluminium chloride (AlCl3) is an inorganic compound of aluminium and chlorine. The anhydrous form is a white or pale-yellow solid with a low melting and boiling point that exists as the dimer Al2Cl6; it is a powerful Lewis acid and one of the most important industrial catalysts. The common commercial form is the hexahydrate, AlCl3·6H2O.

Production and structure

Anhydrous aluminium chloride is produced industrially by chlorinating aluminium metal or alumina (with carbon) at high temperature. In the solid it has a layered structure; on melting or vaporising it forms Al2Cl6 molecules with chlorine bridges, and at high temperatures monomeric, trigonal-planar AlCl3. It reacts violently with water, hydrolysing to release hydrogen chloride.

Uses

Aluminium chloride is best known as the catalyst of the Friedel–Crafts alkylation and acylation reactions, used to manufacture ethylbenzene, anthraquinone dyes, detergents and pharmaceuticals. It is also used in petroleum refining, in the production of aluminium metal, and (as the hydrate) in antiperspirants, water treatment and cosmetics. Because of its reactivity and corrosiveness it must be handled under dry conditions.
