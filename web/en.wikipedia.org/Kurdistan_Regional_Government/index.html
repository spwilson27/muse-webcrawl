Kurdistan Regional Government

The Kurdistan Regional Government (KRG; Kurdish: حكوومەتی هەرێمی کوردستان, Hikûmeti Herêmi Kurdistan) is the official governing body of the Kurdistan Region, the autonomous federal region of northern Iraq. Headquartered in Erbil, it administers the governorates of Erbil, Sulaymaniyah, Dohuk, and Halabja, and maintains its own security forces, the Peshmerga.

History

Kurdish autonomy was first promised by the 1970 March Agreement with Baghdad but never implemented. After the 1991 Gulf War and the Kurdish uprising, a Western-enforced no-fly zone sheltered the region, and in 1992 the Kurds held elections creating a regional parliament and government. A civil war between the Kurdistan Democratic Party (KDP) and the Patriotic Union of Kurdistan (PUK) in 1994–98 split the region into rival administrations, later reunified under the 2006 unification agreement. The 2005 Iraqi constitution recognised the Kurdistan Region as a federal region.

Politics and government

The KRG is led by a president and a prime minister, with ministries covering finance, natural resources, education, health, and the Peshmerga. The KDP and PUK dominate politics, alongside smaller parties such as Gorran (Movement for Change) and the New Generation Movement. The KRG conducted an independence referendum on 25 September 2017, in which over 92% voted for independence; Iraq's federal government responded by retaking Kirkuk and other disputed territories and imposing sanctions, and the KRG froze the referendum's results.

Economy

The region's economy rests on oil and gas, agriculture, and trade with Turkey and Iran. Disputes with Baghdad over oil exports and budget shares — including the halt of pipeline exports through Turkey from 2023 — have repeatedly strained finances and public-sector salaries.

Foreign relations

Although not a sovereign state, the KRG hosts numerous consulates and representative offices abroad and coordinates directly with foreign governments, notably during the war against the Islamic State (2014–17), when the Peshmerga were a key partner of the international coalition.
