The Appalachian National Scenic Trail, generally known as the Appalachian Trail or simply the A.T., is a marked hiking trail in the Eastern United States extending between Springer Mountain in Georgia and Mount Katahdin in Maine. Conceived by forester Benton MacKaye in 1921 and built largely by volunteers and the Civilian Conservation Corps, it was completed in 1937 and designated a National Scenic Trail in 1968.

Route

The trail passes through 14 states — Georgia, North Carolina, Tennessee, Virginia, West Virginia, Maryland, Pennsylvania, New Jersey, New York, Connecticut, Massachusetts, Vermont, New Hampshire, and Maine — and through the Great Smoky Mountains, Shenandoah, and White Mountain national parks and forests. Virginia contains the longest share, about a quarter of the trail. The path is marked with white rectangular blazes.

Hiking

Some two to three million people visit the trail each year. "Thru-hikers" attempt the whole route in a single season (typically five to seven months, northbound from spring); the first reported continuous thru-hike was by Earl Shaffer in 1948. More than 270 three-sided shelters ("lean-tos") are spaced along the route, and the trail is maintained by the Appalachian Trail Conservancy, the National Park Service, and volunteer clubs. Bill Bryson's 1998 book A Walk in the Woods greatly increased its public profile.
