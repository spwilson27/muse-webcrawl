Moulting

Moulting (or molting) is the periodic shedding and replacement of an outer covering, such as feathers, hair, skin, or an exoskeleton. In birds, moulting is the replacement of feathers by shedding old ones and producing new ones. In arthropods, the shedding of the cuticle — ecdysis — is essential for growth.

In birds

Birds typically moult at least once a year, often after the breeding season. Many replace flight feathers gradually and symmetrically so they can still fly, while waterfowl such as ducks and geese shed all flight feathers at once and are flightless for several weeks. Moulting is energetically expensive and tied to hormonal cycles and daylight length.

In other animals

Mammals shed hair seasonally; snakes shed their outer skin in one piece; spiders and crustaceans cast off their exoskeleton as they grow, controlled by the hormone ecdysone. Some spiders can regenerate lost legs over successive moults.
