Subaltern (postcolonialism)

In postcolonial studies, the subaltern refers to populations that are socially, politically, and geographically outside the hegemonic power structure of the colony and of the colonial homeland. The term was adapted from the Italian Marxist Antonio Gramsci, who used it for subordinate social groups excluded from ruling institutions.

Field: Postcolonial studies
Key figures: Gramsci, Spivak, Guha
Group: Subaltern Studies Group

Subaltern Studies

The South Asian Subaltern Studies Group, founded by Ranajit Guha in the 1980s, sought to rewrite the history of colonial India from the perspective of peasants and other groups ignored by elite nationalist historiography.

"Can the Subaltern Speak?"

Gayatri Chakravorty Spivak's influential 1988 essay asked whether subaltern voices—especially those of women—can be heard within colonial and patriarchal discourse, arguing that representation by intellectuals risks silencing them further.

Criticism

Critics have charged the concept with vagueness and with romanticizing subordinate groups, while defenders stress its value in exposing the exclusions of official history.
