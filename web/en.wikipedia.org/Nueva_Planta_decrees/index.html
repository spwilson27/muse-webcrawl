Nueva Planta decrees

Decretos de Nueva Planta
Issued byPhilip V of Spain
Dates1707 (Aragon and Valencia); 1715 (Mallorca); 1716 (Catalonia)
PurposeAbolish the institutions and fueros of the Crown of Aragon; impose Castilian law and administration
ContextWar of the Spanish Succession

The Nueva Planta decrees (Spanish: Decretos de Nueva Planta, "Decrees of the New Plant/Fabric") were a series of royal decrees issued by Philip V, the first Bourbon king of Spain, in the early 18th century. They abolished the political institutions, laws, and privileges (the fueros) of the territories of the Crown of Aragon—Aragon, Valencia, Mallorca, and the Principality of Catalonia—which had supported the Habsburg claimant, Archduke Charles, in the War of the Spanish Succession.

Content

The first decree, dated 29 June 1707, suppressed the fueros of Aragon and Valencia "by right of conquest". After the fall of Barcelona on 11 September 1714, the decree for Catalonia was issued on 16 January 1716. The decrees dissolved the Catalan Corts, the Generalitat, and the Consell de Cent; replaced local government with Castilian corregidores and audiencias; imposed Castilian administrative law and taxation models; and made Castilian the language of administration and justice. Universities in Catalonia were closed and replaced by a single university at Cervera.

Consequences

The decrees transformed the composite Spanish monarchy into a far more centralised and uniform state modelled on Bourbon France. Navarre and the Basque provinces, which had supported Philip, retained their fueros. In Catalan historical memory the decrees are a foundational grievance, invoked in later Catalan nationalism; the date of the fall of Barcelona, 11 September, is commemorated as the National Day of Catalonia (Diada).
