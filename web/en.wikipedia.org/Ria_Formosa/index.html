Ria Formosa

The Ria Formosa lagoon, located in the Algarve region of southern Portugal, is a system of barrier islands and tidal lagoons stretching about 60 km along the eastern Algarve coast from near Loulé to Manta Rota, close to the Spanish border.

Description

Unlike a typical ria, the Formosa is not a flooded river valley but a lagoon protected from the open Atlantic by a chain of sandy barrier islands and peninsulas. Between them run shifting inlets through which tides flush the shallow lagoons, salt marshes, mudflats and salt pans.

Ecology

Designated a Natural Park in 1987 and recognised as a Ramsar wetland of international importance, Ria Formosa is a crucial habitat for migrating and wintering waterbirds, including flamingos, spoonbills and the rare purple swamphen (also called purple gallinule), the symbol of the park. Its waters support one of the world's most important populations of seahorses and are a major nursery for fish and shellfish; clam and oyster farming are traditional local industries.
