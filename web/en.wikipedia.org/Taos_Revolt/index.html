Date19 January – July 1847
LocationTaos and northern New Mexico

The Taos Revolt was an insurrection in January 1847 by Hispano and Pueblo allies against the United States occupation of New Mexico during the Mexican–American War. After U.S. forces under General Stephen W. Kearny occupied Santa Fe in August 1846 without a fight, resentment of the new civil government of Governor Charles Bent grew among the local population.

Course of the revolt

On 19 January 1847, rebels led by Pablo Montoya and Tomás Romero (Tomasito) attacked Taos, killing Governor Bent — scalped alive at his home — Sheriff Stephen Lee, and other officials. Related uprisings flared at Arroyo Hondo and elsewhere in the north. U.S. forces under Colonel Sterling Price marched from Santa Fe, fighting engagements at Santa Cruz de la Cañada (24 January) and Embudo Pass (29 January), before reaching Taos Pueblo in early February. After an artillery bombardment on 3–4 February, the rebels' stronghold in the pueblo church was stormed; some 150 rebels were killed.

Trials and aftermath

Captured leaders were tried for murder and treason — controversially, since the defendants were Mexican citizens of a territory still contested. Montoya was hanged shortly after capture; Romero was killed in his cell; others were hanged in April 1847 after trials presided over by officials including Bent's relatives. Sporadic resistance continued until July 1847. The revolt remains a sensitive episode in New Mexican memory, emblematic of the violent transfer of the Southwest from Mexico to the United States, formalised by the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo in 1848.
