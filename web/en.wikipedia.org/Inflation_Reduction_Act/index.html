The Inflation Reduction Act of 2022 (IRA) is a United States federal law enacted on 16 August 2022. A scaled-down successor to the proposed Build Back Better Act, it is the largest climate investment in U.S. history and also contains major healthcare and tax provisions, including a 15% corporate minimum tax and funding for Internal Revenue Service enforcement.

Climate and energy provisions

The law provides roughly $369 billion (initial estimates) in energy security and climate spending, chiefly through tax credits for clean electricity, electric vehicles, home energy efficiency, and domestic manufacturing of batteries, solar and wind components. Analysts projected it could cut U.S. greenhouse-gas emissions to about 40% below 2005 levels by 2030.

Healthcare provisions

The IRA caps annual out-of-pocket prescription-drug costs for Medicare beneficiaries at $2,000 (from 2025), caps insulin cost-sharing at $35 per month in Medicare, extends enhanced Affordable Care Act premium subsidies, and for the first time authorises Medicare to negotiate prices for selected high-cost drugs, with negotiated prices taking effect from 2026.

Debate and implementation

Passed through budget reconciliation with only Democratic votes, the act was praised by supporters as transformative industrial and climate policy and criticised by opponents over spending, drug-price negotiation and EV credit rules. Its uncapped tax credits led to substantially higher projected clean-energy investment — and fiscal cost — than first estimated.
