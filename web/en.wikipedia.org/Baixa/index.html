Baixa

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Baixa ("Downtown"), also known as the Baixa Pombalina ("Pombaline Downtown"), is a neighbourhood in the historic centre of Lisbon, Portugal. It consists of the grid of streets north of the Praça do Comércio, roughly between the Cais do Sodré and the Rossio and extending up to the Avenida da Liberdade. It is one of the first examples of planned urban rebuilding after a disaster and a landmark of Enlightenment town planning and early anti-seismic construction.

History

The medieval downtown was destroyed by the 1755 Lisbon earthquake and the fires and tsunami that followed. Under the direction of the Marquis of Pombal, royal engineers Manuel da Maia, Eugénio dos Santos and Carlos Mardel designed a completely new, rational grid of wide streets on the valley floor between the hills of the old city. Streets were named for trades — Rua do Ouro (Goldsmiths), Rua da Prata (Silversmiths), Rua dos Sapateiros (Shoemakers) — and trades were assigned to them by regulation.

The Pombaline style

Buildings were erected in the restrained neoclassical "Pombaline" style on timber lattice frameworks known as the gaiola pombalina ("Pombaline cage"), a pioneering earthquake-resistant structure of wooden trusses embedded in masonry walls. Uniform facades, prefabricated elements, firebreak walls rising above the roofs, and the first large-scale use of standardised building components made the Baixa a model of Enlightenment urbanism. Today the district is Lisbon's main commercial and tourist quarter, centred on the Rossio, Rua Augusta with its triumphal arch, and the Santa Justa Lift; the Baixa Pombalina is on Portugal's tentative list for UNESCO World Heritage status.
