The Populist Party, officially the People's Party, was a left-wing agrarian political party in the United States in the late 19th century. It grew out of the Farmers' Alliance movement and championed the interests of farmers and laborers against banks, railroads, and large corporations.

Omaha Platform

At its 1892 convention in Omaha, Nebraska, the party adopted the Omaha Platform, demanding the free coinage of silver, a graduated income tax, government ownership of railroads and telegraphs, the direct election of senators, and an eight-hour workday. Its presidential candidate, James B. Weaver, won over a million votes and 22 electoral votes in 1892.

Fusion and decline

In 1896 the Populists endorsed the Democratic candidate William Jennings Bryan, whose "Cross of Gold" speech championed free silver. The fusion strategy split the party, and after Bryan's defeat the Populists rapidly declined, effectively disappearing by 1908. Many of their proposals were later enacted during the Progressive Era and the New Deal.
