Proton Mail is an end-to-end encrypted email service founded in 2013 at CERN in Switzerland and launched publicly in May 2014. It was created by scientists who met at CERN, including Andy Yen, Jason Stockman, and Wei Sun, and is operated by Proton AG (formerly Proton Technologies AG), based in Geneva. Messages between Proton Mail users are encrypted end-to-end, and messages stored on its servers are held under "zero-access" encryption, so the company cannot read their contents.

History

A 2014 crowdfunding campaign raised over $550,000, and demand at launch forced a temporary suspension of sign-ups. The service was renamed from ProtonMail to Proton Mail in 2022 as part of a wider brand consolidation that also brought together Proton VPN, Proton Drive, and Proton Calendar under a single subscription. Proton reported more than 100 million accounts across its services by 2023, and in 2024 the company announced a transition toward a non-profit foundation structure.

Security model

Proton Mail uses OpenPGP for end-to-end encryption; email to outside recipients can be protected with a password-protected message link. Client applications are open source and the service publishes the results of independent security audits. Because Switzerland lies outside EU and US jurisdiction, the company argues users benefit from Swiss privacy law, though it must comply with Swiss court orders; a 2021 case in which Swiss authorities compelled logging of a user's IP address led Proton to revise its privacy claims and default protections.
