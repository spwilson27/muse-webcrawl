Pulmonary alveolus

A pulmonary alveolus (plural: alveoli, from Latin alveolus, "little cavity"), also known as an air sac, is a hollow cup-shaped cavity in the lungs where gas exchange takes place. Oxygen from inhaled air diffuses across the alveolar epithelium into the blood of surrounding capillaries, while carbon dioxide diffuses in the opposite direction.

Structure

The two human lungs contain roughly 400–500 million alveoli with a combined surface area of about 70 square metres. Alveolar walls contain type I pneumocytes, which form the gas exchange surface, and type II pneumocytes, which secrete surfactant that reduces surface tension and prevents collapse. Alveolar macrophages patrol the air spaces, ingesting inhaled particles and pathogens.

Disease

Alveoli are damaged in emphysema, filled with fluid in pulmonary edema and pneumonia, and scarred in pulmonary fibrosis.
