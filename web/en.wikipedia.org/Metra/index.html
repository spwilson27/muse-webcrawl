Metra is the commuter rail system in the Chicago metropolitan area serving the city of Chicago and its surrounding suburbs via the Union Pacific Railroad, BNSF Railway, and other railroads. The system operates 243 stations on 11 lines and is one of the largest commuter rail systems in the United States by route miles and ridership. It is operated by the Northeast Illinois Regional Commuter Railroad Corporation under the oversight of the Regional Transportation Authority (RTA).

History

Metra was created in the early 1980s when the RTA, reorganised in 1983, took responsibility for subsidising commuter rail services previously run by freight railroads such as the Milwaukee Road and Rock Island. The Metra brand was introduced in 1985. Downtown terminals include Chicago Union Station, Ogilvie Transportation Center, LaSalle Street Station, and Millennium Station.

Services

Lines include the Metra Electric District, the BNSF Line (Metra's busiest), three Union Pacific lines, the Milwaukee District lines, the Rock Island District, and the North Central Service. Trains are mostly diesel-hauled double-decker gallery cars, with electric multiple units on the Electric District. Fares are zone-based, and the Ventra app provides ticketing.
