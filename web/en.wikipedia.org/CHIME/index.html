Canadian Hydrogen Intensity Mapping Experiment

The Canadian Hydrogen Intensity Mapping Experiment (CHIME) is an interferometric radio telescope at the Dominion Radio Astrophysical Observatory in British Columbia, Canada. It consists of four cylindrical reflectors, each 100 metres long, with no moving parts, scanning the sky as the Earth rotates.

Science goals

CHIME was built to map the distribution of neutral hydrogen over redshifts 0.8–2.5, measuring baryon acoustic oscillations to probe the accelerating expansion of the universe and dark energy. Operating in the 400–800 MHz band, it forms a powerful transit telescope for transient astronomy.

Fast radio bursts

Since 2018 the CHIME/FRB project has transformed the study of fast radio bursts, detecting hundreds of sources, including repeating bursts and the first periodic FRB, FRB 180916, with a 16.35-day cycle. CHIME also monitors pulsars daily.
