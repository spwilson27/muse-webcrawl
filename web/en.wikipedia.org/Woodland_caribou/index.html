Woodland caribou

The woodland caribou (Rangifer tarandus caribou), also known as the boreal forest caribou, is a subspecies (or ecotype) of reindeer native to the boreal forests and taiga of Canada and formerly parts of the northern United States. It is the largest and darkest of the caribou subspecies.

KingdomAnimalia
FamilyCervidae
GenusRangifer
SpeciesR. tarandus
SubspeciesR. t. caribou

Ecology

Unlike the great migratory tundra herds, woodland caribou live in small, scattered groups in mature coniferous forest, feeding in winter largely on terrestrial and arboreal lichens. Their dispersed distribution is an adaptation to wolves and other predators.

Conservation

Populations have declined sharply owing to habitat loss and fragmentation from logging, roads, and energy development, which also increases predator access. The boreal population is listed as threatened in Canada, and several herds have been extirpated, including those of the contiguous United States.
