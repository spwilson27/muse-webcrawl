Giovanni di Pietro di Bernardone, better known as Saint Francis of Assisi (Italian: San Francesco d'Assisi; c. 1181 – 3 October 1226), was an Italian Catholic friar, deacon, mystic, and preacher. He founded the men's Order of Friars Minor, the women's Order of Saint Clare, and the Third Order of Saint Francis for lay people. He is one of the most venerated religious figures in Christianity and is the patron saint of animals and the environment.

Early life and conversion

Francis was born in Assisi, the son of a prosperous silk merchant, Pietro di Bernardone, and his French wife Pica. As a young man he lived a carefree life of wealth and pleasure and aspired to military glory; he was captured in battle at Collestrada in 1202 and held prisoner for a year. A gradual conversion followed: an encounter with a leper, and a vision in the ruined chapel of San Damiano in which the crucifix seemed to speak — "Francis, go and repair my house" — led him to renounce his inheritance, famously stripping off his clothes before the bishop of Assisi, and embrace a life of radical poverty.

The Franciscan orders

Francis began preaching repentance and gathered followers; in 1209 he received oral approval from Pope Innocent III for his small band of brothers, the beginnings of the Order of Friars Minor (Franciscans). With Clare of Assisi he founded the Poor Clares in 1212, and later a Third Order for laypeople living in the world. In 1219 he travelled to Egypt during the Fifth Crusade and met Sultan al-Kamil, seeking to preach to him and end the conflict. In 1223 he arranged the first live nativity scene at Greccio. In 1224, while praying on Mount La Verna, he is said to have received the stigmata — the wounds of Christ — the first recorded case in Christian history.

Death and legacy

Francis died at the Porziuncola chapel on 3 October 1226 and was canonised less than two years later by Pope Gregory IX. He is remembered for his devotion to the Eucharist, his love of all creation — expressed in his "Canticle of the Sun", one of the earliest works of Italian literature — and his preaching to animals; he is often depicted with birds or a wolf. The Basilica of Saint Francis in Assisi, decorated with frescoes attributed to Giotto, remains a major pilgrimage site. In 1979 Pope John Paul II proclaimed him patron saint of ecologists.
