Body positivity

Body positivity advocates acceptance of all body types

Body positivity is a social movement that promotes a positive view of all bodies, regardless of size, shape, skin tone, gender, or physical abilities. It challenges unrealistic beauty standards and the stigmatization of particular body types, and it stresses that one's worth is not determined by physical appearance. The movement grew out of the fat acceptance movement of the 1960s and gained wide visibility through social media in the 2010s.

History

Precursors include Victorian-era dress reform movements protesting corsets, and the fat acceptance activism of the 1960s, including the founding of the National Association to Advance Fat Acceptance in 1969 and "fat-in" protests in New York. The modern body positivity movement developed in the 1990s and 2000s through feminist and activist communities, then spread rapidly on platforms such as Instagram in the 2010s, where hashtags and unretouched photography campaigns popularized its message.

Core ideas

Advocates encourage acceptance of one's body as it is, appreciation of the body's functionality and health rather than its appearance, and self-care and self-compassion. Psychologists have linked positive body image with better mental health outcomes, while negative body image is associated with eating disorders, depression, and low self-esteem.

Criticism

The movement has been criticized from several directions: that commercialization by fashion and beauty brands has diluted its radical roots; that mainstream body positivity still centers relatively privileged bodies and marginalizes the fat, disabled, and racialized people who originated it; and, conversely, that it may discourage healthy weight management. The related concept of body neutrality — focusing on what the body can do rather than loving its appearance — has emerged partly in response.
