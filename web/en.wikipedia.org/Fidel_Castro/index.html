Fidel Castro

Fidel Castro (1926–2016)

Fidel Alejandro Castro Ruz (13 August 1926 – 25 November 2016) was a Cuban revolutionary and politician who governed Cuba from 1959 to 2008 — as prime minister until 1976 and then as president. Ideologically a Marxist–Leninist, he led the 26th of July Movement to victory in the Cuban Revolution, overthrowing the dictatorship of Fulgencio Batista.

Rule

Under Castro, Cuba became a one-party socialist state: industry and business were nationalised, and major programmes expanded education and healthcare, while political dissent was suppressed. His government survived the failed U.S.-backed Bay of Pigs invasion in 1961, and in 1962 the Cuban Missile Crisis brought the world close to nuclear war. Cuba supported revolutionary movements abroad, notably in Angola, and defied the long-standing United States embargo.

Retirement and legacy

Citing ill health, Castro transferred power to his brother Raúl Castro in 2006 and formally stepped down in 2008. Supporters praise his anti-imperialism and Cuba's social achievements; critics condemn his authoritarian rule and human rights record. He remains one of the most divisive figures of the 20th century.
