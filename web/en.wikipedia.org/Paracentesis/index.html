Paracentesis

A paracentesis is a medical procedure in which a needle or catheter is inserted through the abdominal wall into the peritoneal cavity to remove ascitic fluid. It is performed both for diagnosis and for the relief of symptoms caused by ascites.

Indications

Diagnostic paracentesis is used to determine the cause of new-onset ascites and to detect spontaneous bacterial peritonitis in patients with cirrhosis. Therapeutic paracentesis removes large volumes of fluid to relieve abdominal distension and breathlessness. Laboratory analysis of the fluid typically includes cell count, albumin (used to calculate the serum–ascites albumin gradient), total protein, and culture.

Procedure

The procedure is usually performed at the bedside, often with ultrasound guidance to select a safe site, commonly in the left lower quadrant of the abdomen. After local anaesthesia, a needle or catheter is advanced into the peritoneal cavity and fluid is drained. When large volumes are removed, intravenous albumin may be given to prevent circulatory dysfunction. Complications are uncommon but include bleeding, infection, and bowel perforation.
