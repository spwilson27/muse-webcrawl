Thoracentesis

Thoracentesis
Also calledPleural tap
PurposeRemoval of fluid from the pleural space for diagnosis or relief of breathlessness
GuidanceUltrasound (standard of care)
Main risksPneumothorax, bleeding, infection

Thoracentesis (also spelled thoracocentesis) is a procedure in which a needle or fine catheter is passed through the chest wall into the pleural space to withdraw pleural fluid. It is the thoracic analogue of abdominal paracentesis used for ascites. It may be diagnostic — obtaining a sample to determine why fluid has accumulated — or therapeutic, draining a large effusion to relieve breathlessness.

Analysis

Fluid is sent for protein, lactate dehydrogenase, pH, cell counts, Gram stain and culture, and cytology. Light's criteria compare pleural fluid protein and LDH with serum values to distinguish exudates (from infection, malignancy or inflammation) from transudates (from heart failure or cirrhosis).

Technique and complications

The patient usually sits upright leaning forward; the needle is inserted just above a rib to avoid the neurovascular bundle, under ultrasound guidance, which substantially reduces complications. The most important risk is pneumothorax (collapsed lung); others include haemothorax, infection and re-expansion pulmonary oedema after very large-volume drainage.
