Ascites

Ascites
DefinitionAbnormal accumulation of fluid in the peritoneal cavity
Commonest causeCirrhosis with portal hypertension
Key testDiagnostic paracentesis; serum–ascites albumin gradient (SAAG)

Ascites is the pathological accumulation of fluid within the peritoneal cavity. In developed countries about 85% of cases result from cirrhosis and portal hypertension; other causes include malignancy, heart failure, tuberculosis and pancreatic disease. Small volumes cause no symptoms; large volumes produce abdominal distension, discomfort, early satiety and breathlessness.

Diagnosis

Examination may reveal shifting dullness and a fluid thrill. Ultrasound detects as little as 100 mL. Diagnostic paracentesis — needle aspiration of a sample through the abdominal wall — is essential in new-onset or deteriorating ascites: the serum–ascites albumin gradient (SAAG) distinguishes portal-hypertensive ascites (≥1.1 g/dL) from other causes, and a neutrophil count above 250 cells/mm³ diagnoses spontaneous bacterial peritonitis, a dangerous complication.

Treatment

Management includes sodium restriction, diuretics (spironolactone with or without furosemide), and large-volume paracentesis with albumin cover for tense ascites. Refractory ascites may be treated with a transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS) or, where suitable, liver transplantation. The appearance of ascites in cirrhosis marks decompensation and worsens prognosis.
