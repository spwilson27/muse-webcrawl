Digital curation

Digital curation is the selection, preservation, maintenance, collection and archiving of digital assets. It involves maintaining, preserving and adding value to digital research data throughout its lifecycle, ensuring that digital content remains accessible and usable over time despite technological change.

Overview

Digital curation generally refers to the process of establishing and developing long-term repositories of digital assets for current and future reference by researchers, scientists, historians, and scholars. It combines practices from library and information science, records management, and data management.

Activities

Key activities include appraisal and selection of materials, ingest into a repository, metadata creation, storage management, preservation planning (such as format migration and emulation), and providing access to users. The data curation lifecycle model developed by the Digital Curation Centre describes these stages as a cycle.

Challenges

Curators face file format obsolescence, media degradation, the sheer volume of born-digital material, and questions of authenticity and provenance. Standards such as the OAIS reference model guide repository design.
