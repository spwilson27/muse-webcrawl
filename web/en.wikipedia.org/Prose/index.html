Prose

Prose is the ordinary form of written or spoken language, structured in sentences and paragraphs, without the metrical structure of verse. It is the most common form of writing, used in novels, essays, journalism, letters, and most everyday communication. The word derives from Latin prosa, "straightforward discourse."

Characteristics

Unlike poetry, prose follows the natural flow of speech and standard grammatical structures. Its basic units are the sentence and the paragraph rather than the line and stanza. Prose may nonetheless employ rhythm, imagery, and rhetorical devices, and the boundary between prose and poetry is sometimes deliberately blurred in forms such as the prose poem.

Categories

  • Fiction — novels, short stories, and novellas
  • Non-fiction — essays, biographies, history, and journalism
  • Heroic prose — sagas and legendary narratives preserving oral traditions
  • Prose poetry — poetic language written in prose form

History

Prose writing developed in the ancient world for law, administration, and history — from Mesopotamian records to Greek historians such as Herodotus and Thucydides. The rise of the novel in the 17th and 18th centuries made prose the dominant vehicle of literature.
