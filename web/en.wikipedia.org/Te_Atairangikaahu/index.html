Te Atairangikaahu

Te Atairangikaahu (23 July 1931 – 15 August 2006) was the Māori Queen of New Zealand from 1966 until her death, the longest-serving leader of the Kīngitanga (Māori King movement). She was the first woman to hold the position and the first Māori monarch to be made a Dame Commander of the Order of the British Empire.

Reign

Born Pikimene Koroki Mahuta, she succeeded her father, Koroki Mahuta, on his death in May 1966. Over her 40-year reign she became a widely respected figure across New Zealand society, supporting Māori cultural revival, education and the settlement of Treaty of Waitangi claims — notably the Waikato-Tainui settlement of 1995, signed at her home marae, Tūrangawaewae in Ngāruawāhia. On her death in 2006, tens of thousands attended her tangihanga, and she was succeeded by her son, Tūheitia Paki.
