Ackee and saltfish

Place of origin: Jamaica
Main ingredients: Ackee, salted codfish
Usually served: Breakfast, with dumplings, breadfruit, or callaloo

Ackee and saltfish is the national dish of Jamaica. It combines the ackee fruit — originally from West Africa and brought to Jamaica in the late 18th century — with salted codfish, sautéed with onions, tomatoes, peppers, and spices such as Scotch bonnet.

Preparation

The ackee's bright red pods must open naturally on the tree; only the creamy yellow arils are eaten, and they are boiled before use. Unripe or improperly prepared ackee contains the toxin hypoglycin and can cause severe poisoning known as Jamaican vomiting sickness, which is why fresh ackee imports are restricted in some countries. The saltfish is soaked or boiled to remove excess salt, flaked, and sautéed with aromatics; the cooked ackee — delicate and similar in texture to scrambled eggs — is folded in at the end.

Serving

Ackee and saltfish is traditionally a breakfast dish, served with boiled green bananas, fried or boiled dumplings, hard-dough bread, roasted breadfruit, or fried plantain. It is also popular in the Jamaican diaspora, where canned ackee is used.

The dish reflects Jamaica's history of cultural fusion: an African fruit, European preserved fish (salted cod was a cheap import used to feed enslaved people), and local produce and seasonings.
