Acetyl-CoA

FormulaC₂₃H₃₈N₇O₁₇P₃S
Molar mass809.57 g/mol
RoleCentral intermediate in metabolism

Acetyl coenzyme A (acetyl-CoA) is a molecule that participates in many biochemical reactions in protein, carbohydrate and lipid metabolism. Its main function is to deliver the acetyl group to the citric acid cycle (Krebs cycle) to be oxidised for energy production. It consists of an acetyl group (CH₃CO–) attached by a thioester bond to coenzyme A, which is derived from the vitamin pantothenic acid.

Production

Acetyl-CoA is generated principally by the oxidative decarboxylation of pyruvate (catalysed by the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex at the end of glycolysis), by the β-oxidation of fatty acids, and by the catabolism of certain amino acids. In the cytosol, it can also be formed from citrate exported from mitochondria, a step important for fatty acid synthesis.

Functions

Beyond the citric acid cycle, acetyl-CoA is the building block for fatty acid and cholesterol synthesis, the source of acetyl groups for the neurotransmitter acetylcholine, and the acetyl donor for histone acetylation, a key mechanism of epigenetic gene regulation. It also feeds the glyoxylate cycle in plants and some microorganisms.
