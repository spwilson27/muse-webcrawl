Ngāti Toa

Ngāti Toa, also called Ngāti Toarangatira, is a Māori iwi (tribe) of the lower North Island and upper South Island of New Zealand. Traditionally based at Kāwhia Harbour on the west coast of the Waikato, the tribe migrated south in the 1820s under the leadership of the great chief Te Rauparaha.

History

Pressured by Waikato and Ngāti Maniapoto rivals armed with muskets, Te Rauparaha led the famous migration (heke) to the Kāpiti Coast, conquering territory around Cook Strait, including Kāpiti Island, Porirua, Wellington, and parts of the northern South Island. Ngāti Toa were signatories to the Treaty of Waitangi in 1840, but land disputes followed; Te Rauparaha was controversially arrested in 1846 and held without trial.

Ka Mate and settlement

Te Rauparaha is credited with composing the haka "Ka Mate," now famous worldwide through the All Blacks; legislation in the tribe's 2014 Treaty settlement formally recognized this attribution. The Ngāti Toa Rangatira settlement with the Crown, signed in 2012 and enacted in 2014, included an apology and financial redress of about NZ$70 million.
