Clock tower

A clock tower is a tower with one or more clock faces, freestanding or part of a civic or religious building such as a town hall, church or university. Clock towers became prominent in European towns from the 14th century as mechanical turret clocks spread, providing a public time signal before personal watches were common.

Notable examples

  • The Elizabeth Tower (housing Big Ben) in London, completed in 1859.
  • The Torre dell'Orologio in St Mark's Square, Venice (1499).
  • The Zytglogge in Bern, Switzerland, with its astronomical clock.
  • The Abraj Al Bait Clock Tower in Mecca, among the tallest buildings in the world at 601 m.
  • The Rajabai Clock Tower in Mumbai and the clock tower of the Philadelphia City Hall.

Beyond telling time, clock towers served as symbols of civic pride, landmarks for orientation, and in earlier centuries often doubled as belfries, fire lookouts or part of city fortifications.
