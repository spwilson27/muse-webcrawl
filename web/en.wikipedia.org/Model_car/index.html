Model car

A model car, or toy car, is a miniature representation of an automobile. Model cars range from simple children's toys to precision-built collectibles and assemble-it-yourself kits, and are produced in scales such as 1:12, 1:18, 1:24, 1:32, 1:43, and 1:64.

Types

  • Die-cast models — made of metal (typically zamak alloy) with plastic details; pioneered as toys by brands such as Dinky Toys, Corgi, and Matchbox, and later Hot Wheels (from 1968). Detailed adult-collectible die-cast in 1:18 and 1:43 is a major market.
  • Plastic kits — unassembled model kits from manufacturers such as AMT, Revell, Monogram, and Tamiya, which the hobbyist builds and paints; promotional "promo" models were once distributed through car dealerships.
  • Resin and hand-built models — low-volume, high-detail replicas.
  • Slot cars and RC cars — functional models raced on slotted tracks or by radio control.

History and collecting

Miniature cars appeared soon after real automobiles, first in tinplate and cast iron. Mass-produced die-cast toys flourished after World War II, and by the 1960s–70s kit building was a mainstream hobby. Today collecting focuses on limited editions, racing liveries, and mint-in-box vintage examples, with values driven by rarity and condition; communities of collectors trade through specialist dealers, shows, and online marketplaces.
