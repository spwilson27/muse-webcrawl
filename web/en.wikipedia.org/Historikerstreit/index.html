Historikerstreit

Date1986–1988
LocationWest Germany
Principal figuresErnst Nolte, Andreas Hillgruber, Jürgen Habermas
SubjectInterpretation of Nazi crimes and German national identity

The Historikerstreit ("historians' dispute") was a public controversy in West Germany in 1986–88 about the interpretation of National Socialism and the Holocaust, and their place in German history and identity. It was conducted largely in newspaper feuilletons and involved historians, philosophers, and public intellectuals.

The dispute

The debate was ignited by Ernst Nolte's 1986 article suggesting that the Nazi "racial murder" was a fearful reaction to Bolshevik "class murder" and that the Gulag was logically and factually prior to Auschwitz. Andreas Hillgruber's Zweierlei Untergang was also criticised for its perspective on the Eastern Front. The philosopher Jürgen Habermas attacked these positions as apologetic revisionism aimed at rehabilitating a conventional German national identity.

Outcome and legacy

Most historians sided with Habermas's critique, and the uniqueness of the Holocaust became a settled position in German scholarship and education. The Historikerstreit remains a landmark in debates about memory politics and historical revisionism, and is sometimes invoked in later controversies (a "Historikerstreit 2.0") over German memory culture.
