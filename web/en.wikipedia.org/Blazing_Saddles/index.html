Blazing Saddles is a 1974 American Western black comedy film directed by Mel Brooks. Starring Cleavon Little and Gene Wilder, the film was written by Brooks, Andrew Bergman, Richard Pryor, Norman Steinberg, and Alan Uger, and was based on Bergman's story and draft. The film is a parody of Western films, centering on a Black railroad worker who becomes sheriff of a frontier town about to be destroyed to make way for a railroad.

Plot

In 1874, corrupt political boss Hedley Lamarr schemes to drive the residents of Rock Ridge from their town so a railroad can be built through it, increasing his land's value. To ruin the town's reputation, he appoints Bart, a Black railroad worker condemned to death, as sheriff. Bart, aided by drunken gunfighter Jim, "The Waco Kid," eventually wins over the townspeople and defeats Lamarr's hired thugs in a finale that breaks the fourth wall onto a modern Hollywood studio lot.

Reception and legacy

The film was a commercial success and received three Academy Award nominations, including Best Supporting Actress for Madeline Kahn, Best Film Editing, and Best Original Song for its title theme. In 2006, Blazing Saddles was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry by the Library of Congress as being "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant." The American Film Institute ranked it sixth on its AFI's 100 Years...100 Laughs list.
