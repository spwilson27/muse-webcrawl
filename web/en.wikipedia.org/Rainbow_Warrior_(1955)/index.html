The Rainbow Warrior was the first ship of the environmental organisation Greenpeace, a former North Sea trawler built in 1955 as the Sir William Hardy for the British Ministry of Agriculture, Fisheries and Food. Greenpeace acquired her in 1978 and refitted her as its flagship for campaigns against whaling, seal hunting, and nuclear testing.

Sinking

In 1985 the ship was in Auckland, New Zealand, preparing to lead a protest flotilla to Moruroa Atoll against French nuclear tests. On the night of 10 July 1985, agents of the French foreign intelligence service (DGSE) attached two limpet mines to the hull; the explosions sank the ship and killed Greenpeace photographer Fernando Pereira. Two agents were captured by New Zealand police and later pleaded guilty to manslaughter — a scandal that strained relations between France and New Zealand and led to the resignation of French defence minister Charles Hernu and an official French admission of responsibility.

Legacy

The wreck was refloated, then scuttled in Matauri Bay in 1987 as an artificial reef and dive site. Greenpeace replaced her with a second Rainbow Warrior in 1989, and a purpose-built third ship of the name in 2011.
