Hail Mary

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Hail Mary (Latin: Ave Maria) is a traditional Catholic prayer addressing Mary, the mother of Jesus, asking for her intercession. It forms the core of the Rosary and the Angelus. The prayer combines two passages from the Gospel of Luke — the angel Gabriel's greeting at the Annunciation ("Hail, full of grace, the Lord is with thee") and Elizabeth's greeting at the Visitation ("Blessed art thou among women, and blessed is the fruit of thy womb") — with a concluding petition added by the Church.

Text

The common English form reads: "Hail Mary, full of grace, the Lord is with thee. Blessed art thou amongst women, and blessed is the fruit of thy womb, Jesus. Holy Mary, Mother of God, pray for us sinners, now and at the hour of our death. Amen."

History

The scriptural greeting circulated in the liturgy from late antiquity and appeared in the Roman Breviary by the eleventh century; the petitionary second half was standardised by the sixteenth century, and Pope Pius V fixed the present form in 1568. In the Eastern Orthodox and Eastern Catholic traditions, a corresponding prayer addresses Mary as Theotokos ("God-bearer").

Music

The prayer has been set to music countless times, most famously in settings attributed to composers from Josquin des Prez to Franz Schubert (whose Ellens Gesang III is popularly known as "Ave Maria") and Charles Gounod, who superimposed a melody on a Bach prelude.
