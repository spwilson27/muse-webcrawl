Horror fiction

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Horror is a genre of speculative fiction that is intended to disturb, frighten, or scare. Horror is often divided into the sub-genres of psychological horror and supernatural horror, the latter commonly featuring ghosts, demons, vampires, werewolves, and other monsters. Literary historian J. A. Cuddon has defined the horror story as "a piece of fiction in prose of variable length ... which shocks, or even frightens the reader, or perhaps induces a feeling of repulsion or loathing".

History

The genre has ancient roots in folklore and religious traditions centred on death, the afterlife, evil, and the demonic. It took recognisable literary form with the Gothic novel, beginning with Horace Walpole's The Castle of Otranto (1764). Nineteenth-century landmarks include Mary Shelley's Frankenstein (1818), the tales of Edgar Allan Poe, Robert Louis Stevenson's Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde (1886), and Bram Stoker's Dracula (1897).

Twentieth century and beyond

H. P. Lovecraft reshaped the genre in the 1920s and 1930s with his Cthulhu Mythos and "cosmic horror". The modern horror boom of the 1970s and 1980s was led by authors such as Stephen King, Clive Barker, and Anne Rice. Horror also became a major genre in film, from the Universal monster movies through slasher films to contemporary psychological horror.

Characteristics

Horror fiction typically works by confronting characters and readers with the unknown, the uncanny, or the transgressive. Common themes include death and mortality, madness, forbidden knowledge, monstrosity, and the fragility of civilisation. Scholars such as Noël Carroll have analysed the genre's emotional effect, which he called "art-horror" — a combination of fear and disgust directed at a threatening and impure entity.

Subgenres

Major subgenres include Gothic horror, supernatural horror, psychological horror, body horror, cosmic horror, folk horror, splatterpunk, and the ghost story. Horror overlaps extensively with thriller, dark fantasy, and science fiction.
