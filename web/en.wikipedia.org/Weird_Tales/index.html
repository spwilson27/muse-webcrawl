Weird Tales

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Weird Tales is an American fantasy and horror pulp magazine founded by J. C. Henneberger and J. M. Lansinger in late 1922. The first issue, dated March 1923, appeared on newsstands on February 18. The magazine is best known as the home of H. P. Lovecraft, Robert E. Howard and Clark Ashton Smith, and as the cradle of the Cthulhu Mythos and of sword and sorcery fiction.

History

Under editor Farnsworth Wright (1924–1940) the magazine published Lovecraft's Cthulhu stories, Howard's tales of Conan the Barbarian and Solomon Kane, and early work by Robert Bloch, Ray Bradbury and Tennessee Williams. Its covers by artists such as Margaret Brundage became classics of pulp art. The original run ended in 1954 after 279 issues, a victim of the collapse of the pulp market; the title has since been revived several times, including a notable incarnation from the 1980s onward and a relaunch under editor Marvin Kaye in 2011.

Influence

Weird Tales shaped modern horror and fantasy: it launched the careers of many of the genres' defining writers, gave a name to the weird fiction tradition descending from Lovecraft and his circle, and its letters column, "The Eyrie", helped incubate organised science-fiction fandom.
