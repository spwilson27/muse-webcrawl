Phillips Brooks (December 13, 1835 – January 23, 1893) was an American Episcopal clergyman and author, long the rector of Trinity Church in Boston and later Bishop of Massachusetts. One of the most celebrated preachers of his era, he is best remembered today as the author of the Christmas carol "O Little Town of Bethlehem."

Life and ministry

Brooks was educated at Harvard and at the Virginia Theological Seminary, and ordained in 1859. He served parishes in Philadelphia, where his reputation as a powerful, rapid-fire preacher grew during the Civil War years, before becoming rector of Trinity Church, Boston, in 1869. The new Trinity building in Copley Square, designed by H. H. Richardson, became a landmark of American architecture during his tenure.

A trip to the Holy Land in 1865, including a Christmas Eve service at Bethlehem, inspired the carol he wrote three years later for his Sunday school, set to music by his organist Lewis Redner. Brooks's published sermons and his 1877 Yale lectures on preaching — in which he defined preaching as "the bringing of truth through personality" — influenced generations of clergy.

Bishop and legacy

Brooks was consecrated Bishop of Massachusetts in 1891 but died less than two years later. A statue of him stands outside Trinity Church, and he is commemorated in the Episcopal Church calendar of saints.
