Nadsat

Nadsat is a fictional register or argot used by the teenage narrator and his friends in Anthony Burgess's dystopian novel A Clockwork Orange (1962). The name comes from the Russian suffix -nadtsat, equivalent to "-teen" in English number words such as thirteen and nineteen.

Sources

Burgess built Nadsat largely from Russian vocabulary, combined with Cockney rhyming slang, Romani words, biblical language, and invented compounds. Words are often anglicised in spelling and take English grammar and inflections. Burgess wanted the slang to sound timeless rather than tied to the youth slang of the early 1960s, which he believed would date quickly.

Function in the novel

The argot distances the reader from the violence described, forces slow decoding of the narration, and, in Burgess's intention, "brainwashes" the reader into learning a foreign vocabulary. Early American editions included a glossary, against Burgess's wishes.
