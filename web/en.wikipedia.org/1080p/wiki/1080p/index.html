1080p

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Resolution: 1920 × 1080 pixels
Scanning: Progressive
Aspect ratio: 16:9
Also known as: Full HD (FHD)

1080p (1920 × 1080 progressively displayed pixels; also known as Full HD or FHD) is a set of HDTV high-definition video modes characterised by 1,080 horizontal lines of vertical resolution and progressive scanning, as opposed to interlaced scanning in 1080i. The term usually assumes a widescreen aspect ratio of 16:9, implying a resolution of 2.1 megapixels per frame.

Usage

1080p became the standard resolution for Blu-ray Disc, most flat-panel televisions and monitors, game consoles and streaming video in the 2000s and 2010s. Broadcast use is more limited than 1080i or 720p in some regions, but satellite, cable and streaming services widely offer 1080p at frame rates of 24, 25, 30, 50 and 60 frames per second. Marketing terms such as "Full HD" distinguish 1080p displays from 720p "HD ready" sets.

Successors

Higher resolutions including 1440p (QHD), 4K UHD (3840 × 2160) and 8K UHD have since been marketed, but 1080p remains extremely common for displays, cameras and content.
