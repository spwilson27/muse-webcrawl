4K resolution

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Consumer standard: 3840 × 2160 (4K UHD)
Cinema standard: 4096 × 2160 (DCI 4K)
Aspect ratios: 16:9 and 256:135

4K resolution refers to a horizontal display resolution of approximately 4,000 pixels. Two main standards are in use: the television and media standard 3840 × 2160, known as 4K UHD, adopted from ITU-R BT.2020; and the digital cinema standard 4096 × 2160 defined by the Digital Cinema Initiatives (DCI). 4K UHD has exactly four times the pixel count of 1080p Full HD.

History and adoption

The first DCI 4K specification dates from 2005, and 4K projectors and cameras appeared in cinemas and production in the following years. Consumer 4K televisions became mainstream in the mid-2010s, followed by streaming services, Ultra HD Blu-ray and game consoles offering 4K output. Broadcasters have adopted 4K more slowly because of its bandwidth demands.

Resolutions

Besides 3840 × 2160 and 4096 × 2160, formats such as 3996 × 2160 (DCI flat cropped), 4096 × 1716 (DCI scope cropped) and 5120 × 2160 (5K ultrawide-adjacent formats marketed alongside 4K) exist. 4K content is often delivered using the HEVC and AV1 codecs.
