1080i

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Resolution: 1920 × 1080 pixels
Scanning: Interlaced (two fields of 540 lines)
Aspect ratio: 16:9

1080i (also known as Full HD or BT.709) is a combination of frame resolution and scan type used in high-definition television (HDTV). The number "1080" denotes 1,080 horizontal lines of vertical resolution, and the letter "i" stands for interlaced scanning: each frame is drawn as two fields of 540 alternating lines.

Broadcasting

1080i is commonly broadcast at 50 or 59.94/60 fields per second depending on region and is specified in ITU-R BT.709. It has been widely used by terrestrial, satellite and cable broadcasters because interlacing halves the bandwidth compared with 1080p at the same frame rate. A related letterboxed variant with 1440 columns, sometimes called 1080i HD Lite, has been used by some satellite providers.

Comparison with 1080p

Unlike progressive-scan 1080p, 1080i images must be deinterlaced for display on modern flat-panel screens, which can introduce artefacts on fast motion. Nonetheless 1080i retains the full 1920-line vertical sampling on static material and remains a major broadcast format worldwide.
