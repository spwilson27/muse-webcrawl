720p

720p (720 progressive lines) is a progressive high-definition (HD) resolution format with 720 horizontal lines of vertical resolution and a horizontal resolution of 1280 pixels, giving a total frame resolution of 1280 × 720. The letter p stands for progressive scan, meaning that each frame is drawn in a single pass rather than split into two interlaced fields.

Diagram comparing 720p with other common display resolutions.

Overview

720p is one of the two original high-definition television formats, alongside 1080i. In ATSC broadcasts it is typically transmitted at frame rates of 24, 25, 30, 50, or 60 frames per second, with 720p60 favoured by some networks for fast-motion sports coverage because progressive scanning handles motion without interlace artefacts. Displays marketed as "HD ready" were generally native 720p-class panels, while "Full HD" denotes 1080p.

Comparison with 1080i

Although 1080i carries a higher nominal pixel count (1920 × 1080, transmitted as alternating 1920 × 540 fields), 720p delivers all 720 lines in every frame. Advocates of 720p point to its smoother rendering of motion and lack of interlace flicker; advocates of 1080i point to greater detail in static scenes. Both formats were standardised for digital terrestrial, cable, and satellite broadcasting, and 720p remains widely used for streaming video and broadcast television.

Use in broadcasting and media

Networks such as ABC, Fox, and ESPN in the United States adopted 720p as their HD broadcast format. The resolution is also common in online video, where 720p is labelled "HD" by major streaming platforms, and in video games, where many titles on seventh- and eighth-generation consoles rendered natively at 720p before upscaling.
