Potential flow

In fluid dynamics, potential flow or irrotational flow is the description of the motion of a fluid in a region where the vorticity is zero and, consequently, the flow can be described as the gradient of a velocity potential. Potential flows are characterized by a velocity field that can be written as the gradient of a scalar function, the velocity potential φ, that is: v = ∇φ.

As a result of the flow being irrotational, potential flow is an approximation for the flow of a fluid in several circumstances: steady flow, inviscid flow, and flow occurring away from boundary layers. In such cases, potential flow forms the basis of much of classical aerodynamics and hydrodynamics.

Description and characteristics

The description that a region of a flow may be irrotational is a consequence of the flow being inviscid, or nearly so, and the absence of vorticity in the fluid. In regions of flow in which the vorticity is known to be negligible, it is often possible to simplify the analysis by using the potential flow approximation. This is a useful approximation because the velocity potential satisfies Laplace's equation, and so a variety of known solutions of Laplace's equation can be used to describe the potential flow in a given geometry, using the method of superposition.

When applied to a flow past a solid body, such as flow around a cylinder, potential flow predicts d'Alembert's paradox: that the drag on the body is zero. Real fluids have viscosity, which generates vorticity near the body's surface, leading to boundary layers and flow separation. However, potential flow still correctly predicts the pressure distribution and lift in many engineering applications, especially when combined with the Kutta condition for airfoils.

Applications

Potential flow is used in aerodynamics to calculate the lift on airfoils, in the analysis of flow past ships and in ocean engineering, and in groundwater flow modelling. Classical solutions include flow around a cylinder, flow around a sphere, the Hele-Shaw cell, and corner flows. The method of images and conformal mapping are commonly used techniques to construct potential flow solutions for complicated geometries.
