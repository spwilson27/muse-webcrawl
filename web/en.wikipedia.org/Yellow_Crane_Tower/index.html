The Yellow Crane Tower (Chinese: 黃鶴樓; pinyin: Huánghèlóu) is a traditional Chinese tower located in Wuhan, Hubei province, on Snake Hill overlooking the Yangtze River. It is one of the most celebrated landmarks of southern China and, with Yueyang Tower and the Pavilion of Prince Teng, one of the Three Great Towers of Jiangnan.

History

The tower was first built in AD 223 by Sun Quan, ruler of Eastern Wu, as a military watchtower. Over the following centuries it was destroyed and rebuilt many times, evolving into a scenic and literary landmark. Its fame rests largely on poetry, above all the Tang-dynasty poem "Yellow Crane Tower" by Cui Hao, which Li Bai is said to have admired so much that he set down his own brush. Legend holds that a Daoist immortal ascended to heaven from the tower on the back of a yellow crane.

The modern tower

The last Qing-dynasty tower was destroyed by fire in 1884. The present five-storey tower, 51 metres high, was rebuilt between 1981 and 1985 in Qing style on a site about one kilometre from the original, the earlier site being occupied by the approach to the Wuhan Yangtze River Bridge.
