North Water Polynya

LocationNorthern Baffin Bay, between Greenland and Ellesmere Island (Canada)
AreaUp to about 85,000 km²
Indigenous namePikialasorsuaq ("the great upwelling")
SignificanceLargest Arctic polynya; key habitat for marine mammals and seabirds

The North Water Polynya is the largest recurring polynya — an area of open water surrounded by sea ice — in the Arctic, located in northern Baffin Bay between Greenland and Canada's Ellesmere Island. In Inuit knowledge it is called Pikialasorsuaq, "the great upwelling". At its maximum it covers some 85,000 square kilometres.

Formation

The polynya forms each winter largely because an ice bridge typically forms across the narrow Nares Strait to its north, blocking the southward drift of thick Arctic ice, while prevailing northerly winds and currents continually push newly formed ice away from the bridge's southern edge. Upwelling of relatively warm water also contributes, bringing nutrients to the surface.

Ecology

Because open water admits sunlight early in spring, the North Water supports an exceptionally productive phytoplankton bloom, which sustains dense populations of zooplankton, Arctic cod, seabirds (notably thick-billed murres and little auks) and marine mammals including narwhal, beluga, walrus and polar bears. It has been called an oasis of life in the High Arctic and has supported Inuit hunting cultures on both sides of Baffin Bay for millennia. Scientists monitor it closely, since failure of the Nares Strait ice bridge under climate change could alter its formation.
