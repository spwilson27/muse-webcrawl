Aguascalientes

Aguascalientes is one of the smallest states of Mexico, located in the north-central part of the country, and also the name of its capital city. The state's name means "hot waters" in Spanish, referring to the numerous hot springs in the area. The capital, founded in 1575 as a supply stop on the silver route to Zacatecas, is now a major industrial and commercial center.

Geography

The state covers about 5,600 square kilometres, bordered by Zacatecas to the north and west and Jalisco to the south and east. Most of it lies on a high plateau at roughly 1,800–2,000 metres elevation, with a temperate, semi-arid climate. The population is about 1.4 million, most of it concentrated in the capital metropolitan area.

Economy

Aguascalientes is a hub of the Mexican automotive industry, hosting large Nissan assembly plants and many suppliers, alongside electronics, textiles, and food processing. The city is known for the Feria Nacional de San Marcos, one of Mexico's largest fairs, held each spring around the feast of Saint Mark.

History

The territory was separated from Zacatecas in 1835 and became a state in 1857. In 1914 the city hosted the Convention of Aguascalientes, a key meeting of revolutionary factions during the Mexican Revolution. The painter and printmaker José Guadalupe Posada, creator of the iconic La Calavera Catrina, worked in the city.
