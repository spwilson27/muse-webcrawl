Mincome

Mincome, an abbreviation of "Manitoba Basic Annual Income Experiment", was a Canadian guaranteed annual income social experiment held in Manitoba in the 1970s, jointly funded by the federal government and the province.

Design

From 1974, low-income households in Winnipeg and the rural town of Dauphin received a guaranteed minimum income, reduced as other earnings rose. Dauphin was a "saturation site" where every resident was eligible, allowing researchers to study community-wide effects.

Findings

The project ended in 1979 without a final report, and the data sat largely unanalyzed for decades. Later research, notably by economist Evelyn Forget, found modest reductions in work (mainly among mothers and students), improved school completion, and significantly reduced hospitalizations in Dauphin — results that continue to inform basic income debates.
