Reserve requirement

The reserve requirement (or cash reserve ratio) is a central bank regulation that sets the minimum amount of reserves that must be held by a commercial bank, typically as a fraction of its deposit liabilities. The reserves may be held as vault cash or as deposits with the central bank.

Function

Reserve requirements were historically used to influence the money supply through the money multiplier, and as a prudential safeguard against bank runs. Many central banks now rely primarily on interest-rate policy; several, including the United Kingdom, Canada, and Australia, have no formal reserve requirements, and the United States Federal Reserve reduced reserve requirement ratios to zero in March 2020.

By country

China's People's Bank has used changes in the reserve requirement ratio as an active monetary policy tool, while the euro area applies a low minimum reserve ratio. High ratios persist in some emerging economies.
