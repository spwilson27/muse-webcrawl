Hayam Wuruk, fourth monarch of Majapahit

Hayam Wuruk (1334–1389), also called Rajasanagara, was the fourth king of the Majapahit Empire in Java. His reign, from 1350 to 1389, together with that of his prime minister Gajah Mada, is generally regarded as the empire's golden age, when Majapahit's influence extended across much of the Indonesian archipelago.

Reign

Hayam Wuruk was born in 1334, a year associated in Javanese tradition with the eruption of Mount Kelud, and succeeded his mother, the queen Tribhuvana, in 1350. The court eulogy Nagarakretagama, written by the Buddhist monk Prapanca in 1365, describes his kingdom in detail—its capital at Trowulan, its royal progresses, and a list of tributary regions encompassing parts of Sumatra, the Malay Peninsula, Borneo, Sulawesi, and the eastern islands—though historians debate how far this represented direct administration versus a network of trade and ritual overlordship (a mandala polity).

The Bubat incident and later years

In 1357 a planned marriage to Dyah Pitaloka, princess of the Sunda Kingdom, ended in disaster at Bubat field, where the Sundanese royal party died fighting rather than surrender the princess as a subordinate bride—an episode that poisoned Majapahit–Sunda relations for centuries and became a famous literary theme. Gajah Mada, blamed for the affair, lost favour and died in 1364; no successor of equal stature emerged, and Hayam Wuruk distributed his minister's duties among several officials. The king died in 1389, leaving a succession dispute between his son-in-law Wikramawardhana and his son Bhre Wirabhumi that erupted into civil war (the Paregreg War, 1404–1406), from which Majapahit never fully recovered.

Legacy

Hayam Wuruk's era is remembered in Indonesia as a high point of pre-colonial civilisation; Gadjah Mada University (named for his minister) and streets across the archipelago commemorate the period, and the Nagarakretagama supplied the phrase "Bhinneka Tunggal Ika"—Indonesia's national motto.
