Wrigley Field

Wrigley Field is a baseball stadium on the North Side of Chicago, Illinois. It is the home of the Chicago Cubs of Major League Baseball. Opened in 1914, it is the second-oldest ballpark in the majors after Boston's Fenway Park, and is famous for its ivy-covered outfield walls, its hand-operated scoreboard, and the rooftop seating on surrounding buildings.

History

The park opened in 1914 as Weeghman Park for Charles Weeghman's Chicago Whales of the Federal League. After the league folded, Weeghman acquired the Cubs and moved them to the park in 1916. Chewing-gum magnate William Wrigley Jr. gained control of the club, renamed the park in 1926, and the Cubs remained family-owned until 1981. The famous ivy was planted in 1937 under Bill Veeck, who also installed the manual centre-field scoreboard that still stands.

Features and traditions

Because the surrounding neighbourhood resisted lights, Wrigley hosted only day games until August 8, 1988, when lights were finally installed. Fans watch from bleachers and from rooftops across Waveland and Sheffield Avenues; home-run balls landing on the street are a hallmark of games. Harry Caray popularised the seventh-inning-stretch rendition of "Take Me Out to the Ball Game", a tradition continued by guest conductors. The park's intimate scale, its red marquee at Clark and Addison, and the "W" flag flown after victories are beloved icons.

Memorable events

Wrigley was the site of Babe Ruth's disputed "called shot" in the 1932 World Series and of the Cubs' National League pennant triumph in 2016, though the World Series-clinching Game 7 was played in Cleveland. It has also hosted football, hockey (the 2009 NHL Winter Classic), concerts and soccer. Major renovations under the Ricketts family's "1060 Project" were completed in the late 2010s, modernising facilities while preserving the park's historic character; the stadium was designated a National Historic Landmark in 2020.
