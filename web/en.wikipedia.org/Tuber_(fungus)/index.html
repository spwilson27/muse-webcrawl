Tuber (fungus)

KingdomFungi
DivisionAscomycota
FamilyTuberaceae
GenusTuber

Tuber is a genus of fungi in the family Tuberaceae. It includes many species of truffles, highly prized edible fungi that grow underground in symbiotic (mycorrhizal) association with the roots of trees such as oak, hazel, beech, and poplar.

Description

The fruiting bodies of Tuber species are roughly spherical, hypogeous (underground) ascomata with a firm, warty outer skin (peridium) and a marbled interior (gleba) traversed by pale veins. Spores are produced inside sac-like asci scattered through the gleba.

Notable species

Tuber magnatum, the white truffle of Piedmont, and Tuber melanosporum, the black Périgord truffle, are among the most valuable culinary fungi in the world. Other species include the summer truffle T. aestivum and the Chinese truffle T. indicum.

Cultivation and hunting

Truffles are traditionally located by trained dogs or pigs that detect their strong aroma. Cultivation by planting inoculated tree seedlings (trufficulture) has expanded production in France, Spain, Australia, and elsewhere.
