The Ukrainian Navy (Ukrainian: Військово-морські сили Збройних сил України, literally "Naval Forces of the Armed Forces of Ukraine") is the maritime service branch of the Armed Forces of Ukraine. It is responsible for operations in the Black Sea and the Sea of Azov, and for protecting Ukraine's coastline and maritime interests. Its headquarters is in Odesa.

History

Ukrainian naval forces trace their origins to the navy of the Ukrainian People's Republic in 1917–1918, when much of the Imperial Russian Black Sea Fleet raised Ukrainian flags. After the Soviet period, the modern navy was re-established in 1992 following Ukraine's independence. The division of the Soviet Black Sea Fleet between Ukraine and Russia was contentious and was only settled by agreements in 1997, under which Ukraine received a minority share of the fleet and leased facilities in Sevastopol, Crimea, to the Russian Black Sea Fleet.

The navy's flagship became the frigate Hetman Sahaidachny, completed in 1993. Ukrainian ships participated in international exercises such as Sea Breeze with NATO navies, and in counter-piracy operations off Somalia.

2014 loss of Crimea

Russia's annexation of Crimea in 2014 was a catastrophe for the navy: its main base at Sevastopol and most of its ships were seized, and many personnel defected to Russia, though around a third of the fleet's personnel remained loyal and relocated to Odesa. Most captured ships were returned in damaged condition during 2014, but the navy lost the bulk of its combat capability and spent the following years rebuilding around patrol craft, coastal defence and naval infantry.

Russo-Ukrainian War

During the full-scale Russian invasion beginning in February 2022, the navy and other Ukrainian forces contested the northwestern Black Sea. The flagship Hetman Sahaidachny was scuttled in Mykolaiv to prevent capture. Ukraine's sinking of the Russian cruiser Moskva in April 2022 with Neptune anti-ship missiles — a programme of the Ukrainian defence industry used by coastal defence forces — was a landmark of the war at sea. Ukrainian forces subsequently used uncrewed surface vessels (naval drones), missiles and special operations to attack the Russian Black Sea Fleet in Sevastopol, compelling Russia to relocate much of the fleet eastward and enabling Ukraine to reopen a maritime export corridor from Odesa ports despite the loss of most of its conventional warships.

Structure and rebuilding

The navy includes surface forces, naval aviation, coastal defence troops (including missile and artillery units), naval infantry and special forces. Wartime and post-war rebuilding has involved foreign-supplied vessels — including Island-class and other patrol boats from the United States, Ada-class corvettes ordered from Turkey (the first named Hetman Ivan Mazepa), and mine countermeasures ships transferred by the United Kingdom and the Netherlands.
