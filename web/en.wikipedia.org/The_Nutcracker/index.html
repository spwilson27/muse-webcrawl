The Nutcracker (Russian: Щелкунчик, Shchelkunchik), Op. 71, is an 1892 two-act classical ballet by Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky, set on Christmas Eve at the foot of a Christmas tree in a child's imagination. The plot is an adaptation of Alexandre Dumas's 1844 short story The Nutcracker, itself a retelling of E. T. A. Hoffmann's 1816 story "The Nutcracker and the Mouse King". Although the original production was not a popular success, the ballet eventually became the most frequently performed of all ballets and a defining Christmas tradition, especially in North America.

Synopsis

At a Christmas Eve party, young Clara receives a nutcracker doll from her godfather, the mysterious Drosselmeyer. At midnight the toys come to life; the Nutcracker battles the Mouse King and is transformed into a prince. He leads Clara through the Land of Snow to the Kingdom of Sweets, ruled by the Sugar Plum Fairy, where dances from many lands are performed in their honour, including the Arabian, Chinese, and Russian dances and the Waltz of the Flowers. Clara awakens back at home — or was it all a dream?

Music

The score introduced the celesta to orchestral use in its famous "Dance of the Sugar Plum Fairy." Tchaikovsky extracted a 20-minute suite, Op. 71a, before the premiere; the suite became immediately popular. The full score features a large orchestra with children's chorus in the Snow Scene.

Reception and productions

George Balanchine's 1954 New York City Ballet staging established the work as an American holiday institution; ballet companies commonly earn a large share of annual revenue from it. Major productions include those by Rudolf Nureyev, Mikhail Baryshnikov, and Matthew Bourne.
