Sannyasa

Sanskritसंन्यास (saṃnyāsa)
Meaning"Renunciation", "laying down"
SystemFourth āśrama of life in Hinduism
PractitionerSannyāsī (male), sannyāsinī (female)
GoalMoksha (liberation)

Sannyasa (Sanskrit: संन्यास, romanized: Saṃnyāsa) is the life stage of renunciation within the Hindu philosophy of the four āśramas (life stages). It is the fourth stage, following brahmacharya (student), grihastha (householder) and vanaprastha (forest-dweller), and is traditionally entered in old age, when the renouncer — a sannyasi — abandons material possessions and social obligations to devote the remainder of life to spiritual practice and the pursuit of moksha, liberation from the cycle of rebirth.

Practice

Initiation into sannyasa is conferred by a guru in a ceremony that includes the renouncer's own symbolic funeral rites, marking civil and ritual death to his former identity. The sannyasi typically wears plain ochre or saffron robes, shaves the head, carries a staff (daṇḍa) and water pot (kamaṇḍalu), owns no property, and subsists on alms. Vows include celibacy, non-violence, truthfulness and detachment from family, caste and ritual status.

Orders and traditions

The most influential organisation is the Dashanami Sampradaya, the "ten-name" order traditionally founded by the 8th-century philosopher Adi Shankara, whose monastic centres (mathas) anchor Advaita Vedanta. Shaiva, Vaishnava and later movements maintain their own renunciant lineages; Swami Vivekananda's Ramakrishna Order recast sannyasa as a life of organised social service. Comparable renunciation exists in Buddhism and Jainism, whose monastic ideals historically interacted with the Hindu sannyasa tradition.

Modern usage

While classical texts reserve the stage for the final quarter of life, many modern Hindus take sannyasa in youth or middle age, and the institution adapts to ashram-based teaching, publishing and charitable work.
