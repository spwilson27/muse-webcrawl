Garter snakes are snakes of the genus Thamnophis, widespread in North America from Canada to Central America. Many species have longitudinal yellow, green or pale stripes resembling a garter, though patterns vary widely and some are checkered or nearly plain.

Habitat and diet

They live in meadows, woodland edges, marshes, gardens and near ponds and streams. Garter snakes feed on frogs, toads, fish, earthworms, slugs, small rodents and other prey. Several species are semi-aquatic.

Reproduction and defence

Most garter snakes are ovoviviparous, giving birth to live young. When threatened they may flatten the head, strike, release musk or flee. They were long considered harmless, but mild venom and prey-specific toxins occur in some species; bites to humans are rarely medically significant.

Ecology

Garter snakes are important predators of amphibians and invertebrates and prey for birds, mammals and larger snakes. The common garter snake is the state reptile of Massachusetts.
