Volcanic crater lake

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A volcanic crater lake is a lake in a crater that was formed by explosive activity or a collapse during a volcanic eruption. Crater lakes form in volcanic craters and calderas when precipitation fills the depression, or when a pre-existing lake survives an eruption. Lakes in maars (broad, low-relief craters caused by phreatomagmatic eruptions) are sometimes included, as are acidic lakes heated by volcanic gases.

Formation and types

After an eruption ends, rainwater and groundwater collect in the bowl of the crater faster than they can seep away or evaporate, and a lake forms. Some are among the deepest lakes on Earth: Crater Lake in Oregon, USA, is 594 m deep, the deepest in the United States. Others are highly acidic, such as the lake at Kawah Ijen in Indonesia (pH near 0.3) and Poás in Costa Rica, making them hostile to most life. Temperature and colour changes in crater lakes are monitored as indicators of volcanic unrest.

Hazards

Crater lakes can be dangerous. They may be expelled catastrophically in eruptions, generating lahars — as at Kelud (Indonesia) and Ruapehu (New Zealand). A peculiar hazard is the limnic eruption: in 1986, Lake Nyos in Cameroon suddenly released a cloud of dissolved carbon dioxide that asphyxiated about 1,700 people and thousands of livestock in nearby villages; Lake Kivu, on the Rwanda–DRC border, holds far larger quantities of CO2 and methane and is monitored for the same risk.

Notable examples

Crater Lake (Oregon), Lake Toba (Indonesia — the largest volcanic lake in the world), Lake Taupō (New Zealand), Lake Nyos (Cameroon), Kawah Ijen (Indonesia), Lake Batur (Bali), and the crater lakes of the Azores, including Lagoa das Sete Cidades.
