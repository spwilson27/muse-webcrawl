Ezana of Axum

Ezana's later coins bear the Christian cross.

Ezana (Ge'ez: ዒዛና) was a 4th-century ruler of the Kingdom of Axum in what is now Ethiopia and Eritrea, reigning from roughly 320 to 360 AD. He is best known for converting to Christianity, making Axum one of the earliest states in the world to adopt the Christian faith officially.

Reign

Ezana succeeded his father Ella Amida while still young, with his mother as regent. His inscriptions—recorded in Ge'ez, Sabaean, and Greek—describe military campaigns against the Noba, the Beja, and other peoples around the Nile valley and the Red Sea, extending Axumite power. Early inscriptions invoke traditional gods such as Astar, Beher, and Mahrem; later ones speak of "the Lord of Heaven", reflecting his conversion.

Conversion

According to church tradition, two shipwrecked Syrian Christian brothers, Frumentius and Aedesius, rose in the Axumite court; Frumentius was later consecrated bishop by Athanasius of Alexandria and evangelized the kingdom. Ezana's coinage marks the transition: early issues bear the disc and crescent, while later coins carry the cross—among the first uses of the cross on coinage anywhere.

Legacy

Ezana is venerated as a saint in the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church. His stelae at Axum, his trilingual inscriptions, and his coins are key sources for the history of the late antique Horn of Africa.
