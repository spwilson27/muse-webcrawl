The Xbox One is a home video game console developed by Microsoft. Announced in May 2013, it is the successor to the Xbox 360 and the third console in the Xbox series. It competed with Sony's PlayStation 4 and Nintendo's Wii U and later the Nintendo Switch in the eighth generation of consoles.

History

Microsoft initially positioned the Xbox One as an "all-in-one entertainment system", emphasizing television integration, the bundled Kinect sensor, and digital rights management policies that required periodic online check-ins and restricted used games. Strong criticism of these policies led Microsoft to reverse them before launch. The console launched on November 22, 2013, in 13 markets.

Hardware revisions

The smaller, cheaper Xbox One S was released in 2016, adding HDR support and 4K video playback. In November 2017 Microsoft released the Xbox One X, described as "the world's most powerful console", with a 6 teraflop GPU targeting native 4K gaming. An All-Digital Edition of the One S, without an optical drive, followed in 2019.

Software and services

The Xbox One runs a system software derived from Windows. It introduced broad backward compatibility with Xbox 360 and original Xbox games from 2015 onward, and the Xbox Game Pass subscription service, launched in 2017, became a central part of Microsoft's gaming strategy.

Reception and sales

The console was praised for its controller, multimedia features and later hardware revisions, but criticized for its higher launch price and weaker graphical performance compared to the PlayStation 4. Microsoft stopped reporting sales figures early in the generation; estimates place lifetime sales at roughly 50–58 million units. Production of all Xbox One models ended in 2020 as focus shifted to the Xbox Series X and Series S.
