The Maritime Alps (French: Alpes Maritimes; Italian: Alpi Marittime) are a mountain range in the southwestern part of the Alps. They form the border between the French region of Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur and the Italian regions of Piedmont and Liguria, and are the southernmost major chain of the Alps, reaching almost to the Mediterranean coast near Nice.

Geography

The range runs roughly from the Col de Tende in the east to the Col de Larche in the northwest. Its highest summit is Monte Argentera (3,297 m) in Italy, followed by Monte Clapier (3,045 m), the southernmost three-thousander of the Alps, and Mont Gélas (3,143 m) on the border. Deep valleys carved by the Verdon, Tinée, Vésubie, and Roya rivers cut into the range; the Verdon Gorge is among Europe's largest canyons.

Nature

The Maritime Alps are renowned for exceptional biodiversity, sitting at the junction of Mediterranean and alpine climates and hosting many endemic plant species; the area served as a glacial refugium during the Ice Ages. Wolves returned naturally here from Italy in the 1990s, the first confirmed wolf presence in France in decades. The French Mercantour National Park (established 1979) and the adjoining Italian Alpi Marittime park form a transboundary protected complex, and the Vallée des Merveilles preserves thousands of Bronze Age rock engravings.
