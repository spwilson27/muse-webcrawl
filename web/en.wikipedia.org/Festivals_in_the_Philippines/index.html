Festivals in the Philippines

Festivals in the Philippines are a central feature of national culture. Most are religious celebrations honouring the patron saint of a town or an image of Jesus or Mary — a legacy of three centuries of Spanish Catholic rule — typically held as a town fiesta with processions, street dancing, beauty pageants, feasting, and competitions. Others commemorate harvests, historical events, or local products. There are hundreds of festivals throughout the year, at least one somewhere in the country on almost any given day.

Major festivals

  • Sinulog (Cebu City, January) — honours the Santo Niño (Child Jesus) with a grand street parade and fluvial procession.
  • Ati-Atihan (Kalibo, Aklan, January) — the "Mother of All Philippine Festivals", in which devotees blacken their faces with soot and chant "Hala Bira!" in honour of the Santo Niño.
  • Dinagyang (Iloilo City, January) — a related Santo Niño festival famous for its choreographed tribal dance competitions.
  • Panagbenga (Baguio, February) — the Flower Festival, featuring parades of floats covered in fresh flowers.
  • Moriones (Marinduque, Holy Week) — penitents wear masks and costumes of Roman soldiers re-enacting the legend of Longinus.
  • Pahiyas (Lucban, Quezon, May) — houses are decorated with colourful rice wafers called kiping in thanksgiving for the harvest, honouring San Isidro Labrador.
  • Kadayawan (Davao City, August) — a harvest thanksgiving celebrating the region's indigenous peoples, flowers, and fruits.
  • MassKara (Bacolod, October) — the Festival of Smiles, created in 1980, known for dancers in grinning masks.
  • Giant Lantern Festival (San Fernando, Pampanga, December) — a competition of huge illuminated parol lanterns, earning the city the title "Christmas Capital of the Philippines".

Character

Philippine festivals blend Catholic devotion with pre-colonial ritual and community celebration. They are major engines of domestic tourism and homecomings for overseas Filipino workers, and they express a strongly local identity: nearly every province, city, and municipality maintains its own signature fiesta.
