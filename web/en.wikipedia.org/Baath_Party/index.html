Ba'ath Party

The Arab Socialist Ba'ath Party ("Ba'ath" meaning "renaissance" or "resurrection") was a political party founded in Damascus, Syria, in 1947 by Michel Aflaq, Salah al-Din al-Bitar and Zaki al-Arsuzi. It promoted Ba'athism, an ideology combining Arab nationalism, pan-Arabism and Arab socialism under the slogan "Unity, Freedom, Socialism".

History

The party grew rapidly across the Arab world after merging with Akram al-Hawrani's Arab Socialist Party in 1952. Ba'athists seized power in Iraq and Syria in coups in 1963, but a bitter split in 1966 divided the party into rival Syrian- and Iraqi-dominated factions that became implacable enemies. In Syria the party ruled continuously from 1963 until the fall of Bashar al-Assad's government in December 2024; in Iraq it was the vehicle of Saddam Hussein's rule from 1979 until the 2003 invasion, after which it was banned and its members purged under de-Ba'athification.

Organisation

The party was organised hierarchically, with regional (national) commands in each Arab country nominally subordinate to a pan-Arab National Command, and it functioned in power as the core of authoritarian one-party states fused with the military and security services.
