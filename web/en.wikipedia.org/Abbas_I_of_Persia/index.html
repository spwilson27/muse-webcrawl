Abbas I of Persia

Shah of Persia1588–1629
Born1571, Herat
Died1629, Behshahr
DynastySafavid

Abbas I (1571–1629), commonly known as Abbas the Great, was the fifth Safavid shah of Persia, reigning from 1588 until his death. He is generally considered the greatest ruler of the Safavid dynasty, under whom Persia reached the height of its political and cultural power.

Reign

Abbas came to the throne amid disorder, with the Qizilbash factions dominant at court and Ottoman and Uzbek armies occupying Persian territory. He reduced Qizilbash influence by building a standing army of Georgian, Circassian, and Armenian converts (ghulams) and musketeers, advised in part by the English Shirley brothers. After a humiliating peace with the Ottomans in 1590, he rebuilt his forces, defeated the Uzbeks, and then reconquered territory from the Ottomans, recovering Baghdad in 1623. With English naval help he expelled the Portuguese from Hormuz in 1622.

Isfahan

In 1598 Abbas moved his capital to Isfahan, which he rebuilt into one of the great cities of the early modern world, centered on the Naqsh-e Jahan Square with its mosques and palace. He settled Armenian merchants from Julfa in the suburb of New Julfa and fostered the silk trade through a royal monopoly.
