Indian Institute of Science

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Indian Institute of Science (IISc) is a public, deemed research university for higher education and research in science and engineering, located in Bengaluru, Karnataka, India. Founded in 1909 with the visionary support of industrialist Jamsetji Tata, it is widely regarded as India's premier research institution.

The institute was conceived after a chance meeting between Jamsetji Tata and Swami Vivekananda in 1893, and was established with land granted by the Maharaja of Mysore, Krishnaraja Wadiyar IV. Its first director was the British chemist Morris Travers, and its first Indian director was the Nobel laureate Sir C. V. Raman, who also founded its physics department.

Academics

IISc is primarily a postgraduate institution, with more than 3,000 research students pursuing PhD and master's degrees, alongside an undergraduate programme introduced in 2011. It is organised into six divisions — Biological Sciences; Chemical Sciences; Electrical, Electronics and Computer Sciences; Interdisciplinary Research; Mechanical Sciences; and Physical and Mathematical Sciences — comprising over 40 departments and centres.

Campus and reputation

The main campus occupies about 400 acres in northern Bengaluru and is noted for its greenery and heritage buildings. IISc consistently tops Indian university rankings, has been designated an Institution of Eminence by the Government of India, and its faculty have included G. N. Ramachandran, Satish Dhawan, and C. N. R. Rao.
