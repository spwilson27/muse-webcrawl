William Henry Harrison

William Henry Harrison (February 9, 1773 – April 4, 1841) was an American military officer and politician who served as the ninth president of the United States in 1841, having died of pneumonia just 31 days after taking office.

Early military career

Harrison was born in Charles City County, Virginia, the youngest son of Benjamin Harrison V, a signer of the Declaration of Independence. He joined the army and fought in the Northwest Indian War, then became the first governor of the Indiana Territory (1801–1812), negotiating treaties that ceded large amounts of Native American land.

War of 1812

He won fame at the Battle of Tippecanoe (1811) against Tecumseh's confederacy — giving him the nickname "Old Tippecanoe" — and later commanded American forces in the Northwest during the War of 1812, winning the Battle of the Thames (1813), where Tecumseh was killed.

Presidency and death

After service in Congress and as minister to Gran Colombia, Harrison ran as the Whig candidate in 1840 ("Tippecanoe and Tyler Too"), defeating Martin Van Buren. At 68 he was the oldest president elected at that time, and he delivered the longest inaugural address in American history in cold, wet weather. He died a month later — the first president to die in office — and was succeeded by John Tyler.
