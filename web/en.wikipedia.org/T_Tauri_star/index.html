T Tauri stars are a class of variable stars less than about ten million years old, named after the prototype T Tauri in the constellation Taurus. They are pre-main-sequence stars — young stars still contracting toward the main sequence — found near molecular clouds, and they represent the stage in stellar evolution immediately before a star like the Sun begins sustained hydrogen fusion.

Characteristics

T Tauri stars have masses similar to or less than the Sun and are larger and cooler than main-sequence stars of the same mass. They show irregular brightness variations, strong chromospheric emission lines (notably H-alpha), infrared excess from a surrounding protoplanetary disk, and powerful stellar winds and jets. As they accrete material from the disk their rotation and activity decline; over millions of years the disk disperses, potentially forming planets.

Classification

Astronomers distinguish classical T Tauri stars, which show strong emission and active accretion, from weak-lined (naked) T Tauri stars, whose disks have largely dissipated. Together with the more massive Herbig Ae/Be stars they are the principal observable window into the earliest stages of star and planet formation.
