Merida, Yucatan

Mérida
Country: Mexico
State: Yucatán
Founded: 6 January 1542
Population: approx. 920,000 (city); over 1.2 million metro

Mérida (Spanish: Mérida) is the capital and largest city of the Mexican state of Yucatán, and the cultural and financial centre of the Yucatán Peninsula. Founded in 1542 by the Spanish conquistador Francisco de Montejo the Younger on the site of the Maya city of T'hó, it was named after Mérida in Spain.

History

The colonial city grew around its cathedral — one of the oldest in mainland America — and the Casa de Montejo. In the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries Mérida became extraordinarily wealthy from the henequen (sisal) boom, building the mansions of the Paseo de Montejo; it was said to have more millionaires per capita than anywhere else in the world. The Caste War of Yucatán and later political reforms reshaped the region.

Culture and sights

Known as the "White City" (La Ciudad Blanca), Mérida is celebrated for its safety, colonial architecture, cuisine — including cochinita pibil, sopa de lima and papadzules — and living Maya heritage; Yucatecan Spanish retains many Maya words and the regional dialect is distinctive. Weekly cultural programming fills the historic centre with music, dance (jarana), and the traditional Monday vaquería. The Gran Museo del Mundo Maya displays Maya art and artefacts. Mérida has twice been named American Capital of Culture.

Geography and climate

The city sits on flat limestone about 9 metres above sea level, some 35 km inland from the Gulf port of Progreso. Its climate is tropical savanna, hot year-round with a rainy season from June to October; cenotes dot the surrounding countryside.
