Glee club

A glee club is a musical group or choir group, historically of male voices but now often mixed, which traditionally specialises in the singing of short songs known as glees, as well as part songs, folk songs, and other choral repertoire. Glee clubs are most often associated with schools, colleges, and universities, particularly in the United States.

History

The name derives from the glee, an unaccompanied English part song popular in the 18th and early 19th centuries; London's Glee Club, founded in 1783, gathered professional musicians for convivial singing. The first collegiate glee club in America was founded at Harvard University in 1858, and similar groups proliferated at Yale, Michigan, and elsewhere, often paired with mandolin and banjo clubs on concert tours.

Modern glee clubs

Many university glee clubs — including the Harvard Glee Club, the Yale Glee Club, and the University of Michigan Men's Glee Club — evolved into serious choral ensembles performing major works with orchestra. In secondary schools, "glee club" commonly denotes a general choral ensemble. The television series Glee (2009–2015), centred on a high-school show choir, revived popular interest in the term.
