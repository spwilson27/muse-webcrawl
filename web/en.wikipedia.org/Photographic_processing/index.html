Photographic processing

Photographic processing, or film development, is the chemical means by which photographic film or paper is treated after photographic exposure to produce a negative or positive image. Processing transforms the latent image into a visible image, makes it permanent, and renders it insensitive to light.

Stages

For black-and-white film the main stages are development, in which exposed silver halide crystals are reduced to metallic silver; a stop bath; fixing, which removes unexposed halide; and washing and drying. Colour processes such as C-41 (negatives) and E-6 (slides) add dye-forming chemistry and bleaching steps.

Darkroom practice

Processing is carried out in total darkness or under safelight conditions, with careful control of temperature, time and agitation to ensure consistent results.
