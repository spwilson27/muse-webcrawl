Child support

Child support (or child maintenance) is an ongoing, periodic payment made by a parent for the financial benefit of a child following the end of a marriage or other similar relationship. It is typically paid by a non-custodial parent to a custodial parent, caregiver, or the state, and is intended to share the costs of raising the child.

Legal basis

The obligation rests on the principle that both parents are legally bound to support their children regardless of their relationship with each other. The United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child (Article 27) recognises the child's right to an adequate standard of living and the duty of states to secure recovery of maintenance. In the United States, child support is governed by state guidelines — commonly income-shares, percentage-of-income, or Melson formula models — under a federal framework established in 1975 and strengthened by the Family Support Act of 1988, which made guidelines presumptive and wage withholding routine.

Calculation and enforcement

Amounts are generally calculated from both parents' incomes, the number of children, custody time, and costs such as health insurance and childcare, and orders usually continue until the child reaches the age of majority or finishes secondary school. Enforcement tools include automatic income withholding, tax-refund intercepts, liens, licence suspension, and, for wilful non-payment, contempt of court. International recovery is facilitated by the 2007 Hague Child Support Convention. In England and Wales, the Child Maintenance Service calculates and collects payments for most separated parents.
