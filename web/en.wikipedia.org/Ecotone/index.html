Ecotone

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The forest–grassland boundary is a classic ecotone.

An ecotone is a transition area between two biological communities, where two communities meet and integrate. It may be narrow or wide, and it may be local (the zone between a field and forest) or regional (the transition between forest and grassland ecosystems). An ecotone may appear on the ground as a gradual blending of the two communities across a broad area, or it may manifest itself as a sharp boundary line.

Characteristics

Ecotones often contain species from both adjoining communities plus species characteristic of the transition itself, and may therefore have greater biological diversity than either neighbouring community — a phenomenon known as the edge effect. Animals that use edge habitats, such as deer, are called edge species. Some organisms are confined to ecotones entirely.

Formation and change

Ecotones arise along environmental gradients — of temperature, moisture, soil type or elevation — or where disturbance maintains a mosaic of habitats. Because they sit at the junction of communities, ecotones can shift rapidly in response to climate change, making them sensitive indicators of environmental change; alpine treelines and wetland margins are widely monitored examples.

Examples

Estuaries (between rivers and the sea), riparian zones along streams, hedgerows between fields, and the treeline between subalpine forest and alpine tundra are all ecotones.
