Helium-3

Helium-3 (3He) is a light, stable isotope of helium with two protons and one neutron. It is the only stable isotope of any element with more protons than neutrons.

Properties and uses

Helium-3 is rare on Earth, occurring at about 0.000137% of natural helium, most of it primordial. Most helium-3 used today is produced by the radioactive decay of tritium. Because helium-3 strongly absorbs thermal neutrons, it is used in neutron detectors, and it is essential for dilution refrigerators that reach temperatures below 1 kelvin. Liquid helium-3 also exhibits superfluidity at about 2.5 millikelvin.

Helium-3 has attracted attention as a potential fuel for aneutronic nuclear fusion, and its presence in lunar regolith has led to proposals for mining it on the Moon, though the practicality of such schemes remains debated.
