Lebanon

Lebanon, officially the Republic of Lebanon, is a country in the Levant region of West Asia, bordered by Syria to the north and east, Israel to the south, and the Mediterranean Sea to the west. Its capital and largest city is Beirut. Lebanon has a population of roughly five to six million people and an area of 10,452 square kilometres, making it one of the smallest countries in Asia.

History

The coast of present-day Lebanon was home to the Phoenicians, a maritime culture that flourished for over a thousand years and spread its alphabet across the Mediterranean. The region later fell under Persian, Hellenistic, Roman, Byzantine, Arab, Crusader, Mamluk, and Ottoman rule. After the First World War, France administered Lebanon under a League of Nations mandate; the country gained independence in 1943.

Lebanon's confessional political system allocates power among its religious communities. The country suffered a devastating civil war from 1975 to 1990, and in the 21st century has faced severe economic crisis beginning in 2019, the Beirut port explosion of 2020, and ongoing regional conflict.

Geography and culture

The terrain is dominated by the Lebanon and Anti-Lebanon mountain ranges, between which lies the fertile Beqaa Valley. The cedar of Lebanon is the national emblem and appears on the flag. Lebanon is known for its cuisine — including tabbouleh, hummus, and kibbeh — its ancient cities such as Byblos, Tyre, and Baalbek, and a large global diaspora.
