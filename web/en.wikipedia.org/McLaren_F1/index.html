McLaren F1

The McLaren F1 is a sports car designed and manufactured by British automobile manufacturer McLaren Cars (now McLaren Automotive) and powered by the BMW S70/2 V12 engine. The original concept was conceived by Gordon Murray, who successfully convinced Ron Dennis to back the project and hired car designer Peter Stevens to design the exterior and interior of the car.

On 31 March 1998, the XP5 prototype set the record for the world's fastest production car, reaching 386.4 km/h (240.1 mph), surpassing the Jaguar XJ220's previous record of 349.4 km/h (217.1 mph) from 1992. The F1 held this naturally aspirated production-car record for decades and remains the fastest naturally aspirated production car ever built.

Design

The F1 was the first production car to use a carbon-fibre monocoque chassis. Its most distinctive feature is the central driving position — the driver's seat is in the middle, ahead of and between two passenger seats — giving superior visibility and balance. It produces 461 kW (627 PS; 618 hp) from its 6.1-litre BMW V12, weighs just 1,138 kg (2,509 lb), and uses gold foil as an engine-bay heat shield. The car has no turbocharging, power steering, or anti-lock brakes, reflecting Murray's purist design goals.

Production and racing

Production ran from 1992 to 1998, totalling 106 cars including prototypes, road cars, and racing variants. The racing F1 GTR won the 1995 24 Hours of Le Mans outright — an extraordinary result for a car derived from a road design. Individual examples have sold at auction for over US$20 million, making the F1 one of the most valuable modern collector cars.
