Mutant (Marvel Comics)

In the Marvel Comics universe, a mutant is a human being who possesses a genetic trait — the X-gene — that grants them superhuman powers and abilities, which typically manifest at puberty. Mutants are classified in-universe as Homo sapiens superior or Homo superior, presented as the next stage in human evolution.

Publication history

The concept was introduced by Stan Lee and Jack Kirby in The X-Men #1 (1963). It gave Marvel a simple, flexible origin: characters needed no elaborate accidents or experiments — they were simply born with their powers. The mutant metaphor has been read as an allegory for marginalized groups, including ethnic minorities and the LGBTQ community.

Notable mutants

Famous mutant characters include Professor Charles Xavier, founder of the X-Men; Magneto, master of magnetism and leader of the Brotherhood of Mutants; Wolverine; Storm; Jean Grey; Cyclops; Mystique; and Emma Frost. Mutants are frequently depicted facing prejudice, registration laws, and the mutant-hunting Sentinels.

Major storylines

Landmark mutant story arcs include the "Dark Phoenix Saga", "Days of Future Past", "Age of Apocalypse", and "House of M", after which most mutants were depowered in the event known as Decimation. Later eras introduced the mutant nation of Krakoa in House of X and Powers of X (2019).
