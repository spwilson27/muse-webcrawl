The Morganza Spillway or Morganza Control Structure is a flood-control structure in the U.S. state of Louisiana along the western bank of the Lower Mississippi River at river mile 280, near Morganza in Pointe Coupee Parish. The spillway stands primed to divert flood water from the Mississippi River into the Atchafalaya Basin when the combined flow at Red River Landing threatens the design limits of the Mississippi River and Tributaries Project.

Purpose and design

The structure was authorized by the Flood Control Act of 1928 following the Great Mississippi Flood of 1927, and built by the United States Army Corps of Engineers, opening in 1954. Together with the Old River Control Structure upstream and the Bonnet Carré Spillway downstream, it forms part of the system that keeps the Mississippi on its present course past Baton Rouge and New Orleans and prevents an uncontrolled capture by the Atchafalaya River. When operated, the spillway's 125 gates release water into a floodway that carries it roughly 20 miles (32 km) to the Atchafalaya Basin.

Operation

Before 2011 the spillway had operated only once, during the Mississippi flood of 1973, when 42 gates were opened. During the Great Mississippi Flood of 2011, with record flows forecast, the Corps opened the Morganza Spillway for just the second time, beginning on 14 May 2011; a small number of bays were opened to divert water away from Baton Rouge and New Orleans, inundating parts of the Atchafalaya Basin as designed.
