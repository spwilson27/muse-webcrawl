Abbottabad is a city in the Hazara Division of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province, Pakistan. Founded in 1853 by Major James Abbott, the first deputy commissioner of the district, it is known throughout Pakistan for its pleasant weather, high-standard educational institutions, and military establishments — most notably the Pakistan Military Academy at Kakul.

History

Under British rule Abbottabad became a cantonment and hill station on the route to the northern areas. The city is famously the subject of the poem "Abbottabad" by Major James Abbott, expressing his affection for the town.

2011 raid

Abbottabad drew global attention on 2 May 2011, when United States special forces raided a compound in the city and killed Osama bin Laden, the founder of al-Qaeda, who had been living there in hiding.
