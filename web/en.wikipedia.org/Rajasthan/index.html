Rajasthan (Hindi: राजस्थान, literally "Land of Kings") is a state in northwestern India. It is the largest Indian state by area, covering 342,239 square kilometres (10.4% of India's total area), and the seventh largest by population. Rajasthan is bordered by Pakistan to the west and northwest, and by the Indian states of Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, and Gujarat. Its capital and largest city is Jaipur.

Geography

The state's dominant geographical feature is the Thar Desert (Great Indian Desert), which occupies much of its western portion, and the Aravalli Range, one of the world's oldest mountain ranges, running diagonally across the state from southwest to northeast. East of the Aravallis the land is more fertile, drained by rivers including the Chambal and Banas. The climate ranges from arid in the west to more humid in the southeast.

History

Rajasthan contains sites of the Indus Valley Civilisation such as Kalibangan. In the medieval period the region was known as Rajputana, a land of Rajput kingdoms — including Mewar (Udaipur), Marwar (Jodhpur), Amber (Jaipur), and Bikaner — whose rulers alternately resisted and allied with the Delhi Sultanate and the Mughal Empire; Maharana Pratap of Mewar became a celebrated symbol of resistance. Under British rule the Rajputana Agency grouped some twenty princely states, which were integrated into the Indian Union after 1947, with the present state taking shape by 1956.

Culture and tourism

Rajasthan is renowned for its forts and palaces — Amber Fort, Mehrangarh Fort, Jaisalmer Fort, and the hill forts collectively inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site — as well as the lake city of Udaipur, the desert city of Jaisalmer, the pink city of Jaipur, and the holy town of Pushkar with its camel fair. Jaipur's Jantar Mantar observatory and the Keoladeo National Park bird sanctuary are also World Heritage Sites. The state's culture features vivid textiles, turbans, folk music and dance (such as ghoomar), miniature painting, and festivals including the Desert Festival and Teej. Rajasthani cuisine includes dal baati churma and laal maas.

Economy

Agriculture — wheat, barley, pulses, oilseeds, and cotton — remains important despite scarce water; the Indira Gandhi Canal irrigates northwestern districts. Rajasthan leads India in production of several minerals (zinc, lead, marble, sandstone) and is a major centre for textiles, cement, tourism, and increasingly solar power generation, exploiting its desert insolation.
