Harper Lee

Nelle Harper Lee (April 28, 1926 – February 19, 2016) was an American novelist best known for To Kill a Mockingbird (1960), which won the Pulitzer Prize for Fiction in 1961 and became a classic of modern American literature. In 2015, a second novel, Go Set a Watchman — an early draft of Mockingbird — was published amid wide interest and some controversy.

Early life

Lee was born in Monroeville, Alabama, the youngest child of Amasa Coleman Lee, a lawyer whose practice partly inspired Atticus Finch. As a child she was close friends with Truman Capote, who became the model for the character Dill in her novel, and later helped Capote research his true-crime book In Cold Blood. She studied at Huntingdon College and the University of Alabama, including a period in its law school, before moving to New York City in 1949 to pursue writing.

To Kill a Mockingbird

Working as an airline reservations agent, Lee wrote in her spare time; in 1956 friends gave her a year's wages as a Christmas gift so she could write full-time. The resulting novel, published in July 1960, tells of racial injustice in a small Southern town through the eyes of Scout Finch. It was an immediate success, won the Pulitzer Prize, and was adapted into the acclaimed 1962 film starring Gregory Peck. The novel has sold more than 40 million copies and remains a fixture of school curricula.

Later life

Famously private, Lee gave few interviews and published little for decades. In 2007 she received the Presidential Medal of Freedom from President George W. Bush. The 2015 publication of Go Set a Watchman, announced after the manuscript was rediscovered, sparked debate about her capacity to consent; the Alabama authorities investigated and found no evidence of elder abuse. Lee died in Monroeville on February 19, 2016, aged 89.
