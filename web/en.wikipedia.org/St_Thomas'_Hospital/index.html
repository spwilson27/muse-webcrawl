St Thomas' Hospital is a large National Health Service teaching hospital in Lambeth, central London, facing the Palace of Westminster across the River Thames. It is one of London's oldest hospitals and forms part of Guy's and St Thomas' NHS Foundation Trust, serving as a teaching hospital for King's College London's medical school.

History

The hospital traces its origins to an Augustinian infirmary attached to the Priory of St Mary Overie in Southwark in the 12th century, later named for St Thomas Becket. It was rebuilt in Southwark after the Dissolution and moved to its present Lambeth site in 1871, where Florence Nightingale established her nursing school in 1860 — its pavilion-plan buildings reflecting her principles. The hospital was badly damaged in the Second World War and substantially rebuilt in the 1970s.

Services

St Thomas' is a tertiary referral centre particularly noted for cardiology and cardiothoracic surgery, the Evelina London Children's Hospital on its campus, maternity services at the Westminster Bridge site, and specialist care including the Lane Fox respiratory unit. Its emergency department serves the Westminster and Lambeth area.

Notable associations

Physicians associated with the hospital include Thomas Addison, William Gull and Richard Bright (the latter two at Guy's). The Florence Nightingale Museum is located within the hospital grounds.
