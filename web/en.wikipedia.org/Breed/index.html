Breed

A breed is a specific group of breedable domestic animals having homogeneous appearance (phenotype), homogeneous behavior, and/or other characteristics that distinguish it from other organisms of the same species. In livestock, breeds are typically defined and maintained through selective breeding by breeders, and recognised by breed registries and breed standards.

The term arose in the 16th–17th centuries among animal breeders. A breed is formed when animals with desirable traits are bred with each other over many generations, until the offspring consistently exhibit the same characteristics. When bred together, individuals of the same breed pass on these predictable traits to their offspring, and this ability — known as "breeding true" — is a requirement for a breed.

Breeding and breed standards

Most breeds originate from a relatively small founder population and are maintained by controlled mating. Breed standards describe the ideal characteristics of the breed, and breed associations register animals that conform to them. Examples include the many recognised breeds of dogs (such as the Labrador Retriever), cats, cattle (such as the Holstein Friesian), horses, sheep, pigs, and poultry.

Purebreds and crossbreds

An animal whose parents are both members of the same breed is termed purebred or pedigreed, while the offspring of parents of different breeds is a crossbred. Deliberate crossbreeding may be used to combine traits, and hybrid vigour (heterosis) often makes crossbred animals healthier.
