Ranking (information retrieval)


Placeholder image: Ranking (information retrieval)

In information retrieval, ranking is the process of ordering documents in response to a query by their estimated relevance. Search engines rank results so that the most useful documents appear first, typically showing only the top few.

Models

Classic ranking models include the vector space model with TF–IDF weights, the probabilistic BM25 formula, and language models. Web search added link-analysis signals such as PageRank and anchor text. Modern systems use learning-to-rank: machine-learned models trained on relevance judgments and click data to combine hundreds of features.

Evaluation

Ranking quality is measured with metrics such as precision at k, mean average precision, and normalised discounted cumulative gain (NDCG), using test collections with human relevance judgments like those from TREC.
