Earl of Sandwich is a title in the Peerage of England, created in 1660 for the naval commander and Restoration statesman Sir Edward Montagu. The title takes its name from Sandwich, Kent, though the Montagu family has been seated elsewhere for centuries, latterly at Mapperton House in Dorset.

History

Edward Montagu (1625–1672), a former Commonwealth general-at-sea, played a key role in the Restoration by bringing Charles II back to England, and was killed at the Battle of Solebay. The most famous holder is John Montagu, 4th Earl of Sandwich (1718–1792), First Lord of the Admiralty and a patron of Captain James Cook, who named the Sandwich Islands (Hawaii) after him. The 4th Earl is best known for the enduring legend — likely embellished — that he asked for meat between slices of bread so as not to leave the gaming table (or his desk), giving the English language the word "sandwich".

Earls of Sandwich

Holders include Edward Montagu, 1st Earl (1660–1672); the diplomat Edward Montagu, 3rd Earl; John Montagu, 4th Earl; George Montagu, 8th Earl, a Conservative MP; Victor Montagu, 10th Earl, who disclaimed the peerage in 1964 under the Peerage Act to pursue a Commons career; and John Montagu, 11th Earl (1943–2025), a crossbench peer and businessman who co-founded the Earl of Sandwich restaurant chain in the United States.
