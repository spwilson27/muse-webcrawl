Mahdist State

Capital: Omdurman
Period: 1885–1899
Founder: Muhammad Ahmad, the Mahdi
Successor ruler: Khalifa Abdallahi ibn Muhammad

The Mahdist State, also known as Mahdist Sudan, was a state based on a religious and nationalist movement founded by Muhammad Ahmad bin Abdullah, who declared himself the Mahdi — the prophesied redeemer of Islam — in 1881. From its capital at Omdurman, across the Nile from Khartoum, it ruled much of present-day Sudan between 1885 and 1899.

Rise

The Mahdist revolt rose against Turco-Egyptian rule in Sudan. Mahdist forces annihilated Egyptian garrisons, defeated an expedition under Hicks Pasha in 1883, and captured Khartoum in January 1885 after a long siege, in which General Charles Gordon was killed. The Mahdi himself died only months later, in June 1885, and was succeeded by his Khalifa, Abdallahi ibn Muhammad.

Rule and fall

Under the Khalifa the state was organised as a theocratic Islamic polity with its own army, treasury, judiciary, and coinage, though it endured famine, internal purges, and wars with Ethiopia, Egypt, and the French and Italians on its frontiers. Anglo-Egyptian forces under Herbert Kitchener advanced up the Nile and destroyed the Mahdist army at the Battle of Omdurman on 2 September 1898; the Khalifa was killed the following year at Umm Diwaykarat, ending the state and establishing the Anglo-Egyptian condominium over Sudan.
