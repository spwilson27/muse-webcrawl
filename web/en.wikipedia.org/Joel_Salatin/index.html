Joel F. Salatin (born 24 February 1957) is an American farmer, lecturer and author whose Polyface Farm in Virginia's Shenandoah Valley uses rotational grazing and direct marketing. He is a prominent figure in the sustainable agriculture and local food movements, describing himself as a "Christian libertarian environmentalist capitalist lunatic farmer".

Polyface Farm

The Salatin family bought the 550-acre Polyface Farm in 1961. Joel and his wife Teresa took over its management in the 1980s. The farm raises grass-fed beef, pastured poultry (broilers and laying hens in mobile shelters), pigs raised in woodland, turkeys and rabbits. Key methods include management-intensive rotational grazing of cattle in daily paddock moves, mobile chicken shelters ("egg mobiles" and broiler pens) following the cattle to sanitise pasture and spread manure, and composting of livestock bedding.

Writing and advocacy

Salatin has written numerous books, including Pastured Poultry Profits (1993), Salad Bar Beef (1995), You Can Farm (1998), Everything I Want to Do Is Illegal (2007), Folks, This Ain't Normal (2011) and The Marvelous Pigness of Pigs (2016). He gained wider fame through Michael Pollan's The Omnivore's Dilemma (2006) and the documentary Food, Inc. (2008). He advocates local food systems, criticises industrial agriculture and food-safety regulation he sees as biased against small producers, and sells primarily within his region, declining to ship meat long distances.

Criticism

Critics question the scalability of his model and some of his positions on regulation and labour (the farm's use of interns and apprentices). Salatin's outspoken libertarian views have also drawn controversy.
