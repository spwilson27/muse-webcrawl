Isle of Harris

The Isle of Harris (Scottish Gaelic: Na Hearadh) is the southern part of Lewis and Harris, the largest island in the Outer Hebrides of Scotland. Although called an isle, it is joined to the Isle of Lewis by a narrow isthmus at Tarbert, its main village and ferry port.

Geography

North Harris is mountainous, with Clisham (799 m) the highest peak in the Outer Hebrides, while the west coast is famed for white-sand beaches such as Luskentyre and Scarista, backed by machair grassland. The east coast, known as the Bays, is a rocky landscape of small inlets settled after the Highland Clearances.

Harris Tweed

Harris is the home of Harris Tweed, the handwoven woollen cloth protected by law and trademarked with the Orb symbol: by definition it must be handwoven by islanders at their homes in the Outer Hebrides from pure virgin wool dyed and spun in the islands. The industry remains a mainstay of the local economy alongside fishing, fish farming and tourism.
