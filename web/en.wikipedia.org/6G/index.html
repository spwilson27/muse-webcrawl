6G is the planned sixth generation of cellular mobile communication technology, intended to succeed 5G. Still in the research and standardization stage, 6G is expected to be commercially launched around 2030, following the roughly ten-year cadence of previous generations.

Vision and targets

The ITU's IMT-2030 framework, agreed in 2023, describes usage scenarios including immersive communication, hyper-reliable low-latency links, massive communication, ubiquitous connectivity, integrated sensing and communication, and integration of artificial intelligence into the network itself. Research targets include peak data rates of up to 1 Tbit/s, air-interface latency below 0.1 ms, and native support for joint communication and sensing.

Proposed technologies

Candidate technologies include operation in new spectrum between 7 and 15 GHz and at sub-terahertz frequencies above 100 GHz, reconfigurable intelligent surfaces, cell-free massive MIMO, AI-native air interfaces, and non-terrestrial networks linking satellites directly into the system. National research programs — including the EU's Hexa-X, China's IMT-2030 group, and the US Next G Alliance — coordinate early work, with 3GPP expected to publish the first 6G specifications late in the decade.
