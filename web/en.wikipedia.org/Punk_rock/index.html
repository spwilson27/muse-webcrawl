Punk rock (also known as simply punk) is a music genre that emerged in the mid-1970s. Rooted in 1950s rock and roll, 1960s garage rock, and the protopunk bands of the late 1960s and early 1970s (the Stooges, MC5, the New York Dolls), punk rejected the perceived excesses of mainstream 1970s rock — progressive rock virtuosity, long solos, and arena spectacle — in favour of short, fast, hard-edged songs, stripped-down instrumentation, do-it-yourself production, and lyrics that were often political and confrontational.

First wave (1974–1978)

In New York, the scene centred on the club CBGB produced the Ramones, Television, Patti Smith, Blondie, and Talking Heads; the Ramones' 1976 debut album, with most songs under two and a half minutes, became a template. In London, the Sex Pistols, managed by Malcolm McLaren and outfitted by Vivienne Westwood's King's Road shop, ignited a British explosion with "Anarchy in the U.K." (1976) and "God Save the Queen" (1977); the Clash fused punk with reggae and political commentary, while the Damned, the Buzzcocks, and Siouxsie and the Banshees broadened the sound. Australia's the Saints and Radio Birdman developed in parallel.

Ethos and aesthetics

Punk popularised the DIY (do-it-yourself) ethic: self-released records on independent labels, photocopied fanzines such as Sniffin' Glue, and the idea that technical proficiency was secondary to energy and intent — summed up in the famous chord-diagram slogan "this is a chord, this is another, this is a third. Now form a band." Punk fashion — torn clothing, safety pins, spiked and dyed hair, leather jackets — became a durable visual language of rebellion.

Successors and subgenres

By the late 1970s punk fragmented and regenerated: post-punk (Joy Division, Gang of Four) took the experimental path; new wave commercialised it; Oi! and UK82 hardened it; and in the United States, hardcore punk (Black Flag, Minor Threat, Bad Brains, Dead Kennedys) pushed speed and intensity further, growing an independent network of labels (SST, Dischord), all-ages shows, and straight-edge culture. Anarcho-punk (Crass), riot grrrl (Bikini Kill) in the 1990s, and the mainstream success of pop punk (Green Day, Blink-182) and grunge (Nirvana, steeped in punk) carried punk's influence to global audiences.

Legacy

Punk reshaped popular music's economics (independent labels, alternative distribution), fashion, graphic design, and youth culture, and it remains a live global subculture. The Rock and Roll Hall of Fame has inducted the Ramones, the Sex Pistols, the Clash, Patti Smith, and others, marking punk's canonisation even as new scenes continue to contest its meaning.
