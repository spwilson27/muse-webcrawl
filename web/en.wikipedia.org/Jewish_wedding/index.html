Jewish wedding

A Jewish wedding is a wedding ceremony that follows Jewish laws and traditions. Central elements include the signing of the ketubah (marriage contract), the ceremony under the chuppah (canopy), the exchange of a ring, the recitation of the seven blessings (Sheva Brachot), and the breaking of a glass.

The ceremony has two ancient stages: erusin (betrothal) and nissuin (marriage), now combined. Customs vary among Ashkenazi, Sephardi, and other communities, and may include the bride circling the groom, the badeken veiling ceremony, and festive dancing.
