Financial Action Task Force

The Financial Action Task Force (FATF), also known by its French name Groupe d'action financière (GAFI), is an intergovernmental organisation founded in 1989 on the initiative of the G7 to develop policies to combat money laundering. Its mandate was expanded in 2001 to include terrorist financing, and later proliferation financing. The FATF Secretariat is housed at the OECD headquarters in Paris.

Role and recommendations

The FATF sets international standards in the form of 40 Recommendations covering criminal justice, financial regulation, and international cooperation. It evaluates its members through a process of mutual evaluations and publishes lists of jurisdictions with weak anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing (AML/CFT) regimes: a "black list" of high-risk jurisdictions subject to a call for action, and a "grey list" of jurisdictions under increased monitoring. Listing can restrict a country's access to the international financial system, giving the FATF's soft-law standards substantial practical force.

Membership

The FATF has around forty members, including most major financial centres, along with a network of FATF-style regional bodies whose associate members extend its standards to more than 200 jurisdictions worldwide.
