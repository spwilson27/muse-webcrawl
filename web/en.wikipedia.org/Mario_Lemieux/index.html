Mario Lemieux (born 1965) is a Canadian former professional ice hockey player, widely considered one of the greatest players of all time. Nicknamed "Le Magnifique" and "Super Mario", he played his entire NHL career with the Pittsburgh Penguins, later becoming the team's owner.

Career

Drafted first overall in 1984, Lemieux combined size, reach and extraordinary skill, winning the Art Ross Trophy six times and the Hart Trophy three times. He led Pittsburgh to consecutive Stanley Cups in 1991 and 1992, winning the Conn Smythe Trophy both years. His career was repeatedly interrupted by illness and injury, including Hodgkin lymphoma in 1993 — from which he returned the same season to win the scoring title — and chronic back problems.

Ownership and comeback

After retiring in 1997 and entering the Hockey Hall of Fame immediately, Lemieux led a group that bought the bankrupt Penguins in 1999, then returned to the ice in 2000, a feat unmatched in North American major sports. He captained Canada to Olympic gold in 2002.
