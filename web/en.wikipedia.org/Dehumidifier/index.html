Dehumidifier

A dehumidifier is an air conditioning device which reduces and maintains the level of humidity in the air. This is done usually for health or thermal comfort reasons, or to eliminate musty odor and to prevent the growth of mildew by extracting water from the air. It can be used for household, commercial, or industrial applications. Large dehumidifiers are used in commercial buildings such as indoor ice rinks and swimming pools, as well as manufacturing plants or storage warehouses.

Overview

Dehumidifiers extract water from air that passes through the unit. There are two common types: condensate dehumidifiers and desiccant dehumidifiers, which operate on different principles. Condensate dehumidifiers, also known as refrigerative dehumidifiers, draw moist air over a refrigerated coil; the water vapor condenses on the cold surface and drips into a collection tank or drain. Desiccant dehumidifiers use a moisture-absorbing material such as silica gel, which is periodically regenerated by heating.

Applications

Maintaining indoor relative humidity in the range of roughly 30 to 50 percent helps protect buildings and furnishings from moisture damage, discourages dust mites and mold, and improves comfort. Dehumidifiers are commonly used in basements, crawl spaces, laundry rooms, and archives, and for drying buildings after flooding or during construction.

Thermal and ionic types

Beyond the refrigerative and desiccant types, thermoelectric dehumidifiers use the Peltier effect to create a cold surface for condensation, and are used in small, quiet applications. Ionic membrane dehumidifiers move water vapor through a membrane by electrolysis and are used in sealed enclosures such as electrical cabinets.

History

The first dehumidifier was created by American inventor Willis Carrier in 1902 as a byproduct of his work on air conditioning for a printing plant in Brooklyn, where controlling humidity was essential to keep paper dimensions and ink registration stable.
