Conway is a city in the U.S. state of Arkansas and the county seat of Faulkner County, located in the state's most populous Metropolitan Statistical Area, about 30 miles (48 km) northwest of Little Rock.

Conway is known as the home of Hendrix College and the University of Central Arkansas. It is also home to Central Baptist College, giving it the nickname "The City of Colleges".

History

Conway was founded in 1872 by Asa P. Robinson, who came shortly after the Civil War to the area while locating his railroad's roundhouse. It was first the site of a roundhouse for the Little Rock and Fort Smith Railroad, later the St. Louis, Iron Mountain and Southern Railway (now Union Pacific). The settlement was named for the Conway family, prominent planters from the area.

Lake Conway, east of the city, was built in 1949 and is considered one of the largest lakes built by a game and fish commission in the United States at 6,700 acres (27 km²).

Geography

Conway is located at 35°5′N 92°27′W, near the geographic center of Arkansas. According to the United States Census Bureau, the city has a total area of 45.6 square miles (118 km²), of which 45.3 square miles (117 km²) is land and 0.2 square miles (0.52 km²) is water.

Demographics

As of the census of 2020, there were 64,134 people residing in the city. The population density was about 1,416 inhabitants per square mile. The University of Central Arkansas and Hendrix College contribute substantially to the population.

Economy

Conway was formerly a major agricultural area, but its economic base has diversified. It is a regional shopping, medical and educational center. Employers include the University of Central Arkansas, Conway Regional Health System, and several technology and service companies. The city has promoted itself as a technology hub in central Arkansas.

Education

Conway is home to three institutions of higher education: the University of Central Arkansas (a public research university founded in 1907 as Arkansas State Normal School), Hendrix College (a private liberal arts college founded in 1876 and affiliated with the United Methodist Church), and Central Baptist College (founded in 1952). Public primary and secondary education is provided by the Conway School District.

Transportation

Interstate 40 and U.S. Route 64 pass through Conway, and the city is served by Conway Municipal Airport. Little Rock's Clinton National Airport is about 35 miles (56 km) southeast.
