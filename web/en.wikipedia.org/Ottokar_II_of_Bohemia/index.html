Ottokar II of Bohemia

Reign1253–1278
Bornc. 1233, Městec Králové
Died26 August 1278, Dürnkrut
DynastyPřemyslid

Ottokar II (Czech: Přemysl Otakar II; c. 1233 – 26 August 1278), called "the Iron and Golden King", was King of Bohemia from 1253 until his death. He built a vast Přemyslid realm stretching from Silesia to the Adriatic Sea, ruling Austria, Styria, Carinthia, and Carniola as well.

Rise

Ottokar seized Austria in 1251 and, after succeeding his father Wenceslaus I in Bohemia, defeated Béla IV of Hungary at the Battle of Kressenbrunn (1260), confirming his hold on Styria. He founded cities and castles across his lands, including Königsberg during his crusades into Prussia, and promoted German settlement and mining.

Conflict with Rudolf of Habsburg

Ottokar refused to recognise Rudolf of Habsburg's election as King of the Romans in 1273. Defeated diplomatically and militarily, he was forced to surrender his Alpine lands in 1276. Attempting to recover them, he was killed at the Battle on the Marchfeld in 1278 fighting Rudolf.

Legacy

Ottokar is remembered in Czech tradition as one of the greatest Bohemian kings, a founder of towns and a patron of Gothic architecture, though his ambition brought the Přemyslid power to its peak just before its decline.
