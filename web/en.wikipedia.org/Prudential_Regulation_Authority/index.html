The Prudential Regulation Authority (PRA) is part of the Bank of England and is responsible for the prudential regulation and supervision of banks, building societies, credit unions, insurers and major investment firms in the United Kingdom. It was created on 1 April 2013 under the Financial Services Act 2012, which dismantled the former Financial Services Authority and split its functions between the PRA (safety and soundness) and the Financial Conduct Authority (conduct of business).

Objectives and approach

The PRA's general objective is to promote the safety and soundness of the firms it regulates, with a specific objective for insurers of contributing to the securing of an appropriate degree of protection for policyholders. It supervises around 1,500 firms, focussing its resources on those whose failure would pose the greatest risk to the financial system. Its approach is judgement-based and forward-looking: it sets capital and liquidity requirements (implementing standards such as Basel III and Solvency II), runs annual stress tests of the major banks, and can require firms to hold additional capital buffers or change their business models.

Governance

The PRA is governed by the Prudential Regulation Committee of the Bank of England, chaired by the Governor. Its chief executive also serves as a Deputy Governor of the Bank. Following the UK's departure from the European Union, the Financial Services and Markets Act 2023 gave the PRA new secondary objectives relating to growth and international competitiveness.
