The Pararaton (Javanese: ꦥꦫꦫꦠꦺꦴꦤ꧀), also known as the Serat Pararaton or "Book of Kings", is a Javanese chronicle written in Middle Javanese (Kawi). It recounts the history of the kings of Singhasari and Majapahit in eastern Java, covering the period from the legendary founder Ken Arok in 1222 down to the end of the Majapahit era around 1478.

Contents

The text opens with the story of Ken Arok, a low-born adventurer who, through a mixture of cunning, prophecy and violence, founded the kingdom of Singhasari. It proceeds through the reigns of his successors, the unification of Singhasari and Kediri, the Mongol invasion of 1293, and the founding of Majapahit by Raden Wijaya. The latter part deals with the Majapahit kings, including the celebrated prime minister Gajah Mada, and ends as the empire declines. Interspersed with dynastic narrative are myths, prophecies, and accounts of omens and portents.

Historical value

Scholars treat the Pararaton with caution. Unlike the older Nagarakretagama (1365), a court poem written close to the events it describes, the Pararaton was composed centuries later and blends history with legend — most famously in its elaborate, semi-mythical biography of Ken Arok. Nevertheless, its regnal dates and king lists often agree with inscriptional evidence, and it preserves traditions found nowhere else. The text is one of the principal sources for the political history of pre-Islamic Java.

Manuscript and editions

The work survives in a single palm-leaf manuscript kept in Bali. It was first published, with a Dutch translation, by J.L.A. Brandes in 1896 and revised by N.J. Krom in 1920. Later Indonesian scholars, including Padmapuspita, produced transliterations and translations into modern Indonesian.
