Necromancy

Necromancy is the practice of magic involving communication with the dead by summoning their spirits as apparitions or visions, or by resurrection of their bodies, particularly for the purpose of divination — imparting the means to foretell future events and discover hidden knowledge. Sometimes categorised under death magic, the term was rarely used in a general sense until the 17th century to encompass varieties of black magic and witchcraft.

Antiquity

Necromancy is attested in ancient Mesopotamia, Egypt, Greece, and Rome. The oldest literary account is found in Book 11 of Homer's Odyssey, in which Odysseus travels to the underworld and raises the spirits of the dead using rituals recorded from Circe's instructions. The biblical Witch of Endor raised the spirit of Samuel for King Saul, an episode that shaped later attitudes toward the practice in Judaism and Christianity.

Medieval and early modern periods

In the late Middle Ages, necromancy came to denote a learned, clerical form of ceremonial magic involving conjuring demons as much as the dead, as in the Munich Manual of Demonic Magic. The Church condemned the practice as demonic, and it was prosecuted under witchcraft statutes, notably in the English Witchcraft Act 1542.

Modern usage and fiction

Modern occult movements such as Spiritualism revived claims of contact with the dead through mediums, séances, and spirit boards. In fantasy literature and games, "necromancer" has shifted meaning to a magician who raises corpses as undead servants, a usage popularized in the 20th century by novels, role-playing games such as Dungeons & Dragons, and video games.
