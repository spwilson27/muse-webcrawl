Bastille Day is the common name given in English-speaking countries to the national day of France, which is celebrated on 14 July each year. In French it is called the Fête nationale du 14 juillet or simply le 14 juillet.

The day commemorates the Storming of the Bastille on 14 July 1789, a turning point of the French Revolution, as well as the Fête de la Fédération that celebrated national unity one year later, in 1790. Celebrations are held throughout France; the oldest and largest regular military parade in Europe takes place in the morning on the Champs-Élysées in Paris, attended by the President of the Republic.

History

On 14 July 1789 a Parisian crowd stormed the Bastille, a royal fortress-prison that held only seven prisoners but symbolized the despotism of the Bourbon monarchy. The event came amid a crisis over bread prices and the dismissal of the reforming minister Jacques Necker. The fall of the Bastille became the iconic event of the Revolution. On 14 July 1790, the Fête de la Fédération celebrated the unity of the French nation on the Champ de Mars.

The Third Republic made 14 July the national holiday by law in 1880, deliberately leaving ambiguous whether it marked 1789 or 1790.

Celebrations

Highlights include the Paris military parade (first held in 1880), a flypast by the Patrouille de France, fireworks at the Eiffel Tower and across France, and public dances — the traditional bals des pompiers (firefighters' balls) held in Paris on the evening of 13 July. The Louvre offers free admission, and the day is a leg of the Tour de France's July racing calendar.
