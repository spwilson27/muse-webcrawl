Nikkei 225

The Nikkei 225, commonly called the Nikkei, is a stock market index for the Tokyo Stock Exchange, consisting of 225 large, publicly traded Japanese companies. Calculated daily by the newspaper Nihon Keizai Shimbun (The Nikkei) since 1950, it is a price-weighted index, similar in construction to the Dow Jones Industrial Average.

History

The index is the most widely quoted measure of Japanese equities. It reached its famous bubble-era peak of 38,915.87 on 29 December 1989, a level it did not surpass until 2024, after decades of deflation and stagnation following the asset price bubble. Futures and options on the Nikkei 225 trade in Osaka, Singapore and Chicago.

Components

Constituents span sectors such as technology, automobiles, pharmaceuticals and finance, and are reviewed periodically. Companies have included Toyota, Sony, SoftBank and Fast Retailing, whose high share price gives it outsized weight in the price-weighted index.
