Baden

Baden is a historical territory in southwestern Germany, along the east bank of the Upper Rhine. It was successively a margraviate, an electorate, a grand duchy and a republic (Land) of the German Empire and Weimar Republic, before becoming part of the modern state of Baden-Württemberg in 1952.

Geography

Baden stretches from Lake Constance in the south to Mannheim in the north, bordered by the Rhine to the west and south and by Württemberg to the east. It includes the Black Forest, the fertile Rhine plain, and cities such as Freiburg, Karlsruhe, Heidelberg and Baden-Baden.

History

The Margraviate of Baden was founded in 1112 by the House of Zähringen's successors, the House of Baden. Enlarged greatly under Napoleon, it became a Grand Duchy in 1806. After the Second World War the territory was divided between French and American occupation zones; reunification with Württemberg followed a 1951 referendum.
