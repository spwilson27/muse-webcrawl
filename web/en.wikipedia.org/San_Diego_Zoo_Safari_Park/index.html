San Diego Zoo Safari Park

Opened: 10 May 1972 (as San Diego Wild Animal Park)

Location: Escondido, San Diego County, California

Area: 1,800 acres (730 ha)

Operator: San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance

Known for: Open-range savanna habitats, California condor breeding

The San Diego Zoo Safari Park, originally named the San Diego Wild Animal Park until 2010, is an 1,800-acre (730-hectare) zoo and wildlife park in the San Pasqual Valley near Escondido, California. Operated by the San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance, it is the sister facility of the San Diego Zoo. The park is famous for its vast open-range enclosures, in which herds of African and Asian animals roam landscapes designed to resemble their native savannas, and can be viewed from an electric tram tour.

History

The park grew out of the San Diego Zoo’s need for space to breed large animals away from its cramped Balboa Park site. It opened on 10 May 1972 as the San Diego Wild Animal Park, centred on a monorail (later tram) loop around huge field exhibits. The facility became a world leader in breeding endangered hoofstock, and its “frozen zoo” concept — storing genetic material of rare species — was pioneered by its conservation research arm. The name was changed to San Diego Zoo Safari Park in 2010 to strengthen the connection with the zoo brand.

Animals and conservation

The park keeps more than 3,600 animals of over 300 species, with particular strengths in giraffes, rhinoceroses, antelope, cheetahs, tigers and gorillas, along with extensive botanical gardens of acacia, cycads and other dry-climate plants. It played a decisive role in the recovery of the California condor: condors bred at the park (and the Los Angeles Zoo) from the last wild birds taken into captivity in the 1980s have been reintroduced to the wild in California, Arizona and Baja California. Breeding successes also include the Przewalski’s horse, Arabian oryx and southern white rhinoceros. Attractions include the Africa Tram, cheetah runs, a walk-through kangaroo habitat, a tethered balloon ride and zip-line “flightline” safaris.
