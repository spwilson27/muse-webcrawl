Fall of Tripoli

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Battle of Tripoli (Arabic: معركة طرابلس), sometimes called the fall of Tripoli, was a military confrontation in Tripoli, Libya, between loyalists of Muammar Gaddafi's government and anti-Gaddafi forces supported by NATO, fought from 20 to 28 August 2011 during the Libyan Civil War of 2011. The battle ended with rebel forces capturing the capital, decisively breaking the Gaddafi regime's hold on power.

Background

Protests against Gaddafi's 42-year rule began in February 2011 and escalated into civil war. Following UN Security Council Resolution 1973, NATO enforced a no-fly zone and struck regime targets. Through the summer, rebel forces advanced from the Nafusa Mountains toward the coast while an uprising was prepared inside Tripoli itself.

The battle

On the evening of 20 August 2011, as rebel columns approached from Zawiya in the west, an uprising began within Tripoli — signalled, according to rebel accounts, by calls from mosque loudspeakers at the end of Ramadan fasting. Rebel fighters seized neighbourhoods, and within days loyalist resistance collapsed in most of the city. On 23 August, rebels stormed Gaddafi's Bab al-Azizia compound, though Gaddafi himself had fled. Green Square was renamed Martyrs' Square amid celebrations broadcast around the world. Remaining loyalist pockets, notably in Abu Salim, were cleared by 28 August.

Aftermath

The fall of Tripoli effectively ended Gaddafi's rule, though he remained at large until his capture and death in Sirte on 20 October 2011. The National Transitional Council relocated to the capital and was recognised by most of the international community as Libya's government. The battle is sometimes known by the rebels' codename for the operation, Operation Mermaid Dawn — Tripoli's nickname being "the Mermaid".
