The Rhine (German: Rhein; French: Rhin; Dutch: Rijn) is one of the major rivers of Europe, flowing about 1,230 km from the Swiss Alps through or alongside six countries to the North Sea near Rotterdam. It is among the world's busiest waterways, the backbone of industry and commerce in western Europe, and a river of deep historical and cultural significance.

Course

The Rhine begins in the Swiss canton of Graubünden, where the Vorderrhein and Hinterrhein join at Reichenau. It flows into and out of Lake Constance as the High Rhine, forming part of the Swiss–German border and passing the Rhine Falls at Schaffhausen, Europe's largest plain waterfall. At Basel it turns north as the Upper Rhine, marking the Franco-German frontier through the Rhine Rift Valley. The Middle Rhine — the romantic gorge of castles, vineyards and the Lorelei rock between Bingen and Bonn — is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The Lower Rhine crosses the industrial Ruhr and Cologne before entering the Netherlands, where it divides into distributaries (Waal, Lek and IJssel) forming the Rhine–Meuse delta.

History

The Rhine formed part of the Roman frontier (the limes) between the Empire and Germania, and its banks are studded with Roman-founded cities such as Cologne, Mainz and Strasbourg. In the Middle Ages it was a corridor of trade and ecclesiastical power (the Rhenish prince-electors), and in the 19th and 20th centuries its control was contested in Franco-German conflicts. Tulla's 19th-century correction straightened and shortened the Upper Rhine for navigation and flood control.

Economy and environment

The Rhine carries hundreds of millions of tonnes of freight annually, linking the port of Rotterdam — Europe's largest — with the industrial heartland of Germany. Severe pollution in the 20th century, symbolised by the 1986 Sandoz chemical spill, prompted the Rhine Action Programme, which dramatically improved water quality and restored salmon to the river.

Major tributaries

The principal tributaries include the Aare, Neckar, Main, Moselle, Lahn, Ruhr and Meuse (via the delta system).
