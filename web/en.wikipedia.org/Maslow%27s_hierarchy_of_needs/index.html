Maslow's hierarchy of needs is a psychological theory proposed by Abraham Maslow in his 1943 paper A Theory of Human Motivation. It describes human needs in a five-tier model, often depicted as a pyramid: physiological needs, safety, love and belonging, esteem, and self-actualisation.

The five stages

Physiological needs such as food and water form the base. Above them come safety needs, social needs of love and belonging, esteem needs, and at the top self-actualisation, the realisation of personal potential.

Criticism and influence

The hierarchy has been criticised for lacking empirical support and for cultural bias, yet it remains widely taught and influential in management, education, and marketing.
