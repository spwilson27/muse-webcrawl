Gian Lorenzo (or Gianlorenzo) Bernini (7 December 1598 – 28 November 1680) was an Italian sculptor and architect. The dominant artistic figure of Baroque Rome, he was responsible for many of the city's defining works and for shaping the theatrical, emotional character of Baroque sculpture.

Early career

The son of the sculptor Pietro Bernini, Gian Lorenzo was a child prodigy in Rome. His early masterpieces for Cardinal Scipione Borghese — Aeneas, Anchises, and Ascanius, The Rape of Proserpina, Apollo and Daphne, and David (1619–1625) — displayed unprecedented virtuosity in rendering marble as flesh, hair, and foliage.

Papal Rome

Under Pope Urban VIII, Bernini became the pre-eminent artist of the papal court, producing the bronze Baldacchino over the high altar of St. Peter's Basilica and the tomb of Urban VIII. His masterpiece of mystical sculpture, the Ecstasy of Saint Teresa (1647–1652) in the Cornaro Chapel, fuses sculpture, architecture, painting, and light. Later works include the Fountain of the Four Rivers in Piazza Navona, the colonnade of St. Peter's Square, and the Scala Regia in the Vatican.

Legacy

Bernini also designed palaces, churches such as Sant'Andrea al Quirinale, and numerous fountains. A brief, partly unsuccessful visit to the court of Louis XIV in 1665 produced a celebrated bust of the French king. He is regarded as the founder of Baroque sculpture and one of the greatest sculptors of all time.
