Gatun Lake (Spanish: Lago Gatún) is a large artificial lake in Panama, formed by damming the Chagres River during construction of the Panama Canal. At the time of its creation it was the largest artificial lake in the world. The lake forms the central, elevated reach of the canal: ships are raised by locks to the lake's level of about 26 metres above the sea, cross the isthmus on its waters, and descend by locks on the other side.

Formation

The Gatun Dam, an earthen dam across the Chagres valley completed in 1912, flooded a vast area of jungle. The flooding created hundreds of islands, the largest of which, Barro Colorado Island, became a celebrated tropical research station administered by the Smithsonian Tropical Research Institute. The lake's water also supplies, by gravity, the locks of the canal and much of Panama City's drinking water.

Ecology and water use

Gatun Lake supports abundant wildlife, including monkeys, sloths and crocodiles on its forested islands, and introduced peacock bass have made it a popular sport-fishing destination. Because each canal transit consumes large volumes of fresh water, droughts in the lake's watershed periodically force draft restrictions on shipping, as occurred severely in 2023–2024.
