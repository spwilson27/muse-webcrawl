Vestfold

Vestfold
Country: Norway
County seat: Tønsberg
Area: approx. 2,200 km²
Population: approx. 250,000

Vestfold is a county and traditional region on the western shore of the Oslofjord in Eastern Norway. Its principal towns include Tønsberg — often described as Norway's oldest town — Sandefjord, Larvik, and Horten. The county was merged with Telemark in 2020 into Vestfold og Telemark, but Vestfold was re-established as a separate county on 1 January 2024.

History

Vestfold was a petty kingdom in the Viking Age and is exceptionally rich in Viking heritage. The Oseberg ship (buried 834) and the Gokstad ship (buried c. 900), both among the finest preserved Viking ships, were excavated here and are now exhibited in Oslo. The Kaupang trading settlement near Larvik was one of Scandinavia's earliest towns. According to saga tradition, Vestfold was the seat of the Yngling dynasty and the homeland of Halfdan the Black and Harald Fairhair, the first king of a unified Norway.

In the 19th and 20th centuries, Sandefjord became a centre of the whaling industry, commemorated by its whaling museum and the restored whaler Southern Actor. Horten hosted the Royal Norwegian Navy's main base at Karljohansvern.

Geography

The county has a long, island-dotted coastline on the fjord, popular for summer recreation, and a fertile agricultural hinterland. The Raet moraine ridge and the islands of Nøtterøy and Tjøme are characteristic features.
