The Sinaloa Cartel (Spanish: Cártel de Sinaloa), also known as the CDS, the Guzmán-Loera Organization, the Federation, the Blood Alliance, or the Pacific Cartel, is a large, international organised crime syndicate based in the city of Culiacán, Sinaloa, Mexico, specialising in international drug trafficking and money laundering. It has long been regarded by United States authorities as one of the most powerful drug trafficking organisations in the world.

History

The cartel emerged in the late 1980s from the fragmentation of the Guadalajara Cartel after the arrest of its leader Miguel Ángel Félix Gallardo. Its most famous leader, Joaquín "El Chapo" Guzmán, became notorious for two escapes from Mexican maximum-security prisons before his final capture in 2016, extradition to the United States in 2017, and conviction in New York in 2019, after which he was sentenced to life imprisonment. Alongside Guzmán, Ismael "El Mayo" Zambada led a major faction of the organisation for decades.

Operations

The cartel has trafficked cocaine, heroin, methamphetamine, marijuana and, more recently, fentanyl into the United States and other markets, using routes through Mexico's Pacific coast, tunnels under the US–Mexico border, and networks of corruption and front businesses. It has fought violent turf wars with rival organisations including the Juárez Cartel, the Tijuana Cartel, Los Zetas and the Jalisco New Generation Cartel.

Internal conflict

Following the 2024 arrest in the United States of Zambada and of Joaquín Guzmán López, a son of El Chapo, open warfare broke out in Sinaloa between the factions known as Los Chapitos and La Mayiza, causing widespread violence in Culiacán and the surrounding state.
