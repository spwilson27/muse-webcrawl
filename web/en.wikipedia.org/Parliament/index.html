Parliament
Type: Legislature
Structures: Unicameral, bicameral, tricameral

A parliament is a type of legislature, especially in countries whose system is based on the Westminster system of the United Kingdom. Its members are elected (or, in some upper houses, appointed) and it typically debates and enacts laws, approves budgets, and in parliamentary systems selects and can dismiss the government.

History

The word derives from the French parler ("to talk"). The Parliament of England developed in the 13th and 14th centuries from the king's council; the Icelandic Althing (founded 930) and the Isle of Man's Tynwald are among the oldest continuous legislative assemblies. The English Parliament's model — a sovereign legislature of a monarch, an upper house and an elected Commons — was widely imitated.

Functions

Core functions are legislation, representation, scrutiny of the executive, and control of public finance. Parliamentary systems (as in the UK, Germany, India, Japan and Canada) fuse executive and legislature, while presidential systems separate them.
