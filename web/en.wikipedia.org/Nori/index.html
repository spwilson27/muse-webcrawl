Nori

Nori is a Japanese name for edible seaweed species of the red algae genus Pyropia, including P. yezoensis and P. tenera. It has a strong and distinctive flavour, and is used to wrap rolls of sushi or onigiri, in which case the term is hosomaki wrapping. The finished dried sheets are made by a shredding and rack-drying process that resembles papermaking.

Production

Nori farming developed in Japan in the Edo period, but seed production baffled farmers until British phycologist Kathleen Mary Drew-Baker described the alga's life cycle in 1949, enabling modern aquaculture; she is honoured in Japan as the "Mother of the Sea". Seaweed is grown on nets in bays, harvested in winter, washed, chopped and dried into sheets. Japan, China and Korea are the major producers.

Nutrition and uses

Nori is rich in protein, iodine, vitamins and minerals, though its vitamin B12 is largely an inactive analogue. Beyond sushi, it flavours soups, noodles (as a garnish), furikake seasoning and snacks.
