Superstring theory

Superstring theory is a theoretical framework in which the point-like particles of particle physics are replaced by one-dimensional objects called strings, combined with supersymmetry. It attempts to explain all particles and fundamental forces of nature in one theory by modelling them as vibrations of tiny supersymmetric strings.

Overview

Superstring theories require ten spacetime dimensions and include gravitons among the vibrational states of closed strings, making them candidate theories of quantum gravity. There are five consistent superstring theories — Type I, Type IIA, Type IIB, and the two heterotic theories — which were shown in the 1990s to be related by dualities and unified as limits of an eleven-dimensional framework called M-theory.

Features

The extra dimensions are usually assumed to be compactified on Calabi–Yau manifolds or other small geometries. Superstring theory has produced influential ideas in mathematics and in gauge/gravity duality, though it has not yet yielded testable experimental predictions.
