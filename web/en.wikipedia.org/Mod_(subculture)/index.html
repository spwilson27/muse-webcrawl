Mod (subculture)

Mod, from "modernist," is a subculture that began in late-1950s London and spread across the United Kingdom and abroad in the 1960s. Centered on fashion and music, mods favored tailored Italian-style suits, clean-cut looks, and motor scooters such as Vespas and Lambrettas, often decorated with mirrors and lights.

Origins and style

The original mods were working-class and lower-middle-class youths who spent their wages on clothes and nightlife, in contrast to the leather-clad rockers. Their musical tastes ran to modern jazz, American soul, R&B, Motown, ska, and British beat groups such as The Who, The Small Faces, and The Yardbirds. All-night clubs, amphetamines, and scooters were hallmarks of the scene. The movement peaked in the mid-1960s and is indelibly associated with bank-holiday clashes with rockers at seaside resorts such as Brighton in 1964, sensationalized by the press as a moral panic.

Decline and revivals

By the late 1960s the scene fragmented into psychedelic and hard-mod offshoots, with some mods becoming skinheads. A mod revival in the late 1970s, fueled by bands like The Jam and the 1979 film Quadrophenia (based on The Who's 1973 rock opera), restored the style to prominence, and mod influences persisted through Britpop in the 1990s and in periodic revivals worldwide, including a notably enduring scene in Japan.
