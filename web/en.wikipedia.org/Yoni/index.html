Yoni (Sanskrit: योनि, IAST: yoni) is a Sanskrit word whose meanings include "womb", "vulva", "source", and "origin". In Hinduism it is the symbol of the goddess Shakti and of female creative power, and is worshipped together with the lingam, the symbol of Shiva, their union representing the totality of existence and the cycle of creation.

Iconography

In temples and shrines the yoni is typically rendered as a flat, circular or square stone base with a spouted rim, into which the cylindrical lingam is set. Offerings of water, milk, or flowers poured over the lingam are channelled out through the yoni's spout. The form appears from early in Indian religious history and is found across South and Southeast Asia, including in the art of the Indus Valley tradition, though its interpretation there is debated.

Broader significance

Beyond anatomy, yoni in Sanskrit texts denotes origin, birthplace, and the divine feminine principle. In Tantric traditions the yoni is venerated as the seat of the goddess and a focus of meditation; yoni mudra and yoni worship appear in Shakta practice. The concept has also been taken up in modern feminist and goddess movements as a symbol of female divinity.
