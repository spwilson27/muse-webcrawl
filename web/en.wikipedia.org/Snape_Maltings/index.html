Snape Maltings


The concert hall at Snape Maltings

Snape Maltings is an arts complex on the banks of the River Alde at Snape, Suffolk, England. It is best known for its concert hall, which is the main venue of the Aldeburgh Festival, founded by composer Benjamin Britten and tenor Peter Pears. The complex occupies a group of 19th-century industrial buildings originally used for malting barley for breweries.

History

The maltings were built from the 1850s by Newson Garrett, an East Anglian businessman, who developed Snape into a busy inland port for shipping malt and grain. Malting ceased in the 1960s, and Britten and Pears, seeking a larger venue for the Aldeburgh Festival than Aldeburgh's Jubilee Hall, acquired and converted the largest malthouse into a concert hall. The Snape Maltings Concert Hall was opened by Queen Elizabeth II on 2 June 1967.

A fire destroyed the hall two years later, on the opening night of the 1969 festival; it was rebuilt and reopened for the 1970 festival. The surrounding buildings have since been converted into additional performance spaces including the Britten Studio and the Jerwood Kiln Studio, as well as shops, galleries, restaurants, and holiday accommodation.

Britten Pears Arts

The site is the home of Britten Pears Arts (formerly the Britten–Pears Foundation and Aldeburgh Music), which runs the Aldeburgh Festival, the Britten Pears Young Artist Programme, and year-round education and performance programmes. The Red House in Aldeburgh, Britten and Pears's former home, is administered by the same organisation. The concert hall, seating about 830, is praised for its acoustic and for its setting overlooking the Alde marshes and reedbeds, a landscape closely associated with Britten's music, notably the opera Peter Grimes.
