The President of the People's Republic of China is the ceremonial head of state of China. Under the constitution the presidency is largely a formal office; political power is exercised principally through the concurrent posts of General Secretary of the Chinese Communist Party and Chairman of the Central Military Commission, which the president normally also holds.

History

The office was created by the 1954 constitution under the title "Chairman of the People's Republic of China", first held by Mao Zedong, followed by Liu Shaoqi. It was abolished during the Cultural Revolution era and left vacant in the 1975 and 1978 constitutions, then restored as "President" by the 1982 constitution, first held by Li Xiannian. Since the 1990s the roles of party leader, military chief and president have been combined in one person.

Powers and election

The president is elected by the National People's Congress (NPC) for a five-year term. Presidential functions include promulgating laws, appointing the premier and other officials on the NPC's decision, conferring honours, issuing pardons, proclaiming states of emergency and martial law, and ratifying treaties. A 2018 constitutional amendment removed the previous two-term limit on the presidency.

List

Presidents since 1983 have been Li Xiannian (1983–1988), Yang Shangkun (1988–1993), Jiang Zemin (1993–2003), Hu Jintao (2003–2013) and Xi Jinping (2013–present).
