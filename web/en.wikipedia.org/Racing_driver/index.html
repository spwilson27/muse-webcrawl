Racing driver
Person who drives racing cars in automobile competitions
Licensed by national motorsport authorities

A racing driver is a person who drives racing cars in automobile racing competitions. Racing drivers compete in disciplines including Formula One, rallying, endurance racing, touring cars, stock cars and drag racing, often progressing through karting and junior single-seater categories.

Career path

Most professional drivers begin in karting as children, then advance through formulae such as Formula 4, Formula 3 and Formula 2, or through sports-car and touring-car ladders. A competition licence graded by the national sanctioning body — and, for the top level, an FIA Super Licence — is required.

Skills and demands

The role demands high physical fitness (neck and core strength to withstand sustained lateral g-forces, heat tolerance, and fast reflexes), precise car control at the limit of grip, racecraft, and increasingly, data analysis with engineers. Endurance events such as the 24 Hours of Le Mans require drivers to share stints and race through the night.

Notable drivers

Historically celebrated drivers include Juan Manuel Fangio, Ayrton Senna, Michael Schumacher and Lewis Hamilton in Formula One; Sébastien Loeb in rallying; and Tom Kristensen in endurance racing. See also rail transport for a very different kind of professional driving.
