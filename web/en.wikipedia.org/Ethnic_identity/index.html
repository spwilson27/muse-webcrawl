Ethnic identity

Ethnic identity is a term used in social sciences to describe the part of a person's identity that derives from membership in an ethnic group, including the sense of belonging, and the attitudes, feelings, and behaviours associated with that membership. It is distinct from ethnic background itself: two people of the same ancestry may have very different strengths of ethnic identification.

Development

Psychologist Jean Phinney proposed an influential model of ethnic identity development, adapted from James Marcia's ego-identity statuses: an unexamined stage, a period of exploration or moratorium (often prompted by adolescence or an experience of discrimination), and an achieved ethnic identity combining exploration with commitment. Instruments such as the Multigroup Ethnic Identity Measure (MEIM) assess exploration and belonging.

Outcomes

Research associates a secure, achieved ethnic identity with higher self-esteem, better academic outcomes, and buffering against the psychological effects of discrimination, though effects vary by context and group. Among immigrants and their children, ethnic identity interacts with national identity in patterns of assimilation, integration, and separation.
