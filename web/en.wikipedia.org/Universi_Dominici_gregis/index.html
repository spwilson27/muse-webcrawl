SubjectVacancy of the Apostolic See and the election of the Roman Pontiff

Universi Dominici gregis is an apostolic constitution of the Catholic Church issued by Pope John Paul II on 22 February 1996. Subtitled On the Vacancy of the Apostolic See and the Election of the Roman Pontiff, it lays down the rules governing the period of sede vacante following a pope's death or resignation and the conduct of the conclave that elects his successor.

Provisions

The constitution confirmed that only cardinals under the age of eighty may vote, set the maximum number of electors at 120, and prescribed the conclave's residence in the Domus Sanctae Marthae and voting in the Sistine Chapel. It reaffirmed strict secrecy, forbade any alteration of the rules during the vacancy, and abolished election by acclamation or by committee, leaving scrutiny (secret ballot) as the only method. John Paul II originally provided that after about thirty-three deadlocked ballots a simple majority could decide to proceed by absolute majority; Pope Benedict XVI in 2007 restored the requirement of a two-thirds majority in all cases.

Amendments

Benedict XVI further amended the constitution in 2013 with the motu proprio Normas nonnullas, allowing the conclave to begin earlier than fifteen days after the vacancy if all electors were present, a provision used after his own resignation. These rules governed the conclaves of 2005, 2013 and subsequently.

Name

The title comes from the document's opening Latin words, drawn from the image of the pope as shepherd of "the whole flock of the Lord" (Universi Dominici gregis).
