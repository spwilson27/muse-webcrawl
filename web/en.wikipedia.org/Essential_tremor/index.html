SymptomsRhythmic shaking of hands, head, voice

Essential tremor (ET) is a neurological disorder characterised by involuntary, rhythmic shaking, most commonly of the hands during voluntary movement or posture holding. It is among the most prevalent movement disorders, affecting roughly 1% of the population and over 4% of people over 65. Many cases are inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern and were historically called "familial tremor".

Signs and symptoms

The tremor typically affects both hands and arms symmetrically, and may also involve the head, voice, and, less often, the legs. It is an action tremor—present when the muscles are in use—which distinguishes it from the resting tremor of Parkinson's disease. Symptoms often worsen with stress, fatigue, and caffeine, and are temporarily relieved by alcohol.

Cause and treatment

The precise cause is unknown; research implicates abnormal oscillation in the cerebello-thalamo-cortical circuit and loss of Purkinje cells in the cerebellum. First-line medications are the beta blocker propranolol and the anticonvulsant primidone. For severe, drug-resistant cases, deep brain stimulation of the thalamus or focused ultrasound thalamotomy can be effective.
