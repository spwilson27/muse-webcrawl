The House of Bourbon is a European royal house of French origin, a branch of the Capetian dynasty. Bourbons succeeded to the French throne with Henry IV in 1589 and ruled France, with interruptions, until 1848; Bourbon monarchs still reign in Spain and Luxembourg.

Origins

The house descends from Robert, Count of Clermont (1256–1317), sixth son of Louis IX of France, who married the heiress of Bourbon. The senior line became dukes of Bourbon; the Vendôme branch inherited the crown when the Valois line ended with Henry III's assassination in 1589, bringing Henry IV to the throne after the Wars of Religion.

France and Spain

Under Louis XIV the Bourbons made France the dominant power in Europe. In 1700 his grandson Philip V succeeded to Spain, founding the Spanish Bourbons after the War of the Spanish Succession. The French line was overthrown in 1792, restored in 1814–15, and finally displaced in 1848; cadet branches include Bourbon-Orléans, Bourbon-Parma and Bourbon-Two Sicilies.

Modern Bourbons

King Felipe VI of Spain and Grand Duke Henri of Luxembourg are Bourbon monarchs today. Legitimist pretenders to the French throne descend from the Spanish line.
