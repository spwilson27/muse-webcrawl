Dunvegan Castle from Loch Dunvegan

Dunvegan Castle is a castle at Dunvegan on the Isle of Skye in Scotland. It is the ancestral seat of the chiefs of Clan MacLeod and is reputed to be the oldest continuously inhabited castle in Scotland, having been home to the family for some 800 years. The castle stands on a basalt outcrop overlooking Loch Dunvegan, about a mile north of the village of Dunvegan.

History

A fortified site since the 13th century, the castle was progressively rebuilt between the 14th and 19th centuries into a romantically picturesque stronghold; Sir Roderick Mor MacLeod added the Fairy Tower in the 16th century, and the 19th-century chiefs remodelled the exteriors with battlements and the present entrance. Samuel Johnson and James Boswell were entertained here during their Highland tour of 1773.

Treasures

The castle's most famous relics are the Fairy Flag (Am Bratach Sìth), a tattered silk banner of uncertain, possibly Middle Eastern, origin said to bring victory when unfurled; the Dunvegan Cup, a ceremonial wooden mazer dating to 1493; and Sir Roderick Mor's Horn, a drinking horn each chief must traditionally drain in one draught. The gardens, developed from an 18th-century formal layout, are a major visitor attraction on Skye.
