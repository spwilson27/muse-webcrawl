Thomas Macdonough (31 December 1783 – 10 November 1825) was an early American naval officer noted for his victory over a British squadron in the Battle of Plattsburgh during the War of 1812, one of the most decisive American naval victories of the war.

Early service

Macdonough was born in Delaware and joined the United States Navy as a midshipman in 1800. He served in the Quasi-War with France and in the First Barbary War, taking part in Stephen Decatur's celebrated raid to burn the captured frigate USS Philadelphia in Tripoli harbour in 1804. He subsequently commanded gunboats and served in various stations before the outbreak of war with Britain.

Battle of Plattsburgh

In 1814 Macdonough commanded the American naval force on Lake Champlain. Anticipating a British invasion down the lake corridor toward New York, he built a squadron centred on the corvette USS Saratoga and anchored it in Plattsburgh Bay. On 11 September 1814 the British squadron under Captain George Downie attacked. Macdonough had laid out anchor lines and springs in advance, allowing him, after his flagship's starboard guns were disabled, to wind the ship around and bring a fresh broadside to bear. The British flagship HMS Confiance struck her colours, Downie was killed, and the entire British squadron surrendered. The victory forced the British land army to abandon its invasion, and it strengthened the American negotiating position at the Treaty of Ghent.

Later life

Congress awarded Macdonough a gold medal, and New York and Vermont granted him land. He later commanded the frigate USS Constitution and the Mediterranean Squadron. Stricken with tuberculosis, he died at sea on 10 November 1825 while returning home aboard USS Edwin. Several United States Navy destroyers have been named USS Macdonough in his honour, and Macdonough Island in Lake Champlain bears his name.
