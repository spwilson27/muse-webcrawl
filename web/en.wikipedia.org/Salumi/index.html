Salumi (singular salume) are Italian cured meat products, predominantly made from pork, that form the centrepiece of the antipasto course. The word derives from sale, "salt", the fundamental preservative. Salumi should not be confused with salame, which is one specific type of salume — a cured sausage.

Types

Salumi divide broadly into whole-muscle cures and ground-meat products. Whole cuts include prosciutto crudo (dry-cured ham, as in Prosciutto di Parma and San Daniele), coppa/capocollo (cured neck), pancetta (cured belly), bresaola (air-dried beef from Lombardy) and speck (smoked cured ham from South Tyrol). Ground and encased products include the many regional salami, soppressata, finocchiona (fennel salami) and 'nduja (spreadable spicy salami from Calabria). Some, like mortadella, are cooked rather than cured raw.

Production

Meat is salted (often with spices), sometimes fermented with bacterial starter cultures, then air-dried for weeks to many months in cool, ventilated rooms. Many salumi carry EU protected status (PDO/PGI), tying them to particular regions, breeds and methods — a heritage descending from Roman and medieval preservation practice, when autumn pig slaughter and winter curing stocked the larder.

Serving

Salumi are sliced thin and served at room temperature on boards with bread, cheese, pickles and wine, or used in cooking — pancetta in carbonara, for example.
