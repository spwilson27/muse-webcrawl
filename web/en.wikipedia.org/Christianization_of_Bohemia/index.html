Christianization of Bohemia

The Christianization of Bohemia refers to the spread of Christianity in the medieval Lands of the Bohemian Crown, a process that unfolded mainly during the 9th and 10th centuries as the Přemyslid dukes adopted the new faith and built churches throughout their realm.

Beginnings

Bohemian chiefs first encountered Christianity through contact with the Frankish Empire; in 845, fourteen Bohemian leaders were baptised at Regensburg, though the conversion did not then take hold. Tradition credits Duke Bořivoj I and his wife Ludmila as the first Bohemian rulers to accept baptism, around the 880s, at the hands of Methodius or his disciples from Great Moravia, bringing the Slavonic liturgy into Bohemia.

Consolidation

Bořivoj founded the first churches, including St. Clement at Levý Hradec and the church at Prague Castle. His grandson Wenceslaus (Václav), later canonised as Bohemia's patron saint, promoted Christianity before being murdered in 935 by followers of his brother Boleslaus — who paradoxically further strengthened the Church. The founding of the Bishopric of Prague in 973, under the Archdiocese of Mainz, anchored Bohemia firmly in Latin Christendom, though Slavonic-rite monasticism survived at Sázava Abbey into the 11th century.
