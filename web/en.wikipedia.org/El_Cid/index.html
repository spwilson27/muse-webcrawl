El Cid

Rodrigo Díaz de Vivar
Born: c. 1043, Vivar, Castile
Died: 10 July 1099, Valencia
Known as: El Cid Campeador
Allegiance: Kingdom of Castile; Taifa of Zaragoza; Lordship of Valencia

Rodrigo Díaz de Vivar (c. 1043 – 10 July 1099) was a Castilian knight and warlord in medieval Spain. Known as El Cid (from the Arabic al-Sīd, "the Lord") and El Campeador ("the Champion"), he became the national hero of Spain through the epic poem Cantar de mio Cid.

The statue of El Cid in Burgos, the city where he is buried.

Life and career

Rodrigo was born in Vivar, near Burgos, and rose to prominence in the service of Sancho II of Castile. After Sancho's death he served Alfonso VI, but was exiled in 1081 after raiding Toledo against the king's wishes. In exile he offered his sword to the Muslim rulers of Zaragoza, winning victories against both Christian and Muslim opponents, most famously the defeat of the Count of Barcelona at Almenar in 1082.

Conquest of Valencia

Recalled and exiled a second time, Rodrigo conquered the Mediterranean city of Valencia after a long siege in 1094, defeating the Almoravid armies sent to relieve it at the battles of Cuarte and Bairén. He ruled Valencia as an independent lordship until his death in 1099. His widow Jimena held the city until 1102, when it was evacuated before the Almoravids.

Legend

The anonymous Cantar de mio Cid, composed around 1200, transformed Rodrigo into the ideal knight—loyal, pious, and honourable—and remains the oldest preserved Castilian epic poem. His swords Tizona and Colada, his horse Babieca, and his embalmed corpse, said in legend to have been mounted on his horse to rout a final enemy army, all became fixtures of Spanish folklore. He and Jimena are buried in Burgos Cathedral.
