Snowdonia

Snowdonia
Welsh: Eryri
Location: North-west Wales
Area: 2,132 km² (823 sq mi)
Highest point: Snowdon (Yr Wyddfa), 1,085 m
Status: National Park since 1951

Snowdonia (Welsh: Eryri) is a mountainous region and national park in north-west Wales. It contains the highest mountains in Wales, centred on Snowdon (Yr Wyddfa), which rises to 1,085 metres (3,560 ft) above sea level. The area covers 2,132 square kilometres and was designated one of the first three national parks in Wales in 1951.

Geography

The region comprises several mountain ranges, including the Snowdon massif, the Glyderau, the Carneddau, the Moelwynion and the Moel Siabod group. Its landscape was carved by glaciers during the last ice age, leaving deep valleys, hanging cwms and ribbon lakes such as Llyn Idwal and Llyn Ogwen.

Language and culture

Snowdonia is one of the strongest remaining heartlands of the Welsh language; a large proportion of residents speak Welsh as a first language. The slate quarrying industry, centred on towns such as Blaenau Ffestiniog, shaped the area's communities, and the Slate Landscape of Northwest Wales was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2021.

Tourism

Millions of visitors come each year to climb Snowdon via paths such as the Llanberis Path, the Pyg Track and the Miners' Track, or to ride the Snowdon Mountain Railway. Other attractions include the Ffestiniog and Welsh Highland narrow-gauge railways.
