Panagbenga Festival

Panagbenga Festival (also called the Baguio Flower Festival) is a month-long annual flower festival celebrated every February in Baguio City, Philippines. The term Panagbenga comes from a Kankanaey word meaning "season of blooming".

History

The festival was created in 1996, in the aftermath of the devastating 1990 Luzon earthquake, as a tourism event to revive visitor arrivals in Baguio — long marketed as the "City of Pines" and the summer capital of the Philippines. It was scheduled in February, between the Christmas season and Holy Week, to fill a gap in the tourist calendar and to celebrate the city's cut-flower industry.

Events

The festival opens with street-dancing competitions in Igorot-inspired costumes and culminates in the Grand Flower Float Parade along Session Road, where floats are entirely decorated with fresh flowers — anthuriums, chrysanthemums, orchids and everlastings grown in the cool highlands of Benguet. Other highlights include a market encounter and landscaping exhibition at Burnham Park, and a closing fireworks display. The event draws hundreds of thousands of visitors each year and is one of the Philippines' best-known festivals.
