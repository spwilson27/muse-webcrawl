Clebsch–Gordan coefficients

In physics, Clebsch–Gordan (CG) coefficients are numbers that arise in angular momentum coupling in quantum mechanics. They appear as the expansion coefficients of total angular momentum eigenstates in an uncoupled tensor product basis. They are named after German mathematicians Alfred Clebsch and Paul Gordan, who encountered the equivalent problem in invariant theory.

Definition

Suppose two angular momenta J1 and J2 act on different parts of a composite system. The coupled states |j, m⟩ are linear combinations of the uncoupled states |j1, m1⟩|j2, m2⟩, and the coefficients of that expansion are the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients ⟨j1 m1 j2 m2 | j m⟩. Total angular momentum j ranges from |j1 − j2| to j1 + j2 in integer steps, and m = m1 + m2.

Properties

The coefficients vanish unless m = m1 + m2 and the triangle inequality |j1 − j2| ≤ j ≤ j1 + j2 holds. They can be chosen real, and they satisfy orthogonality relations that make the transformation between coupled and uncoupled bases unitary.

Relation to Wigner 3-j symbols

Clebsch–Gordan coefficients are related to the more symmetric Wigner 3-j symbols by a simple proportionality factor. The 3-j symbols are often preferred in advanced work because of their simpler symmetry properties under permutation of columns.

Applications

CG coefficients are used throughout atomic, nuclear, and particle physics: in computing selection rules, decay rates, and the decomposition of products of spherical harmonics. They also play a central role in the representation theory of the rotation group SO(3) and its covering group SU(2).
