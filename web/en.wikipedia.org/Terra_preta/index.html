Terra preta

Terra preta (Portuguese for "black earth") is a type of very dark, fertile anthropogenic soil found in the Amazon Basin. It was created by pre-Columbian Indigenous peoples between roughly 450 BCE and AD 950 through the incorporation of charcoal, bone, broken pottery, compost and manure into the otherwise nutrient-poor Amazonian soils.

Properties

Terra preta is distinguished by its high content of charred carbon (biochar), which resists decomposition, improves nutrient and water retention, and supports unusually active microbial communities. Remarkably, the soils appear to sustain their fertility for centuries, and are still sought after by local farmers.

Significance

The extent of terra preta sites suggests large, settled pre-Columbian populations in Amazonia. The soil has inspired modern interest in biochar as a means of improving agricultural soils and sequestering carbon in the fight against climate change.
