Billboard

A billboard (also called a hoarding in the UK and many other parts of the world) is a large outdoor advertising structure typically found in high-traffic areas such as alongside busy roads. Billboards present large advertisements to passing pedestrians and drivers.

History

Large-format outdoor posters developed in the 19th century with advances in lithography. In the United States, standardized billboard sizes emerged in the early 20th century as automobile travel grew, and industry associations promoted the 24-sheet poster and later the 14-by-48-foot bulletin.

Types

Common forms include static bulletins, poster panels, transit and street-furniture advertising, painted wall signs, and digital billboards that rotate multiple messages on LED displays. Mobile billboards are mounted on trucks or trailers.

Regulation and criticism

Billboards are regulated for size, lighting, spacing and placement in many jurisdictions. Critics cite visual clutter, driver distraction and light pollution; some US states and many cities restrict or ban them. Supporters argue they are an effective local advertising medium and a source of public messages.

Effectiveness

Outdoor advertising is measured by traffic counts and visibility indices. Location near highways, commuter routes and commercial districts commands premium rates.
