Impression, Sunrise (French: Impression, soleil levant) is a painting by Claude Monet, first exhibited in Paris in April 1874 in what became known as the "Exhibition of the Impressionists". It depicts the port of Le Havre, Monet's hometown, in morning fog: an orange sun glows through mist above masts and cranes, with silhouetted rowboats in the foreground. The painting is famous above all because its title gave the name to the Impressionist movement.

Exhibition and naming of a movement

Monet painted the canvas in November 1872 (he signed and dated it 1872, though it was long misdated 1873). In April 1874 it was shown at the First Impressionist Exhibition in the former studio of the photographer Nadar on the Boulevard des Capucines, organised by the Société anonyme des artistes. Asked for a title for the catalogue, Monet replied "Put Impression". The critic Louis Leroy, reviewing for Le Charivari, seized on the word in a satirical article titled "Exhibition of the Impressionists", writing of the picture: "Impression — I was certain of it... wallpaper in its embryonic state is more finished than that seascape." The artists adopted the derisive label, and it became the name of the most influential movement in modern art.

Technique

The work is painted in loose, sketch-like strokes that privilege the perception of light and atmosphere over drawn detail: cool blue-grey mist fills most of the canvas, while the sun and its shimmering reflection are rendered in vivid orange — a contrast that makes the sun appear to pulsate, since the two passages are of similar luminance. Monet's rapid notation of a transient moment exemplifies the plein-air ethos of Impressionism.

Provenance and theft

The painting passed through the collections of Ernest Hoschedé and Georges de Bellio and was bequeathed with de Bellio's collection to the Musée Marmottan in 1940. On 27 October 1985 it was stolen from the museum along with eight other Monets by gunmen; all were recovered in Corsica in 1990, and the painting returned to display at the Musée Marmottan Monet.
