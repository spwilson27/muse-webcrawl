Battle of the Denmark Strait

The Battle of the Denmark Strait was a naval engagement fought on 24 May 1941 between ships of the Royal Navy and the Kriegsmarine. The British battleship Prince of Wales and the battlecruiser Hood fought the German battleship Bismarck and the heavy cruiser Prinz Eugen, which were attempting to break out into the North Atlantic to attack Allied merchant shipping (Operation Rheinübung).

Less than ten minutes after the British opened fire, a shell from Bismarck struck Hood near her aft magazines. Hood exploded and sank within three minutes, with the loss of all but three of her crew of 1,419. Prince of Wales continued to exchange fire with Bismarck but suffered serious malfunctions in her main armament and disengaged under cover of a smokescreen.

Background

The Denmark Strait, between Greenland and Iceland, was one of the routes by which German surface raiders could reach the Atlantic convoy routes. After sighting reports from HMS Suffolk and HMS Norfolk, Vice-Admiral Lancelot Holland took Hood and Prince of Wales to intercept.

The battle

The British ships opened fire at 05:52. Holland's approach angle prevented Hood and Prince of Wales from using their after turrets initially. Bismarck's shooting was accurate; her fifth salvo at 06:00 caused the catastrophic explosion aboard Hood. Damaged and with guns malfunctioning, Prince of Wales withdrew, having nevertheless struck Bismarck three times — hits that caused flooding and an oil leak which ultimately forced Bismarck to abandon her sortie and head for France.

Aftermath

The loss of Hood, long the pride of the Royal Navy, provoked a massive British effort to hunt down Bismarck. She was sunk three days later, on 27 May 1941, after being crippled by torpedoes from Fairey Swordfish aircraft of HMS Ark Royal.
