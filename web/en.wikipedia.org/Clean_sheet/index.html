Clean sheet

Clean sheet
SportAssociation football (and other team sports)
DefinitionA match finished without conceding a goal
Credited toTeam; statistically to goalkeeper and defence

A clean sheet is a sporting term, most common in association football, for a match in which a team prevents its opponents from scoring any goals. The term comes from the scoresheets on which results were once recorded: a sheet with no goals entered against a team remained "clean". Clean sheets are tracked as a statistic for teams, goalkeepers and defenders.

Goalkeeping records

Because the goalkeeper is primarily responsible for preventing goals, clean sheets are a standard measure of goalkeeping achievement. In English football, the Premier League Golden Glove is awarded each season to the goalkeeper with the most clean sheets. Petr Čech holds the Premier League career record, with 202 clean sheets, and also set the single-season record of 24 with Chelsea in 2004–05. In international football, long streaks without conceding — such as Italy's run under Gianluigi Buffon — are widely cited landmarks.

Tactical significance

Coaches often stress that a clean sheet guarantees at least a draw, and defensive organization is measured by it. In two-legged knockout ties, keeping a clean sheet at home historically carried extra value under the away goals rule. Modern analytics qualifies the statistic: clean sheets depend heavily on the whole team's defending and on the quality of shots faced, so analysts supplement them with measures such as expected goals against.

Use in other sports

The term is also used for a shutout in ice hockey and for scoreless innings or games in baseball and cricket contexts, though "shutout" is more common in North American usage. Individual defenders in fantasy football games earn bonus points for clean sheets, further entrenching the term.
