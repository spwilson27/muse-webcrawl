KIT (gene)

KIT
ProteinReceptor tyrosine kinase (CD117, c-Kit)
LigandStem cell factor (SCF)
LocationChromosome 4q12 (human)
Aliasesc-Kit, CD117, PBT

KIT is a human gene encoding the receptor tyrosine kinase protein known as c-Kit or CD117. The KIT receptor sits in the cell membrane and is activated by its ligand, stem cell factor. It is essential for the development and maintenance of hematopoietic stem cells, melanocytes, germ cells and the interstitial cells of Cajal in the gut. The receptor's name derives from its discovery as the cellular homologue of a feline viral oncogene (v-kit, from a feline sarcoma virus).

Clinical significance

Loss-of-function mutations in KIT cause piebaldism, a condition of white patches of skin and hair from absent melanocytes. Activating mutations drive several cancers: the great majority of gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GIST), many cases of systemic mastocytosis (usually the D816V mutation) and some acute myeloid leukemias and seminomas.

Targeted therapy

Mutant KIT is a major drug target. The tyrosine kinase inhibitor imatinib revolutionised the treatment of GIST; later-generation inhibitors such as sunitinib, regorafenib, ripretinib and avapritinib address resistant mutations. Because CD117 is expressed on hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells, it is widely used as a marker in immunology and pathology.
