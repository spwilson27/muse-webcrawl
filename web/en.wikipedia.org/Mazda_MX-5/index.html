Mazda MX-5

The Mazda MX-5, marketed outside Japan as the Mazda Miata and in Japan as the Mazda Roadster, is a lightweight two-person sports car manufactured by Mazda. Introduced in 1989 at the Chicago Auto Show, it revived the classic British roadster formula with Japanese reliability.

Manufacturer: Mazda
Production: 1989–present
Class: Sports car / roadster
Layout: Front-engine, rear-wheel drive

History

The MX-5 was conceived under the guidance of journalist-turned-executive Bob Hall and designed in California and Hiroshima. Its design echoed the Lotus Elan, with pop-up headlights on the first generation (NA), a soft-top convertible roof, and near-perfect 50:50 weight distribution.

Generations

NA (1989–1997) featured a 1.6-litre, later 1.8-litre, inline-four engine. NB (1998–2005) adopted fixed headlights. NC (2005–2015) grew slightly larger. ND (2015–present) returned to a lighter, more compact form under Mazda's Skyactiv philosophy, celebrating low weight and the concept of jinba ittai — horse and rider as one.

Reception

With more than one million sold, the MX-5 is the best-selling two-seat roadster in history, celebrated for affordable, engaging handling and extensive use in motorsport, including the Spec Miata racing series.
