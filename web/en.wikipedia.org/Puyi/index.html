Puyi

Puyi (7 February 1906 – 17 October 1967), also known by his temple name Emperor Xuantong and in Western media as the Last Emperor, was the final Qing dynasty emperor of China. Crowned at the age of two in 1908, he abdicated in February 1912 following the Xinhai Revolution, ending more than two thousand years of imperial rule in China. He later became the figurehead ruler of the Japanese puppet state of Manchukuo (1934–1945).

Early life and abdication

Puyi was chosen by the dying Empress Dowager Cixi to succeed the Guangxu Emperor. After the 1911 Revolution he formally abdicated under the Articles of Favourable Treatment, which allowed him to keep his title and reside in the Forbidden City. He was briefly restored to the throne for twelve days in 1917 by the warlord Zhang Xun, expelled from the Forbidden City by Feng Yuxiang in 1924, and took refuge in the Japanese concession at Tianjin.

Manchukuo

Following Japan's seizure of Manchuria in 1931, Puyi was installed in 1932 as chief executive and in 1934 as emperor (reign name Kangde) of Manchukuo, a puppet state controlled by the Kwantung Army. At the end of the Second Sino-Japanese War in 1945 he was captured by Soviet troops and later testified at the Tokyo war crimes trials.

Later life

Transferred to China in 1950, Puyi spent a decade undergoing "re-education" at the Fushun War Criminals Management Centre before being pardoned in 1959. He worked as a gardener at the Beijing Botanical Garden and then as a literary researcher, published his autobiography From Emperor to Citizen, and died of cancer in 1967. His life was dramatised in Bernardo Bertolucci's 1987 film The Last Emperor.
