List of cognitive biases

[Image: The Cognitive Bias Codex chart]
A chart organising many documented cognitive biases.

Cognitive biases are systematic patterns of deviation from norm or rationality in judgement. Psychologists have documented well over a hundred such biases; the following table lists a selection of the best-known, with brief descriptions.

Selected biases

BiasDescription
AnchoringOver-relying on the first piece of information encountered when making decisions.
Availability heuristicJudging the probability of events by how easily examples come to mind.
Confirmation biasFavouring information that confirms one's prior beliefs.
Hindsight biasPerceiving past events as having been more predictable than they were.
Dunning–Kruger effectLow-ability individuals overestimating their competence.
Framing effectDrawing different conclusions from the same information depending on presentation.
Sunk cost fallacyContinuing an endeavour because of resources already invested.
Status quo biasPreferring the current state of affairs over change.
Bandwagon effectAdopting beliefs because others hold them.
Fundamental attribution errorAttributing others' behaviour to character rather than circumstance.
Negativity biasGiving more weight to negative than to positive experiences.
Survivorship biasFocusing on entities that survived a process and overlooking those that did not.
In-group biasFavouring members of one's own group over outsiders.
Planning fallacyUnderestimating the time needed to complete tasks.
