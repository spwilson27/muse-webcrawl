Poupée de cire, poupée de son

Poupée de cire, poupée de son

Artist: France Gall

Composer: Serge Gainsbourg

Released: 1965

Achievement: Winner, Eurovision Song Contest 1965 (for Luxembourg)

"Poupée de cire, poupée de son" (French for "wax doll, rag/sawdust doll") is a song written by Serge Gainsbourg and performed by the French singer France Gall, then seventeen years old. Representing Luxembourg, it won the Eurovision Song Contest 1965 in Naples — the first winning song that was not a ballad, marking a turning point toward pop in the contest.

The lyric is a playful but pointed double meaning: the singer describes herself as a doll of wax and sound, manipulated by the boys (and, implicitly, the songwriters and industry) around her, singing songs whose words she barely understands. Gainsbourg's wordplay turns on son, meaning both "sound" and "bran", the stuffing of a rag doll.

Impact

The song became a major hit across Europe and was recorded by Gall in several languages. Its success helped define the yé-yé era of French pop and made both Gall and Gainsbourg internationally known; the two collaborated further, controversially, on "Les Sucettes" the following year. The song's self-aware portrait of the manufactured pop singer has given it an enduring reputation as one of Eurovision's most literate winners.
