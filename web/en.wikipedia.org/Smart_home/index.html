Smart home

Connected devices in a smart home, coordinated by a hub or phone app

A smart home, also known as a home automation system, is a home equipped with network-connected devices that automate and remotely control domestic functions such as lighting, heating, ventilation and air conditioning, security, and entertainment systems. Devices communicate over protocols including Wi-Fi, Zigbee, Z-Wave, Thread and Bluetooth, and are typically managed through a smartphone app, a central hub, or a voice assistant.

Applications

Common smart-home applications include programmable and learning thermostats, smart lighting with scheduled and occupancy-based control, video doorbells and networked cameras, smart locks, leak and smoke detectors, and connected appliances. Automation "routines" or scenes can chain devices together — for example, locking doors, lowering blinds and arming an alarm when the household leaves. Energy management is a major motivation: automated heating, cooling and lighting can reduce consumption and cost.

Standards and interoperability

Historically, smart-home ecosystems were fragmented among vendors such as Amazon Alexa, Google Home and Apple HomeKit, with devices from one ecosystem not always working in another. The Matter standard, launched in 2022 by the Connectivity Standards Alliance and running over Thread and Wi-Fi, was designed to make devices interoperable across ecosystems.

Criticism and concerns

Smart homes raise concerns about privacy, as devices may collect detailed data about household behaviour, and about security, since poorly secured devices can be compromised and conscripted into botnets. Dependence on cloud services also means devices may lose functionality if a vendor discontinues support.
