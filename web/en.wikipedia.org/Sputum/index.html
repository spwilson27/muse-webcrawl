Sputum

Sputum is mucus that is coughed up from the lower airways (the trachea and bronchi). In medicine, sputum samples are usually used for a naked-eye exam, microbiological investigations of respiratory infections and cytological investigations of respiratory systems.

Clinical examination

The colour, consistency and odour of sputum can indicate disease: yellow or green sputum suggests infection, blood-stained sputum (haemoptysis) may indicate tuberculosis or malignancy, and frothy pink sputum occurs in pulmonary oedema. Sputum culture and Gram staining help identify pathogens such as Mycobacterium tuberculosis, for which acid-fast staining is used.

Collection

Samples are ideally collected by deep coughing into a sterile container, avoiding contamination with saliva; where patients cannot expectorate, sputum may be induced with nebulised saline.
