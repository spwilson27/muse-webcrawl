Maxwell Street Polish
Place of originChicago, Illinois, United States
Main ingredientsPolish sausage, grilled onions, yellow mustard, poppy seed bun
VariationsSport peppers; pork chop sandwich (companion item)

A Maxwell Street Polish consists of a grilled or deep-fried Polish sausage — typically a blend of pork and beef — topped with grilled onions and yellow mustard, occasionally with hot sport peppers, served on a bun. It is a signature street food of Chicago, Illinois.

History

The sandwich is named for Chicago's historic Maxwell Street open-air market, where immigrant vendors sold food from pushcarts in the early twentieth century. The Polish is widely associated with Jim's Original, a hot dog stand founded in 1939 near Maxwell and Halsted Streets, which claims to have popularized the style; the stand later relocated when the market was displaced by the expansion of the University of Illinois at Chicago.

Preparation

The sausage is commonly a kielbasa-style link with a notably snappy casing, grilled or deep-fried and served with a generous mound of sweet onions caramelized on the griddle, a stripe of yellow mustard and, on request, whole pickled sport peppers. It is a close relative of the Chicago-style hot dog but is distinguished by the larger sausage and the signature pile of grilled onions.
