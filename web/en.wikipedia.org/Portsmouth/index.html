Portsmouth

Portsmouth is a port city and unitary authority in Hampshire, England, mostly located on Portsea Island in the Solent, opposite the Isle of Wight. With a population of around 208,000, it is the United Kingdom's only island city (principally) and is notable for being England's most densely populated city outside London. Portsmouth has been a significant naval port for centuries and is home to the world's oldest dry dock still in use.

History

Though the Romans built Portchester Castle at the head of the harbour, Portsmouth itself was founded in 1180 by the merchant Jean de Gisors, and received its first royal charter in 1194. Henry VII built the world's first permanent dry dock there in 1495, and Henry VIII founded the Mary Rose and watched her sink in 1545. Portsmouth grew with the Royal Navy into a major imperial port, a principal embarkation point for D-Day in 1944, and was heavily bombed in the Portsmouth Blitz.

Landmarks

The Historic Dockyard preserves HMS Victory (Nelson's flagship at Trafalgar), HMS Warrior, and the Mary Rose Museum. The 170-metre Spinnaker Tower, opened in 2005, dominates the harbour at Gunwharf Quays. The city is the birthplace of Charles Dickens and Isambard Kingdom Brunel, and its football club, Portsmouth F.C., won the FA Cup in 2008.

Economy

Today the naval base HMNB Portsmouth hosts much of the Royal Navy's surface fleet, including its aircraft carriers; the city also has cross-channel ferry services, the University of Portsmouth, and a significant marine and aerospace sector.
