Salvador Allende

Salvador Allende, President of Chile 1970–1973.

Salvador Guillermo Allende Gossens (26 June 1908 – 11 September 1973) was a Chilean physician and socialist politician who served as the 28th president of Chile from 1970 until his death during the military coup of 11 September 1973. He was the first Marxist to be elected president of a liberal democracy in Latin America through an open election.

Early life and career

Born in Santiago into an upper-middle-class family, Allende trained as a doctor at the University of Chile and helped found the Socialist Party of Chile in 1933. He served as a deputy, Minister of Health, and senator, running for president three times (1952, 1958 and 1964) before winning in 1970 at the head of the Popular Unity coalition.

Presidency

Allende's government pursued the "Chilean road to socialism": nationalising copper mines and banks, accelerating land reform, expanding education and healthcare, and raising wages. These policies, combined with inflation, shortages, opposition strikes and covert destabilisation by the United States (documented by the US Church Committee), plunged the country into crisis.

Death and legacy

On 11 September 1973 the armed forces under General Augusto Pinochet bombed the La Moneda presidential palace. Allende died that day; Chile's official 2011 investigation concluded he took his own life as soldiers closed in. His overthrow began 17 years of dictatorship. Allende remains a symbol of democratic socialism worldwide, honoured in street names, memorials and the annual commemorations of 11 September in Chile.
