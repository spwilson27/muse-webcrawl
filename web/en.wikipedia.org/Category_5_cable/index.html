Category 5 cable

Category 5 cable (Cat 5) is a twisted pair cable standard for computer networks, defined in ANSI/TIA/EIA-568. Ratified in 1991, it carries signals at frequencies up to 100 MHz and was widely used for 100BASE-TX Fast Ethernet and, over shorter or well-installed runs, Gigabit Ethernet.

Construction and use

Cat 5 cable contains four twisted pairs of copper wire, usually terminated with 8P8C ("RJ45") connectors according to the T568A or T568B pin assignments. Maximum channel length is 100 metres. It also carried telephone and video signals in structured cabling installations.

Category 5e

An enhanced version, Category 5e (Cat 5e, 2001), tightened specifications for crosstalk and became the standard minimum for 1000BASE-T Gigabit Ethernet. Cat 5 itself is obsolete for new installations, superseded by Cat 5e, Category 6 and later standards.
