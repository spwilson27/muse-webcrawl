South African rand

The South African rand (sign: R; code: ZAR) is the official currency of South Africa. It is subdivided into 100 cents. The rand is also legal tender in the Common Monetary Area states of Namibia, Lesotho, and Eswatini, which have their own currencies pegged at par to it. The name derives from the Witwatersrand, the ridge on which Johannesburg is built and where most of South Africa's gold deposits were found.

History

The rand was introduced on 14 February 1961, shortly before the Union of South Africa became the Republic of South Africa, replacing the South African pound at a rate of 2 rand to 1 pound. For much of the 1960s and 1970s the rand was worth more than the US dollar; it depreciated sharply from the 1980s under sanctions pressure and political uncertainty, and it has floated since the 1990s. The rand is one of the most heavily traded emerging-market currencies and is often volatile in response to commodity prices and global risk sentiment.

Coins and banknotes

Current coins run from 10 cents to 5 rand. Banknotes are issued in denominations of 10, 20, 50, 100, and 200 rand; the series introduced in 2012 bears the portrait of Nelson Mandela on the obverse and one of the "Big Five" animals on the reverse. The currency is issued by the South African Reserve Bank.
