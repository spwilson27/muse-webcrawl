Warrior Games

The Warrior Games is an annual multi-sport event in the United States for wounded, injured or ill service members and veterans, organised by the United States Department of Defense. First held in 2010, the Games are designed to support recovery and rehabilitation through adaptive sports, and to raise awareness of the needs of wounded personnel.

History

The inaugural Games took place in May 2010 at the United States Olympic Training Center in Colorado Springs, a joint venture between the Department of Defense and the US Olympic Committee. About 200 athletes from the Army, Marine Corps, Navy, Air Force and Special Operations Command competed. Early editions were hosted in Colorado Springs before the event began rotating among military installations and host cities; the 2016 Games were held at West Point and the 2017 Games in Chicago, the first to take place in a major city rather than on a military base.

Sports and format

Athletes compete in adaptive sports including archery, cycling, shooting, sitting volleyball, swimming, track and field, wheelchair basketball and indoor rowing; later editions added events such as wheelchair rugby, powerlifting and golf. Teams are fielded by each service branch and by United States Special Operations Command, and allied nations such as the United Kingdom, Australia and Canada have sent teams in some years.

Relationship to the Invictus Games

The Warrior Games were an inspiration for the international Invictus Games, founded by Prince Harry and first held in London in 2014, after Harry attended the 2013 Warrior Games in Colorado. The two events share the aim of using sport in the recovery of wounded service personnel.
