Sea surface temperature

Sea surface temperature (SST) is the temperature of ocean water close to the surface, typically measured in the top metre or so of the water column. SST is a fundamental variable in oceanography and climatology, influencing weather, ocean currents, marine ecosystems and the exchange of heat and gases between ocean and atmosphere.

Measurement

Historically SST was measured by ships lowering buckets or reading engine-intake thermometers, and later by moored and drifting buoys. Since the 1980s, satellites have measured SST globally using infrared and microwave radiometers that sense the ocean "skin". Combined records are used to track climate change and phenomena such as El Niño and La Niña in the tropical Pacific.

Importance

Warm SSTs above roughly 26.5 °C are a key ingredient for tropical cyclone formation and intensification. SST patterns also drive fisheries productivity and coral bleaching events, and long-term SST trends are a clear indicator of ocean warming.
