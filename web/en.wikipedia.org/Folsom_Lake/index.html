Folsom Lake

Folsom Lake is a reservoir in Northern California, about 25 miles (40 km) northeast of Sacramento. It is formed by Folsom Dam on the American River and lies at the foothills of the Sierra Nevada, bordered by Sacramento, Placer and El Dorado counties. The lake is the centrepiece of the Folsom Lake State Recreation Area.

History

Folsom Dam was built by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers between 1948 and 1955 and is operated by the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation as part of the Central Valley Project. The reservoir has a capacity of about 977,000 acre-feet and supplies water for irrigation, municipal use, hydroelectric generation and flood control.

Drought exposure

During California droughts, falling water levels have exposed the remains of Mormon Island, a Gold Rush-era settlement submerged by the lake, along with other historic structures and artefacts, drawing attention in 2014 and during the 2020–2022 drought.
