Imagery intelligence (IMINT), pronounced as either as Im-Int or I-Mint, is an intelligence gathering discipline wherein imagery is analyzed (or "exploited") to identify information of intelligence value. Imagery used for defence intelligence purposes is generally collected via satellite imagery or aerial photography.

History

Aerial reconnaissance began with observation balloons and developed rapidly during the First World War. During the Cold War, high-altitude aircraft such as the U-2 and dedicated reconnaissance satellites greatly expanded collection capabilities, and imagery analysis played a central role in events such as the Cuban Missile Crisis.

Modern practice

Contemporary IMINT draws on electro-optical, infrared, and radar imagery from satellites, manned aircraft, and drones. In the United States, imagery analysis is closely associated with the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency, and the broader discipline of geospatial intelligence (GEOINT).
