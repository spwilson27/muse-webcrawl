Our Lady of Lourdes (French: Notre-Dame de Lourdes) is a title of the Virgin Mary invoked by Roman Catholics in honour of the Marian apparitions said to have occurred in 1858 at Lourdes, France. The first of these apparitions, on 11 February 1858, was reported by Bernadette Soubirous, a 14-year-old miller's daughter, who said she saw a "lady" in the cave of Massabielle, about a mile from the town, while she was gathering firewood with her sister and a friend.

Similar apparitions of the "lady" were reported on seventeen further occasions that year. During the apparition of 25 March, the lady identified herself with the words "I am the Immaculate Conception" (in the local dialect, Que soy era Immaculada Councepciou). The local bishop, Bertrand-Sévère Laurence, declared the apparitions worthy of belief on 18 January 1862, and the veneration of Our Lady of Lourdes was authorised.

The apparitions

Bernadette Soubirous described the lady as wearing a white dress and veil, a blue sash, and golden roses on her feet, holding a rosary. In one of the most famous episodes, on 25 February 1858, the lady instructed Bernadette to dig in the ground at the grotto; a spring of water appeared, which became associated with reports of miraculous healings. Bernadette was canonised as a saint by Pope Pius XI in 1933. Her accounts were met with both scepticism and intense popular devotion, and she was repeatedly interrogated by civil and church authorities.

The sanctuary

The Sanctuary of Our Lady of Lourdes, built around the Grotto of Massabielle on the banks of the Gave de Pau, has become one of the world's most visited Catholic pilgrimage sites, receiving several million visitors each year. The complex includes the Upper Basilica, the Rosary Basilica, the underground Basilica of St. Pius X, and the Crowned Statue opposite the grotto. Processions, notably the torchlight Marian procession and the Blessed Sacrament procession, are central features of the pilgrimage season.

Reported healings

The Lourdes Medical Bureau (Bureau des Constatations Médicales), founded in 1883, examines claims of miraculous cures. Of the thousands of claims filed, the Catholic Church has recognised a small number — around seventy — as miraculous after investigation by medical and ecclesiastical commissions. Pilgrims frequently bathe in or drink the water of the spring, although Church teaching emphasises that the water itself has no intrinsic power apart from faith.

Feast day and influence

The feast of Our Lady of Lourdes is celebrated on 11 February, the anniversary of the first apparition. In 1992 Pope John Paul II instituted the World Day of the Sick on this date. Reproductions of the Lourdes grotto exist at parishes and shrines around the world, and the title has inspired numerous churches, schools, and hospitals. Pope John Paul II visited Lourdes in 1983 and again in 2004, shortly before his death.
