Single (music)

In music, a single is a type of release, typically a recording of fewer tracks than an LP record or an album. One can be released for sale to the public in a variety of formats. In most cases, a single is a song that is released separately from an album, although it usually also appears on an album. In other cases a recording released as a single may not appear on an album.

Typically, these are the songs from albums that are released separately for promotional uses such as digital download, or video release. In other cases a recording released as a single may not appear on an album. Singles can also be released to promote an artist or a genre, with the aim of increasing popularity or generating curiosity for a subsequent album.

History

The origins of the single lie in the late 19th century, when music was first commercially distributed on phonograph cylinders. By the 1940s the term "single" came into usage in distinction to 78 rpm shellac discs, and in 1949 RCA Victor introduced the 45 rpm vinyl single with a large centre hole, in competition with Columbia's LP. The seven-inch 45 became the standard format for pop singles, typically with an A-side and a B-side. The 12-inch single, popularised in the disco era, could hold longer mixes. Cassette singles, CD singles and eventually digital downloads followed.

In the digital era

With the rise of downloads and streaming, the single became the dominant unit of popular music consumption; charts in many countries now combine sales and streams. Singles are often released as lead singles in advance of an album, and streaming services have encouraged a strategy of frequent standalone single releases.
