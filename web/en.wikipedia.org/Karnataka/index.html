Karnataka (Kannada: ಕರ್ನಾಟಕ) is a state in the southwestern region of India. It was formed on 1 November 1956 under the States Reorganisation Act as Mysore State, uniting the Kannada-speaking regions of the former states of Mysore, Bombay, Madras, and Hyderabad. It was renamed Karnataka in 1973. The state is bordered by the Arabian Sea to the west, Goa to the northwest, Maharashtra to the north, Telangana to the northeast, Andhra Pradesh to the east, Tamil Nadu to the southeast, and Kerala to the southwest.

History

Karnataka's history spans more than two millennia. The region was ruled by the Kadamba, Ganga, Rashtrakuta, Chalukya, Hoysala, and Vijayanagara empires, which left celebrated monuments such as the cave temples of Badami, the temples of Hampi, and the sculptures of Belur and Halebidu. After the fall of Vijayanagara, the Wodeyars of Mysore, Haidar Ali, and Tipu Sultan dominated the south, until Mysore became a princely state under British paramountcy. Its post-independence maharaja became the first governor of the unified state.

Geography

The state has three main physiographic zones: the coastal Karavali belt, the hilly Malenadu region of the Western Ghats — a UNESCO World Heritage Site and biodiversity hotspot — and the drier Bayaluseeme plateau. Major rivers include the Kaveri, Krishna, Tungabhadra, and Sharavati, the last forming the Jog Falls, among India's highest waterfalls.

Economy

Karnataka is one of India's most industrialised states. Bengaluru is the country's leading information technology hub, hosting hundreds of software companies and research institutions in aerospace, biotechnology, and electronics. The state is also India's largest producer of coffee, raw silk, and sandalwood, and a major exporter of engineering goods.

Culture

Kannada literature, whose earliest surviving work is the 9th-century Kavirajamarga, has won more Jnanpith Awards than any other Indian language. Karnataka gave rise to both major schools of Carnatic and Hindustani classical music traditions in the south, the theatre form Yakshagana, and the Mysore school of painting.
