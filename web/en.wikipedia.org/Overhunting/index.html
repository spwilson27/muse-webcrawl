Overhunting

Overhunting is the hunting of wild animals at rates that exceed the species' capacity to reproduce and recover, leading to population decline or extinction. It is a major driver of biodiversity loss, alongside habitat destruction.

Examples

Historical examples include the near-extermination of the American bison in the 19th century, the extinction of the passenger pigeon in 1914, and industrial whaling in the 20th century. Today, the bushmeat trade, ivory and rhino horn poaching, and demand for traditional medicines threaten many species.

Responses

Regulation includes hunting seasons and quotas, protected areas, and international agreements such as CITES controlling trade in endangered species. Community-based conservation and sustainable-use programmes aim to align local incentives with species recovery.
