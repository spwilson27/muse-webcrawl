Hasmonean dynasty

The Hasmonean dynasty (Hebrew: חַשְׁמוֹנָאִים, romanized: Ḥašmōnāʾīm) was a ruling dynasty of Judea and surrounding regions during classical antiquity, from c. 140 BCE to 37 BCE. Between c. 140 and c. 116 BCE the dynasty ruled Judea semi-autonomously in the Seleucid Empire, and from roughly 110 BCE, with the empire disintegrating, Judea gained further autonomy and expanded into the neighboring regions of Perea, Samaria, Idumea, Galilee, and Iturea.

Origins: the Maccabean revolt

The dynasty was founded under Simon Thassi, son of the priest Mattathias, whose sons — the Maccabees — launched a revolt in 167 BCE against the Seleucid king Antiochus IV Epiphanes, whose decrees had suppressed Jewish religious practice and desecrated the Jerusalem Temple. Judah Maccabee rededicated the Temple in 164 BCE, an event commemorated by the festival of Hanukkah. After Judah's death, his brothers Jonathan Apphus and then Simon continued the struggle; Simon secured recognition of Judean independence and was confirmed as hereditary high priest and ethnarch in 140 BCE, the conventional starting point of the dynasty.

The Hasmonean kingdom

Simon's son John Hyrcanus (134–104 BCE) expanded Judea's borders and destroyed the Samaritan temple on Mount Gerizim. His son Aristobulus I (104–103 BCE) was the first Hasmonean to take the title of king. Alexander Jannaeus (103–76 BCE), the dynasty's most expansionist ruler, brought the kingdom to its greatest extent, from the coastal plain to territories east of the Jordan, but his reign was marred by civil war with the Pharisees. His widow Salome Alexandra (76–67 BCE) presided over a period of relative peace and Pharisaic influence. After her death, her sons Hyrcanus II and Aristobulus II fought a civil war that invited Roman intervention: Pompey captured Jerusalem in 63 BCE.

Fall

The last decades of the dynasty played out under Roman domination, complicated by the Parthian invasion of 40 BCE, which installed Antigonus II Mattathias as king. Herod, backed by Rome, captured Jerusalem in 37 BCE and executed Antigonus, ending the dynasty. Herod's marriage to the Hasmonean princess Mariamne, and his subsequent killing of the remaining Hasmonean heirs, tied his own Herodian dynasty to theirs while eliminating it as a rival. The history of the Hasmoneans is preserved mainly in the Books of the Maccabees and the writings of Josephus.
