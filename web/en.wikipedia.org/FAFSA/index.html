FAFSA

The Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA) is a form completed by current and prospective college students (undergraduate and graduate) in the United States to determine their eligibility for student financial aid. It is administered by Federal Student Aid, an office of the United States Department of Education.

Eligibility and data

Applicants provide financial information — including income and assets of the student and, for dependent students, their parents — much of which can be transferred directly from the Internal Revenue Service. The information is used to compute an index of ability to pay (the Expected Family Contribution, replaced by the Student Aid Index from the 2024–25 award year), which colleges use to assemble aid packages combining federal grants, work-study and loans with state and institutional aid.

Programs funded

Completing the FAFSA determines eligibility for the Pell Grant, Federal Direct Loans, Federal Work-Study, the Federal Supplemental Educational Opportunity Grant and TEACH Grants, and most states and colleges also require it for their own aid programs. The form opens for each academic year in the autumn preceding it, and aid is often awarded first-come, first-served.

Simplification

The FAFSA Simplification Act, passed in 2020, substantially shortened the form, expanded Pell Grant eligibility, and required direct IRS data exchange. Its troubled rollout for the 2024–25 cycle, delayed to December 2023 with processing glitches, drew wide attention and congressional scrutiny.
