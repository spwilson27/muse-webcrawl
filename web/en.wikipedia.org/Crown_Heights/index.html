Crown Heights

Borough: Brooklyn, New York City
Named for: Crown Street

Crown Heights is a neighborhood in the central portion of the New York City borough of Brooklyn. Crown Heights is bounded by Washington Avenue to the west, Atlantic Avenue to the north, Ralph Avenue to the east, and Empire Boulevard to the south. It is notable for its mix of cultures and for institutions such as the Brooklyn Museum, the Brooklyn Botanic Garden, and the Brooklyn Children's Museum on its edges.

A residential street in Crown Heights.

History

The area was rural farmland until the late 19th century, when it developed as an affluent residential district of brownstones and mansions along Eastern Parkway, one of the world's first parkways, designed by Frederick Law Olmsted and Calvert Vaux. Through the 20th century the population changed in waves: first Jewish and Irish residents, then, from the mid-century, a large Afro-Caribbean and African-American community, alongside the world headquarters of the Chabad-Lubavitch Hasidic movement at 770 Eastern Parkway.

Demographics and culture

Crown Heights is home to large Caribbean-American and Orthodox Jewish (especially Lubavitch Hasidic) communities. It hosts the annual West Indian Day Parade along Eastern Parkway, one of New York's largest festivals. The 1991 Crown Heights riot — three days of unrest following a fatal traffic accident and the murder of a Jewish student — remains a significant event in the neighborhood's and the city's history.

Gentrification

Since the 2000s, Crown Heights has experienced rapid gentrification, rising rents, and demographic change, debated locally for both investment in amenities and displacement of long-time residents.
