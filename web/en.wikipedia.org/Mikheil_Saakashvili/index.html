Mikheil Saakashvili

Mikheil Saakashvili
Born: 21 December 1967, Tbilisi, Georgian SSR
Office: President of Georgia (2004–2007, 2008–2013); Governor of Odesa Oblast, Ukraine (2015–2016)

Mikheil Saakashvili (born 21 December 1967) is a Georgian and Ukrainian politician and lawyer. He was the third President of Georgia, serving from 2004 to 2013 after leading the peaceful Rose Revolution that forced Eduard Shevardnadze from power.

Early career

Saakashvili studied law in Kyiv and at Columbia University, and worked at a New York law firm before returning to Georgia in the mid-1990s. He became a member of parliament and, in 2000, Minister of Justice under Shevardnadze, before resigning and founding the United National Movement opposition party.

Presidency

Following the disputed November 2003 parliamentary elections, Saakashvili led mass protests — the Rose Revolution — and was elected president in January 2004. His government pursued rapid economic liberalisation, anti-corruption reforms and a pro-Western foreign policy, seeking NATO and EU integration. His presidency was marked by the August 2008 Russo-Georgian War, after which Russia recognised the independence of Abkhazia and South Ossetia. His party lost the 2012 parliamentary elections, and he left Georgia at the end of his term in 2013.

Ukraine and later years

In 2015 Saakashvili became a Ukrainian citizen and was appointed governor of Odesa Oblast by President Petro Poroshenko; he resigned in 2016, accusing Kyiv of corruption, and was stripped of Ukrainian citizenship in 2017. After a period abroad he returned to Georgia in October 2021, where he was arrested on earlier convictions handed down in absentia and imprisoned, amid international concern about his health.
