The Stone Breakers

The Stone Breakers

Artist: Gustave Courbet

Year: 1849–1850

Medium: Oil on canvas

Status: Destroyed 1945

The Stone Breakers (Les Casseurs de pierres) was an 1849–50 oil painting by the French artist Gustave Courbet, a landmark of the Realist movement. It depicted two labourers — one young, one old — breaking rocks for road gravel by the roadside near Courbet's home town of Ornans.

Description and reception

Painted on a canvas of monumental scale (about 165 × 257 cm), the work presented impoverished manual labourers with the gravity traditionally reserved for history painting, without idealisation or moralising anecdote. Exhibited at the Paris Salon of 1850–51 alongside A Burial at Ornans, it scandalised critics and made Courbet the leader of Realism. Courbet said he had "simply drawn from life" a scene of "poverty and complete misery".

Destruction

The painting was held in Dresden and was destroyed in February 1945 during the Allied bombing of the city, when the truck transporting it and other works was burned. It survives in photographs, sketches and a smaller autograph version.
