Iblis

Iblis (Arabic: إِبْلِيس, Iblīs), also known as Shaitan, is the leader of the devils (shayāṭīn) in Islam. According to the Quran, Iblis was thrown out of heaven after refusing to prostrate himself before the newly created Adam, whom God had commanded the angels to honour. Out of pride, Iblis declared himself superior to Adam because he was created from fire while Adam was created from clay.

Islamic theology

In Islamic tradition, Iblis is generally held to be a jinn — a being created from smokeless fire, endowed with free will — rather than a fallen angel, since angels in Islam are regarded as incapable of disobedience. Granted respite until the Day of Judgment, Iblis vows to lead humanity astray, though the Quran stresses he has no power over God's sincere servants.

Sufi interpretations

Some Sufi traditions portray Iblis paradoxically as the supreme monotheist who refused to bow to anyone but God, accepting damnation rather than compromising his devotion — a reading explored in the works associated with Mansur al-Hallaj and Ahmad Ghazali, though rejected by most orthodox scholars.
