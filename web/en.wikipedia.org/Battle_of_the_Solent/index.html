The Battle of the Solent took place on 18–19 July 1545 during the Italian War of 1542–1546, between the fleets of France and England in the strait between Portsmouth and the Isle of Wight. It is best remembered for the sinking of the English warship Mary Rose, which capsized in view of King Henry VIII.

Background

France, retaliating for Henry VIII's campaign in northern France and the capture of Boulogne, sent a large invasion fleet under Admiral Claude d'Annebault across the Channel. The French landed troops on the Isle of Wight and threatened Portsmouth, while the English fleet under John Dudley, Viscount Lisle, came out to meet them.

The battle

The fighting over two days was largely a long-range gunnery duel in light winds, with neither side able to force a decisive action. On 19 July the Mary Rose, turning to bring her guns to bear, heeled over, flooded through her open gunports and sank with the loss of nearly all her crew of some 400–500 men. The French eventually withdrew, and no full-scale invasion followed.

Legacy

The wreck of the Mary Rose was located in the 1970s and raised in 1982; her remains and thousands of recovered artefacts are displayed at the Mary Rose Museum in Portsmouth.
