Shared universe

A shared universe or shared world is a fictional universe from a set of creative works where multiple writers can contribute.

A shared universe or shared world is a fictional universe from a set of creative works where one or more writers (or other artists) independently contribute works that can stand alone but fit into the joint development of the storyline, characters, or world of the overall project. It is common in genres like science fiction and fantasy, and in comics, film and television franchises.

History

Early examples include the interlocking novels of nineteenth-century literature and the detective stories in which characters crossed between series. The modern conception was strongly shaped by comic books: DC Comics and Marvel Comics built extensive continuities in which superheroes inhabit the same world and meet in crossover events. J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium, the Star Wars expanded universe, and H. P. Lovecraft's Cthulhu Mythos — deliberately opened to other authors — are prominent literary examples.

In film and television

The commercial success of the Marvel Cinematic Universe, beginning in 2008, popularised the shared-universe model in film, encouraging studios to develop interconnected franchises with crossovers and team-up events. Shared universes allow characters from solo works to interact, permit long-form collaborative worldbuilding, and can sustain audience interest across many releases, though they also raise issues of continuity management and creative control.
