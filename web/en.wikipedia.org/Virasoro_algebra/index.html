The Virasoro algebra is an infinite-dimensional Lie algebra that plays a central role in two-dimensional conformal field theory and in string theory. It is the unique (non-trivial) central extension of the Witt algebra of vector fields on a circle, and is named after the physicist Miguel Ángel Virasoro, who introduced it in 1970 in the context of dual resonance models.

Definition

The Virasoro algebra is generated by elements Ln for all integers n, together with a central element c (the central charge), subject to the commutation relations [Lm, Ln] = (m − n) Lm+n + (c/12)(m3 − m) δm+n,0. Setting c to zero recovers the Witt algebra. In physics, the Ln are the modes of the energy–momentum tensor and c measures the number of degrees of freedom of the theory.

Representation theory

Highest-weight representations, built on primary states annihilated by all Ln with n > 0, are the building blocks of conformal field theory. The unitarity of these representations restricts the allowed central charges: either c ≥ 1 or c belongs to a discrete series below 1, realised by the minimal models. The Kac determinant formula determines when Verma modules contain null states, leading to differential equations for correlation functions.

Applications

The algebra constrains two-dimensional critical phenomena (Ising and Potts models), governs the worldsheet symmetries of string theory — where anomaly cancellation fixes the critical dimension — and appears in the study of the Monster group through moonshine and vertex operator algebras.
