Santiago Bernabéu de Yeste (8 June 1895 – 2 June 1978) was a Spanish footballer and one of the most important figures in the history of Real Madrid. He played for the club as a forward, later served as a director and assistant manager, and was president from 1943 until his death in 1978 — one of the longest presidencies in football history. Real Madrid's stadium, the Santiago Bernabéu, is named after him.

Early career

Born in Almansa and raised in Madrid, Bernabéu joined Real Madrid as a youth and made his first-team debut as a striker while still a teenager, scoring many goals before retiring in 1927. He remained involved with the club in administrative roles through the Spanish Civil War, after which he helped rebuild a club left with its ground destroyed and its organization in ruins.

Presidency

Elected president in 1943, Bernabéu oversaw the construction of a new stadium in Chamartín, opened in 1947 and renamed for him in 1955, and built the great team of the 1950s led by Alfredo Di Stéfano. Under Bernabéu, Real Madrid won the first five European Cups (1956–1960) and a sixth in 1966, numerous league titles, and became the dominant club of early European competition. He was a driving force behind the creation of the European Cup itself and pioneered modern ideas of merchandising and global touring.

Death and legacy

Bernabéu died in Madrid on 2 June 1978, during the World Cup in Argentina, after nearly 35 years as president; FIFA declared three days of mourning. The Santiago Bernabéu Trophy, an annual friendly tournament, is held in his memory, and Spain's national stadium honours list routinely ranks him among the most influential administrators in the sport's history.
