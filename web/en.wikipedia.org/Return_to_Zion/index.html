Return to Zion

The Return to Zion (Hebrew: Shivat Zion) refers to the return of the Jewish exiles from Babylonian captivity to the land of Judah in the 6th century BCE, following the decree of Cyrus the Great of Persia in 538 BCE, which permitted them to rebuild the Temple in Jerusalem. The events are described in the books of Ezra and Nehemiah.

Waves of return

Tradition describes several waves of returnees, led first by Sheshbazzar and then by Zerubbabel, who laid the foundations of the Second Temple (completed about 516 BCE). A later wave under Ezra the scribe re-established Torah observance, and Nehemiah, as Persian-appointed governor, rebuilt Jerusalem's walls.

Significance

The return inaugurated the Second Temple period and reshaped Jewish identity around the Temple, the Torah, and the community of returnees. In modern usage, Shivat Zion has also been applied to Jewish immigration to Israel (aliyah) in the Zionist era.
