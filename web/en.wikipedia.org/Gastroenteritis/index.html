Gastroenteritis

Gastroenteritis, also known as infectious diarrhea or stomach flu, is an inflammation of the gastrointestinal tract — the stomach and intestines — typically caused by infection with viruses, bacteria or parasites. It causes some combination of diarrhea, vomiting and abdominal pain and cramping.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms usually begin 12–72 hours after infection and last less than a week in viral cases. Watery diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, low-grade fever, cramps and muscle aches are typical. The main danger, especially in young children and the elderly, is dehydration, signalled by dry mouth, reduced urination and dizziness.

Causes

Globally, most cases in children are caused by rotavirus; norovirus is the leading cause in adults and of foodborne outbreaks. Bacterial causes include Campylobacter, Salmonella, Shigella, pathogenic E. coli and Clostridioides difficile; parasites such as Giardia cause longer illnesses. Transmission is by the faecal–oral route via contaminated food, water or hands.

Prevention and treatment

Prevention relies on hand washing, clean water, safe food preparation and rotavirus vaccination, which has sharply reduced childhood deaths. Treatment is mainly supportive: oral rehydration therapy with water, salts and sugar is the cornerstone; antibiotics are reserved for specific bacterial causes. Gastroenteritis remains a leading cause of death in children under five in low-income countries, causing several hundred thousand deaths annually despite major declines.
