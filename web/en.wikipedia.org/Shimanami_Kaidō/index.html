Shimanami Kaidō

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: a cable-stayed bridge over the Seto Inland Sea]

The Shimanami Kaidō (Japanese: しまなみ海道) is a 60-kilometre toll expressway in Japan that connects Onomichi in Hiroshima Prefecture, on the island of Honshu, to Imabari in Ehime Prefecture, on Shikoku, crossing the Seto Inland Sea via a chain of islands and nine bridges. Officially it is a section of the Nishiseto Expressway, completed in 1999 with the opening of the Kurushima Kaikyō Bridge.

The route is famous internationally as one of Japan's premier cycling routes: it is one of the few road links between Honshu and Shikoku with a dedicated bicycle and pedestrian lane, and it was named by CNN among the world's most incredible bike tours. Rental bicycles are available at terminals along the way, allowing riders to cross some or all of the bridges at leisure.

Bridges

The bridges include the Shin Onomichi Bridge, Innoshima Bridge, Ikuchi Bridge, Tatara Bridge (a cable-stayed bridge once the longest of its kind), Ōmishima Bridge, the Hakata–Ōshima Bridge, Ōshima Bridge, and the three-span Kurushima Kaikyō Bridge, at just over four kilometres one of the longest suspension-bridge structures in the world.

Along the route lie sites such as Ōyamazumi Shrine on Ōmishima and the Murakami Kaizoku Museum, celebrating the medieval Murakami "pirate" clans of the Inland Sea.
