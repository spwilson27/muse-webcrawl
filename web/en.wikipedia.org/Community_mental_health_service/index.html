Community mental health service

A community mental health service is a system of mental health care delivered in community settings rather than in large psychiatric hospitals. Community services typically include community mental health teams, crisis intervention and home treatment, assertive outreach, early intervention in psychosis, outpatient clinics, day services, and supported housing and employment.

History

The community model grew out of the deinstitutionalisation movement of the mid-20th century. In the United States, the Community Mental Health Act of 1963 funded community mental health centers intended to replace state hospitals. In the United Kingdom, policy from the 1959 Mental Health Act onward shifted care from asylums to district general hospital units and community teams, a process accelerated by the NHS and Community Care Act 1990. Italy's Basaglia Law of 1978 closed psychiatric hospitals altogether.

Models of care

Community mental health teams (CMHTs) are multidisciplinary — psychiatrists, community psychiatric nurses, social workers, psychologists, and occupational therapists — and provide assessment, medication management, psychological therapy, and care coordination, often under formal frameworks such as the Care Programme Approach. Crisis resolution and home treatment teams offer intensive short-term care as an alternative to admission.

Criticism

Critics argue that community care has often been underfunded, leaving some severely ill people without adequate support, and that hospital bed reductions have outpaced the growth of community provision.
