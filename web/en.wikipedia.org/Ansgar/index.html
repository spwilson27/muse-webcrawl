Ansgar

Saint Ansgar, apostle of the North

Born: 8 September 801, Amiens, Francia
Died: 3 February 865, Bremen
Venerated in: Catholic Church, Eastern Orthodox Church, Lutheran Church
Feast: 3 February

Ansgar (8 September 801 – 3 February 865), also known as Anskar or Saint Anschar, was Archbishop of Hamburg-Bremen in the ninth century and is remembered as the "Apostle of the North" for his missionary journeys to Denmark and Sweden. His work laid the foundations for the Christianisation of Scandinavia, although much of his mission collapsed after his death and had to be rebuilt by later generations.

Life

Ansgar was born near Amiens in Picardy. After his mother's early death he was educated at the abbey of Corbie, where he later became a monk and schoolmaster. When the newly baptised Danish king Harald Klak sought missionaries, Ansgar volunteered and accompanied him to Denmark around 826, preaching and teaching there for about two years.

In 829, at the invitation of the Swedish king Björn, Ansgar travelled to the trading town of Birka on Lake Mälaren, where he founded the first known Christian congregation in Sweden. Returning to Francia, he was made the first Archbishop of Hamburg in 831 and charged with evangelising the peoples of the north and east. After Hamburg was sacked by Danish Vikings in 845, his see was united with that of Bremen.

Ansgar made a second journey to Birka in the early 850s, securing permission for public Christian worship in Sweden. He died at Bremen in 865. His successor Rimbert wrote his biography, the Vita Anskarii, a key source for ninth-century Scandinavia.

Legacy

Ansgar was canonised shortly after his death. Churches and schools across northern Germany and Scandinavia bear his name, and he is a patron saint of Scandinavia and of Denmark. See also Viking Age, Birka and History of Sweden.
