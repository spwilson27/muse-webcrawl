Personnel selection

Personnel selection is the methodical process used by organisations to assess job applicants and choose those most likely to perform well in a role. As a branch of industrial and organizational psychology, it applies the principles of psychometrics to identify valid predictors of job performance and to make hiring decisions that are effective, fair and legally defensible.

Methods

Selection tools include application forms and CV screening, cognitive ability and job-knowledge tests, personality and integrity tests, structured and unstructured interviews, work samples and simulations, assessment centres, reference checks and, increasingly, game-based and AI-assisted assessments. Meta-analytic research, notably by Schmidt and Hunter, found that general mental ability tests combined with structured interviews or integrity tests predict job performance substantially better than years of experience or unstructured interviews alone.

Validity and fairness

Selection procedures are evaluated by their reliability and criterion validity, and monitored for adverse impact against protected groups. In the United States, the Uniform Guidelines on Employee Selection Procedures (1978) and civil-rights law require that tests with disparate impact be demonstrably job-related; similar equality duties apply in other jurisdictions. Sound selection begins with job analysis, which defines the knowledge, skills, abilities and other characteristics a role requires.
