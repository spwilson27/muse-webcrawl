Cardiff Bay Barrage

The barrage across the mouth of Cardiff Bay (placeholder)

The Cardiff Bay Barrage lies across the mouth of Cardiff Bay, Wales, between Queen Alexandra Head (near Penarth) and the Cardiff Docks. Completed in 1999 and officially opened in 2001, the 1.1 km-long barrage impounds the rivers Taff and Ely to create a 200-hectare (500-acre) freshwater lake, eliminating the vast mudflats formerly exposed at low tide in one of the world's highest tidal ranges and enabling the regeneration of Cardiff's docklands as Cardiff Bay.

Construction

The scheme was promoted by the Cardiff Bay Development Corporation (established 1987) and authorised by the Cardiff Bay Barrage Act 1993, after fierce controversy over the loss of the Taff–Ely estuary mudflats, a feeding ground for wading birds; a compensatory wetland reserve was created at Newport Wetlands. Construction by a Balfour Beatty–Costain joint venture began in 1994. The barrage comprises an embankment and concrete structures housing five sluice gates, a fish pass, and three locks allowing boats to pass between the bay and the Bristol Channel; a control building monitors water quality and levels. It cost about £220 million.

Operation and impact

The impounded bay maintains a constant water level, supporting marinas, watersports, and waterfront developments including the Senedd building and the Wales Millennium Centre. The barrage itself carries a pedestrian and cycle route forming part of the Cardiff Bay Trail. Concerns have included flooding risk, water quality and algal blooms in the early years, and the effects on groundwater in parts of Cardiff, addressed by monitoring and mitigation measures.
