Bastia is a commune in the Haute-Corse department of France, located in the north-east of the island of Corsica at the base of Cap Corse. It is the prefecture of Haute-Corse and the island's principal commercial port and second-largest city.

History

Bastia was founded in 1380 by the Genoese governor Leonello Lomellini, who built a fortress — a bastiglia, the source of the city's name — to defend the harbor against Aragonese and pirate attacks. Genoa ruled Corsica from Bastia for centuries. The island was sold to France in 1768, and Britain briefly held the town during the Anglo-Corsican Kingdom of the 1790s. Bastia suffered heavy bombing in the Second World War before Corsica's liberation in 1943.

Geography and sights

The old town, Terra Vecchia, climbs from the old port (Vieux Port) with its twin-towered church of Saint-Jean-Baptiste, the largest church in Corsica; above it, the Genoese citadel of Terra Nova overlooks the sea. Bastia's ferry port links Corsica with mainland France and Italy, and the nearby airport serves northern Corsica. Local specialties include the aperitif Cap Corse and Brocciu cheese.

Sport

The city is home to SC Bastia, a football club founded in 1905 that reached the UEFA Cup final in 1978 and plays at the Stade Armand-Cesari at Furiani.
