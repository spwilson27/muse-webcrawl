Michael Geoffrey Costin (10 July 1929 – 15 April 2025) was a British engineer who co-founded the racing engine manufacturer Cosworth with Keith Duckworth in 1958. The company's name is a portmanteau of their surnames. Costin had earlier worked at Lotus Cars, where he contributed to chassis and aerodynamic development alongside Colin Chapman.

Cosworth

Founded in London in 1958, Cosworth began by modifying Ford production engines for racing. Under Costin's technical direction on the chassis and installation side, and Duckworth's engine design, the firm produced the Ford Cosworth DFV (Double Four Valve) V8 in 1967. The DFV won its debut race and went on to record 155 Grand Prix victories, making it the most successful engine in Formula One history.

Career and legacy

Costin served as Cosworth's technical director and remained associated with the company through its sales to United Engineering Industries, Vickers, Volkswagen/Audi, and Ford. His partnership with Duckworth is regarded as one of the most fruitful in motorsport engineering, and Cosworth engines powered champions including Graham Hill, Jackie Stewart, Emerson Fittipaldi, and Mario Andretti.
