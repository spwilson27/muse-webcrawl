1976 Summer Olympics boycott

GamesMontreal 1976
Boycotting nationsAbout 28, mostly African
IssueNew Zealand rugby tour of South Africa

The 1976 Summer Olympics boycott was led by Tanzania on behalf of most African nations, protesting the participation of New Zealand at the Montreal Games after the New Zealand national rugby union team had toured apartheid South Africa earlier that year.

Background

African nations demanded that the International Olympic Committee ban New Zealand to uphold the sporting isolation of South Africa. The IOC refused, arguing rugby union was not an Olympic sport and lay outside its jurisdiction.

Effect

Roughly 28 countries withdrew, most after the Games had already begun. The boycott weakened the athletics programme in particular, removing many leading African distance runners, and foreshadowed the larger boycotts of 1980 and 1984.
