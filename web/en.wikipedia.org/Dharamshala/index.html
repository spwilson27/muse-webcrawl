Dharamshala (also spelled Dharamsala) is a town in the Indian state of Himachal Pradesh. Surrounded by cedar forests on the edge of the Himalayas, the town serves as the district headquarters of Kangra district and is best known internationally as the home of the 14th Dalai Lama and the seat of the Central Tibetan Administration, the Tibetan government-in-exile.

Geography

Dharamshala lies in the Kangra Valley, shadowed by the Dhauladhar range of the Himalayas. The town is divided into an upper and a lower section: Lower Dharamshala, at about 1,380 metres, is the commercial centre, while Upper Dharamshala, at about 1,700 metres, is home to the suburb of McLeod Ganj, named after Sir Donald Friell McLeod, a Lieutenant Governor of Punjab.

History

The region was ruled for centuries by the Katoch dynasty of Kangra. After British annexation following the First Anglo-Sikh War in 1846, Dharamshala became a hill station and the summer capital of Punjab province's deputy commissioner. In 1960, following the 1959 Tibetan uprising, the Indian government offered refuge to Tenzin Gyatso, the 14th Dalai Lama, who established his residence in McLeod Ganj. The town has since become a major centre of Tibetan culture and Buddhism.

Landmarks

Notable sites include the Tsuglagkhang Complex, the Dalai Lama's temple; the Namgyal Monastery; the Library of Tibetan Works and Archives; and the Himachal Pradesh Cricket Association Stadium, one of the most picturesque cricket grounds in the world. The nearby village of Dharamkot and the Triund trek are popular with visitors.
