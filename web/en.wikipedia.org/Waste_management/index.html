Municipal waste collection is the most visible part of waste management.

Waste management (or waste disposal) includes the processes and actions required to manage waste from its inception to its final disposal. This includes the collection, transport, treatment and disposal of waste, together with monitoring and regulation of the waste management process and waste-related laws, technologies and economic mechanisms.

Waste hierarchy

The waste hierarchy ranks management options by environmental desirability: prevention (reducing waste at source) is most preferred, followed by preparing for reuse, recycling, other recovery (such as energy recovery from incineration) and finally disposal, principally landfill. The hierarchy underpins waste law in the European Union and many other jurisdictions, and is related to the "reduce, reuse, recycle" slogan and to circular economy policy.

Methods

Landfilling remains the most common disposal method worldwide; modern sanitary landfills line and cap waste and capture landfill gas, while uncontrolled open dumping persists in many low-income countries. Incineration reduces waste volume and can generate electricity and heat, but requires control of air emissions. Recycling recovers materials such as metals, glass, paper and plastics; composting and anaerobic digestion treat organic waste, the latter producing biogas. Hazardous waste — including chemical, medical and electronic waste (e-waste) — requires specialised handling, and its transboundary movement is governed by the Basel Convention (1989).

Scale and challenges

The World Bank's What a Waste 2.0 report estimated global municipal solid waste generation at about 2.01 billion tonnes annually, projected to rise substantially by 2050, with high-income countries generating a disproportionate share per capita. Key challenges include plastic pollution, food waste (roughly a third of food produced is lost or wasted), the informal waste-picking sector on which many recycling systems depend, and financing collection services in rapidly growing cities.
