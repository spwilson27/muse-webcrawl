The International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) is an international organisation working in the field of nature conservation and the sustainable use of natural resources. Founded in 1948 at Fontainebleau under UNESCO auspices—initially as the International Union for the Protection of Nature—it is unique among international bodies in uniting government agencies and non-governmental organisations as members, with a network of thousands of volunteer experts organised in specialist commissions.

The Red List

The IUCN is best known for the IUCN Red List of Threatened Species, founded in 1964, the world's most comprehensive inventory of the conservation status of biological species. Species are assessed against quantitative criteria and assigned to categories—Least Concern, Near Threatened, Vulnerable, Endangered, Critically Endangered, Extinct in the Wild, and Extinct—which have become the global standard for extinction-risk assessment. More than 150,000 species have been assessed, of which over 40,000 are classified as threatened. The IUCN also maintains the World Database on Protected Areas (with UNEP) and a Red List of Ecosystems.

Governance and influence

Members meet every four years at the IUCN World Conservation Congress, which sets the organisation's programme and adopts motions influential in environmental policy. The IUCN holds observer status at the United Nations General Assembly and played a role in founding the World Wide Fund for Nature (WWF) and in shaping the World Heritage Convention, CITES, and the Convention on Biological Diversity. Its Species Survival Commission and World Commission on Protected Areas are among its six expert commissions.
