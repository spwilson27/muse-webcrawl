The Elbphilharmonie (English: Elbe Philharmonic Hall; colloquially Elphi) is a concert hall in the HafenCity quarter of Hamburg, Germany, one of the largest and most acoustically advanced concert halls in the world. Designed by the Swiss firm Herzog & de Meuron, it sits atop a 1960s brick cocoa warehouse (the Kaispeicher A) at the western tip of the Grasbrook peninsula, on the River Elbe.

Construction

First proposed in 2003 as a private–public partnership, the project became notorious for delays and cost overruns: the final cost of about €866 million far exceeded the original estimate of roughly €77 million, and completion slipped from 2010 to 2016. The building's shimmering glass superstructure, with its wave-like silhouette, houses the 2,100-seat Grand Hall arranged in a "vineyard" configuration around the stage, a smaller recital hall, a hotel, apartments and a public plaza at 37 metres above the water offering panoramic views.

Reception

The acoustics, designed by Yasuhisa Toyota, were immediately acclaimed, and the hall quickly became a landmark for Hamburg's musical life and a symbol of the city's redeveloped harbour. Despite the controversies of its construction, the Elbphilharmonie has drawn comparisons with the Sydney Opera House as an icon of contemporary waterfront architecture.
