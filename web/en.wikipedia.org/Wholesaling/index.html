Wholesaling

Wholesaling (or wholesale trade) is the sale of goods in bulk, typically to retailers, industrial users or other businesses rather than to final consumers. Wholesalers buy in large quantities directly from manufacturers, store and break bulk, and resell at a margin, performing functions such as warehousing, transportation, financing, risk-bearing and market information.

Types of wholesalers

Merchant wholesalers take title to the goods they handle and include full-service distributors and limited-service operators such as cash-and-carry warehouses, drop shippers and rack jobbers. Agents and brokers, by contrast, negotiate sales without taking title, earning commissions. Manufacturers' sales branches are a third channel. Warehouse clubs such as Costco blur the boundary by selling to both businesses and individual members.

Role in the economy

Wholesale trade is a major sector of advanced economies, accounting for a substantial share of business sales and employment. Digital marketplaces and direct-to-consumer models have pressured traditional wholesalers, while large distributors in food, pharmaceuticals and electronics remain central links in supply chains.
