Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy
AbbreviationEIS
MeasuresComplex impedance vs frequency
ApplicationsCorrosion, batteries, fuel cells, coatings, biosensors

Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) is a technique that measures the impedance of an electrochemical system over a range of frequencies, revealing the electrical resistance, capacitance and kinetic processes occurring at electrode interfaces.

Principle

A small sinusoidal voltage (typically 5–10 mV) is applied to a cell and the resulting current is measured. The ratio of voltage to current, expressed as a function of frequency, gives the complex impedance Z(ω), which is commonly displayed as Nyquist plots (imaginary versus real impedance) or Bode plots (magnitude and phase versus frequency).

Interpretation

Data are interpreted using equivalent circuits built from resistors, capacitors and specialised elements such as constant-phase elements and Warburg diffusion impedances. The classic Randles circuit models a charge-transfer resistance in parallel with double-layer capacitance, in series with solution resistance.

Applications

EIS is used to evaluate corrosion rates and protective coatings, characterise batteries and fuel cells, study semiconductor interfaces, and develop electrochemical biosensors.
