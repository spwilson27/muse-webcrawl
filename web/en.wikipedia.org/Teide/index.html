Teide

Mount Teide (Spanish: El Teide, or Pico del Teide) is a volcano on the island of Tenerife in the Canary Islands, Spain. At 3,715 metres (12,188 ft) above sea level it is the highest point in Spain and the highest point above sea level of any island in the Atlantic; measured from its base on the ocean floor, it rises about 7,500 metres, making it one of the tallest volcanoes in the world. Teide is an active volcano, though its last eruption was in 1909 from the El Chinyero vent.

Geology

Teide formed within the Las Cañadas caldera, the collapsed remnant of an older, larger volcano. The present cone grew through eruptions over the last roughly 160,000 years. The surrounding Teide National Park, established in 1954 and a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2007, protects a high-altitude volcanic landscape of lava flows, cones, and endemic flora adapted to the harsh climate.

History and culture

Teide was sacred to the Guanches, the indigenous people of Tenerife, who believed the mountain held up the sky and was associated with the god Guayota. The peak is visible across much of Tenerife and from neighbouring islands. A cable car carries visitors most of the way to the summit, and access to the crater itself is by permit. The clarity of the skies above the national park supports the Teide Observatory, an important astronomical site.

Trivia

The shadow cast by Teide at sunrise and sunset, stretching over the sea, is said to be the largest shadow projected onto the ocean in the world.
