Cohabitation (government)

Cohabitation is a system of divided government that occurs in semi-presidential systems such as France, when the president is from a different political party than the majority in the legislature, forcing the president to appoint a prime minister from the opposing party.

France

France has experienced three periods of cohabitation under the Fifth Republic: 1986–1988 (François Mitterrand and Jacques Chirac), 1993–1995 (Mitterrand and Édouard Balladur), and 1997–2002 (Jacques Chirac and Lionel Jospin). During cohabitation the prime minister leads domestic policy while the president retains influence in foreign affairs.

Other countries

Similar arrangements have occurred in Poland, Romania, Sri Lanka, Ukraine and other semi-presidential republics, often producing tension between heads of state and government.
