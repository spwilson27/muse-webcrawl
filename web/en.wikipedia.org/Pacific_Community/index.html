Pacific Community

Pacific Community (SPC)
Founded: February 6, 1947 (Canberra Agreement)
Headquarters: Nouméa, New Caledonia
Membership: 27 member countries and territories
Working languages: English, French
Former name: Secretariat of the Pacific Community

The Pacific Community (SPC) is an international development organisation owned and governed by its 27 country and territory members. Founded in 1947 as the South Pacific Commission, it is the oldest and largest organisation in the Pacific regional system. SPC works in more than 20 sectors, including fisheries science, public health surveillance, geoscience, agriculture, forestry, water resources, disaster risk management, statistics, education, human rights and culture.

The organisation's headquarters is in Nouméa, New Caledonia, with a regional office in Suva, Fiji, and country offices across the Pacific. Its membership includes all 22 Pacific Island countries and territories plus Australia, France, New Zealand and the United States. In 2016 the organisation adopted the name "Pacific Community" while retaining the initials SPC in both English and French (Communauté du Pacifique, CP).

History

SPC was established by the Canberra Agreement of 1947, signed by Australia, France, the Netherlands, New Zealand, the United Kingdom and the United States, initially to promote stability and assist post-war reconstruction in the South Pacific. The Netherlands withdrew after transferring Netherlands New Guinea in 1962, and the name changed from South Pacific Commission to Secretariat of the Pacific Community in 1997 as membership expanded to all Pacific Island nations and territories, including those north of the equator.

Work

SPC provides technical assistance, policy advice and scientific services. Its fisheries programme supplies the stock assessments underpinning the Pacific's tuna fisheries; its public health division coordinates regional disease surveillance; and its geoscience, energy and maritime divisions support water security, energy access and maritime boundary delimitation.
