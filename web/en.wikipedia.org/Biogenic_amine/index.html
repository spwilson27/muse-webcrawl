Biogenic amine

A biogenic amine is a biogenic substance with one or more amine groups. They are mainly formed by the decarboxylation of amino acids or by amination and transamination of aldehydes and ketones. Biogenic amines are organic bases with low molecular weight and are synthesized by microbial, plant and animal metabolism.

Importance

Biogenic amines have important functions in living cells, acting as sources of nitrogen and as precursors for the synthesis of hormones, alkaloids, nucleic acids and proteins. Well-known examples include the neurotransmitters and neuromodulators serotonin, dopamine, histamine and the catecholamines adrenaline and noradrenaline, as well as compounds such as tyramine, putrescine and cadaverine.

In food

Biogenic amines can accumulate in fermented foods and beverages, including cheese, wine and cured meats, when amino-acid-decarboxylating microorganisms are present. High intakes of histamine and tyramine can cause adverse reactions sometimes described as food intolerance, and histamine accumulation in spoiled fish is responsible for scombroid food poisoning.
