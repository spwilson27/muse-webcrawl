Nikolai Leskov

Nikolai Semyonovich Leskov (Russian: Никола́й Семёнович Леско́в; 16 February [O.S. 4 February] 1831 – 5 March [O.S. 21 February] 1895) was a Russian novelist, short-story writer, playwright, and journalist, who also wrote under the pseudonym M. Stebnitsky. Praised by Tolstoy and Chekhov, he is noted for his masterful use of skaz narration — storytelling in a character's distinctive spoken voice — and for his vivid portraits of provincial Russian life.

Life and career

Born in the village of Gorokhovo, Oryol Governorate, Leskov spent years travelling through Russia as an agent for a trading company, gathering the experiences that inform his fiction. He began publishing journalism in 1860 and won notoriety with his anti-nihilist novels No Way Out (1864) and At Daggers Drawn (1870–71), which alienated both radicals and conservatives and left him an independent, often controversial figure.

Major works

Leskov's best-known works include the novella Lady Macbeth of the Mtsensk District (1865) — later adapted by Shostakovich into an opera — the novel Cathedral Folk (1872), and the tale The Tale of Cross-eyed Lefty from Tula and the Steel Flea (1881), whose ingenious craftsman-hero became a symbol of Russian folk ingenuity. His later stories of righteous men and Old Believers, such as The Enchanted Wanderer (1873) and The Sealed Angel (1873), explore Russian spirituality with sympathy and humour.
