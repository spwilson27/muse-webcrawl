Spit (landform)

A spit is a narrow coastal landform — a depositional bar of sand or shingle — that projects from the mainland into a body of water, attached at one end to the coast and free at the other. Spits form by longshore drift, in which currents carry sediment along the shore and deposit it where the coastline changes direction, such as at a river mouth or bay entrance.

Formation and features

The far end of a growing spit often curves landward because of wave refraction, forming a hooked or recurved spit. Behind a spit, sheltered water may develop into salt marsh or a lagoon. Well-known examples include Spurn Head in England, Farewell Spit in New Zealand, and the Curonian Spit in the Baltic Sea.
