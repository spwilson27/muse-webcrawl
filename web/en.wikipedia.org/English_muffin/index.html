English muffin

An English muffin is a small, round, flat yeast-leavened bread, commonly sliced horizontally, toasted, and buttered. Cooked on a griddle rather than baked in an oven, English muffins have a soft interior with characteristic air pockets ('nooks and crannies') and a lightly crisped, semolina- or cornmeal-dusted exterior. In the United Kingdom they are simply called muffins; the 'English' prefix is mainly North American usage, distinguishing them from American-style sweet muffins.

History

Muffins were sold by street vendors in Victorian London — the 'muffin man' of the nursery rhyme — and served at tea. Samuel Bath Thomas, an English immigrant baker in New York, began selling his version in the 1880s and popularised the pre-split, fork-split muffin in the United States, where Thomas' became the best-known brand.

Serving

English muffins are a breakfast staple, commonly topped with jam, eggs or cheese, and form the base of eggs Benedict and of breakfast sandwiches. Variants include whole wheat, sourdough and crumpet-like versions; crumpets differ in being made from a wetter batter cooked in rings on one side only.
