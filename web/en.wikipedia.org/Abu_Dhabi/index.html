Abu Dhabi

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Abu Dhabi (Arabic: أبو ظبي, "Father of a Gazelle") is the capital city of the United Arab Emirates and the seat of government of the Emirate of Abu Dhabi, the largest of the seven emirates. The city, built largely on an island in the Persian Gulf off the central western coast, has a population of roughly 1.5 million. Abu Dhabi is the political and administrative capital of the UAE, while Dubai is its commercial centre; together they dominate the country's economy.

History

The area around Abu Dhabi was home to the nomadic Bani Yas tribal confederation. According to legend, the settlement was founded around 1761 after hunters followed a gazelle to fresh water on the island. Sheikh Shakhbut bin Dhiyab made it his capital in 1793. For over a century the economy rested on pearl diving and fishing until oil was discovered in the emirate in 1958. Under Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan (ruler from 1966, founding president of the UAE in 1971), oil revenues transformed the city into a modern metropolis.

Economy

The emirate holds a large share of the world's proven oil reserves, and hydrocarbons long underpinned its wealth, channelled through sovereign wealth funds such as the Abu Dhabi Investment Authority (ADIA), among the largest in the world. In recent decades the government has pursued diversification into finance, tourism, culture, renewable energy and technology, exemplified by projects such as Masdar City and the Saadiyat Island cultural district, home to the Louvre Abu Dhabi (opened 2017).

Landmarks

The city's best-known landmark is the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque, completed in 2007, one of the largest mosques in the world, accommodating over 50,000 worshippers. Other landmarks include the Emirates Palace hotel, the Corniche waterfront, the leaning Capital Gate tower, Ferrari World and the Yas Marina Circuit, host of the Abu Dhabi Grand Prix.
