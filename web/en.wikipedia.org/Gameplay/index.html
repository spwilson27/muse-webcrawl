Gameplay

Gameplay is the specific way in which players interact with a game, and in particular with video games. Gameplay is the pattern defined through the game rules, connection between player and the game, challenges and overcoming them, plot and player's connection with it. Video game gameplay is distinct from graphics and audio elements.

Overview

The term emerged alongside early video games in the 1970s and 1980s and has remained a central concept in games criticism and design. Gameplay encompasses the core mechanics a player uses repeatedly (the "core loop"), the challenges presented, the controls and feedback systems, and the structure of progression through levels, quests or matches.

Types

Common gameplay types include asymmetric, cooperative and multiplayer gameplay, emergent gameplay (unplanned interactions arising from simple rules), nonlinear and open-world gameplay, and turn-based versus real-time play. Genres such as platformers, shooters, strategy games and role-playing games are each defined substantially by their characteristic gameplay.

Design and criticism

Reviewers frequently assess gameplay separately from presentation, and designers analyze it through frameworks such as mechanics–dynamics–aesthetics (MDA). Good gameplay is often associated with meaningful choices, clear feedback, balanced difficulty and a sense of flow.
