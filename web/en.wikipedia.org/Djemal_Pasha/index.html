Ahmed Djemal Pasha (Turkish: Ahmet Cemal Paşa; 6 May 1872 – 21 July 1922), commonly known as Djemal Pasha or Jamal Pasha, was an Ottoman military leader and statesman. As Minister of the Navy and one of the triumvirate known as the Three Pashas — with Enver Pasha and Talaat Pasha — he effectively ruled the Ottoman Empire during the First World War.

Career

Born on Lesbos and educated at the Ottoman military academy, Djemal rose through the Committee of Union and Progress, serving as military governor of Baghdad and governor of Adana. As Minister of Marine from 1914 he undertook a modernisation of the Ottoman navy. During the war he commanded the Fourth Army in Syria and Palestine, leading two failed offensives against the British-held Suez Canal in 1915 and 1916. His administration in Syria was harsh: he executed Arab nationalist leaders in Beirut and Damascus in 1915 and 1916, events commemorated in Lebanon and Syria as Martyrs' Day (6 May).

Armenian genocide

Djemal's role in the Armenian genocide is debated by historians. As a member of the ruling triumvirate, he bears a share of responsibility for the regime's policies; at the same time, scholars note that he at times resisted deportation orders in his own zone and that his memoirs attempted to distance himself from the massacres. An Ottoman court-martial sentenced him to death in absentia in 1919.

Exile and death

After the armistice, Djemal fled to Germany and Switzerland, then worked as a military adviser in Afghanistan. On 21 July 1922 he was assassinated in Tiflis (Tbilisi) by Armenian operatives as part of Operation Nemesis, the campaign to kill the genocide's organisers.
