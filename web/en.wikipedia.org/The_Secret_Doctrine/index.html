The Secret Doctrine

The Secret Doctrine: The Synthesis of Science, Religion, and Philosophy
Author: Helena Blavatsky
Country: United States / England
Language: English
Subject: Theosophy, occultism
Publication date: 1888

The Secret Doctrine: The Synthesis of Science, Religion, and Philosophy, a book originally published as two volumes in 1888, is the main work of Helena Blavatsky, co-founder of the Theosophical Society. A third, posthumous volume was published in 1897.

Contents

Volume I, Cosmogenesis, presents commentaries on stanzas from the purportedly ancient Book of Dzyan, describing the evolution of the universe; Volume II, Anthropogenesis, describes the origin and evolution of humanity through successive "root races".

Claims and sources

Blavatsky claimed the work synthesised an archaic wisdom-religion underlying world religions and modern science. Critics, notably William Emmette Coleman, documented extensive uncredited borrowing from contemporary sources.

Influence

The book became the foundational text of Theosophy, influencing occultism, New Age thought, and figures in literature and art, while its root-race doctrines were later misappropriated by some racist movements.
