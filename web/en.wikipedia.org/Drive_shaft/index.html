Drive shaft

A drive shaft (also called a propeller shaft or prop shaft) is a mechanical component for transmitting torque and rotation, usually used to connect components of a drivetrain that cannot be connected directly because of distance or the need to allow relative movement between them.

Automotive use

In a front-engine, rear-wheel-drive vehicle, the drive shaft runs the length of the car from the transmission to the differential at the rear axle. Because the axle moves with the suspension while the transmission is fixed to the body, the shaft incorporates universal joints (U-joints) or constant-velocity joints that accommodate changes in angle, and often a sliding spline that accommodates changes in length. Long shafts may be built in two pieces with a centre bearing to control vibration and whirling.

Materials and construction

Drive shafts are typically hollow tubes of steel or aluminium, which provide a good ratio of torsional strength to weight. High-performance and racing applications may use carbon-fibre shafts. The shaft must be carefully balanced, since it rotates at high speed; imbalance produces vibration that increases with vehicle speed.

Other applications

Drive shafts transmit power in ships (where the propeller shaft passes through the hull via a stern tube), in motorcycles with shaft drive, and in industrial machinery, agricultural equipment, and locomotives. Power take-off (PTO) shafts on tractors are a familiar example.
