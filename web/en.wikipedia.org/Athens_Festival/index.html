Athens Festival

The Athens Epidaurus Festival (Greek: Φεστιβάλ Αθηνών Επιδαύρου) is an annual arts festival that takes place in Athens and Epidaurus, Greece, from May to October. Founded in 1955, it is one of Europe's longest-running performing arts festivals.

A night performance at the Odeon of Herodes Atticus, beneath the Acropolis.

Venues

Principal venues include the Odeon of Herodes Atticus, the Roman theatre on the south slope of the Acropolis; the ancient theatre of Epidaurus, famed for its acoustics, where Greek tragedy and comedy are staged each summer; and the industrial complex at 260 Pireos Street.

Programme

The festival presents theatre, ancient drama, opera, classical and contemporary music, and dance. Early editions were graced by figures such as Maria Callas and Dimitri Mitropoulos, and the Epidaurus productions of the National Theatre of Greece — including works of Aeschylus, Sophocles, Euripides, and Aristophanes — remain its signature offering.

History

Established under minister Georgios Rallis in 1955 to promote Greek culture and tourism, the festival continued even during the years of the military junta, and has expanded since the 2000s to include site-specific and experimental work alongside its classical repertoire.
