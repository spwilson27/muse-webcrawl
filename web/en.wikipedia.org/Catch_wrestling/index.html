Catch wrestling

Catch wrestling, also known as catch-as-catch-can, is a style of wrestling that originated in Lancashire, England, in the 19th century. It combines takedowns, throws, ground control and submission holds ("hooks"), and permits winning by pin or submission — "catch any hold you can".

History

The style spread through travelling fairs and carnivals, where professional wrestlers challenged locals. Catch wrestlers such as Farmer Burns, Frank Gotch and Ed "Strangler" Lewis were major American sports stars in the early 20th century, and the techniques underpin much of modern professional wrestling's showmanship.

Legacy

Catch wrestling influenced Japanese shoot wrestling and shootfighting, and through them modern mixed martial arts, particularly through figures like Karl Gotch and Billy Robinson. Practitioners call the submission expert a "hooker" and the art values aggressive joint locks and cranks.
