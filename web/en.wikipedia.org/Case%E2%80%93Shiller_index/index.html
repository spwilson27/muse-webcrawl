The S&P CoreLogic Case–Shiller Home Price Indices are repeat-sales house price indices for the United States, developed in the 1980s by economists Karl E. Case, Robert J. Shiller, and Allan Weiss, and published since 2006 by S&P Dow Jones Indices.

Method

The indices track changes in the value of residential real estate by recording price changes for the same properties when they are resold or refinanced, thus holding housing quality roughly constant. There are individual indices for 20 metropolitan areas, composite indices for the top 10 and top 20 metros, and a quarterly U.S. National Home Price Index, all published with a two-month lag.

Use and significance

The indices underpin futures and options traded on the Chicago Mercantile Exchange and are widely used by economists and policymakers. Shiller's analysis of the long-run index featured prominently in discussion of the 2000s United States housing bubble, which the national index showed peaking in 2006 before falling by roughly a quarter into 2012.
