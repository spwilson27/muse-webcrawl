Moby-Dick

Moby-Dick; or, The Whale is an 1851 novel by American writer Herman Melville. The book is centered on the sailor Ishmael's narrative of the maniacal quest of Ahab, captain of the whaling ship Pequod, for vengeance against Moby Dick, the giant white sperm whale that bit off his leg on a previous voyage.

Plot

Ishmael signs aboard the Pequod at Nantucket, befriending the harpooneer Queequeg. Captain Ahab, having lost a leg to the white whale, nails a doubloon to the mast for whoever first sights Moby Dick and reveals his true purpose: not commercial whaling but revenge. After a long voyage through the world's oceans, filled with whaling lore, cetological essays and meditations on whiteness, good and evil, the Pequod meets the whale. In a three-day chase Moby Dick destroys the boats and sinks the ship; only Ishmael survives, floating on Queequeg's coffin until rescued by the Rachel.

Sources and reception

Melville drew on his own experience aboard the whaler Acushnet, the sinking of the Essex by a whale in 1820, and accounts of a real albino sperm whale called Mocha Dick. The novel was a commercial failure on publication and Melville died in obscurity in 1891. Its reputation was revived in the 1920s, and it is now regarded as one of the greatest American novels, famous for its opening line, "Call me Ishmael."
