Palace Museum

Palace Museum (故宫博物院)
Established: October 10, 1925
Location: Forbidden City, Dongcheng District, Beijing, China
Type: Art and history museum
Collection size: over 1.8 million pieces
Visitors: over 17 million (2018)

The Palace Museum (Chinese: 故宫博物院; pinyin: Gùgōng Bówùyuàn) is a large national museum complex housed in the Forbidden City at the core of Beijing, China. With a collection of over 1.8 million pieces, it is one of the largest and most comprehensive museums of Chinese art and history in the world. The museum's collection was built on the imperial collections of the Ming and Qing dynasties.

The Palace Museum was established on 10 October 1925, shortly after Puyi, the last emperor of China, was expelled from the palace in 1924. Its holdings include paintings, calligraphy, ceramics, bronzes, jade, clocks, and imperial artefacts accumulated over five centuries. During the Second Sino-Japanese War, much of the collection was evacuated across China; a portion was later transported to Taiwan, where it formed the core of the National Palace Museum in Taipei, opened in 1965.

The site

The museum occupies the Forbidden City, the Chinese imperial palace from 1420 to 1924, a UNESCO World Heritage Site comprising 980 surviving buildings across 72 hectares. Major halls open to visitors include the Hall of Supreme Harmony, the Hall of Mental Cultivation, and the Treasure Gallery in the Palace of Tranquil Longevity area.
