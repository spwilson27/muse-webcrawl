Solaris (operating system)

DeveloperSun Microsystems; Oracle Corporation
Initial releaseJune 1992 (as Solaris 2.0)
Latest releaseSolaris 11.4

Solaris is a proprietary Unix operating system originally developed by Sun Microsystems. After the Sun acquisition by Oracle in 2010, it was renamed Oracle Solaris. Solaris is known for its scalability, especially on SPARC systems, and for originating many innovative features, including DTrace, ZFS and Zones.

History

Solaris descends from SunOS, Sun's earlier BSD-based system. In the late 1980s Sun and AT&T merged System V Release 4 with SunOS elements, and the resulting system was marketed from 1992 as Solaris 2. Solaris 10 (2005) was a major release introducing the ZFS filesystem, Solaris Zones containers, and the DTrace dynamic tracing framework; much of its source code was released as OpenSolaris the same year. After Oracle acquired Sun in 2010, OpenSolaris was discontinued, but community forks such as illumos continued its development.

Features

Solaris supports symmetric multiprocessing at large scale, includes the ZFS copy-on-write filesystem with integrated volume management, and provides OS-level virtualisation through Zones. It historically supported the SPARC architecture designed by Sun, and later x86-64 processors as well. Oracle ended most Solaris development in 2017, with support for Solaris 11.4 continuing into the 2030s.
