The Hillsborough disaster was a fatal crowd crush during a football match at Hillsborough Stadium in Sheffield, South Yorkshire, England, on 15 April 1989. It occurred during an FA Cup semi-final between Liverpool and Nottingham Forest, when a crush developed in the standing pens of the Leppings Lane end allocated to Liverpool supporters. Ninety-seven Liverpool fans were fatally injured — men, women, and children, the youngest aged ten — making it the worst disaster in British sporting history.

Causes and aftermath

The crush resulted from failures by South Yorkshire Police, whose match commander ordered an exit gate opened into already full central pens without closing the tunnel feeding them, compounded by the stadium's faulty layout and perimeter fences. The Taylor Report (1990) blamed police failure and led to the conversion of top-division grounds to all-seater stadiums. Nevertheless, an early narrative blaming Liverpool supporters — amplified by The Sun's notorious "The Truth" front page — was maintained for decades, and the original inquests returned verdicts of accidental death.

Justice campaign

Families campaigned for over twenty years. The Hillsborough Independent Panel's 2012 report exposed altered police statements and the extent of the cover-up, prompting a formal apology in Parliament. Fresh inquests (2014–2016) concluded that the 96 victims then recognised (a 97th, Andrew Devine, died in 2021 and was ruled unlawfully killed) had been unlawfully killed and that fan behaviour had played no part. Match commander David Duckenfield was acquitted of gross negligence manslaughter in 2019.
