The Office of Strategic Services (OSS) was the wartime intelligence agency of the United States during the Second World War, and the predecessor of the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA). Created by presidential military order on 13 June 1942 under William J. "Wild Bill" Donovan, the OSS was charged with collecting and analysing strategic information and conducting unconventional and paramilitary operations — espionage, sabotage, subversion and guerrilla warfare — behind enemy lines.

Organisation

The OSS grew out of the Coordinator of Information office established in 1941. Its principal branches included Secret Intelligence (SI) for espionage, Special Operations (SO) for sabotage and guerrilla support, Morale Operations (MO) for black propaganda, Research and Analysis (R&A) — staffed by academics — and the paramilitary Operational Groups. The Maritime Unit pioneered combat swimming techniques that led to the U.S. Navy's Underwater Demolition Teams and, ultimately, the SEALs.

Operations

OSS operatives worked with resistance movements across occupied Europe and Asia: Jedburgh teams parachuted into France ahead of and after D-Day to arm and coordinate the French Resistance; Detachment 101 in Burma conducted guerrilla operations with the Kachin people against Japanese forces; and OSS stations in Bern (under Allen Dulles), Istanbul, Stockholm and Lisbon ran espionage networks. Operation Sunrise, Dulles's negotiations in Switzerland, arranged the surrender of German forces in Italy in May 1945. OSS-trained teams also advised and supplied Ho Chi Minh's Viet Minh against the Japanese in 1945.

Personnel

At its peak the OSS employed some 13,000 people, including future CIA directors Allen Dulles and William Casey, chef Julia Child (who worked in the Emergency Sea Rescue Equipment Section), historian Arthur Schlesinger Jr., and film director John Ford, whose Field Photographic Branch documented the war.

Dissolution and legacy

President Harry S. Truman dissolved the OSS effective 1 October 1945, distributing its functions between the State and War Departments; its analysis and clandestine remnants passed through the Strategic Services Unit and the Central Intelligence Group before the National Security Act of 1947 created the CIA, which consciously modelled itself on the OSS. The Congressional Gold Medal was awarded collectively to OSS members in 2018.
