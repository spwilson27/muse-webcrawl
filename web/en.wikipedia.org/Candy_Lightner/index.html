Candy Lightner

Candace Lynne "Candy" Lightner (born May 30, 1946) is an American activist who was the founding president of Mothers Against Drunk Driving (MADD). She founded the organisation in 1980 after her 13-year-old daughter, Cari, was killed by a drunk driver in Fair Oaks, California.

Founding of MADD

On 3 May 1980, Lightner's daughter Cari was walking to a school carnival when she was struck and killed by a driver who had been arrested repeatedly for drunk driving and was out on bail from another hit-and-run offence the previous day; he received a sentence of two years but served only about nine months. Outraged by the leniency of the system, Lightner quit her job and founded MADD in Sacramento, California, to press for tougher laws. The organisation grew rapidly into a national movement credited with helping to raise the legal drinking age in the United States to 21 and to lower permitted blood-alcohol limits for drivers.

Later work

Lightner left MADD in 1985 amid disagreements over the organisation's direction. She later worked with the American Beverage Institute, a position criticised by some former allies, and continued advocacy on traffic-safety issues, including campaigns against distracted driving through the organisation WeSaveLives, which she founded in 2014.
