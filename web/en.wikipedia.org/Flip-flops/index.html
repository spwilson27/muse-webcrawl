Flip-flops

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia. This article is about the footwear. For the electronics device, see Flip-flop (electronics).

Flip-flops are a type of light sandal, typically worn as casual footwear. They consist of a flat sole held loosely on the foot by a Y-shaped strap — the thong — that passes between the first and second toes and around either side of the foot. Names vary by country: thongs in Australia, jandals in New Zealand, slops in South Africa, and tsinelas in the Philippines.

History

Thong sandals are among the oldest footwear: ancient Egyptians wore sandals of papyrus and palm leaves with a similar strap arrangement, examples surviving from about 1500 BC. The modern flip-flop derives from the Japanese zōri, which American servicemen brought home after World War II. Cheap injection-moulded rubber versions spread in the 1950s and 1960s — in New Zealand, Morris Yock patented the "jandal" (Japanese sandal) in 1957, while in Brazil the Havaianas brand (founded 1962) turned the flip-flop into a national icon.

Use and concerns

Flip-flops are everyday footwear in much of the tropics and standard beach wear elsewhere. Health professionals caution that their lack of support and protection can aggravate foot problems and increase tripping risk, and driving in flip-flops has been flagged as hazardous. Fashion versions in leather and with designer branding have moved the style beyond the beach, occasionally provoking etiquette debates — as when a college lacrosse team wore flip-flops to the White House in 2005.
