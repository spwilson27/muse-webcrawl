Methuselah (tree)

Methuselah is a Great Basin bristlecone pine (Pinus longaeva) in the White Mountains of California, in Inyo National Forest, that is one of the oldest known living non-clonal trees on Earth. Its age has been estimated at about 4,850 years, meaning it germinated around the 29th century BCE, before the construction of the Egyptian pyramids was complete.

Discovery and dating

The tree was sampled by Edmund Schulman and Tom Harlan using increment coring, and its age was established by dendrochronology — counting and cross-dating its annual rings. For many years Methuselah was considered the oldest known living tree; another bristlecone in the same mountains, confirmed older (over 5,000 years), has also been reported, though with debate, and the clonal creosote and aspen colonies elsewhere are older as genetic individuals.

Protection

Methuselah grows in the Ancient Bristlecone Pine Forest at roughly 2,900-3,000 metres elevation. Its exact location is kept secret by the US Forest Service to protect it from vandalism; the Methuselah Trail allows visitors to walk among the ancient grove without knowing which tree it is. The slow growth, dense resinous wood and harsh, dry, rocky dolomite soils of the grove account for the trees' extreme longevity and gnarled, largely dead-wood appearance with a narrow strip of living bark.
