Gulf of Finland

The Gulf of Finland is the easternmost arm of the Baltic Sea, extending between Finland to the north and Estonia to the south, and reaching east to Saint Petersburg in Russia, where the Neva River flows into it.

Geography

The gulf is about 400 km long and up to 130 km wide, with an average depth of only about 38 metres. Its waters are brackish and nearly fresh at the eastern end because of the large inflow of the Neva and Narva rivers. It freezes over for three to five months each winter. Major ports include Helsinki, Tallinn, Saint Petersburg, Vyborg and Primorsk, making it one of the busiest shipping areas in the world, with heavy oil tanker traffic from Russian terminals.

Environment and history

The shallow, enclosed gulf suffers from eutrophication from agricultural and municipal runoff, and its seabed holds dumped chemical munitions and numerous minefields left from the world wars, when the gulf was a contested naval theatre — most famously during the Soviet evacuation of Tallinn in 1941. The Saint Petersburg Dam, completed in 2011, protects the city from the floods that historically surged up the gulf.
