Anguillan secession

Date1967–1980
LocationAnguilla, Caribbean
Key figureRonald Webster
OutcomeAnguilla separated from Saint Kitts and Nevis; British Overseas Territory

The Anguillan secession was the movement by which the island of Anguilla separated from the associated state of Saint Christopher-Nevis-Anguilla and returned to direct British administration, culminating in formal separation in 1980.

Background and revolt

Anguilla had long been administered together with Saint Kitts, whose government Anguillans felt neglected their island. When Saint Christopher-Nevis-Anguilla became an associated state of the United Kingdom in 1967, Anguillans revolted: they expelled the St Kitts police, held referendums rejecting the association, and unilaterally declared independence as a republic under Ronald Webster.

British intervention and settlement

In March 1969 British paratroopers and police landed in "Operation Sheepskin", meeting no resistance, and restored British administration. The Anguilla Act 1971 placed the island under a British commissioner, and the Anguilla Act 1980 legally separated Anguilla from Saint Kitts and Nevis, which proceeded to full independence without it in 1983.

Legacy

Anguilla remains a British Overseas Territory with internal self-government. The events of 1967 are celebrated annually on Anguilla Day, 30 May, and Webster is honoured as the "Father of the Nation".
