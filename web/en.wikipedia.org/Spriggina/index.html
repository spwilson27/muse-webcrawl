Spriggina

Spriggina is a genus of early bilaterian animals whose fossils are known from the Ediacaran period, roughly 555 million years ago. The sole species, Spriggina floundersi, was discovered in the Ediacara Hills of South Australia and named after Reg Sprigg, who found the Ediacaran fossils there in 1946. The fossil is the emblem of the Geological Society of Australia's South Australian division.

Description

Spriggina grew to about 3–5 centimetres in length. Its body was elongated and segmented, with a crescent-shaped "head" region bearing what may be two pairs of antennae-like structures, and a series of V-shaped segments along the body. Unlike many Ediacaran organisms, it appears to have had a distinct head and bilateral symmetry.

Classification

The affinities of Spriggina are debated. It has been interpreted as an annelid worm, an arthropod relative or trilobite ancestor, a segmented stem-group bilaterian, or a member of the extinct rangeomorph group. It is often described as one of the earliest known predators or mobile bilaterians, though no direct evidence of predation exists.
