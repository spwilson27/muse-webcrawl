Dublin

Dublin (Irish: Baile Átha Cliath) is the capital and largest city of Ireland. Situated on the east coast at the mouth of the River Liffey, it is the centre of the Greater Dublin Area, home to roughly 1.4 million people in the city and suburbs. Dublin is Ireland's political, economic, and cultural centre and one of Europe's major technology and financial hubs.

History

The name Dublin derives from the Irish Dubh Linn, "black pool," referring to a dark tidal basin where the River Poddle joined the Liffey. Vikings founded a settlement there in 841, which grew into a trading town and the centre of the Norse Kingdom of Dublin. After the Norman invasion of 1169, Dublin became the seat of English rule in Ireland, centred on Dublin Castle. The city expanded greatly in the Georgian era, when wide streets and squares such as Merrion Square were laid out, and the Guinness brewery at St. James's Gate (founded 1759) became an industrial landmark.

Dublin was the scene of the Easter Rising of 1916, much of it fought around the General Post Office on O'Connell Street, and of fighting in the Irish War of Independence and Civil War. In 1922 it became the capital of the Irish Free State, later the Republic of Ireland.

Geography and landmarks

The Liffey divides the city into the Northside and Southside, crossed by bridges including the Ha'penny Bridge and the Samuel Beckett Bridge. Landmarks include Trinity College Dublin (founded 1592), home of the Book of Kells; St Patrick's and Christ Church cathedrals; the Phoenix Park, one of Europe's largest enclosed city parks; and Temple Bar, a cultural quarter of pubs and galleries.

Culture

Dublin has an outsized literary heritage: it produced Jonathan Swift, Oscar Wilde, Bram Stoker, W. B. Yeats, James Joyce — whose novel Ulysses unfolds across the city on 16 June 1904, celebrated as Bloomsday — Samuel Beckett, and Seamus Heaney. It is a UNESCO City of Literature. The city is also known for traditional and popular music, theatre at the Abbey and Gate, and its pub culture.

Economy

Many multinational technology and pharmaceutical companies have European headquarters in Dublin, particularly around the Docklands ("Silicon Docks"), attracted by Ireland's low corporate tax rate and English-speaking workforce. Finance, education, tourism, and brewing are other major sectors.
