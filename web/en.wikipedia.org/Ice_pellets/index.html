Ice pellets

Ice pellets (Canadian English), also known as sleet in American English, are a form of precipitation consisting of small, hard, translucent balls of ice, usually 5 mm (0.2 in) or less in diameter. They differ from hail, which forms in thunderstorms, and from graupel (soft hail), which is opaque and rime-coated.

Formation

Ice pellets form when snowflakes fall through a warm layer of air and melt partially or completely, then pass through a deep sub-freezing layer near the surface and refreeze before reaching the ground. The pellets bounce on impact and often make a distinctive tapping sound. This layered temperature profile — a "warm nose" aloft over cold air at the surface — is typical ahead of winter warm fronts.

Terminology

In British English, "sleet" refers to a mixture of rain and snow, so British sources generally use "ice pellets" for the frozen form described here. In METAR weather reports, ice pellets carry the code PL. Accumulating ice pellets can make roads extremely slippery, though they do not coat objects as freezing rain does.
