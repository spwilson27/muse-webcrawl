White Castle (restaurant)

White Castle
Founded: 1921, Wichita, Kansas
Founders: Billy Ingram, Walter Anderson
Headquarters: Columbus, Ohio
Industry: Fast food
Known for: Sliders (small square hamburgers)

White Castle is an American regional fast-food restaurant chain, generally credited as the world's first fast-food hamburger chain. Founded in 1921 in Wichita, Kansas, by Billy Ingram and Walter Anderson, it is known for its small, inexpensive, square hamburgers, called "sliders". The chain is headquartered in Columbus, Ohio, and remains privately held and family-owned.

History

Walter Anderson had operated popular hamburger stands in Wichita and developed an efficient griddle method for cooking patties. Partnering with Edgar Waldo "Billy" Ingram in 1921, the founders chose white-painted porcelain-steel buildings and the name "White Castle" to convey cleanliness and purity at a time when the meat industry had a poor reputation. The company standardized its buildings, menus and operations, pioneering many practices of the modern fast-food industry, including take-out service and systematic employee training.

Menu and culture

The signature slider is a small steamed beef patty served on a bun with onions and a pickle. White Castle restaurants also sell breakfast items, chicken sandwiches and crinkle-cut fries. The chain operates a few hundred restaurants, mainly in the Midwest and the New York metropolitan area, and its frozen sliders are sold in supermarkets nationwide. The 2004 comedy film Harold & Kumar Go to White Castle featured the chain prominently.
