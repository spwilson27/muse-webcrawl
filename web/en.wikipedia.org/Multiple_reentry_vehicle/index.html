Multiple reentry vehicle

A multiple reentry vehicle (MRV) is a type of ballistic missile payload containing several warheads, each in its own reentry vehicle, which are released along the missile's trajectory toward a general target area. MRVs are distinct from the later MIRV (multiple independently targetable reentry vehicle) design, in which a "bus" manoeuvres to aim each warhead at a separate target.

Development and purpose

The first MRV was the American Polaris A-3 submarine-launched ballistic missile, deployed in 1964 with three warheads. Spreading several warheads in a pattern around a target increased the probability of destroying it, helped saturate defences, and improved coverage of large or "soft" targets. Because the warheads could not be independently aimed, their impact points formed a single footprint around one aim point.

Limitations and successors

MRVs were largely superseded by MIRVs, beginning with the Minuteman III in 1970, which gave each warhead its own target. Both technologies counted prominently in arms control: the SALT and START treaties counted warheads per missile precisely because multiple-warhead systems multiplied the destructive power of each launcher and complicated verification and strategic stability.
