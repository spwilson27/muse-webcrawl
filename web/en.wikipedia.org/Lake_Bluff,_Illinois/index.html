Lake Bluff is a village in Lake County, Illinois, United States. It is a suburb of Chicago on the western shore of Lake Michigan, located in the North Shore area. The population was 5,616 at the 2020 census.

History

The area was first settled in the 1830s; the first settler is generally reckoned to be John Swanson. In the 1870s the Lake Bluff Camp Meeting Association, affiliated with the Methodist Church, established a popular summer resort around the bluffs above the lake, with a tabernacle, hotels, and cottages. The village was incorporated in 1895. During Prohibition, Lake Bluff gained a reputation for its speakeasies and breweries.

Geography

Lake Bluff is bordered by Lake Michigan to the east, Lake Forest to the south, and Naval Station Great Lakes lies nearby to the north. The village is known for its bluff-top beach, Sunrise Beach, and its tree-lined residential streets.

Transportation

The village is served by Metra's Union Pacific North Line at Lake Bluff station and by U.S. Route 41 (Skokie Highway).
