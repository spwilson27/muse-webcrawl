Meteoroid

A meteoroid is a small rocky or metallic body in outer space, significantly smaller than an asteroid and larger than a grain of interplanetary dust. The International Astronomical Union defines meteoroids as objects roughly between 30 micrometres and one metre in diameter; smaller particles are classed as micrometeoroids or cosmic dust.

Meteors and meteorites

When a meteoroid enters Earth's atmosphere at high speed and burns up, the visible streak of light is called a meteor (a "shooting star"). If a fragment survives the passage and lands on the surface, it is called a meteorite. Exceptionally bright meteors are known as fireballs or bolides.

Origin

Most meteoroids are fragments of comets and asteroids, produced by collisions or shed as debris along a parent body's orbit; their concentrated streams give rise to meteor showers. Tens of thousands of tonnes of meteoroid material, mostly as fine dust, reach Earth each year.

Effects

Larger meteoroids can cause significant damage, as in the 2013 Chelyabinsk event, where the airburst of a ~20-metre object injured over a thousand people, mostly from broken glass.
