Sidney Gottlieb

Born: 3 August 1918, New York City
Died: 7 March 1999, Virginia
Employer: CIA
Known for: Project MKUltra

Sidney Gottlieb (3 August 1918 – 7 March 1999) was an American chemist and CIA officer who headed the agency's Technical Services Staff and directed Project MKUltra, the CIA's mind-control and interrogation research programme.

Career

Born Joseph Scheider in the Bronx, Gottlieb joined the CIA in 1951. From 1953 he ran MKUltra, authorising experiments with LSD and other drugs on unwitting subjects, funding research through front organisations, and overseeing work on poisons and assassination devices, including materials prepared against foreign leaders such as Fidel Castro and Patrice Lumumba.

Exposure and later life

Gottlieb destroyed most MKUltra files in 1973 before retiring. Congressional investigations in the 1970s exposed much of the programme; he testified under immunity and was never prosecuted. He spent his later years in Virginia, practising folk dancing, raising goats and working in a hospice.
