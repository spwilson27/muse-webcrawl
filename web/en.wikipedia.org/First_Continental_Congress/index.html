The First Continental Congress was a meeting of delegates from twelve of the Thirteen Colonies held from 5 September to 26 October 1774, at Carpenters' Hall in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. Called in response to the Intolerable Acts, which Parliament had passed as punishment for the Boston Tea Party, the Congress coordinated a colonial response to British policies and laid the groundwork for unified resistance.

Proceedings and actions

Fifty-six delegates, including George Washington, John Adams, Samuel Adams, Patrick Henry and John Jay, attended. The Congress adopted the Suffolk Resolves, issued a Declaration and Resolves asserting colonial rights — including life, liberty, property and trial by jury — and rejected a plan of union proposed by Joseph Galloway by a single vote. Its most consequential act was creating the Continental Association, an agreement to boycott British goods through a ban on imports, non-consumption, and ultimately exports, enforced by local committees of inspection in every county and town.

Petition and aftermath

The Congress also sent a Petition to the King seeking redress of grievances while professing loyalty to the Crown. Before adjourning, it resolved to reconvene in May 1775 if grievances were not redressed. Fighting at Lexington and Concord in April 1775 ensured that the body which met next — the Second Continental Congress — would manage a war effort and, the following year, declare independence.
