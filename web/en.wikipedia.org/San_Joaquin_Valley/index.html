The San Joaquin Valley is the southern portion of California's Central Valley, extending from the Sacramento–San Joaquin Delta south to the Tehachapi Mountains. Drained by the San Joaquin River, it is one of the most productive agricultural regions in the world.

Agriculture

Enabled by extensive irrigation from Sierra Nevada rivers and groundwater, the valley produces a large share of the United States' grapes, almonds, citrus, cotton, tomatoes and dairy products. Fresno County is perennially among the top agricultural counties in the nation.

Environment and health

The valley suffers from some of the worst air quality in the United States, with particulate and ozone pollution trapped by surrounding mountains, and from land subsidence caused by groundwater overdraft. The fungal disease coccidioidomycosis ("valley fever") is endemic to its soils.

Population

More than four million people live in the valley's eight counties; its cities have grown rapidly while poverty rates remain high relative to coastal California.
