Matter of Britain

The Matter of Britain is the body of medieval literature and legendary material associated with Great Britain and Brittany, centred on King Arthur, his knights, Camelot, and the quest for the Holy Grail. The term, from the poet Jean Bodel, groups it with the Matter of France (Charlemagne) and the Matter of Rome (classical antiquity).

Sources

Key texts include Geoffrey of Monmouth's Historia Regum Britanniae (c. 1136), the romances of Chrétien de Troyes, the Welsh Mabinogion, the Vulgate Cycle, and Thomas Malory's Le Morte d'Arthur (1485). Figures include Merlin, Lancelot, Guinevere, Gawain, Percival, and Mordred.

Themes

Recurring themes are chivalry and courtly love, the fellowship and fall of the Round Table, the Grail quest, and the tragic end of Arthur's kingdom. The legends draw on Welsh and Breton traditions reshaped for Norman and French courts.

Legacy

Arthurian material has been continuously retold, from Tennyson's Idylls of the King and T. H. White's The Once and Future King to modern film, fiction, and games.
