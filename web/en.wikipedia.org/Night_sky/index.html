Night sky
Related topicsAstronomy, celestial sphere, light pollution
Key phenomenaStars, Milky Way, planets, Moon, meteors, aurorae

The night sky is the appearance of the sky at night, particularly on a clear night away from light pollution, when stars, planets, the Milky Way and other celestial objects become visible after astronomical twilight. The term is usually associated with astronomy and the naked-eye observation of celestial objects and phenomena.

On a moonless night far from artificial light, an observer can see several thousand stars, the band of the Milky Way arching across the sky, faint objects such as the Andromeda Galaxy, and phenomena including meteors, satellites, zodiacal light and, at high latitudes, aurorae.

Observation and light pollution

The visibility of the night sky has been a foundation of navigation, calendars and mythology across cultures. In modern times, skyglow from artificial lighting has dramatically reduced what most people can see; the Bortle scale classifies the darkness of observing sites from bright inner cities (class 9) to pristine dark sites (class 1). Dark-sky preserves and international dark sky parks have been established to protect this natural heritage, and astronomers encourage responsible outdoor lighting.
