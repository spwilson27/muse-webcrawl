Travemünde

Travemünde is a borough of the city of Lübeck in Schleswig-Holstein, northern Germany, located at the mouth of the river Trave where it enters the Bay of Lübeck in the Baltic Sea. Founded in the 12th century, it has served for centuries as Lübeck's seaport and is today one of Germany's largest Baltic ferry ports as well as a well-known seaside resort.

History

Travemünde originated as a fortress established by Henry the Lion in 1187 where the Trave met the Baltic, guarding Lübeck's access to the sea. It belonged to Lübeck for most of its history. Bathing tourism began in 1802, making it one of Germany's oldest seaside resorts; the casino, grand hotels and the annual Travemünder Woche sailing regatta (held since 1889) shaped its resort character. During the Cold War, Travemünde lay directly on the inner German border, which ran along the Trave and the Priwall peninsula opposite.

Port and sights

The Skandinavienkai terminal handles ferry services to Sweden, Finland and (historically) other Baltic destinations, carrying millions of passengers annually. Landmarks include the Old Lighthouse of 1539 — among the oldest on the German Baltic coast — the four-masted barque Passat (1911), preserved as a museum ship on the Priwall, and the broad sandy beach and promenade. The Priwall peninsula, accessible by a small passenger ferry, has a quieter, village-like character.
