Traceability

Barcodes and lot codes enable supply-chain traceability

Traceability is the ability to trace the history, application or location of an object, product or requirement through documented, recorded identification. The term is used in several fields, each with a specialised meaning.

Supply chains and food

In supply chain management, traceability means following goods from raw materials through production, distribution and sale, so that defective or contaminated products can be identified and recalled. Food traceability systems, required by law in the European Union and elsewhere, track lots of ingredients "one step back, one step forward" through the chain. Technologies such as barcodes, RFID tags and batch records support this; standards are maintained by organisations such as GS1.

Measurement

In metrology, traceability is the property of a measurement result whereby it can be related to a reference — ultimately the SI units — through a documented unbroken chain of calibrations, each contributing to measurement uncertainty.

Engineering and software

In systems and software engineering, requirements traceability links each requirement to its source, to design elements, code and tests, typically recorded in a requirements traceability matrix. This ensures that all requirements are implemented and verified, and that changes can be assessed for impact — a practice required in regulated industries such as aerospace, automotive and medical devices.
