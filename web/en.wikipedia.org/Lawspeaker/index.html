Lawspeaker

A lawspeaker proclaiming the law at the assembly

A lawspeaker or lawman (Old Norse: lǫgsǫgumaðr; Swedish: lagman) was a Scandinavian legal office in the medieval period. The lawspeaker was responsible for memorizing and reciting the law at the assembly (thing) and for presiding over its legal proceedings.

Role

Before the law was written down, the lawspeaker was the living repository of the legal code. In Iceland, the lawspeaker (lögsögumaður) was elected for a three-year term and recited one third of the laws each year at the Lögberg (Law Rock) during the Althing. He also chaired the legislative council, the Lögrétta.

Regional variations

In Sweden, the lagman presided over the provincial assemblies and later became a royal judge and provincial governor figure. In Norway the office similarly developed into a professional judgeship. The office survived in modified form in Sweden and Finland well into the modern era, with lagman becoming the title of a senior court judge.

Notable lawspeakers

The most famous Icelandic lawspeaker is Snorri Sturluson, who held the office twice in the 13th century. The records of lawspeakers listed in Ari Þorgilsson's Íslendingabók provide an important chronological framework for early Icelandic history.
