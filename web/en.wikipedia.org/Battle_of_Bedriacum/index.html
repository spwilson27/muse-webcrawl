The Battles of Bedriacum were two engagements fought in northern Italy during the Year of the Four Emperors (AD 69), the civil war that followed the death of Nero. They are often called the First and Second Battles of Bedriacum (or of Cremona, the nearby city).

First battle (14 April 69)

After Galba's murder, Otho was emperor in Rome while Vitellius, proclaimed by the Rhine legions, advanced into Italy. The armies met near Bedriacum, close to Cremona. Otho's forces — Praetorians and legions from the Danube frontier, some still arriving — were defeated by the veteran Vitellian legions under Caecina and Valens. Rather than continue the war, Otho committed suicide, and Vitellius became emperor.

Second battle (24 October 69)

Meanwhile the eastern legions had proclaimed Vespasian emperor in July. An advance force under Antonius Primus entered Italy and, after defeating part of the Vitellian army at Cremona, fought a confused night-and-day battle near Bedriacum on 24 October. The Flavians won; the surviving Vitellians withdrew to Cremona, which was then stormed and notoriously sacked for four days by Vespasian's troops. Vitellius was killed in Rome in December, leaving Vespasian master of the empire and founder of the Flavian dynasty.

Significance

Tacitus, the principal source for both battles, treated them as emblematic of the horror of civil war. The battles are remembered as deciding the fate of the empire twice within six months on essentially the same ground.
