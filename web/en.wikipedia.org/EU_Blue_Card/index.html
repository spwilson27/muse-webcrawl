EU Blue Card

EU Blue Card
Type: Work and residence permit
Issued by: EU member states (except Denmark and Ireland)
Established: 2009 directive; implemented from 2011
Purpose: Attracting highly skilled non-EU workers

The EU Blue Card is a work and residence permit for highly qualified non-EU citizens to work in the European Union, modelled in part on the United States' Green Card. It was established by a 2009 Council directive and is issued by 25 of the 27 EU member states — Denmark and Ireland do not participate.

The EU Blue Card gives highly skilled third-country nationals a route to live and work in the EU.

Eligibility

Applicants generally need a higher-education qualification or equivalent professional experience, and a binding job offer or work contract with a salary above a threshold set by each member state (typically at least 1 to 1.5 times the national average gross salary). The card is usually valid for between one and four years and is renewable.

Rights and reform

Blue Card holders may bring family members under facilitated reunification rules and, after a period of legal residence, can move to another member state for highly qualified work and accumulate residence toward EU long-term resident status. A 2021 revision of the directive lowered salary thresholds, shortened the required contract period to six months, and made the scheme more flexible, after uptake in the original scheme had been concentrated mainly in Germany.
