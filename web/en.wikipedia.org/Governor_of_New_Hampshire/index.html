The governor of New Hampshire is the head of government of the U.S. state of New Hampshire. The governor is elected every two years, with no term limits — New Hampshire and Vermont are the only states whose governors serve two-year terms. The office is somewhat weaker than in most states because executive power is shared with a five-member elected Executive Council, which must approve major appointments, contracts, and pardons.

Powers and duties

The governor signs or vetoes bills passed by the New Hampshire General Court, commands the state's National Guard, submits the state budget, and grants pardons (with the Executive Council). Unusually, the governor's veto and appointment powers are checked by the council, a survival of colonial-era institutions.

History

After independence, the state's chief executive was styled "President of New Hampshire" until the constitution of 1784 (effective with the 1786 amendments) adopted the title of governor; Meshech Weare was the first president, and John Sullivan the first to hold the governorship under the new title. The governor's prominence is amplified by New Hampshire's traditional first-in-the-nation presidential primary.
