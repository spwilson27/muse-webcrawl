John Joseph Pershing (September 13, 1860 – July 15, 1948) was a senior United States Army officer who served as commander of the American Expeditionary Forces (AEF) in Europe during World War I. He was promoted to General of the Armies, the highest possible rank, and was mentor to the generation of generals who led the U.S. Army in World War II.

Early career

A graduate of West Point (1886), Pershing served in the Apache and Sioux campaigns, in Cuba during the Spanish–American War, in the Philippines against the Moro resistance, as an observer in the Russo-Japanese War, and in command of the 1916 Punitive Expedition into Mexico pursuing Pancho Villa.

World War I

Appointed to command the AEF in 1917, Pershing insisted that American troops fight as an independent army rather than be amalgamated into Allied units. His forces played a decisive role in the Saint-Mihiel offensive and the Meuse-Argonne offensive of 1918, the largest battle in American military history to that date.

Later life

Pershing served as Army Chief of Staff from 1921 to 1924, reorganised the army's staff system, and wrote memoirs that won the Pulitzer Prize for History. He died in 1948 and was buried at Arlington National Cemetery.
