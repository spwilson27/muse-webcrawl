BS 7671, formally titled Requirements for Electrical Installations, is the British Standard that sets out the regulations for electrical installations in the United Kingdom. Commonly known as the IET Wiring Regulations (or colloquially "the Regs"), it is published jointly by the Institution of Engineering and Technology (IET) and the British Standards Institution (BSI). While compliance with BS 7671 is not itself a statutory requirement in most circumstances, the standard is widely referenced in legislation and is the benchmark by which the safety of electrical installation work is judged.

History

The regulations trace their origin to 1882, when the Society of Telegraph Engineers and Electricians published the first set of "Rules and Regulations for the Prevention of Fire Risks Arising from Electric Lighting". Successive editions were published through the twentieth century as the Institution of Electrical Engineers (IEE) Wiring Regulations. The requirements were adopted as a British Standard, BS 7671, in 1992 with the 16th edition. The 17th edition followed in 2008, and the 18th edition was published in July 2018 and came fully into effect in 2019, with Amendment 1 in 2020 and Amendment 2 in 2022.

Structure

BS 7671 is organised into seven parts:

  1. Scope, object and fundamental principles
  2. Definitions
  3. Assessment of general characteristics
  4. Protection for safety
  5. Selection and erection of equipment
  6. Inspection and testing
  7. Special installations or locations

Later editions added chapters on subjects such as energy efficiency (Part 8, covering "prosumer" installations, introduced in the 18th edition amendment process). The standard covers installations operating at voltages up to 1,000 V AC or 1,500 V DC, and includes detailed requirements for earthing, bonding, overcurrent and fault protection, residual-current devices (RCDs), cable sizing, and special locations such as bathrooms, swimming pools and medical locations.

Relationship to law

In England and Wales, Part P of the Building Regulations makes certain domestic electrical work notifiable, and compliance with BS 7671 is the usual means of demonstrating that work meets the legal requirement of being reasonably safe. The Electricity at Work Regulations 1989 also underpin the standard's practical authority in workplaces.

International equivalents

BS 7671 is closely aligned with the IEC 60364 series of international standards for low-voltage electrical installations. Many other countries base their wiring rules on the same IEC framework, though practices differ from the North American National Electrical Code (NEC).
