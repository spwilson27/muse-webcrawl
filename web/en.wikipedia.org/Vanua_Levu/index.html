Vanua Levu

Location: Pacific Ocean
Archipelago: Vanua Levu Group
Area: 5,587 km²
Highest point: Mount Batini (1,111 m)
Country: Fiji
Largest settlement: Labasa

Vanua Levu (pronounced [βaˈnua ˈleβu], formerly known as Sandalwood Island) is the second largest island of Fiji. Situated 64 kilometres to the north of the larger Viti Levu, it covers an area of about 5,587 square kilometres and has a population of roughly 136,000, concentrated mainly in the towns of Labasa in the north and Savusavu in the south.

Geography

The island is roughly triangular, about 180 km long and 30–50 km wide, and is bisected by a rugged mountain range that divides the wet southeastern coast from the drier northern side. The Wainunu, Labasa and other rivers drain its interior. Coral reefs, including the Great Sea Reef (Cakaulevu Reef) off the northern coast, fringe the island.

History and economy

Dutch explorer Abel Tasman sighted the island in 1643. In the early 19th century it was a centre of the sandalwood trade, and later of copra and sugar. Sugar cane farming, centred on Labasa, remains the mainstay of the northern economy, while Savusavu is a centre for tourism, tuna fishing and copra. The island is administered as part of Fiji's Northern Division.
