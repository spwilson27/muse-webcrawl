Paleoconservatism

IdeologyConservatism, nationalism, traditionalism
Key figuresPat Buchanan, Samuel T. Francis, Paul Gottfried, Russell Kirk
PositionsNon-interventionism, restrictionism, protectionism

Paleoconservatism is a political philosophy and a variety of American conservatism that stresses traditionalism, social stability, and national identity. Paleoconservatives generally advocate restrictions on immigration, non-interventionist foreign policy, economic nationalism and protectionism, and a return to traditional social values, and they oppose what they see as the managerial state and the neoconservative influence on the Republican Party.

History

The term "paleoconservative" (from the Greek paleo, "old") was coined in the 1980s, and popularised by the political scientist Paul Gottfried, to distinguish the older conservative tradition associated with the "Old Right" from neoconservatism. The movement found a political champion in Pat Buchanan, whose presidential campaigns in 1992, 1996, and 2000 emphasised trade protectionism, immigration restriction, and opposition to foreign interventions. Its principal journals included Chronicles magazine, associated with the Rockford Institute.

Relations with other movements

Paleoconservatives were central to the "paleo wars" of the late 20th century within American conservatism, clashing with neoconservatives over foreign policy and the legacy of the New Deal. Elements of paleoconservatism later influenced the national conservatism and populist currents of the 2010s.
