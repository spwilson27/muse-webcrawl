The Battle of Smolensk was a large-scale battle of the Eastern Front in the Second World War, fought from July to September 1941 around the city of Smolensk in the western Soviet Union, roughly 400 kilometres west of Moscow. It formed a major stage of Operation Barbarossa, the German invasion of the Soviet Union.

Background

After encircling large Soviet forces at Białystok and Minsk, the German Army Group Centre advanced toward Smolensk, which guarded the road to Moscow. Soviet forces under Marshal Semyon Timoshenko prepared defences along the Western Dvina and Dnieper rivers.

The battle

German panzer groups under Heinz Guderian and Hermann Hoth crossed the Dnieper and closed an encirclement around Smolensk in late July, trapping elements of three Soviet armies. Nevertheless, determined Soviet counterattacks — including operations toward Yelnya, where the Red Army mounted one of its first successful offensives of the war — repeatedly struck the German flanks, and many encircled Soviet troops escaped eastward. Smolensk itself fell on July 16, but the fighting continued for weeks.

Aftermath

Although a German victory, the battle cost Army Group Centre roughly two months and heavy casualties, delaying the drive on Moscow into the autumn mud and winter. The delay around Smolensk is frequently cited as one of the factors that caused Operation Barbarossa to fall behind its timetable.
