Robert Barclay

Born23 December 1648, Gordonstoun, Scotland
Died3 October 1690 (aged 41), Ury House, Aberdeenshire
OccupationQuaker theologian and writer

Robert Barclay (23 December 1648 – 3 October 1690) was a Scottish Quaker, the most eminent theologian among the early Friends and the author of the Apology for the True Christian Divinity (1676, in Latin; English edition 1678), the first systematic statement of Quaker theology. Educated at the Scots College in Paris, he converted to Quakerism in 1666–67, influenced by his father David Barclay's conversion, and was imprisoned several times for his faith.

The Apology

Barclay's Apology, addressed to Charles II, sets out fifteen propositions covering revelation, the inward Light of Christ, scripture, the fall and redemption, ministry, worship, baptism, communion, and the Friends' refusal of oaths, war and outward honours. It became the standard theological work of the Society of Friends and was translated into many European languages. His other works include a catechism (1673) and treatises defending Quaker practice.

Later life

Barclay enjoyed unusual favour at the court of James II, who in 1681 granted him the governorship of the proprietary colony of East Jersey in America; Barclay governed through deputies and never visited the colony. He died at Ury House in 1690 after falling ill at a religious meeting.
