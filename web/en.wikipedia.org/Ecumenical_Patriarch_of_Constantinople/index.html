The Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople is the Archbishop of Constantinople and primus inter pares (first among equals) among the heads of the several autocephalous churches that compose the Eastern Orthodox Church. He is regarded as the spiritual leader of the world's Orthodox Christians and as the direct representative and spiritual leader of Orthodox believers worldwide.

Role and status

The Ecumenical Patriarch is not considered to have direct jurisdiction over the other patriarchs or autocephalous churches, but his unique position often gives him the role of chief spokesman for the Orthodox Church. The title "Ecumenical" dates to the sixth century and has periodically been contested, including by the Turkish government, which officially treats the Patriarch as the religious leader of Turkey's Greek Orthodox minority.

History

The see traces its foundation to Andrew the Apostle. Constantinople's bishop gained prominence as the city became capital of the Byzantine Empire; the Council of Chalcedon (451) granted its archbishop privileges equal to Rome's. After the Ottoman conquest of 1453, the Patriarch became the ethnarch of the Orthodox millet. The Patriarchate's seat has been at the Church of St George in the Phanar district since 1601.

Bartholomew I

Bartholomew I, elected in 1991, is the longest-serving Ecumenical Patriarch in the modern era and is known as the "Green Patriarch" for his environmental advocacy. In 2019 he granted autocephaly to the Orthodox Church of Ukraine, a decision contested by the Moscow Patriarchate.
