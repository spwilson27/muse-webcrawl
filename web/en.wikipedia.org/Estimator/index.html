Estimator

In statistics, an estimator is a rule for calculating an estimate of a given quantity based on observed data. The quantity of interest is the estimand, the value produced for a particular sample is the estimate, and the estimator is the function of the data.

Properties

Estimators are evaluated by properties such as unbiasedness (expected value equal to the true parameter), consistency (convergence to the true value as sample size grows), efficiency (low variance, cf. the Cramér–Rao bound), and robustness. Bias and variance together determine the mean squared error.

Methods

Common methods of constructing estimators include maximum likelihood estimation, the method of moments, least squares, and Bayesian estimation.
