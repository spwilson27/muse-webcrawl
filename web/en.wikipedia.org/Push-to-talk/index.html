Push-to-talk

Push-to-talk (PTT), also known as press-to-transmit, is a method of having conversations on half-duplex communication lines, including two-way radio, in which the user presses and holds a button to switch from receive mode to transmit mode, releasing it to listen.

Use in radio

PTT is standard on walkie-talkies, amateur radio, and professional land mobile radio used by police, fire, aviation, and military services. Because only one party can transmit effectively at a time, radio procedure uses conventions such as "over" to signal the end of a transmission. Momentary PTT switches are built into handsets and microphones, and may also be operated by foot pedals or headset controls.

Push-to-talk over cellular

From the early 2000s, mobile carriers offered push-to-talk over cellular (PoC), emulating walkie-talkie behaviour on mobile phones — most famously the iDEN service popularised by Nextel with its characteristic chirp. Modern PoC systems run over LTE and 5G networks and are marketed for public safety and enterprise use, while consumer voice-chat apps also use PTT modes for voice messaging and in-game communication.
