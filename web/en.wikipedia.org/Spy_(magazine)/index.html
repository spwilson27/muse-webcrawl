Spy (magazine)

Spy was an American satirical monthly magazine published from 1986 to 1998. Founded by Kurt Andersen and E. Graydon Carter, who served as its first editors, along with entrepreneur Thomas L. Phillips Jr., Spy was an influential New York–based magazine known for its irreverent humour, meticulous research, and mocking coverage of the media, politics, and celebrity culture. It is also remembered as a pioneer of the snarky tone later widespread in media and on the internet.

Content and style

Spy specialised in prankish investigative humour: features such as "Separated at Birth?" (lookalike photographs), "The Spy List", "Naked City" gossip, elaborate stunts on public figures, and the annual "Irony Epidemic" coverage. Its most famous recurring target was real-estate developer (later U.S. president) Donald Trump, whom the magazine branded a "short-fingered vulgarian" — a jab based on a claim about the size of his hands that reportedly provoked irate responses from Trump for years. The magazine's contributors included many writers who went on to prominent careers in journalism, literature and screenwriting.

History

The magazine was launched in October 1986 and published by Spy Publishing Partners. Financial difficulties and changes of ownership through the 1990s led to its decline; it was relaunched under new ownership in the mid-1990s but ceased publication in 1998. A large archive of its issues has been made available online by Google Books, and retrospectives credit Spy with shaping the voice of later outlets such as Gawker and modern satirical media.
