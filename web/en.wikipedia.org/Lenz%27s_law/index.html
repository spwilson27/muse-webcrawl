Lenz's law

Named after: Emil Lenz
Formulated: 1834
Field: Electromagnetism

Lenz's law, formulated by the Baltic German physicist Emil Lenz in 1834, states that the direction of an induced electromotive force (emf) and the resulting current in a closed loop is such that it opposes the change in magnetic flux that produced it.

Relation to Faraday's law

Lenz's law supplies the negative sign in Faraday's law of induction: emf = −dΦ/dt. The opposition it describes is a consequence of the conservation of energy — if the induced current aided the change, a perpetual-motion feedback would result.

Examples

When a magnet is pushed toward a coil, the induced current creates a field repelling the magnet; when the magnet is withdrawn, the field attracts it. Lenz's law also explains eddy-current braking and the behaviour of inductors in circuits.
