Niklaus Wirth

Niklaus Emil Wirth (15 February 1934 – 1 January 2024) was a Swiss computer scientist who designed several influential programming languages, most famously Pascal, and won the Turing Award in 1984 for developing a sequence of innovative computer languages.

Work

Wirth created Euler, PL360, Algol W, Pascal (1970), Modula, Modula-2 and the Oberon language and operating system. Pascal became the dominant teaching language of structured programming; his book Algorithms + Data Structures = Programs (1976) is a classic. At ETH Zurich he also led the Lilith workstation project. "Wirth's law" — the observation that software gets slower faster than hardware gets faster — is attributed to him.

Philosophy

Wirth championed simplicity, elegance and lean design in languages and systems, arguing for step-wise refinement and against feature bloat throughout his career.
