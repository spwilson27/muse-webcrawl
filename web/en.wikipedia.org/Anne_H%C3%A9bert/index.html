Anne Hébert CC OQ (1 August 1916 – 22 January 2000) was a Canadian author and poet from Quebec, widely regarded as one of the most important French-language writers of 20th-century Canada. She wrote novels, poetry, plays and screenplays, often exploring memory, childhood, violence and the constraints placed on women in traditional Quebec society.

Life and career

Hebert was born in Sainte-Catherine-de-la-Jacques-Cartier (then Sainte-Catherine-de-Fossambault) near Quebec City. Her cousin was the poet Hector de Saint-Denys Garneau, whose early death marked her youth. She published her first poetry collection, Les Songes en equilibre, in 1942, and the short-story collection Le Torrent in 1950. Her first novel, Les Chambres de bois (1958), was a landmark of modern Quebec fiction.

Major works

From 1954 she lived for long periods in Paris, while continuing to publish in Quebec. Her novel Kamouraska (1970), a tale of passion and murder based on an 1839 crime, won the Prix des libraires and was adapted into a film by Claude Jutra in 1973. Other major works include the poetry collection Le Tombeau des rois (1953), and the novels Heloise (1980), Les Fous de Bassan (1982) and L'Enfant charge de songes (1992).

Honours

Hebert was made a Companion of the Order of Canada and an Officer of the National Order of Quebec. Quebec's Prix Anne-Hebert, awarded for a first novel, is named in her honour.
