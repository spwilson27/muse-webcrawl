Oculomotor nerve

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The oculomotor nerve, also known as the third cranial nerve (CN III), is a cranial nerve that enters the orbit via the superior orbital fissure and innervates extraocular muscles that enable most movements of the eye, as well as muscles that constrict the pupil and control lens accommodation.

Structure and function

The nerve arises from the oculomotor nucleus complex in the midbrain at the level of the superior colliculus. It carries two components: somatic motor fibres supplying the levator palpebrae superioris, superior rectus, medial rectus, inferior rectus and inferior oblique muscles; and visceral motor (parasympathetic) fibres from the Edinger–Westphal nucleus, which synapse in the ciliary ganglion and supply the sphincter pupillae and ciliary muscles. The nerve passes through the cavernous sinus and divides into superior and inferior divisions before entering the orbit.

Clinical significance

Damage to CN III causes oculomotor nerve palsy: the eye rests "down and out", with ptosis (drooping eyelid), a dilated pupil unreactive to light, and double vision. Causes include posterior communicating artery aneurysm (classically painful, pupil-involving), diabetes and other microvascular disease (often pupil-sparing), tumours, trauma and uncal herniation, in which a blown pupil is a neurosurgical emergency.
