The Duryea Motor Wagon Company, established in 1895 in Springfield, Massachusetts, by brothers Charles Edgar Duryea and James Frank Duryea, was the first American company formed for the serial manufacture of gasoline-powered automobiles. Its founding followed the brothers' successful tests of a single-cylinder gasoline "motor wagon" in 1893.

History

Frank Duryea drove the company's car to victory in the Chicago Times-Herald race of November 1895 — America's first automobile race — covering the snowy 54-mile course at an average of about five miles per hour and securing orders and publicity. In 1896 the company produced thirteen identical motor wagons, making them, by many accounts, the first automobiles built in quantity for sale in the United States.

Decline

Disagreements between the brothers and their backers, mounting costs and a suit over the Selden patent litigation climate undermined the venture; the Springfield company failed in 1898. Charles and Frank went their separate ways, each continuing to build cars under the Duryea name in other firms, and arguing for decades over which brother deserved primary credit for their first car.
