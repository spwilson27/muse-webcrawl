Telugu cinema

Telugu cinema, also known as Tollywood, is the segment of Indian cinema dedicated to motion pictures in the Telugu language, widely produced across the Telugu-speaking states of Andhra Pradesh and Telangana. Centred in the Film Nagar district of Hyderabad, it is one of the largest film industries in India and the world by output and box office, and frequently rivals or exceeds Hindi cinema (Bollywood) in annual revenue.

History

Raghupathi Venkaiah Naidu, regarded as the father of Telugu cinema, built studios and theatres in Madras in the 1910s; the first Telugu silent feature, Bhishma Pratigna, appeared in 1921, and H. M. Reddy directed the first Telugu talkie, Bhakta Prahlada (1932). The industry shifted from Madras to Hyderabad after Andhra Pradesh's formation, flourishing in the 1950s–70s with stars N. T. Rama Rao and Akkineni Nageswara Rao and classics such as Mayabazar (1957), often voted the greatest Indian film in polls. N. T. Rama Rao parlayed screen fame into politics, founding the Telugu Desam Party and becoming chief minister.

Global breakthrough

S. S. Rajamouli's two-part epic Baahubali (2015, 2017) made Telugu cinema a pan-Indian and international phenomenon; Baahubali 2: The Conclusion became the highest-grossing Indian film of its time worldwide. His RRR (2022) won the Academy Award for Best Original Song ("Naatu Naatu") and a Golden Globe, cementing Tollywood's global profile. Contemporary stars include Prabhas, Allu Arjun (whose Pushpa films became pan-Indian hits), Ram Charan, N. T. Rama Rao Jr., and Mahesh Babu, and the industry is known for big-budget action spectacles, mythologicals, and family dramas, with extensive dubbing and remake traffic across Indian languages.
