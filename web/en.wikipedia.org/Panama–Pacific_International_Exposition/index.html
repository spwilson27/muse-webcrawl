Panama–Pacific International Exposition

The Panama–Pacific International Exposition was a world's fair held in San Francisco, California, in 1915, celebrating both the completion of the Panama Canal and the city's recovery from the 1906 earthquake.

Site and buildings

The fair occupied about 635 acres along the northern waterfront in what is now the Marina District. Its principal surviving structure is the Palace of Fine Arts, designed by Bernard Maybeck, with its iconic rotunda and lagoon.

Exhibits

Exhibits showcased the latest technology of the era, including a transcontinental telephone call demonstration and the Liberty Bell, which toured the country en route to the fair.
