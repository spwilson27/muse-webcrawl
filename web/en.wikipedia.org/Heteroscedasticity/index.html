Heteroscedasticity

Residuals whose variance grows with the fitted value — a classic sign of heteroscedasticity

In statistics, a collection of random variables is heteroscedastic (or heteroskedastic) if the variability — measured by standard deviation or variance — differs across the variables or across values of an explanatory variable. The opposite condition, constant variability, is homoscedasticity. The word derives from Greek hetero ("different") and skedasis ("dispersion").

In regression analysis

Ordinary least squares (OLS) regression assumes homoscedastic errors. When heteroscedasticity is present, OLS coefficient estimates remain unbiased and consistent, but they are no longer efficient, and the usual estimates of standard errors are biased, invalidating t-tests, confidence intervals and F-tests. A common pattern is residual variance increasing with the size of the fitted value, as when the dispersion of household expenditure grows with income.

Detection and remedies

Heteroscedasticity can be detected graphically from residual plots or by formal tests such as the Breusch–Pagan test, the White test and the Goldfeld–Quandt test. Remedies include using heteroscedasticity-consistent ("robust") standard errors, weighted least squares, transforming the dependent variable (for example by taking logarithms), or modelling the variance explicitly with generalised least squares or ARCH/GARCH models in time series econometrics.
