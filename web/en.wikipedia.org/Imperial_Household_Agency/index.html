Imperial Household Agency

The Imperial Household Agency (宮内庁, Kunai-chō) is the Japanese government agency responsible for the affairs of the Imperial House of Japan, including the Emperor's official duties, imperial ceremonies, and the custody of the Imperial Regalia and properties such as the imperial palaces and the Privy Seal and State Seal of Japan.

History

The agency traces its origins to the eighth-century Ministry of the Imperial Household under the ritsuryō system. In its modern form it was organized as the Imperial Household Ministry in the Meiji period, becoming an agency under the Prime Minister's Office in 1949. Its headquarters stand on the grounds of the Tokyo Imperial Palace.

Organization and functions

Headed by a Grand Steward, the agency employs roughly a thousand staff, including the Board of Chamberlains who attend the Emperor personally, the Board of Ceremonies, the Archives and Mausolea Department, and offices managing imperial residences, the imperial stables, and the duck-hunting preserves. It also supervises the musical department (gagaku performances) and the imperial mausolea and tombs across Japan.

Criticism

The agency has sometimes been criticized for being insular and controlling, particularly regarding access to imperial tombs for archaeological research and its management of press access to members of the imperial family.
