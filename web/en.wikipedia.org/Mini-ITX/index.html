Mini-ITX

Mini-ITX is a low-power motherboard form factor developed by VIA Technologies in 2001. Measuring 17 × 17 cm (6.7 × 6.7 inches), it is considerably smaller than the desktop ATX standard while retaining compatibility with ATX mounting holes, power supplies, and back panel I/O arrangements, allowing Mini-ITX boards to fit in many ATX and micro-ATX cases as well as compact dedicated enclosures.

The first Mini-ITX boards used VIA's low-power C3 and Eden processors, soldered to the board and designed for embedded and fanless applications such as thin clients, kiosks, and set-top boxes. The format later became popular with enthusiasts for home theater PCs, small home servers, and compact gaming builds; modern boards carry standard CPU sockets, one PCI Express x16 slot, and two memory slots, with power consumption far below full-size desktops.

Related form factors

Mini-ITX is part of VIA's ITX family, which also includes the smaller Nano-ITX (12 × 12 cm) and Pico-ITX (10 × 7.2 cm). Intel and AMD board partners produce Mini-ITX motherboards for mainstream desktop processors, and the standard DC-input variants are common in industrial computing.
