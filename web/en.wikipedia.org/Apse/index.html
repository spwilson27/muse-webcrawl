Apse

Apse
TypeArchitectural element
Found inChurches, basilicas, Roman architecture

An apse is a semicircular or polygonal recess covered with a hemispherical vault or semi-dome, found in Roman and Christian architecture. In a church, the apse is typically located at the eastern end of the chancel, behind the altar. The term derives from the Latin apsis, meaning an arch or vault.

History

Apses appeared in Roman basilicas, baths, and audience halls, where the magistrate's seat was placed in the recess. Early Christian builders adopted the form for basilican churches, positioning the altar and clergy seats within it. In Byzantine and Romanesque architecture the apse became a dominant feature of the church plan, often flanked by smaller subsidiary apses, and in Gothic cathedrals it was incorporated into the ambulatory and chevet of radiating chapels.

Related forms

An apsidal chancel has a curved termination; an apsidiole is a small secondary apse. In astronomy and orbital mechanics, "apsis" (plural apsides) denotes the points of greatest or least distance in an orbit, giving terms such as periapsis and apoapsis.
