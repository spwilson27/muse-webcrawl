Nandalal Bose

Born3 December 1882, Haveli Kharagpur, Bihar, British India
Died16 April 1966 (aged 83), Calcutta, India
MovementBengal School of Art; Contextual Modernism
AwardsPadma Vibhushan (1954)

Nandalal Bose (3 December 1882 – 16 April 1966) was one of the pioneers of modern Indian art and a key figure of Contextual Modernism. A pupil of Abanindranath Tagore, he became a leading artist of the Bengal School and later directed the art school Kala Bhavana at Santiniketan, founded by Rabindranath Tagore.

Life and work

Bose studied at the Government School of Art in Calcutta and worked with Abanindranath Tagore on reviving Indian artistic traditions against colonial academic art. In 1909–1910 he accompanied Lady Herringham to copy the Ajanta cave murals. His celebrated linocut of Mahatma Gandhi walking during the Dandi March (1930) became an iconic image of the Indian independence movement.

Major commissions

At Gandhi's request, Bose designed the decorations for several Congress Party sessions. After independence, he led the team of artists who illuminated the original manuscript of the Constitution of India with illustrations drawn from Indian history. In 1954 he was among the first recipients of the Padma Vibhushan, India's second-highest civilian honour, and in 1956 he became the first artist fellow of the Lalit Kala Akademi.

Style and legacy

Bose's work combined Indian mural and miniature traditions with influences from Japanese wash painting and folk art. As a teacher at Santiniketan, he influenced generations of Indian artists, and his emphasis on craft, nature study, and Indian themes remains central to the Santiniketan style.
