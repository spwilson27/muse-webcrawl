Women's health

Women's health differs from men's health in many unique ways and refers to the health of women, which focuses on the treatment and diagnosis of diseases and conditions that affect a woman's physical and emotional well-being. It is an example of population health.

Scope

The field covers reproductive health — including menstruation, contraception, pregnancy, childbirth, and menopause — as well as conditions that affect women disproportionately or differently, such as breast and cervical cancer, osteoporosis, autoimmune diseases, and cardiovascular disease, which presents differently in women and was historically under-researched.

Global issues

Globally, maternal mortality, access to contraception, and gender-based violence remain major concerns, particularly in low-income countries. The World Health Organization identifies cervical cancer screening and HPV vaccination as key preventive measures. Historically, women were excluded from many clinical trials, a gap addressed by legislation such as the US NIH Revitalization Act of 1993.
