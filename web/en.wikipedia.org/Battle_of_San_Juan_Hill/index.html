The Battle of San Juan Hill, also known as the Battle for the San Juan Heights, was a major battle of the Spanish–American War fought on 1 July 1898 near Santiago de Cuba. American forces stormed the Spanish-held heights east of the city, the most famous action of the war's land campaign.

Background

Major General William Shafter's Fifth Corps landed in Cuba in June 1898 to capture Santiago and force the Spanish Caribbean squadron out of its harbour. The San Juan Heights — San Juan Hill and the smaller Kettle Hill — blocked the road to the city, defended by some 1,700 Spanish troops with modern Mauser rifles and artillery.

The battle

After a bloody preliminary fight at El Caney, American infantry advanced under heavy, accurate fire across the San Juan River valley — dubbed "Hell's Pocket" — supported by Gatling guns under Lieutenant John Parker, whose rapid fire helped suppress the Spanish trenches. In the best-known episode, Lieutenant Colonel Theodore Roosevelt led his volunteer regiment, the Rough Riders, together with regulars including African American Buffalo Soldiers of the 9th and 10th Cavalry, up Kettle Hill and then across to San Juan Hill. By nightfall the heights were in American hands, at a cost of over 1,000 U.S. casualties.

Aftermath

From the heights, American guns commanded Santiago. On 3 July the Spanish squadron was destroyed attempting to escape at the Battle of Santiago de Cuba, and the city surrendered on 17 July. The charge up San Juan Hill made Roosevelt a national hero and propelled him toward the governorship of New York and the Vice Presidency; modern accounts stress the combined work of regulars, volunteers and the Gatling Gun Detachment, and the essential role of Black regular regiments in the victory.
