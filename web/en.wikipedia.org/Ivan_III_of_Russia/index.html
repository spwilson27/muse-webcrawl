Ivan III of Russia

Ivan III Vasilyevich (Russian: Иван III Васильевич; 22 January 1440 – 27 October 1505), also known as Ivan the Great, was Grand Prince of Moscow and all Russia from 1462 until his death in 1505. He is credited with ending the dominance of the Tatars over Russia, renovating the Moscow Kremlin, and laying the foundations of the Russian state.

Reign

Ivan succeeded his father Vasily II in 1462. During his long reign he more than tripled the territory of his state, annexing the Novgorod Republic in 1478 and the Principality of Tver in 1485. In 1480, at the Great Stand on the Ugra River, his forces faced the army of the Great Horde under Akhmat Khan; the Horde retreated without battle, an event traditionally taken to mark the end of the "Mongol yoke".

Consolidation of power

Ivan subordinated the appanage princes to Moscow, compiled the Sudebnik of 1497, the first Russian code of laws since the Russkaya Pravda, and promoted the idea of Moscow as the "Third Rome" after his marriage to Sophia Palaiologina, niece of the last Byzantine emperor. He adopted the double-headed eagle as a state emblem and was the first Russian ruler to style himself "Tsar" in diplomatic correspondence, though he was never formally crowned with that title.

Building programme

Ivan invited Italian architects, including Aristotele Fioravanti, to rebuild the Kremlin in stone. The Dormition Cathedral, the Palace of Facets, and the Kremlin walls and towers date from his reign.

Death and succession

Ivan died in 1505 and was succeeded by his son Vasili III.
