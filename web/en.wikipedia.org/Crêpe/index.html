Crêpe

A crêpe or crepe is a type of very thin pancake. Crêpes originated in Brittany, a region in western France, during the 13th century, and are now consumed worldwide. Crêpes are usually one of two varieties: sweet crêpes (crêpes sucrées) made with wheat flour, or savoury galettes (crêpes salées) made with buckwheat flour.

Preparation

Crêpe batter is made from flour, eggs, milk, butter, and a pinch of salt, and is typically rested before cooking. A small amount of batter is spread thinly over a hot, lightly greased pan or a flat crêpe maker, cooked briefly on each side, and served with fillings such as sugar, jam, chocolate spread, cheese, or ham.

Varieties

Famous preparations include the crêpe Suzette, flambéed with orange butter and liqueur, and the Breton galette complète with egg, ham, and cheese. In France, crêpes are traditionally eaten on Candlemas (La Chandeleur, 2 February), and crêperies are common throughout the country.
