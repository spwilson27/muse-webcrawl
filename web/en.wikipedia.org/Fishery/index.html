Fishery

A fishery is an entity engaged in raising or harvesting fish, or, more generally, the activity of catching fish and other aquatic organisms, the places where it is carried out, and the stock of organisms harvested. Fisheries are usually defined in terms of the people involved, the species or type of fish, the area of water or seabed, the method of fishing, the class of boats, and the purpose of the activity. The term can refer to capture fisheries (wild-caught) as distinct from aquaculture, or fish farming.

Scale and importance

According to the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations, world fisheries and aquaculture production has exceeded 170 million tonnes in recent years, with aquaculture supplying roughly half of the fish consumed by humans. Capture fisheries employ tens of millions of people worldwide, the great majority in small-scale and artisanal operations, and fish provide a significant share of animal protein for billions of people, particularly in coastal developing countries. The largest capture fisheries include the anchoveta fishery off Peru, Alaska pollock, skipjack tuna, and the fisheries of the Northwest Pacific.

Types and methods

Fisheries are classified by environment (marine, freshwater, brackish), target (finfish, crustaceans, molluscs), and gear. Common commercial methods include trawling, purse seining, longlining, gillnetting, dredging, trapping, and hand gathering. Migratory species such as tunas are managed by regional fisheries management organisations because their ranges cross national jurisdictions.

Management and sustainability

Because wild fish stocks are a common-pool resource, unmanaged fisheries tend toward overfishing — the classic "tragedy of the commons." The collapse of the Newfoundland cod fishery in 1992, which led to a moratorium and the loss of tens of thousands of jobs, is a widely cited warning. Management tools include total allowable catches, individual transferable quotas, closed seasons and areas, gear restrictions, and marine protected areas. The FAO estimates that roughly a third of assessed marine fish stocks are fished at biologically unsustainable levels. Illegal, unreported and unregulated fishing, bycatch of non-target species, and habitat damage from bottom trawling remain major concerns, while certification schemes such as the Marine Stewardship Council aim to reward sustainable practice.
