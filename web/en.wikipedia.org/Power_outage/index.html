Power outage

A power outage, also called a power cut, blackout, or power failure, is the loss of the electrical power network supply to an end user. There are many causes of power failures in an electricity network. Examples of these causes include faults at power stations, damage to electric transmission lines, substations or other parts of the distribution system, a short circuit, cascading failure, fuse or circuit breaker operation.

Types

Power outages are categorized in relation to the duration and effect of the outage. A dropout is a momentary loss (a few seconds). A brownout is a drop in voltage in an electrical power supply, which can cause lights to dim and sensitive equipment to malfunction; the name comes from the noticeable dimming of incandescent lighting. A blackout is the total loss of power to an area and is the most severe form of power outage; blackouts that affect large areas or persist for long periods are difficult to recover from quickly. A rolling blackout is an intentionally applied electrical power shutdown, implemented during periods when the demand for electricity exceeds supply, to prevent uncontrolled total failure of the grid.

Causes and notable outages

Causes include severe weather (storms, ice, heat waves), equipment failure, vegetation contacting lines, animal interference, human error, and cascading failures in which the failure of one component overloads others. Notable events include the Northeast blackout of 2003, which affected some 55 million people in the United States and Canada; the 2011 Southwest blackout; the 2019 events in Argentina and Venezuela; and the February 2021 Texas power crisis, in which millions lost power during a winter storm. In 2012, India's grid failures left over 600 million people without power, among the largest outages in history.

Effects and protection

Outages disrupt transportation, water supply, hospitals, communications, and industry, and can pose life-safety risks during extreme temperatures. Critical facilities use uninterruptible power supplies and backup generators. Utilities measure reliability with indices such as SAIDI and SAIFI, and grid operators employ protection schemes, load shedding, and black start procedures to restore service after a collapse.
