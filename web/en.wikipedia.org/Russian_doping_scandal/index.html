Russian doping scandal

PeriodRevelations from 2014; manipulation documented c. 2011–2015
Key reportsWADA Independent Commission (2015); McLaren Report (2016)
WhistleblowersYuliya Stepanova, Vitaly Stepanov, Grigory Rodchenkov
ConsequencesRUSADA non-compliance; bans and neutral participation at Olympic Games

The Russian doping scandal concerns a state-sponsored doping programme in Russia, exposed from 2014 onwards, in which Russian officials and laboratory staff systematically supplied performance-enhancing drugs to athletes, falsified test results and swapped urine samples — most notoriously at the 2014 Sochi Winter Olympics.

Revelations

In December 2014, a German ARD documentary, Top Secret Doping: How Russia makes its Winners, presented evidence from athletes including Yuliya Stepanova. WADA's Independent Commission, chaired by Dick Pound, reported in November 2015 on widespread doping and corruption in Russian athletics; the IAAF suspended the All-Russia Athletic Federation and RUSADA was declared non-compliant.

McLaren Report and Sochi sample swapping

In 2016, former Moscow laboratory director Grigory Rodchenkov described a scheme in which "dirty" urine samples from Russian athletes at Sochi were passed through a hole in the laboratory wall and replaced with clean stored samples, with the help of state security officers. The two-part McLaren Report concluded that the manipulation was directed by the Ministry of Sport and involved more than 1,000 athletes across many sports between 2011 and 2015.

Sanctions

Russian track and field athletes were barred from the Rio 2016 Olympics, and Russia competed at Pyeongchang 2018, Tokyo 2020 and Beijing 2022 without its flag, anthem or team name (as "Olympic Athletes from Russia" and later "ROC"). In 2019 WADA imposed a four-year package of sanctions, halved to two years by the Court of Arbitration for Sport in 2020. Russia has consistently denied operating a state-sponsored programme, characterising the findings as politically motivated.
