Constantin Brâncoveanu

Reign1688–1714 (Prince of Wallachia)
Born1654
Died15 August 1714, Constantinople
Venerated inEastern Orthodox Church (martyr and saint)

Constantin Brâncoveanu (1654 – 15 August 1714) was Prince of Wallachia between 1688 and 1714. His reign was a period of cultural flourishing and political balancing between the Ottoman Empire, the Habsburgs and Russia, and he is venerated as a martyr and saint by the Romanian Orthodox Church.

Reign

Brâncoveanu succeeded his uncle Șerban Cantacuzino in 1688. A wealthy and cultivated ruler, he maintained Wallachia's tributary status under the Ottomans while secretly negotiating with Austria and Russia. His long tenure was unusual for the period and allowed sustained patronage of the arts and learning.

Brâncovenesc style

His court gave its name to the Brâncovenesc style of Romanian architecture, synthesising Ottoman, Venetian and local elements in buildings such as the Mogoșoaia Palace, Horezu Monastery and Bucharest's churches. He founded schools and printing presses, supported scholars such as Antim the Iberian, and issued books in Romanian, Greek and other languages.

Downfall and death

Suspected of treasonable dealings, Brâncoveanu was deposed in 1714, taken to Constantinople and, after torture, executed on 15 August 1714 together with his four sons and his counsellor Ianache Văcărescu, refusing offers of life in exchange for conversion to Islam. The family was canonised by the Romanian Orthodox Church in 1992.
