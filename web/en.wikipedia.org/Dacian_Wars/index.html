The Dacian Wars (101–102 and 105–106 AD) were two military campaigns fought between the Roman Empire and Dacia during the reign of the Roman emperor Trajan. Their outcome was the conquest of the Dacian kingdom under Decebalus and the annexation of much of its territory as the Roman province of Dacia.

Background

Under Decebalus, Dacia had become a powerful and wealthy kingdom — rich in gold and silver mines — that repeatedly threatened Rome's Danube frontier. Domitian's earlier wars had ended in a negotiated peace in 89 AD that left Decebalus effectively subsidised by Rome, an arrangement Trajan was determined to end.

The wars

In the first campaign (101–102), Trajan advanced into Dacia, defeated the Dacians at Tapae, and imposed a harsh peace. After Decebalus rebuilt his forces and attacked Roman garrisons, Trajan returned in 105. Roman forces, crossing the Danube on Apollodorus of Damascus's great bridge, besieged and destroyed the capital, Sarmizegetusa, in 106; Decebalus took his own life to avoid capture.

Legacy

The conquest brought Rome vast treasure, celebrated in Trajan's Forum and on the spiral reliefs of Trajan's Column in Rome, the principal visual source for the wars. Roman colonisation reshaped the region; the modern Romanian language descends from the Latin spoken in the province.
