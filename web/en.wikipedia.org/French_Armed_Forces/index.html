French Armed Forces

The French Armed Forces (French: Forces armées françaises) encompass the Army, the Navy, the Air and Space Force, and the National Gendarmerie of the French Republic. The President of France is the commander-in-chief of the armed forces, with the title "chef des armées". France maintains one of the largest military forces in Europe and one of the few full-spectrum militaries in the world, including an independent nuclear deterrent.

Organisation

The armed forces are under the authority of the Ministry of the Armed Forces. The Chief of the Defence Staff (Chef d'État-Major des Armées) is the senior military officer and advises the President and the government. The principal services are:

  • French Army (Armée de terre) — including the Foreign Legion, special forces, and army aviation.
  • French Navy (Marine nationale) — centred on the aircraft carrier Charles de Gaulle, ballistic missile submarines and a global network of overseas bases.
  • French Air and Space Force (Armée de l'air et de l'espace) — renamed in 2020 to reflect its space responsibilities; operates Rafale multirole fighters and the airborne leg of the nuclear deterrent.
  • National Gendarmerie — a military force performing police duties, especially in rural areas and overseas.

Nuclear deterrent

France is one of the five nuclear-weapon states recognised by the Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons. Its deterrent, the Force de dissuasion, rests on two components: four Triomphant-class ballistic missile submarines armed with M51 missiles, and air-launched ASMPA cruise missiles carried by Rafale aircraft. France developed its weapons independently after withdrawing from NATO's integrated command structure in 1966, returning to full participation in 2009 while keeping its nuclear forces outside NATO command.

History

France raised one of Europe's first standing armies under Charles VII in the fifteenth century. Its forces played central roles in the wars of Louis XIV, the Revolutionary and Napoleonic Wars, both World Wars, and the colonial wars in Indochina and Algeria. Peacetime conscription, a legacy of the Revolution's levée en masse, was suspended in 1996–2001 under President Jacques Chirac in favour of an all-volunteer professional force. Since the Cold War, French forces have operated in the Balkans, Afghanistan, Libya, Mali and the wider Sahel, and the Levant, as well as in domestic counter-terrorism operations.

Overseas presence

France maintains forces in its overseas departments and territories and garrisons or facilities agreements in Djibouti, the United Arab Emirates, Senegal, Gabon, Côte d'Ivoire, and elsewhere, giving it a global reach second among European states only to the United Kingdom.
