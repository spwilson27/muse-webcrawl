Catholic Church and climate change

The Catholic Church has increasingly engaged with climate change as a moral and theological issue, framing care for the environment as a duty of stewardship over creation and of justice toward the poor, who are most affected by climate impacts. Its teaching draws on Scripture, natural law and Catholic social teaching.

Teaching

Pope John Paul II called for an "ecological conversion" and warned of the moral dimensions of environmental destruction; Pope Benedict XVI linked environmental protection to human development in Caritas in veritate (2009) and oversaw the installation of solar panels at the Vatican. The most comprehensive statement is Pope Francis's encyclical Laudato si' (2015), which accepts the scientific consensus on human-caused warming, criticises consumerism and the "throwaway culture," and calls for an "integral ecology" uniting care for nature, the poor and future generations. Francis returned to the theme in the 2023 exhortation Laudate Deum, urging stronger international action ahead of climate negotiations.

Action

The Holy See acceded to the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change and the Paris Agreement in 2022. Bishops' conferences, religious orders and Catholic development agencies (such as CIDSE and Caritas networks) campaign on climate policy, while dioceses and Catholic institutions have adopted fossil-fuel divestment through the Laudato Si' Movement and committed to net-zero targets.
