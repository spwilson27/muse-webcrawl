Kalash people

Total populationc. 4,000–6,000
RegionsChitral District, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan
LanguagesKalasha, Khowar
ReligionKalash religion (traditional animist/polytheist faith); a minority Muslim
Related groupsNuristanis, other Dardic peoples

The Kalash (Kalasha: Kaḷaśa), also known as the Kalasha, are an Indo-Aryan indigenous people of the Chitral District in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province of Pakistan. They speak the Kalasha language, a member of the Dardic family, and practise an ancient polytheistic religion that sets them apart from the predominantly Muslim population around them. They are often regarded as Pakistan's smallest ethnoreligious group.

Geography

The Kalash live in three valleys of Chitral — Bumburet, Rumbur and Birir — carved by tributaries of the Kunar River in the Hindu Kush mountains. The valleys' isolation historically protected Kalash culture. Related peoples across the border in Afghanistan, the Nuristanis, were forcibly converted to Islam in the 1890s, an event that spared the Kalash valleys on the British-administered side of the Durand Line.

Culture and religion

Traditional Kalash religion centres on a creator god, Dezau, and a pantheon of deities and spirits associated with nature, with sacrifices of goats and wine offered at temples and sacred groves. Society is organised around concepts of purity (onjesta) and impurity (pragata), which structure the layout of villages, altars and the women's menstrual house, the bashali. Colourful festivals mark the seasons, most famously Chilam Joshi in spring, Uchal in summer and Choimus, the winter solstice festival. Kalash women wear distinctive black robes with vivid embroidery and elaborate headdresses adorned with cowrie shells and beads.

Origins

A popular legend claims the Kalash descend from soldiers of Alexander the Great who settled in the region, though genetic and linguistic studies instead place them among the early Indo-Aryan populations of the Hindu Kush. Kalash culture faces pressure from conversion, tourism and development, and community members and activists campaign for its protection.
