Epona was a goddess of Celtic origin, protector of horses, ponies, donkeys, and mules, worshipped throughout the Roman Empire between the first and third centuries AD. She is the only Celtic deity to have been given a feast day in the Roman calendar (18 December), and her name derives from the Gaulish epos, "horse".

Iconography

Epona is usually depicted seated side-saddle on a mare, or between two horses, holding a cornucopia, ears of grain, or a bowl (patera), and often accompanied by foals, dogs, or ravens. Small images of her were placed in stables throughout Gaul, Roman Germany, Britain, and as far east as the Balkans.

Worship

Her cult was especially popular with cavalry units of the Roman army, who carried it across the empire, and with horse breeders and muleteers. Juvenal and Apuleius both mention shrines to Epona in stables. She is sometimes regarded as a psychopomp or fertility goddess as well, and has been linked, speculatively, to the Welsh figure Rhiannon and the Irish Macha.
