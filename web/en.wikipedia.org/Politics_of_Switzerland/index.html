Politics of Switzerland

The politics of Switzerland take place in the framework of a federal, parliamentary, democratic republic, in which the Federal Council is the head of government and of state, and of a multi-party system. Switzerland is known for its system of direct democracy, in which citizens vote frequently on referendums and popular initiatives at federal, cantonal and municipal levels.

Government

Federal executive power is exercised by the seven-member Federal Council, elected by the Federal Assembly for four-year terms; the President of the Swiss Confederation rotates annually among its members. The bicameral Federal Assembly consists of the National Council (200 members) and the Council of States (46 members representing the cantons).

Direct democracy and federalism

Citizens can challenge laws passed by parliament by collecting 50,000 signatures for an optional referendum, and propose constitutional changes by popular initiative with 100,000 signatures. Switzerland's 26 cantons retain extensive autonomy in education, health and taxation.

Political parties

Major parties include the Swiss People's Party, the Social Democratic Party, The Liberals, The Centre, and the Green Party. For decades the Federal Council followed the "magic formula" distributing seats among the four largest parties.
