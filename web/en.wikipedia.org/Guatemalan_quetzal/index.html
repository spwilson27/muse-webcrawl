Guatemalan quetzal

The Guatemalan quetzal (Pharomachrus mocinno), commonly known as the resplendent quetzal, is a strikingly coloured bird of the trogon family found in the cloud forests of Central America, from southern Mexico to western Panama. It is the national bird of Guatemala, appears on that country's flag and coat of arms, and gives its name to the Guatemalan currency, the quetzal.

Description

Males are iridescent green above with a crimson belly, a helmet-like crest, and twin tail streamers that can reach 65 centimetres or more in length. Females are duller, with shorter tails and grey-brown underparts. Quetzals feed chiefly on wild avocados and other fruits of the laurel family, swallowing them whole and regurgitating the seeds, and also take insects and small vertebrates.

Cultural significance

The resplendent quetzal was sacred in Maya and Aztec cultures; its tail feathers were prized as tribute and used in the feathered headdress associated with the deity Quetzalcoatl (Kukulkan). Because the birds were not to be killed, feathers were plucked and the birds released. In Guatemalan tradition the quetzal is a symbol of liberty, based on the belief that it cannot survive in captivity — in practice it fares poorly in zoos, though it has been bred successfully at least once in Mexico.

Conservation

Destruction of cloud forest has reduced populations across its range, and the species is listed as Near Threatened. Protected areas such as Guatemala's Biotopo del Quetzal preserve its habitat.
