Cardiac resynchronization therapy

SpecialtyCardiology / electrophysiology
DeviceBiventricular pacemaker (CRT-P) or with defibrillator (CRT-D)
IndicationHeart failure with ventricular dyssynchrony

Cardiac resynchronization therapy (CRT) is a treatment for heart failure in which a special pacemaker coordinates the contractions of the left and right ventricles. In many patients with heart failure, especially those with left bundle branch block, the ventricles contract out of sequence (dyssynchrony), reducing pumping efficiency; CRT uses simultaneous pacing of both ventricles to restore a more synchronous contraction.

Device and procedure

A CRT system adds a third lead, threaded through the coronary sinus to pace the left ventricle, alongside standard right atrial and right ventricular leads. Devices are either pacemakers alone (CRT-P) or combined with an implantable cardioverter-defibrillator (CRT-D).

Evidence

Trials such as COMPANION, CARE-HF, MADIT-CRT, and RAFT showed that CRT improves symptoms, exercise capacity, and quality of life, reduces heart-failure hospitalizations, promotes reverse remodeling of the left ventricle, and reduces mortality in appropriately selected patients.

Patient selection

Guidelines recommend CRT for patients with reduced ejection fraction (typically ≤35%), sinus rhythm, and a wide QRS complex—greatest benefit occurring with QRS duration ≥150 ms and left bundle branch block morphology—despite optimal medical therapy.
