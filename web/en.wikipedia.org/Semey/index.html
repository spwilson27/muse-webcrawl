Semey (Kazakh: Семей), formerly known by its Russian name Semipalatinsk, is a city in eastern Kazakhstan, on the banks of the Irtysh River near the border with Russia. It is the administrative centre of the Abai Region, created in 2022. With a population of roughly 300,000–350,000, it is one of the country's major regional cities.

History

The city began in 1718 as the Russian fortress of Semipalatinsk, named for seven ruined stone chambers nearby. Fyodor Dostoevsky spent five years of exile there in the 1850s after his Siberian imprisonment, and the city retains a Dostoevsky museum. The Kazakh poet and philosopher Abai Qunanbaiuly lived in the city, and his house museum is an important cultural site. In 2007 the city officially adopted the Kazakh name Semey.

Semipalatinsk Test Site

About 150 kilometres west of the city lies the former Semipalatinsk Test Site, where the Soviet Union conducted 456 nuclear tests between 1949 and 1989. The tests caused lasting health and environmental damage in surrounding communities. Kazakhstan closed the site in 1991 and became a leading advocate of nuclear disarmament; the UN marks 29 August, the anniversary of the first test and of the site's closure, as the International Day against Nuclear Tests.
