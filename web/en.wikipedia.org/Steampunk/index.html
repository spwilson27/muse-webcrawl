Steampunk is a subgenre of science fiction that incorporates retro-futuristic technology and aesthetics inspired by 19th-century industrial steam-powered machinery. Steampunk works are often set in an alternative history of the Victorian era or the American frontier, featuring anachronistic inventions such as steam-driven computers, mechanical automata, airships, and brass-and-copper machinery imagined by people of the period or created in their style.

History

The term was coined in 1987 by author K. W. Jeter, tongue-in-cheek, to describe the "Victorian fantasies" of writers such as Tim Powers, James Blaylock, and Jeter himself, whose novels had appeared in the wake of cyberpunk. Foundational works include William Gibson and Bruce Sterling's The Difference Engine (1990), which imagined a Victorian Britain transformed by Charles Babbage's mechanical computers. The genre's roots reach back to the scientific romances of Jules Verne and H. G. Wells.

Subculture and aesthetics

Since the 1990s steampunk has grown into a broader subculture embracing fashion (corsets, goggles, waistcoats, and modified accessories), maker culture and DIY craft, music, film, and video games. Related "-punk" genres include dieselpunk, clockpunk, and atompunk. Critics debate the genre's nostalgia for empire alongside its celebration of invention, craftsmanship, and do-it-yourself resistance to mass production.

Examples

Notable steampunk works include Gibson and Sterling's The Difference Engine, Philip Pullman's His Dark Materials, Alan Moore's The League of Extraordinary Gentlemen, the anime films of Hayao Miyazaki such as Castle in the Sky, and video games such as BioShock Infinite and the Thief series.
