Half Dome is a granite dome at the eastern end of Yosemite Valley in Yosemite National Park, California. It is a well-known rock formation in the park, named for its distinct shape: one side is a sheer face while the other three sides are smooth and round, making it appear like a dome cut in half. The granite crest rises more than 4,737 feet (1,444 m) above the valley floor.

Geology

Although long imagined as a full dome split by glaciation, the formation is largely the product of exfoliation and jointing in the granite; glaciers undercut and steepened its face but never covered its summit, and no missing "other half" was ever carried away on the scale once supposed.

Hiking

The most famous route to the summit is a 14- to 16-mile round-trip hike from the valley floor. The final 400 feet ascend a pair of steel cables installed in 1919, which hikers climb holding on with gloved hands; permits, distributed by lottery, are required for the cable section. George G. Anderson made the first recorded ascent in 1875 by drilling iron eye-bolts into the rock.
