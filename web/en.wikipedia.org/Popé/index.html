Popé (also spelled Po'pay; c. 1630 – c. 1688/1692) was a Tewa religious leader and medicine man from Ohkay Owingeh (San Juan Pueblo) in Spanish New Mexico. He organised and led the Pueblo Revolt of 1680, the most successful Indigenous uprising against European colonial rule in North America, which expelled the Spanish from New Mexico for twelve years.

Background

Spanish colonisation from 1598 imposed the encomienda system, heavy tribute and labour demands, and Franciscan missions that suppressed Pueblo religion. In 1675 Popé was among 47 Pueblo religious leaders arrested and flogged in Santa Fe for "sorcery"; released after Pueblo protests, he withdrew to Taos Pueblo and began planning a coordinated uprising. A drought, famine and intensifying Apache raids had further discredited Spanish protection.

The revolt

Using runners carrying knotted cords to synchronise the date, the Pueblos rose on 10 August 1680. Some 400 Spanish colonists and 21 Franciscan friars were killed, missions were destroyed, and the survivors besieged in Santa Fe fled south to El Paso. Popé then directed a deliberate restoration of Pueblo religion and customs, ordering the destruction of churches and Christian symbols.

Aftermath

Unity among the Pueblos frayed under drought, disease and inter-pueblo conflict, and Popé's authority weakened; he died before the Spanish return. Diego de Vargas re-entered Santa Fe in 1692, and Spanish rule was re-established — though on terms that left Pueblo land grants and religious practice more secure. In 2005 a statue of Popé by Cliff Fragua became one of New Mexico's two statues in the National Statuary Hall of the United States Capitol — the first by a Native American sculptor.
