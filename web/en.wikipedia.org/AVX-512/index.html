AVX-512

AVX-512
Designer: Intel
Introduced: 2013 (announced); 2016 (first products)
Type: SIMD instruction set extension
Registers: 32 × 512-bit (zmm)

AVX-512 is a set of 512-bit SIMD (single instruction, multiple data) extensions to the x86 instruction set architecture announced by Intel in 2013. It extends the Advanced Vector Extensions (AVX) by doubling register width to 512 bits, expanding the register file to 32 vector registers (zmm0–zmm31), and adding eight opmask registers that enable per-element predication of instructions.

Subsets

Unlike its predecessors, AVX-512 is not a single extension but a family of subsets. The Foundation (AVX-512F) set debuted in Knights Landing (Xeon Phi) in 2016 and in Skylake-X server processors in 2017. Other subsets include conflict-detection (CD), exponential/reciprocal (ER), prefetch (PF), byte/word (BW), doubleword/quadword (DQ), vector length (VL), and later AI-focused extensions such as VNNI (vector neural network instructions) and BF16 support on Cooper Lake and later Xeons.

Adoption and controversy

AVX-512 was long confined mostly to Intel server and workstation chips; AMD implemented it starting with Zen 4 (2022) using double-pumped 256-bit datapaths, and expanded it in Zen 5. On Intel consumer chips it appeared in Rocket Lake and in early Alder Lake samples, but Intel disabled it on later Alder Lake and Raptor Lake products to keep the product line consistent, since their efficiency cores lacked it. Heavy AVX-512 workloads can also trigger frequency throttling on some Intel processors, a subject of much debate among performance engineers — notably, Linus Torvalds publicly criticized the fragmentation of the extension family.
