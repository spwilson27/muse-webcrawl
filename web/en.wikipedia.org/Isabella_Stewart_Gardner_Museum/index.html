Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum

Location: 25 Evans Way, Boston, Massachusetts

Established: 1903

Founder: Isabella Stewart Gardner

Type: Art museum

The Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum is an art museum in Boston, Massachusetts, which houses significant examples of European, Asian, and American art. Its holdings include paintings, sculpture, tapestries, and decorative arts. It was founded in 1903 by Isabella Stewart Gardner (1840–1924), whose will stipulated that the collection be maintained essentially unchanged for the education and enjoyment of the public forever.

The building and collection

Gardner designed the museum, called Fenway Court, in the style of a fifteenth-century Venetian palace, built around a flower-filled courtyard. Advised by Bernard Berenson, she assembled a collection including masterpieces by Titian (The Rape of Europa), Rembrandt, Vermeer, Raphael, Botticelli, and the first Matisse to enter an American collection. A glass-roofed extension wing designed by Renzo Piano opened in 2012.

The 1990 theft

In the early hours of 18 March 1990, two men disguised as police officers entered the museum and stole 13 works of art valued at about $500 million — the largest art theft in US history. Stolen works include Vermeer's The Concert, three Rembrandts including his only seascape, The Storm on the Sea of Galilee, and Manet's Chez Tortoni. None has been recovered; the empty frames still hang in the galleries, as Gardner's will forbids changing the installation. A $10 million reward remains outstanding.
