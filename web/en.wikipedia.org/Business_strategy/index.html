Business strategy

Business strategy
Branch of strategic management
Concerned with: Competitive advantage at the business-unit level
Key thinkers: Michael Porter, Igor Ansoff

Business strategy concerns how a firm or business unit should compete within a particular industry or market — as distinct from corporate strategy, which concerns the overall scope of the firm. It addresses the basis of competitive advantage and the positioning of products and services against rivals.

Generic strategies

Michael Porter's generic strategies hold that advantage comes from cost leadership, differentiation, or focus on a narrow segment. Porter's five forces framework analyses industry attractiveness in terms of rivalry, new entrants, substitutes, and the bargaining power of buyers and suppliers.

Other approaches

The resource-based view attributes advantage to valuable, rare and hard-to-imitate internal resources and capabilities. The Ansoff matrix maps growth options by products and markets (penetration, development, diversification). Blue Ocean strategy argues for creating uncontested market space rather than competing in crowded ones.

Formulation

Strategy formulation typically involves environmental and internal analysis (such as SWOT), setting objectives, and selecting a course of action, followed by implementation through structure, systems and leadership.
