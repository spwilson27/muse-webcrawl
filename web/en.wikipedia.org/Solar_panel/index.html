Solar panel

Rooftop photovoltaic panels

A solar panel (photovoltaic module) is an assembly of solar cells connected electrically, mounted in a frame, and protected by glass and a weatherproof backing. Panels generate direct current (DC) electricity from sunlight, which an inverter converts to alternating current (AC) for use in buildings or export to the grid. A collection of panels forms a photovoltaic system or solar array.

Construction

A typical residential panel contains 60 or 72 crystalline silicon cells (or 120–144 half-cut cells), encapsulated in EVA film beneath tempered low-iron glass, with an aluminium frame and a junction box. Panels are commonly rated between 300 and 600 watts. Cells are wired in series strings, with bypass diodes to limit losses from partial shading.

Performance

Panel efficiency for commercial modules is typically 15–23%. Output depends on irradiance, temperature (efficiency falls as cells heat up), orientation, and tilt. Performance is rated under Standard Test Conditions (1,000 W/m², 25 °C cell temperature). Panels degrade slowly, typically about 0.5% per year, and are usually warranted for 25 years.

Uses

Solar panels are deployed in utility-scale solar farms, commercial and residential rooftops, off-grid systems, and increasingly in agrivoltaics and floating installations. Global cumulative photovoltaic capacity passed one terawatt in 2022.
