Semiconductor fabrication

Semiconductor device fabrication is the process used to manufacture semiconductor devices, typically integrated circuits (ICs) such as computer processors and memory chips, which are present in everyday electrical and electronic devices. It is a multiple-step sequence of photolithographic and chemical processing steps during which electronic circuits are gradually created on a wafer made of pure semiconducting material, most commonly silicon.

Overview

Fabrication takes place in highly controlled cleanrooms, because even microscopic dust particles can ruin a chip. A modern wafer passes through hundreds of steps: thin films are deposited on the wafer, patterns are defined by photolithography — in state-of-the-art plants using extreme ultraviolet (EUV) light — unwanted material is removed by etching, dopant atoms are introduced by ion implantation or diffusion, and chemical-mechanical polishing (CMP) flattens the surface between layers. The cycle repeats for each layer of transistors and metal interconnect.

Front-end and back-end

Processing is conventionally divided into the front end of line (FEOL), in which the transistors themselves are formed, and the back end of line (BEOL), in which the metal wiring that interconnects them is built. After wafer fabrication, each die is tested, the wafer is diced, and good dies are packaged and tested again (assembly and test are collectively part of OSAT — outsourced semiconductor assembly and test).

Feature size and scaling

Progress in fabrication is measured by the smallest features that can be patterned, marketed as the "technology node" (e.g. 28 nm, 7 nm, 3 nm). Shrinking features, following Moore's law, has driven decades of increases in transistor density, although node names no longer correspond directly to any single physical dimension.

Economics

Fabs are among the most expensive factories ever built, with leading-edge facilities costing well over US$10 billion. This cost structure produced the modern industry split between design-focused "fabless" companies and contract manufacturers ("foundries") such as TSMC and Samsung, alongside integrated device manufacturers such as Intel.
