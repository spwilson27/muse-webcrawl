Hussein bin Talal (14 November 1935 – 7 February 1999) was King of Jordan from 11 August 1952 until his death in 1999. A member of the Hashemite dynasty, he reigned for over 46 years, making him one of the longest-reigning monarchs of the 20th century, and steered Jordan through the Arab–Israeli conflicts and the Cold War politics of the Middle East.

Early life and accession

Hussein was born in Amman, the eldest son of Crown Prince Talal and Princess Zein al-Sharaf. He was educated at Victoria College in Alexandria and at Harrow School and the Royal Military Academy Sandhurst in England. As a schoolboy he stood beside his grandfather, King Abdullah I, when the king was assassinated at Al-Aqsa Mosque in Jerusalem in 1951; a bullet struck a medal on Hussein's chest, an incident often recounted in his biography. His father Talal reigned briefly before abdicating on health grounds in 1952, and Hussein ascended the throne aged 16, assuming full constitutional powers on 2 May 1953.

Reign

Hussein's reign saw the Six-Day War of 1967, in which Jordan lost the West Bank to Israel, and the crisis of Black September in 1970, when the Jordanian army expelled the Palestine Liberation Organization after a brief civil war. He survived numerous assassination attempts and regional upheavals, maintaining close ties with the West while asserting Arab credentials. In 1988 he formally severed Jordan's administrative ties to the West Bank. In 1994 he signed the Israel–Jordan peace treaty, the second such treaty between Israel and an Arab state, for which he was widely praised internationally. His address to the Israeli Knesset and his eulogy at Yitzhak Rabin's funeral in 1995 marked his reputation as a peacemaker.

Death and succession

After treatment for cancer in the United States, Hussein returned to Jordan in January 1999 and died on 7 February 1999. Shortly before his death he replaced his brother Hassan as crown prince with his eldest son, who succeeded him as Abdullah II. His funeral in Amman was attended by leaders from around the world.
