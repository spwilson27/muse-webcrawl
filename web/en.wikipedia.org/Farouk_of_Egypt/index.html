Farouk I (11 February 1920 – 18 March 1965) was the tenth ruler of Egypt from the Muhammad Ali dynasty and the penultimate King of Egypt and the Sudan, reigning from 1936 to 1952. He succeeded his father, Fuad I, as king at the age of sixteen. Initially popular, his reign was marked by corruption, extravagance, and growing nationalist opposition to continued British influence, ending with his overthrow in the Egyptian revolution of 1952 led by the Free Officers Movement.

Reign

Farouk came to the throne in 1936 and was crowned in 1937. During the Second World War, British pressure on Egypt culminated in the Abdeen Palace Incident of February 1942, when British forces surrounded his palace to compel him to appoint a pro-British government, a humiliation that damaged his prestige. His lavish lifestyle and reputation for kleptomania and womanising made him a figure of ridicule abroad — he was famed as a prodigious collector of everything from coins and stamps to cars and Fabergé eggs.

Egypt's defeat in the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, widely blamed on corrupt procurement under his regime, and the 1952 Cairo Fire further eroded support. On 23 July 1952 the Free Officers under Muhammad Naguib and Gamal Abdel Nasser seized power; Farouk abdicated on 26 July 1952 in favour of his infant son, Fuad II, and sailed into exile aboard the royal yacht.

Exile and death

Farouk lived in Monaco and Italy, retaining his playboy lifestyle, and his vast possessions in Egypt were auctioned by the new regime. He became an Italian citizen in 1959 and died in Rome on 18 March 1965 after collapsing at a meal. He was buried in Cairo, in the Al-Rifa'i Mosque.
