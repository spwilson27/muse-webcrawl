Elbląg (German: Elbing) is a city in northern Poland, in the Warmian-Masurian Voivodeship, lying on the Elbląg River near its outflow into the Vistula Lagoon, about 55 kilometres southeast of Gdańsk. With around 115,000 inhabitants it is a river port, industrial city and the starting point of the Elbląg Canal.

History

The Teutonic Knights founded a castle here in 1237 beside the Old Prussian trading place of Truso, and the town received Lübeck law in 1246. Elbląg became a member of the Hanseatic League and an important Baltic trading port. It passed to the Polish Crown in 1466 under the Second Peace of Thorn, was taken by Prussia in the First Partition of Poland (1772), and — as Elbing — remained German until 1945, when it was transferred to Poland and its German population expelled.

The Old Town and industry

The medieval Old Town was largely destroyed in 1945; from the 1980s it has been rebuilt using a distinctive "retroversion" method that recovers the historic street plan and parcel shapes with modern architecture. Landmarks include St. Nicholas Cathedral, the Market Gate (Brama Targowa) and the Elbląg Museum. In the 19th and 20th centuries the Schichau shipyard and engineering works made the city an industrial centre.

Transport

Elbląg is the western terminus of the Elbląg Canal, famous for its inclined planes that haul boats overland. A new shipping channel through the Vistula Spit, opened in 2022 near the city, gives its port direct access to the Baltic without passing Russian waters.
