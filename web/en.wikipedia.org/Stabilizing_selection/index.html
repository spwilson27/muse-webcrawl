Stabilizing selection
Field: Evolutionary biology
Effect: Favours average phenotypes
Contrasts with: Directional, disruptive selection

Stabilizing selection is a type of natural selection in which the population mean of a trait stabilizes, favouring intermediate phenotypes and reducing genetic variation around that mean. Extreme variants are selected against.

Examples

Classic examples include human birth weight, where very small and very large babies historically faced higher mortality; clutch size in birds, where intermediate numbers of eggs yield the most surviving offspring; and the body size of coho salmon.

Mechanism

Stabilizing selection often results from a well-adapted population in a stable environment. Over time it narrows the distribution of a trait without shifting its average.
