Port Sunlight is a model village and suburb in the Metropolitan Borough of Wirral, Merseyside, England. It was built from 1888 by William Lever (later Lord Leverhulme) to house the workforce of his soap factory, Lever Brothers, and was named after the company's best-known product, Sunlight soap.

Architecture

Port Sunlight contains about 900 Grade II listed houses designed by some thirty architects in a variety of Arts and Crafts and revival styles, set among parks, gardens, and wide avenues. Public buildings include the Lady Lever Art Gallery (1922), a theatre, schools, a church, and a hospital — an exceptional example of paternalistic industrial planning and an early influence on the garden city movement.

Today

The factory is now operated by Unilever. The village is managed largely by the Port Sunlight Village Trust, and the Port Sunlight Museum tells the story of Lever's enterprise and the residents' lives.
