Agner Krarup Erlang (January 1, 1878 – February 3, 1929) was a Danish mathematician, statistician, and engineer who invented the fields of traffic engineering and queueing theory.

Work

Working for the Copenhagen Telephone Company from 1908, Erlang studied the statistical behavior of telephone calls to determine how many circuits and operators were needed to provide a given quality of service. His 1909 paper on the theory of probabilities and telephone conversations, and his 1917 formula for loss and waiting time, founded the mathematical study of telecommunications traffic.

Legacy

The erlang, a dimensionless unit of telecommunications traffic, and the Erlang distribution in probability theory are named after him, as is the Erlang programming language developed by Ericsson for telecom systems. His formulas remain foundational to call-center and network engineering.
