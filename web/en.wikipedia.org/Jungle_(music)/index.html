Jungle (music)

Jungle
Stylistic originsBreakbeat hardcore, techno, reggae, dub, dancehall, hip hop
Cultural originsEarly 1990s, London, England
Typical instrumentsSampler, drum machine, synthesizer, turntables
Derivative formsDrum and bass, UK garage, grime

Jungle is a genre of dance music that developed in the United Kingdom in the early 1990s, emerging from the breakbeat hardcore and rave scenes. It is characterised by fast tempos (roughly 150–170 BPM), rapid breakbeats sampled from funk and soul records, heavy sub-bass lines drawn from reggae and dub sound system culture, and chopped, time-stretched vocal and instrumental samples.

History

Jungle grew out of London's multiracial rave culture and pirate radio. Producers such as Goldie, LTJ Bukem, Fabio and Grooverider helped define the sound. Tracks like "Terminator" by Metalheadz and Shy FX's "Original Nuttah" (1994), featuring UK APACHE, became anthems. The genre took much of its vocal style and bass weight from Jamaican sound system culture, dancehall and ragga, alongside influences from hip hop and techno.

Characteristics

The most recognisable element of jungle is the Amen break, a six-second drum solo from the Winstons' "Amen, Brother" (1969), chopped and rearranged at high speed. Producers layered this with deep, rolling sub-bass, dub-style echo and reverb, and ragga MC vocals. Time-stretching — altering a sample's pitch and length independently — became a signature technique.

Legacy

By the mid-1990s jungle evolved into the more polished, technologically refined genre known as drum and bass. Jungle retained a dedicated following, and its influence can be heard in UK garage, dubstep, grime and footwork. A revival of the classic jungle sound emerged in the 2010s and 2020s among younger producers.
