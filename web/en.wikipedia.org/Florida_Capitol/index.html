Florida Capitol

The Florida Capitol is the state capitol of Florida, in Tallahassee. The Florida Capitol complex comprises the twenty-two story executive office building — the "New Capitol" — flanked by the chambers of the Florida House of Representatives and the Florida Senate, together with the restored Historic Capitol of 1902, which now serves as a museum. The complex is home to the government of the state of Florida.

History

Tallahassee was chosen as the territorial capital in 1824, and the first permanent capitol building was completed in 1845, on the eve of statehood. It was enlarged over the following decades, with a dome and wings added in 1902, 1923, and later. By the 1970s the building was overcrowded, and a new Capitol was constructed behind it to designs by Edward Durell Stone and Reynolds, Smith & Hills; the executive tower was completed in 1977 and the legislative chambers in 1978.

The Historic Capitol

Originally slated for demolition, the Old Capitol was saved by a citizens' campaign and restored to its 1902 appearance, reopening in 1982 as the Florida Historic Capitol Museum, containing exhibits on the state's political history.

Architecture

The 22-storey tower, among the tallest buildings in Florida, houses the offices of the Governor and Cabinet. The Heritage Hall atrium and the plaza between the old and new buildings display monuments including a replica of the Liberty Bell and the Martin Luther King Jr. memorial.
