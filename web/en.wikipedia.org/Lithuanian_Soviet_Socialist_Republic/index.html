The Lithuanian Soviet Socialist Republic (Lithuanian: Lietuvos Tarybų Socialistinė Respublika), abbreviated Lithuanian SSR, was one of the constituent republics of the Soviet Union from 1940 to 1990. It was proclaimed after the Soviet occupation of Lithuania in June 1940 and formally incorporated into the USSR that August — an annexation that Lithuania and many Western states regard as illegal and never recognized de jure.

History

Occupation and annexation

Under the secret protocols of the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact (1939), Lithuania fell into the Soviet sphere. After an ultimatum in June 1940, the Red Army occupied the country; a staged election produced a "People's Seimas" that proclaimed the Lithuanian SSR on 21 July 1940 and petitioned to join the USSR. Mass arrests and the June 1941 deportations sent thousands to Siberia before Germany invaded.

Second World War and aftermath

Nazi Germany occupied Lithuania from 1941 to 1944, during which the vast majority of Lithuanian Jews were murdered in the Holocaust. The returning Soviet regime faced armed partisan resistance — the "Forest Brothers" — into the early 1950s, alongside collectivization and further deportations, notably Operation Priboi in 1949.

Soviet period

Postwar decades brought industrialization, urbanization, and Russification pressures, but also a relatively strong Lithuanian cultural identity within the republic's institutions. Antanas Sniečkus led the republic's Communist Party from 1940 until 1974. In the late 1980s the Sąjūdis reform movement mobilized mass support for sovereignty, culminating in the Baltic Way human chain of August 1989.

Restoration of independence

On 11 March 1990 the Supreme Council, led by Vytautas Landsbergis, declared the restoration of the independent State of Lithuania — the first Soviet republic to do so. Moscow responded with an economic blockade and, in January 1991, a violent crackdown in Vilnius. Following the failed August 1991 coup in Moscow, the USSR formally recognized Lithuanian independence on 6 September 1991.
