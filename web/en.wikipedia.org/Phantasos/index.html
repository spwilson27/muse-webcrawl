In Greek mythology, Phantasos (Greek: Φάντασος, "apparition" or "fantasy") is one of the Oneiroi, the dream gods and sons of Hypnos (Sleep). He is known principally from Ovid's Metamorphoses, where he appears as one of the three foremost dream-shapers who serve in the cave of Somnus.

The house of Somnus, home of Morpheus, Phobetor and Phantasos

According to Ovid, Phantasos specialises in appearing in dreams in the form of inanimate objects — earth, rocks, water, trees and other lifeless things — in contrast to his brother Morpheus, who takes human form, and Phobetor (also called Icelos), who appears as animals and other living creatures. The three are the most skilful of the thousand sons of Somnus and are sent by the gods to deliver dreams to sleeping mortals, as when Morpheus is dispatched to appear to Alcyone in the likeness of her drowned husband Ceyx.

Name and influence

Phantasos's name derives from the Greek word from which "fantasy" and "phantom" descend through Latin. Unlike Morpheus, he has attracted little independent cult or iconography, but he recurs in later literature as a personification of deceptive dream imagery and has given his name to characters in modern fantasy works.
