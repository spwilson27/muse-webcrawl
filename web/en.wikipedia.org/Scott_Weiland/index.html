Scott Richard Weiland (born Kline; 27 October 1967 – 3 December 2015) was an American singer and songwriter, best known as the lead vocalist of the rock band Stone Temple Pilots, with whom he recorded six studio albums and won a Grammy Award for the song "Plush." He later fronted the supergroup Velvet Revolver alongside former members of Guns N' Roses.

Career

Weiland formed Stone Temple Pilots with brothers Robert and Dean DeLeo and drummer Eric Kretz; their debut Core (1992) and follow-up Purple (1994) made them one of the biggest rock acts of the 1990s, with hits including "Interstate Love Song," "Vasoline," and "Creep." Known for his versatile baritone, flamboyant stage persona, and use of a megaphone on stage, Weiland also released solo albums beginning with 12 Bar Blues (1998). With Velvet Revolver (2003–2008) he scored a Grammy-winning hit, "Slither."

Personal life and death

Weiland's career was repeatedly disrupted by drug addiction, arrests, and rehabilitation stays; Stone Temple Pilots fired him in 2013. He was found dead on his tour bus in Bloomington, Minnesota, on 3 December 2015, while touring with his band the Wildabouts; the cause was determined to be an accidental overdose of cocaine, alcohol, and MDA.
