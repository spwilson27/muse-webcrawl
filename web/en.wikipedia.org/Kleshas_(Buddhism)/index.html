Kleshas (Buddhism)

Sanskrit: kleśa
Pali: kilesa
Translations: afflictions, defilements, poisons

In Buddhism, kleshas (Sanskrit: क्लेश, kleśa; Pali: kilesa) are mental states that cloud the mind and manifest in unwholesome actions. They are often translated as "afflictions," "defilements," or "poisons."

The three poisons

The root kleshas are the three poisons: delusion or ignorance (moha/avidyā), attachment or greed (rāga/lobha), and aversion or hatred (dveṣa/dosa). These are depicted at the centre of the bhavacakra (wheel of life) and are considered the root cause of suffering (dukkha) and continued rebirth in saṃsāra.

In the Abhidharma

Abhidharma literature gives expanded lists of afflictive mental factors — for example, six root afflictions and twenty secondary afflictions in the Yogācāra tradition. The path to liberation involves uprooting the kleshas through wisdom, ethical conduct, and meditation.
