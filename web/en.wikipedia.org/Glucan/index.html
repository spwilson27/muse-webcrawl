Glucan

A glucan is a polysaccharide derived from D-glucose, linked by glycosidic bonds. Glucans are among the most abundant natural polymers: they include the energy-storage molecules starch and glycogen, the structural molecule cellulose, and the beta-glucans of fungal cell walls and cereal grains. The properties of a glucan depend on the stereochemistry (alpha or beta) and positions of its glycosidic linkages.

Types

Alpha glucans

  • Starch — amylose (alpha-1,4 linkages) and amylopectin (alpha-1,4 with alpha-1,6 branches); the storage carbohydrate of plants.
  • Glycogen — a highly branched alpha glucan used for energy storage in animals and fungi.
  • Dextran — an alpha-1,6 glucan produced by bacteria such as Leuconostoc, used medically as a plasma volume expander.
  • Pullulan — a fungal alpha glucan used in films and food coatings.

Beta glucans

  • Cellulose — a linear beta-1,4 glucan forming the primary structural component of plant cell walls; indigestible by humans.
  • Beta-glucans of oats and barley — mixed-linkage beta-(1,3)/(1,4) glucans that form viscous soluble fibre; their cholesterol-lowering effect is recognised in authorised health claims.
  • Fungal beta-glucans — beta-(1,3) backbones with beta-(1,6) branches found in yeast and mushroom cell walls; they are recognised by the innate immune receptor dectin-1 and are studied as immunomodulators.
  • Curdlan and laminarin — beta-1,3 glucans from bacteria and brown algae respectively.

Biological roles

Glucans serve as energy stores, structural materials and signalling molecules. In fungi and plants they also act as elicitors of defence responses. Soluble cereal beta-glucans slow gastric emptying and glucose absorption, which underlies their use as a functional food ingredient, while particulate yeast beta-glucans are used in dietary supplements and as vaccine adjuvants in research.
