The Alborz (Persian: البرز) is a mountain range in northern Iran that stretches from the border with Azerbaijan along the southern shore of the Caspian Sea to the borders of Turkmenistan. It forms a barrier between the arid Iranian plateau and the humid Caspian lowlands.

Geography

The range's highest summit is Mount Damavand, a dormant stratovolcano and the highest peak in Iran at about 5,610 metres. Other notable peaks include Alam-Kuh. The northern slopes are covered by the lush Hyrcanian forests, while southern slopes facing Tehran are dry and rocky.

Importance

The Alborz shelters ski resorts such as Dizin and Shemshak, supplies water to Tehran and the Caspian provinces, and figures prominently in Persian mythology — Mount Damavand is a legendary symbol of Iranian resistance in the Shahnameh.
