Crustacean

Crustaceans
Phylum: Arthropoda
Subphylum: Crustacea
Described species: ~67,000
Examples: Crabs, lobsters, shrimp, barnacles, krill

Crustaceans form a large, diverse subphylum of arthropods that includes crabs, lobsters, crayfish, shrimp, krill, prawns, woodlice, and barnacles. About 67,000 species have been described, most of them aquatic.

Anatomy

Crustaceans have a chitinous exoskeleton that is shed by moulting as they grow, biramous (two-branched) limbs, two pairs of antennae, and bodies divided into head, thorax, and abdomen — often with a fused cephalothorax covered by a carapace. Many breathe through gills; the terrestrial woodlice are a notable exception.

Ecology and importance

Crustaceans occupy nearly every aquatic habitat, from deep-sea vents to temporary pools. Krill and copepods are foundational to marine food webs. Commercially, shrimp, crab, and lobster fisheries are worth billions of dollars, and crustaceans are also popular in the aquarium trade.
