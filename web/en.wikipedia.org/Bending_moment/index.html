Bending moment

In solid mechanics, a bending moment is the reaction induced in a structural element when an external force or moment is applied to the element, causing it to bend. The most common structural element subjected to bending moments is the beam. The bending moment at a section through a structural element may be defined as the sum of the moments about that section of all external forces acting to one side of that section.

Definition and calculation

Bending moment is measured in units of force times length (e.g. newton-metres, N·m, or pound-feet). For a beam in static equilibrium, the internal bending moment at a cut must balance the algebraic sum of moments of the external forces on one side of the cut. The bending moment is related to the shear force V by the differential relation dM/dx = V, and to the distributed load q by dV/dx = −q (with sign conventions varying).

The maximum bending stress in a beam is given by the flexure formula, σ = My/I, where M is the bending moment at the section, y is the distance from the neutral axis, and I is the second moment of area of the cross-section. Positive bending moments that cause compression in the top fibres and tension in the bottom fibres, making the beam sag concave-upward, are called sagging moments; the reverse are hogging moments.

Shear and moment diagrams

Engineers represent the variation of shear force and bending moment along a beam graphically as shear and bending moment diagrams. For a simply supported beam with a concentrated load P at midspan, the maximum moment is PL/4 at the centre; for a uniformly distributed load w, the maximum is wL²/8. Identifying the maximum bending moment is a central step in sizing beams in structural engineering.
