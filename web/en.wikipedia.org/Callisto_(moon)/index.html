Callisto (Moon)

Callisto is the second-largest moon of Jupiter and the third-largest satellite in the Solar System. Discovered in 1610 by Galileo Galilei as one of the four Galilean moons, it is an ancient, heavily cratered world of rock and ice.

Surface

Callisto's surface is the oldest and most cratered in the Solar System, essentially unchanged for four billion years. Dark plains are dotted with bright impact rings, including Valhalla, a multi-ring basin some 3,800 km across, and Asgard. There is no evidence of volcanism or tectonics.

Interior and ocean

Callisto is only partially differentiated. Magnetic measurements by the Galileo spacecraft suggest a salty ocean may lie some 100 km or more beneath the icy crust, induced by Jupiter's magnetic field.

Orbit

It orbits Jupiter at about 1.88 million km, tidally locked, outside the main radiation belt — making it comparatively favourable for future human exploration and a prime target of the ESA JUICE mission, which will fly by it repeatedly en route to Ganymede.
