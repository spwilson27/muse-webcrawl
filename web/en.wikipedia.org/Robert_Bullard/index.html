Robert Bullard

Robert D. Bullard
Born: 21 December 1946, Elba, Alabama, US
Known for: environmental justice research

Robert Doyle Bullard (born 1946) is an American academic and activist widely described as the "father of environmental justice". His research documented how hazardous waste sites and polluting industries were disproportionately sited in Black and low-income communities in the United States.

Career

Bullard's work began in the late 1970s, when, as a sociologist at Texas Southern University, he was drawn into the Bean v. Southwestern Waste Management case in Houston, for which he compiled data showing that most of the city's landfills and incinerators were located in Black neighbourhoods. His book Dumping in Dixie: Race, Class, and Environmental Quality (1990) became a foundational text of the environmental justice movement.

Later work

Bullard has held posts at Clark Atlanta University and Texas Southern University, where he served as dean of the Barbara Jordan–Mickey Leland School of Public Affairs, and he co-founded the HBCU Climate Change Consortium. He has received numerous honours, including being named one of Newsweek's "Environmental Leaders of the Century".
