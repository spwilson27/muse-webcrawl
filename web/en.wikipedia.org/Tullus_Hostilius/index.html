Tullus Hostilius (r. 673–642 BC) was the legendary third king of Rome. He succeeded Numa Pompilius and was succeeded by Ancus Marcius. Unlike his predecessor, Tullus was known as a warlike king who was more interested in the waging of war and the expansion of Roman territory.

Tullus Hostilius was the grandson of Hostus Hostilius, who had fought with Romulus and died during the Sabine invasion of Rome. The principal feature of Tullus's reign was the defeat of Alba Longa — the ancestral home of the Julian family and rival of Rome — and its complete destruction.

War with Alba Longa

During Tullus's reign, Rome and Alba Longa went to war. To avoid the heavy casualties of a full battle, the two sides agreed that the outcome would be decided by combat between two sets of triplets: the Roman Horatii and the Alban Curiatii. The Horatii won, and Alba Longa submitted to Roman rule. When the Alban dictator Mettius Fufetius later betrayed Rome during a battle against Fidenae and Veii, Tullus had him executed by being torn apart by two chariots driving in opposite directions, and Alba Longa was razed, its population transferred to Rome and settled on the Caelian Hill.

Other wars and works

Tullus also waged successful wars against Fidenae, Veii, and the Sabines. He is credited with building the Curia Hostilia, the original meeting place of the Roman Senate in the Forum.

Death

According to Livy, Tullus neglected religious observances during his reign. After a plague struck Rome, Tullus attempted to perform a secret rite to Jupiter Elicius but performed it incorrectly, and he and his house were struck by lightning and burned to ashes. His reign was said to have lasted 32 years. Many historians regard Tullus as a largely legendary figure, a "double" of Romulus representing the warlike aspect of Roman kingship.
