Logogram

Chinese characters are the most widely used logographic system today

A logogram is a written character that represents a word or a morpheme (a meaningful unit of language). Logograms are the individual symbols of a logographic writing system, in contrast to alphabets and syllabaries, whose symbols primarily represent sounds.

Characteristics

Unlike phonetic scripts, a logogram's relationship to pronunciation is indirect: the same character can be read differently in different languages or dialects. Chinese characters, for example, may be read with entirely different pronunciations in Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese (kanji) and Korean (hanja), while retaining related meanings. The cost of this flexibility is a large inventory of symbols — thousands of characters must be learned for literacy.

No writing system is purely logographic. Even Chinese characters mostly consist of a semantic component combined with a phonetic component, and historical logographies such as Sumerian cuneiform and Egyptian hieroglyphs made extensive use of phonetic signs and determinatives.

Examples

Logographic or partly logographic systems include Chinese characters, Sumerian cuneiform, Egyptian hieroglyphs, and Mayan glyphs. Logograms also appear inside alphabetic systems: the numerals (1, 2, 3), and symbols such as %, $, & and @ are read as whole words regardless of language — & represents "and," et, or und depending on the reader.
