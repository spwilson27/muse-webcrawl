Hornworts are a group of non-vascular plants constituting the division Anthocerotophyta. The common name refers to the elongated horn-like structure, which is the sporophyte. As in mosses and liverworts, hornworts have an alternation of generations in which the flattened, green gametophyte is the dominant phase of the life cycle.

Description

The gametophyte is usually a flat rosette or ribbon-like thallus growing on damp soil. Unusually among land plants, each cell typically contains a single large chloroplast with a pyrenoid, a feature more reminiscent of algae. Many hornworts host symbiotic nitrogen-fixing cyanobacteria of the genus Nostoc in internal cavities. The sporophyte grows from a basal meristem and can photosynthesize for months, splitting open lengthwise to release spores.

Distribution and classification

Hornworts occur on moist soil and rocks worldwide, especially in disturbed habitats. Molecular studies generally place them among the three lineages of bryophytes, and recent analyses often support hornworts as the sister group to a combined moss–liverwort clade.
