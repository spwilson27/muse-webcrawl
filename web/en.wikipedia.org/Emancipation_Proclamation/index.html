Emancipation Proclamation

The Emancipation Proclamation, officially Proclamation 95, was a presidential proclamation and executive order issued by United States President Abraham Lincoln on January 1, 1863, during the American Civil War. It declared that all persons held as slaves within the rebellious states "are, and henceforward shall be free."

Lincoln issued a preliminary proclamation on September 22, 1862, warning that slaves in states still in rebellion on January 1 would be freed. Because the proclamation applied only to Confederate territory not under Union control, it did not immediately free all slaves, and it exempted the border states and certain occupied areas. Nevertheless, it transformed the character of the war: emancipation became an explicit Union war aim, and Black men were authorised to enlist in the Union Army and Navy.

The proclamation paved the way for the total abolition of slavery in the United States through the Thirteenth Amendment, ratified in 1865.
