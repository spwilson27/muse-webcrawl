Battle of Atbara

The Battle of Atbara, fought on 8 April 1898, was a decisive engagement of the Mahdist War between an Anglo-Egyptian army under Herbert Kitchener and the forces of the Mahdist Sudanese state commanded by the Emir Mahmud Ahmad. The battle took place near the confluence of the Nile and the Atbara River in northern Sudan.

Background

Kitchener, Sirdar of the Egyptian Army, was advancing south to reconquer Sudan from the Mahdists. Mahmud Ahmad led an army of some 15,000 men north to confront him, establishing a fortified camp, or zariba, near the Atbara River. After weeks of manoeuvre, Kitchener marched overnight to attack the position at dawn on Good Friday, 8 April 1898.

The battle

Following a brief artillery bombardment, the Anglo-Egyptian infantry — British and Egyptian and Sudanese battalions — stormed the zariba. Inside, the defenders had dug a complex system of trenches and rifle pits, but the attackers broke through after fierce hand-to-hand fighting. Mahmud's army was routed, suffering several thousand killed, and Mahmud himself was captured hiding in a hut.

Aftermath

The victory opened the road to Omdurman and Khartoum, where Kitchener destroyed the remaining Mahdist army five months later at the Battle of Omdurman, completing the reconquest of Sudan.
