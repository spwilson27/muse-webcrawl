Transportation Security Administration
Abbreviation: TSA
Formed: 19 November 2001
Parent agency: U.S. Department of Homeland Security
Role: Security of U.S. transportation systems, esp. airport screening

The Transportation Security Administration (TSA) is an agency of the United States Department of Homeland Security responsible for the security of transportation systems within and connecting to the United States.

History

The TSA was created by the Aviation and Transportation Security Act, signed on 19 November 2001 in response to the September 11 attacks, which federalised passenger and baggage screening at U.S. airports. It was initially placed in the Department of Transportation and moved to the new Department of Homeland Security in 2003.

Operations

TSA Transportation Security Officers screen more than two million passengers a day at about 450 airports. The agency also oversees the Federal Air Marshal Service, canine explosive-detection teams, and security programmes for rail, pipelines and ports, and runs the TSA PreCheck expedited screening programme.
