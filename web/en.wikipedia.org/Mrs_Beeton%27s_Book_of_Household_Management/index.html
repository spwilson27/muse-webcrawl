Mrs Beeton's Book of Household Management

Mrs Beeton's Book of Household Management, also published as Beeton's Book of Household Management, is an extensive guide to running a household in Victorian Britain, written by Isabella Beeton and first published in book form in 1861. It is one of the most famous cookery and domestic-management books ever published in Britain.

The book ran to some 1,112 pages in its first edition and contained nearly 2,000 recipes, alongside advice on every aspect of domestic life.

Publication

The text first appeared in 24 monthly instalments in The Englishwoman's Domestic Magazine, published by Isabella's husband Samuel Beeton, beginning in 1859. The collected volume followed in 1861. Isabella Beeton died in 1865 at the age of 28, but the book was repeatedly revised and expanded by later editors and remained in print in updated editions well into the twentieth century.

Contents

Besides recipes — most of them, as was customary at the time, adapted from earlier authors such as Eliza Acton — the book gives instructions on the duties of the housekeeper, cook, kitchen-maid, butler, footman, nursemaid and other servants, and advice on entertaining, children, the sickroom, legal matters and household accounts. Each recipe lists its ingredients, mode of preparation, cooking time, cost and number of servings, a format that became the standard template for cookery writing.

The famous instruction 'First catch your hare', often attributed to the book, in fact derives from an earlier work by Hannah Glasse.

Legacy

'Mrs Beeton' became a byword for household authority, and the original 1861 edition is still reprinted and studied both as a practical cookery book and as a primary document of middle-class Victorian life.
