Montparnasse

Montparnasse is a neighbourhood on the Left Bank of Paris, France, spanning parts of the 6th, 14th, and 15th arrondissements. The name derives from Mount Parnassus: seventeenth-century students mockingly nicknamed the hillock of rubble in the area after the Greek mountain sacred to the Muses. The district is famed as the centre of Parisian artistic and intellectual life in the early twentieth century.

Montparnasse
Location: Left Bank, Paris
Landmarks: Tour Montparnasse, Gare Montparnasse, Montparnasse Cemetery

Artistic heyday

Before the First World War, artists began leaving Montmartre for Montparnasse's cheaper studios and lively cafés. In the interwar years — les années folles — the quarter around the Boulevard du Montparnasse and Boulevard Raspail became the heart of modern art and letters. Cafés such as Le Dôme, La Rotonde, La Coupole, and Le Sélect hosted Picasso, Modigliani, Chagall, Man Ray, Kiki de Montparnasse, Hemingway, Fitzgerald, and James Joyce. The academies of the Rue de la Grande-Chaumière taught generations of foreign art students.

Landmarks

The district is dominated by the Tour Montparnasse (1973), long Paris's only skyscraper and widely criticised for its intrusion into the skyline. The Gare Montparnasse serves Brittany and western France — and was the scene of the famous 1895 derailment in which a locomotive burst through the station's glass front. Montparnasse Cemetery holds the graves of Baudelaire, Sartre, Simone de Beauvoir, Beckett, and Maupassant, among many others.
