Pollux is the brightest star in the constellation of Gemini. It has the Bayer designation β Geminorum, which is Latinised to Beta Geminorum and abbreviated Beta Gem or β Gem. It is a red giant star of spectral type K0 III, located about 34 light-years from the Sun, making it the closest red giant to Earth.

With an apparent visual magnitude of 1.14, Pollux is the 17th-brightest star in the night sky and is slightly brighter than its "twin" Castor (α Geminorum), despite its Beta designation. The star is about twice the mass of the Sun and roughly nine times its radius, with a luminosity some 40 times solar. Its name refers to Pollux, one of the Dioscuri twins of Greek and Roman mythology.

Planetary system

In 2006 observations confirmed the existence of an exoplanet, Pollux b (later named Thestias), orbiting the star with a period of about 590 days and a minimum mass roughly 2.3 times that of Jupiter. The planet was detected by radial velocity measurements that had first been reported in the 1990s but were initially suspected to arise from stellar activity rather than a planet.
