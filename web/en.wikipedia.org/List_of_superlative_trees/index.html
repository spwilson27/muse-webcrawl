List of superlative trees

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

This list of superlative trees records record-holding individual trees and tree species by height, trunk volume, girth, age, and other measures. Records are determined by direct measurement, typically with laser rangefinders and tape drops by specialist climbers, and by dendrochronology (tree-ring counting) or radiocarbon dating for age.

Tallest

  • Hyperion — a coast redwood (Sequoia sempervirens) in Redwood National Park, California, measured at about 115.9 m (380 ft); the tallest known living tree. Its exact location is kept secret to protect it.
  • The tallest known tropical tree is Menara, a yellow meranti in Sabah, Malaysia, about 100.8 m.
  • The tallest known flowering plant overall is a mountain ash (Eucalyptus regnans) called Centurion in Tasmania, about 100 m.

Largest by volume

  • General Sherman — a giant sequoia in Sequoia National Park, California; the largest known living single-stem tree by trunk volume, about 1,487 m³, standing 83.8 m tall.
  • General Grant, another giant sequoia, is the second largest by volume.

Greatest girth

  • Árbol del Tule — a Montezuma cypress in Oaxaca, Mexico, with a trunk circumference of roughly 42 m measured around its fluted base; often cited as the stoutest tree in the world.

Oldest

  • Methuselah — a Great Basin bristlecone pine in California's White Mountains, dated to roughly 4,850 years old; for a time an older tree nicknamed Prometheus (about 4,900 years) held the record until it was cut down in 1964.
  • The oldest clonal organism is Pando, a quaking aspen colony in Utah whose root system may be many thousands of years old, though individual stems are much younger.
  • Old Tjikko, a Norway spruce in Sweden, has a root system dated to about 9,550 years.
