Flaming Gorge Reservoir is a reservoir in the western United States, on the Green River in southwestern Wyoming and northeastern Utah. It was created by the Flaming Gorge Dam, a 502-foot (153 m) concrete thin-arch dam built by the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation between 1958 and 1964 as part of the Colorado River Storage Project. The reservoir takes its name from the brilliant red canyon walls of the area, which John Wesley Powell's expedition of 1869 described as a "flaming gorge."

Geography

At full pool the reservoir extends about 91 miles (146 km) northward from the dam into Wyoming, with most of its surface in Sweetwater County, Wyoming, and its southern arms in Daggett County, Utah. The surrounding country is high desert of sagebrush and juniper, cut by the red sandstone and shale cliffs of the gorge. The area is administered as the Flaming Gorge National Recreation Area by the U.S. Forest Service (Ashley National Forest).

Uses

The reservoir stores water for use under the Colorado River compacts, provides hydroelectric power from the dam's three generators (about 152 megawatts of capacity), and supports flood control. It is a major recreation destination for boating, water skiing, and fishing; its cold, deep waters support kokanee salmon, lake trout (mackinaw) — including record-class fish — rainbow trout, and smallmouth bass. The tailwater fishery on the Green River below the dam is a renowned trout stream.

History

The dam was authorized by the Colorado River Storage Project Act of 1956, the same legislation under which the controversial Echo Park Dam inside Dinosaur National Monument was dropped — a landmark in the American environmental movement. Construction required building the new town of Dutch John, Utah, for workers. The dam was dedicated by President Lyndon B. Johnson in 1964.
