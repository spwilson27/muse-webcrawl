Cyclone Giselle

Cyclone Giselle was a tropical cyclone that struck New Zealand on 10 April 1968, producing the strongest winds ever recorded in the country and contributing to the sinking of the inter-island ferry TEV Wahine in Wellington Harbour, in which 53 people died. It remains one of New Zealand's most significant weather disasters.

Meteorological history

Giselle formed as a tropical cyclone in the Coral Sea region and moved south-east toward New Zealand, deepening as it combined with a cold front to become an intense extratropical low. As the storm approached Wellington on the morning of 10 April 1968, pressure fell rapidly and winds reached extraordinary speeds around Cook Strait.

The Wahine disaster

The Union Steam Ship Company's ferry Wahine, on its regular overnight sailing from Lyttelton to Wellington with 734 passengers and crew, entered Wellington Harbour in near-hurricane conditions. Battered by enormous seas near the harbour entrance, the ship struck Barrett Reef, lost steering and later capsized at Steeple Rock. Fifty-one people died that day and two more died later of their injuries, making it New Zealand's worst modern maritime disaster.

Impact

Beyond the harbour, Giselle caused widespread damage: roofs were torn off, trees and power lines brought down, and flooding and landslips occurred across parts of both main islands. Wind gusts around Wellington and in the Canterbury high country were among the highest ever measured in New Zealand.

Legacy

The disaster led to inquiries into ferry safety, weather forecasting, and harbour operations, and remains a defining event in Wellington's civic memory, commemorated at Wahine memorials around the harbour.
