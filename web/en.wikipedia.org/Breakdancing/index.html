Breakdancing

Breakdancing (breaking)
OriginThe Bronx, New York City, 1970s
CultureHip hop
PractitionersB-boys and B-girls
Olympic debutParis 2024

Breakdancing, also called breaking or b-boying/b-girling, is a dynamic style of street dance that originated among African American and Puerto Rican youth in the Bronx, New York City, during the 1970s as one of the pillars of hip hop culture. Dancers, known as B-boys and B-girls, perform to the "breaks" of funk and hip hop records extended by DJs such as Kool Herc.


A B-boy holding a freeze.

Technique

Breaking is commonly described as having four foundational elements: toprock (steps performed standing), downrock or footwork (moves on the floor with hands supporting the body), power moves (acrobatic spinning moves such as the headspin and windmill), and freezes (stylish poses balanced on the hands or other body parts).

History

The dance grew at Bronx block parties in the mid-1970s, was developed by crews such as the Rock Steady Crew and the Zulu Kings, and reached global audiences in the early 1980s through films such as Wild Style and Flashdance and through music videos and tours. Major international competitions include Battle of the Year and Red Bull BC One.

Olympic sport

Breaking appeared at the 2018 Summer Youth Olympics in Buenos Aires and made its full Olympic debut at the Paris 2024 Games, with competitions for B-boys and B-girls judged on technique, vocabulary, execution, musicality and originality.
