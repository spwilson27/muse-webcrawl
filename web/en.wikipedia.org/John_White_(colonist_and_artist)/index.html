John White (colonist and artist)

John White's watercolours are among the earliest European images of North America.
Bornc. 1540, England
Diedc. 1593, Ireland (probably)
Known forWatercolours of Native Americans; governor of the Roanoke Colony

John White (c. 1540 – c. 1593) was an English colonist, explorer and artist. He accompanied Sir Richard Grenville's expedition to the New World in 1585 and produced a celebrated series of watercolour drawings of the Native American peoples, plants and animals of the Carolina coast. In 1587 he returned as governor of Sir Walter Raleigh's colony on Roanoke Island, which became known as the "Lost Colony" after its inhabitants vanished.

Voyages to America

White sailed with Grenville's fleet in 1585, landing on the Outer Banks of present-day North Carolina. Working alongside the scientist Thomas Harriot, he documented the Algonquian-speaking peoples of the region, including the Secotan. His drawings — later engraved by Theodor de Bry and published with Harriot's Briefe and True Report in 1590 — shaped European perceptions of America for generations.

Governor of Roanoke

In 1587 White led a second colony of about 115 men, women and children to Roanoke. His daughter Eleanor Dare gave birth there to Virginia Dare, the first English child born in the Americas. Short of supplies, White reluctantly returned to England for relief, but the Spanish Armada delayed his return until 1590. He found the settlement deserted, with only the word "CROATOAN" carved on a post as a clue. The fate of the colonists remains unknown.

Artwork

About seventy of White's watercolours survive, held by the British Museum. They are remarkably accurate ethnographic records, depicting Algonquian dress, ceremonies, fishing methods and village life, as well as natural history subjects. Although de Bry's engraved versions Europeanised the figures, the originals remain primary documents of first contact in the region.

Later life

After 1590 White retired to Raleigh's estates in Ireland, where he wrote a final account of his voyages. He is thought to have died around 1593.
