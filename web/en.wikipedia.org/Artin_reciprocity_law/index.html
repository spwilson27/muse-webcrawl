Artin reciprocity law

The Artin reciprocity law, established by Emil Artin in a series of papers in the 1920s, is a general theorem in number theory that forms the central result of class field theory. It describes the abelian extensions of a number field in terms of the arithmetic of the field itself, generalising the quadratic reciprocity law of Gauss.

Statement

For a finite abelian extension of number fields L/K, the Artin map associates to each prime ideal of K not ramified in L a Frobenius element in the Galois group Gal(L/K), and extends to a surjective homomorphism from the ray class group of K (for a suitable modulus) onto Gal(L/K), with kernel corresponding to the norms from L. In its idele-theoretic form, due to Chevalley, the reciprocity map identifies Gal(Kab/K), the Galois group of the maximal abelian extension, with a quotient of the idele class group of K.

Significance

Artin reciprocity unifies and explains earlier reciprocity laws — quadratic, cubic and biquadratic — as special cases, and determines exactly how prime ideals split in abelian extensions. It was a milestone of twentieth-century number theory and a forerunner of the Langlands program, which seeks vast non-abelian generalisations.
