Barnes Wallis


A prototype bouncing bomb.

Sir Barnes Neville Wallis (26 September 1887 – 30 October 1979) was an English scientist, engineer, and inventor. He is best known for inventing the bouncing bomb used by the Royal Air Force in Operation Chastise (the "Dambusters" raid) in May 1943. Among his other inventions were the geodetic airframe and the earthquake bomb.

Early life and airships

Wallis was born in Ripley, Derbyshire, and left school at 17 to work in engineering, eventually joining Vickers. He designed airships, including the R80 and work contributing to the R100, and developed the geodetic (geodesic) lattice structure used in the Wellesley and Wellington bombers, which gave great strength for low weight.

Second World War

Wallis proposed attacking German dams with a spinning spherical bomb that would bounce across the water, skipping over torpedo nets, and sink against the dam wall. Despite initial skepticism, the weapon was developed and used by No. 617 Squadron RAF on the night of 16–17 May 1943 against the Möhne, Eder, and Sorpe dams. He went on to design the Tallboy and Grand Slam "earthquake bombs", among the heaviest conventional bombs used in the war.

Later work

After the war Wallis worked on swing-wing aircraft concepts, hypersonic flight research, and other advanced projects at Vickers and later British Aircraft Corporation. He was knighted in 1968. His wartime story was told in the 1955 film The Dam Busters, in which he was played by Michael Redgrave.
