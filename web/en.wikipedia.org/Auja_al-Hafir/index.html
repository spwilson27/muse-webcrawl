Auja al-Hafir

Auja al-Hafir was a road junction and settlement in the Negev desert, near the border between Mandatory Palestine and Egypt, located in the vicinity of the wells of Bir Hafir and along the route between Beersheba and the Sinai. The name "Auja" derives from nearby water sources, and the site guarded one of the few reliable water points in the northern Negev.

History

During the First World War the area changed hands as Ottoman and British Empire forces contested the Sinai and Palestine campaign. In December 1948, during the Arab–Israeli War, Israeli forces captured Auja al-Hafir in Operation Horev, securing the Negev. Under the 1949 armistice agreements the El Auja triangle became a demilitarised zone supervised by the United Nations, and its status remained a source of friction; Israeli forces seized the zone during the clashes of 1955.

Later use

The modern border crossing at Nitzana stands nearby, and the remains of the ancient Nabataean city of Nessana lie close to the site. The area today is used largely by the Israeli military as training ground and by border infrastructure.
