Java is one of the Greater Sunda Islands in Indonesia and the world's most populous island, home to more than half of Indonesia's population. It lies between Sumatra to the northwest and Bali to the east, bordered by the Java Sea to the north and the Indian Ocean to the south. The capital of Indonesia, Jakarta, is on Java's northwestern coast.

Geography

Java is predominantly volcanic in origin, with a chain of more than 100 volcanoes running east–west, of which about 45 are active. The highest is Mount Semeru (3,676 m). Volcanic ash has made much of the island's soil exceptionally fertile, supporting dense rice cultivation on terraced paddies.

History

Java was home to early human ancestors — "Java Man" (Homo erectus) fossils were found near Trinil in the 1890s. Hindu-Buddhist kingdoms such as Mataram built the great temples of Borobudur and Prambanan in the 8th–10th centuries. Islam spread along the north coast from the 15th century, and the Mataram Sultanate dominated the interior. Dutch colonial rule, consolidated under the Dutch East India Company and later the Netherlands Indies government, lasted until the Japanese occupation of 1942–45, followed by the Indonesian declaration of independence in 1945.

Demographics and culture

The island's largest ethnic group is the Javanese, followed by the Sundanese of the west and the Madurese in the east. Javanese language and culture, gamelan music, wayang shadow theatre and batik textiles are emblematic of the island. Java is the political and economic centre of Indonesia.
