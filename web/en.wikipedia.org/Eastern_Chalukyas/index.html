The Eastern Chalukyas, also known as the Chalukyas of Vengi, were a South Indian dynasty that ruled parts of the present-day Andhra Pradesh region for more than five centuries, from the 7th to the 12th century AD. They began as governors of the Chalukyas of Badami in the Vengi region and became independent when the Western Chalukya line declined.

History

The dynasty was founded by Kubja Vishnuvardhana, brother of the Badami Chalukya emperor Pulakeshin II, who conquered the coastal Andhra country around 624 AD. The kingdom, centred on Vengi between the Krishna and Godavari rivers, was strategically coveted by the Pallavas, Rashtrakutas, Cholas and Western Chalukyas, and was repeatedly invaded. A long period of succession disputes in the 10th century weakened the state, and from the late 10th century the Eastern Chalukyas became closely tied by marriage and alliance to the Chola empire; Kulottunga I, of Eastern Chalukya descent through his mother, united the two crowns in 1070 AD.

Culture

Early inscriptions were issued in Sanskrit, but from the mid-9th century Telugu became the language of court and literature. The poet Nannaya began the Telugu rendering of the Mahabharata under Rajaraja Narendra in the 11th century, earning the language its classical literary foundation. The dynasty patronised temple building at centres such as Bikkavolu, Draksharama and Samalkot, developing a style blending Deccan and Tamil elements, and supported both Hindu sects and Jainism.
