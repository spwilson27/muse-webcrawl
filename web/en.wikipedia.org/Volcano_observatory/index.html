Volcano observatory

A volcano observatory is an institution that monitors the activity of one or more volcanoes to forecast eruptions, assess hazards and protect nearby populations. Observatories combine seismic monitoring, ground-deformation measurements (GPS and InSAR), gas sampling, thermal imaging and visual observation.

Examples

The oldest volcano observatory is the Vesuvius Observatory in Italy, founded in 1841. The United States Geological Survey operates observatories including the Hawaiian Volcano Observatory (founded 1912), the Alaska Volcano Observatory and the Cascades Volcano Observatory, and coordinates with the World Organization of Volcano Observatories (WOVO). Communication between observatories, civil authorities and the public — including colour-coded alert levels — is central to their work, as demonstrated in the successful forecasts before the 1991 eruption of Mount Pinatubo.
