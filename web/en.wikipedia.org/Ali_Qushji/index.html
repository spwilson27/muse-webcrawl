Ala al-Dīn Ali ibn Muhammed (1403 – 16 December 1474), known as Ali Qushji (Persian: علی قوشچی; Turkish: Ali Kuşçu), was a Timurid and later Ottoman astronomer, mathematician and physicist. He is best known for his work at the Samarkand observatory under Ulugh Beg, his contribution to the Zij-i Sultani star catalogue, and his arguments for the independence of astronomy from natural philosophy, including a discussion of the possible rotation of the Earth.

The epithet Qushji derives from the Turkic term for "falconer", from his father's position as royal falconer to Ulugh Beg.

Life

Ali Qushji was born in Samarkand in 1403. He studied under Qadi Zada al-Rumi and Jamshid al-Kashi, and worked at Ulugh Beg's observatory, contributing to the Zij-i Sultani, completed in 1437. After Ulugh Beg's assassination in 1449, the political situation deteriorated, and Qushji eventually left Samarkand. He served as an envoy of the Aq Qoyunlu ruler Uzun Hasan to the Ottoman Sultan Mehmed II, and in 1472 he settled in Constantinople, where Mehmed appointed him professor at the Ayasofya madrasa. He died there in 1474.

Astronomy

In his treatise Concerning the Supposed Dependence of Astronomy upon Philosophy, Qushji argued that astronomy should be treated as a mathematical science independent of Aristotelian natural philosophy. He rejected the Aristotelian proof of the Earth's immobility, arguing that the rotation of the Earth could not be ruled out by philosophy and was consistent with astronomical observation — an argument later noted for anticipating, though not asserting, a view associated with Copernicus. His commentary on Nasir al-Din al-Tusi's Tajrid al-I'tiqad and his treatises on astronomy were widely taught in Ottoman madrasas.

Mathematics and language

Qushji wrote treatises on arithmetic and algebra, including works on the extraction of roots and on Muhammad's arithmetic. At Mehmed's request, he also wrote a Persian work on linguistics, the Unkud-üz-Zevahir. His works remained part of the Ottoman scholarly curriculum for centuries.
