Anatomical position

The anatomical position, or standard anatomical position, is the agreed reference posture used in anatomy and medicine to describe the locations of structures in the human body unambiguously. In this position a person stands upright, facing forward, with the feet together and flat on the ground, the legs straight, the arms hanging at the sides, and the palms of the hands facing forward (so that the radius and ulna are parallel, not crossed).

Purpose

Directional terms such as anterior and posterior, superior and inferior, medial and lateral, and proximal and distal are all defined relative to this position, which prevents confusion regardless of the body's actual orientation — for example, when a patient is lying face down (prone) or face up (supine). Likewise, the standard anatomical planes (sagittal, coronal/frontal, and transverse/axial) are defined for a body in this position.

Regional variants

Corresponding reference positions are defined for other animals, adjusted for their stance (for example, quadrupeds), and veterinary anatomy uses related but distinct terminology. In quadrupeds, "dorsal" and "ventral" serve where humans use posterior and anterior for the trunk.
