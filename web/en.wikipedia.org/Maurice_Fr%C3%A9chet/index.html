Maurice Fréchet

Maurice René Fréchet (2 September 1878 – 4 June 1973) was a French mathematician. He made major contributions to the topology of point sets and introduced the concepts of abstract spaces, including metric spaces, in his doctoral thesis of 1906. He also made important contributions to statistics and probability, and to functional analysis; the Fréchet derivative and Fréchet distribution are named after him.

Contributions

In his 1906 thesis, supervised in the orbit of Jacques Hadamard, Fréchet axiomatized distance and convergence in abstract sets, founding the theory of metric spaces. He extended differential calculus to infinite-dimensional spaces (the Fréchet derivative), studied compactness and functionals, and later worked on probability, Markov chains, and extreme value distributions.

Career

He held chairs at Poitiers, Strasbourg, and Paris, and was elected to the French Academy of Sciences. His work laid foundations used across modern analysis.
