Bomber

RoleAttack of ground and naval targets with bombs and missiles
ClassesStrategic bomber, tactical bomber, fighter-bomber
Notable examplesB-17 Flying Fortress; B-52 Stratofortress; B-2 Spirit; Tu-95

A bomber is a military combat aircraft designed to attack ground and naval targets by dropping air-to-ground weaponry (such as bombs), launching torpedoes, or deploying air-launched cruise missiles. Bombers have historically been classified as light, medium, and heavy according to their payload and range, and as strategic or tactical according to their mission.

History

Aircraft first dropped bombs during the Italo-Turkish War of 1911 and in the Balkan Wars. In the First World War, large bombers such as Germany's Zeppelin Staaken R.VI and Britain's Handley Page Type O raided cities. The Second World War saw massed strategic bombing campaigns with aircraft like the B-17 Flying Fortress, B-24 Liberator, Avro Lancaster, and B-29 Superfortress, culminating in the atomic bombings of Japan.

Cold War and after

Jet-powered strategic bombers — the B-52, Tu-95, and later stealth aircraft such as the B-2 Spirit — became legs of the nuclear triad. Tactical bombing largely passed to fighter-bombers and attack aircraft carrying precision-guided munitions, while heavy bombers gained roles in close air support and stand-off missile strikes.

Modern bombers

Only the United States, Russia, and China operate dedicated strategic bombers today, including the B-52, B-1B, B-2, Tu-160, Tu-95, and H-6, with new stealth bombers such as the B-21 Raider in development.
