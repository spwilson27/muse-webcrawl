Can Tho

Can Tho (Vietnamese: Cần Thơ) is the fourth-largest city in Vietnam and the largest city in the Mekong Delta region in southern Vietnam. It is a centrally governed municipality (one of Vietnam's five), located on the south bank of the Hau River, the larger southern branch of the Mekong, about 170 km southwest of Ho Chi Minh City. The city is famous for its network of canals, its floating markets — notably Cai Rang, the largest in the delta — and its role as the economic, educational and health-care hub of the Mekong Delta, often called the region's "western capital" (Tây Đô).

History

The area was known in Khmer as Prek Russei before Vietnamese settlement expanded southward (Nam tiến) in the 17th–18th centuries. Under the Nguyen dynasty the town grew as a river port and administrative centre. In the French colonial period it became a major rice-exporting hub of Cochinchina, and canals such as the Vinh Te were dug to open the delta to agriculture. During the Vietnam War, Can Tho hosted a large ARVN and US presence, including the airfield at Binh Thuy (Tra Noc). In 2004 the former Can Tho province was split: the city became a centrally governed municipality while the remainder formed Hau Giang province.

Geography and economy

Can Tho sits on flat alluvial land 0–2 m above sea level, crisscrossed by the Hau River and canals, and is the trading heart of the delta's rice bowl — Vietnam is among the world's largest rice exporters. Aquaculture (especially pangasius catfish), fruit orchards, food processing, and services dominate the economy. The cable-stayed Can Tho Bridge, opened in 2010, spans the Hau River and is the longest main-span cable-stayed bridge in Southeast Asia; its construction was marred by a 2007 ramp collapse that killed more than 50 workers. Can Tho University (founded 1966) is the delta's leading university. The city faces serious climate risks: saline intrusion, flooding and sea-level rise threaten the delta that feeds it.

Tourism

Visitors come chiefly for the early-morning floating markets of Cai Rang and Phong Dien, where wholesale boats advertise their produce on tall poles; for Ninh Kieu wharf and its pedestrian bridge; for the ornate Ong Pagoda and Binh Thuy ancient house; and for canal tours to fruit gardens and rice-paper and noodle workshops in the surrounding districts.
