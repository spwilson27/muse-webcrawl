The President of Israel (Hebrew: Nesi Medinat Yisra'el) is the head of state of Israel. The position is largely ceremonial and apolitical; executive power rests with the government headed by the prime minister. The president embodies national unity and serves as a moral figurehead above party politics.

Selection and term

The president is elected by an absolute majority of the Knesset in a secret ballot, held in up to two rounds. Since a 1998 amendment the term is a single, non-renewable seven years (previously five years, renewable once). Any Israeli citizen resident in the country is eligible.

Functions

Presidential duties include signing laws and treaties, accrediting diplomats, appointing judges, the governor of the Bank of Israel and other officials on recommendation, and granting pardons and commutations. After general elections, the president consults party leaders and tasks the Knesset member judged most likely to form a government with doing so—a role that can be consequential in fragmented parliaments.

List of presidents

The office has been held by Chaim Weizmann, Yitzhak Ben-Zvi, Zalman Shazar, Ephraim Katzir, Yitzhak Navon, Chaim Herzog, Ezer Weizman, Moshe Katsav, Shimon Peres, Reuven Rivlin and, since 2021, Isaac Herzog.
