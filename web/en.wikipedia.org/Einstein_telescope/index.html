Einstein Telescope

The Einstein Telescope (ET) is a proposed third-generation gravitational-wave detector, planned to be built underground with 10-kilometre arms in a triangular layout.

The Einstein Telescope (ET) is a proposed third-generation gravitational-wave observatory, under development by European institutions. The detector is designed to be around ten times more sensitive than current instruments such as LIGO and Virgo, allowing it to observe gravitational waves from almost the entire observable universe.

Design

Unlike the L-shaped detectors of today, the Einstein Telescope is planned as an underground equilateral triangle with three arms of 10 kilometres each. Each arm would house two interferometers — a "xylophone" configuration with one optimized for low frequencies and one for high frequencies — operating at cryogenic temperatures with the mirrors cooled to suppress thermal noise.

Site selection

Candidate sites have included the Euregio Meuse–Rhine region near the Netherlands–Belgium–Germany border and Sardinia in Italy, chosen for low seismic noise and favourable geology. Construction of an underground facility is expected to take several years, with the detector intended to begin observations in the 2030s.

Science goals

The Einstein Telescope aims to detect millions of gravitational-wave events per year, including binary black hole and neutron star mergers from the earliest epochs of the universe, test general relativity in the strong-field regime, and probe the equation of state of neutron stars and the nature of dark matter and dark energy.
