Stollen

Stollen is a fruit bread of nuts, spices and dried or candied fruit, coated with powdered sugar or icing, traditionally made in Germany during the Christmas season. The most famous variety is Dresdner Stollen from Dresden, whose name is protected within the European Union.

History

Stollen was first baked in Dresden in the 15th century as a fasting food for Advent; because Church rules forbade butter during the fast, the earliest loaves were made with oil and were famously tasteless. Saxon nobles petitioned the Pope for permission to use butter, granted in the so-called "butter letter" of 1490 after several years of negotiation. Thereafter the bread grew richer, acquiring the rum-soaked fruit, almonds and spices of the modern recipe. The loaf's folded shape is traditionally said to represent the infant Jesus wrapped in swaddling clothes.

Production

The heavy yeast dough contains a high proportion of butter and fruit — raisins, currants, candied orange and lemon peel — and sometimes a core of marzipan. After baking, the loaves are brushed with melted butter and thickly dusted with icing sugar; matured for several weeks, they keep well and are shipped worldwide. Dresden bakers must follow a certified recipe, and an annual Stollen festival, the Stollenfest, is held in the city before Christmas.
