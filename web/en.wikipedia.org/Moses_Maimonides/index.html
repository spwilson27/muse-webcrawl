Moses ben Maimon (1138–1204), commonly known as Maimonides and by the Hebrew acronym Rambam, was a medieval Sephardic Jewish philosopher, rabbi and physician. Born in Córdoba in Muslim Spain, he became one of the most prolific and influential Torah scholars of the Middle Ages and court physician to the Ayyubid rulers of Egypt.

Life

The Almohad conquest of Córdoba forced Maimonides's family into exile; after years of wandering in Spain and Morocco he settled in Fustat, Egypt, around 1168. There he served as head of the Egyptian Jewish community and as physician at the court of Saladin's vizier and later of Saladin himself, while writing his major works.

Works

His Commentary on the Mishnah introduced the famous Thirteen Principles of Faith. The Mishneh Torah (completed c. 1180) is a comprehensive fourteen-volume codification of Jewish law. His philosophical masterpiece, The Guide for the Perplexed (c. 1190, written in Judeo-Arabic), sought to reconcile Aristotelian philosophy with Jewish theology and profoundly influenced Jewish, Christian scholastic and Islamic thought; Aquinas cited him extensively. He also wrote treatises on medicine, logic and astronomy.

Legacy

The saying "From Moses to Moses there arose none like Moses" marks his stature in Jewish tradition. His legal code remains a standard reference, and the Guide continues to be studied by philosophers and theologians.
