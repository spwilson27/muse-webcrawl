Cairo

Cairo is the capital and largest city of Egypt and the Cairo Governorate, and home to more than ten million people in the city proper, with over twenty million in its metropolitan area — one of the largest in Africa and the Middle East. Known in Arabic as al-Qāhirah, "the Victorious", it is also nicknamed the City of a Thousand Minarets.

History

Cairo was founded in 969 AD by the Fatimid dynasty, near the earlier Islamic settlement of Fustat. It became the seat of the Ayyubid and Mamluk sultanates; Saladin built its Citadel in the 12th century. Under the Mamluks it flourished as a centre of learning and trade before Ottoman conquest in 1517.

Geography

The city straddles the Nile just south of the delta. Across the river lies Giza, with the famous pyramids on the desert plateau. Islamic Cairo, with its medieval mosques, madrasas and markets such as Khan el-Khalili, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Culture and education

Al-Azhar University, founded in the 10th century, is among the world's oldest universities. Cairo is the centre of Arabic publishing, film, and media, and hosts the Egyptian Museum with the treasures of Tutankhamun.

Modern city

Growth and congestion have prompted construction of a New Administrative Capital east of the city, while Tahrir Square became the focal point of the 2011 Egyptian revolution.
