Physician–patient privilege

Physician–patient privilege is a legal concept, related to medical confidentiality, that protects communications between a patient and their doctor from disclosure in legal proceedings. The privilege belongs to the patient, who may waive it, and it generally covers information the physician acquires while attending the patient in a professional capacity.

Scope and exceptions

The privilege is created by statute in most United States jurisdictions rather than by common law, and its details vary widely. Common exceptions include court-ordered examinations, proceedings in which the patient places their physical or mental condition at issue (such as personal injury suits), child abuse reporting, and cases involving a patient's threat of harm to others. Mandatory reporting laws for certain infectious diseases and wounds also limit confidentiality.

Rationale

The privilege encourages patients to disclose embarrassing or sensitive information fully and frankly, which is necessary for accurate diagnosis and treatment, without fearing that the information will be revealed in court.
