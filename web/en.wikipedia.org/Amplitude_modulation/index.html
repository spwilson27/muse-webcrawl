Amplitude modulation

Amplitude modulation (AM) is a modulation technique used in electronic communication, most commonly for transmitting messages with a radio wave. In amplitude modulation, the amplitude (signal strength) of the carrier wave is varied in proportion to the message signal, such as an audio signal.

Principle

An AM signal consists of a carrier at frequency fc and two sidebands at fc ± fm, so the bandwidth is twice the highest modulating frequency. The depth of modulation is expressed by the modulation index. Demodulation can be performed with a simple envelope detector — a diode and filter — which made early receivers (crystal sets and simple radios) cheap to build.

Broadcasting

AM broadcasting in the medium-wave band (roughly 530–1700 kHz in the Americas) was the dominant form of radio from the 1920s until FM became popular in the later 20th century. AM is also used in shortwave broadcasting, aviation communications, and amateur radio, as well as in single-sideband (SSB) variants that suppress the carrier and one sideband.

Advantages and disadvantages

AM receivers are simple and inexpensive, and AM signals travel long distances at night by ionospheric reflection. However, AM is susceptible to static and interference and offers limited audio fidelity compared with FM.
