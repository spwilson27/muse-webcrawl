Max Born

Max Born (11 December 1882 – 5 January 1970) was a German physicist and mathematician who was instrumental in the development of quantum mechanics. He also made contributions to solid-state physics and optics and supervised the work of a number of notable physicists in the 1920s and 1930s.

Work

At Göttingen, Born worked with Werner Heisenberg and Pascual Jordan on matrix mechanics. In 1926 he proposed the probabilistic interpretation of the quantum mechanical wave function — the Born rule — for which he received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1954, shared with Walther Bothe. With J. Robert Oppenheimer he developed the Born–Oppenheimer approximation in molecular physics.

Later life

Born fled Nazi Germany for Britain in 1933, became a professor at the University of Edinburgh, and became a British citizen. He was a grandfather of the singer Olivia Newton-John.
