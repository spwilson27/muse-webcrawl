Solid angle

In geometry, a solid angle (symbol: Ω) is a measure of the amount of the field of view from some particular point that a given object covers. That is, it is a measure of how large the object appears to an observer looking from that point. The point from which the object is viewed is called the apex of the solid angle, and the object is said to subtend its solid angle at that point.

In the International System of Units (SI), a solid angle is expressed in a dimensionless unit called a steradian (symbol: sr). One steradian corresponds to the area of a segment of unit radius sphere which has the same area in square units as the radius squared. The total solid angle around a point is 4π steradians, analogous to the way the total plane angle around a point is 2π radians. Solid angles can also be measured in square degrees.

Definition

The solid angle subtended by a surface patch is defined as the area of the projection of that surface patch onto a unit sphere centered at the apex, divided by the square of the sphere's radius — which, for a unit sphere, is simply the area of the projection. In practice, for a small surface element of area dA at distance r whose normal makes an angle θ with the line of sight, the solid angle is dΩ = (dA cos θ) / r².

Applications

Solid angles are used in physics wherever a flux radiates from a point: photometry (luminous intensity is measured per steradian), radiometry, antenna theory, and astrophysics all depend on the concept. The solid angle of the Sun as seen from Earth, for example, is about 6.8 × 10−5 steradians.
