Carl Anton Larsen

Born7 August 1860, Østre Halsen, Norway
Died8 December 1924, Ross Sea, Antarctica
OccupationWhaler, explorer

Carl Anton Larsen (7 August 1860 – 8 December 1924) was a Norwegian-born whaler and Antarctic explorer who made important contributions to the exploration of Antarctica, the most significant being the first discovery of fossils in Antarctica, for which he received the Back Award from the Royal Geographical Society.

Antarctic exploration

Larsen led an expedition aboard the Jason in 1892–94, exploring the east coast of the Antarctic Peninsula; Larsen Ice Shelf, Larsen Bay, and other features bear his name. He captained the Antarctic on the Swedish Antarctic Expedition of 1901–03 under Otto Nordenskjöld, surviving a shipwreck and winter on Paulet Island before rescue.

Whaling

In 1904 Larsen founded the whaling station of Grytviken on South Georgia with Argentine capital, establishing the first land-based whaling station in the Antarctic region and managing the Compañía Argentina de Pesca. He later became a British citizen and promoted modern pelagic whaling in the Ross Sea, where he died in 1924 aboard the factory ship Sir James Clark Ross.
