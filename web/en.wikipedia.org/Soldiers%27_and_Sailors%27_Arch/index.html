Soldiers' and Sailors' Arch

LocationGrand Army Plaza, Brooklyn, New York
Built1889–1892
ArchitectJohn H. Duncan
Dedicated21 October 1892

The Soldiers' and Sailors' Arch is a triumphal arch at Grand Army Plaza in Brooklyn, New York City, at the entrance to Prospect Park. It commemorates the defenders of the Union during the American Civil War.

History and sculpture

Designed by John H. Duncan (later architect of Grant's Tomb), the granite arch was built between 1889 and 1892. Sculptural groups were added over the following decade: Frederick MacMonnies created the bronze quadriga on top (1898) showing Columbia in a chariot, and the Army and Navy groups on the south pedestals (1901). Bas-reliefs inside the arch depict Presidents Abraham Lincoln and Ulysses S. Grant.

Landmark status

The arch was designated a New York City landmark in 1973. Its observation deck, long closed, was restored and reopened to visitors in 2024.
