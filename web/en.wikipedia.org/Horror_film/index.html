Horror film

A horror film is one that seeks to elicit fear or disgust in its audience for entertainment purposes. Horror films often explore dark subject matter and may deal with transgressive topics or themes. Broadly, they can be divided into the subgenres of supernatural horror, psychological horror, slasher films, body horror, folk horror, and many others. Horror also overlaps with the fantasy and thriller genres.

History

Georges Méliès created some of the first horror films in the 1890s. The 1920s brought German Expressionist classics such as The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari (1920) and Nosferatu (1922). In the 1930s, Universal Pictures launched its "Universal Monsters" cycle with Dracula and Frankenstein (both 1931). Hammer Film Productions revived gothic horror in Britain from the late 1950s.

Modern horror

The 1960s and 1970s introduced psychological and occult horror with Psycho (1960), Rosemary's Baby (1968), The Exorcist (1973) and The Texas Chain Saw Massacre (1974), while Halloween (1978) popularised the slasher film. Japanese horror (J-horror) achieved worldwide success in the late 1990s, and the 2000s saw found-footage hits such as Paranormal Activity. In the 2010s, critically acclaimed "elevated horror" films such as Get Out (2017) achieved both commercial and awards success.

Themes and analysis

Scholars often read horror as a reflection of social anxieties — war, disease, technology, the "other" — and note the genre's consistent popularity despite low budgets, since fear is inexpensive to produce and highly exportable across cultures.
