BINAP

BINAP (2,2′-bis(diphenylphosphino)-1,1′-binaphthyl) is an organophosphorus compound widely used as a chiral bidentate ligand in transition-metal catalysed asymmetric synthesis. It consists of a pair of diphenylphosphino groups attached to a 1,1′-binaphthyl backbone.

Applications

Ruthenium and rhodium complexes of BINAP, developed by Ryoji Noyori and co-workers, catalyse asymmetric hydrogenation of alkenes and ketones with high enantioselectivity. The industrial synthesis of menthol uses a Rh-BINAP catalyst, and Noyori shared the 2001 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for this work.

Structure

BINAP exists as (R) and (S) enantiomers because rotation about the bond joining the two naphthyl rings is restricted (atropisomerism). TolBINAP is an analogous ligand bearing p-tolyl groups on phosphorus.
