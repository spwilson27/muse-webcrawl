Bradford Hill criteria

The Bradford Hill criteria, also known as Hill's criteria for causation, are a group of nine principles for establishing epidemiological evidence of a causal relationship between a presumed cause and an observed effect. They were proposed by the English epidemiologist Sir Austin Bradford Hill in a 1965 address, in the context of debates over smoking and lung cancer.

The nine viewpoints are: strength of association, consistency, specificity, temporality, biological gradient (dose–response), plausibility, coherence, experiment, and analogy. Hill stressed they were considerations to aid judgement, not a checklist or necessary conditions; only temporality — cause preceding effect — is indispensable.

The criteria remain widely cited in epidemiology, public health, and evidence-based medicine.
