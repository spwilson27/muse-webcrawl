Prophet

Prophets are regarded as messengers of the divine in many religions.

In religion, a prophet is an individual regarded as being in contact with a divine being and as speaking on that being's behalf, delivering messages, teachings, or revelations to others. The word derives from the Greek prophētēs, "one who speaks forth." Prophecy is central to the Abrahamic religions and appears in many other traditions.

In the Hebrew Bible and Judaism

The Nevi'im (Prophets) form a major division of the Hebrew Bible, including figures such as Moses, Samuel, Elijah, Isaiah, and Jeremiah. The biblical prophets often functioned as critics of kings and of social injustice as much as predictors of the future; much of their message concerned morality, covenant faithfulness, and repentance.

In Islam and Christianity

In Islam, prophets (anbiyāʾ) are messengers sent by God to every nation; twenty-five are named in the Quran, and Muhammad is regarded as the final prophet, the "Seal of the Prophets." In Christianity, prophecy is also listed among the gifts of the Holy Spirit, and figures such as John the Baptist are honoured as prophets. Other traditions with prophetic figures include the Baháʼí Faith, Sikhism, and various indigenous religions.
