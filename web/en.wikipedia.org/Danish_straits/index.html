Danish straits

The Danish straits are the straits connecting the Baltic Sea to the North Sea through Denmark and Sweden. Historically, the Danish straits are the three channels: the Great Belt, the Little Belt and the Øresund (the Sound), which link the Baltic with the Kattegat sea area, in turn connected to the Skagerrak and the North Sea.

The three straits

The Great Belt (Storebælt) runs between the islands of Zealand and Funen and is the largest of the three, carrying major shipping and crossed since 1998 by the Great Belt Fixed Link. The Little Belt (Lillebælt) lies between Funen and the Jutland peninsula. The Øresund (Öresund in Swedish) runs between Zealand and the Swedish region of Scania and is crossed by the Øresund Bridge between Copenhagen and Malmö, opened in 2000.

History and law

Control of the straits long gave Denmark strategic leverage: from the 15th century until 1857 Denmark levied the Sound Dues on ships passing through the Øresund. The Treaty of Copenhagen of 1857 abolished the dues and made the Danish straits an international waterway. Modern passage is governed by long-standing treaty arrangements and the law of the sea.
