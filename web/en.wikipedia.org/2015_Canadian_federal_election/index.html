2015 Canadian federal election

The 2015 Canadian federal election was held on October 19, 2015, to elect members to the House of Commons of the 42nd Canadian Parliament. The Liberal Party, led by Justin Trudeau, won 184 seats, forming a majority government and defeating the incumbent Conservative government of Stephen Harper, which had been in power since 2006.

Background

The election was called following the dissolution of Parliament on August 2, 2015, beginning an unusually long 78-day campaign. It was the first election contested on new electoral boundaries, with the House expanded from 308 to 338 seats.

Campaign

The campaign centred on the economy, infrastructure spending, taxation, and the conduct of the Harper government. The Liberals began the campaign in third place in opinion polls but gained momentum after strong debate performances by Trudeau and a policy platform promising deficit-financed infrastructure investment.

Results

The Liberals won 39.5% of the popular vote and 184 seats. The Conservatives won 99 seats and formed the Official Opposition. The New Democratic Party, which had been the Official Opposition, fell to 44 seats. The Bloc Québécois won 10 seats and the Green Party one.

Aftermath

Stephen Harper resigned as Conservative leader on election night. Justin Trudeau was sworn in as the 23rd Prime Minister of Canada on November 4, 2015.
