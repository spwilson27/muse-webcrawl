Beluga caviar

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Beluga caviar consists of the roe (eggs) of the beluga sturgeon (Huso huso), found primarily in the Caspian Sea. It is the largest, rarest and most expensive type of caviar.

Characteristics

Beluga eggs are the largest of the commonly eaten caviars, ranging in colour from light silver-grey to black and prized for their delicate, buttery flavour and soft texture. The female sturgeon may take up to 20 years to mature and can live for more than a century, reaching over 4 metres in length historically.

Conservation and trade

Overfishing and poaching collapsed wild stocks, and the beluga sturgeon is listed as Critically Endangered; international trade is regulated under CITES. The United States banned beluga caviar imports in 2005. Farmed beluga and beluga hybrids (such as "bester") increasingly supply the legal market.
