Sultanate of Brunei

The Bruneian Empire at its greatest extent, around the 16th century.

The Sultanate of Brunei, historically the Bruneian Empire, is a Malay sultanate centred on the northern coast of the island of Borneo in Southeast Asia. According to local tradition it was founded in the 7th century and became an important trading state; by the 15th and 16th centuries, after its rulers embraced Islam, Brunei controlled much of coastal Borneo and the neighbouring Sulu Archipelago, and exerted influence over parts of Luzon, where it founded the settlement that became Manila.

Rise and extent

Brunei rose to prominence after the decline of earlier Bornean polities and as a trading partner — and at times tributary — of China. Under Sultan Bolkiah (r. 1485–1524), remembered as the empire's golden age, Brunei's thalassocracy reached its height. The visit of Ferdinand Magellan's expedition in 1521, recorded by Antonio Pigafetta, described a wealthy court at the water city of Kota Batu.

Decline

From the late 16th century the empire declined through conflict with Spain (including the brief Castilian War of 1578, when Spanish forces occupied Brunei), internal succession disputes, and the growing encroachment of European colonial powers and of the Sulu Sultanate. In the 19th century Brunei ceded Sarawak to James Brooke and lost Sabah to lease and sale, shrinking to roughly its modern size; in 1888 it became a British protectorate, surviving as the modern state of Brunei, independent in 1984.
