Coffin ship

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Coffin ship is the name given to the emigrant ships that carried impoverished passengers, most famously Irish emigrants fleeing the Great Famine (1845–1852) to North America, under conditions so overcrowded and unsanitary that many passengers died during the voyage. The term evokes the ships' resemblance to coffins for those who perished aboard; it was also applied to dangerously overloaded, unseaworthy vessels insured for more than their value.

The famine emigration

During the Great Famine, over a million Irish people emigrated, many on cargo vessels — often timber ships returning to Canada — that were hastily fitted with temporary berths. Passage was cheap (a few pounds) but conditions were appalling: steerage passengers were packed below deck with minimal ventilation, little or no food and water beyond rations, primitive sanitation, and no medical care. Typhus ("ship fever") and dysentery spread rapidly in the cramped holds.

Mortality

The worst year was 1847, "Black '47." Of roughly 100,000 emigrants who sailed to British North America that year, an estimated 17,000–20,000 died at sea or shortly after arrival in quarantine. At Grosse Isle, the quarantine station in the St. Lawrence River below Quebec City, more than 5,000 Irish emigrants died in 1847 and are buried there; thousands more died at the fever sheds of Montreal, Kingston and Toronto, and at Partridge Island off Saint John, New Brunswick. Voyages to the United States, where Passenger Acts imposed somewhat stricter standards on space and provisions, generally had lower mortality than the cheaper Canada route.

Reform

Reports of the horrors prompted new British and American passenger legislation tightening requirements for space, food, water and medical attendance, and raising penalties for overloading. The phrase "coffin ship" entered the language as a symbol of exploitation of the desperate, and the ships and their dead are commemorated in memorials in Ireland and Canada, including the National Famine Memorial at Murrisk, County Mayo, which depicts a coffin ship with skeletal figures in its rigging.
