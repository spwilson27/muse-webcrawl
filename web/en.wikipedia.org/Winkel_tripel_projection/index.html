The Winkel tripel projection (German tripel, "triple") is a modified azimuthal map projection of the world introduced by the German cartographer Oswald Winkel in 1921. It is constructed as the arithmetic mean of the equirectangular projection and the Aitoff projection, and is designed to minimise three kinds of distortion — area, direction, and distance — hence its name.

Properties and use

The projection is neither conformal nor equal-area but balances overall distortion, making it popular for general-purpose world maps. It shows the world in a slightly rounded rectangular frame with curved meridians; polar regions appear stretched but less severely than on the Mercator projection. In 1998 the National Geographic Society adopted the Winkel tripel as its standard projection for world maps, replacing the Robinson projection; it used the projection until switching to the Equal Earth projection in 2022. The projection cannot be inverted analytically with a simple formula; approximate inverses computed numerically are used in practice.
