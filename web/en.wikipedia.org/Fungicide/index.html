Fungicide

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fungicides are pesticides that kill parasitic fungi or their spores, or inhibit their growth. They are used chiefly in agriculture to protect crops against diseases such as mildew, rust, blight, and mould, which would otherwise cause large losses of yield; they are also used in medicine (where they are called antimycotics or antifungals) and in materials protection, for example in paints and timber.

History

Sulfur has been used against plant fungi since antiquity. A turning point came in 1885, when Pierre-Marie-Alexis Millardet in Bordeaux published the recipe for "Bordeaux mixture" — copper sulfate and slaked lime — which controlled downy mildew of grapevines and became the first widely used fungicide. Synthetic organic fungicides arrived in the twentieth century: captan and the dithiocarbamates in the 1940s–50s, systemic triazoles in the 1970s, and strobilurins in the 1990s.

Modes of action

Contact (protectant) fungicides remain on the plant surface and prevent spore germination; they are generally multi-site inhibitors and must be applied before infection. Systemic fungicides are absorbed and move within the plant, acting on specific biochemical targets such as ergosterol biosynthesis (triazoles), mitochondrial respiration (strobilurins and SDHIs), or tubulin assembly (benzimidazoles).

Resistance

Because many systemic fungicides act at a single site, fungal populations readily evolve resistance — a serious problem documented in cereal powdery mildew, potato late blight, and Botrytis. Management strategies include rotating or mixing modes of action, combining with multi-site protectants, and limiting the number of applications, coordinated internationally by FRAC (the Fungicide Resistance Action Committee).

Safety and environment

Fungicides are regulated as pesticides; residues on food are limited by maximum residue levels. Some older fungicides (such as mercury seed dressings) were withdrawn for toxicity. Effects on non-target fungi, including beneficial mycorrhizae, and on aquatic organisms are part of modern assessment.
