University of Königsberg

Type: University (defunct)
Active: 1544–1945
Location: Königsberg, Prussia (now Kaliningrad, Russia)
Name: Albertina

The University of Königsberg (German: Albertus-Universität Königsberg), known as the Albertina, was the university of Königsberg in East Prussia. It was founded in 1544 by Duke Albert of Prussia as a Protestant institution, modelled on the universities of Kraków and Wittenberg, and was one of the oldest universities in the German-speaking world.

History

Immanuel Kant studied and taught at the Albertina for most of his life, serving as its rector; his philosophy made the small university famous across Europe. Later scholars included the mathematician David Hilbert, physicist Gustav Kirchhoff and philosopher Johann Gottlieb Fichte. The city's seven bridges problem, solved by Euler, originated in Königsberg's geography.

Dissolution

The university was destroyed in the Second World War during air raids and the Battle of Königsberg in 1944–45, and it ceased to exist when the city was transferred to the Soviet Union and renamed Kaliningrad. The present Immanuel Kant Baltic Federal University claims continuity with the Albertina's traditions.
