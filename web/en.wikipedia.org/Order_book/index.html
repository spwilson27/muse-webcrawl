Order book

An electronic order book lists bids and offers by price level for a traded security.

An order book is the list of orders (manual or electronic) that a trading venue, in particular a stock exchange, uses to record the interest of buyers and sellers in a particular financial instrument. A matching engine uses the book to determine which orders can be fulfilled, that is, what trades can be made.

Structure

The order book contains buy orders (bids) and sell orders (offers or asks) arranged by price. The highest bid and lowest ask define the best market, and the difference between them is the bid–ask spread. The quantity available at each price level is called the depth of the book.

Order matching

Exchanges typically match orders by price–time priority: orders offering the best price are executed first, and among orders at the same price, earlier orders take precedence. Types of orders include market orders, limit orders, and stop orders, along with instructions such as fill-or-kill and iceberg orders.

Market data

Exchanges sell access to order-book data at different levels of detail. Aggregate displays showing only the best bid and offer are called Level 1, while full depth-of-book feeds are known as Level 2 data. A dark order book, or dark pool, keeps orders hidden from public display.
