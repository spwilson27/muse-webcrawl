Palestinian refugees

Palestinian refugees are citizens of Mandatory Palestine, and their descendants, who fled or were expelled from their homes during the 1948 Palestine war and the 1967 Six-Day War. Most live in the West Bank, Gaza Strip, Jordan, Lebanon, and Syria, many in refugee camps administered with UNRWA assistance.

Background

During the 1948 war, more than 700,000 Palestinian Arabs fled or were expelled from the territory that became Israel — an event Palestinians call the Nakba ("catastrophe"). In 1967 a further roughly 300,000 were displaced from the West Bank and Gaza. Unlike other refugee populations, Palestinian refugee status under UNRWA's definition passes to descendants.

UNRWA

The United Nations Relief and Works Agency (UNRWA), established in 1949, provides education, health care, and relief services to registered Palestinian refugees, whose number has grown to around 5.9 million.

Political issues

The "right of return" claimed by refugees and their descendants, based partly on UN General Assembly Resolution 194, remains one of the core final-status issues of the Israeli–Palestinian conflict, alongside borders, settlements, and Jerusalem.
