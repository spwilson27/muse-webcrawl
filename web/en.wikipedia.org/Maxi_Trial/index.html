Maxi Trial

The Maxi Trial (Italian: Maxiprocesso) was a criminal trial against the Sicilian Mafia that took place in Palermo, Sicily, between 1986 and 1992. It was the largest anti-Mafia trial in history, with 475 defendants.

Background

The trial grew out of the investigations of the Antimafia Pool led by judges Giovanni Falcone and Paolo Borsellino, and rested heavily on the testimony of pentiti (turncoats), above all Tommaso Buscetta, whose evidence first established the existence of the Mafia's governing Commission.

Outcome

In December 1987, 360 defendants were convicted, receiving a combined total of 2,665 years of prison; 19 bosses, including Michele Greco, received life sentences. The verdicts were upheld by the Court of Cassation in January 1992, confirming Buscetta's theorem on the unitary structure of Cosa Nostra.
