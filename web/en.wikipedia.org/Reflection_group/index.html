Reflection group

Reflections in the sides of a triangle generate a finite reflection group in the plane.

In group theory and geometry, a reflection group is a discrete group which is generated by a set of reflections of a finite-dimensional Euclidean space (or, more generally, of a sphere or hyperbolic space). The reflections are isometries that fix a hyperplane pointwise. Finite reflection groups are intimately connected with the classification of regular polytopes, root systems and Lie algebras, and they form a special class of Coxeter groups.

Finite reflection groups

The finite reflection groups in Euclidean space were classified in the 1930s by H. S. M. Coxeter. They include the symmetry groups of the regular polygons in two dimensions (the dihedral groups) and, in higher dimensions, the Weyl groups of the simple Lie algebras — of types A, B/C, D, E, F and G in the Dynkin classification — together with the symmetry groups of the icosahedron (type H), the hypercube families and a few exceptional examples. Every finite real reflection group can be realised as the symmetry group of a regular polytope or a related uniform figure.

Kaleidoscopes and chambers

The hyperplanes fixed by the reflections divide space into congruent chambers, like the mirrors of a kaleidoscope; the group acts simply transitively on the chambers, and each chamber is a fundamental domain. Choosing one chamber gives a set of simple reflections that generate the group with Coxeter relations, encoded in a Coxeter–Dynkin diagram. This picture extends to infinite discrete reflection groups acting on spheres and hyperbolic spaces, which arise in the theory of tessellations, automorphic forms and Lorentzian lattices.

Applications

Reflection groups appear throughout mathematics: as Weyl groups in Lie theory and algebraic geometry, as the symmetry groups of crystals and quasicrystals in physics, in singularity theory (where finite reflection groups correspond to the ADE classification), and in the study of hyperbolic manifolds and reflection orbifolds. Complex reflection groups, generated by pseudo-reflections in complex vector spaces, were classified by Shephard and Todd in 1954.
