Prosopography of the Later Roman Empire

Prosopography of the Later Roman Empire
Editors: A. H. M. Jones, J. R. Martindale, J. Morris
Volumes: 3 (in 4 parts)
Published: 1971–1992
Publisher: Cambridge University Press
Coverage: AD 260–641

The Prosopography of the Later Roman Empire (PLRE) is a multi-volume prosopographical reference work recording, so far as sources allow, all known persons who lived in the Roman Empire from AD 260, the accession of Gallienus, to AD 641, the death of Heraclius. It is a foundational tool for scholars of late antiquity.

Volumes

Volume I (1971), edited by A. H. M. Jones, J. R. Martindale, and John Morris, covers AD 260–395. Volume II (1980) covers 395–527, and Volume III, published in two parts (1992), covers 527–641; J. R. Martindale was the principal editor of the later volumes. Entries list offices held, family connections, and source references for each person, from emperors down to minor officials and churchmen.

Significance

Together with its Byzantine successor project, the PLRE underpins prosopographical study of the later Roman world, providing dated career records used to reconstruct administration, aristocratic networks, and chronology.
