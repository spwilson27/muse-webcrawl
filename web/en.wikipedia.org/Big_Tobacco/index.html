Big Tobacco

Big Tobacco is a term used to describe the largest global tobacco industry companies. The five largest are Philip Morris International, British American Tobacco, Imperial Brands, Japan Tobacco International, and China Tobacco. The term is often used critically, particularly in discussions of the industry's marketing practices and its history of denying the health risks of smoking.

History

The modern industry grew from 19th-century cigarette manufacturers such as R. J. Reynolds and the American Tobacco Company. In 1953, facing early studies linking smoking to lung cancer, the major US companies jointly formed the Tobacco Industry Research Committee and issued the "Frank Statement", beginning decades of public doubt-casting over the science of smoking and health.

Litigation and settlement

In 1998, the four largest US tobacco companies signed the Master Settlement Agreement with 46 states, agreeing to marketing restrictions and payments of hundreds of billions of dollars over 25 years to settle state claims for smoking-related healthcare costs. In 2006, a US federal court found the major companies liable under racketeering law for a decades-long scheme to defraud the public about smoking's dangers.

Regulation and decline

Smoking rates have fallen sharply in many Western countries under taxation, advertising bans, plain packaging, and smoke-free laws. The industry has responded by expanding in developing markets and investing in alternative nicotine products such as e-cigarettes and heated tobacco, even as public health advocates remain sceptical of its role in harm reduction.
