GNU Privacy Guard

GnuPG is the GNU project's free implementation of OpenPGP

GNU Privacy Guard (GnuPG or GPG) is free encryption software that is a complete and free implementation of the OpenPGP standard (RFC 4880 and its successor RFC 9580). It allows users to encrypt and sign data and communications, and features a versatile key management system and access modules for public key directories.

History

GnuPG was originally developed by Werner Koch, who released the first production version, 1.0.0, on 7 September 1999. Development was prompted in part by export restrictions on cryptography and by the patent encumbrance of early versions of Phil Zimmermann's Pretty Good Privacy (PGP). The German government funded early work including the Windows port. GnuPG 2.0 (2006) introduced a restructured architecture with a background agent for passphrase handling and support for the S/MIME standard in addition to OpenPGP.

Features

GnuPG uses hybrid encryption: data is encrypted with a symmetric session key (such as AES), and the session key is encrypted with the recipient's public key (RSA, ElGamal, or elliptic-curve schemes including Ed25519 and Curve25519). It supports digital signatures, smartcards and hardware tokens, SSH authentication via gpg-agent, and integration with email clients through tools such as Enigmail's successors and Gpg4win on Windows. Because it is a command-line tool, it is readily scripted and is used to sign software releases and Linux distribution packages.

Use and funding

GnuPG underpins much of the free software world's release-signing infrastructure. After years of precarious finances, a widely publicised funding appeal in 2015 raised substantial donations and corporate sponsorship, securing full-time development.
