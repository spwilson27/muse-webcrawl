Sōtō

Sōtō Zen (曹洞宗, Sōtō-shū) is the largest of the three traditional sects of Zen in Japanese Buddhism, alongside Rinzai and Ōbaku. It emphasises shikantaza — "just sitting" meditation — and the gradual, everyday expression of enlightenment.

Origins

The school descends from the Chinese Caodong tradition of Dongshan Liangjie and Hongzhi Zhengjue. It was transmitted to Japan by the monk Dōgen Zenji, who returned from China in 1227 and founded the head temple of Eihei-ji in Fukui Prefecture. His masterwork, the Shōbōgenzō, is a cornerstone of Japanese Buddhist philosophy.

Practice

Sōtō practice centres on zazen performed without striving for a goal, reflecting the doctrine that practice and enlightenment are one. Keizan Jōkin, the school's "second founder", founded Sōji-ji and helped spread the school among laypeople throughout Japan. Today Sōtō has roughly 14,000 temples in Japan and communities worldwide.
