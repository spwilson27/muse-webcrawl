Antitheism

Antitheism, also spelled anti-theism, is the opposition to theism. The term has had a range of applications; in secular contexts it typically refers to direct opposition to the belief in any deity, while in some settings it denotes opposition to organized religion or to the influence of religious institutions.

Distinctions

Antitheism differs from atheism: atheism is the absence of belief in deities, whereas antitheism adds the judgment that theism is harmful or undesirable and should be opposed. It also differs from anticlericalism, which opposes the political power of clergy without necessarily rejecting belief in God.

History

Explicit antitheist views appeared in the Enlightenment and in 19th-century materialist philosophy. In the 20th century, state atheism in communist states was often enforced antitheistically. In the 2000s, writers associated with New Atheism — among them Christopher Hitchens, who wrote that "religion poisons everything" — were frequently described as antitheists.
