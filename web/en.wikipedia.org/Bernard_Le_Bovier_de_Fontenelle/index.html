Bernard Le Bovier de Fontenelle

Bernard de Fontenelle
Born11 February 1657, Rouen, France
Died9 January 1757, Paris, France
OccupationAuthor, philosopher, scientist

Bernard Le Bovier de Fontenelle (11 February 1657 – 9 January 1757), normally cited as Fontenelle, was a French author and philosopher and a central figure of the early Enlightenment. Living to within a month of his hundredth birthday, he bridged the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries and helped popularise the new science of Descartes and Newton for a lay audience.

Works

His most famous book, Conversations on the Plurality of Worlds (1686), presents astronomy and the idea of inhabited worlds in the form of witty dialogues with a marquise, and was one of the most successful works of popular science of its age. His History of Oracles (1687) cast sceptical doubt on pagan miracles and, by implication, on credulity generally. In the Quarrel of the Ancients and the Moderns he sided with the Moderns.

Career

Fontenelle was elected to the Académie française in 1691 and became perpetual secretary of the Academy of Sciences in 1697, a post he held for over forty years; his annual éloges of deceased academicians became classics of scientific biography.
