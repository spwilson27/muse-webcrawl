Undavalli Caves

Undavalli Caves
LocationUndavalli, Guntur district, Andhra Pradesh, India
Date4th–5th century CE (attributed to the Vishnukundina kings)
TypeRock-cut cave temple
DeityVishnu (Anantasayana)

The Undavalli Caves, a monolithic example of Indian rock-cut architecture and one of the finest testimonials to ancient viswakarma sthapathis, are located in the village of Undavalli in Guntur district, Andhra Pradesh, about 6 km from Vijayawada, on the southern bank of the Krishna River. The caves are associated with the Vishnukundina kings of the 4th–5th centuries CE.

Description

Carved from a solid sandstone hillside, the main cave is a four-storeyed structure. The ground floor is an unfinished pillared hall; upper floors contain shrines and sculptures, and the third storey holds a large sculpture of Vishnu in a reclining posture (Anantasayana) carved from a single block of granite, along with reliefs of deities and scenes from the epics. Originally possibly Buddhist or Jain in origin, the caves were converted into a Hindu temple.

Setting

The caves are a nationally protected monument maintained by the Archaeological Survey of India and a popular tourist attraction in the Amaravati–Vijayawada region, set amid green surroundings overlooking fields along the Krishna.
