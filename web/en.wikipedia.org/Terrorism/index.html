Terrorism

Terrorism, in its broadest sense, is the use of violence against non-combatants to achieve political or ideological aims. The term is used primarily to refer to intentional violence by non-state actors during peacetime or in the context of war against targets including civilians, typically to instill fear in a population broader than the immediate victims. There is no universally agreed, legally binding definition of terrorism, and the concept has been subject to sustained academic, political, and legal debate. Scholars estimate that more than one hundred distinct definitions of the term exist.

Definition

The absence of a consensus definition complicates counterterrorism policy and international cooperation. Most definitions share certain elements: the deliberate use or threat of violence; the intention to spread fear ("terror"); and a political, religious, or ideological motivation. Distinctions are often drawn between terrorism and other forms of violence, such as guerrilla warfare, organised crime, and state violence, though the boundaries are contested. The phrase "one person's terrorist is another person's freedom fighter" is frequently invoked in debates over the term's application.

History

The word derives from the French terrorisme, coined during the Reign of Terror of the French Revolution (1793–1794), when it described state-enforced violence. In the nineteenth century the term came to be associated with revolutionary and anarchist movements; assassins of Narodnaya Volya in Russia and anarchist "propaganda of the deed" attacks in Europe and the Americas brought terrorism into international consciousness. The twentieth century saw anti-colonial campaigns, ethnonationalist separatism, and ideological terrorism of the far left and far right. The attacks of 11 September 2001, in which al-Qaeda operatives killed nearly 3,000 people in the United States, inaugurated a global "war on terror" that reshaped security policy worldwide. Subsequent years saw the rise of the Islamic State, and a growing threat from lone-actor and far-right extremist attacks.

Types and tactics

Terrorist tactics include bombing, suicide attacks, assassinations, hijackings, hostage-taking, mass shootings, and cyberattacks. Typologies commonly distinguish nationalist-separatist, religious, left-wing, right-wing, and single-issue (such as anti-abortion or environmental) terrorism. Suicide terrorism, though a minority of attacks, accounts for a disproportionate share of fatalities.

Counterterrorism

Counterterrorism measures include intelligence gathering, law enforcement, military intervention, financial disruption, and efforts at deradicalisation. Such measures are frequently debated in terms of their effects on civil liberties and their long-term effectiveness.
