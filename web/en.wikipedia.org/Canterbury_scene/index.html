Canterbury scene

The Canterbury scene (or Canterbury sound) is a loosely defined group of progressive rock and jazz fusion musicians, many of whom were based in or around the city of Canterbury, Kent, England, in the late 1960s and early 1970s. Bands associated with the scene include Soft Machine, Caravan, Gong, Hatfield and the North, and National Health.

Characteristics

Canterbury music typically blends the improvisation and harmonic sophistication of jazz with whimsical English psychedelia, quirky humour, and extended instrumental passages featuring prominent organ, fuzz bass, and virtuoso drumming. Lyrics often play with surreal imagery in the tradition of Lewis Carroll. Critics have described the sound as jazzier and more whimsical than the symphonic progressive rock of contemporaries such as Yes or Genesis.

History

The scene's roots lie in The Wilde Flowers, a mid-1960s Canterbury beat group whose members went on to form both Soft Machine (Robert Wyatt, Kevin Ayers, Hugh Hopper) and Caravan (Pye Hastings, Richard Sinclair). Daevid Allen's Gong, Steve Hillage's Khan, and the bands Egg, Hatfield and the North, and National Health extended the network through the 1970s, with musicians circulating freely between line-ups. Key albums include Soft Machine's Third (1970), Caravan's In the Land of Grey and Pink (1971), and Hatfield and the North's debut (1974).

Legacy

Though never commercially dominant, the Canterbury scene has been highly influential on later progressive and experimental musicians, and its recordings remain staples of progressive rock reissue programs.
