Bob Moses

Robert Parris Moses (23 January 1935 – 25 July 2021) was an American educator and civil rights activist. As field secretary of the Student Nonviolent Coordinating Committee (SNCC), he was a central figure in the voter-registration drives in Mississippi in the early 1960s and a principal organiser of the 1964 Freedom Summer. He later founded the Algebra Project, which promotes mathematics literacy for minority students.

Early life and education

Moses was born in New York City and raised in Harlem. He graduated from Hamilton College in 1956 and received a master's degree in philosophy from Harvard University, where he began doctoral studies before leaving to teach in New York and, in 1960, to join the civil rights movement in the South.

Civil rights work

In Mississippi, Moses worked to register Black voters in some of the state's most violently resistant counties, enduring beatings and arrests. He helped found the Mississippi Freedom Democratic Party, which challenged the all-white regular delegation at the 1964 Democratic National Convention. When SNCC turned toward Black Power and the movement fractured, Moses left the United States, worked as a teacher in Tanzania, and returned in the late 1970s.

The Algebra Project

In 1982 Moses founded the Algebra Project, arguing that competence in algebra is a gatekeeper to citizenship in a technological society, just as the vote had been in the 1960s. The project developed experiential mathematics curricula and community-organising approaches in schools across the United States. Moses received a MacArthur “Genius” Fellowship in 1982.
