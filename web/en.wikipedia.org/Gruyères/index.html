Gruyères

Gruyères (French: [ɡʁɥijɛʁ]; German: Greyerz) is a town in the district of Gruyère in the canton of Fribourg in Switzerland. The medieval town is an important tourist location in the upper valley of the Saane River, and gives its name to Gruyère cheese.

Geography and history

Gruyères stands on a small hill above the Saane valley at the foot of the pre-Alpine peaks, including the distinctive Moléson. The town was the seat of the Counts of Gruyères, whose line ruled the surrounding county from the 11th century until 1555, when the last count, Michael, went bankrupt and his lands were divided between Fribourg and Bern. The old town is largely traffic-free, with a cobbled main street lined by well-preserved medieval and early modern houses.

Sights

The principal landmark is the Castle of Gruyères (Château de Gruyères), dating from the 13th century, which today houses a museum covering eight centuries of regional history and art. The town also contains the H. R. Giger Museum, opened in 1998 in the Château St. Germain and devoted to the Swiss artist H. R. Giger, with a Giger-themed bar opposite. The Tibet Museum, opened in 2009, displays Buddhist art from the Himalayas. Nearby attractions include the demonstration cheese dairy La Maison du Gruyère at Pringy and the Cailler chocolate factory at Broc.

Demographics

The municipality has a population of roughly 2,200 (as of the early 2020s), most of whom speak French. Tourism is the dominant economic activity.
