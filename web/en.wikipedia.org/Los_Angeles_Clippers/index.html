Los Angeles Clippers

The Los Angeles Clippers are an American professional basketball team based in Los Angeles, competing in the National Basketball Association (NBA) as a member of the Pacific Division of the Western Conference. The franchise was founded in 1970 as the Buffalo Braves, became the San Diego Clippers in 1978, and moved to Los Angeles in 1984. Since 2024 the team has played at its own arena, the Intuit Dome in Inglewood.

History

The Braves featured early stars such as Bob McAdoo, who won the NBA Most Valuable Player Award in 1975, but the Clippers' years in San Diego and early Los Angeles were marked by losing seasons and frequent coaching changes, and the franchise long laboured under a reputation for dysfunction under owner Donald Sterling.

A revival began in 2009–2011 with the drafting of Blake Griffin and the acquisition of Chris Paul, forming the "Lob City" teams with DeAndre Jordan, which produced the most successful sustained run in franchise history, though without a conference finals appearance. In 2014, recordings of racist remarks by Sterling led to his lifetime ban from the NBA and the sale of the team to former Microsoft CEO Steve Ballmer for $2 billion.

Recent years

Under Ballmer, the Clippers signed Kawhi Leonard and traded for Paul George in 2019. In 2021, coached by Tyronn Lue, they reached the Western Conference finals for the first time in franchise history. The team has never won an NBA championship or reached the NBA Finals. In 2024 the Clippers left Crypto.com Arena, long shared with the Lakers, for the purpose-built Intuit Dome.
