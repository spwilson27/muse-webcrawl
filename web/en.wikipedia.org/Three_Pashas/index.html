The Three Pashas — Mehmed Talaat Pasha, Ismail Enver Pasha and Ahmed Djemal Pasha — were the triumvirate that effectively ruled the Ottoman Empire during the First World War, as leaders of the Committee of Union and Progress (CUP).

[Image: Talaat, Enver and Djemal Pasha — hero image placeholder]

Rise to power

After the CUP's 1913 Raid on the Sublime Porte, the three men dominated the Ottoman government: Talaat, Grand Vizier from 1917 and earlier Minister of the Interior, became the regime's chief political figure; Enver, Minister of War, drove the alliance with Germany and led disastrous campaigns such as the Caucasus offensive crushed at Sarikamish; Djemal governed Syria with sweeping powers and commanded the Fourth Army.

War and genocide

The triumvirate brought the empire into the First World War on the side of the Central Powers and presided over its defence at Gallipoli and eventual defeat. Their regime was chiefly responsible for the Armenian genocide of 1915–1917, in which roughly a million or more Armenians perished, as well as persecutions of Assyrian and Greek minorities.

Fates

The three fled Istanbul aboard a German vessel in November 1918 as the empire collapsed. Ottoman courts-martial sentenced them to death in absentia. Talaat was assassinated in Berlin in 1921 by Soghomon Tehlirian; Djemal was assassinated in Tiflis in 1922; Enver was killed in 1922 fighting in Central Asia.
