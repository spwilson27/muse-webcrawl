Fota Wildlife Park is a 100-acre (40 ha) zoo and wildlife park located on Fota Island in Cork Harbour, County Cork, Ireland. Opened in 1983, it is operated by the Zoological Society of Ireland, which also runs Dublin Zoo, and is one of Ireland's most popular visitor attractions, drawing several hundred thousand visitors a year.

Animals and design

The park is designed around open, naturalistic enclosures and island habitats. Free-roaming animals such as ring-tailed lemurs, wallabies, and peafowl share parts of the grounds with visitors, while large paddocks house giraffes, zebras, ostriches, bison, and antelope. The Asian Sanctuary, opened in phases from 2015, provides habitats for Sumatran tigers, Indian rhinos, and Asiatic lions. Other notable animals include red pandas, meerkats, penguins, and zebras, and a Tropical House with reptiles, amphibians, and butterflies.

Conservation

Fota is internationally known for its cheetah breeding programme, one of the most successful in Europe; the park has bred well over two hundred cheetah cubs and supplies animals to zoos worldwide. The park participates in European Endangered Species Programmes (EEPs) for many of its species and supports in-situ conservation projects. In 2015 the park recorded the birth of a cheetah cub to a first-time mother as part of the programme's continued success.

Fota House and gardens

The wildlife park shares Fota Island with Fota House, a Regency-era mansion of the Smith-Barry family, whose arboretum and gardens—planted in the 19th century with rare trees and shrubs from around the world—are managed by the Irish Heritage Trust and the Office of Public Works.
