Kinemacolor

Kinemacolor was the first successful colour motion picture process, used commercially from 1908 to 1915. Developed by George Albert Smith and promoted by Charles Urban, it was a two-colour additive system.

How it worked

Black-and-white film was shot and projected at 32 frames per second through alternating red and green filters, so successive frames recorded the red and green components of the image; the viewer's persistence of vision blended them. The system could not reproduce blue well and suffered fringing on moving objects, but its travelogues and spectacles — such as films of the 1911 Delhi Durbar — were sensations. Litigation over patents and the rise of rival processes, above all Technicolor, ended its commercial life.
