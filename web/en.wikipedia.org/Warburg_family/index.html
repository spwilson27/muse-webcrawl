Warburg family

The Warburg family is a prominent German and American banking family of German Jewish descent, noted for their varied accomplishments in biochemistry, botany, political activism, economics, investment banking, law, physics, classical music, art history, pharmacology, physiology, finance, private equity, and philanthropy. They took their name from the town of Warburg in Westphalia and were established in Hamburg from the late eighteenth century.

History

The family's Hamburg bank, M. M. Warburg & Co., was founded in 1798 by brothers Moses Marcus and Gerson Warburg and became one of Germany's leading private banks. In the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries the family divided into distinguished German and American branches: Max Warburg headed the Hamburg bank and advised the German government, while his brothers Paul and Felix emigrated to the United States, married into the Schiff and Loeb families, and became partners in Kuhn, Loeb & Co. in New York.

Notable members

  • Paul Warburg (1868–1932), a principal architect of the U.S. Federal Reserve System.
  • Felix Warburg (1871–1937), banker and philanthropist, founder of the American Jewish Joint Distribution Committee.
  • Max Warburg (1867–1946), head of M. M. Warburg & Co.; his bank was "Aryanized" under the Nazis and restored to the family after the war.
  • Aby Warburg (1866–1929), art historian and founder of the Warburg Institute in Hamburg, later moved to London.
  • Otto Heinrich Warburg (1883–1970), biochemist and Nobel laureate (of a related branch of the family).
  • Eric Warburg (1900–1990), who re-established the Hamburg bank after the Second World War.

Legacy

The Warburg Institute in London remains a major center for the study of art history and the classical tradition, and the family name persists in finance through institutions such as Warburg Pincus, founded with Eric Warburg's involvement in New York.
