Modal fabric

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Modal is a type of rayon — a semi-synthetic cellulosic fibre made from regenerated cellulose, traditionally from beechwood pulp. Developed in Japan in the 1950s and commercialised by the Austrian company Lenzing AG (which markets branded variants such as TENCEL™ Modal), it is classed as a high-wet-modulus rayon because it retains much of its strength when wet, unlike ordinary viscose rayon.

Production

Beechwood is pulped and the cellulose dissolved and extruded through spinnerets into fibres, in a modified viscose process using higher concentrations of certain treatments to yield stronger, more dimensionally stable fibres. Modern closed-loop production recovers and reuses most process chemicals and solvents, which manufacturers cite as an environmental advantage.

Properties and uses

Modal is soft and smooth, with good moisture absorption (about 50 percent more absorbent than cotton), breathability, resistance to shrinkage and a silky drape. It is widely used for underwear, pyjamas, bathrobes, bed sheets, towels and sportswear, often blended with cotton, elastane or other fibres. Fabrics are generally machine washable, though they can pill and lose shape under harsh treatment.

Environmental considerations

Because it derives from wood pulp, modal's sustainability depends on forestry practice and factory emissions. Regulators have required producers to substantiate "biodegradable" and compostability claims; modal is biodegradable under suitable conditions, but older viscose-type production was heavily polluting, and certification schemes such as FSC and PEFC are used to assure responsibly sourced pulp.
