20th Century Limited

The 20th Century Limited was an express passenger train operated by the New York Central Railroad (NYC) from 1902 to 1967 between New York City and Chicago, Illinois. It was the flagship train of the New York Central and became famous as one of the most luxurious trains in the United States, known for its red-carpet treatment of passengers at Grand Central Terminal.

History

The train began service on June 17, 1902, covering the roughly 960-mile route in 20 hours; the schedule was progressively shortened to 15½ hours by the late 1930s. It competed with the Pennsylvania Railroad's Broadway Limited for the New York–Chicago market.

Streamlined era

In 1938, industrial designer Henry Dreyfuss styled a new streamlined trainset hauled by the celebrated J-3a Hudson locomotives, an Art Deco icon. The train offered all-Pullman sleeping car accommodation, fine dining and observation cars.

Decline and end

Post-war competition from airlines and automobiles eroded ridership. The train lost its all-sleeping-car status in 1958, and the New York Central discontinued the 20th Century Limited on December 3, 1967, as part of a general restructuring of its passenger services.

Legacy

The train remains a symbol of the golden age of American rail travel and has featured in films such as North by Northwest. Its route is now served in part by Amtrak's Lake Shore Limited.
