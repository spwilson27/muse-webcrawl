Oral cancer

Oral cancer, also known as mouth cancer, is a cancer of the lining of the lips, mouth or upper throat. Most oral cancers are squamous cell carcinomas arising from the mucosal epithelium. Common sites include the tongue, the floor of the mouth, the lips and the gums.

SpecialtyOncology, oral and maxillofacial surgery
SymptomsNon-healing ulcer, lump, red or white patch in the mouth
Risk factorsTobacco, alcohol, betel nut, HPV, sun exposure (lip)
PreventionAvoiding tobacco and excess alcohol, HPV vaccination

Signs and symptoms

Early oral cancer may appear as a persistent sore or ulcer that does not heal, a lump, or red (erythroplakia) or white (leukoplakia) patches. Later symptoms include pain, loose teeth, difficulty swallowing, speech problems and a neck lump from spread to lymph nodes.

Causes and risk factors

The dominant risk factors are tobacco (smoked and smokeless), heavy alcohol consumption — which act synergistically — and chewing of betel quid or areca nut, an important cause in South and Southeast Asia. Human papillomavirus, particularly HPV-16, is strongly linked to cancers of the oropharynx, and ultraviolet light causes some lip cancers.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis requires biopsy of the lesion, with imaging such as CT, MRI or PET to stage the disease. Treatment typically combines surgery, radiotherapy and chemotherapy depending on stage and site. Five-year survival is much higher when the cancer is detected early, underscoring the value of dental and medical screening of suspicious lesions.
