Great Union

Date: 1 December 1918

Location: Alba Iulia, Transylvania

Event: Proclamation of the union of Transylvania with Romania

The Great Union (Romanian: Marea Unire) refers to the unification in 1918 of the Kingdom of Romania with the Romanian-majority regions of Bessarabia, Bukovina and Transylvania, creating the enlarged state often called Greater Romania. It is celebrated in Romania on 1 December, Great Union Day, the country's national day.

Background

Romania entered the First World War in 1916 on the Allied side, hoping to gain Transylvania from Austria-Hungary, but was defeated and occupied. In 1918, as the Russian and Austro-Hungarian empires collapsed, the Romanians of the neighbouring provinces moved to unite with the kingdom. Bessarabia's council, Sfatul Țării, proclaimed union on 9 April 1918; the General Congress of Bukovina voted for union on 28 November 1918.

Alba Iulia

On 1 December 1918 the Great National Assembly of Alba Iulia, attended by 1,228 delegates and a crowd estimated at 100,000, adopted a resolution proclaiming the union of Transylvania, Banat, Crișana and Maramureș with Romania. The resolution also promised democratic reforms, universal suffrage and minority rights. King Ferdinand I sanctioned the union by decree later that month, and the new borders were recognised internationally by the Treaty of Trianon (1920) and other post-war settlements.

Legacy

The Great Union roughly doubled Romania's territory and population and realised the long-standing national ideal of uniting all Romanians in one state. Its territorial settlement was partly undone in 1940, when Bessarabia and northern Bukovina were ceded to the Soviet Union and northern Transylvania temporarily to Hungary; Transylvania was recovered after the war, while Bessarabia (most of today's Republic of Moldova) remained outside Romania. The unification of 1918 remains the key reference point of Romanian national history, and the centenary was marked extensively in 2018.
