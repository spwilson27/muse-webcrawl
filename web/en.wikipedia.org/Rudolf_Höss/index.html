Rudolf Franz Ferdinand Höss (also spelled Hoess or Höß; 25 November 1901 – 16 April 1947) was a German SS officer and the commandant of Auschwitz concentration camp, the longest-serving in that role. Under his direction Auschwitz became the largest centre of mass murder in the Holocaust; historians estimate that at least 1.1 million people, the overwhelming majority of them Jews, were murdered there, most in gas chambers using Zyklon B.

Career

Höss served as a soldier in the First World War, joined the Nazi Party in 1922, and in 1923 was convicted of participating in a political murder, serving five years in prison. He joined the SS in 1934 and served at Dachau and Sachsenhausen concentration camps before being appointed commandant of the new Auschwitz camp in May 1940. There he oversaw the camp's expansion and, from 1941, the construction of the Birkenau extermination facilities. In 1943 he was transferred to the SS Economic and Administrative Main Office, but returned to Auschwitz in 1944 to direct the murder of some 400,000 Hungarian Jews.

Capture, trial and execution

After the war Höss lived under the alias Franz Lang and worked on a farm near Flensburg. British forces captured him in March 1946. He testified at the Nuremberg trials, giving a strikingly cold account of the killing operations, and wrote an autobiographical memoir in custody. Extradited to Poland, he was tried by the Supreme National Tribunal in Warsaw, sentenced to death on 2 April 1947, and hanged on 16 April 1947 on a gallows erected beside the crematorium of the former Auschwitz main camp.
