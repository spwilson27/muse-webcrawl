Santa Fe (Spanish for "Holy Faith"; full name La Villa Real de la Santa Fe de San Francisco de Asís) is the capital of the U.S. state of New Mexico. Founded by Spanish colonists in 1610, it is the oldest state capital in the United States and one of the oldest continuously inhabited European-founded cities in the country. It sits at the foot of the Sangre de Cristo Mountains at over 7,000 feet, making it the highest state capital.

History

The site was occupied by Tanoan and Keresan Pueblo peoples for centuries before the Spanish. Governor Pedro de Peralta founded the villa in 1610 and built the Palace of the Governors, now the oldest continuously used public building in the U.S. The city was abandoned during the Pueblo Revolt of 1680 and retaken by Diego de Vargas in 1692–93. Santa Fe became the northern terminus of the Camino Real and, after 1821, of the Santa Fe Trail from Missouri. It passed to the United States in 1846.

Culture

Santa Fe is famed for its Pueblo Revival and Territorial architecture, protected by strict building ordinances, and for a major art market — the third largest in the U.S. — centred on Canyon Road galleries and the annual Indian Market. Institutions include the Georgia O'Keeffe Museum, the Santa Fe Opera, and nearby Los Alamos. The city's motto-style slogan, adopted in the 20th century, is "The City Different."
