Komnenos

The Komnenos family (Greek: Κομνηνός, pl. Komnenoi) was a Byzantine Greek noble family and imperial dynasty that ruled the Byzantine Empire from 1081 to 1185, and the Empire of Trebizond from 1204 to 1461. Under the Komnenian emperors the empire experienced a notable military, economic, and cultural revival known as the Komnenian restoration.

Origins

The family came from the village of Komne in Thrace and rose through military service. Isaac I Komnenos briefly held the throne in 1057–59. His nephew Alexios I seized power in 1081, founding the dynasty proper.

Komnenian emperors

Alexios I (r. 1081–1118) stabilised the empire after the catastrophe of Manzikert, appealed to the West for military aid — inadvertently prompting the First Crusade — and reformed the currency and administration. John II (r. 1118–1143) consolidated gains in the Balkans and Anatolia and was praised for his piety and clemency. Manuel I (r. 1143–1180) pursued an ambitious, Western-oriented foreign policy and made Constantinople a great diplomatic centre. After Manuel's death the dynasty declined under the regency for Alexios II and the tyrannical Andronikos I, who was lynched in 1185.

Trebizond and later history

In 1204, just before the Fourth Crusade sacked Constantinople, Alexios and David Komnenos — grandsons of Andronikos I — founded the Empire of Trebizond on the Black Sea coast. Their descendants, styled "Grand Komnenoi" (Megaskomnenoi), ruled until the Ottoman conquest of Trebizond in 1461, the last Byzantine successor state to fall. Branches of the family, and later families claiming descent such as the Corsican Comneno and Demelliè families, persisted for centuries.
