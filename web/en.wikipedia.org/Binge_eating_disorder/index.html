Binge eating disorder

Binge eating disorder (BED) is an eating disorder characterised by recurrent episodes of eating large quantities of food, often very quickly and to the point of discomfort, accompanied by a feeling of loss of control and followed by distress, shame or guilt. Unlike bulimia nervosa, binge eating episodes are not regularly followed by compensatory behaviours such as purging, fasting or excessive exercise.

Signs and symptoms

During a binge, a person may eat much more rapidly than normal, eat until feeling uncomfortably full, eat large amounts when not physically hungry, eat alone because of embarrassment, and feel disgusted, depressed or very guilty afterwards. Diagnosis under the DSM-5 requires binge episodes at least once a week for three months, with marked distress, and without the recurrent compensatory behaviours of bulimia.

Causes and risk factors

BED is thought to arise from a combination of genetic predisposition, psychological factors such as low self-esteem, depression, anxiety and a history of dieting or weight stigma, and environmental influences. It is the most common eating disorder in the United States, affecting roughly 2–3% of adults, and occurs in both sexes and across weight ranges, though it is associated with an increased risk of obesity and metabolic problems.

Treatment

Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) is the best-supported psychological treatment; interpersonal therapy and dialectical behaviour therapy are also used. The stimulant lisdexamfetamine is approved in some countries specifically for moderate to severe BED in adults, and antidepressants may help some patients. Treatment also addresses associated conditions such as depression and type 2 diabetes.
