Custom car

A custom car is a passenger vehicle that has been substantially altered to improve its performance, change its aesthetics, or combine both. Custom cars are distinct from hot rods, though the two cultures overlap; hot rods are typically older, modified for speed, while customs emphasise styling.

History

Customising began in the United States in the 1930s and 1940s, particularly in Southern California, where builders such as the Barris brothers became famous for radical body modifications. The classic era of the custom car ran from the late 1940s through the early 1960s, centred on 1949–1951 Mercury coupes and similar models.

Common modifications

Signature techniques include chopping (lowering the roofline), channelling (lowering the body over the frame), sectioning, shaving door handles, frenched headlights, and elaborate candy-apple and metalflake paintwork. Lowriders, a related style, use hydraulic or air suspension to ride extremely low and to "hop".

Culture

Custom cars are showcased at shows such as the Grand National Roadster Show, and builders like George Barris, Ed Roth, and Chip Foose became prominent figures in American car culture.
