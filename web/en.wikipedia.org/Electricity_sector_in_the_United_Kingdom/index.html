Electricity sector in the United Kingdom

The electricity sector in the United Kingdom comprises generation, transmission, distribution, and supply of electricity in Great Britain and Northern Ireland.

The industry was nationalized after 1947 under the Central Electricity Generating Board and area boards, then privatized and restructured under the Electricity Act 1989. The National Grid operates the transmission system in England, Scotland and Wales; Northern Ireland has a separate system linked to Ireland's all-island market.

Generation mix

Coal, once dominant, declined to near zero, with the last coal plant closing in 2024. Generation now comes mainly from natural gas, wind (including major offshore wind farms), nuclear power, solar, biomass, and interconnectors to France, the Netherlands, Belgium, Norway, Denmark, and Ireland. The regulator is Ofgem, and policy targets net-zero emissions.
