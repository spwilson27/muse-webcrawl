Council of Florence

Date: 1431–1449 (Basel, Ferrara, Florence, Rome)

Convoked by: Pope Martin V; continued by Pope Eugene IV

Key outcome: Decree of union Laetentur Caeli (1439)

The Council of Florence is the seventeenth ecumenical council recognised by the Catholic Church, held between 1431 and 1449. It began at Basel, moved to Ferrara in 1438, and then to Florence in 1439, where a large Byzantine delegation — including Emperor John VIII Palaiologos and Patriarch Joseph II — negotiated union between the Greek and Latin churches.

Union with the Greeks

After months of debate over the Filioque, purgatory, the azymes (unleavened bread in the Eucharist), and papal primacy, the decree of union Laetentur Caeli was promulgated on 6 July 1439 in Florence Cathedral. The union was proclaimed in Constantinople in 1452 but was widely rejected by the Byzantine clergy and populace and never effectively implemented before the fall of the city to the Ottomans in 1453. Unions with the Armenians (1439) and Copts (1442) followed at Florence.

Conflict with Basel

The fathers who remained at Basel refused to dissolve, deposed Eugene IV, and elected the antipope Felix V — the last historical antipope. The rump council lingered until 1449, by which time most of its support had evaporated. The council is also notable for its role in the conciliarist controversy over whether a council is superior to the pope.
