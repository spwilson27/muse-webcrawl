The Italian Parliament (Italian: Parlamento italiano) is the national legislature of the Italian Republic. It is a bicameral legislature with 600 elected members: 400 deputies in the Chamber of Deputies and 200 senators in the Senate of the Republic, supplemented by a small number of senators for life. Italy is notable for its "perfect bicameralism": the two houses have equal powers, and a government must enjoy the confidence of both.

History

The Parliament traces its origins to the Parliament of the Kingdom of Italy established in 1861, itself descended from the Subalpine Parliament of the Kingdom of Sardinia. Under Fascism the elected chamber was replaced by the Chamber of Fasces and Corporations. After the Second World War, the Constituent Assembly drafted the republican constitution of 1948, which established the present two houses. A constitutional reform confirmed by referendum in 2020 reduced the number of parliamentarians from 945 to 600 beginning with the 2022 general election.

Functions

Parliament holds legislative power, votes confidence in the government, approves the budget, and elects, in joint session with regional delegates, the President of the Republic. Bills must pass both houses in identical text before promulgation. The Chamber of Deputies sits in Palazzo Montecitorio and the Senate in Palazzo Madama, both in Rome.
