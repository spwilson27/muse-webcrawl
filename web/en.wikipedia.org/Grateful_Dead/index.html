Grateful Dead

OriginPalo Alto, California, U.S.
Years active1965–1995
GenresRock, psychedelic rock, jam, folk rock, blues rock
Key membersJerry Garcia, Bob Weir, Phil Lesh, Bill Kreutzmann, Mickey Hart, Ron "Pigpen" McKernan, Robert Hunter

The Grateful Dead was an American rock band formed in 1965 in Palo Alto, California. Known for their eclectic style, which fused elements of rock, folk, country, jazz, bluegrass and psychedelia, and for their lengthy live improvisations, the band developed one of the most devoted fan followings in popular music — fans known as "Deadheads".

History

The band emerged from the San Francisco Bay Area folk and jug band scene; lead guitarist Jerry Garcia and lyricist Robert Hunter anchored a songwriting output that included "Truckin'", "Ripple", "Uncle John's Band", "Touch of Grey" and "Dark Star". The group was a central act of the 1960s counterculture, performing at Ken Kesey's Acid Tests and the Woodstock festival. Albums such as American Beauty and Workingman's Dead (both 1970) remain landmarks of Americana rock.

Live culture

The Dead played more than 2,300 concerts, and famously allowed — even encouraged — fans to record their shows, creating a vast culture of tape trading. No two concerts were alike: set lists varied nightly and songs were extended through improvisation. The band was among the top-grossing touring acts in the world despite modest record sales and only one top-10 single, "Touch of Grey" (1987).

Legacy

The band effectively ended with Garcia's death in 1995. Surviving members have since performed in offshoot projects including Dead & Company. The Grateful Dead were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1994 and received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award; their concert archive is held by the University of California, Santa Cruz.
