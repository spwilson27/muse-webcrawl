Isabeau of Bavaria

Queen consort of France: 1385–1422

Born: c. 1370, Munich, Duchy of Bavaria

Died: 24 September 1435, Paris

Spouse: Charles VI of France

House: Wittelsbach

Isabeau of Bavaria (or Isabelle; also Elisabeth of Bavaria-Ingolstadt; c. 1370 – 24 September 1435) was Queen of France from 1385 to 1422 as the wife of King Charles VI. Born into the powerful House of Wittelsbach, she married the young king at Amiens in July 1385 and was crowned in Paris the following month amid extravagant celebrations.

Regency and civil war

When Charles VI suffered his first bout of madness in 1392 — the beginning of recurrent psychotic episodes that plagued the rest of his reign — Isabeau became a central political figure, presiding over the council of regency and mediating between the rival Armagnac and Burgundian factions led by the king's brother Louis of Orléans and his cousin John the Fearless, Duke of Burgundy. Their feud erupted into civil war after Orléans' assassination in 1407. Following Agincourt (1415) and the Burgundian seizure of Paris, Isabeau was associated with the Treaty of Troyes (1420), which disinherited her son the Dauphin Charles in favour of Henry V of England.

Reputation

Victorian and later historians repeated hostile propaganda portraying Isabeau as frivolous, adulterous and treasonous; modern scholarship has substantially rehabilitated her, emphasising the near-impossible position of a foreign queen mediating during her husband's insanity and the Armagnac–Burgundian civil war. She died in Paris in 1435 and was buried in the Basilica of Saint-Denis.
