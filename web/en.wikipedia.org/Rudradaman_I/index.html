Rudradaman I (r. c. 130–150 CE) was a Saka ruler from the Western Kshatrapa dynasty of western India. He was the grandson of the famous ruler Chashtana and is regarded as the greatest king of the Western Satraps, restoring the fortunes of his house and ruling a large empire in western India.

Reign

Rudradaman recovered territories lost to the Satavahanas and twice defeated the Satavahana king Satakarni, sparing him, the Junagadh inscription says, because of a close family relationship. His dominion included Malwa, Gujarat, Sindh, northern Konkan and parts of Rajasthan and the Narmada valley.

Junagadh inscription

Rudradaman is famous for issuing the Junagadh rock inscription of about 150 CE — the first long inscription in classical Sanskrit. It records his repair of the embankment of Sudarshana Lake, originally built under Chandragupta Maurya and improved under Ashoka, after it was breached by a storm; the work was carried out by his governor Suvishakha without imposing extra taxes or forced labour.

Legacy

Though a foreigner of Saka origin, Rudradaman was a patron of Sanskrit culture and himself composed Sanskrit verses, reversing the earlier practice of his dynasty's Prakrit inscriptions. His coinage in the Indo-Greek tradition remained influential for centuries.
