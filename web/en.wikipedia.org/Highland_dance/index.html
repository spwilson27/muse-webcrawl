Highland dance

Highland dance or Highland dancing (Scottish Gaelic: dannsa Gàidhealach) is a style of competitive dancing developed in the Scottish Highlands in the 19th and 20th centuries, in the context of competitions at public events such as the Highland games. It is performed in traditional Highland dress, usually to the accompaniment of the Great Highland Bagpipe.

The dances

The repertoire is conventionally divided into Highland dances and national dances. The classic Highland dances are the Highland Fling, the Sword Dance (Gille Calum), the Seann Triubhas, and the Reel of Tulloch (or Strathspey and Reel). National dances include the Scottish Lilt, Flora MacDonald's Fancy, the Village Maid, and the Earl of Errol. Dancers are judged on technique, timing, and deportment, executing precise footwork, springing steps, and arm positions codified by governing bodies.

History and organization

Tradition holds that dances such as the sword dance originated as martial dances performed by warriors, though the modern competitive form was standardized much later. In Scotland, the Royal Scottish Official Board of Highland Dancing (RSOBHD, founded 1950, today the Royal Scottish Official Board) sets the championship steps and rules; worldwide, bodies such as ScotDance Canada and similar organizations in Australia, New Zealand, South Africa, and the United States administer exams and competitions. Dancers progress through graded levels from Primary to Premier, and World Championships are held annually at the Cowal Highland Gathering in Dunoon.

Costume and music

Male dancers wear the kilt with jacket, sporran, and hose; female dancers traditionally wear either a kilt-based outfit or the Aboyne dress for national dances. Shoes are soft leather pumps called ghillies. Most dances are performed solo for a piper.
