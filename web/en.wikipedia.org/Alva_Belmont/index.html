Alva Belmont

Alva Belmont (born Alva Erskine Smith; January 17, 1853 – January 26, 1933) was an American multi-millionaire socialite and a major figure in the women's suffrage movement. After her first marriage into the Vanderbilt family ended in a scandalous divorce, she used her fortune to build landmark mansions and later to bankroll the fight for women's voting rights.

Life

Alva Smith married William Kissam Vanderbilt in 1875 and commissioned Marble House in Newport, Rhode Island. Her 1895 divorce, nearly unheard of in her social circle, was followed by marriage to Oliver Belmont. Widowed in 1908, she devoted herself to suffrage, funding the National Woman's Party of Alice Paul and opening her Newport mansion for suffrage conferences. Her funeral saw suffragists carry a banner reading "Failure is impossible," a phrase associated with Susan B. Anthony that Belmont had adopted as the movement's motto.
