Logarithmic scale

A logarithmic scale (or log scale) is a way of displaying numerical data over a very wide range of values in a compact way — typically the largest numbers in the data are hundreds or even thousands of times larger than the smallest numbers. On such a scale, equal distances correspond to equal ratios rather than equal differences.

Properties and uses

Each major interval on a logarithmic axis represents multiplication by a constant factor, commonly ten (one "order of magnitude" or decade). Logarithmic scales are used for quantities such as sound intensity (decibels), earthquake magnitude (Richter scale), acidity (pH), stellar brightness, and in log–log and semi-log graphs in science and engineering economics.

Interpretation

Because equal steps mean equal percentage changes, a straight line on a semi-logarithmic plot indicates constant exponential growth or decay. A logarithmic scale cannot display zero or negative values, since the logarithm of zero is undefined and logarithms of negative numbers are not real.
