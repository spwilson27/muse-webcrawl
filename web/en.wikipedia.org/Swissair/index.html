Swissair

Swissair
Founded: 1931
Ceased operations: 2002
Hub: Zurich Airport
Parent: SAirGroup

Swissair (Swiss Air Transport Company Limited) was the national airline of Switzerland from 1931 until its bankruptcy in 2002. Long famed for punctuality, quality of service, and financial solidity — it was nicknamed "the flying bank" — the airline collapsed after an aggressive expansion strategy and the downturn following the September 11 attacks left its parent SAirGroup unable to pay its debts.

History

Swissair was formed in 1931 from the merger of Ad Astra Aero and Balair. It pioneered many European routes, introduced flight attendants in the 1930s, and after the Second World War grew into a major intercontinental carrier serving destinations worldwide from Zurich. In the 1990s the SAirGroup pursued a "hunter strategy," buying stakes in numerous foreign airlines, many of them loss-making.

Collapse

On 2 October 2001 Swissair's fleet was grounded when banks refused further credit, stranding thousands of passengers in an event that shocked Switzerland. Flights resumed briefly with government emergency funding, but operations ended on 31 March 2002. Much of the network was taken over by Crossair, which became Swiss International Air Lines, later part of the Lufthansa Group. The 1998 crash of Swissair Flight 111 off Nova Scotia, which killed all 229 people aboard, remains one of the airline's defining tragedies.
