The Indian Army is the land-based branch and the largest component of the Indian Armed Forces. With well over a million active soldiers plus substantial reserves, it is one of the largest standing armies in the world. Its primary mission is to ensure national security and national unity, to defend India from external aggression and internal threats, and to maintain peace and security within its borders.

History

The army traces its lineage to the sepoy regiments of the East India Company, formalised as the British Indian Army in 1895. Indian soldiers served in both world wars — some 2.5 million in the Second World War, the largest all-volunteer force in history. At independence in 1947, regiments were divided between India and Pakistan, and the army immediately fought the first Kashmir war. It subsequently fought China in 1962 and Pakistan in 1965 and 1971, and the Kargil conflict of 1999. In 1971 the army, in joint operations, achieved the surrender of over 90,000 Pakistani troops in the east and the liberation of Bangladesh.

Organisation

The army is commanded by the Chief of the Army Staff from Army Headquarters in New Delhi and is organised into geographic and functional commands — Northern, Western, Eastern, Southern, Central, South Western and the Army Training Command — each controlling corps, divisions and brigades. Its combat arms include the infantry, armoured corps, mechanised infantry and artillery, supported by engineers, signals, aviation and logistics services. The regimental system, inherited from the British Indian Army, organises infantry regiments around regional, linguistic or community identities — such as the Rajput, Sikh, Gorkha and Madras regiments — whose battle honours and traditions remain central to unit cohesion.

Role and operations

Beyond conventional defence of the borders with Pakistan and China — including the high-altitude Siachen Glacier, the world's highest battlefield — the army undertakes counter-insurgency operations, extensive United Nations peacekeeping deployments, and humanitarian assistance and disaster relief, from Himalayan earthquakes to floods. Specialist formations include the Para (Special Forces), mountain divisions and one of the world's largest corps of military engineers. Modernisation programmes cover artillery, air defence, armour, night-fighting and network-centric warfare, with growing emphasis on indigenous production.
