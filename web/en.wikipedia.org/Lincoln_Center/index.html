Lincoln Center


Lincoln Center for the Performing Arts is a 16.3-acre complex in the Lincoln Square neighborhood of Manhattan, New York City, home to the Metropolitan Opera, the New York Philharmonic and the New York City Ballet.

Lincoln Center for the Performing Arts (also simply known as Lincoln Center) is a 16.3-acre (6.6 ha) complex of buildings in the Lincoln Square neighborhood on the Upper West Side of Manhattan. It has thirty indoor and outdoor facilities and is host to more than 3 million visitors annually.

History

Built in the 1960s under an urban renewal program championed by Robert Moses and funded largely by John D. Rockefeller III, the center opened in stages from 1962 (Philharmonic Hall) to 1969. Its construction displaced the San Juan Hill neighborhood.

Constituents

Resident organisations include the Metropolitan Opera, the New York Philharmonic, the New York City Ballet, Lincoln Center Theater, the Juilliard School, Jazz at Lincoln Center and the School of American Ballet. Major venues include David Geffen Hall, the Metropolitan Opera House and the David H. Koch Theater around the Revson Fountain plaza.
