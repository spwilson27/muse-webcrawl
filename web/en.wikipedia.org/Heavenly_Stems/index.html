The Heavenly Stems or Celestial Stems (Chinese: 天干, tiāngān) are a system of ten ordinal signs used in China and other East Asian cultures, dating back to the Shang dynasty. Combined with the twelve Earthly Branches, they form the sexagenary cycle used for recording years, days and hours.

The ten stems

The stems are: 甲 (jiǎ), 乙 (yǐ), 丙 (bǐng), 丁 (dīng), 戊 (wù), 己 (jǐ), 庚 (gēng), 辛 (xīn), 壬 (rén) and 癸 (guǐ). Each stem is associated with yin or yang and with one of the five elements (wood, fire, earth, metal, water), so the sequence runs: yang wood, yin wood, yang fire, yin fire, yang earth, yin earth, yang metal, yin metal, yang water, yin water.

Uses

Stems appear on Shang-dynasty oracle bones, including in the posthumous names of kings. In the traditional calendar, a year or day is named by pairing a stem with a branch; because 10 and 12 have a least common multiple of 60, the pairs repeat every sixty years. The stems are also used in Chinese astrology and feng shui, in naming conventions, and in modern Chinese as ordinal labels equivalent to "A, B, C..." — for example in contracts (party A is 甲方, party B 乙方), school grades and chemistry nomenclature.
