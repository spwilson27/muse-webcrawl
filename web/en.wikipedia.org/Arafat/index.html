Arafat

Mount Arafat
Location~20 km southeast of Mecca, Saudi Arabia
Elevation~70 m hill (Jabal al-Rahmah)
SignificanceSite of the Wuquf, the central rite of the Hajj, on 9 Dhu al-Hijjah
Named featuresMount Arafat (Mount of Mercy), Masjid Namirah

Arafat (Arabic: عَرَفَات, ʿArafāt) is a plain about 20 kilometres southeast of Mecca in the Hejaz region of Saudi Arabia. It is the site where Muslim pilgrims perform the wuqūf — the "standing" vigil of prayer and supplication — on the ninth day of Dhu al-Hijjah, the second day of the Hajj pilgrimage. The ritual at Arafat is considered the pinnacle of the Hajj; according to a well-known hadith, "Hajj is Arafat," meaning that missing the standing at Arafat invalidates the pilgrimage.

The Day of Arafah

On the Day of Arafah, millions of pilgrims gather on the plain from noon until sunset, praying, reciting the Quran and asking for forgiveness. A sermon is delivered at Masjid Namirah, commemorating the Farewell Sermon of the Prophet Muhammad, who delivered it in the Uranah valley of Arafat during his final pilgrimage in 632 CE. After sunset, pilgrims proceed to Muzdalifah for the night.

Geography and features

The granite hill known as Mount Arafat or Jabal al-Rahmah ("Mount of Mercy"), about 70 metres high, stands on the plain; tradition holds that it is where Adam and Eve were reunited after their expulsion from Paradise, and where Muhammad delivered part of his Farewell Sermon. A white pillar marks the summit. The plain is outside the boundary of the Haram (the sacred territory of Mecca), so pilgrims must be present within Arafat's marked limits during the prescribed hours for the rite to be valid.

Theological significance

In Islamic tradition, the Day of Arafah is regarded as a day of exceptional mercy and forgiveness, and fasting on that day is recommended for Muslims not performing the Hajj. The gathering at Arafat is often interpreted as a rehearsal or symbol of the Day of Judgment, with all pilgrims dressed alike in simple white garments regardless of wealth or status.
