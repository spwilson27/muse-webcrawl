Music industry

The music industry consists of the individuals and organizations that earn money by writing songs and musical compositions, creating and selling recorded music and sheet music, presenting concerts, and the organizations that aid, train, represent, and supply music creators. Among its major components are record labels, music publishers, recording studios, producers and songwriters, live-music promoters and venues, collecting societies, and streaming and retail distribution platforms.

Recording studios, labels, publishers, and streaming services form the backbone of the modern music business.

History

Before recording, the industry centred on sheet-music publishing and live performance. The phonograph and gramophone records of the early twentieth century created a market for recordings, dominated by companies such as Victor and Columbia, and radio added a powerful promotional channel. The post-war era brought the LP, the 45 rpm single, and the rise of rock and roll; the compact disc drove a revenue boom in the 1980s and 1990s. Widespread internet file sharing, beginning with Napster in 1999, collapsed recorded-music sales in the 2000s, until licensed downloads and then streaming services such as Spotify restored growth in the mid-2010s. Streaming now supplies the great majority of recorded-music revenue worldwide, per the IFPI, while live performance and vinyl revivals contribute significant additional income.

Structure and major companies

The global recorded-music business is dominated by three major label groups — Universal Music Group, Sony Music Entertainment, and Warner Music Group — alongside independent labels and distributors. Music publishers administer songwriters' composition rights, while performing rights organizations such as ASCAP, BMI, and PRS collect royalties for public performance. Artists earn income from recording royalties, publishing, touring, merchandise, synchronization licensing to film, television, and advertising, and brand deals. Modern "360 deals" give labels a share of several of these revenue streams.

Issues

Recurring controversies include the fairness of streaming royalty rates, the split of revenue between platforms, labels, and artists, copyright infringement and sampling disputes, payola and chart manipulation, and the impact of artificial intelligence on composition and voice cloning. Ticketing for major concerts has drawn scrutiny over fees and dynamic pricing.
