VistA (an acronym for Veterans Health Information Systems and Technology Architecture) is a health information system deployed across the United States Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) healthcare facilities. It consists of over 180 clinical, financial, and administrative applications and has been widely regarded as one of the most successful large-scale electronic health record (EHR) implementations.

History

VistA originated in the late 1970s as the Decentralized Hospital Computer Program (DHCP), developed largely by VA clinicians and programmers — the so-called "Hardhats" — working with the MUMPS programming language. Because it was written by a U.S. federal agency, the core software is in the public domain and has been released under Freedom of Information Act requests, giving rise to variants such as WorldVistA EHR and OpenVista.

Architecture

The system is built on a MUMPS database and a client-server architecture; its best-known graphical interface is the Computerized Patient Record System (CPRS), written in Delphi. VistA supports order entry, results reporting, pharmacy, laboratory, radiology, and clinical documentation across the VA's hospitals and outpatient clinics.

Influence

VistA's longevity and clinician-driven design have been studied extensively; the system won recognition including a Smithsonian award and praise in health-policy literature. In 2018 the VA decided to migrate to a commercial EHR platform, a transition planned over the following decade, while VistA derivatives continue in use in hospitals in the United States and other countries.
