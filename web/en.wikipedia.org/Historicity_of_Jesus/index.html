Historicity of Jesus

Historical Jesus
Bornc. 6–4 BCE, Judaea
Diedc. 30 or 33 CE, Jerusalem
SourcesPaul, Gospels, Josephus, Tacitus

The historicity of Jesus concerns whether Jesus of Nazareth existed as a historical figure and how much of his biography can be established by historical methods. The overwhelming consensus of scholars in the field is that a Jewish preacher named Jesus was baptised by John the Baptist and crucified under Pontius Pilate in the early 1st century CE; views denying his existence altogether ("mythicism") are a fringe position.

Sources

The earliest evidence is the letters of Paul (c. 50s CE), who knew Jesus's brother James. The canonical Gospels (c. 65–110 CE) preserve extensive tradition about his teaching, baptism, ministry in Galilee, and death in Jerusalem. Non-Christian sources include Josephus's Antiquities (c. 93 CE), which mentions Jesus and, more securely, his brother James, and Tacitus's Annals (c. 116 CE), which records his execution under Pilate.

Methods and secure facts

Historians apply criteria such as multiple attestation, embarrassment (the baptism by John and crucifixion were awkward for early Christians, suggesting authenticity) and coherence with the Jewish context of Galilee. Widely agreed facts: Jesus was a Galilean Jew, a disciple-then-successor figure around John the Baptist, preached about the Kingdom of God, attracted followers, caused a disturbance at the Temple, and was crucified by Roman order.

The "quests"

Scholarship since the Enlightenment has moved through an "old quest", the scepticism of Bultmann's school, a "new quest" and a "third quest" emphasising Jesus's Jewishness. Reconstruction of his exact words and self-understanding remains debated, as does the historical basis of miracle reports, which historical method as such cannot adjudicate. See historical Jesus.
