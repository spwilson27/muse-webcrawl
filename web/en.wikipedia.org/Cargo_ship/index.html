Cargo ship

TypeMerchant vessel
PurposeCarriage of cargo by sea
Major typesContainer ship, bulk carrier, tanker, ro-ro

A cargo ship (or freighter) is a merchant ship that carries cargo, goods, and materials from one port to another. Thousands of cargo carriers ply the world's seas and oceans each year and handle the bulk of international trade; although passenger liners once carried cargo too, dedicated freighters dominate today.

Types

Common types include container ships, which carry standardized intermodal containers; bulk carriers for unpackaged cargo such as grain and ore; tankers for liquids such as crude oil and liquefied gas; roll-on/roll-off (ro-ro) ships for vehicles; refrigerated ships; and general cargo and multi-purpose vessels.

Size categories

Ships are grouped by the routes they can serve: Handysize, Handymax, Panamax (limited by the Panama Canal's locks), Capesize (too large for the canals, sailing via the capes), and ultra-large container vessels exceeding 20,000 TEU capacity.

Environmental impact

Shipping produces a few percent of global greenhouse-gas emissions and has historically burned high-sulfur residual fuel; International Maritime Organization rules, including the 2020 sulfur cap, have pushed the industry toward cleaner fuels, efficiency measures, and experimental wind and alternative-fuel propulsion.
