Dharana

Dhāraṇā (Sanskrit: धारणा) is the sixth limb of the eightfold path of yoga described by Patanjali in the Yoga Sutras. Commonly translated as "concentration" or "single focus", dharana is the practice of holding the mind on one fixed object or place, serving as the preliminary stage of meditation (dhyana) and ultimately of absorption (samadhi).

Dharana: one-pointed concentration on a chosen object.

Definition

Patanjali defines dharana in Yoga Sutra 3.1 as "the binding of consciousness to a [single] place" (deśa-bandhaś cittasya dhāraṇā). The chosen focus may be external, such as the tip of the nose, an image, or a candle flame, or internal, such as a chakra or a mantra. Through repeated practice, the fluctuating thoughts of the mind (citta-vritti) are progressively restrained.

Place in the eight limbs

Dharana follows pratyahara (withdrawal of the senses) and precedes dhyana (meditation) and samadhi (absorption). Together, the last three limbs are known collectively as samyama, the combined practice directed toward a single object, which Patanjali says yields insight into the object's true nature.

Practice

Because the mind naturally wanders, dharana is traditionally cultivated gradually: the practitioner returns attention to the object each time it drifts away. Sustained success in dharana, when the flow of attention becomes continuous and effortless, shades into dhyana.
