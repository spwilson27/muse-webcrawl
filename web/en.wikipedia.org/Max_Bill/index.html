Max Bill (22 December 1908 – 9 December 1994) was a Swiss architect, artist, painter, typeface designer, industrial designer and graphic designer. He studied at the Bauhaus in Dessau from 1927 to 1929 and became the leading figure of Swiss "concrete art", developing a mathematically ordered, non-representational art that influenced design across Europe.

Bill was a co-founder of the Ulm School of Design (Hochschule für Gestaltung Ulm) in 1953 and served as its first rector, designing its buildings; the school became a model for post-war design education. He designed the Swiss pavilion at several international exhibitions, created influential exhibitions and posters, and designed objects ranging from clocks and watches (for Junghans) to furniture such as the Ulm stool. He was also active in politics, serving on the Swiss National Council (1967–1971) and the Zurich city parliament.

Concrete art

In 1936 Bill formulated the principles of concrete art in a manifesto for an exhibition in Basel, arguing that works should be constructed entirely from pure plastic elements according to mathematical and geometric laws, without reference to nature. His sculptures — including the Endless Ribbon based on the Möbius strip — and his series of variations on a single theme exemplified this approach.
