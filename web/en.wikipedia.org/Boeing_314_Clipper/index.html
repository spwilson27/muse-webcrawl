Boeing 314 Clipper

The Boeing 314 Clipper was an American long-range flying boat produced by the Boeing Airplane Company from 1938 to 1941. One of the largest aircraft of its time, it had the range to cross the Atlantic and Pacific oceans. For its wing, Boeing re-used the design from its earlier XB-15 bomber prototype. Twelve Clippers were built, nine of which served with Pan American World Airways.

Design and development

Pan American had requested a flying boat capable of providing reliable transoceanic passenger service. Boeing's Model 314 used the wing of the XB-15, a large experimental bomber, powered by four Wright Twin Cyclone radial engines of 1,600 horsepower each. The aircraft pioneered the use of a double-deck layout: the upper deck housed the flight crew, while the main deck accommodated up to 74 passengers in day configuration or 40 in sleeping berths.

The Clipper's interior was famously luxurious, featuring a dining room, private compartments, dressing rooms and a bridal suite on some aircraft. Ticket prices were extremely high — a one-way trip from San Francisco to Hong Kong cost about $760 in 1939, equivalent to many thousands of dollars today.

Operational history

The first scheduled transatlantic passenger service began on 28 June 1939, when the Yankee Clipper flew from New York to Lisbon and Marseille. Pan Am's Clippers also operated the San Francisco–Hong Kong route via the "stepping stone" islands of Hawaii, Midway, Wake, Guam and the Philippines.

During the Second World War, the Clippers were pressed into military service, carrying high-priority passengers and cargo. In January 1943, the Yankee Clipper carried President Franklin D. Roosevelt to the Casablanca Conference, making him the first sitting U.S. president to fly. After the war, the development of long runways and land-based airliners such as the Lockheed Constellation and later Boeing airliners rendered the flying boats obsolete, and the last Clipper was retired in 1951.

No complete Boeing 314 survives today, although a full-scale replica is displayed at the Foynes Flying Boat Museum in Ireland.
