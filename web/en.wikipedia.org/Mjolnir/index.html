Mjölnir (Old Norse: Mjǫlnir, probably "the Grinder" or "the Crusher") is the hammer of Thor, the Norse god of thunder. Depicted as a short-handled war hammer of devastating power, it is Thor's principal weapon against the giants and the forces of chaos, the instrument with which he consecrates marriages, births, and funerals, and the symbol of his protection over gods and humans. During the Viking Age, small hammer pendants — "Thor's hammers" — were widely worn as amulets and religious symbols.

Mythology

According to Snorri Sturluson's Skáldskaparmál in the Prose Edda, Mjölnir was forged by the dwarven brothers Sindri (Eitri) and Brokkr as part of a wager with Loki, who had boasted that the sons of Ivaldi could not be surpassed. While Sindri worked the forge, Loki — transformed into a biting fly — stung Brokkr on the forehead as he worked the bellows, causing him to pause and swat at it; as a result the hammer's handle came out shorter than intended, making it a one-handed weapon. Despite the flaw, the gods judged Mjölnir the greatest of the treasures.

The hammer appears throughout the Eddic poems. In Þrymskviða, the giant Thrym steals Mjölnir and demands the goddess Freyja in marriage as ransom; Thor, disguised as the bride, recovers the hammer when it is laid in his lap to hallow the wedding, and slaughters Thrym and his household. The hammer could kill and also restore life: Thor uses it to resurrect his goats Tanngrisnir and Tanngnjóstr, consumed the previous evening. At Ragnarök, Thor will slay the world-serpent Jörmungandr with Mjölnir before taking nine steps and dying of its venom.

Thor's hammer amulets

Hundreds of Thor's hammer pendants, in silver, iron, amber, and other materials, are known from Viking Age Scandinavia and the Norse diaspora; the Scania pendant and the Købelev pendant (which bears a runic inscription reading "this is a hammer") are famous examples. The amulets are often interpreted as signs of Norse pagan identity, possibly in conscious rivalry with the Christian cross, and they feature in burials and hoards across the Viking world, including the great silver hoard finds of the period.

In modern culture

Mjölnir is a central element of Marvel Comics' and the Marvel Cinematic Universe's Thor, where the hammer bears an enchantment that only the worthy may lift it. The hammer also lends its name to military units, bands, and a crater-naming tradition, and powerfully built devices and software projects frequently adopt it as an emblem.
