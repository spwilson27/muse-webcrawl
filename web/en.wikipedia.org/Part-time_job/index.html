Part-time job

A part-time job is a form of employment that carries fewer hours per week than a full-time job. Workers are considered part-time if they commonly work fewer than 30 or 35 hours per week, depending on the jurisdiction and employer.

Characteristics

Part-time work is common in retail, hospitality, food service, and education. It is often taken by students, parents, semi-retired people, and those combining several jobs. Part-time schedules may offer flexibility but frequently come with fewer benefits, such as paid leave or employer-sponsored health insurance.

Regulation

Many countries regulate the treatment of part-time workers to prevent discrimination compared with full-time colleagues. In the European Union, the Part-time Work Directive requires that part-time workers not be treated less favourably than comparable full-time workers solely because they work part time, unless justified.

Trends

The share of part-time employment varies widely between countries and has grown with the service economy and flexible working arrangements. Some part-time work is involuntary, taken by workers who would prefer full-time hours.
