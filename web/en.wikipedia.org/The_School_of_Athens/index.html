Raphael, The School of Athens (1509–1511), Stanza della Segnatura, Vatican.

The School of Athens (Italian: Scuola di Atene) is a fresco by the Italian Renaissance artist Raphael. It was painted between 1509 and 1511 as part of Raphael's commission to decorate the rooms now known as the Stanze di Raffaello in the Apostolic Palace in the Vatican. It is considered one of the most famous works of the High Renaissance and a masterpiece of Renaissance humanism.

The fresco depicts a gathering of the great philosophers, mathematicians, and scientists of classical antiquity within an idealized classical architectural setting. At its center walk Plato and Aristotle, the two towering figures of Western philosophy, engaged in conversation.

Subject and figures

The painting groups dozens of figures representing the schools of thought of ancient Greece. Plato, depicted with the features of Leonardo da Vinci, holds his Timaeus and points upward toward the realm of ideas; Aristotle, modeled perhaps on the architect Bastiano da Sangallo, holds his Ethics and gestures horizontally toward the earth, symbolizing his empirical philosophy. Around them, other figures have been identified — some with certainty, others debatably — as Pythagoras, Euclid (drawn with the features of Bramante, stooping over a slate with a compass), Ptolemy, Zoroaster, Socrates, Diogenes (lounging on the steps), Heraclitus (whose brooding figure is a portrait of Michelangelo, added after Raphael saw the Sistine Chapel ceiling), Alcibiades, Averroes, Hypatia, and Raphael himself, who included a self-portrait at the far right edge beside his teacher Perugino.

Composition and architecture

The scene is set within a grand vaulted basilica-like space rendered in rigorous one-point perspective, with the vanishing point falling between Plato and Aristotle. The architecture, influenced by Bramante's designs for the new St. Peter's Basilica, features coffered barrel vaults, piers, and statues of Apollo and Minerva presiding over the arts and sciences. The fresco is one of four in the Stanza della Segnatura representing the branches of human knowledge: philosophy, theology (The Disputation of the Sacrament), poetry (The Parnassus), and law.

Legacy

The School of Athens has long been considered Raphael's masterpiece and the perfect embodiment of the classical spirit of the Renaissance. It remains an iconic image of philosophy itself, reproduced countless times in textbooks, universities, and popular culture.
