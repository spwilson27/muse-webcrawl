Guelders

Guelders (Dutch: Gelre; German: Geldern) is a historical county, later duchy, of the Low Countries, situated in what is now the Netherlands and Germany. Its territory corresponds broadly to the modern Dutch province of Gelderland together with parts of the German state of North Rhine-Westphalia, including the town of Geldern from which it took its name.

History

Guelders first emerged as a county in the eleventh century around the Wassenberg and Geldern area. It grew through marriage and purchase into four "quarters" — Nijmegen, Arnhem (the Veluwe), Zutphen and the Upper Quarter around Roermond and Geldern. In 1339 the county was raised to a duchy. Under dukes such as Charles of Egmond, Guelders long resisted Burgundian and Habsburg expansion, conducting the Guelders Wars into the sixteenth century, before Charles V incorporated it into the Habsburg Netherlands in 1543.

Later fate

During the Dutch Revolt the northern three quarters joined the Dutch Republic, while the Catholic Upper Quarter remained with the Spanish Netherlands. Subsequent partitions by the treaties of Utrecht (1713) and Vienna (1815) divided the Upper Quarter among Prussia, the Netherlands and other powers. The name survives today in the Dutch province of Gelderland and the German town of Geldern.
