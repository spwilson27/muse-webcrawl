Susanne Wenger

Susanne Wenger (4 July 1915 – 12 January 2009), also known by her Yoruba name Adunni Olorisha, was an Austrian-Nigerian artist, sculptor, and Yoruba priestess who became a central figure in the revival of traditional Yoruba religion and art in Osogbo, Nigeria.

Life

Born in Graz, Austria, Wenger studied art in Graz and Vienna and worked in Paris before travelling to Nigeria in 1950 with her husband, the linguist Ulli Beier. After their marriage ended, she remained in Nigeria, immersing herself in Yoruba culture and religion and becoming an initiated priestess of the orisha Obatala.

Osun-Osogbo Sacred Grove

Wenger is best known for her defence and artistic transformation of the Osun-Osogbo Sacred Grove, a forest sanctuary dedicated to the river goddess Osun on the outskirts of Osogbo. Working with local artists of the New Sacred Art movement, she created monumental cement-and-iron sculptures and shrines throughout the grove, and fought to protect it from logging, poaching, and encroachment. The grove was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2005, one of only two in Nigeria. Wenger lived in Osogbo until her death there in 2009.
