Infotainment

Infotainment (a portmanteau of information and entertainment), also called soft news, is a type of media that blends factual content with entertainment formats. The term is often used critically to describe the trend of news media prioritizing engaging, human-interest, celebrity and sensational stories over "hard news" about politics, economics and public affairs.

Infotainment blends news presentation with entertainment techniques to attract audiences.

History and criticism

The term emerged in the 1980s as television news increasingly adopted entertainment techniques — dramatic music, graphics, reenactments and personality-driven presentation — under competitive pressure from cable channels. Programmes such as Entertainment Tonight and tabloid-style newsmagazines exemplified the genre. Critics, following Neil Postman's Amusing Ourselves to Death (1985), argue that infotainment trivializes public discourse and displaces substantive reporting; defenders note that accessible formats can draw otherwise disengaged audiences toward current events, as with satirical news programmes.

Other uses

In the automotive industry, "infotainment" separately denotes in-car systems combining information (navigation, vehicle data) with entertainment (audio, video, connectivity), typically centred on a dashboard touchscreen.
