Northern Virginia Campaign

[Image placeholder: Northern Virginia Campaign]

The Northern Virginia Campaign, also known as the Second Bull Run Campaign, was a series of battles fought in Virginia during August and September 1862 in the American Civil War. Confederate General Robert E. Lee's Army of Northern Virginia defeated Union Major General John Pope's Army of Virginia.

Course of the campaign

After the Peninsula Campaign stalled before Richmond, Pope advanced into northern Virginia. Lee sent Stonewall Jackson's wing on a wide flanking march around Pope's right, destroying his supply depot at Manassas Junction on 27 August. Jackson then took up a defensive position along an unfinished railroad grade near the old Bull Run battlefield. Pope attacked Jackson repeatedly on 29 August, unaware that James Longstreet's wing had arrived on the field. On 30 August Longstreet launched a massive counterattack that crushed Pope's army at the Second Battle of Bull Run (Second Manassas).

Aftermath

Fighting at Chantilly on 1 September ended the campaign. Pope retreated to the defences of Washington and was relieved of command; Lee, victorious, crossed into Maryland, beginning the Maryland Campaign.
