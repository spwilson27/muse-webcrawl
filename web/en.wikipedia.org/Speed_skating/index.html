Speed skating

Speed skating is a competitive form of ice skating in which the competitors race each other in travelling a certain distance on skates. Types of speed skating are long track speed skating, short track speed skating, and marathon speed skating. In the Olympic Games, long-track speed skating is usually referred to as just "speed skating", while short-track speed skating is known as "short track".

Long track

Long-track races take place on a 400-metre oval. Distances range from 500 m to 10,000 m, skated in pairs against the clock, with skaters changing lanes each lap. The Netherlands has historically dominated the sport; famous champions include Sven Kramer, Ireen Wüst, Eric Heiden, and Bonnie Blair.

Short track

Short track races are held on a 111.12 m oval inside a hockey rink, with mass starts of four to six skaters, emphasising pack strategy and passing. Distances include 500 m, 1000 m, and 1500 m, plus relays.

Equipment

Long-track skaters use clap skates, whose blade detaches at the heel, allowing longer strides. Skinsuits and aerodynamic technique are crucial at speeds exceeding 55 km/h.

History

Ice skating originated in Scandinavia and the Netherlands; racing over frozen canals was popular from the 18th century. Speed skating has been an Olympic sport for men since 1924 and for women since 1960.
