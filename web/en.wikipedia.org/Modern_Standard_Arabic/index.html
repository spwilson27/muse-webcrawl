Modern Standard Arabic
Arabic: العربية الفصحى (al-ʿArabiyya al-Fuṣḥā)
Family: Afroasiatic → Semitic
Status: official language of the Arab League; liturgical language of Islam
Script: Arabic alphabet

Modern Standard Arabic (MSA) is the standardised, literary variety of Arabic used in writing and in formal speech across the Arab world. It descends from Classical Arabic, the language of the Qur'an and of classical literature, and differs from it mainly in vocabulary and some stylistic features rather than in grammar. MSA is one of the six official languages of the United Nations.

Use and diglossia

No community speaks MSA as a mother tongue. Instead, children acquire a regional colloquial variety (such as Egyptian, Levantine, Gulf, or Maghrebi Arabic) at home and learn MSA at school — a situation known as diglossia. MSA is the language of news broadcasts, newspapers, official documents, education and most published books, while everyday conversation uses the local dialect; educated speakers often mix the two in a middle register sometimes called Educated Spoken Arabic.

Features

MSA retains the case and mood endings (iʿrāb) of Classical Arabic in principle, though they are often omitted in ordinary reading aloud except in formal or religious contexts. Its vocabulary has expanded through coinages by language academies to cover modern technology, science and politics.
