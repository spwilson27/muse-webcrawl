Cocoa butter

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cocoa butter, also called theobroma oil, is a pale-yellow, edible fat extracted from the cocoa bean (Theobroma cacao). It is used to make chocolate, as well as some ointments, toiletries, and pharmaceuticals. Cocoa butter has a cocoa flavour and aroma, and its melting point, just below human body temperature, makes it melt smoothly in the mouth.

Production

Cocoa butter is obtained by grinding fermented and roasted cocoa beans into chocolate liquor and pressing it to separate the butter from the cocoa solids (cocoa powder). The Broma process allows butter to drip from hanging bags of roasted beans. Cocoa butter accounts for roughly half the weight of the cocoa bean.

Composition and properties

The fat is composed mainly of triglycerides of palmitic, stearic, and oleic acids. It is one of the most stable natural fats, with a long shelf life due to its naturally occurring antioxidants. Cocoa butter is polymorphic — it crystallizes in several forms; proper tempering of chocolate promotes the desirable Form V crystals, which give well-made chocolate its gloss and "snap". Because of its value, cocoa butter is sometimes partly replaced by cheaper cocoa butter equivalents in compound chocolate, subject to labeling regulations in many jurisdictions.

Uses

Beyond chocolate, cocoa butter is used in cosmetics and skin-care products as an emollient, in soap making, and in pharmacy as a base for suppositories and ointments because it melts at body temperature and is non-toxic. It is also used, in deodorized form, where a neutral fat is desired.
