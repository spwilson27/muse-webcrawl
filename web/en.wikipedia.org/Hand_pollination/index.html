Hand pollination

Hand pollination, also known as mechanical pollination, is a technique used when natural pollination is insufficient or undesirable. Pollen is physically transferred from the anther of one flower to the stigma of another by hand or with tools.

Uses

Hand pollination is practised in hybrid seed production, in orchards lacking pollinators, and for crops such as vanilla, whose natural pollinator is absent outside its native Mexico. In parts of China, notably Sichuan's pear orchards, widespread pesticide use and pollinator decline have made human "pollinator crews" with feather brushes a routine necessity.

Technique

Methods range from rubbing flowers together, to using small brushes, cotton swabs or collected pollen dusted or sprayed onto blossoms, and are timed to the flowers' receptivity.
