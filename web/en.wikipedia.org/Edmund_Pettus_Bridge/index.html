Edmund Pettus Bridge

The Edmund Pettus Bridge is a bridge that carries U.S. Route 80 Business (US 80 Bus.) across the Alabama River in Selma, Alabama. Built in 1940, it is named after Edmund Pettus, a former Confederate brigadier general, U.S. senator from Alabama, and state-level leader of the Ku Klux Klan. The bridge is a steel through-arch bridge with a central span of 250 feet.

Bloody Sunday

The bridge became a landmark of the American civil rights movement on March 7, 1965, a day known as Bloody Sunday. Some 600 civil rights marchers, led by John Lewis of SNCC and Hosea Williams of the SCLC, set out for Montgomery to protest the denial of voting rights to African Americans and the killing of activist Jimmie Lee Jackson. As they crossed the bridge, they were met at its foot by Alabama state troopers and a county posse, who attacked the unarmed marchers with billy clubs and tear gas. Images of the assault, broadcast nationwide, galvanised public support for voting rights legislation.

Two weeks later, on March 21, marchers protected by federalised National Guard troops again crossed the bridge on the successful Selma to Montgomery march. The events helped spur Congress to pass the Voting Rights Act of 1965.

Legacy and renaming debate

The bridge was declared a National Historic Landmark in 2013 and forms part of the Selma to Montgomery National Historic Trail. Because of Pettus's association with the Confederacy and the Klan, there have been repeated campaigns to rename the bridge, including proposals to name it for the late congressman and civil rights leader John Lewis after his death in 2020; some local residents have argued for keeping the name so that its meaning is transformed rather than erased. As of the mid-2020s the name remains unchanged.
