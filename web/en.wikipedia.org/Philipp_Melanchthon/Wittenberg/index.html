Wittenberg, officially Lutherstadt Wittenberg ("Luther City Wittenberg"), is a town in Saxony-Anhalt, Germany, on the River Elbe. It is famous as the birthplace of the Protestant Reformation: here Martin Luther taught theology, preached, and is said to have posted his Ninety-five Theses in 1517. Its Luther memorials — the Castle Church, Luther's House, the Town Church and the Melanchthon House — are inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.

History

Mentioned as early as 1180, Wittenberg developed as a settlement of Flemish colonists on the Elbe. It became a residence of the Ascanian dukes of Saxe-Wittenberg and, by the Golden Bull of 1356, of one of the Empire's seven Electors. In 1502 Elector Frederick the Wise founded the University of Wittenberg (the Leucorea), attracting scholars including the young Philipp Melanchthon, who arrived in 1518 as professor of Greek and became Luther's closest collaborator. Lucas Cranach the Elder, court painter and the town's mayor, made Wittenberg a centre of Renaissance art and printing.

The Reformation town

Tradition holds that on October 31, 1517 Luther nailed his Ninety-five Theses to the door of the Castle Church (Schlosskirche); whether or not the posting occurred exactly as described, Wittenberg became the intellectual capital of the early Reformation. Luther preached at the Town Church (Stadtkirche St. Marien), the oldest building in town, whose altarpiece by Cranach depicts the reformers. Luther lived in the former Augustinian monastery, the Lutherhaus — today one of the world's largest Reformation museums — and is buried with Melanchthon in the Castle Church, whose bronze door of 1858 bears the text of the Theses.

Later history

Wittenberg suffered in the Thirty Years' War and was fortified under Prussian rule after 1815, when its university was moved and merged with Halle's. The nineteenth century saw industrialisation along the Elbe, and the 500th anniversary of Luther's birth in 1883 began the town's career as a place of Protestant pilgrimage and commemoration. In the German Democratic Republic the town's chemical industry grew while the Luther sites were maintained as national heritage. Since reunification the historic centre has been extensively restored, and the town hosted major celebrations for the 500th anniversary of the Reformation in 2017 ("Reformationsjubiläum").

Sights

  • Castle Church (Schlosskirche) — site of the Theses door; burial place of Luther, Melanchthon and Frederick the Wise.
  • Lutherhaus — Luther's home from 1508, now a museum; part of the Luther Memorials UNESCO site (inscribed 1996).
  • Town Church of St. Mary — where Luther preached; Cranach altarpiece.
  • Melanchthon House — home of Philipp Melanchthon, author of the Augsburg Confession.
  • Cranach courtyards — workshops of Lucas Cranach the Elder and Younger.
