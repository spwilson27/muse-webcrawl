Curtis Publishing Company

The Curtis Publishing Company, founded in 1891 in Philadelphia by Cyrus H. K. Curtis, was one of the largest and most influential American magazine publishers. Its flagship titles included The Saturday Evening Post, Ladies' Home Journal, and The Country Gentleman.

Curtis Publishing Company
Founded: 1891, Philadelphia
Founder: Cyrus H. K. Curtis
Key titles: The Saturday Evening Post, Ladies' Home Journal
Headquarters: Curtis Building, Philadelphia

Rise

Curtis bought the struggling Saturday Evening Post in 1897 and, under editor George Horace Lorimer, rebuilt it into America's best-selling weekly, famous for fiction and for Norman Rockwell's covers, which appeared from 1916. The Ladies' Home Journal, acquired in 1883 by Curtis's earlier venture, pioneered the modern women's magazine and mass advertising.

Decline

Rising costs, television competition, and changing advertising markets eroded Curtis's profits in the 1960s. The company sold the Post in 1969 after heavy losses; the magazine ceased weekly publication that year (later revived by new owners). Curtis's other assets were sold off, and the landmark Curtis Building in Philadelphia was redeveloped, though a publishing successor bearing the name continues licensing the magazines' heritage.
