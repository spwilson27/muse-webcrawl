Alcubierre drive

The Alcubierre drive is a speculative idea for a faster-than-light spacecraft propulsion system proposed in 1994 by Mexican theoretical physicist Miguel Alcubierre. Based on a solution of the field equations of general relativity, it describes a "warp bubble" in which spacetime is contracted in front of a spacecraft and expanded behind it, allowing the craft to effectively travel faster than light without locally exceeding the speed of light.

Concept

Rather than accelerating the ship through space, the drive would move space itself. A region of flat spacetime — the bubble — carries the ship along as the surrounding spacetime is distorted by a wave. Because the ship sits in locally flat space, it experiences no inertial effects and does not violate the light-speed limit locally.

Requirements and criticism

The metric requires enormous amounts of negative energy density, which would need exotic matter that has never been observed. Early estimates of the energy required exceeded the mass-energy of the observable universe; later refinements by Alcubierre and others reduced the estimate considerably, but the drive remains purely theoretical. Problems include how to create and control the bubble, causality violations associated with closed timelike curves, and quantum inequalities that may forbid the required energy configurations.

In popular culture

The concept is named in explicit homage to the warp drive of Star Trek, and Alcubierre has said the idea was inspired in part by the franchise's terminology.
