Piacenza is a city and commune in the Emilia-Romagna region of northern Italy, and the capital of the province of Piacenza. Situated on the right bank of the Po River near its confluence with the Trebbia, it has long been a strategic crossroads between Milan, Genoa, and Bologna.

History

Founded by the Romans in 218 BC as the colony of Placentia, it was one of the earliest Roman settlements in Cisalpine Gaul and endured sieges by Hannibal's forces. In the Middle Ages it became a prosperous free commune, later passing under Visconti, Sforza, and Farnese rule. The Palazzo Farnese and the Gothic Palazzo Comunale (il Gotico) remain landmarks of its civic past.

Sights and culture

The Romanesque cathedral, begun in 1122, and the equestrian statues of Ranuccio I and Alessandro Farnese in Piazza dei Cavalli are notable. Piacenza is also known for its cured meats — coppa, pancetta, and salame piacentino hold protected status — and for the wines of the surrounding Colli Piacentini hills.
