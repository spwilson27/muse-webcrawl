Castle Clinton, also known as Fort Clinton and previously as Castle Garden, is a circular sandstone fort in Battery Park at the southern tip of Manhattan, New York City. Built between 1808 and 1811 as the Southwest Battery, one of five forts defending New York Harbor before the War of 1812, it never fired a shot in anger. It is now operated by the National Park Service as Castle Clinton National Monument.

History

The fort originally stood on an artificial island about 90 metres offshore, connected to Manhattan by a wooden causeway; landfill later joined it to Battery Park. Renamed for Governor DeWitt Clinton in 1815, it was ceded to the city in 1823 and reinvented as Castle Garden — an entertainment centre and opera house where the Swedish soprano Jenny Lind made her celebrated American debut in 1850, promoted by P. T. Barnum.

From 1855 to 1892 Castle Garden served as the Emigrant Landing Depot, the main immigration station of New York before Ellis Island; over 8 million immigrants, mostly German and Irish, passed through its rotunda. It then housed the New York Aquarium from 1896 until 1941, when Parks Commissioner Robert Moses, planning a bridge approach, demolished parts of it; preservationists saved the structure, and it was designated a national monument in 1946.

Today

Restored to an approximation of its original fort appearance, Castle Clinton now serves as the ticket office and departure point for ferries to the Statue of Liberty and Ellis Island, and hosts ranger programmes and small concerts.
