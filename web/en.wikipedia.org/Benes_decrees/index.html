Benes decrees

The Benes decrees are a series of laws promulgated in 1945 by the Czechoslovak government-in-exile and President Edvard Beneš during and shortly after the Second World War.

Content

The decrees provided for the confiscation of property and loss of citizenship of ethnic Germans and Hungarians in Czechoslovakia deemed disloyal, forming the legal basis for the expulsion of about three million Sudeten Germans after the war.

Legacy

The decrees remained politically sensitive for decades, affecting Czech relations with Germany and Austria and debates during the Czech Republic's accession to the European Union. Czech governments maintained they were an inseparable part of the post-war legal order, while expellee organisations sought their repeal.

Key decrees

Decree No. 12/1945 concerned confiscation of agricultural property; Decree No. 108/1945 ordered general confiscation of enemy property.
