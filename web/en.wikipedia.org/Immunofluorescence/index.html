Immunofluorescence

Immunofluorescence (IF) is a laboratory technique that uses the specificity of antibodies to their antigen to target fluorescent dyes (fluorophores) to specific biomolecule targets in cells or tissues, allowing visualisation under a fluorescence microscope.

Methods

In direct immunofluorescence, the primary antibody is itself conjugated to a fluorophore. In indirect immunofluorescence, an unlabelled primary antibody is detected by a labelled secondary antibody, amplifying the signal and adding flexibility. Common fluorophores include FITC, TRITC, and the Alexa Fluor dyes.

Applications

IF is used to localise proteins, identify pathogens, and diagnose autoimmune diseases — for example antinuclear antibody testing and direct immunofluorescence of skin and kidney biopsies in blistering and glomerular diseases.

Limitations

Issues include photobleaching, autofluorescence, spectral overlap between channels, and antibody cross-reactivity; careful controls are required.
