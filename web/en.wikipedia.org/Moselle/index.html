The Moselle (French: Moselle; German: Mosel; Luxembourgish: Musel) is a river that rises in the Vosges mountains and flows through north-eastern France and Luxembourg to western Germany. It is a left bank tributary of the Rhine, which it joins at Koblenz. A small part of Belgium is in its basin as it includes the Sauer and the Our.

Course

The Moselle flows through the Lorraine region of France, passing Épinal, Toul, Pont-à-Mousson, Metz, and Thionville, then forms the border between Luxembourg and Germany near Schengen — where the 1985 Schengen Agreement was signed aboard a river cruise ship — before entering Germany and winding past Trier in a valley of steep, vineyard-covered slate slopes through Bernkastel-Kues and Cochem to Koblenz, where it meets the Rhine at the Deutsches Eck.

Navigation and economy

The river was canalised between 1958 and 1964 with 28 locks, allowing large barges to travel between the Rhine and French industrial Lorraine. The valley is internationally famous for wine, especially Riesling grown in the German Mosel wine region and its Luxembourg and French counterparts, and for tourism along the Moselle cycle path.
