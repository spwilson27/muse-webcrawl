Van Buren v. United States

Van Buren v. United States
Supreme Court of the United States
Argued: 30 November 2020
Decided: 3 June 2021
Citation: 593 U.S. 374

Van Buren v. United States, 593 U.S. 374 (2021), was a United States Supreme Court case concerning the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA). The Court held that a person who is authorised to access information on a computer does not "exceed authorised access" under the CFAA merely by accessing that information for an improper purpose.

Background

Nathan Van Buren, a police sergeant in Georgia, was paid by an acquaintance to search a police database for a licence plate number. Although Van Buren was entitled to access the database for law-enforcement purposes, he was convicted of felony computer fraud under the CFAA for exceeding authorised access. The Eleventh Circuit upheld the conviction, deepening a circuit split over the statute's scope.

Decision

In a 6–3 decision written by Justice Amy Coney Barrett, the Court reversed. It held that the CFAA's "exceeds authorized access" clause covers only those who obtain information from areas of a computer — such as files, folders or databases — that are off limits to them, a "gates-up-or-down" inquiry. Using information one is otherwise entitled to access for a forbidden purpose does not violate the Act.

Significance

The ruling narrowed the CFAA, a statute long criticised as overbroad, and was welcomed by security researchers and civil-liberties groups concerned that the government's reading would criminalise common computer use such as violating a website's terms of service.
