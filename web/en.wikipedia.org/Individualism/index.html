Individualism

Individualism is the moral stance, political philosophy, ideology, and social outlook that emphasizes the intrinsic worth of the individual. Individualists promote realizing one's goals and desires, valuing independence and self-reliance, and advocating that the interests of the individual should gain precedence over the state or a social group, while opposing external interference upon one's own interests by society or institutions such as the government.

History

Ideas of individual autonomy have roots in ancient philosophy and in the religious notion of the individual soul, but modern individualism developed during the Enlightenment. Thinkers such as John Locke grounded political order in natural rights held by individuals, while 19th-century liberals like John Stuart Mill defended liberty of thought and self-development in On Liberty (1859). The term itself entered English usage in the 19th century, partly through translations of Alexis de Tocqueville's Democracy in America.

Types and debates

Political individualism underlies classical liberalism and libertarianism, stressing civil liberties, private property, and limited government. Methodological individualism in the social sciences explains social phenomena in terms of individual actions. Critics from communitarian and collectivist perspectives argue that individualism undervalues community, social obligation, and the ways identity is socially formed. Cultural psychologists contrast individualist cultures, common in much of the West, with collectivist cultures, where group harmony and interdependence are emphasized.
