William P. Hobby Airport (IATA: HOU) is an international airport in Houston, Texas, located about 7 miles (11 km) southeast of downtown. It was Houston's primary commercial airport until George Bush Intercontinental Airport opened in 1969, and it remains a major domestic airport, serving around 14 million passengers a year.

History

The site began as a private flying field in 1927 and was bought by the City of Houston in 1937, becoming Houston Municipal Airport. It was renamed Houston International Airport in 1954 and took its present name in 1967, honouring William P. Hobby, a former governor of Texas and publisher of the Houston Post. After the opening of Intercontinental Airport, Hobby retained a strong role in short-haul services; Southwest Airlines has operated there since 1971 and makes Hobby one of its largest stations.

Facilities and services

Hobby has four runways and a single central terminal with two concourses. A new international concourse opened in 2015 restored scheduled international service, with flights to Mexico, Central America and the Caribbean. The 1940 Air Terminal Museum, housed in the airport's original art deco terminal, preserves its early aviation history.
