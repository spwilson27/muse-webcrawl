The Mueller special counsel investigation was an investigation into Russian interference in the 2016 United States presidential election, links between associates of Donald Trump and Russian officials, and possible obstruction of justice by Trump. It was conducted by special counsel Robert Mueller from May 2017 to March 2019.

Background

Mueller was appointed by Deputy Attorney General Rod Rosenstein, acting as Attorney General for the matter after Jeff Sessions recused himself, following President Trump's dismissal of FBI director James Comey.

Findings

The investigation produced indictments of 34 people and three companies, guilty pleas from several Trump associates, and the conviction of campaign chairman Paul Manafort. The final report concluded that Russia interfered in the election in a "sweeping and systematic fashion" but did not establish that the Trump campaign conspired or coordinated with the Russian government. On obstruction of justice, the report neither concluded Trump committed a crime nor exonerated him.
