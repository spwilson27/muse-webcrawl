Federal Court of Appeal

The Federal Court of Appeal (French: Cour d'appel fédérale) is a Canadian appellate court that hears cases concerning federal law. It reviews decisions of the Federal Court, the Tax Court of Canada, and a wide range of federal boards and tribunals. Its judgments may be appealed, with leave, to the Supreme Court of Canada.

History

The court was created in 2003 when the former Federal Court of Canada was split into two separate courts: the Federal Court (the trial court) and the Federal Court of Appeal. Before that, appeals within the federal court system were heard by the Appeal Division of the Federal Court.

Jurisdiction

The Federal Court of Appeal hears:

  • Appeals from judgments and orders of the Federal Court
  • Appeals from the Tax Court of Canada in general procedure cases
  • Judicial review applications directed at federal boards and tribunals listed in the Federal Courts Act, such as the Canadian Radio-television and Telecommunications Commission and the Competition Tribunal
  • Appeals concerning certain national security and immigration matters

Composition and sittings

The court is composed of a chief justice and a number of other judges appointed by the Governor in Council; the Chief Justice of the Federal Court of Appeal holds a senior position in the Canadian judicial hierarchy. The court sits in Ottawa and in cities across Canada, usually in panels of three judges.
