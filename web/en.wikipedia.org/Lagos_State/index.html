Lagos, the economic capital of Nigeria

Lagos State (Yoruba: Ìpínlẹ̀ Èkó) is a state in southwestern Nigeria. Though the smallest state by land area (3,577 km², of which about a fifth is water), it is the most populous and the country's economic centre, containing Lagos, the most populous city in Africa. Its administrative capital is Ikeja.

Geography

Lagos State lies on the Atlantic coast of the Bight of Benin, bounded by Ogun State to the east and north and the Republic of Benin to the west. Much of its territory consists of lagoons, creeks and wetlands — principally Lagos Lagoon and Lekki Lagoon — along with barrier islands and sandy beaches. The metropolitan area sprawls from the islands and mainland of Lagos city northward into neighbouring Ogun State.

History

The state was created on 27 May 1967 from the Colony Province of the old Western Region and the former federal territory of Lagos, which had been Nigeria's capital until the capital moved to Abuja in 1991. Lagos itself grew from an Awori Yoruba fishing settlement on Lagos Island, became a major nineteenth-century port (notorious in the era of the Atlantic slave trade, then a centre of legitimate palm-oil commerce), and was annexed by Britain in 1861.

Economy and government

Lagos State generates a substantial share of Nigeria's non-oil GDP, hosting the country's principal ports (Apapa and Tin Can Island), its stock exchange, most major banks, and the Nollywood film and Afrobeats music industries. Internally generated revenue is the highest of any Nigerian state. The state is governed by an elected governor and House of Assembly and is divided into 20 local government areas, supplemented by local council development areas.
