Brighton

The Royal Pavilion and Brighton seafront

Brighton is a seaside resort town in East Sussex, England, forming part of the city of Brighton and Hove. Situated on the English Channel about 47 miles (76 km) south of London, it developed from the fishing village of Brighthelmstone into Britain's most fashionable resort after the Prince Regent built the Royal Pavilion there. The wider city had a population of about 277,000 in 2021.

History

Brighthelmstone was recorded in Domesday Book (1086) as a small fishing settlement. Its transformation began in the 1750s when Dr Richard Russell promoted sea-bathing and drinking seawater as cures. The patronage of the Prince Regent (later George IV), who first visited in 1783, made Brighton the height of fashion; John Nash remodelled the Royal Pavilion in Indo-Saracenic style between 1815 and 1822. The London and Brighton Railway (1841) opened the town to mass tourism. In 2000 Brighton merged with neighbouring Hove to form the unitary authority and city of Brighton and Hove.

Geography and landmarks

Brighton lies between the South Downs and the sea. Landmarks include the Royal Pavilion, Brighton Palace Pier (opened 1899), the ruined West Pier, the i360 observation tower, the Lanes shopping quarter, and the Regency terraces of Brunswick and Kemp Town. The beach is shingle.

Culture

Brighton is known for its arts scene, nightlife, and large LGBTQ+ community, centred on Kemptown, and hosts the Brighton Festival and Brighton Fringe each May, Great Escape music festival, and London to Brighton Veteran Car Run. The local football club is Brighton & Hove Albion. Two universities — the University of Sussex and the University of Brighton — give the city a large student population.
