Dungeons & Dragons

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Dungeons & Dragons (commonly abbreviated as D&D or DnD) is a fantasy tabletop role-playing game (RPG) originally created and designed by Gary Gygax and Dave Arneson. The game was first published in 1974 by Tactical Studies Rules (TSR). It has been published by Wizards of the Coast, later a subsidiary of Hasbro, since 1997. The game was derived from miniature wargames, with a variation of the 1971 game Chainmail serving as the initial rule system.

Gameplay

In a typical D&D game, each player creates a player character — such as a fighter, wizard, cleric, or rogue — choosing a species (race) and class. One participant, the Dungeon Master (DM), describes the world, narrates events, and plays the part of monsters and non-player characters. Outcomes of risky actions are resolved by rolling polyhedral dice, most distinctively the twenty-sided die (d20), against ability and skill statistics. Campaigns unfold as a series of adventures and may continue for years.

Editions

The game has passed through several editions: the original 1974 boxed set, the split Basic and Advanced Dungeons & Dragons lines, AD&D 2nd Edition (1989), 3rd edition (2000) with the d20 System and Open Game License, 4th edition (2008), and 5th edition (2014), which prompted a major revival of the hobby. Revised 5th-edition core rulebooks were published in 2024.

Settings

Official campaign settings include Greyhawk, the Forgotten Realms, Dragonlance, Ravenloft, Eberron, and Dark Sun. The game also supports countless home-made worlds.

Cultural impact

D&D was the first modern role-playing game and has had a profound influence on video games, fantasy literature, and popular culture. It survived a moral panic in the 1980s and grew dramatically in the 2010s and 2020s, helped by actual-play streaming shows and the 2023 film Dungeons & Dragons: Honor Among Thieves.
