Applied science

Applied science is the use of scientific knowledge and methods to achieve practical goals. It is contrasted with basic (or pure) science, which seeks knowledge for its own sake. Applied sciences include engineering, medicine, agriculture, computer science, architecture and many branches of the social sciences such as applied psychology and criminology.

Relationship to basic science

The boundary between basic and applied research is often blurred: discoveries made for their own sake, such as quantum mechanics or the structure of DNA, later underpin technologies from transistors to genetic medicine, while practical problems frequently stimulate fundamental inquiry — a pattern sometimes called "Pasteur's quadrant", use-inspired basic research. The linear model, in which basic research leads to applied research and then development, is now considered an oversimplification of how innovation occurs.

Institutions

Applied science is pursued in industry laboratories, government agencies, hospitals and universities of applied sciences and polytechnics, which in countries such as Germany and Finland award degrees oriented toward professional practice.
