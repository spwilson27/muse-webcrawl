Mérida is the capital and largest city of the Mexican state of Yucatán, and the largest city on the Yucatán Peninsula. It was founded in 1542 by the Spanish conquistador Francisco de Montejo on the site of the Maya city of T'hó, and named after Mérida in Extremadura, Spain.

History

The Maya city of T'hó ("five hills") had been occupied for centuries before the Spanish conquest. After a hard-fought campaign, Montejo established the colonial city on its ruins, using stone from Maya temples to build the cathedral and civic buildings around the Plaza Grande. The Cathedral of San Ildefonso, built between 1561 and 1598, is among the oldest cathedrals in the Americas.

In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, Mérida grew wealthy from the henequen (sisal) boom; the mansions of the Paseo de Montejo date from this era, when the city was said to have more millionaires than anywhere else in Mexico.

Culture

Mérida is a major cultural centre, known for Yucatecan cuisine, trova music and its historic centre of colonial architecture. In 2000 UNESCO recognised it within the region's heritage landscape, and the city was named an American Capital of Culture in 2000 and 2017.

Geography and climate

Mérida lies in the northwest of Yucatán, about 35 km inland from the Gulf port of Progreso. It has a tropical savanna climate with a rainy season from June to October.
