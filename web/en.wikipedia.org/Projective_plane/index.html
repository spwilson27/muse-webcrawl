Projective plane

In mathematics, a projective plane is a geometric structure that extends the concept of a plane. In the ordinary Euclidean plane, two distinct lines either intersect at one point or are parallel and do not meet; in a projective plane, any two distinct lines intersect at exactly one point, and any two distinct points determine exactly one line.

Examples

The real projective plane is obtained from the Euclidean plane by adding "points at infinity" (one for each direction) and a "line at infinity". It can be modelled as the set of lines through the origin in three-dimensional space, or as a sphere with antipodal points identified; it is a non-orientable surface of Euler characteristic 1. Over finite fields, finite projective planes exist: the smallest is the Fano plane, with 7 points and 7 lines. Planes are classified by their order n, having n² + n + 1 points; planes of order 10 have been shown not to exist, while existence for order 12 remains open.

Axioms

Abstractly, a projective plane is an incidence structure satisfying: any two distinct points lie on a unique line, any two distinct lines meet at a unique point, and there exist four points, no three collinear. Projective planes over fields connect to algebraic geometry and coding theory.
