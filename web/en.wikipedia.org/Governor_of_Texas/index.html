Governor of Texas

The governor of Texas is the head of government of the U.S. state of Texas and the commander-in-chief of the state's military forces. The governor has the power to approve or veto bills passed by the Texas Legislature, convene the legislature in special session, grant pardons, and appoint officials to boards and commissions. The governor's official residence is the Texas Governor's Mansion in Austin.

Incumbent: Greg Abbott (since 20 January 2015)

Term: 4 years, no term limit

Residence: Texas Governor's Mansion, Austin

First holder: J. Pinckney Henderson (1846)

History

The office was created by the state constitution of 1845 when Texas joined the Union; J. Pinckney Henderson was the first governor. Under Reconstruction, the Constitution of 1869 gave the governor expanded powers, but the Constitution of 1876 — still in force — deliberately weakened the office, creating a "plural executive" in which the lieutenant governor, attorney general, comptroller, land commissioner, and others are independently elected. Despite this design, the Texas governor is influential through the veto (including a powerful line-item veto over appropriations), appointments, and agenda-setting for special sessions.

Powers and election

The governor is elected every four years in even-numbered years without midterm federal elections (midterm cycles) and has no term limit. Rick Perry (2000–2015), who succeeded George W. Bush when Bush resigned to become President, is the longest-serving governor in state history. The governor is second in prominence to the lieutenant governor in many analyses of legislative power, since the lieutenant governor presides over the Texas Senate.

Notable facts

Miriam "Ma" Ferguson and Ann Richards are the two women who have served as governor. The governor's salary and the office's budget are set by the legislature, and the governor may call out the Texas Military Forces, including the National Guard, in emergencies such as hurricanes and border operations.
