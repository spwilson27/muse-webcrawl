Rabbeinu Tam

Jacob ben Meir ("Rabbeinu Tam")
Born: c. 1100, Ramerupt, France
Died: 9 June 1171, Troyes
Occupation: Tosafist, halakhist, poet
Grandfather: Rashi

Jacob ben Meir (c. 1100 – 1171), commonly known as Rabbeinu Tam ("Our Rabbi, the Perfect One"), was one of the most renowned French rabbis of the Middle Ages and a leading Tosafist. He was the grandson of Rashi, the great commentator on the Bible and Talmud, and brother of the Rashbam and Rivam.

Life and work

Rabbeinu Tam directed a major yeshiva at Ramerupt in Champagne, attracting students from across Europe, and supported himself through viticulture and moneylending. Wounded during a pogrom connected with the Second Crusade in 1146, he later convened influential synods whose ordinances regulated Jewish communal life in northern France.

His rulings and glosses appear throughout the Talmud as Tosafot, and his independent decisions were collected in Sefer ha-Yashar. He is perhaps best known today for the dispute over the order of the scriptural passages inside tefillin: "Rabbeinu Tam tefillin" follow his ordering, differing from those of Rashi, and some observant Jews wear both pairs.

Poetry

Rabbeinu Tam also composed liturgical poetry (piyyutim) in the style of the Spanish Hebrew poets, and corresponded in verse with contemporaries — a reflection of his standing as the pre-eminent halakhic authority of his generation.
