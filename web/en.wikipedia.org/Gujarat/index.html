Gujarat

Ahmedabad, the largest city of Gujarat, on the Sabarmati River.

Gujarat is a state on the western coast of India with a coastline of about 1,600 km — the longest of any Indian state — and a population of roughly 60 million (2011 census). Its capital is Gandhinagar, and its largest city and former capital is Ahmedabad. The state was formed on 1 May 1960 out of the Gujarati-speaking districts of Bombay State.

History

Gujarat was home to major sites of the Indus Valley civilisation, including the port city of Lothal and Dholavira. It later formed part of the Mauryan, Gupta and Chalukya empires, the Gujarat Sultanate, and the Mughal and Maratha spheres before British rule. Mahatma Gandhi was born at Porbandar in Gujarat, and the state was central to the Indian independence movement, including the Salt March of 1930.

Economy and culture

Gujarat is one of India's most industrialised states, a leader in chemicals, pharmaceuticals, refining, textiles and diamond processing (most of the world's diamonds are cut in Surat). It is the birthplace of both Gandhi and Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, commemorated by the Statue of Unity, the world's tallest statue. The Asiatic lions of Gir National Park are found nowhere else in the wild. Gujarati, an Indo-Aryan language written in its own script, is the official language.
