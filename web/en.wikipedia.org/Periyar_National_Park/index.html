Periyar National Park

Periyar Lake, formed by the Mullaperiyar Dam, at the heart of the reserve.

Periyar National Park and Wildlife Sanctuary (PNP) is a protected area in the districts of Idukki, Kottayam, and Pathanamthitta in Kerala, India. It is notable as an elephant reserve and a tiger reserve. The protected area encompasses 925 km2 (357 sq mi), of which 350.54 km2 (135.34 sq mi) of the core zone was declared as the Periyar National Park in 1982. The park is a repository of rare, endemic, and endangered flora and fauna and forms the major watershed of two important rivers of Kerala, the Periyar and the Pamba.

Geography

The park lies high in the Cardamom Hills and Pandalam Hills of the south Western Ghats, along the border with Tamil Nadu. At its center lies Periyar Lake, a 26 km2 (10 sq mi) artificial reservoir created in 1895 by the construction of the Mullaperiyar Dam on the Periyar River. Dead tree trunks still rise from the lake's waters, and herds of elephants, sambar, and wild boar frequently gather along its marshy edges.

Flora and fauna

More than 1,900 taxa of flowering plants have been recorded in the park, including a large number of species endemic to the Western Ghats. The wildlife includes Bengal tigers, Indian elephants, gaur, sambar deer, wild pig, Indian giant squirrel, lion-tailed macaque, Nilgiri langur, and the elusive Nilgiri tahr in the higher grasslands. The park is also renowned among birdwatchers, with more than 260 species recorded, including the great Indian hornbill and the endemic Malabar grey hornbill.

Management and tourism

Periyar is administered by the Kerala Forest Department and is managed as a Project Tiger reserve. It is frequently cited as an example of participatory conservation, with former poachers employed as guides through community-based ecotourism programs. The town of Thekkady, near the park entrance at Kumily, is the principal gateway for visitors, who explore the park chiefly by boat cruises on Periyar Lake, nature walks, and bamboo rafting.
