Tosa Province (土佐国, Tosa no kuni) was a province of Japan in the area of southern Shikoku, corresponding to modern-day Kōchi Prefecture. It was sometimes called Doshū (土州). Tosa bordered on Iyo Province to the northwest and Awa Province to the northeast, with its long southern coastline on the Pacific Ocean.

History

The ancient capital of the province was located near modern Nankoku, and the provincial temple (kokubun-ji) and the ichinomiya shrine, Tosa Kokubun-ji and Tosa shrine, survive. During the Sengoku period the province came under the rule of the Chōsokabe clan, and after the Battle of Sekigahara in 1600 it was granted to Yamauchi Kazutoyo, whose descendants ruled Tosa Domain from Kōchi Castle throughout the Edo period. Tosa is celebrated in Japanese history for the loyalists it produced in the final years of the Tokugawa shogunate, most famously Sakamoto Ryōma, who helped negotiate the alliance between Satsuma and Chōshū and drafted proposals for a new national government.

Later use

With the abolition of the han system in 1871, Tosa Province became Kōchi Prefecture. The name "Tosa" survives in local products and institutions, and gives its name to the Tosa dog breed. The writer Yosano Akiko's contemporary, and the diarist Ki no Tsurayuki before her, are linked to the province: Tsurayuki wrote the Tosa Diary (Tosa nikki, c. 935), a famous account of a voyage from Tosa back to Kyoto, while serving as governor of the province.
