Marietta is a city in and the county seat of Washington County, Ohio, located at the confluence of the Muskingum and Ohio rivers. Founded in 1788 by settlers of the Ohio Company of Associates led by Rufus Putnam, it was the first permanent United States settlement in the Northwest Territory.

History

The settlers built their community near the great prehistoric earthworks of the Fort Ancient and Hopewell cultures, preserving the large mound cemetery (Mound Cemetery) and the platform mounds now known as Quadranaou and Capitolium. Marietta became an important river port and shipbuilding centre in the nineteenth century and is home to Marietta College, founded in 1835.

Geography

The city lies in southeastern Ohio across the Ohio River from West Virginia. Its historic downtown and brick streets survive largely intact, and sternwheel riverboats remain a symbol of the city, celebrated at the annual Ohio River Sternwheel Festival.
