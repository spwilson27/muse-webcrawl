Antakya is a city in south-central Turkey, the capital of Hatay Province, lying on the Orontes River (Asi) close to the Syrian border and the Mediterranean coast. It occupies the site of ancient Antioch, one of the great cities of the Hellenistic and Roman worlds and an early centre of Christianity, where the followers of Jesus were first called Christians according to the Acts of the Apostles.

History

Antioch was founded around 300 BC by Seleucus I Nicator and became capital of the Seleucid Empire. Under Rome it grew into one of the empire's largest cities, famed for its wealth, its mosaics, and its patriarchate—one of the five great sees of early Christianity. The city passed through Byzantine, Arab, Seljuk, Crusader (as the Principality of Antioch, 1098–1268), Mamluk, and Ottoman hands.

Modern Antakya was part of the French Mandate of Syria after the First World War; the Hatay State voted to join Turkey in 1939. The city is known for its multicultural mix of Turkish, Arab, and other communities, its cuisine (including künefe and Antakya kebab), and the Hatay Archaeology Museum, which holds one of the world's finest collections of Roman mosaics.

2023 earthquake

Antakya was devastated by the earthquakes of 6 February 2023, which destroyed large parts of the old city, including historic mosques, churches, and synagogues, and caused very heavy loss of life across Hatay Province. Reconstruction has proceeded slowly amid debate over rebuilding the historic fabric.
