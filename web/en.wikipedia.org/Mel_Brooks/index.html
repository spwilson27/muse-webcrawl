Mel Brooks (born Melvin Kaminsky; 28 June 1926) is an American actor, comedian and filmmaker. With a career spanning over seven decades, he is one of the few entertainers to win all four major North American entertainment awards: an Emmy, a Grammy, an Oscar and a Tony (EGOT).

Career

Brooks began as a comedy writer for Sid Caesar's Your Show of Shows and co-created the sitcom Get Smart with Buck Henry. He found film success with The Producers (1967), winning the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay, and went on to direct a string of hit parodies including Blazing Saddles and Young Frankenstein (both 1974), Silent Movie (1976), History of the World, Part I (1981) and Spaceballs (1987).

Stage work

Brooks adapted The Producers into a Broadway musical that won a record 12 Tony Awards in 2001. He later created a musical version of Young Frankenstein.

Legacy

His films are frequently ranked among the funniest ever made, and he received a Kennedy Center Honor in 2009 and the AFI Life Achievement Award in 2013.
