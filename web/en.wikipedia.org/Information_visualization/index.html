Information visualization

FieldComputer science, human–computer interaction, graphic design
FocusInteractive visual representations of abstract data
Classic worksPlayfair (1786), Minard (1869), Tukey (1977), Tufte (1983)

Information visualization (or information visualisation) is the study and practice of creating interactive visual representations of abstract data to reinforce human cognition. By mapping data values to visual properties such as position, length, colour, and shape, visualization lets people perceive patterns, trends, and outliers that would be hard to detect in raw numbers or text.

History

The field descends from statistical graphics: William Playfair invented the line chart, bar chart, and pie chart in the late 18th century; Florence Nightingale used polar-area diagrams to argue for sanitary reform; and Charles Joseph Minard's 1869 map of Napoleon's Russian campaign is celebrated as a masterpiece of multivariate display. In the 20th century, Jacques Bertin's Semiology of Graphics (1967), John Tukey's exploratory data analysis, and Edward Tufte's The Visual Display of Quantitative Information (1983) laid the theoretical foundations for the modern, computer-centred discipline.

Techniques

Common techniques include scatter plots, heat maps, tree maps, node-link diagrams for networks, parallel coordinates, choropleth maps, and small multiples. Interactive systems add brushing and linking, filtering, zooming, and details-on-demand, following Ben Shneiderman's mantra: "overview first, zoom and filter, then details on demand". Research distinguishes information visualization (abstract data) from scientific visualization (spatially structured physical data).

Applications

Information visualization is used in business intelligence dashboards, journalism, genomics, finance, digital humanities, and exploratory data science. Widely used tools and libraries include D3.js, Vega, Tableau, and ggplot2.
