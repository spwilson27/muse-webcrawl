Constitutional Reform Act 2005

The Constitutional Reform Act 2005 (c. 4) is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom, which provided for a Supreme Court of the United Kingdom to replace the existing role of the Law Lords as the final court of appeal, and for the reform of the office of Lord Chancellor. It also removed the function of the Lord Chancellor as head of the judiciary and established a Judicial Appointments Commission for England and Wales.

Background

The Act was introduced following a government announcement in June 2003 by Prime Minister Tony Blair of a major reshuffle that included plans to abolish the office of Lord Chancellor, create a new Supreme Court, and reform the system for appointing judges. The perceived conflict between the Lord Chancellor's roles in the executive, legislature, and judiciary — he was a cabinet minister, Speaker of the House of Lords, and head of the judiciary — had long attracted criticism, including under the European Convention on Human Rights.

Main provisions

The Act established the Supreme Court of the United Kingdom, which took over the appellate jurisdiction of the House of Lords and the devolution jurisdiction of the Judicial Committee of the Privy Council. The Court began sitting on 1 October 2009, in the former Middlesex Guildhall in Parliament Square, London. The Act also created the Judicial Appointments Commission, an independent body responsible for selecting candidates for judicial office in England and Wales, and reformed the role of the Lord Chancellor, who ceased to be head of the judiciary — a role transferred to the Lord Chief Justice.

Effects

The creation of the Supreme Court marked a significant step in the separation of powers in the United Kingdom, physically and institutionally separating the highest court from the legislature for the first time. The first justices of the Supreme Court were the serving Law Lords, with Lord Phillips of Worth Matravers as its first President.
