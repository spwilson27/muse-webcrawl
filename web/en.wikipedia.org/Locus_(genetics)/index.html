Locus (genetics)

A locus is a fixed position on a chromosome

In genetics, a locus (plural loci) is a specific, fixed position on a chromosome where a particular gene or genetic marker is located. Each chromosome carries many genes, with each gene occupying a different position or locus; in humans, the total number of protein-coding genes is roughly 20,000, distributed across 23 pairs of chromosomes.

Nomenclature

The chromosomal locus of a gene can be written using cytogenetic band notation, for example "6p21.3": the chromosome number (6), the arm (p for the short arm, q for the long arm), and the band numbers indicating distance from the centromere. The specific variant forms of a gene that can occupy a locus are called alleles. A diploid organism carrying two identical alleles at a locus is homozygous for that locus, while one carrying two different alleles is heterozygous.

Quantitative trait loci

A quantitative trait locus (QTL) is a region of DNA associated with variation in a quantitative trait, such as height or yield. QTL mapping uses the statistical association between trait values and molecular markers to locate genomic regions influencing the trait. Similarly, expression quantitative trait loci (eQTLs) are loci that explain variation in gene expression levels.

Centisome map

A genetic map unit, the centimorgan, measures distances between loci along a chromosome based on recombination frequency: loci separated by one centimorgan recombine in about 1% of meioses.
