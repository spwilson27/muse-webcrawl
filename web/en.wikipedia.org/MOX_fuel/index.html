MOX fuel
Type: Nuclear fuel
Composition: Plutonium dioxide + uranium dioxide

Mixed oxide fuel, commonly referred to as MOX fuel, is nuclear fuel that contains more than one oxide of fissile material, usually consisting of plutonium blended with natural uranium, reprocessed uranium, or depleted uranium. MOX fuel is an alternative to the low-enriched uranium (LEU) fuel used in the light-water reactors that predominate nuclear power generation.

Use

MOX fuel has been used in thermal reactors in Europe and Japan, and in fast reactors. It is one way of disposing of surplus weapons-grade plutonium.

Manufacture and concerns

Because plutonium is highly toxic and a proliferation concern, MOX fabrication plants are heavily safeguarded. Critics argue the economics favour fresh uranium fuel.
