Cousin prime

In number theory, cousin primes are prime numbers that differ by four. They are analogous to twin primes (difference two) and sexy primes (difference six).

Examples

Pairs below 250 include (3,7), (7,11), (13,17), (19,23), (37,41), (43,47), (67,71), (79,83), (97,101), (103,107), (109,113), (127,131), (163,167), (193,197), (223,227), (229,233), (277,281) in extended lists. The only prime belonging to two cousin pairs is 7.

Properties

It is conjectured, by the first Hardy–Littlewood conjecture, that there are infinitely many cousin primes, but this remains unproven.
