Golkonda

Golkonda (also spelled Golconda) is a fortified citadel and ruined city in Hyderabad, Telangana, India. It was the capital of the medieval Golconda Sultanate ruled by the Qutb Shahi dynasty (c. 1518–1687) and was famed throughout the early modern world as the great trading centre for diamonds mined in the surrounding region.

History

An earlier mud fort on the granite hill was built by the Kakatiya dynasty in the 12th–13th centuries. The Qutb Shahis rebuilt it in massive granite, ringing the hill with nearly 10 km of walls, bastions, and gates, and engineering a sophisticated water supply and acoustic system — a handclap at the Fateh Darwaza entrance can be heard at the Bala Hisar pavilion a kilometre away. Golconda withstood an eight-month Mughal siege before falling to Aurangzeb in 1687 through treachery.

Diamonds

The Kollur and other mines of the Krishna valley supplied rough diamonds that were cut and traded at Golconda, for centuries the world's only known source of diamonds. Stones associated with the region include the Koh-i-Noor, the Hope Diamond, the Daria-i-Noor, and the Regent Diamond; the very name "Golconda" became a generic term for exceptional diamonds and, by extension, any source of great wealth.
