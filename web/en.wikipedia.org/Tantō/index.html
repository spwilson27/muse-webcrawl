Tantō

Tantō
TypeJapanese dagger
Blade length15–30 cm (one shaku or less)
OriginHeian period, Japan

A tantō (短刀, "short blade") is a single- or double-edged Japanese knife or dagger with a blade between 15 and 30 cm in length. It was designed primarily as a stabbing weapon, traditionally carried by the samurai class of feudal Japan, and worn together with the tachi or katana.

History

The tantō emerged in the Heian period and was produced in large numbers during the wars of the Muromachi period. In the Edo period, women of the samurai class carried a smaller version, the kaiken, in their obi, sometimes associated with self-defense or ritual suicide (jigai). Ceremonial and gift tantō were often richly mounted, while wartime examples were plain.

Types

Variants include the yoroi-dōshi (armor piercer), hamidashi, and aikuchi (without a guard). Famous smiths such as Awataguchi Yoshimitsu and Shintōgo Kunimitsu produced celebrated tantō.
