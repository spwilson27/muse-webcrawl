Biblioteca Malatestiana
LocationCesena, Emilia-Romagna, Italy
Established1452
FounderMalatesta Novello

The Biblioteca Malatestiana is a public library in Cesena, Italy. It was the first European civic library, that is, a library belonging to the commune rather than to an ecclesiastical or private owner, and open to the general public. Commissioned by the lord of Cesena, Malatesta Novello, it was designed by Matteo Nuti of Fano, a pupil of Leon Battista Alberti, and opened in 1454.

History

Building work began in 1447 and was completed in 1452, and the library was formally opened on 15 August 1454. It formed part of the Franciscan convent of San Francesco but was owned by the city, which held one of the two keys to its doors; the friars held the other.

The hall

The library is a single long hall in the form of a basilica, lit by windows on both sides, with three naves divided by columns. The original furnishings survive: 58 reading desks with carved coats of arms, to which the manuscripts remain chained as they were in the 15th century. The entrance door, carved in walnut, dates to 1454.

Collection

The library preserves 343 manuscripts (codici) covering law, medicine, the classics, and theology, many illuminated. Because the collection was never dispersed and the room never remodelled, the Malatestiana is a remarkably complete survival of a Renaissance library. In 2005 UNESCO included it in the Memory of the World Register.
