Ferdinand II (9 July 1578 – 15 February 1637) was Holy Roman Emperor, King of Bohemia, and King of Hungary from 1619 until his death. A staunch Catholic educated by Jesuits, his attempts to impose religious uniformity on his lands helped provoke the Thirty Years' War.

Early rule

As Archduke of Inner Austria from 1596, Ferdinand pursued aggressive Counter-Reformation policies in Styria, Carinthia, and Carniola, expelling Protestant preachers and restoring Catholic worship. He succeeded his cousin Matthias as emperor in 1619, but the Protestant estates of Bohemia had already deposed him as their king in favor of Frederick V of the Palatinate, following the Defenestration of Prague (1618).

Thirty Years' War

Imperial and Catholic League forces under Tilly and Wallenstein crushed the Bohemian revolt at the Battle of White Mountain (1620) and occupied much of northern Germany. Ferdinand's Edict of Restitution (1629) demanded the return of church lands secularized since 1552, alarming Protestant princes and drawing in Denmark and then Sweden under Gustavus Adolphus. After Wallenstein's dismissal, defeat at Breitenfeld (1631), and Wallenstein's assassination in 1634, the Peace of Prague (1635) moderated Ferdinand's religious demands. The war continued under his son and successor, Ferdinand III.
