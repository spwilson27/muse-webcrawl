Strain theory (sociology)

Key figureRobert K. Merton (1938)
FieldSociology, criminology
AdaptationsConformity, innovation, ritualism, retreatism, rebellion

Strain theory is a sociological framework, most famously developed by Robert K. Merton in his 1938 essay "Social Structure and Anomie", holding that deviance arises when a society promotes culturally approved goals — such as wealth and success — without providing equal access to the legitimate means of achieving them.

Merton's adaptations

Merton identified five individual adaptations to this strain: conformity (accepting both goals and means), innovation (accepting goals but using illegitimate means, as in crime), ritualism (abandoning goals while rigidly following rules), retreatism (rejecting both, as in addiction), and rebellion (seeking to replace both goals and means). Later scholars, including Richard Cloward and Lloyd Ohlin and Robert Agnew, extended the theory.
