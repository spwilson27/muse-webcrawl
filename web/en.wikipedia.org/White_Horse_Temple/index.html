White Horse Temple

White Horse Temple (Baima Si)
Location: Luoyang, Henan, China
Tradition: Chinese Buddhism
Founded: traditionally 68 CE
Status: major pilgrimage site

White Horse Temple (simplified Chinese: 白马寺; traditional Chinese: 白馬寺; pinyin: Báimǎ Sì) is a Buddhist temple in Luoyang, China, traditionally regarded as the first Buddhist temple in China. According to tradition, it was established in 68 CE under the patronage of Emperor Ming of the Eastern Han dynasty.

Legend and history

Legend tells that Emperor Ming dreamed of a golden deity and sent envoys westward, who returned with Buddhist scriptures and images carried on a white horse, accompanied by the Indian monks Kāśyapa Mātaṅga and Dharmaratna. The temple was named for the white horse, and the two monks were later buried within its grounds. The temple was rebuilt and expanded many times, notably under Wu Zetian in the Tang dynasty, and suffered damage during later conflicts including the Cultural Revolution, after which it was restored.

Architecture

The complex covers about 13 hectares and includes halls in Ming and Qing styles, the ancient Qiyun Pagoda, and, in recent decades, full-scale replica temples in the styles of India, Thailand and Myanmar built through international cooperation.
