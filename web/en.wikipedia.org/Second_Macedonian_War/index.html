Second Macedonian War

The Second Macedonian War (200–197 BC) was fought between Macedon, led by Philip V, and Rome, allied with Pergamon and Rhodes. It ended the dominance of Macedon in the Greek world and marked a decisive step in Rome's expansion into the eastern Mediterranean.

Course

Rome declared war in 200 BC after appeals from its eastern allies alarmed by Philip's ambitions in the Aegean. After two years of inconclusive campaigning, the Roman consul Titus Quinctius Flamininus took command and in 197 BC defeated Philip decisively at the Battle of Cynoscephalae in Thessaly, where the Roman legion's flexibility overcame the Macedonian phalanx.

Settlement

Philip was confined to Macedon, forced to pay an indemnity, surrender his fleet, and give up his possessions outside Macedon. At the Isthmian Games of 196 BC, Flamininus proclaimed the "freedom of the Greeks."
