Clarksville, Tennessee

State: Tennessee
County: Montgomery
Founded: 1784
Population: about 166,000 (2020 census)
Nickname: Queen of the Cumberland

Clarksville is a city in northwestern Tennessee and the county seat of Montgomery County. Located at the confluence of the Cumberland and Red rivers, about 45 miles northwest of Nashville, it is Tennessee's fifth-most populous city and one of the fastest-growing metropolitan areas in the state.

History

Founded in 1784 and named for Revolutionary War general George Rogers Clark, Clarksville developed as a river port and a centre of the dark-fired tobacco trade; the Clarksville tobacco market was once among the most important in the world for that variety. The city was occupied during the Civil War after the fall of nearby Fort Donelson. A devastating tornado struck downtown in January 1999, and the Cumberland River flood of 2010 also caused extensive damage.

Economy and institutions

The economy is anchored by Fort Campbell, the vast U.S. Army post straddling the Kentucky line and home to the 101st Airborne Division, much of whose population lives in Clarksville. Austin Peay State University, named for a Tennessee governor from the city, is located downtown. Industry includes manufacturing plants producing tires, appliances, and other goods.

Culture

The city is commemorated in the Monkees' 1966 hit "Last Train to Clarksville", whose writers cited the town's name from a map, and guitarist Jimi Hendrix was stationed at Fort Campbell and formed a band in Clarksville in the early 1960s.
