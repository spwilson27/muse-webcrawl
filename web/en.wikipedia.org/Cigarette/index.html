Cigarette

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A cigarette is a narrow cylinder containing a combustible material, typically tobacco, that is rolled into thin paper for smoking. The cigarette is ignited at one end, causing it to smolder, and the resulting smoke is orally inhaled via the opposite end. Cigarette smoking is the most common method of tobacco consumption. The term cigarette is French, meaning "little cigar", from cigare and the diminutive suffix -ette.

History

The earliest forms of cigarettes were similar to their predecessor, the cigar. Cigarettes appear to have had antecedents in Mexico and Central America around the 9th century in the form of reeds and smoking tubes. Manufactured cigarettes became popular in Europe in the 19th century, and mechanized production — notably James Bonsack's cigarette-rolling machine of 1880 — made them cheap and ubiquitous. Smoking rates rose dramatically during the World Wars, when cigarettes were included in soldiers' rations, and peaked in many Western countries in the mid-20th century.

Manufacture

Modern manufactured cigarettes are engineered products containing processed tobacco leaf blended with hundreds of additives, wrapped in paper and usually fitted with a cellulose acetate filter. Major manufacturers have included Philip Morris International, British American Tobacco, and Japan Tobacco. Nicotine, the principal psychoactive component of tobacco smoke, makes cigarette smoking highly addictive.

Health effects

Cigarette smoking is a leading cause of preventable death worldwide. It causes most cases of lung cancer and contributes to heart disease, stroke, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, and many other cancers and illnesses. Tobacco smoke contains thousands of chemicals, dozens of which are known carcinogens. Exposure to secondhand smoke also harms non-smokers. The World Health Organization estimates that tobacco kills more than 8 million people each year.

Regulation

Following the landmark 1964 report of the US Surgeon General linking smoking to lung cancer, governments have imposed advertising bans, health warnings, taxes, smoke-free laws, and plain-packaging rules. Smoking prevalence has declined in many high-income countries but remains high in parts of the world. Electronic cigarettes and heated tobacco products have emerged as alternatives, prompting ongoing public-health debate.
