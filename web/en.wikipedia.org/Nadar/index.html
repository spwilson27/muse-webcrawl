Nadar was the pseudonym of Gaspard-Félix Tournachon (1820–1910), a French photographer, caricaturist, and journalist who became the most celebrated portrait photographer of nineteenth-century Paris. His studio produced penetrating portraits of many of the era's leading writers, artists, and politicians, including Charles Baudelaire, George Sand, Sarah Bernhardt, and Victor Hugo.

Career

Tournachon began as a caricaturist and writer for satirical journals before turning to photography in the 1850s. His portraits, posed against plain backgrounds with careful attention to expression and natural light, broke with stiff studio convention and are considered landmarks of early photographic portraiture. His Paris studio, with its famous red signature sign, became a cultural landmark; in 1874 it hosted the first exhibition of the Impressionists.

Ballooning and aerial photography

Nadar was an enthusiastic balloonist. In 1858 he took the first aerial photographs, from a tethered balloon over the outskirts of Paris (the negatives have not survived), and he later patented ideas for aerial photography and built the enormous balloon Le Géant. During the Siege of Paris in 1870–71 he organised balloon flights that carried mail and passengers out of the encircled city. He also pioneered photography underground, using artificial light in the Paris catacombs and sewers.
