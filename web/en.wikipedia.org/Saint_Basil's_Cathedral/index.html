Saint Basil's Cathedral, formally the Cathedral of the Intercession of the Most Holy Theotokos on the Moat, is an Orthodox church at the southern end of Red Square in Moscow. Built between 1555 and 1561 on the orders of Ivan the Terrible to commemorate the capture of Kazan and Astrakhan, its nine chapels crowned by vividly patterned onion domes make it one of the most recognisable buildings in Russia.

History

The cathedral was erected on the site of the earlier Trinity Church beside the moat of the Kremlin. Legend holds that Ivan blinded the architects, Barma and Postnik Yakovlev, so they could never create anything more beautiful — a story historians regard as a myth, since Postnik later built other churches. A tenth chapel was added over the grave of Saint Basil the Blessed, the beloved Moscow "fool for Christ" from whom the cathedral takes its popular name. The now-famous multicoloured domes acquired their bright painting in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries.

Architecture

The building consists of nine individual churches arranged around a central tent-roofed tower, united on a single foundation and connected by galleries. Its asymmetrical, flame-like composition had no direct precedent in Russian architecture and has inspired countless interpretations, from a bonfire rising to heaven to a vision of the Heavenly Jerusalem.

Later history

Confiscated by the Soviet state in 1929, the cathedral narrowly escaped demolition plans under Stalin and was converted into a museum branch of the State Historical Museum. Since 1990 it has formed part of the Kremlin and Red Square UNESCO World Heritage Site; occasional Orthodox services have been held there since 1991.
