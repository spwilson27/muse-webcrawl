Harvard Medical School (HMS) is the medical school of Harvard University, located in the Longwood Medical Area of Boston, Massachusetts. Founded in 1782, it is the third-oldest medical school in the United States and is consistently ranked among the top research medical schools in the world. Unlike most American medical schools, HMS does not own or operate a teaching hospital; instead it is affiliated with a network of independent hospitals in the Boston area.

History

The school was founded by Harvard president Joseph Willard and professors including John Warren, a surgeon who had served in the Revolutionary War. Lectures initially took place in Cambridge before the school moved to Boston in the nineteenth century. In 1906 it moved to its present quadrangle in the Longwood area, whose marble buildings remain its administrative and research core. HMS admitted its first women students in 1945.

Education

HMS offers the Doctor of Medicine (MD) degree through its Pathways and Health Sciences & Technology (HST) programs, the latter run jointly with MIT. It also offers PhD programs in the biomedical sciences, master's degrees, and joint degrees such as MD–PhD and MD–MBA. The curriculum emphasises early clinical exposure, case-based learning and research.

Affiliated hospitals and research

Teaching affiliates include Massachusetts General Hospital, Brigham and Women's Hospital, Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center, Boston Children's Hospital, and the Dana–Farber Cancer Institute. The school's faculty — numbering over 10,000 across affiliates — have included numerous Nobel laureates, and HMS receives among the largest amounts of NIH research funding of any medical school.

Notable contributions

Research associated with HMS and its affiliates includes the introduction of ether anesthesia at Massachusetts General Hospital (1846), early work on insulin, the development of the first successful hormonal treatment for cancer by Charles Huggins' collaborators, and advances in immunology and genetics.
