Assistive technology

Assistive technology (AT) is a term for assistive, adaptive, and rehabilitative devices for people with disabilities and the elderly. It includes products, equipment, and systems that enhance learning, working, and daily living for persons with disabilities, from low-tech aids such as magnifying glasses to high-tech devices such as powered wheelchairs, prosthetics, and screen readers.

Categories

Assistive technology spans mobility aids (wheelchairs, walkers, prosthetic limbs), communication aids (speech-generating devices and augmentative and alternative communication systems), computer access tools (screen readers, alternative keyboards, eye trackers), hearing and vision aids, and environmental control systems.

Law and provision

In the United States, the Assistive Technology Act of 1988 and its successors support state programmes, and the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act requires schools to consider AT for students with disabilities. The World Health Organization estimates that more than 2.5 billion people need one or more assistive products, with access varying enormously worldwide.

Development

Advances in robotics, artificial intelligence, brain–computer interfaces, and 3D printing are broadening the field, while universal design seeks to make mainstream products usable by all without specialised adaptation.
