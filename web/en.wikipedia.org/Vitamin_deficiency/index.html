Vitamin deficiency
SpecialtyEndocrinology, nutrition
CausesPoor diet, malabsorption, increased requirements, certain medications
ExamplesScurvy (C), beriberi (B₁), rickets (D), pellagra (B₃)

Vitamin deficiency is the condition of a long-term lack of a vitamin. When caused by not enough vitamin intake from the diet, it is classified as a primary deficiency, whereas when due to an underlying disorder such as malabsorption it is called a secondary deficiency. Well-known vitamin deficiency diseases include scurvy, beriberi, pellagra, rickets, and night blindness.

Common deficiencies

Vitamin D deficiency is among the most widespread globally and is associated with rickets in children and osteomalacia and osteoporosis in adults. Vitamin A deficiency is a leading cause of preventable blindness in children in low-income countries. Vitamin B₁₂ deficiency causes megaloblastic anemia and neurological symptoms, while folate deficiency in pregnancy is linked to neural tube defects. Vitamin C deficiency causes scurvy, historically notorious among sailors.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis typically relies on clinical signs, dietary history, and laboratory measurement of vitamin levels or functional markers. Treatment involves dietary correction and supplementation; food fortification programs — such as iodized salt analogues for vitamins, folic-acid fortification of flour, and vitamin D fortification of milk — have reduced deficiency rates in many countries.

Hypervitaminosis

The opposite condition, excessive vitamin intake, is called hypervitaminosis and applies mainly to the fat-soluble vitamins A and D, which accumulate in the body; megadoses of supplements are the usual cause.
