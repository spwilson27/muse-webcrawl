The Cradle Will Rock

Music & lyricsMarc Blitzstein
Premiere16 June 1937
ProductionsFederal Theatre Project, 1937
GenreMusical, labour opera

The Cradle Will Rock is a 1937 musical in ten scenes with music, lyrics, and book by Marc Blitzstein. A pro-union allegory set in "Steeltown, USA", it was originally produced by the Federal Theatre Project and directed by Orson Welles.

The 1937 premiere

Days before the scheduled opening, the WPA shut down the production, officially for budget reasons, and the theatre was padlocked. Welles and producer John Houseman moved the audience to another theatre, where, barred by their unions from performing on stage, the cast sang and acted from seats in the audience while Blitzstein played the score alone at a piano. The improvised performance became one of the most famous episodes in American theatre history.

Plot and legacy

The story contrasts the corrupt Liberty Committee of Steeltown, in thrall to the steel magnate Mr. Mister, with the steelworker Larry Foreman, who urges his fellow workers to unionise. The incident was dramatised in Tim Robbins's 1999 film Cradle Will Rock, and the musical has been regularly revived.
