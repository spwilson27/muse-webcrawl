Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet

Johann Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet (German: [ləˈʒœn diʁiˈkleː]; 13 February 1805 – 5 May 1859) was a German mathematician who made foundational contributions to number theory, analysis and mathematical physics. He proved the theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions that bears his name, introduced the modern concept of a function, and devised the pigeonhole principle.

Life

Born in Düren, then under French rule, Dirichlet studied in Paris, where he came to the attention of Fourier and other leading mathematicians. In 1825 he presented a celebrated partial proof of Fermat's Last Theorem for the case n = 5, completed with Legendre. He became a professor at Breslau and Berlin, and in 1855 succeeded Gauss at Göttingen.

Work

Dirichlet's 1837 proof that every coprime arithmetic progression contains infinitely many primes founded analytic number theory, using the L-functions now named for him. His work on Fourier series (1829) gave the first rigorous convergence conditions; the Dirichlet kernel, Dirichlet conditions and Dirichlet's approximation theorem in Diophantine approximation all bear his name, as do the Dirichlet boundary condition in partial differential equations, the Dirichlet distribution in probability, and the box (pigeonhole) principle he used to great effect.
