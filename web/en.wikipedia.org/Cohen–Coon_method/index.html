Cohen–Coon method

The Cohen–Coon method is a procedure for tuning the parameters of a PID controller, published by G. H. Cohen and G. A. Coon in 1953. Like the earlier Ziegler–Nichols method, it is based on an open-loop step-response test of the process, but it explicitly takes the process dead time into account and is better suited to processes with relatively large time delays.

Procedure

The process is modelled as first-order plus dead time (FOPDT): a steady-state gain K, a time constant τ, and a dead time θ, all read from the reaction curve after a step change in the controller output. Cohen and Coon derived formulas giving the proportional gain, integral time and derivative time as functions of these three parameters, aiming for a quarter-amplitude decay ratio with minimum integral error for load disturbances.

Comparison

Compared with Ziegler–Nichols open-loop tuning, Cohen–Coon rules tend to give faster responses for processes where the dead time is a substantial fraction of the time constant, but the resulting controllers can be aggressive and sensitive to modelling error. The method remains a standard reference in process control textbooks.
