Guidance system

A guidance system is a virtual or physical device, or a group of devices, that implements the process of guidance — the calculation and execution of the movements of a vehicle, such as a spacecraft, aircraft, missile or ship, along a desired trajectory. A guidance system typically works in concert with navigation (which determines position, velocity and attitude) and control (which actuates the needed forces), together forming a guidance, navigation and control (GNC) system.

Principles

Guidance algorithms compare the vehicle's actual state with the desired trajectory and generate steering commands. Missile guidance commonly follows laws such as proportional navigation, in which the missile turns at a rate proportional to the rotation of the line of sight to the target. Guidance may be based on inertial measurements alone, on external references such as GPS or celestial fixes, or on a seeker that senses the target directly, as in radar, infrared or laser homing.

Types

Systems are described by where sensing and computation occur: command guidance sends instructions from a ground station; beam-riding weapons follow an illuminating beam; homing weapons carry their own seekers, in active, semi-active or passive form; and inertial and satellite-guided systems navigate autonomously to pre-programmed coordinates. Modern precision-guided munitions typically combine inertial navigation with GPS updates and a terminal seeker.

History

Guidance research accelerated during the Second World War with radio-controlled weapons and the inertially guided V-2 rocket. The Cold War produced intercontinental ballistic missile guidance of extreme accuracy and the Apollo Guidance Computer, a landmark in digital computing, while satellite navigation later made precise guidance widely available.
