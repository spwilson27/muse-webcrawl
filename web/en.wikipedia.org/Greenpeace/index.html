Greenpeace

Greenpeace is an independent global environmental campaigning network founded in Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada, in 1971. It grew out of the Don't Make a Wave Committee, which protested United States nuclear weapons testing at Amchitka Island, Alaska. Greenpeace uses non-violent direct action, lobbying and research to expose environmental problems and promote solutions, campaigning on issues including climate change, deforestation, overfishing, whaling, genetic engineering and nuclear power. Its international coordinating body, Greenpeace International, is based in Amsterdam, and national and regional offices operate in more than 55 countries.

Notable campaigns

Greenpeace's campaign against commercial whaling and French nuclear testing in the Pacific brought it worldwide prominence. In 1985 its flagship Rainbow Warrior was bombed and sunk in Auckland, New Zealand, by French intelligence agents, killing photographer Fernando Pereira; the scandal damaged France's government and greatly increased Greenpeace's support. The organisation played a role in securing the Antarctic-Environmental Protocol, the moratorium on commercial whaling, and bans on ocean dumping. It accepts no funding from governments or corporations, relying on individual supporters and foundations.
