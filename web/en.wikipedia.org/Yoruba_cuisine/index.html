Yoruba cuisine

A selection of Yoruba dishes

Yoruba cuisine is the traditional cooking of the Yoruba people of southwestern Nigeria and neighbouring parts of Benin and Togo. It is characterised by starchy staples made from yam, cassava and plantain, served with richly flavoured soups and stews, often cooked with palm oil, peppers, tomatoes and locust beans (iru).

Staples

The central place in a Yoruba meal is held by soft dough-like foods known generally as okele or "swallows". These include amala, made from yam flour (elubo) or cassava flour and often dark brown in colour; iyan (pounded yam); eba, made from garri (roasted cassava granules); and fufu. They are eaten with the fingers and dipped into accompanying soups.

Soups and stews

Well-known soups include egusi, thickened with ground melon seeds; efo riro, a spinach or leafy-green stew cooked with peppers, palm oil and assorted meats; gbegiri, a smooth bean soup often served with ewedu (jute leaf soup) and amala in the combination known as abula; and okra-based soups. Stews (obe) are typically built on a blended base of tomatoes, scotch bonnet peppers (ata rodo) and onions fried in palm oil or vegetable oil.

Rice dishes and snacks

Jollof rice is a celebratory staple at parties, alongside fried rice and coconut rice. Street foods and snacks include akara (fried bean cakes), moi moi (steamed bean pudding), dodo (fried ripe plantain), puff-puff, and roasted corn. Asun (spicy grilled goat meat) and suya-style grilled meats are popular at gatherings.

Ceremonial food

Food plays a central role in Yoruba festivals, weddings and naming ceremonies, where large communal trays of rice, meats and fried snacks are served. Certain foods, such as kola nuts, also carry ritual significance in traditional religion and hospitality.
