Academic journal

An academic journal or scholarly journal is a periodical publication in which scholarship relating to a particular academic discipline is published. Academic journals serve as permanent and transparent forums for the presentation, scrutiny, and discussion of research. They differ from magazines in that their content is usually peer reviewed and written for an expert audience.

History

The first academic journals appeared in 1665: the French Journal des sçavans and the English Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society, the latter edited by Henry Oldenburg. Over the following centuries, journals multiplied with the professionalisation of science, shifting from general learned-society proceedings to highly specialised titles, and in the late 20th and 21st centuries from print to online publication.

Peer review

Most academic journals subject submissions to peer review, in which independent experts evaluate a manuscript's quality, originality and significance before publication. Review may be single-blind, double-blind or open. Acceptance rates at prestigious journals can be below 10%.

Prestige and metrics

Journals are ranked informally by prestige and quantitatively by citation metrics such as the impact factor, calculated by Clarivate in the Journal Citation Reports, and alternatives such as the h-index and Scopus CiteScore. Publishing in high-impact journals is a major factor in academic hiring and promotion, a practice often criticised under declarations such as DORA.

Open access

The open access movement, crystallised by the 2002 Budapest Open Access Initiative, promotes free online access to journal literature. Models include gold open access (author- or funder-paid article processing charges), green open access (self-archived preprints) and diamond open access (no charges to readers or authors).
