Reaction norm

In ecology and genetics, a reaction norm (or norm of reaction) is the pattern of phenotypes produced by a single genotype across a range of environments. Plotting a trait against an environmental variable for one genotype yields a curve; different genotypes give different curves whose shapes reveal genotype-by-environment interaction.

Concept

The term was developed by Richard Woltereck (1909) from studies of Daphnia, and formalised by evolutionary biologists such as Ivan Schmalhausen and Theodosius Dobzhansky. A flat reaction norm indicates canalisation — the trait is insensitive to environment — while a steep or crossing set of norms indicates phenotypic plasticity. Crossing norms, where the ranking of genotypes changes between environments, are especially important in plant and animal breeding and in the evolution of plasticity.
