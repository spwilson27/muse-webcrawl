The MG Rover Group was a British car manufacturer that operated from 2000 to 2005, the last domestically owned volume car maker in the United Kingdom. It was formed when the Phoenix Consortium bought the Rover and MG businesses from BMW for a nominal £10 in May 2000.

History

BMW had owned Rover since 1994 and, facing heavy losses, sold Land Rover to Ford and retained Mini, leaving the Longbridge-based volume car business to the Phoenix buyers, led by John Towers. MG Rover launched sporting MG derivatives of the Rover 25, 45 and 75 (the ZR, ZS and ZT) and continued the MG F/TF roadster, winning favourable reviews, but its model range aged and sales fell from about 200,000 toward 100,000 cars a year.

Collapse

A planned joint venture with China's Shanghai Automotive (SAIC) — seen as the company's only route to new models — collapsed in April 2005. MG Rover entered administration on 8 April 2005 with debts of about £1.4 billion; some 6,000 jobs at Longbridge were lost, with many more in the supply chain. Nanjing Automobile subsequently bought the remaining assets; SAIC, which had acquired rights to the Rover 75 design, later launched the Roewe brand, and MG production was revived in China and, for a time, at Longbridge.
