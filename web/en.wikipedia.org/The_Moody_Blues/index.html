The Moody Blues

The Moody Blues were an English rock band formed in Birmingham in 1964. Initially an R&B group, they reinvented themselves with the 1967 album Days of Future Passed, which fused rock with orchestral music and helped pioneer progressive rock. Their best-known songs include "Nights in White Satin", "Tuesday Afternoon", and "Question".

History

The original line-up scored a number-one hit in 1965 with a cover of "Go Now". After commercial decline, Justin Hayward and John Lodge joined in 1966, and the band, working with producer Tony Clarke and the London Festival Orchestra, recorded Days of Future Passed — a landmark concept album built around a day in the life of an ordinary man, distinguished by Mike Pinder's Mellotron. A run of successful albums followed, including In Search of the Lost Chord (1968), To Our Children's Children's Children (1969), and A Question of Balance (1970).

Later years and legacy

After a hiatus in the 1970s, the band returned with Octave (1978) and Long Distance Voyager (1981), reaching a new audience in the synth-pop era with hits like "Your Wildest Dreams" (1986). The Moody Blues sold over 70 million albums worldwide and were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2018.
