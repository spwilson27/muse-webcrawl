Leap second

A leap second inserts the extra second 23:59:60 into UTC.

A leap second is a one-second adjustment that is occasionally applied to Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) to accommodate irregularities in the Earth's rotation. Because the solar day slowly lengthens over time, leap seconds keep UTC within 0.9 seconds of astronomical time (UT1).

Insertion

Leap seconds are announced by the International Earth Rotation and Reference Systems Service (IERS) and are inserted at the end of June or December, as 23:59:60 UTC. A total of 27 leap seconds were inserted between 1972, when the system began, and 2017, bringing the difference between UTC and International Atomic Time (TAI) to 37 seconds.

Problems and future

Leap seconds have caused software malfunctions and outages in computing systems, which assume each minute has exactly 60 seconds. In 2022 the General Conference on Weights and Measures resolved to discontinue leap seconds by or before 2035, allowing UTC and UT1 to diverge further before a new adjustment mechanism is needed.
