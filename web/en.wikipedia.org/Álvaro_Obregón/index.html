Álvaro Obregón

Born19 February 1880, Navojoa, Sonora, Mexico
Died17 July 1928 (aged 48), Mexico City, Mexico
OfficePresident of Mexico (1920–1924)

Álvaro Obregón Salido (19 February 1880 – 17 July 1928) was a Mexican military general and statesman who served as President of Mexico from 1920 to 1924. A brilliant strategist of the Mexican Revolution, he was the revolution's outstanding battlefield commander and never lost a battle he personally directed.

Military career

A chickpea farmer and mechanic from Sonora, Obregón rose to prominence fighting for Venustiano Carranza's Constitutionalists. His victories over Pancho Villa's Division of the North in the Bajío campaign of 1915 — including the battles around Celaya, where he lost his right arm — broke Villa's power. He later broke with Carranza, issued the Plan of Agua Prieta in 1920, and became president after Carranza's death.

Presidency and assassination

As president he consolidated the revolutionary state: land reform, labour rights, recognition by the United States, and a flourishing of public education and muralist art under José Vasconcelos. Constitutionally barred from consecutive re-election, he returned to the presidency-elect in 1928 after a constitutional amendment, but was assassinated in July 1928 by José de León Toral, a Catholic militant angered by the government's persecution of the Church during the Cristero War.
