Kayaking

Kayaking is the use of a kayak for moving over water. It is distinguished from canoeing by the sitting position of the paddler and the number of blades on the paddle. A kayak is a low-to-the-water, canoe-like boat in which the paddler sits facing forward with legs in front, using a double-bladed paddle to pull front-to-back on alternating sides to rotate and move forwards.

History

Kayaks were originally developed by the Inuit, Yupik, and Aleut peoples of the Arctic regions, who used them for hunting on inland lakes, rivers and coastal waters. The name kayak derives from the Greenlandic word qajaq. Traditional kayaks had a wooden or whalebone frame covered with stretched sealskin or other animal hides.

Types

Modern kayaking includes a wide range of recreational and competitive disciplines: sea kayaking and touring on open water, whitewater kayaking on rapids, surf kayaking, kayak fishing, and flatwater sprint and slalom racing, both of which are Olympic sports. Kayak designs vary accordingly, from long, narrow sea kayaks to short, highly manoeuvrable whitewater playboats, and are now usually made of moulded plastic or composite materials.

Safety

Kayakers typically wear personal flotation devices, and in whitewater also helmets and protective clothing against cold water. Key skills include wet exits, the Eskimo roll for righting a capsized kayak, and reading river features.
