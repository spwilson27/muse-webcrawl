The United Arab Emirates (UAE) is a country in West Asia, in the Middle East, at the eastern end of the Arabian Peninsula. It is a federal elective monarchy made up of seven emirates: Abu Dhabi, Ajman, Dubai, Fujairah, Ras Al Khaimah, Sharjah and Umm Al Quwain. Abu Dhabi is the capital and the largest emirate by area; Dubai is the largest city and a global centre of trade, finance and tourism.

History

The region was historically known as the Trucial States, a group of sheikhdoms bound by treaties with Britain from the 19th century. Following the British withdrawal, six emirates formed the federation on 2 December 1971, with Ras Al Khaimah joining in February 1972. Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan of Abu Dhabi, the federation's founding president, used oil revenues to build infrastructure, healthcare and education.

Economy

The UAE has one of the most diversified economies in the Gulf, though hydrocarbons remain a major revenue source, especially in Abu Dhabi. Dubai built its economy on trade, logistics, aviation (Emirates airline), real-estate and tourism; landmarks include the Burj Khalifa, the world's tallest building at 828 metres, and the Palm Islands. The country is a founding member of the Gulf Cooperation Council and a member of OPEC.

Demographics

Expatriates make up the large majority of the population, with South Asians forming the largest group. Emirati citizens are a minority in their own country. Islam is the official religion; Arabic is the official language, while English is widely used in business and education.

Government

The Federal Supreme Council, composed of the rulers of the seven emirates, elects the president and vice president. The presidency has traditionally been held by the ruler of Abu Dhabi and the prime ministership by the ruler of Dubai.
