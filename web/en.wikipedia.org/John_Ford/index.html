John Ford

Born: John Martin Feeney, 1 February 1894, Cape Elizabeth, Maine, U.S.
Died: 31 August 1973 (aged 79), Palm Desert, California
Occupation: Film director, producer
Years active: 1913–1971
Awards: Four Academy Awards for Best Director

John Ford (February 1, 1894 – August 31, 1973) was an American film director and producer. He is regarded as one of the most important and influential filmmakers of all time, renowned both for Westerns such as Stagecoach (1939), The Searchers (1956) and The Man Who Shot Liberty Valance (1962), and for literary adaptations including The Grapes of Wrath (1940).

Career

Born John Martin Feeney to Irish immigrants, Ford followed his brother Francis to Hollywood and rose from stunt work to directing silent films. His career spanned more than 130 films over six decades. Ford won a record four Academy Awards for Best Director — for The Informer (1935), The Grapes of Wrath, How Green Was My Valley (1941) and The Quiet Man (1952) — plus two for Second World War documentaries.

Style and collaborators

Ford's films are noted for their location photography, especially in Monument Valley, long takes, and themes of community, sacrifice and American mythology. His stock company included John Wayne, Henry Fonda, Maureen O'Hara and Ward Bond. During the Second World War he headed the U.S. Navy's photographic unit and filmed the Battle of Midway.

Legacy

Directors from Akira Kurosawa to Steven Spielberg have cited Ford as a formative influence. He received the AFI Life Achievement Award in 1973, the first ever presented.
