Environmental movement

The environmental movement is a diverse social and political movement advocating protection of ecosystems, public health, and natural resources. It includes conservation groups, climate activists, environmental justice organizations, Indigenous land defenders, scientists, and green parties.

Origins

Modern environmentalism drew on nineteenth-century conservation and preservation movements, public health reforms, and reactions to industrial pollution. Rachel Carson's Silent Spring in 1962 and the first Earth Day in 1970 helped turn environmental protection into mass politics in many countries.

Issues and strategies

Campaigns address air and water pollution, deforestation, biodiversity loss, toxic waste, nuclear risks, ocean protection, and climate change. Tactics range from litigation, lobbying, and shareholder action to boycotts, civil disobedience, habitat restoration, and community organizing.

Global development

Since the late twentieth century the movement has become more global and more focused on justice, because pollution and climate impacts fall heavily on low-income communities and the Global South. Debates continue over economic growth, technology, population, and the role of markets. See also environmentalism, climate movement, and environmental justice.
