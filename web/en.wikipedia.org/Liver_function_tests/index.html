Liver function tests

Liver function tests (LFTs), also known as a liver panel, are groups of blood tests that provide information about the state of a patient's liver. Despite the name, most of these tests measure liver injury or cholestasis rather than true liver function.

Standard panel

  • Alanine transaminase (ALT) and aspartate transaminase (AST) — enzymes released when hepatocytes are damaged.
  • Alkaline phosphatase (ALP) — elevated in bile duct obstruction and bone disease.
  • Gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT) — supports a hepatic source of raised ALP; also raised with alcohol use.
  • Bilirubin — elevated in jaundice from liver or biliary disease or haemolysis.
  • Albumin and prothrombin time / INR — reflect the liver's synthetic function.

Patterns of abnormality

Clinicians interpret results as hepatocellular (transaminase-predominant), cholestatic (ALP/bilirubin-predominant), or mixed patterns, guiding investigation of causes such as viral hepatitis, fatty liver disease, alcohol-related liver disease, drug injury, gallstones and autoimmune liver disease.
