Confucianism, also known as Ruism, is a system of thought and behaviour originating in ancient China, variously described as a tradition, a philosophy, a religion, or a way of life. Developed from the teachings of the Chinese philosopher Confucius (551–479 BCE), it centres on the cultivation of virtue, the maintenance of social harmony through ritual and hierarchy, reverence for ancestors, and rule by moral example.

Core doctrines

Confucian ethics emphasise ren (humaneness or benevolence), li (ritual propriety and social norms), xiao (filial piety), and zhong (loyalty). The ideal person is the junzi ("gentleman" or "superior person"), who embodies virtue rather than merely noble birth. Society is ordered through the five relationships — ruler and subject, parent and child, husband and wife, elder and younger sibling, friend and friend — each with reciprocal duties. Heaven (Tian) and the Mandate of Heaven provided a moral order to which rulers were answerable.

History

Confucius's disciples compiled his sayings in the Analects. Mencius (4th century BCE) developed his ideas, holding human nature to be good, while Xunzi taught that nature is bad and must be shaped by ritual and education. Under Emperor Wu of Han (r. 141–87 BCE), Confucianism became the official ideology of the Chinese state, and mastery of the Confucian classics became the basis of civil service examinations for over a millennium.

In the Song dynasty, Neo-Confucianism, led by Zhu Xi (1130–1200), synthesised Confucian thought with elements drawn from Buddhism and Daoism, organising doctrine around li (principle) and qi (material force); Wang Yangming later stressed the unity of knowledge and action. Confucianism spread to Korea, Japan and Vietnam, shaping their institutions and ethics.

The tradition was attacked during China's twentieth-century convulsions — from the May Fourth Movement to the Cultural Revolution's "Criticise Lin, Criticise Confucius" campaign — but has been revived and officially promoted in China since the 1980s, and remains influential in family life, education and governance across East Asia.
