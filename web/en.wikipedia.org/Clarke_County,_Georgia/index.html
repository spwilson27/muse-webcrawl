Clarke County is a county in the northeastern part of the U.S. state of Georgia. As of the 2020 census, the population was 128,671. Its county seat is Athens. Clarke County is the smallest county by land area in Georgia and is unusual in that the city of Athens and the county operate as a single consolidated city–county government, the Unified Government of Athens–Clarke County, formed in 1991.

History

Clarke County was created on 5 December 1801, from land taken from Jackson County. It was named for Elijah Clarke, a hero of the American Revolutionary War in Georgia. In 1801 the trustees of the newly chartered University of Georgia selected a site on the Oconee River within the county for the university, and the town of Athens grew up around it.

Geography

The county covers about 121 square miles in the Piedmont region of Georgia. The North and Middle Oconee Rivers flow through it, joining south of Athens. Despite its small area it is densely developed around Athens, while the southern and eastern edges retain wooded and rural character.

Economy and culture

The University of Georgia, founded in 1785 and the state's flagship university, dominates the county's economy and culture. Athens is also renowned for its music scene, which produced bands such as R.E.M. and the B-52's, and for venues including the 40 Watt Club and the Georgia Theatre. Major employers include the university, regional hospitals, and manufacturing plants.

Transportation

U.S. Route 29 and Georgia State Route 316 connect the county westward toward Atlanta; the Athens Perimeter (State Route 10 Loop) circles the city. Athens–Ben Epps Airport provides general aviation service.
