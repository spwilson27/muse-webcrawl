Commonwealth War Graves Commission

The Commonwealth War Graves Commission (CWGC) is an intergovernmental organisation of six independent member states whose principal function is to mark, record, and maintain the graves and places of commemoration of Commonwealth of Nations military service members who died in the two World Wars. Founded by Royal Charter in 1917 as the Imperial War Graves Commission, it was renamed in 1960.

Work

The Commission commemorates about 1.7 million men and women at some 23,000 locations in more than 150 countries. It maintains cemeteries and memorials built to principles established after the First World War: each of the dead should be commemorated individually by name on a uniform headstone or on a memorial, regardless of military rank or social status, and the commemoration should be permanent. Signature features include the Cross of Sacrifice, the Stone of Remembrance designed by Edwin Lutyens, and registers and horticulture maintained to a uniform standard.

The Commission was the creation largely of Fabian Ware, who had commanded a British Red Cross unit recording graves during the First World War. It also maintains an extensive online casualty database and archives.
