Stimulus (psychology)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Diagram of the stimulus–response pathway in behaviour.

In psychology, a stimulus is any object, event, or change in the environment that can be detected by the senses and can influence the behaviour of an organism. Stimuli are the inputs of the stimulus–response framework that underlies behaviourism and much of experimental psychology.

Types

Stimuli may be external (light, sound, touch, odours) or internal (hunger pangs, thoughts). In classical conditioning, following Pavlov, researchers distinguish the unconditioned stimulus, which naturally elicits a response, from the conditioned stimulus, which acquires that power through association. In operant conditioning a discriminative stimulus signals that a response will be reinforced.

Properties

The effectiveness of a stimulus depends on its intensity, novelty, and salience relative to background stimulation. Phenomena such as habituation, sensitization, stimulus generalization and stimulus discrimination describe how organisms adjust their responses to stimuli over time and across similar inputs.

Study

Psychophysics quantifies the relationship between stimulus magnitude and sensation, while neuroscience traces how sensory stimuli are transduced by receptors and processed by the nervous system into perception and action.
