Dong Son culture

The Dong Son culture (c. 1000 BCE – 1st century CE) was a Bronze Age culture centered at the Red River Valley of northern Vietnam. It is named for the site of Đông Sơn, a village in Thanh Hóa province where many of its characteristic artifacts were found.

Characteristics

The culture is best known for its advanced bronze casting, especially the large ceremonial bronze drums decorated with geometric patterns, star motifs, and scenes of daily life, warfare, and water rituals. The drums were ritual objects and status symbols, traded across Southeast Asia and southern China.

Society and legacy

Dong Son people cultivated wet rice, built settlements on river terraces, and made a wide range of bronze tools, weapons, and ornaments. The culture is associated with the early Vietnamese state of Văn Lang and the legendary Hùng kings, and is regarded as foundational to Vietnamese cultural identity.
