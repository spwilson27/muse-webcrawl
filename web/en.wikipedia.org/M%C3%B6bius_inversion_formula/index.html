Möbius inversion formula
Introduced byAugust Ferdinand Möbius
Year1832
FieldNumber theory, combinatorics

The Möbius inversion formula is a relation between a pair of arithmetic functions that allows one to be recovered from the divisor sums of the other. It was introduced by August Ferdinand Möbius in 1832 and is a cornerstone of multiplicative number theory.

Statement

If f and g are arithmetic functions such that g(n) equals the sum of f(d) over all divisors d of n, then f(n) can be recovered as the sum of μ(d)·g(n/d) over all divisors d of n, where μ is the Möbius function. In other words, summation over divisors and Möbius-weighted summation are inverse operations.

Applications

The formula expresses Euler's totient function as a Möbius sum over divisors, underlies the counting of aperiodic necklaces and irreducible polynomials over finite fields, and generalises to Möbius inversion on any locally finite partially ordered set, where it includes the principle of inclusion–exclusion as a special case.
