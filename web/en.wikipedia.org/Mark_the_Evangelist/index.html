Mark the Evangelist (Greek: Μᾶρκος, Markos) is the traditionally ascribed author of the Gospel of Mark. Most modern scholars agree that the Gospel was written anonymously, but Christian tradition from the 2nd century identifies its author with the John Mark mentioned in the Acts of the Apostles, a companion of the apostles Paul, Barnabas and Peter.

Tradition

According to the church historian Eusebius, citing Papias of Hierapolis, Mark served as Peter's interpreter and wrote down accurately, though not in order, the teachings Peter recalled of Jesus. Mark is traditionally regarded as the founder of the Church of Alexandria in Egypt and its first bishop; tradition relates that he was martyred there in 68 AD, dragged through the streets by pagans.

Relics and Venice

In 828 Venetian merchants were said to have stolen Mark's relics from Alexandria and brought them to Venice, where they were enshrined in St Mark's Basilica; the saint became the patron of the Venetian Republic, and his winged lion became its emblem. The Coptic Orthodox Church, which regards Mark as the first Patriarch of Alexandria, holds that his head remained in Alexandria.

Symbol

In the traditional scheme of the four Evangelists, Mark's symbol is the winged lion, associated with the opening of his Gospel — "the voice of one crying in the wilderness" — and with Christ's resurrection and royalty.
