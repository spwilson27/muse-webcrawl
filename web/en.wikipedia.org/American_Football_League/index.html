American Football League
Sport: American football
Founded: 1959
First season: 1960
Ceased: 1970 (merged into the NFL)
Countries: United States

The American Football League (AFL) was a major professional American football league that operated for ten seasons from 1960 until 1970, when it merged with the older National Football League (NFL) and became the American Football Conference. Founded by Lamar Hunt and other businessmen who had been refused NFL franchises, the AFL established itself as a credible rival league.

History

The AFL's innovations included names on jerseys, a two-point conversion, a shorter regular-season schedule at first, revenue sharing of television rights, and aggressive signing of college talent, sparking a costly bidding war with the NFL. The leagues agreed to merge in 1966, playing the first AFL–NFL World Championship Games — the forerunners of the Super Bowl.

Legacy

AFL teams won Super Bowls III and IV, capped by the New York Jets' upset of the Baltimore Colts. All ten AFL franchises survive in the modern AFC.
