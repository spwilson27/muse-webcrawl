Astrakhan Khanate

The Khanate of Astrakhan (1466–1556) was a Tatar feudal state in the lower Volga region, one of the successor states of the Golden Horde. Its capital, Xacitarxan (Astrakhan), stood near the mouth of the Volga River on the Caspian Sea and controlled an important trading crossroads between Persia, Central Asia and Muscovy.

Founding

The khanate emerged around 1466 under Mahmud bin Küchük, a descendant of the khans of the Golden Horde, as the Horde fragmented into the khanates of Kazan, Crimea, Astrakhan and Siberia. Its territory covered the Volga delta and the steppe between the Terek and Yaik rivers.

Society and economy

The population consisted largely of Nogai and Tatar nomads in the steppe and a settled, ethnically mixed urban population in Astrakhan itself. The city was a major entrepôt for silk, spices, slaves, salt and fish; its markets attracted merchants from Persia, Bukhara, India and Russia.

Relations with neighbours

The khanate oscillated between dependence on the Crimean Khanate, the Nogai Horde and the Ottoman Empire, and was the target of expanding Muscovite influence down the Volga. Several khans were installed or deposed with Crimean or Russian backing during the 1550s.

Conquest by Russia

In 1554 Ivan IV of Russia intervened, installing the compliant Dervish Ali as khan; when he defected to the Crimean side, Russian forces returned in 1556 and annexed the khanate outright. Astrakhan became a Russian fortress city, opening the Volga to Russian trade and settlement.
