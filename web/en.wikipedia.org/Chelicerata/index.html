Chelicerata

The Chelicerata are a subphylum of arthropods characterised by the presence of chelicerae — pointed appendages used for feeding — above the mouthparts. The group includes arachnids (spiders, scorpions, mites and ticks), horseshoe crabs, and sea spiders.

Anatomy

The chelicerate body is divided into a cephalothorax (prosoma) bearing six pairs of appendages — chelicerae, pedipalps and four pairs of walking legs — and an abdomen (opisthosoma). Unlike other arthropods they have no antennae.

Diversity and evolution

Over 77,000 living species have been described, and mites and ticks alone may number far more. Chelicerates originated in the sea in the Cambrian period; arachnids colonised land early, and the extinct eurypterids (sea scorpions) included some of the largest arthropods ever known.

Classification

Living classes are Arachnida, Xiphosura (horseshoe crabs) and Pycnogonida (sea spiders), though molecular studies often place horseshoe crabs within Arachnida.
