Charles Stark "Doc" Draper (October 2, 1901 – July 25, 1987) was an American scientist and engineer, known as the "father of inertial navigation". He founded the Massachusetts Institute of Technology's Instrumentation Laboratory, later renamed the Charles Stark Draper Laboratory, which under his direction designed the Apollo Guidance Computer.

Career

Draper earned degrees from Stanford and MIT, joining the MIT faculty in the 1930s. During the Second World War his laboratory developed gyroscopic gunsights, notably the Mark 14 sight used by the U.S. Navy. After the war he pioneered inertial guidance — using gyroscopes and accelerometers to navigate without external references — demonstrating transcontinental inertial flight in 1953 and supplying guidance systems for Polaris and other ballistic missiles.

Apollo programme

In 1961 the MIT Instrumentation Laboratory won the first major Apollo contract, to design the guidance, navigation and control system for the Apollo spacecraft. The resulting computer and inertial platform guided every Apollo mission, including the Apollo 11 Moon landing in 1969. In 1973 the laboratory was spun off from MIT as the independent Charles Stark Draper Laboratory in Cambridge, Massachusetts.

Honours

Draper received the National Medal of Science in 1964 and was inducted into the National Inventors Hall of Fame. The Charles Stark Draper Prize, established in 1989, is one of the world's foremost engineering awards.
