Circle group

In mathematics, the circle group, denoted by T (or 𝕋), is the multiplicative group of all complex numbers with absolute value 1; that is, the unit circle in the complex plane. Under multiplication it forms an abelian group, and with the subspace topology it is a topological group and a one-dimensional Lie group.

Definition and properties

The circle group consists of complex numbers of the form eiθ = cos θ + i sin θ for real θ, with multiplication corresponding to addition of angles. It is isomorphic to the quotient group ℝ/ℤ of the real numbers by the integers, and to the group of rotations of the plane about the origin, SO(2). Unlike most groups of its kind, the circle group is divisible and its torsion subgroup consists of the roots of unity, which form a dense subgroup isomorphic to ℚ/ℤ.

Representations and duality

The circle group is the archetypal compact abelian group. Its continuous irreducible representations are the characters z ↦ zn for each integer n, and its Pontryagin dual is the discrete group ℤ — the duality at the heart of Fourier analysis. Every compact abelian group can be built from circle groups and finite cyclic groups.

Applications

The circle group appears as the gauge group U(1) of electromagnetism, as the phase group in quantum mechanics, and throughout harmonic analysis, number theory (via characters modulo n), and the theory of Lie groups, where it is the simplest nontrivial compact connected Lie group.
