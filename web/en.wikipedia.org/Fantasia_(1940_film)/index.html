Fantasia is a 1940 American animated musical anthology film produced and released by Walt Disney Productions. It consists of eight animated segments set to pieces of classical music conducted by Leopold Stokowski and performed by the Philadelphia Orchestra. Music commentator Deems Taylor provides live-action introductions to each segment. Fantasia was Disney's third animated feature, following Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs (1937) and Pinocchio (1940).

Segments

  • Toccata and Fugue in D Minor by J. S. Bach — abstract patterns inspired by the music.
  • Nutcracker Suite by Tchaikovsky — nature scenes of the changing seasons.
  • The Sorcerer's Apprentice by Paul Dukas — Mickey Mouse, as an apprentice magician, enchants a broom to do his chores, with disastrous results.
  • Rite of Spring by Igor Stravinsky — the history of Earth from its formation to the extinction of the dinosaurs.
  • The Pastoral Symphony by Beethoven — a mythological Greco-Roman setting with centaurs, fauns, and the gods of Olympus.
  • Dance of the Hours by Ponchielli — a ballet performed by ostriches, hippos, elephants, and alligators.
  • Night on Bald Mountain by Mussorgsky and Ave Maria by Schubert — the demon Chernabog summons evil spirits, dispelled at dawn by a procession of monks.

Production and release

The film grew out of The Sorcerer's Apprentice, a Silly Symphony short intended to restore Mickey Mouse's popularity, which expanded into a full concert feature. Disney developed Fantasound, a pioneering stereophonic sound system, for the film's roadshow engagements, but high installation costs limited its rollout. Fantasia initially lost money, partly because World War II cut off the European market, but repeated reissues made it profitable, and it is now regarded as a landmark of animation. It was followed by a sequel, Fantasia 2000, released in 1999.
