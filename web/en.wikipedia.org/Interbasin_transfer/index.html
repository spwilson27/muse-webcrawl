Interbasin transfer

Interbasin transfer or transbasin diversion describes man-made conveyance schemes which move water from one river basin, where it is available, to another basin where water is less available, in order to fulfill specific human needs. The water is transferred through canals, pipelines, tunnels, or a combination of them, sometimes over hundreds of kilometres and across drainage divides.

Examples

Large examples include China's South–North Water Transfer Project, the largest water-transfer scheme in the world; the California State Water Project and Colorado River Aqueduct supplying Southern California; the Snowy Mountains Scheme in Australia; and the Lesotho Highlands Water Project, which exports water from Lesotho to South Africa. South Africa, Spain, and India also operate major transfer schemes.

Impacts and controversy

Transfers can secure drinking water and irrigation for water-scarce regions, but they may reduce flows and damage ecosystems in the donor basin, spread invasive species and pollutants between basins, and raise contentious questions of water rights and compensation. Proposals are frequently opposed by communities in the basin of origin.
