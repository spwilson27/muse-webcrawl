Warli people

The Warli are an Adivasi (indigenous) people living in the mountainous and coastal areas of western India, chiefly in the Thane and Palghar districts of Maharashtra and adjoining parts of Gujarat. They speak the Warli language, an Indo-Aryan language.

Culture

The Warli are renowned for Warli painting, a tribal art form in which white pigment (usually rice paste) is applied to red-ochre or mud walls. Human figures are formed from two triangles joined at the tip, and paintings depict harvests, dances, the tarpa dance around a central musician, and the goddess Kansari.

Livelihood

Traditionally agriculturalists growing rice and other crops, many Warli also work as labourers. Their society is organised around nature worship and seasonal festivals tied to the agricultural cycle.

Notable figures

The artist Jivya Soma Mashe brought Warli painting to national and international attention, receiving the Padma Shri in 2011.
