Chief financial officer

A chief financial officer (CFO) is an officer of a company or organization who is assigned the primary responsibility for managing the company's finances, including financial planning, management of financial risks, record-keeping, and financial reporting. The CFO typically reports to the chief executive officer (CEO) and the board of directors.

Role

The CFO oversees the finance unit and is the chief financial spokesperson for the organization. Responsibilities include cash flow and financial planning, analyzing the company's financial strengths and weaknesses, preparing financial statements, managing investments and capital structure, and ensuring compliance with financial regulations such as the Sarbanes–Oxley Act in the United States.

Evolution of the role

Traditionally focused on accounting and reporting, the modern CFO role has expanded to include strategic planning, mergers and acquisitions, and investor relations, making the position one of the most common routes to the CEO office in large corporations.
