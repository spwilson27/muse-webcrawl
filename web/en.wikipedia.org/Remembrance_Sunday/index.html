Remembrance Sunday

Remembrance Sunday is held in the United Kingdom on the second Sunday in November, the closest to 11 November, Armistice Day. It commemorates the contribution of British and Commonwealth military and civilian servicemen and servicewomen in the two World Wars and later conflicts.

National Service of Remembrance

The national ceremony is held at the Cenotaph in Whitehall, London. At 11 a.m. the King leads the nation in a two-minute silence, marked by the firing of a gun on Horse Guards Parade; wreaths are then laid by the monarch, members of the royal family, the Prime Minister and other party leaders, representatives of the armed forces, and Commonwealth high commissioners. A short service led by the Bishop of London follows, after which thousands of veterans march past in the Royal British Legion's parade.

Local observances

Across the country, local services are held at war memorials, usually organised by the Royal British Legion, with wreaths laid by civic leaders, uniformed youth organisations and the armed forces. In Northern Ireland and Scotland, parallel national events take place in Belfast and Edinburgh. From 1946 the main national observance moved from 11 November itself to the Sunday, while a two-minute silence on Armistice Day was revived in the 1990s.
