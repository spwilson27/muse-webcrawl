Tatars

The Tatars are a group of Turkic peoples speaking languages of the Kipchak branch of the Turkic family. The name originally applied to a Mongolian tribal confederation of the eastern steppe, but after the Mongol conquests of the thirteenth century it came to denote the Turkic-speaking, largely Muslim populations of the Mongol successor states of Eastern Europe and Siberia.

Principal groups

  • Volga Tatars — the largest group, centred on Tatarstan (capital Kazan) and Bashkortostan in Russia, descendants of the Volga Bulgars and the Kipchaks of the Golden Horde and Khanate of Kazan.
  • Crimean Tatars — the indigenous Turkic people of Crimea, deported en masse to Central Asia by Stalin in 1944 and permitted to return only from the late 1980s.
  • Siberian Tatars — indigenous groups of southwestern Siberia.
  • Lipka Tatars — a historic community settled since the fourteenth century in Lithuania, Poland, and Belarus.
  • Astrakhan, Mishar, and other regional groups.

History

Tatar states — the Golden Horde, and after its fragmentation the khanates of Kazan, Crimea, Astrakhan, and Sibir — dominated the steppe between Eastern Europe and Siberia for three centuries. Russia conquered Kazan in 1552 and Astrakhan in 1556; Crimea was annexed in 1783. Under Catherine the Great, Tatar merchants and mullahs became intermediaries of Russian trade and Islam in Central Asia. In the late nineteenth century, Crimean Tatar reformer Ismail Gaspirali pioneered Jadidism, a modernising educational and pan-Turkic movement influential across the Muslim world of the Russian Empire.

Modern era

The Soviet Union created the Tatar ASSR in 1920. Crimean Tatars suffered total deportation in May 1944, in which a large fraction died; their long exile movement became one of the Soviet Union's most prominent dissident causes. Today the Republic of Tatarstan is one of Russia's most economically developed federal subjects, and Tatar communities remain spread across Russia, Ukraine, Central Asia, Turkey, and the wider diaspora. Most Tatars are Sunni Muslims of the Hanafi school, though the Kryashens are Orthodox Christians.
