Kraljevo


Kraljevo is a city in central Serbia, administrative centre of the Raška District.

Kraljevo (Serbian Cyrillic: Краљево, pronounced [krǎːʎeʋo]) is a city and the administrative centre of the Raška District in central Serbia. It is situated on the banks of the Ibar River, west of its confluence with the West Morava. The city proper has a population of roughly 60,000–70,000, with the wider municipality numbering about 125,000.

History

The settlement was long known as Rudo Polje and later Karanovac. It received its present name — meaning "the King's Town" — from King Milan I of Serbia in 1882, commemorating the coronation of medieval Serbian kings at the nearby Žiča Monastery, where Stefan the First-Crowned was crowned in 1217. Nearby are the monasteries of Žiča and Studenica, both important medieval monuments; Studenica is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

In October 1941, during the Second World War, German occupying forces massacred several thousand civilians from Kraljevo and Kragujevac in reprisal killings. In November 2010 the city was struck by a magnitude 5.4 earthquake that caused damage and casualties.

Economy

Kraljevo is an industrial and transport centre, historically known for the Magnohrom refractory works and a wagon and machinery factory, and as a rail junction in the Ibar valley.
