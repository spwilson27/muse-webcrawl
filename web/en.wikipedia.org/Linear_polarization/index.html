Linear polarization

In electrodynamics, linear polarization or plane polarization of electromagnetic radiation is a confinement of the electric field vector or magnetic field vector to a given plane along the direction of propagation. The orientation of a linearly polarised electromagnetic wave is defined by the direction of the electric field vector.

Description

A linearly polarised plane wave can be written as the sum of two orthogonal components oscillating in phase. If the two components have equal amplitude and a phase difference of a quarter cycle, the result is instead circular polarization; unequal amplitudes with a general phase difference give elliptical polarization, of which linear and circular are limiting cases. Light can be linearly polarised using a polarizer, by reflection at Brewster's angle, or by passing through certain birefringent crystals.

Applications

Linear polarization is used in polarised sunglasses to cut glare, in LCD displays, in stress analysis of transparent materials (photoelasticity), in radio antennas whose orientation defines the transmitted polarization, and in 3D cinema projection systems.
