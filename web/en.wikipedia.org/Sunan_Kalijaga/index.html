Sunan Kalijaga

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sunan Kalijaga (born Raden Mas Said, c. 1450 – c. 1592 in Javanese tradition) was one of the Wali Songo, the nine saints credited with spreading Islam in Java, Indonesia. He is among the most beloved of the nine, renowned for blending Islamic teaching with Javanese culture, arts, and traditions rather than displacing them.

Life in tradition

According to Javanese chronicles (babad), Raden Said was the son of a regent of Tuban. As a young man he is said to have rebelled against unjust taxation, robbing the wealthy to give to the poor, and was banished. In the legend, he meditated for years by a river (Javanese kali) while keeping watch (jaga) over his staff at the instruction of Sunan Bonang — the origin of the name "Kalijaga". He then became a preacher of Islam closely associated with the Demak Sultanate.

Cultural legacy

Sunan Kalijaga is credited in tradition with creating or adapting forms of Javanese art used to teach Islam, including wayang shadow-puppet theatre, gamelan music, and the sekaten festival. He is also associated with the design of the Great Mosque of Demak and with popular songs such as "Lir-ilir" and "Gundul Pacul", which remain widely known children's songs in Java. His tomb at Kadilangu, near Demak, is an important pilgrimage site.
