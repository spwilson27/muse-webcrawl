Minenwerfer

Minenwerfer ("mine thrower") is the German name for a class of short-range mortars used extensively during the First World War by the Imperial German Army. The weapons were intended for use by engineers to clear obstacles including bunkers and barbed wire that longer-range artillery would not be able to target accurately.

Background

The Germans had been studying the siege of Port Arthur, where heavy artillery had been unable to destroy defensive structures like barbed wire and bunkers. The German military engineer committee (Ingenieurkomitee) began working with Rheinmetall to study the issue. They decided to build a muzzle-loading mortar that could be fired from a pit for protection and that could be moved relatively easily between positions.

Design

The Minenwerfer family came in several sizes. The 25 cm schwerer Minenwerfer (heavy mine thrower) was the largest, a short, squat weapon with a rifled barrel fired electrically or by lanyard. Lighter weapons included the 17 cm mittlerer Minenwerfer (introduced in 1916) and the 7.58 cm leichter Minenwerfer, the latter notable for using a recoil system and capable of direct fire. Their projectiles, filled with high explosive, were fired at high angles with a low muzzle velocity, producing a plunging trajectory well suited to trench warfare.

Combat use

When the First World War broke out, the German army had 160 Minenwerfer. They were successfully used in Belgium at Liège and Namur, and against the French forts around Maubeuge. Within a few months, the Minenwerfer began to be used by German forces across the Western Front, and the concept was soon copied by the other belligerent armies, who had initially been caught without comparable short-range artillery.
