Omayra Sánchez

BornAugust 28, 1972, Armero, Tolima, Colombia
DiedNovember 16, 1985 (aged 13), Armero, Colombia
Cause of deathTrapped by debris during the Armero tragedy

Omayra Sánchez Garzón (August 28, 1972 – November 16, 1985) was a Colombian girl who became the emblematic victim of the Armero tragedy, the volcanic disaster caused by the eruption of the Nevado del Ruiz volcano on November 13, 1985. The eruption melted the mountain's ice cap, sending lahars — torrents of mud and debris — that destroyed the town of Armero and killed more than 23,000 people.

Death

Sánchez was trapped up to her neck in water, concrete, and the remains of her home, with her legs pinned beneath a door and the body of her deceased aunt. Rescue workers were unable to free her; pumps and equipment needed to move the debris were unavailable, and amputation was considered too dangerous without proper medical support. Journalists and Red Cross workers stayed with her over roughly three days, during which she sang, asked for sweet food, and was interviewed on camera. She died on November 16, after some 60 hours trapped, likely from gangrene and hypothermia.

Legacy

A photograph of Sánchez taken by photojournalist Frank Fournier shortly before her death, titled "The Agony of Omayra Sánchez," won the World Press Photo of the Year award and circulated worldwide. Her death sparked debate about the Colombian government's failure to evacuate Armero despite warnings, and about the ethics of journalists documenting a dying child. The site of Armero is now a memorial, and Sánchez is remembered in Colombia as a symbol of the disaster; the Omayra Sánchez Foundation was established to assist disaster victims.
