1979 Fastnet race

Date11–14 August 1979
CourseCowes – Fastnet Rock – Plymouth
Starters303 yachts
Deaths15 competitors (plus others at sea)

The 1979 Fastnet race was the 28th edition of the biennial offshore yacht race from Cowes, around the Fastnet Rock off Ireland, and back to Plymouth. A severe storm struck the fleet in the Irish Sea on 13–14 August 1979, turning the race into one of the worst disasters in sailing history.

The storm

A fast-deepening Atlantic depression produced winds of Force 10–11 and waves reported up to 12 metres. Of 303 starters, only 86 finished; 24 yachts were abandoned and several sank. Fifteen sailors died, as did others aboard vessels in the area.

Rescue and aftermath

The ensuing rescue operation involved the Royal Navy, RNLI lifeboats, helicopters, and merchant ships, and was the largest peacetime rescue in the region. Official inquiries led to major reforms in offshore racing safety: mandatory safety equipment, stability requirements, and improved weather forecasting.
