The Chicago Symphony Orchestra (CSO) is an American symphony orchestra based in Chicago, Illinois. Founded by Theodore Thomas in 1891, the ensemble is widely regarded as one of the world's finest orchestras, celebrated especially for its powerful brass section. The CSO is one of the five American orchestras commonly referred to as the "Big Five". Its home is Orchestra Hall at Symphony Center on Michigan Avenue, and since 1936 it has been the resident orchestra of the Ravinia Festival in Highland Park.

History

Businessman Charles Norman Fay invited conductor Theodore Thomas to found the orchestra after the Great Chicago Fire era's musical ambitions grew. Thomas led the orchestra until his death in 1905, weeks after Orchestra Hall opened in December 1904. Frederick Stock, his successor, served 37 years and built the orchestra's civic role. Later music directors included Désiré Defauw, Artur Rodziński, Rafael Kubelík, Fritz Reiner — whose 1950s tenure produced legendary recordings with the orchestra — Jean Martinon, and Sir Georg Solti (1969–1991), who raised the CSO to global fame through international tours and a long series of Grammy-winning recordings. Daniel Barenboim led from 1991 to 2006; Riccardo Muti served as music director from 2010 to 2023 and was succeeded (from the 2027–28 season) by Klaus Mäkelä, announced as the CSO's next music director.

Recordings and reputation

The CSO's recordings have won dozens of Grammy Awards. Its sound is distinguished by a brilliant, burnished brass choir, an inheritance of the Reiner and Solti eras. The orchestra maintains the CSO Radio broadcast series and its own record label, CSO Resound.

Related ensembles

The Civic Orchestra of Chicago, founded in 1919, is the CSO's training orchestra and one of the oldest pre-professional orchestras in the United States. The Chicago Symphony Chorus, founded by Margaret Hillis in 1957, performs regularly with the orchestra.
