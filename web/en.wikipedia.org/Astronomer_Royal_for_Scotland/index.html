Astronomer Royal for Scotland

Astronomer Royal for Scotland
Established: 1834
First holder: Thomas Henderson
Associated institution: Royal Observatory, Edinburgh

The Astronomer Royal for Scotland is a senior post in the United Kingdom, created in 1834 and originally linked to the directorship of the Royal Observatory, Edinburgh. For most of its history the post was held jointly with the Regius Professorship of Astronomy at the University of Edinburgh.

History

The first Astronomer Royal for Scotland was Thomas Henderson, who was appointed in 1834 and is remembered for measuring the distance to Alpha Centauri, one of the first successful stellar parallax measurements. Successors included Charles Piazzi Smyth, noted for his work on spectroscopy and his pyramidological theories, and Ralph Copeland.

In the twentieth century the post was held by figures such as William Greaves, Hermann Brück, and Vincent Reddish. Following institutional changes at the Royal Observatory Edinburgh, the title was decoupled from the directorship in the 1990s and became an honorary distinction. The astrophysicist John Campbell Brown served from 1995 until his death in 2019, and Catherine Heymans, the first woman to hold the post, was appointed in 2021.

Role

The Astronomer Royal for Scotland is an honorary royal appointment. The holder promotes astronomy and science to the Scottish public, advises on astronomical matters, and traditionally presents an annual public lecture.
