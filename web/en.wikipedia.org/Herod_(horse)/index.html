Herod (horse)

Herod (originally King Herod; foaled 1758) was a Thoroughbred racehorse and one of the most influential sires in the breed's history. Bred by Prince William, Duke of Cumberland, Herod was by Tartar out of Cypron. Although less celebrated as a racehorse than his contemporary Eclipse, Herod's impact through his progeny was enormous: he was the leading sire in Great Britain and Ireland eight times (1777–1784).

Racing career

Herod raced mainly in four-mile heats, as was customary for the era, and won several matches and plates, though he was beaten in notable contests against horses such as Bay Malton. He retired to stud after an inconsistent racing career by the standards of the great foundation sires.

Stud record

At stud Herod sired 497 winners of races worth a total of about £201,505. His best offspring included the undefeated Highflyer, who became an outstanding sire in his own right; Woodpecker; and Florizel, sire of the Derby winner Diomed. Through Highflyer and other descendants, Herod's blood appears in the pedigree of nearly every Thoroughbred alive today. He is regarded as one of the three great 18th-century foundation stallions of the breed, alongside Eclipse and Matchem — though, unlike them, he is not a direct tail-male ancestor of most modern Thoroughbreds through an unbroken sire line of comparable dominance. Herod died in 1780.
