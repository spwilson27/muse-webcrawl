Analytical chemistry

Analytical chemistry studies and uses instruments and methods to separate, identify, and quantify matter. In practice, separation, identification or quantification may constitute the entire analysis or be combined with another method. Separation isolates analytes; qualitative analysis identifies analytes, while quantitative analysis determines the numerical amount or concentration. Analytical chemistry is the science of obtaining, processing, and communicating information about the composition and structure of matter.

Classical methods

The classical ("wet chemical") methods include qualitative tests such as colour reactions, flame tests and precipitation, and quantitative techniques such as titrations (volumetric analysis) and gravimetric analysis, in which the analyte is converted to a precipitate or volatile product and weighed. These methods, developed through the 18th and 19th centuries by chemists such as Carl Remigius Fresenius, remain in daily use.

Instrumental methods

Modern analytical chemistry is dominated by instrumental analysis. Major families include:

  • Spectroscopy — ultraviolet–visible, infrared, atomic absorption and emission, NMR, and mass spectrometry; measuring how matter interacts with electromagnetic radiation or is separated by mass-to-charge ratio.
  • Chromatography and separations — gas chromatography, high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC), and electrophoresis.
  • Electroanalytical methods — potentiometry (including pH measurement), voltammetry, and coulometry.
  • Mass spectrometry — often coupled with chromatography (GC-MS, LC-MS) for ultra-trace identification.

Applications and quality

Analytical chemistry underpins pharmaceutical quality control, environmental monitoring, clinical diagnostics, forensic science, food safety, and materials characterisation. Statistical concepts — calibration, sensitivity, detection limits, precision, accuracy, and measurement uncertainty — are central to the field, as are standardised methods and reference materials.
