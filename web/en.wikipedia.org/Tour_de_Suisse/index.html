The Tour de Suisse is an annual multi-stage bicycle race held in Switzerland. Traditionally run in June, it is one of the most prestigious events on the UCI World Tour calendar and is widely regarded as the most important stage race after the three Grand Tours. Founded in 1933, the race traverses the Swiss Alps, featuring high mountain stages, individual time trials, and sprint finishes, and it serves as the principal preparation event for riders targeting the Tour de France two weeks later.

History

The race was first held in 1933 and was won by Max Bulla of Austria. Swiss riders dominated many early editions, and home champions such as Hugo Koblet and Ferdinand Kübler became celebrated figures of the race. Italy's Pasquale Fornara won a record four editions in the 1950s. The race did not take place during much of World War II, but returned as a cornerstone of the postwar calendar.

Since joining the UCI World Tour, the Tour de Suisse has attracted the sport's leading stage racers. Overall winners have included Lance Armstrong, Alberto Contador, Richie Porte, Egan Bernal and Adam Yates. Organizers frequently design routes crossing famous passes such as the Gotthard, Furka, Susten and Nufenen.

Race format

The event typically consists of eight or nine stages over nine days, including at least one individual time trial — often a mountain time trial — and several demanding alpine days. The general classification is decided by cumulative time. The leader wears a distinctive jersey, and subsidiary competitions reward points, mountains, and the best young rider.

Jerseys and classifications

  • General classification — lowest cumulative time overall.
  • Points classification — awarded to the most consistent stage finisher.
  • Mountains classification — points at designated summits.
  • Young rider classification — best placed rider under a set age.

Notable moments

The 2023 edition was overshadowed by the death of Swiss rider Gino Mäder following a crash on the descent of the Albula Pass, which led to tributes across the peloton and renewed debate about rider safety on high-speed descents. The race introduced additional safety measures in subsequent editions.
