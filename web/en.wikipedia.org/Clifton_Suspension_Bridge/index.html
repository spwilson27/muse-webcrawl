The Clifton Suspension Bridge is a suspension bridge spanning the Avon Gorge and the River Avon, linking Clifton in Bristol to Leigh Woods in North Somerset, England. Designed by the engineer Isambard Kingdom Brunel, it is one of the most recognisable symbols of Bristol. Brunel described the bridge, his first major commission, won in a competition when he was 24, as "my first child, my darling".

Construction

Work began in 1831 but was plagued by funding shortages; by 1843, with the towers built, money ran out and construction stopped for two decades. After Brunel's death in 1859, his colleagues in the Institution of Civil Engineers raised funds to complete the bridge as a memorial to him, reusing chains from Brunel's demolished Hungerford Bridge in London. The bridge finally opened on 8 December 1864.

Structure

The bridge's deck, 214 metres between the two stone towers, hangs 75 metres above the high-water level of the tidal Avon from wrought-iron chains. Although designed for horse-drawn carriages, it proved strong enough for modern motor traffic, and carries some four million vehicles a year, subject to a weight limit. It is a Grade I listed building.

Later history

The bridge has hosted notable events, from the first modern bungee jump, made by members of the Oxford University Dangerous Sports Club in 1979, to the final flight of Concorde over Bristol in 2003. A visitor centre stands on the Leigh Woods side, and the bridge is illuminated at night as a city landmark.
