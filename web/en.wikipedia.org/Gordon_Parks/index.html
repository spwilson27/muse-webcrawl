Gordon Parks

Gordon Roger Alexander Buchanan Parks (November 30, 1912 – March 7, 2006) was an American photographer, composer, author and film director, notable for his photographic essays for Life magazine on issues of civil rights, poverty and African Americans, and for directing the 1971 film Shaft.

Parks, largely self-taught, worked for the Farm Security Administration and the Office of War Information during the 1940s, producing the iconic photograph American Gothic, Washington, D.C. (1942). His semi-autobiographical novel The Learning Tree (1963), which he adapted as a film in 1969, made him the first African American to write and direct a major Hollywood studio feature. His later work with Life documented figures from Malcolm X to Muhammad Ali and the everyday realities of segregation and poverty.
