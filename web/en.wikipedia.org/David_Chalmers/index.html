David Chalmers

David John Chalmers (born 20 April 1966) is an Australian philosopher and cognitive scientist specializing in the philosophy of mind and the philosophy of language. He is a professor of philosophy at New York University and at the Australian National University, and co-director of the Center for Mind, Brain, and Consciousness at NYU. Chalmers is best known for formulating the "hard problem of consciousness" and for his arguments against physicalism about the mind.

Education and career

Chalmers studied mathematics at the University of Adelaide and then at the University of Oxford as a Rhodes Scholar. He earned his PhD in philosophy and cognitive science at Indiana University Bloomington in 1993, working in Douglas Hofstadter's research group. He taught at the University of California, Santa Cruz, and the University of Arizona before moving to ANU and later NYU.

The hard problem of consciousness

In his 1995 paper "Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness" and his 1996 book The Conscious Mind, Chalmers distinguished the "easy problems" of consciousness — explaining functions such as discrimination, integration of information, and reportability — from the "hard problem": why physical processing is accompanied by subjective experience at all. The terminology reshaped subsequent debate in philosophy and neuroscience.

Other work

Chalmers defended a form of property dualism, or "naturalistic dualism," and the conceivability argument based on philosophical zombies. He is also known for the extended mind thesis, developed with Andy Clark in the 1998 paper "The Extended Mind," and for work on two-dimensional semantics, virtual reality (in his 2022 book Reality+), and the philosophy of artificial intelligence. With David Bourget he founded the PhilPapers database and conducts surveys of philosophers' views.
