Marni Nixon

Marni Nixon (February 22, 1930 – July 24, 2016) was an American soprano and ghost singer for featured actresses in movie musicals. She is best known for dubbing the singing voices of leading actresses in several classic Hollywood musical films, including Deborah Kerr in The King and I (1956), Natalie Wood in West Side Story (1961), and Audrey Hepburn in My Fair Lady (1964).

Marni Nixon, the "ghost singer" behind several of Hollywood's most famous musical performances.

Early life

Nixon was born Margaret Nixon McEathron in Altadena, California. A child prodigy on the violin, she began performing at an early age and sang with the Roger Wagner Chorale. She studied singing with some of the leading teachers of the day and was performing professionally while still in her teens, appearing as a soloist with major American orchestras.

Career

Ghost singing

Nixon's most famous work was uncredited dubbing for other actresses. She had earlier dubbed songs for Margaret O'Brien and Jeanne Crain, and provided the singing voice of Marilyn Monroe's high notes in "Diamonds Are a Girl's Best Friend" in Gentlemen Prefer Blondes (1953). Her major assignments included:

  • Deborah Kerr in The King and I (1956)
  • Annette Funicello's songs in several Disney productions
  • Natalie Wood in West Side Story (1961)
  • Audrey Hepburn in My Fair Lady (1964)
  • Deborah Kerr again in An Affair to Remember (1957)

For years her contributions were contractually concealed, and she received no royalties for her film work. She later became an outspoken advocate for singers' credit and compensation.

On-screen and concert work

Nixon appeared on screen herself as Sister Sophia in The Sound of Music (1965) and later voiced Grandmother Fa in Disney's Mulan (1998). She was also a noted concert soprano, performing contemporary works by composers such as Anton Webern, Charles Ives, and Pierre Boulez, and she premiered works written for her by Igor Stravinsky. She recorded extensively and taught voice.

Personal life and death

Nixon was married three times, including to composer Ernest Gold, with whom she had three children. She died of breast cancer in New York City on July 24, 2016, aged 86.
