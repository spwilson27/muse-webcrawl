The Black Tortoise is one of the Four Symbols of the Chinese constellations, representing the north and the season of winter. It is usually depicted as a tortoise entwined with a snake. In Chinese, it is known as Xuánwǔ (玄武, "dark warrior"); in Japanese as Genbu, in Korean as Hyeonmu, and in Vietnamese as Huyền Vũ.

Astronomy

The Black Tortoise presides over the seven lunar mansions of the northern palace of the sky, stretching across parts of the modern constellations Sagittarius, Capricornus, Aquarius, Pegasus, and Andromeda. Its associations with the north, darkness, and water shaped its iconography of a armored, long-lived reptile.

In religion and culture

In Taoism, Xuanwu evolved into a major deity, the Perfected Warrior (Zhenwu), a god of the north and of martial protection, whose cult became especially prominent under the Ming dynasty. Temples to Xuanwu are found throughout China, and the tortoise-and-snake motif appears on coins, roof tiles, mirrors, and tomb guardians. Together with the Azure Dragon, Vermilion Bird, and White Tiger, it remains a staple of East Asian mythology, astronomy, and feng shui.
