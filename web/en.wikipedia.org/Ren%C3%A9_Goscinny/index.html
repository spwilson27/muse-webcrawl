René Goscinny

René Goscinny (French: [ʁəne ɡosini]; 14 August 1926 – 5 November 1977) was a French comic editor and writer, who created the Asterix comic book series with illustrator Albert Uderzo. He was raised in Buenos Aires, Argentina, where he attended French schools, and he lived in New York from 1945 to 1947. With his characteristic verbal wit and wordplay, he is regarded as one of the founding figures of modern Franco-Belgian comics.

Born: 14 August 1926, Paris, France
Died: 5 November 1977 (aged 51), Paris
Nationality: French
Notable works: Asterix; Lucky Luke (writer); Le Petit Nicolas; Iznogoud

Career

In New York Goscinny worked for a small publishing house and met the Belgian cartoonists Morris and Jijé, and future Mad magazine contributors Harvey Kurtzman and Jack Davis. Returning to Europe in the early 1950s, he became editor-in-chief of Pilote magazine, which he co-founded in 1959 and in which Asterix first appeared that year.

For Morris he scripted Lucky Luke from 1955 to 1977, producing what are often considered the series' classic albums. With Jean Tabary he created Iznogoud; with Jean-Jacques Sempé he wrote the beloved children's stories of Le Petit Nicolas. His Asterix scripts combined satire of contemporary France, historical parody, and elaborate puns, and the series became one of the best-selling comics in the world.

Death and legacy

Goscinny died of a heart attack during a routine stress test in Paris in 1977, aged 51. Uderzo continued Asterix alone. The René Goscinny Prize for comic scriptwriting and numerous schools and streets in France bear his name.
