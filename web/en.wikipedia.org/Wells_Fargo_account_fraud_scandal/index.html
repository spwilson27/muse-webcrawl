Wells Fargo account fraud scandal

The Wells Fargo account fraud scandal was a controversy arising from the creation of millions of fraudulent savings and checking accounts by employees of Wells Fargo without customers' consent. Revelations in September 2016 led to fines, congressional hearings, executive resignations and lasting regulatory sanctions against the bank.

Background

Wells Fargo's retail banking culture emphasized aggressive cross-selling — selling multiple products per household — under programs with demanding sales quotas. Employees, under pressure, opened deposit and credit card accounts, enrolled customers in online bill pay, and issued debit cards without authorization; some created fake email addresses and PINs to simulate consent.

Revelations and penalties

In September 2016 the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency and the City of Los Angeles fined the bank a combined 85 million, and Wells Fargo said about 5,300 employees had been fired over five years. The bank initially estimated about 2.1 million potentially unauthorized accounts; a later review raised the figure to about 3.5 million. In 2020 Wells Fargo agreed to pay billion to resolve criminal and civil investigations by the Department of Justice and the SEC.

Consequences

Chief executive John Stumpf resigned in October 2016, and his successor Tim Sloan resigned in 2019. In 2018 the Federal Reserve imposed an unprecedented asset cap restricting the bank's growth until governance improved. Former executives faced clawbacks of compensation and individual enforcement actions, including a lifetime industry ban and a 7.5 million penalty for Stumpf.
