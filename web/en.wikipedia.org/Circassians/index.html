Circassians

Circassians (Adyghe: Адыгэхэр)
Regions: North Caucasus; large diaspora in Turkey, Jordan, Syria, Israel
Languages: Adyghe, Kabardian, Russian, Turkish, Arabic
Religion: Predominantly Sunni Islam

The Circassians (endonym: Adyghe) are an ethnic group native to Circassia in the Northwest Caucasus, speaking the related Adyghe and Kabardian languages of the Northwest Caucasian family. They are known for a martial culture, an elaborate code of honour and hospitality (Adyghe Xabze), and a history shaped by resistance to Russian conquest and by mass expulsion.

History

Circassian tribes — among them the Shapsug, Abzakh, Bzhedug, Temirgoy, and Kabardians — inhabited the mountains and coast between the Kuban River and the Black Sea for millennia, trading with Greeks, Genoese, and Ottomans. Islam spread among them between the 16th and 18th centuries. In the Russo-Circassian War (1763–1864), the longest war of Russian imperial expansion, Circassian resistance under leaders including (in alliance) Imam Shamil's deputies ended with defeat in 1864. The Russian Empire then expelled the great majority of Circassians to the Ottoman Empire; hundreds of thousands died of disease, starvation, and drowning during the deportations, which many Circassians and a number of historians and governments describe as genocide. May 21 is commemorated as the Circassian Day of Mourning.

Diaspora and homeland

Today more Circassians live in the diaspora — especially Turkey, and communities in Jordan (where Circassians helped found Amman), Syria, and Israel (Kfar Kama and Rehaniya) — than in the homeland republics of Adygea, Kabardino-Balkaria, and Karachay-Cherkessia in Russia. Diaspora communities preserve distinctive dance, music, cuisine, and the green-and-gold twelve-star flag symbolising the twelve historical tribes.
