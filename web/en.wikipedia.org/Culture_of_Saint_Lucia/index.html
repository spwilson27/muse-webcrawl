Culture of Saint Lucia

The culture of Saint Lucia reflects the island's history as a contested colony that changed hands between Britain and France fourteen times, earning it the nickname "Helen of the West Indies." African heritage predominates, blended with French and English influences. English is official, but Saint Lucian Creole French (Kwéyòl) is widely spoken.

Literature

Saint Lucia has produced two Nobel laureates: poet and playwright Derek Walcott, who won the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1992 and whose epic poem Omeros reimagines Homer in a Caribbean setting, and economist Arthur Lewis, a co-laureate in Economics. Walcott founded the Trinidad Theatre Workshop and remains the island's most celebrated cultural figure.

Festivals and music

The Saint Lucia Jazz & Arts Festival, held each May, is a major Caribbean event. Carnival in July features calypso and soca competitions. Two traditional flower festivals, La Rose and La Marguerite, are celebrated by rival floral societies with roots in colonial-era traditions. Indigenous folk forms include the quadrille and the storytelling tradition of the conte.

Cuisine

The national dish is green figs and saltfish — boiled green bananas with salted cod. Other staples include callaloo soup, bouyon, accra (fish cakes), and cocoa tea. Jounen Kwéyòl (Creole Day), celebrated in October, showcases Creole language, food, and dress, including the madras-patterned wob dwiyet.
