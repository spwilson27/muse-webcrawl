Dashcam

A dashcam or dashboard camera is an onboard camera that continuously records the view through a vehicle's front windscreen and sometimes rear or other windows. Dashcams are used to provide video evidence in the event of a road accident, to deter fraud, and to record driving journeys.

Features

Modern dashcams typically record in high definition, loop-record over the oldest footage on a memory card, and may include GPS logging, G-sensors that lock footage when an impact is detected, parking-mode monitoring, and dual front-and-rear cameras. Recordings are widely used by police, insurers and courts, and dashcam footage of events such as the 2013 Chelyabinsk meteor brought the devices to worldwide attention.

Legality

The legality of dashcams varies by country. They are extremely common in Russia, South Korea and Taiwan; some European jurisdictions restrict recording or publication of footage on privacy grounds, while in the United Kingdom and much of the United States their use is broadly permitted.
