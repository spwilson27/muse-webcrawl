Nicolas Fouquet

Nicolas Fouquet, marquis de Belle-Île, vicomte de Melun et Vaux (27 January 1615 – 23 March 1680) was the Superintendent of Finances in France under King Louis XIV from 1653 until his arrest in 1661. His spectacular fall is one of the famous episodes of Louis XIV's early reign.

Rise and Vaux-le-Vicomte

Fouquet amassed great wealth and patronised artists and writers including Molière, La Fontaine and Poussin. He rebuilt the château of Vaux-le-Vicomte with architect Louis Le Vau, painter Charles Le Brun and gardener André Le Nôtre — the team Louis XIV would later take for Versailles. A lavish fête held at Vaux in August 1661 reportedly provoked the king's jealousy.

Arrest and imprisonment

Accused by Jean-Baptiste Colbert of embezzlement, Fouquet was arrested by d'Artagnan at Nantes in September 1661. After a long trial he was sentenced to banishment, but Louis XIV increased the sentence to life imprisonment. He died in captivity at Pinerolo in 1680. Conspiracy lore has sometimes linked him, without good evidence, to the Man in the Iron Mask.
