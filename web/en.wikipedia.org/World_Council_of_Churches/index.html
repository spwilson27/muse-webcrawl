World Council of Churches

The World Council of Churches (WCC) is a worldwide inter-church organisation founded in 1948 to promote Christian unity and cooperation. Its members include most of the world's Orthodox, Anglican, and Protestant churches; the Roman Catholic Church is not a member but cooperates closely with it.

Founded23 August 1948, Amsterdam
HeadquartersGeneva, Switzerland
MembershipOver 350 member churches
TypeEcumenical organisation

History

The WCC grew out of the early 20th-century ecumenical movement, including the Faith and Order and Life and Work movements, and was formally constituted at its first assembly in Amsterdam in 1948. Subsequent assemblies have met roughly every seven years on different continents.

Work

The Council coordinates theological dialogue, humanitarian and development work through partners, and campaigns on peace, justice, and the environment. Its 1982 convergence text Baptism, Eucharist and Ministry is a landmark of ecumenical theology.
