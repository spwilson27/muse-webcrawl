The Russo-Japanese War (8 February 1904 – 5 September 1905) was fought between the Empire of Japan and the Russian Empire over rival imperial ambitions in Manchuria and the Korean Empire. It was the first major war of the twentieth century in which an Asian power defeated a European great power, and its outcome reshaped the balance of power in East Asia.

Causes

After the First Sino-Japanese War (1894–1895), pressure from Russia, France and Germany (the Triple Intervention) forced Japan to return the Liaodong Peninsula to China; Russia then leased the peninsula itself and built the Chinese Eastern Railway across Manchuria. Russia's occupation of Manchuria after the Boxer Rebellion and its growing influence in Korea alarmed Japan, which regarded Korea as vital to its security. Negotiations broke down, and on the night of 8–9 February 1904 Japanese destroyers launched a surprise torpedo attack on the Russian Pacific Squadron at Port Arthur, before Japan formally declared war.

Course of the war

Land campaigns

Japanese armies landed in Korea and southern Manchuria, winning battles at the Yalu River, Nanshan and Liaoyang. Port Arthur fell in January 1905 after a costly siege directed by General Nogi Maresuke. In February–March 1905 the largest land battle yet fought, at Mukden, ended with a Russian retreat, though the Japanese were too exhausted to destroy Kuropatkin's army.

Naval war and Tsushima

Russia dispatched its Baltic Fleet under Admiral Zinovy Rozhestvensky on an epic 18,000-nautical-mile voyage to the Pacific. On 27–28 May 1905 Admiral Tōgō Heihachirō's Combined Fleet annihilated it at the Battle of Tsushima: most Russian ships were sunk or captured, while Japan lost only three torpedo boats. It remains one of the most decisive naval battles in history.

Treaty of Portsmouth

With both sides financially exhausted, US President Theodore Roosevelt mediated peace talks at Portsmouth, New Hampshire. The treaty of September 1905 recognised Japan's paramount interests in Korea, transferred the Liaodong lease and the South Manchuria Railway to Japan, and ceded the southern half of Sakhalin island. Russia paid no indemnity, which provoked riots in Tokyo. Roosevelt received the Nobel Peace Prize for his mediation.

Consequences

Defeat discredited the tsarist regime and helped ignite the Russian Revolution of 1905. Japan emerged as a recognised great power and annexed Korea in 1910. Military observers worldwide studied the war's lessons — machine guns, barbed wire, trenches and modern artillery — that foreshadowed the First World War.
