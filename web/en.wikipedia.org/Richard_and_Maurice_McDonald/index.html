Richard and Maurice McDonald

Richard James McDonald (February 16, 1909 – July 14, 1998) and Maurice James McDonald (November 26, 1902 – December 11, 1971), known collectively as the McDonald brothers, were American entrepreneurs who founded the McDonald's restaurant chain.

Early ventures

Born in New Hampshire to Irish immigrant parents, the brothers moved to California, where they operated a hot dog stand and a barbecue restaurant in San Bernardino. In 1948 they closed and reopened it as a self-service drive-in with a radically simplified menu of hamburgers, cheeseburgers, fries, shakes and soft drinks.

The Speedee Service System

The brothers developed the "Speedee Service System", applying factory assembly line principles to the kitchen, with each worker specialising in one task. Their octagonal restaurant design and low prices made the operation highly profitable and attracted franchising interest.

Sale to Ray Kroc

In 1954 they partnered with Ray Kroc, their milkshake machine salesman, who founded the franchising company and in 1961 bought them out for $2.7 million. The brothers retained their original restaurant, renamed The Big M, which Kroc eventually drove out of business with a rival outlet nearby. Their story is retold in the 2016 film The Founder.
