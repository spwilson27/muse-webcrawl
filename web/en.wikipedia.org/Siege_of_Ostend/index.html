Siege of Ostend
Date5 July 1601 – 20 September 1604
LocationOstend, Spanish Netherlands (modern Belgium)
ResultSpanish victory
BelligerentsDutch Republic; England — versus — Spain

The Siege of Ostend was a three-year siege during the Eighty Years' War and the Anglo-Spanish War, fought between Spanish forces and the garrison of the Dutch-held port of Ostend, the last Protestant stronghold in the southern Netherlands. It was one of the longest and bloodiest sieges of the early modern period.

The siege

Spanish forces under Archduke Albert invested Ostend in July 1601, but the garrison — a rotating force of Dutch, English, Scottish, and French soldiers — could be indefinitely supplied and reinforced by sea, and the Netherlands' fleets kept the harbour open. Military engineers on both sides, notably the Italian Pompeo Targone for the Spanish, devised elaborate assault machines and earthworks; the struggle earned epithets such as "the school of war" and "the longest carnival of death." A great Spanish assault in January 1602 was repulsed with heavy losses.

Fall of the city

In 1603 Ambrogio Spinola took command of the besiegers and prosecuted the siege methodically. The defenders progressively lost their outer works and finally, with the ruined town levelled by bombardment, negotiated surrender terms; the surviving garrison marched out on 22 September 1604. Casualty estimates for both sides combined run to 60,000–100,000 killed and wounded.

Aftermath

Spain's capture of a devastated Ostend was strategically barren: the Dutch compensated by taking Sluis, and the exhaustion of both sides contributed to the Twelve Years' Truce of 1609.
