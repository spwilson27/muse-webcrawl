The Battle of Greece (also known as Operation Marita) was a campaign of World War II fought on mainland Greece in April 1941. German forces, intervening to rescue their Italian allies, whose invasion of Greece from Albania in October 1940 had stalled in the Greco-Italian War, attacked Greece from Bulgaria simultaneously with the invasion of Yugoslavia. Greek and Commonwealth forces were overwhelmed within three weeks; Athens fell on 27 April and the mainland campaign ended by 30 April 1941.

Background

Italy invaded Greece on 28 October 1940, but the Greek army counter-attacked and drove deep into Italian-held Albania. Fearing for its southern flank and Romanian oil supplies while preparing Operation Barbarossa, Germany resolved to intervene. Britain, honouring its guarantee to Greece, sent W Force — an expeditionary corps of British, Australian and New Zealand troops under General Henry Maitland Wilson — which began landing in March 1941.

The campaign

Operation Marita opened on 6 April 1941. The German Twelfth Army under Field Marshal Wilhelm List attacked from Bulgaria. German armour thrust through the Struma valley and outflanked the Greek Metaxas Line, capturing Thessaloniki on 9 April and severing the Greek forces in Macedonia from those in Albania. In the west, panzer divisions advanced through Yugoslavia and the Monastir Gap into the rear of the Greek First Army in Albania, which was forced to surrender on 20 April; a controversial separate armistice signed by General Tsolakoglou with the Germans (and then Italy) angered the Greek government. Commonwealth forces fell back through successive positions at Olympus and Thermopylae, then evacuated by sea from southern ports to Crete and Egypt between 24 and 30 April. Athens was entered on 27 April.

Aftermath

Greece was divided into German, Italian and Bulgarian occupation zones, and endured a harsh occupation marked by famine and resistance. The fall of the mainland was followed in May by the Battle of Crete, which completed the Axis conquest of Greece. Historians debate whether the Balkan campaign delayed Operation Barbarossa; the more common view is that weather and logistics, rather than Marita itself, governed the start date of the invasion of the Soviet Union.
