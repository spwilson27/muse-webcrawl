Sikorsky SH-3 Sea King

The Sikorsky SH-3 Sea King (company designation S-61) is an American twin-engined anti-submarine warfare (ASW) helicopter designed and built by Sikorsky Aircraft. It was a landmark in helicopter design, being the world's first amphibious helicopter and one of the first ASW rotorcraft to use turboshaft engines.

Introduced in 1961, the Sea King served with the United States Navy as its primary ASW and search and rescue helicopter for several decades, and was built under licence in the United Kingdom (as the Westland Sea King), Italy, Canada, and Japan. The type also famously recovered the Apollo space capsules and their crews after splashdown, and variants long flew as Marine One transports for the President of the United States.

Design and development

The S-61 was developed to meet a US Navy requirement for an all-weather hunter-killer ASW helicopter combining the search and attack roles previously split between two aircraft. It has a boat hull fuselage enabling water landings, a dipping sonar, Doppler radar, and could carry torpedoes or depth charges. Two General Electric T58 turboshaft engines drive the five-bladed main rotor.

Operational history

Sea Kings served extensively in the Vietnam War in rescue and utility roles, and aboard US carriers until replaced by the SH-60 Seahawk in the 1990s and 2000s. Westland-built Sea Kings saw combat in the Falklands War, the Gulf War, and the Balkans, and British examples remained in service in airborne early warning and rescue roles into the 2010s. Naval and air force operators in numerous countries, including India, continue to fly versions of the S-61 family.
