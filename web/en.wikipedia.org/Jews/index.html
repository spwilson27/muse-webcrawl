Jews

Jews (Hebrew: יְהוּדִים, ISO 259-2: Yehudim, Israeli pronunciation: [jehuˈdim]) or Jewish people are an ethnoreligious group and nation originating from the Israelites of ancient Israel and Judah. Jewish ethnicity, religion, and community are highly interrelated, as Judaism is an ethnic religion, although not all ethnic Jews profess it. Despite this, religious Jews regard individuals who converted to Judaism as part of the community.

History

The Jewish people trace their origins to the ancient kingdoms of Israel and Judah in the southern Levant. Following the Roman destruction of the Second Temple in 70 CE and the suppression of later revolts, much of the population was dispersed, forming the Jewish diaspora in Europe, North Africa, and the Near East. Over subsequent centuries, distinct communities such as the Ashkenazim of Central and Eastern Europe, the Sephardim of Iberia and its diaspora, and the Mizrahim of the Middle East and North Africa developed their own customs, languages, and liturgies.

The 20th century was marked by the Shoah (the Holocaust), in which Nazi Germany and its collaborators murdered about six million Jews, and by the establishment of the State of Israel in 1948, which became home to a large share of the world's Jewish population. Today the largest Jewish populations are in Israel and the United States, with an estimated world population of roughly 15 million in the early 2020s.

Religion and culture

Judaism, the traditional faith of the Jewish people, encompasses a wide range of movements — Orthodox, Conservative, Reform, Reconstructionist, and others — alongside secular and cultural forms of Jewish identity. Jewish culture includes major contributions to philosophy, literature, science, music, cinema, and law.
