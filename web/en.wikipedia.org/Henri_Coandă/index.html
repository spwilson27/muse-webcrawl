Henri Coandă

Born7 June 1886, Bucharest, Kingdom of Romania
Died25 November 1972 (aged 86), Bucharest, Romania
NationalityRomanian
Known forCoandă effect; Coandă-1910 aircraft
FieldsAeronautics, fluid dynamics, engineering

Henri Marie Coandă (7 June 1886 – 25 November 1972) was a Romanian inventor, aerodynamics pioneer, and builder of an experimental aircraft, the Coandă-1910, which never flew. He later claimed in his old age that the aircraft flew in 1910, which was disputed by some contemporaries, although Coandă's most famous claim attributed the fire that ended the aircraft's first (and only) public exhibition to unintended consequences of Coandă effect airflow. He invented many devices, designed a "flying saucer", and discovered the Coandă effect of fluid dynamics.

Early life

Coandă was born in Bucharest, the second child of General Constantin Coandă, a mathematics professor and later Prime Minister of Romania. Fascinated by flight from childhood — he reportedly marvelled at the wind — he studied in Iași and Bucharest, at the Saint Sava National College, then attended military and technical schools in Germany, Belgium, and France, graduating at the head of the first class of the École supérieure d'aéronautique et de constructions mécaniques in Paris in 1910.

The Coandă-1910

At the Second International Aeronautical Exhibition in Paris in October 1910, Coandă exhibited an aircraft powered by a piston engine driving a ducted fan compressor — a design he called a turbo-propulseur — rather than a conventional propeller. Accounts differ on whether it left the ground during tests at Issy-les-Moulineaux in December 1910; the aircraft was damaged by fire after exhaust flames were deflected along the fuselage, an observation Coandă said led him to the fluid-attachment phenomenon that bears his name. Claims that it was the world's first jet aircraft have been debated ever since.

Later career

During the First World War Coandă designed reconnaissance aircraft built at Bristol in England, and afterwards worked in France on a wide variety of inventions, including aeronautical devices, a system for transporting liquids by pipeline in the desert, and disc-shaped "aerodynes" resembling flying saucers. He returned to Romania in 1969, where he was honoured as a national scientific figure. Bucharest's international airport is named Henri Coandă International Airport in his memory.

The Coandă effect

The Coandă effect is the tendency of a fluid jet to stay attached to a convex surface. Coandă discovered it, by his account, during the 1910 tests when flames and hot gases from his engine's exhaust followed the fuselage sides. The principle is used today in aeronautics (blown flaps), fluidic devices, air-conditioning diffusers, and medical ventilators.
