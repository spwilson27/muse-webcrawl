Wilayah
Arabicولاية‎ (wilāya)
MeaningProvince, governorate, state
Administered byA wali (governor)

A wilayah (Arabic: ولاية‎, wilāya) is an administrative division, usually translated as "state", "province", or "governorate". The word derives from the Arabic root w-l-y, "to govern", and the official in charge of a wilayah is a wali (governor).

Usage

Historically, a wilayah was a province of a caliphate or sultanate governed by a wali appointed by the central ruler. In the Ottoman Empire the term was adapted as vilayet, the empire's first-level provinces from the 1864 administrative reforms. Today the term remains in official use in several countries: Algeria is divided into 58 wilayas; Oman into governorates historically called wilayats; in Sudan the states are wilayat; and in Malaysia, federal territories are called Wilayah Persekutuan. In Afghanistan, the provinces are known in Persian as velayat, the same concept filtered through Persian.

In Islamic jurisprudence and Sufism, wilayah also carries theological meanings — guardianship, authority, or the spiritual sainthood and closeness to God of a wali (saint/friend of God) — and in Twelver Shi'a theology the doctrine of wilayat, the authority of the Imams, is central; in modern Iran it underlies the ruling doctrine of velayat-e faqih, guardianship of the jurist.
