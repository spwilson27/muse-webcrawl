Commissioners of Irish Lights

The Commissioners of Irish Lights (Irish: Coimisinéirí Soilse na hÉireann), often shortened to Irish Lights, is the general lighthouse authority for Ireland and Northern Ireland. It operates and maintains lighthouses, lightvessels, buoys, and other aids to navigation around the entire coast of the island of Ireland, and is one of three general lighthouse authorities for the United Kingdom and Ireland, alongside Trinity House and the Northern Lighthouse Board.

History

The commissioners trace their origins to an Act of the Irish Parliament in 1786, which charged the Revenue Commissioners with lighthouse duties; a separate board was established in 1810. Under the engineer George Halpin and his son, the nineteenth century saw a great programme of lighthouse construction around the Irish coast, including famous stations such as the Fastnet Rock lighthouse. Uniquely, the service remained an all-island body after partition, a cross-border arrangement continued to this day.

Present day

Irish Lights maintains around 70 lighthouses as well as hundreds of buoys, beacons, and electronic aids to navigation such as AIS and DGPS services. Automation completed in the 1990s ended the era of resident lighthouse keepers. The organisation is headquartered in Dún Laoghaire and operates the vessel ILV Granuaile. Several historic lighthouses are now open to visitors or used as heritage accommodation under the Great Lighthouses of Ireland initiative.
