Thought-terminating cliché

A thought-terminating cliché (also known as a semantic stop-sign, a thought-stopper, or a cliché thinking) is a form of loaded language, often passing as folk wisdom, intended to shut down cognitive dissonance and end deliberation on a topic. The term was popularized by psychiatrist Robert Jay Lifton in his 1961 book Thought Reform and the Psychology of Totalism, in which he examined the language of ideological totalism.

Origin

Lifton described how totalist environments use "loaded language" — jargon and clichés that compress complex human problems into brief, definitive-sounding phrases that are easily memorized and expressed, so that critical questions are answered, or dismissed, by formula rather than by thought.

Examples

Examples commonly cited include phrases such as "it is what it is", "that's just your opinion", "everything happens for a reason", and "boys will be boys", which can serve to deflect further inquiry or excuse behaviour rather than explain it.
