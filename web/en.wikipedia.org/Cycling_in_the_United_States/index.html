Cycling in the United States

Cycling in the United States encompasses recreational, sporting, and utility cycling. Although the U.S. has a strong recreational and racing cycling culture, bicycle mode share for commuting is low compared with many European countries — under one percent nationally — though some cities, such as Portland, Oregon, and Davis, California, achieve much higher rates.

History

The 1890s bicycle boom transformed American transport and road politics: cyclists organised through the League of American Wheelmen to campaign for paved roads. After a mid-20th-century decline as automobiles dominated, cycling revived with the 1970s bike boom, the rise of mountain biking in California, and later the growth of bike-share systems in major cities from 2010 onward.

Infrastructure and safety

American cycling infrastructure ranges from off-road rail trails to protected bike lanes in cities such as New York and Minneapolis. Safety remains a major concern, with per-trip fatality rates higher than in the Netherlands or Denmark. Advocacy organisations including the League of American Bicyclists promote education, legislation, and the federal Complete Streets approach.

Sport

The U.S. has produced prominent road racers and hosts events such as the Tour of California; mountain biking, BMX, and gravel racing are also popular.
