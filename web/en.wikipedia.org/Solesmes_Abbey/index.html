Solesmes Abbey (Abbaye Saint-Pierre de Solesmes) is a Benedictine monastery in Solesmes, Sarthe, France. Founded in 1010 as a priory of Le Mans, it was suppressed during the French Revolution and famously refounded in 1833 by Dom Prosper Guéranger, becoming the mother house of the Solesmes Congregation. The abbey is renowned worldwide as the centre of the restoration of Gregorian chant.

Gregorian chant

Under Guéranger, the monks of Solesmes undertook a scholarly restoration of the chant of the Roman liturgy, comparing medieval manuscripts. Dom Joseph Pothier and Dom André Mocquereau developed the Solesmes method of chant interpretation, and the abbey's editions — including the Liber Usualis (1896) — became the standard for the Roman Catholic Church after Pope Pius X's 1903 motu proprio Tra le sollecitudini. The abbey's recordings of chant achieved international popularity in the 1990s.

History

The community was expelled from France in 1901 under the Law of Associations and settled at Quarr Abbey on the Isle of Wight, returning to Solesmes in 1922. The abbey church houses remarkable sixteenth-century sculptural groups known as the Saints of Solesmes. A community of nuns at the nearby Abbey of Sainte-Cécile follows the same observance.
