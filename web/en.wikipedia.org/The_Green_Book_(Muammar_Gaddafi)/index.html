The Green Book (Muammar Gaddafi)

The Green Book
Author: Muammar Gaddafi
Original title: الكتاب الأخضر
Language: Arabic
Subject: Political philosophy
Published: 1975–1981 (three volumes)

The Green Book (Arabic: الكتاب الأخضر, al-Kitāb al-Akhḍar) is a short book setting out the political philosophy of Libyan leader Muammar Gaddafi. Published in three volumes between 1975 and 1981, it describes Gaddafi's views on democracy and economics — his so-called "Third Universal Theory" — and was a foundational text of the Libyan Arab Jamahiriya, analogous in role to Mao Zedong's Little Red Book in China.

Contents

The first volume, The Solution of the Problem of Democracy (1975), rejects representative parliamentary democracy as a form of dictatorship and advocates direct democracy through popular congresses and committees. The second volume, The Solution of the Economic Problem (1977), denounces wage labor as a form of slavery and promotes workers' self-management and partnership in production. The third volume, The Social Basis of the Third Universal Theory (1981), addresses family, women, education, sport, and culture.

Influence in Libya

The book's ideas were embedded in Libya's political system after the 1977 Declaration of Sabha established the Jamahiriya ("state of the masses"). Quotations were taught in schools, displayed in public spaces, and broadcast on state media. Critics inside and outside Libya dismissed the work as incoherent propaganda, and during the 2011 Libyan civil war copies were publicly burned by anti-Gaddafi protesters.

Reception

Outside Libya the book received interest mainly as a political curiosity. It was translated into many languages and distributed by Libyan cultural centers abroad. Following Gaddafi's death in October 2011, the book lost its official status.
