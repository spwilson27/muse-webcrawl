Vigna unguiculata

Family: Fabaceae
Genus: Vigna
Species: V. unguiculata

Vigna unguiculata is a species of legume in the family Fabaceae, commonly known as the cowpea. It is an annual herbaceous plant grown for its edible seeds and pods, and is one of the most important food legumes in the semi-arid tropics, especially in Africa.

Description

The plant is highly variable in growth habit — erect, semi-erect, prostrate, or climbing — with trifoliate leaves, white, yellow, or violet flowers, and pendulous pods typically 20–30 cm long containing 6–13 seeds. Seeds vary in size, shape, and colour, including the black-eyed pea type (subsp. unguiculata), yardlong bean (subsp. sesquipedalis), and catjang (subsp. cylindrica).

Origin and cultivation

The cowpea is believed to have been domesticated in Africa, possibly in West Africa, and was spread by trade to Asia and, via the transatlantic slave trade and other routes, to the Americas. It tolerates drought, heat, and poor sandy soils, fixes nitrogen in the soil, and is commonly intercropped with cereals such as millet and sorghum. Nigeria is the world's largest producer.

Uses

Cowpeas are a staple protein source, eaten as dry beans, fresh pods, and leaves. Dishes include akara and moi-moi in West Africa, Hoppin' John (with black-eyed peas) in the southern United States, and lobia curries in South Asia. The plant is also used as fodder and green manure.
