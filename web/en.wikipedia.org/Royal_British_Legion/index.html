Royal British Legion

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Remembrance poppies sold during the annual Poppy Appeal

The Royal British Legion (RBL), formerly the British Legion, is a British charity providing financial, social and emotional support to members and veterans of the British Armed Forces, their families and dependants. Founded in 1921, it is the United Kingdom's largest Armed Forces charity, with a network of local branches and clubs across the country and overseas.

History

The British Legion was formed on 15 May 1921 through the amalgamation of several ex-service organisations, with Field Marshal Earl Haig as its first president. It campaigned for the welfare of veterans of the First World War and their families, pressing for pensions, employment and housing. In 1921 the Legion also organised the first Poppy Day, selling artificial poppies to raise funds; the red poppy became the enduring emblem of remembrance in Britain. The organisation received the prefix "Royal" in 1971 on its fiftieth anniversary.

Poppy Appeal and remembrance

The annual Poppy Appeal in the weeks before Remembrance Sunday is the Legion's best-known activity, raising tens of millions of pounds each year through the work of volunteer collectors. The Legion also organises the Festival of Remembrance at the Royal Albert Hall and plays a central role in the national commemorations at the Cenotaph in London, including the two-minute silence on 11 November.

Services

The Legion provides lifelong support to serving and ex-serving personnel and their families, including financial assistance, benefits and money advice, housing support, recovery programmes, and care homes. It also campaigns on behalf of the Armed Forces community, on issues such as compensation, mental health and the Armed Forces Covenant.
