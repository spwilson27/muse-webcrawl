Chengjiang Fossil Site

Chengjiang Fossil Site
LocationChengjiang, Yunnan, China
Age~518 million years (Cambrian Stage 3)
StatusUNESCO World Heritage Site (2012)

The Chengjiang Fossil Site is a fossil conservation area in Chengjiang, Yunnan Province, China, preserving an exceptionally rich assemblage of early Cambrian marine organisms in the Maotianshan Shales. Inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2012, it is among the most important windows on the Cambrian explosion — the rapid diversification of animal life.

Discovery

The biota was brought to scientific attention in 1984, when Hou Xianguang discovered the naraoiid arthropod Naraoia in the shales. Subsequent excavation has recovered nearly 200 species across some twenty phyla, many preserving soft tissues such as guts, nerves, and eyes.

Fauna

Notable fossils include the arthropods Fuxianhuia and Anomalocaris-relatives, the lobopodian Hallucigenia fortis, early chordates such as Myllokunmingia and Haikouichthys — among the earliest known vertebrates — and the enigmatic vetulicolians. The assemblage preserves the earliest known complex marine community and diverse ecological relationships, including predators and their prey.

Significance

At about 518 million years old, the Chengjiang biota is roughly ten million years older than Canada's Burgess Shale and documents an earlier phase of animal diversification, including stem-group representatives of most living phyla. The site is protected as a national geopark and is managed to balance research excavation with conservation.
