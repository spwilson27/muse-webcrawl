Military formation
Senses: (1) a tactical arrangement of troops or vehicles; (2) a military unit or organisation above a certain size

The term military formation has two related meanings. In tactics, a formation is the physical arrangement of soldiers, vehicles, ships or aircraft adopted for movement, combat or ceremony — such as the line, column, wedge or phalanx. In organisation, a formation is a military unit or grouping, generally a large one commanded by a senior officer, such as a division, corps or army.

Tactical formations

Formations balance firepower, protection, mobility and control. Ancient armies fought in dense blocks — the Greek phalanx and the Roman legion's maniples. Gunpowder weapons favoured lines that maximised musket fire, giving way in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries to dispersed formations: skirmish lines, echelon, herringbone and bounding overwatch. Armoured units use wedges and V-formations; naval fleets adopt line-ahead or screen formations; aircraft fly in finger-four, echelon or trail.

Organisational formations

In British and Commonwealth usage especially, "formation" denotes any unit larger than a sub-unit: brigades, divisions, corps and armies are formations, while platoons, companies and battalions are units. Formations combine subordinate units with headquarters and support elements and are given standard map symbols in systems such as NATO's APP-6.
