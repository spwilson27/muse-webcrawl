The Gothic Line (German: Gotenstellung) was a German defensive line of the Italian Campaign of World War II. Built under Field Marshal Albert Kesselring, it stretched across the northern Apennine Mountains from the Tyrrhenian Sea to the Adriatic, using more than 2,000 machine-gun nests, casemates, bunkers, observation posts, and artillery positions to repel Allied attempts to break through into the Po Valley and towards Austria.

Construction and renaming

Hitler was concerned that the name "Gothic Line" would lend grandeur to an Allied breakthrough, so in June 1944 it was renamed the "Green Line" (Grüne Linie), though the original name stuck. The line was constructed in part by the Organisation Todt using forced labour, including thousands of Italian workers.

Allied attacks

In Operation Olive (August–October 1944), the British Eighth Army and U.S. Fifth Army attacked the line and in places broke into it, but winter weather, stiff resistance, and German counterattacks halted the offensive short of a decisive breakthrough. The line was finally breached in the spring offensive of April 1945, shortly before the German surrender in Italy.
