Satsuma Domain

The Satsuma Domain in southern Kyushu, Japan.

The Satsuma Domain (薩摩藩, Satsuma-han) was a Japanese domain of the Edo period, ruled by the Shimazu clan from Kagoshima Castle in Satsuma Province, in the south of Kyushu. It was one of the most powerful feudal domains in Tokugawa Japan and played a major role in the events leading to the Meiji Restoration.

History

The Shimazu had ruled Satsuma since the 12th century. After siding with the losing western army at the Battle of Sekigahara in 1600, the clan unusually retained its lands. In 1609 Satsuma invaded the Ryukyu Kingdom, making it a vassal while allowing it to continue its tributary trade with China — a profitable back channel for foreign goods throughout the period of Japan's seclusion. The domain was assessed at about 770,000 koku, among the highest of any domain.

Bakumatsu and the Restoration

In the 1860s Satsuma, initially inclined toward compromise between the shogunate and the imperial court, fought Britain in the brief Anglo-Satsuma War of 1863, then allied with its former rival Chōshū in the Satsuma–Chōshū Alliance of 1866. Satsuma forces were central to the imperial victory in the Boshin War (1868–1869). Leaders from Satsuma — Ōkubo Toshimichi, Saigō Takamori, and Kuroda Kiyotaka among them — dominated the early Meiji government.

Satsuma Rebellion

Disillusioned with the government's reforms abolishing samurai privileges, Saigō Takamori led the Satsuma Rebellion of 1877, the last and largest revolt against the Meiji state. The rebels were defeated at the Battle of Shiroyama, where Saigō died; the rebellion's suppression confirmed the conscript Imperial Army's strength.

Legacy

The domain system was abolished in 1871, Satsuma becoming part of Kagoshima Prefecture. Sweet potatoes (satsumaimo) and Satsuma ware pottery retain the domain's name, as does the satsuma mandarin, introduced to the United States from the region.
