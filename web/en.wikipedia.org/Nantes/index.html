Nantes

Nantes (French: [nɑ̃t]; Breton: Naoned) is a city in Loire-Atlantique on the Loire River, 50 km (31 mi) from the Atlantic coast. It is the prefecture of the Pays de la Loire region and has a population of over 300,000 in the city proper, with around a million in the metropolitan area, making it one of the largest cities in France. Historically the capital of Brittany, Nantes is now administratively outside the modern Brittany region, a subject of continuing debate.

History

Founded by the Namnetes, a Gallic tribe, around 70 BCE as Condevincnum, the city became a major port. In the 18th century Nantes was France's largest slave-trading port, a source of its merchant wealth, memorialised today by the Memorial to the Abolition of Slavery. The Edict of Nantes (1598), granting rights to Protestants, was issued by Henry IV at the Château des ducs de Bretagne. Nantes was heavily bombed in the Second World War and rebuilt afterwards; shipbuilding declined in the late twentieth century, and the former shipyards on the Île de Nantes have been redeveloped, now home to the Machines of the Isle and the giant mechanical Grand Éléphant.

Culture

Nantes is the birthplace of Jules Verne, whose museum overlooks the Loire. The city is also known for the Voyage à Nantes art trail, the Lieu Unique cultural centre, and Petit Beurre biscuits, created there by Lefèvre-Utile (LU).
