Sambor Prei Kuk

Sambor Prei Kuk (Khmer: ប្រាសាទសំបូរព្រៃគុក) is an archaeological site in Cambodia located in Kampong Thom Province, about 25 km north of the provincial capital, Kampong Thom. It preserves the remains of Ishanapura, the capital city of the Chenla Empire, which flourished in the late sixth and seventh centuries AD, centuries before Angkor. The site was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2017.

History

Ishanapura was founded as the capital of Isanavarman I, king of Chenla, who reigned in the early seventh century. At its height the walled city covered some 800–1,000 hectares, making it one of the earliest large urban centres of Southeast Asia. Its temples — built in a pre-Angkorian style — established artistic and architectural conventions that later Khmer builders at Angkor would develop on a grander scale.

Layout and monuments

The monuments, numbering about 150 of which over fifty are substantial brick towers (prasat), fall into three main groups: Prasat Sambor (north group), Prasat Tao (central group), and Prasat Yeai Poeun (south group). The brick towers are famed for their carved sandstone lintels and for unusual round and octagonal shrines — some octagonal temples are unique in Khmer architecture. "Floating temples", shrines decorated with miniature palace pavilions in relief, adorn several towers. The central group's towers are guarded by stone lions.

Inscriptions and art

Sanskrit and Old Khmer inscriptions found at the site identify it as Ishanapura and record royal donations and foundations. Its sculpture, including celebrated images of Harihara and Durga, ranks among the masterpieces of pre-Angkorian art.

Conservation

Scattered through forest, the site suffered during Cambodia's civil war and from looting. Conservation work by Cambodian authorities with international partners stabilised many towers, and the site was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site on 8 July 2017 under the name "Temple Zone of Sambor Prei Kuk, Archaeological Site of Ancient Ishanapura".
