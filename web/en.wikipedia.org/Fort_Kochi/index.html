Fort Kochi

Fort Kochi
City: Kochi, Kerala, India
Known for: Chinese fishing nets, colonial architecture
Landmarks: St. Francis Church, Santa Cruz Basilica, Mattancherry Palace
Former rulers: Portuguese, Dutch, British

Fort Kochi (also known as Fort Cochin) is a neighbourhood of the city of Kochi in Kerala, India. It was the first European township in India, established after the Portuguese were granted permission by the Kingdom of Cochin in 1503 to build Fort Emmanuel.

History

Pedro Álvares Cabral arrived in 1500, and Vasco da Gama died in Cochin in 1524 and was buried at St. Francis Church, the oldest European church in India. The Dutch captured the fort in 1663, and the British took control in 1795.

Attractions

Fort Kochi is famous for its Chinese fishing nets, Jew Town and the Mattancherry (Dutch) Palace, colonial bungalows, and biennale-era art spaces.
