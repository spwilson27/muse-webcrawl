John C. Calhoun

John Caldwell Calhoun (March 18, 1782 – March 31, 1850) was an American statesman from South Carolina who served as the seventh vice president of the United States (1825–1832), as Secretary of War and Secretary of State, and in both houses of Congress.

One of the "Great Triumvirate" with Henry Clay and Daniel Webster, Calhoun began as a nationalist War Hawk but became the leading theorist of states' rights, nullification, and the concurrent majority, precipitating the Nullification Crisis of 1832–33. He defended slavery as a "positive good", and his doctrines profoundly influenced the secessionist South.
