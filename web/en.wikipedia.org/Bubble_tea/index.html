Bubble tea (also known as boba tea or pearl milk tea) is a tea-based drink that originated in Taiwan in the early 1980s. It most commonly consists of tea accompanied by chewy tapioca balls ("boba" or "pearls"), but it can be made with other toppings as well, such as grass jelly, aloe vera, red bean, and popping boba. The drink is typically served cold in a sealed cup, often with a wide straw through which the pearls can be drunk.

History

Two Taiwanese tea houses claim to have invented bubble tea in the 1980s: the Chun Shui Tang tea room in Taichung and the Hanlin Tea Room in Tainan. The Chun Shui Tang account holds that employee Lin Hsiu Hui poured sweetened tapioca pudding into iced tea during a meeting in 1988. Hanlin's owner, Tu Tsong-he, said he added white tapioca pearls to tea in 1986 after seeing them at a market. The dispute over the invention went to court in Taiwan, and in 2019 a court declined to rule decisively, holding that bubble tea was not patented and its exact origins did not need to be determined.

Varieties

Bubble tea falls broadly into two categories: teas without milk and milk teas. Milk teas may be made with dairy milk or non-dairy creamer, and popular flavours include taro, matcha, brown sugar, Thai tea, and various fruit flavours such as mango, strawberry, and passion fruit. Sugar and ice levels are commonly customizable. The tapioca pearls are made from cassava starch, giving them a chewy texture; "brown sugar boba", cooked in brown sugar syrup, became a popular variant in the late 2010s.

Global spread

From Taiwan, bubble tea spread first through East and Southeast Asia and then to North America, Europe, and beyond, becoming an international beverage phenomenon with thousands of specialty shops. The word "boba" derives from a slang term referring to the round shape of the pearls.
