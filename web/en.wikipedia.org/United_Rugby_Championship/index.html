United Rugby Championship

The United Rugby Championship (URC) is an annual rugby union competition involving professional teams from Ireland, Scotland, Wales, Italy and South Africa. Founded in 2001 as the Celtic League, it grew through the Magners League and RaboDirect Pro12 eras into the Guinness Pro14, and took its present name and sixteen-team format in 2021 when four South African franchises joined. It is one of the three major professional leagues in Europe, alongside England's Premiership and France's Top 14, and teams qualify from it for the European Rugby Champions Cup.

History

The Celtic League began in 2001 with Irish provinces, Welsh clubs and Scottish districts, and became a full home-and-away league in 2003 as Welsh rugby adopted regional teams. Italian teams Aironi and Benetton Treviso joined in 2010, making the competition the Pro12. In 2017 two South African teams expelled from Super Rugby, the Cheetahs and Southern Kings, joined to form the Pro14. After the COVID-disrupted 2020–21 season and a short "Rainbow Cup", the league relaunched as the URC with the Bulls, Lions, Sharks and Stormers from South Africa replacing the Cheetahs and Kings, who had ceased operating.

Format

The sixteen teams are grouped into four regional pools or "shields" (Irish, Welsh, Scottish/Italian and South African). Each team plays the others once, plus extra derby fixtures, for an 18-round regular season; the top eight enter knockout play-offs culminating in a Grand Final. Regional shield winners earn silverware, and URC results determine seeding and qualification for European competitions. The Stormers won the inaugural URC title in 2022, Munster in 2023 and Glasgow Warriors in 2024.
