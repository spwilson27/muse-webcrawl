The Russo-Persian War of 1804–1813 was one of the many wars between the Persian Empire (Qajar dynasty) and Imperial Russia over control of the Caucasus. It began after Russia annexed the Georgian kingdom of Kartli-Kakheti and moved into the khanates of the eastern Caucasus, which Persia claimed as its sphere of influence.

Course of the war

Russian forces under generals such as Pavel Tsitsianov and Pyotr Kotlyarevsky captured Ganja, repelled Persian offensives, and won decisive victories at Aslanduz (1812) and Lankaran (1813), despite Russia being simultaneously engaged in the Napoleonic Wars.

Treaty of Gulistan

The war ended with the Treaty of Gulistan (1813), by which Persia ceded to Russia most of its Caucasian territories north of the Aras River, including Dagestan, eastern Georgia, and the khanates corresponding to much of present-day Azerbaijan and Armenia, and recognised Russia's exclusive naval presence on the Caspian Sea.
