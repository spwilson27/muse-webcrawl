Sviatoslav I of Kiev

Sviatoslav I Igorevich (c. 942 – 972), known as Sviatoslav the Brave, was Prince of Novgorod and Grand Prince of Kiev from 945 until his death, and the first ruler of the Kievan Rus' to bear a distinctly Slavic name. The son of Igor of Kiev and Olga of Kiev — who acted as regent during his minority — he is remembered as an exceptionally energetic warrior prince who spent most of his reign on campaign.

Campaigns

In 965 Sviatoslav destroyed the Khazar Khaganate, Rus' great eastern rival, capturing the fortress of Sarkel and the capital Atil, and subjugated the Vyatichi, the last East Slavic tribe still paying tribute to the Khazars. Invited by the Byzantine emperor Nikephoros II Phokas to fight the Bulgarians, he invaded Bulgaria in 967–968 and occupied much of the Danube plain, choosing Pereyaslavets as a prospective new capital. His presence alarmed Constantinople; war with the Byzantines under John I Tzimiskes followed, ending in 971 with a hard-fought campaign at Dorostolon and a treaty by which Sviatoslav withdrew from Bulgaria.

Death and legacy

Returning to Kiev in the spring of 972, Sviatoslav was ambushed and killed at the Dnieper rapids by the Pechenegs of Khan Kurya — reportedly warned by the Bulgarians or Byzantines. According to the Primary Chronicle, Kurya had Sviatoslav's skull made into a drinking cup. Sviatoslav left three sons — Yaropolk, Oleg and Vladimir — whose rivalry led to civil war, eventually won by Vladimir the Great, who Christianised the Rus'. Sviatoslav himself remained a pagan despite his mother's conversion, arguing that his retinue would mock a change of faith. His campaigns mark the high point of early Rus' military expansion, though his neglect of government at home left the state's internal organisation undeveloped.
