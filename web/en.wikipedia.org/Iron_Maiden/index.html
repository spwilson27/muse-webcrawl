Iron Maiden are an English heavy metal band formed in Leyton, East London, in 1975 by bassist and primary songwriter Steve Harris. They are widely regarded as pioneers of the new wave of British heavy metal (NWOBHM) and are among the most commercially successful metal bands of all time, having sold well over 100 million records worldwide.

History

The band built a following in London pubs before signing to EMI in 1979. Their self-titled debut album (1980) and Killers (1981), sung by Paul Di'Anno, established their raw twin-guitar sound. Vocalist Bruce Dickinson joined for The Number of the Beast (1982), a landmark album that topped the UK chart and launched them internationally.

A run of classic albums followed: Piece of Mind (1983), Powerslave (1984), Somewhere in Time (1986) and Seventh Son of a Seventh Son (1988). Dickinson left in 1993 and was replaced by Blaze Bayley for two albums; Dickinson and guitarist Adrian Smith returned in 1999, beginning a late-career resurgence with albums such as Brave New World (2000), A Matter of Life and Death (2006), The Book of Souls (2015) and Senjutsu (2021).

Style and imagery

Iron Maiden's music features galloping bass lines, harmonised twin lead guitars and literate lyrics drawn from history, literature, film and mythology — songs such as "The Trooper", "Hallowed Be Thy Name", "Fear of the Dark" and "Run to the Hills" are genre standards. Their mascot, the zombie-like Eddie, appears on nearly all album artwork, originally created by artist Derek Riggs.

Legacy

The band are noted for their elaborate world tours, travelling since 2008 in a customised jet ("Ed Force One") piloted by Dickinson. They have played thousands of concerts and remain one of the most influential metal acts, cited as an inspiration by generations of bands.
