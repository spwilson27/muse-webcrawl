The Siege of Fort Stanwix (also known at the time as Fort Schuyler) began on 2 August 1777 and ended on 22 August 1777, during the American Revolutionary War. Fort Stanwix, in the Mohawk Valley in what is now Rome, New York, was held by Continental Army forces under Colonel Peter Gansevoort. The besieging force was composed of British regulars, Loyalists, Hessians, and Indigenous warriors led by Brigadier General Barry St. Leger, whose expedition was part of the British strategy to divide the colonies in the Saratoga campaign.

The American defence of the fort contributed to the failure of St. Leger's advance down the Mohawk Valley and thus to the eventual British defeat at Saratoga, since St. Leger's force was to have joined General John Burgoyne's army near Albany.

Background

Fort Stanwix guarded the portage between the Mohawk River and Wood Creek, a key route between the Atlantic seaboard and Lake Ontario. Abandoned after the French and Indian War, it was reoccupied and repaired by Continental forces in 1776–77 and renamed Fort Schuyler, though the older name persisted. St. Leger advanced from Oswego with about 750 regulars and Loyalists and some 1,000 Indigenous allies, mainly Haudenosaunee.

The siege

St. Leger's light artillery could not breach the fort's earthworks, so he settled into a siege. On 6 August, a relief column of New York militia under Nicholas Herkimer was ambushed at the Battle of Oriskany, one of the bloodiest engagements of the war; during the battle, a sortie from the fort under Lieutenant Colonel Marinus Willett raided and looted the besiegers' camps. According to a popular account, Willett's men raised a makeshift American flag — improvised from shirts, a blue coat, and a red petticoat — said to be the first Stars and Stripes flown in battle.

A second relief expedition under Benedict Arnold advanced up the valley and, using a ruse carried by the Loyalist prisoner Hon Yost Schuyler (and Oneida messengers), spread exaggerated reports of its size among the besiegers' Indigenous allies, many of whom deserted. With his siege collapsing, St. Leger abandoned the attempt on 22 August and retreated to Canada, leaving supplies and equipment behind.

Aftermath

The successful defence secured the Mohawk Valley and deprived Burgoyne of expected support before his surrender at Saratoga in October 1777. The fort later served as a base for American expeditions against the Iroquois; the site is now preserved as Fort Stanwix National Monument.
