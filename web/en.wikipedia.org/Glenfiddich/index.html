Glenfiddich

Glenfiddich (Scottish Gaelic: Gleann Fidhdeach, meaning "valley of the deer") is a Speyside single malt Scotch whisky distillery in Dufftown, Moray, Scotland, owned by William Grant & Sons. Founded by William Grant in 1886, with the first spirit running on Christmas Day 1887, Glenfiddich is one of the world's best-selling single malt Scotch whiskies and remains one of the few major distilleries still owned by the founding family.

History

William Grant, who had worked at the Mortlach distillery, built Glenfiddich with his family using second-hand stills bought from the Cardhu distillery. The distillery survived Prohibition and the Great Depression, and in 1963 Glenfiddich pioneered the marketing of single malt whisky outside Scotland, helping to create the modern single malt category. Its distinctive triangular bottle, designed in 1957, became an iconic package.

Production and range

Glenfiddich is unusual in using water from a single source, the Robbie Dhu springs, and in retaining on-site cooperage and bottling. The core range includes 12-, 15-, 18-, and 21-year-old expressions, along with experimental and limited-edition releases. The 15 Year Old is matured using a solera vat system adapted from sherry production. The distillery operates numerous copper pot stills and produces millions of litres of spirit annually.
