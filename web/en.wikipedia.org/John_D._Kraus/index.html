John D. Kraus

[Image placeholder: the Big Ear radio telescope]

John Daniel Kraus (June 28, 1910 – July 18, 2004) was an American physicist and electrical engineer known for his contributions to electromagnetics, antenna theory, and radio astronomy. A professor at Ohio State University, he invented the helical antenna and the corner reflector antenna, and wrote the widely used textbook Antennas.

Kraus earned his doctorate from the University of Michigan in 1933. During World War II he worked on radar countermeasures and degaussing at Harvard's Radio Research Laboratory. Returning to Ohio State, he founded its Radio Observatory and designed innovative telescopes, culminating in the "Big Ear", a large flat-plane radio telescope completed in 1963.

The Big Ear and SETI

The Big Ear conducted the Ohio Sky Survey, cataloguing tens of thousands of radio sources, and later devoted much of its time to the search for extraterrestrial intelligence. In August 1977 it received the famous "Wow! signal", a strong narrowband burst that volunteer Jerry Ehman annotated with "Wow!" on the printout; the signal was never seen again and remains unexplained. Kraus also wrote popular accounts of radio astronomy, including Big Ear and Big Ear Two.

Kraus received the IEEE Edison Medal in 1985 and numerous other honours. The Big Ear was demolished in 1998 to make way for a golf course and housing development.
