Etta James

Born: Jamesetta Hawkins, 25 January 1938, Los Angeles, California, US
Died: 20 January 2012 (aged 73), Riverside, California, US
Genres: Blues, R&B, soul, gospel, jazz, rock and roll

Etta James (born Jamesetta Hawkins; January 25, 1938 – January 20, 2012) was an American singer who performed in various genres, including blues, R&B, soul, rock and roll, jazz, and gospel. Having started her career in 1954, she gained fame with hits such as "The Wallflower", "At Last", "Tell Mama", "Something's Got a Hold on Me", and "I'd Rather Go Blind".

Career

James was discovered as a teenager by bandleader Johnny Otis and recorded "The Wallflower" (also known as "Roll with Me, Henry") in 1955. In 1960 she signed with Chess Records in Chicago, where she recorded her signature song, "At Last," and a string of acclaimed albums blending blues, jazz, and pop standards. Her powerful, earthy voice bridged the gap between rhythm and blues and rock and roll.

Later life and legacy

Despite struggles with heroin addiction that disrupted her career, James enjoyed a major comeback from the late 1980s, winning several Grammy Awards, including a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2003. She was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1993. James died of leukemia in 2012, five days before her 74th birthday. She is regarded as one of the greatest voices in American popular music.
