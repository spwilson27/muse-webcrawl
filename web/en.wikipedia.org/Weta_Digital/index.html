Weta Digital — renamed Wētā FX in 2021 — is a New Zealand digital visual effects and animation company based in Miramar, Wellington. It was founded in 1993 by Peter Jackson, Richard Taylor and Jamie Selkirk to produce effects for Heavenly Creatures, growing out of the Weta Workshop practical-effects studio.

The company achieved worldwide prominence with The Lord of the Rings film trilogy (2001–2003), for which it developed the MASSIVE crowd-simulation software and created the digital character Gollum, performed by Andy Serkis through pioneering motion capture. It has since produced landmark effects for King Kong (2005), James Cameron's Avatar (2009) and its sequels, the Planet of the Apes reboot series, and many Marvel and other blockbuster films.

Technology and awards

Weta has won multiple Academy Awards for Best Visual Effects, including for The Fellowship of the Ring, The Return of the King, King Kong, Avatar and The Jungle Book. Its research groups work on performance capture, facial animation, physically based rendering and simulation. In 2021, a majority stake in the company's technology division was sold to Unity Technologies, after which the visual-effects business adopted the name Wētā FX.
