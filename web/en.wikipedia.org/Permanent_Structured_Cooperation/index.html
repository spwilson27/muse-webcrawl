PESCO is the EU framework for deeper defence cooperation between member states.

The Permanent Structured Cooperation (PESCO) is the part of the European Union's Common Security and Defence Policy in which member states pursue greater cooperation in defence through binding commitments and collaborative projects. It was provided for by the Treaty of Lisbon (2009) and activated by a Council decision on 11 December 2017, initially with 25 participating member states; Denmark joined after its 2022 referendum abolishing the defence opt-out, bringing the total to 26 — all EU members except Malta.

Commitments and projects

Participating states subscribed to twenty binding commitments, including regularly increasing defence budgets, supplying combat units for EU operations, harmonising requirements, and filling capability gaps. Under the framework they undertake collaborative projects in areas such as military mobility, cyber defence, maritime surveillance, medical support, and joint training; dozens of projects have been adopted in successive waves. The European Defence Agency and the EU diplomatic service act as the PESCO secretariat, and the Coordinated Annual Review on Defence (CARD) and the European Defence Fund complement the framework.

Third-country participation

Since 2020, third states may be invited to individual projects under defined conditions; the United States, Canada and Norway joined the military mobility project in 2021, and the United Kingdom was later invited to the same project.
