Infinite impulse response

Infinite impulse response (IIR) is a property applying to many linear time-invariant systems that are distinguished by having an impulse response which does not become exactly zero past a certain point but continues indefinitely. IIR systems, including most digital filters of that class, contain feedback: the output depends not only on the current and past inputs but also on past outputs.

Structure of an IIR filter: the feedback path makes the impulse response theoretically infinite in duration.

Definition

A discrete-time IIR filter's output can be written as a sum over current and previous input samples plus a sum over previous output samples. In transfer-function terms, the system function is a rational function of z, whose poles (if outside the unit circle) make the filter unstable. By contrast, a finite impulse response (FIR) filter has no feedback and its impulse response settles to zero in finite time.

Advantages and disadvantages

IIR filters generally require far fewer coefficients than FIR filters to meet a given frequency-response specification, so they are computationally cheaper and introduce less delay. However, their phase response is typically nonlinear, they can become unstable due to feedback and quantization, and their design is more involved. Classical IIR designs are derived from analogue prototypes — Butterworth, Chebyshev, elliptic and Bessel filters — using transformations such as the bilinear transform.

Applications

IIR filters are widely used in audio equalization, biomedical signal processing, control systems, and telecommunications, wherever sharp frequency selectivity at low computational cost is needed.
