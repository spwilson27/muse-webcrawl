Sidney Webb

Born: 13 July 1859, London
Died: 13 October 1947
Spouse: Beatrice Webb
Known for: Fabian Society, LSE

Sidney James Webb, 1st Baron Passfield (13 July 1859 – 13 October 1947) was a British socialist, economist and reformer, co-founder of the London School of Economics and a leading Fabian.

Life and work

Webb, with his wife Beatrice Webb, George Bernard Shaw and Graham Wallas, shaped Fabian socialism — gradual, administrative reform rather than revolution. The Webbs wrote influential studies including The History of Trade Unionism (1894) and Industrial Democracy (1897), founded the New Statesman magazine in 1913, and helped draft the Labour Party's 1918 constitution, including Clause IV.

Government

Webb served as President of the Board of Trade in 1924 and as Secretary of State for the Colonies and for Dominion Affairs under Ramsay MacDonald, becoming Baron Passfield in 1929. The Webbs' later admiration for the Soviet Union, expressed in Soviet Communism: A New Civilisation? (1935), remains controversial.
