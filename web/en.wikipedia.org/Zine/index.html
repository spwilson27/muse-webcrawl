A zine (short for fanzine or magazine) is a small-circulation, self-published work of original or appropriated texts and images, usually reproduced by photocopier or risograph and distributed outside commercial channels. Zines are a characteristic medium of do-it-yourself subcultures, motivated by expression and community rather than profit.

History

Zines descend from the science-fiction fanzines of the 1930s, through which fans published stories, reviews, and correspondence. The medium exploded with punk in the 1970s — titles such as Sniffin' Glue (1976) defined the cut-and-paste aesthetic — expanded again with the photocopier-driven "zine explosion" and review journals like Factsheet Five in the 1980s, and became a voice of the feminist riot grrrl movement in the 1990s.

Culture

Zines cover any subject their makers care about: music, politics, personal diaries ("perzines"), poetry, comics, cooking, and fandom. They circulate through distros (small distributors), independent bookshops, zine fairs and libraries — including major archival collections such as the one at Barnard College — and have enjoyed a revival in the 21st century alongside renewed interest in print.
