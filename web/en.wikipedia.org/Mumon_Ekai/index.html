Mumon Ekai

Born: 1183, Hangzhou, China
Died: 1260
School: Linji (Rinzai) Zen
Known for: The Gateless Barrier (Mumonkan)

Mumon Ekai (Chinese: Wumen Huikai; 1183–1260) was a Chinese Chan (Zen) master of the Linji school during the late Song dynasty. He is best known as the compiler of The Gateless Barrier (Japanese: Mumonkan), one of the most famous collections of Zen koans, completed in 1228.

Life

Mumon was born in Hangzhou, the Song capital. He studied under several masters and received dharma transmission from Yuelin Shiguan. After years of struggling with the koan "Mu" — the single word answering Joshu's famous exchange about whether a dog has Buddha-nature — he attained awakening, reportedly upon hearing the sound of a drum. He later served as abbot of temples including the Longxiang monastery, teaching large assemblies and insisting on vigorous, doubt-driven practice.

The Gateless Barrier

The Mumonkan contains 48 cases, each consisting of a koan, Mumon's brief comment, and a verse. In his preface Mumon declared that Zen has no gates, yet those who pass through walk freely; the collection is presented as a barrier that is at once an obstacle and an entrance. His commentaries are deliberately provocative, mocking students who cling to words or seek easy answers.

Legacy

Together with the Blue Cliff Record, the Mumonkan became a central text of koan study, especially in the Japanese Rinzai tradition. Its first case, "Joshu's Dog" — Joshu's "Mu" — remains the classic introductory koan given to beginning students, and Mumon's approach of "great doubt, great faith, great determination" continues to shape Zen training.
