Maratha Confederacy

Existed1674–1818
CapitalRaigad, then Satara / Pune

The Maratha Confederacy (also called the Maratha Empire) was a confederation of Indian states that dominated much of the Indian subcontinent in the 18th century. It was founded by Shivaji, who was crowned Chhatrapati in 1674, in resistance to the Mughal Empire.

Structure

Real power came to be shared between the hereditary Chhatrapati at Satara, the Peshwa (prime minister) at Pune, and great sardar houses such as the Scindias of Gwalior, Holkars of Indore, Gaekwads of Baroda and Bhonsles of Nagpur, who ruled semi-autonomously while nominally owing allegiance to the confederacy.

Decline

The confederacy reached its greatest extent under the Peshwas, covering much of modern India, but defeat by Ahmad Shah Durrani at the Third Battle of Panipat (1761) checked its expansion. Internal rivalries led to a series of Anglo-Maratha wars, ending in defeat by the British East India Company in 1818.
