Culture of Japan

The culture of Japan has evolved over millennia, from the country's prehistoric Jōmon period through its absorption of influences from China and Korea to its distinctive modern blend of tradition and technology. Japanese culture is characterised by its language and writing system, its religions — chiefly Shinto and Buddhism — its arts, cuisine, and social customs.

Language and literature

Japanese is written with a combination of kanji (characters borrowed from Chinese) and two kana syllabaries, hiragana and katakana. Japanese literature includes the world's first novel, Murasaki Shikibu's The Tale of Genji (c. 1000), the poetry forms of waka and haiku, and modern Nobel laureates Yasunari Kawabata and Kenzaburō Ōe.

Arts

Traditional arts include ikebana (flower arranging), the tea ceremony, calligraphy, origami, ukiyo-e woodblock prints, and the theatre forms of Noh, Kabuki and Bunraku. Contemporary Japanese culture has achieved global reach through manga, anime, video games and fashion.

Cuisine

Japanese cuisine, inscribed by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage, is based on rice, miso soup and seasonal dishes, including sushi, tempura, ramen and kaiseki haute cuisine, with an emphasis on presentation and seasonality (shun).

Society

Social life emphasises group harmony (wa), politeness encoded in honorific language (keigo), and seasonal festivals (matsuri) such as cherry-blossom viewing (hanami) and Obon. Martial arts including judo, karate and kendo remain widely practised alongside baseball and football.
