Microscope

A microscope (from Ancient Greek mikrós 'small' and skopeîn 'to look') is a laboratory instrument used to examine objects that are too small to be seen by the naked eye. The science of investigating small objects and structures using a microscope is called microscopy.

History

The compound microscope was invented in the Netherlands around 1590, commonly credited to Hans and Zacharias Janssen. In the 1660s and 1670s, Robert Hooke published Micrographia, and Antonie van Leeuwenhoek, using powerful single-lens microscopes, was the first to observe bacteria and other microorganisms.

Types

The most common type is the optical microscope, which uses visible light and lenses. Other types include the electron microscope (transmission and scanning), which uses electron beams to achieve far greater magnification and resolution, and scanning probe microscopes such as the atomic force microscope and scanning tunneling microscope.

Applications

Microscopes are essential in biology, medicine, materials science, geology, and forensics, enabling the study of cells, microorganisms, crystals, and microstructures.
