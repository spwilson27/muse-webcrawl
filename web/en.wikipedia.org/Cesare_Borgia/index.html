Cesare Borgia (13 September 1475 – 12 March 1507) was an Italian cardinal and condottiero (mercenary leader) and a member of the Spanish-Aragonese House of Borgia. He was the son of Pope Alexander VI and his long-term mistress Vannozza dei Cattanei, and the brother of Lucrezia Borgia. Made a cardinal at seventeen, he became the first person in history to resign the cardinalate (1498) in order to pursue a military career and marriage alliance with France, receiving the Duchy of Valentinois from Louis XII — hence his nickname Il Valentino.

Career

As captain-general of the papal armies, Cesare conquered much of the Romagna and central Italy between 1499 and 1503, carving out a state of his own through a mixture of military skill, diplomacy, and ruthlessness, famously exemplified by the massacre of rebellious condottieri at Senigallia. Niccolò Machiavelli, who observed Cesare as a Florentine envoy, later held him up in The Prince as a model — if ultimately fortune-crossed — new ruler. Cesare also employed Leonardo da Vinci as his chief military engineer and architect in 1502–1503.

Fall and death

The death of Alexander VI in August 1503, while Cesare himself lay gravely ill, destroyed his position. Arrested under Pope Julius II, he escaped from imprisonment in Spain and took service with his brother-in-law, King John III of Navarre. He was killed in a skirmish at the siege of Viana on 12 March 1507, aged 31.
