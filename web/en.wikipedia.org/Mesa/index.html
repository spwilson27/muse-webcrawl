Mesa

A mesa is an isolated, flat-topographical landform with steep sides, typically composed of flat-lying soft sedimentary rocks capped by a more resistant layer of rock. The word comes from the Spanish for "table". Mesas are characteristic of arid environments, particularly in the southwestern United States — such as Monument Valley and the Colorado Plateau — though they occur worldwide. A mesa is generally wider than it is tall; a similar but narrower landform is called a butte.

Formation

Mesas form by the differential erosion of horizontally layered strata. A hard caprock, such as sandstone or conglomerate, protects the softer shale beneath it. As surrounding rock is worn away by water and wind, the capped remnant stands as a plateau fragment. Continued erosion along the cliffs shrinks a plateau into mesas, and mesas into buttes; fallen blocks accumulate as talus slopes at the base. The Grand Mesa in Colorado, covering about 1,300 km², is often described as the largest flat-topped mountain in the world.

Other uses of the term

Features resembling mesas are found on Mars, where flat-topped mountains in chaotic terrain bear the same name. "Mesa" also appears in many place names in the American Southwest, including the city of Mesa, Arizona.
