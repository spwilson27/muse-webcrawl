Tanzania, officially the United Republic of Tanzania, is a country in East Africa within the African Great Lakes region. It borders Kenya and Uganda to the north, Rwanda, Burundi, and the Democratic Republic of the Congo to the west, Zambia, Malawi, and Mozambique to the south, and the Indian Ocean to the east. Mount Kilimanjaro, Africa's highest peak, is in northeastern Tanzania.

History

The mainland territory of Tanganyika was a German colony until the First World War, then a British mandate. It gained independence in 1961 under Julius Nyerere, whose policy of African socialism (ujamaa) shaped the new nation. In 1964 Tanganyika united with the islands of Zanzibar to form the United Republic of Tanzania.

Geography and wildlife

Tanzania is home to the Serengeti National Park, famous for the annual wildebeest migration, the Ngorongoro Conservation Area, and the Selous and Ruaha reserves. Lake Tanganyika, the world's longest freshwater lake, forms its western border.
