African-American music

African-American music is an umbrella term for the musical genres created by African Americans, rooted in the musical traditions of enslaved Africans brought to the Americas and blended with European forms. It has profoundly shaped popular music worldwide.

Development

From spirituals, work songs and field hollers grew gospel and the blues; from ragtime and New Orleans brass traditions came jazz. In the twentieth century African-American artists created or transformed rhythm and blues, soul, funk, rock and roll, disco, hip hop and house music, while composers such as William Grant Still and Florence Price worked in classical forms.

Characteristics

Recurring features include call and response, improvisation, syncopation and blue notes, polyrhythm, and the centrality of the performing artist's individual expression.
