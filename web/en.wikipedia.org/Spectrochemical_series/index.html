Spectrochemical series

A spectrochemical series is a list of ligands ordered by ligand "strength", and a list of metal ions based on oxidation number, group and ligands. In crystal field theory, ligands modify the difference in energy between the d orbitals (Δ) through the ligand field. The spectrochemical series ranks ligands from weak field (small Δ, high spin) to strong field (large Δ, low spin).

The series

A commonly cited ordering, from weak-field to strong-field ligands, is: I− < Br− < S2− < SCN− < Cl− < F− < OH− < C2O42− < H2O < NCS− < NH3 < en < bipy < phen < NO2− < PPh3 < CN− < CO. Weak-field ligands such as the halides generally produce high-spin complexes with first-row transition metals, while strong-field ligands such as cyanide and carbon monoxide produce low-spin complexes.

Explanation

The ordering is understood in ligand field theory through σ donation and π interactions: π-donor ligands decrease Δ, π-acceptor ligands such as CO and CN− increase Δ by back-bonding. The series was first established empirically from absorption spectra of metal complexes in the 1930s, notably by Ryutaro Tsuchida.

Consequences

Because Δ determines the energy of d–d transitions, the spectrochemical series also predicts the colours of complexes: strong-field ligands shift absorption to higher energies (shorter wavelengths). It likewise influences magnetic properties and substitution reactivity.
