Bar chart

A bar chart or bar graph is a chart or graph that presents categorical data with rectangular bars with heights or lengths proportional to the values that they represent. The bars can be plotted vertically or horizontally. A vertical bar chart is sometimes called a column chart and has been described as more reliable for the purpose of data visualisation than a pie chart.

History

Many sources credit the Scottish engineer and political economist William Playfair with inventing the bar chart; his Commercial and Political Atlas (1786) contained the first published examples, including a chart of Scotland's imports and exports. Earlier coordinate-based plotting, in the 14th century, had been used by Nicole Oresme to represent varying quantities, but Playfair's work popularised the form.

Usage

Bar charts are used for comparing values across discrete categories: sales by region, populations of countries, survey responses, and the like. Bars may be grouped (clustered) to compare several series side by side, or stacked to show both totals and the contribution of parts. Where categories have long names or there are many of them, horizontal bars are preferred. A bar chart of continuous binned data is a histogram, which differs by having no gaps between adjacent bars.

Design considerations

Good practice generally requires the value axis of a bar chart to start at zero, because truncating the axis visually exaggerates differences. Ordering bars by value can aid comparison for nominal categories, and direct labelling often works better than a legend. Misleading bar charts are a standard example in discussions of how graphics can distort data.
