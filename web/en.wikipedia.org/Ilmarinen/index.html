Ilmarinen

Ilmarinen, the Eternal Hammerer, is a blacksmith and inventor in Finnish mythology and one of the principal characters of the Kalevala, Finland's national epic. He is the archetypal maker, credited with forging the dome of the sky and the miraculous artefact known as the Sampo.

Role in the Kalevala

In the Kalevala, compiled by Elias Lönnrot in the nineteenth century from oral folk poetry, Ilmarinen is persuaded by the sage Väinämöinen to travel to Pohjola, the far north, where he forges the Sampo — a magical mill that produces grain, salt, and gold — as a bride-price for the daughter of Louhi, the mistress of Pohjola. He later quests with Väinämöinen and Lemminkäinen to steal the Sampo back, and it is shattered in the struggle at sea. Ilmarinen also forges other wonders, including a bride of gold and silver when his first wife is murdered, and attempts to chain the giant fish of Tuonela.

Background

The name Ilmarinen is related to the Finnish word ilma, "air" or "weather", and the character may descend from an ancient sky god. In older folk poems he is sometimes a god of the air who helped create the world.
