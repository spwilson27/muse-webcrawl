Binding (linguistics)

In linguistics, binding is the distribution of anaphoric elements—pronouns, reflexives and other expressions—with respect to their antecedents. Binding theory, a component of generative grammar developed notably by Noam Chomsky, states conditions on the structural relations between anaphors, pronouns and referential expressions.

Principles A, B and C

Principle A: an anaphor (reflexive/reciprocal, e.g. himself, each other) must be bound in its governing category. Principle B: a pronoun must be free in its governing category. Principle C: a referential expression (name) must be free everywhere. Binding means coindexation with a c-commanding antecedent.

C-command

A node c-commands its sister and everything inside its sister. This structural relation explains contrasts such as "Johni saw himselfi" (grammatical) versus "*Himselfi saw Johni".

Developments

Later work refined governing categories, logophoricity, exempt anaphors, and cross-linguistic binding in languages with long-distance reflexives such as Mandarin ziji and Icelandic sig. Binding data remain central evidence in syntactic theory.
