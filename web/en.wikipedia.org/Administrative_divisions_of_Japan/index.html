The administrative divisions of Japan are organized chiefly in two tiers: 47 prefectures (todōfuken) and the municipalities within them. Japan is a unitary state, but prefectures and municipalities enjoy substantial local autonomy under the Local Autonomy Law of 1947.

Prefectures

The 47 prefectures comprise 43 ordinary prefectures (ken), the metropolis of Tokyo (to), the "urban prefectures" of Osaka and Kyoto (fu), and the single dō of Hokkaido. Each prefecture is headed by an elected governor and a unicameral assembly. Prefectures were established in the Meiji era, replacing the feudal domains (han) in 1871–72.

Municipalities

Municipalities consist of cities (shi), towns (machi) and villages (mura); the 23 special wards (ku) of Tokyo function as municipalities. Large cities may be designated by government ordinance and subdivided into wards. Waves of municipal mergers, most recently the "Great Heisei mergers" of the 2000s, reduced the number of municipalities from over 3,200 to about 1,700.
