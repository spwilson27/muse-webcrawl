Sertão

The sertão (Portuguese: "backcountry" or "outback") is the semi-arid interior region of Northeast Brazil, characterised by thorny scrub vegetation known as caatinga, recurrent droughts, and a distinctive culture shaped by cattle ranching and subsistence farming.

Geography and climate

The sertão covers much of the states of Bahia, Pernambuco, Paraíba, Rio Grande do Norte, Ceará, Piauí, and parts of Alagoas, Sergipe, and Minas Gerais. Rainfall is low and erratic, concentrated in a short wet season; severe droughts (secas) have periodically driven mass migration to the coast and to cities such as São Paulo.

Culture

The region is associated with the figure of the vaqueiro (cowboy), with bandit-heroes such as Lampião, and with religious movements like Antônio Conselheiro's settlement at Canudos. Euclides da Cunha's Os Sertões (1902) is its classic literary portrait.
