Site of Special Scientific Interest

A Site of Special Scientific Interest (SSSI) is a conservation designation denoting a protected area in the United Kingdom. SSSIs are the basic building blocks of site-based nature conservation legislation in Great Britain, and most other legal nature and geological conservation designations are based upon them, including national nature reserves, Ramsar sites and Special Protection Areas. In Northern Ireland the equivalent designation is Area of Special Scientific Interest (ASSI).

Selection and notification

Sites are selected and "notified" by the statutory nature conservation bodies — Natural England, NatureScot and Natural Resources Wales — under the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981, using published guidelines for biological and geological features. There are more than 4,000 SSSIs in England alone, covering around 8% of the country's land area; across Great Britain they protect habitats ranging from ancient woodland and lowland heath to geological exposures and fossil localities.

Legal protection

Owners and occupiers must obtain consent from the conservation body before carrying out operations listed as likely to damage the special features of a site. Public bodies have duties to further the conservation of SSSIs, and the Countryside and Rights of Way Act 2000 strengthened protections and introduced condition monitoring, which classifies sites as favourable, recovering, unfavourable or destroyed.
