Pyramid Lake Paiute Tribe

The Pyramid Lake Paiute Tribe is a federally recognized tribe of Northern Paiute people in northwestern Nevada. The tribe's reservation, established in 1874, encompasses Pyramid Lake entirely, together with surrounding desert lands and the lower reaches of the Truckee River, the lake's principal inflow. The tribal headquarters is in Nixon, Nevada, with other communities at Wadsworth and Sutcliffe. In their own language, the Northern Paiute language, tribal members call themselves Kooyooe Tukadu, "cui-ui eaters", after the cui-ui fish found only in Pyramid Lake.

History

The Paiute people around Pyramid Lake subsisted on the lake's fish — cui-ui and Lahontan cutthroat trout — as well as pine nuts, game, and seeds from the surrounding desert. Contact with emigrant parties on the California Trail in the 1840s turned violent; the Pyramid Lake War of 1860, one of the largest conflicts between Native peoples and settlers in Nevada, followed settler provocations. A reservation around the lake was set aside by executive order in 1874. In the twentieth century, upstream diversions of the Truckee River, principally the Derby Dam and the Newlands Reclamation Project from 1905, lowered the lake level by some 80 feet and devastated its fishery; the cui-ui was listed as endangered and the lake's giant Lahontan cutthroat trout were extirpated, later reintroduced from another strain.

Water rights and restoration

The tribe has been a central party in decades of Truckee River litigation and negotiation, asserting senior water rights for the lake. The Truckee River Operating Agreement, finalized in 2015, improved flows for the lake and its fish. The tribal fishery program operates the Marble Bluff fish facility and has supported the recovery of cui-ui and the re-establishment of large Lahontan cutthroat trout, which again attract anglers to the lake under tribal permit.

Government and economy

The tribe is governed by an elected tribal council under a constitution adopted under the Indian Reorganization Act of 1934. Tribal enterprises include fisheries management, recreation permits for fishing and boating on the lake, and small businesses in the reservation communities.
