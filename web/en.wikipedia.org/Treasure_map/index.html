Treasure map

A treasure map is a map that marks the location of buried treasure, a lost mine, a valuable secret, or a hidden location. Treasure maps are a common motif in fiction, most famously in Robert Louis Stevenson's 1883 novel Treasure Island, which popularised the image of a hand-drawn map with an "X" marking the spot.

In fiction

Stevenson's map, drawn by the author himself, set the template for the fictional treasure map: a chart of an island with landmarks, bearings, and an X over the burial site. The device has since appeared in countless adventure novels, films, and games, from pirate stories to works such as The Goonies and the Indiana Jones series.

Real treasure maps

Authentic historical treasure maps are rare. The most famous example is the Copper Scroll, one of the Dead Sea Scrolls, which lists locations of hidden gold and silver caches. Legends also surround the maps said to be associated with Captain Kidd, although the charts linked to him in the 20th century are generally considered fabrications. Tales of buried pirate treasure, such as on Oak Island in Nova Scotia or Cocos Island off Costa Rica, continue to attract searchers.
