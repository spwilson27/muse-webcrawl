Elliptic operator

In the theory of partial differential equations, elliptic operators are a class of differential operators that generalize the Laplace operator. They are defined by the condition that the coefficients of the highest-order derivatives be positive — or, more precisely, that the principal symbol of the operator be invertible for every nonzero covector.

Definition

A linear differential operator L of order m mapping smooth functions to smooth functions is called elliptic at a point if its principal symbol σL(ξ) is an invertible linear map for every nonzero covector ξ. For a second-order operator this reduces to the condition that the matrix of coefficients of the second derivatives be positive definite everywhere.

Examples

The prototypical elliptic operator is the Laplacian Δ = Σ ∂²/∂xᵢ², whose associated equation Δu = 0 is Laplace's equation. Other important examples include the Helmholtz operator, the Lamé operator of elasticity, and the Cauchy–Riemann operator in complex analysis.

Properties

Elliptic operators enjoy a number of powerful regularity properties:

  • Elliptic regularity: solutions of elliptic equations are smooth (infinitely differentiable) wherever the data are smooth — a consequence of hypoellipticity.
  • Maximum principles: solutions of many elliptic equations attain their maxima and minima on the boundary of the domain.
  • Fredholm theory: on a compact manifold, an elliptic operator is a Fredholm operator between suitable function spaces, with finite-dimensional kernel and cokernel.

These properties culminate in the Atiyah–Singer index theorem, which relates the analytic index of an elliptic operator on a compact manifold to topological data, one of the landmark results of 20th-century mathematics.
