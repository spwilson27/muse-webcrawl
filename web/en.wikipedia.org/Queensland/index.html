Queensland is a state in northeastern Australia. It is the second-largest and third-most populous of the Australian states and territories. Located in the north-east of the country, it is bordered by the Northern Territory, South Australia, and New South Wales to the west, south-west, and south respectively. To the east, Queensland borders the Coral Sea and the Pacific Ocean. The state is nicknamed the Sunshine State, reflecting its warm climate.

Geography

Queensland's landscape ranges from tropical rainforests in the far north, including the Daintree Rainforest, to arid interior plains. The Great Dividing Range runs roughly parallel to the coast. Offshore lies the Great Barrier Reef, the world's largest coral reef system, stretching more than 2,300 kilometres along the Queensland coast. Major cities include the capital Brisbane, the Gold Coast, the Sunshine Coast, Townsville, Cairns, and Toowoomba.

History

Indigenous Australians inhabited Queensland for tens of thousands of years before European settlement. The Moreton Bay penal settlement was established in 1824, and Queensland was proclaimed a separate colony on 6 June 1859—celebrated as Queensland Day—named in honour of Queen Victoria. It federated with the other colonies in 1901 to form the Commonwealth of Australia.

Economy

Queensland's economy is driven by mining (coal, natural gas, bauxite, and mineral sands), agriculture (sugarcane, bananas, beef cattle), and tourism, which is drawn to the Great Barrier Reef, tropical islands, and beach resorts. It is one of the world's largest exporters of seaborne coal.

Government

Queensland is a constitutional monarchy with a parliamentary system. Unusually among Australian states, its parliament is unicameral: the Legislative Council (upper house) was abolished in 1922, leaving only the Legislative Assembly.
