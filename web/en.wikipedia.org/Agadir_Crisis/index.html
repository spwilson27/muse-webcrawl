Agadir Crisis

The Agadir Crisis, also called the Second Moroccan Crisis, was a 1911 international crisis sparked by the deployment of the German gunboat SMS Panther to the Moroccan port of Agadir. It heightened tensions between Germany and the Franco-British Entente shortly before the First World War.

Background

France, seeking to consolidate its influence over Morocco, sent troops to Fez in April 1911, in breach, Germany argued, of the Algeciras Conference arrangements. On 1 July 1911 the Panther arrived at Agadir, ostensibly to protect German interests.

Resolution

Negotiations ended with the Treaty of Fez (1911): Germany recognised France's position in Morocco in exchange for territory in French Equatorial Africa, added to German Kamerun as Neukamerun. The crisis strengthened the Anglo-French alignment and increased naval spending.
