Friesland

Friesland (Dutch: Friesland; West Frisian: Fryslân) is a province of the Netherlands located in the northern part of the country. It is situated west of Groningen, northwest of Drenthe and Overijssel, north of Flevoland, northeast of North Holland, and south of the Wadden Sea. As of January 2023, the province had a population of about 660,000, and a total area of about 5,700 km², of which a large share is water.

The capital and largest city is Leeuwarden (West Frisian: Ljouwert). Other large municipalities include Súdwest-Fryslân, Heerenveen, and Smallingerland, whose seat is Drachten. The province is named after the Frisians, a Germanic ethnic group native to the coastal regions of the Netherlands and Germany, and is known for the West Frisian language, which has official status in the province alongside Dutch.

Geography and culture

Friesland includes a chain of West Frisian Islands in the Wadden Sea, among them Vlieland, Terschelling, Ameland, and Schiermonnikoog. A large part of the province is lake country, popular for water sports; the Elfstedentocht, an ice skating tour of almost 200 kilometres along frozen canals connecting eleven Frisian cities, is held in winters cold enough to freeze the course, most recently in 1997. Friesland is strongly associated with dairy farming, the black-and-white Friesian cattle breed, and the Friesian horse.

History

In the early Middle Ages the Frisian lands enjoyed a large measure of independence under the so-called Frisian freedom, with no feudal overlord. The region was gradually incorporated into the Dutch Republic in the sixteenth century, and the present province took shape after the Napoleonic era.
