Justus Freiherr von Liebig (12 May 1803 – 18 April 1873) was a German scientist who made major contributions to agricultural and biological chemistry and is considered a founder of organic chemistry. As a professor at the University of Giessen he devised the modern laboratory-oriented method of teaching science, training a generation of chemists and making his laboratory the most famous in the world.

Contributions

Liebig perfected a method of organic elemental analysis (the Kaliapparat) that made chemical analysis rapid and routine, discovered that nitrogen is an essential plant nutrient, and formulated the Law of the Minimum — plant growth is limited by the scarcest nutrient. His advocacy launched the artificial fertiliser industry. With Friedrich Wöhler he established the concept of radicals and isomers. Later in Munich he worked on nutrition, developing meat extract (commercialised as Liebig's Extract of Meat) and an early infant formula, and popularised the baking powder and the Liebig condenser.

Influence

His students — including August Wilhelm von Hofmann and Lord Playfair — spread the Giessen model of research-based teaching to Britain, America, and beyond. The university at Giessen now bears his name (Justus Liebig University).
