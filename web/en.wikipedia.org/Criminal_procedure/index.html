Criminal procedure

Criminal procedure is the body of law governing the process by which crimes are investigated, prosecuted, and adjudicated, from arrest through trial, sentencing, and appeal. It balances the state's interest in enforcing the law against the rights of the accused.

Stages

Typical stages include investigation, arrest, charging, arraignment, pretrial motions, trial, verdict, sentencing, and appeal. Procedures differ between adversarial systems (such as the United States and England) and inquisitorial systems (common in continental Europe), though many jurisdictions blend features of both.

Rights of the accused

Criminal procedure protects rights such as the presumption of innocence, the right to counsel, protection against unreasonable search and seizure, the right against self-incrimination, and the requirement of proof beyond a reasonable doubt. In the United States these protections derive principally from the Fourth, Fifth, Sixth, and Fourteenth Amendments.
