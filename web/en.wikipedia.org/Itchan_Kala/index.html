Itchan Kala

Location: Khiva, Uzbekistan
Area: approx. 26 ha
UNESCO World Heritage Site: inscribed 1990

Itchan Kala (Uzbek: Ichan-qalʼa) is the walled inner town of the oasis city of Khiva in Uzbekistan. It was the first site in Uzbekistan to be inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List (in 1990), and it preserves, almost intact, the fabric of a Central Asian Muslim city of the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries.

Description

The inner town forms a rough rectangle of about 26 hectares, enclosed by massive mud-brick ramparts some 10 metres high and more than 2 kilometres in circuit, pierced by four gates at the cardinal points. Inside, some fifty historic monuments and a few hundred traditional houses survive, and a resident population still lives within the walls, which function as an open-air museum-city.

Principal monuments

The skyline is dominated by minarets, above all the squat, brilliantly tiled Kalta Minor (1855), intended by the khan Muhammad Amin to be the tallest minaret in the Islamic world but left unfinished at his death, and the slender Islam Khoja minaret (1910), the tallest in Khiva. The Kunya-Ark, the khans' fortress-palace, contains a mint, harem and throne hall with an open-air iwan. The Juma (Friday) Mosque is a vast hypostyle hall whose roof rests on more than two hundred carved wooden columns, some of medieval date. Other major monuments include the Tash Khauli palace, the madrasas of Muhammad Amin Khan and Muhammad Rahim Khan, and the mausoleum of the poet and wrestler Pahlavon Mahmud, patron saint of Khiva, covered by a great turquoise dome.

History

Khiva, on the edge of the Kyzylkum desert near the Amu Darya, was a staging post on the Silk Road and capital of the Khanate of Khiva. Local legend derives the city from a well called Kheivak, supposedly dug by Shem, son of Noah. The surviving inner town is mostly the product of rebuilding under the khans of the seventeenth to early twentieth centuries. The khanate was abolished in 1920, and in the Soviet period Itchan Kala was declared a museum reserve, a status that helped preserve it.
