Men's movement

The men's movement is a social movement that emerged in the 1960s and 1970s, principally in Western countries, consisting of groups and organizations of men and their allies who focus on gender issues and undertake activities ranging from self-help and support to lobbying and activism. It encompasses several distinct strands, some aligned with feminism and others critical of it.

Strands

The early men's liberation movement, influenced by second-wave feminism, questioned traditional male gender roles, emotional restraint and the provider role. In the 1980s and 1990s the mythopoetic men's movement, associated with Robert Bly's book Iron John (1990), emphasized retreats, ritual and archetypal models of masculinity. The men's rights movement focuses on issues its supporters see as disadvantages faced by men, including family law, conscription and suicide rates. Promise Keepers, founded in 1990, represented an evangelical Christian strand emphasizing male spiritual leadership. Profeminist men's organizations, such as those campaigning against men's violence toward women, form another branch.

Reception

Sociologists describe the men's movement less as a unified campaign than as a self-help network with divergent politics. Feminist commentary has ranged from support for profeminist and men's liberation work to criticism of men's rights activism, which some scholars associate with antifeminist backlash. Later online communities in the so-called manosphere are sometimes treated as descendants, though their relationship to the earlier movements is debated.
