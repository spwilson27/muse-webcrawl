Norman Graham Hill OBE (15 February 1929 – 29 November 1975) was a British racing driver and team owner, who won the Formula One World Drivers' Championship twice, in 1962 and 1968. He remains the only driver ever to win the Triple Crown of Motorsport — the Formula One World Championship, the Indianapolis 500 (1966) and the 24 Hours of Le Mans (1972) — and he won the Monaco Grand Prix five times, earning the nickname "Mr Monaco".

Career

Hill came to motor racing late, taking his first driving lesson at 24. He joined Colin Chapman's Lotus as a mechanic and worked his way into the cockpit, later winning his first championship with BRM in 1962. Returning to Lotus, he took the 1968 title in a season overshadowed by the death of his team-mate Jim Clark. After breaking both legs at Watkins Glen in 1969, he recovered to win Le Mans in 1972 and went on to found his own team, Embassy Hill.

Death

Hill was killed on 29 November 1975 when the light aircraft he was piloting crashed in fog near Arkley Golf Course, north London, while returning from a test session at Paul Ricard. Five members of his team, including driver Tony Brise, also died. His son Damon Hill became Formula One World Champion in 1996, making them the first father-and-son F1 champions.
