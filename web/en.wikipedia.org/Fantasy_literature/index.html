Fantasy literature

Fantasy literature is literature set in an imaginary universe, often but not always without any locations, events, or people from the real world. Magic, the supernatural and magical creatures are common in many of these imaginary worlds. It is a subgenre of speculative fiction.

History

Fantastic elements appear in ancient epics such as the Epic of Gilgamesh and Homer's Odyssey, in medieval romance, and in fairy tales. Modern fantasy was shaped by George MacDonald and William Morris in the 19th century, and reached mass popularity with J. R. R. Tolkien's The Hobbit (1937) and The Lord of the Rings (1954–55), and C. S. Lewis's The Chronicles of Narnia.

Subgenres include high fantasy, low fantasy, sword and sorcery, urban fantasy, and dark fantasy. Major modern authors include Ursula K. Le Guin, Terry Pratchett, George R. R. Martin, and Brandon Sanderson.
