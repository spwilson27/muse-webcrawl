Charter 08

Charter 08 (Chinese: 零八宪章; pinyin: Língbā Xiànzhāng) is a manifesto initially signed by over 300 Chinese intellectuals and human rights activists calling for democratic reform in the People's Republic of China. It was published on 10 December 2008, the 60th anniversary of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, and its name echoes Czechoslovakia's Charter 77. It ultimately gathered more than 10,000 signatures despite censorship and the detention of signatories.

Contents

The charter sets out fundamental principles — freedom, human rights, equality, republicanism, democracy, and constitutional rule — and nineteen specific proposals, including an end to one-party rule, separation of powers, an independent judiciary, freedom of association, press, and religion, direct elections, protection of private property, social welfare, environmental protection, and a truth commission to address the injustices of past political campaigns.

Response and Liu Xiaobo

The Chinese government suppressed the document, blocked its distribution online, and detained its principal drafter, the literary critic and activist Liu Xiaobo, who was sentenced in December 2009 to eleven years' imprisonment for "inciting subversion of state power." While imprisoned, Liu was awarded the 2010 Nobel Peace Prize, which he could not collect; he died of liver cancer in custody in July 2017, the first Nobel laureate to die in detention since Carl von Ossietzky in Nazi Germany. Charter 08 remains a landmark statement of Chinese liberal dissent, though it is effectively banned within China.
