Afrika Bambaataa (born Lance Taylor; April 17, 1957) is an American DJ, rapper, and producer from the South Bronx, New York. He is widely credited as one of the originators of breakbeat DJing and is often called the "Godfather" of hip hop culture, having helped spread hip hop around the world through his Universal Zulu Nation organization.

Early life and career

Taylor grew up in the Bronx River Houses, a public housing project in the South Bronx. Influenced by his mother's record collection and by the block parties of DJ Kool Herc, he began organizing his own parties in the late 1970s. He took the name Afrika Bambaataa from a 19th-century Zulu chief after watching the film Zulu.

In 1982, Bambaataa and his group the Soulsonic Force released "Planet Rock", a landmark single that fused hip hop with the electronic sound of Kraftwerk's "Trans-Europe Express" and helped create the electro-funk genre. The record became a worldwide hit and profoundly influenced electronic dance music.

Universal Zulu Nation

Bambaataa founded the Universal Zulu Nation, a hip hop awareness group that promoted peace, unity, love, and having fun, as an alternative to gang culture. The organization grew into an international movement with chapters around the world.

Later career and legacy

He collaborated with artists including James Brown ("Unity", 1984), John Lydon of the Sex Pistols (as Time Zone on "World Destruction", 1984), and Bill Laswell. In 2016, allegations of sexual abuse emerged, and Bambaataa stepped down as head of the Zulu Nation. His foundational role in the creation and global spread of hip hop culture remains widely acknowledged.
