Chimay Brewery

LocationScourmont Abbey, Chimay, Belgium
Opened1862
OwnerTrappist monks of Scourmont Abbey
Known forChimay Rouge, Bleue, Blanche (Tripel)

Chimay Brewery (Brasserie de Chimay) is a brewery at Scourmont Abbey, a Trappist monastery in Chimay, Hainaut, Belgium. It is one of the world's recognised Trappist breweries, authorised to display the "Authentic Trappist Product" label, and one of the largest producers of Trappist beer.

History

The monks of Scourmont began brewing in 1862 to support the abbey and local employment. The brewery was destroyed during the Second World War and rebuilt afterwards; the celebrated brewer Father Théodore, working with the chemist Jean De Clerck, isolated the yeast strain still used in Chimay beers.

Beers

The principal beers are Chimay Rouge (a dubbel, 7%), Chimay Blanche (a tripel, 8%), and Chimay Bleue (a dark strong ale, 9%), named after their capsule or label colours, along with the monks' own lower-strength Dorée. The abbey also produces Chimay cheeses. Profits support the monastery and charitable works.
