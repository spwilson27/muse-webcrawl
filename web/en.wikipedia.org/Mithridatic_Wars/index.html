Mithridatic Wars

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Mithridatic Wars were three wars fought between the Roman Republic and the Kingdom of Pontus between 88 and 63 BC. They are named for Mithridates VI, the king of Pontus, whose ambitions to dominate Anatolia and the Black Sea brought him into repeated conflict with Rome. The wars ended with Mithridates' death and the Roman annexation of much of Anatolia and Syria.

First Mithridatic War (88–85 BC)

In 88 BC Mithridates overran the Roman province of Asia and ordered the massacre of Roman and Italian residents — the so-called Asiatic Vespers, in which tens of thousands were killed. He then intervened in Greece. The Roman general Sulla recovered Athens after a brutal siege and defeated Pontic armies at Chaeronea and Orchomenus. The Peace of Dardanus (85 BC) restored the status quo ante, requiring Mithridates to surrender his conquests and pay an indemnity.

Second Mithridatic War (83–81 BC)

The second war was a brief, inconclusive conflict sparked when the Roman commander Murena invaded Pontus without clear authorisation. Sulla ordered a halt, and the earlier settlement was reimposed.

Third Mithridatic War (73–63 BC)

When King Nicomedes IV bequeathed Bithynia to Rome, Mithridates invaded the new province, opening the third and decisive war. Lucullus drove him from Pontus and defeated his son-in-law and ally Tigranes of Armenia, but mutiny and political opposition in Rome led to Lucullus's replacement by Pompey in 66 BC. Pompey crushed the remaining Pontic forces, annexed Syria, and reorganised the East. Mithridates fled to the Crimea, where, betrayed by his son Pharnaces and facing capture, he died in 63 BC — according to tradition ordering a soldier to kill him after his attempts at suicide by poison failed, his body having been inured by lifelong doses of antidotes.

Consequences

The wars destroyed the last major Hellenistic power opposing Rome in the East, vastly enriched Roman commanders and tax farmers, and gave both Sulla and Pompey military prestige that shaped the fall of the Republic.
