Ismail I

Ismail I (1487–1524) founded the Safavid Empire.

Ismail I (Persian: اسماعیل یکم, 17 July 1487 – 23 May 1524), also known as Shah Ismail, was the founder of the Safavid dynasty of Iran, ruling from 1501 until his death in 1524. His reign is of crucial significance in the history of Iran, marking the re-establishment of a unified Iranian state and the official adoption of Twelver Shia Islam as the state religion.

Rise to power

Ismail was the hereditary leader of the Safavid Sufi order of Ardabil, descended from Shaykh Safi al-Din. Supported by the Qizilbash Turkmen tribesmen, the fourteen-year-old Ismail captured Tabriz in 1501 and proclaimed himself Shah of Iran. Within a decade he had conquered all of Persia, parts of Iraq and eastern Anatolia.

Reign and legacy

Ismail imposed Twelver Shi'ism on a largely Sunni population, a policy that profoundly shaped Iranian identity and set Persia against the Sunni Ottoman Empire. His expansion was checked by the Ottoman victory at the Battle of Chaldiran in 1514. Ismail was also a prolific poet in Azerbaijani Turkish under the pen name Khata'i, and is regarded as a founding figure of Azerbaijani literature.
