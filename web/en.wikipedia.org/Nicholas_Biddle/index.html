Nicholas Biddle

Nicholas Biddle (January 8, 1786 – February 27, 1844) was an American financier who served as the third president of the Second Bank of the United States (1823–1836). A precocious scholar from a prominent Philadelphia family, he had earlier edited the journals of the Lewis and Clark expedition and served as secretary to James Monroe.

The Bank War

Biddle ran the Bank as a powerful central institution regulating state banks and the money supply. His successful application for early recharter in 1832 provoked President Andrew Jackson's veto and the "Bank War"; Jackson removed government deposits in 1833, and Biddle's retaliatory contraction of credit deepened the political conflict. The federal charter lapsed in 1836.

Later life

Biddle continued the institution as a Pennsylvania state bank, which failed in the aftermath of the Panic of 1837; it collapsed entirely in 1841. He retired to his estate, Andalusia, on the Delaware River and died in 1844.
