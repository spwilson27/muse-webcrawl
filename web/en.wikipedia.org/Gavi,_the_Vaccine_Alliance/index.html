Gavi, the Vaccine Alliance (formerly the Global Alliance for Vaccines and Immunization) is a public–private global health partnership with the goal of increasing access to immunisation in poor countries. Founded in 2000 with support from the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation and headquartered in Geneva, Switzerland, Gavi brings together governments of donor and implementing countries, the World Health Organization, UNICEF, the World Bank, vaccine manufacturers, and civil society organisations.

Work

Gavi finances the purchase of vaccines for children in the world's lowest-income countries, negotiates lower prices with manufacturers through pooled procurement, and supports the strengthening of national immunisation systems and cold chains. It has supported the introduction of vaccines against diseases including hepatitis B, Haemophilus influenzae type b, pneumococcal disease, rotavirus, measles, yellow fever, meningitis A, and human papillomavirus.

Gavi co-led COVAX, the vaccines pillar of the Access to COVID-19 Tools Accelerator, which aimed to ensure equitable global access to COVID-19 vaccines. By the mid-2020s, Gavi reported having helped vaccinate more than a billion children and prevented many millions of future deaths since its founding.

Funding

Gavi is financed primarily by donor governments and private foundations, and raises funds through periodic replenishment conferences. It pioneered mechanisms such as the International Finance Facility for Immunisation, which issues bonds against long-term donor pledges, and the pneumococcal Advance Market Commitment.
