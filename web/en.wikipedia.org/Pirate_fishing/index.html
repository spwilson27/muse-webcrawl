Pirate fishing

Pirate fishing is another term for illegal, unreported and unregulated fishing (IUU fishing): fishing conducted in violation of national laws or international obligations, fishing that is not reported to the relevant authorities, or fishing in areas or for stocks for which there are no applicable conservation measures, often by stateless vessels or vessels flying flags of convenience.

Scope and impact

Estimates commonly cited by the Food and Agriculture Organization suggest that IUU fishing may account for up to roughly one in five fish caught worldwide, worth many billions of dollars annually. It undermines fisheries management, depletes stocks — including high-value species such as Patagonian toothfish and bluefin tuna — distorts markets for legitimate fishers, and is frequently associated with poor labor conditions and other transnational crime. Pirate vessels often change names and flags, switch off tracking transponders, and transship catches at sea to disguise their origin.

Countermeasures

Responses include port state controls under the FAO's Port State Measures Agreement (in force since 2016), catch documentation schemes, vessel monitoring systems and satellite surveillance, blacklists of IUU vessels maintained by regional fisheries management organizations, and market measures by major importing states such as the European Union's "carding" system for non-cooperating countries.
