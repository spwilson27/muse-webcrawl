Scientific visualization

Scientific visualization (also spelled scientific visualisation) is an interdisciplinary branch of science concerned with the visual representation of scientific data. It focuses on three-dimensional phenomena such as architectural, meteorological, medical and biological systems, using computer graphics to help scientists understand, illustrate and gain insight from their data.

History

The field emerged in the 1980s as computers became capable of rendering complex datasets. A landmark 1987 report by the U.S. National Science Foundation, "Visualization in Scientific Computing", is often cited as the field's founding document. Classic examples include the visualization of airflow over aircraft, weather simulations and medical imaging volumes.

Techniques

Common techniques include volume rendering, isosurface extraction, flow visualization with streamlines and glyphs, and the use of colour mapping to encode scalar fields. Major software systems include ParaView, VisIt and VTK, as well as general tools such as MATLAB and Python libraries.

Applications

Scientific visualization is used in computational fluid dynamics, climate modelling, astrophysics, molecular biology, medical imaging and engineering simulation, where it turns large numerical datasets into images that reveal structure and anomalies.
