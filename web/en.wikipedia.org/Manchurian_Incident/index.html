Manchurian Incident

The Manchurian Incident, also known as the Mukden Incident or the September 18 Incident, was a staged event engineered by Japanese military personnel in 1931 and used as a pretext for the Imperial Japanese Army's invasion of Manchuria in northeastern China.

The incident

On 18 September 1931, officers of Japan's Kwantung Army detonated a small quantity of dynamite beside a section of the Japanese-owned South Manchuria Railway near Mukden (Shenyang); the explosion was too weak to damage the track — a train passed safely minutes later — but the Army blamed Chinese dissidents and launched a full-scale attack on Chinese garrisons. Within months, acting largely without authorisation from the civilian government in Tokyo, the Kwantung Army had overrun all of Manchuria.

Consequences

In 1932 Japan proclaimed the puppet state of Manchukuo, installing the former Qing emperor Puyi. The League of Nations' Lytton Report (1932) found Japan to be the aggressor but the League could do little; Japan withdrew from the League in 1933. The incident marked the start of fifteen years of escalating Japanese aggression in Asia, culminating in the Second Sino-Japanese War of 1937–1945.
