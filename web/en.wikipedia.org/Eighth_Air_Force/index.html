The Eighth Air Force is a numbered air force of the United States Air Force. Activated in 1942, it became famous in the Second World War as the American strategic bombing force based in England — "the Mighty Eighth" — whose B-17 Flying Fortresses and B-24 Liberators conducted the daylight precision bombing campaign against Nazi Germany alongside RAF Bomber Command's night offensive.

Second World War

The Eighth flew its first heavy bomber mission over Europe on 17 August 1942, against Rouen in France. Under commanders Carl Spaatz, Ira Eaker and, from January 1944, James Doolittle, it grew into the largest air force ever assembled, with over 2,000 heavy bombers on peak missions, escorted by P-47 Thunderbolt and later P-51 Mustang fighters. Costly unescorted deep-penetration raids such as the Schweinfurt–Regensburg missions of 1943 gave way in 1944 to the attrition of the Luftwaffe during "Big Week" and pre-invasion attacks. The Eighth dropped more ordnance than any other US air force in the war, but lost over 26,000 airmen killed — more than the entire US Marine Corps.

Post-war

After the war the Eighth became a core Strategic Air Command bomber force, flying B-29s, B-47s, B-52s and nuclear deterrent patrols through the Cold War, and serving in Korea and Vietnam. Today, under Air Force Global Strike Command at Barksdale Air Force Base, it controls the Air Force's B-2 Spirit and B-52H bomber forces and related strategic missions.
