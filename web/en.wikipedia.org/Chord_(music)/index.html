Chord (music)

A chord, in music, is any harmonic set of pitches or notes that is heard as if sounding simultaneously. Most chords in Western tonal music are built in thirds on a root note: a triad combines a root, third, and fifth, while seventh chords and extended chords add further thirds. Chords provide the harmonic framework of a piece and underlie melody through chord progressions.

Triads

The four basic triad types are major (root, major third, perfect fifth), minor (root, minor third, perfect fifth), diminished (root, minor third, diminished fifth), and augmented (root, major third, augmented fifth). Any rearrangement of the same pitch classes is an inversion, named by which chord member is in the bass.

Seventh and extended chords

Adding a seventh above the root yields dominant, major, minor, half-diminished, and fully diminished seventh chords, central to tonal harmony and jazz. Further stacking of thirds produces ninth, eleventh, and thirteenth chords. Chords may also be built in fourths (quartal harmony), by added tones, or as clusters, especially in 20th-century and popular music.

Notation and function

Chords are analysed in Roman numerals relative to a key (I–V–vi–IV, for example) in classical theory, and by letter-name symbols (C, Am7, G/B) in lead sheets and popular music. Functional harmony describes chords as tonic, dominant, or predominant, guiding the progressions that drive cadences.
