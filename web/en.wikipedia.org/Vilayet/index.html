Vilayet

Vilayet
Type: first-level administrative division
Empire: Ottoman Empire
Established: Vilayet Law of 1864
Head: vali (governor-general)

A vilayet (from Arabic wilāya) was a major first-order administrative division of the Ottoman Empire, introduced by the Vilayet Law of 1864 as part of the Tanzimat reforms. It replaced the older eyalet system and was modelled in part on the French département.

Organisation

Each vilayet was governed by a vali appointed by the sultan, and was subdivided into sanjaks, kazas and nahiyes. The first model vilayet was the Danube Vilayet (1864), administered by Midhat Pasha. At the empire's late-19th-century extent there were around thirty vilayets, including Baghdad, Damascus, Beirut, Salonica, Bosnia, and the "Six Vilayets" of eastern Anatolia with large Armenian populations.

Legacy

Several successor states retained the term: provinces of modern Turkey were called vilayets into the 20th century, and Arabic wilaya remains the word for provinces in countries such as Algeria and Oman.
