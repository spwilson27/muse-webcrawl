Peach

Scientific namePrunus persica
FamilyRosaceae
OriginNorthwest China
Fruit typeDrupe

The peach (Prunus persica) is a deciduous tree first domesticated and cultivated in Zhejiang province of Eastern China. It bears edible juicy fruits with various characteristics, most called peaches and others (the glossy-skinned, non-fuzzy varieties) nectarines.

Description

The tree grows to about 7 metres (23 ft) tall. Its leaves are lanceolate, 7–16 cm long, and its pink flowers are produced in early spring before the leaves. The fruit is a drupe with a single large seed encased in a hard, pitted stone. The skin of peaches is fuzzy and velvety; the flesh ranges from white to yellow to red and may cling to the stone (clingstone) or separate freely (freestone).

History

Although its botanical name persica refers to Persia, genetic studies indicate peaches originated in China, where they have been cultivated since at least the Neolithic period. Peach pits have been found at archaeological sites dating back thousands of years. The fruit travelled west along the Silk Road to Persia, and Alexander the Great's conquests helped spread it around the Mediterranean. Peaches arrived in the Americas with Spanish explorers in the 16th century.

Cultivation

Peaches are grown throughout warmer temperate regions of both hemispheres. They require a period of winter chilling to flower properly but are damaged by severe frosts. China is by far the world's largest producer, followed by Spain and Italy. Peaches are eaten fresh, canned, dried, and used in desserts, jams, and beverages.

Cultural significance

In Chinese culture the peach is a symbol of longevity and immortality; the Queen Mother of the West is said to grow peaches of immortality that ripen only once every few thousand years. Peach blossoms are also celebrated in Japan and Korea.
