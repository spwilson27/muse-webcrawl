Kangxi Emperor

Kangxi Emperor
Reign: 1661–1722
Dynasty: Qing
Born: 1654, Beijing
Died: 1722, Beijing

The Kangxi Emperor (1654–1722) was the third emperor of the Qing dynasty and the second Qing emperor to rule over China proper. Ascending the throne at age seven, he reigned for 61 years — the longest reign of any Chinese emperor — and is regarded as one of China's greatest rulers, consolidating Qing power and presiding over an era of prosperity and territorial expansion.

Reign

Initially governed by regents, Kangxi took personal control in 1669 and arrested the powerful regent Oboi. He suppressed the Revolt of the Three Feudatories (1673–1681), conquered Taiwan from the Zheng regime in 1683, halted Russian expansion at the Amur River and settled the border by the Treaty of Nerchinsk (1689), and campaigned personally against the Dzungar leader Galdan, bringing Outer Mongolia under Qing control. Later campaigns extended Qing influence into Tibet.

Government and culture

Kangxi cultivated Confucian learning to win over Han Chinese scholars, sponsoring the Kangxi Dictionary, a vast encyclopedia, and the compilation of the history of the Ming dynasty. He employed Jesuit missionaries such as Ferdinand Verbiest as astronomers, cartographers, and interpreters, and for much of his reign tolerated Christianity before the Chinese Rites controversy soured relations with Rome. His late reign was troubled by a prolonged succession struggle among his many sons; he was succeeded by the Yongzheng Emperor.
