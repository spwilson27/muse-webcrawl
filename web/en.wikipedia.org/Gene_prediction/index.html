Gene prediction

In computational biology, gene prediction or gene finding refers to the process of identifying the regions of genomic DNA that encode genes. This includes protein-coding genes as well as RNA genes, but often also includes prediction of other functional elements such as regulatory regions. Gene finding is one of the first and most important steps in understanding the genome of a species once it has been sequenced.

Gene prediction
FieldComputational biology, bioinformatics
InputsGenomic DNA sequence
Notable toolsGENSCAN, Augustus, GeneMark, Glimmer

Approaches

Gene prediction methods fall broadly into two categories. Ab initio methods rely on intrinsic signals in the sequence itself — such as open reading frames, start and stop codons, splice site consensus sequences, and statistical properties of coding regions — often modelled with hidden Markov models. Evidence-based methods align expressed sequence tags, transcripts, or protein sequences from related organisms to the genome in order to infer gene structures.

Prokaryotic gene finding

Prokaryotic genomes are comparatively simple: they are gene-dense and lack introns, so genes can often be identified as long open reading frames. Tools such as Glimmer and GeneMark exploit codon usage statistics to distinguish coding from non-coding sequence with high accuracy.

Eukaryotic gene finding

In eukaryotes, genes are split into exons and introns, may be alternatively spliced, and are separated by large stretches of non-coding DNA, making prediction considerably harder. Modern pipelines combine ab initio predictions with RNA sequencing evidence and homology information, and their output is integrated into genome annotation databases such as Ensembl and RefSeq.

Evaluation

The accuracy of gene finders is measured at the nucleotide, exon, and gene levels using sensitivity and specificity against curated reference annotations. Community assessments showed steady improvement, but prediction of alternative splice forms and non-coding RNA genes remains challenging.
