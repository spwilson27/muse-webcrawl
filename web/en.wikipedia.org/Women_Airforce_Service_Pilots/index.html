Women Airforce Service Pilots

The Women Airforce Service Pilots (WASP) was a civilian women's pilot organisation of the United States during the Second World War. Merged in August 1943 from earlier women's ferrying programmes, it employed about 1,074 women who ferried aircraft, towed targets, and tested planes, freeing male pilots for combat duty.

Active1943–1944
CountryUnited States
RoleAircraft ferrying, target towing, testing

History

Pioneering aviators Jacqueline Cochran and Nancy Harkness Love had each proposed using women pilots in non-combat roles. Their programmes were united as the WASP in 1943, training at Avenger Field in Sweetwater, Texas. WASPs flew more than 60 million miles in every type of U.S. military aircraft; 38 women died in service.

Recognition

Although they served as civilians, WASPs were granted veteran status in 1977, and in 2009 the surviving members received the Congressional Gold Medal.
