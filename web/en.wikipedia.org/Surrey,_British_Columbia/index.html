Surrey, British Columbia

Surrey is a city in the province of British Columbia, Canada, located south of the Fraser River within the Metro Vancouver regional district. It is one of the fastest-growing and most culturally diverse cities in Canada, and is projected to surpass Vancouver as the province's most populous city.

History

The area is the traditional territory of Coast Salish peoples, including the Semiahmoo, Kwantlen and Katzie First Nations. European settlement began in the 19th century; the municipality was incorporated in 1879 and named after Surrey, England. For much of its history Surrey was a farming and suburban community, linked to New Westminster and Vancouver by bridges over the Fraser River, beginning with the Pattullo Bridge in 1937.

Geography and neighbourhoods

Surrey is organised around six town centres: City Centre (in Whalley, the emerging downtown served by SkyTrain), Fleetwood, Guildford, Newton, Cloverdale and South Surrey. The city combines dense urban development with one of the largest municipal farmland bases in the region, protected in part by British Columbia's Agricultural Land Reserve, and a large system of parks.

Demographics and economy

Surrey has a large South Asian community — by 2021 roughly a third of residents were of South Asian origin, with one of the largest Punjabi-speaking populations outside India. The economy centres on health care (Surrey Memorial Hospital), education (Simon Fraser University's Surrey campus and Kwantlen Polytechnic University), manufacturing, logistics, and film and technology, while rapid transit expansion along the Fraser Highway remains a major civic project.
