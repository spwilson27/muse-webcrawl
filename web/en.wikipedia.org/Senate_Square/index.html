Senate Square

Senate Square (Russian: Сенатская площадь, Senatskaya ploshchad) is a square in Saint Petersburg, Russia. It is located on the left bank of the Bolshaya Neva, in front of Saint Isaac's Cathedral. It is famous as the site of the Decembrist revolt of 1825 and for the equestrian statue of Peter the Great known as the Bronze Horseman.

History

The square was laid out in the early 18th century as the city was founded and took its name from the Governing Senate, which was housed in the building on its western side (now home to the Constitutional Court of Russia). In 1925, to mark the centenary of the Decembrist uprising, it was renamed Decembrists' Square; its original name was restored in 2008.

The Bronze Horseman

The square's dominant feature is the Bronze Horseman, an equestrian statue of Peter the Great commissioned by Catherine the Great and sculpted by Étienne Maurice Falconet. Unveiled in 1782, it stands on an enormous single block of granite, the "Thunder Stone", which was transported to the site in the 1770s. The statue was immortalized by Alexander Pushkin's 1833 poem The Bronze Horseman.

Decembrist revolt

On 14 December 1825 (Old Style), some 3,000 soldiers assembled on the square in protest against the accession of Nicholas I, demanding a constitutional government. The revolt was suppressed by loyal troops and artillery, and became a defining event of 19th-century Russian history.
