Middle Ages

A medieval castle and its court, from a manuscript illumination.

In the history of Europe, the Middle Ages or medieval period lasted approximately from the late 5th to the late 15th centuries, similar to the post-classical period of global history. It began with the fall of the Western Roman Empire in 476 AD and transitioned into the Renaissance and the Age of Discovery.

The Middle Ages is the middle period of the three traditional divisions of Western history: classical antiquity, the medieval period, and the modern period. The medieval period is itself subdivided into the Early, High, and Late Middle Ages.

Early Middle Ages

Population decline, de-urbanisation, and barbarian invasions that had begun in Late Antiquity continued. Germanic peoples formed new kingdoms in the former western empire, while the Eastern Roman (Byzantine) Empire survived. In the 7th century, the expansion of Islam transformed the Middle East and North Africa. In Western Europe, monasticism, the Carolingian Empire of Charlemagne (crowned emperor in 800), and the spread of Christianity reshaped society.

High Middle Ages

Beginning around 1000, population growth, agricultural innovation (such as the heavy plough and three-field system), and a warming climate supported the rise of towns, trade, and cathedral schools and universities. Feudalism and manorialism organised much of society, though their definitions are debated. The Crusades (from 1095) drew Latin Christendom into the eastern Mediterranean; Gothic architecture, scholastic philosophy, and vernacular literatures flourished.

Late Middle Ages

The 14th century brought crisis: the Great Famine (1315–1317), the Black Death (1347–1351), which killed a third or more of Europe's population, and the Hundred Years' War between England and France. Peasant revolts, the Western Schism of the papacy, and the fall of Constantinople to the Ottoman Turks in 1453 punctuated the era. By about 1500, printing, new monarchies, and Atlantic exploration opened the early modern period.

Terminology

The term "Middle Ages" (Latin: medium aevum) was coined by Renaissance humanists who saw the period as a middle age between antiquity and their own rebirth of learning; the popular image of a uniformly "dark age" is largely rejected by modern historians, who emphasise the era's cultural and institutional creativity.
