Madison Square Garden (1890)

The second Madison Square Garden was an indoor arena and entertainment complex in New York City, standing at Madison Square — at East 26th Street and Madison Avenue — from 1890 to 1925. Designed by the celebrated architect Stanford White of McKim, Mead & White, it replaced the first Garden, a converted railway depot operated by P. T. Barnum and later William Vanderbilt.

Architecture

The building was styled in a Moorish-influenced Beaux-Arts design, with a grand arena seating about 8,000, a theatre, a concert hall, a restaurant, and a roof garden. Its most famous feature was a 32-storey tower modelled on the Giralda of Seville, topped by a revolving statue of the goddess Diana by Augustus Saint-Gaudens — the first version of which proved too heavy and was replaced by a lighter, now-celebrated figure.

Events and notoriety

The Garden hosted circuses, horse shows, boxing, six-day bicycle races, balls, and exhibitions. It is also remembered as the scene of scandal: in 1906 the architect Stanford White was shot and killed in its roof-garden theatre by Harry Kendall Thaw, leading to the sensational "trial of the century".

Demolition

Despite its fame, the building was never very profitable. It was demolished in 1925 to make way for the New York Life Building, and a third Garden opened the same year at Eighth Avenue and 50th Street.
