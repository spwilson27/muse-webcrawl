"Ka Mate" is a Māori haka composed by Te Rauparaha, war leader of the Ngāti Toa tribe, around 1820. It is by far the best-known haka worldwide because it has been performed by the New Zealand national rugby union team, the All Blacks, before international matches since the early twentieth century.

Composition

According to tradition, Te Rauparaha composed the words while in hiding from enemies of the Ngāti Maniapoto and Waikato tribes. Concealed in a kūmara (sweet potato) storage pit beneath a woman, he heard his pursuers search above him; when the last searcher departed, he emerged into the sunlight and composed the haka celebrating his escape: "Ka mate, ka mate! Ka ora, ka ora!" — "It is death, it is death! It is life, it is life!" The final lines greet the sun shining again upon him.

Use in rugby

The haka entered rugby through the 1888–89 New Zealand Native football team tour and the 1905 "Originals" tour of Britain, after which it became a fixture of All Blacks matches. Since 2005 the All Blacks have alternated "Ka Mate" with a second haka, "Kapa o Pango", composed for the team. Visiting crowds and opposing teams have developed their own responses to the challenge the haka presents.

Ownership and protection

Ngāti Toa's authorship claim was formally acknowledged in the tribe's Treaty of Waitangi settlement with the New Zealand Crown, legislated in 2014, which recognises the significance of "Ka Mate" to the iwi and requires attribution for certain public uses, though it confers no royalty rights and does not restrict performance.
