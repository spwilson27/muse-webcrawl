Wudu

Wudu (Arabic: wuḍūʾ) is the Islamic ritual washing of parts of the body with water, performed by Muslims before prayer (salah) and certain other acts of worship.

Procedure

The washing typically includes the hands, mouth, nostrils, face, forearms to the elbows, wiping the head, and washing the feet to the ankles, usually three times each, in a prescribed order. Its Qur'anic basis is verse 5:6. Scholars detail obligatory and recommended elements, which vary somewhat among legal schools.

Related practices

Wudu is invalidated by sleep, excretion and other specified events. The fuller ritual bath is ghusl; where water is unavailable, dry ablution (tayammum) with clean earth is permitted. Mosques commonly provide ablution fountains or rooms.
