Paul Schrader


Paul Schrader (born 1946)

Paul Joseph Schrader (born July 22, 1946) is an American screenwriter, film director, and film critic. A central figure of the New Hollywood era, he wrote or co-wrote Martin Scorsese's Taxi Driver (1976) and Raging Bull (1980), and directed films including Blue Collar (1978), American Gigolo (1980), Mishima: A Life in Four Chapters (1985), Affliction (1997), and First Reformed (2017), the last of which earned him his first Academy Award nomination, for Best Original Screenplay.

Early life and criticism

Schrader was born in Grand Rapids, Michigan, into a strict Christian Reformed family and was famously forbidden to watch films until the age of seventeen. He studied theology at Calvin College and film at UCLA, and became a film critic in Los Angeles. His 1972 book Transcendental Style in Film: Ozu, Bresson, Dreyer remains an influential work of film theory, examining spiritual austerity in cinema.

Screenwriting and directing

His breakthrough came with Taxi Driver (1976), written during a period of illness and depression and directed by Scorsese. He went on to write Obsession (1976), Rolling Thunder (1977), Raging Bull (1980, with Mardik Martin), and The Last Temptation of Christ (1988). As a director he explored guilt, masculinity, and redemption in American Gigolo, Cat People (1982), Light Sleeper (1992), Affliction, Auto Focus (2002), and First Reformed, whose story of a tortured pastor revived his critical standing. Later works include The Card Counter (2021), Master Gardener (2022), and Oh, Canada (2024).

Schrader's films frequently centre on a solitary man keeping a journal and moving toward a violent or redemptive climax — a figure he has called the "man in a room" — drawing on his Calvinist upbringing and the films of Robert Bresson.
