Brittany

Brittany (French: Bretagne; Breton: Breizh) is a peninsula and cultural region in the northwest of France, covering the western part of what was known as Armorica in Roman Gaul. It is divided administratively between the modern region of Brittany — comprising the departments of Finistère, Morbihan, Côtes-d'Armor, and Ille-et-Vilaine, with its capital at Rennes — and the department of Loire-Atlantique, historically Breton, whose capital Nantes was the seat of the Dukes of Brittany.

History

The peninsula is famed for its Neolithic megaliths, including the alignments at Carnac. After Roman and Frankish rule, it was settled from the fourth to sixth centuries by Brittonic-speaking migrants from Britain — fleeing Anglo-Saxon expansion — who gave the region its name, its Breton language, and its patron saints. Brittany became a duchy that long resisted absorption by France, until the marriage of Duchess Anne of Brittany to two successive French kings and the Edict of Union of 1532 bound it to the French crown.

Culture and economy

Brittany retains a strong Celtic identity expressed in the Breton language (now endangered but revived through bilingual schooling), Gallo in the east, fest-noz dance gatherings recognised by UNESCO, crêpes and galettes, cider, and the striped Breton shirt. Its economy combines agriculture and food processing, fishing around ports such as Lorient and Concarneau, shipbuilding at Saint-Nazaire, and tourism along a dramatic coastline that includes Mont Saint-Michel bay, the Pink Granite Coast, and the Gulf of Morbihan.
