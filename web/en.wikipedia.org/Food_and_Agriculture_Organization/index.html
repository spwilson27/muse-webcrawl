Food and Agriculture Organization

Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations
Abbreviation: FAO
Formation: 16 October 1945, Quebec City, Canada
Headquarters: Rome, Italy
Director-General: Qu Dongyu (since 2019)
Parent organization: United Nations

The Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO) is a specialized agency of the United Nations that leads international efforts to defeat hunger and improve nutrition and food security. Its Latin motto, fiat panis, translates to "let there be bread." The FAO was founded on 16 October 1945.

History

The FAO was established at a conference in Quebec City in 1945 and moved to its permanent headquarters in Rome (the Palazzo FAO, former Department of Italian East Africa building) in 1951.

Activities

The FAO provides data and statistics (FAOSTAT), technical assistance, policy advice, standards (including the Codex Alimentarius jointly with the WHO), and emergency food assistance. It monitors food security worldwide and publishes The State of Food Security and Nutrition in the World.

Structure

The FAO Conference of 194 member states meets biennially; a 49-member Council oversees activities between sessions, and the Director-General serves a four-year term, renewable once.
