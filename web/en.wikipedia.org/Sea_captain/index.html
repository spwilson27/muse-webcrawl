Sea captain

A sea captain, or master, is the licensed officer in command of a ship. The captain holds ultimate responsibility for the vessel, its crew, cargo and passengers, and for compliance with maritime law, safety and navigation. At sea the captain's authority is near-absolute in matters of safety, a tradition reflected in the legal concept that the master is responsible for the ship as a whole.

Duties and qualifications

The captain supervises navigation, cargo operations, crew discipline, communications and emergency response, and is the shipowner's legal representative in foreign ports. Merchant captains must hold a master mariner's certificate of competency, issued after years of service as a deck officer and examinations in navigation, seamanship, cargo handling and maritime law, under international standards such as the STCW Convention.

In culture

Sea captains figure prominently in maritime literature and legend, from Captain Ahab in Moby-Dick to the historical masters of the age of sail and exploration. The phrase "the captain goes down with the ship" expresses the traditional expectation that the master is the last to leave a sinking vessel.
