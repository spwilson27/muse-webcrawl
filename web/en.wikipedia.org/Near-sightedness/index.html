Near-sightedness

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Near-sightedness, also known as myopia and short-sightedness, is an eye condition where light from distant objects focuses in front of, instead of on, the retina, while near objects appear clear. Distant objects appear blurry and near objects appear normal. Other symptoms may include headaches and eye strain. Severe near-sightedness is associated with an increased risk of retinal detachment, cataracts, and glaucoma.

Causes

The underlying cause is believed to be a combination of genetic and environmental factors. The eyeball grows too long, or less commonly the lens or cornea is too curved. Risk is higher among those who do significant near work, spend little time outdoors, or have affected parents. The condition has risen sharply in prevalence in recent decades, reaching very high levels in parts of East and Southeast Asia; it affects roughly a third of the world's population, projected to reach half by 2050.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is by an eye exam, and correction is with eyeglasses, contact lenses, or refractive surgery such as LASIK. Glasses are the simplest and safest method of correction. Low-dose atropine drops, multifocal lenses, and increased outdoor time can slow progression in children. High myopia carries risks of degenerative changes to the retina requiring ongoing monitoring.
