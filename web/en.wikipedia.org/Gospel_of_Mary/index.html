Gospel of Mary

The Gospel of Mary is an early Christian text discovered in 1896 in a fifth-century papyrus codex written in Sahidic Coptic, the Berlin Codex (Papyrus Berolinensis 8502). The text is not included in the New Testament canon and is generally dated to the second century AD. It takes its name from its principal figure, Mary, usually identified by scholars with Mary Magdalene.

Contents

The surviving text is fragmentary: the first six pages and several middle pages are missing. In the extant portion, the risen Saviour answers the disciples' questions about sin, matter, and the nature of the world, then departs. The disciples grieve, and Mary comforts them, recounting teachings the Saviour gave her privately in a vision, including an account of the soul's ascent past the powers that seek to detain it.

The apostles Andrew and Peter question whether the Saviour would have taught such things to a woman, and Levi (Matthew) defends Mary, rebuking Peter — an exchange frequently read as reflecting early Christian debates over women's authority and leadership.

Discovery and scholarship

The Berlin Codex was purchased in Cairo in 1896 by Carl Reinhardt but was not published until 1955. Two additional Greek fragments, Papyrus Oxyrhynchus 3525 and Papyrus Rylands 463, were published in the 20th century. The Gospel of Mary is often associated with Gnostic Christianity, though the extent of its Gnostic character is debated among scholars.
