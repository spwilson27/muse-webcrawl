Gaussian copula

Density of a Gaussian copula with correlation parameter.

In probability theory and statistics, a copula is a multivariate cumulative distribution function for which the marginal probability distribution of each variable is uniform on the interval [0, 1]. The Gaussian copula is a copula derived from a multivariate normal distribution: it is constructed by applying the probability integral transform to a multivariate Gaussian distribution, so that the dependence structure is governed entirely by the correlation matrix of the underlying normal distribution.

Definition

Given a correlation matrix R, the Gaussian copula is defined by applying the inverse cumulative distribution function of the standard normal distribution to each coordinate of a multivariate normal random vector with correlation matrix R, and then evaluating the joint cumulative distribution function. The Gaussian copula is an imputation method and is sometimes referred to as the normal copula.

Use in finance

The Gaussian copula became widely used in quantitative finance to model the joint default risk of portfolios of assets, in particular in the pricing of collateralized debt obligations (CDOs). A formula popularized by David X. Li in 2000 allowed traders to correlate the default risks of many securities using a single correlation parameter.

The model was heavily criticized after the 2007–2008 financial crisis: critics argued that the Gaussian copula underestimated the risk of extreme joint events because it has no tail dependence for correlation values below one, and that its widespread use contributed to the mispricing of mortgage-backed securities. The copula nevertheless remains a standard tool in credit risk modelling and is still used with more careful calibration.
