Chief executive officer

A chief executive officer (CEO) is the highest-ranking executive in a company or organization, responsible for overall management, major corporate decisions, and implementing the policies set by the board of directors. The CEO is the public face of the firm and typically reports to the board, whose members shareholders elect.

Responsibilities

The CEO sets strategy, allocates capital and other resources, builds the senior management team, and is ultimately accountable for performance. In large public companies the role is distinct from that of the chair of the board, though the two positions are sometimes combined, a practice common in the United States and criticized by some governance advocates.

Terminology

The equivalent title in the United Kingdom and some Commonwealth countries was traditionally "managing director," though "CEO" is now widespread. In nonprofit organizations the analogous role is often called "executive director." In government and the military, "chief executive" can also refer to the head of government.

Compensation

CEO pay — salary, bonuses, and equity awards — has grown far faster than typical worker wages in many countries since the 1970s, making executive compensation a subject of persistent public and academic debate.
