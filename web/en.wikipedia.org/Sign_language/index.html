Sign language

Sign languages (also known as signed languages) are languages that use the visual-manual modality to convey meaning, instead of spoken words. Sign languages are expressed through manual articulation in combination with non-manual elements such as facial expressions, body posture, and mouth patterns. They are full natural languages with their own grammar and lexicon, unrelated in structure to the spoken languages of the surrounding hearing communities.

There are estimated to be between 138 and 300 different sign languages in use around the world today, including American Sign Language (ASL), British Sign Language (BSL), and French Sign Language (LSF), from which ASL descends. Sign language is the predominant language of many deaf communities.

Linguistic structure

Linguistic research since the work of William Stokoe in the 1960s has established that signs are built from a finite set of phonological parameters—handshape, orientation, location, movement, and non-manual features—analogous to the phonemes of spoken language. Sign languages express grammatical relations through use of signing space, verb agreement, and classifier constructions.

History

Signed communication has existed as long as deaf people have gathered in communities. Systematic deaf education using sign began in 18th-century Paris under the Abbé Charles-Michel de l'Épée, and the method spread to the United States through Thomas Hopkins Gallaudet and Laurent Clerc in 1817. The 1880 Milan Conference favoured oralist education and drove signing out of many classrooms, but sign languages persisted in deaf communities and were formally recognized by linguists from the 1960s onward.

Legal status and use

Many countries now legally recognize their national sign languages, and interpretation is provided in courts, legislatures, broadcasting, and education. Sign languages are also used by some hearing people, including interpreters, the children of deaf adults (CODAs), and communities in which high rates of hereditary deafness led to village sign languages.
