Sustainable forest management

Sustainable forest management (SFM) is the management of forests according to the principles of sustainable development: maintaining and enhancing the long-term health of forest ecosystems while providing environmental, economic, social and cultural benefits for present and future generations. The concept was internationally consolidated after the 1992 Rio Earth Summit and the non-binding Forest Principles adopted there.

Criteria and indicators

Regional processes such as the Montréal Process and FOREST EUROPE have developed sets of criteria and indicators to measure SFM, covering conservation of biological diversity, maintenance of productive capacity, ecosystem health and vitality, conservation of soil and water resources, forests' contribution to global carbon cycles, socio-economic benefits, and the legal and institutional framework. The Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) reports on these in its periodic Global Forest Resources Assessment.

Certification

Third-party forest certification schemes — principally the Forest Stewardship Council (FSC, founded 1993) and the Programme for the Endorsement of Forest Certification (PEFC, 1999) — audit management against SFM standards and licence chain-of-custody labels on timber and paper products. By the 2020s, several hundred million hectares of forest worldwide were certified under one scheme or the other, though coverage remains concentrated in temperate and boreal regions.

Practices

SFM practices include setting harvest levels at or below the rate of growth, reduced-impact logging, protection of high-conservation-value areas and riparian buffers, replanting and natural regeneration, fire management, and recognition of the rights and participation of indigenous peoples and local communities.
