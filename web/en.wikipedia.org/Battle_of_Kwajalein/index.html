Battle of Kwajalein

The Battle of Kwajalein was fought from 31 January to 3 February 1944 in Kwajalein Atoll, in the Marshall Islands, during the Pacific War of the Second World War. United States forces of the Fifth Fleet under Admiral Raymond Spruance landed simultaneously on Kwajalein island at the southern end of the atoll and on the Roi-Namur island pair at its northern end, opening the campaign to break Japan's outer defence perimeter in the central Pacific.

Background

Kwajalein is one of the world's largest coral atolls and anchored Japan's pre-war mandates in the Marshalls. Following the costly lessons of Tarawa in November 1943, the Americans brought overwhelming naval and aerial bombardment to Kwajalein: the pre-invasion shelling, some 36,000 shells from warships alone, was among the heaviest of the war. The 4th Marine Division assaulted Roi-Namur, while the Army's 7th Infantry Division attacked Kwajalein island.

The battle

Landings began on 31 January 1944 on small offshore islets to establish artillery positions, followed by the main assaults on 1 February. The Marines took Roi-Namur in two days of fighting against roughly 3,500 defenders. The 7th Division advanced more methodically across Kwajalein island, reducing Japanese strongpoints with tanks, flamethrowers and demolition charges; the island was declared secure on 4 February. Japanese losses were severe: of some 8,700 defenders across the atoll, nearly all were killed. American deaths numbered about 370, a markedly better exchange ratio than at Tarawa.

Aftermath

The rapid fall of Kwajalein demonstrated the effectiveness of improved American amphibious doctrine—massive preliminary bombardment, reef-crossing amphibian tractors, and underwater demolition teams—and allowed Admiral Chester Nimitz to accelerate the advance across the Pacific, moving on to Eniwetok later in February and bypassing many Japanese-held islands. Kwajalein has since served as a major US missile test range.
