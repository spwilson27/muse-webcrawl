Marjoram

Marjoram (Origanum majorana) is a cold-sensitive perennial herb in the mint family Lamiaceae, grown for its aromatic leaves, which are used fresh or dried as a culinary seasoning. Native to the Mediterranean region and western Asia, it is also known as sweet marjoram to distinguish it from oregano (Origanum vulgare), sometimes called wild marjoram.

Description and cultivation

The plant grows to about 30–60 cm tall, with square stems, small oval grey-green leaves, and clusters of tiny pink or white flowers. In cooler climates it is grown as an annual; it prefers full sun and well-drained soil. Leaves are most flavourful just before flowering.

Culinary use

Marjoram has a sweeter, milder, and more citrus-and-pine flavour than oregano. It is a component of French herbes de Provence and Middle Eastern za'atar, and is widely used in German cooking to season sausages (hence its German name Wurstkraut, "sausage herb"), as well as in soups, dressings, and meat dishes across Mediterranean cuisine.
