Katharine Meyer Graham (16 June 1917 – 17 July 2001) was an American newspaper publisher who led The Washington Post through its most celebrated era, including the publication of the Pentagon Papers and the Watergate investigation that brought down President Richard Nixon.

Life and career

Graham was the daughter of Eugene Meyer, who bought the struggling Post in 1933. She worked as a reporter before marrying Philip Graham, who became the paper's publisher; after his death by suicide in 1963 she unexpectedly took control of the Washington Post Company herself. Initially diffident in a male-dominated industry, she grew into a formidable publisher: she took the company public in 1971, backed editor Ben Bradlee, and made the momentous decision to publish the Pentagon Papers despite legal and financial risk, followed by steadfast support for reporters Bob Woodward and Carl Bernstein during Watergate.

Legacy

Her memoir Personal History (1997) won the Pulitzer Prize for Biography. Graham's Georgetown salon made her a central figure in Washington political society, and her stewardship transformed the Post into one of the world's great newspapers. She died in 2001 after a fall during a conference in Idaho; her son Donald had succeeded her as publisher in 1979 and as chairman in 1991.
