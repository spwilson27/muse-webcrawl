Central New York

Central New York is the central region of the U.S. state of New York, roughly encompassing the area around the city of Syracuse. Definitions vary, but the region is commonly said to include Cayuga, Cortland, Madison, Oneida, Onondaga, and Oswego counties, and sometimes Tompkins County and parts of the Finger Lakes.

Geography and economy

The region lies between the Finger Lakes to the west, the Tug Hill Plateau and Adirondacks to the north and east, and the hills of the Southern Tier to the south. The Erie Canal and, later, the New York State Thruway (Interstate 90) made it a transport corridor. Syracuse, home to Syracuse University, is the economic and cultural centre; other cities include Utica, Rome, Auburn, Cortland, and Oswego on Lake Ontario.

History

Central New York was the homeland of the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) Confederacy, whose Oneida, Onondaga, and Cayuga nations remain present in the region. After the American Revolution, the area was surveyed as the Military Tract and settled rapidly, growing with salt production around Onondaga Lake and manufacturing along the canal.
