Badlands is a 1973 American neo-noir period crime drama film written, produced, and directed by Terrence Malick, in his directorial debut. The film stars Martin Sheen and Sissy Spacek, and follows Holly Sargis (Spacek), a 15-year-old girl from a dead-end South Dakota town, who narrates her first love with the older Kit Carringer (Sheen), a troubled, James Dean–like garbage collector, and the series of murders they commit across the Montana badlands after Kit kills Holly's father.

The film is loosely based on the real-life murder spree of Charles Starkweather and his girlfriend Caril Ann Fugate in 1958. Badlands is often cited as one of the greatest and most influential films of all time; in 1993 it was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry by the Library of Congress as being "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant." Spacek's deadpan voice-over narration and Sheen's performance are hallmarks of the film.

Plot

In 1959 Fort Dupree, South Dakota, 15-year-old Holly Sargis meets 25-year-old Kit Carringer, a garbage collector who resembles James Dean. They fall in love despite her father's objections. After her father shoots her dog as punishment for seeing Kit, Kit murders him, and the pair flee after burning down her house to make it look like a suicide pact. They live for a time in a treehouse by a river, but after bounty hunters and police close in, they embark on a killing spree across the Montana badlands, killing several people. Eventually, Kit surrenders theatrically at a roadblock, carving a stone marker to record the spot of his capture, and Holly narrates that he was later executed while she married the son of her defense attorney.

Production

Malick, a former philosophy graduate student and journalist, raised much of the budget himself and shot the film independently in Colorado standing in for South Dakota and Montana. The production was famously turbulent, with multiple crew members quitting and three cinematographers ultimately credited. The film makes striking use of natural light and wide landscapes, a style that would become Malick's signature.

Music

The soundtrack prominently features the piece "Gassenhauer" from Carl Orff's Schulwerk, as well as "Theme from A Summer Place" and Mickey and Sylvia's "Love Is Strange." The Orff xylophone motif became indelibly associated with the film and was later echoed in Hans Zimmer's score for True Romance, whose script by Quentin Tarantino was a deliberate homage to Badlands.

Reception and legacy

Premiering at the New York Film Festival, the film was critically acclaimed, with Sheen in particular praised. It holds an enduring place in American cinema; filmmakers from Tarantino to Oliver Stone have cited it as an influence, and the "lovers on the run" genre it helped define includes Bonnie and Clyde, Wild at Heart, Natural Born Killers, and True Romance.
