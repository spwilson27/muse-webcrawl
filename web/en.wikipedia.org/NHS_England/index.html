NHS England is an executive non-departmental public body of the Department of Health and Social Care. It oversees the budget, planning, delivery and day-to-day operation of the commissioning side of the National Health Service in England, as set out in the Health and Social Care Act 2012. It was formally established on 1 October 2012 as the NHS Commissioning Board and took up its full statutory functions on 1 April 2013, adopting the name NHS England.

Role

NHS England commissions primary care services such as GPs, pharmacists and dentists, as well as some specialised hospital services. Since 2022, most hospital, community and mental-health services have been planned and funded locally by 42 Integrated Care Boards (ICBs), established under the Health and Care Act 2022, which operate within NHS England's framework. NHS England also runs national programmes including vaccination, screening and the NHS Long Term Plan, and is responsible for the performance and oversight of NHS trusts and ICBs.

Scale

The NHS in England treats more than a million patients every 36 hours and employs around 1.5 million people, making it one of the largest employers in the world. NHS England's budget for 2023/24 was around £170 billion, the majority of the UK health budget; the devolved administrations run the separate NHS services of Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.

History

The 2012 reforms of health secretary Andrew Lansley created clinical commissioning groups and the NHS Commissioning Board to put commissioning at arm's length from ministers. Subsequent reforms, culminating in the Health and Care Act 2022, merged NHS England with NHS Improvement and emphasised integrated care systems intended to join up health and social care. In March 2025 the government announced that NHS England would be abolished and its functions brought back into the Department of Health and Social Care, with the transition to take place over about two years.
