Alexandra Asanovna Elbakyan (Russian: Александра Асановна Элбакян; born 6 November 1988) is a Kazakhstani computer programmer and creator of the website Sci-Hub, which provides free access to research papers without regard for copyright. According to a 2016 interview with Science, Elbakyan began the service out of frustration that the high costs of many research papers kept her from accessing them as a graduate student doing research in neuroscience.

Elbakyan has been dubbed a "spiritual successor" to Aaron Swartz. In 2016, she was named one of Nature's 10 people who mattered in science. Since 2011, when she launched Sci-Hub, a number of publishers have sued Elbakyan, leading to her going into hiding.

Biography

Elbakyan was born in Almaty, Kazakhstan, on 6 November 1988. She is of Armenian, Slavic, and Asian descent. In 2009, she received her Bachelor of Science degree in computer science from Satbayev University, with a specialization in information security. In 2010, she joined a neuroscience laboratory at the University of Freiburg, following which she was a summer intern at the Georgia Institute of Technology in the United States, and then began a master's degree at the Higher School of Economics in Moscow in 2012.

Sci-Hub

Elbakyan founded Sci-Hub in 2011 while she was a graduate student at the Higher School of Economics in Moscow. The site allows anyone to download academic papers free of charge, bypassing publishers' paywalls. The site has been highly controversial: publishers see it as copyright infringement on a massive scale, while many academics praise it for making research more accessible, especially to researchers in countries and institutions without subscription access.

In 2015, academic publisher Elsevier filed a legal complaint in New York against Sci-Hub for copyright infringement, leading to the loss of the original sci-hub.org domain. In 2017, the American Chemical Society filed a lawsuit against Sci-Hub and was awarded $4.8 million in damages. Elbakyan has remained defiant, stating that the site will continue to operate as long as it is technically possible.

Views

Elbakyan has described her work as aligned with the idea that knowledge should be freely accessible to everyone, and she identifies with communist principles regarding information sharing. She has argued that copyright law as applied to academic publishing obstructs the spread of knowledge and contradicts the spirit of science. She has also cited Article 27 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, which states that everyone has the right "to share in scientific advancement and its benefits."
