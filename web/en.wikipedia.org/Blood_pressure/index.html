Blood pressure

Blood pressure (BP) is the pressure of circulating blood against the walls of blood vessels. Most of this pressure results from the heart pumping blood through the circulatory system. When used without qualification, the term refers to the pressure in the large arteries, expressed as two measurements: systolic pressure, the maximum during one heartbeat, and diastolic pressure, the minimum between two heartbeats, written as a ratio such as 120/80 mmHg (millimetres of mercury).

Physiology

Arterial pressure is determined by cardiac output and peripheral resistance, and is regulated in the short term by baroreceptors and the autonomic nervous system, and in the long term by the kidneys through the renin–angiotensin system and fluid balance. Mean arterial pressure and pulse pressure (the difference between systolic and diastolic values) are also clinically significant.

Measurement

Blood pressure is most commonly measured non-invasively with a sphygmomanometer — a cuff around the upper arm — using either auscultation of Korotkoff sounds or automated oscillometric devices. Readings vary with posture, activity, stress and time of day, and are spuriously elevated in some patients by the "white coat effect"; ambulatory 24-hour and home monitoring give a fuller picture.

Disorders

Hypertension (high blood pressure) — conventionally at or above 140/90 mmHg, or 130/80 mmHg under the 2017 American guidelines — usually has no symptoms but greatly increases the risk of stroke, heart failure, coronary disease, kidney failure and dementia; it is managed with lifestyle changes and antihypertensive drugs. Hypotension (low blood pressure) may cause dizziness and fainting and, when severe, shock.
