Splintercat

The splintercat is a legendary fearsome critter of the United States, particularly associated with the forests of the Pacific Northwest and the Rocky Mountains. According to lumberjack folklore, it is a nocturnal feline animal of ferocious appearance and remarkable speed.

Description

The splintercat is said to fly or leap from tree to tree, using its hard head to smash into trunks and splinter them in search of the raccoons and bees it uses for food. Trees are left broken and shattered in its wake. The creature was described in Henry H. Tryon's 1939 book Fearsome Critters and in earlier tall tales told in logging camps. A stream in Oregon, Splintercat Creek, is named after the creature.

In folklore collections

The splintercat belongs to the genre of "fearsome critters" — humorous tall-tale animals invented by lumberjacks of the North American frontier, alongside creatures such as the hidebehind and the teakettler.
