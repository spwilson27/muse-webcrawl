Mount Vesuvius

Mount Vesuvius (Italian: Monte Vesuvio) is a somma–stratovolcano located on the Gulf of Naples in Campania, Italy, about 9 km east of Naples and a short distance from the shore. It is one of several volcanoes forming the Campanian volcanic arc. Vesuvius consists of a large cone partially encircled by the steep rim of a summit caldera, Monte Somma, resulting from the collapse of an earlier, taller structure.

The eruption of 79 AD

Vesuvius is best known for its eruption in 79 AD, one of the deadliest in European history, which buried the Roman cities of Pompeii and Herculaneum under ash, pumice, and pyroclastic surges. The eruption ejected a cloud of superheated gases and rock to a height of about 33 km; Pliny the Elder died at Stabiae during a rescue mission, and his nephew Pliny the Younger's letters give the first detailed description of a Plinian eruption — a term derived from his account.

Later activity and risk

Vesuvius has erupted many times since, notably in 1631 and 1944. It is the only volcano on Europe's mainland to have erupted within the last hundred years and is regarded as one of the world's most dangerous volcanoes because some three million people live in its vicinity; the Italian authorities maintain an evacuation plan for the "red zone". The Vesuvius Observatory, founded in 1841, monitors the volcano continuously.
