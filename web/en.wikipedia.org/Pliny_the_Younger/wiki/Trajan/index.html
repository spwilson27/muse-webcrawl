Trajan

Trajan (Marcus Ulpius Traianus; 18 September 53 – c. 11 August 117) was Roman emperor from 98 to 117 AD, the second of the Five Good Emperors of the Nerva–Antonine dynasty. He was a philanthropic ruler known for his building projects and military conquests. Born in Italica (near modern Seville in Spain), Trajan was the first Roman emperor born outside Italy. Under his rule the Roman Empire reached its greatest territorial extent.

Reign and conquests

Adopted by the elderly emperor Nerva in 97, Trajan succeeded peacefully on Nerva's death. His Dacian Wars (101–102 and 105–106) annexed Dacia (modern Romania) and funded a vast building programme in Rome, commemorated by Trajan's Column, whose spiral relief narrates the campaigns. In 113–117 he conquered Armenia and Mesopotamia from Parthia, pushing the empire to the Persian Gulf. In Rome he built Trajan's Forum and Markets and the great Baths of Trajan, and expanded the alimenta welfare scheme for poor children.

Correspondence with Pliny

Trajan's surviving correspondence with Pliny the Younger, governor of Bithynia (c. 110–113), is a key source for imperial administration — including the famous exchange on how to deal with Christians, in which Trajan instructed that they not be sought out and anonymous accusations be ignored. Dante placed the pagan Trajan in Paradise, reflecting the medieval legend of his justice; the Senate acclaimed him Optimus Princeps, "the best ruler". He died on campaign at Selinus in Cilicia and was succeeded by Hadrian, whom he may have adopted on his deathbed.
