Pliny the Elder

Gaius Plinius Secundus (AD 23/24 – 79), known as Pliny the Elder, was a Roman author, naturalist, natural philosopher, and naval and army commander of the early Roman Empire, and a friend of the emperor Vespasian. He wrote the encyclopaedic Naturalis Historia (Natural History), which became an editorial model for encyclopaedias. He spent most of his spare time studying, writing, and investigating natural and geographic phenomena in the field.

Life and works

Pliny served as a cavalry officer in Germania and later as procurator in several provinces before commanding the imperial fleet at Misenum. His nephew and adopted son, Pliny the Younger, described his uncle's prodigious routine of reading and dictation. Of his many works only the 37-book Natural History survives, covering astronomy, geography, zoology, botany, medicine, and mineralogy, and claiming to record 20,000 facts drawn from 2,000 volumes.

Death at Vesuvius

On 24 August 79 AD, while stationed at Misenum, Pliny launched a rescue fleet across the Bay of Naples during the eruption of Mount Vesuvius. Landing at Stabiae, he died the following day — probably overcome by fumes or an asthma attack — as recorded in his nephew's famous letters to Tacitus. The younger Pliny's account of the eruption column is the origin of the term "Plinian eruption".
