The Volkswagen Karmann Ghia is a sports car marketed by Volkswagen from 1955 to 1974, combining the chassis and running gear of the Beetle with elegant Italian styling by Carrozzeria Ghia and hand-shaped bodywork by the German coachbuilder Karmann.

Development

As West Germany's economy recovered in the early 1950s, Volkswagen sought a halo model to show the Beetle platform's potential. Karmann, which built Beetle convertibles, commissioned Ghia in Turin to design a coupé; Ghia's Luigi Segre adapted a design originally prepared for Chrysler. Volkswagen approved the car, and it debuted at the Paris Motor Show in October 1953 as a concept before entering production in 1955 as the Type 14.

Design

The Karmann Ghia's flowing lines, low nose, and pronounced rear wings contrasted sharply with the upright Beetle beneath. Because the body was welded and hand-finished rather than stamped, production was slow and the car cost noticeably more than a Beetle, yet it shared the Beetle's modest air-cooled flat-four engine, so performance was leisurely despite the sporting looks. A convertible followed in 1957. The larger, more angular Type 34 "Razor Edge" Ghia, based on the Type 3, was sold from 1961 to 1969 outside the United States.

Production and legacy

About 445,000 Karmann Ghias were built in Osnabrück, Germany, plus some 41,600 in Brazil, where a distinct TC variant also appeared. Production ended in 1974 as the car was replaced by the Volkswagen Scirocco. The model is now prized by collectors as an example of accessible, design-led motoring.
