Book value

Book value
DefinitionTotal assets minus total liabilities (shareholders' equity)
Per shareBook value ÷ shares outstanding
Related ratioPrice-to-book (P/B) ratio
Book value equals the equity shown on the balance sheet

In accounting, book value (also known as carrying value) is the value of an asset according to its balance sheet account balance. For assets, the value is based on the original cost of the asset less any depreciation, amortization or impairment costs made against the asset. For a company as a whole, book value is the total value of its assets minus its total liabilities, which is the same as its shareholders' equity.

Book value = Total assets − Total liabilities

Book value per share

Investors commonly use book value per share (BVPS): the total shareholders' equity (excluding preferred equity) divided by the number of common shares outstanding. Comparing a stock's market price to its book value per share gives the price-to-book (P/B) ratio; a ratio below 1 may suggest the shares are undervalued relative to the firm's recorded net assets, while a high ratio may reflect valuable intangible assets — such as brands or intellectual property — that accounting rules do not record on the balance sheet.

Uses and limitations

Book value is a central measure in value investing, popularised by Benjamin Graham, because it offers a conservative estimate of what a company would be worth if liquidated. Its limitations follow from historical-cost accounting: assets are recorded at purchase price rather than current market value, so book value can badly understate (or, for obsolete assets, overstate) economic worth. It is most informative for companies holding tangible, financial assets — such as banks and insurers — and least informative for service and technology companies whose value lies in intangibles.

Tangible book value

Tangible book value deducts intangible assets (goodwill, patents, trademarks) and preferred equity from book value, giving a stricter measure of the net value of a firm's physical and financial assets.
