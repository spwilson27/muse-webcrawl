Anxiety

Anxiety is an emotion which is characterised by an unpleasant state of inner turmoil and includes feelings of dread over anticipated events. It is often accompanied by nervous behavior such as pacing back and forth, somatic complaints, and rumination. Anxiety is a feeling of uneasiness and worry, usually generalised and unfocused as an overreaction to a situation that is only subjectively seen as menacing.

Symptoms

Physical symptoms can include increased heart rate, rapid breathing, muscle tension, sweating, trembling, and fatigue. Anxiety differs from fear in that fear is a response to a present, real threat, while anxiety anticipates future threats. Short-term anxiety is a normal, adaptive response to stress; persistent, excessive anxiety may indicate an anxiety disorder.

Anxiety disorders

Anxiety disorders include generalised anxiety disorder, panic disorder, social anxiety disorder, and specific phobias. They are among the most common mental disorders. Treatments include psychotherapy — notably cognitive behavioral therapy — and medications such as selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) and, for short-term use, benzodiazepines.

Causes

Anxiety arises from a combination of genetic predisposition, brain chemistry involving neurotransmitters such as serotonin and GABA, life stressors, and medical conditions or substances such as caffeine.
