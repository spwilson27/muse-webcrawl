Swayambhunath

Image placeholder: Swayambhunath stupa overlooking Kathmandu.

Swayambhunath (Devanagari: स्वयम्भू स्तूप) is an ancient religious complex atop a hill in the Kathmandu Valley, west of the city of Kathmandu, Nepal. The Tibetan name for the site means "sublime trees". For Buddhist Newars it is among the most sacred pilgrimage sites; for Hindus a temple of Hariti is also located on the hill. The complex is sometimes popularly known as the Monkey Temple, owing to the holy monkeys living in its northwest parts.

The stupa

The complex consists of a large stupa, a variety of shrines and temples, a Tibetan monastery, a museum and a library. The stupa has Buddha's eyes and eyebrows painted on each of its four sides, with the number one rendered in Devanagari script in place of a nose. According to the Swayambhu Purana, the valley was once filled with an enormous lake out of which grew a lotus bearing the self-existent (svayambhū) flame of Adi-Buddha.

History

Swayambhunath is among the oldest religious sites in Nepal; tradition dates its founding to the early 5th century CE, and an inscription found within the complex is dated to 460 CE. The site was damaged by the April 2015 Nepal earthquake but has since been substantially restored. It forms part of the Kathmandu Valley UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Access

The main approach is a stairway of 365 steps leading from the eastern side of the hill to the stupa platform.
