Parametric insurance

Parametric policies pay out when a measured parameter — such as wind speed — crosses a preset threshold.

Parametric insurance (also called index-based insurance) is a type of insurance that does not indemnify the actual loss incurred, but instead pays a predetermined amount when a specific event meeting or exceeding a defined parameter (or index) occurs — for example, an earthquake of a given magnitude, a hurricane of a given wind speed at a location, or rainfall below a set threshold. Because payouts are triggered by objective data rather than loss adjustment, claims can be settled within days.

How it works

A parametric policy specifies three things up front: the parameter and its data source (such as a national meteorological service or seismic agency), the trigger threshold, and the payout scale, which may increase with the severity of the measured parameter. Once the trigger is verified, payment follows automatically, without proof of loss. Premiums are priced from historical and modelled data on the parameter rather than from claims history.

Basis risk

The chief drawback is basis risk: the mismatch between the payout and the policyholder's actual loss. A farmer may suffer drought damage while recorded rainfall just misses the trigger, or receive a payout despite modest losses. Careful index design — dense weather-station networks, satellite data and modelled loss indices — reduces but cannot eliminate this gap.

Uses

Parametric covers are used for natural catastrophes (Caribbean and African sovereign risk pools such as CCRIF and African Risk Capacity pay governments quickly after hurricanes, earthquakes and droughts), agriculture and microinsurance for smallholder farmers, travel disruption, and increasingly for climate-related risks such as floods and wildfires that are hard to cover conventionally. Catastrophe bonds typically employ parametric or index triggers on the same principle.
