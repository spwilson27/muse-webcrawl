Schloss Drachenburg is a private palace-villa built in the historicist style on the Drachenfels hill in Königswinter, near Bonn, Germany. Constructed between 1882 and 1884 for the financier Baron Stephan von Sarter, it is one of the most prominent examples of nineteenth-century castle-style villa architecture in the Rhineland and a landmark of Rhine romanticism.

History

Stephan von Sarter, a Bonn-born stockbroker and banker who had made his fortune in Paris—partly through investments connected with the Suez Canal—commissioned the Düsseldorf architects Bernhard Tüshaus and Leo von Abbema to build him a grand residence overlooking the Rhine. The building rose remarkably fast using some of the era's most modern techniques and was lavishly decorated with stained glass, murals, and carved stone. Sarter, however, never lived in the castle; he remained in Paris, where he died in 1902.

In the twentieth century the property passed through many uses: a tourist attraction with a hotel and restaurant, a Catholic boarding school, an Adolf Hitler School under the Nazi regime, and, after the Second World War, a railway training school. By the 1970s it had fallen into serious decay and was threatened with demolition. It was saved through heritage protection and the efforts of the private owner Paul Spinat, and in 1986 it came under the care of the state of North Rhine-Westphalia and its NRW Foundation. Extensive restoration was completed in stages, and the castle reopened fully in 2010.

Today

Schloss Drachenburg is now a museum presenting its restored historicist interiors, alongside an exhibition on the history of nature conservation in Germany. It is reached from Königswinter by the Drachenfels Railway, Germany's oldest cog railway, and forms part of the popular hiking area of the Siebengebirge, below the ruined medieval Drachenfels Castle.
