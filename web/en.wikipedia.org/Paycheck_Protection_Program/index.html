Paycheck Protection Program


The program was administered by the U.S. Small Business Administration

The Paycheck Protection Program (PPP) was a United States federal loan program created by the CARES Act in March 2020 to help businesses keep their workforce employed during the COVID-19 pandemic. Administered by the Small Business Administration (SBA), it provided potentially forgivable loans to small businesses, nonprofits, self-employed individuals, and some larger businesses in hard-hit industries. More than $790 billion in loans were approved before the program closed on May 31, 2021.

How it worked

Loans were made through participating banks and lenders, were 100% guaranteed by the SBA, and were sized at up to 2.5 times an employer's average monthly payroll costs, capped at $10 million for a first loan. Borrowers who spent at least 60% of the proceeds on payroll, and maintained staffing and pay levels, could have the loan fully forgiven. A second round, funded under the Consolidated Appropriations Act of 2021, allowed qualifying hard-hit businesses to take a second, smaller "draw".

Criticism and fraud

The PPP was criticised for money reaching large and publicly traded companies in its chaotic first weeks, for chaotic rules on forgiveness, and for extensive fraud. Economists debated its cost-effectiveness, with estimates of the cost per job-year saved running into the hundreds of thousands of dollars, and federal prosecutors brought many cases against applicants who fabricated payrolls or spent funds on luxury goods. The program is generally credited, however, with supporting employment during the sharp 2020 downturn.
