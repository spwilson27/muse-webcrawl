Cruise ship

A cruise ship is a passenger ship used for pleasure voyages, where the voyage itself and the amenities of the ship are part of the experience, and transportation to a destination is not the prime purpose. Unlike ocean liners, which carried passengers from port to port on scheduled routes, cruise ships typically sail round-trip itineraries, calling at ports of interest and returning passengers to their port of embarkation.

Characteristics

Modern cruise ships are among the largest vessels afloat, resort-like floating hotels with multiple restaurants, theatres, pools, casinos and shops, carrying several thousand passengers and crew. The largest class, Royal Caribbean's Icon class, entered service in 2024 with a gross tonnage above 240,000. The biggest markets are the Caribbean and the Mediterranean, followed by Alaska and Northern Europe.

Industry and issues

The industry is dominated by three groups — Carnival Corporation, Royal Caribbean Group and Norwegian Cruise Line Holdings — and ships are usually registered in flag-of-convenience states such as Panama, the Bahamas and Liberia. Criticisms include air and water pollution from heavy fuel oil, the impact of mass tourism on ports such as Venice, and shipboard disease outbreaks; the COVID-19 pandemic suspended most cruising in 2020. Recent ships increasingly use liquefied natural gas and shore power to reduce emissions.
