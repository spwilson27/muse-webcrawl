Phototypesetting

Phototypesetting is a method of setting type that uses photography to produce columns of type on photographic paper or film. It replaced hot metal typesetting in much of the printing industry from the 1950s through the 1980s, before being displaced in turn by desktop publishing.

How it worked

Characters were stored as images on a spinning disc, glass matrix or film strip. A light source flashed through the selected character and a lens system, exposing photographic paper character by character; changing point size was as simple as changing the lens.

Impact

Phototypesetting made type cheaper and far more versatile than cast metal, enabling the explosion of typefaces and layout styles in postwar graphic design. Machines from companies such as Photon, Mergenthaler (Linofilm) and Compugraphic dominated the era.
