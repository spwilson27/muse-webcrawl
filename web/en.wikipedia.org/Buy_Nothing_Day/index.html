Buy Nothing Day

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia (recreated from memory)

Buy Nothing Day is an international day of protest against consumerism, observed annually by participants who refrain from making purchases for 24 hours. In North America it is held on the Friday after U.S. Thanksgiving — the day known as Black Friday, one of the busiest shopping days of the year — while in much of the rest of the world it falls on the following Saturday.

History

The day was founded in 1992 by Vancouver artist Ted Dave and was subsequently promoted by the Canada-based magazine Adbusters, founded by Kalle Lasn. Early attempts to buy television advertising for the campaign were refused by major networks, which itself became part of the campaign's publicity. The observance spread to dozens of countries during the 1990s and 2000s.

Activities

Participants mark the day by simply not shopping, and activists stage events such as "zombie walks" through shopping malls, credit-card cut-ups, and the formation of long, non-buying conga lines. Supporters argue that the day prompts reflection on overconsumption, advertising pressure, and the environmental costs of consumer culture; critics contend the gesture is symbolic and has little measurable effect on retail sales.
