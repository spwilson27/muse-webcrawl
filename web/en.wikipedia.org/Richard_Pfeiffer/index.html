Richard Pfeiffer

Richard Friedrich Johannes Pfeiffer (27 March 1858 – 15 September 1945) was a German physician and bacteriologist, a student of Robert Koch. He is known for discovering Haemophilus influenzae and for his work on bacterial endotoxins and immune bacteriolysis.

Career

Pfeiffer studied medicine in Berlin and served as a military physician before joining Koch's Institute for Hygiene. In 1892, during an influenza epidemic, he isolated a small bacterium from the sputum of patients, proposed it as the cause of influenza, and named it Bacillus influenzae; the organism was later renamed Haemophilus influenzae. It was shown in 1933 that influenza is caused by a virus, though Pfeiffer's bacillus is a significant secondary invader.

Discoveries

Working on cholera in 1894, Pfeiffer demonstrated that living vibrios injected into immunised guinea pigs were destroyed in the body — the "Pfeiffer phenomenon" of bacteriolysis — a foundational observation in immunology. He also identified bacterial endotoxins as heat-stable components of the cell wall responsible for toxic effects.

Later life

He held chairs at Königsberg and Breslau, where he directed the Institute of Hygiene. He died in Bad Landeck, Silesia, in 1945.
