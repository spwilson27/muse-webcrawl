Deinonychosauria is a clade of paravian theropod dinosaurs, living from the Middle Jurassic until the Late Cretaceous, which includes the dromaeosaurids ("raptors") and troodontids. They were the closest relatives of birds (Avialae) and shared with them a number of features, including feathers. Deinonychosaurs are characterised especially by an enlarged, sickle-shaped claw on the second toe of each foot, which was held retracted off the ground during locomotion.

Description

Deinonychosaurs ranged in size from the crow-sized Microraptor to the large dromaeosaurids Achillobator and Utahraptor, which reached several metres in length. They had relatively large brains among dinosaurs, binocular vision, and long arms; some, such as Microraptor, bore long flight feathers on both the arms and legs. Well-known genera include Deinonychus, Velociraptor and Troodon.

Classification

The group was named by Colbert and Russell in 1969. Most analyses unite Dromaeosauridae and Troodontidae as sister taxa within Deinonychosauria, which together with Avialae forms the clade Paraves. Some studies have suggested that certain deinonychosaurs, such as Anchiornis, may lie closer to birds, making the boundaries of the group a subject of ongoing debate.
