Mount Apo

Mount Apo is the highest mountain in the Philippines, rising to 2,954 metres (9,692 ft) above sea level on the island of Mindanao, between Davao City and the provinces of Davao del Sur and Cotabato. It is a potentially active stratovolcano and the centrepiece of Mount Apo Natural Park.

Geography and environment

The summit area features a crater lake, Lake Venado, and extensive boulder fields. The mountain's slopes hold mossy forests and are home to the critically endangered Philippine eagle and the waling-waling orchid. Geothermal energy is tapped on its northwestern flank. Apo is sacred to several Lumad peoples, including the Bagobo and Manobo, who regard it as ancestral land.

Climbing

First climbed in 1880 by an expedition led by Joaquin Rajal, Apo is a popular but regulated trek, typically taking two to three days via trails from Kidapawan or Kapatagan. The park was proclaimed a protected area in 1936.
