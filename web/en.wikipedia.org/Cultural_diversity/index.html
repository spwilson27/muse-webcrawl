Cultural diversity


Festivals of many cultures in one city
DefinitionThe coexistence of varied cultural groups and expressions within a society
Key instrumentUNESCO Universal Declaration on Cultural Diversity (2001)
ObservedWorld Day for Cultural Diversity, 21 May

Cultural diversity is the quality of diverse or different cultures, as opposed to monoculture, and the coexistence within a society of people with distinct languages, religions, traditions, arts and ways of life. It also names the policy goal of recognising, protecting and promoting that variety — in nations, cities, schools, workplaces and the global cultural marketplace.

International framework

UNESCO adopted the Universal Declaration on Cultural Diversity in 2001, calling cultural diversity "the common heritage of humanity" and as necessary for humankind as biodiversity is for nature, and followed it in 2005 with the Convention on the Protection and Promotion of the Diversity of Cultural Expressions, which affirms states' right to support their own cultural industries. The UN marks 21 May as the World Day for Cultural Diversity for Dialogue and Development.

Debates

Supporters link diversity to creativity, economic innovation, social cohesion and human rights, including the rights of indigenous peoples and minorities. Critics and commentators debate how diversity policies interact with national identity, integration and freedom of expression, and scholars distinguish descriptive diversity from programs — in education, hiring and media — that aim to increase representation. Globalisation is seen both as a threat that homogenises cultures and as a channel that spreads them.
