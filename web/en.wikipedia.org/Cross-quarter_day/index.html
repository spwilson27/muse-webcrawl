Cross-quarter day

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A cross-quarter day is a day falling approximately halfway between a solstice and an equinox. There are four such days in the year, and they mark the traditional beginnings of the seasons in the Gaelic calendar of Ireland and Scotland. The four cross-quarter days are Imbolc (around 1 February), Beltane (1 May), Lughnasadh (1 August), and Samhain (1 November).

Gaelic festivals

In Irish and Scottish tradition the year was divided into a dark half beginning with Samhain and a light half beginning with Beltane. The cross-quarter festivals were major communal occasions associated with bonfires, fairs, assemblies, and rituals concerned with livestock, crops, and the turning of the agricultural year. They are among the principal festivals revived and observed in modern Celtic neopaganism and Wicca, where together with the solstices and equinoxes they form the eightfold Wheel of the Year.

Related observances

Dates near the cross-quarter days coincide with other festivals in various cultures: Candlemas and Groundhog Day near Imbolc, May Day near Beltane, Lammas near Lughnasadh, and All Saints' Day and Halloween near Samhain. Astronomically, the exact midpoints between solstices and equinoxes fall a few days after the traditional 1st-of-the-month dates.
