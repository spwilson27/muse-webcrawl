Ayrton Senna da Silva (21 March 1960 – 1 May 1994) was a Brazilian racing driver who won the Formula One World Drivers' Championship three times, in 1988, 1990 and 1991, with McLaren. Widely regarded as one of the greatest drivers in the history of the sport, he was renowned for his extraordinary qualifying speed — he held the record for pole positions (65) for over a decade — and his mastery in wet weather, displayed in celebrated drives such as the 1984 Monaco Grand Prix and the 1993 European Grand Prix at Donington Park.

Career

Senna began in karting and won the British Formula Three Championship in 1983 before debuting in Formula One with Toleman in 1984. With Lotus (1985–87) he took his first wins. At McLaren from 1988, his fierce rivalry with teammate Alain Prost defined the era: the championship was decided by collisions between them at Suzuka in both 1989 and 1990. After Prost's retirement, Senna moved to Williams for 1994.

Death and legacy

Senna was killed on 1 May 1994 when his car crashed at the Tamburello corner while leading the San Marino Grand Prix at Imola — a weekend that also claimed the life of Roland Ratzenberger. His death, televised live, prompted a sweeping overhaul of Formula One safety that transformed car and circuit design; no driver died in a Formula One race for another twenty years. Brazil declared three days of national mourning, and an estimated three million people lined the streets of São Paulo for his funeral.

Senna remains a national hero in Brazil. The Instituto Ayrton Senna, founded by his sister Viviane, has supported the education of millions of Brazilian children. He is consistently ranked at or near the top of polls of the greatest Formula One drivers of all time.
