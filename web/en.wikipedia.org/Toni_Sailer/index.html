Anton Engelbert "Toni" Sailer (17 November 1935 – 24 August 2009) was an Austrian alpine ski racer, considered among the greatest of all time. At the 1956 Winter Olympics in Cortina d'Ampezzo he won all three alpine gold medals — downhill, slalom and giant slalom — by record margins, a sweep not matched until Jean-Claude Killy in 1968. Because the Olympics then doubled as World Championships, and with four further titles in 1958, Sailer amassed seven world championship golds (plus a silver) before retiring at just 22.

Career

Sailer grew up in Kitzbühel, the son of a ski instructor, and learned to ski almost as soon as he could walk. He shot to fame in the mid-1950s on the Lauberhorn and Hahnenkamm circuits. At Cortina in February 1956 he won the giant slalom by 6.2 seconds, the slalom by 4 seconds, and the downhill — skiing part of the course after a strap broke and borrowing a replacement ski — by 3.5 seconds, margins almost unheard of in Olympic skiing. At the 1958 World Championships in Bad Gastein he won three more golds and a silver. He announced his retirement in 1958, at 22, after disputes over amateurism rules that barred him from commercial endorsements.

Later life

Sailer became a film and television actor and a popular singer in Austria, recording numerous songs, and acted in dozens of films. He later served as technical director and race director of the Hahnenkamm races in Kitzbühel for over two decades and was chief of the course at the 1976 Innsbruck Winter Olympics. He died in Innsbruck in August 2009. In Austria he is remembered as a national hero, and he was voted Austrian sportsperson of the century in polling around 1999–2000.
