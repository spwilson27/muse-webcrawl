Serial (podcast)

Serial is an investigative journalism podcast hosted by Sarah Koenig, narrating one true story over the course of a season. It was created by Koenig, Julie Snyder, and Dana Chivvis, and developed by This American Life; it launched in October 2014. Serial is widely credited with popularising the podcast medium and igniting the true-crime podcast genre, and in 2015 it became the first podcast to win a Peabody Award.

Season 1: the murder of Hae Min Lee

The first season investigated the 1999 murder of Baltimore high-school student Hae Min Lee and the conviction of her ex-boyfriend, Adnan Syed, who maintained his innocence. Koenig re-examined the evidence, the testimony of key witness Jay Wilds, and Syed's alibi over twelve episodes. The season was a cultural phenomenon — downloaded hundreds of millions of times — and renewed legal scrutiny of the case. In September 2022 Syed's conviction was vacated and he was released; Baltimore prosecutors dropped the charges the following month, though the case continued through appellate litigation over victims' rights.

Later seasons

Season 2 (2015–16) examined the case of US Army sergeant Bowe Bergdahl, who left his post in Afghanistan in 2009 and was held by the Taliban for five years. Season 3 (2018) spent a year inside Cleveland's Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas, telling everyday stories of the American criminal-justice system. Season 4 (2024), co-reported with Chana Joffe-Walt, examined the history of Guantánamo Bay detention camp through the stories of prisoners and guards.

Impact

Serial transformed podcasting from a niche medium into a mainstream one, spawning a wave of serialized true-crime and narrative shows and new business models built on hit podcasts. In 2020 The New York Times acquired Serial Productions; the studio later produced shows including Nice White Parents, The Trojan Horse Affair, and The Retrievals.
