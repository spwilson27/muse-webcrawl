The Dassault Mirage 5 is a French supersonic attack aircraft designed by Dassault Aviation during the 1960s. It was derived from the successful Mirage III fighter, with a longer nose, simplified avionics and increased fuel capacity, optimised for clear-weather ground attack.

Development

The Mirage 5 originated in an Israeli request for a simplified, cheaper version of the Mirage III for daytime attack missions in the clear skies of the Middle East. Removing the radar and other avionics freed space for more fuel and weapons stations. France embargoed the completed aircraft (the Mirage 5J) after the Six-Day War, taking them into its own air force instead, while Israel went on to build unlicensed derivatives, the IAI Nesher and Kfir.

Service

The Mirage 5 was exported widely, to countries including Belgium (which built it under licence), Pakistan, Egypt, Libya, Colombia, Peru and Venezuela. Many received extensive upgrades, such as Pakistan's ROSE-retrofitted aircraft, and the type remained in service into the 21st century.
