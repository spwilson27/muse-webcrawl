Barbecue

Barbecue (often shortened to BBQ) is a term used with significant regional variation for cooking methods using live fire and smoke. In the United States, barbecue usually means meat cooked low and slow with wood smoke, while in much of the world it means grilling over direct heat, and the social gathering built around it.

Etymology

The word derives from the Spanish barbacoa, from a Taíno word for a wooden framework used to roast or smoke meat above a fire in the Caribbean.

Regional styles

American barbecue regions include the Carolinas (pulled pork with vinegar, mustard or tomato sauces), Memphis and Kansas City (ribs), Texas (beef brisket), and Alabama (white sauce). Worldwide traditions include South African braai, Australian "barbie", Korean barbecue, Japanese yakitori, and Argentine asado.

Techniques

Key elements are low temperatures (around 110–135 °C), hardwood smoke, spice rubs, and long cooking that renders tough cuts tender through collagen breakdown.
