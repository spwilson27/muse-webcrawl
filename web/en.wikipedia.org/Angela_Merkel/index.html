Angela Merkel

Angela Dorothea Merkel
Born: 17 July 1954, Hamburg, West Germany
Office: Chancellor of Germany (2005–2021)
Party: Christian Democratic Union (CDU)
Background: Physical chemist

Angela Dorothea Merkel (born 17 July 1954) is a German retired politician and scientist who served as Chancellor of Germany from 2005 to 2021. The country's first woman chancellor and its first from the former East Germany, she led the Christian Democratic Union from 2000 to 2018 and was widely regarded during her tenure as the de facto leader of the European Union and the most powerful woman in the world.

Early life and entry into politics

Born in Hamburg, Merkel moved in infancy to East Germany, where her father was a Lutheran pastor. She earned a doctorate in quantum chemistry in 1986 and worked as a research scientist in East Berlin. Entering politics as the Berlin Wall fell, she joined the Democratic Awakening movement, served as deputy spokesperson of the last East German government, and after reunification rose in Helmut Kohl's CDU cabinets as minister for women and youth and then for the environment.

Chancellorship

Merkel became CDU leader in 2000 and, after the narrow 2005 election, chancellor of a grand coalition. She won re-election in 2009, 2013, and 2017. Her sixteen years in office were defined by crisis management: the global financial crisis and eurozone debt crisis, over which she insisted on austerity and reform in exchange for bailouts; the 2011 decision, after Fukushima, to accelerate Germany's nuclear phase-out; the 2015 refugee crisis, when her refusal to close borders—summed up in the phrase “Wir schaffen das” (“We can do this”)—admitted over a million asylum seekers and reshaped German politics; and the COVID-19 pandemic.

Retirement and legacy

Having announced in 2018 that she would not seek a fifth term, Merkel left office in December 2021 and was succeeded by Olaf Scholz. She is remembered for pragmatic, incremental governance—critics called it immobility, admirers steadiness—and for defending multilateralism and liberal democracy during a turbulent era. Her memoir Freedom appeared in 2024.
