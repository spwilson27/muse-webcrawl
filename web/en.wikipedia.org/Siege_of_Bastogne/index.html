Siege of Bastogne

The siege of Bastogne was an engagement during the Battle of the Bulge in December 1944, in which German forces encircled the Belgian town of Bastogne, a key road junction defended by the American 101st Airborne Division and elements of other units.

The siege

Bastogne's seven converging roads made it essential to the German advance toward Antwerp. Airborne troops rushed to the town held the perimeter against superior numbers from 20 to 26 December. When German envoys demanded surrender on 22 December, acting division commander Brigadier General Anthony McAuliffe replied with the famous one-word message: "NUTS!"

Relief

Supplied precariously by air drops, the garrison held until 26 December 1944, when tanks of the U.S. 4th Armored Division of Patton's Third Army broke through the corridor and relieved the town, though fighting around it continued into January.

Legacy

The defence of Bastogne became one of the most celebrated episodes of the war for the United States Army and is commemorated by the Mardasson Memorial and the Bastogne War Museum.
