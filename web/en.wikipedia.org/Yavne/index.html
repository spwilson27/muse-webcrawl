Yavne

Yavne
City in Central District, Israel
Ancient names: Jabneh, Jamnia, Iamnia
Population: c. 50,000+

Yavne (Hebrew: יַבְנֶה) is a city in the Central District of Israel, south of Tel Aviv. In ancient times it was a major port and scholarly centre known in Greek as Jamnia and in Arabic as Yibna.

Ancient history

The Philistine and later Jewish town of Jabneh stood near the coast; its mound, Tel Yavne, overlies remains from the Bronze Age onward. After the Roman destruction of the Second Temple in 70 CE, Rabbi Yohanan ben Zakkai — according to tradition, smuggled out of besieged Jerusalem in a coffin — persuaded the Roman commander Vespasian to let him establish a rabbinic academy at Yavne. There the Sanhedrin reconvened, Rabban Gamaliel II presided, and foundations of rabbinic Judaism were laid, including the fixing of prayer liturgy and, traditionally associated with Yavne, discussions over the biblical canon ("Council of Jamnia," a hypothesis now treated cautiously by scholars).

Later periods and modern city

The site became Byzantine Iamnia and the Arab village of Yibna, a Crusader lordship (Ibelin), and later a Palestinian town depopulated in the 1948 war. The modern Jewish city of Yavne was founded in 1949 as a transit camp near the ancient site and grew into a suburban city on the coastal plain, with a railway station on the line to Ashdod. In 2022 the world's oldest known purple-dyed (tekhelet-argaman) fabric scraps from the Timna valley research and a huge Byzantine winepress complex at Yavne drew archaeological attention.
