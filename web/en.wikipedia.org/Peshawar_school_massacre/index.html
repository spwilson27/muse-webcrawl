Peshawar school massacre

On 16 December 2014, six gunmen of the Tehrik-i-Taliban Pakistan (TTP) attacked the Army Public School in Peshawar, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan, murdering 149 people, including 132 schoolchildren, in the deadliest terrorist attack in Pakistan's history. The attackers, who wore army uniforms, entered the school through a rear graveyard, opened fire in the auditorium and classrooms, and were killed by Pakistani special forces after an hours-long operation.

Background and attack

The TTP said the attack was retaliation for the Pakistan Army's Operation Zarb-e-Azb offensive in North Waziristan, claiming the school was targeted because many pupils were children of military personnel. The school taught roughly 1,100 students, mainly children of army families. Survivors described gunmen moving classroom to classroom; teachers were among the dead, including the principal.

Response

The massacre provoked national mourning and international condemnation. Pakistan lifted its moratorium on the death penalty for terrorism cases, established military courts through the Twenty-first Amendment, and adopted a National Action Plan against terrorism. Prime Minister Nawaz Sharif declared the attack a turning point, vowing that no distinction would be made between militant groups. Several planners and facilitators were later tried by military courts and executed.

Legacy

16 December is commemorated annually in Pakistan, and the attack is remembered as a defining moment in the country's fight against militancy. Security at schools nationwide was overhauled.
