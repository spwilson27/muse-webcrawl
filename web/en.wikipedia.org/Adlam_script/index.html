Adlam is an alphabetic script used to write Fulfulde, the language of the Fula people of West and Central Africa. It was devised in 1989 in Guinea by two brothers, Abdoulaye Barry and Ibrahima Barry, who created it so that the Fula language could be written accurately in its own script rather than in adaptations of the Latin or Arabic alphabets. The name Adlam is an acronym of the first letters of the alphabet: Alkule Dandaydi Leydi Muninor, "the letters that protect a people from vanishing."

Features

Adlam is written from right to left and has 28 letters in its original form, including letters for sounds such as implosives and glottal stops that are important in Fulfulde. It is a true alphabet with distinct letters for consonants and vowels, and letters change shape depending on position, joining to one another somewhat like Arabic script.

Adoption

Taught informally through community networks and later through books, apps, and fonts, Adlam spread across the Fula diaspora. It was encoded in Unicode in 2016 (version 9.0), and gained support in major operating systems and in Google's Noto fonts, which greatly expanded its use in print and digital communication.
