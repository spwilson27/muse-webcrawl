Doujinshi

Doujinshi (同人誌), also romanized as dōjinshi, are self-published print works in Japan, usually magazines, manga, or novels. The term is formed from dōjin (同人, "same person", referring to a group of people who share an interest) and shi (誌, magazine). Creators, often organised in "circles", produce and sell their own works outside the commercial publishing industry.

Contents and genres

Although some doujinshi feature original characters and stories, a large proportion are derivative works based on existing manga, anime, games, or other media — including parodies and unofficial sequels, alternative pairings of characters, and adult-oriented material. Copyright holders typically tolerate this unofficial production, which functions as a training ground: many professional manga artists, such as the group CLAMP, began in doujinshi.

Comiket and distribution

Doujinshi are sold at specialist shops and at conventions, above all Comiket (Comic Market), held twice yearly in Tokyo since 1975 and attracting hundreds of thousands of visitors. Circulation is often small — many titles are printed in runs of a few hundred copies — though popular circles can sell tens of thousands.

Legal status

Derivative doujinshi formally infringe copyright, but enforcement is rare in Japan, where rights holders and publishers benefit from the fan activity and talent pipeline the scene provides. The issue became a debated topic during negotiations over the Trans-Pacific Partnership's copyright provisions, amid fears that stricter enforcement could damage the culture.
