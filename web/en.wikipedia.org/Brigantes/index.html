Brigantes

Brigantes
Capital: Isurium Brigantum (Aldborough)
Government: Tribal kingdom / confederation
Era: Iron Age, Roman Britain
Notable rulers: Cartimandua, Venutius

The Brigantes were Ancient Britons who in pre-Roman and Roman times controlled the largest section of what would become Northern England. Their territory, often referred to as Brigantia, was bordered by the rivers Tyne and Humber and extended across much of the region now known as Yorkshire, Lancashire, County Durham, Cumbria and Northumberland. They were the most populous British tribe at the time of the Roman invasion.

History

The name Brigantes may derive from the Celtic root brig, meaning "high" or "elevated" — possibly referring to the high ground of the Pennines, or to the goddess Brigantia, who was worshipped by the tribe. The Brigantes may in fact have been a confederation of smaller tribes rather than a single unified people.

During the Roman conquest of Britain in the 1st century AD, Queen Cartimandua ruled the Brigantes and maintained an alliance with Rome. In AD 51 she handed over the fugitive British resistance leader Caratacus to the Romans in chains. Her pro-Roman policy, and her divorce of her husband Venutius in favour of his armour-bearer Vellocatus, provoked rebellion. Venutius attacked in AD 69, during the Roman Year of the Four Emperors, and Cartimandua was rescued by Roman troops but lost her kingdom.

Under the governor Quintus Petillius Cerialis, Roman forces campaigned against the Brigantes in the AD 70s, and by the governorship of Gnaeus Julius Agricola the tribe had been subjugated. The Roman town of Eboracum (York) and forts such as those along Stanegate stood within former Brigantian territory.

Settlements

The Brigantes are associated with a number of settlements and hillforts, including Stanwick Fortifications in North Yorkshire — possibly Cartimandua's stronghold — and the oppidum at Isurium Brigantum, modern Aldborough. Other sites include Wincobank, Almondbury and Ingleborough.
