Socialism is an economic and political philosophy encompassing a range of systems characterized by social ownership of the means of production, as opposed to private ownership, alongside doctrines emphasizing equality and collective or democratic control of economic life. It originated in the late 18th and early 19th centuries in reaction to the social and economic conditions created by industrial capitalism.

History

Early socialist thinkers—later labelled "utopian socialists" by Marx and Engels—included Henri de Saint-Simon, Charles Fourier, and Robert Owen, who proposed cooperative communities and planned production. In the mid-19th century, Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels developed a materialist theory of history arguing that capitalism would be superseded by socialism through class struggle, most famously in The Communist Manifesto (1848) and Das Kapital (1867).

The founding of the International Workingmen's Association (1864) and later the Second International (1889) spread socialist organization. After the Russian Revolution of 1917, the socialist movement split between communist parties aligned with the Soviet Union and social democratic parties pursuing parliamentary reform. During the 20th century, socialist or social-democratic governments implemented welfare states in much of Western Europe, while states identifying as socialist governed the Soviet Union, Eastern Europe, China, Cuba, and elsewhere, with widely varying economic models and political systems.

Variants

  • Marxism and its branches, including Leninism and Trotskyism, emphasize revolution, the dictatorship of the proletariat as a transitional state, and central planning.
  • Democratic socialism advocates a democratic political system alongside social ownership, achieved through electoral means.
  • Social democracy in its modern form generally accepts a mixed market economy with extensive public services and redistribution.
  • Libertarian socialism, including anarchist schools, opposes both capitalism and centralized state control in favor of federated, self-managed institutions.
  • Market socialism proposes social ownership of firms operating within markets.

Economics

Socialist economic proposals range from comprehensive central planning by the state, to decentralized participatory planning, to cooperative ownership of enterprises within a market. Critics such as Ludwig von Mises and Friedrich Hayek argued that without market prices, rational economic calculation is impossible—the economic calculation problem—while supporters have responded with models of democratic planning, labor-time accounting, and computational planning.

Criticism

Criticism of socialism includes arguments about inefficiencies of planning, suppression of individual liberty in states governed in socialism's name, and the historical record of authoritarian rule and economic failure in several 20th-century socialist states. Defenders distinguish between socialist ideals and particular regimes, and point to achievements of social-democratic welfare states in health, education, and poverty reduction.
