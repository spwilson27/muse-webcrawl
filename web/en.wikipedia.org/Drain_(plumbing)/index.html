Drain (plumbing)

A drain is the primary vessel or conduit for unwanted water or waste liquids to flow away, and to be carried to a more suitable area for disposal. In plumbing, a drain is a pipe or channel that carries waste water and sewage from a building to a sewer or septic system.

Typical household drains include sinks, bathtubs, showers, washing machines, dishwashers, and floor drains. Each fixture drain connects to a trap — a U-, S-, or P-shaped bend in the pipe — which holds a small amount of water that seals the drain against sewer gases entering the building. Beyond the trap, drain pipes connect to a vent system that maintains air pressure and prevents siphoning of the trap seal.

Types

  • Fixture drains — short pipes directly beneath sinks, baths, and showers.
  • Floor drains — flush fittings in floors of basements, garages, and utilities rooms.
  • Waste and vent systems — the network of pipes carrying waste to the building sewer.
  • Storm drains — carry rainwater from roofs and surfaces to storm sewers (separate from sanitary drains in many cities).
  • French drains — gravel-filled trenches with perforated pipe, used to redirect groundwater.

Maintenance

Drains can become blocked by hair, grease, soap residue, and debris. Common remedies include plungers, drain snakes, and chemical drain cleaners, though persistent blockages may indicate root intrusion or pipe damage requiring professional attention.
