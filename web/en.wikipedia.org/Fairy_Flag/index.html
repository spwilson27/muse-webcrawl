Fairy Flag

The Fairy Flag (Scottish Gaelic: Am Bratach Sìth) is a fragment of silk banner preserved at Dunvegan Castle on the Isle of Skye, the treasured heirloom of the chiefs of Clan MacLeod. According to clan tradition, the flag was a gift from the fairies and possesses the power, when unfurled, to save the clan in battle — a power that may be invoked only three times, twice of which, legend says, have already been used.

Origins and legends

Several origin stories surround the flag. One tells of a MacLeod chief who married a fairy wife; when she was obliged to return to her own people after twenty years, she gave him the banner at the "Fairy Bridge", warning that it would summon fairy aid when waved in dire need, but only three times. Another tradition holds that it was a war banner brought from the Holy Land or the East during the Crusades, or a gift connected with Harald Hardrada's banner, Land Ravager, carried at the Battle of Stamford Bridge in 1066. Clan tradition credits the flag with victories at the battles of Glendale and Trumpan, where it was said to multiply the MacLeods' apparent numbers or blind their enemies.

The physical flag

The surviving flag is a worn piece of silk, now about 46 cm square, carefully preserved in a case at Dunvegan. Scientific examination has dated the silk to between the 4th and 7th centuries AD and suggested it originated in the eastern Mediterranean — possibly Rhodes or Syria — consistent with a precious relic or banner cloth of great antiquity, though how it reached Skye remains unknown.
