Cauvery water dispute

The Cauvery water dispute is a long-running conflict over the sharing of the waters of the Kaveri (Cauvery) River in southern India, principally between the states of Karnataka and Tamil Nadu, with Kerala and the union territory of Puducherry also parties. The river originates in Karnataka's Western Ghats and flows through Tamil Nadu to the Bay of Bengal, and both states depend on it for irrigation and drinking water.

History

Agreements between the Kingdom of Mysore and the Madras Presidency in 1892 and 1924 regulated water use under British rule. After Indian independence and the reorganization of states, the 1924 agreement lapsed in 1974, and disputes intensified as Karnataka expanded irrigation in the basin. Negotiations failed repeatedly, and tensions sometimes erupted in violence, notably riots in 1991 and 2016.

Tribunal and Supreme Court

The Cauvery Water Disputes Tribunal, constituted in 1990, delivered its final award in 2007, allocating shares of the river's assessed yield among the four parties. The award was challenged, and in 2018 the Supreme Court of India modified the allocation slightly — increasing Karnataka's share for Bengaluru's drinking water — and directed the creation of the Cauvery Water Management Authority to implement the scheme.

Continuing issues

Shortfalls in monsoon rainfall repeatedly strain the arrangement, with distress-sharing in dry years remaining contentious, as does the proposed Mekedatu dam project in Karnataka.
