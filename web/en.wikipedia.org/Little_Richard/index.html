Little Richard
Born: Richard Wayne Penniman
December 5, 1932, Macon, Georgia, U.S.
Died: May 9, 2020 (aged 87), Tullahoma, Tennessee
Genres: Rock and roll, rhythm and blues, gospel
Instruments: Vocals, piano

Richard Wayne Penniman (December 5, 1932 – May 9, 2020), known professionally as Little Richard, was an American singer, songwriter and pianist. A foundational figure in rock and roll, he broke into the mainstream in 1955 with "Tutti Frutti", whose opening cry — "A-wop-bop-a-loo-bop-a-wop-bam-boom!" — became one of the most famous sounds in popular music.

Career

Born in Macon, Georgia, Penniman sang gospel in church and performed in travelling shows before recording rhythm and blues for RCA and Peacock. His explosive Specialty Records sides of 1955–1957 — "Long Tall Sally", "Rip It Up", "Lucille", "Good Golly, Miss Molly" — combined pounding piano, a shrieking, ecstatic vocal style and androgynous stage presentation that influenced virtually every rock performer who followed, including Elvis Presley, the Beatles, James Brown and Prince. At the height of his fame in 1957 he abruptly quit rock and roll for gospel ministry, a pattern of renunciation and return that recurred throughout his life.

Legacy

Little Richard was among the first inductees into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1986. He is frequently described as "the architect of rock and roll". His music crossed racial barriers in the segregated United States, and his flamboyant persona helped define the image of the rock star.
