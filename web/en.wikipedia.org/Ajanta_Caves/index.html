Ajanta Caves

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The Ajanta Caves in Maharashtra, India

The Ajanta Caves are 29 rock-cut Buddhist cave monuments dating from the second century BCE to about 480 CE in the Aurangabad district of Maharashtra state in India. The caves include paintings and rock-cut sculptures described as among the finest surviving examples of ancient Indian art, particularly expressive paintings that present emotions through gesture, pose and form.

According to UNESCO, these are masterpieces of Buddhist religious art that influenced Indian art that followed. The caves were built in two phases, the first starting around the second century BCE and the second occurring from 400 to 650 CE, according to older accounts, or in a brief period of 460–480 CE according to later scholarship.

History

The first phase of construction, in the Satavahana period, produced a group of chaitya-grihas (sanctuaries) and viharas (monasteries) excavated around the 2nd and 1st centuries BCE. The second phase, under the Vakataka dynasty in the 5th century CE, added the majority of the caves, including the celebrated painted caves 1, 2, 16 and 17.

The caves were largely abandoned after the decline of Buddhism in the region and were covered by jungle. In 1819 a British officer, Captain John Smith, stumbled upon the entrance to Cave 10 while hunting and inscribed his name and the date on one of the pillars, bringing the site to the attention of the Western world.

Site and layout

The caves are carved into a 75-metre (246 ft) wall of rock in a horseshoe-shaped gorge of the Waghora River, now dry. A steep stairway climbs from the river to the caves, and a viewpoint across the gorge offers a panoramic view of the whole complex.

Art and painting

The paintings at Ajanta depict the Jataka tales — stories of the Buddha's previous lives — as well as scenes of courtly and everyday life. They were executed in tempera using mineral and vegetable pigments on a ground of mud plaster. The most famous images include the Bodhisattva Padmapani and Vajrapani in Cave 1.

UNESCO status

The Ajanta Caves have been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1983. Together with the nearby Ellora Caves, they are among the most popular tourist destinations in Maharashtra.
