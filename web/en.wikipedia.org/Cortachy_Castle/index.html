Cortachy Castle is a castellated mansion house near the village of Cortachy, around five miles north of Kirriemuir in Angus, Scotland. It is the seat of the Ogilvy family, Earls of Airlie. The castle stands in parkland on the banks of the River South Esk.

History

The lands of Cortachy came into the hands of the Ogilvy family in the 15th century, and the earliest parts of the present building date to that period, centred on a tower house. The castle was substantially remodelled and expanded in the 17th century, and again in the 19th century in the Scots Baronial style, including work associated with the architect David Bryce in the 1870s, which gave the house much of its current appearance of turrets and crow-stepped gables.

Cortachy has connections with notable episodes in Scottish history through the Ogilvys' roles as hereditary supporters first of the Jacobite cause and later as prominent courtiers: David Ogilvy, Lord Ogilvy, was attainted for his part in the 1745 rising, while later earls served in royal households, and Charlotte Ogilvy, Countess of Airlie, was a lady-in-waiting to Queen Victoria. A fire in the 20th century damaged part of the interior, which was subsequently restored.

The castle today

The castle remains the private home of the Earl and Countess of Airlie and is not generally open to the public, though the gardens have occasionally opened under Scotland's Gardens Scheme. The estate includes formal gardens, specimen trees, and the Airlie Monument on nearby Tulloch Hill, erected in memory of David Ogilvy, 9th Earl of Airlie. Cortachy Castle is protected as a category A listed building.
