Soyuz (rocket family)

A Soyuz-FG launch from Baikonur

The Soyuz (Russian: Союз, meaning "union", GRAU index 11A511) is a family of expendable Russian and Soviet carrier rockets developed by OKB-1 and manufactured by Progress Rocket Space Centre in Samara, Russia. The Soyuz is the most frequently used launch vehicle in the world, and with over 1,900 flights since 1966 the family is the most flown orbital rocket lineage in history.

History

The Soyuz rocket was introduced in 1966, deriving from the Vostok launcher, which in turn was based on the R-7 Semyorka intercontinental ballistic missile. It was initially built to launch Soyuz spacecraft as part of the Soviet human spaceflight programme. All Soyuz rockets use RP-1 and liquid oxygen propellants, with four strap-on boosters clustering around a central core — the characteristic "Korolev cross" formed as the boosters separate.

Variants

Major variants include the original Soyuz (11A511), Soyuz-L, Soyuz-M, Soyuz-U (the most-flown variant, retired in 2017), Soyuz-U2, Soyuz-FG (used for crewed launches until 2019) and the modernised Soyuz-2 series, including the Soyuz-2.1a, 2.1b and the light Soyuz-2.1v. Soyuz rockets have launched from Baikonur Cosmodrome, Plesetsk Cosmodrome, Vostochny Cosmodrome and, as the Soyuz-ST operated by Arianespace, from the Guiana Space Centre.

Role

The family launches crewed Soyuz spacecraft and Progress cargo spacecraft to the International Space Station, as well as commercial and military satellites. Following the retirement of the Space Shuttle in 2011, Soyuz was for nearly a decade the only vehicle able to carry crews to the ISS.
