Der Ring des Nibelungen (The Ring of the Nibelung), WWV 86, is a cycle of four German-language epic music dramas composed by Richard Wagner. The works are based loosely on characters from Germanic heroic legend, including Norse legendary sagas and the Nibelungenlied. Wagner wrote the libretto and music over twenty-six years, from 1848 to 1874. The four parts — a "preliminary evening" and three "days" — are Das Rheingold (The Rhinegold), Die Walküre (The Valkyrie), Siegfried, and Götterdämmerung (Twilight of the Gods).

Story

The dwarf Alberich steals the Rhinegold and forges from it a ring granting world dominion, renouncing love to do so. Wotan, chief of the gods, seizes the ring to pay the giants who built Valhalla, but is forced to surrender it; Alberich's curse condemns its bearers. Through generations of Wotan's mortal descendants — Siegmund and Sieglinde, and their son Siegfried — the ring passes by heroism and treachery until Siegfried is murdered and his beloved Brünnhilde, Wotan's Valkyrie daughter, immolates herself on his funeral pyre, returning the ring to the Rhinemaidens as Valhalla and the gods burn. The plot weaves together themes of power, law, love, and the corruption of nature.

Composition and first performance

Wagner worked backwards from a single drama about Siegfried's death, expanding it into the tetralogy; he interrupted composition for twelve years (during which he wrote Tristan und Isolde and Die Meistersinger) before finishing Götterdämmerung in 1874. The music is through-composed, structured by a vast web of leitmotifs — short themes associated with characters, objects, and ideas. Das Rheingold and Die Walküre were premiered individually in Munich (1869, 1870) against Wagner's wishes, at the insistence of his patron Ludwig II of Bavaria. The complete cycle was first performed at the purpose-built Bayreuth Festspielhaus in August 1876, conducted by Hans Richter.

Influence

The Ring is the centrepiece of the Bayreuth Festival and one of the most influential works in Western music and theatre, transforming opera's scale, orchestration, and dramatic technique. Famous productions include the centenary Bayreuth staging by Patrice Chéreau (1976). Its motifs and structure have permeated popular culture, and J. R. R. Tolkien's The Lord of the Rings is frequently compared with it — a comparison Tolkien himself resisted ("Both rings were round, and there the resemblance ceases"). Complete performances, mounted over four nights, remain a benchmark for opera companies and conductors.
