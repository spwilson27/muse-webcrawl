Félibrige

The Félibrige is a literary and cultural association founded in 1854 by Frédéric Mistral and six other Provençal poets to defend and promote the Occitan language and culture of southern France.

Founded21 May 1854, Font-Ségugne, Provence
FoundersFrédéric Mistral, Joseph Roumanille, Théodore Aubanel, Jean Brunet, Paul Giéra, Anselme Mathieu, Alphonse Tavan
PurposePromotion of Occitan language and culture

History

The seven founders, calling themselves the félibres, met at the Château de Font-Ségugne near Avignon. The movement established an orthography for Provençal, published the almanac Armana Prouvençau, and produced major works including Mistral's epic poem Mirèio (1859) and his dictionary Lou Tresor dóu Felibrige.

Organization and influence

The association is headed by a capoulié (president) and holds annual gatherings such as the Fête de la Santo-Estello. The Félibrige inspired language-revival movements elsewhere, including in Catalonia, and remains active in maintaining Occitan traditions, festivals, and literature.
