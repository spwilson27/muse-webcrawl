Abel Janszoon Tasman (1603–1659) was a Dutch seafarer and explorer in the service of the Dutch East India Company (VOC). He is best known for his voyages of 1642 and 1644, during which he became the first known European to reach the islands of Tasmania and New Zealand and to sight the Fiji islands.

Voyage of 1642–43

Sailing from Batavia with the ships Heemskerck and Zeehaen, Tasman was instructed to explore the southern ocean. In November 1642 he sighted the west coast of the island he named Van Diemen's Land, after his patron, the governor-general of the Dutch East Indies; it was later renamed Tasmania in his honor. Continuing east, he reached New Zealand, which he called Staten Landt, and had a violent encounter with Māori at the bay he named Murderers' Bay (now Golden Bay). He then sailed north, sighting Tonga and Fiji before returning to Batavia.

Second voyage and later life

In 1644 Tasman mapped much of the northern coast of Australia, establishing that New Guinea was separated from the Australian mainland by what is now Torres Strait only in part being clarified later. He spent his final years as a landowner in Batavia, where he died in 1659.
