Cloverfield is a 2008 American found-footage monster film directed by Matt Reeves, produced by J. J. Abrams, and written by Drew Goddard. It follows six young New York City residents fleeing from a massive monster that attacks the city while they are having a farewell party.

The film is presented as footage from a personal camcorder recovered by the United States Department of Defense in the area "formerly known as Central Park", with a title card noting the designation "Cloverfield".

Plot

During a going-away party for Rob Hawkins, who is leaving for a job in Japan, an earthquake-like tremor rocks Manhattan and a colossal creature begins destroying the city, decapitating the Statue of Liberty and collapsing skyscrapers. Rob's friend Hud documents the group as they cross the collapsing Brooklyn Bridge and venture into the war zone to rescue Rob's former girlfriend Beth, trapped in her damaged apartment near Columbus Circle. Parasitic creatures drop from the monster's body, and one of them bites the group's friend Marlena, who later dies violently in a triage station.

The survivors are evacuated by helicopter, which the monster brings down in Central Park. Hud is killed by the creature on camera; Rob and Beth record final messages beneath a bridge as bombs fall in the military's "Hammer Down" protocol, and the tape ends with a brief pre-attack clip of the couple at Coney Island.

Production and marketing

Abrams conceived the film after visiting Japan and seeing Godzilla merchandise, wanting an American monster with a more "insane and intense" character. The creature was designed by Neville Page. The project was filmed in secrecy under working titles including Slusho! and Cheese. A teaser trailer debuting before Transformers in July 2007, which showed no title, launched an extensive viral marketing campaign involving fictional companies such as Tagruato and the drink Slusho!.

Reception and sequels

The film opened on January 18, 2008, grossing $172.4 million on a $25 million budget, and was praised for its cinéma vérité style, though some viewers reported motion sickness from the handheld camerawork. It began the Cloverfield franchise, followed by 10 Cloverfield Lane (2016) and The Cloverfield Paradox (2018).
