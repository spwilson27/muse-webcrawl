Skyline Drive

Skyline Drive follows the Blue Ridge crest through Shenandoah National Park

Skyline Drive is a 105-mile (169 km) scenic road that runs the entire length of Shenandoah National Park in the Blue Ridge Mountains of Virginia, United States. It follows the ridge crest from Front Royal in the north to Rockfish Gap near Waynesboro in the south, where it connects with the Blue Ridge Parkway.

History

Construction began in 1931, employing local farmers hit by drought and Depression as well as Civilian Conservation Corps crews. The road was built largely with hand tools and completed in 1939. President Franklin D. Roosevelt dedicated Shenandoah National Park in 1936. Much of Skyline Drive was listed on the National Register of Historic Places as part of a historic district.

Features

The drive has 75 overlooks providing views across the Shenandoah Valley to the west and the Piedmont to the east. Its speed limit is 35 mph, and it is famous for autumn foliage, wildflowers, deer and black bear sightings. Milepost markers run from 0 at Front Royal; notable points include Mary's Rock Tunnel, Big Meadows and Dark Hollow Falls. The Appalachian Trail parallels and crosses the road many times along its length.
