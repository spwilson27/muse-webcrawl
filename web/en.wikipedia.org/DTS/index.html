DTS (sound system)

DTS is a series of multichannel audio technologies owned by DTS, Inc. (formerly Digital Theater Systems, Inc.), used in cinemas and home audio systems. The original DTS system debuted with the film Jurassic Park in 1993, competing with Dolby Digital. In theatres, DTS audio was stored on separate CD-ROMs synchronised to the film by a timecode track on the print.

Formats

Home formats include core DTS Digital Surround (5.1 channels), DTS-ES with a centre-surround channel, the 96/24 high-resolution variant, DTS-HD High Resolution Audio and the lossless DTS-HD Master Audio, which is widely used on Blu-ray discs. DTS:X, introduced in 2015, is an object-based immersive audio format competing with Dolby Atmos.

Company

DTS, Inc. was acquired by Tessera Technologies in 2016; the combined company later became Xperi. DTS technologies are licensed for use in AV receivers, soundbars, televisions, game consoles and automotive audio systems.
