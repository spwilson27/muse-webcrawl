Sir Andrzej Panufnik (24 September 1914 – 27 October 1991) was a Polish composer and conductor. One of the leading Polish composers of the 20th century, he defected from communist Poland to the United Kingdom in 1954, where he spent the rest of his life and career and was knighted in 1991.

Early life

Panufnik was born in Warsaw, the son of a distinguished violin-maker. He studied composition at the Warsaw Conservatory (1932–36) and conducting in Vienna and Paris. During the Second World War he played piano duets in underground concerts in occupied Warsaw with his friend Witold Lutosławski; nearly all of his early manuscripts were destroyed in the Warsaw Uprising of 1944, though he later reconstructed several works from memory, including the Tragic Overture (1942).

Post-war Poland and defection

After the war Panufnik became chief conductor of the Kraków Philharmonic and then the Warsaw Philharmonic. Although initially honoured by the communist regime, he grew disillusioned with socialist realism and official interference in artistic life. In 1954, while on a conducting tour, he escaped to London and sought political asylum, an act that led to his music being banned in Poland until 1977.

Career in Britain

Panufnik was music director of the City of Birmingham Symphony Orchestra (1957–59) and thereafter devoted himself mainly to composition, supported by commissions from conductors such as Leopold Stokowski, Seiji Ozawa, André Previn and Sir Georg Solti, and from performers including Mstislav Rostropovich and Yehudi Menuhin. His output is notable for its economy, symmetrical structures and geometric designs; it includes ten symphonies — among them Sinfonia Sacra (1963), written for the millennium of Christianity in Poland — concertos for piano, violin, cello and bassoon, string quartets, and choral works.

Personal life and death

In 1963 he married the photographer and writer Camilla Jessel. Their daughter Roxanna Panufnik also became a composer. Panufnik became a British citizen in 1961 and was knighted in 1991, the year of his death at Twickenham, London. After 1989 his music was again performed in Poland, and he made a celebrated return visit in 1990.
