Nine Lyric Poets

The Nine Lyric Poets were a canonical group of Greek lyric poets esteemed by the scholars of Hellenistic Alexandria as worthy of critical study. They composed in the Archaic and early Classical periods of ancient Greece and were divided by the Alexandrians into composers of choral song and of monody (song for a single voice).

The nine

  • Alcman of Sparta — choral lyric poet of the 7th century BCE
  • Sappho of Lesbos — monodic poet famed for songs of love and desire
  • Alcaeus of Lesbos — monodic poet of politics, war, and drinking songs
  • Anacreon of Teos — monodic poet of wine and love
  • Stesichorus of Himera — choral narrative poet, "the Homer of lyric"
  • Ibycus of Rhegium — choral and monodic poet
  • Simonides of Ceos — choral poet, famed for epigrams and elegies
  • Pindar of Thebes — the great choral poet of victory odes (epinikia)
  • Bacchylides of Ceos — choral poet, nephew of Simonides and rival of Pindar

Transmission

Their works were edited and commented upon in antiquity, but most survive only in fragments quoted by later authors or preserved on Egyptian papyri; substantial portions of Pindar's victory odes and parts of Bacchylides survive through medieval manuscripts and papyrus finds, respectively.
