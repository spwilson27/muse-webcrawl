Paulo Freire
BornSeptember 19, 1921, Recife, Brazil
DiedMay 2, 1997, São Paulo, Brazil
Known forCritical pedagogy; Pedagogy of the Oppressed

Paulo Reglus Neves Freire (1921–1997) was a Brazilian educator and philosopher, a leading advocate of critical pedagogy. His book Pedagogy of the Oppressed (1968), one of the most cited works in the social sciences, proposed a method of teaching built on dialogue and the development of "critical consciousness" (conscientização) among the oppressed.

Life

Freire grew up in poverty in Recife during the Great Depression, an experience that shaped his concern with the education of the poor. He studied law and became a secondary school teacher of Portuguese. In the early 1960s his adult literacy programs in northeastern Brazil taught peasants to read and write in as little as 45 days using words drawn from their own lives. After the 1964 military coup he was imprisoned and then exiled for sixteen years, working in Chile and with the World Council of Churches, and teaching at Harvard. He returned to Brazil in 1980 and later served as Secretary of Education of São Paulo.

Pedagogy

Freire criticized the "banking model" of education, in which a teacher deposits information into passive students. Against it he set problem-posing education: teacher and students learn together through dialogue, beginning from themes in the learners' own experience, so that education becomes an act of liberation rather than domination.

Legacy

Freire's ideas influenced adult education, literacy campaigns, and educational theory worldwide, and remain controversial among critics who regard his pedagogy as overtly political.
