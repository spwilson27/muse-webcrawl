Pensées

The Pensées (literally "Thoughts") is a collection of fragments written by the French philosopher and mathematician Blaise Pascal. Pascal's religious conversion led him into a life of asceticism, and the Pensées was in many ways his life's work — a planned but unfinished defence of the Christian religion, published posthumously in 1670.

Content

The fragments explore the wretchedness of man without God, the limits of reason, and the famous "Pascal's wager", which argues that a rational person should live as though God exists, since the potential gain outweighs any finite loss. Other celebrated passages describe man as a "thinking reed" and remark that "the heart has its reasons which reason knows nothing of."

Editions

Because Pascal left the work as loose, unordered papers, editions differ greatly in arrangement and numbering; the Brunschvicg and Lafuma numberings are the most cited. The Pensées is regarded as a masterpiece of French prose and of Christian apologetics.
