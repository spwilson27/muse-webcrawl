Cabinet Office

The Cabinet Office is a department of the Government of the United Kingdom responsible for supporting the Prime Minister and Cabinet. It is composed of various units that support Cabinet committees and which co-ordinate the delivery of government objectives via other departments.

History

The department was formed in December 1916 from the secretariat of the Committee of Imperial Defence under Sir Maurice Hankey, the first Cabinet Secretary. Its creation formalised the keeping of Cabinet minutes and the circulation of papers, replacing the earlier practice in which the only record of decisions was a letter from the Prime Minister to the monarch.

Responsibilities

The Cabinet Office supports the Prime Minister and the Cabinet, helps co-ordinate policy across departments, oversees the Civil Service in partnership with HM Treasury, and houses functions including the Joint Intelligence Organisation, the Government Digital Service, and the Civil Contingencies Secretariat. It is based at 70 Whitehall, adjacent to Downing Street.

Leadership

The department is headed by the Cabinet Secretary, the most senior civil servant in the United Kingdom, who also serves as Head of the Civil Service. Ministers in the department include the Chancellor of the Duchy of Lancaster and the Minister for the Cabinet Office.
