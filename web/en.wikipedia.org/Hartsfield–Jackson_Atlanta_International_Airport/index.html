Hartsfield–Jackson Atlanta International Airport (IATA: ATL) is the primary international airport serving Atlanta, Georgia, United States, located about 10 miles (16 km) south of downtown. It has been the world's busiest airport by passenger traffic in nearly every year since 1998, handling over 110 million passengers in 2019, and it is the largest hub of Delta Air Lines, whose headquarters and main maintenance base adjoin the field.

History

The airport began in 1925 as Candler Field, an airfield on an abandoned racetrack owned by Coca-Cola magnate Asa Candler; its first flight was a mail plane in September 1926. Renamed Atlanta Municipal Airport and then William B. Hartsfield Atlanta International Airport in 1971 for the mayor who championed its growth, it gained the Jackson name in 2003 in honour of Mayor Maynard Jackson after his death. The vast midfield terminal complex, with parallel concourses linked by an underground people mover (the Plane Train), opened in 1980, and the Maynard H. Jackson Jr. International Terminal (Concourse F) opened in 2012.

Facilities and operations

The airport covers about 4,700 acres with five parallel runways and 192 gates across seven concourses. The domestic terminal (with landside check-in for Concourses T–D) and the international terminal at Concourse F are connected airside, and the Plane Train carries passengers along the spine of the concourses. Delta and its partners account for roughly three-quarters of traffic; a saying among travellers holds that whether you are going to heaven or hell, you will connect through Atlanta. Its location within a two-hour flight of 80 per cent of the US population made it the archetypal hub-and-spoke airport.

Traffic and significance

ATL regularly records around 900,000 aircraft movements a year and total passengers exceeding 100 million, anchoring an aviation economy that is Georgia's largest employer cluster. A December 2017 power outage that halted the airport for eleven hours, grounding more than a thousand flights, underlined its systemic importance to the US air network.
