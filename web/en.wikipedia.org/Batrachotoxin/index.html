Batrachotoxin
Chemical formulaC31H42N2O6
ClassSteroidal alkaloid neurotoxin
SourceGolden poison frog (Phyllobates terribilis) and other poison dart frogs
TargetVoltage-gated sodium channels

Batrachotoxin (BTX) is an extremely potent cardiotoxic and neurotoxic steroidal alkaloid, among the most powerful non-peptide neurotoxins known. It occurs in the skin secretions of certain poison dart frogs of Colombia, including the golden poison frog (Phyllobates terribilis), and also in some New Guinean birds such as the hooded pitohui, which likely obtain it from their diet.

Mechanism

Batrachotoxin binds to voltage-gated sodium channels in nerve and muscle cells, locking them open. The resulting massive influx of sodium ions depolarizes the cell permanently, blocking nerve signal transmission and causing paralysis, cardiac arrhythmia and death. There is no known antidote. The toxin has no effect on the frogs themselves, whose sodium channels carry mutations conferring resistance.

Source and use

The frogs do not synthesize the toxin themselves but accumulate it from dietary arthropods, probably melyrid beetles. Indigenous Emberá people of Colombia have used the frogs' secretions to poison blow darts for hunting. Captive-raised frogs are non-toxic because they lack the dietary source. A single golden poison frog may carry around a milligram of toxin — enough, by common estimates, to kill many humans.
