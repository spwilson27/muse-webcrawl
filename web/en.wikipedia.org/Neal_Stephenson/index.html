Neal Stephenson

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Neal Town Stephenson (born 31 October 1959) is an American writer known for his works of speculative fiction, which have been categorized as science fiction, historical fiction, cyberpunk, postcyberpunk, and baroque. His novels are noted for their maximalist detail, mathematical and scientific themes, and exploration of cryptography, linguistics, and the history of science.

Life and career

Stephenson was born in Fort Meade, Maryland, into a family of engineers and scientists, and studied physics and geography at Boston University, graduating in 1981. After early novels The Big U (1984) and Zodiac (1988), he achieved breakthrough success with Snow Crash (1992), which popularized the term "metaverse" and the use of "avatar" for virtual identities. The Diamond Age (1995) won the Hugo Award for Best Novel.

Later works

Cryptonomicon (1999) interweaves World War II codebreaking with present-day data havens. The three-volume Baroque Cycle—Quicksilver (2003), The Confusion (2004), and The System of the World (2004)—is a vast historical epic of the 17th and 18th centuries. Subsequent novels include Anathem (2008), Reamde (2011), Seveneves (2015), The Rise and Fall of D.O.D.O. (2017, with Nicole Galland), Fall; or, Dodge in Hell (2019), and Termination Shock (2021).

Other work

Beyond fiction, Stephenson has written essays such as "In the Beginning... Was the Command Line" (1999) and has worked in technology ventures, including as a co-founder of the metaverse company Lamina1 and earlier involvement with Blue Origin and Magic Leap.
