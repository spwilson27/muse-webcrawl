Joaquim Maria Machado de Assis

Joaquim Maria Machado de Assis (21 June 1839 – 29 September 1908), often called Machado de Assis, was a Brazilian novelist, poet, playwright, and journalist, widely regarded as the greatest writer of Brazilian literature.

Born poor in Rio de Janeiro, the grandson of freed slaves, he taught himself literature and rose to found and chair the Brazilian Academy of Letters. His mature realist works, beginning with The Posthumous Memoirs of Brás Cubas (1881) and including Papéis Avulsos and Dom Casmurro (1899), broke with romanticism and influenced writers worldwide.
