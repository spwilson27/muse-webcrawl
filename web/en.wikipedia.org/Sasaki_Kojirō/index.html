Sasaki Kojirō (Japanese: 佐々木 小次郎; c. 1575 – 13 April 1612) was a Japanese swordsman of the late Sengoku and early Edo periods, remembered as the opponent of Miyamoto Musashi in the most famous duel in Japanese history. Kojirō founded the Ganryū school of swordsmanship and was renowned for his technique tsubame gaeshi ("swallow's tail cut"), said to mimic the motion of a swallow's tail in flight. He fought with an exceptionally long nodachi, nicknamed the "Laundry-Drying Pole".

Statues commemorating the duel on Ganryūjima (Funashima).

The duel with Musashi

In 1612 Kojirō, then a fencing instructor under the Hosokawa clan in Kokura, was challenged by Musashi. The duel took place on 13 April 1612 on the island of Funashima, later called Ganryūjima after Kojirō's school. According to the well-known accounts, Musashi arrived hours late by boat — whether by design, to unsettle his opponent — and fought with a wooden sword he had carved from an oar during the crossing. Kojirō was killed in the brief encounter. Details of the bout were embellished in later chronicles, plays, and novels, and historians debate several elements of the traditional story.

Legacy

Kojirō has become a fixture of Japanese popular culture, appearing as Musashi's noble rival in Eiji Yoshikawa's novel Musashi and in countless films, manga, anime, and video games, where his swallow-cut technique and his long blade are enduring motifs.
