Sales taxes in Canada

Canadian sales taxes combine a federal goods and services tax with provincial taxes.

In Canada, sales taxes are levied at both the federal and provincial levels. The federal Goods and Services Tax (GST), introduced in 1991 under Prime Minister Brian Mulroney, is a value-added tax currently set at 5% on most goods and services. It replaced the earlier federal manufacturers' sales tax.

Harmonized Sales Tax

Several provinces have merged their provincial sales tax with the GST into a single Harmonized Sales Tax (HST), administered by the Canada Revenue Agency. Ontario and the Atlantic provinces apply the HST, with combined rates varying by province (for example 13% in Ontario and 15% in much of Atlantic Canada, though some provinces have announced reductions). British Columbia adopted the HST in 2010 but reverted to a separate provincial sales tax in 2013 after a referendum.

Provincial sales taxes

Quebec levies its own Quebec Sales Tax (QST), administered by Revenu Québec, which is harmonized with the GST in most respects. Saskatchewan, Manitoba and British Columbia maintain separate retail sales taxes. Alberta and the three territories have no provincial sales tax, so consumers there pay only the 5% GST.

Exemptions and rebates

Basic groceries, prescription drugs and many medical devices are zero-rated, while residential rent and financial services are generally exempt. Low- and modest-income households receive a GST/HST credit paid quarterly.
