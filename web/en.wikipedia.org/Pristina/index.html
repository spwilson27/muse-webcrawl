Pristina

Pristina (Albanian: Prishtina; Serbian: Приштина, Priština) is the capital and largest city of Kosovo, with a population of around 200,000, predominantly ethnic Albanian. It is the country's political, economic, and cultural centre, seat of the government and the University of Pristina.

History

The area has been inhabited since prehistory; nearby Ulpiana was an important Roman city. Pristina developed as a Balkan trading town under Ottoman rule from the 15th century. In the 20th century it became the capital of the autonomous province of Kosovo within Yugoslavia and grew rapidly. After the Kosovo War (1998–99), Pristina hosted the UN administration (UNMIK), and following Kosovo's 2008 declaration of independence from Serbia it became the capital of the new republic, whose status remains partially disputed internationally.

Landmarks

Notable landmarks include the National Library of Kosovo, famous for its distinctive domed modernist architecture (opened 1982), the Newborn monument unveiled on independence day 2008, the Imperial Mosque (1461), and the Cathedral of Saint Mother Teresa, consecrated in 2017. See also Serbia.
