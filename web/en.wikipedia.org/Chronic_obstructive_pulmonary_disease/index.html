Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a chronic lung disease characterized by persistent respiratory symptoms and airflow limitation that is not fully reversible. It is caused principally by long-term exposure to tobacco smoke and other inhaled particles and gases, which produce chronic bronchitis (inflammation and narrowing of the airways) and emphysema (destruction of the alveoli). COPD is one of the leading causes of death worldwide, responsible for roughly three million deaths per year.

Emphysema destroys alveolar walls, trapping air and reducing gas exchange.

Signs and symptoms

COPD develops slowly. Typical features are progressive breathlessness on exertion, a chronic cough often productive of sputum, and frequent chest infections, especially in winter. Patients may experience acute worsenings called exacerbations, often triggered by infection, which accelerate decline. Advanced disease can cause weight loss, ankle swelling, and respiratory failure.

Causes

Cigarette smoking is by far the dominant risk factor in high-income countries, though only a minority of smokers develop clinically significant COPD, indicating genetic susceptibility. Other causes include occupational dusts and chemicals, indoor air pollution from biomass cooking fuels (a major cause in low-income countries), outdoor air pollution, and, rarely, alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency, an inherited condition that predisposes to early emphysema.

Diagnosis and management

Diagnosis is confirmed by spirometry showing persistent airflow obstruction. Severity is graded by the GOLD system using FEV1 and symptom/exacerbation assessment. No treatment reverses the disease, but smoking cessation slows its progression more than any drug. Inhaled bronchodilators (beta-agonists and muscarinic antagonists) relieve symptoms; inhaled corticosteroids reduce exacerbations in selected patients. Pulmonary rehabilitation, influenza and pneumococcal vaccination, and long-term oxygen therapy for severe resting hypoxemia all improve outcomes.
