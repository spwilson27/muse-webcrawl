Ruan Ji

Ruan Ji (Chinese: 阮籍; pinyin: Ruǎn Jí; Wade–Giles: Juan Chi; 210–263), courtesy name Sizong, was a Chinese musician, poet, and politician of the late Han dynasty and Three Kingdoms period. He was one of the Seven Sages of the Bamboo Grove, a group of scholar-musicians who cultivated Daoist nonconformity under the dangerous politics of the Wei–Jin transition.

Life

Ruan Ji's father, Ruan Yu, was one of the Seven Scholars of Jian'an. Ruan Ji lived through the fall of the Cao Wei to the rising Sima clan; to avoid dangerous political entanglements he adopted eccentric behaviour — drinking heavily, feigning madness, and pointedly breaking Confucian mourning rituals. Tradition credits him with the ability to roll the whites of his eyes at visitors he disliked (the famous "white eyes") while greeting friends warmly.

Works

His greatest literary achievement is the series of 82 Yonghuai ("Singing My Feelings") poems, dense with allusion and melancholy, which profoundly influenced Chinese poetry. He also wrote essays such as the "Biography of Master Great Man" (Daren xiansheng zhuan) and was famed as a player of the guqin; the melody Jiukuang ("Drunken Ecstasy") is attributed to him.
