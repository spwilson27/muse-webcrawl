Hypophosphatemia

Field: Endocrinology, nephrology
Normal phosphate: 2.5–4.5 mg/dL (adults)
Symptoms: Weakness, bone pain, confusion

Hypophosphatemia is an electrolyte disorder in which the blood level of phosphate is abnormally low. Phosphate is essential for energy production (as ATP), bone mineralisation, cell membranes, and oxygen delivery via 2,3-bisphosphoglycerate in red blood cells.

Causes

Causes fall into three groups: redistribution of phosphate from blood into cells (as in refeeding syndrome, insulin treatment of diabetic ketoacidosis, and respiratory alkalosis); decreased intestinal absorption or increased losses (malnutrition, alcohol use disorder, diarrhoea, phosphate-binding antacids, vitamin D deficiency); and increased urinary excretion (hyperparathyroidism, Fanconi syndrome, and FGF23-mediated disorders such as X-linked hypophosphatemia and tumour-induced osteomalacia).

Signs and symptoms

Mild deficiency is often asymptomatic. Severe hypophosphatemia can cause muscle weakness, respiratory failure, heart failure, seizures, coma, rhabdomyolysis, haemolysis, and impaired white cell function. Chronic deficiency produces rickets in children and osteomalacia in adults.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is by blood phosphate measurement; urine phosphate and hormone studies identify the mechanism. Treatment addresses the cause and replaces phosphate orally for mild cases or intravenously for severe or symptomatic cases, with monitoring of calcium and potassium.
