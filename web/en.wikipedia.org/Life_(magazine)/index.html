Life is an American magazine that ran as a weekly from 1936 to 1972 and in various revived formats thereafter. Founded by Henry Luce, it was the first American all-photographic news magazine and defined photojournalism for a generation, dominating the market for more than 40 years with a circulation that peaked at over 13.5 million copies a week.

Earlier humour magazine

The name came from an earlier Life, a general-interest and humour magazine founded in 1883 by John Ames Mitchell and Andrew Miller, known for light verse, cartoons, and social commentary. In 1936 Time Inc. purchased the title, masthead, and goodwill of this magazine (but not its content) for US$92,000 in order to use the name for Luce's new picture magazine.

The Luce photo weekly, 1936–1972

The first issue of Luce's Life, dated November 23, 1936, carried Margaret Bourke-White's photograph of the Fort Peck Dam on its cover and sold out its initial print run. Luce's prospectus stated the magazine's purpose: "To see life; to see the world; to eyewitness great events..." The magazine's staff photographers and contributors — including Alfred Eisenstaedt, Bourke-White, W. Eugene Smith, Gordon Parks, and Robert Capa — produced iconic images of the Second World War, the Korean War, the space race, and American social life. Famous photo essays included Smith's "Country Doctor" (1948) and "Spanish Village" (1951), and the magazine published such enduring images as Eisenstaedt's V-J day in Times Square (1945).

Television and rising production and mailing costs eroded the mass weekly magazines in the 1960s; Life ceased weekly publication with its issue of December 29, 1972.

Later incarnations

From 1973 the title continued in occasional special issues, then as a monthly (1978–2000), and briefly again as a weekly newspaper supplement (2004–2007). In 2008, Time Inc. and Getty Images launched Life.com as an online photo archive, and the magazine's vast photographic archive — some ten million images — was made extensively available online. The brand has since been used for special newsstand editions, and in 2024 Dotdash Meredith announced plans to return Life to regular print publication.
