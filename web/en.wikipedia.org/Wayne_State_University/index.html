Wayne State University (WSU) is a public research university in Detroit, Michigan, United States. Founded in 1868 as the Detroit Medical College, it is Michigan's third-largest university, with an enrollment of roughly 24,000 undergraduate and graduate students, and is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities — Very high research activity". Its main campus of about 200 acres sits in Midtown Detroit, adjacent to the Detroit Medical Center.

History

The university's oldest component, the medical college, was founded in 1868; other units followed, including the Detroit Teachers College and a college of engineering. In 1933 the Detroit Board of Education consolidated its colleges into Wayne University, named for Wayne County (itself named for General Anthony Wayne). The institution joined the state system in 1956, becoming Wayne State University. Old Main, built in 1896 as Detroit's Central High School, remains a campus landmark.

Academics

Wayne State comprises 13 schools and colleges, offering more than 350 academic programs. It is known for its School of Medicine — among the largest in the United States — its law school, the Mike Ilitch School of Business, the College of Engineering, and the Eugene Applebaum College of Pharmacy and Health Sciences. The university is a major urban research institution with particular strengths in health sciences, and its Institute of Gerontology and Karmanos Cancer Institute affiliations anchor its research enterprise.

Campus and student life

Anchoring Detroit's Midtown cultural district, the campus is near the Detroit Institute of Arts, the Detroit Public Library, and the Detroit Symphony Orchestra. Athletic teams, the Warriors, compete in NCAA Division II's Great Lakes Intercollegiate Athletic Conference. Notable alumni include actor and comedian Lily Tomlin, playwright Arthur Miller briefly attended, and many Michigan civic and business leaders.
