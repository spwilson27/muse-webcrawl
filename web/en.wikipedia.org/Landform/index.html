A landform is a natural or artificial feature of the solid surface of the Earth or another planetary body. Landforms together make up a given terrain, and their arrangement is described as topography. They range in scale from molehills and drumlins to continents and ocean basins.

Formation

Landforms are shaped by endogenic processes such as tectonics and volcanism, which build relief, and exogenic processes such as weathering, erosion, and deposition by water, wind, ice, and gravity, which wear it down. Their study is the science of geomorphology, which classifies forms by origin — fluvial, glacial, aeolian, coastal, karst, and tectonic among them.

Examples

Common landforms include mountains, plateaus, plains, valleys, canyons, dunes, deltas, fjords, and moraines. The largest are first-order landforms (continents and ocean basins); mountain ranges and mid-ocean ridges are second-order; individual hills and valleys are third-order features.
