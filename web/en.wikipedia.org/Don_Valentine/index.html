Don Valentine

Don Valentine, founder of Sequoia Capital.

Donald Thomas Valentine (June 26, 1932 – October 25, 2019) was an American venture capitalist who founded Sequoia Capital in 1972. Often described as the "grandfather of Silicon Valley venture capital", Valentine led early investments in companies including Atari, Apple, Electronic Arts, Google, LSI Logic, Oracle, and Cisco Systems.

Early life and career

Valentine was born in Manhattan and grew up in Yonkers, New York. He studied chemistry at Fordham University, graduating in 1954, and then served in the United States Navy. He worked in sales and marketing at Raytheon and at Fairchild Semiconductor, where he was a founding member of the sales team, and later at National Semiconductor.

Sequoia Capital

In 1972 Valentine founded Sequoia Capital in Menlo Park, California. The firm's early successes included the video game company Atari and, in 1977, Apple Computer, in which Sequoia invested when the company was still young. Valentine served on the boards of many portfolio companies and was chairman of Cisco Systems for three decades after leading its first venture round.

Philosophy and legacy

Valentine was known for asking "Who cares?" when evaluating startups, insisting that founders articulate why a product mattered. He favoured investing in markets rather than individuals, remarking that great markets make mediocre management teams look good. He died in Woodside, California, in 2019, aged 87.
