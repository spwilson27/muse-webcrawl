Driving under the influence

Driving under the influence (DUI) is the crime of operating a vehicle while impaired by alcohol or other drugs, including prescribed medication, to a level that renders the driver incapable of operating a motor vehicle safely. Related terms include driving while intoxicated (DWI) and, in the United Kingdom, drink-driving.

Legal standards

Most jurisdictions set a per se blood alcohol concentration (BAC) limit — commonly 0.08 g/dL in the United States, 0.05 in much of Europe and Australia, and 0.02 or zero in some countries; lower limits apply to commercial and novice drivers. Impairment can be proven by officer observation, field sobriety tests, and chemical testing by breathalyser or blood draw; "implied consent" laws make refusal itself penalised. Drug-impaired driving is harder to standardise because drug levels correlate imperfectly with impairment.

Effects and enforcement

Alcohol slows reaction time, impairs judgement and coordination, and raises crash risk sharply with BAC; alcohol-impaired driving is implicated in roughly a third of traffic deaths in the United States. Countermeasures include sobriety checkpoints, ignition interlock devices, licence suspension, fines and imprisonment, together with advocacy by groups such as Mothers Against Drunk Driving (MADD, founded 1980), which helped drive legal and cultural change.
