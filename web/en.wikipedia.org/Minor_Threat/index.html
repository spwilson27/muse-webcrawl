Minor Threat
Origin: Washington, D.C., United States
Years active: 1980–1983
Genre: Hardcore punk
Members: Ian MacKaye, Lyle Preslar, Brian Baker, Jeff Nelson, Steve Hansgen

Minor Threat was an American hardcore punk band formed in Washington, D.C., in 1980 by vocalist Ian MacKaye and drummer Jeff Nelson. Despite a career of only about three years and a small recorded output—two EPs, one studio album, and outtakes—the band is regarded as one of the most influential in hardcore punk, pioneering the straight edge movement and helping define the do-it-yourself ethic through Dischord Records.

History

MacKaye and Nelson had played together in the Teen Idles, whose earnings funded the founding of Dischord Records in 1980. Minor Threat added guitarist Lyle Preslar and bassist Brian Baker and released the EPs Minor Threat (1981) and In My Eyes (1981), the sole studio album Out of Step (1983), and the posthumous Salad Days EP (1985). Their songs were extremely fast, short—many under a minute—and shouted, with lyrics attacking conformity and mindless rebellion as much as authority. Internal tension and the changing D.C. scene led to their breakup in 1983; MacKaye later co-founded Fugazi, and Baker played in Bad Religion.

Straight edge

The 46-second song "Straight Edge" (1981) voiced MacKaye's personal rejection of drugs and alcohol: "I'm a person just like you / But I've got better things to do / Than sit around and smoke dope." Fans adopted the term as the name of a subculture, symbolized by X's drawn on hands—originally marking underage clubgoers who could not be served alcohol. MacKaye has repeatedly said he never intended to found a movement or set of rules, but straight edge became a lasting identity within punk worldwide.

Legacy

Minor Threat's catalog has been kept continuously in print by Dischord at low prices, and Complete Discography (1989) is a canonical hardcore document. The sheep photograph on the cover of Out of Step and Alec MacKaye's image on the first EP are iconic. The band influenced generations of hardcore, metalcore, and emo acts and remains a touchstone for independent music economics.
