Cutin

Cutin is one of two waxy polymers that are the main components of the plant cuticle, which covers all aerial surfaces of terrestrial plants. The other major component is cutan. Cutin is a polyester composed mainly of omega-hydroxy fatty acids and their derivatives, cross-linked by ester bonds into a tough, insoluble network.

Structure and function

The cuticle limits uncontrolled water loss from the plant, protects against ultraviolet radiation, and forms a first barrier against pathogens and insects. Cutin forms the structural framework of the cuticle, into and onto which intracuticular and epicuticular waxes are deposited; these waxes give many leaves their glossy appearance and water-repellent surface.

Biosynthesis

Cutin monomers are synthesised in epidermal cells from C16 and C18 fatty acids, hydroxylated and modified by enzymes including cytochrome P450 hydroxylases, then exported across the cell wall and polymerised by cutin synthases. Mutants defective in cutin production show increased permeability, fused organs, and greater susceptibility to drought and infection.

Related polymers

Cutin is chemically related to suberin, the polyester found in cork and root endodermis, which likewise contains hydroxy fatty acids but also significant amounts of phenolic compounds. Together these lipid polymers were key adaptations in the evolution of land plants.
