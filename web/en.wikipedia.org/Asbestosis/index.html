Asbestosis

Asbestosis
SpecialtyPulmonology, occupational medicine
CauseProlonged inhalation of asbestos fibres
SymptomsShortness of breath, cough, chest pain, clubbing
ComplicationsRespiratory failure, lung cancer, mesothelioma

Asbestosis is a long-term inflammation and scarring of the lungs due to breathing in asbestos fibres. It is a type of pneumoconiosis and interstitial pulmonary fibrosis that typically develops after many years of occupational exposure in mining, construction, shipbuilding, and insulation work.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms usually appear 10–40 years after initial exposure and include progressive breathlessness, a persistent dry cough, chest tightness, loss of appetite, and finger clubbing. Crackles are often heard on auscultation.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis rests on an exposure history combined with chest imaging showing interstitial fibrosis and pleural plaques, and on pulmonary function tests showing a restrictive pattern. There is no cure; management is supportive, including oxygen therapy, pulmonary rehabilitation, vaccination against respiratory infections, and smoking cessation. Prevention depends on eliminating asbestos exposure, which has led to bans or strict regulation in many countries.
