The Moderna COVID-19 vaccine, sold under the brand name Spikevax and codenamed mRNA-1273, is a COVID-19 vaccine developed by the American company Moderna in collaboration with the U.S. National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases (NIAID). It is a lipid nanoparticle–encapsulated messenger RNA (mRNA) vaccine encoding the spike protein of SARS-CoV-2.

Development and authorisation

Moderna designed the vaccine sequence within weeks of the SARS-CoV-2 genome being published in January 2020. Phase III trials involving about 30,000 participants showed roughly 94% efficacy against symptomatic COVID-19. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration issued an Emergency Use Authorization on 18 December 2020, and full approval for adults followed in January 2022. Updated bivalent and variant-adapted formulations were authorised from 2022 onwards.

Use

The vaccine is given by intramuscular injection, originally as two doses four weeks apart, with booster doses recommended for many groups. It must be stored frozen but is stable for months under standard refrigeration. Common side effects include injection-site pain, fatigue, headache, and fever; rare serious events include myocarditis, mainly in young males.

Impact

Hundreds of millions of doses were administered worldwide, making it one of the principal vaccines of the COVID-19 pandemic response.
