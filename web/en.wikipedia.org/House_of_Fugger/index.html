The House of Fugger was a German family of merchants and bankers from Augsburg who rose in the 15th and 16th centuries to become the leading financiers of Europe, bankers to the Habsburgs and the papacy, and for a time among the richest people in the world.

Rise

The family began in the textile trade; the brothers Ulrich, Georg and Jakob Fugger expanded into metal trading — silver and copper from Tyrolean and Hungarian mines — and into lending secured on mining revenues. Jakob "the Rich" (1459–1525) financed the election of Charles V as Holy Roman Emperor in 1519 with over 500,000 florins and held monopolies across European mining and trade.

Peak and decline

Under Jakob's nephew Anton Fugger, the firm's assets peaked around 1546. Its fortunes declined with Spanish crown bankruptcies, the shift of trade to the Atlantic, and division of the inheritance, though branches of the family remain prominent landowners in Bavaria. Jakob's foundations endure: the Fuggerei in Augsburg (1521), the world's oldest social housing complex still in use, charges residents a nominal annual rent.
