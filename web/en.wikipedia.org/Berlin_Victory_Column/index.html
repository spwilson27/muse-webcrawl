Berlin Victory Column

Siegessäule
LocationGroßer Stern, Tiergarten, Berlin, Germany
Completed1873
Height66.89 m (including statue)

The Berlin Victory Column (German: Siegessäule, from Sieg "victory" + Säule "column") is a monumental column in Berlin, Germany, commemorating Prussia's victories in the wars of German unification. Designed by Heinrich Strack, it was built between 1864 and 1873 and stands on the Großer Stern roundabout in the Tiergarten, crowned by a gilded bronze statue of Victoria, the Roman goddess of victory.

Description

The column's shaft is decorated with the gilt barrels of captured cannon and with mosaic friezes depicting the wars against Denmark (1864), Austria (1866), and France (1870–71). The 8.3-metre statue of Victoria, designed by Friedrich Drake and weighing about 35 tonnes, is affectionately known to Berliners as Goldelse ("Golden Lizzy"). A viewing platform beneath the statue, reached by a spiral staircase of 285 steps, overlooks the city.

History

The column originally stood on Königsplatz in front of the Reichstag. In 1938–39 the Nazi regime moved it to its present location and raised it on a taller pedestal as part of the planned monumental axis of "Germania." It survived the Second World War largely intact.
