Conscience

Conscience is a cognitive process that elicits emotion and rational associations based on an individual's moral philosophy or value system. It stands in contrast to elicited emotion or thought due to associations based on immediate sensory perceptions. Conscience is commonly described as leading to feelings of remorse when a person commits an act that conflicts with their moral values, and feelings of rectitude or integrity when actions conform to them.

Philosophical and religious views

In religious thought conscience is often understood as the voice of God or an innate awareness of divine law; in Catholic theology it must be formed by objective moral truth yet followed even when in error. Philosophers have variously treated conscience as the judgement of practical reason (Aquinas), as respect for the moral law within (Kant), or as the internalised voice of society and parental authority (Freud's superego).

In law and psychology

Conscience is protected in international human-rights law as freedom of thought and conscience, the basis of conscientious objection. Psychologists study its development in children through the internalisation of rules, empathy and guilt, and its absence or impairment in certain personality disorders.
