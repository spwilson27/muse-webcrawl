Wikipedia is a free online encyclopedia written and maintained by a community of volunteers, known as Wikipedians, through open collaboration and the wiki software MediaWiki. Founded by Jimmy Wales and Larry Sanger on January 15, 2001, it is the largest and most widely read reference work in history. Wikipedia features articles in over 340 languages, with the English edition containing more than seven million articles.

Wikipedia's content is governed by core policies including a neutral point of view, verifiability, and the exclusion of original research. Anyone with internet access can edit most articles, which has made the site both famously comprehensive and a subject of continuing debate about reliability. The encyclopedia is operated by the non-profit Wikimedia Foundation, based in San Francisco, and is funded primarily by reader donations; it carries no advertising.

History

Wikipedia began as a side project of Nupedia, an expert-written free encyclopedia founded by Wales's company Bomis, with Sanger as editor-in-chief. Because Nupedia's peer-review process was slow, Sanger proposed a wiki-based feeder project, and Wikipedia launched on January 15, 2001. It grew explosively — reaching 20,000 articles within its first year — and quickly eclipsed Nupedia, which shut down in 2003. In June 2003, Wales announced the Wikimedia Foundation to operate the project as a non-profit. Dedicated Wikipedia Day is celebrated on January 15 in commemoration of its launch.

Governance and culture

The community of volunteer editors develops and enforces Wikipedia's policies by consensus. Elected administrators can protect pages and block disruptive users, while an Arbitration Committee serves as a final court of appeal for serious disputes in the English edition. Articles progress through informal quality tiers from "stubs" up to "featured articles", recognised after peer review as the project's finest work. Wikipedia's internal culture, lore, and in-jokes are extensive, and its editors have organised themselves into WikiProjects to coordinate work by topic.

Reception and impact

A 2005 study in Nature found that Wikipedia's science articles were comparable in accuracy to Encyclopaedia Britannica's, though subsequent research has found uneven coverage, systemic bias — including a marked gender gap among editors and biographical subjects — and vulnerability to vandalism, hoaxes, and paid editing. Despite this, Wikipedia consistently ranks among the world's most-visited websites and has become the default first stop for general knowledge online; its content is reused by search engines, digital assistants, and, in the 2020s, as training data and a grounding source for artificial intelligence systems.

Sister projects

The Wikimedia Foundation also operates Wiktionary, Wikiquote, Wikibooks, Wikisource, Wikivoyage, Wikimedia Commons, and Wikidata, among others, all of which share Wikipedia's free-content model.
