Suya

Suya is a spicy grilled meat skewer originating from the Hausa people of northern Nigeria and the wider West African Sahel, including Niger, northern Cameroon and parts of Ghana. It is traditionally made with beef, ram or chicken, and is a popular street food across Nigeria, commonly sold in the evenings by vendors known as mai suya.

Preparation

Thin slices of meat are coated in a dry spice mixture called yaji — a blend of ground peanuts (groundnuts), dried ginger, cayenne and other peppers, garlic, salt and sometimes grains of paradise or ground kuli kuli (defatted peanut paste) — then threaded onto skewers and grilled over an open fire. The peanut coating crisps as it cooks and gives suya its characteristic flavour and heat.

Serving

Suya is usually served wrapped in paper or newspaper with extra yaji on the side, accompanied by sliced raw onions, tomatoes and cucumber. Variations include balangu (ram suya), chicken suya and offal versions using liver, kidney or tripe (abacha-style assorted meats). In Nigeria suya spots are social fixtures, and the dish has spread with the Nigerian diaspora to cities worldwide.
