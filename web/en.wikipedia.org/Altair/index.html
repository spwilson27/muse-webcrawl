Altair (α Aquilae) is the brightest star in the constellation of Aquila and the twelfth-brightest star in the night sky. It lies about 16.7 light-years from Earth and, with Vega and Deneb, forms the prominent Summer Triangle asterism. Its name derives from the Arabic an-nasr aṭ-ṭāʾir, "the flying eagle".

Properties

Altair is an A-type main-sequence star of about 1.8 solar masses and roughly 11 times the Sun's luminosity. It is remarkable for its extremely rapid rotation: it spins once in about 9 hours, compared with the Sun's roughly 25 days, reaching a large fraction of the speed at which it would break apart. This rotation flattens it into an oblate spheroid — its equatorial diameter exceeds its polar diameter by more than 20 per cent — an effect confirmed by direct interferometric imaging in 2007. Rapid rotation also causes gravity darkening, with the poles hotter and brighter than the equator.

Observation and culture

Altair is flanked by the stars Beta and Gamma Aquilae, forming the Eagle's head. In the Chinese legend of the Cowherd and the Weaver Girl, Altair is the cowherd Niulang, separated from Vega across the Milky Way and reunited once a year on the Qixi festival; the tale is also celebrated as Tanabata in Japan.
