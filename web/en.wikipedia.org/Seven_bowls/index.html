Seven bowls

The seven bowls (or seven vials) are a set of plagues mentioned in the Book of Revelation, chapters 15 and 16. Seven angels are given seven golden bowls filled with "the wrath of God," which they pour out upon the earth as the final series of judgements, following the seven seals and the seven trumpets.

The bowls

  1. First bowl: poured on the earth — painful, malignant sores afflict those bearing the mark of the beast.
  2. Second bowl: poured on the sea — the sea becomes like blood and every living creature in it dies.
  3. Third bowl: poured on rivers and springs — they too turn to blood.
  4. Fourth bowl: poured on the sun — the sun scorches people with fire.
  5. Fifth bowl: poured on the throne of the beast — its kingdom is plunged into darkness.
  6. Sixth bowl: poured on the Euphrates — the river dries up, preparing the way for the kings of the East and the gathering at Armageddon.
  7. Seventh bowl: poured into the air — a great earthquake and huge hailstones accompany the cry "It is done!", and Babylon the Great is judged.

Interpretation

Interpreters differ over whether the bowls depict literal future events (futurism), events of John's own era (preterism), recurring patterns in church history (historicism), or symbolic truths about divine judgement (idealism). The imagery echoes the plagues of Egypt in Exodus, a parallel drawn by many commentators.
