Franz Stangl

Franz Stangl (1908–1971), commandant of the Sobibor and Treblinka extermination camps.

Franz Paul Stangl (26 March 1908 – 28 June 1971) was an Austrian police officer and SS officer who served as commandant of the Sobibor and Treblinka extermination camps during Operation Reinhard, the Nazi plan to murder the Jews of occupied Poland. At Treblinka, where an estimated 700,000 to 900,000 people were killed during his tenure, he was known to prisoners as the "White Death" for the white uniform he wore.

Career in the Holocaust

Stangl, a former Austrian policeman and textile worker, joined the Nazi Party and after the Anschluss worked in the Aktion T4 euthanasia programme at Hartheim killing centre. Transferred to Poland in 1942, he organised the construction and operation of Sobibor, then took command of Treblinka in September 1942, greatly increasing the camp's killing capacity and administrative "efficiency".

Escape, capture and trial

After the war Stangl escaped from internment and, aided by the ratlines associated with Bishop Alois Hudal, fled to Syria and then to Brazil, where he lived under his own name and worked at a Volkswagen plant in São Paulo. He was tracked down by Nazi hunter Simon Wiesenthal, arrested in 1967 and extradited to West Germany. Tried in Düsseldorf in 1970, he was convicted of the murder of at least 400,000 people and sentenced to life imprisonment. In prison he gave extensive interviews to journalist Gitta Sereny, published as Into That Darkness (1974), a rare study of a camp commandant's mind. He died of heart failure in prison in June 1971.
