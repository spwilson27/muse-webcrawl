Acoustics

Acoustics is the interdisciplinary science that deals with the study of mechanical waves in gases, liquids, and solids, including topics such as vibration, sound, ultrasound, and infrasound. A scientist who works in the field is known as an acoustician, and the application of acoustics in technology is called acoustical engineering.

Branches

Major branches include architectural acoustics (sound in buildings), musical acoustics, psychoacoustics (how humans perceive sound), electroacoustics (loudspeakers and microphones), underwater acoustics, aeroacoustics, and physical acoustics. Hearing is one of the most crucial means of survival in the animal world, and speech is one of the most distinctive characteristics of human development and culture, so the science of acoustics spreads across many facets of society.

History

The word derives from the Greek akoustos, meaning "heard." Early studies of vibrating strings go back to Pythagoras. Modern acoustics was placed on a firm mathematical footing by Lord Rayleigh's Theory of Sound (1877). Sound waves travel through air at about 343 metres per second at room temperature; they can be described by frequency, wavelength, amplitude, and speed, and exhibit reflection, refraction, diffraction, and interference.
