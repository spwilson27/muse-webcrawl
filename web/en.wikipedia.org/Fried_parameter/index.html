Fried parameter

The Fried parameter, or Fried's coherence length, usually denoted r0, is a measure of the quality of optical transmission through the atmosphere due to random inhomogeneities in its refractive index. It is named after David L. Fried, who introduced the concept in 1965. The Fried parameter describes the size of a typical turbulent cell in the atmosphere: over apertures of diameter comparable to r0, wavefront distortions remain roughly coherent, while larger apertures average over many independent cells.

Definition

Formally, r0 is defined through the integral of the refractive-index structure constant along the propagation path. It scales with wavelength as λ6/5, so seeing is better at longer wavelengths. At a good observatory site at visible wavelengths, r0 is typically about 10–20 cm, corresponding to astronomical seeing of about one arcsecond.

Significance

The Fried parameter sets the effective resolution of a ground-based telescope: telescopes larger than r0 gain little resolution without correction. It is therefore a central design parameter for adaptive optics systems — the actuator spacing and correction bandwidth of an adaptive optics system are chosen relative to r0 and the related coherence time.
