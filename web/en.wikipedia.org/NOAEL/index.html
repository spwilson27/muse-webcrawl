NOAEL

A dose–response curve showing NOAEL and LOAEL

The no-observed-adverse-effect level (NOAEL) denotes the level of exposure of an organism, found by experiment or observation, at which there is no biologically or statistically significant increase in the frequency or severity of any adverse effects in the exposed population compared to an appropriate control group.

In toxicology, the NOAEL is a key part of the process of risk assessment, particularly in setting safe exposure levels for chemicals in food, drinking water, pharmaceuticals and the workplace. It is typically determined in animal studies and then divided by safety (uncertainty) factors — commonly 100, accounting for differences between animals and humans and variability among humans — to derive reference values such as the acceptable daily intake (ADI) or tolerable daily intake (TDI).

Definitions

  • NOAEL — the highest exposure level at which no statistically or biologically significant adverse effect is observed.
  • NOEL — no-observed-effect level: the highest dose at which no effect of any kind (adverse or not) is observed.
  • LOAEL — lowest-observed-adverse-effect level: the lowest dose at which an adverse effect is observed.

Use in regulation

Agencies such as the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) and the FDA use NOAELs from guideline toxicology studies as "points of departure" for setting exposure limits. Regulatory guidance (for example for pharmaceuticals, the ICH M3 guideline) uses the NOAEL in animal studies, converted to a human equivalent dose, as one basis for the maximum recommended starting dose in first-in-human clinical trials.

Limitations

The NOAEL depends entirely on the doses chosen in a study and on statistical power: a poorly designed study with few animals may yield a higher NOAEL simply because effects were not detectable. It also does not use information about the shape of the dose–response curve above and below it. For these reasons, benchmark dose (BMD) modelling is increasingly preferred as an alternative point of departure.
