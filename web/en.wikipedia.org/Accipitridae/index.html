Accipitridae

Scientific classification
Kingdom:Animalia
Phylum:Chordata
Class:Aves
Order:Accipitriformes
Family:Accipitridae

The Accipitridae is one of the three families within the order Accipitriformes, and is a family of small to large birds of prey with strongly hooked bills and variable morphology based on diet. They feed on a range of prey items from insects to medium-sized mammals, with a number feeding on carrion and a few feeding on fruit. The Accipitridae have a cosmopolitan distribution, being found on all the world's continents (except Antarctica) and a number of oceanic island groups. Some species are migratory.

Many well-known birds such as hawks, eagles, kites, harriers and Old World vultures are included in this group. The osprey is usually placed in a separate family (Pandionidae), as is the secretary bird (Sagittariidae), and the New World vultures are now usually placed in a separate family or order. Karyotype data indicate the accipitrids analysed are indeed a distinct monophyletic group.

Description

The Accipitridae vary in size from the tiny pearl kite (Gampsonyx swainsonii) and little sparrowhawk (Accipiter minullus), both around 23 cm (9.1 in) in length and 85 g (3.0 oz) in weight, to the Cinereous Vulture (Aegypius monachus), measuring up to 130 cm (51 in) and weighing up to 12.5 kg (28 lb). They have hooked, powerful beaks adapted for tearing flesh, strong legs and powerful talons. Vision is exceptionally keen, allowing them to detect prey from a great distance.

Diet and behaviour

Accipitrids are predominantly predators and most hunt by sudden dashes from a concealed perch, but they are also innovative, opportunistic feeders. Their diets include mammals, birds, reptiles, fish, amphibians, insects and carrion depending on species. Some, such as the snail kite, are highly specialised feeders; others are generalists. Most species build stick nests in trees or on cliffs and lay one to a few eggs.

Taxonomy

The family includes roughly 250–260 species in about 70 genera, grouped into subfamilies such as the Accipitrinae (true hawks), Buteoninae (buzzards and eagles), Aegypiinae (Old World vultures), Circinae (harriers), Milvinae (kites), and Perninae (honey-buzzards). Molecular studies have repeatedly reshuffled the relationships among these groups.
