Beta-glucan

Beta-glucans comprise a group of β-D-glucose polysaccharides naturally occurring in the cell walls of cereals, bacteria, and fungi, with significantly differing physicochemical properties depending on their source. Typically, β-glucans form long chains of glucose units linked by β-glycosidic bonds — most commonly β(1→3) and β(1→4) linkages in cereals, and β(1→3) with β(1→6) branches in fungi and yeast.

Sources

Oats and barley are the richest cereal sources; oat β-glucan is a soluble dietary fibre concentrated in the bran. Yeast and medicinal and edible fungi, including shiitake and maitake mushrooms, contain particulate β-glucans in their cell walls. Some bacteria, such as Agrobacterium, produce the β(1→3) glucan curdlan.

Health effects

Soluble oat β-glucan increases the viscosity of intestinal contents, slowing glucose absorption and binding bile acids; regulators in several jurisdictions have authorised health claims linking daily consumption of at least 3 grams of oat or barley β-glucan to the maintenance of normal blood cholesterol levels. Fungal and yeast β-glucans are studied for their ability to modulate innate immune responses through receptors such as dectin-1 on macrophages.
