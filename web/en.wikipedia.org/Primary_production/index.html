Primary production

In ecology, primary production is the synthesis of organic compounds from atmospheric or aqueous carbon dioxide, principally through photosynthesis, with a smaller contribution from chemosynthesis. All life on Earth depends directly or indirectly on primary producers—autotrophic organisms such as plants, algae, and cyanobacteria—which form the base of nearly every food web.

Gross and net production

Gross primary production (GPP) is the total amount of carbon fixed by primary producers in an area and time period. Because producers use part of this energy for their own respiration, the remainder—net primary production (NPP)—is the rate at which biomass accumulates and becomes available to consumers. NPP is commonly measured in units of carbon per unit area per year.

Global patterns

Global NPP is roughly evenly divided between terrestrial and marine ecosystems, each fixing on the order of 50 billion tonnes of carbon per year. On land, production is highest in tropical forests and limited by water, temperature, and nutrients; in the oceans, phytoplankton production is concentrated in sunlit, nutrient-rich surface waters such as upwelling zones and high-latitude seas. Satellite remote sensing of ocean colour and vegetation indices now allows continuous global monitoring of primary production.
