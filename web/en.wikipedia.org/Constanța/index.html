Constanța

Constanța is the oldest continuously inhabited city in Romania, founded as the Greek colony of Tomis around 600 BC. Located on the western coast of the Black Sea, it is the fifth most populous city in Romania and the seat of Constanța County. The Port of Constanța is the largest port on the Black Sea and one of the largest in Europe.

History

Tomis was founded by Greek colonists from Miletus. The Roman poet Ovid was exiled here by Augustus in AD 8 and died here, a fact commemorated by Ovid's Square and a statue in the city. The city was renamed in honour of Constantia, the half-sister of Emperor Constantine the Great (274–337). In 1878, after the Romanian War of Independence, Constanța and the rest of Northern Dobruja were ceded by the Ottoman Empire to Romania.

Geography and economy

Constanța lies on the western shore of the Black Sea, about 185 kilometres east of Bucharest. Its economy centers on the port, shipping, petrochemicals (via the nearby Năvodari refinery), and tourism; the Mamaia resort, administered by the city, is Romania's most popular Black Sea beach resort. The Danube–Black Sea Canal links the port to the Danube at Cernavodă.

Landmarks

Sights include the early-20th-century Art Nouveau Casino, the Genoese Lighthouse, the Great Mahmudiye Mosque, the Roman mosaics of ancient Tomis, and the Constanța Museum of National History and Archaeology. The city's population includes significant Turkish, Tatar, Greek, and Aromanian minorities, reflecting its cosmopolitan past.
