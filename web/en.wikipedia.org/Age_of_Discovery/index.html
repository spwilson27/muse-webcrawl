Age of Discovery

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Age of Discovery, also known as the Age of Exploration, was a period in history from the early 15th century to the 17th century during which European powers explored and mapped the world, establishing overseas empires and initiating sustained contact between the Old World and the Americas, Asia, Africa and Oceania.

Background

Several factors drove exploration: the desire for direct trade routes to Asia for spices and silk after the fall of Constantinople in 1453 and Ottoman control of overland routes; advances in shipbuilding (notably the caravel and carrack), navigation (the astrolabe, compass and improved charts); and competition among emerging nation-states such as Portugal and Spain.

Portuguese exploration

Under the patronage of Prince Henry the Navigator, Portuguese sailors pushed down the West African coast, reaching Madeira and the Azores, rounding Cape Bojonius and, in 1488, the Cape of Good Hope under Bartolomeu Dias. In 1498 Vasco da Gama reached India by sea, opening a direct maritime spice route.

Spanish voyages and the Americas

In 1492 Christopher Columbus, sailing for Spain, reached the Americas. Subsequent expeditions — by Amerigo Vespucci, Vasco Núñez de Balboa, and others — revealed two continents previously unknown to Europeans. Ferdinand Magellan's expedition (1519–1522), completed by Juan Sebastián Elcano, achieved the first circumnavigation of the globe. Spanish conquistadors such as Hernán Cortés and Francisco Pizarro overthrew the Aztec and Inca empires.

Later exploration

England, France and the Netherlands later joined the expansion, founding colonies in North America, the Caribbean and Asia, and chartering companies such as the Dutch East India Company (VOC, 1602) and the English East India Company.

Impact

The Age of Discovery brought the Columbian exchange of plants, animals, diseases and technologies between hemispheres, transforming diets and economies worldwide. It also led to the devastation of indigenous populations in the Americas through disease and conquest, the transatlantic slave trade, and the rise of global colonial empires that shaped the modern world.
