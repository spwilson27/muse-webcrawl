The House of Plantagenet was a royal house that originated in Anjou, France, and ruled the Kingdom of England from 1154 to 1485. Its name derives from the nickname of Geoffrey of Anjou, father of King Henry II, who was said to wear a sprig of broom (planta genista) in his helmet.

Angevin kings

Henry II, who came to the English throne in 1154, built the "Angevin Empire", ruling England, Normandy, Anjou, and — through his marriage to Eleanor of Aquitaine — much of western France. His sons Richard I ("the Lionheart") and John succeeded him; John's losses in France and quarrels with the barons led to Magna Carta in 1215.

Later Plantagenets

Under Henry III and Edward I the kingdom was reshaped by law, parliament, and wars in Wales and Scotland. Edward III's claim to the French throne began the Hundred Years' War. In the 15th century the cadet branches of Lancaster and York fought the Wars of the Roses; the main line is usually considered to end with the death of Richard III at Bosworth in 1485, and the male line became extinct with the execution of Edward Plantagenet, Earl of Warwick, in 1499.
