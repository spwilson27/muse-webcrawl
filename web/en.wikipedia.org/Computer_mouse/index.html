Computer mouse

A computer mouse is a hand-held pointing device that detects two-dimensional motion relative to a surface and translates it into movement of a pointer (cursor) on a display. A typical mouse also has buttons and a scroll wheel.

History

The mouse was invented by Douglas Engelbart at the Stanford Research Institute and publicly demonstrated in 1968 in the famous "Mother of All Demos", using a wooden shell with two perpendicular wheels. Engineers at Telefunken developed a ball mouse the same year. Xerox PARC adopted the ball mouse for the Alto (1973), and the device reached mass markets with the Apple Lisa and Macintosh (1983–84) and the spread of graphical user interfaces.

Technology

Mechanical mice used a rubber ball driving roller encoders; optical mice (commercialised from the late 1990s) illuminate the surface with an LED or laser and track movement with a small image sensor. Mice connect by cable, USB, or wireless links such as Bluetooth and proprietary 2.4 GHz receivers.
