Western Front (Soviet Union)

The Western Front bore the brunt of the German invasion in June 1941 and was largely destroyed in the Białystok–Minsk encirclement.

The Western Front (Russian: Западный фронт) was a front — a formation equivalent to an army group — of the Soviet Red Army during the Second World War. It was created on 22 June 1941 from the Western Special Military District at the start of the German invasion of the Soviet Union (Operation Barbarossa), and fought on the central sector of the Eastern Front until it was reorganised as the 3rd Belorussian Front in April 1944.

1941 disasters

Under General Dmitry Pavlov, the Front covered the Białystok salient and the approaches to Minsk. Caught by surprise, its air forces were largely destroyed on the ground, and within two weeks German panzer groups had encircled the bulk of the Front in the Battle of Białystok–Minsk, inflicting catastrophic losses. Pavlov and several senior officers were arrested and executed. Successive commanders — Andrey Yeryomenko and Marshal Semyon Timoshenko — stabilised the front along the Dnieper and at the Battle of Smolensk, delaying the German advance on Moscow, but the Front again suffered heavy losses in the Vyazma encirclement during Operation Typhoon in October 1941. Reinforced, it took part in the defence of Moscow and the Soviet winter counteroffensive of 1941–42.

1942–1944

The Front spent much of 1942 in the bloody and inconclusive Battles of Rzhev against German Army Group Centre. Under Ivan Konev and then Vasily Sokolovsky it conducted the Smolensk offensive of 1943 (Operation Suvorov), liberating Smolensk in September 1943, and repeated, costly offensives towards Orsha and Vitebsk in the autumn and winter of 1943–44. On 24 April 1944 the Front was split and the bulk of it redesignated the 3rd Belorussian Front, which went on to play a central role in Operation Bagration, the destruction of Army Group Centre in the summer of 1944.
