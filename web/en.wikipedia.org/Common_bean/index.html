Common bean

Common bean
Scientific name: Phaseolus vulgaris
Family: Fabaceae
Origin: Americas
Varieties: kidney, pinto, black, navy, cranberry beans

The common bean (Phaseolus vulgaris) is an herbaceous annual plant grown worldwide for its edible dry seeds and green pods. It is one of the most important legume crops for direct human consumption and a staple across Latin America, Africa and parts of Asia.

Domestication

Wild P. vulgaris is native to the Americas. Genetic evidence points to two independent domestications, in Mesoamerica and in the southern Andes, giving rise to distinct gene pools with different seed sizes and types.

Cultivation

Plants occur in bush and climbing (pole) forms. Like other legumes, beans fix nitrogen through symbiosis with rhizobia bacteria, improving soil fertility in rotations with cereals such as maize, with which they are traditionally intercropped in the "Three Sisters" system.

Nutrition and toxicity

Dry beans are rich in protein, complex carbohydrates, folate and iron. Raw kidney beans contain the lectin phytohaemagglutinin, which is destroyed by thorough boiling; slow cookers that fail to reach boiling point can increase toxicity.
