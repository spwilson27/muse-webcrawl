Droeshout portrait

The Droeshout portrait or Droeshout engraving is a portrait of William Shakespeare engraved by Martin Droeshout as the frontispiece for the title page of the First Folio collection of Shakespeare's plays, published in 1623. It is one of only two works of art definitively identifiable as a depiction of the poet; the other is the statue erected for his funerary monument in Stratford-upon-Avon. Both were created posthumously.

Description

The engraving shows Shakespeare's head and shoulders, facing slightly left, wearing a black doublet with a plain white collar. The image exists in three states, with the second and third adding shading to the hair, moustache and eyes. It accompanies verses by Ben Jonson addressed "To the Reader", assuring readers that the engraver "had a strife with Nature, to out-do the life" and urging them to "look not on his picture, but his book".

Authorship and reception

Two engravers bore the name Martin Droeshout: the elder (1560s–c. 1642), a Flemish-born artist working in London, and his nephew Martin Droeshout the younger (1601–1650). Scholars have debated which of them executed the portrait; the signature "Martin Droeshout sculpsit London" appears beneath the image.

Art historians have often criticised the engraving for its stiff, mask-like face, disproportionately large head, and the odd suggestion that the doublet shows the back of the garment on one side and the front on the other. Despite its faults, the Droeshout portrait remains the most widely reproduced image of Shakespeare, and its general features are corroborated by the Stratford bust.
