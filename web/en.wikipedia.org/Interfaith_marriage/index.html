Interfaith marriage

An interfaith marriage, or mixed marriage, is a marriage between partners who profess different religions. Such unions have historically been discouraged or forbidden by many religious traditions, though attitudes have liberalised in much of the world.

By tradition

In Judaism, traditional authorities oppose marriage between Jews and non-Jews, while Reform movements have become more accepting. Roman Catholic canon law requires a dispensation for marriage to a non-Catholic and promises regarding the upbringing of children. Islamic jurisprudence traditionally permits a Muslim man to marry a Jewish or Christian woman but not the reverse. Hindu, Sikh and other traditions vary widely in practice.

Trends

Rates of interfaith marriage have risen substantially in Western countries since the late twentieth century, reflecting secularisation and social mixing; survey research finds such couples navigate questions of festivals, diet and children's religious upbringing in diverse ways.
