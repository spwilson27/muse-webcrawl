Logical fallacy

A logical fallacy is an error in reasoning that renders an argument invalid or unsound, despite often being persuasive. The study of fallacies goes back to Aristotle's Sophistical Refutations.

Classification

Formal fallacies are errors in the logical form of an argument, such as affirming the consequent. Informal fallacies arise from content: fallacies of relevance (ad hominem, straw man, appeal to authority), of ambiguity (equivocation), and of presumption (begging the question, false dilemma, slippery slope).

Common examples

Well-known fallacies include ad hominem attacks, appeals to ignorance, circular reasoning, hasty generalisation, the naturalistic fallacy and the Texas sharpshooter fallacy.

Use

Identifying fallacies is a tool of critical thinking, though a fallacious argument for a claim does not by itself show the claim is false — that error is itself called the fallacy fallacy.
