Adrien-Marie Legendre

Adrien-Marie Legendre (18 September 1752 – 9 January 1833) was a French mathematician who made foundational contributions to number theory, celestial mechanics, elliptic integrals, and statistics. He is one of the 72 names inscribed on the Eiffel Tower.

Work

Legendre's Essai sur la théorie des nombres (1798) was the first systematic treatise on number theory; it contains the law of quadratic reciprocity and the Legendre symbol. In 1805 he published the method of least squares — the subject of a famous priority dispute with Gauss. He systematised the study of elliptic integrals, classifying them into three kinds, and introduced the Legendre polynomials and Legendre transformation used throughout physics and analysis. The Legendre conjecture (that a prime always lies between n² and (n+1)²) remains unproved. His Éléments de géométrie (1794) was a widely used textbook for a century.

Life

Born in Paris into a wealthy family, Legendre studied at the Collège Mazarin and taught at the École Militaire. He worked on the geodetic survey linking Paris and Greenwich, served on commissions that defined the metre, and held posts at the Bureau des Longitudes. A bureaucratic dispute cost him his pension in 1824, and he died in poverty in 1833.
