Dual (category theory)

In category theory, a branch of mathematics, duality is a correspondence between properties of a category C and so-called dual properties of the opposite category Cop. Given a statement that is true in C, interchanging the source and target of every morphism and the order of composing any two morphisms gives a corresponding dual statement that is true in Cop.

Formal definition

The opposite category Cop has the same objects as C, but a morphism from X to Y in Cop is a morphism from Y to X in C. Composition is defined by reversing the order of composition in C. Duality asserts that a statement is true in C if and only if the corresponding dual statement is true in Cop.

Examples

A morphism that is a monomorphism in C is an epimorphism in Cop, and vice versa; products dualise to coproducts, limits to colimits, initial objects to terminal objects, and functors to oppositely-directed functors. Results proved for one concept therefore automatically yield results for its dual, halving the work in many arguments.

Significance

Duality is one of the organising principles of category theory. Many definitions come in dual pairs, indicated by the prefix "co-", such as coproduct, coequaliser and colimit.
