The Russian Social Democratic Labour Party (RSDLP) was a socialist political party founded in Minsk in 1898, uniting various Russian Marxist groups into a single organization. It became the principal vehicle of revolutionary Marxism in the Russian Empire and the parent organization of both the Bolsheviks and the Mensheviks.

History

The party's First Congress in 1898 was broken up by arrests, and effective organization came with the newspaper Iskra ("The Spark"), founded in 1900 by figures including Vladimir Lenin, Julius Martov, and Georgi Plekhanov. At the Second Congress, held in Brussels and London in 1903, the party split over questions of party membership and organization into Lenin's Bolshevik ("majority") faction and Martov's Menshevik ("minority") faction.

Revolution and aftermath

The factions operated as effectively separate parties after 1912, though both participated in the revolutionary events of 1917. Following the February Revolution, Mensheviks joined the Provisional Government's coalition, while the Bolsheviks seized power in the October Revolution and renamed themselves the Russian Communist Party in 1918. The Menshevik party was suppressed in Soviet Russia in the early 1920s.
