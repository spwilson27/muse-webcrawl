Gene Wilder

Born: Jerome Silberman, June 11, 1933, Milwaukee, Wisconsin, U.S.

Died: August 29, 2016 (aged 83), Stamford, Connecticut, U.S.

Occupation: Actor, comedian, writer, director

Years active: 1961–2016

Gene Wilder (born Jerome Silberman; June 11, 1933 – August 29, 2016) was an American actor, comedian, writer, and filmmaker. Known for his neurotic, wide-eyed comic persona, he became one of the most beloved screen comedians of the 1970s through collaborations with director Mel Brooks and a celebrated pairing with Richard Pryor.

Early life

Wilder was born in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, to Jeanne and William J. Silberman. He studied at the University of Iowa and the Bristol Old Vic Theatre School in England. After serving in the United States Army, he studied acting under Lee Strasberg at the Actors Studio in New York City, where he adopted the stage name Gene Wilder, combining the first name of a character from Thomas Wolfe's Look Homeward, Angel with the surname of playwright Thornton Wilder.

Career

Wilder's film breakthrough came with a small role in Bonnie and Clyde (1967), followed by an Academy Award nomination for playing Leo Bloom in Mel Brooks's The Producers (1968). He achieved lasting fame as Willy Wonka in Willy Wonka & the Chocolate Factory (1971). His collaborations with Brooks peaked with Blazing Saddles and Young Frankenstein (both 1974), the latter of which he co-wrote with Brooks, earning another Oscar nomination for its screenplay.

Beginning with Silver Streak (1976), Wilder formed a popular screen duo with comedian Richard Pryor, appearing together in Stir Crazy (1980), See No Evil, Hear No Evil (1989), and Another You (1991). Wilder also wrote, directed, and starred in films including The Woman in Red (1984). He largely retired from screen acting in the 1990s to focus on writing, publishing a memoir, Kiss Me Like a Stranger (2005), as well as novels and short stories.

Personal life and death

Wilder was married four times, most famously to comedian Gilda Radner, whose death from ovarian cancer in 1989 led Wilder to co-found Gilda's Club, a support network for people affected by cancer, and to promote cancer awareness. He died at his home in Stamford, Connecticut, on August 29, 2016, from complications of Alzheimer's disease, a diagnosis he had kept private.
