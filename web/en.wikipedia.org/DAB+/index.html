DAB+

Illustration: DAB+

DAB+ is an upgraded version of the Digital Audio Broadcasting (DAB) standard for digital radio, using the HE-AAC version 2 audio codec (AAC+) instead of the original MPEG-1 Audio Layer II coding. It was adopted as ETSI TS 102 563 in 2007.

Advantages

AAC+ compression is roughly twice as efficient as MP2 at comparable quality, so a DAB+ multiplex can carry more stations, and DAB+ adds stronger Reed–Solomon error correction, improving reception robustness. DAB+ receivers are generally backward compatible with transmissions using the new codec only; original DAB receivers cannot decode DAB+.

Adoption

DAB+ has been adopted across most of Europe, Australia, and parts of Asia and Africa. Norway became the first country to switch off national FM radio, completing the transition in 2017 in favour of DAB+.
