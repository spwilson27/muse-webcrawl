Super Mario 64

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Super Mario 64 is a 1996 platform game developed and published by Nintendo for the Nintendo 64. It is the first Super Mario game to feature 3D gameplay, and one of the system's launch titles in Japan and North America. It is widely regarded as one of the greatest video games of all time and a landmark in the development of 3D platform games.

Gameplay

Players control Mario as he explores Princess Peach's castle, jumping into magical paintings that lead to open, mission-based levels. Collecting Power Stars unlocks new areas; 70 of the 120 Stars are required to face the final boss, Bowser, who has kidnapped the Princess. Mario's moveset — including the triple jump, wall jump, long jump, and ground pound — and the game's free-moving analog camera defined conventions for the genre.

Development

Directed by Shigeru Miyamoto, the game was developed by Nintendo EAD over roughly three years, beginning during the final stages of work for the Super NES. The team experimented extensively with camera systems and character movement in three dimensions, and the soundtrack was composed by Koji Kondo, including the series' first voice acting for Mario by Charles Martinet.

Reception and legacy

Super Mario 64 sold more than eleven million copies and received near-universal acclaim for its ambition, controls, and level design. It was remade as Super Mario 64 DS (2004) and re-released in the Super Mario 3D All-Stars compilation (2020). Its influence on 3D game design — and its enduring speedrunning community — remain substantial.
