Rascia

Rascia (Serbian: Raška, Рашка) was a medieval Serbian state and the name of the region centred on the fortified town of Ras, in the southwestern part of modern Serbia. It formed the historical core of the medieval Serbian kingdom under the Nemanjić dynasty.

History

The principality of Serbia centred on Ras emerged in the 9th–10th centuries under the Vlastimirović dynasty, situated between Byzantine, Bulgarian, and later Hungarian spheres of influence. Under the grand princes (župans) of the Vukanović dynasty in the 12th century, Raška expanded, and under Stefan Nemanja it became the nucleus of the Nemanjić state, which grew into the Serbian Kingdom (1217) and Empire (1346).

Legacy

The names Raška District and the Raška region survive in modern Serbia, and "Rascian" was used in Habsburg-era sources for Serbs. The old town of Ras, together with the monastery of Sopoćani, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
