Chandravansha (Sanskrit: Candravaṃśa, "moon lineage"), also called the Lunar dynasty, is one of the two great legendary royal lineages of Hindu tradition, the other being the Suryavansha (solar dynasty) from which the hero Rama descends. Dynasties claiming Chandravansha descent claim to trace their origin to Chandra, the moon god. The royal house of the Mahabharata — the Kuru clan of Hastinapura, including the Pandavas and Kauravas — belongs to this lineage.

Mythic genealogy

According to the Puranas, the dynasty begins with Budha, son of Chandra, who wed Ila, child of the solar-line progenitor Manu. Their son Pururavas became the first Chandravansha king and is celebrated for his love for the apsara Urvashi. The line continues through Ayu and Nahusha to Yayati, whose five sons — Yadu, Turvasu, Druhyu, Anu, and Puru — founded major branches. From Puru descended Bharata (whose name is linked with "Bhārata" for India) and then the Kuru line: Shantanu, Bhishma, and the warring cousins of the Mahabharata. The Yadu branch (Yadavas) produced Krishna, and is also associated in later tradition with various historical clans and royal houses.

Historical claims

Numerous historical Indian dynasties and Rajput and other clans claimed Chandravansha origin to legitimize their rule, including the Chandelas of Bundelkhand (whose very name is traditionally linked to the moon lineage) and various Yadava-descended houses. Such genealogies, codified in courtly and bardic traditions, mix myth, memory, and political claims and are treated by historians as ideological rather than literal records.
