Italian Football Federation

Crest of the FIGC

The Italian Football Federation (Italian: Federazione Italiana Giuoco Calcio, FIGC) is the governing body of football in Italy. Founded in Turin on 26 March 1898, it is headquartered in Rome and governs the national football leagues and the Italy national teams. The FIGC is a founding member of both FIFA (1905) and UEFA (1954).

Responsibilities

The federation oversees professional football (in coordination with the Lega Serie A and other leagues), amateur and youth football, women's football, futsal and beach soccer, refereeing appointments and disciplinary matters, as well as the men's and women's national teams and their age-group sides.

National team success

Under the FIGC, the Italy men's national team (the Azzurri) won the FIFA World Cup four times (1934, 1938, 1982 and 2006), the UEFA European Championship twice (1968 and 2020) and an Olympic gold medal in 1936. Italy hosted the World Cup in 1934 and 1990 and the European Championship in 1968 and 1980.

History highlights

The first Italian championship was staged in a single day in May 1898, won by Genoa. The federation weathered schisms in the 1920s, administered the game under Fascism (when league formats were reorganized and the round-robin Serie A created in 1929), and managed major crises including the 1980 Totonero and 2006 Calciopoli scandals.
