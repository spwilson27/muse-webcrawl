Swing music

A swing big band of the 1930s.

Swing music is a style of jazz that developed in the United States during the late 1920s and early 1930s. It became nationally popular music in the United States during the late 1930s through the mid-1940s. The name derives from its emphasis on the off-beat, or nominally weaker beat, and on a propulsive rhythmic "swing" feel.

Swing bands usually featured soloists who would improvise on the melody over a walking bass line, piano comping, and a steady drum pattern, with arranged horn sections providing riffs and harmonies.

History

The swing era is often dated from Benny Goodman's celebrated 1935 engagement at the Palomar Ballroom in Los Angeles. Major bandleaders included Duke Ellington, Count Basie, Glenn Miller, Artie Shaw, Tommy Dorsey, and Chick Webb; singers such as Ella Fitzgerald and Billie Holiday rose to fame with swing bands. Swing was the dominant popular music for dancing, heard in ballrooms and on radio broadcasts nationwide.

Decline and legacy

By the mid-1940s the big-band era waned under wartime pressures and changing tastes, giving way to bebop and rhythm and blues. Swing rhythms and big-band instrumentation continue to influence jazz, popular music, and modern swing revivals.
