ISO 9001 is an international standard that specifies requirements for a quality management system (QMS). Organizations use the standard to demonstrate the ability to consistently provide products and services that meet customer and regulatory requirements, and it is the most widely used standard in the ISO 9000 series and the only one in the series to which organizations can be certified.

Requirements structure

ISO 9001:2015 is organized according to the Annex SL high-level structure in ten clauses: (1) Scope; (2) Normative references; (3) Terms and definitions; (4) Context of the organization; (5) Leadership; (6) Planning; (7) Support; (8) Operation; (9) Performance evaluation; (10) Improvement. The standard follows the Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA) cycle and applies a process approach combined with risk-based thinking. Unlike earlier editions, the 2015 version does not require a quality manual or documented procedures in the prescriptive sense, referring instead to "documented information".

Principles

The standard is based on seven quality management principles: customer focus, leadership, engagement of people, process approach, improvement, evidence-based decision making, and relationship management.

Certification

Certification is granted by independent, accredited certification bodies after audits of the organization's QMS (a Stage 1 documentation review and Stage 2 implementation audit). Certificates are valid for three years subject to annual surveillance audits. More than one million ISO 9001 certificates have been reported in ISO's annual survey across nearly 200 countries; the largest numbers are in China and Europe.

Criticism

Critics argue that certification can become a paperwork exercise driven by procurement requirements rather than genuine quality improvement, and that conformity to the standard does not itself guarantee product quality. Nonetheless ISO 9001 remains a de facto condition of trade in many supply chains.
