Along the River During the Qingming Festival

Detail of the original scroll: the great Rainbow Bridge over the Bian River.

Along the River During the Qingming Festival (Chinese: 清明上河圖) is a panoramic handscroll painting by the Song dynasty artist Zhang Zeduan (1085–1145). One of the most famous works of Chinese art, it captures the daily life of people in Bianjing (present-day Kaifeng), the capital of the Northern Song, during the Qingming festival.

The painting

The scroll is about 25 cm high and over five metres long, and contains more than 800 human figures together with animals, boats, carts, shops and houses. Read from right to left, it moves from quiet countryside through busy suburbs to the crowded city gate, with the dramatic centre at the Rainbow Bridge, where a boat with its mast half-lowered struggles against the current beneath a dense crowd.

Interpretation

The painting is celebrated as a vivid document of urban life, commerce and technology in twelfth-century China. Some scholars have read into its crowded scenes of disorder and unattended defences a subtle warning to the emperor about the fragility of the dynasty, which fell to the Jurchen Jin shortly after the scroll was painted.

Copies and legacy

The original is preserved in the Palace Museum in Beijing. Numerous copies were made in later dynasties, including a celebrated Ming dynasty version and an eighteenth-century Qing court version now in the National Palace Museum in Taipei. An animated version was a centrepiece of the China Pavilion at Expo 2010 in Shanghai.
