Half-Life is a 1998 first-person shooter game developed by Valve and published by Sierra Studios for Windows. It was Valve's debut product and the first game in the Half-Life series. The player assumes the role of Gordon Freeman, a theoretical physicist whose experiment at the Black Mesa Research Facility accidentally opens interdimensional portals, releasing hostile alien creatures into the facility.

Gameplay

Unlike most contemporary shooters, Half-Life presents its story entirely in real time through scripted sequences viewed from Freeman's perspective, with no cutscenes, and the player almost never loses control of the character. Combat alternates between gunfights with soldiers of the Hazardous Environment Combat Unit — a military clean-up force sent to silence the facility — and encounters with alien creatures such as headcrabs, Vortigaunts and the giant tentacles of the Blast Pit. Freeman's equipment includes the iconic crowbar, an arsenal of firearms and experimental weapons, and the HEV suit, whose computer voice reports health and hazards. Environmental puzzles and platforming are integrated into the campaign.

Plot

Freeman, a 27-year-old MIT graduate, works in the Anomalous Materials laboratory at Black Mesa in New Mexico. During an experiment on a crystal sample of unknown origin, a "resonance cascade" tears open a portal to the border world Xen. Freeman fights through the ruined facility, launches a satellite to help close the rift, and is captured and later freed before travelling to Xen itself. There he destroys the Nihilanth, the entity holding the rift open. He is then confronted by the enigmatic G-Man, who offers him employment; the game ends with Freeman entering a portal into stasis.

Development

Valve, founded in 1996 by former Microsoft employees Gabe Newell and Mike Harrington, licensed id Software's Quake engine and extensively rewrote it, producing the GoldSrc engine with skeletal animation and improved artificial intelligence. Dissatisfied with the game's progress, the team famously scrapped and redesigned most of it a year before release; the redesigned game was unveiled at E3 1997 to strong interest. Science fiction author Marc Laidlaw shaped the story and level concepts.

Reception and legacy

Half-Life was a critical and commercial success, winning more than fifty PC Game of the Year awards and selling over nine million copies by 2008. Reviewers praised its narrative presentation, enemy AI and level design, and it is routinely listed among the greatest video games ever made. Its software development kit made it a foundation of the modding scene, spawning Counter-Strike, Team Fortress Classic and Day of Defeat. A sequel, Half-Life 2, followed in 2004.
