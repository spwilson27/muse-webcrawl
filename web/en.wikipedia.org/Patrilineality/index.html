Patrilineality, also known as the male line, the spear side or agnatic kinship, is a common kinship system in which an individual's family membership derives from and is recorded through their father's lineage. It generally involves the inheritance of property, rights, names or titles by persons related through male kin. A patriline ("father line") is a person's line of descent from a male ancestor to a descendant (of either sex) through intervening male ancestors.

In kinship and inheritance

In patrilineal societies, individuals belong to their father's descent group (lineage or clan), and inheritance of land, office and surnames typically passes from father to children. Patrilineal descent often correlates with patrilocal residence, in which a married couple lives with or near the husband's family. Many societies in Africa, the Middle East, South and East Asia have historically been patrilineal, as were the ancient Romans — whose legal concept of agnatio traced kinship exclusively through males.

Surnames and titles

Family names in much of Europe, China, Korea, Japan and the Islamic world traditionally pass patrilineally, though practices vary and many countries now permit alternatives. Hereditary titles and thrones in monarchies commonly followed male-preference or strictly agnatic succession (such as the Salic law), under which women could neither inherit nor transmit claims; many European monarchies have since adopted absolute primogeniture.

Genetic genealogy

In genetic genealogy, the patriline corresponds to Y-chromosome descent: the Y chromosome passes from father to son largely unchanged, allowing researchers to trace direct paternal lines and define Y-chromosomal haplogroups. The patrilineal counterpart of the "mitochondrial Eve" is termed Y-chromosomal Adam.

Religious and legal traditions

Some traditions define religious status partly patrilineally: in Zoroastrianism and in historical understandings of Jewish identity for priestly status (kohen and levite descent is patrilineal), while Jewish communal membership is classically matrilineal. Roman Catholic and Orthodox canon law and heraldry also employ agnatic concepts of descent.
