Machine ethics
Field: Ethics of artificial intelligence
Also called: Machine morality, computational ethics

Machine ethics (or machine morality, computational morality, or computational ethics) is a part of the ethics of artificial intelligence concerned with adding or ensuring moral behaviours and ethics considerations in man-made machines that use artificial intelligence, otherwise known as artificial intelligent agents.

Approaches

Research divides roughly into top-down approaches, which implement explicit ethical theories such as deontology or consequentialism, bottom-up approaches, which learn values from examples, and hybrid approaches. The trolley problem is frequently used as a test case, for example for autonomous vehicles.

Related topics

Machine ethics is distinct from roboethics (human use of robots) and from AI alignment research, though it overlaps both.
