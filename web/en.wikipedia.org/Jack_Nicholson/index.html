Jack Nicholson

Born: 22 April 1937, Neptune City, New Jersey, U.S.
Occupation: Actor, filmmaker
Years active: 1955–2010
Awards: 3 Academy Awards from 12 nominations

John Joseph Nicholson (born 22 April 1937) is an American retired actor and filmmaker, regarded as one of the greatest actors of the twentieth century. In a career of more than fifty years he played a wide range of rebellious, neurotic and charismatic antiheroes, and he is the most nominated male actor in Academy Awards history, with twelve nominations and three wins.

Early life

Nicholson was born in Neptune City, New Jersey, and raised in nearby Manasquan by John and Ethel Nicholson, whom he believed to be his father and mother. In fact, Ethel was his grandmother; the woman he thought was his sister June was his mother — a fact he learned only in 1974, from a Time magazine researcher, after both women had died. He moved to Los Angeles at 17, worked in MGM's animation department, and trained as an actor, spending the 1960s in low-budget films, many for Roger Corman, and as a screenwriter (The Trip, Head).

Breakthrough and peak years

His breakthrough came as the drunken lawyer George Hanson in Easy Rider (1969), earning his first Oscar nomination. In the 1970s he gave a string of defining performances of the New Hollywood era: Five Easy Pieces (1970), The Last Detail (1973), Chinatown (1974) as private eye Jake Gittes, and One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest (1975), for which he won the Academy Award for Best Actor. He starred as Jack Torrance in Stanley Kubrick's The Shining (1980) and won a Best Supporting Actor Oscar for Terms of Endearment (1983).

Later career

Nicholson remained a major star through the 1980s and 1990s — Batman (1989), in which his Joker earned him a fortune in profit participation; A Few Good Men (1992); and As Good as It Gets (1997), which brought his third Oscar. Acclaimed later performances included About Schmidt (2002) and The Departed (2006). After How Do You Know (2010), he quietly retired from acting. A famous courtside fixture at Los Angeles Lakers games and a major art collector, Nicholson holds the Golden Globe Cecil B. DeMille Award (1999) and was a Kennedy Center honoree (2001).
