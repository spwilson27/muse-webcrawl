Repentigny, Quebec

Repentigny
Province: Quebec
Region: Lanaudière
Founded: 1670
Population: approx. 86,000

Repentigny is an off-island suburb of Montreal, in southwestern Quebec, Canada, situated on the north bank of the Saint Lawrence River at its junction with the L'Assomption River. It is part of the Lanaudière region and is one of the oldest municipalities founded by French settlers in the area.

History

Repentigny was founded in 1670 by Jean-Baptiste Le Gardeur de Repentigny, who received the seigneury from the Company of New France. Agriculture dominated the local economy until the postwar suburban boom transformed it into a commuter town.

Demographics and transport

The city is served by the Repentigny commuter rail station on the Mascouche line of the Exo network, and by Autoroutes 40 and 640. The Le Gardeur and Lepage bridges connect it to surrounding communities.
