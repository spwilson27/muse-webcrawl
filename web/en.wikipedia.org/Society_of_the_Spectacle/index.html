The Society of the Spectacle (French: La Société du spectacle) is a 1967 work of philosophy and Marxist critical theory by Guy Debord, in which the author argues that modern society has replaced authentic lived experience with its representation: "All that once was directly lived has become mere representation."

The spectacle

For Debord, the spectacle is not simply a collection of images or mass media but "a social relation among people, mediated by images" — the mature form of commodity fetishism, in which consumption and media organise passivity and separation. He distinguished the concentrated spectacle of dictatorships from the diffuse spectacle of consumer democracies, and in his 1988 Comments described their merger into an "integrated spectacle".

Key concepts

The book develops ideas central to the Situationist International, including détournement (the subversive reuse of existing cultural material), dérive, and the critique of urbanism and leisure. Its 221 numbered theses echo the style of Marx and Feuerbach.

Influence

Published on the eve of the May 1968 events in France, the book became a classic of media criticism and has been widely applied to advertising, celebrity culture, politics, and social media. Debord himself directed a film version in 1973.
