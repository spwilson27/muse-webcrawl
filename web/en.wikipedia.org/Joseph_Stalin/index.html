Joseph Stalin

Born: 18 December 1878, Gori, Russian Empire

Died: 5 March 1953, Moscow, Soviet Union

Leader of the USSR: 1924–1953

Joseph Vissarionovich Stalin (born Ioseb Besarionis dze Jughashvili) was a Soviet politician and dictator who led the Soviet Union from the mid-1920s until his death in 1953. As General Secretary of the Communist Party and later premier, he transformed the USSR into an industrial and military superpower through centralised command policies, at the cost of millions of lives.

Early life

Born in Gori, Georgia, to a cobbler father, Stalin studied at a theological seminary in Tiflis before joining the Marxist Russian Social Democratic Labour Party. He became a Bolshevik organiser, editor of Pravda, and participant in revolutionary activity and exiles to Siberia.

Rise to power

After the 1917 Revolution and Lenin's death in 1924, Stalin outmanoeuvred rivals including Leon Trotsky, consolidating power through his control of the party apparatus and promoting "Socialism in One Country".

Rule

His Five-Year Plans imposed rapid industrialisation and collectivisation of agriculture, contributing to famine including the Holodomor in Ukraine. The Great Purge of 1936–38 executed or imprisoned hundreds of thousands. During the Second World War — the Great Patriotic War — the USSR, after initially signing the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact with Nazi Germany, joined the Allies after the German invasion of 1941 and played a decisive role in defeating Germany, at enormous human cost.

Legacy

After his death, Nikita Khrushchev denounced his cult of personality in the 1956 Secret Speech, beginning de-Stalinisation. Historians debate his record: industrial and wartime achievement against terror, famine and the Gulag system. See also Socialist realism, his favoured cultural doctrine.
