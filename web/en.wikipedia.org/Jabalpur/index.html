Jabalpur

Jabalpur
District: Jabalpur district
State: Madhya Pradesh, India
Area: approx. 374 km²
Population: approx. 1.27 million (2011)

Jabalpur is a city in the Indian state of Madhya Pradesh, situated on the banks of the Narmada River in the Mahakoshal region. It is one of the largest cities in the state and serves as an important administrative, industrial and educational centre. The city is known for the Marble Rocks at Bhedaghat, where the Narmada flows through a gorge of white marble cliffs.

History

The region around Jabalpur was ruled in turn by the Kalchuri dynasty, whose capital was at nearby Tripuri, and later by the Gond kings. It came under Maratha control in the 18th century and was ceded to the British after the Third Anglo-Maratha War in 1818. During British rule it became an important military cantonment and railway junction. The city played a notable role in the Indian independence movement.

Geography and climate

Jabalpur lies at an elevation of about 411 metres. It has a humid subtropical climate with hot summers, a monsoon season from June to September, and mild winters. The Bargi Dam on the Narmada, upstream of the city, supplies water and hydroelectric power.

Economy and education

The city hosts ordnance factories, the Gun Carriage Factory and Vehicle Factory Jabalpur, making defence production a major employer. It is home to Rani Durgavati University, Jawaharlal Nehru Agricultural University and an Indian Institute of Information Technology. Jabalpur is also the headquarters of the Madhya Pradesh High Court.

Tourism

Major attractions include the Marble Rocks and Dhuandhar Falls at Bhedaghat, the Madan Mahal fort, the Balancing Rock, and the Rani Durgavati Museum.
