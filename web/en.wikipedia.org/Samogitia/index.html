Samogitia (Lithuanian: Žemaitija; Samogitian: Žemaitėjė) is one of the five historical ethnographic regions of Lithuania, located in the northwest of the country. Its traditional capital is Telšiai, and the region is known for its distinct Samogitian dialect, often considered a separate language by its speakers.

History

Samogitia was long a contested territory between the Lithuanian tribes and the Teutonic Order. The region was repeatedly ceded to the Order and recovered, most notably after the Battle of Grunwald in 1410. Samogitia was among the last regions of Europe to be Christianised, formally adopting Christianity in 1413. During the period of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth it formed the Duchy of Samogitia within the Grand Duchy of Lithuania.

Geography

The region is a lowland area characterised by rolling hills, forests and numerous lakes. It borders the Baltic Sea coast near Palanga and extends inland toward the Dubysa and Venta rivers.

Culture

Samogitians are known for their strong regional identity, preserved folk traditions, and characteristic cuisine, including dishes such as kastinis, a spiced sour-cream spread. The region's coat of arms features a black bear on a silver field.
