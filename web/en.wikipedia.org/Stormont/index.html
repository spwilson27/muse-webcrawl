Stormont

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Parliament Buildings at Stormont, Belfast.

Stormont may refer first to the Stormont Estate in east Belfast, Northern Ireland, but by metonymy it denotes the devolved institutions of Northern Ireland that sit there — above all the Northern Ireland Assembly, housed in Parliament Buildings, and the Northern Ireland Executive.

Parliament Buildings

Parliament Buildings, designed by Arnold Thornely in Greek neoclassical style and opened in 1932, was built as the seat of the Parliament of Northern Ireland created by the Government of Ireland Act 1920. It stands at the end of a mile-long avenue on the Stormont Estate and is fronted by a statue of Edward Carson.

Devolution

After the old parliament was abolished in 1973, the estate hosted various administrations. Under the Belfast (Good Friday) Agreement of 1998, the Northern Ireland Assembly and the power-sharing Executive were established at Stormont. The Assembly has been suspended several times — notably 2002–2007 and 2017–2020 — during which Northern Ireland was run from Westminster.

The estate

The grounds also include Stormont Castle, the official residence of the First Minister and the site of the Executive offices, and Stormont House.
