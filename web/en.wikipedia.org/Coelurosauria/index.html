Coelurosauria

Coelurosauria is a clade of theropod dinosaurs that includes most feathered dinosaurs and all modern birds. The name means "hollow-tailed lizards," from the Greek koilos (hollow), oura (tail), and sauros (lizard). Coelurosaurs first appeared in the Late Jurassic, roughly 165 million years ago, and survived the Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event through their avian descendants.

Characteristics

Coelurosaurs are generally distinguished by a long, narrow hand with three functional fingers, a brain large relative to body size, and hollow bones. Many members bore feathers, ranging from simple filamentous structures in small forms such as Sinosauropteryx to fully developed flight feathers in birds. Most were carnivorous or omnivorous, though some lineages, such as the therizinosaurs, became herbivorous.

Classification

Major coelurosaur groups include the tyrannosauroids, ornithomimosaurs, alvarezsauroids, therizinosaurs, oviraptorosaurs, dromaeosaurids, troodontids, and Avialae (birds and their closest relatives). The group sits within Tetanurae, and its exact internal relationships remain an active area of research.

Evolution

During the Cretaceous, coelurosaurs diversified into a wide range of body plans and sizes, from tiny insect-eaters to the enormous Tyrannosaurus rex. The evolution of flight within the group led to Aves, the only dinosaur lineage to survive to the present day, with more than 11,000 living species.
