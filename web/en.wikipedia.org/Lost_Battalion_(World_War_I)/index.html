Lost Battalion (World War I)

The Lost Battalion is the name given to elements of the US 77th Division — principally the 1st Battalion, 308th Infantry, under Major Charles Whittlesey — that were cut off and surrounded by German forces in the Argonne Forest during the Meuse-Argonne Offensive in October 1918.

For several days some 550 men held a small pocket near Binarville, short of food, water and ammunition, enduring both enemy attacks and friendly artillery fire. Their last carrier pigeon, Cher Ami, delivered a message that helped stop the barrage despite being badly wounded. About 194 men emerged when relief arrived. The episode became one of the most famous American actions of the First World War.
