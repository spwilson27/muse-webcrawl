Creel, Chihuahua

Country: Mexico
State: Chihuahua
Municipality: Bocoyna
Elevation: about 2,345 m
Designation: Pueblo Mágico

Creel is a town in the Sierra Tarahumara of the Mexican state of Chihuahua, in the municipality of Bocoyna. Situated at roughly 2,345 metres in the Sierra Madre Occidental, it is the principal gateway town for tourism in the Copper Canyon (Barrancas del Cobre) region and a major station on the Chihuahua al Pacífico ("Chepe") railway.

History

The town was founded in 1907 as a railway station and named after Enrique Creel, then governor of Chihuahua and a promoter of the railroad. Logging was long the economic mainstay; the surrounding pine-oak forests supplied mills served by the rail line. Creel was designated a Pueblo Mágico by Mexico's tourism ministry in 2007.

Tourism and culture

Visitors use Creel as a base for reaching Divisadero, the Urique Canyon, Lake Arareco, the Valley of the Mushrooms and Valley of the Frogs (rock formations near the Tarahumara community of San Ignacio), Cusárare Falls, and the Basaseachic Falls. The area is the homeland of the Rarámuri (Tarahumara) people, renowned for long-distance running, whose crafts are sold in the town. The regional airport near Creel opened in the 2020s to improve access to the canyon country.
