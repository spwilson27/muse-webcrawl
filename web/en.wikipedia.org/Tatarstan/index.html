Tatarstan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Tatarstan (Russian: Татарстан; Tatar: Татарстан), officially the Republic of Tatarstan, is a republic of Russia located in Eastern Europe, at the confluence of the Volga and Kama rivers. It is one of the most economically developed regions of Russia. Its capital and largest city is Kazan, an important centre of Islam in Russia and host of the 2013 Summer Universiade and matches of the 2018 FIFA World Cup.

Geography and population

Tatarstan covers about 68,000 square kilometres and has a population of roughly four million, divided mainly between ethnic Tatars (about 53 per cent) and ethnic Russians (about 40 per cent), with Chuvash and other minorities. The official languages are Tatar and Russian. Major industries include oil production (the Romashkino field), petrochemicals, the KamAZ truck plant in Naberezhnye Chelny, and aircraft manufacturing in Kazan.

History

The region was the heartland of Volga Bulgaria, which adopted Islam in 922, and later of the Khanate of Kazan, conquered by Ivan the Terrible in 1552 — an event commemorated by St Basil's Cathedral in Moscow. The Tatar Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic was created in 1920. In 1990 Tatarstan declared state sovereignty, and a 1994 bilateral treaty with Moscow granted it unusually broad autonomy, though this was progressively curtailed in the 2000s. The republic's head was long styled President; the title was changed to Rais (an Arabic-derived term for leader) in 2023.
