The 1984 Rajneeshee bioterror attack was the deliberate contamination of salad bars at ten restaurants in The Dalles, Oregon, United States, with Salmonella bacteria by a group of followers of the Indian guru Bhagwan Shree Rajneesh (later known as Osho). The attack sickened 751 people and hospitalized 45; there were no deaths. It is regarded as the largest bioterrorist attack in United States history.

Background

In 1981 followers of Rajneesh founded the intentional community of Rajneeshpuram on a ranch in Wasco County, Oregon. Conflicts with local residents and county officials over land use and the community's expansion turned into a struggle for control of the Wasco County government.

The attack

Hoping to incapacitate voters opposed to Rajneeshpuram candidates in the November 1984 county elections, senior commune members including Ma Anand Sheela, Rajneesh's personal secretary, cultured Salmonella enterica serotype Typhimurium and sprinkled it on produce at salad bars in The Dalles, the county seat. Two county commissioners were also poisoned with contaminated drinking water during an earlier test. The poisonings were initially attributed to poor food handling.

Investigation and aftermath

The attack was not publicly identified as deliberate until 1985, when Rajneesh himself accused Sheela and her associates of the crimes after she fled the commune. Investigators found a vial of Salmonella matching the outbreak strain in a laboratory at Rajneeshpuram. Sheela and Ma Anand Puja were convicted of attempted murder and other crimes and served prison sentences; Rajneesh pleaded guilty to immigration fraud and was deported from the United States.

Significance

The incident remains the only large-scale bioterror attack on American soil carried out by a religious movement, and it is frequently cited in discussions of agroterrorism and food defence.
