Woodwind instrument

Woodwind instruments
ClassificationWind instruments (aerophones)
Sound productionReed(s) or an air edge
ExamplesFlute, oboe, clarinet, bassoon, saxophone

A woodwind instrument is a wind instrument whose sound is produced by air vibrating within a tube, set in motion either by the player's breath through one or two reeds or by air blown across an edge. The pitch is controlled by opening and closing holes along the body, which changes the effective length of the vibrating air column.

Types

Woodwinds fall into two families. Flutes (including the piccolo and the recorder) produce sound when air strikes a sharp edge. Reed instruments use a vibrating strip of cane or synthetic material: single-reed instruments include the clarinet and saxophone, while double-reed instruments include the oboe, cor anglais and bassoon.

Materials

Despite the name, woodwinds are not necessarily made of wood: modern flutes and saxophones are metal, and clarinets may be plastic, though oboes, bassoons and orchestral clarinets are traditionally made from dense hardwoods such as grenadilla. The name derives from the instruments' historical construction and distinguishes them from the brass family, whose sound is produced by the player's lips.

In the orchestra

The standard orchestral woodwind section sits at the centre of the orchestra and typically comprises pairs of flutes, oboes, clarinets and bassoons, often supplemented by piccolo, cor anglais, bass clarinet and contrabassoon. Woodwinds supply much of the orchestra's colour and agility.
