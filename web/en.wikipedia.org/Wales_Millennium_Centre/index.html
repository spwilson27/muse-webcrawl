Wales Millennium Centre


Location: Cardiff Bay, Cardiff, Wales
Opened: 26 November 2004
Architect: Jonathan Adams (Percy Thomas Architects)
Main hall: Donald Gordon Theatre, 1,897 seats
Nickname: "The Armadillo"

The Wales Millennium Centre (Welsh: Canolfan Mileniwm Cymru) is an arts centre in Cardiff Bay, Cardiff, Wales. One of the country's most recognisable modern buildings, it is home to the Welsh National Opera and several other national performing arts companies, and hosts opera, ballet, dance, contemporary music and large-scale West End musicals.

Architecture

Designed by Jonathan Adams of Percy Thomas Architects, the building is constructed from Welsh materials: multi-coloured slate from Welsh quarries, native hardwoods, and a steel shell coated in bronze-coloured metal intended to evoke the industrial heritage of South Wales. Its layered form earned it the popular nickname "the Armadillo". Above the entrance, huge letter-shaped windows carry a bilingual inscription devised by the poet Gwyneth Lewis: in Welsh, Creu Gwir fel Gwydr o Ffwrnais Awen ("Creating truth like glass from the furnace of inspiration"), and in English, "In These Stones Horizons Sing".

History and residents

The centre replaced an earlier abandoned scheme for a Cardiff Bay opera house designed by Zaha Hadid, which collapsed in the mid-1990s amid funding controversy. Built with lottery and Welsh Assembly funding, the Millennium Centre opened in November 2004 with a gala weekend of performances. Resident organisations include Welsh National Opera, the BBC National Orchestra of Wales (based in the 350-seat Hoddinott Hall, opened 2009), National Dance Company Wales, Literature Wales and the youth organisation Urdd Gobaith Cymru. In science fiction, the building's plaza and its water tower became famous as the Cardiff rift location in Doctor Who and its spin-off Torchwood.
