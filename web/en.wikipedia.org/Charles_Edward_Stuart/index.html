Charles Edward Louis John Sylvester Maria Casimir Stuart (31 December 1720 – 31 January 1788), often called Bonnie Prince Charlie, was the elder son of James Francis Edward Stuart and the Jacobite claimant to the thrones of England, Scotland, and Ireland from 1766 as Charles III. He is best remembered for leading the Jacobite rising of 1745, the last serious attempt to restore the House of Stuart, which ended in defeat at the Battle of Culloden.

Early life

Born and raised in Rome, where his exiled father maintained a court recognized by the papacy, Charles was groomed for a restoration. He gained military experience at the Siege of Gaeta in 1734 and cultivated support among Jacobite exiles and the French court. When France's planned invasion of England collapsed in 1744, Charles borrowed money, purchased arms, and resolved to land in Scotland with only a handful of companions.

The Forty-Five

Landing on Eriskay in the Outer Hebrides in July 1745, Charles raised his father's standard at Glenfinnan on 19 August. His Highland army took Edinburgh and routed government forces at Prestonpans, then invaded England, reaching Derby before his council, fearing isolation and lacking the promised French and English Jacobite support, voted to retreat. Pursued by the Duke of Cumberland's army, the Jacobites made their last stand at Culloden on 16 April 1746 and were crushed.

Exile

After five months as a fugitive in the Highlands and islands — aided famously by Flora MacDonald, who helped him escape disguised as her maid "Betty Burke" — Charles escaped to France. A £30,000 price on his head produced no betrayal. Expelled from France under the Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle, he spent his later years in decline, marrying Princess Louise of Stolberg-Gedern in 1772 in an unhappy, childless union. He died in Rome in 1788 and was buried in St Peter's Basilica alongside his father and brother.
