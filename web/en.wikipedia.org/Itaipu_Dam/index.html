The Itaipu Dam (Guarani: Yjapy'u; Portuguese: Barragem de Itaipu; Spanish: Represa de Itaipú) is a hydroelectric dam on the Paraná River on the border between Brazil and Paraguay, near the cities of Foz do Iguaçu and Ciudad del Este. Its name comes from an island that existed near the construction site and means "the singing stone" in Guarani. With an installed capacity of 14,000 MW it is one of the largest power plants in the world, and it has generated more electricity in a single year (103.1 TWh in 2016) than any other facility in history; by cumulative output it is the most productive hydroelectric plant ever built.

Construction

Brazil and Paraguay signed the Itaipu Treaty in April 1973 and created the binational company Itaipu Binacional to build and run the plant. Construction, which began in earnest in 1975, employed up to some 40,000 workers and required the Paraná's course to be diverted through a channel blasted through rock — at the time the largest diversion channel ever excavated. The first generating unit entered service in May 1984; the eighteenth in 1991, and two additional units completed in 2007 brought the total to twenty units of 700 MW each.

Power sharing

Each country owns half the plant's output. Paraguay, whose demand is far smaller than its share, historically sold most of its unused portion to Brazil at treaty-set prices — a long-standing source of political contention in Asunción, renegotiated in 2009 (tripling payments) and again in the 2020s as the treaty's financial annex came up for revision after the construction debt was paid off in 2023. Itaipu typically supplies around 10–15 percent of Brazil's electricity and the great majority of Paraguay's.

Impact

The reservoir, covering about 1,350 km², submerged farmland, forest, and the Guaíra Falls (Sete Quedas) — once among the largest waterfalls on Earth by volume — whose drowning in 1982 remains one of the dam's most lamented costs. Around 65,000 people were displaced. At the same time, the project became a major tourist attraction and was named one of the Seven Wonders of the Modern World by the American Society of Civil Engineers.
