Nonlinear optics

Nonlinear optics (NLO) is the branch of optics that describes the behaviour of light in nonlinear media, that is, media in which the polarization density P responds non-linearly to the electric field E of the light. The non-linearity is typically observed only at very high light intensities, such as those provided by lasers.

Second-harmonic generation in a nonlinear crystal

History

The field began in 1961 when Peter Franken and colleagues at the University of Michigan observed second-harmonic generation shortly after the invention of the laser. Robert W. Boyd's textbook Nonlinear Optics helped standardize the field.

Nonlinear processes

Important phenomena include second-harmonic generation (SHG), sum-frequency and difference-frequency generation, optical parametric oscillation, the Kerr effect (intensity-dependent refractive index), four-wave mixing, self-phase modulation, and stimulated Raman scattering. These processes underpin frequency conversion, ultrafast pulse generation, and much of laser spectroscopy.

Phase matching

Efficient conversion requires phase matching, conserving momentum among the interacting waves; techniques include birefringent phase matching and quasi-phase matching in periodically poled materials such as lithium niobate.
