In Flanders Fields

"In Flanders Fields" is a war poem in the form of a rondeau, written during the First World War by Canadian physician Lieutenant-Colonel John McCrae. He was inspired to write it on May 3, 1915, after presiding over the funeral of friend and fellow soldier Lieutenant Alexis Helmer, who died in the Second Battle of Ypres. The poem was first published on December 8, 1915, in the London magazine Punch.

Text

The poem speaks in the voice of the fallen:

In Flanders fields the poppies blow
Between the crosses, row on row,
That mark our place; and in the sky
The larks, still bravely singing, fly
Scarce heard amid the guns below.

Its second stanza voices the dead ("We are the Dead. Short days ago / We lived, felt dawn, saw sunset glow..."), and the third urges the living to "take up our quarrel with the foe" and not break faith with those who died.

Legacy

"In Flanders Fields" is one of the most quoted poems from the war, and it is a staple of Remembrance Day ceremonies in Canada and other Commonwealth nations. Its reference to the red poppies that grew over the graves of fallen soldiers helped make the remembrance poppy one of the world's most recognized memorial symbols. The poem's manuscript is held in Canada, and the In Flanders Fields Museum in Ypres, Belgium, is named after it.
