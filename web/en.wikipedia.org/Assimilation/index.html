Assimilation

Assimilation may refer to several distinct concepts across the social sciences, biology, linguistics, and other fields:

Sociology and culture

In sociology, cultural assimilation is the process by which a person or group adopts the culture, language, and customs of another, usually more dominant, group, often losing elements of their original culture in the process. It is a central concept in the study of immigration and ethnic relations.

Other uses

  • Biology: the conversion of nutrients into components of the body, as in plant assimilation of carbon during photosynthesis.
  • Phonology: a sound change in which one speech sound becomes more like a neighbouring sound.
  • Psychology: in Piaget's theory of cognitive development, fitting new information into existing mental schemas.
  • Meteorology: data assimilation, the combining of observations with model forecasts.
  • Geology: the incorporation of country rock into a magma body.

For the social process, see Assimilation (sociology).
