Charles Henry Bennett (born 1943) is an American physicist and information theorist at IBM Research, recognised as one of the founders of quantum information science. He received his PhD in chemical physics from Harvard University in 1970 and joined IBM's Thomas J. Watson Research Center in 1972.

Contributions

In 1973 Bennett showed that computation can in principle be carried out reversibly, without the thermodynamic cost associated with erasing information, clarifying the resolution of Maxwell's demon in work building on Rolf Landauer. In 1984, with Gilles Brassard, he devised the BB84 protocol, the first quantum key distribution scheme. In 1993, with collaborators, he proposed quantum teleportation, showing that an unknown quantum state can be transmitted using entanglement and classical communication. He also contributed to the theory of algorithmic information and the concept of Bennett's logical depth.

Honours

Bennett is a Fellow of the American Physical Society and a member of the National Academy of Sciences, and has received major prizes in quantum information, including the Wolf Prize in Physics (2018, shared with Brassard) and the Breakthrough Prize in Fundamental Physics (2023).
