Most penetrating particle size

The most penetrating particle size (MPPS) is the particle diameter at which a filter is least efficient — that is, the size at which the greatest fraction of particles passes through the filter medium. For mechanical air filters such as HEPA filters, the MPPS is typically around 0.1 to 0.3 micrometres.

Physical basis

Filter capture mechanisms depend on particle size in opposing ways. Large particles are captured efficiently by inertial impaction and interception, while very small particles are captured efficiently by Brownian diffusion, which brings them into contact with filter fibres. Between these regimes lies an intermediate size where neither mechanism is strong, producing a minimum in filtration efficiency — the MPPS.

Testing significance

Because efficiency at the MPPS is the worst case, high-efficiency filters are commonly rated at this size. European standards for HEPA and ULPA filters (EN 1822) classify filters by their efficiency at the MPPS, and respirator certification testing uses aerosols near this size range so that the rating reflects the most difficult particles to capture.
