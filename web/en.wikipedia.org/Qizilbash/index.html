Qizilbash

The Qizilbash (also spelled Kizilbash; Azerbaijani: Qızılbaş; Turkish: Kızılbaş; Persian: قزلباش, "red head") were a diverse array of mainly Turkoman Shia militant groups that flourished in Iranian Azerbaijan, Anatolia, the Armenian Highlands, and the Caucasus from the late 15th century onwards. They are known as Qizilbash because of their distinctive twelve-gored crimson headwear (taj), indicating their devotion to the Twelve Imams and to Shaykh Haydar, head of the Safavid Sufi order.

Role in founding the Safavid Empire

The Qizilbash tribes — including the Shamlu, Rumlu, Ustajlu, Teke, Takalu, Dhu'l-Qadr, Qajar, Afshar and others — formed the military backbone that brought Shah Ismail I to power in 1501 and founded the Safavid Empire, which proclaimed Twelver Shi'ism the state religion of Iran. Qizilbash amirs dominated the early Safavid court and administration, though their factional rivalries prompted Shah Abbas I (r. 1588–1629) to counterbalance them with a new ghulam corps drawn from Georgian, Circassian and Armenian converts.

Later usage

In Ottoman usage, "Kızılbaş" was often applied pejoratively to Alevi and other heterodox Shia communities in Anatolia. The term also survives in Afghanistan, where Qizilbash communities descended from Safavid- and Afsharid-era officials live mainly in Kabul and other cities and historically served in government and commerce.
