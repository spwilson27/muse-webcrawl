Indian national calendar

Adopted22 March 1957
EraShaka era (epoch 78 CE)
TypeSolar calendar
Months12, beginning with Chaitra

The Indian national calendar, based on the Shaka era, is the official civil calendar used in India alongside the Gregorian calendar for purposes such as the Gazette of India, All India Radio news broadcasts, and official communications. It was adopted on 22 March 1957 (1 Chaitra 1879 Shaka).

Structure

The calendar is a solar calendar whose year begins near the vernal equinox. Its months are Chaitra, Vaishakha, Jyeshtha, Ashadha, Shravana, Bhadrapada, Ashwin, Kartika, Agrahayana, Pausha, Magha and Phalguna. Chaitra has 30 days (31 in leap years) and begins on 22 March (21 March in leap years); the following five months have 31 days and the remaining six have 30.

History

A Calendar Reform Committee chaired by physicist Meghnad Saha was appointed in 1952 to examine India's many regional calendars and devise a unified, scientifically accurate civil calendar. The committee's recommendations produced the national calendar, and Saha's work also contributed to the standardisation of calendar calculations across India.

Usage

Dates in the national calendar correspond to fixed Gregorian dates, making conversion straightforward: the Shaka year is 78 years behind the Gregorian year for most of the year. Despite official status, everyday civil life in India overwhelmingly uses the Gregorian calendar, while Hindu religious festivals follow traditional lunisolar calendars.
