Micius

Micius (Chinese: 墨子; pinyin: Mòzǐ), formally the Quantum Experiments at Space Scale (QUESS) satellite, is a Chinese space mission launched in August 2016 to conduct experiments in quantum communication. Named after the ancient Chinese philosopher Mozi, it was the world's first quantum communications satellite.

Mission

Launched on 16 August 2016 from Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center aboard a Long March 2D rocket, the roughly 600-kilogram satellite was placed in a Sun-synchronous orbit about 500 kilometres above Earth. It was developed under the leadership of physicist Pan Jianwei of the University of Science and Technology of China.

Experiments and achievements

Micius demonstrated satellite-to-ground quantum key distribution (QKD) over distances exceeding 1,200 kilometres, far beyond the range of terrestrial optical fibres. In 2017 its results, published in Science and Nature, included the distribution of entangled photon pairs to ground stations separated by about 1,200 km, a test of Bell's inequality, and quantum teleportation from ground to satellite. The satellite also enabled an intercontinental quantum-encrypted videoconference between Beijing and Vienna in 2017.

Significance

The mission was a landmark in China's quantum communications program and in the global effort to build a quantum internet, demonstrating that satellite-based entanglement distribution can span intercontinental distances.
