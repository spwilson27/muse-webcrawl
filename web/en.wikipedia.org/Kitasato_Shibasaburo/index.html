Kitasato Shibasaburo

Kitasato Shibasaburō (1853–1931) was a Japanese physician and bacteriologist, known for co-discovering the infectious agent of bubonic plague and for pioneering work on antitoxins.

Career

Kitasato studied in Berlin under Robert Koch from 1885 to 1891, where he was the first to grow the tetanus bacillus in pure culture and, with Emil von Behring, demonstrated antitoxin immunity, laying foundations for serum therapy. Sent to Hong Kong during the 1894 plague outbreak, he identified the bacterium Yersinia pestis (independently described by Alexandre Yersin).

Legacy

He founded the Institute for Infectious Diseases in Tokyo and later Kitasato University. He was nominated for the first Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, awarded to his collaborator Behring alone.
