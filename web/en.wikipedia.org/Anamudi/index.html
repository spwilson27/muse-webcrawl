Anamudi

Anamudi is a mountain located in Ernakulam district and Idukki district of the Indian state of Kerala. At an elevation of 2,695 metres (8,842 feet), it is the highest peak in the Western Ghats and in South India, and the highest point in India south of the Himalayas. The name Anamudi translates as "elephant's head", referring to the peak's resemblance to an elephant's forehead.

Geography and ecology

Anamudi lies within Eravikulam National Park, in the southern part of the Western Ghats' Anaimalai Hills. The area is characterised by rolling montane grasslands interspersed with shola forests and is home to the largest surviving population of the endangered Nilgiri tahr, as well as Asian elephants, gaurs, lion-tailed macaques and the Neelakurinji flowers that bloom en masse once every twelve years.

Climbing

The peak was first climbed in 1862 by General Douglas Hamilton of the Madras Army. Access today is regulated by the Kerala Forest Department, and treks to the summit are generally arranged through Eravikulam National Park authorities.
