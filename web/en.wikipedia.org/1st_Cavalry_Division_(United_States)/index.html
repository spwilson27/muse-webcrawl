The 1st Cavalry Division ("First Team") is a United States Army combined arms division, one of the most famous and decorated divisions in US military history. It is based at Fort Cavazos, Texas, and was formed in 1921. Although organised today as a heavy armoured division, it retains the name and heraldry of the horse cavalry: its large shoulder patch features a black horse's head on a yellow field, with a black diagonal stripe commemorating the unit's dead.

History

The division was constituted in 1921 and spent the interwar years patrolling the Mexican border. In the Second World War it fought dismounted in the Pacific, in the Admiralty Islands, Leyte and Luzon, and was the first American division to enter Manila and, later, Tokyo, where it performed occupation duty in Japan.

In the Korean War the division fought from the Pusan Perimeter north to Pyongyang, becoming the first division to enter the North Korean capital, before being caught by Chinese intervention. Reorganised as an airmobile division in 1965, it deployed to Vietnam, where it pioneered helicopter-borne warfare at the Battle of Ia Drang and relieved the Marines at Khe Sanh during Operation Pegasus. Elements later fought in Cambodia in 1970.

Modern service

As a heavy division, the 1st Cavalry deployed to the Gulf War in 1991, conducting a feint toward the Wadi al-Batin. After 2001 its brigade combat teams rotated repeatedly through Iraq — including command of Multi-National Division–Baghdad in 2004 — and Afghanistan. Today the division comprises armoured brigade combat teams, a division artillery, an aviation brigade and a sustainment brigade, and remains one of the Army's principal heavy formations.
