Underground press

The underground press is the term used for the alternative, countercultural newspapers and magazines that flourished in the 1960s and early 1970s, especially in the United States and Britain, outside mainstream media.

History

Early landmarks included the Los Angeles Free Press (1964), the East Village Other, the Berkeley Barb, and Britain's International Times and Oz. The Underground Press Syndicate (1966) let member papers reprint each other's material freely.

Content and legacy

The papers covered civil rights, opposition to the Vietnam War, rock music, psychedelic art, and the sexual revolution, often with experimental layouts by artists such as Robert Crumb. Many titles folded or became "alternative weeklies" in the 1970s.
