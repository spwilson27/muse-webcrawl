Greenstone belt

Greenstone belts are zones of variably metamorphosed mafic to ultramafic volcanic and sedimentary rock sequences found within Archaean and Proterozoic cratons, typically between granite and gneiss bodies. The name comes from the green hue imparted by metamorphic minerals such as chlorite, actinolite, and other green amphiboles formed from basaltic protoliths.

Formation and examples

Greenstone belts range from a few tens to several thousand kilometres long and are interpreted as remnants of ancient volcanic arcs, back-arc basins, and oceanic crust accreted onto early continental nuclei. Notable examples include the Barberton Greenstone Belt in South Africa (about 3.5 billion years old, preserving evidence of early life), the Abitibi Greenstone Belt in Canada, and the Yilgarn Craton belts of Western Australia.

Economic significance

Greenstone belts are among the world's most important sources of gold, with major deposits in the Witwatersrand-adjacent belts, Canada's Abitibi, and Australia's Kalgoorlie region. They also host significant nickel, copper, zinc, and other base-metal deposits associated with komatiite and volcanic-hosted massive sulfide mineralization.
