Feta

Feta cheese served with olives

Feta (Greek: φέτα) is a Greek brined white cheese made from sheep's milk or a mixture of sheep and goat's milk. It is crumbly, tangy and salty, with small holes, and is typically produced in blocks and stored in brine to preserve freshness.

History and production

Feta has been made in Greece since antiquity; a cheese of this kind is described as early as Homer's Odyssey. The curd is cut, drained, salted and aged in barrels or tins of brine for at least two months. Since 2002, feta has held protected designation of origin status in the European Union: only cheese produced in certain regions of Greece from the milk of local sheep and goats may be sold as feta within the EU.

Uses

Feta is a central ingredient of Greek salad and of dishes such as spanakopita and tyropita, and is widely crumbled over salads, pasta, grains and roasted vegetables around the world.
