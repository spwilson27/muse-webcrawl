Vanna Venturi House

Vanna Venturi House
Location: Chestnut Hill, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, U.S.
Architect: Robert Venturi
Completed: 1964
Style: Postmodern

The Vanna Venturi House, in the Chestnut Hill neighbourhood of Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, is one of the first prominent works of postmodern architecture. It was designed by architect Robert Venturi for his mother, Vanna Venturi, and built between 1962 and 1964.

Design

The house plays with the conventional image of a home: a large gabled front with a chimney and a central split in the gable, an off-centre entrance and applied ornament that subvert expectations of symmetry. Inside, elements such as the fireplace and stair compete for centrality and are distorted by diagonal walls and unexpected openings. Venturi described such effects in his 1966 book Complexity and Contradiction in Architecture, widely regarded as a founding text of postmodernism.

Recognition

Though small, the house is considered a landmark of 20th-century architecture. In 1989 Venturi received the Pritzker Architecture Prize, in part for the ideas the house embodied. The building remains a private residence.
