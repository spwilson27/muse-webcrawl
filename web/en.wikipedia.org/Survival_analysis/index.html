Survival analysis

Survival analysis is a branch of statistics for analysing the expected duration of time until one or more events of interest occur, such as death in biological organisms, failure in mechanical systems, relapse of disease, or customer churn. Its distinctive feature is handling censoring — subjects for whom the event has not yet been observed when observation ends.

Key methods

The survival function S(t) gives the probability that the event has not occurred by time t, while the hazard function describes the instantaneous event rate among those still at risk. The Kaplan–Meier estimator provides a non-parametric estimate of the survival curve; the log-rank test compares groups; and the Cox proportional hazards model relates the hazard to covariates semi-parametrically. Parametric models assume specific distributions such as the exponential or Weibull.

Applications

Survival analysis is central to clinical trials and epidemiology, reliability engineering, actuarial science, and economics (as duration analysis).
