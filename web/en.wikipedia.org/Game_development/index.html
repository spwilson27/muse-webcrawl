Video game development

Video game development (or game development) is the process of designing, programming, producing and releasing a video game. It combines software engineering with art, sound design, writing and production management, and is carried out by development teams ranging from a single independent creator to studios of hundreds of people.

Process

Development is usually divided into pre-production, in which concepts, prototypes and a game design document are prepared; production, the longest phase, in which code, art, levels, audio and narrative content are created; and post-production, covering testing, bug fixing, optimisation and release. Milestones such as vertical slices, alpha (feature complete) and beta (content complete) structure the schedule. Testing, localisation and certification precede the "gold master" and launch, frequently followed by patches and downloadable content.

Roles

Key roles include the game designer, programmers, artists and animators, level designers, sound designers and composers, writers, producers and quality-assurance testers. Publishers often fund and market games, while platform holders control certification for consoles.

Tools

Most games are built with a game engine such as Unreal Engine, Unity or Godot, together with digital content creation tools for 3D modelling, animation and audio. Middleware supplies physics, animation and networking. Independent development expanded greatly in the 2000s as engines and digital distribution became accessible.

Production practices

The industry is known for intense "crunch" periods near deadlines, which have drawn criticism and unionisation efforts. Agile methods, live-service models and early-access releases have changed how games are developed and maintained after launch.
