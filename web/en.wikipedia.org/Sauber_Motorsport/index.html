Sauber Motorsport

Sauber Motorsport AG is a Swiss motorsport engineering company. It was founded in 1970 (as PP Sauber AG) by Peter Sauber, who progressed through hillclimbing and the World Sportscar Championship to reach Formula One in 1993. The team has operated under various names, most recently competing as the Stake F1 Team Kick Sauber following the end of its title sponsorship with Alfa Romeo in 2023, and is being transformed into the Audi works team for the 2026 season. Having not won a Formula One race as an independent team, Sauber's greatest success came as BMW Sauber, which won the 2008 Canadian Grand Prix.

History

Peter Sauber built his first car, the Sauber C1, in his parents' basement in Zurich. The company achieved major success in sports car racing in partnership with Mercedes-Benz, winning the World Sportscar Championship in 1989 and 1990 and the 24 Hours of Le Mans in 1989 with the Sauber Mercedes C9. The programme also developed future Formula One world champion Michael Schumacher, who drove for the Mercedes junior team.

Sauber entered Formula One in 1993 with engines supplied by Ilmor-badged Mercedes and drivers Karl Wendlinger and JJ Lehto. The team became a respected midfield competitor, scoring a third-place finish with Heinz-Harald Frentzen at the 1995 Italian Grand Prix and a best constructors' championship finish of fourth in 2001. In 2006 BMW acquired the team and raced it as BMW Sauber until withdrawing at the end of 2009, after which Peter Sauber repurchased it.

Audi takeover

In October 2022, Audi announced it would enter Formula One in 2026 as an engine manufacturer and acquire a stake in Sauber, making the Hinwil team its works outfit. The German manufacturer subsequently moved to acquire full ownership. The team races under the Stake F1 Team Kick Sauber name in the interim seasons, with Nico Hülkenberg joining for 2025 alongside rookie Gabriel Bortoleto.
