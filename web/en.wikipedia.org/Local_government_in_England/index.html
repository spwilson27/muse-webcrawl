Local government in England

Local government in England is administered by elected councils responsible for services such as education, social care, transport, planning, housing, libraries, waste and trading standards. England has no separate parliament of its own, so Parliament at Westminster legislates for English local government.

Structure

Outside London there are two main structures: two-tier areas with county councils and district councils dividing responsibilities, and single-tier unitary authorities that provide all services. Metropolitan boroughs in the large conurbations are also single-tier. Civil parishes, with parish or town councils, form the lowest tier in many areas.

London

Greater London is administered strategically by the Greater London Authority — the Mayor of London and London Assembly — and locally by 32 London borough councils plus the City of London Corporation.

Combined authorities

Since 2011, combined authorities, several led by directly elected metro mayors, have taken on devolved powers over transport, skills and economic development in city regions such as Greater Manchester and the West Midlands.

History and finance

The modern system descends from reforms including the Municipal Corporations Act 1835 and the Local Government Act 1972, which reorganised counties and districts in 1974. Councils are funded by council tax, business rates, fees and central government grants, and councillors are elected by local residents, usually every four years.
