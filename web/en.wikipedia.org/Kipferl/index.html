A crescent-shaped Kipferl, ancestor of the croissant.

A Kipferl (also Kipfel, Kipf, or Gipfel in parts of German-speaking Europe; Hungarian: kifli; Czech/Slovak: rohlík) is a traditional Austrian crescent-shaped pastry. Kipferl are made by rolling out a wedge of yeasted (or brioche-type) dough and curling it into a crescent, then baking until golden. They may be plain or made with sweet fillings such as poppy seed (Mohnkipferl) or walnut, and in biscuit form as the Christmas Vanillekipferl.

History

Crescent-shaped breads have a long history in Central Europe; the Kipferl is documented in Austria since at least the 13th century and was a familiar product of Viennese bakeries for centuries. A popular but apocryphal legend connects its shape to the crescent on the Ottoman flag, said to have been baked in Vienna to celebrate the lifting of the sieges of 1529 or 1683. More reliably, the Kipferl is the direct ancestor of the French croissant: when the Viennese baker August Zang opened his Boulangerie Viennoise in Paris in the 1830s, the Kipferl he sold there was adapted by French bakers using laminated puff pastry, becoming the croissant.

Variants

  • Vanillekipferl – small shortcrust crescents flavoured with vanilla and ground nuts, dusted with vanilla sugar; a classic Christmas biscuit in Austria, Germany, and Central Europe.
  • Mohnkipferl – filled with sweet poppy-seed paste.
  • Nusskipferl – filled with ground walnuts.
  • Salzkipferl / Salzstangerl – savoury versions sprinkled with salt and caraway.
