Gummy candy

Gummy bears, the most famous form of gummy candy, invented in Germany in 1922.

Gummy candies (also spelled gummi) are a broad category of gelatin-based chewable sweets. The gelling agent — traditionally gelatin, but also starch, pectin or agar in vegetarian versions — gives gummies their characteristic elastic chew. They are typically flavoured with fruit juices and coloured brightly.

History and types

The precursors of gummies include Turkish delight and gum drops, but the modern form began with the gummy bear, created in 1922 by Hans Riegel of Bonn, Germany, founder of Haribo. Gummy worms were introduced by the German company Trolli in 1981. Other common shapes include rings, cola bottles, frogs, cherries, snakes and sharks; sour variants are coated in citric or malic acid. Wine gums and jelly babies are related British confections with a firmer texture.

Production and market

Gummies are mass-produced in starch mogul moulds, then steamed, dried and polished with carnauba wax or oil. Beyond sweets, the format is widely used for vitamin and supplement "gummies" for children and adults.
