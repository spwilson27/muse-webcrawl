Autoroutes of France

The autoroute system in France is a national network of controlled-access highways, designated by the letter A followed by a number. Most autoroutes are toll roads operated under concession by private companies, while some urban and western sections are free and state-run.

History

The first French autoroute, west of Paris, opened in the early 1940s. A 1955 law established the concession model under which companies build and operate motorways financed by tolls. The network expanded rapidly in the 1960s and 1970s — famous routes include the A6/A7 "Autoroute du Soleil" from Paris to Marseille via Lyon — and now totals over 11,000 kilometres.

Rules and features

The general speed limit is 130 km/h in dry weather and 110 km/h in rain. Autoroutes have rest areas (aires) at regular intervals, electronic toll collection via the Liber-t tag, and emergency telephones. Many routes are known by names, such as La Languedocienne or L'Aquitaine, as well as by number.
