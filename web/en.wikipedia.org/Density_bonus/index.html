Density bonus

A density bonus is an incentive-based zoning tool that allows developers to build more housing units, taller buildings, or greater floor area than normally permitted on a site, in exchange for providing a public benefit — most commonly affordable housing.

How it works

Base zoning sets a maximum density or height. Under a density bonus ordinance, a developer who reserves a specified share of units for low- or moderate-income households can exceed those limits by a defined percentage. Programs often add other concessions, such as reduced parking requirements, waived fees, or expedited permitting.

Examples

California's Density Bonus Law, first enacted in 1979 and strengthened repeatedly since, entitles qualifying projects to a percentage increase in allowed units — up to 50% or more — depending on the share and affordability level of the reserved homes. Similar mechanisms appear in inclusionary zoning programs in cities across North America, which may also grant bonuses for amenities such as public open space, childcare facilities, or transit improvements.

Criticism and debate

Supporters argue density bonuses leverage private development to produce affordable homes without direct subsidy. Critics contend that the value of the bonus may not always offset the cost of the affordable units, that bonuses can be too small to be used, or that taller and denser projects face community opposition.
