Jean-François Lyotard

Jean-François Lyotard (10 August 1924 – 21 April 1998) was a French philosopher, sociologist and literary theorist, best known for articulating the concept of postmodernism in The Postmodern Condition (1979), where he famously defined the postmodern as "incredulity toward metanarratives".

Life

Lyotard studied philosophy at the Sorbonne and taught in Algeria in the early 1950s, an experience that shaped his political engagement. He was a member of the revolutionary socialist group Socialisme ou Barbarie before breaking with Marxism. He taught at the University of Paris X Nanterre and the experimental University of Vincennes (Paris VIII), and later at Emory University in the United States.

The Postmodern Condition

Commissioned as a report on knowledge for the government of Quebec, La condition postmoderne (1979) argued that the grand legitimating narratives of modernity — progress, emancipation, speculative totality — had lost credibility in the most highly developed societies, and that knowledge was increasingly legitimated by performativity. Drawing on Wittgenstein's notion of language games, Lyotard described a plurality of incommensurable discourses.

Later work

In The Differend (1983) Lyotard explored wrongs that cannot be articulated within the idiom of the tribunal judging them. He also wrote extensively on the sublime, aesthetics and art, including essays on Kant, and organised the landmark 1985 exhibition Les Immatériaux at the Centre Pompidou. He died of leukaemia in Paris in 1998.
