Technosignature

Radio telescopes are among the instruments used to search for technosignatures.

A technosignature or technomarker is any measurable property or effect that provides scientific evidence of past or present technology, analogous to a biosignature for life. The term is most often used in the search for extraterrestrial intelligence (SETI) and in the study of exoplanets, where technosignatures would indicate an extraterrestrial technological civilization.

Types

Proposed technosignatures include narrow-band radio transmissions and pulsed laser signals deliberately or incidentally broadcast by a civilization; anomalous atmospheric composition, such as industrial pollutants like chlorofluorocarbons or nitrogen dioxide on an exoplanet; waste heat from large-scale energy use; artificial structures transiting a star, such as hypothetical Dyson swarms; artificial illumination on a planet's night side; and solar-system artefacts such as probes or debris from another civilization.

Search history

Radio SETI has searched for technosignatures since Frank Drake's Project Ozma in 1960, and large programs such as Breakthrough Listen (announced 2015) have expanded the search across radio and optical wavelengths. The unusual dimming of Boyajian's star (KIC 8462852) in 2015 prompted speculation about megastructures before natural explanations involving dust gained support, illustrating both the interest in and the difficulty of interpreting candidate technosignatures. NASA convened a technosignatures workshop in 2018 and has funded technosignature research as a complement to biosignature science.
