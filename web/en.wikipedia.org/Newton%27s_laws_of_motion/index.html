Newton's laws of motion

FieldClassical mechanics
Formulated byIsaac Newton
Published1687, Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica

Newton's laws of motion are three physical laws that form the foundation of classical mechanics. They describe the relationship between the motion of an object and the forces acting on it. The laws were first compiled by Isaac Newton in his work Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica (1687).

The laws

First law (law of inertia): An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion with the same speed and in the same direction, unless acted upon by an unbalanced external force.

Second law: The net force on an object is equal to the rate of change of its linear momentum: F = ma, where m is mass and a is acceleration.

Third law: For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction: forces between two bodies are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.

Scope and limits

The laws apply to macroscopic objects at speeds much less than the speed of light and form the basis of Newtonian mechanics. They were superseded at very high speeds by special relativity and at atomic scales by quantum mechanics, but remain an excellent approximation for everyday phenomena.
