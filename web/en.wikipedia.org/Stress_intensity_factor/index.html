Stress intensity factor
Symbol: K
Field: Fracture mechanics
Units: MPa√m

The stress intensity factor, K, is used in fracture mechanics to predict the stress state near the tip of a crack caused by a remote load or residual stresses. Developed from the work of G. R. Irwin in the 1950s, it characterises the singular stress field at a crack tip.

Definition

For a through crack of length 2a in an infinite plate under remote tensile stress σ, K = σ√(πa). In general K = Yσ√(πa), where Y is a dimensionless geometry factor. Separate factors KI, KII and KIII describe opening, sliding and tearing crack modes.

Fracture criterion

When K reaches a material's fracture toughness KIc, unstable crack growth occurs. Comparing applied K with toughness underlies damage-tolerant design of aircraft, pressure vessels and bridges.

Applications

Stress intensity solutions are tabulated in handbooks for common geometries and underpin fatigue crack growth laws such as the Paris–Erdogan equation.
