The Silesian Uprisings were a series of three armed uprisings in Upper Silesia between 1919 and 1921, in which Polish Silesians fought to separate the region from Germany and join the newly re-established Polish state. The uprisings occurred against the backdrop of the Upper Silesia plebiscite mandated by the Treaty of Versailles, in which the region's inhabitants were to vote on whether to remain in Germany or join Poland.

Background

Upper Silesia was a wealthy industrial region whose population mixed Polish-speaking and German-speaking communities. After the First World War, both Poland and Germany claimed the territory, and tensions rose sharply as the plebiscite approached.

The uprisings

The First Uprising (August 1919) was sparked by the massacre of Polish miners at the Mysłowice mine and was quickly suppressed by German forces. The Second Uprising (August 1920) followed violence by German security police and succeeded in disbanding the German-dominated police in the plebiscite area. The Third Uprising (May–July 1921), led by Wojciech Korfanty, was the largest: after the plebiscite of March 1921 returned an overall majority for Germany but Polish majorities in many eastern districts, insurgents seized much of the industrial district. German Freikorps counterattacked, and fighting at the Battle of Annaberg became the largest battle of the uprisings before an Allied-brokered ceasefire.

Outcome

The League of Nations divided Upper Silesia in 1922, awarding Poland the more industrialised eastern portion, including Katowice and most of the coal mines and steelworks, while Germany retained the west.
