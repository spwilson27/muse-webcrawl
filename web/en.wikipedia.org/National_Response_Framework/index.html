National Response Framework

The National Response Framework (NRF) is a United States government guide that sets out how the nation responds to all types of disasters and emergencies, from natural disasters to terrorist attacks. It is one of the five National Planning Frameworks developed under Presidential Policy Directive 8 and is administered by the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA).

History

The NRF replaced the National Response Plan in 2008, following lessons from Hurricane Katrina about unclear roles and poor coordination. It was substantially updated in 2013 and again in 2019, when a Community Lifelines construct was added to prioritise restoring essential services such as power, water, communications and health care.

Principles

The Framework is built on tiered response — incidents are handled at the lowest jurisdictional level possible, with state and federal support when local capacity is exceeded — and on the National Incident Management System (NIMS) and Incident Command System. Emergency Support Function annexes assign coordinating agencies for tasks such as transportation, mass care, and public health.
