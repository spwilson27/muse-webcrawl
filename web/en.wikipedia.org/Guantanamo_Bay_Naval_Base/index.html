Guantanamo Bay Naval Base

Guantanamo Bay Naval Base (Spanish: Base Naval de la Bahía de Guantánamo), officially Naval Station Guantanamo Bay or NSGB and nicknamed GTMO (pronounced "Gitmo"), is a United States military base located on 45 square miles (117 km²) of land and water on both sides of the shore of Guantánamo Bay at the south-eastern end of Cuba. It is the oldest overseas US naval base and the only one in a country with which the United States does not have diplomatic relations at the level of an embassy (relations, restored in 2015, were later curtailed).

History and lease

The United States took control of the bay during the Spanish-American War in 1898 and obtained a perpetual lease under the Cuban-American Treaty of 1903, at a nominal annual rent. A 1934 treaty reaffirmed the lease, making it terminable only by mutual agreement or US abandonment. Since the Cuban Revolution, the Cuban government has rejected the lease's legitimacy, and has refused to cash the annual rent cheques (about US$4,085 per year), save for one cashed in 1959 amid post-revolutionary confusion. The perimeter is fortified and was mined on both sides during the Cold War; the US removed its minefield by 1999.

Operations

The base supports US Navy and Coast Guard operations in the Caribbean, houses several thousand personnel and dependents, and includes facilities such as McCalla airfield, a school, a hospital, and even the only McDonald's in Cuba. In the 1990s it held tens of thousands of Haitian and Cuban refugees intercepted at sea.

Detention camp

The base is best known internationally for the Guantanamo Bay detention camp, established in 2002 to hold detainees captured in the conflicts in Afghanistan and elsewhere during the "war on terror." The camp's legality and treatment of prisoners drew sustained worldwide controversy, litigation reaching the US Supreme Court, and repeated pledges by US presidents to close it; a small number of detainees remain.
