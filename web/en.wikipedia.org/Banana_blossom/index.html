Banana blossom

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Banana blossom, also called banana flower or banana heart, is the tear-shaped purple flower at the end of a cluster (hand) of bananas. It is a common ingredient in South and Southeast Asian cuisines, where it is treated as a vegetable, and has a flaky, slightly bitter, artichoke-like character; its texture has also made it popular in contemporary vegan cooking as a fish substitute.

Structure and preparation

The blossom consists of layers of dark red-purple bracts, each sheltering rows of small tubular flowers. In preparation, the tough outer bracts and the inedible parts of the florets (the translucent calyx and the pistil) are removed; the tender inner bracts and heart are sliced and soaked in acidulated water, lemon juice, or diluted vinegar to prevent discoloration and reduce bitterness, then boiled, stir-fried, or used raw in salads depending on the cuisine.

Culinary uses

In Burmese cooking it is sliced into mohinga and salads; in Thai cuisine it accompanies pad thai raw and is cooked in curries and tom kha; in the Philippines it appears in dishes such as kare-kare and kilawin. In Sri Lanka it is curried as kesel muwa, and in India — especially Kerala, West Bengal, and the Northeast — it is made into stir-fries (thoran), cutlets, and curries. Canned banana blossom in brine has become a widely marketed vegan alternative to flaky fish, used for plant-based "fish and chips" and curries.
