Air France

Air France (legally Société Air France, S.A.) is the flag carrier of France, headquartered in Tremblay-en-France. It is a subsidiary of the Air France–KLM Group and a founding member of the SkyTeam airline alliance. The airline's main hub is Paris Charles de Gaulle Airport, with Paris Orly Airport as a secondary hub. Air France serves a global network of domestic and international destinations across Europe, the Americas, Africa, Asia, and Oceania.

History

Air France was formed in 1933 from the merger of several French airlines, including Air Orient, Air Union, and Compagnie Générale Aéropostale, the airmail pioneer associated with aviators such as Antoine de Saint-Exupéry. Nationalized after the Second World War, the airline became an early operator of the Sud Aviation Caravelle and, in 1976, one of only two airlines to fly the supersonic Concorde. In 2004 Air France merged with the Dutch carrier KLM to form one of the world's largest airline groups, while both brands continued operating separately.

Fleet and services

The fleet comprises Airbus and Boeing aircraft, including the A320 family for short haul and the A350 and Boeing 777 for long haul. Cabins range from Economy to La Première first class on selected routes. Its frequent-flyer programme, Flying Blue, is shared with KLM and other group airlines. Subsidiaries and affiliates have included the regional carrier HOP! and the leisure airline Transavia France.

Incidents

Notable accidents include Air France Flight 447, an Airbus A330 that crashed into the Atlantic Ocean on 1 June 2009 en route from Rio de Janeiro to Paris, killing all 228 people aboard — an accident that led to worldwide changes in pitot-tube maintenance and high-altitude stall training.
