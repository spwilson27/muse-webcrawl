Hillary Diane Rodham Clinton (born 26 October 1947) is an American politician, diplomat and author. She served as the 67th United States Secretary of State under President Barack Obama from 2009 to 2013, as a U.S. senator from New York from 2001 to 2009, and as First Lady of the United States from 1993 to 2001 during the presidency of her husband, Bill Clinton. In 2016 she became the first woman to win a major American party's nomination for president, winning the popular vote in the general election while losing the Electoral College to Donald Trump.

Early life and career

Clinton was raised in Park Ridge, Illinois, graduated from Wellesley College in 1969, and earned a law degree from Yale Law School in 1973, where she met Bill Clinton. She worked as a congressional legal counsel and co-founded Arkansas Advocates for Children and Families, and became the first female partner at the Rose Law Firm in Little Rock.

Senate and Secretary of State

Elected to the Senate in 2000, Clinton served on the Armed Services Committee and supported the Iraq War authorization, a vote that later complicated her 2008 presidential campaign, which she narrowly lost to Barack Obama in the Democratic primaries. As Secretary of State she championed a "reset" with Russia, the pivot to Asia, sanctions on Iran, and the response to the Arab Spring, and she was in office during the 2012 attack on the U.S. mission in Benghazi.

2016 campaign and later work

Clinton's 2016 campaign emphasised experience and inequality; controversies included her use of a private email server as Secretary of State. After the election she wrote the memoir What Happened (2017) and, with her daughter Chelsea, The Book of Gutsy Women. She later served as Chancellor of Queen's University Belfast.
