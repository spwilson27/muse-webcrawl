Minority group

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In the social sciences, a minority group (or simply minority) is a category of people distinguished by physical or cultural characteristics who are singled out for differential and unequal treatment within a society. Contrary to the everyday meaning of the word, sociological minority status is defined by power and subordination rather than by numbers: a group may be a numerical majority yet occupy a minority position, as Black South Africans did under apartheid.

Definition

Sociologist Louis Wirth defined a minority group as one whose members are "singled out from the others in the society in which they live for differential and opposite treatment" and who regard themselves as objects of collective discrimination. Characteristics commonly identified include distinguishing physical or cultural traits held in low esteem by the dominant group, self-consciousness as a group, membership largely determined at birth, and tendencies toward in-group marriage. Minority status is commonly associated with inequalities in wealth, political power, and access to education and employment.

Categories and law

Minority groups may be defined by race, ethnicity, religion, language, gender, sexual orientation, or disability. International law protects national, ethnic, religious, and linguistic minorities, notably through the UN Declaration on the Rights of Persons Belonging to National or Ethnic, Religious and Linguistic Minorities (1992), while many states pursue remedies such as anti-discrimination legislation, affirmative action, and political representation mechanisms.
