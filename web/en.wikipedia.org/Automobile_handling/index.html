Automobile handling

Automobile handling and vehicle handling are descriptions of the way a wheeled vehicle responds and reacts to the inputs of a driver, as well as how it moves along a track or road. It is commonly judged by engineers and reviewers in terms of cornering ability, directional stability, steering feel, and behavior at the limit of tire grip.

Key concepts

A vehicle's tendency to turn less than commanded is called understeer, and to turn more, oversteer; a neutral balance lies between the two. These tendencies arise from weight distribution, suspension geometry, tire characteristics, and the transfer of load during acceleration, braking, and cornering. Roll stiffness distribution between the front and rear axles is a principal tuning tool.

Factors affecting handling

Important factors include the center of gravity height, wheelbase and track width, suspension design, steering ratio, tire compound and pressure, and electronic aids such as stability control and torque vectoring. Unsprung mass and aerodynamic downforce also influence grip and response.

Testing

Handling is evaluated subjectively by test drivers and objectively with maneuvers such as the Moose test, constant-radius skidpad runs, and slalom courses measuring lateral acceleration and transient response.
