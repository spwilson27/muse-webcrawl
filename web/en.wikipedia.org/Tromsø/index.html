Tromsø (Northern Sami: Romsa) is a city and municipality in Troms county, Norway, and the largest urban area in Northern Norway. The city centre is situated on the island of Tromsøya, over 300 km (190 mi) north of the Arctic Circle, yet its climate is moderated by the North Atlantic Current: winters are mild for the latitude and the port is ice-free. Tromsø experiences the midnight sun from about 20 May to 22 July and a polar night period in late November to mid-January, though the high latitude makes it one of the world's best-populated places to observe the northern lights.

History

The area has been inhabited since the late Ice Age, and a church stood at Tromsø in the 13th century. The town received its royal charter in 1794, breaking Bergen's trade monopoly. In the 19th century Tromsø became a centre for Arctic hunting and the starting point of polar expeditions by explorers such as Fridtjof Nansen and Roald Amundsen, who recruited crews there and was last seen leaving Tromsø in 1928. During the Second World War the German battleship Tirpitz was sunk near Tromsø in 1944.

Modern city

Today Tromsø is home to UiT The Arctic University of Norway (founded 1968, the world's northernmost university), the Norwegian Polar Institute, the Arctic Council secretariat, and a major fishing and tourism industry, with visitors drawn by aurora watching, whale safaris, and the Arctic Cathedral. The municipality's population is around 78,000. Tromsø Airport (Langnes) and the Hurtigruten coastal ferry connect the city to the rest of Norway.
