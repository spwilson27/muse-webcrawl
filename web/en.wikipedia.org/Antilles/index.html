Antilles

The Antilles arc along the eastern edge of the Caribbean Sea

The Antilles are an archipelago in the Caribbean Sea bordered by the Atlantic Ocean to the north and east. The islands form the northern and eastern boundary of the Caribbean and are conventionally divided into the Greater Antilles and the Lesser Antilles. Together with the Lucayan Archipelago (the Bahamas and Turks and Caicos), they constitute the West Indies.

Greater Antilles

The Greater Antilles comprise the four largest islands of the Caribbean: Cuba, Hispaniola (divided between Haiti and the Dominican Republic), Jamaica, and Puerto Rico, together with the Cayman Islands. These islands are geologically older, formed partly from continental rock, and contain the region's highest elevations, such as Pico Duarte in the Dominican Republic.

Lesser Antilles

The Lesser Antilles form a long volcanic arc from the Virgin Islands in the north to Grenada and the islands off the Venezuelan coast (including Aruba, Bonaire, and Curaçao) in the south. They are subdivided into the Leeward Islands, the Windward Islands, and the Leeward Antilles. Most are small volcanic or coral islands.

History

Before European contact, the Antilles were inhabited by Indigenous peoples including the Taíno, Kalinago (Island Caribs), and Ciboney. Christopher Columbus reached the islands in 1492, and Spain, France, Britain, the Netherlands, and Denmark subsequently colonized them, introducing plantation agriculture based on enslaved African labour—particularly sugar production—which shaped the islands' demographics and cultures.
