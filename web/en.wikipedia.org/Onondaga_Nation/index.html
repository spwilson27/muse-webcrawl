The Onondaga Nation (Onondaga: Onöñda'gega', "people on the hills") is a federally recognized sovereign Native American nation in Onondaga County, New York, south of Syracuse. It is the capital of the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois Confederacy): the Onondaga are the Keepers of the Fire and Keepers of the Wampum, hosting the Grand Council of the Confederacy, and the Tadodaho, the council's presiding chief, is an Onondaga title.

Governance and sovereignty

Uniquely, the Onondaga Nation has never accepted allotment or a federally imposed constitution; it continues to be governed by its traditional system of chiefs (hoya:ne) nominated by clan mothers under the Great Law of Peace. The Nation issues its own passports — famously asserted in 2010 when the Iroquois Nationals lacrosse team refused to travel on U.S. or Canadian documents — operates its own fire department and programs, and declines federal funding that entails surrendering sovereignty.

History and land rights

The Onondaga heartland around Onondaga Lake was the meeting place where, by tradition, the Peacemaker and Hiawatha persuaded the sorcerer Tadodaho to accept the Great Law, uniting the Five Nations. New York State took most Onondaga land through transactions the Nation maintains were illegal; in 2005 the Nation filed a landmark land-rights action seeking recognition and environmental restoration rather than eviction of residents, though federal courts dismissed the claim on equitable grounds. The Nation has been a leading voice in the cleanup of sacred Onondaga Lake.

Lacrosse

Lacrosse — the "Creator's Game" — is central to Onondaga identity. The Nation fields the Onondaga Redhawks box lacrosse team and is home to the Thompson brothers, among the most celebrated players in the sport's history.
