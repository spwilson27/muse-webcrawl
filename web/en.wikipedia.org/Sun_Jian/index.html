Sun Jian

Sun Jian (155–191), courtesy name Wentai, was a Chinese military general, politician, and warlord who lived during the late Eastern Han dynasty of China. Allied with Yuan Shu, he rose to prominence fighting the Yellow Turban Rebellion and the coalition against Dong Zhuo. He was the father of Sun Ce and Sun Quan, and the posthumous founder-figure of the state of Eastern Wu of the Three Kingdoms.

Early life and career

Sun Jian was born in Fuchun, Wu Commandery (in present-day Zhejiang). He claimed descent from the strategist Sun Tzu, author of The Art of War, though the lineage is unverified. As a young official he distinguished himself suppressing a pirate-backed rebellion, and he later served with distinction against the Yellow Turbans and in Liang Province, gaining a reputation as a fierce frontline commander.

Campaign against Dong Zhuo

In 190 Sun Jian joined the coalition of warlords against Dong Zhuo, who had seized the imperial capital. Serving under Yuan Shu, he advanced on Luoyang, defeated Dong Zhuo's forces, and entered the ruined capital — where, according to tradition, his men recovered the Imperial Seal from a well. His success made him one of the most powerful men in China.

Death

In 191 Yuan Shu ordered Sun Jian to attack Liu Biao, the governor of Jing Province. While pursuing the enemy general Huang Zu near Xiangyang, Sun Jian was struck and killed by an arrow — or fell in an ambush — at the age of 36. Sun Quan posthumously honoured him as Emperor Wulie of Wu.
