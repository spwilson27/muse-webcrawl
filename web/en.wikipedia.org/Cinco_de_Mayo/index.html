Cinco de Mayo

Observed byMexico (esp. Puebla); United States
TypeHistorical / cultural
DateMay 5
CommemoratesBattle of Puebla (1862)

Cinco de Mayo (Spanish for "Fifth of May") is a yearly celebration held on May 5 to commemorate the Mexican Army's victory over the French Empire at the Battle of Puebla, on May 5, 1862, under the leadership of General Ignacio Zaragoza.

Background

After defaulting on foreign debts, Mexico faced intervention by Britain, Spain, and France. While the British and Spanish withdrew, France under Napoleon III sought to establish a monarchy in Mexico. At Puebla, a poorly equipped Mexican force of about 4,000 defeated a larger, better-equipped French army of some 6,000. The victory was of limited strategic consequence — French forces captured Mexico City the following year — but it greatly boosted Mexican morale and resistance.

Celebration

In Mexico, the day is primarily observed in the state of Puebla, where it is known as El Día de la Batalla de Puebla, with military parades and re-enactments; it is not a federal statutory holiday. In the United States, Cinco de Mayo has become a broader celebration of Mexican-American culture, with parades, festivals, mariachi music, folk dancing, and traditional foods in cities with large Mexican-American populations. It is frequently, and incorrectly, assumed to be Mexico's Independence Day, which is actually September 16.
