Tier 1 within the bank capital stack

Tier 1 capital is the core capital of a bank under the Basel Accords, consisting mainly of shareholders' equity and retained earnings. It measures a bank's financial strength from a regulator's point of view and is the highest-quality capital because it can absorb losses while the bank continues to operate.

Components

Under Basel III, Tier 1 capital is split into two parts. Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) comprises common shares, share premium, retained earnings and other comprehensive income, minus regulatory deductions such as goodwill. Additional Tier 1 (AT1) consists of instruments such as contingent convertible bonds ("CoCos") and non-cumulative preference shares, which are perpetual and can be written down or converted to equity if capital falls below a trigger.

Ratios and requirements

Banks must hold CET1 of at least 4.5% of risk-weighted assets, total Tier 1 of at least 6%, and total capital (Tier 1 plus Tier 2 capital) of at least 8%, with additional buffers — including a capital conservation buffer of 2.5% — imposed under Basel III after the 2008 financial crisis. A separate leverage ratio requires Tier 1 capital of at least 3% of total exposures, unweighted by risk.

Stress events

The hierarchy of loss absorption was prominently illustrated in March 2023, when the takeover of Credit Suisse by UBS wrote CHF 16 billion of AT1 bonds to zero while shareholders received some value — an outcome permitted by the bonds' terms that provoked litigation and market turbulence.
