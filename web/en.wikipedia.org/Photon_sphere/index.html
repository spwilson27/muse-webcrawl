Photon sphere

A photon sphere (or photon circle) is a spherical region of space around a compact object, such as a black hole, where gravity is so strong that photons are forced to travel in unstable circular orbits. For a non-rotating Schwarzschild black hole the photon sphere lies at radius 1.5 times the Schwarzschild radius (r = 3GM/c²).

Properties

Photons orbiting at this radius follow unstable trajectories: a small perturbation causes them to spiral either outward to escape or inward across the event horizon. In rotating (Kerr) black holes the photon orbits form a more complex photon region of spherical, non-planar orbits.

Observational relevance

The photon sphere shapes the appearance of a black hole's shadow. The Event Horizon Telescope images of M87* and Sagittarius A* show bright emission rings whose size is set largely by lensing near the photon sphere.
