Algiers Agreement

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Signed: 6 March 1975 (announced); treaty 13 June 1975

Location: Algiers, Algeria

Parties: Iran and Iraq

Mediator: Houari Boumédiène of Algeria

The Algiers Agreement (also called the Algiers Accord) was an agreement between Iran and Iraq to settle their border disputes, announced on 6 March 1975 during an OPEC summit in Algiers and formalised in a treaty signed in Baghdad on 13 June 1975. It was negotiated between Shah Mohammad Reza Pahlavi of Iran and Saddam Hussein, then vice-president of Iraq, under the mediation of Algerian president Houari Boumédiène.

Terms

The agreement settled the boundary along the Shatt al-Arab waterway — the confluence of the Tigris and Euphrates forming the countries' southern border — along the thalweg, the deepest navigable channel, rather than the Iranian shore as under earlier treaties. In exchange, Iran agreed to end its support for the Kurdish rebellion in northern Iraq led by Mustafa Barzani; deprived of Iranian backing, the Kurdish uprising collapsed within weeks. Both sides also undertook to end subversion against each other and to settle their land border by demarcation.

Aftermath

On 17 September 1980, following the Iranian Revolution, Saddam Hussein unilaterally abrogated the agreement on Iraqi television, claiming the Shatt al-Arab in its entirety. Five days later Iraq invaded Iran, beginning the eight-year Iran–Iraq War. In August 1990, on the eve of the Gulf War, Iraq formally accepted the terms of the Algiers Agreement again in order to free its forces for the confrontation over Kuwait.
