The Way to Wealth is an essay written by Benjamin Franklin in 1758. It is a collection of aphorisms and advice presented as a speech given by "Father Abraham" to a group of people gathered for an auction, who in turn quotes the maxims of "Poor Richard", Franklin's almanac persona. It was originally the preface to the twenty-fifth (and final) edition of Poor Richard's Almanack.

Content

The essay counsels industry, frugality, and prudence in debt as the means to personal prosperity. Its famous aphorisms include "Early to bed, and early to rise, makes a man healthy, wealthy, and wise", "There are no gains without pains", "God helps them that help themselves", "A penny saved is a penny earned" (in Franklin's version, "A penny saved is two pence clear"), and "Drive thy business, let not that drive thee." Father Abraham warns against idleness, expensive folly, and reliance on credit, arguing that taxes are heavy but "we are taxed twice as much by our idleness, three times as much by our pride, and four times as much by our folly."

Legacy

The Way to Wealth became one of the most widely reprinted essays of the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, translated into many languages and often published as a standalone pamphlet. It is regarded as a distillation of the American values of self-reliance and thrift, and its maxims remain embedded in everyday proverbial speech.
