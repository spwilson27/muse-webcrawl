Richard Feynman

Richard Phillips Feynman (May 11, 1918 – February 15, 1988) was an American theoretical physicist known for his work in the path integral formulation of quantum mechanics, the theory of quantum electrodynamics (QED), and the physics of superfluidity. He shared the 1965 Nobel Prize in Physics with Julian Schwinger and Sin-Itiro Tomonaga.

Life and work

Born in Queens, New York City, Feynman studied at MIT and Princeton. During the Second World War he worked on the Manhattan Project at Los Alamos. He later taught at Cornell and the California Institute of Technology.

Contributions

Feynman developed the pictorial representation schemes now called Feynman diagrams, reformulated quantum mechanics with the path integral, proposed the parton model, and pioneered ideas in quantum computing and nanotechnology in his 1959 talk "There's Plenty of Room at the Bottom".

Later life

He served on the Rogers Commission investigating the Space Shuttle Challenger disaster, famously demonstrating the failure of the O-ring seals in ice water. His semi-autobiographical books Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman! became bestsellers.
