V-tail

A V-tail is an unconventional aircraft tail configuration in which the conventional horizontal and vertical tail surfaces are replaced by two surfaces set in a V shape. The two surfaces, fitted with "ruddervators," combine the functions of rudder and elevators through a mechanical or electronic mixer.

Examples and trade-offs

The best-known V-tail aircraft is the Beechcraft Bonanza Model 35, produced for decades; the Fouga Magister jet trainer and several UAVs and modern military aircraft (in inverted form) also use the arrangement. Advantages claimed include reduced drag and weight; disadvantages include more complex controls and, in the Bonanza's case, a history of structural concerns that led to reinforcements.
