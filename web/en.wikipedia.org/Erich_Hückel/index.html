Erich Armand Arthur Joseph Hückel (9 August 1896 – 16 February 1980) was a German physicist and theoretical chemist. He is known for two major contributions: the Debye–Hückel theory of electrolyte solutions (with Peter Debye) and the Hückel molecular orbital theory of π-electron systems, including Hückel's rule for aromaticity.

Life and career

Hückel was born in Berlin and studied physics at Göttingen, gaining his doctorate under Peter Debye. He worked in Zürich with Debye, where in 1923 they published their theory explaining the behaviour of strong electrolyte solutions through ionic atmospheres. He spent time in England and Denmark, including in Niels Bohr's institute in Copenhagen, before holding posts at Stuttgart and, from 1937, a chair of theoretical physics at Marburg.

Hückel molecular orbital theory

In the early 1930s Hückel developed a simplified quantum-mechanical treatment of π electrons in planar conjugated molecules. Applying it to benzene and cyclic conjugated systems, he derived the famous 4n + 2 rule: planar monocyclic conjugated systems with (4n + 2) π electrons are aromatic and especially stable, while 4n systems are antiaromatic. The approach, though approximate, was foundational for theoretical organic chemistry.
