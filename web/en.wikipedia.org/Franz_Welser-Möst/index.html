Franz Leopold Maria Möst (born 16 August 1960), known professionally as Franz Welser-Möst, is an Austrian conductor. He is music director of The Cleveland Orchestra, a position he has held since 2002, making him one of the longest-serving music directors of a major American orchestra.

Early life

Born in Linz, Welser-Möst studied violin and composition and sang in the choir of Wels Abbey, from which he took part of his professional name ("Welser"). A car accident in 1978 that injured his hand ended his prospects as a violinist and turned him to conducting.

Career

He made his Salzburg Festival debut in 1985 and soon after became music director of the Norrköping Symphony Orchestra. He served as principal conductor of the London Philharmonic Orchestra (1990–1996) — a tenure marked by controversy with local critics — and as music director of the Zurich Opera (1995–2000, and again 2005–2008 as chief conductor). From 2010 to 2014 he was chief conductor of the Vienna State Opera while remaining in Cleveland.

Welser-Möst is a regular guest at the Salzburg Festival and has conducted the Vienna Philharmonic's New Year's Concert three times (2011, 2013, and 2023). His repertoire ranges from Mozart through contemporary premieres, and his Cleveland tenure has been noted for opera performances at Severance Hall and extensive touring.
