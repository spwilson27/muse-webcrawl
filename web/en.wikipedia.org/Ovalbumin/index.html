Ovalbumin

Ovalbumin (abbreviated OVA) is the main protein found in egg white, making up approximately 55% of the total protein. It is a glycoprotein of 385 amino acids with a molecular mass of about 42.7 kDa, and a member of the serpin superfamily, although it does not inhibit proteases.

Properties and uses

Ovalbumin denatures on heating, contributing to the setting of cooked egg white. Its sequence and three-dimensional structure are well characterised, making it a standard model protein in biochemistry. In immunology, ovalbumin is widely used as a model antigen to study immune responses, and in allergy research as a major egg allergen (Gal d 2), though the principal allergens in egg-allergic children are often ovomucoid.
