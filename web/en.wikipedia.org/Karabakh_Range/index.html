Karabakh Range

The Karabakh Range (Azerbaijani: Qarabağ silsiləsi; Armenian: Ղարաբաղի լեռնաշղթա) is a mountain range in the Lesser Caucasus, in the Karabakh region of Azerbaijan (corresponding largely to the former Nagorno-Karabakh area). It extends roughly 100 km in a northwest–southeast arc, forming the southeastern spur of the Lesser Caucasus system.

Geography

The range rises from the Mil plain and the Karabakh lowland toward the Armenian highlands. Its highest summits include Mount Boyuk Kirs (Great Kirs, 2,725 m) and Mount Ziyarət (2,478 m). Rivers such as the Khachin, Terter and Qarqar drain its slopes toward the Kura River. Forests of oak, beech and hornbeam cover the middle elevations, giving way to subalpine and alpine meadows; the Azerbaijani name Qarabağ itself is often glossed as "black garden".

Context

The range lies within the area contested between Armenia and Azerbaijan in the Nagorno-Karabakh conflict; following the 2020 war and Azerbaijan's 2023 offensive, the entire region came under Azerbaijani control. The mountains contain villages and historic monasteries, and their pastures have long been used for transhumant grazing by Azerbaijani and Armenian communities.
