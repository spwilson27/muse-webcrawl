Electroclash

Electroclash is a genre of music that emerged in the late 1990s and early 2000s, fusing 1980s electro, new wave, and synth-pop with techno, punk attitude, and contemporary electronic dance music. The term was coined by New York DJ and promoter Larry Tee, who organized the Electroclash Festival in 2001 and popularized the label.

Characteristics

Electroclash tracks typically feature vintage synthesizer sounds, drum machines, deadpan or theatrical vocals, and an ironic, fashion-conscious aesthetic referencing the early 1980s. Lyrics often satirize glamour, nightlife, and celebrity culture. The scene overlapped with art and fashion worlds, especially in New York and Berlin.

Key artists

Fischerspooner, with their elaborate stage shows and single "#1" (later "Emerge"), were the movement's flagship act. Other central figures include Peaches, Miss Kittin & The Hacker, Chicks on Speed, Ladytron, Felix da Housecat (whose Kittenz and Thee Glitz was a defining album), Vitalic, and Tiga. Precursors included the 1980s electro of Afrika Bambaataa, the synth-pop of the Human League and Soft Cell, and the 1990s work of DJ Hell's International Deejay Gigolo Records in Munich.

Legacy

The electroclash moment was brief — by the mid-2000s the term had fallen out of fashion — but it strongly influenced later electronic pop and dance music, from the blog house era to artists such as Lady Gaga, and it revived mainstream interest in analog synthesizer sounds and 1980s pop aesthetics.
