Worcestershire sauce

Worcestershire sauce is a fermented liquid condiment created in the city of Worcester, Worcestershire, England, in the 19th century. The best-known brand, Lea & Perrins, was first sold commercially in 1837 and is still made in Worcester. The sauce has a savoury, tangy and slightly sweet flavour with a strong umami character.

Ingredients and history

According to the traditional (and possibly embellished) story, chemists John Wheeley Lea and William Henry Perrins recreated a sauce recipe brought from Bengal by a local nobleman; finding the first batch unpalatable, they left it in a cellar, where fermentation transformed it. Typical ingredients include barley malt vinegar, spirit vinegar, molasses, sugar, salt, anchovies, tamarind extract, onions, garlic, and spices such as cloves and soy sauce. The mixture is matured for around 18 months before bottling, and the exact recipe remains a trade secret.

Uses

Worcestershire sauce is used to season meat dishes, soups, cheese on toast (Welsh rarebit), Caesar salad dressing, and cocktails such as the Bloody Mary and the michelada. Because the original contains anchovies, vegetarian and vegan variants made without fish are also produced. Generic versions are sold worldwide, and similar sauces — such as Japanese usutā sōsu — developed from it.
