Port of Lázaro Cárdenas

The Port of Lázaro Cárdenas (Spanish: Puerto de Lázaro Cárdenas) is a deep-water seaport in the municipality of Lázaro Cárdenas, Michoacán, on Mexico's Pacific coast. Built from the 1970s as part of an industrial-port development project, it has grown into Mexico's largest port by cargo tonnage and one of its principal container gateways, serving the industrial heartland of central Mexico via rail links operated by Kansas City Southern de México (now Canadian Pacific Kansas City).

Container terminals at Lázaro Cárdenas, Mexico's largest port by tonnage.

Facilities

The port occupies a large land reserve on both banks of the Balsas River delta, divided into terminals for containers, automobiles, bulk minerals, grain, and steel products. Its container terminals — operated by APM Terminals and Hutchison Ports (LCT) — were among the first in Mexico with semi-automated operations. The adjacent industrial zone hosts steel plants (including ArcelorMittal's major works) built around iron ore shipped from nearby mines.

Significance

With natural depths exceeding 16–18 metres, Lázaro Cárdenas can receive the largest bulk carriers and post-Panamax container ships calling on Mexico's Pacific coast, and it competes with Manzanillo for container traffic to Mexico City and the Bajío. The port is named for President Lázaro Cárdenas del Río, a native of Michoacán, as is the city, formerly known as Melchor Ocampo.
