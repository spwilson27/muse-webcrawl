Five solae

The five solae (from Latin sola, "alone") are five Latin phrases that summarize the core theological principles of the Protestant Reformation. They are:

  • Sola scriptura — "by Scripture alone": the Bible is the sole infallible authority for faith and practice.
  • Sola fide — "by faith alone": sinners are justified before God through faith, not works.
  • Sola gratia — "by grace alone": salvation is entirely a gift of God's grace.
  • Solus Christus — "through Christ alone": Christ is the only mediator between God and humanity.
  • Soli Deo gloria — "glory to God alone": all glory for salvation belongs to God alone.

History

Although the individual phrases date from the Reformation era, the grouping of the five together is a later summary, first widely articulated in the early 20th century. The solae distinguish Protestant from Roman Catholic teaching, which at the Council of Trent affirmed the role of tradition alongside Scripture and rejected justification by faith alone as Protestants defined it.

Use

The five solae remain a touchstone of evangelical and confessional Protestant identity and are frequently cited in doctrinal statements and Reformation commemorations.
