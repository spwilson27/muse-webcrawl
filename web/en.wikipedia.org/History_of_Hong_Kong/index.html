History of Hong Kong

British colony1841/1842–1997
Japanese occupation1941–1945
Handover to China1 July 1997

The history of Hong Kong begins with Neolithic settlement of the area and its incorporation into imperial China from the Qin dynasty. Hong Kong Island was ceded to Britain by the Treaty of Nanking (1842) after the First Opium War; Kowloon followed in 1860, and the New Territories were leased for 99 years in 1898, making Hong Kong a British Crown colony.

Colonial development

Hong Kong grew as a free port and entrepôt. It endured Japanese occupation from 1941 to 1945. After the Second World War, refugees from the Chinese Civil War swelled its population, and manufacturing — textiles, plastics, electronics — drove rapid growth that made Hong Kong one of the "Four Asian Tigers" and a global financial centre.

Handover and after

Under the 1984 Sino-British Joint Declaration, Hong Kong was transferred to China on 1 July 1997 as a Special Administrative Region under the "one country, two systems" principle, with its Basic Law guaranteeing a high degree of autonomy for 50 years. Mass protests, including the 2014 Umbrella Movement and the 2019 anti-extradition bill protests, and the 2020 National Security Law have reshaped its political landscape.
