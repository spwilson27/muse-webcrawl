Oviparity

Oviparity is a mode of reproduction in which animals lay eggs, with little or no embryonic development inside the mother; the embryos develop and hatch outside the mother's body. Oviparous animals include almost all birds, most reptiles, amphibians, fish, insects, and other invertebrates, and two mammals — the echidnas and the platypus (monotremes).

Characteristics

Fertilization may be internal (as in birds and reptiles) or external (as in many fish and amphibians, which spawn eggs into water). Eggs contain yolk that nourishes the embryo, and amniotes' eggs are protected by shells and membranes that prevent desiccation. Oviparity contrasts with viviparity, in which young are born live after developing inside the mother, and with ovoviviparity, in which eggs are retained and hatch within the mother's body.

Evolution

Egg-laying is the ancestral reproductive mode for animals; live birth has evolved independently many times, particularly in squamate reptiles and mammals. Oviparity avoids the burden of carrying developing young but exposes eggs to predators, leading to strategies such as guarding, camouflage, and producing very large numbers of eggs.
