Janitzio is an island and village in Lake Pátzcuaro in the Mexican state of Michoacán. It is famous for the monumental statue of José María Morelos on its hilltop, for its Purépecha fishing community whose fishermen use butterfly nets, and for Day of the Dead (Noche de Muertos) celebrations that draw visitors from across Mexico.

Statue of Morelos

The 40-metre statue of independence hero José María Morelos, built in the 1930s under President Lázaro Cárdenas, dominates the island. Inside the statue, murals by Ramón Alva de la Canal depict scenes from Morelos's life, and visitors can climb to a viewpoint in the statue's raised fist.

Culture

The island's Purépecha inhabitants maintain traditional fishing with butterfly-shaped nets (redes de mariposa) cast from wooden canoes. On the night of 1–2 November, families hold candlelit vigils in the cemetery as part of the Day of the Dead observances around Lake Pátzcuaro.

Access

Janitzio has no roads for cars; it is reached by boat from the docks at Pátzcuaro, and its steep lanes are pedestrian only.
