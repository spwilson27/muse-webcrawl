Wedding ring

A wedding ring (or wedding band) is a finger ring that indicates its wearer is married. It is usually forged from metal, traditionally gold or another precious metal. Rings were used in ancient Rome during marriage, and the custom spread through Christian Europe. Depending on the culture, a wedding ring is typically worn on the base of the left ring finger; the custom derives from the ancient belief in the vena amoris, a vein said to run from that finger directly to the heart. In many European and Latin American countries the ring is worn on the right hand instead.

History

The exchange of wedding rings dates back 4,800 years to ancient Egypt, where braided rings of hemp, reeds, ivory or leather symbolized eternity through the circle. The Romans adopted the custom, using iron rings. By the Middle Ages, gemstones such as sapphires and rubies were set in bands; the first recorded diamond wedding ring was given by Archduke Maximilian of Austria to Mary of Burgundy in 1477.

Customs

In many Western ceremonies the best man holds the rings until they are exchanged during the vows. Some couples engrave the inside of the band with a date or inscription. Plain gold bands for men became common only during the Second World War, when soldiers wore them as reminders of home. In Orthodox traditions the ring may be moved between hands, and in Jewish ceremonies the ring must be a plain, unbroken band owned by the groom.
