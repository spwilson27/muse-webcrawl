Kōchi (Japanese: 高知市, Kōchi-shi) is the capital and largest city of Kōchi Prefecture on the island of Shikoku, Japan. (Not to be confused with Kochi, the port city in Kerala, India — see Kochi (disambiguation).) The city grew up around Kōchi Castle in the province of Tosa, and is known for its castle, its lively Sunday market, the Yosakoi festival and its association with the Meiji-era reformer Sakamoto Ryōma, who was born here.

History

Tosa Province was ruled from the early 17th century by the Yamauchi clan, granted the domain by Tokugawa Ieyasu after the Battle of Sekigahara; their seat, Kōchi Castle — completed in 1611 and rebuilt in 1748 after a fire — is one of only twelve Japanese castles whose main keep survives from the Edo period. In the Bakumatsu period Tosa produced leading figures of the movement to overthrow the shogunate, above all Sakamoto Ryōma, whose vision helped broker the Satsuma–Chōshū Alliance, and Itagaki Taisuke, later a founder of Japan's first political party. Kōchi retains a reputation for political independence and for the Freedom and People's Rights Movement of the 1880s.

Geography and climate

The city lies in the Kōchi Plain on the northern shore of Urado Bay, framed by mountains to the north and crossed by the Kagami River. Its climate is humid subtropical and among the rainiest in Japan, with a pronounced typhoon season in late summer and autumn.

Culture and attractions

Kōchi Castle and its museum, the Sakamoto Ryōma Memorial Museum at Katsurahama beach — where a famous statue of Ryōma gazes over the Pacific — and the 300-year-old Sunday Market stretching over a kilometre from the castle are the city's signatures. Each August the Yosakoi Festival, founded in 1954, fills the streets with thousands of dancers using wooden naruko clappers, a style exported across Japan and abroad. Local cuisine centres on katsuo no tataki, bonito seared over straw flames and served with garlic and salt or ponzu, and the city's drinking culture is proverbially robust.

Transport

Kōchi is served by Kōchi Ryōma Airport, JR Shikoku's Dosan Line from Takamatsu and Okayama, expressways to the other Shikoku prefectures, and one of Japan's surviving tram networks, operated by Tosaden Kōtsū — one of the oldest in the country.
