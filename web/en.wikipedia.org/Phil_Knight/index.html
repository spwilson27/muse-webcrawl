Philip Hampson Knight (born February 24, 1938) is an American billionaire businessman and philanthropist. A native of Oregon, he is the co-founder and chairman emeritus of Nike, Inc., and was previously its chairman and CEO. Knight is one of the richest people in the world, with a fortune built largely on his stake in Nike.

Early life and career

Knight was a middle-distance runner at the University of Oregon, coached by Bill Bowerman. After graduating in 1959 and earning an MBA from Stanford in 1962, he travelled to Japan, where he secured distribution rights for Onitsuka Tiger running shoes. In 1964 he and Bowerman founded Blue Ribbon Sports to import the shoes; the company became Nike in 1971, launching its own line of footwear with the famous Swoosh logo.

Philanthropy

Knight and his wife Penny have donated hundreds of millions of dollars to institutions including the University of Oregon, Stanford University, and Oregon Health & Science University. His 2016 memoir, Shoe Dog, recounts Nike's founding years.
