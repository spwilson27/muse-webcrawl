Adenosine deaminase

EC number3.5.4.4
ReactionAdenosine + H2O → inosine + NH3
GeneADA (chromosome 20q13)
DeficiencyADA-SCID (severe combined immunodeficiency)

Adenosine deaminase (ADA) is an enzyme involved in purine metabolism. It catalyses the irreversible hydrolytic deamination of adenosine and deoxyadenosine to inosine and deoxyinosine, which can then be further degraded and excreted. ADA is found in nearly all human tissues, with the highest activity in lymphoid tissue, where it is important for the proliferation and differentiation of lymphocytes.

Deficiency

Inherited deficiency of ADA, caused by mutations in the ADA gene, allows deoxyadenosine and its metabolite dATP to accumulate to toxic levels in lymphocytes, causing one of the most common forms of severe combined immunodeficiency (ADA-SCID). Affected infants have profoundly impaired T, B and NK cell function and present with recurrent infections. Treatments include enzyme replacement with pegylated ADA (pegademase), haematopoietic stem cell transplantation, and gene therapy — the autologous gene-corrected cell product Strimvelis was approved in Europe in 2016 as one of the first ex vivo gene therapies.

Clinical use

ADA activity in pleural fluid is a widely used biomarker supporting a diagnosis of tuberculous pleuritis, and abnormal ADA levels occur in various liver diseases and haematological malignancies.
