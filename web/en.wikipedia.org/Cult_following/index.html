A cult following is a group of fans who are highly dedicated to a person, idea, object, movement or work — typically a film, television series, book, musician or game that is not, or was not originally, a mainstream success. The phrase borrows the intensity implied by "cult" without implying religious belief: members of a cult following share an intense, often communal devotion out of proportion to the work's wider popularity.

Characteristics

Cult followings are marked by repeated consumption, collecting, quotation and ritual behaviour, and by social bonding among fans, now often organised through fan conventions, forums and social media. Television series such as Star Trek, Doctor Who, Twin Peaks and Firefly, and films from The Rocky Horror Picture Show to The Big Lebowski, are classic cases; dedicated followings can revive cancelled works, sustain long-running franchises, and turn commercial failures into enduring "cult classics".

In marketing

Brands, too, are said to have cult followings when customers show strong loyalty and identity attachment; marketers study how such communities form around niche products and media properties.
