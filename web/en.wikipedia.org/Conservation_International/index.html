Conservation International (CI) is an American nonprofit environmental organisation founded in 1987 and headquartered in Arlington, Virginia. Its mission is to protect nature for the benefit of people, on the premise that human well-being depends on healthy ecosystems.

Approach

CI works in dozens of countries, combining field conservation with science, policy and finance. It is known for developing the concept of biodiversity hotspots — biogeographic regions with exceptional endemic diversity under severe threat — together with Norman Myers and Russell Mittermeier, and for programmes such as the Global Conservation Fund and large-scale marine protected area initiatives.

Programmes

The organisation's work spans rainforest protection, ocean conservation, sustainable agriculture and climate mitigation through nature-based solutions, including carbon funds that pay for avoided deforestation. It partners with governments, businesses and Indigenous and local communities, and produces public campaigns such as the "Nature Is Speaking" film series.

Criticism

Like other large conservation NGOs, CI has faced criticism over corporate partnerships and the social effects of protected areas, debates it addresses through safeguards and community-led conservation policies.
