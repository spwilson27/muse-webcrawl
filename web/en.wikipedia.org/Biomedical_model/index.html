Biomedical model

The biomedical model of medicine is the dominant framework of medical practice in Western societies. It holds that health is the absence of disease and that illness can be explained in terms of biological processes such as infection, injury or biochemical imbalance.

Principles

The model assumes a dualism between mind and body, treats disease as an entity separable from the patient's social and psychological context, and emphasises diagnosis, technological intervention and cure by medical professionals.

Criticism

Critics, notably George Engel, who proposed the biopsychosocial model in 1977, argue that the biomedical model neglects psychological, social and environmental determinants of health and can lead to over-medicalisation of everyday problems.
