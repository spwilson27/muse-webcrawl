Ophthalmoparesis

Ophthalmoparesis is weakness (paresis) of one or more of the extraocular muscles that move the eye. When the weakness is complete, it is termed ophthalmoplegia. The condition can affect one or both eyes and may involve any combination of the muscles supplied by the oculomotor, trochlear and abducens nerves (cranial nerves III, IV and VI).

SpecialtyNeurology, ophthalmology
SymptomsLimited eye movement, diplopia, ptosis
CausesNerve palsies, myasthenia gravis, chronic progressive external ophthalmoplegia, stroke

Signs and symptoms

Patients may report double vision, difficulty moving the eyes, drooping of the eyelid (ptosis) and, in bilateral cases, a restricted field of gaze. Examination reveals limitation of ductions in the direction of action of the affected muscles.

Causes

Causes include cranial nerve palsies from diabetes, aneurysm or trauma; neuromuscular junction disorders such as myasthenia gravis; mitochondrial myopathies, notably chronic progressive external ophthalmoplegia (CPEO) and Kearns–Sayre syndrome; and brainstem lesions affecting the ocular motor nuclei or the medial longitudinal fasciculus, as in internuclear ophthalmoplegia.

Diagnosis and treatment

Assessment includes orthoptic measurement of eye movements, imaging of the brain and orbits, antibody testing and, where mitochondrial disease is suspected, genetic studies. Treatment is directed at the underlying cause; prisms, occlusion or strabismus surgery may relieve diplopia.
