Kyivan Rus (also Kievan Rus') was a medieval state in Eastern and Northern Europe from the late 9th to the mid-13th century, centred on Kyiv. Founded by the Varangian (Viking) prince Rurik and his successors, who established control over East Slavic and Finnic tribes, it grew into the largest state in Europe, encompassing territory in modern-day Ukraine, Belarus, Russia and neighbouring countries. It is regarded as a foundational state in the histories of all three East Slavic nations.

History

According to the Primary Chronicle, Rurik settled in Novgorod around 862; his successor Oleg seized Kyiv in 882 and made it his capital. The Rus' grew rich on the trade route from the Varangians to the Greeks, linking Scandinavia with Constantinople. Prince Volodymyr (Vladimir) adopted Orthodox Christianity from Byzantium in 988, a turning point in the region's history. Under Yaroslav the Wise (r. 1019–1054) the state reached its cultural zenith, producing the law code Russkaya Pravda and Saint Sophia Cathedral.

After Yaroslav's death the realm fragmented into competing principalities. The Mongol invasion of 1237–1240 destroyed Kyiv and ended Kyivan Rus, though the south-western principality of Galicia-Volhynia continued its traditions.

Legacy

The name "Rus" survives in Russia, Belarus and the Rusyn identity, and the Kyivan heritage remains a subject of historical and political contention.
