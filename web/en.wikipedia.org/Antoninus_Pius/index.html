Titus Aelius Hadrianus Antoninus Pius (19 September 86 – 7 March 161) was Roman emperor from 138 to 161. He was the fourth of the Five Good Emperors of the Nerva–Antonine dynasty. Born into a senatorial family, he was adopted by the emperor Hadrian as his heir and successor shortly before Hadrian's death.

Early life and career

Antoninus was born near Lanuvium in Italy to a family whose wealth derived from estates in Gaul and Italy. He pursued a conventional senatorial career, serving as quaestor, praetor and consul (in 120), and later as one of the four consular officials administering Italy and as proconsul of Asia, where he governed with distinction. He became a trusted counsellor of Hadrian, who adopted him on 25 February 138 on condition that Antoninus in turn adopt Marcus Aurelius and Lucius Verus.

Reign

Antoninus succeeded Hadrian on 10 July 138. The Senate granted him the title Pius, possibly for his dutiful insistence on Hadrian's deification despite senatorial opposition, or for his clemency toward senators. His reign was comparatively peaceful and prosperous: he never left Italy after his accession and directed the empire's armies through his legates. Military activity included the construction of the Antonine Wall in northern Britain, advancing the frontier beyond Hadrian's Wall, as well as campaigns in Mauritania, Dacia and along the German frontier.

Administration and law

Antoninus was an able administrator who husbanded the imperial treasury, reduced taxes where possible, and patronised building works across the empire, including the completion of the Temple of Hadrian and the construction of the Temple of Antoninus and Faustina in the Roman Forum. His reign saw important developments in Roman law: he employed leading jurists, including Gaius, and extended protections in matters such as the treatment of slaves and the rights of the accused. He also received embassies from as far afield as India and maintained diplomatic contact with the Parthian Empire.

Death and succession

Antoninus died at his estate at Lorium on 7 March 161, having handed power smoothly to his adopted sons Marcus Aurelius and Lucius Verus — the first time Rome was ruled jointly by two emperors. He was deified by the Senate, and the Column of Antoninus Pius was erected in the Campus Martius in his honour. Historians have generally regarded his reign as a high point of stability and good government in the Roman Empire.
