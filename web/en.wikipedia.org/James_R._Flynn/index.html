James R. Flynn

James Robert Flynn (1934 – 11 December 2020) was a New Zealand intelligence researcher and philosopher, best known for the discovery of the Flynn effect, the sustained rise in IQ test scores observed throughout the 20th century.

Research

Flynn, a professor at the University of Otago, documented in the 1980s that average IQ scores had been rising roughly three points per decade in many countries, a finding with major implications for the debate over intelligence, heredity, and environment. He argued the gains were too rapid to be genetic and too uneven across subtests to represent gains in general intelligence alone, attributing them to environmental changes such as better education, nutrition, and the cognitive demands of modern life.

Other work

Flynn also wrote on political philosophy and ethics, including How to Defend Humane Ideals (2000) and What Is Intelligence? (2007). He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society of New Zealand and remained an active and sometimes controversial public intellectual.
