Jiangsu


Jiangsu is a coastal province of eastern China, with its capital at Nanjing.

Jiangsu (Chinese: 江苏; pinyin: Jiāngsū) is an eastern coastal province of the People's Republic of China. It is one of the leading provinces in finance, education, technology, and tourism, with a GDP among the largest of China's provinces and one of the highest GDP per capita. Its capital is Nanjing, a former national capital, and its most populous city is Suzhou. Jiangsu borders Shandong to the north, Anhui to the west, Zhejiang and Shanghai to the south, and the Yellow Sea to the east.

Geography

Jiangsu is largely flat, consisting of the alluvial plains of the Yangtze River delta in the south and the North Jiangsu Plain, with very low-lying land criss-crossed by rivers, lakes, and canals. The Yangtze and Huai rivers cross the province, and it contains China's third-largest freshwater lake, Taihu, and fourth-largest, Hongze Lake. The Grand Canal runs its full length from north to south.

History

The province's name combines Jiangning (Nanjing) and Suzhou. Historically part of the cultural heartland of southern China, the region prospered on silk, salt, and grain shipped via the Grand Canal; Suzhou and Yangzhou became renowned centres of wealth and culture in the Ming and Qing dynasties. Jiangsu province was formally created in the Qing dynasty in 1667. Nanjing served as capital of the Republic of China from 1927 to 1949, and suffered the Nanjing Massacre during the Second Sino-Japanese War.

Economy and culture

Jiangsu is a manufacturing powerhouse, strong in electronics, machinery, textiles, and chemicals, with major development zones in Suzhou, Wuxi, and Nanjing. Culturally it is the home of Suzhou's Classical Gardens (a UNESCO World Heritage Site), Kunqu opera, Suzhou embroidery, and Yangzhou cuisine, one of the Four Great Traditions of Chinese cooking.
