Audio codec

An audio codec is a device or computer program capable of encoding and/or decoding a digital data stream of audio. In the context of software, an audio codec is a set of algorithms implementing an audio coding format.

Function

Audio codecs reduce the size of audio data through compression, either lossy (discarding some perceptually less important information, as in MP3, AAC, Vorbis, and Opus) or lossless (fully reversible, as in FLAC and ALAC). Uncompressed formats include PCM, commonly stored in WAV or AIFF containers.

History and standards

Speech coding developed early for telephony, with standards such as G.711 (µ-law and A-law companding). Wideband consumer compression became widespread with MP3 in the 1990s, followed by AAC in the Advanced Audio Coding standard. Opus, standardized by the IETF in 2012, unified speech and music coding at low latency and is widely used for internet telephony and streaming.

Hardware codecs

In hardware, an audio codec may refer to a chip that converts analog audio signals to digital and back (combining ADC and DAC functions), found in sound cards, phones, and embedded systems.
