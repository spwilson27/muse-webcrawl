Northampton is a town and civil parish in the East Midlands of England, lying on the River Nene some 60 miles (97 km) north-west of London. It is the county town of Northamptonshire. Northampton's population was recorded at roughly 245,000 in the 2021 census, making it one of the largest towns in England without city status.

History

The town grew around a Norman castle and was a significant medieval centre: parliaments met there, Thomas Becket was tried at Northampton Castle in 1164, and one of England's earliest universities was founded in 1261 before being dissolved a few years later. The Battle of Northampton (1460), during the Wars of the Roses, saw the Yorkists capture King Henry VI. A great fire destroyed much of the town in 1675, after which it was rebuilt.

Industry

From the eighteenth century Northampton became the centre of the English shoemaking and leather industry — "the shoe town" — supplying boots to the army and housing famous makers of luxury footwear. Though mass production has largely departed, high-end shoemaking survives. Modern Northampton is a logistics and distribution hub and home to the University of Northampton.

Sport

Northampton Saints (rugby union, Premiership champions and European cup winners) play at Franklin's Gardens; Northampton Town F.C., "the Cobblers", play at Sixfields Stadium.
