Creatinine

Creatinine is a chemical waste product produced by muscle metabolism. It is generated from creatine, a molecule important in energy production in muscles, at a fairly constant rate depending on muscle mass. Creatinine is transported through the bloodstream to the kidneys, which filter it out and excrete it in urine.

Clinical significance

Measuring serum creatinine is the most commonly used indicator of kidney function. A rising creatinine level suggests impaired filtration by the kidneys. Creatinine clearance, estimated from serum levels together with age, sex, and body size, is used to approximate the glomerular filtration rate (GFR).

Production and excretion

Creatinine is produced mainly from the spontaneous breakdown of phosphocreatine and creatine in skeletal muscle. Because the production rate is relatively steady, blood concentrations serve as a useful marker. It is freely filtered by the glomerulus and also secreted in small amounts by the renal tubules.
