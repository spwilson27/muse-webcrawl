Abidjan

CountryIvory Coast (Côte d'Ivoire)
Population~5.6 million (metropolitan, 2021)
StatusEconomic capital; seat of government
LanguageFrench

Abidjan is the largest city of Ivory Coast and its economic capital. Situated on the Ébrié Lagoon on the Gulf of Guinea, it was the political capital from 1933 until 1983, when Yamoussoukro was designated the capital; most government institutions and embassies nevertheless remain in Abidjan. It is the most populous French-speaking city in West Africa.

History

Abidjan grew from a small fishing village after being chosen in 1903 as the terminus of the railway into the interior. The Vridi Canal, opened in 1951, cut through the sandbar to the sea and created a deep-water port, triggering rapid growth during the "Ivorian miracle" decades after independence in 1960. The city comprises ten communes, including the business district of Plateau, the commercial hub of Treichville, the affluent suburb of Cocody and the vast popular quarters of Yopougon and Abobo.

Economy

The Port of Abidjan is one of the largest in West Africa and handles the bulk of the country's exports of cocoa — Ivory Coast is the world's largest cocoa producer — coffee, timber and petroleum products. The city is the headquarters of many regional businesses and of the African Development Bank (which returned to Abidjan in 2014). St. Paul's Cathedral, one of the largest churches in Africa, is a prominent landmark.
