Ludum Dare (Latin for "to give a game", also rendered "to give a play") is an accelerated video game development competition, or game jam, held online. Participants must create a playable game from scratch within a strict time limit — 48 hours for the solo "Compo" or 72 hours for the team-based "Jam" — following a theme that is revealed at the start of the event and chosen by community vote. It is one of the oldest and largest game jams in the world.

History

The first Ludum Dare was organised by Geoff Howland in April 2002 and attracted only a handful of entries. The competition was subsequently run for many years by Mike Kasprzak through the site's community, growing steadily: by the 2010s individual events regularly drew several thousand submitted games, with multiple events per year. The community migrated to a new site, ldjam.com, in 2017, and the event's numbering passed LD50 in 2022.

Rules

In the Compo, a single developer must create everything — code, art, music and sound — within 48 hours, and must release the source code. In the more relaxed Jam, teams may enter, pre-existing and licensed assets may be used, and the deadline extends to 72 hours. After submission, entrants play and rate each other's games over about three weeks in categories such as innovation, fun, theme, graphics, audio, humour and mood.

Notable games and developers

Many Ludum Dare prototypes have grown into acclaimed commercial releases. Notch created early prototypes in the competition before developing Minecraft; other jam-born works include Cadence of Hyrule's precursor experiments, Evoland, which began as the winning entry of Ludum Dare 24 ("Evolution"), and the prototypes of games such as Broforce and Titan Souls. The event has been credited with helping popularise the game-jam format and rapid-prototyping culture in independent game development.
