Rice is the seed of the grass species Oryza sativa (Asian rice) or, less commonly, Oryza glaberrima (African rice). As a cereal grain it is the most widely consumed staple food for a large part of the world's population, especially in Asia and Africa, providing about a fifth of the calories consumed by humans worldwide.

Domestication and history

Asian rice was domesticated in the Yangtze River valley of China roughly 8,000–9,000 years ago, from where cultivation spread across Asia. Two major subspecies emerged: japonica, adapted to temperate climates, and indica, grown in tropical regions. African rice was independently domesticated in the Niger River delta about 3,000 years ago. Rice reached Europe through trade and was introduced to the Americas by colonists and enslaved Africans, whose knowledge underpinned the rice economies of South Carolina and elsewhere.

Cultivation

Rice is typically grown in flooded fields called paddies, which suppress weeds and pests; the plant itself has adaptations (aerenchyma tissue) that transport oxygen to submerged roots. Upland varieties grow in rain-fed, unflooded fields. The International Rice Research Institute in the Philippines developed high-yielding semi-dwarf varieties such as IR8 that were central to the Green Revolution. Major challenges include water scarcity, methane emissions from paddies, blast disease and the need for climate-resilient varieties such as flood-tolerant SUB1 rice. Golden rice, engineered to produce vitamin A precursor beta-carotene, addresses vitamin A deficiency.

As food

Rice is usually milled to remove the husk (producing brown rice) and then the bran and germ (producing white rice). It is cooked by boiling or steaming and forms the basis of dishes worldwide: biryani, risotto, paella, sushi, congee, jollof and many others. Broken grains, glutinous rice and rice flour have specialised uses. Parboiling before milling retains more nutrients in the grain.

Production

World production of paddy rice exceeds 750 million tonnes annually. China and India together account for roughly half of global output, and over 90% of rice is both produced and consumed in Asia — only a small fraction of production enters world trade, making prices sensitive to export restrictions.
