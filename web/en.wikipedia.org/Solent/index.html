The Solent

The Solent is a strait between the Isle of Wight and Great Britain, in Hampshire, England. It is about 20 miles (32 km) long and varies in width between 2.5 and 5 miles (4 and 8 km), although the Hurst Spit which projects 1.5 miles (2.4 km) into the Solent at its western end narrows the effective width to just over 1 mile (1.6 km).

Major ports on the Solent include Portsmouth and Southampton; smaller ports include Cowes on the Isle of Wight. The city of Portsmouth lies on Portsea Island on the mainland side of the strait. The Solent is a major shipping route for both passenger and freight ships and is one of the world's most important areas for yachting and recreational sailing, being famous for the Cowes Week regatta and for the start of round-the-world yacht races from Cowes and Portsmouth.

Geology

Originally the Solent was a river valley. It was formed during the end of the last Ice Age as rising sea levels flooded the valley of the River Solent, a major river that once flowed eastward through what is now the strait, receiving tributaries including the Test, Itchen, and Hamble. The Isle of Wight was separated from the mainland around 5,000 years ago.

History

The Solent has been an important maritime gateway for centuries. In 1545 the Mary Rose, the flagship of Henry VIII, capsized and sank in the Solent during the Battle of the Solent against a French invasion fleet. The wreck was famously raised in 1982. The Royal Navy's great anchorage at Spithead, at the eastern end of the Solent, has hosted numerous fleet reviews.
