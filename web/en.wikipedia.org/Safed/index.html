Safed

Safed (Hebrew: צְפַת, Tzfat; Arabic: صفد, Ṣafad) is a city in the Northern District of Israel, located in the Galilee at an elevation of about 900 metres (2,953 ft), making it the highest city in the region. It is one of the Four Holy Cities of Judaism, together with Jerusalem, Hebron and Tiberias.

History

Safed rose to prominence in the 16th century, when after the expulsion of Jews from Spain in 1492 many Sephardic scholars settled there. The city became a major centre of Kabbalah, home to figures such as Moses ben Jacob Cordovero and Isaac Luria, and Joseph Karo compiled his code of Jewish law, the Shulchan Aruch, there. The first Hebrew printing press in the region was established in Safed in 1577.

The city was a district capital under the Crusaders, Mamluks and Ottomans. During the 1948 war, Safed was captured by Palmach forces in May 1948 and its Arab population fled or was expelled.

Today

Modern Safed has a population of roughly 38,000, many involved in religious study, art and tourism; its old Jewish quarter, synagogues and artists' colony are notable attractions.
