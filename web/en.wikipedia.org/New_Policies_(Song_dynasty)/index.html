New Policies (Song dynasty)

The New Policies (Chinese: 新法) were a series of reforms implemented in Song dynasty China by the chancellor Wang Anshi under Emperor Shenzong, beginning in 1069. Also known as the Xining Reforms, they were among the most ambitious programmes of state-led economic and administrative reform in pre-modern Chinese history.

Background and aims

The Song state faced chronic fiscal deficits driven by a huge standing army and bureaucracy, and humiliating payments to the Liao and Western Xia. Wang Anshi argued that an active state could enrich the country and strengthen the military — "enrich the state and strengthen the army" — through better management rather than heavier taxes.

Measures

The reforms included the Green Sprouts programme of state agricultural loans to peasants; the baojia system of mutual-responsibility households for local security; labour service commuted to money payments; state trading and price-stabilisation measures; land surveys to equalise taxation; military colony and horse-breeding schemes; and educational and examination reforms emphasising practical learning and Wang's own commentaries on the classics.

Opposition and fate

The policies provoked fierce conservative opposition led by Sima Guang, who argued they harmed the people and enriched the state at society's expense. After Shenzong's death in 1085, the regent Empress Dowager Gao and Sima Guang repealed most of the New Policies, though factional struggle continued and some measures were reinstated under Emperor Huizong.
