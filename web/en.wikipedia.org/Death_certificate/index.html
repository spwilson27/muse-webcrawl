Death certificate

A blank example of a medical certificate of cause of death

A death certificate is a legal document issued by a medical practitioner or government civil registration office certifying the death of a person, recording the date, location, and cause of death. It is distinct from the medical certificate of cause of death filled out by a physician.

Purpose

Death certificates are required to settle the deceased's estate, claim life insurance and pensions, arrange burial or cremation, and compile mortality statistics. They are considered public or semi-public vital records in many jurisdictions, though access rules vary.

Contents

A typical certificate records the decedent's name, date and place of birth and death, occupation, residence, cause of death, and the informant's identity. The World Health Organization promotes an international standard form for certifying cause of death.
