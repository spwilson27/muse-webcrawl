Caste

Caste
DefinitionHereditary, endogamous social stratum ranked within a hierarchy
EtymologyPortuguese/Spanish casta, "race, lineage"
Best-known systemIndia (varna and jati)
Other examplesJapan (burakumin), Yemen, parts of Africa, historical Europe

A caste is a form of social stratification characterised by hereditary, endogamous (marrying within the group) and hierarchically ranked social groups, often associated with traditional occupations and rules of ritual purity. The word comes from the Spanish and Portuguese casta, applied by Portuguese observers in the 16th century to the hereditary groups (jati) of India. Caste systems are most famously associated with India, but analogous systems have existed in many societies.

Characteristics

Caste membership is ascribed at birth and generally cannot be changed within a lifetime. Castes typically practise endogamy, observe hereditary occupations to varying degrees, and are ranked by notions of purity and pollution that govern social interaction, including rules about food, touch and access to temples, wells and other shared resources. Unlike social class, caste status is considered fixed and divinely or karmically sanctioned in its classical religious justifications.

Caste in India

In India, caste comprises two related concepts: varna, the fourfold theoretical order of Brahmins (priests), Kshatriyas (rulers and warriors), Vaishyas (merchants and farmers) and Shudras (labourers); and jati, the thousands of local birth groups that function as castes in everyday life. Communities historically treated as "untouchable", now generally self-described as Dalit, fell outside the varna scheme and suffered severe exclusion. The Constitution of India (1950) abolished untouchability and prohibits caste discrimination, while affirmative-action "reservations" in education, employment and legislatures are provided for Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes and Other Backward Classes.

Caste elsewhere

Comparable hereditary hierarchies include Japan's outcaste burakumin, the akhdam of Yemen, occupational castes in West and East Africa, and the casta system of colonial Spanish America, which classified people by racial ancestry. Scholars continue to debate how far the Indian model applies to these cases.
