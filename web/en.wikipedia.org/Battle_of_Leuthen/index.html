Battle of Leuthen

The Battle of Leuthen was fought on 5 December 1757 at Leuthen in Silesia (modern Lutynia, Poland), during the Seven Years' War. Frederick the Great's Prussian army of about 36,000 routed an Austrian army nearly twice as large under Prince Charles Alexander of Lorraine and Leopold von Daun. The victory — won by Frederick's celebrated "oblique order" attack — recovered Silesia for Prussia and is widely regarded as his tactical masterpiece, studied in military academies ever since.

Course of the battle

Marching behind a screen of low hills, Frederick concealed his army, demonstrated against the Austrian right, then wheeled his battalions against the weak Austrian left flank held by Württemberg and Bavarian auxiliaries near Sagschütz. The Prussian infantry, advancing in echelon with artillery support, rolled up the Austrian line from south to north "like a roll of cloth". A desperate Austrian counterattack by Lucchese's cavalry was destroyed by Prussian cavalry under Driesen. Fierce fighting around the village of Leuthen ended at dusk with the Austrian army in retreat across the Schweidnitzer Wasser; as the Prussians bivouacked, the infantry sang the hymn "Nun danket alle Gott", remembered as the Choral of Leuthen. Breslau surrendered two weeks later, restoring Prussian control of Silesia.
