Otto IV, Holy Roman Emperor

Otto IV (1175 – 19 May 1218) was Holy Roman Emperor from 1209 until his death, and one of two rival kings of Germany during the disputed succession after the death of Henry VI. A member of the House of Welf, he was the only German king raised at the English court, as a nephew of Richard I of England.

Rise

Otto grew up at the Angevin court and was briefly made Count of Poitou by Richard. After Henry VI's death in 1197, the Welf party elected Otto king in 1198, in opposition to the Hohenstaufen Philip of Swabia. Years of civil war ended with Philip's murder in 1208, after which Otto was generally recognised and crowned emperor by Pope Innocent III in Rome in 1209.

Fall

Otto quickly broke his promises to the papacy by invading southern Italy, and Innocent excommunicated him in 1210 and backed the young Hohenstaufen Frederick II instead. Otto's defeat, fighting alongside King John of England, by Philip II of France at the Battle of Bouvines in 1214 destroyed his position. He withdrew to his Brunswick hereditary lands and died in 1218, leaving Frederick II as undisputed ruler.
