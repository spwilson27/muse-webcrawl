Northwest Seaport Alliance

The Northwest Seaport Alliance (NWSA) is a marine cargo operating partnership between the Port of Seattle and the Port of Tacoma in Washington State, United States. Formed in 2015, it unifies the container, breakbulk, and auto cargo operations of the two ports under a single management structure while the ports themselves remain separate public entities. The alliance is one of the largest container gateways in North America, handling trade principally with Asia through Puget Sound.

Formation

Seattle and Tacoma, about 30 miles apart, had competed for decades for container shipping business, despite serving the same hinterland and shipping lines. Facing consolidation among shipping lines into large vessel alliances and competition from Canadian ports at Vancouver and Prince Rupert, the two port commissions announced in October 2014 that they would merge their marine cargo operations. The Federal Maritime Commission approved the arrangement in 2015, and the NWSA began operating in August 2015 — an unprecedented partnership between two traditionally rival public ports.

Operations

The alliance operates international container terminals in both harbours ("North Harbor" in Seattle and "South Harbor" in Tacoma) as a single gateway, allowing shipping lines to call at either with unified services. It also handles breakbulk cargo, roll-on/roll-off vehicles, and project cargoes. Major terminal investments have included the modernisation of Seattle's Terminal 5 to handle the largest container ships. Combined container volumes have generally ranged between 3 and 4 million TEUs per year, making the gateway the fourth-largest container complex in North America.

Governance

The NWSA is governed by the elected commissions of the two home ports sitting together as its managing members, with a chief executive officer responsible for cargo operations. Each home port retains ownership of its assets and responsibility for non-cargo functions such as Seattle's airport and cruise facilities.
