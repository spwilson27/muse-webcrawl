Elementary and Secondary Education Act

The Elementary and Secondary Education Act (ESEA) is a United States federal law enacted on April 11, 1965, as part of President Lyndon B. Johnson's "War on Poverty" agenda. The act fundamentally altered the federal government's role in public education by providing substantial federal funding to primary and secondary education, with an emphasis on equity for disadvantaged students.

The act's most influential provision was Title I, which distributed federal funds to school districts serving large numbers of children from low-income families, using a formula based on poverty counts. Other titles funded school libraries, textbooks for public and private school children, supplementary educational centers, educational research, and grants to strengthen state departments of education.

Legislative history

The ESEA passed Congress with broad bipartisan support and was signed by Johnson at the one-room schoolhouse he had attended as a child in Stonewall, Texas. It marked the first major general aid to education statute at the federal level and roughly doubled federal spending on education, committing more than $1 billion in its first year.

The act has been reauthorized repeatedly, with the reauthorizations reshaping federal education policy:

  • Bilingual Education Act (1968) — added Title VII, funding bilingual education programs.
  • No Child Left Behind Act (2001) — required annual standardized testing, adequate yearly progress, and accountability measures.
  • Every Student Succeeds Act (2015) — replaced No Child Left Behind, returning substantial control over accountability to the states while retaining annual testing.

The ESEA remains the principal federal framework for K–12 education funding in the United States.
