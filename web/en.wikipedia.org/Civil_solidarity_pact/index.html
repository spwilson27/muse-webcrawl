Civil solidarity pact

A civil solidarity pact is a registered form of partnership open to both same-sex and different-sex couples.

A civil solidarity pact (French: pacte civil de solidarité, abbreviated PACS) is a form of civil partnership under French law, open to both same-sex and different-sex couples. Established by the law of 15 November 1999, it provides a registered partnership short of marriage, granting couples certain rights and obligations in areas including taxation, inheritance, housing and social welfare.

Characteristics

Unlike marriage, a PACS is contracted by joint declaration before a registrar (officier d'état civil) or notary and can be dissolved unilaterally by either partner without a court procedure. Partners must organise their financial life together and owe each other mutual material and moral support, but the regime is more flexible and carries lighter formalities than marriage. PACS partners do not automatically take each other's names, and the partnership was originally conceived to give legal recognition to couples — particularly same-sex couples, for whom marriage was unavailable in France before 2013 — without redefining marriage itself.

Adoption and spread

The PACS proved enormously popular: millions have been signed, the great majority by heterosexual couples, and for several years new PACS registrations outnumbered marriages in some French cities. The model inspired similar registered partnerships in other countries. Following the introduction of same-sex marriage in France (law of 17 May 2013), the PACS remains available and widely used as an alternative to marriage.
