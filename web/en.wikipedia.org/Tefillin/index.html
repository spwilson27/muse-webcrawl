Tefillin

Tefillin (Hebrew: תְּפִלִּין; also called phylacteries) are a set of small black leather boxes with leather straps containing scrolls of parchment inscribed with verses from the Torah. They are worn by adult Jews during weekday morning prayers, one on the arm (shel yad) and one on the head (shel rosh).

Biblical source

The commandment derives from four passages in the Torah — Exodus 13:1–10, Exodus 13:11–16, Deuteronomy 6:4–9 and Deuteronomy 11:12–21 — which speak of binding God's words "as a sign on your hand" and "as frontlets between your eyes". These four passages are written on the parchment scrolls inside the tefillin.

Construction

The boxes (batim) are made of leather from a kosher animal, painted black and shaped with precise halakhic requirements. The straps (retzu'ot) are also black on the outside and are tied with distinctive knots, including a shin-shaped knot on the head tefillin.

Use

Tefillin are traditionally worn by men from the age of bar mitzvah during weekday morning services; some women in non-Orthodox movements also wear them. They are not worn on Shabbat or major festivals, which are themselves described as a "sign".
