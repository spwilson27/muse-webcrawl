Pipe band

A pipe band is a musical ensemble consisting of pipers and drummers, most commonly playing the Great Highland bagpipe together with snare, tenor, and bass drums. Pipe bands are strongly associated with Scotland but are found throughout the Scottish diaspora and Commonwealth, in military regiments, police and fire services, schools, and civilian communities worldwide.

A pipe band in full Highland dress on parade.

Instrumentation

A typical band fields a pipe corps led by the pipe major, a drum corps of side (snare) drummers led by the leading drummer, tenor drummers who add rhythmic flourishes, and a single bass drummer. The drum major, carrying a mace, leads the band on parade. All pipers play identical chanters so the corps sounds as one instrument, performing in unison.

History

Pipe bands developed in the British Army in the 19th century, when Highland regiments combined their pipers with regimental drummers. Civilian bands spread with emigration to Canada, Australia, New Zealand, and beyond. The format standardised further in the 20th century under the influence of competition.

Competitions

Bands compete in graded levels at Highland games and championships. The pinnacle is the World Pipe Band Championships, held annually in Glasgow since 1947, where top Grade One bands such as Field Marshal Montgomery and Simon Fraser University have dominated in recent decades. Judged elements include piping, drumming, ensemble, and marching (deportment).

Dress

Members traditionally wear Highland dress: kilts in a band tartan, sporrans, tweed or barathea jackets, glengarry bonnets, and hose with flashes. Military bands may wear full ceremonial uniforms.
