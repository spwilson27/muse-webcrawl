Concertina

A concertina, a free-reed musical instrument.

TypeFree-reed aerophone
InventorCharles Wheatstone (attributed)
Developed1820s–1830s, England
RelatedAccordion, harmonica, bandoneon

A concertina is a free-reed musical instrument, like the various accordions and the harmonica. It consists of expanding and contracting bellows, with buttons (or keys) usually on both ends, unlike accordion buttons which are on the front. The concertina was developed independently in both England and Germany. The English version was invented in 1829 by Sir Charles Wheatstone, while Carl Friedrich Uhlig announced the German version independently in 1834.

Types

Concertinas are categorized by their button systems. The English concertina is fully chromatic, with buttons arranged so that the same note sounds on push and pull of the bellows (unisonoric), and the scale alternates between the two hands. The Anglo concertina, developed from the German design, is bisonoric: each button produces different notes on push and pull, and is strongly associated with folk music in England, Ireland, and South Africa. The duet systems, such as the Maccann, Crane, and Hayden systems, allow melody and accompaniment to be played simultaneously.

History

Wheatstone's first instrument, the symphonium, led to the concertina's development in the 1830s; by the mid-19th century the English concertina was a respected instrument in drawing-room and even classical music, with virtuoso performers such as Giulio Regondi composing for it. Cheaper German-made Anglo instruments spread widely, becoming staples of Morris dancing, Irish traditional music, and the music of the Boer settlers in South Africa, where it is central to boeremusiek.

Construction

A concertina has two end frames connected by bellows, usually of five to seven folds. Pressing a button opens a pad covering a hole, allowing air to flow over a steel or brass reed when the bellows are worked. Instrument quality depends heavily on the reeds: fine English concertinas by makers such as Wheatstone and Jeffries are highly prized by players and collectors.
