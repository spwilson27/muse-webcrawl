Priming (psychology)

Priming is a phenomenon whereby exposure to one stimulus influences a response to a subsequent stimulus, without conscious guidance or intention. For example, the word NURSE is recognized more quickly following the word DOCTOR than following the word BREAD. Priming can be perceptual, associative, repetitive, positive, negative, affective, semantic, or conceptual.

Types of priming

Semantic priming involves words that are associated in a logical or linguistic way. Repetitive priming occurs when a stimulus and response are repeatedly paired. Conceptual priming is based on the meaning of a stimulus and is enhanced by semantic tasks. Masked priming uses partial masking so that the prime is not consciously perceived, yet still influences responses.

Positive and negative priming

Positive priming speeds up processing, while negative priming slows it down. Negative priming was first described in experiments in which participants had to ignore a distractor stimulus and later respond to it; responses were slower than to new stimuli.

Applications

Priming research informs cognitive psychology, psycholinguistics, and marketing. In everyday life, priming effects help explain how recent experiences subtly shape behaviour, memory, and decision-making.
