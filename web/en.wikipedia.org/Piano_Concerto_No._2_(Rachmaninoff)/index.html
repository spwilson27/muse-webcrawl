Piano Concerto No. 2 (Rachmaninoff)

The Piano Concerto No. 2 in C minor, Op. 18, is a concerto for piano and orchestra composed by Sergei Rachmaninoff between June 1900 and April 1901. The piece marked his recovery from a depression and creative block following the disastrous premiere of his First Symphony in 1897, a recovery aided by hypnotherapy sessions with Dr. Nikolai Dahl, to whom the concerto is dedicated.

The concerto premiered in Moscow on 9 November 1901 (27 October Old Style), with the composer as soloist and his cousin Alexander Siloti conducting. It was an immediate success and remains one of the most popular piano concertos in the repertoire.

Structure

The work is in three movements: Moderato, Adagio sostenuto, and Allegro scherzando. It opens famously with the piano alone playing a series of bell-like chords that crescendo from pianissimo, before the orchestra states the sweeping first theme. The slow movement is lyrical and nocturne-like, and the finale combines a scherzando character with a broad, passionate melody.

In popular culture

The concerto's themes have been widely borrowed: the second movement inspired the song "All by Myself" (1975), and the work features prominently in the film Brief Encounter (1945). Its enduring popularity is often credited with restoring the concerto format's Romantic appeal in the 20th century.
