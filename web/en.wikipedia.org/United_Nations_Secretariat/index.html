United Nations Secretariat

The United Nations Secretariat is one of the six principal organs of the United Nations, alongside the General Assembly, Security Council, Economic and Social Council, International Court of Justice and the (suspended) Trusteeship Council. It carries out the day-to-day administrative and substantive work of the organization, as directed by the deliberative organs. The Secretariat is headed by the Secretary-General, the UN's chief administrative officer, and comprises tens of thousands of international civil servants working at headquarters in New York and at duty stations, commissions and peacekeeping missions around the world.

Organization

The Secretariat includes the Executive Office of the Secretary-General; departments and offices covering political and peacebuilding affairs, peace operations, economic and social affairs, management, legal affairs, humanitarian coordination (OCHA), disarmament, and safety and security; five regional commissions (for Africa, Asia-Pacific, Europe, Latin America and Western Asia); and offices away from headquarters in Geneva, Vienna and Nairobi. A major restructuring under Secretary-General António Guterres took effect in 2019, splitting the former Department of Political Affairs and Department of Peacekeeping Operations into new departments for political/peacebuilding affairs and peace operations.

Staff and principles

Under Articles 97–101 of the UN Charter, staff are appointed by the Secretary-General under regulations set by the General Assembly, with recruitment based on efficiency, competence and integrity and due regard to wide geographical distribution. Staff owe their loyalty to the organization alone and must not seek or accept instructions from any government — the foundation of the international civil service. The Secretary-General may bring to the Security Council's attention any matter that in his opinion may threaten international peace and security (Article 99).
