The Port of Shanghai (Chinese: 上海港) is the world's busiest container port, a position it has held since overtaking the Port of Singapore in 2010. Located at the mouth of the Yangtze River on the East China Sea, it serves as the principal gateway for the Yangtze River Delta, one of the most productive manufacturing regions on Earth. In 2023 the port handled over 49 million TEU of containerised cargo, and in 2024 it became the first port in the world to exceed 50 million TEU in a single year. It is operated by the Shanghai International Port Group (SIPG).

Geography and facilities

The port comprises several distinct areas. The older terminals lie along the Huangpu River and at Waigaoqiao on the Yangtze estuary, but the estuary's shallow, silting channels limited vessel size. To overcome this, Shanghai built the Yangshan Deep Water Port on islands in Hangzhou Bay some 32 kilometres offshore, connected to the mainland by the Donghai Bridge, one of the longest sea bridges in the world. Yangshan's fourth phase, opened in 2017, is the largest single automated container terminal in the world.

History

Shanghai grew into China's leading port after it was opened to foreign trade as a treaty port in 1842. By the 1930s it was the dominant commercial hub of East Asia. After 1949 its development slowed, but from the 1990s, with the opening of the Pudong district and China's accession to the WTO in 2001, traffic grew explosively, propelling Shanghai past Hong Kong and Singapore to become the world's top container port in 2010.
