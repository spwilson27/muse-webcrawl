Critical chain project management

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Critical chain project management (CCPM) is a method of planning and managing projects that emphasizes the resources (people, equipment, physical space) required to execute project tasks. It was developed by Eliyahu M. Goldratt and introduced in his 1997 novel Critical Chain. CCPM is an application of the theory of constraints to project management.

Concepts

Unlike the critical path method, which considers only task dependencies, the critical chain is the longest chain of dependent tasks when resource contention is also taken into account. CCPM strips the safety time that estimators traditionally pad into individual task durations and instead aggregates that protection into buffers placed at strategic points: a project buffer at the end of the critical chain, feeding buffers where non-critical chains join it, and resource buffers that act as wake-up calls for critical resources.

Execution

During execution, progress is monitored through buffer management: the consumption of the project buffer is compared with the completion of the critical chain, often displayed on a "fever chart". Tasks are worked on in a relay-race fashion, avoiding multitasking by key resources, and start dates are treated flexibly rather than as commitments to specific milestones. Proponents report shorter project durations and more reliable completion dates, though empirical evaluations remain mixed.
