Roger Daltrey

Roger Harry Daltrey
Born: 1 March 1944, Hammersmith, London, England
Occupation: Singer, actor
Known for: Lead vocalist and co-founder of The Who

Roger Harry Daltrey (born 1 March 1944) is an English singer and actor best known as the lead vocalist and a founding member of the rock band The Who. His powerful voice and stage presence — including his signature practice of swinging the microphone by its cable — made him one of the defining frontmen of rock. With the Who he recorded classics such as "My Generation", "Pinball Wizard" (from the rock opera Tommy), "Won't Get Fooled Again", and "Baba O'Riley".

Early life

Daltrey was born in Hammersmith, London, during the Second World War and grew up in Acton. He made his first guitar from a block of wood and formed a skiffle band, the Detours, which evolved into the Who with Pete Townshend, John Entwistle, and Keith Moon. He worked in a sheet-metal factory while the band built its reputation in London clubs.

The Who

Formed in 1964, the Who became leading figures of the British Invasion and of mod culture, famed for explosive live shows and Townshend's songwriting. Daltrey's voice matured from R&B covers to the operatic demands of Tommy (1969) and Quadrophenia (1973). He starred as the title character in the 1975 film adaptation of Tommy, earning a Golden Globe nomination. The band continued recording and touring after the deaths of Moon (1978) and Entwistle (2002), with Daltrey and Townshend as the remaining original members.

Solo work and acting

Daltrey released solo albums beginning with Daltrey (1973), including the hit "Giving It All Away". He also pursued an acting career in films such as Lisztomania and McVicar, and in television. He has been a prominent patron of the Teenage Cancer Trust, organising annual charity concerts at the Royal Albert Hall. He was appointed CBE in 2005 and was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame with the Who in 1990.
