Pinewood Derby

Founded1953, Manhattan Beach, California
FounderDon Murphy
OrganisationCub Scouts (Scouting America)
Car weight limit5 oz (142 g)

The Pinewood Derby is a model car racing event for Cub Scouts, in which children, with the help of an adult, build a small gravity-powered car from an official kit consisting of a block of pine wood, four plastic wheels and four nails used as axles. Cars race head-to-head down a sloped, multi-lane track, and the event has become one of the most recognisable traditions of American Cub Scouting.

History

The derby was created in 1953 by Cubmaster Don Murphy of Pack 280C in Manhattan Beach, California, whose son was too young for the soap box derby; the first race was held on 15 May 1953. The idea spread rapidly through the Boy Scouts of America and was adopted nationally, and similar events are now run by other youth organisations in several countries.

Rules and design

Official rules limit the car to a maximum weight of 5 ounces (about 142 g), a width of 2¾ inches and a length of 7 inches, and require the kit's wheels and axles to be used. Because the cars are unpowered, performance depends on minimising friction at the axles, aligning the wheels, polishing the nails, lubricating with graphite, and placing the added weight so the centre of mass sits just ahead of the rear axle to maximise potential energy. Races are usually run as elimination heats with electronic timing, and prizes may reward speed and design or paintwork alike.
