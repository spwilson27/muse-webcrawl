Ife (Yoruba: Ifẹ̀, also Ilé-Ifẹ̀, "the place of dispersion") is an ancient Yoruba city in south-western Nigeria, in present-day Osun State. In Yoruba mythology, Ife is the place where the gods descended to create the world and the first humans, and it remains the spiritual heartland of the Yoruba people: the Ooni of Ife is among the most senior of Yoruba traditional rulers. The city is world-famous for the refined naturalistic sculptures in brass, bronze, copper and terracotta produced there roughly between the 12th and 15th centuries.

Mythology and early history

According to Yoruba tradition, the supreme god Olodumare sent Obatala (in some versions Oduduwa) down from the sky with a chain, a handful of earth and a five-toed hen to create dry land from the primordial waters at Ife. Oduduwa became the first Ooni and progenitor of the Yoruba kings, whose crowns are still said to derive legitimacy from Ife. Archaeology shows settlement at Ife by at least the first millennium AD, flourishing as a city-state between about 1100 and 1400, with paved courts (the famous potsherd pavements), glass-bead production at Olokun Grove, and long-distance trade in glass, beads and ivory.

Ife art

In 1910 the German ethnographer Leo Frobenius saw Ife heads and, unable to credit their African origin, attributed them to a lost Greek colony of "Atlantis". In 1938 workers digging near the Ooni's palace unearthed a cache of life-size brass heads of extraordinary naturalism, now icons of African art. Made by the lost-wax (cire perdue) method, the heads likely represent Oonis and court figures; terracotta heads and figures are equally accomplished. Ife art strongly influenced the later court art of the Benin Kingdom, whose brass-casting tradition traces its origin to Ife master Igueghae.

Modern city

Modern Ife is a university city, home to Obafemi Awolowo University (founded in 1961 as the University of Ife) and the Natural History Museum of Nigeria. The Olojo festival, celebrating Ogun, god of iron, draws pilgrims and tourists annually. The city was affected by the Ife–Modakeke communal conflicts over land and local government in the late 20th century.
