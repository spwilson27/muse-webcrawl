A cult film or cult movie, also commonly referred to as a cult classic, is a film that has acquired a cult following — a small but exceptionally devoted group of fans who watch it repeatedly, quote it, and build rituals and communities around it. Cult films are often eccentric, transgressive, obscure or commercially unsuccessful on first release, finding their audience later through midnight screenings, home video and word of mouth.

Characteristics

No single definition commands agreement, but cult films typically defy mainstream conventions, provoke strong divided reactions, and reward repeated viewing with dense detail, quotable dialogue or deliberate strangeness. The Rocky Horror Picture Show (1975), the longest-running theatrical release in history, is the archetype: audiences attend in costume, shout callbacks and act out the film. Other canonical examples include The Big Lebowski, Donnie Darko, Blade Runner, This Is Spinal Tap and Tommy Wiseau's The Room, celebrated precisely for its incompetence.

History

The phenomenon grew out of 1970s midnight-movie programming and grindhouse and exploitation cinema, expanded enormously with the VHS and DVD era, and now thrives through streaming, fan conventions and quote-along events.
