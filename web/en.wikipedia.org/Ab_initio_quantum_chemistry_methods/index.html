Ab initio quantum chemistry methods

Ab initio quantum chemistry methods are computational chemistry methods based on quantum mechanics that solve the electronic Schrödinger equation for molecules using only fundamental physical constants — without empirical parameters fitted to experiment. (The term ab initio is Latin for "from the beginning".) The goal is the prediction of molecular structures, energies, spectra, and reaction properties, in the Born–Oppenheimer approximation with the nuclear positions treated as parameters.

Basis of the methods

Nearly all practical ab initio methods expand molecular orbitals in a finite basis set of functions centered on the atoms (for example, Gaussian-type orbitals in the Pople-style STO-3G, 6-31G, and correlation-consistent cc-pVnZ families) and build approximate wavefunctions or densities within that basis. Accuracy is limited jointly by the method's treatment of electron correlation and by basis-set completeness.

Hierarchy of methods

The foundational approach is Hartree–Fock (HF) theory, a self-consistent mean-field method that includes exact exchange but neglects electron correlation. Post-Hartree–Fock methods recover correlation systematically: Møller–Plesset perturbation theory (MP2, MP4, …), configuration interaction (CISD and full CI), multi-configurational self-consistent field (MCSCF, including CASSCF for bond breaking and excited states), and coupled cluster theory (CCSD, CCSD(T) — the latter often called the "gold standard" of quantum chemistry for single-reference systems). Quantum Monte Carlo and explicitly correlated (F12) variants further improve accuracy. Density functional theory (DFT), while strictly a first-principles rather than wavefunction ab initio method in common parlance, is usually grouped with these approaches because of its similar aim and usage.

Scaling and software

Computational cost rises steeply with system size — roughly N⁴ for HF, N⁵ for MP2, and N⁷ for CCSD(T) in the number of basis functions — which drives research into local-correlation, fragmentation, and linear-scaling methods. Major software packages implementing ab initio methods include Gaussian, ORCA, Q-Chem, MOLPRO, NWChem, GAMESS, Psi4, and CP2K. The field's importance was recognized by the 1998 Nobel Prize in Chemistry to Walter Kohn (density functional theory) and John Pople (computational quantum chemistry methods).

Applications

Ab initio calculations are used to determine equilibrium geometries and vibrational frequencies, thermochemistry and reaction barriers, NMR and electronic spectra, noncovalent interaction energies for force-field development, and properties of systems that are difficult to study experimentally, such as transition states and reactive intermediates.
