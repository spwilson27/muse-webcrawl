Civil defense

Civil defense, or civil protection, is the organization and training of civilian populations to protect themselves and their property against the effects of war, natural disasters, and other emergencies, and to support recovery afterward.

History

Modern civil defense emerged during the First World War with air raids on cities, and expanded greatly in the Second World War: Britain's Air Raid Precautions organization, wardens, blackouts, evacuation of children, and public shelters became familiar features of the home front. During the Cold War, civil defense focused on nuclear attack—fallout shelters, warning sirens, evacuation plans, and public information campaigns such as the American "duck and cover" film. Critics questioned whether survival of a full-scale nuclear exchange was realistic, and funding declined from the 1960s.

Modern practice

Today civil protection agencies in most countries emphasize all-hazards emergency management: flood, earthquake, and storm response; search and rescue; and continuity of essential services. Some states, notably Switzerland and several Nordic and Baltic countries, maintain extensive shelter networks and conscription-based civil protection services. International humanitarian law recognizes civil defence organizations under the 1977 Additional Protocols to the Geneva Conventions, with the blue triangle on an orange field as its distinctive sign.
