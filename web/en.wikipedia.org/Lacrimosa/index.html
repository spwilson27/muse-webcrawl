Lacrimosa

The Lacrimosa (Latin for "weeping") is the final stanza of the Dies Irae sequence in the Roman Catholic Requiem Mass. Its text begins "Lacrimosa dies illa, qua resurget ex favilla judicandus homo reus" — "That day is one of weeping, on which shall rise again from the ashes the guilty man to be judged."

As part of the Dies Irae, the Lacrimosa has been set to music by many composers of Requiems. Mozart's Requiem in D minor (1791) famously breaks off in the Lacrimosa: Mozart died having composed only its first bars, and the movement was completed by Franz Xaver Süssmayr. Other notable settings occur in the Requiems of Hector Berlioz, Giuseppe Verdi and Benjamin Britten, whose War Requiem interleaves the Latin text with Wilfred Owen's poetry.
