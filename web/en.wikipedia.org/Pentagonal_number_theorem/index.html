Pentagonal number theorem

In mathematics, the pentagonal number theorem, originally due to Leonhard Euler, describes the expansion of the infinite product (1 − x)(1 − x²)(1 − x³)⋯ as a power series whose nonzero coefficients are ±1, occurring at exponents that are generalized pentagonal numbers.

Statement

The series takes the form 1 − x − x² + x⁵ + x⁷ − x¹² − x¹⁵ + ⋯, where the exponents 1, 2, 5, 7, 12, 15, … are given by gk = k(3k − 1)/2 for k = 1, −1, 2, −2, …

Relation to partitions

The theorem yields a recurrence for the partition function p(n), since the product is the generating function for 1/p(n)'s series. Euler discovered the identity around 1750; it was later given combinatorial interpretations, including Franklin's involution proof of 1881.
