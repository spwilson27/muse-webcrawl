Peace Corps

The Peace Corps is an independent agency of the United States government that trains and deploys American volunteers to work abroad in international development. Established by executive order of President John F. Kennedy on 1 March 1961 and authorised by Congress that September, it has sent more than 240,000 volunteers to over 140 countries.

Mission

The agency's three goals are to help the people of interested countries meet their need for trained men and women, to promote a better understanding of Americans abroad, and to promote a better understanding of other peoples among Americans. Volunteers typically serve for two years after about three months of training, working in education, health, agriculture, community economic development, environment, and youth development.

History

Kennedy proposed the idea in a late-night speech at the University of Michigan in October 1960. R. Sargent Shriver became its first director. The programme expanded rapidly, peaking at over 15,000 volunteers in 1966. Volunteers were evacuated worldwide in March 2020 during the COVID-19 pandemic — the first global evacuation in the agency's history — before service gradually resumed.
