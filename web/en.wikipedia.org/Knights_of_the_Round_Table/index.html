Knights of the Round Table

The Knights of the Round Table are the legendary companions of King Arthur in the Matter of Britain. First appearing in French romance of the 12th century, they sit at a round table so that none may claim precedence over another.

LeaderKing Arthur
SeatCamelot
Notable membersLancelot, Gawain, Galahad, Percival, Tristan, Bedivere, Kay

Origins

The Round Table itself first appears in Wace's Roman de Brut (1155). Later writers, including Chrétien de Troyes and Thomas Malory, expanded the fellowship and its quests, above all the quest for the Holy Grail.

Notable knights

Sir Lancelot is famed as the greatest knight and for his love of Queen Guinevere; Sir Galahad, his son, achieves the Grail through purity; Sir Gawain is the hero of Sir Gawain and the Green Knight; and Sir Bedivere returns Arthur's sword Excalibur to the Lady of the Lake.

Legacy

The fellowship's ideals of chivalry, courtesy and loyalty have shaped Western literature and popular culture for eight centuries.
