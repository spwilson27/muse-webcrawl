Antarctic oscillation

The Antarctic oscillation (AAO), also known as the Southern Annular Mode (SAM), is a low-frequency mode of atmospheric variability of the southern hemisphere that is defined as a belt of strong westerly winds or low pressure surrounding Antarctica which moves north or south as its mode of variability.

Phases and effects

In its positive phase, the belt of westerly winds contracts towards Antarctica, strengthening the circumpolar vortex, generally bringing drier conditions to southern Australia, New Zealand and southern South America while Antarctica itself cools in the interior but warms on the Peninsula. In its negative phase the westerlies expand toward the equator, allowing more mid-latitude weather systems to bring rain to southern continents.

Trends

The SAM has trended positive in recent decades, a change attributed largely to stratospheric ozone depletion and increasing greenhouse gases. The recovery of the Antarctic ozone hole is expected to counteract the greenhouse-gas-driven trend in coming decades.
