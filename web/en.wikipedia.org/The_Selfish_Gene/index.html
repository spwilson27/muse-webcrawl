The Selfish Gene
Author: Richard Dawkins
Country: United Kingdom
Language: English
Subject: Evolutionary biology
Publisher: Oxford University Press
Publication date: 1976

The Selfish Gene is a 1976 book on evolution by the ethologist Richard Dawkins, in which he popularised the gene-centred view of evolution and coined the word meme. The book builds on the work of George C. Williams and others, presenting organisms as "survival machines" built by genes to propagate themselves.

Argument

Dawkins argues that natural selection acts most fundamentally at the level of the gene: genes that enhance their own replication spread, producing apparently altruistic behaviour through kin selection and reciprocal altruism. The extended phenotype, developed in his later book, extends a gene's effects beyond its own body.

Memes

The final chapter introduces the meme as a unit of cultural transmission analogous to the gene — the origin of memetics and of the modern popular sense of "meme".

Reception

The book became a bestseller and a landmark of science popularisation; later editions (1989, 2006, 2016) added chapters and endnotes. Critics have debated the metaphor of "selfishness" and the reductionism of the gene's-eye view.
