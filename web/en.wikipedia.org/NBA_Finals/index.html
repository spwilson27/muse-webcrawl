The NBA Finals is the annual championship series of the National Basketball Association (NBA). The Eastern and Western Conference champions play a best-of-seven series to determine the league champion, who is awarded the Larry O'Brien Championship Trophy. The series is played in June following the regular season and the earlier rounds of the NBA playoffs.

Format and awards

The Finals has used a best-of-seven format since 1947, with the higher-seeded team holding home-court advantage. The home/away pattern has been 2–3–2 (1985–2013) and otherwise 2–2–1–1–1. Since 1969 the NBA Finals Most Valuable Player Award has been presented to the outstanding player of the series; Jerry West remains the only player to win it while on the losing team, in the award's first year. The championship trophy was renamed for former commissioner Larry O'Brien in 1984.

History highlights

The Boston Celtics have won the most championships (eighteen as of 2024), including a record eight consecutive titles from 1959 to 1966 under coach Red Auerbach and centre Bill Russell, who holds the individual record of eleven rings. The Los Angeles Lakers are second with seventeen titles. Other dynasties include the Chicago Bulls of Michael Jordan, who won six titles in the 1990s, and the modern Golden State Warriors. Rivalries such as Celtics–Lakers, which has produced twelve Finals meetings, are central to the league's history.
