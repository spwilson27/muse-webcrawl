Halych

Halych (Ukrainian: Галич)
City in Ivano-Frankivsk Oblast, Ukraine
Population: approx. 6,200

Halych is a historic city on the Dniester River in western Ukraine, in Ivano-Frankivsk Oblast. It gave its name to the region of Galicia (Halychyna) and to the medieval Principality of Halych, one of the most powerful Rus' principalities of the 12th and 13th centuries.

History

The original seat of the princes of Halych stood a few kilometres from the modern town, at the site now occupied by the village of Krylos, where excavations have uncovered the foundations of the great Dormition Cathedral, once among the largest churches of Kievan Rus'. The principality reached its height under Yaroslav Osmomysl and later united with Volhynia under Roman the Great to form the Kingdom of Galicia–Volhynia.

After the Mongol invasion of 1240–41 the old capital declined, and the modern town of Halych grew around a castle on the Dniester. The town passed through Polish, Austrian, and Soviet rule before becoming part of independent Ukraine in 1991.

Landmarks

Notable sites include the ruins of Halych Castle, the Church of St. Pantaleon (late 12th century) in nearby Shevchenkove, one of the oldest surviving churches in Ukraine, and the Halych National Nature Park, which protects the Krylos archaeological reserve and surrounding forests.
