Partition function (statistical mechanics)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Each energy level contributes a Boltzmann factor to the partition function

In statistical mechanics, the partition function is a quantity that encodes the statistical properties of a system in thermodynamic equilibrium. It is a function of temperature and other parameters, such as the volume enclosing a gas, and most of the aggregate thermodynamic variables of the system — internal energy, free energy, entropy and pressure — can be expressed in terms of the partition function or its derivatives.

Canonical partition function

For a system in thermal equilibrium with a heat bath at absolute temperature T, with discrete energy levels Ei, the canonical partition function is

Z = Σi e−Ei / (kBT)

where kB is the Boltzmann constant. Each exponential term, called a Boltzmann factor, weights the probability that the system occupies a state of a given energy. For a system with a continuous spectrum the sum becomes an integral over phase space.

Thermodynamic quantities

The Helmholtz free energy is directly proportional to the logarithm of the partition function:

F = −kBT ln Z

From this relation the entropy, pressure and chemical potential follow by partial differentiation, and the mean internal energy is given by U = kBT2 (∂ ln Z / ∂T). The partition function is therefore sometimes described as the bridge between the microscopic description of a system and its macroscopic thermodynamics.

Variants

The grand canonical partition function applies to systems that can exchange both energy and particles with a reservoir, and depends on the chemical potential as well as the temperature. In quantum statistical mechanics the partition function is written as the trace of the operator e−βĤ, where Ĥ is the Hamiltonian and β = 1/(kBT). Partition functions also play a central role in quantum field theory and in the path integral formulation.
