Arthur Penn

Arthur Hiller Penn (1922–2010) was an American film and theatre director and producer. He is best known for Bonnie and Clyde (1967), a landmark of the New Hollywood era, and for The Miracle Worker (1962) and Little Big Man (1970).

Career

Born in Philadelphia, Penn began directing in live television drama in the 1950s and won a Tony Award for the Broadway production of The Miracle Worker, which he then adapted for film, winning Academy Awards for its stars Anne Bancroft and Patty Duke. Bonnie and Clyde, starring Warren Beatty and Faye Dunaway, broke conventions on screen violence and sexuality and helped inaugurate a new era of American filmmaking.

Later work

Subsequent films included the revisionist western Little Big Man, the neo-noir Night Moves (1975), and The Missouri Breaks (1976). Penn remained active in theatre throughout his career. He died in New York in 2010, a day after his 88th birthday.
