Paralysis

Paralysis
SpecialtyNeurology, neurosurgery
SymptomsLoss of muscle function in part of the body
CausesStroke, spinal cord injury, multiple sclerosis, cerebral palsy, peripheral neuropathy, infections

Paralysis (or plegia) is the complete loss of muscle function for one or more muscle groups. Paralysis can cause the loss of mobility in the affected area. It most often results from damage to the nervous system, especially the spinal cord, but also from stroke, trauma, poliomyelitis, cerebral palsy, peripheral neuropathy, Parkinson's disease, ALS, botulism, spina bifida, multiple sclerosis, and Guillain-Barré syndrome.

Classification

Paralysis is described by its extent: monoplegia affects one limb; hemiplegia one side of the body; paraplegia both legs and the lower trunk; and quadriplegia (tetraplegia) all four limbs and the torso. It may be flaccid, from lower motor neuron damage, or spastic, from upper motor neuron damage. Partial loss of function is called paresis. Facial paralysis such as Bell's palsy affects the muscles of one side of the face.

Management

Treatment targets the underlying cause where possible and emphasizes rehabilitation: physiotherapy, occupational therapy, assistive devices, and management of complications such as pressure injuries and spasticity. Sleep paralysis, a brief inability to move on waking or falling asleep, is a distinct and usually benign phenomenon.
