Subsequence

A subsequence is formed by deleting some elements while preserving the order of the rest.

In mathematics, a subsequence of a given sequence is a sequence that can be derived from the given sequence by deleting some or no elements without changing the order of the remaining elements. For example, the sequence A, B, D is a subsequence of A, B, C, D, E, F obtained after removal of elements C, E, and F. The relation of one sequence being the subsequence of another is a preorder.

Definitions and properties

Formally, given a sequence (xn), a subsequence is a sequence of the form (xnk) where (nk) is a strictly increasing sequence of indices. Every sequence is a subsequence of itself, and every subsequence of a subsequence is a subsequence of the original.

Subsequences must not be confused with substrings or subsequences in the contiguous sense: a substring requires consecutive elements, whereas a subsequence does not. Subsequences are central in analysis: the Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem states that every bounded sequence of real numbers has a convergent subsequence. In combinatorics, the Erdős–Szekeres theorem guarantees long monotone subsequences in sufficiently long sequences of distinct real numbers, and the concept underlies the longest common subsequence problem in computer science, applied in diff tools, bioinformatics and version control.
