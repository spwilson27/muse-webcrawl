Agency (law)

Agency in law is the relationship between an agent and a principal, where the agent is authorised to act on behalf of the principal to create legal relations with a third party. It is a central doctrine of contract law and commercial law, underlying relationships such as those between employers and employees, companies and their directors, and clients and professional advisers.

Creation of agency

Agency may be created in several ways. An express agency arises from an agreement, oral or written, to that effect. An implied agency arises from the conduct of the parties or the circumstances of a relationship. Agency by estoppel (or apparent authority) arises where a principal's words or conduct lead a third party reasonably to believe that another person is their agent. Agency by necessity can arise in emergencies, and agency by ratification occurs where a principal retrospectively adopts an act done on their behalf without prior authority.

Authority of the agent

An agent's authority may be actual (express or implied) or apparent (ostensible). Actual authority is what the principal has in fact conferred; implied actual authority includes powers incidental to carrying out the agent's express instructions. Apparent authority protects third parties who reasonably rely on the principal's representations, even where the agent exceeds actual authority. Where an agent acts within authority, the resulting contract generally binds the principal and the third party directly, and the agent drops out of the transaction.

Duties

Agents owe their principals fiduciary duties, including duties of loyalty, obedience to lawful instructions, reasonable care and skill, and a duty to account. Principals in turn owe agents duties to pay agreed remuneration, reimburse expenses, and indemnify the agent for liabilities properly incurred. An agent who acts without authority may be liable to the third party for breach of warranty of authority.

Termination

Agency may be terminated by agreement, by revocation or renunciation, by completion of the task, by effluxion of time, or by the death, incapacity, or insolvency of either party. Notice to third parties may be required to end an agent's apparent authority.
