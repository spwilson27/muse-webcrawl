Substellar object

A substellar object, sometimes called a substar, is an astronomical object whose mass is below the minimum mass required to sustain fusion of hydrogen in its core — about 0.08 solar masses (roughly 75–80 times the mass of Jupiter). The term encompasses brown dwarfs and also, under some usages, planetary-mass objects. Unlike main-sequence stars, substellar objects cannot reach a stable equilibrium powered by hydrogen fusion and instead cool and fade over time.

Classification

Brown dwarfs (roughly 13 to 75–80 Jupiter masses) can fuse deuterium (and, at the high end, lithium) for limited periods. Objects below the deuterium-burning limit (~13 Jupiter masses) overlap in mass with gas giant planets; free-floating examples are sometimes termed rogue planets or sub-brown dwarfs. The boundary definitions remain a subject of discussion, including in the working definition adopted by the International Astronomical Union.

Properties

Substellar objects emit most of their energy in the infrared. Brown dwarfs are assigned spectral types M, L, T, and Y in order of decreasing temperature, and many have been discovered by infrared surveys such as 2MASS and WISE.
