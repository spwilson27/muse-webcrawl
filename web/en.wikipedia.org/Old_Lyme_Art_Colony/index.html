The Old Lyme Art Colony of Old Lyme, Connecticut was one of the most famous art colonies in the United States and a centre of American Impressionism. It was founded in 1899 when the artist Henry Ward Ranger began spending summers in the town, and it centred on the boarding house of Florence Griswold.

History

Ranger and his Tonalist circle first gathered at Griswold's Georgian mansion on Lyme Street. In 1903 the painter Childe Hassam arrived, and the colony shifted decisively toward Impressionism. Other members included Willard Metcalf, Walter Griffin, and Edward Francis Rook. The artists famously painted panels on the doors and walls of the Griswold house dining room.

Legacy

The Florence Griswold Museum now preserves the house and the colony's works. The Lyme Art Association, founded by colony artists in 1914, built one of the first galleries in the United States run by artists. President Woodrow Wilson's wife Ellen Axson Wilson, herself a painter, was among the colony's associates.
