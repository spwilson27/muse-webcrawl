Inner function

FieldComplex analysis, functional analysis
DomainOpen unit disc
Defining propertyBounded analytic, radial limits of modulus 1 almost everywhere on the unit circle
Associated theoremBeurling's theorem; inner–outer factorisation

In complex analysis, an inner function is a bounded holomorphic function on the open unit disc whose radial boundary values have modulus equal to 1 almost everywhere on the unit circle. Inner functions are the building blocks of the Hardy spaces H∞ and H2, and play a central role in the theory of invariant subspaces and in operator theory.

Structure

Every inner function can be written (up to a unimodular constant) as the product of a Blaschke product — built from the zeros of the function inside the disc — and a singular inner function, an exponential of an integral against a positive singular measure on the circle. The simplest singular inner function is exp((z+1)/(z−1)) composed with a positive scale factor. By the inner–outer factorisation, any nonzero H∞ (or Hp) function factors uniquely as an inner function times an outer function determined by the modulus of its boundary values.

Beurling's theorem

Arne Beurling's celebrated theorem of 1949 states that every nontrivial closed subspace of the Hardy space H2 invariant under the shift operator (multiplication by z) has the form θH2 for some inner function θ. This result made inner functions fundamental to the study of operators on Hilbert space and influenced the development of control theory and signal processing, where inner functions correspond to all-pass filters.
