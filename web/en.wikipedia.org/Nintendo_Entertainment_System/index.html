Nintendo Entertainment System

The Nintendo Entertainment System (NES) is an 8-bit home video game console developed and marketed by Nintendo. It was released in Japan in 1983 as the Family Computer (Famicom), and redesigned for international markets as the NES, launching in North America in 1985 and in Europe in 1986–1987. The console is widely credited with revitalising the video game industry after the North American video game crash of 1983.

History

Following the success of its arcade games in the early 1980s, Nintendo designed a cartridge-based home system under the direction of Masayuki Uemura. The Famicom launched in Japan on 15 July 1983. After the video game crash of 1983 devastated the North American market, Nintendo repositioned the machine as an "entertainment system" rather than a game console, packaging it with the R.O.B. robot accessory and marketing it through toy departments.

The NES popularised features including the directional pad (D-pad) controller design, third-party licensing controls enforced by the 10NES lockout chip, and side-scrolling platform games.

Games

The NES library includes landmark titles such as Super Mario Bros., The Legend of Zelda, Metroid, Mega Man, Castlevania, Final Fantasy and Tetris. More than 700 licensed games were released for the system. The best-selling title, Super Mario Bros., was bundled with the console and sold over 40 million copies.

Legacy

The NES was succeeded by the Super Nintendo Entertainment System in 1990. In 2016 Nintendo released the NES Classic Edition, a miniature dedicated console with 30 built-in games, which became a major commercial success. The original system's influence on game design, business practices and popular culture is extensive.
