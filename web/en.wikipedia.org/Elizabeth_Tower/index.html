Elizabeth Tower

The Elizabeth Tower is the clock tower at the north end of the Palace of Westminster in London, England. It houses the Great Bell, known as Big Ben — a name often applied, inaccurately, to the whole tower.

History

The tower was designed by Augustus Pugin and Charles Barry in the Gothic Revival style and completed in 1859 as part of the new palace built after the fire of 1834. Standing 96 metres (316 ft) tall, it was originally called the Clock Tower, was renamed the Elizabeth Tower in 2012 to mark the Diamond Jubilee of Elizabeth II.

The clock and bells

The Great Clock of Westminster, designed by Edmund Beckett Denison and installed by Edward John Dent, is famous for its reliability. Big Ben first struck in July 1859; the bell cracked two months later and has since been struck with a lighter hammer, giving it its distinctive tone. The tower underwent a major conservation programme from 2017 to 2022.
