The First Quebec Conference, codenamed QUADRANT, was a highly secret military conference of the Allied governments of the United Kingdom and the United States held in Quebec City from 17 to 24 August 1943, during the Second World War. The meetings took place at the Citadelle (the official residence of the Governor General) and the Château Frontenac hotel. The chief representatives were Winston Churchill, Franklin D. Roosevelt and the Combined Chiefs of Staff; Canada's prime minister William Lyon Mackenzie King acted as host but did not take part in the military deliberations.

Decisions

The conference approved the plan for Operation Overlord, the Allied invasion of Normandy, setting a target date of 1 May 1944 and confirming it as the principal Anglo-American effort for 1944, with a supporting landing in southern France (Operation Anvil). The Combined Bomber Offensive against Germany was reaffirmed and intensified, operations in the Mediterranean and Burma were planned, and the leaders discussed stepping up the war against Japan, including operations in the Pacific and the use of bases in China.

Quebec Agreement

On 19 August 1943 Churchill and Roosevelt signed the Quebec Agreement, merging the British and American atomic bomb projects (Tube Alloys and the Manhattan Project) into a single combined effort, with pledges not to use the weapon against each other or against third parties without mutual consent, and establishing the Combined Policy Committee to govern the collaboration.

Aftermath

QUADRANT was followed by the Cairo and Tehran conferences later in 1943 and by a Second Quebec Conference (OCTAGON) in September 1944, which dealt with the occupation of Germany and the Morgenthau Plan.
