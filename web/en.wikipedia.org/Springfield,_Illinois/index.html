Springfield is the capital city of the U.S. state of Illinois and the county seat of Sangamon County. With a population of about 114,000, it is the sixth-most populous city in Illinois and the largest in the state's central region. Springfield is best known as the home of Abraham Lincoln before he became the 16th president of the United States.

History

Settled in the late 1810s and founded in 1821 as Calhoun, the settlement was renamed Springfield after Spring Creek. It became the state capital in 1839, largely through the efforts of a group of legislators known as the "Long Nine", which included the young lawyer Abraham Lincoln. Lincoln lived in Springfield from 1837 until 1861, practising law and serving in the state legislature and one term in the U.S. House of Representatives.

Lincoln sites

Springfield preserves numerous Lincoln-associated landmarks, including the Lincoln Home National Historic Site, the Abraham Lincoln Presidential Library and Museum, the Old State Capitol where Lincoln delivered his "House Divided" speech in 1858, and Lincoln's Tomb at Oak Ridge Cemetery, the second most visited cemetery in the United States after Arlington.

Government and economy

As the state capital, government is the dominant employer, alongside healthcare, insurance and education. The current Illinois State Capitol, completed in 1888, has a dome taller than that of the U.S. Capitol. The city lies on historic Route 66 and claims the invention of the corn dog (as the "Cozy Dog") and the horseshoe sandwich, a local speciality.

Geography and climate

Springfield sits on the Sangamon River floodplain in central Illinois, about 320 km (200 miles) southwest of Chicago. It has a humid continental climate with hot summers and cold, snowy winters.
