Vittorio Gallese (born 1959) is an Italian professor of psychobiology at the University of Parma, Italy, where he teaches experimental aesthetics. He is best known for his work with the Parma research group that discovered mirror neurons, and for developing the theory of embodied simulation.

Career and research

Gallese studied medicine and neurology at the University of Parma. Together with Giacomo Rizzolatti, Leonardo Fogassi and other colleagues, he was involved in the experiments of the early 1990s that identified mirror neurons in the ventral premotor cortex of the macaque monkey — neurons that discharge both when an individual performs an action and when it observes another individual performing a similar action.

Building on this discovery, Gallese proposed that the mirror neuron system provides a functional basis for understanding the actions, intentions and emotions of others through an internal simulation process, a mechanism he later generalized as "embodied simulation" and linked to empathy, intersubjectivity and aesthetic experience. His work, conducted with philosopher Alvin Goldman and others, has influenced cognitive neuroscience, philosophy of mind and film theory.

Selected works

  • With Alvin Goldman, "Mirror neurons and the simulation theory of mind-reading" (1998).
  • "The 'shared manifold' hypothesis" (2001).
  • With David Freedberg, "Motion, emotion and empathy in esthetic experience" (2007).
