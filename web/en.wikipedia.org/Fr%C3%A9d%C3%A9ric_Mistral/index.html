Frédéric Mistral (1830–1914) was a French writer and lexicographer of the Occitan language, the leading figure of the Félibrige movement that sought to revive Provençal literature and language. He shared the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1904.

Life and work

Born in Maillane in the Bouches-du-Rhône, Mistral studied law at Aix-en-Provence but devoted himself to poetry in his native Provençal. In 1854 he co-founded the Félibrige with six other poets. His epic poem Mirèio (1859), a twelve-canto tale of a young Provençal girl, established his reputation across Europe.

Major works

His other works include Calendau (1867), Lis Isclo d'or (1875), Nerto (1884), La Rèino Jano (1890), and the epic Lou Pouèmo dóu Rose (1897). His monumental dictionary of Provençal, Lou Tresor dóu Felibrige (1878–1886), remains a foundational work of Occitan lexicography.

Legacy

Mistral used part of his Nobel Prize money to found the Museon Arlaten in Arles, a museum of Provençal culture. The Chilean poet and Nobel laureate Gabriela Mistral took her pen name in his honour.
