Charles Townshend, 2nd Viscount Townshend (1674–1738) was an English Whig statesman and agricultural innovator. As Secretary of State for the Northern Department he was, with his brother-in-law Robert Walpole, one of the dominant figures in British government in the 1720s. His enthusiasm for growing turnips earned him the nickname "Turnip Townshend."

Political career

Townshend succeeded to the viscountcy in 1687 and entered politics as a Whig. He helped negotiate the Treaty of Utrecht settlement, served as ambassador to the Dutch Republic, and after the Hanoverian succession of 1714 became Secretary of State. Dismissed in 1716, he returned to office and from 1721 shared power with Walpole, directing foreign policy, including the Treaty of Hanover alliance. Their rivalry ended with Townshend's resignation in 1730.

Agriculture

In retirement at Raynham Hall in Norfolk, Townshend promoted the four-course crop rotation (wheat, turnips, barley, clover) associated with the Norfolk system, which dispensed with fallow land and boosted yields. His popularisation of turnips as a field crop made him a symbol of the British Agricultural Revolution.
