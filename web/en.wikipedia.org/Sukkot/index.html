Sukkot

A sukkah, the temporary hut erected for the festival.

Sukkot is a Torah-commanded Jewish holiday celebrated for seven days, beginning on the 15th day of the month of Tishrei. It is one of the Three Pilgrimage Festivals (Shalosh Regalim) on which Israelites were commanded to make a pilgrimage to the Temple in Jerusalem. The holiday has dual significance: it commemorates the harvest and the exodus from Egypt, specifically the forty years of wandering in the desert during which the Israelites lived in temporary shelters.

Observances

The central observance is dwelling — eating meals and, where customary, sleeping — in a sukkah, a temporary booth with a roof of natural material (s'chach) through which the stars can be seen. Another key ritual is taking the Four Species (arba'at ha-minim): a lulav (palm frond), etrog (citron), hadassim (myrtle branches) and aravot (willow branches), which are waved in all directions during prayers.

The first day (two days in the diaspora traditionally) is a full festival with work restrictions; the intermediate days are Chol HaMoed. The seventh day is Hoshana Rabbah, marked by processions with the Four Species; the festival is followed immediately by Shemini Atzeret and Simchat Torah. Historically the Temple service included the water-drawing celebration (Simchat Beit HaShoeivah) and the willow ceremony.
