Time-of-flight mass spectrometry

Time-of-flight mass spectrometry (TOFMS) is a method of mass spectrometry in which an ion's mass-to-charge ratio is determined via a time of flight measurement. Ions are accelerated by an electric field of known strength. This acceleration results in an ion having the same kinetic energy as any other ion that has the same charge. The velocity of the ion depends on the mass-to-charge ratio (heavier ions of the same charge reach lower speeds, although ions with higher charge will also increase in velocity). The time that the ion takes to reach a detector at a known distance is measured. This time will depend on the velocity of the ion, and therefore is a measure of its mass-to-charge ratio. From this ratio and known experimental parameters, one can identify the ion.

Theory

The potential energy of a charged particle in an electric field is related to the charge of the particle and the strength of the electric field. When the charged particle is accelerated into a time-of-flight tube by a voltage U, its potential energy is converted to kinetic energy. Because all ions ideally have the same kinetic energy after acceleration, lighter ions travel faster and reach the detector sooner; the flight time is proportional to the square root of the mass-to-charge ratio.

Delayed extraction and reflectron

Two key improvements made modern TOF instruments practical. Delayed extraction (time-lag focusing) compensates for the initial spread of ion velocities by delaying the extraction pulse. The reflectron, developed by Boris Mamyrin in the 1970s, is an electrostatic mirror that reflects ions back through the flight tube; faster (more energetic) ions penetrate deeper into the reflectron and take a longer path, so ions of the same mass but different energies arrive at the detector at the same time, greatly improving resolution.

Applications

TOF analyzers are combined with ionization methods such as MALDI (MALDI-TOF) for analysis of proteins, peptides and polymers, and with electrospray in Q-TOF instruments for high-resolution accurate-mass measurements. TOF instruments offer a theoretically unlimited mass range and very fast acquisition, making them suited to proteomics, imaging mass spectrometry, and monitoring of fast processes. Space probes, such as those aboard Rosetta, have carried compact TOF mass spectrometers.
