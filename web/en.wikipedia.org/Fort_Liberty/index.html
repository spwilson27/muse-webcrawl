Fort Liberty

Fort Liberty, formerly Fort Bragg, is a United States Army installation in North Carolina, near Fayetteville and Spring Lake. Covering about 251 square miles across several counties, it is one of the largest military installations in the world by population, home to roughly 54,000 military personnel. It hosts the XVIII Airborne Corps, the 82nd Airborne Division, United States Army Special Operations Command, and the headquarters of U.S. Army Forces Command and Army Reserve Command.

History

The installation was established in 1918 as Camp Bragg, an artillery training ground named for Confederate general Braxton Bragg, a North Carolina native. It became a permanent post, Fort Bragg, in 1922. During the Second World War it expanded enormously, training infantry, artillery, and airborne units. The 82nd Airborne Division was reactivated there in 1942, and the post became synonymous with American airborne and special operations forces. In the 1950s the Psychological Warfare Center was established, leading to the creation of the Special Forces ("Green Berets"), whose John F. Kennedy Special Warfare Center and School remains on post.

Renaming

Following the 2021 National Defense Authorization Act and the work of the Naming Commission, which recommended removing names honouring Confederate figures, the post was redesignated Fort Liberty on 2 June 2023 — the new name honouring the value of liberty rather than an individual. In February 2025, the Department of Defense announced the installation would again be called Fort Bragg, this time in honour of Private First Class Roland L. Bragg, a decorated Second World War paratrooper, restoring the familiar name with a new namesake.

Role

Fort Bragg/Liberty is the Army's principal power-projection platform on the East Coast: the 82nd Airborne's Immediate Response Force can deploy anywhere in the world within 18 hours. Pope Field, adjoining the post, provides airlift support. The installation also hosts the Womack Army Medical Center and extensive training ranges in the North Carolina Sandhills.
