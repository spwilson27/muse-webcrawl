Regulatory body

A regulatory body, also called a regulatory authority or regulator, is a public authority or government agency responsible for exercising autonomous authority over an area of human activity in a licensing and regulatory capacity. Regulators are typically established by statute and given powers to set standards, monitor compliance, and enforce rules within a defined sector.

Functions

Regulatory bodies commonly grant licences or permits, conduct inspections, investigate complaints, and impose sanctions ranging from warnings and fines to suspension or revocation of a licence. Many regulators also issue guidance and codes of practice intended to clarify how regulated firms should meet their obligations.

Independence

Although regulators are usually created by government, many operate at arm's length from ministers in order to make decisions on technical grounds and to provide stability and predictability to regulated industries. Funding may come from government appropriations or from levies on the regulated sector.

Examples

Well-known regulators include financial supervisors such as central banks and securities commissions, environmental protection agencies, food safety authorities, and professional licensing boards for medicine, law, and engineering.
