Ken–Betwa River Link

The Ken–Betwa River Link is a river interlinking project in India that proposes to transfer water from the Ken River to the Betwa River, both tributaries of the Yamuna, in order to irrigate the drought-prone Bundelkhand region of Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh. It is the first project under India's National Perspective Plan for interlinking of rivers to reach the implementation stage.

The project

The centrepiece of the project is the Daudhan dam on the Ken River in Madhya Pradesh, along with a link canal of roughly 220 kilometres transferring surplus Ken water to the Betwa basin. The project is intended to provide irrigation to over a million hectares, supply drinking water to several million people in Bundelkhand, and generate hydroelectric power. An agreement between the central government and the states of Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh was signed in March 2021, and construction work began in the following years.

Controversy

The project has been criticised by environmentalists because the Daudhan dam and reservoir will submerge a significant portion of the Panna Tiger Reserve, a habitat for Bengal tigers and the endangered gharial population of the Ken River. Hydrologists have also questioned the assumption that the Ken basin has surplus water. Supporters argue that the chronic water scarcity and agrarian distress of Bundelkhand justify the project.
