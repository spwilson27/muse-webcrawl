Kelvingrove Park is a public park in the West End of Glasgow, Scotland, laid out on the banks of the River Kelvin from 1852 to a design by Sir Joseph Paxton. It is one of the city's best-loved green spaces and contains the Kelvingrove Art Gallery and Museum.

History

The park hosted three great exhibitions — the International Exhibition of 1888, the Scottish Exhibition of National History, Art and Industry of 1911, and the Empire Exhibition of 1938 in part — and contains monuments including the Stewart Memorial Fountain (1872) and statues of Lord Kelvin and Joseph Lister. It remains a venue for festivals, bowling greens, a skatepark and the annual Glasgow Mela.
