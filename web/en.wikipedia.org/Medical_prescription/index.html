Medical prescription

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A prescription, often abbreviated ℞ or Rx, is a formal communication from a physician or other registered health-care professional to a pharmacist, authorising them to dispense a specific prescribed drug for a specific patient. Historically, it was a doctor's instruction to an apothecary listing the materials to be compounded into a treatment; the symbol ℞ derives from the Latin word recipe, "take".

Contents

A typical prescription includes the patient's name, the prescriber's details, the medication name, strength, dosage form, quantity, directions for use (the signa), and the number of refills permitted. Many jurisdictions now use electronic prescribing (e-prescribing), which transmits orders directly to the pharmacy and can reduce handwriting errors.

Legal regulation

Prescription-only medicines may be dispensed only against a valid prescription from an authorised prescriber, such as a physician, dentist, advanced practice nurse or pharmacist prescriber, depending on the jurisdiction. Controlled substances are subject to additional restrictions.

Abbreviations

Traditional Latin abbreviations (such as bid for twice daily and po for by mouth) remain in use, though safety organisations discourage error-prone abbreviations.
