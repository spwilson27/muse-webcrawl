Namadgi National Park

Namadgi National Park is a national park in the south-western part of the Australian Capital Territory, Australia, covering about 1,060 square kilometres — some 46 per cent of the ACT. Gazetted in 1984, it protects the northern end of the Australian Alps, adjoining Kosciuszko National Park in New South Wales.

Landscape and ecology

The park includes rugged granite mountains, including Mount Bimberi (1,912 m), the highest peak in the ACT, alpine and subalpine snow-gum woodland, wet tall forests and grassland valleys. It forms most of the catchment for Canberra's water supply. Wildlife includes the endangered northern corroboree frog and the broad-toothed rat.

Heritage

The name Namadgi derives from the local Aboriginal name for the ranges. The park contains Aboriginal rock-art sites such as the Yankee Hat paintings, as well as European heritage including pastoral huts and the Orroral and Honeysuckle Creek space-tracking stations. The park was badly affected by the January 2020 bushfires, which burnt a large proportion of its area.
