Korean drama

Korean drama (Korean: 한국 드라마), also known as K-drama, refers to television series produced in South Korea in the Korean language. Formats range from short web series to long-running daily serials, with prime-time miniseries of typically 12–24 episodes being the most internationally prominent.

Popularity

From the late 1990s, K-dramas became a central part of the Korean Wave (Hallyu), winning mass audiences across Asia and later worldwide through streaming platforms such as Netflix. Historical epics (sageuk), melodramas, romantic comedies and thrillers are major genres; internationally successful titles include Winter Sonata, Jewel in the Palace, Crash Landing on You and the survival drama Squid Game.

Production

Series are often written and filmed close to broadcast, allowing audience response to shape later episodes, and are characterised by single-season story arcs, prominent product placement and official soundtracks (OSTs).
