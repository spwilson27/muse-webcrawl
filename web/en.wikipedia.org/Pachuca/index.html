Pachuca, formally Pachuca de Soto, is the capital and largest city of the Mexican state of Hidalgo. It is located in the south-central part of the state, about 90 kilometres (56 mi) north of Mexico City, in a valley at high elevation in the Sierra de Pachuca. The city is nicknamed La Bella Airosa ("the beautiful windy one") for the strong winds that blow through its hills for much of the year.

History

The area was settled long before the Spanish conquest; the name derives from Nahuatl, though its exact meaning is disputed. Silver was discovered in the surrounding hills in 1552, and mining defined Pachuca's economy for centuries. In the 19th century, miners from Cornwall, England, arrived to work the silver mines, leaving a lasting legacy: Cornish pasties (locally pastes) remain a signature food, and the Cornish introduced association football to Mexico here — C.F. Pachuca, founded by Cornish miners in 1901, is one of Mexico's oldest football clubs.

Landmarks

The city's emblem is the Reloj Monumental, a 40-metre clock tower in Plaza Independencia built between 1904 and 1910 with machinery from the same London company that made Big Ben's clock. Other sights include the former monastery and church of San Francisco, the Casa Rule, the David Ben Gurión park with its large mosaic mural, and the nearby mining town of Mineral del Monte (Real del Monte). The Universidad Autónoma del Estado de Hidalgo is based in the city.
