Demersal fish

Demersal fish are fish that live on or near the bottom of the sea or lakes. They occupy the sea floors and lake beds, which usually consist of mud, sand, gravel or rocks. In coastal waters they are found on or near the continental shelf, and in open waters they may be found on the continental slope or in the deep sea.

Characteristics

Demersal fish are often grouped into benthic fish, which physically rest on the bottom, and benthopelagic fish, which float in the water column just above it. Many demersal species have adaptations for bottom life: flattened bodies in flatfish such as flounder and halibut; downturned mouths for feeding on invertebrates in the sediment; and, in deep-sea forms, reduced or absent swim bladders and large mouths. Many are important food fish — cod, haddock, plaice, sole, turbot, groupers, and snappers among them — and they are targeted by demersal (bottom) trawl fisheries.

Feeding

Demersal fish feed on benthic invertebrates such as crustaceans, molluscs, worms, and echinoderms, or on other fish. Some are ambush predators that bury themselves in sediment or rely on camouflage; others, like cods, actively forage over the seabed.
