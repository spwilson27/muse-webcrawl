Robert Boyle
Born: 25 January 1627, Lismore, Ireland
Died: 31 December 1691, London, England
Known for: Boyle's law; The Sceptical Chymist

Robert Boyle (1627–1691) was an Anglo-Irish natural philosopher, chemist, physicist and inventor, regarded as one of the founders of modern chemistry and a pioneer of the experimental scientific method.

Life and work

Born at Lismore Castle in Ireland, the seventh son of the Earl of Cork, Boyle settled in Oxford in the 1650s, where he worked with his assistant Robert Hooke. Using an improved air pump built by Hooke, Boyle published experiments on the properties of air in 1660. His work established the relationship, now called Boyle's law, that the pressure of a given mass of gas is inversely proportional to its volume at constant temperature.

The Sceptical Chymist

In The Sceptical Chymist (1661) Boyle argued against the classical four elements and the alchemical three principles, proposing instead that matter consisted of corpuscles — a founding statement of modern chemistry. A founding member of the Royal Society, Boyle insisted on experiment and careful publication of methods, including accounts of failed experiments.
