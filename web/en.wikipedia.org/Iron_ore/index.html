Iron ore

Iron ores are rocks and minerals from which metallic iron can be economically extracted. The ores are usually rich in iron oxides and vary in colour from dark grey, bright yellow, or deep purple to rusty red. The iron is usually found in the form of magnetite (Fe3O4), hematite (Fe2O3), goethite, limonite, or siderite.

Ore minerals

Magnetite is magnetic and can be concentrated with magnets; high-grade magnetite concentrates exceed 70% iron. Hematite, the principal ore in Brazil and Australia, typically ranges from 58% to 65% iron for direct shipping ore. Banded iron formations (BIFs), laid down in the Precambrian, host most of the world's large deposits.

Mining and processing

Most iron ore is mined in open pits. Lower-grade ores are beneficiated — crushed, ground, and concentrated — and often pelletized before shipment. The ore is then reduced in a blast furnace with coke and limestone to produce pig iron, or by direct reduction processes using natural gas.

Production and markets

Australia is the world's largest producer, exporting chiefly from the Pilbara region of Western Australia; Brazil's Carajás and Minas Gerais deposits are the other great source. Iron ore is one of the most traded bulk commodities, with prices benchmarked against a 62% Fe index delivered to China.
