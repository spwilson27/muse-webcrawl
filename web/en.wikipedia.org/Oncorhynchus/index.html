Oncorhynchus

KingdomAnimalia
FamilySalmonidae
GenusOncorhynchus Suckley, 1861
Common namePacific salmon and trout

Oncorhynchus is a genus of fish in the family Salmonidae that contains the Pacific salmon and Pacific trout. The name derives from Greek words meaning "hooked snout," referring to the hooked jaws (kype) developed by males during the spawning season. The genus is native to the North Pacific Ocean and the rivers that drain into it, from East Asia to western North America.

Species

Well-known species include Chinook (king) salmon (O. tshawytscha), sockeye salmon (O. nerka), coho salmon (O. kisutch), pink salmon (O. gorbuscha), chum salmon (O. keta), masu salmon (O. masou), rainbow trout (O. mykiss), and cutthroat trout (O. clarkii).

Life cycle

Most Pacific salmon are anadromous: they hatch in freshwater, migrate to the ocean to feed and grow, and return to their natal streams to spawn. The true Pacific salmon species are semelparous, dying after spawning once, and their carcasses deliver marine nutrients to freshwater and forest ecosystems. Trout species in the genus may spawn repeatedly.

Ecology and fisheries

Oncorhynchus species support major commercial, recreational, and subsistence fisheries and are keystone species for predators from bears to orcas. Many populations have declined because of habitat loss, dams, overfishing, and climate change; several are listed under endangered-species legislation. Rainbow trout have been introduced worldwide for sport fishing and aquaculture.
