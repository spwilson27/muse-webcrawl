Libera (mythology)

Libera is a goddess in ancient Roman religion and mythology. With Ceres and Liber she formed the Aventine Triad, a plebeian counterpart to the Capitoline Triad of Jupiter, Juno and Minerva. Her temple on the Aventine Hill, dedicated in 493 BC, became a centre of plebeian organisation and housed the plebeian archives.

Identity and cult

Libera was associated with fertility, wine and freedom. She was identified with the Greek Persephone (Proserpina in Latin), as consort or female counterpart of Liber, who was assimilated to Dionysus. Along with Ceres and Liber, she presided over the Liberalia festival on 17 March, marking the coming of age of young men.

The Aventine Triad reflected Greek influence transmitted through the Greek cities of southern Italy and was closely tied to Rome's grain supply and the political struggles of the plebeian order. See also Aventine Triad.
