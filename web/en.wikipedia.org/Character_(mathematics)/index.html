Character (mathematics)


A character in mathematics: a homomorphism into the multiplicative group of a field, or the trace of a representation.

In mathematics, a character is (most generally) a homomorphism from a group to the multiplicative group of a field. The term is used in several related senses across algebra and number theory, all descending from the Dirichlet characters introduced in analytic number theory and from the character theory of group representations.

Multiplicative characters

If G is a group and F a field, a character of G over F is a group homomorphism from G to F×, the multiplicative group of nonzero elements of F. For a finite abelian group, the set of characters with values in the complex numbers themselves forms a group, the dual group, fundamental to harmonic analysis and to the theory of Fourier transforms on finite groups.

Characters of representations

If ρ is a finite-dimensional representation of a group G on a vector space V, its character is the function χρ(g) = tr(ρ(g)), the trace of the representing matrix. Characters are class functions, and for finite groups the irreducible characters form an orthonormal basis of the space of class functions — the basis of the character table, a central tool in the classification of group representations developed by Frobenius and Schur.

Dirichlet characters

A Dirichlet character modulo n is a totally multiplicative periodic arithmetic function, used to define Dirichlet L-functions and to prove Dirichlet's theorem on arithmetic progressions. Characters also appear in the theory of Hecke characters and in Pontryagin duality for locally compact abelian groups.
