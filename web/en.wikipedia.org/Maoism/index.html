Maoism

Red Guards with the Quotations from Chairman Mao

Maoism, officially Mao Zedong Thought (Chinese: 毛泽东思想) in China, is a variety of Marxism–Leninism developed by Mao Zedong, leader of the Chinese Communist Party (CCP), to achieve a socialist revolution in the agricultural, pre-industrial society of the Republic of China and later the People's Republic of China. It differs from other forms of Marxism–Leninism in its emphasis on the peasantry, rather than the urban proletariat, as the principal revolutionary force.

Key concepts

Central ideas include protracted people's war — mobilizing the rural population, establishing base areas, and encircling the cities; the mass line ("from the masses, to the masses"), a method of leadership by which policy is formed from the people's scattered ideas and returned to them for action; the theory of contradictions, distinguishing antagonistic from non-antagonistic contradictions among the people; New Democracy, a staged revolution in alliance with national bourgeois classes; and the doctrine of continuing revolution, holding that class struggle persists under socialism and the party itself can degenerate into a new ruling stratum — the rationale offered for the Cultural Revolution (1966–1976).

History

Mao articulated his ideas in works such as On Practice and On Contradiction (1937), On New Democracy (1940), and On the Correct Handling of Contradictions Among the People (1957). After the CCP's victory in 1949, Maoism guided the policies of the People's Republic, including the Great Leap Forward (1958–1962) and the Cultural Revolution, with catastrophic consequences including famine and widespread persecution. After Mao's death in 1976, Deng Xiaoping's reforms retained "Mao Zedong Thought" as an official ideology of the CCP while abandoning much of its economic content in practice.

International influence

Maoism inspired insurgent movements worldwide, including the Shining Path in Peru, the Communist Party of Nepal (Maoist), the Naxalite movement in India, and the Communist Party of the Philippines. In the 1990s many such movements consolidated their doctrine as "Marxism–Leninism–Maoism."
