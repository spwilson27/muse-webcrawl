The PC(USA) is the largest Presbyterian denomination in the United States.

The Presbyterian Church (USA), abbreviated PC(USA), is a mainline Protestant Christian denomination in the United States, the largest of the Presbyterian bodies in the country. It was formed in 1983 by the reunion of the predominantly northern Presbyterian Church in the United States and the southern Presbyterian Church in the United States, healing a split that dated to the Civil War era.

Beliefs and governance

True to the Reformed tradition descending from John Calvin and John Knox, the PC(USA) confesses the Westminster Confession of Faith among other historic creeds collected in its Book of Confessions. Congregations are governed by elected ruling elders (the session); regional bodies are presbyteries and synods, and the biennial General Assembly is the highest governing council — the classic presbyterian polity of rule by elders.

Contemporary issues

The denomination has taken theologically liberal positions in recent decades: it ordains women, and in 2011 removed barriers to ordination of partnered gay and lesbian candidates, while in 2014–2015 it authorised its ministers to perform same-sex marriages. These changes, along with general mainline decline, prompted many conservative congregations to depart for bodies such as ECO and the Presbyterian Church in America. Membership has fallen from about 4.2 million at the 1983 merger to roughly 1.1 million in the 2020s.
