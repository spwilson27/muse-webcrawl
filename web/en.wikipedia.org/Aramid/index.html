Aramid

Aramid fibres are a class of heat-resistant and strong synthetic fibres. They are used in aerospace and military applications, for ballistic-rated body armour fabric and ballistic composites, in bicycle tyres, marine cordage, marine hull reinforcement, and as an asbestos substitute. The name is a blend of "aromatic polyamide".

History

Aromatic polyamides were first introduced commercially in the early 1960s, with a meta-aramid fibre produced by DuPont under the trade name Nomex. In 1973 DuPont introduced a para-aramid called Kevlar, developed by Stephanie Kwolek, which became the best-known aramid and is used in bulletproof vests and vehicle armour. A similar fibre, Twaron, was developed by the Dutch company Akzo and is now produced by Teijin.

Properties and uses

Aramid fibres have high tensile strength, good resistance to abrasion and organic solvents, no melting point (they degrade above about 500 °C), low flammability and good fabric integrity at high temperatures. They are sensitive to ultraviolet radiation, acids, and some salts, and are difficult to dye. Para-aramids are used where strength-to-weight performance is crucial; meta-aramids are used mainly for thermal and electrical insulation and protective clothing for firefighters and industrial workers.
