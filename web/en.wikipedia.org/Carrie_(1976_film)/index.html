Carrie (1976 film)

Theatrical release poster

Carrie is a 1976 American supernatural horror film directed by Brian De Palma from a screenplay by Lawrence D. Cohen, adapted from Stephen King's 1974 epistolary novel Carrie. The film stars Sissy Spacek as Carrie White, a shy teenage girl who is bullied by her classmates and abused by her fanatically religious mother, played by Piper Laurie, before unleashing her latent telekinetic powers at her high-school prom.

Plot

Carrie White is a timid, friendless 16-year-old raised by her domineering, religiously fanatical mother Margaret. When Carrie experiences her first period in the school showers and panics, not understanding what is happening, her classmates torment her. A sympathetic classmate, Sue Snell, repenting of her part in the cruelty, asks her boyfriend Tommy Ross to take Carrie to the prom. Another student, Chris Hargensen, vengeful after being banned from the prom, arranges for Carrie to be crowned prom queen and then doused with a bucket of pig's blood. Humiliated before the whole school, Carrie loses control of her telekinetic powers and seals the gym, killing most of those inside. She returns home, where her mother attacks her as a witch; Carrie kills her in self-defence and the house collapses in flames. Sue, the lone survivor of the prom, is left traumatised.

Reception and legacy

Carrie was a major commercial and critical success, earning about $33.8 million against a budget of $1.8 million, and Spacek and Laurie both received Academy Award nominations. It is widely regarded as one of the greatest horror films ever made and one of the finest adaptations of King's work; it was the first of many film adaptations of his fiction. In 2022 the film was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry.
