Weight and balance

Weight and balance in aviation is the process of ensuring that an aircraft's total weight and its centre of gravity (CG) remain within the limits specified by the manufacturer and aviation authorities, so that the aircraft is safe and controllable in flight. Before every flight the pilot or dispatcher calculates the weight of the aircraft, fuel, crew, passengers and cargo, and the position of the resulting centre of gravity along the longitudinal axis.

Concepts

Weights are computed from standard reference data: the basic empty weight, a datum point from which distances (arms) are measured, and moments — the products of weight and arm. Key limits include maximum take-off weight, maximum landing weight and zero-fuel weight. The CG must fall within a forward and aft envelope that varies with weight.

Risks

An aircraft loaded too heavily may fail to become airborne or may suffer structural damage, while an out-of-limits centre of gravity can make the aircraft unstable (CG too far aft) or unable to rotate or flare (CG too far forward). Several fatal accidents, including cargo flights with shifting loads, have been attributed to weight and balance errors.
