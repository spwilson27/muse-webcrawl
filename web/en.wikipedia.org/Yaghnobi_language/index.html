Yaghnobi language

Yaghnobi (яғнобӣ, yaɣnobī) is an Eastern Iranian language spoken in the Yaghnob Valley of northwestern Tajikistan by the Yaghnobi people. It is the direct descendant of a dialect of Sogdian, the language of the ancient Sogdian civilization of Samarkand and Bukhara, and is the only surviving language of the Sogdic branch other than its close extinct relatives.

Speakers and history

According to legend and tradition, the Yaghnobis descend from Sogdians who fled into the remote Yaghnob Valley during the Arab conquest of Transoxiana in the 8th century, preserving their language in isolation. In 1970–71 the Soviet authorities forcibly resettled the entire Yaghnobi population to cotton-growing lowland districts at Zafarobod; many died, and the community's language and cohesion suffered severely. After Tajik independence, some families returned to the valley. Today the language has perhaps 12,000–13,500 speakers counting the diaspora, of whom only a few thousand live in the valley itself. Most speakers are bilingual in Tajik.

Yaghnobi retains archaic features of Sogdian lost elsewhere and has borrowed vocabulary from Tajik (a Western Iranian language) and Uzbek. It is traditionally unwritten; where written, it uses an adapted Cyrillic alphabet, sometimes Tajik Latin or Arabic script. Two main dialects, eastern and western, differ in phonology and vocabulary.

Preservation

Since the 1990s, linguists have documented Yaghnobi and produced teaching materials; small amounts of Yaghnobi-language schooling and publications exist, but Tajik dominance in media and education keeps the language under pressure.
