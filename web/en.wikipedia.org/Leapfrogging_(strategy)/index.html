Leapfrogging (strategy)

Leapfrogging is a military strategy in which advancing units bypass enemy strongpoints and attack objectives deeper in the rear, leaving the bypassed positions to be dealt with by following forces. The term is also used in economics and technology for skipping intermediate stages of development.

Military use

The strategy was used extensively by Allied forces in the Pacific War against Imperial Japan (1943–1945): under General Douglas MacArthur and Admiral Chester Nimitz, strongly defended islands such as Rabaul were isolated and left to "wither on the vine" while forces seized lightly held islands closer to Japan as air and naval bases. Leapfrogging conserved manpower and maintained momentum, avoiding costly frontal assaults.

Other uses

In development economics, leapfrogging describes countries adopting the newest technology directly — for example, regions skipping landline telephone networks in favour of mobile phones, or off-grid solar power instead of centralised grids.
