Black Forest

The Black Forest (German: Schwarzwald) is a large forested mountain range in the state of Baden-Württemberg in south-western Germany, bounded by the Rhine valley to the west and south. Its highest peak is the Feldberg with an elevation of 1,493 metres (4,898 ft) above sea level. Roughly triangular in shape, the region is about 160 km (99 mi) long and up to 50 km (31 mi) wide, famous for its dense conifer forests, cuckoo clocks, and Black Forest gateau.

Geography

The Black Forest runs from the High Rhine in the south to the Kraichgau in the north, separating the Rhine plain from the Swabian plateau. Major rivers rising in the range include the Danube's headstreams (the Brigach and Breg), the Neckar, the Kinzig, the Murg and the Enz. Popular lakes include the Titisee and Schluchsee. Important towns include Freiburg im Breisgau, Baden-Baden, Freudenstadt, Villingen-Schwenningen and Pforzheim.

Geology

The range is an uplifted block of crystalline basement rock — gneiss and granite — partly covered by red sandstone (Buntsandstein) in the north and east. It was raised during the formation of the Upper Rhine Graben in the Tertiary period and sculpted by glaciers during the Ice Ages, leaving cirques, moraines and small lakes.

Culture and tourism

The region is associated with cuckoo clocks, wood carving, the Bollenhut pom-pom hat, cuckoo-clock houses, and the Black Forest gateau (Schwarzwälder Kirschtorte). Fairy tales collected by the Brothers Grimm are popularly imagined in its dark woods. The Black Forest National Park, established in 2014, protects a large area near Baiersbronn and Ruhestein. Tourism, forestry and precision engineering (historically clocks and watches) dominate the economy.
