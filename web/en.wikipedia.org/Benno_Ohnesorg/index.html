Benno Ohnesorg

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Benno Ohnesorg (15 October 1940 – 2 June 1967) was a West German university student killed by a policeman during a demonstration in West Berlin. His death is considered a pivotal moment in post-war German history, radicalizing the student movement of 1968 and contributing to the emergence of left-wing terrorism in West Germany in the 1970s.

Death

On 2 June 1967, Ohnesorg, a student of Romance studies and German literature at the Free University of Berlin, took part in a protest against the state visit of the Shah of Iran, Mohammad Reza Pahlavi. That evening, demonstrators gathered outside the Deutsche Oper, where the Shah was attending a performance of Mozart's The Magic Flute. In the confusion as police cleared the area, Ohnesorg was shot in the back of the head in a courtyard at Krumme Straße 66 by plainclothes police officer Karl-Heinz Kurras. He died shortly afterwards. It was the first demonstration Ohnesorg had attended.

Aftermath

Kurras was tried twice and acquitted, a verdict that outraged the student movement. Ohnesorg's death galvanized the Außerparlamentarische Opposition (APO) and the protests of 1968. Gudrun Ensslin, co-founder of the Red Army Faction, called 2 June 1967 the turning point at which the state had shown its violent face. The left-wing militant group Movement 2 June later named itself after the date of his death.

In 2009 it was revealed that Kurras had been an informant for the East German Stasi and a member of the Socialist Unity Party of Germany, though no evidence emerged that he had acted on Stasi orders. A memorial marks the site of the shooting in Berlin-Charlottenburg.
