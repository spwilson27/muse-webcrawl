Alkmaar

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Country: Netherlands

Province: North Holland

City rights: 1254

Population: approx. 110,000 (municipality)

Known for: Cheese market; victory over Spain in 1573

Alkmaar is a city and municipality in the Netherlands, in the province of North Holland, about 30 km northwest of Amsterdam. It is best known internationally for its traditional cheese market and for its successful resistance to a Spanish siege during the Eighty Years' War.

History

Alkmaar is first mentioned in the tenth century and received city rights in 1254. In 1573 the city famously withstood a siege by Spanish troops during the Dutch Revolt; when the dikes were opened and the surrounding land flooded, the Spanish withdrew, giving rise to the Dutch saying "Victory begins at Alkmaar" (Bij Alkmaar begint de victorie). The relief of Alkmaar on 8 October is still celebrated locally every year.

Cheese market

Alkmaar's cheese market, held on the Waagplein square in front of the Renaissance weigh house (the Waag) on Friday mornings from spring to autumn, is one of the oldest and most famous in the Netherlands, with records of a cheese market dating to the fourteenth century. Porters in traditional guild uniforms carry huge rounds of cheese on wooden sledges to be weighed and traded in a ceremony that draws large numbers of tourists. The city is also home to the Dutch Cheese Museum and a museum devoted to the Beatles, whose first museum John Lennon memorabilia collections in the country.

Modern city

Alkmaar is a regional centre for the surrounding agricultural area and has a well-preserved old town of canals, courtyards and monuments, including the Grote or Sint-Laurenskerk with its famous organs. Its football club, AZ Alkmaar, won the Dutch championship in 1981 and 2009 and plays at the AFAS Stadion.
