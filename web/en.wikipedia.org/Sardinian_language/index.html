Sardinian (sardu) is a Romance language spoken on the island of Sardinia in the western Mediterranean. It is often considered the most conservative of the Romance languages in its phonology and vocabulary, retaining many features of the Vulgar Latin from which it descends that were lost elsewhere in the Romance world. Sardinian forms its own branch of the Romance family and is not a dialect of Italian.

History

Latin was introduced to Sardinia after Rome annexed the island in 238 BC. Because of Sardinia's relative isolation, its Latin developed distinctive features, and the language also absorbed words from the pre-Roman Paleo-Sardinian substrate and, over the centuries, loanwords from Catalan, Spanish and Italian through periods of foreign rule. Some of the earliest documents in any Romance vernacular are Sardinian: the condaghes (monastic records) of the 11th–13th centuries and the legal code known as the Carta de Logu, promulgated in the 1390s under Eleanor of Arborea.

Dialects

Sardinian is traditionally divided into two main written and spoken varieties: Logudorese in the north and centre of the island, regarded as the most conservative and the basis of much Sardinian literature, and Campidanese in the south, including the capital Cagliari, which has the larger number of speakers. A standardisation effort produced the Limba Sarda Comuna (Common Sardinian Language), adopted experimentally by the regional government in 2006 as an official written standard.

Status

Sardinian is recognised and protected under Italian national law (Law 482/1999) and regional legislation, and is listed by UNESCO as an endangered language: although roughly a million people can speak it, transmission to younger generations has declined as Italian dominates education, media and urban life.

Features

Notable features include the retention of Latin velar stops before front vowels (Latin centum gives Sardinian kentu, versus Italian cento), a definite article derived from Latin ipse (su, sa — comparable to Balearic Catalan), and a stock of pre-Latin substrate words for local flora, fauna and topography.
