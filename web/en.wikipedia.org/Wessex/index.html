Wessex

Periodc. 519 – 927 AD (kingdom)
PeopleWest Saxons
Notable kingsAlfred the Great, Æthelstan
RegionSouthern England: Hampshire, Wiltshire, Dorset, Somerset and surroundings

Wessex was an Anglo-Saxon kingdom in southern England, one of the traditional seven kingdoms of the Heptarchy. From its legendary founding in the 6th century it grew by conquest and, uniquely among the English kingdoms, survived the Viking invasions of the 9th century. Under Alfred the Great and his successors, Wessex led the reconquest of the Danelaw, and Alfred's grandson Æthelstan became the first king of a unified England in 927.

History

According to the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle, the West Saxons under Cerdic landed in Hampshire in 495 and founded the kingdom in 519. Early expansion took in the Isle of Wight and the upper Thames valley. In the 7th and 8th centuries Wessex contended with Mercia for supremacy in southern England. After the Battle of Ellandun (825), Wessex gained control of Kent, Sussex, Surrey and Essex.

Alfred and after

Alfred (r. 871–899) defended Wessex against the Great Heathen Army, winning at Edington in 878, and reformed the army, navy, law and education. His children Edward the Elder and Æthelflæd, and grandson Æthelstan, completed the unification of England. The name Wessex later denoted an earldom and, in modern times, a cultural region famously used by Thomas Hardy as the setting of his novels.
