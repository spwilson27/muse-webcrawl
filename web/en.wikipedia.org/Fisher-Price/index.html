Fisher-Price

Fisher-Price, Inc. is an American company that produces educational toys for infants, toddlers and preschoolers, headquartered in East Aurora, New York. The company was founded in 1930 during the Great Depression by Herman Fisher, Irving Price, Margaret Evans Price and Helen Schelle. It has been a wholly owned subsidiary of Mattel since 1993.

History and products

The founders aimed to make well-crafted wooden toys with intrinsic play value; early successes such as Dr. Doodle and the Snoopy Sniffer established the name. The company was a pioneer in the use of plastic, which allowed brighter colours and finer detail, and in the 1960s introduced the Little People line of small figures and playsets. Other iconic products include the Chatter Telephone (1961), the See 'n Say talking toy, the Rock-a-Stack, and later licensed lines and the Laugh & Learn electronic toys. The company's Play Lab, founded in 1961, was among the first in-house child-development research facilities in the toy industry.

Business

Fisher-Price was acquired by the Quaker Oats Company in 1969, spun off in 1991, and acquired by Mattel in 1993 for about $1.2 billion. Its recalled Rock 'n Play sleeper, withdrawn in 2019 after being linked to infant deaths, became a prominent product-safety case study.
