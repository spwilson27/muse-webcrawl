Partition of Palestine

The partition of Palestine refers principally to the proposal of the United Nations in 1947 to divide British Mandatory Palestine into independent Arab and Jewish states, with Jerusalem placed under international administration. The plan was adopted by the UN General Assembly as Resolution 181 (II) on 29 November 1947 but was never implemented as designed, owing to the 1947–1949 Palestine war and the subsequent armistice lines.

Background

Palestine had been administered by Britain under a League of Nations mandate since 1920, following the Ottoman defeat in the First World War. The 1917 Balfour Declaration committed Britain to a "national home for the Jewish people", while Arabs of Palestine sought independence in a unitary state. Partition was first officially proposed by the Peel Commission in 1937, which recommended a small Jewish state, an Arab state, and continued British control of Jerusalem; the plan was rejected by the Arab leadership and abandoned by Britain. After the Second World War and the Holocaust, Britain referred the problem to the United Nations.

Resolution 181

The UN Special Committee on Palestine (UNSCOP) proposed partition into two states joined by an economic union. The Jewish state was allotted about 55–56% of the territory (much of it the Negev desert) for roughly a third of the population; the Arab state about 43%; Jerusalem and Bethlehem formed a corpus separatum under UN trusteeship. The General Assembly adopted the plan by 33 votes to 13, with 10 abstentions. The Jewish Agency accepted it; the Arab Higher Committee and the Arab states rejected it as unjust and illegal.

Aftermath

Civil war between Jewish and Arab forces in Palestine followed immediately. On 14 May 1948 Israel declared independence; neighbouring Arab states invaded, and the ensuing war ended with the 1949 Armistice Agreements ("Green Line"), under which Israel controlled about 78% of Mandate Palestine, Jordan held the West Bank and East Jerusalem, and Egypt held the Gaza Strip. Between 700,000 and 750,000 Palestinian Arabs fled or were expelled — remembered by Palestinians as the Nakba. Partition as a concept survived in later diplomacy: the two-state solution endorsed in UN resolutions and the Oslo process still rests on dividing the land between Israeli and Palestinian states.
