Dún Laoghaire–Rathdown (Irish: Dún Laoghaire–Ráth an Dúin) is a county in Ireland, in the province of Leinster and the Eastern and Midland Region. It is one of three successor counties created when the historic County Dublin was divided for local government purposes on 1 January 1994, alongside Fingal and South Dublin. Named after the town of Dún Laoghaire and the barony of Rathdown, it is administered by Dún Laoghaire–Rathdown County Council.

Geography

The county stretches along the Irish Sea coast south of Dublin city, from Booterstown and Blackrock through Dún Laoghaire, Dalkey, and Killiney to the border with County Wicklow, and inland to suburbs such as Dundrum, Sandyford, Stillorgan, and Leopardstown, rising to the Dublin Mountains in the south-west. At about 127 km² it is the smallest county in Ireland by area, but one of the most densely populated and affluent.

Features

The county contains Dún Laoghaire harbour, the National Maritime Museum, the dlr LexIcon library, and the seaside towns of Dalkey and Killiney, sometimes dubbed Ireland's "Bay of Naples". University College Dublin's Belfield campus lies on its northern edge. Major employers cluster in the Sandyford business district, and the county is served by the DART railway and the Luas Green Line.
