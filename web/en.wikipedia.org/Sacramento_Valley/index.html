Sacramento Valley

The Sacramento Valley is the northern half of California's Central Valley.

The Sacramento Valley is the area of the Central Valley of the U.S. state of California that lies north of the Sacramento–San Joaquin River Delta and is drained by the Sacramento River. It encompasses roughly the northern third of the Central Valley, extending about 290 kilometers (180 miles) from the delta to the vicinity of Redding.

Geography

The valley is bounded by the Sierra Nevada to the east, the Cascade Range to the north, and the Coast Ranges to the west. Its floor is a deep alluvial plain built from sediments carried down from the surrounding mountains. The isolated volcanic Sutter Buttes rise abruptly from the valley floor near Yuba City and are sometimes called the "world's smallest mountain range."

Major cities include Sacramento, Chico, Redding, Yuba City, Marysville, Woodland, and Davis. Tributaries of the Sacramento River draining the valley include the Feather, Yuba, and American rivers from the Sierra Nevada and Cache Creek and Putah Creek from the Coast Ranges.

Agriculture

The Sacramento Valley is one of the most productive agricultural regions in the world. Irrigated crops include rice — the valley produces most of California's rice crop — along with almonds, walnuts, tomatoes, sunflowers, prunes, and olives. Water is supplied by federal and state projects, including the Central Valley Project's Shasta Dam. Historically the valley floor supported vast wetlands along the river's flood basins, most of which were reclaimed for farming; remnants are preserved in national wildlife refuges that host millions of migratory waterfowl on the Pacific Flyway.
