Ottoman Navy


Founded: 14th century
Disbanded: 1922
Headquarters: Imperial Arsenal, Golden Horn, Constantinople
Senior commander: Kapudan Pasha

The Ottoman Navy was the naval warfare arm of the Ottoman Empire. Established as the empire expanded to the coasts in the late 14th century, it grew into one of the world's greatest naval forces, for much of the 16th century dominating the Mediterranean, the Red Sea and reaching into the Indian Ocean. It was commanded by the Kapudan Pasha (grand admiral) and built and maintained principally at the Imperial Arsenal on the Golden Horn.

Age of dominance

Under admirals such as Hayreddin Barbarossa, victor at Preveza in 1538, Ottoman fleets of galleys secured Algiers, Tripoli and the eastern Mediterranean, raided the coasts of Italy, France and Spain, and projected power to Yemen, Oman and even Aceh in Southeast Asia. The catastrophic defeat at Lepanto in 1571 destroyed much of the fleet, yet the empire rebuilt it within a year and retained control of the eastern Mediterranean for another century, conquering Crete in 1669.

Decline and modernisation

From the 18th century the navy lagged behind European rivals, suffering annihilation at Çeşme (1770), Navarino (1827) and Sinop (1853). Nineteenth-century sultans repeatedly rebuilt it with steamships and ironclads — under Abdülaziz it briefly ranked among the world's largest fleets — but financial weakness and neglect followed. In the First World War the navy, its battlecruiser Yavuz (the former German Goeben) prominent, fought in the Black Sea and defended the Dardanelles. The force passed to the Turkish Republic after 1923 as the nucleus of the Turkish Naval Forces.
