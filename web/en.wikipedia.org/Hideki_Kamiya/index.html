Hideki Kamiya (神谷 英樹, Kamiya Hideki, born December 19, 1970) is a Japanese video game designer and director. He began his career at Capcom in 1994, where he directed Resident Evil 2 (1998) and went on to create the Devil May Cry series. He later directed Viewtiful Joe (2003) and Ōkami (2006) at Capcom's Clover Studio. In 2006 he co-founded PlatinumGames, where he directed Bayonetta (2009) and The Wonderful 101 (2013) and supervised Bayonetta 2 and Bayonetta 3. Kamiya is noted for stylish action games built around demanding combat systems, as well as for his blunt, often humorous public persona on social media, where he is famous for blocking users.

Early life

Kamiya was born in Matsumoto, Nagano Prefecture. As a child he was an avid reader and a fan of video games; he has cited the manga and anime of Go Nagai, the manga Space Adventure Cobra by Buichi Terasawa, and games such as Gradius and Castlevania as formative influences. He decided early that he wanted to make games and read publications such as Famitsu while studying how development studios were organised.

Career

Capcom (1994–2006)

Kamiya joined Capcom in 1994 as a planner. His first project was Arthur to Astaroth no Nazomakaimura: Incredible Toons. He then worked as a planner on the original Resident Evil (1996) under director Shinji Mikami. When the first version of Resident Evil 2 was scrapped, Kamiya was promoted to director of the restarted project; the game was released in 1998 to critical and commercial success.

For his next project, Kamiya led a team that initially set out to make a more action-oriented Resident Evil sequel. The project grew so distinct in tone—centred on a stylish, sword-and-gun-wielding hero—that it was spun off into a new property, Devil May Cry (2001), which established a genre of character action games.

Kamiya subsequently joined Clover Studio, Capcom's semi-autonomous development house. There he directed Viewtiful Joe (2003), a side-scrolling action game with cel-shaded graphics and time-manipulation mechanics, and Ōkami (2006), an action-adventure game styled after Japanese sumi-e ink painting in which the player controls the sun goddess Amaterasu in wolf form. Ōkami was critically acclaimed but sold poorly, and Clover Studio was dissolved in 2007.

PlatinumGames (2006–2023)

In 2006 Kamiya, together with Shinji Mikami and Atsushi Inaba, left Capcom and founded the studio that became PlatinumGames. His first title there, Bayonetta (2009), published by Sega, followed the witch Bayonetta in an over-the-top action game widely regarded as a pinnacle of the genre. Kamiya supervised the sequels Bayonetta 2 (2014) and Bayonetta 3 (2022) and directed The Wonderful 101 (2013) for the Wii U. He was also director of Microsoft's Scalebound, an action role-playing game announced in 2014 and cancelled in 2017.

Kamiya announced his departure from PlatinumGames in September 2023, leaving the company on October 12, 2023, stating that he wanted to continue making games in his own style.

Clovers (2024–present)

In 2024 Kamiya founded a new studio, Clovers Inc., taking its name and four-leaf-clover motif in homage to Clover Studio. In December 2024 it was announced that Clovers would develop a sequel to Ōkami in collaboration with Capcom, with Kamiya directing.

Public image

Kamiya is well known for his activity on Twitter (now X), where he answers questions from fans in English and Japanese and is notorious for blocking accounts that ask questions he has already answered, send him messages in languages other than Japanese or English, or otherwise annoy him. He has repeatedly advised followers to "ask your mom" or to search before asking.
