Keeshond

The Keeshond (plural Keeshonden) is a medium-sized dog breed of the spitz type, originating in the Netherlands. It has a two-layer coat of long, straight silver-grey and black fur with a thick ruff around the neck, black markings on the face resembling spectacles, and a tail curled over its back.

History

The Keeshond was historically kept as a watchdog and companion on the barges that travelled the canals and rivers of the Netherlands, giving it the nickname "Dutch Barge Dog". In the 1780s it became the mascot of the Dutch Patriots party, whose leader was Cornelis ("Kees") de Gijselaar — hence the breed's name, "Kees's dog". After the Patriots' defeat the breed nearly vanished but survived among barge owners and farmers. It was revived in the early twentieth century in the Netherlands and England, and was officially recognised in the Netherlands in 1933.

Characteristics

Keeshonden stand about 43–46 cm at the shoulder and weigh roughly 15–20 kg. They are intelligent, lively and affectionate companions, generally good with children, and make alert watchdogs though they are not guard dogs. The dense double coat requires regular brushing and sheds heavily. The breed is generally healthy, with a life expectancy of about 12–15 years, though it can be prone to hip dysplasia, epilepsy and certain skin and heart conditions.
