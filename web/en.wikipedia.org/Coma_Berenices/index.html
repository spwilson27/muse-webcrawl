Coma Berenices

Coma Berenices is an ancient asterism in the northern sky, which has been defined as one of the 88 modern constellations. Its name means "Berenice's Hair" in Latin and refers to the legend of Queen Berenice II of Egypt, who sacrificed her long hair as a votive offering to ensure her husband's safe return from war. It is located near Leo and Boötes and contains the north galactic pole.

History and mythology

According to the legend preserved by Callimachus and retold by Catullus, Berenice, wife of Ptolemy III Euergetes, vowed to cut off her hair and place it in the temple of Arsinoe (Aphrodite) if the king returned safely from the Third Syrian War. When the hair disappeared from the temple, the court astronomer Conon of Samos declared that the goddess had placed it among the stars. The asterism was known to Greek astronomers, though it was not one of Ptolemy's 48 constellations; it was elevated to a constellation in the sixteenth century, appearing in the works of Caspar Vopel and Tycho Brahe.

Features

Coma Berenices is a faint constellation whose brightest stars form a right angle: Alpha Comae Berenices (Diadem), Beta Comae Berenices, and Gamma Comae Berenices. It hosts the Coma Star Cluster (Melotte 111), a loose open cluster of about 40 stars, and the Coma Cluster of galaxies, one of the richest nearby galaxy clusters. Because it lies near the north galactic pole, away from the obscuring dust of the Milky Way, the constellation is rich in external galaxies, including the Black Eye Galaxy (Messier 64) and the spiral galaxies Messier 88, Messier 98, Messier 99, and Messier 100, as well as the Needle Galaxy (NGC 4565).
