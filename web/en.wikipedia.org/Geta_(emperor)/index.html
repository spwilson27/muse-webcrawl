Publius Septimius Geta (189–211) was Roman emperor from February to December 211, ruling jointly with his father Septimius Severus and his elder brother Caracalla. After Severus's death at Eboracum (York) in Britain, the brothers returned to Rome as co-emperors, but their mutual hatred made joint rule impossible, and Caracalla had Geta murdered within the year.

Life

Geta was the younger son of Septimius Severus and Julia Domna. He was made Caesar in 198 and Augustus in 209, campaigning with his father and brother in Britain against the Caledonians. Severus reportedly advised his sons on his deathbed to "be harmonious, enrich the soldiers, and scorn all others" — advice the brothers ignored. In Rome they divided the imperial palace, intrigued against each other, and each sought the other's death.

Murder and damnatio memoriae

On 26 December 211, Caracalla arranged a meeting, ostensibly for reconciliation, in the presence of their mother, and had centurions of his guard stab Geta, who died in Julia Domna's arms. Caracalla then ordered a damnatio memoriae: Geta's name and images were erased from inscriptions, coins, and monuments throughout the empire — most famously, his face was chiselled out of the family portrait known as the Severan Tondo and from the Arch of Septimius Severus — and many of his supporters were killed.
