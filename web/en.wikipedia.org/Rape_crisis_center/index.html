Rape crisis centres provide free, confidential support to survivors.

A rape crisis center (also called a sexual assault crisis centre) is a community-based organisation that supports survivors of sexual violence and works to prevent it. Services typically include 24-hour telephone hotlines, crisis intervention, hospital and court accompaniment, individual and group counselling, and advocacy with medical, police and legal systems — generally free and confidential.

History

The first centres emerged from the feminist anti-rape movement in the United States in the early 1970s; the Bay Area Women Against Rape (1971) and the Washington, DC Rape Crisis Center (1972) were among the earliest. Modelled on consciousness-raising and mutual aid, they challenged victim-blaming and pressed for legal reform, including rape-shield laws and the recognition of marital rape. Similar networks later formed in the United Kingdom (Rape Crisis England & Wales), Canada, Australia and elsewhere, often federated in national coalitions.

Approach

Centres work from an empowerment model: the survivor controls decisions about reporting, medical care and counselling, and advocates support rather than direct. Many also run prevention education in schools and communities. Funding comes from government grants, donations and fundraising, and services are delivered by a mix of professional staff and trained volunteers.
