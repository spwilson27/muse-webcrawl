The Hakone Ekiden, officially the Tokyo–Hakone Round-Trip College Ekiden Race (東京箱根間往復大学駅伝競走), is one of the most prominent university ekiden (long-distance relay) races in Japan. Held every year on January 2 and 3, teams of ten runners from universities in the Kantō region race from Ōtemachi, Tokyo, to Lake Ashi in Hakone and back, covering 217.1 kilometres in ten stages over two days.

Course

Day one (the "outward" leg) runs 107.5 km from Tokyo to Hakone in five stages, finishing with the famous steep mountain climb to Lake Ashi (stage 5, the "mountain god" leg). Day two reverses the route over 109.6 km, beginning with a long mountain descent. Runners pass a cloth sash, the tasuki, to the next teammate instead of a baton, and teams that fall more than a set time behind the leader are forced to restart with a mass start, a dreaded and emotional moment of the race.

History and popularity

The race was founded in 1920, inspired by the idea of developing Japanese long-distance talent after the country's Olympic marathon appearances. Interrupted by war, it became a fixture of the New Year holidays. Televised nationally by Nippon Television since 1987, the Hakone Ekiden draws some of Japan's highest sports TV ratings and over a million roadside spectators, and it serves as the premier showcase for future professional and Olympic marathon runners. The top ten teams earn "seeded" places for the following year; others must qualify through the Yosenkai half-marathon qualifier each October.

Aoyama Gakuin University dominated much of the 2015–2024 era under coach Susumu Hara; other traditional powers include Komazawa, Waseda, Juntendo, and Toyo universities.
