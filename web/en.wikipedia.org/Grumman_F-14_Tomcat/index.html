Grumman F-14 Tomcat

The Grumman F-14 Tomcat is an American carrier-capable supersonic, twin-engine, two-seat, twin-tail, variable-sweep wing fighter aircraft. The Tomcat was developed for the United States Navy's Naval Fighter Experimental (VFX) program after the collapse of the General Dynamics/Grumman F-111B project. The F-14 was the first of the American "teen-series" fighters, designed around combat experience against MiG fighters during the Vietnam War, and it served as the Navy's primary maritime air superiority fighter, fleet defence interceptor and tactical aerial reconnaissance platform from 1974 to 2006. It was retired and replaced by the Boeing F/A-18E/F Super Hornet; the Islamic Republic of Iran Air Force, its sole export customer, has continued to operate upgraded F-14s.

Design

The F-14's variable-geometry wings sweep automatically between 20° and 68° in flight, optimising performance from slow carrier approaches to Mach 2.3 intercepts. Its AWG-9 radar and AIM-54 Phoenix missiles were designed to engage multiple targets at ranges beyond 100 nautical miles, defending the carrier battle group against Soviet bombers. The two crew — pilot and Radar Intercept Officer — sit in tandem. Late models (F-14A+/B and the extensively rebuilt F-14D Super Tomcat) replaced the troublesome TF30 engines with General Electric F110s and added precision ground-attack capability, earning the nickname "Bombcat".

Operational history

Tomcats made their combat debut over the Gulf of Sidra in 1981, shooting down two Libyan Su-22s, and downed two Libyan MiG-23s in a second Sidra incident in 1989. Iranian F-14s fought extensively in the Iran–Iraq War and were credited with numerous victories. In US service the type flew in the Gulf War, the Balkans, Afghanistan and Iraq. The last US Navy Tomcat squadron, VF-31, retired the aircraft on 22 September 2006 at Naval Air Station Oceana. The F-14 achieved worldwide fame through the 1986 film Top Gun.
