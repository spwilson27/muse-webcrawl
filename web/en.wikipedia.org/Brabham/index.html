Brabham was a British Formula One racing team and constructor, founded in 1961 by Australian driver Jack Brabham and designer Ron Tauranac under the company Motor Racing Developments. The team won four Drivers' World Championships and two Constructors' World Championships in its 30-year Formula One history and became famous for technical innovation under designer Gordon Murray.

History

Jack Brabham, who had won the 1959 and 1960 world championships with Cooper, set up his own team and won the 1966 title in a Brabham-Repco — remaining the only driver to win the championship in a car bearing his own name. Denny Hulme added a second consecutive drivers' title in 1967. Brabham retired from driving in 1970 and sold the team to Bernie Ecclestone in 1972. Under Ecclestone and Murray, the team introduced the radical BT46B "fan car," which won its only race (the 1978 Swedish Grand Prix) before being withdrawn, and developed the ground-effect BT49 and the BMW turbo-powered BT52. Nelson Piquet won the Drivers' Championship for Brabham in 1981 and again in 1983, the latter the first title won with a turbocharged engine.

Decline and closure

Brabham sat out the 1988 season and passed through several owners, including the Japanese Middlebridge group, amid financial difficulty and, in 1989, the embarrassment of the uncompetitive pre-qualifying era. The team folded during the 1992 season, its last race being the Hungarian Grand Prix. The Brabham name later appeared in endurance racing and in the Brabham Automotive road-car venture launched by Jack Brabham's son David.
