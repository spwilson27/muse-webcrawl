Hamilcar Barca

Hamilcar Barca
c. 275 – 228 BC
Carthaginian general
Father of Hannibal

Hamilcar Barca (c. 275–228 BC) was a Carthaginian general and statesman, leader of the Barcid family, and father of Hannibal, Hasdrubal, and Mago. His surname Barca derives from the Punic barak, "lightning".

First Punic War

Hamilcar took command in Sicily in 247 BC during the First Punic War, waging a successful guerrilla campaign from Mount Ercte and later Mount Eryx against the Romans. Undefeated in the field, he negotiated the peace of 241 BC after Carthage's naval defeat at the Aegates Islands, which ended the war on terms that stripped Carthage of Sicily.

Mercenary War and Spain

He then suppressed the Mercenary War (240–238 BC) against Carthage's own unpaid rebel soldiers with great severity. In 237 BC Hamilcar sailed to Iberia and spent nine years conquering and consolidating Carthaginian Spain, building a new power base whose silver mines rebuilt Carthage's finances. According to tradition, he made his young son Hannibal swear an oath of eternal enmity toward Rome. Hamilcar died in 228 BC, reportedly drowning in the Júcar River while retreating from battle.

Legacy

His conquests in Spain set the stage for Hannibal's campaigns in the Second Punic War, and the Barcid family's semi-independent rule there became a lasting feature of Carthaginian politics.
