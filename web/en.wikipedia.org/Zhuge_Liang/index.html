Zhuge Liang

Born181, Yangdu, Langya (present-day Yinan County, Shandong)
DiedAugust or September 234 (aged 53), Wuzhang Plains, Shaanxi
Courtesy nameKongming (孔明)
OccupationStatesman, military strategist, inventor
Posthumous nameMarquis Zhongwu (忠武侯)

Zhuge Liang (181 – August/September 234), courtesy name Kongming, was a Chinese statesman, military strategist and inventor who served as chancellor (丞相) and later regent of the state of Shu Han during the Three Kingdoms period of China. He is recognised as the most accomplished strategist of his era and has been compared to Sun Tzu, the author of The Art of War. Often depicted wearing a Taoist robe and holding a fan of crane feathers, he is celebrated in Chinese culture as the embodiment of wisdom and loyalty.

Living in reclusion as a farmer-scholar in Longzhong, Zhuge Liang was recruited by the warlord Liu Bei, who visited him three times in 207 before Zhuge Liang presented the Longzhong Plan, a grand strategy for dividing China into three. He engineered the alliance between Liu Bei and Sun Quan that produced victory over Cao Cao at the Battle of Red Cliffs (208), and later planned the conquest of Yi Province, the territorial base of Shu Han.

Chancellorship and campaigns

After Liu Bei's death in 223, Zhuge Liang governed Shu Han as regent for the young emperor Liu Shan. He pacified the south in the campaign of 225, securing the Nanzhong region, and from 228 launched five Northern Expeditions against the rival state of Cao Wei in an effort to restore the Han dynasty. Despite administrative brilliance and tactical successes, the expeditions failed to overcome Wei's superior resources; overwork ruined his health, and he died at the Wuzhang Plains in 234 during a standoff with the Wei general Sima Yi. His memorials, notably the Chu Shi Biao (Memorial on Dispatching Troops), remain classics of Chinese prose. He is also credited with inventions including the repeating crossbow and the sky lantern (Kongming lantern).
