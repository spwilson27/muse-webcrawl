Passerine

A passerine is any bird of the order Passeriformes, which includes more than half of all bird species. Sometimes known as perching birds, passerines are distinguished from other orders of birds by the arrangement of their toes—three pointing forward and one back—which facilitates perching. With more than 140 families and some 6,500 identified species, Passeriformes is the largest order of birds.

Characteristics

Most passerines are small, though the group ranges from tiny kinglets to the large lyrebirds and ravens. Many, the songbirds (suborder Passeri), possess a complex syrinx capable of learned vocal song. Passerines occur on every continent except Antarctica and in nearly every terrestrial habitat.

Evolution

Molecular studies place the origin of passerines in the Southern Hemisphere in the late Cretaceous or early Paleogene, with the earliest diversifications in the Australasian region. The two great radiations are the oscines (songbirds) and suboscines, the latter concentrated in South America.

Families

Well-known passerine families include corvids (crows and jays), finches, sparrows, thrushes, warblers, swallows, starlings, and the birds-of-paradise of New Guinea.
