Modulus of continuity

In mathematical analysis, a modulus of continuity is a function used to measure quantitatively how uniformly continuous a function is. A function f from a metric space to another is uniformly continuous if and only if there is a modulus of continuity ω such that the distance between f(x) and f(y) is at most ω of the distance between x and y, for all points x and y.

Definition

Formally, ω is a non-decreasing function from [0, ∞) to [0, ∞] with ω(0) = 0 and ω continuous at 0. Important special cases include ω(t) = Ct for Lipschitz functions and ω(t) = Ct^α, for 0 < α ≤ 1, for Hölder continuous functions.

Uses

Moduli of continuity appear in approximation theory, where they control the rate at which functions can be approximated by polynomials or splines (as in Jackson's theorems), in the theory of Fourier series, and in the regularity theory of partial differential equations, where the modulus of continuity of solutions is a central estimate.
