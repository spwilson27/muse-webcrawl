The Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith (DDF) is the oldest among the departments of the Roman Curia. Its stated mission is to "promote and safeguard the doctrine on the faith and morals throughout the Catholic world". It is the modern successor of the Supreme Sacred Congregation of the Roman and Universal Inquisition — the institution historically known simply as the Inquisition, later as the Holy Office.

History

Pope Paul III established the body on 21 July 1542 with the apostolic constitution Licet ab initio, to combat heresy in the wake of the Protestant Reformation. Reorganised by St Pius X in 1908 as the Congregation of the Holy Office, it was given its long-standing name — the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith — by Pope Paul VI in 1965. Pope John Paul II defined its role in Pastor bonus (1988), and Pope Francis's Praedicate evangelium (2022) renamed it a "dicastery" and restructured it into doctrinal and disciplinary sections.

Notable prefects and work

Cardinal Joseph Ratzinger, later Pope Benedict XVI, served as prefect from 1981 to 2005. The Dicastery examines publications and teachings for doctrinal soundness, handles crimes reserved to it such as clerical sexual abuse cases, and issues documents including the declaration Dominus Iesus (2000) and the declaration Fiducia supplicans (2023). Cardinal Víctor Manuel Fernández became prefect in 2023.
