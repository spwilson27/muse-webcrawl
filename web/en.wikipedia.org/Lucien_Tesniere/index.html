Lucien Tesnière (13 May 1893 – 6 December 1954) was a French linguist. He is regarded as the founder of modern dependency grammar and of valency theory, set out in his posthumously published work Éléments de syntaxe structurale (1959).

Life

Tesnière studied Germanic and Slavic languages and taught at the universities of Strasbourg (1924) and Montpellier (1937). Captured during the First World War, he spent his captivity studying languages. His principal work appeared only after his death, edited by Jean Fourquet.

Dependency grammar

Tesnière's syntax rests on the notion of connexion: a structural relation between a governing word (regent) and a subordinate word. The verb occupies the top node of the sentence, and its dependents are arranged as a tree (a stemma). The sentence is analysed not through the binary subject–predicate division of traditional grammar but through hierarchical dependency relations.

Valency

Borrowing a metaphor from chemistry, Tesnière described the verb's capacity to govern a fixed number of participants as its valency. Verbs may be avalent (weather verbs), monovalent, divalent or trivalent according to the number of actants they require, a concept that deeply influenced later theories of argument structure and case grammar.

Legacy

Tesnière's work underlies modern dependency parsing in computational linguistics, including the dependency treebanks used in natural language processing, and influenced linguists such as Igor Mel'čuk and the Meaning–Text Theory.
