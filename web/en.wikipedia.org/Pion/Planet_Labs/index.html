Planet Labs PBC is an American Earth-imaging company based in San Francisco, California. Founded in 2010 by former NASA scientists Chris Boshuizen, Will Marshall and Robbie Schingler, Planet operates the largest fleet of Earth observation satellites in history, imaging the entire landmass of the Earth nearly every day.

Satellite constellations

Planet's PlanetScope constellation consists of hundreds of small CubeSats known as "Doves", each roughly the size of a loaf of bread, which capture imagery at about 3–5 meters per pixel. The company's SkySat satellites, acquired from Terra Bella (formerly Skybox Imaging) in 2017, provide higher-resolution imagery of around 50–70 centimeters per pixel and can be tasked to revisit specific locations multiple times per day.

Approach and customers

Planet pioneered an "agile aerospace" approach, borrowing rapid-iteration methods from consumer electronics: building satellites from commercial off-the-shelf components, launching them in large batches, and accepting shorter individual lifetimes in exchange for frequent technology refresh. Its daily global imagery is used in agriculture, forestry and deforestation monitoring, defense and intelligence, disaster response, mapping, and journalism.

History

The company launched its first prototype satellites in 2013 and began deploying Doves in flocks from 2014 onward, including a record-setting launch of 88 satellites aboard an Indian PSLV rocket in 2017. In 2021 Planet merged with a special-purpose acquisition company and became a public benefit corporation listed on the New York Stock Exchange.
