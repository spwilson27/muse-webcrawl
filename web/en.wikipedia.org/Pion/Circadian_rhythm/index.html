Circadian rhythm

A circadian rhythm, or circadian cycle, is a natural oscillation that repeats roughly every 24 hours. Circadian rhythms have been observed in plants, animals, fungi and cyanobacteria, and are generated by an internal biological clock even in constant conditions, though they are normally entrained to the environment by cues such as daylight.

Mechanism

In mammals, the master clock is located in the suprachiasmatic nucleus of the hypothalamus, which receives light input from the retina. At the molecular level, circadian rhythms arise from transcription–translation feedback loops involving clock genes such as PER and CRY. The pineal gland's hormone melatonin, secreted in darkness, helps signal night to the body.

Health

Disruption of circadian rhythms — through shift work, jet lag or irregular sleep — is associated with sleep disorders, metabolic disease and mood disorders. The 2017 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine was awarded to Jeffrey Hall, Michael Rosbash and Michael Young for their discoveries of the molecular mechanisms controlling circadian rhythm.
