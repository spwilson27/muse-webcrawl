Remote sensing

Remote sensing is the acquisition of information about an object or phenomenon without making physical contact with it, typically by means of aircraft or satellite sensors that detect reflected or emitted electromagnetic radiation. Remote sensing is used extensively in geography, land surveying, Earth science and agriculture to observe and map large areas from a distance.

Principles

Sensors mounted on aircraft or orbiting satellites measure radiation across portions of the electromagnetic spectrum. Passive sensors record energy naturally reflected from the surface, such as sunlight, while active sensors — including radar and lidar — emit energy and measure its return. The resulting imagery is processed to extract information about land cover, vegetation health, water bodies and human structures.

Applications

Remote sensing data underpin weather monitoring, crop forecasting, deforestation tracking, disaster assessment and topographic mapping. Satellite constellations such as Landsat and Sentinel provide regular, consistent imaging of the Earth's surface, enabling long-term studies of environmental change.
