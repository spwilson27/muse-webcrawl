CubeSat

TypeMiniaturised satellite standard
Unit size10 cm cube (1U)
First launched2003

A CubeSat is a class of small satellites built from standard 10 × 10 × 10 cm units. Combining several units (3U, 6U, 12U) creates larger spacecraft while keeping the standardised deployment interface.

Design and deployment

The CubeSat specification, developed at California Polytechnic State University and Stanford University in 1999, defines the mechanical and electrical interfaces so that satellites can fly as secondary payloads and be ejected from a common deployer. Commercial off-the-shelf electronics keep costs low, allowing universities, startups and space agencies to fly missions quickly.

Missions

CubeSats have been used for Earth observation, communications, astrophysics and technology tests. Planet's Dove constellation and NASA's interplanetary MarCO pair are prominent examples, and CubeSats are now also used by remote sensing networks for rapid-revisit imaging.
