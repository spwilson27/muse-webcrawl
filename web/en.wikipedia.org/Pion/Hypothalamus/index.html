Hypothalamus

The hypothalamus is a portion of the brain, located below the thalamus, that contains a number of small nuclei with a variety of functions. One of the most important functions is to link the nervous system to the endocrine system via the pituitary gland.

Roles

The hypothalamus controls body temperature, hunger, thirst, fatigue, sleep and circadian rhythms. Its suprachiasmatic nucleus acts as the body's master clock, entrained by light and signalling through pathways that interact with melatonin from the pineal gland. Hypothalamic releasing hormones direct the pituitary to secrete hormones controlling growth, stress, reproduction and metabolism throughout the body.
