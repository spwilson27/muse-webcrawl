Melatonin

Chemical nameN-acetyl-5-methoxytryptamine
Produced byPineal gland
FunctionRegulation of sleep–wake cycle

Melatonin is a hormone produced in the brain by the pineal gland in response to darkness. It is involved in synchronising circadian rhythms, including the sleep–wake cycle, and its nightly rise helps signal to the body that it is time to sleep.

Production and regulation

Melatonin synthesis is controlled by the suprachiasmatic nucleus of the hypothalamus: light detected by the retina suppresses production, while darkness permits it. Melatonin is synthesised from serotonin, itself derived from the amino acid tryptophan. Levels are high at night and low during the day, and also vary seasonally.

Uses

Melatonin is sold as a dietary supplement in some countries and as a prescription medicine in others. It is used for jet lag and certain sleep disorders, though evidence for general insomnia is modest. Melatonin also functions as an antioxidant and is found across animals, plants and microorganisms.
