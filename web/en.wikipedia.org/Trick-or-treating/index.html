Trick-or-treating
Observed: Halloween, 31 October
Custom: children in costume ask for sweets door to door

Trick-or-treating is a traditional Halloween custom for children and adults in some countries. In the evening before All Saints' Day (1 November), children in costumes travel from house to house, asking for treats with the phrase "trick or treat". The "treat" is usually some form of candy, while the "trick" is a mischievous threat, usually played as a joke, if no treat is given.

History

The custom has roots in the Celtic festival of Samhain and in mediaeval practices of "souling" and guising, in which the poor went door to door receiving food in return for prayers for the dead. It became established in North America in the early twentieth century and spread widely after the Second World War.

Modern practice

Communities may set official trick-or-treat hours, and alternatives such as trunk-or-treat events in car parks have become popular. The custom has spread beyond North America to parts of Europe, Latin America and elsewhere.
