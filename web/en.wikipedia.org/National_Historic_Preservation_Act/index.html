National Historic Preservation Act of 1966

The National Historic Preservation Act (NHPA) is United States federal legislation, enacted in 1966, intended to preserve historical and archaeological sites. It represents the most comprehensive preservation law ever enacted in the United States and created the framework within which American historic preservation still operates.

Provisions

The Act established the National Register of Historic Places, the list of National Historic Landmarks, and the system of State Historic Preservation Offices. It also created the Advisory Council on Historic Preservation, an independent agency that advises the President and Congress.

Section 106

The best-known provision, Section 106, requires federal agencies to consider the effects of their undertakings — projects they fund, permit or carry out — on historic properties, and to give the Advisory Council a reasonable opportunity to comment. The Section 106 review process involves consultation with state and tribal preservation officers and the public.
