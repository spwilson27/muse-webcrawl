Kingdom of Israel

The Kingdom of Israel was the northern of the two Israelite kingdoms that emerged after the division of the united monarchy, traditionally dated to about 930 BCE. Its capital was first Shechem, then Tirzah, and finally Samaria, founded by Omri.

History

According to the Hebrew Bible, Jeroboam I led the ten northern tribes in revolt against Rehoboam. The kingdom endured some two centuries, ruled by dynasties including the Omrides; Ahab is a prominent king in both biblical narrative and Assyrian records. Israel was generally wealthier and more populous than Judah to the south.

Fall

The Neo-Assyrian Empire under Tiglath-Pileser III, Shalmaneser V and Sargon II progressively reduced and then conquered the kingdom; Samaria fell about 722 BCE, its population partly deported, ending the northern kingdom. Its former tribes are remembered in later tradition as the Ten Lost Tribes.
