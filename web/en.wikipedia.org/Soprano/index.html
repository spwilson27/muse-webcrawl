Soprano

Typical rangec. C4 (middle C) to C6

A soprano in operatic performance

A soprano is the highest type of classical singing voice, usually sung by women and, before puberty, by boys (boy sopranos or trebles). The term derives from the Italian sopra, "above", since the soprano traditionally carries the highest vocal line in choral and operatic music. A typical soprano range extends from about middle C (C4) to the "high C" two octaves above (C6), although coloratura sopranos can sing considerably higher.

Subtypes

Soprano voices are classified by weight, agility, and timbre. The light, agile coloratura soprano specialises in florid ornamentation, as in the Queen of the Night's arias in Mozart's The Magic Flute. The lyric soprano has a warm, mellow sound suited to roles such as Mimì in La bohème; the spinto can push the voice to dramatic climaxes; the dramatic soprano possesses the power for Wagner and Strauss heroines; and the soubrette sings light, comic roles. Famous sopranos include Maria Callas, Joan Sutherland, Renée Fleming, and Leontyne Price.

In ensembles

In four-part choral writing (SATB), the soprano section sings the top part, often the melody. In the Baroque era, soprano roles were also written for castrati — male singers castrated before puberty — whose parts are now sung by women in trouser roles, countertenors, or boy sopranos. The word "soprano" is also applied to the highest member of instrument families, such as the soprano saxophone.
