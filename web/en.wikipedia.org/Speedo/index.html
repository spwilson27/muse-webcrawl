Speedo

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Speedo International Limited is an Australian-born manufacturer and distributor of swimwear and swim-related accessories. Founded in Sydney, Australia, in 1914 by Alexander MacRae, the company is now headquartered in Nottingham, England, and is a subsidiary of the Pentland Group. Speedo is the world's largest selling brand of swimwear, and its name has become a generic term — "speedos" — for men's brief-style swim briefs in many countries.

History

Alexander MacRae, a Scottish immigrant, founded MacRae Knitting Mills in 1914 and began producing swimwear under the "Fortitude" brand. The Speedo name was adopted in 1928 after a slogan competition produced "Speed on in your Speedos." The company's racerback suit worn at the 1932 Olympics by Clare Dennis brought international attention. Speedo pioneered nylon swimwear in the 1950s and introduced increasingly technical racing suits in subsequent decades.

Products and controversy

Speedo produces swimsuits, goggles, caps, and equipment for competition and leisure. Its LZR Racer suit, launched in 2008, was credited with helping swimmers break numerous world records at the Beijing Olympics; the ensuing controversy over "technological doping" led FINA to ban full-body polyurethane suits from 2010. Speedo sponsors many national teams and elite swimmers and divers.

The generic term

In Australia and elsewhere, "speedos" commonly denotes tight men's swim briefs regardless of brand; the style is also jokingly known in Australia as "budgie smugglers." Speedo has at times sought to protect its trademark against generic use.
