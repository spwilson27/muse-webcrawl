Detective Comics

Detective Comics
Publisher: DC Comics
First issue: March 1937
Notable debut: Batman (#27, 1939)

Detective Comics is an American comic book series published by Detective Comics, Inc., later DC Comics. First published in 1937, it is one of the longest continuously published comic books in the United States, and the company itself took its initials — DC — from the series.

History

The early issues were anthologies of hard-boiled detective stories featuring characters such as Slam Bradley. The series changed comics history with issue #27 (cover-dated May 1939), which introduced Batman, created by Bob Kane and Bill Finger. Batman rapidly became the dominant feature, and most issues since have starred him, with back-up features including Robin, Batgirl, the Martian Manhunter (introduced in #225) and many others.

Numbering and milestones

Unlike most long-running American series, Detective Comics retained its original numbering for decades. It passed issue #500 in 1981 and #881 in 2011, when DC relaunched its line with new #1 issues; the original numbering was restored with issue #934 in 2016, and the series celebrated its 1000th issue in 2019, eighty years after Batman's debut. Alongside Action Comics, it anchors DC's publishing history.

Significant runs

Notable creative eras include the 1970s stories of Denny O'Neil and Neal Adams, the Steve Englehart/Marshall Rogers run, and acclaimed modern tenures by writers such as Greg Rucka (including the Eisner-winning "Half a Life"), Scott Snyder, James Tynion IV and Ram V.
