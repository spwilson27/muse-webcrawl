Peder Severin Krøyer


P. S. Krøyer, self-portrait

Peder Severin Krøyer (23 July 1851 – 21 November 1909), usually known as P. S. Krøyer, was a Norwegian-born Danish painter. He is one of the best-known and most beloved painters of the Skagen Painters, the Scandinavian artists' colony that gathered each summer at Skagen in the far north of Denmark, and is celebrated for his virtuoso technique and his luminous depictions of the "Skagen light".

Life

Born in Stavanger, Norway, Krøyer was raised in Copenhagen by his aunt and her husband, the zoologist Henrik Krøyer. A child prodigy, he entered the Royal Danish Academy of Fine Arts at fourteen and travelled extensively in Europe, studying in Paris under Léon Bonnat and absorbing the naturalism of the French and Spanish schools. In 1889 he married the painter Marie Triepcke, who appears in many of his works. His later years were troubled by mental illness and failing eyesight; he died in Skagen in 1909.

Works

Krøyer's most famous paintings include Summer Evening at Skagen. The Artist's Wife and Dog by the Shore (1892), Summer Evening on Skagen's Southern Beach (1893) — depicting Anna Ancher and Marie Krøyer strolling at dusk — Midsummer Eve Bonfire on Skagen Beach (1906), and the monumental group portrait A Meeting in the Royal Danish Academy of Sciences (1897). His earlier masterpieces of social realism include The Iron Foundry, Burmeister & Wain (1885) and Hip, Hip, Hurrah! (1888). His works are held chiefly by Skagens Museum and the Hirschsprung Collection in Copenhagen.
