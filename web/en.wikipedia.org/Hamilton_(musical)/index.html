Hamilton (musical)

Hamilton: An American Musical
Music, lyrics and book: Lin-Manuel Miranda
Premiere: 2015, The Public Theater, New York

Hamilton: An American Musical is a sung-and-rapped-through musical by Lin-Manuel Miranda telling the life story of American Founding Father Alexander Hamilton, drawing heavily on Ron Chernow's 2004 biography. Its score blends hip hop, R&B, jazz, and traditional Broadway styles, and its original casting of non-white actors as the Founding Fathers became a signature of the show.

History

Miranda first performed the opening number at the White House in 2009, when the project was conceived as a concept album, "The Hamilton Mixtape". The musical premiered Off-Broadway at the Public Theater in February 2015 and transferred to Broadway's Richard Rodgers Theatre that August, directed by Thomas Kail with choreography by Andy Blankenbuehler.

Reception and awards

Hamilton received a record 16 Tony Award nominations in 2016 and won 11, including Best Musical. It won the 2016 Pulitzer Prize for Drama and the Grammy Award for Best Musical Theater Album. A filmed version of the original Broadway cast was released on Disney+ in 2020. Productions have run in London's West End, where it won seven Olivier Awards, and on tours worldwide.

Synopsis

The two acts trace Hamilton's rise from orphaned immigrant in the Caribbean to George Washington's aide-de-camp, first Secretary of the Treasury, and finally to his fatal duel with Aaron Burr in 1804, framed by the question "Who lives, who dies, who tells your story?"
