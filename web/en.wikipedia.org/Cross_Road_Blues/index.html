Cross Road Blues

Song by: Robert Johnson
Recorded: 27 November 1936, San Antonio, Texas
Genre: Delta blues
Label: Vocalion

"Cross Road Blues", commonly known as "Crossroads", is a blues song written and recorded by American blues artist Robert Johnson in 1936. Johnson performed it as a solo piece with his vocal and acoustic slide guitar in the Delta blues style. The song has come to be recognized as a blues standard and a signature piece of Johnson's, known for its lyrical themes and for inspiring the mythology surrounding his life.

A Mississippi Delta crossroads, the setting of the song's lyrics.

Lyrics and interpretation

The lyrics tell of a man standing at a crossroads at sundown, failing to flag a ride ("didn't nobody seem to know me, everybody pass me by"), and pleading for mercy — "you can run, you can run, tell my friend Willie Brown". Although the song itself does not mention a bargain, it became attached to the Faustian legend that Johnson sold his soul to the Devil at a crossroads in exchange for his musical gifts — a myth more directly drawn from Johnson's "Me and the Devil Blues" and the earlier recorded works of other bluesmen, and amplified by later writers and musicians.

Recording

Johnson recorded two takes of the song on 27 November 1936 in a makeshift studio in the Gunter Hotel in San Antonio, Texas. One take was released by Vocalion Records in 1937; the other surfaced decades later on reissues. The song features Johnson's intricate guitar work and falsetto moans.

Covers and legacy

Cream's 1968 live recording "Crossroads", arranged by Eric Clapton, became a rock standard and one of the band's signature performances; it was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame. Johnson's original recording was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame in 1998 and is preserved in the Library of Congress's National Recording Registry. The song has been covered by numerous artists including Elmore James, Lynyrd Skynyrd, Rush, and John Mayer.
