A snake boat (chundan vallam in Malayalam) is a long, narrow traditional war canoe of Kerala in southwest India, famous for the spectacular boat races held on the region's rivers and backwaters during the harvest festival of Onam. Among the largest watercraft rowed in sport, a snake boat can exceed 40 metres in length, with a raised stern that rises like a cobra's hood.

History and construction

Snake boats descend from the war canoes of the princely states of Travancore and Cochin, whose rulers fought along Kerala's waterways; tradition links their design to the 17th-century ruler Devanarayanan. The hull is built from planks of anjili (wild jackfruit) wood stitched and caulked by master craftsmen, following specifications recorded in local tradition, and is maintained with regular applications of fish oil, egg white and coconut-shell soot.

Races

In the races (vallamkali), crews of a hundred or more paddlers row in unison to the beat of drums and cymbals and the singing of boat songs (vanchipattu), steered by men standing at the stern with long oars. The most famous event is the Nehru Trophy Boat Race, held on Punnamada Lake at Alappuzha on the second Saturday of August; the trophy was instituted after Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru visited in 1952 and was thrilled by an impromptu race. The Aranmula race, held during Onam, has a more ritual character.

Cultural role

Snake boats belong to villages and temples rather than individuals, and their upkeep and crews are a focus of community pride and rivalry. The races draw large crowds and are a signature spectacle of Kerala tourism.
