Submission wrestling

Submission wrestling, also known as submission grappling or no-gi jiu-jitsu, is a form of competition and a general term for martial arts and combat sports that focus on clinch and ground fighting with the aim of obtaining a submission through the use of submission holds. The term "submission wrestling" usually refers only to the form of competition and training that does not use a gi, or "combat kimono", of the sort often worn with belts that indicate rank.

The sport brings together techniques from Brazilian jiu-jitsu, folk wrestling (catch wrestling and freestyle wrestling), judo, and sambo. Submission fighting as an element of a larger sport setting is very common in mixed martial arts, Brazilian jiu-jitsu, catch wrestling, and other martial arts.

History and development

Submission wrestling developed out of the blending of grappling traditions in the late twentieth century, particularly through the growth of Brazilian jiu-jitsu outside Brazil and the popularity of no-gi competition divisions. The ADCC Submission Fighting World Championship, first held in 1998 in Abu Dhabi, became the sport's most prestigious tournament and helped standardize rule sets emphasizing positional control and submissions over points scored with a gi.

Techniques

Core techniques include takedowns, guard passing, sweeps, and submissions such as the rear naked choke, triangle choke, armbar, kimura, guillotine choke, and a variety of leg locks including heel hooks, which are permitted in many advanced rule sets. Because no gi is worn, practitioners rely on underhooks, overhooks, wrist control, and body locks rather than gripping cloth.
