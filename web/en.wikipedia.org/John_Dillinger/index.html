John Herbert Dillinger (June 22, 1903 – July 22, 1934) was an American bank robber who operated during the Great Depression. Nicknamed "the Jackrabbit" for his graceful movements during heists, he robbed banks and police arsenals across the Midwest and twice escaped from jail. The Federal Bureau of Investigation declared him "Public Enemy No. 1," and his exploits captured the public imagination at a time when banks were widely resented.

Early life and first crimes

Dillinger grew up in Indianapolis and Mooresville, Indiana. After a troubled youth and a brief stint in the U.S. Navy, he was convicted in 1924 of robbing a grocer and spent nearly nine years in prison, where he learned the bank-robbing trade from experienced criminals. Paroled in May 1933, he embarked on a year-long spree of robberies.

The Dillinger gang

With associates including "Baby Face" Nelson, Homer Van Meter and John "Red" Hamilton, Dillinger's gang robbed about a dozen banks and raided several police stations for weapons and bulletproof vests. Captured in Tucson, Arizona, in January 1934, he was jailed at Crown Point, Indiana, from which he made a celebrated escape in March — legend holds he carved a fake gun from wood. In April 1934 the gang shot its way out of an FBI raid at the Little Bohemia Lodge in Wisconsin, a fiasco that intensified pressure on the bureau.

Death

Betrayed by Anna Sage, the "Woman in Red," Dillinger was ambushed by federal agents led by Melvin Purvis as he left the Biograph Theater in Chicago on July 22, 1934. He was shot dead in an alley beside the theater. His death, and the crusade against Depression-era outlaws, helped reshape the FBI into a powerful national police agency.
