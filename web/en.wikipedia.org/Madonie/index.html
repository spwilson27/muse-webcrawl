Madonie

Country: Italy (Sicily)

Highest peak: Pizzo Carbonara, 1,979 m

Location: Metropolitan City of Palermo

Protected area: Madonie Regional Natural Park (est. 1989)

The Madonie (Sicilian: Madunìi) are a mountain range in northern Sicily, Italy, extending along the Tyrrhenian coast in the province of Palermo. Together with the Nebrodi to the east and the Peloritani, they form the Sicilian Apennines, the island's continuation of the Calabrian mountain chain. Their highest summit, Pizzo Carbonara (1,979 m), is the second-highest peak in Sicily after Mount Etna.

Geography and nature

The range is built largely of limestone and dolomite, with karst plateaus, dolines and caves. Much of it is protected as the Madonie Regional Natural Park, established in 1989 and covering about 40,000 hectares, which forms part of the UNESCO Global Geoparks network. The park preserves Sicily's most diverse flora outside Etna: more than half of the island's plant species occur here, including the critically endangered Sicilian fir (Abies nebrodensis), reduced to a few dozen mature trees, as well as beech and holm-oak forests, manna ash cultivation around Castelbuono and Pollina, and wild orchids.

Towns and culture

Hill towns of the Madonie include Petralia Soprana and Petralia Sottana, Castelbuono, Polizzi Generosa, Gangi (voted one of Italy's most beautiful villages), Geraci Siculo and Isnello. The circuit of the old Targa Florio road race winds through the range near Collesano and Cerda. The mountains are noted for chestnuts, hazelnuts, the slow food presidium of the Madonie provola cheese, and winter skiing at Piano Battaglia, one of Sicily's two ski areas.
