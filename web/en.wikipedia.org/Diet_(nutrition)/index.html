Diet (nutrition)

A varied diet supplies the nutrients the body requires.

In nutrition, a diet is the sum of the food consumed by a person or other organism. The word is often used more narrowly for a deliberate, regulated pattern of eating — chosen for health, weight management, medical treatment, religious observance, or ethical reasons. An organism's habitual diet strongly influences its health, growth, and disease risk.

Components of the human diet

A diet must supply energy and the raw materials of the body: proteins (amino acids), fats (including essential fatty acids), carbohydrates, dietary fibre, vitamins, minerals, and water. Public-health guidance generally recommends a diet rich in vegetables, fruits, whole grains, and legumes, moderate in protein foods, and limited in salt, free sugars, and saturated and industrially produced trans fats.

Dietary patterns

Named diets include cultural patterns such as the Mediterranean diet; ethical or environmental patterns such as vegetarian and vegan diets; weight-loss regimens (low-carbohydrate, low-fat, intermittent fasting); and therapeutic diets prescribed for disease, such as gluten-free diets for coeliac disease, carbohydrate counting in diabetes, or low-sodium diets in hypertension.

Diet and health

Both undernutrition and overnutrition cause enormous burdens of disease: deficiency diseases such as scurvy and kwashiorkor at one extreme, and obesity, type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and some cancers — associated with energy-dense, highly processed diets — at the other. Nutritional science studies these relationships, though its findings are often contested because diet is difficult to measure and confounded by lifestyle. The healthiest approach supported by evidence is a varied, minimally processed diet eaten in amounts matching energy needs.
