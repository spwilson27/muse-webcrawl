480i

480i is the shorthand name for a video mode with 480 lines of horizontal resolution and interlaced scanning. Formally written 480i60 (or 480i59.94), it was the standard-definition television format used by the NTSC analogue broadcasting system in North America, Japan, and other countries, and it remains a supported mode in digital television, DVD, and legacy video equipment.

Technical details

In analogue NTSC, the picture comprises 525 total scan lines, of which approximately 480–486 carry visible image. Each frame is divided into two fields of about 240 lines — first the odd lines, then the even lines — transmitted at 59.94 fields per second, giving 29.97 complete frames per second. The digital version of 480i, defined in standards such as ITU-R BT.601, samples the picture at 720 × 480 pixels, with non-square pixels producing either a 4:3 or anamorphic 16:9 picture.

Usage

480i was the native format of NTSC broadcasts, VHS recordings, LaserDiscs, and standard DVDs sold in NTSC regions, and the output of many classic video game consoles. Its progressive-scan counterpart is 480p, which was marketed as enhanced-definition television. The PAL and SECAM counterpart, with 576 visible lines, is 576i.
