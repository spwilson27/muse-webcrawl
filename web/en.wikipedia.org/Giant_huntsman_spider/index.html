Heteropoda maxima is the largest known huntsman spider by leg span.

The giant huntsman spider (Heteropoda maxima) is a species of huntsman spider in the family Sparassidae, notable as the largest known member of the family by leg span, which can reach 30 centimetres (12 inches) — about the size of a dinner plate. It is nonetheless lighter-bodied and less massive than the Goliath birdeater tarantula, which is heavier overall. The species inhabits caves in Laos, in Southeast Asia.

Discovery and description

The species was formally described in 2001 by the German arachnologist Peter Jäger of the Senckenberg Research Institute, from specimens collected in caves in Khammouane Province, Laos. Its pale colouration and reduced eyes are consistent with a cave-dwelling (troglobitic) lifestyle.

Description and behaviour

Like other huntsman spiders, H. maxima has legs that curve forward in a crab-like fashion, with laterigrade leg orientation that lets it move sideways as well as forwards. Huntsmen do not build webs to catch prey; instead they are active, fast-moving ambush predators that chase down insects and other invertebrates, relying on speed and eyesight rather than silk traps. Females carry their egg sacs in their chelicerae and guard them. The venom of huntsman spiders is used to subdue prey and is not considered medically dangerous to humans, though a bite may cause local pain.

Habitat

The giant huntsman spider lives in the entrance zones and deeper passages of limestone caves in Laos, where it hunts on cave walls. Cave ecosystems in the region face threats from habitat disturbance and collecting, and the species' restricted known range makes it of conservation interest, though it has been widely reported in popular media chiefly for its size.
