Orient Express

The Orient Express was a long-distance passenger train service created in 1883 by the Belgian company Compagnie Internationale des Wagons-Lits (CIWL), linking Paris with Constantinople (Istanbul). Its name became synonymous with luxury rail travel and intrigue.

History

The first through service ran in 1889; routes and schedules changed with wars and politics, and after 1919 the Simplon route through Milan and Venice became the classic itinerary. The train inspired numerous works of fiction, notably Agatha Christie's Murder on the Orient Express (1934).

Decline and legacy

Competition from air travel eroded the service; the last Paris–Istanbul through train ran in 1977, and the name survived on a Strasbourg–Vienna service until 2009. Since 1982 the private Venice Simplon-Orient-Express has operated restored 1920s carriages as a luxury tourist train.
