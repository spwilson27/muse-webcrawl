Inkjet printing

Inkjet printing is a type of computer printing that recreates a digital image by propelling droplets of ink onto paper, plastic or other substrates. It is the most common type of printer for home and small office use, and is also used for industrial, textile and large-format printing.

Technology

Continuous inkjet systems deflect a stream of droplets electrostatically. Drop-on-demand systems eject droplets only when needed, using thermal bubble formation, as developed by Canon and Hewlett-Packard, or piezoelectric crystals, as used by Epson.

History

Development began in the 1950s; commercial inkjet printers became widespread in the 1980s with the HP ThinkJet and DeskJet. Costs per page, ink formulations and print quality improved steadily.

Applications

Beyond documents and photographs, inkjet is used for printed electronics, bioprinting, ceramics and packaging.
