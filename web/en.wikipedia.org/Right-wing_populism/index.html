Ideology: Populism combined with nationalism, anti-elitism, opposition to immigration

Right-wing populism

Right-wing populism is a political ideology that combines populism — the claim to represent "the people" against a corrupt elite — with right-wing positions such as nationalism, strong opposition to immigration, law-and-order policies, and cultural conservatism. Scholars such as Cas Mudde describe its core as nativism, authoritarianism, and populism.

Characteristics

Right-wing populist parties and leaders typically portray mainstream politicians, media, and international institutions as betraying ordinary citizens, promise to restore national sovereignty and cultural identity, and favour welfare and protection mainly for the native population ("welfare chauvinism"). Economic policies vary, mixing market liberalism with protectionism.

Examples and history

The phenomenon grew from the late twentieth century in Europe with parties such as France's National Front (now National Rally), Austria's Freedom Party, and the Dutch Party for Freedom, and spread globally, influencing movements and leaders in the Americas and Asia. Its rise is linked to globalisation, economic insecurity, cultural change, and distrust of established parties.
