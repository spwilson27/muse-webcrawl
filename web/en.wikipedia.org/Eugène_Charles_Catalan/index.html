Eugène Charles Catalan

Eugène Charles Catalan (30 May 1814 – 14 February 1894) was a French and Belgian mathematician who worked on continued fractions, descriptive geometry, number theory and combinatorics. He is remembered for Catalan's conjecture (now Mihăilescu's theorem) and for the Catalan numbers in combinatorics.

Life

Born in Bruges to a French jeweller father, Catalan studied at the École Polytechnique in Paris, where Joseph Liouville was a mentor. Expelled for leftist political activity in 1834, he later taught in Châlons-en-Champagne and, after returning to Paris, eventually took a post at the University of Liège in 1865, where he remained, teaching analysis.

Work

In 1844 Catalan conjectured that the only consecutive powers greater than 1 are 8 and 9 (3² − 2³ = 1); this was proved only in 2002 by Preda Mihăilescu. The Catalan numbers 1, 1, 2, 5, 14, 42, … count many combinatorial structures (binary trees, bracketings, polygon triangulations) and arise in his work, though they were known earlier to others including Euler. Catalan's constant, Catalan solids and Catalan's triangle also bear his name.

Legacy

Catalan founded the journal Nouvelle correspondance mathématique and published prolifically, including work on surfaces (Catalan surfaces) and hypergeometric series. See also Catalan number, Catalan's conjecture and Catalan's constant.
