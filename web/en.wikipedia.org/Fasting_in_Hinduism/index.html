Fasting in Hinduism

Sanskrit termUpavāsa; vrata
ReligionHinduism
Common occasionsEkadashi, Mahashivaratri, Navaratri, Karva Chauth, Purnima

Fasting in Hinduism is the voluntary abstention from some or all food — and sometimes water — for religious purposes. In Sanskrit the practice is called upavāsa ("dwelling near [God]") and is often undertaken as part of a vrata, a vow or observance. Fasting is a common feature of Hindu devotion, believed to purify the body and mind, cultivate self-discipline, and express devotion to a deity.

Forms and occasions

Hindu fasts range from complete abstinence to partial fasts in which grains, salt, or certain foods are avoided and fruits, milk, and nuts (phalahar) are eaten instead. Widely observed fasts include Ekadashi (twice monthly, dedicated to Vishnu), Monday fasts for Shiva, Mahashivaratri, the nine nights of Navaratri, Karva Chauth (observed by married women in North India), Purnima (full moon) fasts, and fasts during festivals such as Janmashtami and Ramadan-equivalent observances are not part of Hindu practice. In Maharashtra and elsewhere, Nirjala Ekadashi is kept without water.

Beliefs

Scriptures and tradition associate fasting with the control of the senses, the earning of merit (punya), and the fulfilment of wishes. Weekly fasts are dedicated to particular deities — for example, Thursdays to Vishnu or Brihaspati, Fridays to Santoshi Mata or Lakshmi, and Saturdays to Shani.
