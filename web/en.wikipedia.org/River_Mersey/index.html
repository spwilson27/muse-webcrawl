The River Mersey is a major river in North West England. Its name derives from Old English Mǣres-ēa, "border river", referring to its role as the boundary between the ancient kingdoms of Mercia and Northumbria. The river rises in the hills east of Greater Manchester, forms at Stockport from the confluence of the Rivers Tame and Goyt, flows westward for about 112 km (70 mi) through the Manchester Ship Canal corridor and Cheshire plain, and widens into a broad tidal estuary between Liverpool and the Wirral Peninsula before emptying into Liverpool Bay and the Irish Sea.

Course and estuary

Below Warrington the Mersey becomes tidal. It passes through the Runcorn Gap, spanned by the Silver Jubilee Bridge and the Mersey Gateway Bridge, then widens into an estuary up to 5 km across at high water. The estuary has the second-highest tidal range in Britain, after the Severn, and the tidal bore-like rush created the notorious "Narrows" at Liverpool's Pier Head. Two road tunnels and a rail tunnel of the Merseyrail network cross beneath the river between Liverpool and Birkenhead.

History and culture

The Mersey powered the growth of Liverpool into one of the world's great ports and of Manchester, linked to it in 1894 by the Manchester Ship Canal. Industrial pollution rendered the river almost biologically dead by the mid-20th century; a celebrated cleanup campaign from the 1980s restored water quality, and salmon and otters have returned. In popular culture the river gives its name to the Merseybeat sound of the 1960s and to the song "Ferry Cross the Mersey" by Gerry and the Pacemakers.
