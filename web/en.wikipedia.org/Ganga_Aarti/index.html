Ganga Aarti

Ganga Aarti is a Hindu ritual of worship in which light, offered in the form of lamps and fire, is presented to the goddess Ganga, the personification of the river Ganges. The ceremony is performed daily at dawn and, most famously, at dusk on the ghats (riverfront steps) of the holy cities along the river, including Varanasi, Haridwar, and Rishikesh. It is one of the best-known public devotional ceremonies in India and draws large crowds of pilgrims and visitors.

The ceremony

The evening aarti is conducted by a group of priests, usually dressed in matching saffron or cream-coloured robes, standing on a raised platform facing the river. In a synchronised sequence they raise large multi-tiered oil lamps, incense burners, conch shells, and peacock-feather and yak-tail fans, moving them in circular motions to the accompaniment of chanting, hymns, bells, and drums. Fire is regarded as a purifier and as a medium that carries the devotees' prayers to the goddess.

Devotees often set small offerings of flowers and a lit earthen lamp (diya) afloat on the river on a leaf boat during or after the ceremony. The best-known performance takes place at Dashashwamedh Ghat in Varanasi, where the ritual has been organised in its present choreographed form since the early 1990s; the aarti at Har Ki Pauri in Haridwar and at Triveni Ghat in Rishikesh are also widely attended.

Significance

In Hindu belief the Ganges is a goddess and a mother (Ganga Maiya) whose waters purify sins and grant liberation. The aarti expresses gratitude and devotion, and forms part of the broader tradition of river worship in India. The word aarti derives from the Sanskrit ārātrika, a ritual in which light is waved before a deity to dispel darkness and ignorance.
