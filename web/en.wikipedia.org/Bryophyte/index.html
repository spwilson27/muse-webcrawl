Bryophyte

Bryophytes are a group of land plants comprising the mosses, liverworts, and hornworts — some 20,000 species. They are non-vascular plants that reproduce by spores and typically grow in moist, shaded habitats.

Unlike vascular plants, the dominant generation in bryophytes is the haploid gametophyte. They lack true roots, stems, and leaves, absorbing water over their surfaces, and many tolerate desiccation and revive on rewetting.

Bryophytes play important ecological roles: peat mosses (Sphagnum) store vast amounts of carbon in peatlands, and mosses stabilise soils, retain moisture, and provide microhabitats for invertebrates.
