Charleston, West Virginia

Charleston is the capital and most populous city of the U.S. state of West Virginia. It is the county seat of Kanawha County and lies at the confluence of the Elk and Kanawha rivers in the west-central part of the state. The city has a population of roughly 48,000, with about 300,000 people in its metropolitan area.

History

The area was settled after the American Revolutionary War; Fort Lee was built in 1788 and the town was chartered in 1794 as Charlestown, named after Charles Clendenin, father of its founder George Clendenin. Salt brines, coal, natural gas and timber made the Kanawha valley an early industrial centre. Charleston briefly hosted the state capital in 1870 and became the permanent capital after a statewide vote in 1877; the present gold-domed State Capitol, designed by Cass Gilbert, was completed in 1932.

Economy

State government is the largest employer, followed by health care and a chemical industry that grew up along the Kanawha River — the "Chemical Valley". Union Carbide and other firms built major plants in the area during the twentieth century. The city has faced population decline as coal and chemical employment contracted, and has invested in downtown redevelopment along the riverfront.

Culture and transport

Charleston is home to the West Virginia Culture Center and State Museum, the Clay Center for the Arts and Sciences, and West Virginia Public Broadcasting. Yeager Airport serves the city from a hilltop northeast of downtown, and Amtrak's Cardinal route stops at Charleston station.
