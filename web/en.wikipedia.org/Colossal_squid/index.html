Colossal squid

Scientific nameMesonychoteuthis hamiltoni
FamilyCranchiidae
HabitatSouthern Ocean, deep water
Estimated massUp to ~495 kg (largest known invertebrate by mass)

The colossal squid (Mesonychoteuthis hamiltoni) is a species of glass squid in the family Cranchiidae, native to the deep waters of the Southern Ocean around Antarctica. Described in 1925 from fragments found in a sperm whale's stomach, it is the largest known invertebrate by mass, with estimates for mature adults of up to roughly 495 kg and total lengths of 10 metres or more. Unlike the giant squid, whose arms bear suckers with sharp rings, the colossal squid's arms carry swivelling hooks, and its tentacle clubs bear two rows of sharp hooks.

Description

The colossal squid has the largest eyes of any known animal — up to about 27 cm across in the famous 2007 specimen — an adaptation to detecting predators such as sperm whales in dim deep water. Its beak is among the largest of any squid relative to the mantle, and its body is wider and stouter than that of the giant squid, with a proportionally shorter mantle and longer arms.

Distribution and ecology

The species is circumpolar around Antarctica, inhabiting waters south of roughly 40°S at depths from the surface down to more than 2,000 m; juveniles occur nearer the surface. It is an ambush predator feeding on fish such as Antarctic toothfish, and is itself preyed upon by sperm whales and sleeper sharks; its remains are frequently found in sperm whale stomachs.

Specimens

In February 2007 a New Zealand fishing vessel caught a live colossal squid of about 495 kg in the Ross Sea; it was frozen and taken to the Museum of New Zealand Te Papa Tongarewa, where it was thawed, examined and put on display. Very few complete specimens are known, and the species' biology remains poorly understood.
