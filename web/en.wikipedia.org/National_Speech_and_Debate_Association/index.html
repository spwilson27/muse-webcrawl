National Speech and Debate Association

The National Speech and Debate Association (NSDA) is an American non-profit organization that promotes speech and debate activities among high school and middle school students. Founded in 1925 as the National Forensic League, it is one of the oldest and largest honor societies for student speakers and debaters in the United States.

History

The organization was founded in 1925 by Bruno E. Jacob, a teacher at Ripon College in Wisconsin. Jacob sought to motivate students to participate in speech activities by creating an honor society that recognized achievement in forensics. The league grew rapidly and for decades operated under the name National Forensic League (NFL). In 2014, the organization adopted the name National Speech and Debate Association to better reflect its mission, while the National Forensic League name was retained for its honor society.

Activities

The NSDA sanctions a wide range of competitive events, including policy debate, Lincoln–Douglas debate, public forum debate, congressional debate, and individual speech events such as original oratory, dramatic interpretation, humorous interpretation, and extemporaneous speaking. Each June, the association hosts the National Speech and Debate Tournament, widely regarded as the largest academic competition in the world, drawing thousands of students from across the country.

Membership and honors

Students earn points through competition and service, progressing through degrees of membership in the honor society. Alumni of the organization include numerous prominent figures in law, politics, business, and entertainment. The NSDA also maintains a Hall of Fame honoring distinguished coaches and alumni.
