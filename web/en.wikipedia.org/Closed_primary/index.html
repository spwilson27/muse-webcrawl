Closed primary

A closed primary is a type of primary election in which only voters who have registered with a political party in advance may vote in that party's primary. Closed primaries are used in a number of U.S. states and contrast with open primaries, in which any registered voter may participate in any party's primary.

How it works

In a closed primary, a voter may only cast a ballot in the primary of the party with which they are affiliated. Unaffiliated or independent voters are generally excluded, though some jurisdictions allow parties to invite unaffiliated voters. Party registration deadlines typically fall weeks or months before the election.

Arguments

Supporters argue that closed primaries ensure that a party's nominee is chosen by its own members and prevent "raiding" by supporters of opposing parties. Critics argue that closed primaries disenfranchise independent voters and can produce more ideologically extreme nominees.

Use in the United States

States using closed primaries for congressional and state elections have historically included New York, Florida, Pennsylvania, and several others, though rules change over time and semi-closed variants exist.
