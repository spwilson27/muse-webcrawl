John Nelson Darby

Born18 November 1800, Westminster, London, England
Died29 April 1882, Bournemouth, England
OccupationClergyman, theologian, Bible translator
Known forPlymouth Brethren; dispensationalism; pretribulation rapture

John Nelson Darby (18 November 1800 – 29 April 1882) was an Anglo-Irish Bible teacher and evangelist, and an influential figure among the original Plymouth Brethren. He is regarded as the father of modern dispensationalism, a system of biblical interpretation that divides history into distinct dispensations, and he popularised the doctrine of a pretribulation rapture of the church — ideas carried around the world by the Scofield Reference Bible and modern evangelicalism.

Early life and ministry

Darby was educated at Trinity College Dublin, trained as a barrister, then was ordained in the Church of Ireland in 1825 and served as a curate in County Wicklow. Disillusioned with the established church, he left the ministry and from the late 1820s met with other believers in Dublin in informal assemblies that became the Brethren movement. He spent years evangelising in Europe, especially Switzerland, France and Germany, and later made repeated journeys to North America.

Teaching and influence

Darby taught that the church was in ruins, that believers should separate from denominational structures, and that prophecy foretold the restoration of Israel and a secret catching-away of the saints before the great tribulation. A schism in 1848 split the Brethren into "Exclusive" (Darbyite) and "Open" branches. He produced the Darby Bible, a literal translation into English (completed by his followers in 1890), and versions in French and German.

Death

Darby died at Bournemouth in 1882. Though little known outside Brethren circles in his lifetime, his ideas profoundly shaped fundamentalist and evangelical theology in the twentieth century.
