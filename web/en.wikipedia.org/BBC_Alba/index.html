BBC Alba

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

BBC Alba is a Scottish Gaelic-language free-to-air television channel jointly run by the BBC and MG Alba. Launched on 19 September 2008, it is the first channel devoted to Gaelic broadcasting, airing a mix of news, drama, documentaries, entertainment, children's programmes and sport, with subtitles in English on most programmes.

Programming

Flagship productions include the long-running soap Machair-era successors and the popular drama Bannan, the European news programme Eòrpa, Gaelic news service An Là, and extensive coverage of Scottish football, shinty and rugby, including Scottish Premiership and women's football. Children's output airs under the CBBC Alba brand, dubbing and originating Gaelic content for young speakers.

Audience and role

Though the Gaelic-speaking population is small (tens of thousands of speakers, mainly in the Hebrides and Highlands), the channel reaches a much larger audience — several hundred thousand viewers a week — thanks largely to sport and subtitled programming. It is regarded as a cornerstone of Gaelic media policy alongside BBC Radio nan Gàidheal, supporting language revitalization by giving Gaelic a presence in prestige genres such as drama and national news.

Availability

BBC Alba broadcasts on Freeview in Scotland, satellite, cable and BBC iPlayer, having expanded Freeview carriage after the digital switchover freed capacity in 2011.
