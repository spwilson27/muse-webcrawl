Transfiguration of Jesus

The Transfiguration of Jesus is an event reported in the Synoptic Gospels of the New Testament in which Jesus is transfigured and becomes radiant in glory upon a mountain. In the accounts, Jesus and three of his apostles — Peter, James, and John — go to a mountain (later identified as Mount Tabor) to pray, where Jesus begins to shine with bright rays of light. The prophets Moses and Elijah appear next to him, and Jesus is called "Son" by a voice in the sky, assumed to be God the Father.

Theological significance

The Transfiguration is one of the five major milestones in the gospel narrative of the life of Jesus, the others being Baptism, Crucifixion, Resurrection, and Ascension. In Christian theology it is a pivotal moment: the human nature of Jesus meets God, and the temporal and eternal are joined, with Jesus as the connecting point, acting as the bridge between heaven and earth.

In hesychasm

The light seen at the Transfiguration — the Tabor Light — became central to the hesychast tradition defended by Gregory Palamas in the fourteenth century. Palamas taught that this light is the uncreated energy of God, genuinely visible to purified human eyes, a doctrine known as the essence–energies distinction.

The Feast of the Transfiguration is celebrated on 6 August in both Eastern and Western churches.
