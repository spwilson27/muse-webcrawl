Gregory Palamas

Fresco of Saint Gregory Palamas

Saint Gregory Palamas (Γρηγόριος Παλαμᾶς) was the foremost theologian of hesychasm. Born in Constantinople around 1296 and tonsured a monk in his early twenties, he withdrew to Mount Athos, where the Jesus Prayer was practised with psycho-physical technique. His Triads defended the monks against the charge that God cannot be experienced in this life.

Teaching

Palamas held that the grace of deification, the light of the Transfiguration and the operations of the Holy Trinity are all uncreated energies of God. Because the essence of God remains inaccessible, this distinction preserves both truths: God is utterly transcendent, yet truly gives himself to the saints.

Canonisation

He died at Thessalonica on 14 November 1359, was canonised in 1368, and is commemorated as one of the great teachers of the Orthodox Church; relics of the saint are kept at Thessalonica.
