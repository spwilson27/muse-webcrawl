Mount Athos

Mount Athos (Greek: Όρος Άθως, "Holy Mountain") is a mountain and peninsula in northeastern Greece and an important centre of Eastern Orthodox monasticism. It is home to 20 monasteries under the direct jurisdiction of the Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople. The mountain and most of the Athos peninsula are governed as the Monastic Community of Mount Athos, an autonomous region within the Hellenic Republic, formally the "Autonomous Monastic State of the Holy Mountain."

History

Christian hermits were living on the peninsula by the early Byzantine period, but organised monastic life is conventionally dated from 963, when St Athanasius the Athonite founded the Monastery of Great Lavra with the support of Emperor Nikephoros II Phokas. Imperial patronage continued, and Athos grew into a pan-Orthodox centre drawing monks from Greece, Georgia (Iviron), the Slavic lands, and Romania. The avaton — the rule prohibiting women (and female domestic animals, with a few traditional exceptions) from entering the territory — traces to a 1045 edict of Emperor Constantine IX Monomachos and is still enforced.

Athos was the spiritual hearth of hesychasm: in the 14th century its monks, led by Gregory Palamas, defended contemplative prayer and the theology of the uncreated light, which was vindicated by councils at Constantinople. Under Ottoman rule the monasteries survived through taxation and diplomacy, experiencing revival in the 18th century with the Philokalia movement, and again from the late 20th century onward, when numbers rose from a low of about 1,100 ageing monks in the 1970s to roughly 2,000, including many young and university-educated entrants.

Governance and life

The Holy Community (Iera Koinotis), a council of representatives of the 20 ruling monasteries based in the capital, Karyes, administers the mountain together with a civil governor appointed by the Greek foreign ministry. Entry is limited: Orthodox pilgrims and a small number of non-Orthodox visitors require a permit (diamonitirion), and only a fixed number are admitted each day. The monasteries keep Byzantine time and the Julian calendar, 13 days behind the civil calendar. The 20 monasteries have a fixed hierarchy, headed by Great Lavra and including Vatopedi, Iviron, Hilandar (Serbian), Dionysiou, and the Russian St Panteleimon's. Dependencies include sketes, cells, and hermitages.

Culture and treasures

Athos preserves one of the world's greatest concentrations of Byzantine and post-Byzantine art: illuminated manuscripts, icons, frescoes, vestments, and relics, including libraries of tens of thousands of manuscripts. It has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1988, inscribed for both its cultural and natural significance.
