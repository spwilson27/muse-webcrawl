Theosis (Eastern Christian theology)

Transfiguration: the goal of theosis

Theosis (θέωσις) is the Orthodox doctrine of deification: the process in which, by divine grace, human beings come to share in the uncreated light and life of God — the same light manifested on Mount Tabor. It is summarised in the patristic formula that God became man so that man might become god.

Foundations

Scriptural warrants include Psalm 82:6 ("I said, you are gods"), John 10:34 and 2 Peter 1:4 ("partakers of the divine nature"). The doctrine was developed by Irenaeus, Athanasius, the Cappadocians and Maximus the Confessor, and given its classic precision by Gregory Palamas through the essence–energies distinction: what is shared is God's energy, never his essence.

Practice

Theosis is received in the sacraments and deepened by the ascetic and prayerful life mapped in The Philokalia: purification, illumination, and finally union. Its icon is the Transfiguration, whose feast (6 August) is among the great feasts of the Orthodox year.
