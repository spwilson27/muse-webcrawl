Essence–energies distinction

The uncreated Tabor Light as divine energy

The essence–energies distinction is the doctrine, definitive for Eastern Orthodox theology, that in God there is a real distinction between the essence (ousia), which is wholly unknowable, and the energies (energeiai), the uncreated operations in which God gives himself to the world. Its most famous application is to the Tabor Light: the light of the Transfiguration is an uncreated energy, not a created symbol.

Formulation

Gregory Palamas, arguing against Barlaam's claim that knowledge of God is only indirect, taught that the hesychasts who saw the uncreated light truly participated in God — in his energies — while his essence remained imparticipable even to the angels. The councils of 1341–1351 made this teaching dogma for the Orthodox Church.

Debate

Western theologians from the scholastic period to the present have debated whether the distinction contradicts the doctrine of divine simplicity; Orthodox respondents such as Vladimir Lossky and John Meyendorff argue that it expresses the patristic balance of apophatic theology and real deification (theosis).
