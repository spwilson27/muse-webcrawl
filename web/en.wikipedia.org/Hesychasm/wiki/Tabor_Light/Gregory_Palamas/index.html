Gregory Palamas

Saint Gregory Palamas

Gregory Palamas (1296–1359) was the Byzantine theologian whose writings defended the hesychasts' vision of the Tabor Light. Educated at Constantinople, professed a monk on Mount Athos, and eventually consecrated Archbishop of Thessalonica in 1347, he spent much of his career in controversy with Barlaam of Calabria, Gregory Akindynos and Nicephorus Gregoras.

Works

His writings include the Triads in Defence of the Holy Hesychasts, the Capita 150, homilies — many delivered at Thessalonica — and the confession of faith incorporated into the Synodikon of Orthodoxy. In them he grounds theosis in the distinction between God's inaccessible essence and his uncreated, participable energies.

Significance

Palamas is regarded by the Orthodox Church as a pillar of the faith, commemorated on the Second Sunday of Great Lent; his teaching has been called "Palamism" and stands at the centre of modern Orthodox identity.
