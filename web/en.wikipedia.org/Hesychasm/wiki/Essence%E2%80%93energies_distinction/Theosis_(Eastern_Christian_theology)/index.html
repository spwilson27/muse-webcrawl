Theosis (Eastern Christian theology)

The uncreated light of the Transfiguration

Theosis (Greek: θέωσις), or deification (from Latin deus, "god"), is a transformative process whose aim is likeness to or union with God, as taught by the Eastern Orthodox Church and the Byzantine Catholic Churches. It is the purpose of human life in Orthodox theology: through purification (katharsis), illumination (theoria) and deification, the Christian comes to participate in the life of God "by grace" rather than by nature.

Teaching

The doctrine draws on 2 Peter 1:4, which speaks of believers becoming "partakers of the divine nature", and on the saying of Saint Athanasius of Alexandria: "For He was made man, that we men might be made gods." Orthodox theology insists, following Saint Gregory Palamas, that human beings are united to God's uncreated energies, not to his incomprehensible essence, so that the distinction between Creator and creature is preserved.

Practice

Theosis is pursued through the sacramental life of the Church, prayer — especially the Jesus Prayer of the hesychast tradition — fasting, almsgiving and the keeping of the commandments. Texts collected in The Philokalia map out the stages of this ascent, from the practice of the virtues to contemplation of the uncreated light of Mount Tabor.
