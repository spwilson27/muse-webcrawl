Jesus Prayer

A monk praying the Jesus Prayer with a prayer rope

The Jesus Prayer, also known as the Prayer of the Heart, is a short formulaic prayer esteemed and advocateded especially within the Eastern churches: "Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner." The prayer has been widely taught and discussed throughout the history of the Orthodox Church, and is often repeated continually as part of personal ascetic practice.

Origins

The prayer's origin is usually traced to the Egyptian desert Fathers, including Saint Macarius of Egypt and Evagrius Ponticus, and to the biblical plea of the publican, "God, be merciful to me a sinner" (Luke 18:13), and the cry of Bartimaeus, "Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me." The tradition of unceasing prayer follows Saint Paul's exhortation to "pray without ceasing" (1 Thessalonians 5:17).

Practice

Practitioners often use a prayer rope (komboskini in Greek, chotki in Russian) to count repetitions, and may coordinate the prayer with the rhythm of breathing. Within the hesychast tradition, the prayer is joined with techniques of posture and breath control intended to bring the mind into the heart. The Philokalia, an anthology of spiritual texts compiled by Saints Nicodemus the Hagiorite and Macarius of Corinth, is the classic guide to the practice.
