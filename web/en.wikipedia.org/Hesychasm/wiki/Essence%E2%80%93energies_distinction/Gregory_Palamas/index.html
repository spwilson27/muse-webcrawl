Gregory Palamas

Icon of Saint Gregory Palamas

Gregory Palamas (Greek: Γρηγόριος Παλαμᾶς; c. 1296 – 14 November 1359) was a Byzantine Greek theologian and philosopher of Mount Athos. A monk, archbishop of Thessalonica and participant in the hesychast controversy, he formulated the theology of hesychasm in response to the attacks of Barlaam of Calabria. His writings were defended by a series of councils in Constantinople between 1341 and 1351, and he was canonised a saint by the Eastern Orthodox Church in 1368.

Life

Born in Constantinople to an aristocratic family, Palamas was educated at the imperial court before embracing monastic life. He spent much of his life on Mount Athos, where he practised the contemplative prayer of the hesychasts and wrote in defence of their claim that the uncreated light seen in prayer is the same light that shone on Mount Tabor at the Transfiguration of Christ.

Theology

Palamas taught the distinction between God's essence (ousia), which remains utterly unknowable and imparticipable, and God's energies (energeiai), which are uncreated and truly divine, and through which human beings can participate in the divine life. This essence–energies distinction underlies the Orthodox doctrine of theosis, or deification. His principal works include the Triads in Defence of the Holy Hesychasts, the Hagioritic Tome and 150 chapters on theological topics.
