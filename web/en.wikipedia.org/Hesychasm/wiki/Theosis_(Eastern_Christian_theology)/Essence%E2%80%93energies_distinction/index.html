Essence–energies distinction

Essence and energies in Orthodox theology

In Orthodox theology, God is one, yet a real distinction is affirmed between his essence and his energies. The essence is the inner being of God, eternally beyond created comprehension; the energies — goodness, life, wisdom, deifying grace, the Tabor Light — are God himself turned toward the world, uncreated and fully divine.

History

The vocabulary was prepared by Basil the Great, Gregory of Nyssa, Dionysius the Areopagite and Maximus the Confessor, and was forged into a system by Gregory Palamas during the hesychast controversy. The Palamite councils of Constantinople canonised the teaching in the 14th century.

Implications

Without the distinction, Orthodox theology argues, deification would either become impossible (if only the essence were God) or pantheistic (if creatures shared the essence). With it, the saints can be said truly to become "gods by grace" while God remains the uncreated God by nature.
