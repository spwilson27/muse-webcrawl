Jesus Prayer

The Jesus Prayer, also known as the Prayer of the Heart, is a short formulaic prayer esteemed and advocated especially within the Eastern Orthodox and Byzantine Catholic Churches: "Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner." The prayer has been widely taught and discussed throughout the history of the Orthodox Church, and is often repeated continually as part of personal ascetic practice.

Origins and practice

The prayer's roots lie in the Desert Fathers of Egypt and the hesychast tradition. It is traditionally combined with breathing exercises and a prayer rope (komboskini), and is central to the spiritual method described in the Philokalia. Hesychasts taught that through disciplined repetition of the prayer, the mind descends into the heart and the practitioner may experience the uncreated light of God, the same light seen by the disciples at the Transfiguration on Mount Tabor.

Theology

The Jesus Prayer is closely connected with theosis (divinization), the process by which, according to Eastern Christian theology, a person becomes united with God through grace. The prayer is understood not as a mechanical technique but as a continual turning of the mind and heart toward Christ.
