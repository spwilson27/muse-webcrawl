Gregory Palamas

Saint Gregory Palamas, teacher of theosis

Saint Gregory Palamas (Γρηγόριος Παλαμᾶς; 1296–1359) was a monk of the Great Lavra and other Athonite houses, Archbishop of Thessalonica, and the saint of the Second Sunday of Lent. His defence of the monks who beheld the uncreated light in prayer triumphed at the councils of Constantinople and fixed the Orthodox understanding of theosis.

Life

After abandoning a promising court career for monasticism, Palamas lived in sketes at Veria and on Athos, was ordained priest, and was drawn into public controversy in 1337 by Barlaam's writings. He was imprisoned during the civil war of the 1340s, consecrated archbishop in 1347, captured by Turkish pirates in 1354, and died at Thessalonica in 1359.

Theology

His essence–energies distinction and his teaching that the light of Tabor is uncreated remain the touchstones of Orthodox dogmatics. The Philokalia preserves several of his treatises for later generations of monks and lay readers.
