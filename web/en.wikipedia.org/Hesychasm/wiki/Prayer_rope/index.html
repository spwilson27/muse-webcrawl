Prayer rope

A wool prayer rope (komboskini) with 100 knots

A prayer rope (Greek: κομποσκοίνι, komboskini; Russian: чётки, chotki; Romanian: mătănii) is a loop made of complex woven knots formed usually of wool or silk, used by Christians in the Eastern tradition to count the number of times they have prayed the Jesus Prayer or another short prayer.

Form

Traditional prayer ropes have 100 knots, though ropes of 33 (for the years of Christ's earthly life), 50, 150 or 300 knots are also made; each knot is woven from seven overlapping crosses. A cross of beads or a tassel usually marks the join. Monastics receive a prayer rope at their tonsure, together with the charge to pray unceasingly.

Use

The rope is not an object of superstition but a practical counter: the fingers move from knot to knot as the Jesus Prayer is repeated, keeping the mind from wandering over numbers. Variations include the Serbian brojanica of 33 knots and the leather lestovka used by Russian Old Believers.
