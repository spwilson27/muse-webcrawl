Eastern Orthodox Church

The Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople

The Eastern Orthodox Church, officially the Orthodox Catholic Church, is the second-largest Christian church, with approximately 220 million baptised members. It operates as a communion of autocephalous churches, each governed by its bishops in local synods. The church has no central doctrinal or governmental authority analogous to the head of the Roman Catholic Church, the pope; nevertheless, the Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople is recognised by them as primus inter pares ("first among equals").

History

The Eastern Orthodox Church traces its history to the earliest Church established by Jesus Christ and the Apostles. The Great Schism of 1054 divided the Christian Church into the Western (Latin) Church, now the Roman Catholic Church, and the Eastern (Greek) Church, now the Eastern Orthodox Church. Disputes behind the schism included the source of the Holy Spirit (Filioque), whether leavened or unleavened bread should be used in the Eucharist, and the pope's claim to universal jurisdiction.

Theology

Orthodox theology is based on Holy Tradition, which includes the dogmatic decrees of the Seven Ecumenical Councils, the Scriptures, and the teaching of the Church Fathers. The Church teaches that salvation is a process of theosis, or deification, in which the believer is united with the uncreated energies of God. Hesychasm, a tradition of contemplative prayer centred on the repetition of the Jesus Prayer, was defended by Saint Gregory Palamas in the 14th century and remains central to Orthodox spirituality.

Structure

The communion is organised around national and regional autocephalous churches, including the patriarchates of Constantinople, Alexandria, Antioch and Jerusalem, and the churches of Russia, Serbia, Romania, Bulgaria, Georgia and Greece, among others. Its worship centres on the Divine Liturgy, celebrated in a variety of liturgical languages.
