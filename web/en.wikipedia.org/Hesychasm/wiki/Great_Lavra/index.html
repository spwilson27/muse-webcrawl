Great Lavra

The Monastery of Great Lavra on Mount Athos

The Monastery of Great Lavra (Greek: Μονή Μεγίστης Λαύρας) is the first monastery built on Mount Athos, in northeastern Greece. It is located on the southeastern foot of the mountain, at an elevation of 160 metres. Founded in 963 AD by Saint Athanasius the Athonite, it is ranked first in the Athonite hierarchy and is dedicated to the Dormition of the Theotokos, though its feast day honours its founder on 5 July.

History

The construction of the Lavra, which marked the beginning of organised monastic life on the Holy Mountain, was funded by the Byzantine emperor Nikephoros II Phokas, a friend of Athanasius. The monastery's katholikon is regarded as the prototype of Athonite church architecture, and its library holds thousands of manuscripts, including the Codex Tischendorfianus. Athanasius, who was killed by falling masonry during construction work in 1000, is buried there.

Treasures

The treasury of the Great Lavra preserves celebrated relics and icons, including the icon of the Panagia Koukouzelissa, and one of the largest collections of Byzantine manuscripts and printed books on Mount Athos.
