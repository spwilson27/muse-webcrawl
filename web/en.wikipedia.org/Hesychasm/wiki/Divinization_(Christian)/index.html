Divinization (Christian theology)

In Christian theology, divinization, or theosis (Greek: θέωσις), is the transformative process by which a believer is made partaker of the divine nature (cf. 2 Peter 1:4). It is a central theme of Eastern Orthodox theology and is also found, in different formulations, in Western Christian thought.

Teaching

The doctrine was summarised by Athanasius of Alexandria in the formula "He became man, that we men might become gods". In the Orthodox tradition, deification is understood as participation in the uncreated energies of God rather than in the divine essence, a distinction defended by Gregory Palamas in the hesychast controversy of the 14th century.

Divinization is described as the purpose of human life, attained through grace, the sacraments, ascetic practice and prayer — notably the Jesus Prayer cultivated by the hesychasts. Western traditions express related ideas in terms of sanctifying grace and the beatific vision, and the concept has received renewed attention in modern ecumenical theology.
