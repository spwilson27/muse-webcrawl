Nicodemus the Hagiorite

Icon of Saint Nicodemus the Hagiorite

Nicodemus the Hagiorite or Nicodemus of the Holy Mountain (Greek: Νικόδημος ὁ Ἁγιορείτης; 1749 – 14 July 1809) was a Greek monk, theologian and ascetic writer of Mount Athos. He is best known as co-editor, with Macarius of Corinth, of The Philokalia (1782) and as author of the Exomologetarion and other guides to the spiritual life. He was canonised by the Ecumenical Patriarchate in 1955.

Life

Born Nikolaos Kallivourtzis on the island of Naxos, he studied at Smyrna before becoming a monk on Mount Athos in 1775, where he took the name Nicodemus. Apart from a period of exile connected with the Kollyvades controversy — the dispute over memorial services and frequent communion — he spent his life writing, editing and giving spiritual counsel on the Holy Mountain.

Works

Besides the Philokalia, Nicodemus compiled the Evergetinos, edited the canons of the Church in the Pedalion ("Rudder"), and adapted Western works such as Lorenzo Scupoli's Spiritual Combat (as Unseen Warfare) for Orthodox readers. His defence of frequent communion and of the hesychast tradition shaped the modern Orthodox revival.
