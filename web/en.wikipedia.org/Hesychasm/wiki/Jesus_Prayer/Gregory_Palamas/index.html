Gregory Palamas

Saint Gregory Palamas, Archbishop of Thessalonica

Gregory Palamas (c. 1296 – 1359) was a monk of Mount Athos, later Archbishop of Thessalonica, whose defence of hesychasm defined Orthodox teaching on prayer and deification. Opposed by Barlaam of Calabria, who mocked the Athonite monks as omphalopsychoi ("men with their souls in their navels"), Palamas argued that the light experienced in prayer is uncreated and divine.

The controversy

Between 1341 and 1351, a series of councils in Constantinople upheld Palamas's teaching, condemning Barlaam and later Gregory Akindynos. The Hagioritic Tome, signed by the monks of Mount Athos, and the Synodal Tome of 1351 summarised the victorious position: God is imparticipable according to his essence, but participable according to his uncreated energies.

Legacy

Palamas was glorified as a saint in 1368, less than a decade after his death, and his feast is kept on 14 November and on the Second Sunday of Great Lent. His theology, long contested by Western critics, was reaffirmed by modern Orthodox theologians as the authentic expression of the patristic tradition.
