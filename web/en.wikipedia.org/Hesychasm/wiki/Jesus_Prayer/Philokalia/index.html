Philokalia

Title page of the first edition of the Philokalia (1782)

The Philokalia (Greek: φιλοκαλία, "the love of the beautiful") is a collection of texts written between the 4th and 15th centuries by spiritual masters of the Eastern Orthodox hesychast tradition. It was compiled by Saints Nicodemus the Hagiorite and Macarius of Corinth and first published at Venice in 1782.

Contents

The anthology gathers writings of more than thirty authors, including Anthony the Great, Evagrius Ponticus, John Cassian, Maximus the Confessor, Symeon the New Theologian, Gregory of Sinai and Gregory Palamas. Themes include watchfulness (nepsis), the warfare against the thoughts (logismoi), the Jesus Prayer and the vision of the uncreated light.

Translations and influence

The Slavonic translation by Paisius Velichkovsky, the Dobrotolublye (1793), and the expanded Russian edition of Theophan the Recluse carried the Philokalia's teaching across the Orthodox world. In the 20th century, the English translation begun by G. E. H. Palmer, Philip Sherrard and Kallistos Ware introduced it to a wide Western readership.
