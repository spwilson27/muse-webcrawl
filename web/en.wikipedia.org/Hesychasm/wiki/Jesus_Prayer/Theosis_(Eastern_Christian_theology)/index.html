Theosis (Eastern Christian theology)

The deification of the saints in the uncreated light

In Eastern Christian theology, theosis denotes the process by which a Christian becomes "partaker of the divine nature" (2 Peter 1:4). It is the goal of the spiritual life and the meaning of salvation: not merely forgiveness or moral improvement, but real union with God in his energies, while his essence remains forever beyond creaturely comprehension.

Historical development

The idea runs from Irenaeus and Athanasius — "He was made man, that we men might be made gods" — through the Cappadocians and Maximus the Confessor, to Symeon the New Theologian and Gregory Palamas, who grounded it in the essence–energies distinction and in the experience of the uncreated light of Tabor.

Means

Deification is a gift of grace received through the sacraments, above all baptism and the Eucharist, and cultivated by prayer, fasting and love of neighbour. The ascetic texts of The Philokalia serve as its standard guidebook.
