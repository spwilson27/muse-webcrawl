Mount Athos

The Holy Mountain from the sea

Mount Athos, the Holy Mountain of Orthodox monasticism, occupies the easternmost finger of the Chalkidiki peninsula in Greek Macedonia. Its twenty ruling monasteries — headed in rank by the Great Lavra — together with sketes and hermitages, house some two thousand monks. It has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1988.

Hesychasm on Athos

The mountain was the heartland of the hesychast movement: Gregory of Sinai established its methods there, Gregory Palamas defended them, and the Hagioritic Tome of 1340 was signed by the Athonite monks. In the 18th century Nicodemus the Hagiorite and his circle compiled The Philokalia from Athonite manuscripts, sparking a renewal of the prayer of the heart.

Governance

Athos is self-governed by the Holy Community of the twenty monasteries under the spiritual jurisdiction of the Ecumenical Patriarchate, with its capital at Karyes. Entry requires a special permit (diamonitirion), and the ancient avaton bars women from the peninsula.
