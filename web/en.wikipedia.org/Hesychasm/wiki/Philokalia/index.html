Philokalia

The Philokalia (Greek: φιλοκαλία, "love of the beautiful") is a collection of texts written between the 4th and 15th centuries by spiritual masters of the Eastern Orthodox hesychast tradition. Subtitled in full "The Philokalia on Prayer of the Heart," it was compiled by St Nicodemus the Hagiorite and St Macarius of Corinth and first published in Greek in Venice in 1782.

Contents

The Philokalia gathers writings of 36 authors, including Anthony the Great, Evagrius Ponticus, John Cassian, Maximus the Confessor, Symeon the New Theologian, Gregory of Sinai, and Gregory Palamas. The texts teach guarding of the heart (nepsis, watchfulness), the struggle against the passionate thoughts (logismoi), stillness (hesychia), and the practice of the Jesus Prayer, all aimed at purification, illumination, and ultimately deification (theosis). The editors' introduction presents the collection as a manual for both monks and laypeople seeking unceasing prayer.

Influence

A Slavonic translation, the Dobrotolublye, was published by St Paisius Velichkovsky in 1793, followed by a Russian version overseen by Theophan the Recluse (1876–1889); these powered a spiritual revival across the Slavic Orthodox world, feeding the startsy (elders) tradition of Optina Monastery and inspiring the anonymous classic The Way of a Pilgrim. In the 20th century a multi-volume English translation was produced by G. E. H. Palmer, Philip Sherrard, and Metropolitan Kallistos Ware. The Philokalia is credited with renewing hesychast spirituality, shaping modern Orthodox theology, and introducing many Western readers to Eastern Christian contemplative practice.

The term

"Philokalia" — love of the good and beautiful — had previously been used for an anthology of Origen's writings compiled by Basil the Great and Gregory of Nazianzus (the Philokalia of Origen), a different work from the 1782 collection.
