Jesus Prayer

Praying the Jesus Prayer

The Jesus Prayer — "Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner" — is the characteristic prayer of the hesychast tradition documented in The Philokalia. Those who practise it seek the "prayer of the heart", in which the invocation becomes ceaseless and self-acting, continuing even during sleep.

History

From the Egyptian desert through Saint John Climacus and the Byzantine monasteries, short invocations of the name of Jesus were joined to the command to pray without ceasing. Saint Gregory of Sinai revived the practice on Mount Athos in the 14th century, and Gregory Palamas defended its theological basis.

Practice today

The prayer is prayed by monastics and laypeople alike, usually with a prayer rope of 33, 50, 100 or 300 knots, under the guidance of a spiritual father or mother (geron/gerontissa). Its modern popularity in the West owes much to The Way of a Pilgrim and to translations of the Philokalia.
