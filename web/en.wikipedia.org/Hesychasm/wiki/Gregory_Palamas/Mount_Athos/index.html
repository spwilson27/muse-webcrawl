Mount Athos

Mount Athos and its monasteries

Mount Athos (Greek: Ἄθως) is a mountain on the Athos peninsula in northeastern Greece, an important centre of Eastern Orthodox monasticism for over a thousand years. The peninsula, often called the "Holy Mountain" (Greek: Ἅγιον Ὄρος), is home to 20 monasteries under the direct jurisdiction of the Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople and forms an autonomous region of the Greek state governed by the Holy Community.

Monastic life

Monastic presence on Athos dates to the early Byzantine centuries; the Great Lavra, the first great monastery, was founded by Saint Athanasius the Athonite in 963. The mountain is famed for its hesychast tradition: Saint Gregory Palamas lived and taught there, and the compilation The Philokalia was prepared by monks associated with its monasteries. By a tradition maintained since Byzantine times, women are not permitted to enter the peninsula (the avaton).

Geography

The peninsula is some 50 km long and 7–12 km wide, rising to the marble peak of Mount Athos at 2,033 metres. Its monasteries preserve major collections of medieval art, icons and manuscripts.
