Theosis (Eastern Christian theology)

Icon of the Transfiguration, feast of the Tabor Light

Theosis, or deification, is the doctrine that salvation consists in the human person's participation in the life of God. In the words traditionally ascribed to Saint Athanasius, the Word "became man that we might become god". The doctrine is defined by the essence–energies distinction of Saint Gregory Palamas: believers are deified by the uncreated energies of God, never by his essence.

Stages

Spiritual writers describe three stages: purification (katharsis) from the passions through ascetic struggle; illumination (theoria), in which the purified intellect beholds the glory of God; and deification (theosis) itself, a permanent state of union, exemplified by the apostles' vision of the uncreated Tabor Light at the Transfiguration.

Texts

The classic exposition is found in the writings of the Church Fathers collected in The Philokalia, in the works of Saint Maximus the Confessor, Saint Symeon the New Theologian and Saint Gregory Palamas, and in the liturgical texts of the Orthodox Church.
