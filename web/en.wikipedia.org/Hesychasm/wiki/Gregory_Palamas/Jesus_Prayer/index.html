Jesus Prayer

A prayer rope (komboskini) used with the Jesus Prayer

The Jesus Prayer is a short invocation — "Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner" — repeated as the centre of the hesychast tradition of prayer. Rooted in the cries for mercy in the Gospels and in the desert Fathers' pursuit of unceasing prayer, it became the classic form of prayer in the Eastern Orthodox world.

Method

Guides in The Philokalia, such as Saints Nicephorus the Hesychast and Gregory of Sinai, describe joining the prayer to the breathing: the first half is spoken mentally on the in-breath, the second on the out-breath, as the mind is gathered into the heart. A prayer rope is commonly used to keep count of repetitions.

Influence

The anonymous 19th-century Russian work The Way of a Pilgrim popularised the prayer in the West, describing a wanderer's search for instruction in praying without ceasing. The prayer is also used, in adapted forms, by Eastern Catholics and some Anglicans and other Christians.
