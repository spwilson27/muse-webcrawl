Noodle soup

TypeSoup
Main ingredientsNoodles, broth, vegetables, meat or seafood
OriginEast Asia (earliest attested in China)
VariationsRamen, phở, laksa, chicken noodle soup, wonton soup

Noodle soup refers to a variety of soups with noodles and other ingredients served in a light broth. Noodle soup is a common dish across East Asia, Southeast Asia and, in Western adaptations such as chicken noodle soup, North America and Europe. Styles range from the wheat-noodle broths of northern China to Japanese ramen, Vietnamese phở, Malaysian laksa and Korean kal-guksu.

History

The world's oldest known noodles, dating to roughly 4,000 years ago, were found at the Lajia archaeological site in Qinghai, China, preserved in an overturned bowl; they were made from millet flours. By the Han dynasty, noodle dishes in broth were established in China, and the format spread along trade routes to Korea, Japan and Southeast Asia, where local grains, broths and seasonings produced distinct regional traditions.

Regional varieties

China. Lanzhou beef noodle soup, with hand-pulled wheat noodles in a spiced beef broth, is among the best-known styles; others include wonton soup, hot dry noodles served without broth in Wuhan, and crossing-the-bridge noodles from Yunnan.

Japan. Ramen — wheat noodles in a rich broth, regional variants including Sapporo miso, Kitakata shōyu and Hakata tonkotsu — became a national dish in the twentieth century, alongside the older soba (buckwheat) and udon (thick wheat) noodle soups.

Vietnam. Phở, a rice-noodle soup in a clear, star-anise-scented beef or chicken broth, emerged in the early twentieth century in northern Vietnam and became the country's emblematic dish.

Southeast Asia. Laksa combines rice noodles with a spicy coconut-milk or tamarind-based broth in Malaysia and Singapore; Thailand's boat noodles and khao soi are further examples.

Western adaptations

Chicken noodle soup, made with egg noodles and chicken in broth, is a staple comfort food in the United States and is popularly associated with home remedies for the common cold. Canned condensed versions were commercialised in the twentieth century.
