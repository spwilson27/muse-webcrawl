PayPal Holdings, Inc. is an American multinational financial technology company operating an online payments system in most countries that support online money transfers. PayPal allows users to send and receive money electronically, pay merchants, and hold balances in a digital wallet, serving as an electronic alternative to checks and money orders.

History

PayPal began as Confinity, founded in 1998 by Max Levchin, Peter Thiel, and Luke Nosek to develop payment software for handheld devices. It merged in 2000 with X.com, the online banking company founded by Elon Musk, and took the PayPal name in 2001. The service grew rapidly as the preferred payment method on the auction site eBay.

PayPal went public in 2002 and was acquired by eBay the same year for $1.5 billion. It expanded through acquisitions including Braintree and its peer-to-peer app Venmo (2013). In 2015 eBay spun PayPal off as an independent public company. A group of early PayPal employees and investors — including Thiel, Musk, Reid Hoffman, and YouTube's founders — became known as the "PayPal Mafia" for their later influence in Silicon Valley.

Services

PayPal offers consumer and merchant payment processing in over 200 markets, along with Venmo, PayPal Credit, the cashback browser extension Honey, and the stablecoin PayPal USD (launched 2023). It has faced regulatory scrutiny over account freezes, fees, and its role in online fraud.
