Gauss–Codazzi equations

The Gauss–Codazzi equations are fundamental equations of the differential geometry of surfaces in three-dimensional Euclidean space. They are compatibility conditions relating a surface's first and second fundamental forms: the Gauss equation connects intrinsic curvature to the second fundamental form, while the Codazzi equations constrain how the second fundamental form varies from point to point.

Meaning

Together with the fundamental theorem of surface theory, the equations say that a metric and a symmetric bilinear form satisfying the Gauss and Codazzi equations determine, locally and uniquely up to rigid motion, a surface in R³ having them as its first and second fundamental forms.

Gauss equation

In classical notation with fundamental-form coefficients E, F, G and L, M, N, the Gauss equation expresses Gaussian curvature as K = (LN − M²) / (EG − F²), showing Gauss's remarkable theorem that curvature is intrinsic to the metric.

Generalisations

For hypersurfaces in Riemannian manifolds the corresponding Gauss and Codazzi equations include ambient curvature terms. In general relativity, analogous relations underpin the ADM formulation by linking the geometry of a spatial slice to spacetime curvature.
