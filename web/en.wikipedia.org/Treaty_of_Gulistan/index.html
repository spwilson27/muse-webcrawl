Treaty of Gulistan

The treaty transferred most of Persia's Caucasian khanates to the Russian Empire.

PartiesRussian Empire; Qajar Persia

The Treaty of Gulistan (Russian: Гюлистанский договор; Persian: عهدنامه گلستان) was a peace treaty concluded between the Russian Empire and Qajar Persia (Iran) on 24 October 1813 in the village of Gulistan in Karabakh, ending the Russo-Persian War of 1804–1813. The Persian side, represented by Mirza Abolhassan Khan Ilchi, negotiated after the Russian storming of Lankaran by General Pyotr Kotlyarevsky; Britain's envoy Sir Gore Ouseley mediated.

Terms

By the treaty, Persia ceded to Russia the bulk of its territories in the South Caucasus: the khanates of Karabakh, Ganja, Shirvan, Shaki, Quba, Derbent and Baku — covering most of modern Azerbaijan — together with parts of Dagestan and eastern Georgia, which Russia had already annexed in 1801. Russia alone was permitted to maintain a war fleet on the Caspian Sea, and the two empires regulated commerce and the status of merchants. The treaty also vaguely acknowledged Russia's right to be consulted in the Persian succession, recognising Abbas Mirza as heir apparent — a clause Russia later used to intervene in Persian affairs.

Aftermath

The treaty left the frontier between the two empires ill-defined in places, particularly around Talysh, and humiliated the Qajar court. Persian attempts to recover the lost provinces with British encouragement led to a second war in 1826; Russia's victory produced the still harsher Treaty of Turkmenchay (1828), which fixed the Aras River boundary that survives today between Iran and Azerbaijan/Armenia. In Iran, the two treaties remain symbols of Qajar weakness and foreign encroachment.
