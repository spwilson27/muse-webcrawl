The Books of Samuel (Hebrew: Sefer Shmuel) are two books of the Hebrew Bible and the Christian Old Testament, forming a single book in the Jewish tradition. They narrate the transition of Israel from the period of the judges to the monarchy: the life of the prophet Samuel, the reign of Saul, and the reign of David.

Contents

First Samuel opens with Samuel's birth and his call at Shiloh, describes the capture and return of the Ark of the Covenant, and tells how Israel demanded a king. Saul is anointed, wins early victories, but is rejected after disobedience. David is anointed in secret, defeats Goliath, and flees Saul's jealousy until Saul falls at Mount Gilboa.

Second Samuel follows David's rise: king first in Hebron, then over all Israel, capturing Jerusalem and bringing up the Ark. It recounts his sin with Bathsheba, the rebellion of Absalom, and ends with appendices — songs, a list of the "mighty men", and the census plague.

Composition

Scholars see the books as part of the Deuteronomistic History, edited during and after the Babylonian exile from older sources, including what is sometimes called the "Succession Narrative", a remarkably frank account of David's court.
