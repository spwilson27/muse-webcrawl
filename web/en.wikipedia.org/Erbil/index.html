Erbil (Kurdish: هەولێر, Hawler; Arabic: أربيل, Arbīl; ancient Arbela) is the capital and most populous city of the Kurdistan Region of Iraq, and the seat of the Kurdistan Regional Government. Its citadel, a fortified settlement on an artificial mound, is claimed to be among the oldest continuously inhabited places on Earth, with occupation stretching back at least 6,000 years.

History

Erbil appears in Sumerian and Akkadian records as Urbilum/Arba-ilu, a city sacred to the goddess Ishtar. In 331 BC, Darius III of Persia's army was defeated by Alexander the Great at Gaugamela, on the plain northwest of the city; the battle is conventionally named after Arbela. The city was an early centre of East Syriac Christianity and the capital of the small kingdom of Adiabene, whose rulers converted to Judaism in the first century AD. Erbil came under Muslim rule in the seventh century and later passed between Seljuk, Mongol, Ottoman, and other powers. In the twentieth century it became part of the Kingdom of Iraq and then the Republic of Iraq; since 1970 it has been the capital of the Kurdish autonomous region, a status consolidated after 1991 and 2003.

The citadel

The Citadel of Erbil sits on a tell rising 25–32 metres above the surrounding plain. Its roughly 430-by-340-metre oval contains the Grand Mosque, the Kurdish Textile Museum, and traditional courtyard houses, and was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2014. Around its foot lies the old bazaar quarter, including the covered Qaysari Bazaar.

Modern city

Since 2003 Erbil has grown rapidly, attracting foreign investment, consulates, and a large expatriate and displaced population, while hosting many people fleeing conflicts elsewhere in Iraq and Syria. The city is served by Erbil International Airport and contains the Kurdistan Parliament, ministries of the Kurdistan Regional Government, and Salahaddin University, founded in 1968.
