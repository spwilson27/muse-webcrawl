Food bank

A food bank is a non-profit, charitable organisation that distributes food to those who have difficulty purchasing enough to avoid hunger, typically through intermediary organisations such as food pantries, soup kitchens and shelters. Food banks generally operate warehouses that collect, store and distribute large quantities of donated food from manufacturers, retailers, farmers, government programmes and public food drives.

History

The first food bank is widely credited to John van Hengel, who established St. Mary's Food Bank in Phoenix, Arizona, in 1967, after volunteering at a soup kitchen and observing how much edible food grocery stores discarded. The model spread across the United States and abroad; Feeding America, founded in 1979, is now a network of more than 200 food banks. In the United Kingdom, food banks expanded rapidly after 2010, with the Trussell Trust operating a large national network.

Operations

Food banks depend heavily on volunteers and on surplus food that would otherwise go to waste, giving them a dual role in hunger relief and food-waste reduction. Distribution models vary: some supply client-choice pantries, others prepare parcels against referrals from social workers, teachers or health professionals. Critics argue that food banks, while necessary, are an inadequate substitute for income-based solutions to poverty, and usage statistics are often cited in debates about welfare policy.
