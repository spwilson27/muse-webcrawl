Charaka Samhita

The Charaka Samhita (Sanskrit: Caraka Saṃhitā, "Charaka's Compendium") is an early Sanskrit text on Ayurveda (Indian traditional medicine), and one of the two foundational texts of the field, alongside the Sushruta Samhita. Traditionally attributed to the sage Charaka, it is a redaction of the still older Agnivesha Samhita; its layers are dated by scholars to between the 1st century BCE and the 2nd century CE, with revisions and completions by later editors such as Dridhabala.

Contents

The text contains 120 chapters organised into eight books (sthanas): general principles (Sutra), diagnostics (Nidana), specific features (Vimana), the body (Sharira), prognosis (Indriya), therapeutics (Chikitsa), pharmacy (Kalpa), and successful management (Siddhi). It presents the theory of the three doshas (vata, pitta, kapha), digestion and metabolism (agni), causes of disease, and detailed regimens of diet, lifestyle, and herbal treatment.

significance and influence

The Charaka Samhita is noted for its systematic, rational approach — emphasising observation, reasoning, and debate among physicians — and for early discussions of medical ethics, the training of physicians, and preventive medicine through daily and seasonal routines (dinacharya and ritucharya). It remains a core reference in Ayurvedic education and practice today.

Commentaries and translations

The most famous commentary is Chakrapani Datta's 11th-century Ayurveda Dipika. The text has been translated into English several times and was historically transmitted, in adapted forms, into Persian and Arabic medical traditions.
