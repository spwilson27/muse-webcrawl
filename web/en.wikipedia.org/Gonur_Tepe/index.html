Gonur Tepe


Excavated walls at Gonur Tepe.

Gonur Tepe is a major archaeological site in the Karakum Desert of Turkmenistan, near the ancient delta of the Murghab River. It was the principal centre of the Bronze Age Bactria–Margiana Archaeological Complex (BMAC), sometimes called the "Oxus Civilisation", dating to roughly 2400–1600 BCE.

Excavations

The site was excavated from the 1970s by the Soviet and later Russian-Turkmen archaeologist Viktor Sarianidi. Covering some 55 hectares, Gonur includes a palace-temple complex surrounded by thick walls with towers, ritual structures with fire altars and basins, administrative buildings, and extensive necropolises.

Findings

Graves yielded gold and silver vessels, bronze tools, carved stone seals and mosaics, indicating a wealthy society engaged in long-distance trade with Mesopotamia, Elam and the Indus Valley. Sarianidi argued that Gonur was an early centre of Zoroastrian practice and of the ritual drink soma/haoma, pointing to vessels with traces interpreted as ephedra preparations — claims that remain debated among scholars. The city declined as the Murghab delta shifted and water supplies failed.
