Staple food

A staple food, or simply a staple, is a food that is eaten routinely and in such quantities that it constitutes a dominant portion of a standard diet for a given population, supplying a large fraction of energy needs.

Major staples

Most staples are plant-derived cereal grains and legumes or root vegetables. Rice feeds more people than any other crop, followed by maize (corn) and wheat; together with potatoes, cassava, soybeans, sweet potatoes and sorghum they supply the bulk of the world's food energy.

Characteristics

Staples tend to be inexpensive, widely available, storable, and suited to local climates. Dependence on a narrow range of staples can, however, create nutritional deficiencies and vulnerability to crop failure, famously illustrated by the Irish potato famine.
