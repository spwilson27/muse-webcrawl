Witold Roman Lutosławski (25 January 1913 – 7 February 1994) was a Polish composer and conductor. Among the major composers of 20th-century classical music, he is known for works that combine folk influences and modernist technique with passages of controlled aleatorism, in which performers have limited freedom in timing.

Life

Lutosławski was born in Warsaw into a musical family; his father was executed by the Bolsheviks in 1918. He studied piano and composition at the Warsaw Conservatory, graduating in 1937. During the Second World War he played piano duos in Warsaw cafés with Andrzej Panufnik. In the Stalinist era his First Symphony was denounced as "formalist" and banned; he supported himself with functional music, including pieces based on Polish folk tunes.

Music

His international breakthrough came with the Concerto for Orchestra (1954). Later works developed his distinctive techniques: the twelve-note harmonic aggregates and "limited aleatorism" of Jeux vénitiens (1961), the String Quartet (1964), the Cello Concerto (1970, written for Mstislav Rostropovich), and four symphonies, the Fourth completed in 1992. His music balances rigorous architecture with expressive drama.

Legacy

Lutosławski received numerous honours, including the Order of the White Eagle in 1994, and he conducted his own works with leading orchestras worldwide. He is regarded as the most significant Polish composer since Chopin.
