Tagish Lake (meteorite)

TypeCarbonaceous chondrite (C2, ungrouped)
Fall date18 January 2000
LocationTagish Lake, Yukon and British Columbia, Canada
Recovered massAbout 10 kg

The Tagish Lake meteorite fell at approximately 8:43 a.m. PST on 18 January 2000 in the Tagish Lake area of far northern British Columbia and Yukon, Canada. A bright fireball was observed across the region, and more than 500 fragments were later recovered from the frozen surface of the lake. The meteorite is a rare, primitive carbonaceous chondrite containing exceptionally well-preserved organic material and presolar grains.

A fragment of the Tagish Lake meteorite recovered from the frozen lake surface

The fall and recovery

The meteoroid, estimated at roughly 200 tonnes before atmospheric entry, exploded in an airburst at an altitude of about 30–50 km, scattering fragments over a large area. Local resident Jim Brook collected many fragments from the lake ice a week later, keeping them frozen and untouched by bare hands—an unusually pristine recovery that minimized contamination. Additional searches in the spring of 2000 recovered further specimens for scientific study.

Composition and significance

Tagish Lake is classified as a C2 carbonaceous chondrite unlike any previously known group, rich in carbonate minerals and organic carbon. Analyses revealed amino acids, polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons, and unusual carbon-rich globules whose isotopic composition suggests formation in cold interstellar environments. Because of its primitive composition, the meteorite is thought to sample material from the early Solar System, possibly from a comet-like parent body or a D-type asteroid, and it has been compared with samples returned from the asteroids Ryugu and Bennu.
