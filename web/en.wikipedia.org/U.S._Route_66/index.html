U.S. Route 66

Lengthabout 2,400 miles (3,940 km)
Existed1926–1985
EndpointsChicago, Illinois – Santa Monica, California

U.S. Route 66, also known as the Main Street of America and the Mother Road, was one of the original highways in the United States Numbered Highway System. Established on 11 November 1926, it ran from Chicago, Illinois, through Missouri, Kansas, Oklahoma, Texas, New Mexico and Arizona to Santa Monica, California.

Cultural significance

Route 66 carried Dust Bowl migrants westward in the 1930s — immortalised in John Steinbeck's The Grapes of Wrath — and later became an icon of postwar car culture, celebrated in the song "(Get Your Kicks on) Route 66", the television series Route 66 and thousands of roadside motels, diners and attractions.

Decommissioning

Superseded by the Interstate Highway System, the route was officially removed from the U.S. Highway System in 1985, but preservation efforts and "Historic Route 66" signage have kept much of the road drivable and famous worldwide.
