Ministry of Railways (China)

Agency overviewMinistry of Railways of the People's Republic of China
Formed1949
DissolvedMarch 2013
Superseding agenciesNational Railway Administration; China Railway Corporation
HeadquartersBeijing

The Ministry of Railways (Chinese: 铁道部) was a former department of the State Council of the People's Republic of China responsible for passenger services, regulation of the rail industry, and the development and maintenance of railway infrastructure. It was long one of the most powerful ministries in the Chinese government, operating both as regulator and as the operator of nearly the entire national rail network through its network of regional railway bureaus.

The ministry oversaw one of the world's largest railway systems, including the rapid expansion of China's high-speed rail network during the 2000s, when thousands of kilometres of dedicated passenger lines were built. At its height it employed roughly two million people.

Dissolution

In March 2013, as part of a broader restructuring of the State Council approved by the National People's Congress, the Ministry of Railways was dissolved. Its administrative and regulatory functions were transferred to a newly created National Railway Administration under the Ministry of Transport, while its commercial operations were spun off into a state-owned company, the China Railway Corporation (later China State Railway Group). The split was intended to separate government administration from enterprise management and to address longstanding concerns about the ministry's debt and governance.
