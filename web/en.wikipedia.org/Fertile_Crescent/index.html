Fertile Crescent

The Fertile Crescent, from the Nile valley to Mesopotamia

The Fertile Crescent is a crescent-shaped region in Western Asia comprising the Levant, ancient Mesopotamia and (in some definitions) the Nile valley of Egypt. Spanning modern-day Iraq, Kuwait, Syria, Lebanon, Israel, Palestine, Jordan, Türkiye and Iran, it is often called the "cradle of civilization". The term was popularised by archaeologist James Henry Breasted in the early 20th century.

Birthplace of agriculture

The region was home to some of the earliest human civilisations and saw the Neolithic Revolution: around 10,000 BC, its inhabitants were among the first in the world to domesticate plants such as wheat, barley, peas and lentils, and animals including sheep, goats, cattle and pigs.

Civilizations

The river valleys of the Tigris, Euphrates and Nile supported the rise of Sumer, Akkad, Babylon, Assyria and ancient Egypt, and inventions attributed to the region include writing, the wheel, irrigation and early law codes.
