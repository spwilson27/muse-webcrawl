Janis Lyn Joplin (19 January 1943 – 4 October 1970) was an American singer and songwriter and one of the most successful and widely known rock stars of her era. Renowned for her powerful, raspy mezzo-soprano voice and electric stage presence, she was dubbed the "Queen of Psychedelic Soul" and remains an icon of the 1960s counterculture.

Early life and breakthrough

Joplin grew up in the Texas oil town of Port Arthur, an outsider drawn to blues singers such as Bessie Smith, Lead Belly, and Big Mama Thornton. After drifting between Texas and San Francisco, in 1966 she joined the San Francisco psychedelic band Big Brother and the Holding Company. Her show-stealing performance of "Ball and Chain" at the Monterey Pop Festival in June 1967 made her famous overnight. The band's album Cheap Thrills (1968), with "Piece of My Heart", topped the charts.

Solo career

Joplin left Big Brother for a solo career backed first by the Kozmic Blues Band (I Got Dem Ol' Kozmic Blues Again Mama!, 1969) and then by the Canadian Full Tilt Boogie Band, with whom she toured on the Festival Express train across Canada in 1970. She performed at Woodstock in 1969. Her voice, lifestyle, and struggles with alcohol and heroin addiction were intertwined in public perception throughout her career.

Death and legacy

On 4 October 1970 Joplin died of a heroin overdose in a Hollywood hotel room, aged 27 — sixteen days after the death of Jimi Hendrix, and later a founding entry in the so-called "27 Club". Her final album Pearl, released posthumously in 1971, became her biggest seller, and its Kris Kristofferson song "Me and Bobby McGee" gave her a posthumous number-one single. She was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1995 and received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2005.
