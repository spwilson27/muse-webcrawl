Hans Maass (17 June 1911 – 15 April 1992) was a German mathematician best known for introducing Maass forms (also called Maass wave forms), important objects in the theory of modular forms and automorphic forms.

Life

Maass studied mathematics at the University of Hamburg, where he was influenced by Erich Hecke and Emil Artin. He completed his doctorate in 1937. After the Second World War he took up a professorship at Heidelberg University, where he remained for the rest of his career.

Work

In his 1949 paper on non-analytic automorphic forms, Maass introduced what are now called Maass forms: real-analytic eigenfunctions of the hyperbolic Laplacian on modular domains that transform like modular forms. He also made contributions to the theory of Siegel modular forms, theta series, and zeta functions of prehomogeneous vector spaces, including the Maass–Selberg relations developed with Atle Selberg.

Selected works

His books include Lectures on Siegel's Modular Functions and works on modular forms and Dirichlet series, which influenced generations of number theorists.
