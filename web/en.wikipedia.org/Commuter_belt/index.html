Commuter belt
Also called: metropolitan belt, commuterland
Examples: London commuter belt; New York metropolitan area suburbs
Related concepts: suburb, exurb, dormitory town

A commuter belt is the area surrounding a city from which large numbers of residents travel daily to work in the city centre. It typically consists of suburbs, dormitory towns and semi-rural settlements linked to the core by rail and road corridors.

Characteristics

Commuter belts grow where fast transport makes a daily journey feasible while housing is cheaper or more spacious than in the centre. Residents rely on the core city for employment and services while contributing little daytime population to their home towns.

Examples

The London commuter belt extends across the Home Counties and beyond, reaching towns such as Reading, Guildford and even Peterborough. Similar belts surround Paris, Tokyo and New York, where the term "bedroom communities" is common.

Planning issues

Commuter belts raise questions of sprawl, congestion, and protection of countryside. In England, green belt policy was designed partly to contain London's outward commuter growth, pushing development into planned new towns beyond the belt.
