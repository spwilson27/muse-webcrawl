Enschede

Enschede is a city and municipality in the eastern Netherlands, in the province of Overijssel near the German border. It grew around a medieval market settlement and became a major textile center during the industrial era.

History

Enschede received city rights in the fourteenth century and was repeatedly damaged by fires, including major blazes in the nineteenth century. Its cotton and textile industries expanded rapidly with steam power and rail connections, drawing workers from the surrounding Twente region.

Modern city

The decline of textiles in the twentieth century pushed Enschede toward education, technology, and services. It is home to the University of Twente and the football club FC Twente. In 2000 a fireworks depot explosion in the Roombeek district killed 23 people and led to a large reconstruction program.

Culture and transport

The city has direct rail links to Dutch cities and to Münster in Germany. Museums, music venues, and rebuilt neighborhoods reflect its industrial past and post-industrial reinvention. See also Overijssel and University of Twente.
