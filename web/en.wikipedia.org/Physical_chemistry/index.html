Physical chemistry

Physical chemistry is the study of macroscopic and microscopic phenomena in chemical systems by applying the principles, practices, and concepts of physics such as motion, energy, force, time, thermodynamics, quantum chemistry, statistical mechanics, analytical dynamics, and chemical equilibria. It stands at the interface of chemistry and physics and provides the theoretical foundations for much of modern chemistry.

Key areas

Major subfields include chemical thermodynamics (energy changes and equilibrium), chemical kinetics (reaction rates and mechanisms), quantum chemistry (the application of quantum mechanics to molecules), spectroscopy, electrochemistry, statistical mechanics, and surface and colloid science. Physical chemists develop and use instruments such as spectrometers, calorimeters, and lasers to probe matter at the molecular level.

History

The discipline coalesced in the late 19th century. Wilhelm Ostwald founded the first journal of physical chemistry, Zeitschrift für physikalische Chemie, in 1887, and with Jacobus Henricus van 't Hoff and Svante Arrhenius established its central theories of solutions, equilibrium, and chemical affinity. Walther Nernst's heat theorem and the later development of quantum mechanics transformed the field in the early 20th century.
