Gavin Douglas (c. 1474 – September 1522) was a Scottish bishop, makar and translator, the third son of Archibald Douglas, 5th Earl of Angus ("Bell the Cat"). He is chiefly remembered for the Eneados, his translation of Virgil's Aeneid into Scots, completed in 1513—the first complete translation of a major classical poem into any northern European vernacular of the British Isles, and a landmark of Older Scots literature.

Life

Douglas studied at the University of St Andrews (M.A. 1494) and possibly at Paris. A churchman by family strategy, he became provost of St Giles in Edinburgh in 1501 and was implicated in the turbulent politics of the regency of Margaret Tudor after the disaster of Flodden (1513), in which his two elder brothers died. As a Douglas partisan he was nominated to the archbishopric of St Andrews and then obtained the bishopric of Dunkeld (1515), but the factional struggles of the regency—in which he relied at times on English support from his brother-in-law Henry VIII—eventually drove him into exile in London, where he died of plague in 1522.

Works

Douglas's major original poem is The Palice of Honour (c. 1501), a dream-allegory in the Chaucerian tradition. The Eneados, finished on 22 July 1513, renders all twelve books of the Aeneid plus the supplementary thirteenth book of Maffeo Vegio, each book preceded by an original prologue; the May prologue to Book VII and the winter prologues to Books VIII and XII are celebrated for their nature description. In the prologues Douglas defends the Scots tongue as fully sufficient for the task and famously criticizes Caxton's English version. The translation was printed in London in 1553 and in Edinburgh in 1710, and its language preserves much of the Scots literary vocabulary of the period.
