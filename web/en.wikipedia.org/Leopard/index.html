Leopard

The leopard (Panthera pardus) is one of the five extant species in the genus Panthera, a member of the cat family Felidae. It is characterised by its pale yellowish to dark golden fur patterned with dark rosettes, its adaptability to habitats from rainforest to savanna, and its strength — leopards routinely hoist prey as heavy as themselves into trees.

Distribution and subspecies

Leopards have the largest distribution of any wild cat, occurring across much of Africa and parts of Asia, though populations have fallen sharply and the species is listed as Vulnerable on the IUCN Red List. Nine subspecies are generally recognised, including the African leopard, the Indian leopard, the Javan leopard, the Arabian leopard, the Persian leopard, the Amur leopard — among the rarest big cats in the world — the Indochinese, Sri Lankan and North Chinese leopards.

Behaviour

Leopards are solitary, nocturnal apex predators and opportunistic hunters, taking prey from dung beetles to antelope several times their weight. Melanistic leopards, whose rosettes are hidden by black fur, are commonly called black panthers. Compared to other Panthera species, leopards have relatively long bodies and short legs, large skulls and powerful jaw muscles.
