Flathead engine

A flathead engine, also known as a sidevalve engine or valve-in-block engine, is an internal combustion engine whose poppet valves are located in the engine block beside the piston, rather than in the cylinder head. The cylinder head itself is a simple flat casting, giving the design its name.

Design

The valves are operated by a camshaft low in the block, with the intake and exhaust passages cast into the block alongside the bore. This arrangement is mechanically simple, compact in height, and cheap to manufacture, but the tortuous gas path limits breathing and compression ratio, reducing power and efficiency compared with overhead-valve designs.

History

Flatheads dominated early motoring; Ford's flathead V8, produced from 1932 to 1953, was the first mass-produced V8 in an affordable car and became a cornerstone of hot rod culture. By the 1950s and 1960s most car makers had switched to overhead-valve engines, though flatheads persisted in lawn mowers, small engines, and some motorcycles for decades.
