Windlass

[Image: Windlass]

A windlass is a machine used on ships to hoist or lower heavy weights, most commonly the anchor, and is also used ashore for similar hauling tasks. It consists of a horizontal cylinder (the barrel or drum) that is rotated to wind a rope or chain around it, typically powered by hand, steam, hydraulics or an electric motor.

Operation and types

On ships, the windlass is mounted on the forecastle deck and works the anchor chain through a toothed wheel called a wildcat (or gypsy), which engages the links of the chain. It differs from a capstan, which has a vertical axis. Historically, sailors pushed on removable bars set in the windlass barrel, sometimes aided by a pawl-and-ratchet mechanism to prevent the load from running back out.

Etymology and other uses

The word derives from Old Norse vindáss, "winding pole". Windlasses have also been used in construction, mining, forestry, and for drawing water from wells, and the term gave rise to the "windlass" technique in older medicine and to windlass mechanisms in biomechanics, such as the windlass mechanism of the human foot.
