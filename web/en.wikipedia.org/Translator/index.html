Translator

A translator is a person who converts written text from one language (the source language) into another (the target language). The term is distinguished from an interpreter, who converts spoken or signed language. Translation demands not only linguistic competence in both languages but also cultural knowledge, since idioms, register, and context rarely map word-for-word between languages.

Work and specialization

Translators typically work into their native language and specialize in fields such as legal, medical, technical, literary, or audiovisual translation (subtitling and dubbing scripts). Many are certified by professional bodies, and sworn or certified translators produce translations with legal validity for official documents. Saint Jerome, who translated the Bible into Latin as the Vulgate, is regarded as the patron saint of translators; International Translation Day is celebrated on his feast day, 30 September.

Tools and technology

Since the late twentieth century, translators have used computer-assisted translation (CAT) tools, which incorporate translation memories and terminology databases. Machine translation, and more recently neural machine translation, increasingly provides draft output that human translators post-edit, though literary and sensitive texts still rely heavily on human skill.
