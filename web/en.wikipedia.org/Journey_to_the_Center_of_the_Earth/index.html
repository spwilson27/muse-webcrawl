Journey to the Center of the Earth

Journey to the Center of the Earth (French: Voyage au centre de la Terre) is an 1864 science fiction novel by the French writer Jules Verne. It is one of the best known of Verne's Voyages extraordinaires and tells of a German geologist, Professor Otto Lidenbrock, who descends into a volcanic crater in Iceland in an attempt to reach the centre of the Earth, following the path of a 16th-century Icelandic alchemist.

Plot summary

Professor Otto Lidenbrock of Hamburg acquires an old runic manuscript containing a coded note by the Icelandic alchemist Arne Saknussemm, claiming that a passage to the Earth's centre begins in the crater of the Icelandic volcano Snæfellsjökull. With his reluctant nephew Axel, the narrator, and their Icelandic guide Hans Bjelke, Lidenbrock travels to Iceland and descends into the crater in June. Deep underground the party discovers vast caverns, a subterranean ocean (the "Lidenbrock Sea"), prehistoric creatures including ichthyosaurs and plesiosaurs, giant mushrooms and plants, and the remains of early humans. Attempting to blast through a blocked tunnel, they are swept on a raft by an eruption and are expelled through the volcano of Stromboli in Italy, emerging alive to tell their story.

Background and themes

Verne drew on contemporary geology, including the work of Charles Lyell, and on the "hollow Earth" speculations popular in earlier fiction. The novel dramatises the scientific method and the conflict between Lidenbrock's impulsive enthusiasm and Axel's caution. Later editions (1867) added prehistoric material reflecting advances in palaeontology.

Adaptations

The novel has been adapted many times for film and television, most notably the 1959 20th Century Fox film starring James Mason and Pat Boone, and the 2008 film starring Brendan Fraser, which presented the book as a factual account within its story. It has also inspired rides, games, and countless works of subterranean fiction.
