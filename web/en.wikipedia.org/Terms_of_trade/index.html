Terms of trade

Terms of trade
DefinitionIndex of export prices ÷ index of import prices × 100
ImprovementExport prices rise relative to import prices
FieldInternational economics

The terms of trade (TOT) measure the ratio of a country's export prices to its import prices. They represent the quantity of imports a country can obtain for a given quantity of exports: when export prices rise relative to import prices, the terms of trade are said to improve, allowing the country to buy more imports for the same volume of exports.

Measurement

The commodity (or net barter) terms of trade are computed as (export price index / import price index) × 100. Economists also define income terms of trade, which adjust for changes in export volume, and factoral terms of trade, which account for productivity changes in the export sector.

Determinants and effects

Terms of trade shift with world commodity prices, exchange rates, supply and demand conditions, and productivity. They matter greatly for commodity-exporting developing economies: oil exporters gain dramatically when petroleum prices surge, while importers suffer. The Prebisch–Singer hypothesis argued that the terms of trade of primary-commodity exporters tend to decline over the long run relative to exporters of manufactures, a claim debated ever since Raúl Prebisch and Hans Singer advanced it around 1950.

Welfare significance

An improvement in the terms of trade raises national real income, but it can also accompany reduced export volumes or reflect currency movements that harm competitiveness. Sudden deteriorations, as in the 1970s oil shocks for importing nations, contribute to inflation and balance-of-payments crises.
