Endre Szemerédi

Born: 21 August 1940, Budapest, Hungary

Fields: Combinatorics, theoretical computer science

Awards: Abel Prize (2012), Rolf Schock Prize (2008)

Endre Szemerédi (born 21 August 1940) is a Hungarian-American mathematician and computer scientist, working in combinatorics and theoretical computer science. He is known above all for Szemerédi's theorem and the Szemerédi regularity lemma, results that have profoundly influenced modern combinatorics and number theory.

Biography

Szemerédi was born in Budapest. He studied at Eötvös Loránd University and completed his doctorate at Moscow State University under Israel Gelfand. He has held positions at the Alfréd Rényi Institute of Mathematics of the Hungarian Academy of Sciences and at Rutgers University in New Jersey, where he became a professor of computer science.

Research

In 1975 Szemerédi proved a conjecture of Paul Erdős and Pál Turán: every set of integers of positive upper density contains arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions. The result, now called Szemerédi's theorem, had earlier been proved for short progressions by Klaus Roth and Szemerédi himself. His proof introduced the celebrated regularity lemma, which states that every large graph can be partitioned into a bounded number of parts such that the edges between most pairs of parts behave in a pseudo-random way. The lemma has become a fundamental tool in extremal graph theory, property testing, and theoretical computer science.

With Miklós Ajtai and János Komlós, Szemerédi developed the AKS sorting network, an optimal sorting network of depth O(log n). Together with Václav Chvátal he worked on the Chvátal–Szemerédi theorem on graph diameter.

Awards

Szemerédi received the Abel Prize in 2012 "for his fundamental contributions to discrete mathematics and theoretical computer science, and in recognition of the profound and lasting impact of these contributions on additive number theory and ergodic theory." He also received the Rolf Schock Prize in 2008 and is a member of the Hungarian Academy of Sciences and the U.S. National Academy of Sciences.
