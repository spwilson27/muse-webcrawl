New Formalism

New Formalists revived rhyme, metre, and fixed forms in late 20th-century poetry.

New Formalism is a late 20th- and early 21st-century movement in American poetry that has promoted a return to metrical, rhymed verse and narrative poetry, in reaction against the dominance of free verse. Although the label is contested, it is associated with poets such as Dana Gioia, Timothy Steele, Mark Jarman, and Marilyn Hacker, and with the related term "Expansive Poetry."

Origins

The movement coalesced in the 1970s and 1980s. Steele's Missing Measures (1990) offered a historical critique of modernist free verse, and Gioia's essay "Can Poetry Matter?" (1991) argued that poetry had retreated into an insular academic subculture. Anthologies including Rebel Angels (1996) and journals such as The Formalist gave the movement institutional form; the West Chester Poetry Conference, founded in 1995, became its principal gathering.

Debates

Detractors charged the New Formalists with nostalgia and political conservatism, and the label was often applied by critics rather than embraced by the poets themselves; some, like Steele, resisted the term. Defenders replied that metrical craft is ideologically neutral and historically continuous, noted the movement's diversity of practitioners, and pointed to affinities with New Criticism in its attention to form. By the 2000s the "poetry wars" had largely subsided into a pluralistic mainstream in which formal and free verse coexist.
