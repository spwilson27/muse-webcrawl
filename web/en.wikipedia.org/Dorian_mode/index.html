Dorian mode


The Dorian mode scale, shown from D to D.

The Dorian mode is one of the seven diatonic musical modes. In its modern form, it is the natural minor scale with a raised sixth degree: for example, D Dorian consists of D, E, F, G, A, B, C — that is, the white keys of a piano from D to D.

History

The name derives from the ancient Greek Dorian harmonia, although the medieval Dorian mode differed from the Greek system. In the Middle Ages, Dorian was the first church mode, associated with solemn chant. It survived in folk music traditions of the British Isles, Eastern Europe, and elsewhere, and in the twentieth century it became central to modal jazz.

Use

Its combination of a minor third and a major sixth gives Dorian a character often described as bright yet wistful. Examples include Miles Davis's "So What" and "Milestones," as well as many traditional songs such as "Scarborough Fair." Dorian is also widely used in rock, funk, and film music.
