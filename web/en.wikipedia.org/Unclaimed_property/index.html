Unclaimed property

Unclaimed property (also called abandoned property) refers to financial assets that have been left inactive by their owners for an extended period, after which the holder—such as a bank, employer, insurer, or brokerage—must transfer them to a government authority under escheat laws.

Common types include dormant savings and checking accounts, uncashed payroll and dividend cheques, forgotten security deposits, insurance proceeds, safe-deposit box contents, stocks, and unredeemed gift certificates. Dormancy periods vary by jurisdiction and asset type, commonly one to five years. Holders are generally required to attempt to notify owners ("due diligence") before remitting the property.

In the United States, unclaimed property is governed by state law, notably the Uniform Unclaimed Property Acts. States hold billions of dollars in such assets and maintain searchable databases; the National Association of Unclaimed Property Administrators endorses the MissingMoney and Unclaimed.org search portals. Owners (or their heirs) can usually reclaim the property or its sale value indefinitely. Similar regimes, such as the UK's Dormant Assets Scheme, exist in other countries.
