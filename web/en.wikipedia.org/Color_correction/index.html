Color correction

Color correction is a process used in stage lighting, photography, television, cinematography, and other disciplines, which uses color gels, filters, or digital tools to alter the overall color of the light or image. In film and video post-production it refers to adjusting an image to a consistent technical standard, distinct from the creative look of color grading.

Photography and film

Filters correct for mismatches between light source color temperature and film or sensor sensitivity — for example using an 85 filter in daylight with tungsten film. Digital workflows use white balance, curves, and scopes in software such as DaVinci Resolve.

Stage lighting

Gel filters, catalogued by manufacturers such as Lee and Rosco, are placed over lights to warm, cool, or tint illumination for mood and skin-tone fidelity.

Digital color correction

Primary correction adjusts overall exposure, contrast, and white balance; secondary correction targets specific colors or regions using masks and qualifiers.
