Sauk people

The Sauk (also spelled Sac) are a Native American people of the Eastern Woodlands, historically located in the region of the Saint Lawrence River valley and later around Green Bay, Wisconsin, and the upper Mississippi River. They speak an Algonquian language closely related to that of the Meskwaki (Fox), with whom they have long been allied.

A Sauk leader in the early 19th century

History

Pressure from the Iroquois during the Beaver Wars pushed the Sauk westward in the 17th century. By the 18th century they were established in large villages such as Saukenuk, near present-day Rock Island, Illinois, farming the fertile river bottoms in summer and hunting on the plains in winter.

After the contested Treaty of St. Louis in 1804 ceded Sauk lands east of the Mississippi, the war leader Black Hawk resisted removal, culminating in the Black Hawk War of 1832. Defeat led to the tribe's relocation westward. Today three federally recognised tribal nations — the Sac and Fox Nation in Oklahoma, the Sac and Fox Tribe of the Mississippi in Iowa (Meskwaki), and the Sac and Fox Nation of Missouri in Kansas and Nebraska — descend from the Sauk and Meskwaki.
