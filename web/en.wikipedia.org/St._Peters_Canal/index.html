St. Peters Canal

The St. Peters Canal is a small shipping canal located in St. Peter's, Nova Scotia, Canada, on Cape Breton Island. The canal, about 800 metres long, cuts through a narrow isthmus to connect St. Peter's Bay on the Atlantic Ocean with Bras d'Or Lake, allowing vessels to avoid the exposed passage around Cape Breton. It is a National Historic Site of Canada.

History

The isthmus was long used as a portage by the Mi'kmaq people, and French traders built a haul-over road there in the 17th century. Construction of the canal began in 1854 and was completed in 1869, after difficult excavation through solid granite. The canal was enlarged several times; its single lock allows vessels to pass between the tidal waters of the Atlantic and the largely non-tidal Bras d'Or Lake.

Operation

The canal is operated seasonally by Parks Canada and is used mainly by pleasure craft, fishing boats and small commercial vessels travelling to and from Bras d'Or Lake, a popular cruising ground.
