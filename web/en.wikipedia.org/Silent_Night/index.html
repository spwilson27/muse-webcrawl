Silent Night

"Silent Night" (German: "Stille Nacht, heilige Nacht")
Christmas carol, 1818
Lyrics: Joseph Mohr (1816)
Music: Franz Xaver Gruber (1818)
First performed: 24 December 1818, Oberndorf, Austria
Designation: UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage (Austria, 2011)

"Silent Night" (German: "Stille Nacht, heilige Nacht") is a popular Christmas carol, composed in 1818 by Franz Xaver Gruber to lyrics by Joseph Mohr in the small town of Oberndorf bei Salzburg, Austria. It has been translated into about 140 languages and was declared an intangible cultural heritage by UNESCO in 2011.

History

Mohr, a Catholic priest, wrote the poem in 1816 in Mariapfarr. On Christmas Eve 1818, at the St Nicholas parish church in Oberndorf, he asked Gruber, a schoolmaster and organist in the nearby village of Arnsdorf, to compose a melody for guitar accompaniment. The two men performed the carol during the Christmas Eve mass, Mohr singing and playing guitar. The often-repeated story that the church organ was broken — sometimes blamed on mice — is a later legend.

Spread

The carol spread through travelling folk singers such as the Rainer and Strasser families, reaching the United States by 1839; John Freeman Young, later Bishop of Florida, published the English translation in 1859. Young's version of three verses is the one most commonly sung in English. Bing Crosby's 1935 recording is among the best-selling singles of all time.

Cultural significance

The song famously featured in the Christmas truce of 1914, sung simultaneously by English and German troops in the trenches. The original St Nicholas church, damaged by flooding, was replaced by the Silent Night Chapel in Oberndorf, now a place of pilgrimage for visitors from around the world.
