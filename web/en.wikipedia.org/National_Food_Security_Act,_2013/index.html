National Food Security Act, 2013

Enacted by: Parliament of India

Assented to: 10 September 2013

Commenced: 5 July 2013 (retrospective, via ordinance)

Coverage: Up to 75% of rural and 50% of urban population

The National Food Security Act, 2013 (NFSA 2013) is an Act of the Parliament of India which aims to provide subsidised food grains to approximately two-thirds of India's population. It converted existing food security programmes — including the Public Distribution System (PDS), the Midday Meal Scheme and the Integrated Child Development Services — into legal entitlements, adopting a rights-based approach to food security. It is sometimes described as the largest food security programme in the world.

Provisions

Under the Act, eligible "priority households" are entitled to 5 kilograms of food grains per person per month, while households under the Antyodaya Anna Yojana (the poorest of the poor) receive 35 kilograms per household per month. Under the original schedule, rice, wheat and coarse grains were priced at ₹3, ₹2 and ₹1 per kilogram respectively; from January 2023 the government made distribution of NFSA grain free of charge. The Act also provides maternity benefits, nutritional support for children under six through anganwadis, and hot cooked mid-day meals for schoolchildren. The eldest woman of a household is designated as the head of household for the ration card.

Implementation and criticism

State governments are responsible for identifying eligible households and operating fair price shops. The programme has been praised for reducing hunger and supporting nutrition, but criticised for its fiscal cost, leakages and diversion of grain, storage losses at the Food Corporation of India, and exclusions caused by identification errors and digitisation requirements such as biometric authentication failures.
