Appelquist–Carazzone theorem

Appelquist–Carazzone theorem
FieldQuantum field theory
Proposed1975
AuthorsThomas Appelquist, J. Carazzone

The Appelquist–Carazzone theorem is a result in quantum field theory formulated by Thomas Appelquist and J. Carazzone in 1975. It states that, under certain conditions, heavy particles decouple from the low-energy physics of a renormalizable theory: their effects at energies far below their mass are suppressed by powers of the ratio of the energy scale to the heavy mass, apart from effects that can be absorbed into renormalizations of the coupling constants and masses of the light theory.

Statement

In a theory containing both light fields and heavy fields of mass M, scattering amplitudes involving only light external particles at momenta p much smaller than M can be reproduced, up to corrections of order p/M, by an effective theory containing only the light fields, with appropriately matched coupling constants. The heavy particles thus "decouple" in the infrared.

Significance

The theorem provides the theoretical justification for effective field theory descriptions in particle physics. It explains why heavy quarks such as the top quark have only small, calculable effects on low-energy observables in quantum chromodynamics, and underlies the treatment of the charm and bottom quarks in hadronic physics. Exceptions to naive decoupling, such as effects growing with the heavy mass in spontaneously broken gauge theories, are important in precision electroweak physics.
