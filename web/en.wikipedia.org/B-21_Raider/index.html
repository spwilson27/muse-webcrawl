The Northrop Grumman B-21 Raider is an American strategic bomber in development for the United States Air Force (USAF) by Northrop Grumman. As part of the Long Range Strike Bomber (LRS-B) program, it is to be a long-range, stealth, intercontinental strategic bomber capable of delivering both conventional and thermonuclear weapons. The name "Raider" honors the Doolittle Raiders of World War II.

Development

The Air Force awarded the LRS-B development contract to Northrop Grumman in October 2015. The aircraft was publicly unveiled on 2 December 2022 at Northrop Grumman's facilities in Palmdale, California. It made its first flight on 10 November 2023, flying from Palmdale to Edwards Air Force Base for flight testing.

The B-21 is intended to replace the Rockwell B-1 Lancer and Northrop Grumman B-2 Spirit, and eventually the Boeing B-52 Stratofortress, forming the backbone of the future U.S. bomber fleet. The Air Force plans to acquire at least 100 aircraft.

Design

The B-21 is a flying wing design broadly similar in configuration to the B-2 Spirit but smaller, with a single main weapons bay and advanced low-observable features intended to defeat modern integrated air defense systems. It is designed with an open systems architecture to permit rapid upgrades, and its engines are buried more deeply in the fuselage than on the B-2 to reduce its infrared and radar signatures. The aircraft is optionally envisioned to operate with advanced networking and battle-management capabilities, acting as a sensor node as well as a strike platform.
