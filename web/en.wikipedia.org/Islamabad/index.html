Islamabad

Islamabad is the capital city of Pakistan. Located on the Pothohar Plateau in the north-east of the country, within the Islamabad Capital Territory, it is a planned city built during the 1960s to replace Karachi as the capital. It is known for its wide avenues, abundant greenery and the backdrop of the Margalla Hills.

History

The site was selected in 1959 by a commission under President Ayub Khan, partly for its proximity to the military headquarters at Rawalpindi, Islamabad's twin city. A master plan by Greek architect Constantinos Apostolou Doxiadis divided the city into eight zones — administrative, diplomatic, residential, institutional, industrial, commercial, rural and green areas — arranged in numbered sectors on a grid.

Landmarks

The Faisal Mosque, completed in 1986 and named for King Faisal of Saudi Arabia, is one of the largest mosques in the world, its tent-like concrete shell a symbol of the city. Other landmarks include the Pakistan Monument, the Parliament House and the presidential palace (Aiwan-e-Sadr). With a population of about one million in the city proper and over two million in the metropolitan area, Islamabad has among Pakistan's highest literacy rates and living standards.
