Dirk Hartog

Dirk Hartog (Dutch pronunciation: [dɪrk ˈɦɑrtɔx]; baptised 30 October 1580 – buried 11 October 1621) was a Dutch seafarer and explorer. He was the second European to land on Australian soil, in 1616, preceded only by Willem Janszoon in 1606.

The 1616 landing

Commanding the VOC ship Eendracht, Hartog was sailing from Cape Town to Batavia using the Brouwer Route when he drifted too far east and sighted land near present-day Shark Bay, Western Australia, on 25 October 1616. He landed on the island now known as Dirk Hartog Island and, before departing, nailed a pewter plate to a post recording the visit — the famous Hartog Plate, the oldest known artifact of European exploration left in Australia.

The plate

The inscribed plate was found again in 1697 by Willem de Vlamingh, who replaced it with a new plate incorporating Hartog's text; Hartog's original was taken to Amsterdam and is now held in the Rijksmuseum. De Vlamingh's plate remained on the island until removed to Paris in 1818 and was eventually returned to Australia.

Later life

Hartog reached Batavia in December 1616, returned to the Netherlands in 1618, and undertook further voyages in Dutch service before his death in Amsterdam in 1621.
