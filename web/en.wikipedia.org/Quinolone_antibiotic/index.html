Quinolone antibiotic

The quinolone core structure

Quinolone antibiotics constitute a large group of broad-spectrum bactericidal agents, most of them fluoroquinolones derived from a bicyclic core structure related to 4-quinolone. They act by inhibiting bacterial DNA gyrase and topoisomerase IV, enzymes essential for DNA replication.

Generations

The class is commonly divided into generations of increasing spectrum. The first generation, beginning with nalidixic acid (introduced in 1962), was largely confined to urinary tract infections. Later fluorinated generations include ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin, and moxifloxacin, with activity against a wide range of Gram-negative and Gram-positive organisms.

Medical uses

Quinolones have been used for urinary tract infections, prostatitis, respiratory tract infections, anthrax, and some sexually transmitted infections. Growing antimicrobial resistance and serious adverse effects have led regulators to reserve them for cases where other antibiotics are unsuitable.

Adverse effects

Adverse effects include tendinitis and tendon rupture, peripheral neuropathy, central nervous system effects, and QT prolongation. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration has issued boxed warnings concerning these risks, including a warning about aortic aneurysm and dissection.
