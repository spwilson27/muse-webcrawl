Backlot

A backlot is an area behind or adjoining a movie studio containing permanent exterior sets, such as streets, town squares, western towns and building facades, used for filming outdoor scenes.

Characteristics

Backlot buildings are typically facades — full-size fronts supported from behind with unfinished backs — arranged so that a single street can stand in for many places and periods by redressing signs, vehicles and details. Large studio lots in Hollywood's golden age maintained standing sets for New York streets, European villages, jungles and frontier towns, allowing productions to shoot "anywhere in the world" without leaving the lot.

Famous backlots

Notable examples include the Warner Bros. backlot in Burbank, Universal Studios' extensive lot (whose sets feature in its theme park tram tour), and the former Desilu/RKO "Forty Acres" in Culver City, where the Mayberry set of The Andy Griffith Show and parts of Gone with the Wind were filmed before the lot was demolished in the 1970s. The shrinking of studio land values and the move to location shooting and digital environments have reduced backlots' role, though they remain valuable for controlled, repeatable filming.
