Text art

Text art, sometimes called ASCII art in its digital form, is visual art in which words, letters, or typographic characters are themselves the principal medium or subject of the work. Written language in art ranges from illuminated manuscripts and calligrams to modernist and conceptual works made entirely of language.

History

Guillaume Apollinaire's calligrams of 1918 arranged poems into pictures. In the 1960s, conceptual artists such as Joseph Kosuth and Lawrence Weiner made language the artwork itself, while Jenny Holzer's Truisms and Barbara Kruger's bold captioned images brought text art to public spaces and mass media.

Computer text art

On early computers and bulletin board systems, artists composed pictures from the fixed characters of ASCII and ANSI code pages. Textmode art scenes flourished in the 1980s and 1990s, with groups releasing "artpacks" of typewriter-style illustrations, logos, and animations.
