Petroleum engineering

A drilling rig; drilling engineering is one of the discipline's main branches.

Petroleum engineering is a field of engineering concerned with the activities related to the production of hydrocarbons, which can be either crude oil or natural gas. Exploration, by earth scientists, and petroleum engineering are the oil and gas industry's two main subsurface disciplines, which focus on maximizing economic recovery of hydrocarbons from subsurface reservoirs.

Overview

Petroleum engineers design and manage the processes by which oil and gas are extracted from beneath the Earth's surface. Many petroleum engineers work for energy operating companies, service companies such as Schlumberger and Halliburton, or consultancies. The professional society for the field is the Society of Petroleum Engineers (SPE).

Disciplines

  • Reservoir engineering — estimating reserves, modeling fluid flow in porous rock, and optimizing recovery, including enhanced oil recovery techniques such as waterflooding and gas injection
  • Drilling engineering — planning and executing the drilling of wells, including directional and horizontal drilling, casing design, and drilling fluids
  • Production engineering — managing well performance after completion, artificial lift, flow assurance, and surface facilities
  • Petrophysics and formation evaluation — interpreting well logs and core data to characterize reservoirs
  • Petroleum economics and management — evaluating project profitability and risk

History

The profession developed in the early 20th century alongside the modern oil industry, with the first university programs in the United States appearing around 1910–1915. Techniques grew in sophistication with the advent of reservoir simulation, 3D seismic interpretation, hydraulic fracturing, and unconventional resources such as shale oil and gas.
