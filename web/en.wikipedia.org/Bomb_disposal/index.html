Bomb disposal

Bomb disposal is an explosive engineering profession using the process by which hazardous explosive devices are rendered safe. Bomb disposal is an all-encompassing term to describe the separate fields of military explosive ordnance disposal (EOD) and improvised explosive device disposal (IEDD), and the public safety roles of public safety bomb disposal (PSBD) and the bomb squad.

History

Organized bomb disposal units emerged during the Second World War, when British Royal Engineers and other specialists defuzed unexploded German bombs during the Blitz, at high cost in lives. After the war, techniques and protective equipment advanced, including the bomb suit and remotely operated vehicles such as the Wheelbarrow robot, developed in the 1970s for use in Northern Ireland.

Techniques

Operators generally work to render a device safe while keeping as much distance as possible between themselves and the device. Methods include remote disruption with water jets or explosive charges, manual defuzing as a last resort, removal to a safe area in a containment vessel, and controlled detonation. Robots are now used for reconnaissance and disruption in most developed countries.
