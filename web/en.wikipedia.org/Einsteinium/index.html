Einsteinium is a synthetic chemical element; it has symbol Es and atomic number 99. It is a member of the actinide series and is the seventh transuranium element. It was discovered as a component of the debris of the first hydrogen bomb explosion in 1952, and named after Albert Einstein. Einsteinium is a soft, silvery, paramagnetic metal.

Discovery

Einsteinium was discovered in December 1952 by Albert Ghiorso and co-workers at the University of California, Berkeley, in the fallout of the "Ivy Mike" thermonuclear test at Enewetak Atoll. The isotope 253Es formed by neutron capture on uranium-238 followed by beta decays. The discovery was kept secret until 1955 due to Cold War tensions.

Properties

Einsteinium is highly radioactive; its most stable isotope is 252Es with a half-life of 471.7 days. Like other actinides, it is a soft metal that readily tarnishes in air. Its chemistry is dominated by the +3 oxidation state, and it is produced in milligram quantities in specialized high-flux nuclear reactors.

Uses

Because of its intense radioactivity and scarcity, einsteinium has no practical applications outside basic research. It is used to produce mendelevium, the element after fermium, by bombarding einsteinium-253 with alpha particles.
