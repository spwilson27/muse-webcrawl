8.2-kiloyear event

The 8.2-kiloyear event was an abrupt global cooling that occurred about 8,200 years ago, lasting roughly 150 years. It is the most prominent climatic downturn in the Holocene as recorded in Greenland ice cores, where temperatures dropped by an estimated 3.3 °C (within a few decades) before recovering.

Cause

The event is attributed to a sudden pulse of meltwater from the collapsing Laurentide Ice Sheet in North America. The glacial lakes Agassiz and Ojibway drained into the North Atlantic, freshening surface waters and weakening the Atlantic meridional overturning circulation, which reduced northward heat transport.

Effects

Cooling and drying affected much of the Northern Hemisphere. Evidence appears in ice cores, lake sediments, cave deposits, and pollen records from Europe, Asia, and Africa. The event may have disrupted early farming communities in the Near East and contributed to changes in settlement patterns in Europe.
