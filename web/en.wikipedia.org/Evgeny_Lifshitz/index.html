Evgeny Mikhailovich Lifshitz (21 February 1915 – 29 October 1985) was a leading Soviet physicist and a close collaborator of Lev Landau. He is best known as the co-author, with Landau, of the monumental ten-volume Course of Theoretical Physics, the standard advanced textbook series covering mechanics, field theory, quantum mechanics, statistical physics, fluid mechanics, elasticity, electrodynamics of continuous media, physical kinetics, and relativistic quantum theory.

Life and career

Born in Kharkov, Lifshitz studied at the Kharkov Polytechnic Institute and came under Landau's influence while still a teenager. He passed Landau's notoriously demanding "theoretical minimum" examinations and became one of Landau's closest associates at the Ukrainian Physico-Technical Institute, later moving with the school to Moscow. He spent his career at the Institute for Physical Problems of the Soviet Academy of Sciences, and for decades edited the Soviet Journal of Experimental and Theoretical Physics (ZhETF).

Scientific work

Beyond the textbook series — completed after Landau's incapacitating 1962 car accident, with Lifshitz shepherding the remaining volumes — he made original contributions to several fields. His 1956 theory of molecular attractive forces between condensed bodies (the Lifshitz theory of van der Waals and Casimir-type forces) is a cornerstone of surface science. With Isaak Khalatnikov and Vladimir Belinski he developed the BKL description of the generic oscillatory approach to a cosmological singularity. He also worked on second-order phase transitions, ferromagnetism (with Landau, deriving the Landau–Lifshitz equation of micromagnetics), and physical kinetics.

Legacy

The Landau–Lifshitz textbooks, written with unusual physical insight and mathematical economy, trained generations of physicists worldwide and remain in print in many languages. Lifshitz received the Lenin Prize (with Landau, 1962) and was a member of the Soviet Academy of Sciences and a Foreign Member of the Royal Society.
