Wild boar

The wild boar (Sus scrofa), also known as the wild swine, common wild pig, Eurasian wild pig, or simply wild pig, is a suid native to much of Eurasia and North Africa, and has been introduced to the Americas and Oceania.

Description

Adults have a bulky, muscular build, sparse coarse hair ranging from dark grey to brown, and, in males, prominent curved tusks formed from the canine teeth. Body length ranges roughly from 90 to 180 cm, and large males can exceed 150 kg.

Behaviour and diet

Wild boars are omnivorous and highly adaptable, eating roots, bulbs, fallen fruit, invertebrates and small animals; they forage mostly at night by rooting in soil with their snouts. Females and young live in groups called sounders, while adult males are usually solitary outside the breeding season.

Relationship with humans

The wild boar is the ancestor of the domestic pig, domesticated beginning around 13,000–12,700 years ago. Boars feature prominently in the mythology and heraldry of many cultures. In areas where they have been introduced, hybrid feral pigs cause significant agricultural and ecological damage.

Distribution

The species has one of the widest geographic ranges of any terrestrial mammal and is listed as Least Concern by the IUCN.
