Depressant

A depressant, or central nervous system depressant, is a drug that lowers neurotransmission levels, reducing arousal and stimulation in various areas of the brain. Depressants are used medically as sedatives, hypnotics, anxiolytics and anaesthetics, and recreationally for their relaxing and disinhibiting effects.

Examples

Common depressants include alcohol, benzodiazepines (such as diazepam), barbiturates, opioids, GHB, and certain antihistamines and antipsychotics. Many act by enhancing the effect of the inhibitory neurotransmitter GABA.

Effects and risks

At low doses, depressants reduce anxiety and inhibition; at higher doses they cause drowsiness, impaired coordination, respiratory depression, coma and death. Combining depressants — especially alcohol with opioids or benzodiazepines — greatly increases the risk of fatal overdose. Chronic use can produce tolerance and physical dependence, and withdrawal from some depressants, notably alcohol and barbiturates, can itself be life-threatening.
