The Cretaceous–Paleogene (K–Pg) extinction event, formerly called the Cretaceous–Tertiary (K–T) extinction, was a sudden mass extinction of some three-quarters of the plant and animal species on Earth, approximately 66 million years ago. It marked the end of the Cretaceous period and of the Mesozoic Era, and the beginning of the Paleogene period of the Cenozoic Era.

Victims and survivors

The event eliminated all non-avian dinosaurs, along with pterosaurs, mosasaurs, ammonites and many other groups. Birds, mammals, crocodilians, turtles and many fish and invertebrates survived, and mammals subsequently diversified into the ecological niches left vacant, eventually including humans.

Cause

The leading explanation is the impact of an asteroid about 10–15 km wide at Chicxulub, on Mexico's Yucatán Peninsula, which scattered iridium-rich debris worldwide, triggered fires, tsunamis and a prolonged "impact winter" that shut down photosynthesis. Evidence includes the global iridium layer at the K–Pg boundary and the buried Chicxulub crater, identified in the early 1990s. Massive volcanism of the Deccan Traps in India around the same time may have contributed to environmental stress, and the relative roles of impact and volcanism remain debated.
