High Line

High Line
Location: Manhattan, New York City
Length: 1.45 mi (2.33 km)
Opened: 2009 (first section)
Former use: elevated freight railway

The High Line is an elevated linear park, greenway and rail trail created on a former New York Central Railroad spur on the west side of Manhattan in New York City. The park runs for about 1.45 miles (2.33 km) from Gansevoort Street in the Meatpacking District north to West 34th Street near Hudson Yards.

History

The elevated structure was built in the 1930s as part of the West Side Improvement Project, lifting freight trains off the streets where they had caused many accidents. Rail traffic declined with the growth of trucking, and the last train ran in 1980. The structure faced demolition until the non-profit Friends of the High Line, founded in 1999, advocated its conversion into a public park, inspired in part by the Promenade plantée in Paris.

The park

Designed by James Corner Field Operations with planting by Piet Oudolf, the park opened in stages beginning in 2009. It features naturalised plantings, public art, seating built from the old rails, and views over the Hudson River and city streets. The High Line became one of New York's most visited attractions and a widely cited model — and point of debate — for adaptive reuse and urban regeneration, credited with spurring major development, and rising property values, in surrounding neighbourhoods.
