Degustation

Degustation is the careful, appreciative tasting of various food, focusing on the gustatory system, the senses, high culinary art and good company. Dégustation is more likely to involve sampling small portions of all of a chef's signature dishes in one sitting. Usually consisting of eight or more courses, it may be accompanied with a matching wine degustation which complements each dish.

Etymology

The term comes from the French word dégustation, derived from the Latin degustatio, meaning a tasting. Related modern concepts include the tasting menu offered by many fine-dining restaurants, in which a series of small dishes showcases the chef's range and technique.

Tasting menus

A degustation menu, also called a tasting menu, is a meal of many small courses, typically chosen by the chef rather than the diner. The format became prominent in French haute cuisine and spread internationally in the late twentieth and early twenty-first centuries, becoming a signature format for restaurants such as The French Laundry, El Bulli, and Noma. Menus may run from five courses to more than twenty, and frequently offer optional wine pairings selected by a sommelier.

Practices

Degustation etiquette emphasizes attention and pacing: diners are encouraged to savor each course fully, noting aroma, taste, texture, and presentation. In wine degustation, tasters commonly evaluate appearance, nose, palate, and finish, and may spit rather than swallow in order to taste many samples without intoxication.
