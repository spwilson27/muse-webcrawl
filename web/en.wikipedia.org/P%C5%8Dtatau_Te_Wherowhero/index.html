Pōtatau Te Wherowhero

ReignJune 1858 – 25 June 1860
Bornc. 1770s–1800, Waikato, New Zealand
Died25 June 1860, Ngāruawāhia
SuccessorTāwhiao

Pōtatau Te Wherowhero (c. 1770s/1800 – 25 June 1860) was a Māori warrior and chief of the Waikato iwi, and in 1858 became the first Māori King, founder of the Kīngitanga (Māori King Movement).

Early life and chiefly career

Te Wherowhero was the eldest son of the Waikato chief Te Rauangaanga and was descended from the chiefs Hotumauea and Te Putu. A formidable warrior, he fought in numerous battles during the Musket Wars, including campaigns against the Ngāti Toa of Te Rauparaha. Despite the warfare of the era, he was also known for acts of mercy, notably sparing defeated enemies at Mātakitaki pā in 1822.

Relations with the Crown

Te Wherowhero developed a working relationship with the early colonial government; he was one of the chiefs who signed documents associated with the Treaty of Waitangi period and sold land at Māngere to the Crown, maintaining a residence at Auckland where he was treated as an honoured guest of governors.

Māori King

In the 1850s, Māori leaders seeking to halt land sales and unify the tribes under a single figurehead created the Kīngitanga. After several candidates declined, Te Wherowhero accepted the kingship and was crowned at Ngāruawāhia in June 1858. He died there in 1860 and was succeeded by his son Tāwhiao. He is buried at Mount Taupiri, the sacred mountain of Waikato-Tainui.
