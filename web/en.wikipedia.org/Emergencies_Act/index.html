Emergencies Act

The Emergencies Act (French: Loi sur les mesures d'urgence) is a statute passed by the Parliament of Canada in 1988 that allows the federal government to take extraordinary temporary measures during national emergencies. It replaced the War Measures Act, which had been invoked during both World Wars and the October Crisis of 1970.

The Act defines four classes of emergency: public welfare emergencies, public order emergencies, international emergencies and war emergencies. A declaration of emergency must be reviewed by Parliament, is subject to the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms, and expires after a set period unless extended.

The Act was invoked for the first time on February 14, 2022, by Prime Minister Justin Trudeau in response to the "Freedom Convoy" protests and border blockades. The declaration was revoked on February 23, 2022. A public inquiry, the Rouleau Commission, later concluded that the threshold for invocation had been met, though the decision remained politically contentious.
