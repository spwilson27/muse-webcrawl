Hybrid work

Hybrid work is a flexible working model in which employees divide their working time between a traditional workplace (such as an office) and remote locations, typically their homes. It combines elements of remote work and in-person work and became widespread among knowledge workers following the COVID-19 pandemic, during which large-scale remote work demonstrated that many office jobs could be performed from home.

Models

Hybrid arrangements vary: organizations may fix specific in-office days for whole teams ("office-first" with set anchor days), allow employees to choose their days ("remote-first" with optional office use), or set minimum attendance quotas such as two or three days per week. Common supporting practices include hot-desking, meeting rooms designed for video conferencing equity, and asynchronous communication norms.

Effects and evidence

Research — including a large randomized trial at the Chinese travel company Trip.com published in Nature in 2024 — found that working from home two days a week did not harm productivity or promotions and reduced employee attrition substantially. Surveys consistently report that employees value flexibility and time saved commuting, while employers cite collaboration, mentoring, and culture as reasons for retaining office time. Debates over hybrid work have featured prominently in return-to-office disputes at major companies in the mid-2020s.
