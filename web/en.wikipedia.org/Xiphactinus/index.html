Xiphactinus was among the largest bony fish of the Cretaceous seas.

Xiphactinus (from Greek for "sword-ray") is an extinct genus of large predatory marine bony fish that lived during the Late Cretaceous. Reaching an estimated 5 to 6 metres in length, it was one of the largest teleost fish known and a top predator of the shallow seas that covered parts of North America, Europe and Australia.

Description

Xiphactinus had a powerful, streamlined, tarpon-like body, large upturned jaws armed with sharp conical teeth, and a deeply forked tail suggesting it was a fast swimmer. It belonged to the Ichthyodectidae, an extinct family of ray-finned fishes sometimes called "bulldog tarpons".

Palaeobiology

Fossils of Xiphactinus are especially famous from the chalk deposits of the Western Interior Seaway, notably the Niobrara Formation of Kansas. A celebrated specimen, collected by George F. Sternberg in 1952, preserves a 4-metre Xiphactinus with a nearly complete 2-metre fish (Gillicus) inside its ribcage — the famous "fish-within-a-fish" fossil. Such specimens show that it swallowed large prey whole, and may sometimes have died as a result of struggling prey rupturing its stomach.

Extinction

Like the mosasaurs and plesiosaurs with which it shared the seas, Xiphactinus vanished in the Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event 66 million years ago.
