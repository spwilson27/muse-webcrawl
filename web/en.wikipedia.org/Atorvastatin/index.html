Atorvastatin

Atorvastatin
Brand name: Lipitor
Class: Statin (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor)
Approved: 1996 (FDA)
Uses: Lowering LDL cholesterol; prevention of cardiovascular disease

Atorvastatin, sold under the brand name Lipitor among others, is a statin medication used to prevent cardiovascular disease in those at high risk and to treat abnormal lipid levels. By inhibiting the enzyme HMG-CoA reductase, it reduces the liver's production of cholesterol and lowers low-density lipoprotein (LDL) cholesterol in the blood.

Medical use

Atorvastatin is prescribed for primary and secondary prevention of heart attack and stroke in patients with elevated cholesterol, diabetes, or established cardiovascular disease. It is taken orally, typically once daily, in strengths from 10 to 80 mg. Common side effects include joint pain, muscle aches, diarrhoea, and nausea; rare but serious effects include muscle breakdown (rhabdomyolysis) and liver injury, and it should not be used in pregnancy.

History

Atorvastatin was synthesized in 1985 by Bruce Roth at Parke-Davis and approved for medical use in the United States in 1996. Marketed by Pfizer as Lipitor, it became the best-selling pharmaceutical in history, generating more than US$125 billion in sales over about 14.5 years before its US patent expired in 2011 and generic versions became available. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.
