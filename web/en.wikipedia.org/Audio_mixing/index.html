Audio mixing

Audio mixing is the process by which multiple sounds are combined into one or more audio channels. In the process, a source's volume level, frequency content, dynamics, and panoramic position are manipulated or enhanced. This practical, aesthetic, or otherwise creative treatment is done in order to produce a finished version that is appealing to listeners.

Recording and reproduction

Audio mixing is practiced for music, film, television and live sound. The process is generally carried out by a mixing engineer operating a mixing console or digital audio workstation. Techniques include balancing levels with faders, equalization, dynamic range compression, panning, and adding effects such as reverberation and delay.

Live sound

In live sound reproduction, a front-of-house engineer mixes the sound the audience hears, while a monitor engineer may prepare separate mixes for performers' stage monitors or in-ear systems.

Film and television

For film, a re-recording mixer blends dialogue, music, and sound effects into the final soundtrack during post-production, often in a dubbing stage using large-format consoles.
