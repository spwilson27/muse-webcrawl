Jewish Combat Organization


The Jewish Combat Organization (ŻOB) was a Jewish resistance movement in the Warsaw Ghetto during World War II.

The Jewish Combat Organization (Polish: Żydowska Organizacja Bojowa, ŻOB; Yiddish: ייִדישע קאַמף אָרגאַניזאַציע) was a Jewish resistance movement in German-occupied Poland during World War II. It was the principal organisation behind the Warsaw Ghetto Uprising of April–May 1943, the largest single revolt by Jews during the war.

Formation

The ŻOB was founded on 28 July 1942, during the mass deportation of Warsaw's Jews to the Treblinka extermination camp, by Zionist youth movements including Hashomer Hatzair, Dror, and Bnei Akiva, later joined by the Jewish Bund and others. Its commander was the 24-year-old Mordechai Anielewicz. A rival organisation, the right-wing Jewish Military Union (ŻZW), operated separately.

The uprising

In January 1943, ŻOB fighters resisted a renewed deportation Aktion, convincing many Jews that armed resistance was possible. On 19 April 1943, German forces under Jürgen Stroop entered the ghetto to liquidate it and met organised resistance. With home-made weapons, a few smuggled arms, and Molotov cocktails, several hundred fighters held out for nearly a month against tanks, artillery, and flamethrowers. The uprising ended when the Germans burned the ghetto block by block; the ŻOB command bunker at Miła 18 fell on 8 May, where Anielewicz and many of his comrades died. Some survivors escaped through the sewers and continued fighting in the Warsaw Uprising of 1944.

Legacy

The Warsaw Ghetto Uprising became a symbol of Jewish resistance to the Holocaust, commemorated in Israel on Yom HaShoah and in Poland by the Monument to the Ghetto Heroes, unveiled in 1948 on the site of the former ghetto.
