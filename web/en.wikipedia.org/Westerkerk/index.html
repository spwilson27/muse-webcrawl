The Westerkerk (English: Western Church) is a Reformed church within Dutch Protestant Calvinism, in central Amsterdam, Netherlands. It lies in the Jordaan district, next to the Prinsengracht canal, and is the largest church built for Protestants in the Netherlands during the Dutch Golden Age.

Designed by Hendrick de Keyser in Renaissance style, the church was built between 1620 and 1631; after de Keyser's death in 1621 his son Pieter supervised completion. The tower, the Westertoren, is 87 metres (285 ft) tall and is crowned with the Imperial Crown of Austria, a gift associated with Emperor Maximilian I. It is the tallest church tower in Amsterdam and a beloved symbol of the city, mentioned in many songs and in Anne Frank's diary — the church bells and tower are visible from the Achterhuis (now the Anne Frank House) nearby.

Interior and history

The interior is bright and spacious, with a large nave and wooden barrel vaulting. The church contains a famous organ built by Duyschot (1686) with shutters painted by Gerard de Lairesse. Rembrandt van Rijn was buried in the church in 1669, though the exact location of his grave is unknown; a memorial marker commemorates him. Queen Beatrix married Claus von Amsberg in the Westerkerk in 1966.
