Ahmad al-Mansur

Ahmad al-Mansur (1549 – 25 August 1603), also known as Ahmad al-Mansur al-Dhahabi ("the Golden"), was the Saadi sultan of Morocco from 1578 until his death. His reign is considered the high point of the Saadi dynasty.

Reign

Ahmad came to power after the Battle of Alcácer Quibir (1578), in which his brother and the Portuguese king Sebastian were killed. Ransoms for Portuguese prisoners and control of the trans-Saharan gold trade made his court fabulously wealthy; he built the El Badi Palace in Marrakesh.

Songhai expedition

In 1591 he sent an expedition under Judar Pasha across the Sahara that defeated the Songhai Empire at the Battle of Tondibi, seizing Timbuktu and Gao. The conquest disrupted the region's trade and scholarship, and Moroccan control proved difficult to sustain. Ahmad corresponded with Elizabeth I of England about an alliance against Spain.
