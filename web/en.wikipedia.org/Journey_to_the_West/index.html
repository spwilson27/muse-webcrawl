Journey to the West

Journey to the West (Chinese: 西遊記; pinyin: Xīyóujì) is a Chinese novel published in the 16th century during the Ming dynasty and attributed to Wu Cheng'en. It is one of the Four Great Classical Novels of Chinese literature.

Plot

The novel is a fictionalised, mythologised account of the pilgrimage of the Tang dynasty Buddhist monk Xuanzang (in the novel, Tang Sanzang or Tripitaka), who travelled to India to obtain sacred sutras. The Buddha gives the monk three protectors: Sun Wukong, the rebellious Monkey King with his magic staff and 72 transformations; Zhu Bajie (Pigsy), a greedy and lazy pig spirit; and Sha Wujing (Sandy), a river ogre — along with a dragon prince who serves as the monk's horse. Over 81 ordeals, the pilgrims battle demons and monsters, in stories mixing adventure, satire, and Buddhist and Daoist allegory.

Influence

Journey to the West is among the most popular literary works in East Asia. Its characters, especially Sun Wukong, have inspired countless adaptations in opera, film, television, animation, and video games, from the Japanese Dragon Ball to modern Chinese cinema. Arthur Waley's abridged English translation, Monkey (1942), introduced the novel to many Western readers.
