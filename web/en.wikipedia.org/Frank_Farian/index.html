Frank Farian

Frank Farian (1941–2024), creator of Boney M. and Milli Vanilli.

Frank Farian (born Franz Reuther; 18 July 1941 – 23 January 2024) was a German singer and music producer who founded the disco group Boney M. and the pop duo Milli Vanilli. His productions sold hundreds of millions of records worldwide, but his career was overshadowed by the revelation that some of his acts did not sing on their own records.

Career

Born in Kirn, Germany, Farian trained as a cook before pursuing music, first as a schlager singer. In 1974 he recorded the song "Baby Do You Wanna Bump" under the name Boney M.; when it became a hit, he assembled a performing group of Caribbean singers and dancers, including Bobby Farrell and Maizie Williams. Boney M. became one of the biggest disco acts of the late 1970s with hits such as "Daddy Cool", "Sunny", "Ma Baker", "Rasputin" and "Rivers of Babylon". Farian himself sang the group's deep male vocals in the studio.

In the 1980s he created the supergroup Far Corporation and, in 1988, Milli Vanilli, fronted by dancers Rob Pilatus and Fab Morvan. The duo won the Grammy Award for Best New Artist in 1990, but when it emerged that they had not sung on their album at all — the vocals were by session singers — the Grammy was revoked in a major scandal. Farian later produced acts including La Bouche and No Mercy. He died in Miami in January 2024.
