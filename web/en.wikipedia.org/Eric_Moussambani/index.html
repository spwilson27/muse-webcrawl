Eric Moussambani Malonga (born 31 May 1978) is a swimmer from Equatorial Guinea who competed at the 2000 Summer Olympics in Sydney. Nicknamed "Eric the Eel" by the media, he became famous for completing the 100 metres freestyle in 1 minute 52.72 seconds — by far the slowest time in Olympic history for the event — while swimming alone in his heat after the other two swimmers were disqualified for false starts.

Background

Moussambani gained entry to the Olympics through a wildcard programme designed to encourage participation from developing countries. He had taken up swimming only months before the Games and trained in a 12-metre hotel pool and in rivers in Equatorial Guinea, having never before seen a 50-metre Olympic pool.

Sydney 2000

On 19 September 2000, Moussambani swam his heat alone after Karin Bare of Niger and Farkhod Oripov of Tajikistan were disqualified. He struggled visibly over the second length but finished to a standing ovation from the crowd, setting a personal best and an Equatoguinean national record. His time was more than double that of the fastest qualifiers, yet his effort was widely celebrated as embodying the Olympic spirit of participation.

Later life

Moussambani later improved his personal best to under 57 seconds and was appointed coach of the Equatorial Guinea national swimming team in 2012. He remains one of the best-known examples of the universality places that allow small nations to send athletes to the Olympics.
