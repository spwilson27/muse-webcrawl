Alcione (opera)

Alcione is a tragédie en musique in a prologue and five acts by the French composer Marin Marais (1656–1728), to a libretto by Antoine Houdar de la Motte. It was first performed at the Paris Opéra (Académie Royale de Musique) on 18 February 1706 and is Marais's most famous opera.

Synopsis

The opera retells the myth of Alcyone (Alcione) and Ceyx: the marriage of Alcione to Ceix, king of Trachis, is opposed by the sorcerer Phorbas and the treacherous Pélée, who also loves her. After a celebrated storm scene wrecks Ceix's ship, Alcione, deceived by a dream and discovering her husband's body, throws herself into the sea. Neptune restores the pair to life and transforms them into kingfishers (halcyons).

The storm

The Act IV tempest is one of the most celebrated passages of French Baroque music, with rushing scales, tremolos, and what is often cited as the first orchestral use of a double bass solo part, together with effects imitating wind and waves. The opera was frequently revived in the 18th century and its storm served as a model for later operatic tempests.

Modern revivals

Alcione has been recorded and staged in the modern early-music revival, notably by Jordi Savall and Le Concert des Nations, who performed it at the Opéra Comique in 2017.
