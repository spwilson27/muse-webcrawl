Yucatán Peninsula

The Yucatán Peninsula (Spanish: Península de Yucatán) is a large peninsula in southeastern Mexico and northern Central America, separating the Gulf of Mexico from the Caribbean Sea. It comprises the Mexican states of Yucatán, Campeche and Quintana Roo, together with parts of Belize and the Guatemalan department of Petén.

Geography

The peninsula is a flat limestone platform with almost no surface rivers; rainwater drains through sinkholes called cenotes, which were sacred to the Maya. The Chicxulub crater, formed by the asteroid impact linked to the extinction of the dinosaurs 66 million years ago, is buried beneath the northwestern coast near the town of Chicxulub.

History

The region was the heartland of the ancient Maya civilization, home to cities such as Chichén Itzá, Uxmal and Tikal. Today the peninsula is a major tourist destination, with resorts along the Riviera Maya including Cancún and Playa del Carmen.
