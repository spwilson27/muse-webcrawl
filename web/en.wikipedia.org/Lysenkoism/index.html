Lysenkoism

Leader: Trofim Lysenko
Period: c. 1929–1965 in the USSR
Doctrines: Rejection of Mendelian genetics; inheritance of acquired characteristics; vernalisation

Lysenkoism was a political and pseudoscientific campaign in the Soviet Union led by agronomist Trofim Denisovich Lysenko, which rejected Mendelian genetics and Darwinian natural selection in favour of claims that acquired characteristics could be inherited and crops transformed by environmental treatment.

Rise

Lysenko rose in the 1930s promoting vernalisation of wheat. Backed by Joseph Stalin, his doctrines became state orthodoxy after the 1948 VASKhNIL session banned genetics teaching and research. Geneticists were dismissed, imprisoned or killed; Nikolai Vavilov died in prison in 1943.

Consequences and fall

Lysenkoist agriculture contributed to poor yields in the USSR and, exported to China, to failures during the Great Leap Forward. After Nikita Khrushchev's fall in 1964, Lysenko lost power and Soviet genetics was rehabilitated. The term now denotes politically enforced rejection of science.
