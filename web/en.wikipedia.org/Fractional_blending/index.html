Fractional blending

Fractional blending is a method used in the petroleum refining and petrochemical industries in which the composition of a blended product, such as gasoline, is adjusted in small increments rather than by adding whole batches of blendstocks at once. The technique allows refiners to meet tight product specifications — for example octane rating, vapour pressure, and sulphur content — while minimising the giveaway of valuable components.

Process

In a fractional blending operation, blend components such as reformate, alkylate, isomerate, and butane are metered into a common header under the control of an online analyser system. The control system continuously measures properties of the blend and trims individual component flows in fractions of a percent, converging on the target specification as the tank or pipeline batch accumulates.

Advantages

Compared with batch blending, fractional (incremental) blending reduces quality giveaway, lowers reblend costs, and makes it easier to satisfy seasonal and regional fuel specifications. It is closely related to in-line blending and ratio blending systems used at refineries and terminals.
