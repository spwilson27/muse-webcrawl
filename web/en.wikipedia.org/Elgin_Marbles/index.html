Elgin Marbles

The Elgin Marbles, also known as the Parthenon Marbles, are a collection of Classical Greek marble sculptures made under the supervision of the architect and sculptor Phidias and his assistants. They were originally part of the temple of the Parthenon and other buildings on the Acropolis of Athens.

The marbles are displayed in the British Museum in London; Greece has long sought their return.

Removal

Between 1801 and 1812, agents of Thomas Bruce, 7th Earl of Elgin, then British ambassador to the Ottoman Empire, removed roughly half of the surviving sculptures from the Parthenon and other Acropolis buildings, claiming permission from the Ottoman authorities. The original documentation of that permission has been the subject of dispute. Elgin sold the collection to the British government in 1816, and it passed to the British Museum.

The restitution debate

Greece has formally requested the return of the sculptures since the 1980s, arguing they are an integral part of the Parthenon and were taken without the consent of the Greek people, then under Ottoman occupation. In 2009 the Acropolis Museum opened in Athens with a gallery designed to display the complete frieze. The British Museum maintains that it holds the marbles legally and that they are seen by a global audience in a world museum. The dispute remains unresolved and is one of the most prominent cases in the debate over cultural property.
