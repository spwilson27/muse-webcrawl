Abomey is a city in the Zou Department of Benin, in West Africa. It was the capital of the Kingdom of Dahomey from the seventeenth century until the French conquest in the 1890s, and it remains famous for its royal palaces, now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

According to tradition, Abomey was founded by the Fon prince Do-Aklin and rose to prominence under King Houegbadja (c. 1645–1685), who made it the seat of the Dahomey monarchy. Surrounded at its height by a great mud wall and moat, the city was the centre of a powerful militarised kingdom engaged in warfare, the slave trade through the port of Whydah (Ouidah), and later the palm oil trade. Twelve kings reigned from Abomey, each building his own palace within the royal precinct. In 1892, resisting French colonisation, King Béhanzin burned the city before fleeing; Dahomey was annexed by France shortly afterwards.

Royal Palaces

The Royal Palaces of Abomey comprise a complex of earthen palaces decorated with vivid bas-reliefs recording the deeds and symbols of the kings. Two palaces, those of Ghezo and Glélé, house the Historical Museum of Abomey, founded in 1944, displaying thrones, weapons, royal regalia and the famous appliqué banners of Dahomey. The site was inscribed on the World Heritage List in 1985 and is a major monument of Fon culture and of West African history.
