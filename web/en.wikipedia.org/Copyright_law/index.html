Copyright law

Copyright is a type of intellectual property that gives its owner the exclusive right to copy, distribute, adapt, display and perform a creative work, usually for a limited time. The creative work may be in a literary, artistic, educational or musical form. Copyright is intended to protect the original expression of an idea, not the idea itself.

Scope

Copyright arises automatically in most jurisdictions when an original work is fixed in a tangible medium. It protects books, music, films, paintings, photographs, software and other works of authorship. Related rights protect performers, broadcasters and producers of sound recordings.

Limitations

The exclusive rights are limited by doctrines such as fair use (in the United States) and fair dealing (in Commonwealth countries), which permit uses for criticism, commentary, news reporting, teaching and research. Copyright expires after a set term, after which the work enters the public domain.

International framework

The Berne Convention (1886) established minimum standards and national treatment among member states; the TRIPS Agreement later tied copyright obligations into the world trading system.
