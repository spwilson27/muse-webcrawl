The 11 Gorkha Rifles is a Gorkha regiment of the Indian Army. It was re-raised on 1 January 1948 following the independence of India, when the Gorkha regiments of the former British Indian Army were divided between India and the United Kingdom under the Tripartite Agreement between India, Nepal, and Britain. The regiments that went to India were renumbered, and the 11 Gorkha Rifles was formed from Gorkha soldiers who opted to serve in the Indian Army but whose original regiments had transferred to British service.

Composition and role

The regiment recruits Kirati Rai and Limbu Gorkhas of eastern Nepal and north-eastern India. Its battalions have served in most of India's post-independence conflicts, and the regiment is closely associated with the Kargil War of 1999, in which Captain Manoj Kumar Pandey of 1/11 Gorkha Rifles was posthumously awarded the Param Vir Chakra, India's highest military decoration.

Insignia

Like other Gorkha regiments, its soldiers are distinguished by the khukuri, the traditional Nepalese curved knife, which features in the regiment's cap badge along with crossed khukuris and the Ashoka Chakra.
