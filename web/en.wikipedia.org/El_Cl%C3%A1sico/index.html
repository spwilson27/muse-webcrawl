TeamsFC Barcelona and Real Madrid CF
First meeting13 May 1902 (Copa de la Coronación)
StadiumsCamp Nou (Barcelona); Santiago Bernabéu (Madrid)
CompetitionsLa Liga, Copa del Rey, Supercopa, UEFA Champions League

El Clásico (Spanish for "The Classic") is the name given to football matches between Spanish rivals FC Barcelona and Real Madrid. Originally referring only to meetings in the Spanish championship, the term now covers every fixture between the clubs. It is one of the most watched club matches in world sport, with a global television audience in the hundreds of millions.

Rivalry

The rivalry carries political and cultural overtones: Barcelona is the capital of Catalonia and its club a symbol of Catalan identity, while Real Madrid is associated with the Spanish capital and central establishment. The tension deepened during the Franco era, and incidents such as Luis Figo's controversial transfer from Barcelona to Madrid in 2000 — greeted with a pig's head thrown from the Camp Nou stands on his return — remain famous episodes.

Records

The fixture has featured many of the greatest players in history, including Alfredo Di Stéfano, Johan Cruyff, Raúl, Xavi, Lionel Messi and Cristiano Ronaldo; Messi is the match's all-time top scorer. Competitive meetings are finely balanced historically, with each club having won more than 100 official matches. Famous results include Barcelona's 5–0 wins in 1974 and 2010 and Real Madrid's 11–1 victory in the 1943 Copa del Generalísimo semi-final.

Use of the term

Imitation fixtures worldwide are nicknamed after it, and the term has also been applied to export matches between the clubs played abroad, including meetings in the Supercopa de España held in Saudi Arabia.
