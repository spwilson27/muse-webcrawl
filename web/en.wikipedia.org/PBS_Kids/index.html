PBS Kids

PBS Kids
Type: Children's programming brand
Country: United States
Launched: July 11, 1994 (as PTV); September 6, 1999 (as PBS Kids)
Owner: PBS

PBS Kids is the brand for most of the children's programming aired by the Public Broadcasting Service (PBS) in the United States. The brand was launched on July 11, 1994, as PTV, which was rebranded to PBS Kids on September 6, 1999. Programming, targeted at children aged 2 to 8, is designed with a curriculum focused on early literacy, mathematics, science, and social-emotional learning.

In addition to the programming block carried by PBS member stations, PBS Kids also operates a 24-hour digital television channel and cable network, relaunched on January 16, 2017, replacing the former PBS Kids Sprout (later Universal Kids) venture. PBS Kids programming is also distributed online through its website and streaming apps, which feature live streaming of the channel and on-demand episodes.

History

PBS's children's programming traces its roots to long-running series such as Sesame Street (premiered 1969), Mister Rogers' Neighborhood, and Reading Rainbow. The PTV block formalized the service's commitment to educational children's television in 1994, introducing the "P-Pals" mascots. The 1999 rebrand introduced the current logo, designed around the PBS "P-Head" surrounded by playful elements, created by the design firm that modernized the PBS identity.

Programming

Notable PBS Kids series include Arthur, Curious George, Daniel Tiger's Neighborhood, Wild Kratts, Odd Squad, Peg + Cat, Nature Cat, Ready Jet Go!, Pinkalicious & Peterrific, Molly of Denali, and Elinor Wonders Why. Classic programming such as Clifford the Big Red Dog and Super Why! also aired under the brand.
