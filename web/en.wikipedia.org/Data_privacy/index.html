Data privacy

Data privacy, also called information privacy, is the area of data protection concerned with the proper handling of data — consent, notice, and regulatory obligations — and with individuals' control over their personal information. It addresses how data is collected, shared, retained and used, and is distinct from data security, which concerns protecting data from unauthorised access, though the two overlap.

Principles and law

Modern privacy frameworks derive from the Fair Information Practice Principles developed in the 1970s and the OECD Privacy Guidelines of 1980. Major laws include the European Union's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR, 2018), which grants rights of access, erasure and portability and requires a lawful basis for processing; the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA, 2018); and sectoral laws such as HIPAA for health data in the United States. The EU Charter of Fundamental Rights treats data protection as a fundamental right.

Issues

Debates cover online tracking and behavioural advertising, data brokers, government surveillance, children's privacy, and the challenges of anonymisation, since "anonymised" datasets can often be re-identified. Approaches such as privacy by design and privacy-enhancing technologies (including differential privacy) seek to build protection into systems.
