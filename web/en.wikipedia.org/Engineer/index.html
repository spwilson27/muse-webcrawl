Engineer

Engineers, as practitioners of engineering, are professionals who invent, design, analyse, build, and test machines, complex systems, structures, gadgets, and materials to fulfil functional objectives and requirements while considering the limitations imposed by practicality, regulation, safety, and cost. The word engineer derives from the Latin words ingeniare ("to contrive, devise") and ingenium ("cleverness").

Work and roles

Engineering work applies scientific principles and mathematics — including modelling, simulation, and analysis — to real-world problems within constraints of time, budget, materials, and safety. Engineers work in research and development, design, testing, production, construction, operations, and management. Major branches include civil, mechanical, electrical, chemical, and industrial engineering, with many further specialities such as software, aerospace, biomedical, and environmental engineering.

Education and licensing

Engineers typically hold a bachelor's degree in engineering or applied science, often accredited under international agreements such as the Washington Accord. In many countries the title "professional engineer" (PE, or chartered engineer in the UK and Commonwealth) is protected by law and requires examinations, experience, and adherence to ethical codes; licensing is especially strict where public safety is involved, as in structural and civil works. Some people who operate engines or maintain equipment — locomotive engineers, stationary engineers, broadcast or sound engineers — use the title in an occupational sense without being professional engineers.

Ethics

Professional codes require engineers to hold paramount the safety, health, and welfare of the public, to practise only within their competence, and to issue objective and truthful statements. Failures of engineering ethics — from structural collapses to emissions-cheating software — are studied as case material in engineering education.

History

Engineering is as old as the pyramids, aqueducts, and siege engines of antiquity; figures such as Archimedes and Imhotep are often cited as early engineers. The profession formalised in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries with institutions such as the Institution of Civil Engineers (founded 1818) and with the spread of technical universities during the Industrial Revolution.
