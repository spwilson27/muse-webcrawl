The Second Battle of Corinth (which, in the context of the American Civil War, is usually called the Battle of Corinth) was fought October 3–4, 1862, in Corinth, Mississippi. For the second time in the Iuka–Corinth Campaign, Union Maj. Gen. William Rosecrans defeated a Confederate army, this time one commanded by Maj. Gen. Earl Van Dorn.

Background

After the Battle of Iuka, Confederate Maj. Gen. Sterling Price marched west and united with Van Dorn at Ripley, Mississippi. Their combined army of about 22,000 men advanced on Corinth, a vital rail junction of the Mobile and Ohio and Memphis and Charleston railroads, defended by Rosecrans with roughly 23,000 men behind strong earthworks.

The battle

On 3 October the Confederates drove in the Union outer line and pushed Rosecrans's men back to their inner fortifications by evening, in extreme heat with scarce water. On 4 October Van Dorn launched frontal assaults against the inner works. Texans and Missourians briefly broke into the town and stormed Battery Powell, but the attack on Battery Robinett was repulsed with terrible losses, including Col. William P. Rogers, killed leading the charge. By midday the Confederate assault had failed.

Aftermath

Van Dorn retreated toward Holly Springs, narrowly escaping encirclement at the Hatchie River. Confederate casualties were about 4,233 against 2,520 Union. Rosecrans was criticised for a slow pursuit, but Corinth remained firmly in Union hands, clearing the way for Grant's Vicksburg campaign.
