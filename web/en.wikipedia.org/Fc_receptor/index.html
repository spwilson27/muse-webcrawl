Fc receptor

An Fc receptor on a phagocyte binds the constant (Fc) region of an antibody that has coated a bacterium, triggering its engulfment.

An Fc receptor is a protein found on the surface of certain immune cells — including B lymphocytes, natural killer cells, macrophages, neutrophils, mast cells, and dendritic cells — that binds to the Fc (fragment crystallisable) region, the tail portion of an antibody. By linking antibody-coated targets to effector cells, Fc receptors connect the specificity of the humoral immune response to cellular killing, phagocytosis, and inflammation. Their name derives from the part of the antibody they bind: the Fab region binds antigen, while the Fc region binds the receptor.

Classes

Fc receptors are grouped by the class of antibody they recognise. Fc gamma receptors (FcγR) bind IgG, the most abundant antibody in blood; they include activating receptors such as FcγRI (CD64), FcγRIIA (CD32a), and FcγRIIIA (CD16a), and a single inhibitory receptor, FcγRIIB (CD32b), which carries an ITIM motif and dampens cell activation. Fc epsilon receptors (FcεRI) bind IgE with very high affinity on mast cells and basophils and mediate allergic reactions. Fc alpha receptors (FcαRI, CD89) bind IgA. The neonatal Fc receptor, FcRn, is structurally distinct; it rescues IgG and albumin from degradation and transfers maternal IgG across the placenta and from milk to the newborn gut.

Functions

When Fcγ receptors on a phagocyte are cross-linked by immune complexes or antibody-coated (opsonised) particles, they trigger phagocytosis and the release of inflammatory mediators. On natural killer cells, FcγRIIIA mediates antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC), in which the NK cell kills an antibody-coated target cell. On mast cells, cross-linking of IgE-loaded FcεRI by allergen causes degranulation, releasing histamine and producing the symptoms of allergy and anaphylaxis.

Clinical significance

Fc receptors are central to the action of therapeutic monoclonal antibodies: drugs such as rituximab and trastuzumab rely partly on FcγR-mediated ADCC and phagocytosis, and antibody engineering to enhance or silence Fc binding is a major field of drug design. Genetic polymorphisms in Fcγ receptors influence responses to infection, autoimmunity, and antibody therapy, and FcRn blockers have been developed to lower pathogenic autoantibodies in diseases such as myasthenia gravis.
