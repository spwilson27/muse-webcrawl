Kazan Khanate

The Khanate of Kazan was a medieval Tatar Turkic state that occupied the territory of former Volga Bulgaria between 1438 and 1552. Its capital was Kazan, controlling the middle Volga region in present-day Tatarstan, Mari El, Chuvashia, Mordovia and parts of Udmurtia and Bashkortostan.

Founded by Ulugh Muhammad, a former khan of the Golden Horde, the khanate was a successor state of the Golden Horde. It warred repeatedly with the Grand Duchy of Moscow over trade and tribute, conducting raids and suffering invasions in return.

Fall

In 1552, after a siege, Ivan the Terrible conquered Kazan and annexed the khanate to Russia — a landmark in Russian expansion eastward, commemorated by the building of Saint Basil's Cathedral in Moscow.

Society

The population included Volga Tatars, Chuvash, Mari, Mordvins, Udmurts and Bashkirs; Islam was the state religion, and Kazan was a center of trade between Russia, Siberia, Central Asia and Persia.
