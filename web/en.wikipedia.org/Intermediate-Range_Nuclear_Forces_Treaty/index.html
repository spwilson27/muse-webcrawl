The Intermediate-Range Nuclear Forces Treaty (INF Treaty) was an arms control agreement between the United States and the Soviet Union — later its successor state, Russia. Signed in Washington, D.C. on 8 December 1987 by US President Ronald Reagan and Soviet General Secretary Mikhail Gorbachev, the treaty required both parties to eliminate all of their land-based ballistic and cruise missiles with ranges between 500 and 5,500 kilometres (310–3,420 miles), together with their launchers and associated equipment.

The treaty was the first to eliminate an entire class of nuclear weapons.

Background

In the late 1970s the Soviet Union deployed the RSD-10 Pioneer (NATO designation SS-20 Saber), a mobile intermediate-range missile with three warheads, aimed at targets in Western Europe and Asia. In response, NATO adopted the "Dual-Track Decision" of December 1979: the alliance would deploy US Pershing II ballistic missiles and BGM-109G ground-launched cruise missiles in Europe while simultaneously pursuing arms-control negotiations. The deployments, begun in 1983, provoked large protest movements across Western Europe and a Soviet walkout from negotiations.

Terms

The treaty banned all land-based ballistic and cruise missiles — nuclear and conventional — with ranges of 500 to 5,500 km. It did not cover air- or sea-launched missiles. Verification provisions were unprecedented in their intrusiveness, including on-site inspections and continuous portal monitoring of a missile production plant at Votkinsk in the Soviet Union and Magna, Utah, in the United States.

Implementation

By the elimination deadline of 1 June 1991, a total of 2,692 missiles had been destroyed: 846 by the United States (including the Pershing II and ground-launched cruise missiles) and 1,846 by the Soviet Union (including the SS-20). It was the first treaty to abolish an entire category of nuclear weapons.

Collapse

From 2014 the United States accused Russia of violating the treaty by developing and deploying the 9M729 (SSC-8) ground-launched cruise missile, a charge Russia denied while levelling counter-accusations. On 1 February 2019 the United States announced its withdrawal, which took effect on 2 August 2019, ending the treaty.
