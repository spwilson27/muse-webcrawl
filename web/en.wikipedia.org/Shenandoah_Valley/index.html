The Shenandoah Valley is a geographic region in the U.S. Commonwealth of Virginia and the Eastern Panhandle of West Virginia, lying between the Blue Ridge Mountains to the east and the Alleghenies to the west, and drained northward by the Shenandoah River to the Potomac at Harpers Ferry. Part of the Great Appalachian Valley, it is famed for fertile farmland, limestone caverns, Civil War battlefields, and Shenandoah National Park and the Skyline Drive along its eastern ridge.

Geography

The valley runs about 230 km from near Harpers Ferry southwest to the Lexington area. Massanutten Mountain splits its northern half into the main valley and the Page Valley. Its limestone floor supports rich agriculture — historically wheat, hence its Civil War nickname "Breadbasket of the Confederacy" — and dissolved-carbonate caverns such as Luray and Skyline Caverns. Interstate 81 and the old Valley Pike (US Route 11) follow its length.

History

Native peoples used the valley for millennia before Scots-Irish and German settlers moved south from Pennsylvania in the 1730s along the Great Wagon Road. In the American Civil War the valley was a strategic corridor and granary: Stonewall Jackson's celebrated 1862 Valley Campaign, and the 1864 campaigns of Jubal Early and Philip Sheridan — whose "Burning" of farms and mills devastated the region — made it one of the war's most fought-over landscapes. Battles at Winchester, New Market, and Cedar Creek are preserved as historic sites.

Today

Poultry and dairy farming, orchards, and wine-making anchor the rural economy, while tourism centres on Shenandoah National Park (established 1935), Civil War sites, and autumn foliage.
