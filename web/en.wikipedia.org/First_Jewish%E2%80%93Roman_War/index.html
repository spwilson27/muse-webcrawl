First Jewish–Roman War

The First Jewish–Roman War (66–73/74 CE), also known as the Great Jewish Revolt, was the first of three major rebellions by the Jews of Judaea against the Roman Empire and one of the principal events leading to the destruction of Jerusalem and its Temple.

LocationJudaea
ResultRoman victory; destruction of Jerusalem and the Second Temple
BelligerentsRoman Empire vs. Judean provisional government and rebel factions

Course of the war

The revolt began in 66 CE amid rising tensions and misrule by the Roman procurator Gessius Florus. Rebels defeated local Roman forces and a punitive expedition under Cestius Gallus, but internal factional fighting weakened the Jewish cause. The emperor Nero sent Vespasian, and after Vespasian became emperor his son Titus besieged Jerusalem in 70 CE, destroying the city and the Second Temple. The last stronghold, Masada, fell in 73 or 74 CE.

Aftermath

The war devastated Judaea, enslaved or killed large numbers of its inhabitants, and transformed Judaism, which reorganized around rabbinic leadership at Yavneh under Yohanan ben Zakkai. Josephus's The Jewish War is the principal surviving account.
