Piae Cantiones

Piae Cantiones ecclesiasticae et scholasticae veterum episcoporum ("Pious songs, ecclesiastical and scholastic, of the old bishops") is a collection of late medieval Latin songs published in 1582 in Greifswald, Germany. Compiled by the Finnish student Jacobus Finno and edited by Theodoricus Petri of Nyland, it contains 74 songs sung in schools and churches in Finland and Scandinavia, many of them dating to the Middle Ages.

Title page of the first edition of Piae Cantiones (1582).

Contents

The collection is organised by subject: songs for Christmas, Easter, Pentecost, and the Trinity, prayers for the church, school songs, songs on the misery of school life, and songs of spring. Many melodies are of central European origin, adapted in Finnish cathedral schools such as those of Turku and Vyborg.

Legacy

Piae Cantiones is the source of several Christmas carols still sung today. English translations in the 19th century, notably by John Mason Neale and Thomas Helmore, brought songs such as "Christ was born on Christmas Day", "Good King Wenceslas" (to the spring carol tune Tempus adest floridum), and the carols drawn from In dulci jubilo traditions to a wide audience. The collection was reprinted in Finland in the 17th century and remains an important source for early Scandinavian music.
