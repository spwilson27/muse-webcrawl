The First Congo War (1996–1997) was a conflict fought primarily in Zaire, culminating in the overthrow of the long-time dictator Mobutu Sese Seko by rebel forces led by Laurent-Désiré Kabila and backed by Rwanda, Uganda, and other neighbouring states. The war grew out of the aftermath of the 1994 Rwandan genocide and ended with Kabila renaming the country the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

Background

After the Rwandan genocide of 1994, some two million Hutu refugees — including ex-FAR soldiers and Interahamwe militiamen responsible for the genocide — fled into eastern Zaire, where their camps were militarised and used as bases for raids into Rwanda. The new Rwandan government of Paul Kagame demanded action, while Mobutu's decaying Zairean state, weakened by decades of kleptocracy and the end of Cold War support, proved unable or unwilling to disarm the camps. Tensions also rose over the treatment of the Banyamulenge, Congolese Tutsi of South Kivu threatened with expulsion.

Course of the war

In October 1996, Rwandan, Ugandan, and Burundian forces, together with the newly formed Alliance of Democratic Forces for the Liberation of Congo-Zaire (AFDL) under Kabila, attacked the refugee camps in the Kivus. Hundreds of thousands of refugees fled westward into the forest, where large numbers died or were killed; many others returned to Rwanda. The ill-paid and demoralised Zairean armed forces largely collapsed or fled before the advance, and the rebels captured the mineral-rich east, then Lubumbashi, and finally entered Kinshasa on 17 May 1997 after Mobutu fled into exile. Kabila declared himself president and restored the country's former name, the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

Aftermath

Kabila's victory did not bring stability. Relations with his Rwandan and Ugandan backers soured, and his attempt in 1998 to expel them triggered the Second Congo War (1998–2003), a far larger conflict that drew in numerous African states and caused millions of deaths. Kabila was assassinated in 2001 and succeeded by his son Joseph Kabila.
