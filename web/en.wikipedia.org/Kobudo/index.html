Kobudo

Kobudō (古武道, "old martial way") is a Japanese term for traditional martial arts. In common usage it often refers specifically to Okinawan kobudō, the weapons systems of Okinawa, and more generally to the classical martial traditions (koryū) of Japan.

Okinawan kobudō

Okinawan kobudō trains weapons adapted in large part from farming and fishing implements, including the bō (staff), sai, nunchaku, tonfa, kama (sickle) and eku (oar). Its forms (kata) were preserved by masters such as Taira Shinken, who systematized the tradition in the 20th century.

Koryū

In mainland Japan, kobudō denotes the classical schools of the samurai era teaching kenjutsu, jujutsu and other battlefield arts, transmitted through formal lineages.
