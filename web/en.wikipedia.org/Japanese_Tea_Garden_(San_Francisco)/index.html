Japanese Tea Garden (San Francisco)

The Japanese Tea Garden in San Francisco, California, located in Golden Gate Park, is the oldest public Japanese garden in the United States. It was originally created as part of the California Midwinter International Exposition of 1894 and covers about five acres (two hectares), with winding paths, ponds, a tea house and carefully placed stones, plants and sculptures.

Location: Golden Gate Park, San Francisco, California

Created: 1894

Designer: Makoto Hagiwara

Area: ~5 acres (2 ha)

History

After the 1894 exposition closed, the Japanese gardener Makoto Hagiwara was hired to develop the temporary village exhibit into a permanent garden. He and his family lived on the site and tended it for decades, importing plants, birds and koi from Japan and adding features such as the Drum Bridge and the tea house. The Hagiwara family was removed during the Second World War under the internment of Japanese Americans and did not return; the garden was temporarily renamed the "Oriental Tea Garden" before its original name was restored after the war.

Features include the five-storey pagoda, the Zen Garden added in 1953, the bronze Buddha cast in Japan in 1790, and the Lantern of Peace, a gift from Japanese schoolchildren in 1953. The garden is also associated in local tradition with the popularisation of the fortune cookie in America, credited to Hagiwara's tea house.
