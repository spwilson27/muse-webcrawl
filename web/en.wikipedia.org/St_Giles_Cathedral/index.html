St Giles' Cathedral

St Giles' Cathedral
Location: Royal Mile, Edinburgh, Scotland
Denomination: Church of Scotland
Founded: 12th century
Notable feature: Crown steeple

St Giles' Cathedral, formally the High Kirk of Edinburgh, is a parish church of the Church of Scotland on the Royal Mile in Edinburgh. Dedicated to Saint Giles, the patron saint of Edinburgh, it is famous for its distinctive crown steeple.

History

A church has stood on the site since the 12th century; the present building largely dates from the 14th century onwards. St Giles played a central role in the Scottish Reformation, where John Knox preached, and in the events of 1637 that helped spark the Wars of the Three Kingdoms.

Thistle Chapel

The Thistle Chapel, added in 1911, is the chapel of the Order of the Thistle, Scotland's order of chivalry. Despite its name, St Giles has not been a cathedral (seat of a bishop) for most of its history.
