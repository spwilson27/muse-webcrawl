Cornet

The cornet is a brass instrument similar to the trumpet but distinguished by its conical bore, more compact shape and mellower tone quality. It is usually pitched in B♭, with an E♭ soprano version also in use. The cornet is not to be confused with the medieval and Renaissance cornett, an entirely different wood-and-leather instrument.

History

The cornet developed from the post horn in the early 19th century, with valves added in the 1820s in France. It became a virtuoso solo instrument in the hands of players such as Jean-Baptiste Arban, whose Grande méthode remains a standard study. In early jazz, the cornet was the lead horn of New Orleans bands, played by King Oliver and Louis Armstrong before the trumpet took over.

Construction

Like the trumpet, the cornet has three piston valves and a similar length of tubing, but a larger proportion of its bore is conical, giving it a warmer, rounder sound. Its leadpipe and mouthpiece are smaller than the trumpet's, and it has a characteristic shepherd's-crook bend in the leadpipe on many models.

Use

The cornet is a central instrument of the British brass band, where the front row of solo cornets leads the ensemble. It is also used in wind bands, fanfare bands and some orchestral works, particularly French repertoire.
