Belur Math (Bengali: বেলুড় মঠ) is the headquarters of the Ramakrishna Math and Ramakrishna Mission, founded by Swami Vivekananda, the chief disciple of the 19th-century saint Ramakrishna Paramahamsa. It is located in Belur, Howrah, on the western bank of the Hooghly River in West Bengal, India, across the river from the Dakshineswar Kali Temple where Ramakrishna spent most of his life.

History

Vivekananda established the Ramakrishna Math in 1897 after returning from the West. With funds raised partly from Western disciples — including a donation from Miss Josephine MacLeod and a loan arranged through Mrs. Ole Bull — he purchased about 5 acres of land at Belur in 1898, and the monastic order moved there from Alambazar. On 9 December 1898 Vivekananda consecrated the site by installing the relics (atmaramer kauta) of Ramakrishna. The site grew to about 40 acres. Vivekananda spent his final years largely at Belur Math and died there on 4 July 1902.

The Ramakrishna Temple

The centrepiece of the campus is the Sri Ramakrishna Temple, consecrated on 14 January 1938. Vivekananda conceived its design as a synthesis of temple, mosque, and church architecture — symbolising the harmony of religions at the heart of Ramakrishna's teaching — and the plans were drawn up by his brother-disciple Swami Vijnanananda, a trained engineer. The temple is about 112 feet high, built in chunar stone and concrete, with a marble statue of Ramakrishna over the sanctum. Also on the grounds are temples and memorials at the cremation spots of Vivekananda, Sarada Devi (the Holy Mother), and Swami Brahmananda, the first president of the order, as well as the old Math building, Vivekananda's room, and the Ramakrishna Museum.

Activities

From Belur Math the Ramakrishna Math (the monastic order, formally registered as a trust in 1901) and the Ramakrishna Mission (the philanthropic association founded in 1897 and registered in 1909) coordinate more than 200 branch centres in India and abroad engaged in education, healthcare, disaster relief, and rural development. The Math also publishes the journals Prabuddha Bharata (English, founded 1896) and Udbodhan (Bengali, founded 1899). Durga Puja and Kali Puja are celebrated at the Math with large public attendance; the Durga Puja is notable for the Kumari Puja, the worship of a young girl as the Divine Mother, which Vivekananda introduced in 1901.
