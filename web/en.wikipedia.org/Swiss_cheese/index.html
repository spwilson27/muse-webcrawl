Swiss cheese

Swiss cheese
Origin: Switzerland / generic term abroad
Texture: Medium-hard
Characteristic: Large holes ("eyes")
Examples: Emmental, Gruyère, Appenzeller

Swiss cheese is a general name in North America for several varieties of cheese that resemble the traditional cheeses of Switzerland, most famously Emmental, with its characteristic large holes known as "eyes." In Switzerland itself, hundreds of distinct regional cheeses are produced, and the term "Swiss cheese" is not used generically.

The eyes

The holes in Emmental-type cheeses are formed by bubbles of carbon dioxide released during fermentation by the bacterium Propionibacterium freudenreichii. The size of the eyes depends on temperature, acidity, and ripening time; cheeses without holes are described as "blind." Research has also linked eye formation partly to microscopic hay particles in the milk.

Varieties

Well-known Swiss cheeses include Emmental, Gruyère (which is essentially eyeless), Appenzeller, Sbrinz, Tête de Moine, and Vacherin. Many are protected by appellation of origin (AOP) status. Outside Switzerland, mass-produced "Swiss cheese" is typically a mild, nutty, pale-yellow cheese modelled on Emmental and widely used in sandwiches.
