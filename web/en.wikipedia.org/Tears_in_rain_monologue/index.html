Tears in rain monologue

The "Tears in rain" monologue is a speech delivered by the replicant Roy Batty, portrayed by Rutger Hauer, in the closing scenes of the 1982 science fiction film Blade Runner, directed by Ridley Scott. It is widely regarded as one of the most famous monologues in cinema history.

The speech

"I've seen things you people wouldn't believe… Attack ships on fire off the shoulder of Orion. I watched C-beams glitter in the dark near the Tannhäuser Gate. All those moments will be lost in time, like tears in rain. Time to die."

Origins

The speech was substantially rewritten by Hauer the night before filming. Screenwriter David Peoples had written a longer version; Hauer cut much of it and added the closing line about tears in rain. Scott accepted the changes, and the scene was filmed as delivered.

Legacy

The monologue has been widely quoted, parodied and referenced in popular culture. In his autobiography, Hauer wrote that the speech distilled Batty's character: his awareness of mortality and his desire that his memories matter. The line "lost in time, like tears in rain" is frequently cited in discussions of the film's themes of memory, empathy and what it means to be human.
