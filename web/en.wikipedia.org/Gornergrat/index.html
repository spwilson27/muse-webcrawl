Gornergrat


The Gornergrat with the Matterhorn in the background.

The Gornergrat (3,135 m / 10,285 ft) is a mountain ridge in the Pennine Alps of Switzerland, overlooking the Gorner Glacier near Zermatt in the canton of Valais. From its summit station, visitors view one of the most celebrated panoramas in the Alps, taking in the Monte Rosa massif, the Lyskamm, the Breithorn and the Matterhorn.

Gornergrat Railway

The Gornergrat is reached from Zermatt by the Gornergrat Railway, a rack railway opened in 1898 and the first fully electrified rack line in Switzerland. The 9.3 km line climbs to the Gornergrat station at 3,089 m, among the highest railway stations in Europe. The ridge is a popular starting point for hikes and, in winter, part of the Zermatt ski area.

Observatory and hotel

The Kulmhotel at the summit, at 3,120 m one of the highest hotels in the Alps, houses two astronomical observatory towers in its turrets. The south tower formerly held a 600 mm telescope used by the University of Bern, and the Gornergrat has hosted infrared and submillimetre astronomy, including the KOSMA 3-metre radio telescope and the Gornergrat Infrared Telescope (TIRGO). The dry, stable air above the ridge made it a favoured site for observing before larger telescopes moved to higher and drier locations abroad.
