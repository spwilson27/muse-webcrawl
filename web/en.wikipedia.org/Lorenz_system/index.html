Lorenz system
Introduced: 1963, by Edward Norton Lorenz
Field: Dynamical systems, chaos theory
Famous for: The "butterfly" strange attractor
[Image placeholder: The Lorenz attractor — the butterfly-shaped trajectory in three-dimensional phase space]

The Lorenz system is a system of ordinary differential equations first studied by the meteorologist Edward Norton Lorenz in 1963. It is notable for having chaotic solutions for certain parameter values and initial conditions, and its strange attractor — a double-lobed, butterfly-shaped set of trajectories — became an iconic image of chaos theory and the origin of the term "butterfly effect".

The equations

dx/dt = σ(y − x)
dy/dt = x(ρ − z) − y
dz/dt = xy − βz

Lorenz derived the equations as a drastically simplified model of atmospheric convection — the motion of fluid heated from below, as in a Rayleigh–Bénard cell. The variables x, y and z represent the rate of convective overturning, and horizontal and vertical temperature variations. The classic parameter values are σ = 10, β = 8/3 and ρ = 28.

Chaos and sensitive dependence

Lorenz discovered that tiny differences in initial conditions produced wildly different trajectories — the sensitive dependence on initial conditions that limits long-range weather prediction. His 1963 paper "Deterministic Nonperiodic Flow" is a founding document of chaos theory.
