High-speed rail in France

High-speed rail in France is provided by the TGV (train à grande vitesse, 'high-speed train'), operated by the national railway company SNCF on dedicated high-speed lines called LGVs (lignes à grande vitesse). France was the second country, after Japan, to develop a dedicated high-speed rail system, and its network of roughly 2,800 km of LGV is one of the largest in Europe.

History

The first LGV, between Paris and Lyon (LGV Sud-Est), opened in 1981 and cut the Paris–Lyon journey to two hours. It was followed by LGV Atlantique (1989–90), LGV Nord (1993, connecting to the Channel Tunnel), LGV Méditerranée (2001), LGV Est (2007, extended 2016), LGV Rhin-Rhône (2011), and lines to Bordeaux and Brittany in 2017. The network radiates from Paris, enabling TGV trains to continue onto conventional lines to serve many more destinations.

Technology and records

TGVs are articulated electric multiple units with power cars at each end, running at up to 320 km/h in commercial service. On 3 April 2007, a specially prepared TGV V150 set the world speed record for conventional wheeled trains at 574.8 km/h (357.2 mph) on LGV Est. Trains are built by Alstom; the latest generation, the TGV M (Avelia Horizon), entered development in the 2020s with greater capacity and energy efficiency.

Services

In addition to domestic services, international TGV trains link France with Belgium, Germany, Switzerland, Italy, Spain, and the United Kingdom (via Eurostar, now part of the Eurostar Group). The low-cost Ouigo brand, launched in 2013, offers simplified high-speed services. The TGV system has been credited with reviving the French railways and reshaping travel patterns between French cities.
