Provisional government

A provisional government, also called an interim government, an emergency government, or a transitional government, is a temporary government formed during a political transition, typically following the collapse of a previous administration, a revolution, a coup d'état, or the end of a war or foreign occupation. Its purpose is to manage the state until a new permanent government can be established, often through elections or a constituent assembly.

Formation and functions

Provisional governments are usually formed in a political vacuum when the previous regime has lost legitimacy or been overthrown. They typically pledge to restore order, maintain essential services, organise elections, and draft or restore a constitution. Their legitimacy may rest on popular support, recognition by other states, or their role as a neutral caretaker.

Examples

Historical examples include the French Provisional Government of 1848 and of 1944–1946, the Russian Provisional Government of 1917, the Provisional Government of Ireland in 1922, and numerous post-conflict administrations such as the Coalition Provisional Authority in Iraq (2003–2004).
