Chen Kaige (born 12 August 1952) is a Chinese film director and a leading figure of the "Fifth Generation" of Chinese cinema. His films are known for their visual flair and epic narratives and have done much to shape the international image of Chinese film.

Early life

Chen was born in Beijing, the son of film director Chen Huai'ai. During the Cultural Revolution his schooling was interrupted and he was sent to Xishuangbanna in Yunnan to work on a rubber plantation before joining the People's Liberation Army. In 1978 he entered the Beijing Film Academy as part of its famous first post-Cultural Revolution class, alongside Zhang Yimou and Tian Zhuangzhuang.

Career

His directorial debut, Yellow Earth (1984), photographed by Zhang Yimou, was a landmark of the new Chinese cinema. He gained worldwide fame with Farewell My Concubine (1993), which won the Palme d'Or at Cannes — still the only Chinese-language film to do so — and received Academy Award nominations. Later works include Temptress Moon (1996), The Emperor and the Assassin (1998), the Hollywood film Killing Me Softly (2002), the fantasy epic The Promise (2005), Forever Enthralled (2008), Caught in the Web (2012) and Monk Comes Down the Mountain (2015).
