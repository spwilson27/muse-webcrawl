The Melbourne Cricket Ground (MCG), also known locally as "the 'G", is a sports stadium in Yarra Park, Melbourne, Australia. Founded and managed by the Melbourne Cricket Club (MCC), it is the largest stadium in the Southern Hemisphere and the second-largest cricket stadium in the world, with a capacity of 100,024. The MCG is the traditional home of the Boxing Day Test match, the AFL Grand Final, and was the main stadium of the 1956 Summer Olympics and the 2006 Commonwealth Games.

History

The MCC was granted the site in 1853, and the ground hosted the first intercolonial cricket match in 1856. In March 1877 it staged the first-ever Test match, between Australia and England, won by Australia by 45 runs — a margin famously repeated in the Centenary Test of 1977. The ground has been rebuilt in stages: the Great Southern Stand (now Shane Warne Stand) opened in 1992, and the Northern Stand redevelopment was completed for the 2006 Commonwealth Games.

Events

Besides cricket — including the finals of the 1992 and 2015 Cricket World Cups and the record 2015 final crowd of 93,013 — the MCG hosts Australian rules football throughout the winter, including the AFL Grand Final each spring. The all-time record attendance of 121,696 was set at the 1970 VFL Grand Final. The stadium has also hosted Olympic soccer, rugby, and concerts, and its crowd of 143,000 (including standing room estimates disputed) for a 1959 Billy Graham crusade remains among the largest gatherings recorded there. Notably, the ground hosted the 2023 FIFA Women's World Cup matches in its precinct stadiums while serving as an iconic sporting landmark.

National Sports Museum

The MCG houses the Australian Sports Museum (formerly National Sports Museum), containing memorabilia including items from Sir Donald Bradman and the Olympic Games, and the MCC Museum. The stadium is listed on the Victorian Heritage Register and is regarded as one of Australia's most significant cultural sites.
