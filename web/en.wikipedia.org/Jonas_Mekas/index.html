Jonas Mekas (24 December 1922 – 23 January 2019) was a Lithuanian-American filmmaker, poet and artist, often called the "godfather of American avant-garde cinema". His lyrical diary films, shot on handheld 16mm cameras, chronicled his life and the New York art world over seven decades.

Early life

Mekas was born in the village of Semeniškiai, Lithuania. During the Second World War he and his brother Adolfas were seized by the Nazis and spent the war in forced labour camps and displaced-persons camps, where Jonas kept diaries and wrote poetry. They emigrated to New York in 1949.

Career

In New York, Mekas co-founded the magazine Film Culture (1954) and wrote the influential "Movie Journal" column in The Village Voice, championing independent and underground film. He helped found the Film-Makers' Cooperative distribution network and, in 1970, Anthology Film Archives. His films include Guns of the Trees (1961), The Brig (1964), Walden (1969), Reminiscences of a Journey to Lithuania (1972) and the five-hour diary film As I Was Moving Ahead Occasionally I Saw Brief Glimpses of Beauty (2000).

Legacy

Mekas's celebration of everyday moments shaped generations of experimental filmmakers. His archives are held in Lithuania and New York, and he continued filming and posting video diaries online into his nineties.
