White Star Line

The White Star Line was a British shipping line, founded in Liverpool in 1845 and most famous for its fleet of luxury ocean liners, including RMS Titanic, RMS Olympic and HMHS Britannic. The company's ships were distinguished by black hulls with a buff (gold) funnel topped in black, and names ending in -ic.

History

The original White Star Line carried emigrants to Australia during the gold rush era, including with the famous clipper Red Jacket. After the first company failed in 1867, Thomas Henry Ismay bought the name and flag and rebuilt it as a transatlantic steamship line in partnership with the Belfast shipbuilders Harland & Wolff, who built nearly all White Star ships. Under Ismay and his son J. Bruce Ismay, the line emphasised comfort and size over speed.

The Olympic class and Titanic

In 1908 the line ordered three enormous liners: Olympic (1911), Titanic (1912) and Britannic (1914). Titanic struck an iceberg and sank on her maiden voyage on 15 April 1912 with the loss of more than 1,500 lives, one of the deadliest peacetime maritime disasters. Britannic, serving as a hospital ship, struck a naval mine and sank in 1916; Olympic alone survived, serving until 1935.

Decline and merger

In 1902 the line was absorbed into J. P. Morgan's International Mercantile Marine trust. Financial troubles in the 1920s and the Great Depression forced White Star to merge with its rival Cunard in 1934, forming Cunard-White Star Line; the White Star name was dropped in 1950. The last surviving White Star vessel, the tender SS Nomadic, is preserved in Belfast.
