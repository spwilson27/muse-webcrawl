Psychosis

SpecialtyPsychiatry
SymptomsDelusions, hallucinations, disorganised thinking and speech
CausesMental illness, trauma, sleep deprivation, medical conditions, substances
TreatmentAntipsychotics, psychotherapy, social support

Psychosis is a condition of the mind or psyche that results in difficulties determining what is real and what is not. Symptoms may include delusions and hallucinations, as well as incoherent speech and behaviour inappropriate for the situation. There may also be sleep problems, social withdrawal, lack of motivation and difficulties carrying out daily activities.

Causes

Psychosis can occur as a feature of mental disorders such as schizophrenia, schizoaffective disorder and bipolar disorder, in severe depression, and following trauma or extreme stress. It can also result from medical conditions, sleep deprivation, and substances including cannabis, amphetamines and hallucinogens. Postpartum psychosis is a psychiatric emergency occurring after childbirth.

Treatment

Treatment typically involves antipsychotic medication together with psychosocial interventions such as cognitive behavioural therapy for psychosis, family interventions and supported employment. Early intervention services aim to reduce the duration of untreated psychosis, which is associated with better outcomes.

Epidemiology

About 3% of people experience psychosis at some point in their lives. Onset is most often in late adolescence or early adulthood.
