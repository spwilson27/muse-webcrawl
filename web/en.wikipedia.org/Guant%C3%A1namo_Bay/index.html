Guantánamo Bay

Guantánamo Bay (Spanish: Bahía de Guantánamo) is a bay in Guantánamo Province at the southeastern end of Cuba, opening onto the Caribbean Sea. It is best known internationally as the site of the United States Guantanamo Bay Naval Base, held by the US under a lease dating to 1903, and of the detention camp established there in 2002. The bay itself is one of the finest natural harbours in the Caribbean.

History

Christopher Columbus landed in the bay in 1494 and named it Puerto Grande. During the War of Jenkins' Ear, British forces occupied the bay in 1741 and renamed it Cumberland Bay. The United States seized the area during the Spanish–American War of 1898 and, under the Cuban–American Treaty of 1903, leased the land around the bay's southern portion for a naval station. The 1934 Treaty of Relations made the lease effectively permanent unless both governments agree to end it or the US abandons the base; the Cuban government has denounced the arrangement since 1959 and refuses to cash the annual rent cheques.

Naval base and detention camp

The naval base is the oldest overseas US naval base and hosts several thousand service members and contractors. In January 2002 the base's Camp X-Ray and its successors began holding detainees captured in the "war on terror", drawing sustained international legal and human-rights controversy. Successive US administrations have pledged to close the detention facility while the naval base continues to operate.
