Sauternes (wine)

TypeSweet white dessert wine
RegionSauternais, Graves, Bordeaux, France
GrapesSémillon, Sauvignon blanc, Muscadelle
Key processNoble rot (Botrytis cinerea)
Premier estateChâteau d'Yquem (Premier Cru Supérieur, 1855)

Sauternes is a French sweet white wine produced in the Sauternais district of the Graves section of Bordeaux. It is made principally from Sémillon, Sauvignon blanc and Muscadelle grapes that have been affected by Botrytis cinerea, the "noble rot" that shrivels the berries and concentrates their sugars and flavours. Sauternes is among the world's most celebrated dessert wines, capable of ageing for many decades and developing deep golden hues and flavours of honey, apricot and marmalade.

Geography and climate

The appellation covers the communes of Sauternes, Barsac, Bommes, Fargues and Preignac, some 40 km south-east of the city of Bordeaux, along the small Ciron river. The Ciron's cold water flowing into the warmer Garonne generates autumn morning mists, which encourage botrytis, followed by dry sunny afternoons that prevent the rot from turning destructive. Barsac wines may be labelled either Barsac or Sauternes.

Production

Harvesting Sauternes is uniquely laborious: pickers pass through the vineyard many times (the tries), selecting only individual berries or bunches that have been sufficiently raisined by noble rot. Yields are among the lowest in Bordeaux — Château d'Yquem famously averages around nine hectolitres per hectare — and in poor years an estate may make no wine at all under its own label. Fermentation is slow and stops naturally at around 13–14% alcohol, leaving substantial residual sugar, and the wines are typically aged in oak barrels for eighteen months to three years. Because production costs are so high, the wines are frequently sold in half-bottles.

Classification and famous estates

In the Bordeaux Wine Official Classification of 1855, drawn up for the Paris Exposition, Sauternes and Barsac were classified alongside the red wines of the Médoc. Château d'Yquem alone was rated Premier Cru Supérieur, a class of its own; below it stand the Premiers Crus, including Climens, Suduiraut, Coutet and La Tour Blanche, and the Deuxièmes Crus. Yquem's legendary vintages and its consistent quality have made it the benchmark for sweet wine worldwide.

Taste and service

Classic Sauternes balances intense sweetness with fresh acidity, showing aromas of honeysuckle, peach, dried apricot, pineapple, crème brûlée and toasted nuts, deepening with age toward orange marmalade and caramel. It is served chilled, classically with foie gras, blue cheeses such as Roquefort, or fruit-based desserts, and can itself serve as a dessert.
