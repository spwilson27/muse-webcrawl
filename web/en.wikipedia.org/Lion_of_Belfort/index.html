The Lion of Belfort (French: Lion de Belfort) is a colossal monumental sculpture in Belfort, France, created by Frédéric Auguste Bartholdi — the sculptor of the Statue of Liberty. Carved from blocks of red sandstone from the Vosges and set against the rock below the city's citadel, the lion measures 22 metres long and 11 metres high. It commemorates the heroic resistance of Belfort during its siege in the Franco-Prussian War.

Background: the siege of Belfort

During the Franco-Prussian War (1870–71), Belfort, one of the few French strongholds to hold out, resisted a Prussian siege from November 1870 until February 1871 under the command of Colonel Denfert-Rochereau — himself nicknamed "the Lion of Belfort". The garrison surrendered only on direct orders from the French government after the armistice, marching out with the honours of war. In the Treaty of Frankfurt, Belfort and its surrounding territory (the future Territoire de Belfort) remained French, unlike the rest of Alsace, in large part because of this resistance.

The monument

Bartholdi, an Alsatian who had himself served in the war, proposed the monument in 1871–72. After years of controversy over funding and design, construction proceeded between 1875 and 1880, built in situ from sandstone blocks on a terrace below the citadel. The lion is depicted crouching, defiant, its paw resting on a broken arrow, gazing toward the east — toward the lost provinces — and is conventionally read as a symbol of French refusal to accept defeat rather than of triumph: its attitude is one of defence and mourning, and the dedication honoured the fallen, including a monument to the dead designed with an ossuary.

Legacy and replicas

The Lion is the emblem of Belfort and lends its name and image to local institutions and the city's football supporters. A smaller bronze reduction of the Lion stands in the Place Denfert-Rochereau in Paris, and another copy is found in Montreal's Dorchester Square. The name Denfert-Rochereau — borne both by the Paris square and a Paris Métro/RER station — likewise honours the commander the Lion commemorates.
