Kurt Cobain

Kurt Donald Cobain (February 20, 1967 – April 5, 1994) was an American musician, singer, and songwriter, best known as the lead vocalist, guitarist, and primary songwriter of the rock band Nirvana. Through his angst-fueled songwriting and anti-establishment persona, Cobain became the reluctant spokesman of Generation X, and Nirvana's 1991 single "Smells Like Teen Spirit" propelled alternative rock into the mainstream.

Early life

Cobain was born in Aberdeen, Washington, and grew up in the nearby logging town, marked by his parents' divorce when he was nine. He formed Nirvana in 1987 with bassist Krist Novoselic in Aberdeen; drummer Dave Grohl joined in 1990.

Nirvana and fame

Nirvana's debut album Bleach (1989) appeared on Sub Pop. Nevermind (1991) — with "Smells Like Teen Spirit," "Come as You Are," and "Lithium" — displaced Michael Jackson atop the Billboard chart, sold over 30 million copies, and made Cobain a global star. In Utero (1993), produced by Steve Albini, was rawer and more abrasive. The band's MTV Unplugged performance in November 1993 is widely regarded as one of the greatest live albums.

Personal struggles and death

Cobain suffered chronic stomach pain, depression, and heroin addiction, and was ambivalent about fame. On April 5, 1994, he died at his Seattle home from a self-inflicted gunshot wound; his body was found three days later. He was 27, joining the so-called "27 Club." His death is widely considered the end of the grunge era.

Legacy

Cobain is routinely ranked among the most influential musicians of the 20th century; Nirvana was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2014. His journals, artwork, and home recordings have been published posthumously, and a memorial park stands in his native Aberdeen.
