Abbotsford House

Abbotsford
Location: Scottish Borders, near Melrose
Built: 1811–1825
Owner/creator: Sir Walter Scott
Style: Scots Baronial (proto-example)

Abbotsford is a historic country house in the Scottish Borders, near Galashiels on the south bank of the River Tweed. It is the former home of the novelist and poet Sir Walter Scott, who bought the modest farmhouse in 1811 and rebuilt it, in stages, into a romantic mansion filled with historical artefacts and armour.

Scott's home

Scott named the property after the abbots of Melrose Abbey who once forded the Tweed nearby. His study, library (with some 9,000 volumes), armoury, and dining room survive much as he left them at his death in 1832. Abbotsford is widely credited with inspiring the Scots Baronial revival in architecture.

Today

The house opened to visitors in 1833, the year after Scott's death, and remains a museum and visitor attraction, managed by the Abbotsford Trust.
