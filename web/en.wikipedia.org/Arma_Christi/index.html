Arma Christi

Arma Christi

Also known as: Instruments of the Passion

Subject: Objects associated with the Passion of Jesus

Tradition: Christian iconography

The Arma Christi ("weapons of Christ") or Instruments of the Passion are the objects associated with the Passion of Jesus Christ in Christian symbolism and art. They include the cross, the crown of thorns, the nails, the lance that pierced Christ's side, the sponge soaked in vinegar, the pillar of the flagellation, the scourge, and other implements of the arrest, trial, and crucifixion.

History and devotion

Relics of the Instruments were venerated from the early Middle Ages, and the objects came to be depicted systematically in art from around the ninth century onward. In late medieval devotion the Arma Christi were treated almost as heraldic devices — "Christ's arms" in the sense of a coat of arms — and were believed to offer spiritual protection. Indulgences were attached to prayers said before images of the instruments, and they appeared on amulets, in books of hours, and in church carving.

The instruments

  • The True Cross and the titulus (INRI)
  • The Crown of Thorns
  • The Holy Nails
  • The Holy Lance
  • The sponge and reed
  • The column and scourge of the Flagellation
  • The seamless robe and the dice cast for it
  • The ladder, hammer, and pincers of the Deposition
  • The chalice of the Last Supper and the cock that crowed
  • The lantern, swords, and ear of Malchus from the arrest in Gethsemane

In art

The instruments are frequently shown held by angels surrounding Christ as the Man of Sorrows, or arranged as a decorative border in illuminated manuscripts. They also feature prominently in the Mass of Saint Gregory image, in which the instruments appear on the altar behind the celebrating pope.
