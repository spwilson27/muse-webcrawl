Aklan

Aklan, officially the Province of Aklan, is a province in the Western Visayas region of the Philippines. Its capital is Kalibo. Located in the northwestern portion of Panay Island, Aklan is bordered by Antique to the southwest, Capiz to the east, and the Sibuyan Sea and Romblon to the north. The province is best known as the gateway to Boracay Island, part of its municipality of Malay and one of the country's most famous beach destinations, and for the Ati-Atihan festival held in Kalibo each January.

History

Local tradition holds that Aklan is among the oldest provinces in the Philippines, settled in the 13th century by datus said to have fled Borneo — a narrative recorded in the contested Maragtas legend. Aklan was organized as a separate province on 25 April 1956, when it was split from Capiz by Republic Act No. 1414. The Aklanon language and the piña-cloth weaving tradition are distinctive elements of its culture.

Geography and economy

The province comprises 17 municipalities. Its terrain ranges from coastal plains to the mountainous interior along the Antique border. Agriculture — rice, coconut, and abaca — long anchored the economy, but tourism centred on Boracay now dominates; the island's six-month closure in 2018 for environmental rehabilitation drew global attention. Kalibo International Airport and Caticlan's Godofredo P. Ramos Airport serve as the main air gateways.
