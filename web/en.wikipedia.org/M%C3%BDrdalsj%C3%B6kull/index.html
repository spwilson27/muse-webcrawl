Mýrdalsjökull (Icelandic for "(the) mire dale glacier" or "(the) mud valley glacier") is an ice cap in the south of Iceland. It is the country's fourth largest glacier, covering about 596 square kilometres. Beneath the ice lies the caldera of the active volcano Katla.

Geography

The ice cap's highest peak rises to about 1,493 metres. Outlet glaciers, including Sólheimajökull and Kötlujökull, descend from the cap toward the coastal plain. The Mýrdalssandur black sand floodplain south of the glacier was built up by glacial outburst floods and is crossed by Iceland's Ring Road.

Katla and jökulhlaups

Katla is one of Iceland's most dangerous volcanoes; its eruptions, typically occurring beneath the ice, melt huge volumes of water that escape as catastrophic glacial outburst floods called jökulhlaups. The 1918 eruption produced one of the largest floods in recorded history and extended the coastline measurably. Smaller subglacial events, such as the 2011 unrest that washed away a bridge on the Ring Road, show the volcano remains active and closely monitored.
