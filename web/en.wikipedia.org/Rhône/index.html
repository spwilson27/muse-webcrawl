Rhône

The Rhône is a major river in France and Switzerland, rising in the Alps and flowing west and south through Lake Geneva and south-eastern France before discharging into the Mediterranean Sea near Arles. Its length is about 813 kilometres (505 mi).

Course

The Rhône begins as the meltwater of the Rhône Glacier in the Valais canton of Switzerland, flows through Lake Geneva, and enters France near Lyon, where it is joined by the Saône. From Lyon it flows generally south between the Alps and the Massif Central, receiving tributaries including the Isère, Drôme, Ardèche, Durance and Gardon, before splitting into the Grand Rhône and Petit Rhône in the Camargue delta west of Marseille.

History

The river was an important trade route from Roman times; the Greek city of Massalia (Marseille) traded up the Rhône valley, and the river marked part of the frontier between Roman Gaul and the territories beyond. Its swift current and seasonal floods long made navigation difficult, but in the 20th century the Compagnie Nationale du Rhône built a series of hydroelectric dams, locks and canals, generating power, enabling navigation, and supporting irrigation.

Wine

The banks of the northern Rhône are famous for wines made from Syrah grapes, including Hermitage and Côte-Rôtie, while the southern Rhône produces blends such as Châteauneuf-du-Pape and Côtes du Rhône.
