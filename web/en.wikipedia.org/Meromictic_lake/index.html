Meromictic lake

A meromictic lake is a lake which has layers of water that do not intermix. In ordinary, holomictic lakes, the surface and deep waters mix at least once a year; in meromictic lakes the stratification is permanent, with the deeper layer (monimolimnion) never mixing with the surface layer (mixolimnion). The term derives from the Greek meros, "part", and was coined by Ingo Findenegg in 1935.

Causes

Meromixis can arise from three main causes. Ectogenic meromixis results from an external event bringing salt water into a freshwater lake; crenogenic meromixis from saline subsurface springs; and biogenic meromixis from salts generated by decomposition and concentration in the deep layer. The density difference between the layers maintains the stratification.

Consequences

Because the deep water does not circulate, it becomes anoxic and may accumulate dissolved gases such as carbon dioxide and methane. Sudden overturn of such a lake can cause a limnic eruption: in 1986 Lake Nyos in Cameroon released a cloud of carbon dioxide that suffocated about 1,746 people. Famous meromictic lakes include Lake Nyos, Lake Kivu, the Black Sea (the largest meromictic body of water), Soap Lake in Washington, and Big Soda Lake in Nevada.
