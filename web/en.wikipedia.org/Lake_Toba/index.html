Lake Toba (Indonesian: Danau Toba) is a large natural lake in North Sumatra, Indonesia, occupying the caldera of the Toba supervolcano. The lake, 100 kilometres long and 30 kilometres wide, is the largest lake in Indonesia and the largest volcanic lake in the world. It lies in the middle of the northern part of Sumatra, with a surface elevation of about 900 metres, and reaches a maximum depth of about 505 metres.

The Toba eruption

The caldera was formed by the most recent and largest of several eruptions of the Toba volcano, which occurred approximately 74,000 years ago. This "super-eruption" — rated 8 on the Volcanic Explosivity Index, the highest category — ejected an estimated 2,800 cubic kilometres of material, making it the largest known explosive volcanic eruption of the past two million years. Ash from the eruption has been found across South Asia, in the Indian Ocean, and in ice cores in Greenland.

The eruption has been proposed as the cause of a global volcanic winter lasting several years, and of a severe bottleneck in human evolution (the "Toba catastrophe theory"), in which the global human population is hypothesised to have fallen to as few as several thousand breeding individuals. More recent research has questioned the severity of both the climatic impact and the bottleneck, with archaeological evidence from India and Africa suggesting human populations may have been more resilient than the theory requires.

Geology

The Toba caldera complex is part of the volcanic arc associated with subduction along the Sunda megathrust off Sumatra. Following the great eruption, a resurgent dome rose within the caldera, forming the large island of Samosir in the middle of the lake — an island roughly the size of Singapore. The lake drains eastward through the Asahan River to the Strait of Malacca.

People and culture

The region around the lake is the homeland of the Batak peoples, particularly the Toba Batak. Samosir Island and the town of Parapat on the mainland shore are centres of tourism, known for traditional Batak architecture with its distinctive saddleback roofs, wood carving, and ulos weaving. Toba folklore includes a well-known origin legend in which a fisherman marries a woman who was once a fish, and their disobedient son is associated with the creation of the lake in a great flood.
