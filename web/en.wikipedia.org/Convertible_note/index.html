Convertible note

A convertible note (or convertible debt) is a short-term debt instrument, commonly used in seed-stage startup financing, that converts into equity at a later financing round. Instead of negotiating a valuation for a young company immediately, investors lend money under a note that will convert into shares — usually at a discount — when the company raises a priced round.

Terms

Convertible notes typically carry an interest rate and a maturity date, and convert subject to a valuation cap and/or a conversion discount (often around 20 percent) that rewards early investors for their risk. If the company is acquired before conversion, note holders may receive a multiple of their principal or convert into shares of the acquiring arrangement.

Use in startups

Notes became popular in Silicon Valley seed financing because they are quick and inexpensive to issue and postpone the difficult task of valuing an early-stage company. Critics note that accumulating notes can obscure a company's capital structure. The SAFE (simple agreement for future equity), introduced by Y Combinator, is a related instrument that achieves conversion without being structured as debt. See also convertible bond and seed money.
