Shrine

A shrine (Latin: scrinium, "case or chest for books or papers"; Old French: escrin, "box or case") is a sacred or holy space dedicated to a specific deity, ancestor, hero, martyr, saint, daemon, or similar figure of respect, wherein they are revered or worshipped. Shrines often contain idols, relics, or other such objects associated with the figure being venerated. A shrine at which votive offerings are made is called an altar.

Shrines are found in many of the world's religions, including Christianity, Islam, Hinduism, Buddhism, Chinese folk religion, Shinto, indigenous Philippine folk religions, and Asatru, as well as in secular and non-religious settings such as a war memorial. Shrines can be found in various settings, such as churches, temples, cemeteries, museums, or in the home.

Types of shrines

Temple shrines

Many shrines are located within buildings and in the temples designed specifically for worship, such as a church in Christianity, or a mandir in Hinduism. A shrine there is usually the focus of attention in the building and is given a place of prominence.

Household shrines

Historically, in Hinduism and Buddhism, but also in modern faiths such as Neopaganism, a shrine can be built in a home or temple for private worship. In Shinto, the kamidana serves as a household shrine.

Yard shrines

Small outdoor yard shrines are found at the bottom of many people's gardens, following various religions, including historically, Christianity. Many consist of a statue of Christ or a saint in a small niche or alcove, while others may be elaborate altars without ceilings typical in the Prehispanic traditions of Mexico.

Wayside shrines

Religious images, usually in some sort of small shelter, placed by a road or pathway, sometimes in a settlement or at a crossroads.
