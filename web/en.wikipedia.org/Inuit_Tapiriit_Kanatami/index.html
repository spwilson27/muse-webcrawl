Inuit Tapiriit Kanatami (Inuktitut for "Inuit are united in Canada"), formerly the Inuit Tapirisat of Canada, is the national representative organisation for Inuit in Canada. Founded in 1971, it represents the roughly 70,000 Inuit living in Inuit Nunangat, the Inuit homeland comprising the Inuvialuit Settlement Region (Northwest Territories), Nunavut, Nunavik (northern Quebec) and Nunatsiavut (northern Labrador).

Role and history

ITK was founded to give Inuit a unified voice in negotiations with the federal government, initially over land claims and resource development in the Arctic. Its work contributed to the comprehensive land-claims agreements that now cover all of Inuit Nunangat, including the 1993 Nunavut Land Claims Agreement that led to the creation of the territory of Nunavut in 1999. ITK advocates on Inuit self-determination, language and culture (Inuktut), health, housing, food security, education and the effects of climate change in the Arctic.

Governance

ITK's board of directors consists of the presidents of the four Inuit land-claims organisations, alongside the presidents of the Inuit Circumpolar Council (Canada), the National Inuit Youth Council and Pauktuutit Inuit Women of Canada as non-voting members. Its president, elected by the board, serves as the national spokesperson; recent presidents include Mary Simon (later Governor General of Canada) and, since 2018, Natan Obed.

Priorities

In recent years ITK has negotiated the Inuit Nunangat Policy with the Government of Canada (adopted 2022), recognising Inuit Nunangat as a distinct geographic, cultural and political region, and has promoted an Inuit Nunangat food security strategy and action on the housing crisis in northern communities.
