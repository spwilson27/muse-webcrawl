Police

The police are a constituted body of persons empowered by a state, with the aim to enforce the law, protect public order, and ensure the safety, health, and possessions of citizens. Their lawful powers include arrest and the use of force legitimized by the state via the monopoly on violence. The term is most commonly associated with the police forces of a sovereign state authorized to exercise the police power of that state within a defined legal or territorial area of responsibility.

History

Policing in various forms is ancient: officials enforced law and order in China, Egypt, Greece, and Rome. Modern professional policing is usually dated to the Metropolitan Police of London, founded in 1829 by Sir Robert Peel, whose "Peelian principles" emphasized crime prevention and policing by consent. Municipal police forces developed in the United States from the 1830s onward, and the model of uniformed, bureaucratic public police spread worldwide during the 19th and 20th centuries.

Organization and duties

Police forces are commonly organized at national, regional, or municipal levels, and may include specialized units for traffic, criminal investigation (detectives), counterterrorism, forensics, marine and aviation policing, and public order. Core duties include patrolling, responding to emergencies, investigating crimes, arresting suspects, directing traffic, and maintaining public order at gatherings and events.

Powers and accountability

Police are granted powers not available to ordinary citizens — including detention, search, and the use of reasonable force — under legal frameworks that vary by jurisdiction. Accountability mechanisms include internal discipline, civilian review boards, independent inspectorates, prosecution for criminal misconduct, and the exclusion of improperly obtained evidence. The extent of police powers and oversight remains a subject of public debate, particularly concerning use of force, surveillance, and racial bias.

Policing strategies

Approaches include community policing, problem-oriented policing, intelligence-led policing, hot-spots policing, and predictive policing. Since the late 20th century, many forces have shifted from purely reactive response toward prevention and partnership with communities.
