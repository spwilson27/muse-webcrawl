The NFL Draft, officially the NFL Annual Player Selection Meeting, is an annual event in which the 32 teams of the National Football League select newly eligible players, primarily from college football. It serves as the league's principal source of new playing talent and a mechanism for competitive balance, with the weakest teams from the previous season choosing first.

History

The first NFL draft was held in 1936 at the urging of future commissioner Bert Bell, who sought to curb bidding wars for college players. For decades the draft was a low-profile, front-office affair held in hotel ballrooms; it grew into a major televised spectacle beginning with ESPN's first broadcast in 1980, and since 2015 it has rotated among host cities following a long run at Radio City Music Hall in New York.

Format

The draft consists of seven rounds. Selection order in each round generally follows the reverse order of the previous season's standings, with the Super Bowl champion picking last. Teams may trade picks, and additional compensatory selections are awarded for net free-agent losses. The first player chosen each year—the "first overall pick"—receives extensive media attention, while the final selection is jokingly dubbed "Mr. Irrelevant."

Eligibility and scouting

Players are generally eligible once they are three years removed from high school. Prospects are evaluated at the NFL Scouting Combine and college pro days, and most drafted players sign rookie contracts scaled under the collective bargaining agreement.
