Rodrigo Jiménez de Rada

Rodrigo Jiménez de Rada (c. 1170 – 10 June 1247), also known as Archbishop Rodrigo, was a Roman Catholic bishop and historian from the Kingdom of Navarre and the Kingdom of Castile. He served as Archbishop of Toledo from 1209 until his death and was a leading figure in the Reconquista.

Life

Born in Puente la Reina in Navarre, Jiménez de Rada was educated at Bologna and Paris. Appointed Archbishop of Toledo in 1209, he became a close adviser to Alfonso VIII of Castile and played a central role in organizing the crusade that culminated in the Christian victory at the Battle of Las Navas de Tolosa in 1212, in which he personally participated. He later served Ferdinand III of Castile and promoted the primacy of the see of Toledo within the Spanish church.

Works

Jiménez de Rada is best remembered as a historian. His Latin chronicle De rebus Hispaniae (also called the Historia Gothica), completed around 1243, is a history of Spain from its legendary origins to 1243 and a primary source for the history of the Visigoths and the medieval Spanish kingdoms. He also wrote the Breviarium historiae catholicae and other works. His histories drew on earlier chronicles and Arabic sources and were widely copied and translated.

Legacy

He died in 1247 while returning from a visit to the papal court at Lyon. His chronicle shaped later Spanish historiography, including the work of Alfonso X.
