The University of Virginia (UVA) is a public research university in Charlottesville, Virginia, United States. It was founded in 1819 by Thomas Jefferson, who designed its original campus — the "Academical Village" centred on the Rotunda — and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Jefferson considered the founding of the university, with its secular curriculum and student self-governance, among his greatest achievements.

History

Jefferson's vision differed from existing American colleges: there was no religious affiliation, students could study in multiple schools rather than follow a fixed course, and elective courses were offered. The first students enrolled in 1825. Edgar Allan Poe was briefly a student. The university survived the Civil War nearly empty, then expanded steadily; women were admitted to regular full-time undergraduate study only in 1970.

Campus and traditions

The historic Grounds, as the campus is known, feature the Lawn flanked by ten Pavilions, each once home to a professor and his students. Students still live in the Lawn rooms as a senior honour. UVA maintains an influential student-run honour system dating to 1842. The university's teams, the Cavaliers (or "Wahoos"), compete in the Atlantic Coast Conference and have won national titles in men's basketball, lacrosse, soccer, and other sports.

Academics

UVA comprises schools of law, medicine, business (Darden), education, engineering, and arts and sciences, and is consistently ranked among the top public universities in the United States.
