Dog Day Afternoon

Dog Day Afternoon is a 1975 American biographical crime drama film directed by Sidney Lumet and written by Frank Pierson. The film stars Al Pacino as Sonny Wortzik, a bank robber whose attempt to rob a Brooklyn bank to pay for his lover's sex-change operation becomes a media spectacle and an hours-long hostage standoff.

Plot

On August 22, 1972, Sonny Wortzik and his accomplice Sal Naturile attempt to rob the First Brooklyn Savings Bank in Gravesend, Brooklyn. The robbery goes wrong almost immediately: a third accomplice flees, the vault contains little cash, and the police surround the bank. Sonny takes the bank employees hostage and negotiates with Detective Sergeant Eugene Moretti. As the standoff drags on, a crowd gathers and begins chanting "Attica! Attica!" after Sonny invokes the recent Attica Prison riot. Sonny demands a limousine to Kennedy Airport and a jet to fly him and Sal out of the country. The plan collapses at the airport when the FBI arrests Sonny and an agent shoots Sal.

Production and reception

The film was based on the real-life robbery by John Wojtowicz and Salvatore Naturile, described in a Life magazine article. It was nominated for six Academy Awards, and Frank Pierson won the Oscar for Best Original Screenplay. Pacino's performance, including the famous "Attica!" scene, is widely regarded as one of the finest of his career. The film is preserved in the United States National Film Registry.
