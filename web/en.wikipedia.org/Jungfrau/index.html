Jungfrau

Elevation: 4,158 m (13,642 ft)

Prominence: 687 m

Location: Bernese Alps, Switzerland

First ascent: 3 August 1811, by Johann Rudolf Meyer and Hieronymus Meyer

The Jungfrau (German for "maiden" or "virgin"; 4,158 metres) is one of the main summits of the Bernese Alps, located between the southern canton of Bern and the northern canton of Valais, halfway between Interlaken and Fiesch. Together with the Eiger and Mönch, the Jungfrau forms a massive wall of mountains overlooking the Bernese Oberland and the Swiss Plateau, and is considered one of the most emblematic sights of the Swiss Alps.

Geography

The Jungfrau is the third-highest mountain of the Bernese Alps after the nearby Finsteraarhorn and Aletschhorn. The summit lies on the border between the cantons of Bern and Valais. To the north, the mountain rises steeply above the Lauterbrunnen valley; to the south it overlooks the Aletsch Glacier, the largest glacier in the Alps. The Jungfraujoch, the saddle between the Jungfrau and the Mönch, is the site of the Jungfraubahn railway station, the highest in Europe at 3,454 metres.

Climbing history

The first ascent was made on 3 August 1811 by the brothers Johann Rudolf and Hieronymus Meyer of Aarau, accompanied by guides from the Valais. The climb was approached from the south via the Aletsch Glacier. Today the normal route follows the southeast ridge from the Jungfraujoch.

Tourism

The Jungfrau region is a major tourist destination. The Jungfraubahn, a rack railway opened in stages between 1896 and 1912, runs through tunnels inside the Eiger and Mönch to the Jungfraujoch station, marketed as the "Top of Europe." The area, together with the Aletsch Glacier, forms part of the Jungfrau-Aletsch UNESCO World Heritage Site, designated in 2001.
