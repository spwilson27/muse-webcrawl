Brundtland Report

The Brundtland Report, formally Our Common Future, was published in 1987 by the World Commission on Environment and Development, chaired by former Norwegian prime minister Gro Harlem Brundtland. It popularised the concept of sustainable development.

The report famously defined sustainable development as 'development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.' It argued that poverty, environmental degradation, and economic growth are interlinked global problems.

The report led directly to the 1992 Earth Summit in Rio de Janeiro and influenced the creation of Agenda 21 and the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change.
