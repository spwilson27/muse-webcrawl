The Shard

ArchitectRenzo Piano
Height309.6 m (1,016 ft)

LocationLondon Bridge, London, England

The Shard, also known as the Shard of Glass and the London Bridge Tower, is a 72-storey skyscraper in the London Borough of Southwark, designed by the Italian architect Renzo Piano. At 309.6 metres (1,016 feet) it is the tallest building in the United Kingdom and one of the tallest in Europe.

Design and construction

Piano imagined the building as a "shard of glass" — a spire-like sculpture rising from the Thames, its eight sloping glazed facades of angled glass reflecting the sky and dissolving the tower's mass as it tapers. Construction began in 2009 on the site of the demolished Southwark Towers beside London Bridge station, using top-down construction in which the basement and the core were built simultaneously. The structure was topped out in March 2012 and formally inaugurated that July; the building opened to the public in February 2013. The project was financed largely by the State of Qatar, which retains a majority stake.

Uses

The tower houses offices on its lower floors, restaurants, the Shangri-La The Shard hotel, luxury apartments near the summit, and, at levels 68–72, "The View from The Shard" observation gallery — the highest publicly accessible viewpoint in London.

Reception

The Shard divided opinion before it was built: a public inquiry heard objections that it would intrude on protected views of St Paul's Cathedral, though the government ultimately approved it. Since completion it has become a defining feature of the London skyline and has won international architecture awards; the building has also attracted urban explorers and BASE jumpers, and a fox nicknamed Romeo famously lived on its upper floors during construction.
