Chord substitution

In music theory, chord substitution is the technique of using a chord in place of another in a sequence of chords, or a chord progression. Substitutions are widely used in jazz and popular music to add harmonic colour and variety while preserving the function of the original chord — tonic, dominant, or subdominant.

Common substitutions

Well-known examples include substituting the relative minor or major for a tonic chord, the diminished chord built on the third degree for a dominant, and the tritone substitution, in which a dominant seventh chord is replaced by another dominant seventh a tritone away that shares the same guide tones. The backdoor progression and borrowed chords from the parallel minor are also forms of substitution.

Principles

A substitute chord normally shares important notes with the chord it replaces — especially the third and seventh, which define chord quality and function — or leads smoothly by voice leading to the following chord. Guitarists and pianists use chord substitution to reharmonise melodies, and arrangers use it to keep repeated progressions fresh.
