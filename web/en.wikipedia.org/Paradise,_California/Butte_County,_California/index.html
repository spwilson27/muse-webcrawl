Butte County is a county in the Sacramento Valley of northern California, one of the state's original 27 counties, created in 1850. Its name derives from the Marysville Buttes (now Sutter Buttes) that lay within its original borders. The county seat is Oroville; the largest city is Chico, home to a campus of California State University.

Geography

The county rises from flat valley farmland in the west — rice, almonds, walnuts and olives — through foothills to the Sierra Nevada and Cascade crest in the east. The Feather River drains the county and is impounded at Oroville Dam, the tallest earthen dam in the United States, whose spillway failure in February 2017 forced the evacuation of some 180,000 people downstream.

The Camp Fire

On 8 November 2018 the Camp Fire, ignited by faulty Pacific Gas and Electric transmission equipment during high winds, swept through the ridge communities of the county. It destroyed most of the town of Paradise, killed 85 people and destroyed more than 18,000 structures, making it the deadliest and most destructive wildfire in California history. Neighbouring Concow and Magalia were also devastated, and Chico absorbed thousands of displaced residents.

Communities

Incorporated places include Chico, Oroville, Paradise, Gridley and Biggs. Chico is known for Bidwell Park, one of the largest municipal parks in the United States, and for Sierra Nevada Brewing Company, founded there in 1980.
