Camp Fire (2018)

The Camp Fire was the deadliest and most destructive wildfire in California's recorded history. The fire began on November 8, 2018, in Butte County, Northern California, near the community of Pulga. It was ignited by a faulty electric transmission line owned and operated by the Pacific Gas and Electric Company (PG&E). The fire originated above the community of Concow and east of the town of Chico, and it spread rapidly under strong winds through drought-stricken terrain.

Within hours, the fire reached the town of Paradise, destroying much of the community and forcing mass evacuations along Skyway and other routes. By the time it was fully contained on November 25, 2018, the Camp Fire had burned 153,336 acres, destroyed 18,804 structures, and killed 85 people. It was the costliest natural disaster in the world in 2018 in terms of insured losses.

Damage and aftermath

The town of Paradise, home to roughly 27,000 people, was nearly completely destroyed; approximately 90 percent of its buildings were lost. Neighboring communities including Concow, Magalia, and Butte Creek Canyon also suffered severe damage. Smoke from the fire produced some of the world's worst air quality in the San Francisco Bay Area and Sacramento region, closing schools and cancelling public events.

In 2019, PG&E filed for bankruptcy protection, citing tens of billions of dollars in liability from the Camp Fire and other wildfires. In 2020, the company pleaded guilty to 84 counts of involuntary manslaughter and one count of unlawfully causing a fire. A victim trust funded with cash and stock was established to compensate survivors.
