The Feather River is the principal tributary of the Sacramento River in the Sacramento Valley of Northern California. The river's main stem is about 73 miles (117 km) long, but including its headwater tributaries the system extends much farther. Its four branches — the North Fork, Middle Fork, West Branch, and South Fork — drain a large area of the northern Sierra Nevada and the southern Cascade Range.

The river was an important corridor during the California Gold Rush, and its canyon carried rail lines and highways into the Sierra. Near the town of Paradise, the river winds through steep canyons of the Sierra foothills in Butte County.

California State Water Project

The Feather River is the key watershed for the California State Water Project. Oroville Dam, completed in 1968 on the river near Oroville, is the tallest earthfill dam in the United States and creates Lake Oroville, the project's main storage reservoir. Water released from the dam travels down the Feather and Sacramento Rivers to the Sacramento–San Joaquin Delta, where it is pumped into the California Aqueduct to supply Central Valley farms and Southern California cities.

Ecology

The river supports runs of Chinook salmon and steelhead trout, as well as the Feather River Fish Hatchery, built to mitigate habitat lost behind Oroville Dam. Portions of the Middle Fork were among the first rivers designated under the National Wild and Scenic Rivers Act of 1968.
