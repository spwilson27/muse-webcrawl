The Irish Republican Army (IRA) of 1919–1922, sometimes called the "Old IRA", was the guerrilla force that fought the Irish War of Independence against British rule. It grew out of the Irish Volunteers, which were renamed and recognised as the army of the self-declared Irish Republic, owing allegiance to the underground Dáil Éireann.

War of Independence

From January 1919 the IRA waged a campaign of ambushes, assassinations, and raids for arms against the Royal Irish Constabulary and the British Army. Under Michael Collins's intelligence network and local "flying columns", it made much of Ireland ungovernable, though it was always short of weapons and its nominal strength (over 100,000 on paper) far exceeded its active fighters. The British responded with martial law and reprisals by the Black and Tans and Auxiliaries.

Treaty split

A truce in July 1921 led to the Anglo-Irish Treaty of December 1921, which created the Irish Free State. The IRA split over the Treaty: the majority that accepted it formed the nucleus of the National Army, while the anti-Treaty minority fought the Irish Civil War (1922–23). Both later Irish states' armies, and several later organisations that used the IRA name, descend from this body.
