Mental health

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Mental health encompasses emotional, psychological, and social well-being. The World Health Organization defines it as a state of well-being in which an individual realizes their abilities, can cope with the normal stresses of life, can work productively, and is able to contribute to their community. Mental health is more than the absence of mental disorders.

Determinants

Mental health is shaped by interacting biological factors (genetics, brain chemistry), life experiences (trauma, abuse, chronic stress), and social and economic conditions (poverty, discrimination, unemployment, social support). Protective factors include supportive relationships, physical health, and access to care.

Mental disorders

Common conditions include depression, anxiety disorders, bipolar disorder, schizophrenia, eating disorders, and substance use disorders. Worldwide, mental disorders are a leading cause of disability; depression and anxiety alone cost the global economy an estimated US$1 trillion annually in lost productivity. Suicide accounts for more than 700,000 deaths per year.

Care and promotion

Approaches to improving mental health include psychotherapy, medication, community support, early intervention in schools and workplaces, and public policies reducing stigma. Many countries face large treatment gaps, especially in low- and middle-income settings, and advocacy campaigns work to close them.
