Zhang Zuolin

Zhang Zuolin (Chinese: 張作霖; March 19, 1875 – June 4, 1928) was a Chinese warlord who dominated Manchuria from 1916 to 1928 and briefly controlled the Beiyang government in Beijing from 1927 as Generalissimo of the Republic of China. The leader of the Fengtian clique, Zhang rose from banditry and service in the Russo-Japanese War to become the most powerful military figure in northern China, ruling Manchuria as a largely autonomous state with Japanese support, while resisting Japanese attempts to expand their control.

Rise to power

Born in Haicheng, Liaoning, Zhang joined a bandit group and later Qing forces, fighting in the First Sino-Japanese War and as an irregular in the Russo-Japanese War. After the 1911 Revolution he took control of Fengtian province, and by the early 1920s his clique controlled Manchuria's Three Eastern Provinces. Zhang twice intervened in the wars of the central plains — the Zhili–Fengtian Wars of 1922 and 1924 — and after the second he extended his influence over Beijing, becoming head of the Beiyang government in 1927.

Death

As Chiang Kai-shek's Northern Expedition advanced, Zhang prepared to withdraw to Manchuria. On 4 June 1928, officers of the Japanese Kwantung Army, frustrated by his resistance to Japanese demands, blew up his personal train at Huanggutun near Shenyang — the Huanggutun incident. Zhang died of his wounds. His son Zhang Xueliang succeeded him and soon declared allegiance to the Nationalist government.
