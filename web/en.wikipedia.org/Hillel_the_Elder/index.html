Hillel the Elder (c. 110 BCE – c. 10 CE) was a Jewish religious leader, sage, and scholar of Babylonian origin who became one of the most important figures in Jewish history. He founded the House of Hillel, a school of interpretation whose generally lenient rulings were mostly adopted in later Jewish law, and served as Nasi (president) of the Sanhedrin in Jerusalem during the reign of Herod the Great.

Teachings

Hillel is famous for his summary of the Torah for a would-be convert who demanded to be taught it while standing on one foot: "What is hateful to you, do not do to your fellow: this is the whole Torah; the rest is the explanation; go and learn." He is credited with the seven rules of biblical interpretation (middot) and with legal enactments (takkanot) such as the prozbul, which preserved lending across the sabbatical year. His sayings in Pirkei Avot include "If I am not for myself, who will be for me? And being for myself alone, what am I? And if not now, when?"

House of Hillel

The disputes between the House of Hillel and the stricter House of Shammai fill the Mishnah; tradition holds that a heavenly voice declared the law follows Hillel because his disciples were gentle and taught their opponents' views before their own. His dynasty of patriarchs, descended through Gamaliel, led Palestinian Jewry for centuries.
