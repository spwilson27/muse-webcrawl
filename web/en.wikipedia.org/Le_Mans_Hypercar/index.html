Le Mans Hypercar

A Le Mans Hypercar (LMH) is a type of sports prototype race car designed to compete in the Hypercar class of the FIA World Endurance Championship (WEC), including the 24 Hours of Le Mans. Introduced for the 2021 season, the LMH regulations replaced the Le Mans Prototype 1 (LMP1) class as the top category of endurance racing.

Regulations

LMH cars are purpose-built prototypes designed and constructed by automobile manufacturers, with considerable freedom in the design of the chassis and bodywork but a maximum power output of about 500 kW (671 hp). Hybrid all-wheel-drive systems are permitted but not mandatory: Toyota and Ferrari use hybrid powertrains driving the front axle electrically, while other entrants such as the Glickenhaus and Vanwall used non-hybrid internal-combustion-only cars. A Balance of Performance (BoP) system adjusts parameters such as weight, power, and energy per stint to equalise the field.

Comparison with LMDh

From 2023, LMH cars compete alongside LMDh (Le Mans Daytona h) cars, a related formula built on spec next-generation LMP2 chassis with a standard hybrid system, developed for the North American IMSA SportsCar Championship. Both classes were designed to converge in a single Hypercar category, allowing manufacturers to race the same car at Le Mans, Daytona, and Sebring.

Manufacturers

Toyota was the first manufacturer to compete with an LMH car, the GR010 Hybrid, winning the championship and Le Mans in 2021 and 2022. Ferrari returned to top-class endurance racing with the 499P, winning the 24 Hours of Le Mans in 2023, 2024, and 2025. Peugeot (9X8), Glickenhaus, Vanwall, Isotta Fraschini, and Lamborghini (SC63) have also fielded LMH entries, while Porsche, Cadillac, BMW, Acura, and Alpine compete with LMDh machinery.
