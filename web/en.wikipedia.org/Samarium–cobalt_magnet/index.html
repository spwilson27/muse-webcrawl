Samarium–cobalt magnet

A samarium–cobalt (SmCo) magnet is a type of rare-earth magnet, a strong permanent magnet made of an alloy of the rare-earth element samarium and the transition metal cobalt. Developed in the late 1960s and early 1970s, samarium–cobalt magnets were the first rare-earth permanent magnets to be commercialized, preceding the more common neodymium magnets.

Types and properties

Two families are manufactured: SmCo5, with maximum energy products around 130–200 kJ/m³, and Sm2Co17, with energy products up to about 260 kJ/m³. Although somewhat weaker at room temperature than high-grade neodymium magnets, samarium–cobalt magnets retain their strength at much higher temperatures — operating up to 250–350 °C — have a high Curie temperature (700–800 °C), and offer excellent intrinsic coercivity and corrosion resistance, usually needing no protective coating.

Applications

Because of their high-temperature stability, SmCo magnets are used in aerospace and military equipment, motorsport and industrial motors, travelling-wave tubes, magnetic bearings, sensors and high-performance electric motors where operating temperatures exceed the limits of neodymium magnets. Their drawbacks are high cost, largely due to samarium and cobalt prices, and brittleness, which makes them prone to chipping during handling and machining.
