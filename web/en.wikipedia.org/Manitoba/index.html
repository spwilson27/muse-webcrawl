Manitoba

Country: Canada
Capital: Winnipeg
Population: about 1.4 million
Joined Confederation: 15 July 1870
Premier / Lieutenant Governor: provincial government of Manitoba

Manitoba is a province of Canada at the longitudinal centre of the country. It is Canada's fifth-most populous province, with about 1.4 million people, the majority concentrated in its capital and largest city, Winnipeg. Manitoba borders Ontario to the east, Saskatchewan to the west, Nunavut to the north and the US states of North Dakota and Minnesota to the south, with a shoreline on Hudson Bay.

History

Indigenous peoples have lived in the region for thousands of years; it later became the heartland of the fur trade under the Hudson's Bay Company (chartered 1670). Manitoba was created by the Manitoba Act of 1870 following the Red River Resistance led by Louis Riel, which pressed the rights of the Métis people; Riel is now honoured as the province's founder. Settlement boomed with the railway, drawing large Ukrainian, Icelandic, Mennonite and other immigrant communities.

Geography and economy

The province spans prairie in the south, boreal forest and Canadian Shield in the centre, and tundra in the north, and contains over 100,000 lakes, including Lake Winnipeg, one of the world's largest freshwater lakes. Its economy rests on agriculture (wheat, canola, livestock), mining, hydroelectric power exported through Manitoba Hydro, manufacturing and services. Churchill, on Hudson Bay, is famous for polar bears and beluga whales.
