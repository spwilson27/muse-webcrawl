Marcus Aurelius Mausaeus Carausius (died 293) was a Roman military commander of Menapian origin who seized power in Britain and northern Gaul in 286, declaring himself emperor in defiance of Diocletian and Maximian. Appointed to command the Channel fleet against Frankish and Saxon pirates, he was accused of keeping recovered plunder and rebelled rather than face execution.

Rule

Carausius controlled Britain and a strip of the Gallic coast for seven years, issuing high-quality coinage — including famous issues presenting him alongside Diocletian and Maximian as one of three Augusti — and repelling an invasion attempt by Maximian in 289. Constantius Chlorus's campaign in 293 recovered Gaul; Carausius was then murdered by his finance minister Allectus, who succeeded him until Britain was retaken in 296.
