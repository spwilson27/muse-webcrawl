Mariana dam disaster
Date: 5 November 2015
Location: Mariana, Minas Gerais, Brazil
Deaths: 19
Cause: Collapse of the Fundão tailings dam
Operator: Samarco (Vale / BHP joint venture)

The Mariana dam disaster occurred on 5 November 2015, when the Fundão tailings dam at the Germano iron ore mine near Mariana, Minas Gerais, Brazil, collapsed, releasing some 43.7 million cubic metres of mining waste. It is considered the worst environmental disaster in Brazil's history.

Collapse and impact

The mudflow destroyed the district of Bento Rodrigues, killing 19 people, and travelled more than 600 kilometres down the Doce River basin to the Atlantic Ocean, devastating communities, killing aquatic life, and contaminating water supplies for hundreds of thousands of people. The dam was operated by Samarco, a joint venture between Vale and BHP.

Aftermath

Executives faced criminal charges, and the companies agreed to fund the Renova Foundation to manage compensation and environmental restoration, a programme criticised by affected communities as slow and inadequate. In 2024 the companies and Brazilian authorities reached a settlement valued at about R$170 billion for damages and remediation. The disaster, followed in 2019 by the still deadlier Brumadinho dam collapse, forced reforms of tailings dam regulation in Brazil and globally, including the phase-out of upstream-type dams.
