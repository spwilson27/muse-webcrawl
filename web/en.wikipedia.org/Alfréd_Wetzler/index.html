Alfréd Wetzler

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Born: 10 May 1918, Trnava, Austria-Hungary (now Slovakia)

Died: 8 February 1988 (aged 69), Bratislava, Czechoslovakia

Known for: Escape from Auschwitz; co-author of the Vrba–Wetzler report

Alfréd Israel Wetzler (10 May 1918 – 8 February 1988) was a Slovak Jewish man who, together with Rudolf Vrba, escaped from the Auschwitz concentration camp in April 1944 and co-authored a detailed report on the mass murder taking place there, one of the earliest and most authoritative accounts to reach the outside world.

Escape and report

Wetzler was deported to Auschwitz in 1942 and worked in the camp for two years, gathering information about its layout, the gas chambers and crematoria, and the numbers of victims. On 7 April 1944, Wetzler and Vrba hid in a hollowed-out woodpile within the camp perimeter, sprinkling tobacco soaked in petrol to throw off dogs, and after three days hidden — the duration of the SS search — escaped and made their way to Slovakia. There they dictated a report of more than thirty pages to Jewish community leaders in Žilina. The Vrba–Wetzler report was smuggled to the West and to Budapest, where knowledge drawn from it contributed to the pressure that halted the deportation of Hungarian Jews in July 1944, sparing many lives.

Later life

After the war Wetzler worked as an editor and librarian in Bratislava. He wrote about his experiences in the book published in English as Escape from Hell. Unlike Vrba, he lived quietly and received little public recognition during his lifetime; posthumously he has been honoured in Slovakia and Israel.
