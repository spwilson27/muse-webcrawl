Garuda Pancasila is the national emblem of Indonesia. It depicts the Garuda, a mythical bird-like creature from Hindu and Buddhist traditions, carrying a shield divided into five parts representing the five principles of Pancasila, the philosophical foundation of the Indonesian state.

Symbolism

The shield's emblems are a star, a chain, a banyan tree, a bull's head and rice and cotton. They stand for belief in one God, just and civilised humanity, Indonesian unity, democracy guided by consensus and social justice. The Garuda grasps a scroll bearing the national motto Bhinneka Tunggal Ika.

Numbers

The feathers are often explained as encoding 17 August 1945, the date of Indonesian independence: 17 feathers on each wing, 8 tail feathers, 19 and 45 feathers in other groups.

Design history

The emblem was designed under the direction of Sultan Hamid II and refined by President Sukarno; it was adopted in 1950 and later standardised by law. It appears on state buildings, documents, currency and aircraft.
