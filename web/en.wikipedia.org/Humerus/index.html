Humerus

Humerus
Location: Upper arm
Articulations: Shoulder (glenoid), elbow (radius, ulna)
Precursor: Upper limb bud

The humerus is a long bone in the arm that runs from the shoulder to the elbow. It connects the scapula to the lower arm bones, the radius and ulna, and consists of a proximal end with the head, a shaft, and a distal end with the trochlea and capitulum.

Structure

Landmarks include the greater and lesser tubercles, deltoid tuberosity, and the radial groove for the radial nerve — whose injury in shaft fractures can cause wrist drop. The surgical neck is a common fracture site, especially in osteoporosis.

Muscle attachments

Deltoid, pectoralis major, biceps brachii, triceps and rotator cuff muscles attach to or act across it.

The 'funny bone'

The name is a pun on 'humorous' and also reflects the ulnar nerve at the medial epicondyle, striking which gives a tingling sensation.
