Donkey Kong (1981 video game)

The first stage of Donkey Kong (25m), with Jumpman climbing girders.

Donkey Kong is a 1981 platform arcade video game developed and published by Nintendo. It was designed by Shigeru Miyamoto with assistance from Gunpei Yokoi. The player controls Jumpman — later known as Mario — who must climb construction girders and ladders to rescue a damsel (Pauline) from the giant ape Donkey Kong, dodging barrels and other hazards. It was one of the first platform games and one of the first games with a clear narrative premise.

Development

Nintendo of America was struggling with unsold Radar Scope cabinets, and president Minoru Arakawa asked Miyamoto to create a game that could be installed in them. Miyamoto devised the story and stage-based structure; the game shipped in July 1981 and became a major commercial success in North America.

Gameplay

The game consists of four distinct stages: 25m (girders), 50m (cement factory), 75m (elevators), and 100m (rivets). Jumpman scores points by leaping over obstacles, collecting items, and removing rivets, with a bonus timer adding pressure at higher levels. The game is famous at high levels for a "kill screen" at level 22 caused by an overflow in the timer calculation.

Legacy

Donkey Kong established Nintendo in the American market, launched the careers of Miyamoto and Mario, and spawned sequels including Donkey Kong Jr. and Donkey Kong 3, as well as the later Donkey Kong Country series. A licensing dispute with Universal Studios, which claimed the game infringed King Kong, was decided in Nintendo's favour.
