Mechanized infantry

Mechanized infantry are infantry units equipped with armored personnel carriers (APCs) or infantry fighting vehicles (IFVs) for transport and combat. As defined by the United States Army, mechanized infantry is distinguished from motorized infantry in that its vehicles provide a degree of armor protection and armament for use in combat, whereas motorized infantry are provided with "wheeled vehicles" designed mainly for transport.

History

The first mechanized infantry were fielded during the Second World War, when German Panzergrenadier units rode in half-tracks such as the Sd.Kfz. 251 to keep pace with tanks. The British and Americans employed similar half-track formations, while the Soviet Union relied principally on truck-borne and tank-riding infantry. After the war, tracked and wheeled APCs such as the American M113 and the Soviet BTR series became standard, and from the 1960s true infantry fighting vehicles — the Soviet BMP-1 and later the American M2 Bradley — allowed infantry to fight from within the vehicle through firing ports and a turret-mounted cannon.

Organization

A mechanized infantry company typically fields three platoons, each with four IFVs carrying a vehicle crew of two or three and a dismount squad. Mechanized units are usually combined with tank units in combined-arms battalions or brigade combat teams, so that infantry can clear terrain unsuitable for armor while tanks provide direct fire support.

Distinction from other infantry types

  • Motorized infantry — transported in unarmored or lightly armored wheeled trucks.
  • Air assault / airmobile infantry — inserted by helicopter.
  • Armored infantry — an older British term for infantry operating with tank brigades.
