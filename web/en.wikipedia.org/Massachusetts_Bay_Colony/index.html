Massachusetts Bay Colony

The Massachusetts Bay Colony (1628–1691), more formally the Colony of Massachusetts Bay, was an English settlement on the east coast of North America around Massachusetts Bay, one of several colonies later reorganised as the Province of Massachusetts Bay. Founded by Puritan settlers of the Massachusetts Bay Company, it became one of the most populous and influential of the English colonies in New England.

Boston, founded in 1630, became the colony's capital and principal port.

Founding and the Great Migration

The Massachusetts Bay Company received a royal charter in 1629. Unusually, the charter and the company's government were transferred to New England itself, giving the colony an exceptional degree of self-government. In 1630 a fleet led by John Winthrop brought about 1,000 settlers, founding Boston and neighbouring towns; Winthrop's lay sermon "A Model of Christian Charity" gave the settlement its famous image of a "city upon a hill". During the 1630s, the Great Puritan Migration brought some 20,000 English settlers to New England.

Government and religion

The colony was governed by a governor, council of assistants, and a General Court that became a representative assembly. Church membership was initially required for the franchise, and the colony maintained an established Congregational church; dissenters such as Roger Williams and Anne Hutchinson were banished and founded settlements elsewhere (Williams founded Providence in Rhode Island). Relations with Native peoples were marked by the Pequot War (1636–38) and later King Philip's War (1675–76), in which the colony suffered heavy losses.

Charter crisis and end

The colony's autonomy and its coinage of money (the pine tree shilling) irked the Crown. After the Restoration, royal pressure increased; the charter was annulled in 1684, and in 1686 Massachusetts was folded into the Dominion of New England under Sir Edmund Andros, who was overthrown in the Boston revolt of 1689 following England's Glorious Revolution. In 1691 a new charter created the Province of Massachusetts Bay, absorbing Plymouth Colony and Maine, with a royal governor and religious toleration imposed.

Legacy

The colony's traditions of town meetings, representative government, public education (including the founding of Harvard College in 1636 and the first public school laws), and its Puritan cultural legacy profoundly shaped New England and, later, American identity.
