Mode (statistics)

The mode is the value that appears most frequently in a data set. Along with the mean and the median, it is one of the principal measures of central tendency. A data set may have one mode (unimodal), two modes (bimodal), several modes (multimodal), or no mode at all if no value repeats.

Use and interpretation

The mode is the only measure of central tendency applicable to nominal (categorical) data, such as the most common eye colour in a sample. For numerical data it indicates the most typical observation: shoe shops, for example, stock most heavily the modal shoe size. In a sample drawn from a continuous distribution, the mode of the underlying density is the point of maximum probability density.

Comparison with mean and median

In a symmetric distribution the mean, median, and mode roughly coincide. In a skewed distribution they diverge: income distributions typically have a mode below the median, which is itself below the mean, because a minority of very high incomes pulls the mean upward. Karl Pearson proposed empirical rules relating the three measures for moderately skewed distributions.

Computation

For discrete data the mode is found by counting frequencies. For grouped continuous data, the modal class is the class interval with the highest frequency, and an interpolated estimate may be computed. Unlike the mean, the mode is robust to outliers lying far outside the bulk of the data, but it can be unstable in small samples.
