Ivan III of Russia

Ivan III Vasilyevich (22 January 1440 – 27 October 1505), also known as Ivan the Great, was Grand Prince of Moscow from 1462 to 1505. His reign saw the unification of much of Rus' under Moscow and the end of the Mongol-Tatar domination of the Russian lands.

Reign

Ivan annexed the Novgorod Republic in 1478 and Tver in 1485, styling himself sovereign of all Russia. In 1480 his forces faced the Great Horde at the Great Stand on the Ugra River, after which the Horde withdrew, traditionally marking the end of the "Mongol yoke". His second marriage, to Sophia Palaiologina, niece of the last Byzantine emperor, brought Byzantine court customs and the double-headed eagle to Moscow.

Legacy

Ivan commissioned Italian architects to rebuild the Moscow Kremlin, including its cathedrals and walls, and promulgated the Sudebnik law code of 1497. His reign laid the foundations of the centralized Russian state continued by his son Vasily III.
