Plasma exchange

Plasma exchange, formally therapeutic plasma exchange (TPE) or plasmapheresis, is a medical procedure in which a patient's blood plasma is removed and replaced with donor plasma or a substitute such as albumin solution. It is used to remove harmful antibodies, immune complexes or other large molecules from the circulation.

Uses

Established indications include thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura, Guillain–Barré syndrome, chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy, myasthenia gravis in crisis, and certain vasculitides and antibody-mediated transplant rejection.

Procedure

Blood is withdrawn through a venous catheter, the plasma is separated by centrifugation or membrane filtration, and the red cells are returned to the patient together with the replacement fluid. A typical course involves several exchanges over days to weeks.
