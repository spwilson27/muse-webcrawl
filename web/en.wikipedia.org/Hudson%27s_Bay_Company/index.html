The Hudson's Bay Company (HBC) is a Canadian retail business group and America's oldest company, incorporated by English royal charter on 2 May 1670. For much of its history it operated as a fur-trading enterprise controlling trade across the vast Hudson Bay watershed, known as Rupert's Land, where it functioned as the de facto government.

Fur trade era

The company's charter granted it a monopoly over trade in the lands draining into Hudson Bay. It built trading posts along the bay and rivers and competed fiercely with the Montreal-based North West Company before the two merged in 1821.

From territory to retail

In 1870 the company surrendered Rupert's Land to the new Dominion of Canada. Its trading posts gradually evolved into retail stores, and it became one of Canada's major department-store chains, symbolized by the striped Hudson's Bay point blanket.
