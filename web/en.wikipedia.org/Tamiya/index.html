Tamiya

Tamiya Incorporated (株式会社タミヤ, Kabushiki gaisha Tamiya) is a Japanese manufacturer of plastic model kits, radio-controlled cars, battery- and solar-powered educational models, and modelling tools and paints, headquartered in Shizuoka. Founded in 1946 as a sawmill and lumber company by Yoshio Tamiya, it has become one of the world's best-known hobby brands, recognized by its twin-star logo.

History

After its sawmill burned down in 1951, the company pivoted to wooden ship models, then in 1960 released its first plastic model, a 1:800 kit of the battleship Yamato. Its meticulous 1:35 scale military vehicle series, launched in the late 1960s, set new standards for detail and made Tamiya a global leader in static modelling. In 1976 it entered radio-controlled cars with a 1:12 Porsche 934, and its 1980s off-road buggies — the Sand Scorcher, Grasshopper, and Hornet — helped create the modern RC hobby.

Products

Tamiya produces military, aircraft, ship, and automobile model kits in scales including 1:12, 1:20, 1:24, 1:35, 1:48, and 1:350/1:700; radio-controlled vehicles and tanks; Mini 4WD racing cars, a hugely popular line in Japan and Southeast Asia since 1982; and acrylic and enamel paints, cements, and tools. Its "Masterwork" and finished-model lines, plus the Tamiya Model Magazine, extend the brand.

Corporate

The company remains family-led and privately held. Tamiya America and Tamiya Europe handle overseas distribution. The annual Shizuoka Hobby Show is a major showcase for the company and the Japanese modelling industry.
