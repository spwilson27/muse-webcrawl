Pesticide resistance

Pesticide resistance is the heritable ability of a pest population — insects, weeds, fungi or other organisms — to survive exposure to a pesticide that would normally kill it. It evolves by natural selection: repeated applications kill susceptible individuals, leaving resistant ones to reproduce.

Development

Resistance was first reported in the early 20th century and grew rapidly with synthetic pesticides such as DDT after the Second World War. Mechanisms include target-site mutations, enhanced metabolic detoxification, reduced penetration and behavioural avoidance. Herbicide resistance in weeds and insecticide resistance in malaria mosquitoes are major global problems.

Management

Integrated pest management seeks to slow resistance by rotating modes of action, using mixtures and refuges of untreated habitat, minimising applications, and combining chemical with biological and cultural controls. Bt crops, for example, are planted with mandated non-Bt refuges to dilute resistance genes.
