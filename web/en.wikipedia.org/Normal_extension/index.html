Normal extension

In abstract algebra, an algebraic field extension L/K is said to be normal if every irreducible polynomial, either in K[X] or in L[X], that has a root in L splits into linear factors in L. In other words, L is normal over K if for every element of L, its minimal polynomial over K splits completely in L.

Definition

Let L/K be an algebraic extension of fields. The following conditions are equivalent, and L/K is called normal if any (hence all) of them hold:

  • Every irreducible polynomial in K[X] having a root in L splits into linear factors in L[X].
  • L is the splitting field of a family of polynomials in K[X].
  • Every embedding of L into an algebraic closure of K that fixes K maps L onto itself.

Equivalent conditions

For a finite extension, normality is also equivalent to the statement that the number of K-embeddings of L into an algebraic closure equals the degree [L : K] counted in a suitable way combined with separability; normality alone is characterized by the embedding-invariance condition above.

Examples

The field ℚ(√2, √3) is normal over ℚ because it is the splitting field of (x² − 2)(x² − 3). By contrast, ℚ(∛2) is not normal over ℚ: the polynomial x³ − 2 has one root in the field but its other two roots are complex and do not lie in ℚ(∛2). Its normal closure is ℚ(∛2, ω), where ω is a primitive cube root of unity.

Properties

If L/K is normal and M is an intermediate field, then L/M is normal. Normality is not in general inherited by M/K. A finite extension is a Galois extension if and only if it is both normal and separable. Every algebraic extension can be embedded in a normal extension, and the smallest normal extension containing a given one is called its normal closure.
