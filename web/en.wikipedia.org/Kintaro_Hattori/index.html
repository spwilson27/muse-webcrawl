Kintaro Hattori

Kintarō Hattori (1860–1934) was a Japanese watchmaker and jeweller who founded the company that became Seiko. He is regarded as a pioneer of the Japanese watch industry.

Career

Apprenticed to a clock wholesaler in Tokyo as a boy, Hattori opened his own shop, K. Hattori, in Ginza in 1881, importing and repairing foreign clocks and watches. In 1892 he established the Seikosha factory, which produced wall clocks, then pocket watches and, in 1913, Japan's first domestically produced wristwatch, the Laurel.

Legacy

The firm he founded grew into the Seiko Group, a world leader in quartz and precision timekeeping. Hattori's Ginza store, topped by its famous clock tower, remains a Tokyo landmark.
