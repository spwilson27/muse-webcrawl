Cartimandua or Cartismandua (reigned c. AD 43 – c. 69) was a 1st-century queen of the Brigantes, a Celtic tribe occupying what is now northern England, during the period of the Roman conquest of Britain. She is known almost entirely from the writings of the Roman historian Tacitus.

Reign

Cartimandua came to power around the time of the Roman invasion of AD 43 and ruled as a Roman client queen. Her loyalty to Rome was demonstrated in AD 51, when she handed over the fugitive British resistance leader Caratacus, who had sought refuge with her, in chains to the Romans. Her rule was contested by her husband Venutius, whom she divorced in favour of his armour-bearer Vellocatus, provoking civil wars within the Brigantes in which Roman forces repeatedly intervened to keep her on the throne.

Fall

During the Roman Year of the Four Emperors (AD 69), Venutius seized the kingdom while Roman troops could only rescue Cartimandua, who disappears from the historical record thereafter. Her power base is often located at Stanwick St John in North Yorkshire, where extensive Iron Age earthworks survive.
