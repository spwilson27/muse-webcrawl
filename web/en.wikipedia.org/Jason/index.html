Jason

Jason (Greek: Ἰάσων, Iásōn) is a hero in Greek mythology, best known as the leader of the Argonauts and their quest for the Golden Fleece. He was the son of Aeson, the rightful king of Iolcos, whose throne was usurped by his half-brother Pelias. Jason married the sorceress Medea, whose magic made the quest's success possible, and later abandoned her for a Corinthian princess — provoking the terrible revenge dramatized in Euripides' tragedy Medea.

Early life

After Pelias seized the throne of Iolcos, the infant Jason was smuggled to safety and raised by the centaur Chiron on Mount Pelion. An oracle had warned Pelias to beware a man wearing one sandal; when the grown Jason arrived at Iolcos having lost a sandal while helping an old woman (the goddess Hera in disguise) cross a river, Pelias recognized the danger and dispatched him on what he believed was an impossible quest: to fetch the Golden Fleece from Colchis, on the far shore of the Black Sea.

The voyage of the Argo

Jason gathered Greece's greatest heroes — among them Heracles, Orpheus, Castor and Pollux, Atalanta (in some versions), and the sons of Boreas — aboard the ship Argo, built by Argus with Athena's help. Their adventures included the island of Lemnos, the Harpies tormenting the blind king Phineus, and the Symplegades, the clashing rocks at the entrance to the Black Sea.

The Golden Fleece

In Colchis, King Aeëtes demanded that Jason yoke fire-breathing bulls, plough a field and sow it with dragon's teeth, and defeat the army of warriors that sprang from them. The king's daughter Medea, having fallen in love with Jason, supplied the magic that let him survive each task; she then lulled the sleepless dragon guarding the Fleece to sleep so Jason could seize it, and fled with the Argonauts, in some accounts murdering her brother Absyrtus to delay pursuit.

Return and later life

Back in Iolcos, Medea tricked Pelias's daughters into killing their father. Jason and Medea were exiled to Corinth, where Jason repudiated Medea to marry Glauce (Creusa), daughter of King Creon. Medea killed Glauce and Creon and, in Euripides' version, her own sons by Jason. Jason's death was prophesied to come from the decaying stern of the Argo, which fell on him as he slept beneath it.
