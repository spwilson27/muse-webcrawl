Lawrence Lessig

Born: June 3, 1961, Rapid City, South Dakota, U.S.
Occupation: Legal scholar, political activist
Known for: Founding Creative Commons; free culture movement

Lester Lawrence Lessig III (born June 3, 1961) is an American legal scholar and political activist. He is the Roy L. Furman Professor of Law at Harvard Law School and the former director of the Edmond J. Safra Center for Ethics at Harvard University. Lessig is a leading voice on copyright law in the digital age and a critic of the influence of money in politics.

Early life and education

Lessig was born in Rapid City, South Dakota, and raised in Williamsport, Pennsylvania. He earned a B.A. in economics and a B.S. in management from the Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania, an M.A. in philosophy from Trinity College, Cambridge, and a J.D. from Yale Law School in 1989. He clerked for Judge Richard Posner of the Seventh Circuit and for Justice Antonin Scalia of the U.S. Supreme Court.

Academic career and free culture

Lessig taught at the University of Chicago Law School and Stanford Law School, where he founded the Center for Internet and Society. In 2001 he co-founded Creative Commons, a non-profit organisation that provides free copyright licences allowing creators to share their work under flexible terms. His books include Code and Other Laws of Cyberspace (1999), The Future of Ideas (2001), and Free Culture (2004), in which he argued that increasingly restrictive copyright law stifles creativity. He was a board member of the Electronic Frontier Foundation and argued the case of Eldred v. Ashcroft before the Supreme Court, unsuccessfully challenging the extension of copyright terms.

Political activism

From 2007 onward Lessig shifted his focus to institutional corruption in American politics. He co-founded the organisations Change Congress and Rootstrikers, and in 2014 launched Mayday PAC, a crowdfunded super PAC aimed at electing candidates committed to campaign finance reform. In 2015 he briefly ran for the Democratic nomination for President of the United States on a single-issue platform of passing the Citizen Equality Act.
