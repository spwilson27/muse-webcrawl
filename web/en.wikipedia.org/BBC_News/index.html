BBC News

BBC News is an operational business division of the British Broadcasting Corporation (BBC) responsible for the gathering and broadcasting of news and current affairs in the United Kingdom and worldwide. It is the world's largest broadcast news organization and generates about 120 hours of radio and television output each day, as well as online news coverage.

History

The BBC broadcast its first radio news bulletin on 14 November 1922. During the Second World War its news broadcasts acquired a reputation for accuracy that made them a lifeline for listeners in occupied Europe. Television news followed in the postwar years, and the BBC launched its first rolling news channel, BBC News 24, in 1997, alongside the BBC News website.

Services

BBC News produces bulletins and programmes for BBC television channels and radio networks including Radio 4 and the BBC World Service, as well as the international television channel BBC World News. Its online service is among the most visited news websites in the world. Through the BBC World Service, news is provided in dozens of languages.

Editorial standards

Funded principally by the television licence fee in the UK, the BBC is required by its charter to report "with due accuracy and due impartiality". Its editorial guidelines govern fairness, accuracy, and the treatment of controversial subjects, and its output is a frequent subject of public and political debate.
