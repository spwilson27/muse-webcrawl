Canadian Grand Prix

The Canadian Grand Prix (French: Grand Prix du Canada) is an annual Formula One motor race held in Canada since 1961, and a round of the Formula One World Championship since 1967. Since 1978 it has been staged at the Circuit Gilles Villeneuve on Notre Dame Island in Montreal, a semi-permanent street circuit named for Canada's most famous driver, who won the inaugural Montreal race in 1978 and was killed in 1982.

History

The race began at Mosport Park near Toronto in 1961 and alternated between Mosport and Circuit Mont-Tremblant before settling permanently in Montreal in 1978. The circuit — originally Île Notre-Dame Circuit, built on an artificial island created for Expo 67 — was renamed for Gilles Villeneuve after his death. The race has been absent only a few times, including 2009 (contract dispute) and 2020–21 (COVID-19 pandemic).

The circuit

The Circuit Gilles Villeneuve is known for heavy braking zones, close walls, and the "Wall of Champions" at the final chicane, which caught out several world champions in 1999. Long straights and chicanes promote overtaking, while changeable Montreal weather frequently produces dramatic races.

Memorable races

The 2011 Canadian Grand Prix, won by Jenson Button after rain delays, multiple safety cars, and a last-lap pass, is the longest race in F1 history at over four hours. Other classics include Jean Alesi's only career win in 1995 and Robert Kubica's victory in 2008, a year after his heavy crash at the same track.
