The Keizersgracht ("Emperor's Canal") is one of the principal canals of Amsterdam, the Netherlands, and the widest canal in the historic centre. Named in 1615 after Emperor Maximilian I of the Holy Roman Empire, it is the middle of the three main canals of the Grachtengordel (canal belt), lying between the Herengracht and the Prinsengracht.

Notable buildings

The canal is lined with grand canal houses of the Dutch Golden Age, including the "Green Barge" (Keizersgracht 555) and the Huis met de Hoofden ("House with the Heads"). Many addresses now house museums, hotels and offices; the stretch around the Leidsestraat is nicknamed the "Golden Bend"'s neighbour for its opulent façades. In winter the canal historically hosted ice-skating races when frozen.
