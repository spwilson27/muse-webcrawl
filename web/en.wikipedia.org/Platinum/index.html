Platinum is a chemical element with the symbol Pt and atomic number 78. It is a dense, malleable, ductile, highly unreactive, precious, silverish-white transition metal. Its name originates from the Spanish word platino, meaning "little silver". Platinum is one of the rarest elements in Earth's crust, occurring in some nickel and copper ores and in placer deposits.

Properties

Platinum is remarkably resistant to corrosion, does not oxidize in air at any temperature, and is attacked only by certain reagents such as aqua regia. It is one of the densest elements (21.45 g/cm3), slightly less dense than iridium and osmium. Its high melting point and chemical inertness make it valuable in laboratory equipment, electrical contacts, and thermocouples; the standard platinum resistance thermometer is a primary interpolation instrument of ITS-90.

Occurrence and production

The largest known primary platinum deposits are in the Bushveld Igneous Complex of South Africa (the Merensky Reef and UG2 chromitite), which accounts for the majority of world production, followed by the Norilsk region of Russia and the Stillwater Complex in Montana. Annual production is on the order of 180–200 tonnes, far less than gold.

Applications

Platinum's catalytic properties dominate its use: catalytic converters for vehicle exhaust account for a large share of demand, and platinum catalysts are used in petroleum refining and chemical production, as well as in the electrodes of fuel cells. Platinum is also used in jewellery, in chemotherapy drugs such as cisplatin and carboplatin, in glass manufacture, and in investment products (bullion coins and bars). From 1889 to 1960 the metre was defined by a platinum–iridium bar, and until 2019 the prototype kilogram was a platinum–iridium cylinder.

History

Pre-Columbian peoples of South America used platinum in artefacts; European awareness dates to the 16th century, when Spanish colonists encountered the metal in Colombia and dismissed it as an unripe form of silver. It was scientifically described by Antonio de Ulloa and studied in the 18th century by Charles Wood and William Hyde Wollaston, who developed methods of refining it.

Compounds

Common oxidation states are +2 and +4. Notable compounds include chloroplatinic acid, cisplatin (an anticancer drug), platinum dioxide (Adams's catalyst), and the hexafluoroplatinate salts; Bartlett's xenon hexafluoroplatinate (1962) was the first noble-gas compound.
