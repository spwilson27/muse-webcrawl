Data mining

Clustering is a core data-mining task

Data mining is the process of extracting and discovering patterns in large data sets, involving methods at the intersection of machine learning, statistics and database systems. It is the analysis step of the "knowledge discovery in databases" (KDD) process. Typical tasks include classification, regression, clustering, association rule learning, anomaly detection and summarisation.

Process

A standard workflow, formalised in the CRISP-DM model, runs from understanding the problem and the data through data preparation — usually the most time-consuming step — to modelling, evaluation and deployment. Because large datasets inevitably contain coincidental patterns, results must be validated on held-out data to avoid the overfitting and spurious findings associated with data dredging.

Techniques

Core algorithms include decision trees and random forests, k-means clustering, support vector machines, neural networks, and the Apriori algorithm for association rules such as market-basket analysis ("customers who bought X also bought Y"). Text mining, web mining and process mining extend the approach to unstructured data, logs and event records.

Applications and concerns

Data mining is used in retail recommendation, credit scoring, fraud detection, customer relationship management, bioinformatics, surveillance and scientific discovery. Its use on personal data raises privacy concerns: profiles assembled from innocuous records can reveal sensitive attributes, motivating techniques such as anonymisation and differential privacy, as well as legal frameworks such as the GDPR.
