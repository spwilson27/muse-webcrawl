Consumer Product Safety Improvement Act

The Consumer Product Safety Improvement Act (CPSIA) is a United States federal law signed by President George W. Bush on 14 August 2008. Enacted after a wave of recalls of imported toys containing lead, the act substantially increased the funding and authority of the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) and imposed new testing and documentation requirements on manufacturers, importers, and distributors of children's products.

Key provisions

The CPSIA progressively lowered permissible lead content in children's products and restricted phthalates in toys and child-care articles. It requires third-party testing by CPSC-accepted laboratories and Children's Product Certificates attesting to compliance, and it strengthened import surveillance and recall authority. The ASTM F963 toy safety standard was made mandatory.

Implementation and criticism

Small businesses, thrift stores, and makers of handmade goods criticized the testing costs and the law's effect on resale of used children's items; Congress later amended the act in 2011 to grant the CPSC more flexibility and to ease burdens on small-batch manufacturers.
