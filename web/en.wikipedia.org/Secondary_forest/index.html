Secondary forest

A secondary forest (or second-growth forest) is a forest or woodland area which has regenerated through largely natural processes after human-caused disturbances such as logging, shifting cultivation, or agricultural use, as distinct from old-growth (primary) forest that has not undergone such disturbance.

Characteristics

Secondary forests typically have a simpler structure than primary forest: an even-aged canopy dominated by fast-growing pioneer species, lower tree diversity, and less developed understorey. Given time and freedom from further disturbance, they gradually accumulate species and structural complexity, a process called secondary succession.

Importance

Vast areas of secondary forest have regrown in the tropics and temperate zones. They provide significant ecosystem services, including carbon sequestration, watershed protection, and habitat, though usually supporting fewer specialist species than old-growth forest.
