Dogger Bank Wind Farm

LocationDogger Bank, North Sea, 130 km off Yorkshire
Capacity3.6 GW (phases A, B, C)
DevelopersSSE Renewables, Equinor, Vårgrønn
First powerOctober 2023

Dogger Bank Wind Farm is an offshore wind farm under construction on the Dogger Bank in the North Sea, between 130 and 190 kilometres off the north-east coast of England. Developed in three 1.2 GW phases — Dogger Bank A, B, and C — it uses some of the world's largest offshore turbines and, when fully operational, would be one of the largest offshore wind farms in the world, capable of supplying roughly six million homes.

Development

The Dogger Bank zone was awarded in the UK's Round 3 offshore leasing in 2010. The projects won Contracts for Difference in the 2019 allocation round at record-low strike prices. Ownership is shared between SSE Renewables, Equinor, and Vårgrønn (Eni Plenitude). The turbines are GE Vernova Haliade-X units of 13–14 MW, among the most powerful installed offshore.

Construction

Offshore construction began in 2022, with power first generated in October 2023. Grid connection is made via high-voltage direct-current converter stations to landfall points near Ulrome and the Creyke Beck substation in the East Riding of Yorkshire. Construction has faced challenges including vessel availability and blade damage incidents during installation, which delayed full completion of the first phase.

Proposed expansion

A fourth phase, Dogger Bank D, has been proposed, which would raise the zone's capacity further. The projects are a central part of the United Kingdom's target of 50 GW of offshore wind by 2030.
