Public holidays in the United States

The United States observes eleven federal holidays: New Year's Day, Martin Luther King Jr. Day, Washington's Birthday (Presidents' Day), Memorial Day, Juneteenth National Independence Day, Independence Day, Labor Day, Columbus Day, Veterans Day, Thanksgiving Day and Christmas Day. On these days non-essential federal government offices close and federal employees receive paid leave.

Observance

Federal holidays bind the federal government; states, schools and private employers are not required to observe them, though most do for the major holidays. When a holiday falls on a weekend it is typically observed on the nearest weekday. Juneteenth, commemorating the end of slavery, became the newest federal holiday in 2021.

Other observances

Beyond federal holidays, widely observed days include Valentine's Day, St. Patrick's Day, Mother's Day, Father's Day, Halloween and New Year's Eve, along with state holidays such as Patriots' Day in Massachusetts and religious observances for which employers may grant leave.
