Islamic eschatology

Islamic teaching describes signs preceding the end of the world and the final judgment.

Islamic eschatology is the branch of Islamic theology concerned with the afterlife and the end of the world, known as Yawm ad-Din (the Day of Judgment). Drawing on the Quran and hadith literature, it describes a sequence of events including death, the intermediate state of Barzakh, the resurrection (Qiyamah), divine judgment, and the final abodes of paradise (Jannah) and hell (Jahannam).

Signs of the Hour

Tradition distinguishes minor and major signs of the approaching end times. Among the major signs are the appearance of the Mahdi, the return of Isa (Jesus), the emergence of the Dajjal (the false messiah or Antichrist figure), the release of Gog and Magog (Ya'juj and Ma'juj), the rising of the sun from the west, and the appearance of the Beast of the Earth.

Judgment and the afterlife

After the resurrection of the dead, humanity is judged according to deeds recorded by angels; the righteous cross the bridge of Sirat to paradise, while the wicked fall into hell. Intercession by the Prophet Muhammad on behalf of believers is a widely held doctrine. In Twelver Shia Islam, the occultation and return of the twelfth Imam, the Mahdi, occupies a central eschatological role.

Modern interpretations

Eschatological themes have shaped movements from medieval Mahdist revolts to modern revivalist and political movements, and interpretations range from the literal to the symbolic.
