Formalism (literature)

Leading figures of Russian Formalism in the 1910s and 1920s.

In literary theory, formalism is a school of literary criticism and theory that analyzes literary texts by focusing exclusively on their formal features — language, structure, metre, narrative technique and genre conventions — rather than on their social, historical or biographical context. The term most often refers to Russian Formalism, the influential movement centred on two groups in revolutionary Russia, and more loosely to the later Anglo-American New Criticism.

Russian Formalism

The Moscow Linguistic Circle (founded 1915, including Roman Jakobson) and the St. Petersburg Society for the Study of Poetic Language, or OPOJAZ (founded 1916, including Viktor Shklovsky, Boris Eichenbaum and Yuri Tynyanov), sought to define "literariness" — the properties that make a text a work of literature. Shklovsky's 1917 essay "Art as Technique" introduced defamiliarization (ostranenie): art makes the familiar strange so that it is perceived afresh. Vladimir Propp's Morphology of the Folktale (1928) reduced folktales to sequences of thirty-one narrative functions. The Formalists also distinguished fabula (the raw sequence of events) from syuzhet (their artistic arrangement).

Suppression and influence

Attacked as incompatible with Marxism, the movement was effectively suppressed in the Soviet Union by about 1930. Its ideas travelled westward through the Prague Linguistic Circle and Jakobson's later career, shaping structuralism and narratology. Mikhail Bakhtin and the Bakhtin Circle offered a famous critique, arguing that form cannot be separated from social and ideological content.

New Criticism

In the United States and Britain, the New Critics — John Crowe Ransom, Cleanth Brooks, W. K. Wimsatt and others — practised close reading of the autonomous text and coined the "intentional fallacy" and "affective fallacy", doctrines that made the literary work itself, not the author's intention or the reader's emotion, the object of criticism.
