Collective action problem

A collective action problem (also called a social dilemma) is a situation in which all individuals would be better off cooperating, but fail to do so because of conflicting interests between individual and group rationality. Each person has an incentive to "free ride" on the contributions of others, so the cooperative outcome does not occur even though everyone would prefer it to defection by all.

Background

The classic analysis is Mancur Olson's The Logic of Collective Action (1965), which argued that large groups will fail to provide public goods in their common interest unless there is coercion or selective incentives, because each member's contribution is negligible and the benefit is shared regardless of contribution. The problem is modelled in game theory by games such as the prisoner's dilemma and public goods games, and is related to Garrett Hardin's "tragedy of the commons".

Examples and solutions

Examples include climate change mitigation, voting, trade union organising, overfishing and antibiotic resistance. Proposed solutions include binding agreements and enforcement, selective incentives, repeated interaction and reputation, communication, social norms, and institutional design. Elinor Ostrom's work showed that communities can often govern common-pool resources successfully through locally devised rules, monitoring and graduated sanctions, contrary to the prediction that such dilemmas always end in failure.
