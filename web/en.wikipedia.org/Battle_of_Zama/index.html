The Battle of Zama, fought on 19 October 202 BC near Zama in North Africa, was the decisive final battle of the Second Punic War. A Roman army under Publius Cornelius Scipio (afterwards "Africanus"), supported by the Numidian cavalry of Masinissa, defeated Hannibal's Carthaginian forces, ending Carthage's challenge to Rome in the Mediterranean.

Background

After expelling the Carthaginians from Iberia, Scipio invaded Africa in 204 BC, forcing Carthage to recall Hannibal from Italy, where he had campaigned for fifteen years. Masinissa, a Numidian prince allied to Rome, supplied the strong cavalry that Roman armies had previously lacked — the arm whose inferiority had doomed Rome at Cannae.

The battle

Hannibal deployed some 80 war elephants in front of three lines of infantry, keeping his veteran troops from Italy in reserve. Scipio countered the elephants by arranging his maniples in columns with lanes between them, so the charging animals could be channelled through and dispatched, while trumpets panicked some into turning on their own cavalry. After a hard infantry struggle, the Roman and Numidian horse, having routed their Carthaginian counterparts, returned to strike Hannibal's veterans in the rear — reversing the pattern of Cannae. Carthaginian losses were heavy, perhaps 20,000 killed, against roughly 1,500–2,500 Romans.

Aftermath

Carthage accepted harsh peace terms in 201 BC: loss of its overseas empire, surrender of its navy, a huge indemnity, and a ban on waging war without Roman permission. Zama established Rome as the dominant power of the western Mediterranean and made Scipio's reputation second only, in Roman memory, to Hannibal himself.
