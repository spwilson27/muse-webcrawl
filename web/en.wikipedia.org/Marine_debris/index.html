Marine debris

Marine debris, also known as marine litter, is human-created waste that has deliberately or accidentally been released into a sea or ocean. Floating oceanic debris tends to accumulate at the centres of gyres and on coastlines.

Sources and types

Debris originates from land-based sources such as litter, storm drains and landfills, and from ocean-based sources including fishing vessels, offshore platforms and lost cargo. Plastics predominate because they are durable and buoyant; derelict fishing gear ("ghost gear") continues to trap marine life long after abandonment. Abandoned material breaks down into microplastics ingested by marine organisms.

Impacts

Debris injures wildlife through ingestion and entanglement, smothers habitats, transports invasive species, and imposes economic costs on fisheries, tourism and coastal cleanup.

Accumulation zones

Ocean currents concentrate floating debris in subtropical gyres, most famously the Great Pacific Garbage Patch. Cleanup efforts range from beach surveys and river interception to open-ocean collection systems, though prevention at source is widely regarded as most effective.

Policy

International rules such as MARPOL Annex V prohibit dumping plastics at sea, and many jurisdictions have adopted single-use plastic restrictions and extended producer responsibility schemes.
