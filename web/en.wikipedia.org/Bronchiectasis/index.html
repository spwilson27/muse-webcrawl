Bronchiectasis

SpecialtyPulmonology
SymptomsChronic productive cough, breathlessness, recurrent chest infections
CausesInfection, cystic fibrosis, immune deficiency, obstruction
DiagnosisHigh-resolution CT scan

Bronchiectasis is a long-term condition in which the airways (bronchi) of the lungs become widened and permanently damaged, leading to a build-up of excess mucus that can make the lungs more vulnerable to infection. The most common symptoms are a persistent cough that brings up sputum, breathlessness, and frequent chest infections. The term derives from the Greek words for "windpipe" and "dilation".

Causes

Bronchiectasis results from a vicious cycle of infection, inflammation, and airway wall destruction. Causes include severe or repeated lung infections (such as pneumonia, tuberculosis, and whooping cough), cystic fibrosis, primary ciliary dyskinesia, immunodeficiency, allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis, and inhaled foreign bodies. In many cases no single cause is identified.

Diagnosis

High-resolution computed tomography (HRCT) of the chest is the standard diagnostic test, showing dilated bronchi that fail to taper, thickened airway walls, and the "signet ring" sign where a bronchus appears larger than its adjacent pulmonary artery.

Treatment

There is no cure, but treatment can control symptoms and slow progression. Management includes airway clearance physiotherapy, pulmonary rehabilitation, prompt antibiotics for exacerbations, long-term macrolide or inhaled antibiotics in selected patients, bronchodilators, and vaccination against influenza and pneumococcus. Surgery is occasionally used for localised disease.
