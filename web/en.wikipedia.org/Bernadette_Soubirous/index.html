Bernadette Soubirous

Bernadette Soubirous (7 January 1844 – 16 April 1879), also known as Saint Bernadette of Lourdes, was a French miller's daughter who reported eighteen apparitions of the Virgin Mary in 1858 in the grotto of Massabielle near Lourdes in southwestern France. The apparitions, in which the figure identified herself with the words "I am the Immaculate Conception", led to the development of Lourdes into one of the world's foremost Catholic pilgrimage sites.

Life

Bernadette was born into a poor family in Lourdes and suffered from asthma throughout her life. Between 11 February and 16 July 1858, beginning when she was fourteen, she reported a series of visions of a young lady in white at the grotto. During one apparition she was directed to dig in the ground, uncovering a spring whose water became associated with reported healings.

Despite initial scepticism and official investigation, the apparitions were declared worthy of belief by the local bishop in 1862. Bernadette herself withdrew from public attention, entering the convent of the Sisters of Charity at Nevers in 1866, where she worked as a sacristan and in the infirmary. She died of tuberculosis in 1879, aged 35.

Veneration

Bernadette was beatified in 1925 and canonised by Pope Pius XI in 1933. Her body, exhumed several times during the canonisation process, was found remarkably well preserved and is displayed in a glass reliquary at Nevers. Millions of pilgrims visit the Sanctuary of Our Lady of Lourdes each year, and Bernadette's story has been retold in works such as Franz Werfel's novel The Song of Bernadette and its 1943 film adaptation.
