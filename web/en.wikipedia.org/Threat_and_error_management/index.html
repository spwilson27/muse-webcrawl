Threat and error management (TEM) is an overarching safety concept regarding aviation operations and human performance. TEM is not a revolutionary concept, but one that has evolved from the collection and analysis of flight data — principally through the Line Operations Safety Audit (LOSA) programme — and it provides a framework for understanding the interplay between safety margins, threats, errors and undesired aircraft states.

Framework

The model, developed by researchers at the University of Texas at Austin working with airlines in the 1990s and later endorsed by the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO), divides the safety picture into three components:

  • Threats — events or conditions outside the flight crew's influence that increase operational complexity and must be managed to maintain safety margins, such as adverse weather, terrain, congestion, aircraft malfunctions or even pressures created by the organisation.
  • Errors — crew actions or inactions that lead to deviations from organisational or crew intentions or expectations, including handling errors, procedural errors and communication errors.
  • Undesired aircraft states — the position, speed, attitude or configuration states that result when threats and errors are mismanaged and which, if not recovered, can lead to an incident or accident.

Application

TEM is embedded in crew resource management (CRM) training: crews are taught to anticipate threats in briefings, trap errors before they produce consequences, and recognise and recover from undesired states. LOSA observers riding in jump seats record threats and errors on line flights, giving airlines data on how crews actually manage them. The framework has subsequently been adapted to air traffic control, maintenance, rail and healthcare, including surgical and anaesthesia teams.
