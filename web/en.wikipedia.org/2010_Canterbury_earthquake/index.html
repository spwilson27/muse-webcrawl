2010 Canterbury earthquake

Date4 September 2010
Local time04:35 NZST
Magnitude7.1 Mw
EpicentreNear Darfield, Canterbury, New Zealand
Depth10 km
Deaths0 (2 seriously injured)

The 2010 Canterbury earthquake (also known as the Darfield earthquake) struck the South Island of New Zealand with a moment magnitude of 7.1 on Saturday 4 September 2010 at 4:35 am local time. The epicentre was located west of the city of Christchurch, near the town of Darfield.

Damage

Despite its size, the earthquake caused no deaths, an outcome attributed to its early-morning timing, the depth and location of the rupture, and New Zealand's building codes. However, it caused widespread damage to buildings and infrastructure in Christchurch and surrounding districts, including extensive soil liquefaction, and losses estimated in the billions of dollars.

Aftershocks

The quake began a prolonged earthquake sequence in Canterbury. A shallower magnitude 6.3 aftershock on 22 February 2011 devastated Christchurch and killed 185 people, becoming one of New Zealand's deadliest natural disasters.
