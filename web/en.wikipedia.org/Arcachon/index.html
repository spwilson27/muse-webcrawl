Arcachon (French: [aʁ.ka.ʃɔ̃]; Gascon: Arcaishon) is a commune in the southwestern French department of Gironde. It is a popular seaside resort town on the Atlantic coast, on the southern shore of the Arcachon Basin (Bassin d'Arcachon), about 55 km (34 mi) southwest of Bordeaux. The commune is a subprefecture and is traditionally divided into four districts named for the seasons: Ville d'Été (Summer Town), Ville d'Hiver (Winter Town), Ville de Printemps (Spring Town), and Ville d'Automne (Autumn Town).

Geography

Arcachon faces the Île aux Oiseaux and the oyster-farming villages of the Cap Ferret peninsula across the basin. Just south of the town, in La Teste-de-Buch, stands the Dune of Pilat, the tallest sand dune in Europe (about 110 m high), one of France's most visited natural sites. The town is surrounded by the Landes pine forest.

History

Arcachon grew rapidly after the railway from Bordeaux arrived in 1841 and was officially created as a commune in 1857, when Napoleon III signed the decree separating it from La Teste-de-Buch. The Ville d'Hiver, with its ornate villas sheltered among pines, was developed as a health resort for tuberculosis patients and wealthy winter visitors. Oyster farming became the basin's signature industry after the 1860s, and Arcachon's oysters remain celebrated.

Economy and culture

Tourism, oyster cultivation, boating, and fishing dominate the local economy. The town hosts a marina, casinos, and festivals, and serves as the gateway to the basin's beaches and bird sanctuaries, notably the ornithological park of Le Teich.
