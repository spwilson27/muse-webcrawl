Minkowski space

In physics, Minkowski space (or Minkowski spacetime) is the combination of three-dimensional Euclidean space and time into a four-dimensional manifold. Formally, Minkowski space is a four-dimensional real vector space equipped with a nondegenerate, symmetric bilinear form with signature (−, +, +, +) or (+, −, −, −), called the Minkowski metric. It is named after the mathematician Hermann Minkowski, who in 1908 gave a geometric reformulation of Albert Einstein's special theory of relativity.

[Image: A Minkowski (spacetime) diagram showing a light cone dividing events into past, future and elsewhere]

Structure

Points of Minkowski space are events, specified by coordinates (ct, x, y, z). The invariant interval between events replaces the separate spatial distances and time intervals of Newtonian physics: intervals are classified as timelike, lightlike (null) or spacelike, determining whether one event can causally influence another. Light cones partition spacetime about each event into an absolute past, an absolute future, and an "elsewhere" of spacelike-separated events.

Symmetries

The transformations preserving the Minkowski metric form the Poincaré group — Lorentz transformations (rotations and boosts) together with translations. Physical laws in special relativity are required to be Lorentz invariant, and four-vectors such as the four-momentum transform between inertial frames under these symmetries.

Relation to general relativity

Minkowski space is flat spacetime: general relativity generalises it to curved Lorentzian manifolds, but any spacetime is locally approximately Minkowskian, and Minkowski space remains the arena of special relativity and of quantum field theory in flat spacetime.
