It Can't Happen Here is a 1935 political novel by American author Sinclair Lewis. Written during the rise of fascism in Europe, it imagines the election of a demagogic populist, Senator Berzelius "Buzz" Windrip, as President of the United States, and his rapid establishment of a totalitarian dictatorship.

Plot

Windrip, modelled partly on Louisiana politician Huey Long and the populist demagoguery of the Depression era, campaigns on a fifteen-point programme promising every family a large guaranteed income while appealing to "the Forgotten Men". Once elected, he curtails Congress and the Supreme Court, rules through a paramilitary force called the Minute Men, and fills concentration camps with dissenters. The story is told through Doremus Jessup, a Vermont newspaper editor who at first dismisses the possibility of fascism in America — insisting it can't happen here — but gradually joins the underground resistance, the New Underground, after his family and friends suffer under the regime.

Reception and legacy

The novel was a bestseller; a stage adaptation produced under the Federal Theatre Project opened simultaneously in theatres across the United States in 1936, while a planned film was cancelled. The book has repeatedly returned to bestseller lists in later periods of American populism. Its title has become an ironic catchphrase for complacency in the face of democratic backsliding.
