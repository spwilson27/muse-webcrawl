Dirichlet boundary condition

In the mathematical study of differential equations, a Dirichlet boundary condition is a type of boundary condition, named after the German mathematician Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet (1805–1859). When imposed on an ordinary or a partial differential equation, a Dirichlet boundary condition specifies the values that the solution needs to take along the boundary of the domain.

Definition

For an ordinary differential equation such as y'' + y = 0, Dirichlet boundary conditions on the interval [a, b] take the form y(a) = α and y(b) = β, where α and β are given numbers. For a partial differential equation defined on a domain Ω ⊂ ℝn, the condition takes the form y(x) = f(x) for all x on the boundary ∂Ω, where f is a known function defined on the boundary.

Applications

Dirichlet boundary conditions are common in physical problems. In electrostatics, the potential on the surface of a conductor is fixed by a battery or by grounding, giving a Dirichlet condition for Poisson's equation. In heat transfer, holding the surface of a body at a prescribed temperature—for example by immersing it in a melting solid or boiling liquid—imposes a Dirichlet condition on the heat equation. In fluid mechanics, the no-slip condition specifies the fluid velocity at a solid wall. In structural mechanics, prescribing the displacement of part of a structure is a Dirichlet (essential) boundary condition.

Other boundary conditions

Dirichlet conditions contrast with Neumann boundary conditions, which specify the normal derivative of the solution on the boundary, and with Robin (mixed) boundary conditions, which specify a combination of the function value and its derivative. The classical Dirichlet problem asks for a harmonic function in a domain that takes prescribed continuous values on the boundary.
