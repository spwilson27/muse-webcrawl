The Battle of Tannenberg Line (German: Schlacht um die Tannenbergstellung; Estonian: Sinimägede lahing) was fought between the German Army Detachment Narwa and the Soviet Leningrad Front in northeastern Estonia from 25 July to 10 August 1944, during the Battle of Narva.

Background

After holding the Narva river line for months, German forces withdrew in late July 1944 to the prepared Tannenberg Line, a defensive position anchored on three hills — the Sinimäed, or Blue Hills — between the Gulf of Finland and the wetlands to the south. The defenders included German troops and foreign volunteers of the III SS Panzer Corps, among them Estonians, Dutch, Flemings, Danes and Norwegians.

The battle

Soviet forces launched massive artillery bombardments and repeated armoured assaults against the hills, particularly Grenadier Hill, the southernmost of the three. Fighting was extremely intense and often hand-to-hand, but the defences held; the Soviet offensive was called off on 10 August. Both sides suffered heavy casualties, estimated in the tens of thousands in total.

Aftermath

The Tannenberg Line held for several more weeks, allowing the evacuation of civilians and troops from Estonia. Germany abandoned mainland Estonia in September 1944 as its overall position in the Baltic collapsed, and Soviet forces occupied the country. The battle remains an important and debated part of Estonian memory of the Second World War.
