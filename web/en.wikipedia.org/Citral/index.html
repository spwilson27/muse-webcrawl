Citral

Citral
IUPAC name: 3,7-dimethylocta-2,6-dienal
Formula: C10H16O
Molar mass: 152.24 g/mol
Appearance: Pale yellow liquid
Odor: Lemon-like

Citral is an acyclic monoterpene aldehyde with the molecular formula C10H16O. It is the principal aroma compound responsible for the characteristic lemon scent of lemongrass oil and occurs naturally in the oils of several plants, including lemon myrtle, lemongrass, lemon balm, lemon, and orange.

Isomers

Citral is a mixture of two geometric isomers that differ in the configuration of the double bond adjacent to the aldehyde group: geranial (citral A, the E-isomer) and neral (citral B, the Z-isomer). Geranial has a strong lemon odor; neral's odor is less intense but sweeter. Natural citral typically contains geranial and neral in a ratio of roughly 2:1.

Occurrence

Citral makes up about 80% of lemongrass oil and about 90% of the oil of lemon myrtle (Backhousia citriodora), making the latter the richest known natural source. It is also present in the peel oils of citrus fruits.

Uses

Citral is used in perfumery and as a flavoring with a strong lemon character. Industrially it is an important intermediate: it is used in the synthesis of vitamin A, vitamin E, and other terpenoids, and can be converted to ionones used in fragrances. Citral also shows antimicrobial activity, but it is a recognized skin sensitizer and its use in cosmetics is restricted.
