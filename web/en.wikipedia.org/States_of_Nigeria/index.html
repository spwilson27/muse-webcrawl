States of Nigeria

Nigeria is a federation of 36 states and the Federal Capital Territory, where the capital, Abuja, is located. The states are the first-level administrative divisions of the country and are subdivided into 774 Local Government Areas. Each state has an elected governor and a House of Assembly.

History

At independence in 1960, Nigeria was divided into three regions—Northern, Western, and Eastern—with a fourth, the Mid-Western Region, added in 1963. In 1967 General Yakubu Gowon replaced the regions with twelve states, partly to weaken secessionist Biafra. Successive military governments created more states in 1976, 1987, and 1991, and the final expansion under General Sani Abacha in 1996 brought the total to 36.

List of states

The states are: Abia, Adamawa, Akwa Ibom, Anambra, Bauchi, Bayelsa, Benue, Borno, Cross River, Delta, Ebonyi, Edo, Ekiti, Enugu, Gombe, Imo, Jigawa, Kaduna, Kano, Katsina, Kebbi, Kogi, Kwara, Lagos, Nasarawa, Niger, Ogun, Ondo, Osun, Oyo, Plateau, Rivers, Sokoto, Taraba, Yobe, and Zamfara. Lagos State, whose capital is Ikeja, contains the country's largest city, Lagos, and is its economic hub.

Governance

State governments share powers with the federal government under the 1999 constitution. States depend heavily on allocations from the federation account, derived largely from oil revenues, which has driven continuing debate over fiscal federalism and resource control.
