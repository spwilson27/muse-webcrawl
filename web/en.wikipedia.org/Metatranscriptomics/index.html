Metatranscriptomics

Metatranscriptomics is the study of the RNA transcripts of a community of organisms — its metatranscriptome — sampled directly from an environment. Whereas metagenomics catalogues which organisms and genes are present in a sample, metatranscriptomics measures which genes are actually being expressed, and at what levels, at the moment of sampling, giving a picture of the active functions of a microbial community in soil, oceans, the human gut and other habitats.

Methods

A typical workflow extracts total RNA from an environmental sample, depletes the overwhelming ribosomal RNA, converts the remaining messenger RNA to complementary DNA, and sequences it on high-throughput platforms. Reads are then mapped to reference genomes or metagenome assemblies, or assembled de novo, and assigned to genes and taxa so expression can be quantified and compared between samples or conditions.

Challenges

Messenger RNA is short-lived and present in small amounts compared with rRNA; samples must be preserved immediately to freeze expression profiles. Reference databases are incomplete for many environments, short reads can map ambiguously, and separating host from microbial transcripts can be difficult in symbiotic systems.

Applications

Metatranscriptomics has revealed which microbes drive carbon and nitrogen cycling in oceans, how gut communities respond to diet and antibiotics, and which pathways pathogens switch on during infection. Combined with metagenomics and metabolomics it forms part of multi-omics approaches to community function.
