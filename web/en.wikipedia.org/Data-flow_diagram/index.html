Data-flow diagram

A data-flow diagram (DFD) is a graphical representation of the "flow" of data through an information system, modelling its process aspects. A DFD shows what kinds of information will be input to and output from the system, how the data will advance through the system, and where the data will be stored. It does not show information about process timing or whether processes operate in sequence or in parallel, unlike a flowchart.

Components

DFDs use a small set of symbols, in two main notations (Yourdon–DeMarco and Gane–Sarson): processes (rounded rectangles or circles) that transform data, data flows (labelled arrows), data stores (open rectangles) where data is held, and external entities (squares) that are sources or sinks outside the system boundary.

Levelling

Analysts begin with a context diagram (level 0) showing the whole system as a single process interacting with external entities, then decompose processes into progressively more detailed child diagrams ("levelling" or "exploding") until each process is a primitive operation described by a process specification. DFDs were popularised by structured analysis methods in the 1970s, notably the work of Ed Yourdon, Tom DeMarco, Chris Gane and Trish Sarson.
