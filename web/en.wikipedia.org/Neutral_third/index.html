A neutral third is a musical interval wider than a minor third but narrower than a major third, named by analogy with the neutral second. In just intonation, the neutral third may be represented by the ratio 11:9 (about 347 cents), an undecimal interval derived from the 11th harmonic.

In world music

Neutral thirds are characteristic of much Middle Eastern and Arabic music, where they appear in maqam scales, and they are also found in the intonation of blues, where the "blue note" third lies between the minor and major third of Western tuning.

Tuning systems

The interval cannot be produced in 12-tone equal temperament, which divides the third into exactly 300 or 400 cents. It is approximated in quarter-tone (24-TET) systems at 350 cents, and occurs naturally in the harmonic series and in 11-limit just intonation.
