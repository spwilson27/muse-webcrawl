Bupivacaine

Bupivacaine is a local anaesthetic of the amino amide group, used for surgical anaesthesia, labour analgesia, and postoperative pain management. It is marketed under names including Marcaine and Sensorcaine.

Synthesised in 1957 and introduced clinically in 1963, bupivacaine is notable for its long duration of action and potency. It blocks voltage-gated sodium channels in nerves, preventing transmission of pain signals. It is widely used in spinal and epidural anaesthesia and peripheral nerve blocks.

Bupivacaine is more cardiotoxic than other local anaesthetics if accidentally injected intravenously; the single-enantiomer form levobupivacaine and the related ropivacaine were developed for improved safety. A liposomal formulation provides prolonged release.
