Epoch

Epoch

Meaning
A reference instant from which time is measured
Uses
Chronology, astronomy, computing, geology

An epoch is an instant in time chosen as the origin of a particular chronological era or calendar. The term derives from the Greek epochē, meaning a fixed point or cessation. From that reference point, years, days, or seconds are counted forward (and sometimes backward).

Examples in chronology and astronomy

Traditional epochs include the traditional year of the world's creation in Jewish and Byzantine calendars, the founding of Rome (ab urbe condita), the Hijra of Muhammad in the Islamic calendar, and 1 January 1 in the Christian (Dionysian) era. In astronomy, an epoch specifies the date for which the coordinates of celestial objects are given; the standard J2000.0 epoch is 1 January 2000 at noon Terrestrial Time.

In computing and geology

Computer systems measure time from an epoch such as the Unix epoch (00:00:00 UTC, 1 January 1970). In geology, an epoch is a subdivision of a geological period — for example the Holocene and Pleistocene epochs of the Quaternary Period.
