Garri

Garri (also spelled gari) is a granular flour made from cassava tubers in West Africa, especially Nigeria, Ghana, Cameroon and Benin. It is produced by peeling, washing, grating, fermenting, pressing, sieving and roasting cassava mash until dry and crisp.

Preparation

Fermentation reduces moisture and helps lower naturally occurring cyanogenic compounds in cassava. The pressed mash is sifted and roasted in a hot pan, sometimes with palm oil to produce yellow garri; without oil it remains creamy white.

Uses

Garri is stirred into hot water to make eba, a stiff dough eaten with soups and stews. It is also soaked in cold water and eaten as a snack or light meal with sugar, milk, groundnuts or coconut.

Nutrition and safety

Garri is energy-dense and mostly carbohydrate, with little protein. Safe processing is important because improperly prepared cassava products can retain harmful cyanide compounds.
