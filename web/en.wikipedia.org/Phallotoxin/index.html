Phallotoxin

The phallotoxins are a group of bicyclic heptapeptide toxins found in the death cap mushroom (Amanita phalloides) and related fungi. The best known is phalloidin, which binds tightly to filamentous actin (F-actin) in cells.

Properties and effects

Phallotoxins stabilise actin filaments and prevent their depolymerisation, disrupting the cytoskeleton. Although highly toxic to cells in vitro, phalloidin is poorly absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract, so the phallotoxins contribute little to the lethality of death cap poisoning in humans; the more dangerous compounds are the amatoxins, which inhibit RNA polymerase II. Fluorescently labelled phalloidin is widely used in cell biology to stain and visualise the actin cytoskeleton under the microscope.
