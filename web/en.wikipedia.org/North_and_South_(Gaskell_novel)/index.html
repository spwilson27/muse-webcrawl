North and South is a social novel by the English writer Elizabeth Gaskell, first published in serial form in Charles Dickens's magazine Household Words from September 1854 to January 1855, and in book form in 1855. Along with Wives and Daughters and Cranford, it is one of Gaskell's best-known works.

Plot

Margaret Hale, the daughter of a Hampshire clergyman, is uprooted when her father resigns his living on grounds of religious doubt and moves the family to Milton, a fictional northern cotton town based on Manchester. There Margaret confronts the harsh realities of industrial life — poverty, strikes, and the mill-owning class — embodied in John Thornton, a self-made manufacturer who becomes her father's pupil and, after conflict and misunderstanding, her husband. The novel traces Margaret's growing sympathy for the mill workers, including the union leader Nicholas Higgins, alongside her deepening respect for Thornton.

Themes

Gaskell explores the clash between the rural, genteel South and the industrial North, the ethics of capital and labour, gender roles, and religious doubt. Unlike some of Dickens's industrial fiction, the novel gives the manufacturer's viewpoint unusual weight and resolves its conflicts through mutual understanding rather than melodrama.

Adaptations

The BBC adapted the novel in 1975 and again in 2004; the 2004 serial, starring Daniela Denby-Ashe as Margaret and Richard Armitage as Thornton, brought the book a large new readership.
