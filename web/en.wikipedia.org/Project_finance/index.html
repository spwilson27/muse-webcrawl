Project finance

Project finance is the long-term financing of infrastructure and industrial projects based on the projected cash flows of the project rather than the balance sheets of the project's sponsors. It is typically non-recourse or limited-recourse: lenders are repaid primarily from the project's own revenues, and the project assets serve as the principal collateral.

Structure

A project finance deal usually involves a special purpose vehicle (SPV) created by the sponsors solely for the project, with debt and equity arranged against contracts such as construction (EPC) agreements, concession or licence rights, and off-take agreements guaranteeing purchase of the project's output. This structure allocates risks — construction, operating, market, political, and force majeure — to the parties best able to bear them.

Applications

The technique is used worldwide for toll roads, bridges, power plants, renewable energy installations, oil and gas facilities, mines, and public–private partnerships (PPP/PFI). A related earlier form financed mining ventures in 19th-century Britain and the development of the Panama Canal era infrastructure.
