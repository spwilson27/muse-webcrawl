Lincoln is the capital city of the U.S. state of Nebraska and the county seat of Lancaster County. It is the state's second-most populous city after Omaha. The city was founded in 1856 as the village of Lancaster near the salt flats of Salt Creek and was renamed for President Abraham Lincoln in 1867 when it was chosen as the state capital.

History

Speculators were drawn to the area by its salt deposits, but a salt boom never materialised. After Nebraska statehood in 1867, the capital was controversially moved south of the Platte River from Omaha, and Lincoln was platted on the Lancaster site. The arrival of railroads in the 1870s spurred growth, and the state capitol building — the third on the site, completed in 1932 to designs by Bertram Goodhue — is a landmark Art Deco skyscraper topped by the bronze figure "The Sower".

Economy and culture

Government, the University of Nebraska–Lincoln (founded 1869), health care, insurance and banking anchor the economy. The university's Cornhuskers football team plays at Memorial Stadium, which on game days effectively becomes one of the state's largest "cities". The city has notable Vietnamese and refugee-resettlement communities and a growing technology sector nicknamed part of the "Silicon Prairie". Cultural institutions include the Sheldon Museum of Art and the Lied Center for Performing Arts.
