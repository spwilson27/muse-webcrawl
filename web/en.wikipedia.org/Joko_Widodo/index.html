Joko Widodo (born 21 June 1961), popularly known as Jokowi, is an Indonesian politician who served as the seventh president of Indonesia from 2014 to 2024. A member of the Indonesian Democratic Party of Struggle (PDI-P) for most of his career, he rose from mayor of his hometown Surakarta to governor of Jakarta and then the presidency — the first Indonesian president not drawn from the military or political elite.

Early life and career

Jokowi was born in Surakarta (Solo), Central Java, into a poor family that lived in riverside slums and was repeatedly evicted. He studied forestry at Gadjah Mada University and worked in Aceh before returning home to build a furniture business, becoming a successful exporter. The nickname "Jokowi" was given to him by a French buyer.

Mayor and governor

As mayor of Surakarta (2005–2012) he won acclaim for anti-corruption reforms, relocating street vendors by consent, improving public health services and rebranding the city; he was re-elected with an overwhelming majority. In 2012 he won the Jakarta governorship in an upset against the incumbent, launching the MRT subway project, flood control, and the Jakarta Health Card and Smart Card programmes. His habit of unannounced inspections, called blusukan, became his trademark.

Presidency

Jokowi won the 2014 presidential election against Prabowo Subianto and was re-elected in 2019, with Ma'ruf Amin as vice president. His administration pursued massive infrastructure development — toll roads, ports, dams and the Trans-Papua Highway — along with universal health coverage through BPJS, land certification and fuel-subsidy cuts. He championed relocating the capital from Jakarta to the new city of Nusantara in East Kalimantan. His tenure also drew criticism over democratic backsliding, the weakening of the anti-corruption commission, and environmental damage from development.

After the presidency

Jokowi left office on 20 October 2024, succeeded by Prabowo Subianto, whose running mate was Jokowi's son Gibran Rakabuming Raka — a fact that fuelled controversy about political dynasties. He returned to Surakarta as a private citizen while remaining an influential figure in Indonesian politics.
