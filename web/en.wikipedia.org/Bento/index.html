Bento

A bento (弁当, bentō) is a Japanese-style single-portion meal, traditionally consisting of rice or noodles, fish or meat, and pickled or cooked vegetables, packed in a box. Bento are sold widely in Japan — at railway stations (ekiben), convenience stores, and dedicated shops — and are commonly prepared at home for schoolchildren, workers, and travellers.

History

The origins of bento lie in packed meals carried by travellers and workers from at least the Azuchi–Momoyama period; lacquered wooden boxes became popular in the Edo period, and elaborate makunouchi bento were served between acts of kabuki theatre. Mass-produced station bento appeared with the railways in the Meiji era.

Types

Common varieties include makunouchi (a formal assortment), ekiben (station bento, often regional specialities), shōkadō (served in a partitioned lacquer box), and kyaraben (character bento decorated to resemble animals or cartoon figures). Similar boxed meals exist across East and Southeast Asia, including Korean dosirak.
