History of India

Anatomically modern humans first arrived on the Indian subcontinent from Africa between 73,000 and 55,000 years ago. The earliest known human remains in South Asia date to 30,000 years ago. Settled life, which involves the transition from foraging to farming and pastoralism, began in South Asia around 7000 BCE. At the site of Mehrgarh, situated in present-day Balochistan, this evolution is well documented through gradual changes including the domestication of wheat and barley, and later, of goats, sheep, and cattle.

Ancient India

The Indus Valley Civilisation, centred on cities such as Harappa and Mohenjo-daro, flourished in the 3rd and early 2nd millennium BCE. The Vedic period followed, during which the Vedas were composed and Hinduism's roots formed. In the 3rd century BCE, Ashoka of the Maurya Empire patronised Buddhism and erected edicts across the subcontinent. The Gupta period (4th–6th century CE) is remembered as a classical age of art, science and literature.

Medieval and early modern India

From the 8th century, Arab and later Turkic and Afghan powers introduced Islam; the Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526) was succeeded by the Mughal Empire, which under Akbar and his successors ruled most of the subcontinent and presided over a rich synthesis of Persian and Indian culture, exemplified by the Taj Mahal.

Colonial period and independence

European trading companies arrived from the 16th century; the British East India Company progressively gained control, and after the Indian Rebellion of 1857 the Crown ruled directly through the British Raj. The independence movement, led by Mahatma Gandhi's campaigns of non-violent resistance and the Indian National Congress, culminated in independence and the Partition of India in 1947, creating India and Pakistan. The Republic of India's constitution came into force on 26 January 1950.
