Henry Norris Russell

Henry Norris Russell (October 25, 1877 – February 18, 1957) was an American astronomer who, along with Ejnar Hertzsprung, developed the Hertzsprung–Russell diagram (1910). He is generally considered one of the most influential American astronomers of the first half of the 20th century.

Work

Russell spent most of his career at Princeton University, where he directed the observatory. His work spanned stellar evolution, the analysis of eclipsing binary stars, and the chemical composition of the Sun and stars. In 1929 he published an influential analysis of the solar spectrum showing that the Sun is composed mostly of hydrogen — a result that confirmed and extended Cecilia Payne's earlier (1925) conclusion.

He received many honours, including the Gold Medal of the Royal Astronomical Society (1921) and the Bruce Medal (1925). The American Astronomical Society's Henry Norris Russell Lectureship is named after him.
