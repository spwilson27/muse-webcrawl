Full breakfast

Substantial cooked breakfast from Britain and Ireland

A full breakfast is a substantial cooked breakfast meal, most famously the full English breakfast, traditionally including bacon, eggs, sausages, baked beans, grilled tomato, mushrooms, black pudding and fried bread or toast, usually served with tea or coffee. Regional versions across Britain and Ireland — full Scottish, full Irish, full Welsh and the Ulster fry — vary the components.

Components and variants

The classic English plate centres on back bacon and pork sausages, eggs fried, scrambled or poached, baked beans in tomato sauce, grilled or fried tomato, fried mushrooms, and toast or fried bread. A full Scottish breakfast adds haggis, tattie scones and sometimes Lorne (square) sausage; an Irish breakfast features white pudding alongside black pudding and soda bread; a Welsh breakfast may include laverbread and cockles; the Ulster fry is distinguished by soda farls and potato bread, all fried.

History and culture

The cooked breakfast descends from the lavish country-house breakfasts of the Victorian and Edwardian landed gentry, was adopted by the middle classes, and became a working-class institution in cafés (the "greasy spoon") and bed-and-breakfasts across the British Isles. Though less commonly eaten daily today, it remains a weekend and holiday ritual and a standard hotel offering.
