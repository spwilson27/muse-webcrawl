Clebsch–Gordan coefficients

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In physics, the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients (or CG coefficients) are sets of numbers that arise in the quantum-mechanical coupling of angular momenta. They express how the basis states of two interacting quantum systems combine to form states of definite total angular momentum, and they are central to spectroscopy, nuclear physics, particle physics and chemistry. The coefficients are named after the German mathematicians Alfred Clebsch and Paul Gordan, who encountered equivalent objects in invariant theory.

Definition

Given two angular momenta j1 and j2 with projection quantum numbers m1 and m2, the product states |j1 m1>|j2 m2> can be expanded in the coupled basis |J M> of total angular momentum J = |j1 − j2|, …, j1 + j2:

|J M> = Σ ⟨j1 m1 j2 m2|J M⟩ |j1 m1>|j2 m2>

The expansion coefficients ⟨j1 m1 j2 m2|J M⟩ are the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients. They vanish unless m1 + m2 = M and |j1 − j2| ≤ J ≤ j1 + j2.

Example

Coupling two spin-1/2 systems (j1 = j2 = 1/2) yields the familiar singlet and triplet states:

  • |1 1> = |↑↑>
  • |1 0> = (|↑↓> + |↓↑>)/√2
  • |1 −1> = |↓↓>
  • |0 0> = (|↑↓> − |↓↑>)/√2

where the coefficients ±1/√2 are the CG coefficients for this case.

Related symbols

The closely related Wigner 3-j symbols are a more symmetric formulation of the same information, preferred in some contexts because they treat the three angular momenta on an equal footing. Wigner 6-j symbols extend the formalism to the coupling of three angular momenta.

Applications

CG coefficients determine selection rules for atomic transitions, the composition of quark-model hadrons from constituent quarks, nuclear shell-model configurations, and the addition of orbital and spin angular momenta to form total angular momentum states in atoms and molecules.
