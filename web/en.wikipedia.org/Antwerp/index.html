Antwerp

Antwerp's historic centre on the river Scheldt

Antwerp (Dutch: Antwerpen) is a city and municipality in the Flemish Region of Belgium and the capital of Antwerp Province. With a population of over 500,000 in the city and about 1.2 million in the metropolitan area, it is Belgium's second-largest urban area. Its port is one of the largest in the world and the second-largest in Europe after Rotterdam.

History

Antwerp rose to prominence in the sixteenth century as the richest trading city in Europe, a centre for the spice, cloth, and money markets, and home to the painter Peter Paul Rubens. Its golden age ended with the Spanish Fury of 1576 and the fall of Antwerp in 1585 during the Eighty Years' War, after which the Dutch blockade of the Scheldt stifled trade for two centuries. The port revived in the nineteenth century, and the city hosted the 1920 Summer Olympics.

Economy

The Port of Antwerp is a major hub for container traffic, petrochemicals—the largest integrated chemical cluster in Europe—and the diamond trade, for which Antwerp has been a global centre since the fifteenth century.

Culture

Landmarks include the Cathedral of Our Lady, containing works by Rubens; the Plantin-Moretus Museum, a UNESCO World Heritage Site devoted to early printing; the Royal Museum of Fine Arts; and Antwerpen-Centraal railway station. The city is also a noted fashion centre, home to the Royal Academy of Fine Arts and the "Antwerp Six" designers.
