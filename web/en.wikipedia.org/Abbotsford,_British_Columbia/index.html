Abbotsford is a city in British Columbia, Canada, in the Fraser Valley about 70 kilometres east of Vancouver. It is the largest municipality in the province by area and the fifth-largest by population. The city borders the Canada–United States boundary, across from Sumas, Washington.

History

The area is the traditional territory of the Stó:lō people. European settlement began with the Fraser River gold rush of 1858, and the village of Abbotsford was established in 1892; its name is usually connected with Harry Abbott, a Canadian Pacific Railway official. Abbotsford amalgamated with neighbouring districts over the twentieth century, becoming a city in 1995.

Economy and culture

Abbotsford is a major agricultural centre — its motto is "City in the Country" — and is among Canada's most productive farming regions, particularly for dairy, poultry and berries. It is home to the University of the Fraser Valley, Abbotsford International Airport, and the Abbotsford Centre arena. In November 2021 the city was severely affected by flooding of the Sumas Prairie during the Pacific Northwest floods.
