Ohio Stadium is an American football stadium in Columbus, Ohio, on the campus of Ohio State University. It primarily serves as the home venue of the Ohio State Buckeyes football team and is commonly known as "the Horseshoe" because of its distinctive shape, with the south end open until renovations enclosed the stadium. It is one of the largest stadiums in the world, with an official capacity of 102,780.

History

Designed by architect Howard Dwight Smith, the stadium was built in 1921–1922 at a cost of $1.3 million, largely funded by public subscription. It replaced Ohio Field and opened on October 7, 1922. Its concrete double-deck structure, rotunda, and Roman arches were pioneering; it was the first horseshoe-shaped double-deck stadium in the United States. The stadium was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 1974.

Renovations

Major renovations between 1998 and 2001 added seating, suites, and a permanent south stand, lowering the field and raising capacity above 100,000. Further updates in the 2010s added field-level suites and modernised concourses while slightly reducing capacity.

Other uses

Beyond football, Ohio Stadium has hosted concerts by acts including U2, the Rolling Stones, and Pink Floyd, international soccer matches (including Real Madrid and the Columbus Crew during the 2016 International Champions Cup), and the 2025 NHL Stadium Series outdoor hockey game. The attendance record exceeds 110,000, set for the 2016 game against Michigan.
