Mariana and Palau Islands campaign

Operations Forager and Stalemate II in the Central Pacific, 1944.

The Mariana and Palau Islands campaign, codenamed Operation Forager (Marianas) and Operation Stalemate II (Palaus), was a series of offensives by United States forces against Imperial Japanese forces in the Mariana and Palau island groups in the Pacific Ocean between June and November 1944 during the Pacific War. The campaign was part of the United States' island-hopping strategy across the Central Pacific; the seizure of Saipan, Tinian and Guam in the Marianas brought Japan's home islands within range of American B-29 bombers.

Background

After the capture of the Gilbert and Marshall Islands, the American Joint Chiefs directed a drive into the Marianas, held by Japan since 1914 (the Northern Marianas) and 1899 (Guam, captured by Japan in December 1941). The islands offered airfields from which the new long-range B-29 Superfortress could bomb Japan, and harbours for the fleet. Japan regarded the Marianas as part of its absolute national defence sphere, whose loss would be decisive.

The Marianas operations

On 15 June 1944 the 2nd and 4th Marine Divisions landed on Saipan. The Japanese Mobile Fleet sortied to relieve the island and was crushed in the Battle of the Philippine Sea (19–20 June), the "Great Marianas Turkey Shoot", losing three carriers and hundreds of aircraft. Saipan was declared secure on 9 July after a final mass banzai charge and mass civilian suicides at Marpi. Guam was invaded on 21 July and Tinian on 24 July; both were secured by mid-August. The fall of Saipan precipitated the resignation of Japanese Prime Minister Hideki Tojo's cabinet.

The Palaus operations

On 15 September 1944 the 1st Marine Division landed on Peleliu to protect the flank of the planned Philippines invasion. Japanese defenders using a new defence-in-depth of caves and ridges inflicted some of the heaviest Marine casualties of the war; the fighting lasted until late November. Neighbouring Angaur fell more quickly, and Ulithi atoll, seized unopposed, became a great fleet anchorage. The necessity of the Peleliu operation was later questioned, as the Philippines landings proceeded without the expected threat from the Palaus.

Consequences

From the Marianas, B-29s of the Twentieth Air Force began sustained bombing of Japan in November 1944, culminating in the firebombing of Tokyo and, from Tinian, the atomic missions of August 1945. Total American casualties in the campaign exceeded 25,000, while most Japanese garrisons were annihilated. Bypassed islands such as Truk and Rabaul withered under blockade until the war's end.
