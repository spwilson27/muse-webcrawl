Surakarta Sunanate

The Surakarta Sunanate (Indonesian: Kasunanan Surakarta Hadiningrat) is a Javanese monarchy centred in the city of Surakarta (Solo), in Central Java, Indonesia. It was founded in 1745 by Pakubuwono II when the capital of the Mataram Sultanate was moved from Kartasura to the village of Sala, which became Surakarta.

History

The sunanate emerged from the turmoil of the Javanese wars of succession in the early 18th century. By the Treaty of Giyanti in 1755, the Mataram realm was divided between the Surakarta Sunanate and the newly created Yogyakarta Sultanate, a division engineered by the Dutch East India Company (VOC) to weaken Javanese power. A further split in 1757 created the Mangkunegaran principality within Surakarta's sphere.

Through the 19th century the sunanate became increasingly a ceremonial court under Dutch colonial rule, though its rulers retained considerable cultural prestige. After Indonesian independence in 1945, Surakarta briefly held special-region status, which was revoked in 1946 following an anti-swapraja movement. The Sunan (king) of Surakarta today functions as a cultural figurehead, and the palace — the Keraton Surakarta Hadiningrat — remains a centre of Javanese court culture, gamelan music, dance and batik craftsmanship.

Rulers

The rulers of Surakarta bear the title Susuhunan (abbreviated Sunan) with the regnal name Pakubuwono. Pakubuwono X (reigned 1893–1939) was notable for modernising the court and was declared a National Hero of Indonesia. The current sunan, Pakubuwono XIII, ascended the throne in 2004 after the death of Pakubuwono XII; his accession was disputed by a rival claimant, leading to a prolonged succession dispute within the royal family.
