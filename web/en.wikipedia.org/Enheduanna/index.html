Enheduanna

Lived: c. 2285–2250 BCE

Office: High priestess of Nanna at Ur

Father: Sargon of Akkad

Known for: Earliest named author in history

Enheduanna (Sumerian: 𒂖𒃶𒁺𒀭𒈾, also transliterated En-hedu-anna) was the high priestess (en) of the moon god Nanna (Sin) in the Sumerian city-state of Ur in the reign of her father, Sargon of Akkad (c. 2334–2279 BCE). She is widely regarded as the earliest known author whose name is attached to her works.

Life

Sargon appointed his daughter to the priesthood at Ur, in conquered Sumer, probably as part of binding the Sumerian city-states to his Akkadian empire. She held office through the reigns of her brothers Rimush and Manishtushu and into that of her nephew Naram-Sin; in her hymn The Exaltation of Inanna she recounts being driven into exile during a revolt and later restored.

Works

Enheduanna composed hymns in Sumerian, including a cycle of temple hymns — forty-two short hymns addressed to temples throughout Sumer and Akkad — and personal hymns to the goddess Inanna, most famously The Exaltation of Inanna (Nin-me-šarra), which blends autobiography, theology, and prayer. Her works were copied in scribal schools for centuries after her death.

Legacy

The alabaster Disk of Enheduanna, excavated at Ur in 1927, depicts her performing a ritual. A crater on Mercury is named Enheduanna in her honor, and she is frequently cited as the first identifiable author and poet in world literature.
