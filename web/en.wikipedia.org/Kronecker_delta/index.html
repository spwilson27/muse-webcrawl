Kronecker delta

The Kronecker delta as the entries of the identity matrix.

In mathematics, the Kronecker delta (named after Leopold Kronecker) is a function of two variables, usually non-negative integers. The function is 1 if the variables are equal, and 0 otherwise:

δij = 0 if i ≠ j; δij = 1 if i = j.

For example, δ12 = 0, whereas δ33 = 1.

Properties

The Kronecker delta appears in many areas of mathematics, physics and engineering. In linear algebra, the identity matrix can be written as (δij), and the delta acts as a "sifting" tool in sums: Σj δij aj = ai. In tensor calculus it is used with the Einstein summation convention, and in digital signal processing the unit impulse can be represented as δ[n].

Generalised Kronecker delta

The generalised Kronecker delta extends the concept to multiple indices and is related to the Levi-Civita symbol; it can be expressed as a determinant of ordinary deltas and is used in differential geometry and the theory of determinants.
