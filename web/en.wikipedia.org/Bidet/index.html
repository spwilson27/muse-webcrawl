Bidet

A bidet is a bowl or receptacle designed to be sat upon in order to wash one's genitalia, perineum, inner buttocks, and anus. The modern variety includes a plumbed-in water supply and a drainage opening and is thus a type of plumbing fixture subject to local hygiene regulations. Several types exist, including the standalone bidet, the bidet shower, and bidet seats that attach to a toilet.

History

The bidet appears to have been invented in France in the late 17th or early 18th century; the word in French also means a small horse or pony, a reference to the straddling posture used. Early bidets were furniture-like basins set in a wooden frame. Plumbed bidets spread through southern Europe in the 20th century.

Usage around the world

Bidets are common in much of southern Europe, including Italy — where installation in homes has been legally required since 1975 — Spain, and Portugal, as well as in parts of Latin America, the Middle East, and Asia. In Japan, bidet toilet seats with electronic controls, heated seats, and warm-water washing are extremely widespread. Bidets are comparatively rare in the United States and the United Kingdom, though their popularity has grown.

Health and environment

Advocates argue that washing with water is more hygienic and gentler than wiping with toilet paper, and that bidets reduce paper consumption. Medical advice sometimes cautions about hygiene of shared nozzles and water temperature control.
