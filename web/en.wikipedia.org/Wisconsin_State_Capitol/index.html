Wisconsin State Capitol

The Wisconsin State Capitol, located in Madison, is the seat of the Wisconsin state government, housing the Wisconsin Legislature, the state Supreme Court, and the Office of the Governor. The present building, completed in 1917, is the third capitol to stand on the site and the fifth capitol building in the state's history.

History

The first capitol in Madison was completed in 1838, when Wisconsin was still a territory. It was replaced by a larger building in the 1860s whose dome collapsed during an expansion in 1883. In 1904, fire destroyed much of the second capitol, prompting construction of the current Beaux-Arts building, designed by architect George B. Post and built between 1906 and 1917 at a cost of about $7.25 million.

Architecture

The granite-clad building has four wings radiating from a central dome that rises about 284 feet (86.6 m), making it the tallest building in Madison — a height protected by law so the capitol remains visible from around the city. Atop the dome stands Daniel Chester French's gilded statue Wisconsin. The interior features 43 varieties of stone, hand-carved furniture, and murals, including Edwin Howland Blashfield's work in the Assembly chamber. The building was designated a National Historic Landmark in 2001.
