Tsleil-Waututh Nation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Tsleil-Waututh Nation (Halkomelem: səlilwətaɬ), formerly known as the Burrard Indian Band, is a First Nations band government in British Columbia, Canada. The Tsleil-Waututh are a Coast Salish people whose traditional territory centres on Burrard Inlet in present-day Metro Vancouver, including lands in North Vancouver, where their main community and reserve are located.

History and territory

The name səlilwətaɬ means "People of the Inlet." Tsleil-Waututh villages and resource sites extended around Burrard Inlet and Indian Arm long before European contact; oral histories describe stewardship of the inlet's salmon, shellfish, and forests for thousands of years. Colonisation, smallpox, and reserve allocation sharply reduced the community's land base and population in the 19th century. The nation today has roughly 600 members and is governed by an elected chief and council.

Contemporary issues

The Tsleil-Waututh have been prominent in environmental stewardship of Burrard Inlet and were leading opponents of the Trans Mountain pipeline expansion, which would increase tanker traffic from the Westridge Marine Terminal in their territory; their legal challenges reached the Federal Court of Appeal, which in 2018 quashed the project's approval over inadequate consultation, before it was re-approved in 2019. The nation also participates in the Metro Vancouver economy through its development arm, Takaya Developments, and in the 2010 Winter Olympics as one of the Four Host First Nations.
