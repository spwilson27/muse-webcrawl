Eye contact

Eye contact occurs when two people look at each other's eyes at the same time. It is a form of nonverbal communication and is considered to have a large influence on social behavior. The amount and type of eye contact vary greatly between cultures and individuals, and its meaning depends heavily on context.

Eye contact is a key channel of interpersonal communication

Social significance

In many Western cultures, eye contact is interpreted as a sign of attention, confidence, honesty, and engagement. Maintaining eye contact during conversation signals that a person is listening, while avoiding it may be read as disinterest, shyness, or deception. In other cultures, including many East Asian, African, and Latin American societies, prolonged direct eye contact with a superior may be considered disrespectful, and averted gaze is a sign of deference.

Functions

Eye contact serves several functions in interaction: it regulates the flow of conversation, provides feedback, expresses emotion, and helps define relationships. Mutual gaze is also important in courtship and bonding; parents and infants use eye contact to establish attachment. Research suggests that people typically hold eye contact for only a few seconds at a time during conversation before briefly looking away.

Eye contact and disorders

Atypical eye contact is associated with several conditions. Reduced eye contact is a characteristic feature of autism spectrum disorder, while intense or prolonged staring can be perceived as threatening. Anxiety disorders, particularly social anxiety, often involve avoidance of eye contact.
