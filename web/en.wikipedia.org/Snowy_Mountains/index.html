The Snowy Mountains, known informally as "the Snowies", is an IBRA subregion and the highest mountain range on the continent of Australia. Located in southern New South Wales, the range contains Mount Kosciuszko, mainland Australia's highest peak at 2,228 metres (7,310 ft). The region is part of the larger Great Dividing Range and the Australian Alps.

Geography and climate

The Snowy Mountains are the only part of mainland Australia to receive regular heavy winter snowfall, supporting the country's main ski resorts, including Thredbo, Perisher, Charlotte Pass, and Selwyn Snow Resort. Much of the range lies within Kosciuszko National Park. The region gives rise to major rivers including the Snowy, Murray, Murrumbidgee, and Tumut.

Snowy Mountains Scheme

Between 1949 and 1974 the Snowy Mountains Hydro-electric Scheme — one of the largest engineering projects undertaken in Australia — built sixteen major dams, seven power stations, and a network of tunnels and aqueducts to divert water for irrigation and generate hydroelectricity. The scheme employed more than 100,000 people from over thirty countries and is regarded as a defining nation-building project. The expanded Snowy 2.0 pumped-hydro project was announced in 2017.

History and culture

Aboriginal groups visited the high country for thousands of years, gathering to feast on Bogong moths in summer. European pastoralists followed in the nineteenth century, and the region was immortalised in Banjo Paterson's poem "The Man from Snowy River" (1890).
