Rolls-Royce Motor Cars

Founded: 1998 (as Rolls-Royce Motor Cars Limited, under BMW)
Headquarters: Goodwood, West Sussex, England
Parent: BMW Group
Products: Phantom, Ghost, Cullinan, Spectre

Rolls-Royce Motor Cars Limited is a British luxury automobile maker, a wholly owned subsidiary of BMW AG since 2003. It was established in 1998 after BMW licensed the rights to the Rolls-Royce name and logo from Rolls-Royce Holdings plc and acquired the Spirit of Ecstasy and grille trademarks from Volkswagen AG, which had purchased the Crewe factory and the Bentley marque.

History

The Rolls-Royce car business began in 1904 with Charles Rolls and Henry Royce in Manchester. After decades of ownership changes — nationalisation in 1971, privatisation of the car division in 1973, and acquisition by Vickers in 1980 — the 1998 sale split Rolls-Royce (to BMW) from Bentley (to Volkswagen). BMW built a new purpose-designed plant at Goodwood and launched the new Phantom in January 2003.

Models

The range grew with the Ghost (2009), the Wraith fastback (2013), the Dawn convertible (2015), the Cullinan SUV (2018), and the all-electric Spectre coupé (2022). Every car is substantially hand-built and heavily bespoke; the company reports record sales in the early 2020s, exceeding 6,000 cars a year. Rolls-Royce announced that it will sell only electric vehicles from 2030.
