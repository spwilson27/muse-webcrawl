Digital platform

A digital platform is a software-based online infrastructure that facilitates interactions and transactions between users, often leveraging network effects so that the platform becomes more valuable as more participants join. Digital platforms include social media services, online marketplaces, search engines, app stores, streaming services, and sharing-economy services.

A digital platform mediates interactions between producers and consumers.

Characteristics

Platforms typically operate as multi-sided markets, serving two or more distinct user groups that depend on each other. The platform owner provides the core technology, sets rules of participation, and often monetizes through fees, subscriptions, commissions, or advertising. Data generated by user interactions is itself a key resource, informing recommendations and personalization.

Regulation and criticism

The market power of large platform companies has prompted regulatory scrutiny in the European Union, the United States, and elsewhere, including legislation such as the EU Digital Markets Act. Debate concerns competition, content moderation, privacy, and the treatment of gig-economy workers. Issues around immunity-style legal protections for platforms, such as Section 230 in the United States, are frequently discussed.
