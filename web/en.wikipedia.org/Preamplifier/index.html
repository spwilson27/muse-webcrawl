Preamplifier

A preamplifier, also known as a preamp, is a type of electronic amplifier that amplifies a very weak input signal into a stronger output signal that is robust to noise and interference, and suitable for processing by other amplifier stages. It is used to boost the signal strength to drive a cable to the main instrument without significantly degrading the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR).

Applications

Preamplifiers are found in many places: at the output of microphones and phonograph cartridges, in radio receivers next to antennas, and as the first stage of audio power amplifiers. In home audio systems, a preamplifier typically provides source selection, volume and tone controls, and, for turntables, RIAA equalisation and gain to raise the millivolt-level phono signal to line level.

Design considerations

Because a preamplifier works with very small signals, its own noise dominates system noise performance (as described by the Friis formulas for noise). Shielding, short signal paths, and low-noise transistors or valves are used to keep hum, interference, and thermal noise to a minimum.
