Kavli Prize

The Kavli Prize is an international award recognising outstanding achievements in three fields of fundamental science: astrophysics, nanoscience, and neuroscience. It was established in 2005 through a partnership between the Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters, the Norwegian Ministry of Education and Research, and the Kavli Foundation (United States), and was first awarded in 2008.

The prize

The prize is awarded every two years. Each of the three fields receives a shared prize of one million US dollars, together with a gold medal and a scroll for each laureate. The award ceremony takes place in Oslo, and laureates are chosen by committees of international scientists appointed by the Norwegian Academy, with input from academies in China, France, Germany, the United Kingdom, and the United States.

Background

The prize is named for Fred Kavli (1927–2013), the Norwegian-American physicist, engineer, and philanthropist who founded the Kavlico Corporation and the Kavli Foundation, which also endows Kavli Institutes at universities worldwide. Kavli intended the prize to recognise work "from the smallest to the largest" scales of nature — nanoscience, neuroscience, and astrophysics — complementing the Nobel Prizes.

Notable laureates

Laureates have included pioneers of inflationary cosmology, the inventors of the light-emitting diode's underlying nanoscience and of atomic-force microscopy, and neuroscientists who discovered place cells and grid cells in the brain; several Kavli laureates have subsequently received Nobel Prizes for related work.
