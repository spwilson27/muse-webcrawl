Nicaraguan cuisine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Gallo pinto, the national breakfast dish

Nicaraguan cuisine is a mixture of Spanish food and dishes of a pre-Columbian origin. Traditional dishes are also found on the Caribbean coast of Nicaragua, with influences from African and Afro-Caribbean traditions, where coconut milk and seafood are common. The national staples are maize (corn), beans, rice, plantains, and a variety of tropical fruits and root vegetables.

Main dishes

The most famous Nicaraguan dish is gallo pinto, a blend of white rice and red beans, often eaten at breakfast with fried or scrambled eggs, fried cheese and plantains. Other typical dishes include nacatamales (corn masa with pork and vegetables steamed in plantain leaves), vigorón (yuca with chicharrones and cabbage salad, served on a plantain leaf), indio viejo (a cornmeal stew with shredded meat), baho (beef, plantain and yuca steamed in plantain leaves), and grilled meats served with chimichurri. On the Caribbean coast, rondón is a spicy seafood soup cooked in coconut milk.

Drinks and sweets

Traditional drinks include pinolillo (toasted corn and cacao), chicha de maíz, tiste, and fresh fruit juices known as frescos, as well as Nicaraguan rum (Flor de Caña) and coffee from the northern highlands. Desserts and sweets include tres leches cake, pío quinto, cajeta, and rosquillas (cornmeal rings) served with coffee.
