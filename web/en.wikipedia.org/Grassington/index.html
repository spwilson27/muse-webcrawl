Grassington is a market town and civil parish in the Craven district of North Yorkshire, England, situated in Wharfedale within the Yorkshire Dales National Park. Historically part of the West Riding of Yorkshire, the village lies beside the River Wharfe, roughly 14 miles north of Skipton, and serves as a popular base for walking in Upper Wharfedale.

History

Grassington's name is of Old English origin, suggesting a farmstead or settlement at a grazing place. Although often called a village, it holds a market charter dating from 1282. The settlement grew substantially in the 17th and 18th centuries with the lead-mining industry on Grassington Moor, whose remains — shafts, chimneys and the Duke's Level drainage tunnel — survive as industrial archaeology. The arrival of the railway at nearby Threshfield in 1901–1902 (Grassington & Threshfield station, closed in 1930) made the town accessible to tourists from Leeds and Bradford.

The village today

The town centres on a cobbled market square lined with stone cottages, shops, pubs and cafés. Notable institutions include the Grassington Festival, a two-week arts and music festival held each June, and the Upper Wharfedale Museum in the square. Grassington doubled as the fictional village of Darrowby in the Channel 5 television adaptation of James Herriot's All Creatures Great and Small. Nearby attractions include Grass Wood, Linton Falls on the Wharfe, and the market town of Kettlewell up the dale.
