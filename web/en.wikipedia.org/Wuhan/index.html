Wuhan is the capital of Hubei Province in the People's Republic of China. With a population of more than 13 million in its metropolitan area, it is the most populous city in central China and a major transportation hub, sometimes called the "thoroughfare of nine provinces". The city was formed in 1927 from the merger of three towns — Wuchang, Hankou, and Hanyang — divided by the Yangtze and Han rivers.

History

The area has been settled for 3,500 years. The Yellow Crane Tower, first built in AD 223, became a famed landmark celebrated in Tang dynasty poetry. The Wuchang Uprising of 10 October 1911, which triggered the Xinhai Revolution and the fall of the Qing dynasty, began here. Wuhan was a treaty port at Hankou and a major industrial centre; it suffered occupation during the Second Sino-Japanese War and severe flooding in 1954, after which great levees and the first Yangtze River Bridge (1957) were built.

Recent history

In late 2019 Wuhan became the first known centre of the COVID-19 pandemic; the city was placed under a strict lockdown from 23 January to 8 April 2020. Today Wuhan is a centre for optoelectronics, automobile manufacturing, and higher education, home to Wuhan University and Huazhong University of Science and Technology.
