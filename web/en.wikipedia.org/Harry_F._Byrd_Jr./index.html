Harry F. Byrd Jr.

Born: 20 December 1914, Winchester, Virginia, U.S.

Died: 30 July 2013 (aged 98), Winchester, Virginia

Office: United States Senator from Virginia, 1965–1983

Party: Democratic, then Independent

Harry Flood Byrd Jr. (1914–2013) was an American newspaperman and politician who served as a United States Senator from Virginia from 1965 to 1983. He succeeded his father, Harry F. Byrd Sr., patriarch of Virginia's powerful Byrd Organization, and in 1970 became the first independent elected to the U.S. Senate by popular vote.

Early life and career

Byrd attended Virginia Military Institute and the University of Virginia, served in the U.S. Navy during the Second World War, and became editor and publisher of the family's newspapers, including the Winchester Evening Star. He served in the Virginia Senate from 1948, where he supported his father's policy of "massive resistance" to school desegregation.

U.S. Senate

Appointed in 1965 to fill his father's seat, Byrd won election in his own right in 1966. A fiscal conservative, he authored the Byrd Amendment limiting federal borrowing concepts and championed limits on the national debt. Refusing the 1970 Democratic nomination conditions, he ran and won as an independent, and was re-elected in 1976; he retired in 1983 and was succeeded by Paul Trible. He died in 2013 at 98, among the longest-lived former senators.
