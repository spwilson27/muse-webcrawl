Olfaction

Olfaction
SystemOlfactory system
OrganNose / olfactory epithelium
Key structureOlfactory bulb

Olfaction is the sense of smell, a chemosense by which odorant molecules are detected. In humans and other vertebrates, odorants bind to olfactory receptors in the olfactory epithelium at the top of the nasal cavity, and signals travel via the olfactory nerve to the olfactory bulb and onward to the brain.

Mechanism

Each olfactory receptor neuron expresses a single type of receptor; humans have roughly 400 functional receptor types, whose combined patterns of activation allow discrimination of a very large number of odours. Linda Buck and Richard Axel received the 2004 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for discovering the olfactory receptor gene family.

Disorders

Loss of smell is called anosmia; reduced smell is hyposmia, and distorted smell is dysosmia. Smell impairment can arise from infections of the upper respiratory tract, head injury, neurodegenerative diseases such as Parkinson's and Alzheimer's, and viral illness including COVID-19.

Retronasal smell

Much of what is perceived as flavour comes from retronasal olfaction, in which odorants from the mouth reach the nasal epithelium during eating.
