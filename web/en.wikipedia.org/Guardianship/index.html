Guardianship

Guardianship is a legal relationship in which a person or institution (the guardian) is appointed by a court or by a will to make decisions for another person (the ward) who is unable to manage their own affairs, most commonly because the ward is a minor or an adult lacking mental capacity.

Guardians of minors

Parents are the natural guardians of their children. When parents die or are unable to care for a child, a court may appoint a guardian, often a relative or a person named in a parent's will. The guardian assumes duties of care, upbringing and management of the child's property until the child reaches the age of majority.

Guardianship of adults

For adults, guardianship (called conservatorship in some US jurisdictions) may be imposed where a court finds that a person cannot make or communicate responsible decisions about their person or estate, for example because of dementia, intellectual disability or mental illness. A guardian may be given authority over personal decisions, finances, or both. Because guardianship removes fundamental rights, many legal systems require it to be a last resort and favour less restrictive alternatives such as powers of attorney, supported decision-making and limited guardianship tailored to the individual's needs.

Reform and criticism

Guardianship systems have been criticised for abuse and for excessive restriction of wards' autonomy, leading to reforms emphasising the ward's retained rights and periodic court review. The high-profile conservatorship of singer Britney Spears, terminated in 2021, drew worldwide attention to these issues.
