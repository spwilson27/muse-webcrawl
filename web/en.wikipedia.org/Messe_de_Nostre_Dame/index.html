Messe de Nostre Dame

Messe de Nostre Dame
Composer: Guillaume de Machaut
Date: before 1365 (probably 1360s)
Voices: four
Language: Latin

The Messe de Nostre Dame (Mass of Our Lady) is a polyphonic mass composed before 1365 by the French poet and composer Guillaume de Machaut (c. 1300–1377). It is the earliest surviving complete setting of the Mass Ordinary attributable to a single composer, and one of the most famous works of medieval music.

Structure

The mass sets the Kyrie, Gloria, Credo, Sanctus, Agnus Dei and the dismissal Ite missa est for four voices. The Kyrie, Sanctus, Agnus Dei and Ite are composed in isorhythmic style, building melodic patterns (color) over repeating rhythmic patterns (talea) in the tenor, drawn from chant. The Gloria and Credo, too long for strict isorhythm, are written in a more syllabic, conductus-like chordal style that keeps the text intelligible.

Context

Machaut, a canon of Reims Cathedral, may have written the mass for performance there; tradition associates it with the Saturday Lady Mass at Reims and with the memorial endowment of Machaut and his brother Jean. Whether it was conceived as a unified cycle — a landmark idea in Western music — or assembled for the Reims context, its survival in Machaut's complete-works manuscripts ensured its fame. It exemplifies the ars nova style of fourteenth-century France.

Recordings and reception

Recorded by ensembles such as the Tallis Scholars, the Orlando Consort and the Ensemble Musica Nova, the mass remains a cornerstone of early-music performance and scholarship on the origins of the cyclic mass.
