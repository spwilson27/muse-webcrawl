Malus

Kingdom: Plantae
Family: Rosaceae
Genus: Malus Mill.
Type species: Malus domestica

Malus is a genus of about 30–55 species of small deciduous trees and shrubs in the rose family, Rosaceae, including the domesticated orchard apple (Malus domestica) and the various crabapples. The genus is native to the temperate zone of the Northern Hemisphere.

Description

Malus species are typically 4–12 m tall, with simple, often lobed leaves and showy five-petalled flowers in shades of pink and white appearing in spring. The fruit is a pome; in wild species it is often small and sour, while cultivated forms have been selected for large, sweet fruit. The domestic apple descends principally from the Central Asian wild species Malus sieversii, with contributions from other species through hybridisation.

Cultivation and uses

Apples are among the most widely grown temperate fruits. Crabapples are planted as ornamentals for their blossom and fruit and serve as pollinators in orchards. Many species are susceptible to diseases such as apple scab, fire blight and cedar-apple rust.
