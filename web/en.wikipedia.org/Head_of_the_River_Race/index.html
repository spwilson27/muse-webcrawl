Head of the River Race

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Head of the River Race
Sport: Rowing
Location: River Thames, London
Course: Mortlake to Putney, 4 miles 374 yards (6.8 km)
First held: 1926
Format: Processional time trial for men's eights

The Head of the River Race (HORR) is a processional rowing race held annually on the River Thames in London, traditionally on the penultimate or last Saturday in March, depending on the tides. It is contested by men's eights over the 4-mile 374-yard (6.8 km) Championship Course from Mortlake to Putney — the reverse direction of The Boat Race — and is rowed on the ebb tide starting at high water.

History

The race was founded in 1926 by Steve Fairbairn, the influential Cambridge and Thames Rowing Club coach, who believed that long-distance processional racing was ideal winter training and opened the event to all crews, not just elite ones. The first race drew 21 crews. The event grew into one of the largest rowing races in the world, attracting around 400 crews — more than 3,600 competitors — in strong years, including British and international club, university, and occasionally national-team crews.

Format

Crews start at 10-second intervals in the finishing order of the previous year's race (with the previous winner going off first, defending the title "Head of the River"), and are timed over the course; the fastest crew overall wins. Pennants are awarded in numerous categories by status and class. Because it is run on the tidal Thames in early spring, the race is occasionally shortened or cancelled for rough water or safety, notably in several years in the 2000s and 2010s.

Related races

The Head of the River Race inspired many imitations and sister events on the same course, including the Women's Eights Head of the River Race (WEHoRR), the Schools' Head, the Vesta Veterans' Head, and the Pairs and Fours Heads.
