QMA

QMA (Quantum Merlin–Arthur) is a complexity class in quantum computing and computational complexity theory. It is the quantum analogue of the classical complexity class NP, as well as of the probabilistic class MA. In QMA, a polynomial-time quantum verifier (Arthur) receives a quantum state as a proof (witness) from an all-powerful prover (Merlin), and must decide whether the input belongs to the language.

Definition

A language L is in QMA if there exists a polynomial-time uniform family of quantum circuits (the verifier) and a polynomial p such that:

  • Completeness: for every x in L, there exists a p(|x|)-qubit state |ψ⟩ (the witness) that the verifier accepts with probability at least 2/3;
  • Soundness: for every x not in L, every p(|x|)-qubit state is accepted with probability at most 1/3.

The error probabilities can be reduced to exponentially small values by amplification, although the standard parallel repetition used for classical classes does not directly apply because the witness is a quantum state that cannot be cloned.

Complete problems

The canonical QMA-complete problem is the local Hamiltonian problem, introduced by Alexei Kitaev: given a Hamiltonian that is a sum of terms each acting on a constant number of qubits, decide whether its smallest eigenvalue is small or large. This result parallels the Cook–Levin theorem for NP. Variants include the 2-local Hamiltonian problem, which remains QMA-complete, and quantum satisfiability (QSAT) problems.

Relationships

QMA contains both NP and BQP. It is contained in the class PP. A variant, QMA(2), allows the prover to send two unentangled witness states. Whether QMA equals QMA(2) is an open question with connections to the quantum PCP conjecture.
