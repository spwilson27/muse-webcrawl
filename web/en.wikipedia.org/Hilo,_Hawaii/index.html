Hilo, Hawaii

Hilo
State: Hawaii

Hilo is a town and census-designated place in Hawaii County on the Island of Hawaii in the U.S. state of Hawaii. It is the seat of Hawaii County and the largest settlement on the island, with a population of roughly 45,000. Located on Hilo Bay on the island's windward (eastern) coast, Hilo is often described as the rainiest city in the United States, receiving around 130 inches (3,300 mm) of rain per year.

History

The Hilo area was settled by Polynesian voyagers more than a thousand years ago and later became a centre of trade and missionary activity in the nineteenth century. The town was twice devastated by tsunamis generated by distant earthquakes, in 1946 (from Alaska) and 1960 (from Chile), which together killed scores of residents; the waterfront was subsequently kept as open parkland.

Economy and culture

Hilo hosts a campus of the University of Hawaii and serves the island's agricultural east side, historically sugar cane and now macadamia nuts, papaya and floriculture. The annual Merrie Monarch Festival, a major celebration of hula, is held in Hilo, and the town is a gateway to Hawaii Volcanoes National Park.
