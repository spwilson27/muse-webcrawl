Gelderland is a province of the Netherlands, located in the central-eastern part of the country. It is the largest Dutch province by land area. Its capital is Arnhem, while Nijmegen, the oldest city in the Netherlands, is its largest city. Other important centres include Apeldoorn, Ede, Zutphen and Doetinchem.

Geography

Gelderland is crossed by the great rivers Rhine, Waal and IJssel, which shape its southern river landscape of polders, orchards (the Betuwe) and floodplains. The centre and north-west are dominated by the Veluwe, a large area of forests, heathland and sand drifts containing De Hoge Veluwe National Park, famous for the Kröller-Müller Museum. The east, the Achterhoek, is a traditional farming region of small fields and estates.

History

The province takes its name from the medieval Duchy of Guelders, a state of the Holy Roman Empire named after the town of Geldern (now in Germany). Guelders was a frequent rival of the Burgundian and Habsburg Netherlands before being absorbed into them in the sixteenth century. During the Dutch Republic it was one of the seven provinces of the union.

In September 1944 Gelderland was the scene of Operation Market Garden, the Allied attempt to cross the Rhine at Arnhem — the "bridge too far" — and the province suffered heavy fighting and destruction before liberation in 1945. Arnhem's John Frost Bridge commemorates the battle.

Economy and culture

The provincial economy mixes agriculture and horticulture, manufacturing, logistics along the river and motorway corridors, tourism in the Veluwe, and knowledge institutions centred on Wageningen University and Research, renowned for agriculture and food science. The province is governed by the States of Gelderland and a King's Commissioner.
