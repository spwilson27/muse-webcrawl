American League

The American League of Professional Baseball Clubs, known simply as the American League (AL), is one of two leagues making up Major League Baseball (MLB) in the United States and Canada. It developed from the Western League, a minor league based in the Great Lakes states, which was reorganised by Ban Johnson and declared itself a major league in 1901.

History

The American League's first season as a major league was 1901, with eight charter teams including clubs in Boston, Chicago, and Philadelphia. In 1903 the AL champion Boston Americans defeated the National League's Pittsburgh Pirates in the first World Series. The league expanded and relocated franchises through the twentieth century. From 1973, the AL used the designated hitter rule in place of the pitcher batting; the National League adopted a universal designated hitter in 2022, ending the leagues' most visible difference.

Teams and structure

The league comprises 15 teams divided into three divisions of five: East, Central, and West. Its members include the New York Yankees, the Boston Red Sox, the Chicago White Sox, the Houston Astros (who moved from the National League in 2013), and one Canadian team, the Toronto Blue Jays. The AL champion receives the William Harridge Trophy and advances to the World Series.
