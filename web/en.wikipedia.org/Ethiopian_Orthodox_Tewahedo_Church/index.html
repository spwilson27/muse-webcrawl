Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church

The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church is an Oriental Orthodox Christian church in Ethiopia, the largest of the Oriental Orthodox churches and one of the oldest Christian bodies in the world. Tewahedo (Ge'ez for "being made one") refers to its Miaphysite Christology, affirming the united nature of Christ. It counts tens of millions of adherents, mostly in Ethiopia.

History

Christianity was introduced to the Aksumite Empire in the 4th century by Frumentius, who became its first bishop. For centuries the church's head (abuna) was appointed by the Coptic Patriarch of Alexandria; in 1959 it became autocephalous with its own patriarch. It survived the medieval Muslim invasions, the Jesuit episode of the 17th century, Italian occupation and persecution under the Derg after 1974, when it was disestablished.

Faith and practice

The church's canon is the broadest in Christendom, including books such as Enoch and Jubilees preserved fully only in Ge'ez. Practices include fasting for much of the year, observance of Saturday as well as Sunday, dietary laws, circumcision, and veneration of the tabot, a replica of the Ark of the Covenant kept in every church. Liturgy is celebrated in Ge'ez with distinctive chant (zema) attributed to Saint Yared.

Organisation

The Patriarch (Abune) heads the Holy Synod. The Eritrean Orthodox Tewahedo Church became separate after Eritrean independence. A schism beginning in 1991 over a rival patriarch was largely reconciled in 2018, though further regional tensions emerged in the 2020s. See also Oriental Orthodox Churches, Aksumite Empire and Ge'ez.
