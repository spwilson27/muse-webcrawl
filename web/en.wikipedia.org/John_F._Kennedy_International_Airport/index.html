John F. Kennedy International Airport (IATA: JFK) is the main international airport serving New York City and the busiest international air passenger gateway into North America. Located in the Jamaica neighbourhood of Queens, about 16 miles (26 km) southeast of Midtown Manhattan, it is operated by the Port Authority of New York and New Jersey and serves as a hub for American Airlines, Delta Air Lines and JetBlue.

History

Construction began in 1942 on the site of the Idlewild golf course, and the airport opened in 1948 as New York International Airport, commonly called Idlewild. It was renamed in December 1963 in memory of President John F. Kennedy, assassinated a month earlier. During the jet age the airport's "Terminal City" of individual airline terminals included celebrated designs such as Eero Saarinen's TWA Flight Center (1962), now the TWA Hotel.

Facilities and operations

JFK has four runways and five active passenger terminals (1, 4, 5, 7 and 8), connected by the AirTrain JFK light rail system, which also links to the New York City Subway and Long Island Rail Road. More than 90 airlines operate from the airport to destinations on six continents. A major redevelopment programme, announced in the 2020s, includes new Terminal 1 and Terminal 6 projects intended to modernise the airport's facilities.

Traffic

The airport handles over 60 million passengers a year, ranking among the busiest in the United States, and is a major cargo gateway as well.
