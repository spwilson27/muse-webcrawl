Silesian Voivodeship (Polish: województwo śląskie) is a province (voivodeship) in southern Poland, centred on the historic region of Upper Silesia. Created on 1 January 1999 from the former Katowice, Bielsko-Biała, and Częstochowa voivodeships, it is the second most populous of Poland's sixteen voivodeships and the most densely populated, despite being among the smallest in area.

Geography

The voivodeship borders the Czech Republic and Slovakia to the south, where the Beskid Mountains form a popular area for hiking and skiing. Its north and centre are dominated by the Upper Silesian Industrial Region, a conurbation of cities including Katowice, Sosnowiec, Gliwice, Zabrze, Bytom, and Tychy, home to more than two million people.

Economy

Historically built on coal mining and steelmaking, the region's economy has diversified into automotive manufacturing (with major plants at Gliwice and Tychy), information technology, and services. It remains Poland's most important mining region, though many collieries have closed since 1989.

Culture

The region has a distinct Silesian identity, including the Silesian language (dialect), cuisine such as rolada with kluski śląskie, and traditions shaped by mining culture. Jasna Góra Monastery in Częstochowa, in the voivodeship's north, is one of Poland's foremost pilgrimage sites.
