Guelders Wars

The Guelders Wars were a series of conflicts fought between the House of Habsburg and Charles of Egmond, Duke of Guelders, from 1502 to 1543 over control of the Duchy of Guelders and the county of Zutphen in the Low Countries.

Background

Guelders had been claimed by the Burgundian dukes since the fifteenth century. When Charles of Egmond recovered the duchy in 1492 with French support, the Habsburgs refused to accept the loss. Charles allied himself repeatedly with France, then the Habsburgs' principal enemy in the Italian Wars, and raided Habsburg territories including Holland, Friesland and Brabant.

Course of the wars

Fighting flared intermittently for four decades. Charles's forces, led by the notorious mercenary commander Maarten van Rossum, sacked The Hague in 1528. The Habsburgs gradually absorbed the neighbouring lordships of Friesland, Utrecht and Overijssel, isolating Guelders. By the Treaty of Gorinchem (1528) Charles recognised Habsburg overlordship over those territories while retaining Guelders itself.

Conclusion

Charles of Egmond died childless in 1538 and was succeeded by William, Duke of Jülich-Cleves-Berg, who continued the struggle with French support. Defeated by the emperor Charles V, William renounced Guelders and Zutphen by the Treaty of Venlo in 1543, and the duchy was incorporated into the Habsburg Netherlands as one of the Seventeen Provinces.
