Jarnail Singh Bhindranwale

Jarnail Singh Bhindranwale (2 June 1947 – 6 June 1984) was an Indian Sikh religious leader and the fourteenth jathedar (leader) of the Damdami Taksal, a Sikh religious and educational organization. He became a prominent and controversial figure in Punjab during the late 1970s and early 1980s, advocating for Sikh religious revival and the Anandpur Sahib Resolution.

Early life

Bhindranwale was born in the village of Rode in the Moga district of Punjab, into a Jat Sikh farming family. He received religious education and joined the Damdami Taksal, becoming its leader in 1977 after the death of Kartar Singh Khalsa.

Rise to prominence

He preached a return to the Khalsa tradition, urging Sikhs to abandon alcohol, drugs and the cutting of hair, and to take up arms in defence of the faith. His influence grew amid tensions between the Punjab government, the Indian central government and Sikh political groups. In 1982 he launched the Dharam Yudh Morcha (righteous struggle) alongside the Akali Dal to press for greater autonomy for Punjab.

Operation Blue Star and death

In 1983 Bhindranwale took up residence in the Golden Temple complex in Amritsar, which was fortified by his armed followers. In June 1984 the Indian Army launched Operation Blue Star to remove the militants from the temple. Bhindranwale was killed during the assault on 6 June 1984. The operation caused extensive damage to the Akal Takht and deeply angered many Sikhs, contributing to the assassination of Prime Minister Indira Gandhi later that year and the anti-Sikh riots that followed.

Legacy

Bhindranwale remains a divisive figure: regarded by the Indian government and many commentators as a militant extremist, he is honoured as a martyr (sant) by some Sikhs, and his portrait hangs in the Central Sikh Museum in Amritsar.
