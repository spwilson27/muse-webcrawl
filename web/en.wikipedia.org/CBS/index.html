CBS

CBS Broadcasting Inc., commonly known as CBS (an abbreviation of its former legal name, the Columbia Broadcasting System), is an American commercial broadcast television and radio network. It is the flagship property of the CBS Entertainment Group division of Paramount Global.

History

CBS began as a radio network in 1927, founded by William S. Paley, who bought the struggling Columbia Phonographic Broadcasting System and built it into a major competitor to NBC. Under Paley and president Frank Stanton, CBS became renowned for news, led by Edward R. Murrow and later Walter Cronkite, the longtime anchor of the CBS Evening News.

Television

The CBS television network launched commercially in 1941 and became a ratings leader with programmes such as I Love Lucy, The Ed Sullivan Show, 60 Minutes, M*A*S*H, The Big Bang Theory and NCIS. Its eye logo, introduced in 1951, is one of the most recognisable corporate symbols in broadcasting.

Divisions

CBS operations include CBS News, CBS Sports, CBS Studios and a group of owned-and-operated local stations. The network's slogan historically included "Welcome Home America" and it was long nicknamed the "Tiffany Network".
