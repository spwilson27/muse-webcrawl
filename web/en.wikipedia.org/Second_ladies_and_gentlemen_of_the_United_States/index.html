The second lady of the United States or second gentleman of the United States is the informal title held by the spouse of the vice president of the United States, concurrent with the vice president's term of office. The title is a counterpart to the first lady, the spouse of the president. The role has no official duties, salary, or constitutional basis, but second spouses have traditionally acted as hostesses of the vice-presidential residence at Number One Observatory Circle and supported charitable and public causes.

History

The first person to hold the position was Abigail Adams, wife of Vice President John Adams, who served from 1789 to 1797 before becoming first lady herself. For much of the nineteenth century the role was obscure, reflecting the low profile of the vice presidency itself. The position gained prominence in the twentieth century as the vice presidency grew in importance; Pat Nixon, Lady Bird Johnson, and others later became first ladies.

In 2021, Douglas Emhoff became the first second gentleman, as spouse of Vice President Kamala Harris. Usha Vance became second lady in January 2025 as the spouse of Vice President JD Vance.

Role and activities

Second spouses typically accompany the vice president at official functions, undertake goodwill travel, and champion causes such as literacy, military families, education, and the arts. Jill Biden, who held the role from 2009 to 2017, was the first second spouse to hold a paying job during her tenure, continuing to teach English at a community college; she later became first lady. Karen Pence focused on art therapy, and Lynne Cheney, an author and historian, promoted American history education.

Residence

Since 1977, the vice president and family have resided at Number One Observatory Circle on the grounds of the United States Naval Observatory in Washington, D.C., and the second spouse traditionally serves as its host.
