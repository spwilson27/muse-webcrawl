Mosasaur

Mosasaurs (family Mosasauridae) were a group of large marine reptiles that lived during the Late Cretaceous period, roughly 82 to 66 million years ago. They were apex predators of the oceans in the final stages of the age of dinosaurs.

Description

Mosasaurs had streamlined bodies, paddle-like limbs, and powerful tails. Early forms propelled themselves mainly with a long tail; later species developed a crescent-shaped tail fluke. The largest, such as Mosasaurus hoffmannii, exceeded 12 metres in length. Their jaws carried conical teeth suited to gripping fish, ammonites, and other marine reptiles.

Discovery

The first mosasaur fossils were found in the 18th century in a limestone quarry near Maastricht in the Netherlands, along the Meuse River, from which the name Mosasaurus ("Meuse lizard") derives. Georges Cuvier used the fossils as evidence for the concept of extinction.

Evolution and extinction

Mosasaurs were squamates, closely related to monitor lizards and snakes. They diversified rapidly in the Late Cretaceous seas and disappeared in the Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event 66 million years ago, along with the non-avian dinosaurs and plesiosaurs.
