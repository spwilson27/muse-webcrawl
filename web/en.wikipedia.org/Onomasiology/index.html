Onomasiology

Onomasiology is a branch of linguistics concerned with the study of how concepts are expressed by different words and expressions. It starts from a concept or referent and asks which lexical items denote it, in contrast to semasiology, which starts from a word and asks what meanings it has.

The term derives from Greek onomasia, naming. Onomasiological research is central to lexicology, terminology, dialectology and the study of synonymy, word fields and lexical change. Linguistic atlases often use an onomasiological approach, presenting maps for a single concept as named across regions.

Approach

An onomasiological dictionary is organised by concepts rather than alphabetically by word form; Roget's Thesaurus is a classic example. In terminology work, onomasiology underpins concept systems in standards, where definitions precede designation. Cognitive linguistics uses onomasiology to study salience, prototypicality and why a speaker chooses one construal over another.
