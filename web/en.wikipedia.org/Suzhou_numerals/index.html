Suzhou numerals

Suzhou numerals, also called suzhou mazi or huama, are a numeral system formerly used in China in commerce and bookkeeping. Derived from the older rod numerals, they are a positional decimal system with distinctive cursive glyphs for the digits.

Use

The numerals were prized for speed of writing and were commonly used by merchants, in markets and on price tags, especially in Jiangsu and in overseas Chinese communities such as Hong Kong, where they survived on handwritten price labels into the late 20th century.

Characteristics

Digits one to nine have special forms; special combined glyphs exist for twenty and thirty. Because the glyphs are written in a single stroke, amounts must be accompanied by Chinese characters indicating the unit and order of magnitude to prevent alteration.
