North Atlantic Current

The North Atlantic Current (NAC), also known as the North Atlantic Drift or North Atlantic Sea Movement, is a powerful warm western boundary current within the North Atlantic Ocean that extends the Gulf Stream northeastward toward northwestern Europe.

Structure

The current begins where the Gulf Stream leaves the continental slope near the Grand Banks of Newfoundland, around 40°N and 50°W. Unlike the tightly confined Gulf Stream, the North Atlantic Current meanders broadly and breaks into branches. One branch flows toward Iceland and into the Norwegian Sea, becoming the Norwegian Current; another turns south as the Azores Current; and a portion feeds the Canary Current.

Climate effects

Together with its extensions, the North Atlantic Current transports large quantities of warm tropical water to high latitudes. This oceanic heat transport, combined with prevailing westerly winds that blow across it, is a major reason that northwestern Europe enjoys a much milder climate than other regions at the same latitude, such as Labrador in Canada.

Relation to the AMOC

The North Atlantic Current is the surface limb of the Atlantic meridional overturning circulation (AMOC). Cooled, denser water sinks in the Nordic Seas and the Labrador Sea and returns southward at depth. Oceanographers monitor the current for possible changes linked to climate warming and freshwater input from Greenland.
