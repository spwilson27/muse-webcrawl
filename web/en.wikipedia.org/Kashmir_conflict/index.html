Kashmir conflict

The Kashmir conflict is a territorial dispute over the Kashmir region, primarily between India and Pakistan, and between India and China. It began after the partition of India in 1947, when both countries claimed the former princely state of Jammu and Kashmir in full.

Background

At partition, the Hindu maharaja of the Muslim-majority state, Hari Singh, initially sought independence but acceded to India in October 1947 after an invasion by tribal fighters from Pakistan. The resulting Indo-Pakistani War of 1947–48 ended with a United Nations-brokered ceasefire that divided the state: Pakistan controlled the north-western areas (Azad Kashmir and Gilgit-Baltistan), while India held the Kashmir Valley, Jammu and Ladakh. UN resolutions called for a plebiscite, which has never been held.

Wars and insurgency

India and Pakistan fought further wars over Kashmir in 1965 and during the Kargil conflict of 1999. The 1972 Simla Agreement converted the ceasefire line into the Line of Control. China controls Aksai Chin, claimed by India, and received the Trans-Karakoram Tract from Pakistan in 1963; India and China fought a border war in 1962.

An armed insurgency in Indian-administered Kashmir began in 1989, demanding independence or merger with Pakistan; tens of thousands have died in the violence. In August 2019 the Indian government revoked the special autonomous status of Jammu and Kashmir under Article 370 of the Indian constitution and reorganised it into two union territories, a move condemned by Pakistan and China.
