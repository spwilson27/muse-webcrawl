Correlation

In statistics, correlation or dependence is any statistical relationship, whether causal or not, between two random variables or bivariate data. In the broadest sense, correlation is any statistical association, though it most commonly refers to the degree to which a pair of variables is linearly related.

Pearson correlation coefficient

The most familiar measure is the Pearson product-moment correlation coefficient, which is the covariance of two variables divided by the product of their standard deviations. It ranges from −1 to +1: +1 indicates a perfect positive linear relationship, −1 a perfect negative relationship, and 0 no linear relationship. Pearson's coefficient measures only linear association, so strongly dependent variables can still have a coefficient near zero if their relationship is nonlinear.

Rank correlation

Rank correlation coefficients, such as Spearman's rank correlation coefficient and Kendall's tau, measure the degree to which one variable increases as the other increases, without requiring a linear relationship. They are based on the ranked values of the data rather than the raw values.

Correlation and causation

A correlation between two variables does not imply that one causes the other. Observed associations may arise from a common cause, reverse causation, coincidence, or confounding variables. Establishing causation generally requires controlled experiments or careful causal inference.
