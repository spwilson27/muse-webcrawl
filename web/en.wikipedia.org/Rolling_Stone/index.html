Rolling Stone is an American monthly magazine that focuses on music, politics, and popular culture. It was founded in San Francisco, California, in 1967 by Jann Wenner and the music critic Ralph J. Gleason. It was first known for its coverage of rock music and political reporting by Hunter S. Thompson.

History

The first issue was published on November 9, 1967, with John Lennon on the cover. The magazine's name refers to the Muddy Waters song "Rollin' Stone", the band the Rolling Stones, and Bob Dylan's "Like a Rolling Stone". In the 1970s, Rolling Stone became central to rock journalism, publishing writers such as Lester Bangs, Greil Marcus, and Cameron Crowe, and the gonzo journalism of Hunter S. Thompson, whose Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas was first serialized in its pages. Photographer Annie Leibovitz's covers became iconic.

Coverage and features

Beyond music, the magazine has published major political and investigative reporting. It is known for its lists, including the "500 Greatest Albums of All Time" (first published 2003, updated 2020) and the "500 Greatest Songs of All Time". Its cover stories frequently feature leading musicians, actors, and political figures.

Ownership

In 2017, Wenner Media sold a controlling stake in the magazine to Penske Media Corporation, which acquired full ownership in 2019. Edition partnerships publish Rolling Stone in numerous countries and languages.

Criticism

The magazine was widely criticized for its 2014 article "A Rape on Campus" about an alleged gang rape at the University of Virginia, which was retracted after investigations found its central account could not be corroborated; the magazine settled defamation lawsuits arising from the story.
