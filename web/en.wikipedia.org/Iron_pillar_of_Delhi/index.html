Iron pillar of Delhi

Location: Qutb complex, Mehrauli, Delhi, India

Height: 7.21 m (23 ft 8 in)

Weight: Over 6 tonnes

Erected: c. 4th–5th century AD

The iron pillar of Delhi is a structure 7.21 metres (23 feet 8 inches) high with a 41-centimetre (16 in) diameter, constructed by Chandragupta II (reigned c. 375–415 AD) and now standing in the Qutb complex at Mehrauli in Delhi, India. It is famous for the rust-resistant composition of the metals used in its construction.

Description and history

The pillar, made of 98% wrought iron, was originally erected as a Vishnudhvaja (standard of the god Vishnu) on a hill near Udayagiri, in honour of Chandragupta II. A Sanskrit inscription in Brahmi script records that it was set up by a king named Chandra. It was later moved to its present location, likely by the Tomara king Anangpal in the 11th century, and now stands in the courtyard of the Quwwat-ul-Islam Mosque.

Corrosion resistance

Despite some 1,600 years of exposure, the pillar shows remarkably little corrosion. Scientific studies attribute this to an even layer of crystalline iron hydrogen phosphate hydrate forming a protective film, the result of the iron's high phosphorus content (about 1%), low sulfur and manganese, and the slag left by the forge-welding manufacturing process. The pillar is a testament to the skill of ancient Indian iron workers in extracting and processing iron.
