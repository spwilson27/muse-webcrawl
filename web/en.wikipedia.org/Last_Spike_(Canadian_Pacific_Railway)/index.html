Last Spike (Canadian Pacific Railway)

The Last Spike of the Canadian Pacific Railway was driven on 7 November 1885 at Craigellachie, British Columbia, by Donald Smith (later Lord Strathcona), completing Canada's first transcontinental railway and fulfilling the promise made to British Columbia on joining Confederation in 1871.

Ceremony

The famous photograph by Andrew J. Russell's counterpart shows Smith hammering the plain iron spike amid railway officials and workers. The first through train reached Port Moody the following year, and regular service to Vancouver began in 1887.

Legacy

The event is an iconic moment in Canadian history, commemorated by a monument at Craigellachie and in Pierre Berton's bestselling history The Last Spike (1971).
