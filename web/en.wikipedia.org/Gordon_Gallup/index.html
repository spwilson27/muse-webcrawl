Gordon Gallup


Gordon G. Gallup Jr., evolutionary psychologist.

Gordon G. Gallup Jr. (born 1941) is an American psychologist and evolutionary biologist best known for developing the mirror test, also called the mirror self-recognition test, in 1970. He is a professor of psychology at the University at Albany, State University of New York.

Mirror self-recognition

While at Tulane University, Gallup devised an experimental procedure in which chimpanzees were exposed to a mirror and then anaesthetized and marked with an odourless dye on the brow. On recovering, animals that had learned the mirror's significance touched the mark on their own bodies while using the mirror, demonstrating that they recognized the reflection as themselves. Gallup argued that self-recognition reflects a capacity for self-awareness and possibly a rudimentary form of consciousness, found reliably only in the great apes among the animals he tested. The mirror test has since become a standard, if contested, instrument in comparative psychology.

Other research

Gallup's broader research programme applies evolutionary reasoning to human behaviour. With colleagues he studied tonic immobility (the "death feint") in animals, human semen-displacement adaptations, the adaptive significance of morning sickness, and voice characteristics associated with physical formidability. His work is frequently cited within evolutionary psychology, and sometimes criticized for the difficulty of testing adaptive hypotheses about human behaviour.
