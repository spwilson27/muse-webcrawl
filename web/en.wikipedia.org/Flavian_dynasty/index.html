Flavian dynasty

Flavian dynasty
Reign: AD 69 – 96
Emperors: Vespasian, Titus, Domitian
Preceded by: Year of the Four Emperors
Followed by: Nerva–Antonine dynasty

The Flavian dynasty was a Roman imperial dynasty that ruled the Roman Empire from AD 69 to AD 96, comprising the reigns of Vespasian (69–79), and his two sons Titus (79–81) and Domitian (81–96).

Rise to power

The Flavians rose during the civil wars of the Year of the Four Emperors (69). Vespasian, then a general suppressing the First Jewish–Roman War, was proclaimed emperor by his troops in the east and prevailed after the Battle of Bedriacum.

Vespasian

Vespasian restored the empire's finances, began construction of the Flavian Amphitheatre (Colosseum), and saw Jerusalem fall in 70 under his son Titus.

Titus

Titus's brief reign was marked by the eruption of Vesuvius (79), a fire in Rome, and the dedication of the Colosseum (80).

Domitian

Domitian pursued autocratic rule, strengthened the frontier (notably in Germania), and was assassinated in 96, after which the Senate condemned his memory (damnatio memoriae).

Legacy

The Flavians are remembered for monumental building in Rome, fiscal restoration, and the expansion of imperial administration.
