Overseas France

Overseas France (France d'outre-mer) consists of the French-administered territories outside Europe, remnants of the French colonial empire. They give France the world's second-largest exclusive economic zone, largely because of their surrounding oceans.

Statuses

Overseas departments and regions — Guadeloupe, Martinique, French Guiana, Réunion and Mayotte — are integral parts of France and the European Union. Overseas collectivities such as French Polynesia, Saint Martin, Saint Barthélemy, Saint Pierre and Miquelon, and Wallis and Futuna have varying autonomy. New Caledonia has a special status, and French Southern and Antarctic Lands are uninhabited territories administered from Réunion.

Population

About 2.8 million people live in overseas France. Residents are French citizens, represented in the French Parliament and (for departments) use the euro.
