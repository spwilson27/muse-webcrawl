Gutter

A rain gutter collecting water from a roof edge.

A gutter is a channel that collects and diverts water. The term most commonly refers to a rain gutter (also eavestrough), a narrow trough fixed at the edge of a roof that gathers rainwater and discharges it through downspouts, protecting walls, foundations and landscaping from water damage. Gutters are made of aluminium, vinyl, galvanised steel, zinc or copper, in profiles such as K-style and half-round, and may be fitted with guards to exclude leaves and debris.

Street gutters

A street gutter is the shallow channel at the edge of a roadway, between the pavement and the kerb, that conveys stormwater to drains. Street gutters are part of urban drainage design and historically were open sewers in many cities.

Other uses

In bowling, the gutters are the channels flanking the lane into which a misdirected ball falls. In printing and bookbinding, the gutter is the inner margin of a page near the binding. In comics, the gutter is the blank space between panels across which the reader infers the action.
