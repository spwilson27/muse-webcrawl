Victimology

Victimology is the study of the victims of crime and war, and the relationships between victims and offenders. It investigates the patterns of victimization, the psychological effects on victims, and the interactions of victims with the justice system.

[Image: Researchers surveying crime victims in a community interview setting.]

History

The field emerged in the 1940s with scholars such as Benjamin Mendelsohn and Hans von Hentig, who classified victims by degrees of culpability — an approach later criticized for sliding into victim-blaming. The field matured with large victimization surveys, such as the United States' National Crime Victimization Survey, which reveal the "dark figure" of unreported crime.

Concepts

Core topics include victim precipitation, the cycle of violence, secondary victimization by the justice process itself, repeat victimization, and the development of victim impact statements and victims' rights movements.
