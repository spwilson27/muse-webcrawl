Taliban

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Taliban (Pashto: طالبان, ṭālibān, "students"), which also refers to itself as the Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan, is a Pashtun nationalist and Islamist militant movement that has ruled Afghanistan since 2021, having previously governed the country from 1996 to 2001. Founded in 1994 by Mullah Mohammed Omar and other former mujahideen, the movement emerged from the chaos of the Afghan civil war that followed the Soviet withdrawal.

First period of rule (1996–2001)

The Taliban captured Kabul in 1996 and established an Islamic emirate governed by a strict interpretation of Sharia law. Their rule was marked by severe restrictions on women and girls — including bans on education and most employment — public executions, and the destruction of the Buddhas of Bamiyan in 2001. Only Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates recognized the regime diplomatically.

After the September 11 attacks, the Taliban's refusal to hand over al-Qaeda leader Osama bin Laden led to a United States-led invasion in late 2001 that drove the movement from power.

Insurgency and return to power

For two decades the Taliban waged an insurgency against the US-backed Islamic Republic of Afghanistan. Following the 2020 Doha Agreement and the withdrawal of US forces, the Taliban launched a rapid offensive in 2021, capturing Kabul on 15 August 2021. The restored Islamic Emirate has not been formally recognized by any state as Afghanistan's legitimate government, and its policies — particularly the exclusion of girls from secondary school — have drawn widespread international condemnation.
