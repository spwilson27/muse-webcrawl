Ecosophy

Ecosophy or ecophilosophy is a philosophy of ecological harmony or equilibrium. The term was coined by the French post-Marxist philosopher Félix Guattari and the Norwegian philosopher Arne Næss, founder of the deep ecology movement.

Arne Næss

Næss defined ecosophy as "a philosophy of ecological harmony or equilibrium. A philosophy as a kind of sofia (or) wisdom, is openly normative, it contains both norms, rules, postulates, value priority announcements and hypotheses concerning the state of affairs in our universe." For Næss, an ecosophy was a personal philosophy — his own was called "Ecosophy T", T for Tvergastein, his mountain cabin — from which guidelines for ecological action could be derived, as in the deep ecology platform he formulated with George Sessions.

Félix Guattari

In The Three Ecologies (1989), Guattari used the term for an ethico-political articulation among three registers: the environment, social relations, and human subjectivity. His ecosophy treats ecological crisis as inseparable from crises of society and subjectivity.
