Anatolic Theme

The Anatolic Theme (Greek: θέμα Ἀνατολικόν, thema Anatolikon), more properly the Theme of the Anatolics, was a Byzantine theme (a military-civilian province) in central Asia Minor. It was the largest and most important of the themes, providing the empire's senior military commander, and its strategos ranked first among the thematic generals.

History

The theme originated in the late 7th century from the field army of the East (Anatolikon) of the late Roman army, which withdrew into Asia Minor as the Arab conquests overran Syria. Its capital was Amorium. The Anatolic Theme covered much of central Anatolia and was the empire's first line of defence against Arab raids from the Cilician Gates.

Decline

Over the 8th to 10th centuries, portions of the theme were split off to form smaller districts, but the Anatolics retained their prestige; emperors such as Nikephoros II Phokas emerged from its military aristocracy. After the Battle of Manzikert in 1071 and the subsequent Seljuk Turkish advance, the theme was progressively lost and ceased to exist by the end of that century.
