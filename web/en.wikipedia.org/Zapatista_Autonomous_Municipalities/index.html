Zapatista Autonomous Municipalities

The Zapatista Autonomous Municipalities were a system of self-governing communities in the Mexican state of Chiapas, administered by the Zapatista Army of National Liberation (EZLN) and its civilian support bases from 1994 until a major reorganization in 2023.

Background

Following the Zapatista uprising of 1994, indigenous communities in the Lacandon Jungle and highlands of Chiapas established autonomous municipalities (known as MAREZ, from the Spanish Municipios Autónomos Rebeldes Zapatistas) that refused government services and governed themselves through assemblies.

Governance

From 2003 the municipalities were coordinated regionally by Juntas de Buen Gobierno ("Good Government Councils") seated in centres called caracoles. The system emphasized rotating, unpaid, recallable delegates and the principle of mandar obedeciendo ("to rule by obeying"). The communities ran their own schools, clinics, and cooperatives.

Reorganization

In November 2023 the EZLN announced the dissolution of the MAREZ and the Juntas de Buen Gobierno, replacing them with new structures of local autonomous government, citing changing conditions including organized-crime violence in Chiapas.
