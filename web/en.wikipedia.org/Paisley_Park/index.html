Paisley Park

LocationChanhassen, Minnesota, U.S.
Opened1987
OwnerPrince (P.R.N. Productions)
TypeRecording studio complex, residence, museum

Paisley Park is a 65,000-square-foot recording studio complex and former residence of the American musician Prince, located in Chanhassen, Minnesota. Opened in 1987, the complex contained recording studios, a soundstage, rehearsal spaces, and Prince's private residence. It took its name from Prince's 1985 song "Paisley Park".

History

Designed by architect Bret Thoeny, Paisley Park hosted recordings by Prince and many other artists, as well as concerts and private events. Prince lived at the property for much of his life. It was at Paisley Park that he died on 21 April 2016. Following his death, the complex was opened to the public as a museum in October 2016, offering guided tours of its studios, soundstage, and exhibits of Prince's costumes, instruments, and awards.

The vault

Paisley Park is famous for its vault, in which Prince stored an enormous archive of unreleased recordings. After his death, the archive became the subject of considerable public and legal interest, and material from it has been released posthumously by his estate.
