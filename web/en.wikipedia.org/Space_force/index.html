A space force is a military branch of a nation's armed forces dedicated to conducting military operations in outer space and space warfare. The term is used both for independent services, such as the United States Space Force, and for space components within other services, such as Russia's Space Forces within its Aerospace Forces.

Missions

Space forces typically operate military satellites providing communications, navigation and timing (such as GPS), missile launch warning, weather data and reconnaissance; they also track objects in orbit (space domain awareness), operate ground stations and launch ranges, and increasingly plan for defending friendly satellites and denying adversaries the use of space.

History and examples

Military space activities began with Cold War satellite programmes run mainly by air forces and strategic rocket forces. The Soviet Union and then Russia maintained dedicated space troops from the 1990s, reorganised over time. The United States established the United States Space Force on 20 December 2019 as the first independent space service, organised under the Department of the Air Force; its personnel are called Guardians. Other states have followed with commands rather than full services, including France's Space Command (2019), the United Kingdom's UK Space Command (2021), and China's PLA Aerospace Force (2024), while India created a tri-service Defence Space Agency in 2019.
