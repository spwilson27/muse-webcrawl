Las Vegas

Las Vegas (Spanish for "The Meadows"), often known simply as Vegas, is the most populous city in the U.S. state of Nevada and the seat of Clark County. The city anchors the Las Vegas Valley metropolitan area, the largest metropolitan area in the Mojave Desert and the second-largest in the Southwestern United States. Las Vegas is an internationally renowned resort city known primarily for its gambling, shopping, fine dining, entertainment and nightlife. It bills itself as the "Entertainment Capital of the World" and is famous for its luxurious casino-hotels and associated activities, most of them concentrated along the Las Vegas Strip in the neighboring communities of Paradise and Winchester.

History

The Las Vegas Valley was first reported by Europeans in 1829, when Rafael Rivera, a scout for a Mexican trading party led by Antonio Armijo, found artesian springs in the desert and named the area Las Vegas ("the meadows"). In 1855, Mormon missionaries built a fort there; the modern city was founded in 1905 when the San Pedro, Los Angeles and Salt Lake Railroad auctioned off town lots. Nevada's legalization of gambling in 1931, combined with construction of Hoover Dam on the nearby Colorado River, spurred growth. The postwar decades brought large casino resorts — the Flamingo (1946), and later themed megaresorts such as the Mirage (1989) and Bellagio (1998) — along with a historically documented period of organized-crime involvement.

Geography and climate

Las Vegas lies in the Mojave Desert within a basin surrounded by mountain ranges. It has a hot desert climate with long, very hot summers, mild winters, abundant sunshine and very little rain — roughly 106 mm (4.2 inches) annually. Summer high temperatures regularly exceed 40 °C (104 °F), while winter nights can approach freezing.

Economy

Tourism, gaming and conventions drive the economy. The greater Las Vegas area receives around 40 million visitors in a typical year, and the city is a top-three destination in the United States for business conventions. Major employers include the casino resorts of the Strip and Downtown, the healthcare sector, and warehousing and logistics. Nellis Air Force Base lies just northeast of the city.

Culture and entertainment

Beyond gambling, Las Vegas is known for resident headliner shows, Cirque du Soleil productions, championship boxing and mixed martial arts, and quick wedding chapels. Professional sports arrived with the NHL's Vegas Golden Knights (2017) and the NFL's Las Vegas Raiders (2020). The University of Nevada, Las Vegas (UNLV) is the city's principal university.

Government

Las Vegas operates under a council–manager form of government. Unusually, most of the famous "Las Vegas" Strip actually lies outside city limits, in the unincorporated townships of Paradise and Winchester, governed by Clark County.
