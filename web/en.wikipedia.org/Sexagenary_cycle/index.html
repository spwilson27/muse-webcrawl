Sexagenary cycle

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The sexagenary cycle, also known as the Stems-and-Branches or ganzhi (Chinese: 干支; pinyin: gānzhī), is a cycle of sixty terms used for recording days and years in East Asian calendars. It appears as the earliest known Chinese calendar system, inscribed on oracle bones of the Shang dynasty in the second millennium BC, and remains embedded in the traditional calendars of China, Japan, Korea, and Vietnam.

Structure

Each term in the cycle combines one of the ten Heavenly Stems (天干) with one of the twelve Earthly Branches (地支). Because only even-indexed combinations occur, the two sequences — least common multiple 60 — yield exactly sixty stem-branch pairs before repeating.

Heavenly Stems甲 jiǎ, 乙 yǐ, 丙 bǐng, 丁 dīng, 戊 wù, 己 jǐ, 庚 gēng, 辛 xīn, 壬 rén, 癸 guǐ
Earthly Branches子 zǐ, 丑 chǒu, 寅 yín, 卯 mǎo, 辰 chén, 巳 sì, 午 wǔ, 未 wèi, 申 shēn, 酉 yǒu, 戌 xū, 亥 hài

The first term, jiǎzǐ (甲子), pairs the first stem with the first branch; the cycle proceeds by advancing both until it closes at guǐhài (癸亥), the sixtieth term.

Use for days and years

The cycle was applied to days long before years: oracle-bone inscriptions record dates by their ganzhi day within an unbroken cycle that Chinese astronomers maintained for millennia. From the Han dynasty onward it was also used to number years. A person's 60th birthday (huàjiǎ in Chinese, kanreki in Japanese, hwangap in Korean) completes one full cycle and is traditionally celebrated as a major milestone.

Correspondence with the zodiac

The twelve Earthly Branches correspond to the twelve animals of the Chinese zodiac (Rat, Ox, Tiger, Rabbit, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog, Pig), while the stems carry associations with the five elements (wuxing): wood, fire, earth, metal, and water, each in yin and yang forms. A year is therefore described by an animal and an element — for example, a Wood Dragon year — a combination that repeats only every sixty years.
