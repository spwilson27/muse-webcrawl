The premotor cortex is an area of the motor cortex lying within the frontal lobe of the brain, just anterior to the primary motor cortex. It occupies most of Brodmann area 6. The premotor cortex receives input from the posterior parietal cortex and the prefrontal cortex and sends projections to the primary motor cortex, the brainstem and the spinal cord. It is involved in the planning and organization of movements, using sensory information to guide action.

Divisions

The premotor cortex is divided into a lateral premotor cortex on the convexity of the hemisphere — itself split into dorsal (PMd) and ventral (PMv) parts — and a medial premotor cortex, comprising the supplementary motor area (SMA) and pre-SMA on the medial surface. The dorsal lateral area is involved in reaching movements guided by visual cues; the ventral area, including area F5 in monkeys, is involved in grasping and contains mirror neurons that fire both when an action is performed and when it is observed.

Functions

Premotor areas transform sensory information into motor commands: selecting movements based on external cues (lateral premotor), internally generating and sequencing movements (supplementary motor area), preparing movements before execution, and coordinating posture and bilateral movements. Premotor neurons are especially active during the planning delay before a cued movement. In humans, the ventral premotor cortex lies close to Broca's area and is implicated in aspects of speech and language motor control.

Connections and clinical aspects

Premotor cortex connects densely with the primary motor cortex, parietal cortex, basal ganglia (via the motor loop) and cerebellum. Damage produces deficits in conditional motor learning and movement selection rather than outright paralysis, distinguishing it from primary motor lesions.
