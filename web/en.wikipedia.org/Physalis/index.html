Physalis

Physalis is a genus of approximately 75–90 species of flowering plants in the nightshade family (Solanaceae), native to the Americas and cultivated in warm regions worldwide. Members of the genus are known as groundcherries, husk tomatoes, poha berries and Inca berries.

Description

The characteristic feature of Physalis is the large, papery husk (an inflated calyx) that partly or fully encloses the fruit, protecting it from pests and the elements. The fruit itself is a small berry, often orange or yellow, with a sweet-tart flavour.

Cultivated species

Physalis peruviana (cape gooseberry or golden berry), P. philadelphica and P. ixocarpa (tomatillos, essential to Mexican salsa verde), and P. pruinosa and P. pubescens (groundcherries) are grown commercially for food. The winter cherry, P. alkekengi, with bright orange-red lanterns, is a popular ornamental.

Toxicity

Unripe fruit, leaves and other parts of many species contain solanine and other alkaloids and should not be eaten; ripe fruit of cultivated species is safe and rich in vitamin C.
