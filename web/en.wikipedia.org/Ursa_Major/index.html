Ursa Major

Ursa Major and the Big Dipper asterism.

Ursa Major (Latin for "Greater Bear") is a constellation in the northern sky, whose associated mythology likely dates to prehistory. It is one of the 48 constellations listed by the 2nd-century astronomer Ptolemy and remains one of the 88 modern constellations. Its brightest stars form the well-known Big Dipper asterism (called the Plough in the United Kingdom and Ireland), a shape that has served as a navigational aid and cultural symbol around the world.

Notable features

The Dipper's seven bright stars include Dubhe, Merak, Phecda, Megrez, Alioth, Mizar and Alkaid. Mizar forms a famous double star with Alcor. The two stars at the front of the "bowl," Dubhe and Merak, are the Pointers, used to locate Polaris. Ursa Major contains several Messier objects, including the Pinwheel Galaxy (M101), Bode's Galaxy (M81), the Cigar Galaxy (M82), and the Owl Nebula (M97). Most of its bright stars belong to the Ursa Major moving group, a loose stellar association sharing a common motion through space.

Mythology

In Greek myth the constellation represents Callisto, a nymph transformed into a bear and placed in the sky by Zeus. Bear associations also appear in Native American, Siberian, and other northern traditions, reflecting the constellation's circumpolar prominence at northern latitudes.
