Kinsey Reports

The Kinsey Reports are two scholarly books by Alfred Kinsey and colleagues: Sexual Behavior in the Human Male (1948) and Sexual Behavior in the Human Female (1953). Based on thousands of interviews, they were among the first large-scale scientific studies of human sexuality.

Findings and method

The reports described a far wider range of sexual behaviour than publicly acknowledged, and introduced the Kinsey scale, rating sexual orientation from 0 (exclusively heterosexual) to 6 (exclusively homosexual). Data came from detailed face-to-face interviews with about 18,000 people.

Impact and criticism

The best-selling volumes provoked public debate and helped launch the sexual revolution, while statisticians criticised the non-random volunteer samples. Kinsey's institute at Indiana University continues sex research.
