George Szell (born György Széll; June 7, 1897 – July 30, 1970) was a Hungarian-born American conductor and composer. Widely considered one of the twentieth century's greatest conductors, he is remembered for his long tenure as music director of the Cleveland Orchestra, which under his leadership became one of the world's finest ensembles, renowned for precision, clarity, and disciplined ensemble playing.

Early career

A child prodigy at the piano and as a composer, Szell made his debut with the Vienna Philharmonic at sixteen. He held posts at the Berlin State Opera, where he was a protégé of Richard Strauss, and in Strasbourg, Prague, Darmstadt, and Düsseldorf, and conducted the Scottish National Orchestra in the 1930s. He moved to the United States at the outbreak of the Second World War and became an American citizen in 1946.

Cleveland

Appointed music director in Cleveland in 1946, Szell rebuilt the orchestra over 24 seasons into an instrument of chamber-music transparency, extensively touring and recording for Epic and Columbia. His notoriously exacting, sometimes tyrannical rehearsal manner produced legendary recordings of Haydn, Mozart, Beethoven, Brahms, Schumann, Dvořák, and Wagner excerpts that remain reference versions. He also served as artistic advisor to Amsterdam's Concertgebouw Orchestra. Szell died in Cleveland in 1970; his successor in Cleveland was Lorin Maazel.
