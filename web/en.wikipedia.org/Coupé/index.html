Coupé

Body style: Two-door passenger car
Layout: Front-engine, rear- or front-wheel drive
Related: Convertible, fastback, liftback

A coupé or coupe is a passenger car with a sloping or truncated rear roofline, typically two doors, and seating for two or four passengers. The term derives from the French couper, "to cut", and originally described a shortened horse-drawn carriage with a single enclosed compartment.

Definitions

Historically, a coupé had a fixed roof, two doors, and a smaller interior volume than the equivalent sedan; the Society of Automotive Engineers once specified a rear-seat volume below 33 cubic feet for the designation. In recent decades, marketing usage has loosened considerably, with manufacturers applying "coupe" — and even "four-door coupe" — to vehicles with sleek rooflines and frameless doors regardless of door count.

History

Early automobile coupés of the 1910s and 1920s seated the driver in the open while passengers sat enclosed, before fully closed bodies became standard. The style flourished in the post-war decades in personal luxury and sports cars, from the Ford Mustang and Chevrolet Camaro to European grand tourers. As buyers shifted toward SUVs and crossovers in the 21st century, traditional two-door coupes declined sharply in production.

Variants

Related forms include the hardtop coupé (without a B-pillar), the club coupé with a small rear seat, the business coupé with a cargo area instead of rear seating, and the modern "coupé SUV", which applies a sloping roof to a crossover body.
