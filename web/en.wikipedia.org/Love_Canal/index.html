Love Canal

Love Canal is a neighbourhood in Niagara Falls, New York, that became the site of one of the most notorious environmental disasters in the United States. The area took its name from an unfinished canal excavated in the 1890s by William T. Love.

Disaster

Hooker Chemical Company used the canal trench as a chemical waste dump in the 1940s and early 1950s, burying some 21,000 tons of toxic waste, then sold the land to the local school board for one dollar. Homes and a school were built over it. By the late 1970s residents, led by activist Lois Gibbs, documented high rates of illness and birth defects; leaking chemicals surfaced in yards and basements.

Aftermath

President Jimmy Carter declared federal emergencies in 1978 and 1980, and hundreds of families were evacuated. The scandal was a driving force behind the 1980 Superfund law (CERCLA) for cleaning up hazardous waste sites.
