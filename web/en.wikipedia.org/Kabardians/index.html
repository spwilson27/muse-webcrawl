The Kabardians (Kabardian: Къэбэрдей) are a Circassian people of the North Caucasus, historically inhabiting Kabardia in the central northern Caucasus, in what is now the Kabardino-Balkaria republic of Russia. They are the largest Circassian tribal group in the Caucasus and speak Kabardian, an East Circassian language of the Northwest Caucasian family.

History

Kabardia emerged as a powerful principality between the fifteenth and eighteenth centuries, famed for its mounted aristocracy and its influence over neighbouring peoples; Kabardian princes intermarried with the Russian royal house, most famously Maria Temryukovna, second wife of Ivan the Terrible. Kabardia gradually came under Russian control during the Russo-Circassian War, and after the mass expulsion of Circassians from the Caucasus in 1864, large Kabardian communities formed in the Ottoman Empire, in present-day Turkey, Jordan, and Syria. Those who remained were organised first into an autonomous oblast and then, with the Turkic Balkars, into the Kabardino-Balkar ASSR, today a federal republic of Russia whose capital is Nalchik.

Culture

Kabardian society was traditionally stratified, headed by princes (pshi) and nobles, and governed by the customary code of Adyghe Xabze. The Kabardian horse, a hardy breed developed by Circassian breeders, is internationally known. Kabardians are predominantly Sunni Muslims, Islam having spread through the region between the sixteenth and nineteenth centuries, overlaying older Christian and indigenous traditions.
