Veld

Veld (also spelled veldt), from Afrikaans veld meaning "field", is a type of wide open rural landscape in Southern Africa. Particularly, it is a flat area covered in grass or low scrub, especially in the countries of South Africa, Lesotho, Eswatini, Zimbabwe and Botswana. A certain sub-tropical woodland ecoregion of Southern Africa has been officially defined as the Bushveld.

Types

The plateau interior of South Africa is divided by altitude into the Highveld, the higher, cooler grassland around 1,500–2,100 m elevation, and the lower, hotter Lowveld below about 900 m, which borders Mozambique and Zimbabwe and includes the Kruger National Park. Veld is also classified by its grasses as "sweet veld", which stays palatable year-round, "sour veld", which becomes coarse and unpalatable in the dry season, and "mixed veld".

Ecology and land use

Veld grasslands are maintained by a combination of seasonal rainfall, frost, grazing and periodic burning, both natural and managed. They support commercial livestock ranching, maize farming (on the Highveld) and characteristic wildlife including springbok, blesbok and numerous grassland birds.
