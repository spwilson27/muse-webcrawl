Media franchise

A media franchise, also simply franchise, refers to a collection of related media in which several derivative works have been produced from an original creative work, such as a film, a work of literature, a television program or a video game. The intellectual property may be licensed to other parties or studios, and derivative works produced across media — a pattern sometimes called transmedia storytelling. The term also carries connotations of commercialisation: a franchise is designed to generate revenue over time through films, television series, books, comics, games, toys and other merchandise.

Characteristics

Successful franchises include Star Wars, the Marvel Cinematic Universe, Pokémon, Harry Potter, Star Trek and James Bond. Pokémon is the highest-grossing media franchise of all time, with revenue exceeding US$100 billion. Works within a franchise share a fictional universe and recognizable characters, settings, or brand elements, and are often released in instalments such as sequels, prequels, spin-offs and reboots.

History

Franchising in media grew from early 20th-century serial fiction and Hollywood sequel production, and expanded dramatically with the merchandising success of Star Wars (1977), which demonstrated that licensing revenue could rival box office returns. Modern entertainment conglomerates structure much of their production around franchise management.
