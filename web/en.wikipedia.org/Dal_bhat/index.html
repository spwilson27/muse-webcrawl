Dal bhat (Nepali: दाल भात) is a traditional meal from the Indian subcontinent consisting of steamed rice (bhat) and a cooked lentil or pigeon pea soup (dal). It is the staple food of Nepal, where it is eaten twice daily by many households, and is common in India, Bangladesh, and Pakistan. The dal is typically made from lentils, split pigeon peas, or other pulses, simmered with turmeric, salt, and tempered spices (tadka) such as cumin, mustard seed, garlic, and dried chillies fried in ghee or oil.

In Nepal

In Nepal, dal bhat is served as a set meal, dal bhat tarkari, on a round plate or thali: a mound of rice, a bowl of dal, one or more vegetable curries (cauliflower, potato, seasonal greens known as saag), a spicy pickle (achar) — often fermented radish or tomato — and sometimes papad, yoghurt, or a meat curry of chicken, goat, or buffalo. In restaurants and trekking lodges, servings are effectively unlimited, with staff circulating to top up each component; the trekking industry joke that energy from the meal lasts "24 hours" is reflected in the T-shirt slogan "Dal Bhat Power — 24 Hour".

Nutrition and customs

Rice and lentils together provide complementary amino acids, making dal bhat a reasonably complete protein source for vegetarian diets. The meal is traditionally eaten by hand, mixing the dal into the rice. Regional variants include the Newari and Thakali sets, the latter renowned for buckwheat, black dal, and distinctive pickles served in the Mustang and Thak Khola region.
