Linear algebra is the branch of mathematics concerning linear equations, linear maps and their representation in vector spaces by means of matrices. Its central objects are vectors, vector spaces (also called linear spaces), linear transformations and systems of linear equations. Linear algebra is fundamental to almost all areas of mathematics and to applications in physics, engineering, computer science, economics and statistics.

Basic concepts

A vector space is a collection of objects called vectors, which may be added together and multiplied by scalars, subject to axioms generalising the behaviour of geometric arrows and of n-tuples of numbers. A linear map is a function between vector spaces that preserves addition and scalar multiplication. Every linear map between finite-dimensional spaces can be represented by a matrix once bases are chosen, and solving a system of linear equations Ax = b is the subject's prototypical problem, handled systematically by Gaussian elimination.

Key topics

  • Matrices: determinants, rank, inverse matrices, and matrix decompositions such as LU and QR factorisation.
  • Eigenvalues and eigenvectors: vectors unchanged in direction by a linear map, central to stability analysis, quantum mechanics and principal component analysis.
  • Inner product spaces: vector spaces with a notion of length and angle, leading to orthogonality and the Gram–Schmidt process.
  • Linear systems: existence and uniqueness of solutions, described by the rank–nullity theorem.

History

Systems of linear equations were solved in ancient China (the Nine Chapters) and by early modern European algebraists using determinants (Leibniz, Cramer, Gauss). The abstract theory emerged in the 19th century with Grassmann's vector spaces, Cayley's matrix algebra and Peano's axiomatic definition of a vector space (1888), and was consolidated in the 20th century within functional analysis.

Applications

Linear algebra underlies computer graphics, machine learning, optimisation, quantum mechanics, structural engineering, cryptography and the analysis of networks and data.
