HDR10, also known as HDR10 Media Profile, is an open standard for high dynamic range (HDR) video announced on August 27, 2015, by the Consumer Technology Association. It is the most widespread HDR format, supported by Ultra HD Blu-ray (for which it is mandatory), major streaming services, game consoles, and virtually all HDR television sets.

Technical details

HDR10 content is mastered with 10-bit colour depth, the BT.2020 colour space (typically contained within the DCI-P3 gamut), and the perceptual quantizer (PQ) electro-optical transfer function defined in SMPTE ST 2084, with peak brightness of up to 10,000 nits in the signal (in practice usually mastered at 1,000–4,000 nits). Unlike its successor HDR10+, HDR10 uses static metadata: a single set of values (mastering display colour volume, maximum content light level MaxCLL, and maximum frame-average light level MaxFALL) describes the whole programme and does not change scene by scene.

Adoption

Because HDR10 is royalty-free and mandated by the Ultra HD Blu-ray specification, it became the baseline HDR format, competing with the licensed Dolby Vision (which adds dynamic metadata and up to 12-bit support) and the broadcast-oriented Hybrid Log-Gamma (HLG). In 2017 Samsung and partners announced HDR10+, an enhanced royalty-free version adding per-scene dynamic metadata.
