Largest known prime number

Current record: 2136,279,841 − 1 (discovered October 2024)
Digits: 41,024,320
Form: Mersenne prime, found by GIMPS

The largest known prime number is 2136,279,841 − 1, a number with 41,024,320 decimal digits, discovered in October 2024 by Luke Durant, a volunteer in the Great Internet Mersenne Prime Search (GIMPS), using GPU computing in the cloud. It is the 52nd known Mersenne prime.

Why Mersenne primes?

Most record primes are Mersenne primes — primes of the form 2p − 1 — because the Lucas–Lehmer primality test makes them comparatively easy to verify for enormous exponents. Since the 1990s every new record has been found by GIMPS, a distributed computing project founded by George Woltman in 1996, which coordinates tens of thousands of volunteers' computers. The first GPU-discovered record, the 2024 prime superseded 282,589,933 − 1 (2018), which held the record for nearly six years.

History

Euclid proved around 300 BC that there are infinitely many primes, so any "largest known prime" is merely the largest discovered so far. Before computers, records were set by hand — Édouard Lucas verified 2127 − 1 in 1876 — and early electronic computers multiplied digit counts rapidly from 1952 onward. The Electronic Frontier Foundation offered prizes for primes of ten million digits and more, claimed by GIMPS discoveries in 2008 and 2024 respectively.
