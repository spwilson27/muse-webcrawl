The Oyster card is a payment method on the Transport for London (TfL) transport network in London, England. It is a contactless smart card that can hold pay-as-you-go credit, Travelcards and other period tickets, and Bus & Tram Passes. Passengers touch the card on electronic readers when entering and, on most rail services, leaving the transport system in order to pay for their journey or validate a period ticket.

The card was first issued to the public in June 2003. It was promoted as a faster and cheaper alternative to paper tickets: fares charged to Oyster pay-as-you-go are generally lower than cash single fares, and daily and weekly "capping" limits the total charged for travel within a day or week to no more than the price of the equivalent Travelcard.

History

The Oyster card was developed under a private finance initiative contract awarded in 1998 to TranSys, a consortium whose members included Cubic Transportation Systems, which supplied the underlying technology. The name "Oyster" was chosen after a lengthy selection process, evoking the oyster shell protecting a pearl — a metaphor for the card's security — as well as London's long association with oysters fished from the River Thames and the phrase "the world is your oyster".

Initially the card used the MIFARE Classic contactless technology. Following research published in 2008 demonstrating that the MIFARE Classic chip's security could be broken, TfL migrated Oyster cards to the more secure MIFARE DESFire technology. In September 2014, TfL began accepting ordinary contactless bank cards (and later mobile payment devices) for pay-as-you-go travel, using the same reader infrastructure; the Oyster system was subsequently upgraded so that cards could be managed through online accounts and topped up via the TfL app.

Use

Oyster cards can be obtained from stations, Oyster Ticket Stops in shops, and online, generally for a small fee or deposit. Users "touch in" and "touch out" on yellow circular readers on ticket gates and at standalone validators. On buses and trams only a single touch-in is required, since flat fares apply. Failure to touch out at the end of a rail journey results in a maximum fare being charged.

The system calculates fares automatically, applying daily caps by zone and weekly (Monday to Sunday) caps. Reduced-rate photocards, including the Zip Oyster cards for young people, provide discounted or free travel for eligible users.
