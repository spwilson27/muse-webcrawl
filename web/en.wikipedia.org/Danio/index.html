Danio

The zebrafish, Danio rerio

Danio is a genus of small freshwater fish in the family Cyprinidae, found in rivers and streams of South and Southeast Asia. Many species are brightly marked and are popular aquarium fish. The best-known species, the zebrafish (Danio rerio), is one of the most important model organisms in genetics, developmental biology and biomedical research.

Characteristics

Danios are active schooling fish, typically 4–12 cm long, with upturned mouths suited to feeding at the surface and one or two pairs of small barbels. Many species have striking patterns of horizontal stripes or spots. They inhabit clear, well-oxygenated streams and rice paddies, feeding on insects, crustaceans and other small invertebrates.

Taxonomy

The genus was established by Francis Buchanan-Hamilton in 1822. More than two dozen species are currently recognised, including the giant danio (D. aequipinnatus), pearl danio (D. albolineatus), leopard danio and the celestial pearl danio, the latter now usually placed in Danio after earlier assignment to Celestichthys. Molecular studies have repeatedly reshaped the genus and its relationship to Devario and other danionins.

In the aquarium

Hardy, peaceful and active, danios have been kept since the early twentieth century and are often recommended as beginner fish, though they require swimming space and a group of their own kind. Colour variants of the zebrafish, including the fluorescent GloFish lines, are commercially produced.
