Saraswati (Sanskrit: सरस्वती, Sarasvatī) is a Hindu goddess, one of the principal goddesses of Hinduism and a member of the Tridevi along with Lakshmi and Parvati. She is the goddess of knowledge, education, learning, music and the arts, and is revered by Hindus, Jains and some Buddhists.

Origins

Saraswati first appears in the Rigveda as the personification of the Sarasvati River, a great and sacred river praised in many hymns. Over time, as the river's significance merged with the spoken word (vāc), she evolved into the goddess of speech, learning and wisdom. In later texts she is the consort of the creator god Brahma.

Iconography

Saraswati is usually depicted dressed in a plain white sari, seated on a white lotus, symbolising purity and true knowledge. She has four arms holding a book (the Vedas), a rosary (mala), a water pot, and the veena, a large stringed instrument. Her vahana (mount) is the swan (hamsa), which in tradition can separate milk from water, symbolising discernment; a peacock is also associated with her.

Worship

The festival of Vasant Panchami, marking the arrival of spring, is dedicated to Saraswati, especially in educational institutions, where books and instruments are blessed. Students seek her blessings for success in examinations. She is also worshipped during Navaratri. Saraswati temples include the Sringeri Sharada Peetham in Karnataka and the Gnana Saraswati Temple at Basar in Telangana.

Beyond India

Saraswati is venerated in Jainism and in Mahayana Buddhism, and under the name Benzaiten she is one of the Seven Lucky Gods of Japan, where she is associated with music and water.
