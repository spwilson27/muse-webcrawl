Consumer Financial Protection Bureau

CFPB
Formed: 21 July 2011
Created by: Dodd–Frank Act (2010)
Headquarters: Washington, D.C.
First director: Richard Cordray

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) is an agency of the United States government responsible for consumer protection in the financial sector. Created by the Dodd–Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act of 2010 in response to the 2008 financial crisis, it began operating on 21 July 2011.

Mandate

The CFPB regulates consumer financial products and services — including mortgages, credit cards, student loans, payday lending, and debt collection — writes rules, supervises large banks and non-bank financial companies, enforces federal consumer financial law, and operates a public complaint database. Its creation consolidated consumer-protection authorities that had been scattered among several regulators.

History and controversies

The bureau was proposed by then-professor Elizabeth Warren. Its first director, Richard Cordray, was confirmed in 2013 after a recess appointment. The agency's structure — a single director removable only for cause and funding drawn from the Federal Reserve rather than congressional appropriations — was challenged in court; in Seila Law v. CFPB (2020) the Supreme Court held that the president may remove the director at will, while in CFPB v. Community Financial Services Association (2024) the Court upheld its funding mechanism.

Activities

The CFPB reports having returned billions of dollars to consumers through enforcement actions and has issued major rules on mortgage servicing, ability-to-repay standards, prepaid cards, and small-dollar lending, though some rules have been revised or rescinded as administrations have changed.
