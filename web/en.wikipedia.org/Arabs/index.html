Arabs

The Arabs are an ethnic group native to West Asia and North Africa, united primarily by the Arabic language and a shared cultural heritage. Numbering roughly 450 million people in the Arab world, they form the majority populations of the 22 member states of the Arab League, with a large global diaspora, particularly in the Americas and Europe.

History

Arabian peoples are attested in ancient sources from the first millennium BCE, including kingdoms such as Nabataea and Palmyra on the fringes of the Roman and Persian empires. In the 7th century CE, following the rise of Islam, Arab armies expanded from the Arabian Peninsula across the Middle East, North Africa and into Iberia, spreading the Arabic language and Islamic culture. Over the following centuries the medieval Islamic world, centred at different times on Damascus, Baghdad and Cairo, became a major centre of science, philosophy and trade.

Identity and language

Arab identity is generally defined by language and culture rather than ancestry alone: an Arab is commonly described as one whose native language is Arabic and who identifies with Arab history and culture. Most Arabs are Muslims (Sunni or Shia), with significant Christian minorities and smaller communities of other faiths. Modern Standard Arabic serves as a shared literary language, while everyday speech takes the form of regional dialects.
