Trident is a series of submarine-launched ballistic missiles (SLBMs) developed by Lockheed Martin and deployed by the United States Navy and the Royal Navy. The first version, Trident I (C4), entered service in 1979; the larger and far more accurate Trident II (D5) followed in 1990 and remains the sole nuclear deterrent system carried by Britain's Vanguard-class and America's Ohio-class ballistic missile submarines.

Design

The three-stage, solid-fuel Trident II D5 is about 13.6 metres (44 ft) long, has a range of roughly 7,360–11,300 km depending on load, and uses a stellar-inertial guidance system giving an accuracy (circular error probable) of about 90 metres. It can carry multiple independently targetable re-entry vehicles (MIRVs) armed with W76 or W88 warheads in the United States, and the British Holbrook warhead. A retractable aerospike on the nose cone reduces drag.

Service

Trident II missiles are based at Naval Submarine Base Kings Bay, Georgia, and Bangor, Washington, and the UK fleet is based at HMNB Clyde (Faslane) with warheads stored at Coulport. A life-extension programme (D5LE) modernises the missiles to serve aboard the Columbia-class and Dreadnought-class submarines into the 2080s. The D5 has set records for consecutive successful test flights, exceeding 190 by the mid-2020s. Under the 1958 US–UK Mutual Defence Agreement and the Polaris Sales Agreement, Britain buys missiles from a shared pool drawn from the U.S. stockpile.
