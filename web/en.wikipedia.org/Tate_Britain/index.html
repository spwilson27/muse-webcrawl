Tate Britain, known from 1897 to 1932 as the National Gallery of British Art and from 1932 to 2020 as the Tate Gallery, is an art museum on Millbank in the City of Westminster, London. It is the founding gallery of the Tate network and houses the world's largest collection of British art.

History

The gallery was founded by Sir Henry Tate, a sugar refiner who funded the building and donated his collection of Victorian art. Designed by Sidney R. J. Smith, it opened in 1897 on the site of the demolished Millbank Prison. The building has been extended repeatedly, including the Clore Gallery (1987), funded by the Clore Foundation to house the Turner Bequest. When Tate Modern opened in 2000, the Millbank gallery was relaunched as Tate Britain, dedicated to British art from 1500 to the present.

Collection and exhibitions

The chronological displays include major works by William Hogarth, Thomas Gainsborough, Joshua Reynolds, John Constable, the Pre-Raphaelites, and J. M. W. Turner, whose bequest of paintings and watercolours forms the core of the Turner collection. Tate Britain also hosts the annual Turner Prize exhibition (in alternate years shared with other venues) and major temporary exhibitions of British artists.
