Lawrence v. Texas

Lawrence v. Texas, 539 U.S. 558 (2003), is a landmark decision of the US Supreme Court holding that a Texas law criminalising consensual same-sex sexual activity between adults violated the Due Process Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment.

Background and holding

John Lawrence and Tyron Garner were arrested in Lawrence's Houston apartment in 1998 and convicted under Texas's "Homosexual Conduct" law. On 26 June 2003 the Court ruled 6–3, in an opinion by Justice Kennedy, that intimate consensual conduct is part of the liberty protected by substantive due process, expressly overruling Bowers v. Hardwick (1986). Justice O'Connor concurred on equal-protection grounds.

Impact

The decision invalidated sodomy laws in the remaining states that had them and became a foundational precedent for later rights cases, cited in Obergefell v. Hodges (2015).
