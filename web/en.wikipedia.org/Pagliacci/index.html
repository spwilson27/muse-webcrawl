Pagliacci (Italian for "Clowns") is an Italian opera in a prologue and two acts, with music and libretto by Ruggero Leoncavallo. It is the composer's only opera that is still widely staged. It premiered at the Teatro Dal Verme in Milan on 21 May 1892, conducted by Arturo Toscanini, with Adelina Stehle as Nedda, Fiorello Giraud as Canio, Victor Maurel as Tonio, and Mario Ancona as Silvio.

Synopsis

The opera tells the story of Canio, the leader of a travelling troupe of commedia dell'arte players, who discovers that his wife Nedda is having an affair with Silvio. During an on-stage performance, Canio's real-life jealousy overtakes the play: in the famous aria "Vesti la giubba" he resolves to perform despite his heartbreak, and in the final scene he murders Nedda and then Silvio on stage before the horrified audience, announcing "La commedia è finita!" ("The comedy is finished!").

Performance history

Pagliacci is frequently performed in a double bill with Pietro Mascagni's Cavalleria rusticana, a pairing known as "Cav and Pag". Both works are leading examples of the verismo style of opera. Leoncavallo claimed the story was based on a real murder case tried by his father, a magistrate.
