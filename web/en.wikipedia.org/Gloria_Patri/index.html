Gloria Patri

Gloria Patri
TypeDoxology (hymn of praise)
Also known asLesser Doxology; "Glory Be"
LanguageLatin (from Greek original)
SubjectPraise of the Holy Trinity

The Gloria Patri, also known as the Lesser Doxology or the "Glory Be", is a short hymn of praise to the Christian Trinity: God the Father, God the Son and God the Holy Spirit. Its Latin text reads:

Gloria Patri, et Filio, et Spiritui Sancto. Sicut erat in principio, et nunc, et semper, et in saecula saeculorum. Amen.

("Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit. As it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be, world without end. Amen.")

Use

The doxology is appended to the psalms and canticles of the Liturgy of the Hours and historically concluded each psalm in the Roman Breviary. It is recited between the decades of the rosary, sung in Anglican Morning and Evening Prayer after the canticles, and used widely in Lutheran and other Protestant worship. The second half, "sicut erat in principio" ("as it was in the beginning"), is sometimes taken as an affirmation against Arian teaching that the Son had a beginning.

Distinction

The Gloria Patri is the lesser doxology, distinguished from the greater doxology, the Gloria in excelsis Deo, sung at Mass and on feast days.
