Gordon Moore

Gordon Earle Moore (January 3, 1929 – March 24, 2023) was an American businessman, engineer, and the co-founder and emeritus chairman of Intel Corporation. He proposed Moore's law, the observation that the number of transistors on an integrated circuit doubles approximately every two years.

Early life and career

Moore was born in San Francisco and grew up in Pescadero, California. He earned a bachelor's degree in chemistry from the University of California, Berkeley, in 1950 and a PhD in chemistry and physics from Caltech in 1954. In 1957 he was one of the "traitorous eight" who left Shockley Semiconductor Laboratory to found Fairchild Semiconductor, where he became director of research and development.

Intel and Moore's law

In 1968 Moore and Robert Noyce founded Intel, with venture capitalist Arthur Rock; Moore served as executive vice president, president (1975), chief executive officer (1979), and chairman. His 1965 article in Electronics magazine, written while at Fairchild, predicted that the number of components per integrated circuit would double every year — revised in 1975 to roughly every two years. The prediction, later dubbed Moore's law, became a guiding target for the semiconductor industry for over half a century.

Philanthropy

With his wife Betty, Moore established the Gordon and Betty Moore Foundation in 2000, with an endowment of several billion dollars supporting environmental conservation, science, patient care, and the San Francisco Bay Area. He also funded the Gordon and Betty Moore Foundation's support for the Thirty Meter Telescope and donated to Caltech, where he was a trustee. Moore died at home in Hawaii on March 24, 2023, aged 94.
