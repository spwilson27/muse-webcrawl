HP LaserJet

LaserJet is a line of laser printers sold by Hewlett-Packard (now HP Inc.) since 1984. The original LaserJet, introduced in May 1984 at a price of US$3,495, was the first desktop laser printer and helped launch the desktop publishing revolution alongside the Apple LaserWriter and page-description languages such as HP's PCL and Adobe PostScript.

History

The first LaserJet used a Canon CX print engine, printed at 300 dots per inch and eight pages per minute, and was aimed at users of the IBM PC. The LaserJet Plus (1985) added downloadable fonts; the LaserJet II (1987) became a benchmark office printer; the LaserJet III (1990) introduced Resolution Enhancement technology (REt) and PCL 5. The affordable LaserJet IIP brought laser printing to smaller offices and homes. Later milestones included the LaserJet 4 (1992, 600 dpi), colour models (Color LaserJet, 1994), networked enterprise printers, and multifunction (print/scan/copy/fax) devices. HP has sold well over 300 million LaserJet printers.

Technology

LaserJet printers use electrophotographic laser printing: a laser beam draws the page image on a photosensitive drum, which attracts toner that is transferred to paper and fused by heat. HP's Printer Command Language (PCL) became a de facto industry standard, and many models also support PostScript emulation and HP's JetDirect networking. Printers use replaceable toner cartridges, which have drawn criticism and litigation over third-party cartridge blocking via firmware and the "Dynamic Security" feature.

Models

The range spans personal monochrome printers (LaserJet Pro), office workgroup machines, enterprise LaserJet Enterprise/Managed series, and colour models, as well as the later LaserJet Tank and Neverstop models with refillable toner systems.
