Kumari

Kumari, or Kumari Devi, is the tradition of worshipping a prepubescent girl as the living embodiment of the Hindu goddess Taleju (a form of Durga) in Nepal. The most revered is the Royal Kumari of Kathmandu, who resides in the Kumari Ghar at Kathmandu Durbar Square.

Selection

A Kumari is chosen from the Shakya caste of the Newar community through an elaborate process testing her physical perfections (the traditional "32 excellences") and her composure during ritual ordeals. She must have never shed blood and remains goddess until puberty or a significant loss of blood, when she reverts to mortal status.

Life and festivals

The Kumari lives in seclusion, cared for by attendants, and appears publicly mainly during festivals such as Indra Jatra, when her chariot is pulled through Kathmandu and the king (historically) and now the president receives her blessing. Similar Kumari traditions exist in other Newar cities of the Kathmandu Valley.
