Komodo dragon

Scientific classification
Kingdom: Animalia
Family: Varanidae
Genus: Varanus
Species: V. komodoensis
Conservation: Endangered

The Komodo dragon (Varanus komodoensis) is a large species of monitor lizard found on the Indonesian islands of Komodo, Rinca, Flores and Gili Motang. It is the largest living species of lizard, growing up to about 3 metres in length and weighing up to roughly 70 kilograms or more.

Biology

Komodo dragons are apex predators and scavengers, hunting deer, pigs and carrion. They have serrated teeth, a keen sense of smell for carrion over kilometres, and venom glands in the lower jaw whose toxins, together with bacteria-laden bites and blood loss, help subdue prey. Females can on rare occasions reproduce by parthenogenesis.

Conservation

The species is listed as Endangered, threatened by habitat loss, human encroachment and climate-driven sea-level rise affecting its low-lying habitat. Komodo National Park, founded in 1980, protects its core range.
