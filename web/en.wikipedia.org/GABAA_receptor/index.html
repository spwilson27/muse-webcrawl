GABAA receptor

The GABAA receptor is a ligand-gated ion channel that mediates the majority of fast inhibitory neurotransmission in the central nervous system. It responds to gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA), the brain's chief inhibitory neurotransmitter. On binding GABA, the receptor opens an integral chloride channel; chloride influx (in mature neurons) hyperpolarises the cell and suppresses firing.

Structure

The receptor is a pentamer assembled from five subunits drawn from several families (α1–6, β1–3, γ1–3, δ, ε, θ, π, and ρ). The most common synaptic configuration in the brain is two α1, two β2, and one γ2 subunit arranged around the central pore. Subunit composition determines pharmacology: receptors containing particular α subunits mediate different effects of modulatory drugs — for example α1 for sedation and α2/α3 for anxiolysis.

Modulatory sites and drugs

Beyond the GABA binding site itself, the receptor carries allosteric sites exploited by many drug classes: the benzodiazepine site (at the α–γ interface), and sites for barbiturates, neurosteroids, general anaesthetics such as propofol and etomidate, and alcohol. Positive allosteric modulators increase the effect of GABA; antagonists such as bicuculline and the channel blocker picrotoxin reduce inhibition and can provoke seizures. Agents acting here are used as anxiolytics, hypnotics, anticonvulsants, muscle relaxants, and anaesthetics.

GABAA-rho

Receptors assembled from ρ (rho) subunits, formerly called GABAC receptors, are now classified as GABAA-rho. They are prominent in the retina, are insensitive to bicuculline and benzodiazepines, and desensitise slowly. Mutations in GABAA receptor genes underlie several genetic epilepsy syndromes, and altered receptor function is implicated in anxiety, sleep, and mood disorders.
