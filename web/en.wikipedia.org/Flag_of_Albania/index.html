Flag of Albania

The flag of Albania (Albanian: Flamuri i Shqipërisë) is a red flag with a silhouetted black double-headed eagle in the centre. The red stands for bravery, strength, valour, and bloodshed, while the double-headed eagle represents the Albanian state and its sovereignty. The flag has a ratio of 5:7 and its design derives from the banner of the medieval hero Gjergj Kastrioti Skanderbeg, who led the Albanian resistance against the Ottoman Empire in the fifteenth century.

History

The double-headed eagle was a symbol of the Byzantine Empire, used by several noble families of the Balkans; Skanderbeg raised a red banner bearing the black double-headed eagle at Krujë in 1443. The modern flag was raised at Vlorë on 28 November 1912 by Ismail Qemali when Albania declared independence from the Ottoman Empire, a date now celebrated as Flag Day (Dita e Flamurit), a national holiday. Over the twentieth century, the flag's centre changed with the regime: the Kingdom of Albania added Skanderbeg's helmet (1928–1939), Italian occupation added fascist fasces, and the communist People's Republic of Albania placed a red star above the eagle (1946–1992). On 7 April 1992, after the fall of communism, the star was removed, restoring the plain eagle design used today.

Symbolism and use

The eagle is so central to Albanian identity that Albanians are sometimes called "sons of the eagle" (Shqiptarë is popularly linked to shqiponjë, "eagle"), and the country is styled the "Land of the Eagles". A giant flag displayed at Kukës in 2012, sewn for the centenary of independence, was recognised as one of the largest flags ever flown. The flag appears, in adapted form, in the symbols of Kosovo Albanians and in the proposed flags of various Albanian institutions; the national anthem, Himni i Flamurit, is literally the "Hymn to the Flag".
