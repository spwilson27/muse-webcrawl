Six Ministries

The Six Ministries (Chinese: 六部; pinyin: Lùbù) were the principal organs of the central government in imperial China, subordinate to the Department of State Affairs under the Three Departments and Six Ministries system. The system was created under the Sui dynasty and matured under the Tang and Song dynasties, surviving in various forms through the Ming and Qing dynasties until 1911.

Six Ministries

Chinese: 六部
Supervised by: Department of State Affairs
Established: Sui dynasty (6th century)

The six ministries

  • Ministry of Personnel (吏部) — appointments, promotions and evaluations of civil officials.
  • Ministry of Revenue (戶部) — taxation, census registration, land and state finances.
  • Ministry of Rites (禮部) — court ceremonies, sacrifices, religious affairs and the imperial examinations.
  • Ministry of War (兵部) — military administration, officer selection and defence policy.
  • Ministry of Justice (刑部) — judicial administration, prisons and punishments.
  • Ministry of Works (工部) — public works, irrigation, waterways and state construction.

History

The framework was modelled partly on the ancient Rites of Zhou, which described six offices corresponding to heaven, earth and the four seasons. Under the Tang, each ministry was headed by a Minister (shangshu) and supervised bureaus; the Song dynasty layered additional fiscal agencies over them. The Ming founder Zhu Yuanzhang abolished the chancellorship in 1380 and placed the ministries directly under the emperor, a structure the Qing retained.
