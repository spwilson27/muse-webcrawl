Anisocoria

Anisocoria
SpecialtyOphthalmology, neurology
DefinitionUnequal pupil sizes
CausesPhysiological, Horner syndrome, oculomotor nerve palsy, pharmacologic agents, iris damage

Anisocoria is a condition characterised by an unequal size of the eyes' pupils. Affecting up to 20 percent of the population in a mild, benign form, anisocoria can be physiological (normal) or a sign of underlying disease affecting the sympathetic or parasympathetic pathways that control pupil size.

Causes

In physiological anisocoria, the difference between the pupils is small (usually less than 1 mm) and equal in light and darkness, and is harmless. Pathological causes include Horner syndrome, in which a disrupted sympathetic supply causes a smaller pupil (miosis) with a drooping eyelid; oculomotor (third) nerve palsy, in which parasympathetic damage produces a large, poorly reactive pupil; Adie's tonic pupil; pharmacologic dilation or constriction from eye drops or other agents; and direct injury or inflammation of the iris.

Diagnosis

Examination determines whether the abnormal pupil is the smaller or the larger one by comparing the asymmetry in bright light and in darkness. Anisocoria worse in darkness suggests the smaller pupil fails to dilate (a sympathetic problem such as Horner syndrome), whereas asymmetry worse in light suggests the larger pupil fails to constrict (a parasympathetic problem). Pharmacologic tests with agents such as cocaine, apraclonidine, or dilute pilocarpine can help localise the lesion.

Significance

New-onset anisocoria, particularly with pain, ptosis, double vision, or a dilated unreactive pupil, may signal emergencies such as an aneurysm compressing the third cranial nerve and warrants urgent evaluation.
