Mauveine

Also known as: Aniline purple, Perkin's mauve
Discovered: 1856, by William Henry Perkin
Type: Synthetic organic dye

Mauveine, also known as aniline purple and Perkin's mauve, was one of the first synthetic organic dyes. It was discovered serendipitously in 1856 by the 18-year-old chemist William Henry Perkin, who was attempting to synthesise the antimalarial drug quinine.

Discovery

While oxidising aniline extracted from coal tar in his home laboratory, Perkin obtained a black solid whose extract dyed silk a bright purple. He patented the dye, raised capital, and built a factory at Greenford Green near London to manufacture it.

Impact

The vivid mauve colour became fashionable after being worn by Queen Victoria and Empress Eugénie, and its commercial success founded the synthetic dye industry and helped launch the modern chemical industry.
