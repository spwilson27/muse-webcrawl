Myasthenia gravis

Specialty: Neurology
Symptoms: Fluctuating muscle weakness, drooping eyelids, double vision, difficulty swallowing
Causes: Autoimmune attack on acetylcholine receptors
Treatment: Acetylcholinesterase inhibitors, immunosuppressants, thymectomy

Myasthenia gravis (MG) is a long-term autoimmune neuromuscular disease that leads to varying degrees of skeletal muscle weakness. In most patients, antibodies block or destroy nicotinic acetylcholine receptors at the neuromuscular junction, preventing nerve impulses from triggering muscle contraction; in others the antibodies target proteins such as MuSK or LRP4. The hallmark of the disease is weakness that worsens with activity and improves with rest.

Signs and symptoms

Myasthenia most often begins in the eye muscles, causing drooping eyelids (ptosis) and double vision (diplopia). It may remain purely ocular or generalise to the face, speech, swallowing, neck, limbs and breathing. Severe exacerbation — myasthenic crisis — can paralyse the respiratory muscles and require mechanical ventilation. The thymus gland is abnormal in most patients, with hyperplasia common and thymoma present in about 10–15%.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis combines the clinical picture with blood tests for anti-AChR and anti-MuSK antibodies, electrodiagnostic studies such as repetitive nerve stimulation and single-fibre electromyography (the most sensitive test), and imaging of the chest to detect thymoma. Historically the edrophonium (Tensilon) test was used.

Treatment

Symptomatic treatment uses acetylcholinesterase inhibitors such as pyridostigmine. Immunosuppressive therapy — corticosteroids, azathioprine, mycophenolate, and newer agents including complement inhibitors (eculizumab, ravulizumab) and neonatal Fc-receptor blockers (efgartigimod) — modifies the disease course. Removal of the thymus (thymectomy) benefits many patients. Crises are treated with plasma exchange or intravenous immunoglobulin. With modern care, life expectancy is near normal for most patients, though no cure exists.
