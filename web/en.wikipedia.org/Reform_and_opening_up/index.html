Reform and opening up

Reform and opening up (Chinese: 改革开放, Gǎigé kāifàng) is the programme of economic reforms initiated by Deng Xiaoping in the People's Republic of China in December 1978. The reforms introduced market mechanisms into the centrally planned economy, opened China to foreign trade and investment, and laid the foundation for decades of rapid growth that made China the world's second-largest economy.

Course of reform

Reform began in agriculture with the household responsibility system, which decollectivised farming and sharply raised output. In 1980, Special Economic Zones were created at Shenzhen, Zhuhai, Shantou, and Xiamen to attract foreign capital. Township and village enterprises flourished in the 1980s, while price reform proceeded gradually. After a conservative retrenchment following 1989, Deng's 1992 Southern Tour relaunched reform; the 1990s saw restructuring of state-owned enterprises, and in 2001 China joined the World Trade Organization.

Effects

China sustained average GDP growth near 10% for three decades, lifting hundreds of millions out of poverty, though reforms also brought inequality, corruption pressures, and environmental costs. The official framing remains "socialism with Chinese characteristics." See also Deng Xiaoping and economy of China.
