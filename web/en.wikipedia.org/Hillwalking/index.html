Hillwalking

Hillwalking is recreational walking in hill or mountain country, generally understood in Britain and Ireland as walking on hills and lower mountains that does not require the technical skills of mountaineering or rock climbing, though it may involve rough, pathless terrain, considerable ascent and navigation in poor visibility. It is one of the most popular outdoor activities in the United Kingdom, particularly in Scotland, the Lake District, Snowdonia (Eryri) and the Peak District.

Practice

Hillwalkers typically carry waterproof clothing, boots, map and compass, food and water, and navigate using Ordnance Survey or Harvey maps. Many walkers follow lists of summits: in Scotland the Munros (mountains over 3,000 feet) and Corbetts; in England and Wales the Wainwrights of the Lake District and the Hewitts. Completing a full list — "Munro bagging" — is a popular long-term goal.

Access

Access rights underpin British hillwalking. In Scotland, the Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2003 established a statutory right of responsible access to most land. In England and Wales, the Countryside and Rights of Way Act 2000 created a right to walk on mapped open country, supplementing the older network of public rights of way.

Safety

The principal hazards are sudden weather changes, mist and low cloud, steep or boggy ground, and — in winter — snow and ice, which can turn a walk into a mountaineering undertaking requiring ice axe and crampons. Volunteer mountain rescue teams, coordinated in England and Wales by Mountain Rescue England and Wales and in Scotland by Scottish Mountain Rescue, respond to incidents.
