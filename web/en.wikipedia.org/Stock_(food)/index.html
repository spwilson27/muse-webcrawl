Stock (food)

Stock, sometimes called bone broth, is a savoury cooking liquid made by simmering bones, meat, seafood, or vegetables in water, often with aromatics such as onion, carrot, celery, and herbs. Stock forms the base for many dishes, including soups, stews, sauces, gravies, and risottos.

Types

Common types include chicken stock, beef or veal stock (white and brown, the latter made from roasted bones), fish stock (fumet), and vegetable stock. French classical cuisine distinguishes fonds such as fond blanc and fond brun. Japanese dashi, made from kombu and katsuobushi, is a stock-like preparation central to that cuisine.

Preparation

Ingredients are simmered gently for hours; long cooking extracts gelatin from bones and connective tissue, giving good stock body. Stock differs from broth primarily in that broth is typically made with more meat and is seasoned to be eaten on its own, while stock is an ingredient.

Commercial products

Stock is sold as cubes, powders, and ready-made liquids. Concentrated stock reductions such as glace de viande are used in professional kitchens.
