Cain and Abel

In the biblical Book of Genesis, Cain and Abel are the first two sons of Adam and Eve. Cain, the elder, is a farmer who works the soil; Abel is a keeper of sheep. Both bring offerings to God, who regards Abel's offering of the firstborn of his flock but not Cain's offering of the fruit of the ground. Warned by God against sin, Cain nevertheless lures his brother into the field and murders him—the first homicide in the Bible.

The curse and the mark

Confronted by God ("Where is Abel your brother?"), Cain answers, "Am I my brother's keeper?" God curses him from the ground: the soil will no longer yield to him, and he will be a fugitive and wanderer. Fearing vengeance, Cain receives a protective "mark" and departs to the land of Nod, east of Eden, where he builds a city named for his son Enoch. Traditions differ on why Abel's offering was preferred—its quality, the giver's faith (as Hebrews 11 argues), or divine freedom.

Interpretation and legacy

The story is retold in the Quran (as Qabil and Habil) and has been read as an archetype of fratricide, envy and the conflict between farmer and herder. It inspired treatments from medieval mystery plays to Byron's Cain, Steinbeck's East of Eden, and the expressions "raise Cain" and "mark of Cain". Abel is commemorated as a righteous martyr in Jewish and Christian tradition.
