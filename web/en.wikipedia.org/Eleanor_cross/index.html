Eleanor cross

The Eleanor crosses were a series of twelve tall, ornately carved stone monuments erected in England by King Edward I between 1291 and 1295 in memory of his wife, Eleanor of Castile.

After Eleanor died at Harby, Nottinghamshire, in November 1290, her body was carried to Westminster Abbey for burial. Crosses were built at each place where the funeral procession rested overnight: Lincoln, Grantham, Stamford, Geddington, Hardingstone (Northampton), Stony Stratford, Woburn, Dunstable, St Albans, Waltham, Westcheap (Cheapside, London), and Charing.

Survivors

Only three crosses survive substantially intact: those at Geddington, Hardingstone, and Waltham (Waltham Cross). The Charing Cross in London is a Victorian replica of 1865. The crosses influenced later memorial design, and railway stations and districts took names from them, including Charing Cross and Waltham Cross.
