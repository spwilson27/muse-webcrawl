Credit counseling

Type: Financial service
Provides: Budget advice, debt management plans
Regulation: U.S. bankruptcy law requires pre-filing counseling

Credit counseling (known in the United Kingdom as debt counseling) is a service that advises consumers on managing money and debt, helps them develop a budget, and offers education about credit. Many counseling agencies are nonprofit organisations that negotiate with creditors on a client's behalf.

Debt management plans

The best-known product of credit counseling is the debt management plan (DMP): the agency consolidates a client's unsecured debts into a single monthly payment, distributed to creditors, often at reduced interest rates or with waived fees obtained through standing agreements with lenders. Plans typically run three to five years. Unlike debt settlement, a DMP aims to repay the full principal, and unlike bankruptcy it is a voluntary arrangement.

Bankruptcy counseling

Since the Bankruptcy Abuse Prevention and Consumer Protection Act of 2005, individuals filing for bankruptcy in the United States must complete a credit counseling session from an approved agency within 180 days before filing, and a debtor-education course before discharge. The U.S. Trustee Program approves and oversees these agencies.

Criticism and regulation

The industry has faced criticism: some agencies, though nominally nonprofit, charged high fees or steered clients toward plans that served creditors' interests, since agencies historically received "fair share" payments from credit card companies. The IRS revoked the tax-exempt status of numerous agencies in the mid-2000s, and the FTC's 2010 amendments to the Telemarketing Sales Rule banned advance fees for debt relief services.
