Distance education

Also known as: Distance learning, online learning, e-learning

Key institutions: Open University (UK), founded 1969

Media: Print, radio, television, internet

Distance education, also known as distance learning, is the education of students who may not always be physically present at a school or university. Traditionally this involved correspondence courses, in which students communicated with teachers by mail; today it encompasses online learning delivered over the internet.

History

Correspondence education began in the 19th century: Isaac Pitman taught shorthand by post in the 1840s, and the University of London offered external degrees from 1858. In the 20th century, radio and television extended the model. The founding of the Open University in the United Kingdom in 1969 demonstrated that full degree programmes could be delivered at scale to remote learners.

Online learning

The spread of the internet transformed distance education. Learning management systems, recorded lectures, and video conferencing allow synchronous and asynchronous instruction worldwide. Massive open online courses (MOOCs), offered by platforms such as Coursera and edX from around 2012, made university-level courses available to millions of learners free or at low cost.

Advantages and challenges

Distance education offers flexibility for working adults, lower commuting and housing costs, and access for learners in remote areas. Challenges include the need for self-discipline, limited face-to-face contact, concerns over assessment integrity, and the digital divide in access to devices and broadband.

Growth

Enrolment in distance programmes grew steadily through the 2010s and expanded dramatically during the COVID-19 pandemic, when schools and universities worldwide shifted to remote instruction. Hybrid and blended models, combining online and in-person elements, have since become standard in many institutions.
