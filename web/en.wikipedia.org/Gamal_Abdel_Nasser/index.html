Gamal Abdel Nasser Hussein (15 January 1918 – 28 September 1970) was an Egyptian military officer and politician who served as the second president of Egypt from 1956 until his death in 1970. A leading figure of Arab nationalism, he is regarded as one of the most influential Arab leaders of the twentieth century.

Early life and military career

Nasser was born in Alexandria to a postal worker from Upper Egypt. He joined the Royal Military Academy in 1937 and rose through the army, fighting in the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, where he was wounded and decorated during the siege of Faluja. Disillusioned with corruption and the monarchy, he founded the clandestine Free Officers Movement, which overthrew King Farouk in the Egyptian revolution of 1952.

Presidency

After a power struggle with President Muhammad Naguib, Nasser assumed full control in 1954 and became president following a referendum in 1956. He nationalised the Suez Canal in July 1956, prompting the Suez Crisis: Britain, France and Israel invaded Egypt, but withdrew under American and Soviet pressure, a political victory that made Nasser a hero across the Arab world.

Domestically, Nasser pursued Arab socialism: land reform, industrialisation, free education and healthcare, and major infrastructure projects, most notably the Aswan High Dam, completed with Soviet assistance. His pan-Arabism led to the short-lived union with Syria as the United Arab Republic (1958–1961). Egypt's defeat in the Six-Day War of 1967 badly damaged his prestige; he resigned briefly amid mass protests demanding his return, then remained in office while rebuilding the military during the War of Attrition.

Death and legacy

Nasser died of a heart attack in September 1970, shortly after mediating a ceasefire between Jordan and the Palestine Liberation Organization. Millions attended his funeral in Cairo. His legacy remains contested: supporters credit him with modernising Egypt and championing Arab dignity, while critics point to authoritarian rule, political repression and economic shortcomings.
