Kelvin

The kelvin, symbol K, is the base unit of thermodynamic temperature in the International System of Units (SI). It is named after the Belfast-born physicist William Thomson, 1st Baron Kelvin, who proposed an absolute temperature scale in 1848.

Zero kelvin (absolute zero) is the point at which particles have minimal thermal motion. Since the 2019 SI redefinition, the kelvin is defined by fixing the Boltzmann constant to 1.380649×10−23 J/K. One kelvin equals one degree Celsius in size; 0 °C = 273.15 K. The kelvin is used throughout science, from cryogenics to the colour temperature of light sources and the 2.725 K cosmic microwave background.
