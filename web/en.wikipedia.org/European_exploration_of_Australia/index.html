European exploration of Australia
Period1606–1901
RegionAustralia
Key figuresWillem Janszoon, Abel Tasman, James Cook, Matthew Flinders

The European exploration of Australia began in 1606 with the voyage of the Dutch navigator Willem Janszoon and continued through maritime surveys of the coastline and overland expeditions into the interior during the nineteenth century.

Maritime exploration

Dutch East India Company ships charted much of the western and southern coasts in the seventeenth century; Abel Tasman reached Tasmania and New Zealand in 1642. In 1770 James Cook charted the east coast aboard HMS Endeavour and claimed it for Britain. Matthew Flinders completed the first circumnavigation of the continent in 1801–1803 and popularised the name "Australia".

Overland exploration

After the founding of Sydney in 1788, explorers crossed the Blue Mountains in 1813 and pushed into the interior. Major expeditions included those of Charles Sturt along the Murray–Darling river system, Ludwig Leichhardt in the north, and the Burke and Wills expedition of 1860–61, the first to cross the continent south to north, which ended in tragedy. Ernest Giles, John Forrest and others crossed the western deserts in the 1870s.

Impact

Exploration opened the way for pastoral settlement and colonisation, with profound and often devastating consequences for Aboriginal peoples, whose lands were taken and whose populations were displaced by disease and violence.
