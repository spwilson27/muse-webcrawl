Michael Schumacher

Michael Schumacher (born 3 January 1969) is a German former racing driver who competed in Formula One from 1991 to 2006 and again from 2010 to 2012. He won seven Formula One World Drivers' Championship titles — five consecutively with Ferrari from 2000 to 2004 — a record he shares with Lewis Hamilton. His career totals of 91 wins, 155 podiums and 77 fastest laps stood as records for many years, and he is widely regarded as one of the greatest drivers in the sport's history.

Career

After success in karting and junior formulas, Schumacher made a sensational Formula One debut with Jordan at the 1991 Belgian Grand Prix, then immediately joined Benetton, winning the World Championship in 1994 and 1995. Moving to Ferrari in 1996, he rebuilt the struggling team around himself, technical director Ross Brawn and designer Rory Byrne, culminating in five straight drivers' and six straight constructors' titles from 1999/2000 to 2004. He retired at the end of 2006, returned with Mercedes from 2010 to 2012 — adding one final podium at Valencia in 2012 — and retired again.

Later life

In December 2013 Schumacher suffered a severe head injury in a skiing accident at Méribel in the French Alps, sustaining a traumatic brain injury despite wearing a helmet. He was placed in a medically induced coma, underwent multiple operations, and since September 2014 has been cared for at his family home near Lake Geneva; his family closely guards his privacy and releases few details of his condition. His son Mick Schumacher raced in Formula One for Haas (2021–2022), and his younger brother Ralf won six Grands Prix.

Legacy

Schumacher popularised Formula One in Germany, set new standards for driver fitness and technical feedback, and was a major donor to humanitarian causes, serving as a UNESCO special ambassador. His records and his Ferrari era remain benchmarks in the sport.
