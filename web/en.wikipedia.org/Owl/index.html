Owl

OrderStrigiformes
FamiliesStrigidae (true owls); Tytonidae (barn owls)
SpeciesMore than 200

Owls are birds from the order Strigiformes, which includes over 200 species of mostly solitary and nocturnal birds of prey. They are characterised by an upright stance, a large, broad head, binocular vision, binaural hearing, sharp talons, and feathers adapted for silent flight.

Anatomy and senses

Owls have forward-facing eyes set in a facial disc that funnels sound to their ears. Many species have asymmetrically placed ear openings, allowing precise localisation of prey in darkness. Their flight feathers have soft, fringed edges that muffle noise, enabling them to approach prey undetected.

Behaviour and diet

Most owls hunt small mammals, insects, and other birds, swallowing prey whole and later regurgitating indigestible parts as pellets. While most are nocturnal, some species — such as the northern hawk-owl and the burrowing owl — are active by day or at dawn and dusk.

Distribution and symbolism

Owls are found on every continent except Antarctica. In many cultures they symbolise wisdom — notably in association with the Greek goddess Athena — though in others they are omens of death or ill fortune.
