Felony

A felony is a serious crime, traditionally one punishable by death or imprisonment for more than one year.

A felony is traditionally a crime regarded as more serious than a misdemeanour, typically punishable by imprisonment for more than one year or by death. Felonies include violent offences such as murder, rape, robbery, and arson, as well as serious non-violent offences such as grand theft and some drug crimes. The term originates in English common law, where conviction of a felony originally carried forfeiture of land and goods and capital punishment.

United States

In the United States, the federal government generally classifies crimes punishable by death or imprisonment exceeding one year as felonies. States may divide felonies into classes or degrees with corresponding sentencing ranges. A felony conviction can carry long-term collateral consequences beyond the sentence itself, including loss of voting rights in some states (felony disenfranchisement), restrictions on firearm ownership, difficulty obtaining employment or housing, and deportation for non-citizens.

History

The distinction between felonies and misdemeanours arose in medieval English law. England abolished the felony/misdemeanour distinction in 1967, replacing felonies with "arrestable offences," but the classification remains fundamental in the United States and influential in other common-law jurisdictions.
