Libya


Libya, officially the State of Libya, is a country in the Maghreb region of North Africa. Capital: Tripoli. Area: 1,759,541 km². Population: about 6.8 million. Currency: Libyan dinar.

Libya, officially the State of Libya, is a country in the Maghreb region in North Africa. It is bordered by the Mediterranean Sea to the north, Egypt to the east, Sudan to the southeast, Chad to the south, Niger to the southwest, Algeria to the west, and Tunisia to the northwest. With an area of almost 1.8 million km², it is the fourth-largest country in Africa.

History

The region was ruled in turn by Phoenicians, Greeks, Romans, Vandals, Byzantines and Arabs, and later formed part of the Ottoman Empire. Italy colonised Libya in 1911–1912. Libya gained independence in 1951 as a kingdom under King Idris I. In 1969 Muammar Gaddafi seized power in a coup and ruled until his overthrow and death in the 2011 civil war during the Arab Spring. The country has since experienced instability and rival governments.

Economy

Libya has the largest proven oil reserves in Africa, and petroleum accounts for the overwhelming majority of export earnings and government revenue.
