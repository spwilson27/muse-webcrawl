Parliament of India

TypeBicameral
HousesRajya Sabha (Council of States); Lok Sabha (House of the People)
Seats245 (Rajya Sabha); 543 (Lok Sabha)
LocationSansad Bhavan, New Delhi
Established26 January 1950 (Constitution of India)

The Parliament of India (Bhāratīya Saṁsad) is the supreme legislative body of the Republic of India. It is a bicameral legislature composed of the President of India and two houses: the Rajya Sabha (Council of States, the upper house) and the Lok Sabha (House of the People, the lower house).

Composition

The Lok Sabha has 543 elected members chosen by direct election from territorial constituencies (the Anglo-Indian reserved seats were abolished in 2020); its term is five years unless dissolved sooner. The Rajya Sabha is a permanent body of 245 members, most elected by the legislative assemblies of the states and union territories, with 12 nominated by the President for contributions to art, literature, science, and social service; members serve staggered six-year terms.

Functions

Parliament legislates on the Union and Concurrent Lists, passes the national budget, and holds the executive accountable through questions, debates, and committees. Money bills originate only in the Lok Sabha. Constitutional amendments require special majorities in both houses.

Buildings

The original Parliament House, a circular colonnaded building designed by Edwin Lutyens and Herbert Baker, opened in 1927. A new triangular Parliament House, part of the Central Vista redevelopment, was inaugurated on 28 May 2023 and now hosts sittings, while the old building is retained as the Samvidhan Sadan (Constitution House).
