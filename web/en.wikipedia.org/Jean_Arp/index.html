Jean Arp

Jean Arp (also Hans Arp)
Born: 16 September 1886, Strasbourg, German Empire
Died: 7 June 1966 (aged 79), Basel, Switzerland
Nationality: German-French
Movement: Dada, Surrealism, Abstraction-Création
Known for: Sculpture, collage, relief, poetry

Jean Arp or Hans Arp (16 September 1886 – 7 June 1966) was a German-French sculptor, painter, and poet. He was known as a Dadaist and an abstract artist. Born in Strasbourg during the period when the city was part of the German Empire, he used the French form of his name, Jean, when writing in French and the German form, Hans, in German contexts.

Life and work

Arp studied art in Strasbourg, Weimar, and Paris. In 1912 he met Wassily Kandinsky in Munich and exhibited with Der Blaue Reiter. In Zurich in 1915, repelled by the First World War, he helped found the Dada movement, participating in the Cabaret Voltaire performances with Hugo Ball, Emmy Hennings, Tristan Tzara, and others. He developed collages "arranged according to the laws of chance" — dropping torn pieces of paper and fixing them where they fell — and wooden reliefs of organic, biomorphic shapes.

Later career

Arp married the artist Sophie Taeuber in 1922; the two collaborated on many works. In the 1930s he turned to sculpture in the round, creating smooth, swelling forms suggestive of seeds, clouds, and human figures, and co-founded the Abstraction-Création group. After Sophie Taeuber-Arp's accidental death in 1943, he produced some of his most personal work and wrote extensively in her memory. In the 1950s he received major public commissions, including a relief for the UNESCO headquarters in Paris, and won the Grand Prize for Sculpture at the 1954 Venice Biennale.

Style

Arp's art is characterized by curvilinear, organic abstraction and a playful engagement with chance and nature. He wrote poetry in both French and German throughout his life, and his dual identity came to symbolize reconciliation between the two cultures.
