Sinulog

Street dancers during the Sinulog Festival in Cebu City.

The Sinulog Festival (or Sinulog-Santo Niño Festival) is an annual cultural and religious festival held on the third Sunday of January in Cebu City, and in several other places in the Philippines, in honour of the Santo Niño (the Child Jesus). It is the centre of the Santo Niño Catholic celebrations in the country, drawing millions of participants and spectators, and is often described as the Philippines' grandest festival.

The dance

The festival is named for the sinulog dance, a ritual prayer-dance performed by candle vendors and devotees in honour of the Santo Niño. The basic steps — two steps forward and one step back to the beating of drums — imitate the movement of the sulog, the water current of Cebu's Pahina River; the dance is said to predate Spanish colonisation as a native offering to wooden idols, later redirected to the image of the Santo Niño given by Magellan's expedition to Queen Juana of Cebu in 1521.

Celebrations

The festival includes a solemn novena and procession, a fluvial parade down the Mactan Channel, and the Grand Parade, in which contingents from across the Visayas and Mindanao compete in elaborate choreographed street dancing. Ati-Atihan, Dinagyang and other Santo Niño festivals held in the same month share common origins with Sinulog.
