Hippolyta

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Hippolyta, queen of the Amazons

In Greek mythology, Hippolyta (Ἱππολύτη) was a daughter of Ares and the Amazonian queen Otrera, and queen of the Amazons. She possessed a magical girdle (the zoster) given to her by her father Ares, which symbolised her authority over the other Amazons.

The ninth labour of Heracles

Hippolyta is best known from the ninth of the Twelve Labours of Heracles. Eurystheus ordered Heracles to fetch Hippolyta's girdle for his daughter Admete. In the most famous version, Hippolyta received Heracles hospitably and agreed to give him the girdle, but the goddess Hera, disguised as an Amazon, spread a rumour that the Greeks intended to abduct the queen. The Amazons attacked, and in the ensuing battle Heracles killed Hippolyta and took the girdle. Other versions have Heracles capture Hippolyta's sister Melanippe and receive the girdle as ransom.

Hippolyta and Theseus

In another tradition, Theseus of Athens carried Hippolyta (or her sister Antiope, with whom she is often conflated) back to Athens, where she bore him a son, Hippolytus. In Euripides' and later accounts, Theseus later married Phaedra, and at the wedding the Amazons attacked Athens; Hippolyta was killed in the fighting, in some versions accidentally by her sister Penthesilea or by Theseus himself.

In popular culture

Hippolyta appears in Shakespeare's A Midsummer Night's Dream as Theseus's bride, and in modern comics and film as the queen of Themyscira and mother of Wonder Woman.
