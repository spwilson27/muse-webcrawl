Antisense therapy

Antisense therapy is a form of treatment that uses antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) — short, synthetic, single-stranded nucleic acid sequences — to alter RNA and reduce, restore, or modify protein expression through several different mechanisms.

Mechanism

An ASO is designed to be complementary to a specific messenger RNA. Upon binding, it can recruit RNase H to degrade the RNA, block translation, or modify splicing so that a functional protein is produced or a defective exon is skipped. Chemical modifications such as phosphorothioate backbones and 2′-sugar modifications improve stability and binding.

Approved drugs

Examples include fomivirsen (the first approved ASO, for CMV retinitis), nusinersen (Spinraza) for spinal muscular atrophy, which modifies splicing of the SMN2 gene, and eteplirsen for Duchenne muscular dystrophy. Several antisense drugs have also been developed for cardiovascular and rare genetic diseases.
