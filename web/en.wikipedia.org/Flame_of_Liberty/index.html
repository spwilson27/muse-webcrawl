Flame of Liberty

The Flame of Liberty (French: Flamme de la Liberté) is a full-sized, gold-leafed replica of the flame of the torch of the Statue of Liberty in New York City. It stands above the entrance to the Pont de l'Alma tunnel in Paris, near the Place de l'Alma. Donated to the city in 1989, the monument became an unofficial memorial to Diana, Princess of Wales, after her death in a car crash in the tunnel below in 1997.

History

The monument was offered to Paris in 1987 by the International Herald Tribune to mark the newspaper's centenary and to celebrate Franco-American friendship; funds were raised from donors around the world. It was inaugurated on 10 May 1989 by Jacques Chirac, then Mayor of Paris. The flame is a replica, made using the traditional repoussé technique by the same workshops, of the torch flame redesigned for the Statue of Liberty's centennial restoration.

Association with Diana, Princess of Wales

After the fatal crash of 31 August 1997 in the Alma tunnel directly beneath the monument, visitors began leaving flowers, candles and messages at the flame, treating it as a shrine to Diana. In 2019 the surrounding square was officially renamed Place Diana by the Paris city council, formalising the site's de facto role as her memorial.
