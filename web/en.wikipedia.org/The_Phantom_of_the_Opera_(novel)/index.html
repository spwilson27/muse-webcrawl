The Phantom of the Opera (novel)

The Phantom of the Opera (French: Le Fantôme de l'Opéra) is a novel by French author Gaston Leroux, serialized 1909–1910 and published in book form in 1910. It blends Gothic horror, mystery and romance.

Plot

Beneath the Palais Garnier in Paris lives Erik, a disfigured musical genius who haunts the opera house as the "Opera Ghost." He falls in love with young soprano Christine Daaé, tutoring her voice, and becomes murderously jealous of her childhood sweetheart, Viscount Raoul de Chagny. The Persian, a former acquaintance of Erik, helps Raoul navigate Erik's labyrinth; Erik ultimately releases Christine after she shows him compassion.

Adaptations

The novel inspired the 1925 Lon Chaney silent film and Andrew Lloyd Webber's 1986 musical, the longest-running show in Broadway history. See also The Mystery of the Yellow Room.
