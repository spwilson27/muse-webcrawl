Hirakud Dam is built across the Mahanadi River, about 15 kilometres from Sambalpur in the state of Odisha, India. It is one of the longest dams in the world, stretching about 25.8 km including its dykes, and was one of the first major multipurpose river valley projects undertaken after India's independence. Jawaharlal Nehru laid its foundation in 1948, and the dam was inaugurated on 13 January 1957.

Purpose and reservoir

The dam controls floods in the Mahanadi delta, provides irrigation to roughly 436,000 hectares in Odisha and Chhattisgarh, and generates hydroelectric power at two power houses (Burla and Chiplima) with a combined installed capacity of about 347.5 MW. Its reservoir, Hirakud Lake, extends some 55 km and is one of the largest artificial lakes in Asia; the left part of the reservoir lies within the Debrigarh Wildlife Sanctuary.

Displacement and criticism

Construction submerged more than 240 villages and displaced over 100,000 people, many of whom, critics note, were never fully compensated or resettled. Siltation has also reduced the reservoir's capacity over time.
