Volkswagen Golf

The Volkswagen Golf is a compact car produced by the German manufacturer Volkswagen since 1974. Marketed worldwide in hatchback, estate and convertible body styles, it has passed through eight generations and sold more than 37 million units, making it one of the best-selling cars of all time and the car that popularised the "hot hatch" format through the GTI version.

History

The original Golf (Mk1), styled by Giorgetto Giugiaro, succeeded the air-cooled, rear-engined Beetle with a water-cooled, front-wheel-drive design. The Golf GTI of 1976 combined that practical layout with a fuel-injected 1.6-litre engine and created the hot hatchback genre. In North America the first and fifth generations were sold as the Rabbit.

Successive generations grew in size, safety and refinement. The Mk4 (1997) raised interior quality benchmarks in the class; the Mk7 (2012) introduced the modular MQB platform shared across the Volkswagen Group; the current Mk8, launched in 2019, emphasises digital instrumentation and mild-hybrid and plug-in hybrid (GTE) powertrains, alongside the GTI and high-performance Golf R.

Significance

The Golf has twice been European Car of the Year (1992 and 2013) and effectively defined the European C-segment, often called the "Golf class". Electric versions (e-Golf) preceded Volkswagen's dedicated ID. series of battery-electric cars.
