Newton fractal

The Newton fractal is a fractal generated by applying Newton's method for finding roots of a complex polynomial. Each point in the complex plane is used as a starting value; the plane is coloured according to which root the iteration converges to, producing intricate boundary patterns.

Description

For a polynomial p(z), Newton's iteration z → z - p(z)/p'(z) usually converges to one of the roots of p. The basins of attraction of the roots are regions whose shared boundaries are fractal: near the boundary, tiny changes in the starting point can change which root is reached, reflecting chaotic sensitivity. Colouring by root and shading by convergence speed yields the classic images, most often computed for p(z) = z^3 - 1, whose three roots give a threefold symmetric pattern.

Generalisation

The method extends to other root-finding iterations, such as Halley's method, and to other functions besides polynomials, producing related fractal families. Newton fractals are a popular example in the study of complex dynamics, closely related to Julia sets.
