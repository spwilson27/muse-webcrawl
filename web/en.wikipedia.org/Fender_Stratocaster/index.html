Fender Stratocaster

The Fender Stratocaster, colloquially known as the "Strat", is a model of electric guitar introduced by Fender in 1954. Designed by Leo Fender, Bill Carson, George Fullerton, and Freddie Tavares, it has remained in continuous production and is one of the most recognisable and widely copied guitar designs in the world.

Design

The Stratocaster features a contoured double-cutaway solid body, three single-coil pickups, and a synchronised tremolo bridge that allows pitch bending of all six strings at once. A five-way selector switch (standard since 1977; three-way originally) lets players combine pickups, producing the bright, "quacking" tones associated with the in-between positions. Its bolt-on maple neck has a 25.5-inch scale length.

History

Building on the success of the Telecaster (1950), Fender developed the Stratocaster as a more refined, comfortable instrument incorporating feedback from working musicians. Early adopters included Buddy Holly, whose use of a Strat on "That'll Be the Day" helped popularise it in rock and roll.

Notable players

The Stratocaster is inseparable from the playing of Jimi Hendrix, who famously played a right-handed Strat upside-down and set one alight at the Monterey Pop Festival in 1967. Other defining Strat players include Eric Clapton (whose "Blackie" and "Brownie" guitars became icons), David Gilmour, Stevie Ray Vaughan, Jeff Beck, Mark Knopfler, and John Mayer. Vintage examples are highly collectible: Gilmour's "Black Strat" sold at auction in 2019 for nearly $4 million, then a record for a guitar.
