Chandrayaan-1


Chandrayaan-1, India's first lunar probe, launched 22 October 2008.

Chandrayaan-1 (from Sanskrit candra, "Moon", and yāna, "craft") was the first Indian lunar probe under the Chandrayaan programme of the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO). Launched on 22 October 2008 from the Satish Dhawan Space Centre aboard a PSLV-XL rocket, it entered lunar orbit on 8 November 2008 and operated until communications were lost on 29 August 2009.

Mission profile

The spacecraft carried eleven scientific instruments built by India, the United States, the United Kingdom, Germany, Sweden and Bulgaria. It mapped the Moon's surface in high resolution, studied its chemical and mineralogical composition, and searched for water ice in permanently shadowed polar craters. A Moon Impact Probe carrying the Indian national flag was released on 14 November 2008 and struck the surface near the Shackleton crater, making India the fourth entity to place an object on the Moon.

Discovery of water

Data from the Moon Mineralogy Mapper (M3), a NASA instrument aboard the orbiter, revealed widespread traces of water and hydroxyl molecules in the lunar soil, especially towards the poles — a landmark finding announced in September 2009. ISRO's Moon Impact Probe also reported evidence of water in the Moon's tenuous exosphere.

End of mission

Although Chandrayaan-1 completed roughly 95 percent of its planned objectives in less than a year against a planned two-year mission, overheating and a failed star sensor curtailed its life. The orbiter's orbit decayed and it is believed to have impacted the Moon in later years. The mission was followed by Chandrayaan-2 (2019) and Chandrayaan-3 (2023).
