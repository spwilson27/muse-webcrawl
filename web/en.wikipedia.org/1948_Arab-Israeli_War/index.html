LocationFormer British Mandate of Palestine, Sinai Peninsula, southern Lebanon
ResultIsraeli victory; Jordanian partial victory; Egyptian and Palestinian defeat
Territorial changesIsrael kept the area allotted to it under the Partition Plan and captured 50% of the area allotted to the Arab state; Jordan annexed the West Bank; Egypt occupied the Gaza Strip

The 1948 Arab-Israeli War, also known as the War of Independence in Israel and as part of the Nakba ("catastrophe") in Palestinian memory, was fought between the newly declared State of Israel and a coalition of Arab states and Palestinian Arab forces. It was the second phase of the 1948 Palestine war, following the civil war in Mandatory Palestine that had begun after the United Nations adopted the Partition Plan in November 1947.

Background

On 29 November 1947 the UN General Assembly adopted Resolution 181, recommending the partition of Mandatory Palestine into Jewish and Arab states with Jerusalem as an international city. Jewish leaders accepted the plan; Arab leaders rejected it. Fighting between Jewish and Arab forces in Palestine escalated over the following months. On 14 May 1948, David Ben-Gurion proclaimed the establishment of the State of Israel, coinciding with the end of the British Mandate. The next day, armies of Egypt, Transjordan, Syria, Lebanon and Iraq invaded.

Course of the war

The war is conventionally divided into phases separated by two truces brokered by the United Nations. In the first phase (15 May – 11 June 1948), Arab forces advanced on several fronts: Egyptian forces moved up the coast toward Tel Aviv and toward Jerusalem; Transjordan's Arab Legion captured East Jerusalem and the Jewish Quarter of the Old City and held Latrun, blocking the road to Jerusalem; Syrian and Lebanese forces attacked in the north.

During the first truce Israel reorganised and absorbed large shipments of weapons, notably from Czechoslovakia, and integrated its militias into the Israel Defense Forces (IDF). In the "Ten Days" of fighting in July 1948, Israel went on the offensive, capturing Lydda and Ramle, Nazareth, and territory in the Galilee and around Jerusalem. A second truce began on 18 July.

In the final phase, from October 1948, Israeli offensives (including Operation Yoav, Operation Hiram and Operation Horev) secured the Negev, the Galilee and parts of the Sinai, before international pressure forced a withdrawal from Egyptian territory.

Armistices and aftermath

Between February and July 1949 Israel signed armistice agreements with Egypt, Lebanon, Transjordan and Syria at Rhodes, establishing the Green Line boundaries. Israel ended the war controlling about 78% of the former Mandate territory. Jordan annexed the West Bank and East Jerusalem; Egypt administered the Gaza Strip.

During the wider 1948 war, more than 700,000 Palestinian Arabs fled or were expelled from their homes — an exodus Palestinians call the Nakba — and became refugees in the West Bank, Gaza Strip and neighbouring Arab countries. In the following years, a comparable number of Jews left or were expelled from Arab countries, many immigrating to Israel. About 6,000 Israelis and several thousand Arab combatants and civilians were killed. The war's unresolved issues — borders, refugees, and the status of Jerusalem — shaped the Arab-Israeli conflict for decades, leading to further wars in 1956 and 1967.
