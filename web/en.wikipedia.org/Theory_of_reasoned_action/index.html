Theory of reasoned action

Developed byMartin Fishbein and Icek Ajzen
Introduced1975 (Belief, Attitude, Intention, and Behavior)
FieldSocial psychology
Key constructsAttitude, subjective norm, behavioural intention, behaviour
SuccessorTheory of planned behavior (1985/1991)

The theory of reasoned action (TRA) is a model for the prediction of behavioural intention and behaviour, developed by Martin Fishbein and Icek Ajzen in the 1970s. The theory holds that a person's behaviour is determined by their intention to perform it, and that this intention is in turn a function of two factors: their attitude toward the behaviour (their overall positive or negative evaluation of performing it) and the subjective norm (their perception of the social pressure to perform or avoid it).

Components

Attitude is formed from behavioural beliefs — beliefs about the likely outcomes of the behaviour, weighted by the evaluation of those outcomes. Subjective norms are formed from normative beliefs — perceptions of what particular referent individuals or groups think one should do, weighted by one's motivation to comply with each referent. The two factors combine, with weights that vary by person and behaviour, to produce behavioural intention, which — provided the behaviour is under volitional control — predicts actual behaviour.

Development and extensions

Fishbein and Ajzen set out the theory in their 1975 book Belief, Attitude, Intention, and Behavior and in Ajzen and Fishbein's 1980 volume Understanding Attitudes and Predicting Social Behavior. Because the TRA assumes the behaviour is fully under a person's control, Ajzen extended it in the 1980s by adding perceived behavioural control, producing the theory of planned behavior. Together the two models have been applied thousands of times in health psychology (predicting condom use, smoking cessation, diet, and exercise), consumer research, and technology acceptance, and are among the most cited theories in social science.

Criticism

Critics note that the TRA's explanatory reach is limited to reasoned, deliberative behaviour, leaving out habits, emotions, identity, and moral considerations, and that the intention–behaviour gap shows that strong intentions alone often fail to translate into action. Subsequent frameworks, including the Reasoned Action Approach of Fishbein and Ajzen (2010), reorganize the determinants and add background factors.
