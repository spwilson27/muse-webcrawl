Winter

Winter is the coldest and darkest season of the year in polar and temperate climates, occurring between autumn and spring. It is caused by the tilt of Earth's axis away from the Sun in the respective hemisphere.

Dates and characteristics

Astronomically, winter in the Northern Hemisphere runs from the December solstice to the March equinox; meteorologists instead count December, January and February. Winter brings the shortest days and lowest temperatures, and in many regions snow, ice and frost. The Southern Hemisphere experiences winter around June to August.

Ecology and human adaptation

Plants become dormant and many animals hibernate, migrate, or grow insulating coats. Humans mark winter with festivals such as Christmas and New Year, winter sports including skiing and ice hockey, and cultural works from Vivaldi's Four Seasons to Schubert's Winterreise. Historically, severe winters have shaped events from famines to military campaigns.
