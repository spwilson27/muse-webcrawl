Objective-collapse theory

Objective-collapse theories, also known as models of spontaneous wavefunction collapse, are solutions to the measurement problem in quantum mechanics. They modify the Schrödinger equation to include a physical, observer-independent process that collapses the wavefunction, so that macroscopic superpositions rapidly reduce to definite states.

Motivation

Standard quantum mechanics does not specify when or how measurement causes collapse. Objective-collapse models propose that collapse is a real stochastic process occurring spontaneously, with a rate that grows with the size or mass of the system, explaining why everyday objects are never observed in superpositions.

Models

The Ghirardi–Rimini–Weber (GRW) model (1986) posits random spontaneous localisations of each particle roughly once every hundred million years, amplified for entangled macroscopic systems. Continuous spontaneous localisation (CSL) models make the collapse a continuous process driven by a noise field. Roger Penrose has proposed that gravity causes collapse when superposed mass distributions differ significantly.

Testing

Unlike interpretations, collapse models make predictions differing slightly from standard quantum mechanics — such as tiny heating or spontaneous radiation — and are being constrained by experiments.
