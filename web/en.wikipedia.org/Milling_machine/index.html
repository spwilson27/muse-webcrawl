Milling machine

A milling machine is a machine tool that removes material from a workpiece using rotary cutters. It is one of the most versatile machines in a machine shop, able to perform operations ranging from simple face milling and slotting to cutting complex three-dimensional contours and gears.

Types

In a vertical milling machine, the spindle axis is vertical and the cutters are held in a quill that can be raised and lowered; the familiar knee-type "Bridgeport" mill is of this form. In a horizontal milling machine, the spindle is horizontal and cutters are mounted on an arbor. Modern CNC machining centres combine milling with automatic tool changing and multi-axis motion under computer control.

Operation

The workpiece is clamped to a table that moves in the X and Y axes while the knee or head provides Z motion. Cutters include end mills, face mills, ball-nose cutters, and form cutters, chosen according to the material and the geometry required. Milling differs from turning on a lathe because the tool, rather than the workpiece, rotates.

History

Milling machines emerged in the early 19th century in the clock and firearms industries; Eli Whitney and others developed them for interchangeable-parts manufacture. The universal milling machine, with a swivelling table for helical work, appeared in the 1860s.
