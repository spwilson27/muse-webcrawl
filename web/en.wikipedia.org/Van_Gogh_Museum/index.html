The Van Gogh Museum is a Dutch art museum dedicated to the works of Vincent van Gogh and his contemporaries, located at Museumplein in Amsterdam South, close to the Stedelijk Museum and the Rijksmuseum. It houses the largest collection of Van Gogh's paintings and drawings in the world, including The Potato Eaters (1885), Sunflowers (1889), Almond Blossom (1890), and The Bedroom.

History

After Vincent van Gogh's death in 1890, his unsold works passed to his brother Theo, and then to Theo's widow Johanna van Gogh-Bonger, who promoted his art. The collection was inherited by their son, Vincent Willem van Gogh, who founded the Vincent van Gogh Foundation in 1962 and loaned the works to the Stedelijk Museum before the dedicated museum opened in 1973. The main building was designed by Gerrit Rietveld; a later exhibition wing by Kisho Kurokawa was completed in 1999, and a new entrance hall opened in 2015.

Collection and theft

The permanent collection comprises more than 200 paintings, some 500 drawings, and over 700 letters by Van Gogh, alongside works by contemporaries such as Paul Gauguin, Édouard Manet, Claude Monet, and Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec. In December 2002, two paintings — View of the Sea at Scheveningen and Congregation Leaving the Reformed Church in Nuenen — were stolen from the museum; they were recovered in Naples, Italy, in 2016 in a Mafia-linked investigation.
