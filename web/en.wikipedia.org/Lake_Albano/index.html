Lake Albano

Location: Lazio, Italy
Type: volcanic crater lake
Surface area: 6 km²
Max. depth: 168 m
Surface elevation: 293 m

Lake Albano (Italian: Lago Albano or Lago di Castel Gandolfo) is a small volcanic crater lake in the Alban Hills of Lazio, at the foot of Monte Cavo, 20 km southeast of Rome.

The lake, some 3.5 km long and 2.3 km wide, occupies a complex volcanic crater formed by overlapping eruptions of the Alban Hills volcano. It has no natural outlet; in antiquity the Romans constructed an emissary tunnel, completed around 398 BC, to regulate its level — an engineering work still in use.

History

In Roman times the lake lay beside the ancient city of Alba Longa. The town of Castel Gandolfo on its western shore is the site of the Pope's summer residence. For the 1960 Rome Olympics, the lake hosted the canoeing and rowing events; a stadium was built on its shore.

Geology

Lake Albano is the deepest crater lake in Italy. Gas emissions from below the lake bed have raised concerns about a possible limnic eruption similar to that of Lake Nyos, and scientific monitoring of CO2 levels continues.
