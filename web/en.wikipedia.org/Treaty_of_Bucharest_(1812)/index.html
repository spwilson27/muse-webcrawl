Treaty of Bucharest (1812)

The Treaty of Bucharest of 1812 was concluded on 28 May 1812, in Bucharest, between the Ottoman Empire and the Russian Empire, ending the Russo-Turkish War of 1806–1812. It was negotiated for Russia by Admiral Pavel Chichagov's representative, with Mikhail Kutuzov commanding Russian forces, on the eve of Napoleon's invasion of Russia, which made a swift settlement urgent for St. Petersburg.

Terms

Under the treaty, the Ottomans ceded Bessarabia — the eastern half of the Principality of Moldavia between the Prut and Dniester rivers — to Russia, shifting the Russian frontier to the Prut. Russia returned most of its conquests in the Caucasus, retaining only limited gains, and the Ottomans granted autonomy and amnesty privileges to Serbia, then in revolt under Karađorđe, though these provisions were soon undermined. The treaty also confirmed Russian navigation rights on the Danube. Signed days before Napoleon crossed the Niemen, the settlement freed Russia's Army of the Danube for the defence against France.
