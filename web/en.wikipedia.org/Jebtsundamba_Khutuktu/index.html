Jebtsundamba Khutuktu

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Jebtsundamba Khutuktu (Mongolian: Жавзандамба хутагт, Javzandamba xutagt) is the spiritual head of the Gelug lineage of Tibetan Buddhism in Mongolia. The most senior of the tulku (reincarnate lama) lineages in Mongolia, the holder of the title was traditionally the third most important figure in the Gelug hierarchy after the Dalai Lama and the Panchen Lama. The first Jebtsundamba, Zanabazar (1635–1723), was the son of a Mongol khan and a great sculptor and scholar.

History

The lineage began with Zanabazar, recognised as the reincarnation of the Tibetan historian Taranatha. The first two Jebtsundambas were Mongolian; thereafter, in part because the Qing dynasty sought to limit the office's political power in Mongolia, subsequent incarnations were found in Tibet. The eighth Jebtsundamba, Ngawang Lobzang Chökyi Nyima Tenzin Wangchuk (1870–1924), was proclaimed Bogd Khan — emperor — of Mongolia when the country declared independence from the Qing in 1911, making him both spiritual and temporal ruler until his death in 1924.

The ninth Jebtsundamba

After the eighth's death, the communist Mongolian People's Republic suppressed religion and forbade recognition of a successor. Nevertheless, a ninth incarnation, Jampal Namdol Chökyi Gyaltsen (1932–2012), born in Tibet, was identified in secret and later lived in exile in India. He was publicly recognised by the 14th Dalai Lama and, after Mongolia's democratic revolution of 1990, visited and eventually settled in Mongolia, where he was enthroned as head of Mongolian Buddhists and resided at Gandantegchinlen Monastery in Ulaanbaatar until his death in 2012.

Tenth incarnation

In March 2023, the 14th Dalai Lama publicly presented a young boy, born in the United States to Mongolian parents, as the tenth Jebtsundamba Khutuktu during a ceremony in Dharamshala, India — an announcement of major religious significance for Mongolia and one watched closely given Mongolia's position between Russia and China.
