Steveston, British Columbia

Steveston, founded in the 1880s, is a historic fishing village within the city of Richmond, British Columbia, Canada, located at the mouth of the Fraser River on Lulu Island. Named for Manoah Steves, its first settler family, it was once home to dozens of salmon canneries and a large Japanese-Canadian fishing community.

History

By 1900 Steveston was one of the world's busiest salmon ports. Its Japanese-Canadian residents were forcibly removed in 1942 during the Second World War internment, and many never returned. The Gulf of Georgia Cannery, built in 1894, is preserved as a National Historic Site.

Today

Steveston remains Canada's largest commercial fishing port by some measures and is a popular tourist destination, with a working waterfront, seafood restaurants, the Steveston Salmon Festival on Canada Day, and a dockside fish market. It served as the fictional town of Storybrooke in the television series Once Upon a Time.
