The Bighorn River is a tributary of the Yellowstone River in the U.S. states of Wyoming and Montana. It is approximately 461 miles (742 km) long. The upper reaches in Wyoming are named the Wind River; the two names were historically given to different sections of the same river, with the change of name traditionally occurring at the Wedding of the Waters near Thermopolis, where the Wind River emerges from the Wind River Canyon.

Course

Rising in the Wind River Range, the river flows north through the dramatic Bighorn Canyon, now the Bighorn Canyon National Recreation Area, and is impounded by the Yellowtail Dam in Montana, completed in 1967. It then flows northeast across the Montana plains to join the Yellowstone River near Custer.

History

The river takes its name from the bighorn sheep of the region. Fur trappers travelled it in the early 19th century, and the upper valley was contested ground among the Crow, Shoshone, and Sioux nations. The Little Bighorn River, a tributary, was the site of the famous 1876 battle.

Fishing

Below Yellowtail Dam, the Bighorn is one of the most celebrated trout fisheries in the United States, with stable cold-water releases supporting dense populations of rainbow and brown trout.
