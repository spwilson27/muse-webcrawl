Wind of Change (speech)

"Wind of Change" speech
SpeakerHarold Macmillan, Prime Minister of the United Kingdom
Date3 February 1960
LocationParliament of South Africa, Cape Town

The "Wind of Change" speech was an address by British Prime Minister Harold Macmillan to the Parliament of South Africa in Cape Town on 3 February 1960. It is remembered as a landmark of the decolonisation of Africa, signalling that Britain recognised the inevitability of African majority rule and would not indefinitely support white-minority governments.

The speech

Macmillan had first used a version of the phrase in Accra, Ghana, on a tour of Commonwealth Africa. In Cape Town he told the South African parliament: "The wind of change is blowing through this continent, and, whether we like it or not, this growth of national consciousness is a political fact." He urged South Africa's government, then committed to apartheid, to accept the trend toward African nationalism and warned against policies that the Western world could not support.

Impact

The speech was received coldly by Prime Minister Hendrik Verwoerd but marked a turning point in British policy: over the following decade most of Britain's African colonies gained independence, and Britain increasingly distanced itself from South Africa, which became a republic and left the Commonwealth in 1961. The phrase "wind of change" entered political vocabulary worldwide as shorthand for the forces of decolonisation.
