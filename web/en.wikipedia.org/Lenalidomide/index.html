Lenalidomide

Lenalidomide, sold under the brand name Revlimid among others, is an immunomodulatory drug used chiefly to treat multiple myeloma and myelodysplastic syndromes. It is a derivative of thalidomide, developed to be more potent and less sedating than its parent compound.

Mechanism

Lenalidomide binds the protein cereblon, redirecting a ubiquitin ligase to mark certain transcription factors for destruction, which kills myeloma cells and modulates immune responses and blood-vessel growth.

Safety

Because of its thalidomide heritage, lenalidomide carries strict pregnancy-prevention controls: it can cause severe birth defects. Other major risks include low blood counts and venous blood clots, so patients are monitored and often given anticoagulants.
