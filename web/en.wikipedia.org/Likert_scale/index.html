Likert scale

A Likert scale is a psychometric scale named after its inventor, psychologist Rensis Likert, used in research questionnaires to measure respondents' attitudes or feelings towards a statement. Respondents specify their level of agreement or disagreement on a symmetric agree–disagree scale for a series of statements, typically using five or seven points ranging from "strongly disagree" to "strongly agree". The responses to several such items may be summed to yield an overall score.

Scoring and analysis

Strictly, the "Likert scale" refers to the summated score across multiple items, while a single item with Likert-type response options is a "Likert item". Because individual items yield ordinal data, there is long-running debate about whether means and parametric statistics are appropriate; in practice composite scores are often treated as approximately interval data. Design choices include the number of response points, the inclusion of a neutral midpoint, and balanced wording of items to guard against acquiescence bias.

History

Likert introduced the scale in his 1932 article "A Technique for the Measurement of Attitudes", offering a simpler alternative to Thurstone scaling. It has become the most widely used approach to scaling responses in survey research.
