Bell System

The Bell System was a system of telecommunication companies, led by the Bell Telephone Company and later by the American Telephone and Telegraph Company (AT&T), that dominated the telephone services industry in North America for over a century, from its creation in 1877 until its antitrust breakup in 1984. The system included 22 local operating companies (the "Baby Bells" after divestiture), the Western Electric manufacturing arm, and Bell Telephone Laboratories as its research division. At its height it was among the largest private employers in the United States, and its regulated monopoly provided near-universal telephone service under the slogan of "one system, one policy, universal service".

History

Alexander Graham Bell patented the telephone in 1876, and the Bell Telephone Company was founded the following year. Through aggressive patent enforcement and acquisition, and after the expiry of its patents provoked competition from thousands of independent telephone companies, AT&T consolidated control under president Theodore Vail, who accepted regulation in exchange for monopoly status (Kingsbury Commitment, 1913). A 1956 consent decree confined the company to regulated telephone service and government work, keeping it out of the computer industry.

Breakup

In 1974 the United States Department of Justice filed an antitrust suit alleging that the Bell System used monopoly profits from regulated services to subsidise its competitive businesses. A settlement announced in 1982, the Modification of Final Judgment, required AT&T to divest its local operating companies on 1 January 1984. The 22 Bell operating companies were reorganised into seven Regional Bell Operating Companies (Ameritech, Bell Atlantic, BellSouth, NYNEX, Pacific Telesis, Southwestern Bell, and US West), while AT&T retained long-distance service, Western Electric, and Bell Labs.

Legacy

The breakup reshaped global telecommunications, encouraging competition in long-distance and equipment markets. Through subsequent mergers, many of the successor companies were reabsorbed into a new AT&T and Verizon. The Bell System's research legacy, through Bell Labs, includes the transistor, information theory, and Unix.
