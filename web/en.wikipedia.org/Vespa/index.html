Vespa

Vespa is an Italian brand of scooters manufactured by Piaggio. The name means "wasp" in Italian. First patented in 1946, the Vespa evolved from a single model of motor scooter into a full line of scooters and one of seven brands owned by Piaggio, the world's largest manufacturer of two-wheeled vehicles. With its pressed-steel unibody, flat floorboard, and enclosed engine, the Vespa became an enduring icon of Italian design and post-war mobility, and remains in production today.

History

After World War II, Enrico Piaggio sought a cheap, simple vehicle to get a war-torn Italy moving. Aeronautical engineer Corradino D'Ascanio, who disliked motorcycles, designed a scooter with a step-through frame, a front shield protecting the rider from road spray, handlebar gear change, and an engine enclosed so riders' clothes stayed clean. Legend holds that Enrico Piaggio, seeing the prototype's narrow waist and hearing the engine's buzz, exclaimed, "Sembra una vespa!" ("It looks like a wasp!").

The Vespa 98 of 1946 was followed by the 125, and sales soared through the 1950s, aided by appearances in films such as Roman Holiday (1953) with Audrey Hepburn and Gregory Peck. Vespas were built under licence in Germany, Britain, France, Spain, Belgium and India. In Britain the Vespa (and its rival, the Lambretta) became central to the Mod subculture of the 1960s. The PX series, introduced in 1977, became the best-known classic model; modern ranges include the Primavera, Sprint and GTS, and the electric Vespa Elettrica was launched in 2018.

Design

The classic Vespa's distinguishing features are its load-bearing steel body combining frame and bodywork, a flat floorboard, a prominent front fairing, and (on most models) a spare wheel mounted on the side. Engines ranged from 50 cc mopeds to 300 cc on the modern GTS. Later models added automatic (CVT) transmissions, disc brakes, fuel injection and four-stroke engines to meet emissions standards, while retaining the silhouette of the original.
