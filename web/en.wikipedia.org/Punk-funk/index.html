Punk-funk

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Punk-funk is a fusion genre that mixes elements of punk rock and funk music. Emerging in the late 1970s and early 1980s, it typically combines punk's energy, minimalism and confrontational attitude with funk's rhythmic emphasis — interlocking bass lines, slapped bass, syncopated drums and danceable grooves.

History

The style developed on both sides of the Atlantic. In the United Kingdom, post-punk bands such as Gang of Four, The Pop Group and A Certain Ratio absorbed funk and dub influences, producing angular, rhythm-driven music on albums like Gang of Four's Entertainment! (1979). In the United States, New York's no wave scene — including James Chance and the Contortions and Defunkt — fused free jazz, funk and punk abrasion, while Washington, D.C.'s go-go scene provided a related local funk culture.

Influence

Punk-funk fed into later developments including dance-punk and funk metal. The Red Hot Chili Peppers brought a punk-funk sound to mainstream success in the 1980s and 1990s, and bands such as Rage Against the Machine and Fishbone drew on the fusion. The early 2000s dance-punk revival, led by acts like LCD Soundsystem and The Rapture, renewed interest in the original punk-funk and post-punk records.
