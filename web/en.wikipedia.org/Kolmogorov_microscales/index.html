Kolmogorov microscales

The Kolmogorov microscales are the smallest scales in turbulent flow, at which viscosity dominates and turbulent kinetic energy is dissipated into heat. They were described by Andrey Kolmogorov in his 1941 theory of turbulence.

Definitions

From the kinematic viscosity ν and the mean rate of dissipation of turbulent kinetic energy ε, Kolmogorov defined a length scale η = (ν³/ε)1/4, a time scale τ = (ν/ε)1/2, and a velocity scale u = (νε)1/4. At these scales the Reynolds number based on η is of order one.

Significance

In the energy cascade, large eddies transfer energy to progressively smaller eddies until the Kolmogorov scale is reached and viscosity dissipates it. The ratio between the largest and smallest scales grows with Reynolds number, which is why direct numerical simulation of high-Reynolds-number turbulence is so computationally demanding.
