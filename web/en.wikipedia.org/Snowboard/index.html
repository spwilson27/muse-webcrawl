Snowboard


A modern freestyle snowboard

A snowboard is a recreational and sporting item that is designed to be ridden down a slope covered in snow while standing on it. It is typically attached to a rider's feet using bindings mounted on the board, with the rider standing sideways rather than facing forward as in skiing. Snowboards are wider than skis and have no poles; they developed in the 1960s and 1970s from earlier experiments such as Sherman Poppen's "Snurfer" (1965) and the designs of Jake Burton Carpenter, Tom Sims, and Dimitrije Milovich.

Construction

A modern snowboard consists of a laminated core, usually of wood, sandwiched between layers of fiberglass and composite materials, with a sintered or extruded plastic base (P-Tex) and steel edges. Boards are built with camber, rocker, or hybrid profiles, and with varying flex patterns and sidecut radii for different riding styles. Bindings, either strap-in or step-on designs, secure the rider's soft boots to the board at angled stances.

Styles

Freeride boards are designed for all-mountain and powder riding; freestyle boards are shorter and twin-tipped for terrain parks and tricks; alpine or race boards are narrow and stiff, ridden with hard boots in plates for carving and slalom racing. Splitboards separate into two ski-like halves for backcountry touring.

Competition

Snowboarding became a Winter Olympic sport at Nagano in 1998, with giant slalom and halfpipe events; slopestyle, big air, snowboard cross, and parallel slalom have since been added. The sport is governed internationally by the International Ski and Snowboard Federation (FIS), and the World Snowboard Tour and X Games feature its leading freestyle competitions. Notable riders include Shaun White, Terje Håkonsen, Chloe Kim, and Mark McMorris.
