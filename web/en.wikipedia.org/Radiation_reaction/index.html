Radiation reaction

An accelerated charge emits electromagnetic radiation and experiences a recoil, or radiation reaction, force.

Radiation reaction, or radiation damping, is the recoil force exerted on an accelerating charged particle by its own emitted electromagnetic radiation. A charged particle that accelerates emits radiation according to the Larmor formula, and the radiated energy and momentum must come from the particle's kinetic energy, so the emission acts back on the motion as a kind of friction.

The Abraham–Lorentz force

In classical electrodynamics the reaction force on a non-relativistic point charge is given by the Abraham–Lorentz force, proportional to the time derivative of the acceleration (the "jerk"): F = (q²/6πε₀c³)·ȧ. The resulting equation of motion, the Abraham–Lorentz equation, is notorious for pathological solutions, including runaway self-acceleration and pre-acceleration, in which a particle begins to move before a force is applied. The relativistic generalisation is the Abraham–Lorentz–Dirac force.

These difficulties are symptoms of treating the electron as a classical point particle; a fully satisfactory classical theory does not exist, and the Landau–Lifshitz reduction of order is commonly used as a practical approximation that avoids the pathologies. In quantum electrodynamics the problem is subsumed into renormalisation, and radiation reaction appears naturally in the quantum treatment of emission.

Importance

For ordinary accelerations the effect is minuscule, but it becomes significant where charges undergo extreme acceleration: in synchrotrons, where radiation damping shapes the equilibrium of stored particle beams; in astrophysical jets and pulsars; and in the interaction of relativistic electrons with ultra-intense laser pulses, where radiation reaction can measurably slow electrons and is now being probed experimentally as a test of strong-field QED.
