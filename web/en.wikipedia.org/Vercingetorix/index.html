Vercingetorix

Vercingetorix (c. 82 – 46 BC) was a Gallic king and chieftain of the Arverni tribe who united the Gauls in a failed revolt against Roman forces during the last phase of Julius Caesar's Gallic Wars. Despite being divided and outnumbered, he is regarded as the first hero of French national history.

Revolt

In 52 BC Vercingetorix was proclaimed king of the Arverni and built a coalition of Gallic tribes. He adopted a scorched-earth strategy, destroying supplies and settlements to starve Caesar's legions, and defeated the Romans at the Battle of Gergovia. However, after a cavalry defeat he withdrew to the fortified oppidum of Alesia. Caesar besieged the town with extensive double fortifications (Siege of Alesia), defeating both the garrison and a huge Gallic relief army.

Capture and death

Vercingetorix surrendered — legend says he rode out and laid his arms at Caesar's feet — and was taken to Rome, where he was held prisoner for five years. In 46 BC he was paraded in Caesar's triumph and then executed, probably by strangulation.

Legacy

Under Napoleon III, who identified with Caesar yet celebrated Gallic resistance, a colossal statue of Vercingetorix was erected at Alise-Sainte-Reine, the presumed site of Alesia. Vercingetorix remains a central figure of French national mythology and a popular character in the Asterix comics.
