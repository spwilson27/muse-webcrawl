Dutch Sandwich

A Dutch Sandwich is a base erosion and profit shifting (BEPS) corporate tax avoidance strategy used by some multinational corporations to lower their corporate tax worldwide. The strategy makes use of the interaction of the tax rules of Ireland and the Netherlands and certain other features, typically in combination with the "Double Irish" arrangement, to route royalties and other income to tax havens such as Bermuda.

Mechanism

In the classic structure, an Irish company pays royalties to a Dutch company, which in turn pays them to a second Irish company that is tax-resident in a zero-tax jurisdiction. Because Ireland historically did not levy withholding tax on royalties paid to EU companies, and the Netherlands did not withhold on outbound royalty payments, the income could pass through largely untaxed — the Dutch entity forming the "sandwich" filling between the two Irish "slices".

Reform

Ireland closed the Double Irish arrangement to new entrants in 2015, with existing structures grandfathered until 2020. The Netherlands introduced a conditional withholding tax on royalties to low-tax jurisdictions in 2021, and international reforms under the OECD's BEPS project and the global minimum tax have further reduced the effectiveness of such structures.
