Monterrey is the capital and largest city of the northeastern Mexican state of Nuevo León, and the country's third-largest metropolitan area. Set in a valley ringed by mountains — most famously the saddle-shaped Cerro de la Silla — Monterrey is one of Mexico's principal industrial and business centres, home to major steel, cement, glass, brewing and technology firms.

Monterrey and the Cerro de la Silla, its emblematic mountain.

History

After two failed attempts, Diego de Montemayor founded the city in 1596 as Nuestra Señora de Monterrey, named for the Count of Monterrey, viceroy of New Spain. It remained a remote frontier town for centuries. During the Mexican–American War it was the scene of the Battle of Monterrey in 1846. Industrialisation began in earnest in the late 19th and early 20th centuries with the Fundidora steel works and breweries such as Cervecería Cuauhtémoc, transforming the city into Mexico's industrial capital.

Economy and culture

Monterrey hosts headquarters of companies including Cemex, Femsa and Banorte, and its per-capita income is among the highest in Latin America. The Macroplaza, one of the world's largest city squares, anchors the centre alongside the Museum of Contemporary Art (MARCO) and the Barrio Antiguo entertainment district. The Paseo Santa Lucía, an artificial canal, links the Macroplaza to Fundidora Park.

Climate

The climate is semi-arid and can be extreme: summer temperatures frequently exceed 40 °C, while winter cold fronts occasionally bring freezing weather. The city is vulnerable to flooding from tropical storms in the normally dry Santa Catarina River.
