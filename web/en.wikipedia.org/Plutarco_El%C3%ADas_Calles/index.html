Plutarco Elías Calles

40th President of Mexico
In office: 1 December 1924 – 30 November 1928
Preceded by: Álvaro Obregón
Succeeded by: Emilio Portes Gil
Born: 25 September 1877, Guaymas, Sonora
Died: 19 October 1945, Mexico City

Plutarco Elías Calles (born Francisco Plutarco Elías Campuzano; 25 September 1877 – 19 October 1945) was a Mexican politician and military leader who served as President of Mexico from 1924 to 1928. After his term he remained the dominant figure in Mexican politics during the period known as the Maximato (1928–1934), ruling through puppet presidents as Jefe Máximo de la Revolución.

Early life and revolutionary career

Calles was born in Guaymas, Sonora, worked as a schoolteacher (taking the surname Calles from his uncle, who raised him), and joined the Mexican Revolution under Venustiano Carranza. He became governor of Sonora, where his anti-clericalism and defense of the border against Pancho Villa's forces made his reputation, and he served in the cabinet of Álvaro Obregón.

Presidency

As president, Calles pursued agrarian reform, infrastructure building (roads, irrigation, the Bank of Mexico), and strict enforcement of the anti-clerical provisions of the Constitution of 1917. The enforcement of the so-called Calles Law against the Catholic Church provoked the Cristero War (1926–1929), a major armed rebellion in west-central Mexico.

Maximato and exile

After Obregón's assassination in 1928, Calles founded the National Revolutionary Party (PNR), the ancestor of the PRI, and controlled successive presidents. President Lázaro Cárdenas broke with him in 1935 and had him exiled to the United States in 1936; Calles was allowed to return in 1941 and died in Mexico City in 1945.
