Weighing

A balance compares an unknown mass against known reference masses.

Weighing is the process of determining the weight — and, in practice, the mass — of an object using a weighing scale or balance. Weighing is one of the oldest measurement activities: balance scales were used in ancient Egypt and Mesopotamia for trade in grain, metal, and other goods, and standardised masses were among the earliest legal standards.

Instruments

The traditional instrument is the balance, an equal-arm beam that compares the object against calibrated reference masses. Spring scales measure the force of gravity on an object via the extension of a spring (Hooke's law). Most modern scales use strain-gauge load cells, which convert force into an electrical signal. Analytical balances used in laboratories can resolve masses to fractions of a milligram, while industrial weighbridges measure vehicles of tens of tonnes.

Mass versus weight

Strictly, a balance measures mass by comparison, while a spring or load-cell scale measures weight (force). Because commercial weighing takes place in Earth's gravity, scales are calibrated so that their readings give mass directly in units such as kilograms, grams, pounds, and ounces.

Applications

Weighing is used in commerce and trade (where it is legally regulated), medicine, cooking, shipping and logistics, and scientific measurement. Aviation, pharmaceuticals, and precious-metal trading all depend on accurate, verified weighing.
