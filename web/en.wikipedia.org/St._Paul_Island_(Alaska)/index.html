St. Paul Island (Alaska)

St. Paul Island (Aleut: Tangimax) is the largest of the Pribilof Islands, a group of four volcanic islands in the Bering Sea belonging to the U.S. state of Alaska. The island covers about 104 square kilometres and is home to the city of St. Paul, the largest community in the Pribilofs, with a population of several hundred, most of them Alaska Natives of Aleut descent.

History

Russian fur trader Gavriil Pribylov landed on the island on St. Peter and St. Paul's Day in 1788, giving it its name. The Russian-American Company transported Aleut families to work in the fur seal harvest, and after the 1867 Alaska Purchase the United States government administered the island's sealing industry for decades. Islanders were evacuated during the Second World War and interned at Funter Bay.

Geography and wildlife

The island is low and rolling, dotted with volcanic cinder cones and freshwater lakes. Its beaches support the world's largest breeding colony of northern fur seals, and the island is an internationally important bird area visited for rare vagrant species. Commercial fishing, including snow crab and halibut, is now the economic mainstay.
