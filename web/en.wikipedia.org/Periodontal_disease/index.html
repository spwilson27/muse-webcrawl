Periodontal disease

SpecialtyPeriodontology / dentistry
TypesGingivitis; periodontitis
Main causeDental plaque biofilm
Risk factorsSmoking, diabetes, poor oral hygiene, genetics

Periodontal disease, also known as gum disease, is a set of inflammatory conditions affecting the tissues surrounding the teeth — the gingiva (gums), periodontal ligament, cementum and alveolar bone. In its early stage, gingivitis, the gums become swollen and bleed easily; in its more serious form, periodontitis, the supporting tissues and bone are progressively destroyed, and the teeth may loosen and be lost.

Cause and progression

The primary cause is the accumulation of bacterial plaque biofilm along the gumline. In gingivitis the inflammation is confined to the gum and is fully reversible with plaque removal. In susceptible individuals the inflammation extends deeper: the gum detaches from the tooth forming pockets, and the host immune response drives resorption of the supporting bone. Periodontitis is now understood as a dysbiotic, host-mediated disease rather than a simple infection. Smoking is the strongest modifiable risk factor; diabetes worsens the disease, and periodontitis in turn is associated with poorer glycaemic control. Epidemiological studies also link periodontitis with cardiovascular disease and adverse pregnancy outcomes, though causation is debated.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis relies on probing pocket depths around each tooth, bleeding on probing, and radiographs showing bone levels. Treatment begins with professional scaling and root planing (deep cleaning) combined with daily plaque control by the patient. Advanced cases may require periodontal surgery, regenerative procedures or adjunctive antibiotics; regular maintenance visits are essential because the disease is chronic and relapsing.

Epidemiology

Periodontal disease is among the most common human diseases: mild forms affect the majority of adults, and severe periodontitis affects roughly a tenth of the global adult population. It is a leading cause of tooth loss in adults.
