Tea

Tea is an aromatic beverage prepared by pouring hot or boiling water over cured or fresh leaves of Camellia sinensis, an evergreen shrub native to East Asia. It is the most widely consumed drink in the world after water. Tea originated in southwestern China, probably as a medicinal drink, and was popularised during the Tang dynasty, spreading to other East Asian countries and, by the 17th century, to Europe through Portuguese and Dutch traders.

History

According to Chinese legend, the emperor Shennong discovered tea around 2737 BCE when leaves blew into his pot of boiling water. Tea drinking became an art form in China, codified in Lu Yu's Classic of Tea (8th century CE). Buddhist monks carried tea to Japan, where it developed into the tea ceremony. The British East India Company built a vast trade importing tea from China, and in the 19th century the British established tea plantations in India and Ceylon (Sri Lanka), breaking the Chinese monopoly. Tea played a political role in events such as the Boston Tea Party of 1773.

Types

All true teas come from Camellia sinensis and are categorised by processing: black tea is fully oxidised; green tea is heated to prevent oxidation; oolong is partially oxidised; white tea is minimally processed from young buds; and pu-erh is fermented and aged. Infusions of other plants, such as chamomile or rooibos, are called herbal teas or tisanes though they contain no tea leaf.

Cultivation and production

Tea plants are grown in tropical and subtropical climates with abundant rainfall, mainly in China, India, Kenya and Sri Lanka. Plucked leaves are withered, rolled, oxidised and dried. Tea contains caffeine, theanine and polyphenols such as catechins, which contribute to its flavour and reputed health effects.

Culture

Tea is central to social rituals in many cultures: the Chinese gongfu ceremony, the Japanese chanoyu, British afternoon tea, Moroccan mint tea, Indian masala chai and Turkish çay served in tulip glasses. Tea houses and tea customs mark hospitality and daily routine across Asia, the Middle East and Britain.
