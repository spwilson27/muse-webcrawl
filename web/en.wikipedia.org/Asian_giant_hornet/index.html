Asian giant hornet

KingdomAnimalia
OrderHymenoptera
FamilyVespidae
GenusVespa
SpeciesV. mandarinia

The Asian giant hornet (Vespa mandarinia) is the world's largest hornet, native to temperate and tropical East and South Asia. Queens can exceed 5 cm in body length with a wingspan of about 7.5 cm, and the species is notorious for its potent sting and its attacks on honey bee colonies.

Description and behaviour

The hornet has a distinctive orange head and mandibles, a dark thorax and an abdomen banded in orange and brown-black. It nests mainly underground in pre-existing cavities. Workers prey on other insects, tree sap and fruit; scouts mark honey bee hives with pheromones, after which groups of hornets can destroy a colony in a "slaughter phase", decapitating thousands of bees to carry away their larvae as food. Japanese honey bees defend themselves by forming a vibrating "bee ball" that cooks the hornet with heat and carbon dioxide.

Sting and human health

The sting delivers a large dose of venom containing mastoparan and other toxins, and can cause severe pain, allergic reactions and, in dozens of cases each year in Japan and China, death, usually from anaphylaxis or organ failure after multiple stings.

In North America

The species was detected in British Columbia and Washington State in 2019, prompting eradication efforts over fears for honey bee populations; after several years without detections, officials announced in 2024 that it had been eradicated from the United States.
