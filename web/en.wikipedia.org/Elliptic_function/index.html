Elliptic function

In complex analysis, an elliptic function is a meromorphic function on the complex plane that is periodic in two directions, with two periods whose ratio is not real. Elliptic functions are among the most important special functions of mathematics; they arose historically from the study of elliptic integrals, such as the integral giving the arc length of an ellipse.

Properties

An elliptic function repeats on a lattice of period parallelograms. By Liouville's theorem, a holomorphic doubly periodic function must be constant, so every elliptic function has poles. Within one period parallelogram the number of poles (counted with multiplicity) is called the order of the function; there are no elliptic functions of order one, and the sum of residues in a cell is zero. The field of elliptic functions for a given lattice is generated by the Weierstrass ℘-function and its derivative.

Principal examples

The Weierstrass elliptic functions ℘(z) satisfy a differential equation relating them to the equation of an elliptic curve, and provide a parametrisation of complex tori as algebraic curves. The Jacobi elliptic functions sn, cn and dn generalise the trigonometric functions and are defined as inverses of elliptic integrals. Carl Gustav Jacobi and Niels Henrik Abel developed the theory in the 1820s.

Applications

Elliptic functions appear in the exact solution of the pendulum, in number theory through their connection with elliptic curves and modular forms, and in conformal mapping and mathematical physics.
