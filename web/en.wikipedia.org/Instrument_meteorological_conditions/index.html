Instrument meteorological conditions

Instrument meteorological conditions (IMC) are aviation weather conditions in which pilots cannot rely on outside visual references and must fly primarily by reference to flight instruments. IMC is defined as meteorological conditions expressed in terms of visibility, distance from cloud, and ceiling that are less than the minima specified for visual meteorological conditions (VMC). Flight in IMC requires an instrument-rated pilot, an appropriately equipped aircraft, and compliance with instrument flight rules (IFR), including weather minima for takeoff, en-route flight and instrument approaches.

Definition

The exact thresholds depend on airspace class and jurisdiction, but typically conditions are IMC when flight visibility falls below 5 km (or 3 statute miles in the United States) or the cloud ceiling is below 1,000–1,500 ft, or the aircraft cannot maintain the prescribed distance from cloud (commonly 1,000 ft vertically and 1,500 m/1 statute mile horizontally). Typical causes of IMC are flight inside cloud, fog, heavy precipitation, smoke, dust and darkness over featureless terrain or water (sometimes called "actual IMC" versus merely legal marginal conditions).

Risks

Without a visible horizon, human balance organs give dangerously misleading sensations (the vestibular illusions — the leans, graveyard spiral and somatogravic illusion among them). Spatial disorientation in IMC is a leading cause of fatal accidents among non-instrument-rated pilots who fly inadvertently into cloud ("VFR into IMC" accidents have a very high fatality rate, with the untrained pilot's survival time in cloud often quoted at under three minutes). Aircraft icing, turbulence and embedded thunderstorms are additional hazards commonly associated with IMC. Mitigations include instrument training and currency requirements, attitude instruments and autopilots, weather radar and datalink weather, and regulatory equipment requirements for IFR flight.

The term is also used in a training sense: pilots practise "simulated IMC" using view-limiting devices (hoods, foggles) under the supervision of an instructor in visual conditions.
