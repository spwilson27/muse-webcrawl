Major-General John Dutton Frost (31 December 1912 – 21 May 1993) was a British airborne officer of the Second World War, best known as the commander of the small force that held the northern end of Arnhem bridge during Operation Market Garden in September 1944. The bridge was renamed the John Frost Bridge in his honour, and he was portrayed by Anthony Hopkins in the film A Bridge Too Far.

Early service

Frost was commissioned into the Cameronians in 1932 and served in Iraq before volunteering for the new Parachute Regiment. In February 1942 he led C Company of the 2nd Parachute Battalion in the Bruneval Raid, a daring operation that seized components of a German Würzburg radar from the French coast and evacuated by sea. He later jumped in Tunisia, Sicily and Italy.

Arnhem

On 17 September 1944 Frost's battalion seized the northern ramp of the Arnhem road bridge and held it against repeated attacks by the II SS Panzer Corps for three days, far longer than planned, before the survivors were overwhelmed. Frost, wounded, was captured and spent the rest of the war as a prisoner. The stand of his battalion became one of the most famous episodes of the battle.

Post-war career

Frost remained in the army after the war, commanding the 52nd (Lowland) Infantry Division and reaching the rank of major-general before retiring in 1968. He became a farmer in West Sussex and published the memoir A Drop Too Many in 1980.
