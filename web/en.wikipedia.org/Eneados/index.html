Eneados

The Eneados is a translation of Virgil's Aeneid into Middle Scots, completed by the poet and cleric Gavin Douglas in 1513. It is widely regarded as the first complete translation of the Aeneid into any Anglic language, and as one of the greatest achievements of Scots literature in the period of the Northern Renaissance.

Background

Gavin Douglas (c. 1474–1522), later Bishop of Dunkeld, was the third son of Archibald Douglas, 5th Earl of Angus. The translation was undertaken at the request of Henry Sinclair, 4th Lord Sinclair, and was completed on 22 July 1513, only weeks before the disastrous Battle of Flodden, at which Douglas's patron circle suffered heavily.

Contents and character

The work comprises the twelve books of the Aeneid, each prefaced by an original prologue in verse — the prologues are celebrated in their own right for their reflections on poetry, translation, season and nature. Douglas also translated the thirteenth book, the Supplementum of Maffeo Vegio. He criticised the earlier English version by William Caxton (the Eneydos, 1490), which had been translated from a French prose romance rather than from Virgil's Latin.

Language and influence

Douglas wrote in a rich, aureate Middle Scots, and his prefatory material includes some of the earliest uses of the word "Scots" for the language. The Eneados circulated in manuscript and was printed in London in 1553. Ezra Pound later praised it, remarking that Douglas "gets more of Virgil into his translation than any one else has.
