Ian Stevenson

Born: 31 October 1918, Montreal, Canada
Died: 8 February 2007, Charlottesville, Virginia
Field: Psychiatry, parapsychology
Institution: University of Virginia

Ian Pretyman Stevenson (1918–2007) was a Canadian-born American psychiatrist who worked for the University of Virginia School of Medicine for fifty years and became known for his research into alleged cases of reincarnation, particularly children's claims to remember previous lives.

Career and research

Stevenson was chairman of Virginia's Department of Psychiatry from 1957 to 1967, then founded the Division of Perceptual Studies, endowed in part by Chester Carlson, inventor of xerography. He travelled extensively in India, Sri Lanka, Burma, Lebanon, and elsewhere, documenting some 3,000 cases in which young children described details of a deceased person's life, sometimes with birthmarks said to correspond to fatal wounds. His major works include Twenty Cases Suggestive of Reincarnation (1966) and the monograph Reincarnation and Biology (1997).

Reception

Mainstream science largely rejected or ignored his conclusions, attributing cases to cultural influence, cryptomnesia, and selective reporting, while noting Stevenson's careful documentation and his own acknowledgement that the evidence was suggestive rather than conclusive. Colleagues such as Jim Tucker have continued the research programme.
