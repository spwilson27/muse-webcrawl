Display resolution

Common display resolutions compared by pixel dimensions

The display resolution or display modes of a digital television, computer monitor, or other display device is the number of distinct pixels in each dimension that can be displayed. It is usually quoted as width × height, with the units in pixels; for example, 1024 × 768 means the width is 1024 pixels and the height is 768 pixels.

Terminology

The term “display resolution” is usually used to mean pixel dimensions, the maximum number of pixels in each dimension, especially for fixed-pixel displays such as LCDs. For cathode-ray tube displays, resolution could vary with settings. Pixel density, measured in pixels per inch (PPI), additionally depends on the physical size of the screen.

Common resolutions

Standard designations include HD (1280 × 720), Full HD (1920 × 1080), QHD (2560 × 1440), 4K UHD (3840 × 2160), and 8K UHD (7680 × 4320). Earlier computer standards included VGA (640 × 480), SVGA (800 × 600), and XGA (1024 × 768). Aspect ratios have shifted over time from 4:3 toward 16:9 and wider formats.

Considerations

Higher resolutions provide sharper images but demand more processing power and bandwidth. The perceived benefit depends on screen size and viewing distance; beyond a certain pixel density, the human eye cannot distinguish additional detail.
