Roman Gaul

Roman Gaul refers to Gaul — roughly modern France, Belgium, Luxembourg and parts of neighbouring countries — under Roman rule from the 1st century BC until the fall of the Western Roman Empire in the 5th century AD. Rome's involvement began with the annexation of the south (Gallia Narbonensis, 121 BC), and Julius Caesar conquered the remainder in the Gallic Wars of 58–50 BC.

Imperial organisation

Augustus reorganised Gaul into provinces including Gallia Lugdunensis, Aquitania and Belgica, with Lugdunum (Lyon) as a major imperial centre. Over the following centuries Gaul became thoroughly Romanised: cities, roads, aqueducts, villas and Latin speech spread widely, and Gallic elites entered the Senate. In late antiquity the region suffered invasions and usurpations; after AD 476, power passed increasingly to Germanic kingdoms, above all the Franks, whose realm shaped medieval France.
