In chemistry, an alcohol is an organic compound that carries at least one hydroxyl functional group (–OH) bound to a saturated carbon atom. Alcohols range from simple molecules such as methanol and ethanol to complex sugars and sterols. In everyday language, "alcohol" almost always means ethanol, the psychoactive ingredient of alcoholic drinks.

Classification

Alcohols are classified as primary, secondary or tertiary according to how many other carbon atoms are attached to the carbon bearing the –OH group. They may also be described by the number of hydroxyl groups: mono-, di- (glycols) and trihydric alcohols such as glycerol. Sugars, bearing many hydroxyl groups, are polyols.

Properties and production

Lower alcohols are colourless liquids miscible with water, because the hydroxyl group can form hydrogen bonds. Ethanol is produced industrially by hydration of ethylene and on a vast scale by fermentation of sugars with yeast. Methanol, once distilled from wood, is now made from synthesis gas. Alcohols burn readily, can be oxidised to aldehydes, ketones and carboxylic acids, and react with carboxylic acids to form esters.

Uses

Alcohols serve as fuels and fuel additives (ethanol, methanol), solvents, antiseptics and disinfectants (ethanol, isopropanol), antifreezes (ethylene glycol), and as the basis of countless industrial chemicals, cosmetics and pharmaceuticals. Ethanol's use in beverages dates back at least to the Neolithic period; beer and wine residues have been identified in vessels from China and the Near East more than 8,000 years old.

Health

Ethanol consumption affects the central nervous system and is linked, in excess, to liver disease, addiction (alcohol use disorder) and many cancers. Other alcohols are far more toxic: methanol poisoning causes blindness and death, and ethylene glycol damages the kidneys.
