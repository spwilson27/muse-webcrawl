Cantopop (Cantonese popular music) is a genre of popular music sung in Cantonese, centred on the Hong Kong music industry. The term, first used in the late 1970s as a counterpart to "Mandopop," came to prominence as Hong Kong television dramas and local songwriters created a distinctly local pop sound that displaced English-language and Mandarin repertoire in the territory.

A Cantopop concert at the Hong Kong Coliseum, the genre's emblematic venue.

Origins

Cantonese songs in a modern idiom drew on Cantonese opera, the Mandarin shidaiqu of Shanghai, and imported Western styles. In the early 1970s, singers such as Sam Hui brought colloquial Cantonese lyrics about everyday Hong Kong life into television theme songs, hugely expanding the audience. Joseph Koo and lyricists such as James Wong supplied themes for TVB dramas that reached households nightly.

Golden age

The 1980s and early 1990s were Cantopop's golden age, with superstars including Anita Mui, Leslie Cheung, Alan Tam, Danny Chan, and the "Four Heavenly Kings" of the 1990s — Jacky Cheung, Andy Lau, Aaron Kwok, and Leon Lai. Cantopop stars commonly crossed into film, and the industry exported music and celebrity culture across Southeast Asia and the Chinese diaspora. Anita Mui was styled the "Madonna of the East," and Leslie Cheung's career spanned music and acclaimed film work.

Decline and revival

From the late 1990s, piracy, the Asian financial crisis, and the rise of Mandopop in the growing mainland market eroded the industry. In the late 2010s and 2020s, a new generation — notably the boy band Mirror, formed through a 2018 television talent show — sparked a commercial revival of Cantopop among Hong Kong audiences.
