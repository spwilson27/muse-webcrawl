Metric prefix

A metric prefix is a unit prefix that precedes a basic unit of measure to indicate a multiple or submultiple of the unit, in powers of ten. All metric prefixes used today are standardised by the Bureau International des Poids et Mesures (BIPM) as part of the International System of Units (SI), ranging from quetta- (10³⁰) down to quecto- (10⁻³⁰).

History

The first prefixes (kilo, hecto, deca, deci, centi, milli) came with the French metric system of the 1790s. The CGPM added prefixes as science demanded: nano and pico in 1960, femto and atto in 1964, peta and exa in 1975, zetta/zepto and yotta/yocto in 1991, and ronna, quetta, ronto, and quecto in 2022 — the last partly to serve data science and express quantities such as Earth's mass (~one ronnagram).

Usage

Prefix symbols are case-sensitive (m = milli, M = mega) and are written without a space before the unit symbol (e.g., km). In computing, the same prefixes were long used for powers of 1024, a practice the IEC addressed with separate binary prefixes (kibi-, mebi-, gibi-).
