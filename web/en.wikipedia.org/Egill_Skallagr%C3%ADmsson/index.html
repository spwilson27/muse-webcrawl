Egill Skallagrímsson (c. 904 – c. 995) was a Viking Age Icelandic skald, warrior and farmer, and the protagonist of Egils saga, one of the most celebrated of the Icelandic family sagas. He is regarded as one of the greatest skalds, and some of his poetry survives.

Life in the saga

The saga portrays Egill as the ugly, violent, greedy yet deeply poetic son of Skalla-Grímr Kveldúlfsson, a settler at Borg in western Iceland. Egill composed his first verse at the age of three, killed a man at seven, and spent much of his life in feuds and travels in Norway, England and elsewhere, clashing repeatedly with King Eiríkr Bloodaxe. Captured at York, the saga says he saved his life by composing the praise-poem Höfuðlausn ("Head-Ransom") overnight.

Poetry

Surviving works attributed to Egill include Höfuðlausn, Sonatorrek — a raw lament for his drowned son Böðvarr, considered among the finest Old Norse poems — and Arinbjarnarkviða. He lived to a great age at Mosfell with his niece.
