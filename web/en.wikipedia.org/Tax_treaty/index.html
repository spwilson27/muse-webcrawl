Tax treaty

A tax treaty, also called a double tax agreement (DTA) or double tax avoidance agreement, is an agreement between two countries that allocates taxing rights over cross-border income in order to avoid or mitigate double taxation, and typically provides for cooperation against tax evasion.

Provisions

Most treaties follow the OECD Model Tax Convention or the UN Model Convention. They define residence tie-breakers, set maximum withholding tax rates on dividends, interest and royalties, exempt or limit tax on business profits without a permanent establishment, and provide mutual agreement procedures for resolving disputes. The OECD's Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) project and the 2017 Multilateral Instrument amended thousands of treaties to combat treaty abuse.
