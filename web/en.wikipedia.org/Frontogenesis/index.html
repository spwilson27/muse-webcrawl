Frontogenesis

Frontogenesis
FieldMeteorology
DefinitionTightening of horizontal temperature gradients to form a front
OppositeFrontolysis

Frontogenesis is a meteorological process in which the horizontal temperature gradient in a region of the atmosphere tightens, eventually forming or intensifying a weather front. The term is also used for the formation of fronts in the ocean. Its opposite, the weakening of a front, is frontolysis.

Mechanism

Frontogenesis occurs when the wind field acts to compress isotherms together — through confluence, deformation, shear, or tilting of vertical temperature gradients into the horizontal. As the gradient sharpens, the thermal wind adjusts and a secondary ageostrophic circulation develops, with rising air on the warm side of the developing front, often producing cloud and precipitation. The process is described mathematically by the frontogenesis function, developed from work by Petterssen in the 1930s.

Importance

Frontogenesis is a key mechanism in the development of mid-latitude cyclones and the concentration of rainfall and snowfall into narrow bands. Forecasters analyse frontogenesis at various levels of the troposphere to locate zones of enhanced lift and severe weather.
