An enchilada (Spanish: [entʃiˈlaða]) is a Mexican dish consisting of a corn tortilla rolled around a filling and covered with a savoury sauce. The word derives from the Spanish verb enchilar, "to season with chile". Fillings include meats, cheese, beans, potatoes, vegetables, or combinations of these. Sauces can be chile-based (enchiladas rojas), tomatillo-based (enchiladas verdes), mole, bean-based (enfrijoladas), or tomato-based (entomatadas).

History

Rolling tortillas around food dates to pre-Hispanic times; the Maya and Aztecs ate tortillas wrapped around small fish and other fillings. The first written mention of enchiladas appears in the 1831 cookbook El Cocinero Mexicano. In the traditional preparation, plain tortillas are dipped in chile sauce and eaten without filling — the elaborate filled and baked forms developed later, including the Tex-Mex versions common in the United States.

Varieties

  • Enchiladas rojas — with a red chile sauce of dried chiles such as ancho or guajillo.
  • Enchiladas verdes — with a green sauce of tomatillos and green chiles.
  • Enchiladas suizas — "Swiss-style", topped with a creamy dairy sauce and cheese.
  • Enchiladas de mole — covered with mole poblano, typical of Puebla.
  • Enmoladas, enfrijoladas, entomatadas — sauced with mole, beans, or tomato respectively.
  • Enchiladas mineras — "miners' enchiladas" from Guanajuato, topped with vegetables and cheese.
