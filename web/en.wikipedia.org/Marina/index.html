Marina

A marina (from Spanish [maˈɾina], from marino, "of the sea") is a dock or basin with moorings and supplies for yachts and small boats. A marina differs from a port in that it does not handle large passenger ships or cargo from freighters. Marinas may be operated by private clubs, commercial companies or municipalities, and are found on coasts, lakes and navigable rivers.

Facilities

Marinas provide berths or slips, usually with access to fresh water and electricity, plus fuel docks, pump-out stations for sewage, boat ramps and hoists, dry storage, repair yards, showers and laundry for crews, and chandlery and provisioning shops. Many also have restaurants, hotels and residential or leisure developments attached — so-called "marina developments" that grew rapidly in coastal regions from the 1960s onward. Mooring may be by piles, floating pontoons, or Mediterranean-style stern-to berthing.

Economics and environment

Berths are rented by the night for visiting boats or by season or year; liveaboard berths, where the boat serves as a home, are a valued minority. Marinas are significant drivers of local tourism economies but raise environmental concerns about dredging, fuel and antifouling pollution, and habitat disturbance, leading to "clean marina" certification schemes in many countries.
