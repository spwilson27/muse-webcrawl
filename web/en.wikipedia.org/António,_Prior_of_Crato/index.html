António, Prior of Crato (1531 – 26 August 1595), sometimes called The Determined, The Fighter or The Independentist, was a Portuguese royal who claimed the throne of Portugal during the succession crisis of 1580. The grandson of King Manuel I, he was the illegitimate son of Infante Luís, Duke of Beja, and held the office of Prior of Crato, head of the Portuguese branch of the Order of St John.

Claim to the throne

When the elderly Cardinal-King Henry died in January 1580 without an heir, António pressed his claim against Philip II of Spain and the Duchess of Braganza. Proclaimed king by popular acclamation at Santarém in July 1580, he was defeated by the Duke of Alba's Spanish army at the Battle of Alcântara the following month, and Philip took the Portuguese crown, beginning the Iberian Union.

Exile and later campaigns

António fled abroad and sought support in France and England. With French help he held out in the Azores until Spanish victory at the Battle of Ponta Delgada in 1582, and he accompanied the English Armada of 1589, which unsuccessfully attempted to restore him. He died in exile in Paris in 1595, having spent his last years pressing his claim at foreign courts.
