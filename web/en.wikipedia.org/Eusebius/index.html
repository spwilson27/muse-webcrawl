Eusebius

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Eusebius of Caesarea (c. 260/265 – 30 May 339), also known as Eusebius Pamphilus, was a Greek historian of Christianity, exegete, and Christian polemicist. He became bishop of Caesarea Maritima in Roman Palestine about 314 and is often called the "Father of Church History" because of his work in recording the history of the early Christian Church.

Life

Eusebius was born probably in Caesarea and educated there under the presbyter Pamphilus, a disciple of Origen, whose immense library Eusebius inherited and used. He witnessed the Diocletianic Persecution in Palestine, visited Egypt during it, and was imprisoned briefly. After becoming bishop he rose to prominence at the court of Constantine the Great, delivering panegyrics of the emperor and becoming one of his theological advisers. At the First Council of Nicaea (325) he presented a baptismal creed whose wording influenced the Nicene Creed.

Works

His Ecclesiastical History (Historia Ecclesiastica), in ten books, traces the church from the apostles to Constantine's victory in 324, preserving extensive quotations from otherwise lost early Christian writings. His Chronicle attempted a comparative chronology of ancient kingdoms and survives through Jerome's Latin version and an Armenian translation. The Onomasticon is a gazetteer of biblical place names; the Preparation for the Gospel and Proof of the Gospel are major apologetic works; and his Life of Constantine is a key, if laudatory, source for the first Christian emperor. He also devised the Eusebian canons, cross-reference tables for the Gospels still printed in many Bibles.

Legacy

Eusebius's historical method and his preservation of earlier sources shaped all later church historiography, continued by Socrates Scholasticus, Sozomen, and Theodoret. His closeness to Arius and to imperial power drew later criticism, and he was posthumously associated with the Arian controversy despite his signature at Nicaea.
