Louisiana Tech University

Louisiana Tech University (Louisiana Tech, La. Tech, or LTU) is a public research university in Ruston, Louisiana, United States. It is part of the University of Louisiana System and is classified among "R2: Doctoral Universities – High research activity". The university is particularly noted for its engineering, computer science, and business programs, and for the Lady Techsters women's basketball program.

History

The school was founded in 1894 as the Industrial Institute and College of Louisiana, opening to students in 1895. It became Louisiana Polytechnic Institute in 1921 ("Louisiana Tech" long being its informal name) and formally adopted the name Louisiana Tech University in 1970. The institution was one of the first in its region to emphasise engineering and technology alongside teacher training.

Academics and athletics

Louisiana Tech is organised into five colleges: Applied and Natural Sciences, Business, Education and Human Sciences, Engineering and Science, and Liberal Arts. Its integrated engineering curriculum and nanosystems engineering program have drawn national attention. In sport, the Bulldogs and Lady Techsters compete in Conference USA; the women's basketball team won national championships in 1981, 1982, and 1988, and the university counts NFL Hall of Fame quarterback Terry Bradshaw among its alumni.
