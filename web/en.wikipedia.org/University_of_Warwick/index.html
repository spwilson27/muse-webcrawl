The University of Warwick is a public research university on the outskirts of Coventry between the West Midlands and Warwickshire, England. The university was founded in 1965 as part of a government initiative to expand higher education. It is a member of the Russell Group of research-intensive British universities.

History

The establishment of the university was given approval by the government in 1961 and the first students were admitted in 1964, a year before the Royal Charter was granted in 1965. The institution quickly developed close links with industry under its founding vice-chancellor, Jack Butterworth. Warwick Business School, founded in 1967, and Warwick Manufacturing Group (WMG), founded in 1980 by Kumar Bhattacharyya, became nationally prominent.

Campus

The main campus occupies 290 hectares on the boundary between Coventry and Warwickshire, about 5 km south-west of Coventry city centre. It is divided into central, east and west campuses, with lakes, woodland and sculpture including the Warwick Koan. Warwick Arts Centre, opened in 1974, is one of the largest performing arts venues in the United Kingdom outside London. The university also has a campus in Wellesbourne and a base at The Shard in London.

Academics

Warwick is organised into the faculties of Arts, Science, Engineering and Medicine, and Social Sciences. It is consistently ranked among the top ten universities in the United Kingdom and is especially well regarded for mathematics, economics, business and management, and computer science. The university's economics and mathematics departments are among the strongest in the country.

Student life

The Students' Union is one of the largest in the UK, operating venues on campus and representing more than 250 societies and sports clubs. Alumni include the actress Ruth Jones, the author Daisy Goodwin, and the former governor of the Bank of England's contemporaries in public life; graduates are prominent in business, politics and the media.
