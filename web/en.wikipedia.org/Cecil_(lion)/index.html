Cecil (lion)

Cecil (c. 2002 – 1 July 2015) was a male African lion who lived primarily in Hwange National Park in Matabeleland North, Zimbabwe. He was being studied and tracked by a research team of the University of Oxford as part of a long-running study. Cecil was recognizable by his black-fringed mane and by the GPS collar fitted by the researchers.

Killing

On the night of 1 July 2015, Cecil was lured out of the protected area and wounded with an arrow by Walter Palmer, an American recreational big-game hunter from Minnesota, who had paid a reported US$50,000 for a permit to hunt a lion. Cecil was tracked and killed the following day. Palmer's guides included professional hunter Theo Bronkhorst; the hunt's legality was disputed, and Zimbabwean authorities charged the guides, though charges were later dropped.

Reaction

The killing drew intense international media coverage and an outpouring of public anger, one of the largest reactions to a wildlife story in history. Donations to the Oxford research unit surged, airlines banned the shipment of hunting trophies, and several countries tightened rules on importing lion trophies. The incident focused global attention on trophy hunting and lion conservation; researchers also noted a later "Cecil effect" of increased scrutiny of hunting near protected areas.

Aftermath

One of Cecil's sons, Xanda, was also legally trophy-hunted in 2017. In 2024 Zimbabwe and neighbouring countries continued to debate hunting quotas and community benefits from wildlife tourism.
