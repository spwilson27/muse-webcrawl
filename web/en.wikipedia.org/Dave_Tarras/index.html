Dave Tarras

Dave Tarras (1897 – February 13, 1989) was one of the most famous klezmer clarinetists of the twentieth century. Born Dovid Tarraschuk in Ternivka, Ukraine, he immigrated to New York City in 1915 and became the pre-eminent American klezmer musician of his generation, known for his elegant, virtuosic playing and his influence on the sound of Jewish wedding music in the United States.

Career

Tarras came from a family of klezmorim and played several instruments before settling on the clarinet. In New York he worked in silent-movie orchestras, Yiddish theater, and radio, and recorded prolifically from the 1920s onward, both under his own name and as an accompanist to Yiddish singers. Unlike his contemporary Naftule Brandwein, whose style was flamboyant and rough-edged, Tarras favored a smooth, refined tone shaped by his conservatory-style technique.

He was a mainstay of the "Borscht Belt" resort circuit and of Jewish weddings and bar mitzvahs for decades. His 1941 recording Tanz!, made with the Abe Ellstein Orchestra, blended klezmer with big-band swing.

Revival and legacy

When klezmer enjoyed a revival in the 1970s and 1980s, younger musicians such as Andy Statman sought Tarras out as a mentor; their 1978 collaboration Jewish Klezmer Music introduced him to a new audience. Tarras is remembered as the dean of American klezmer, whose recordings remain central documents of the tradition.
