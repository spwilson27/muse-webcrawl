Bakhtiyar Khilji

Muhammad Bakhtiyar Khilji (died 1206), also known as Ikhtiyar al-Din Muhammad Bakhtiyar Khilji, was a Turko-Afghan military general of the Ghurid Empire who led the Muslim conquest of Bengal and Bihar in the early 13th century. He is credited with establishing Muslim rule in Bengal.

Conquests

Serving under Qutb ud-Din Aibak, Bakhtiyar advanced into Bihar around 1200, capturing strongholds including Odantapuri, and then seized the Sena kingdom's capital at Nadia in Bengal in about 1204 with a famously small cavalry force. His campaigns are associated in later accounts with the destruction of the great Buddhist monasteries of Nalanda and Vikramashila.

Death

In 1206 Bakhtiyar led a disastrous expedition toward Tibet through the Brahmaputra valley; he died at Devkot on the retreat, either of illness or assassination.
