Kassel

Kassel is a city in northern Hesse, Germany, on the Fulda River, with a population of roughly 200,000. It is known for the Bergpark Wilhelmshöhe, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and for documenta, the major exhibition of contemporary art held every five years.

History

Kassel was first mentioned in 913 and grew as a residence of the Landgraves of Hesse-Kassel. In the 18th century Landgrave Charles and his successors built palaces, museums and the vast hillside park of Wilhelmshöhe with its waterworks and Hercules monument. The Brothers Grimm lived in Kassel for some thirty years, working as librarians while collecting their famous fairy tales. In 1807 the city became the capital of the Napoleonic Kingdom of Westphalia under Jérôme Bonaparte. Heavy bombing in October 1943 destroyed most of the old town, and Kassel was rebuilt largely in the style of the 1950s.

Culture

Since 1955 Kassel has hosted documenta, founded by Arnold Bode, which has grown into one of the world's most important exhibitions of modern and contemporary art, held roughly every five years. The city also preserves Grimm heritage in the Grimmwelt museum and is home to the University of Kassel, founded in 1971.

Economy and transport

Kassel is a centre of the rail and automotive industries: a major Volkswagen plant at nearby Baunatal, locomotive building traditions (Henschel), and the Kassel-Wilhelmshöhe station on the Hanover–Würzburg high-speed line make it a transport hub between Frankfurt and Hanover.
