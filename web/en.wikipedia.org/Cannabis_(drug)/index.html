Cannabis (drug)

Source plantCannabis sativa, Cannabis indica
Main active compoundTetrahydrocannabinol (THC)
FormsMarijuana, hashish, hash oil, edibles
AdministrationSmoking, vaporisation, oral
Legal statusVaries by jurisdiction

Cannabis, also known as marijuana and by numerous other names, is a preparation of the Cannabis plant intended for use as a psychoactive drug or medicine. The principal psychoactive constituent is tetrahydrocannabinol (THC); the plant contains more than a hundred other cannabinoids, including cannabidiol (CBD). Cannabis is most commonly smoked or vaporised, but is also consumed in foods ("edibles") and extracts such as hashish and hash oil.

Effects and health

Short-term effects include euphoria, relaxation, altered perception of time, increased appetite, and impaired short-term memory and coordination; high doses can provoke anxiety or transient psychotic symptoms. Regular heavy use is associated in some users with dependence (cannabis use disorder), bronchitis from smoking, and an increased risk of psychosis in susceptible individuals, particularly with adolescent use and high-potency products. Cannabis and cannabinoid medicines are used for conditions such as chemotherapy-induced nausea, chronic pain, and spasticity in multiple sclerosis, though the strength of evidence varies by indication.

History and legal status

Cannabis has been used for millennia in Central and South Asia for fibre, food, ritual, and medicine, and spread worldwide through trade. It was criminalised in most countries during the twentieth century and placed under the international drug control treaties, but policy has shifted in recent decades: Canada (2018) and Uruguay (2013) legalised recreational cannabis nationally, as have numerous United States states, while many other countries permit medical use or have decriminalised possession. Cannabis is among the most widely used psychoactive substances in the world, after alcohol, caffeine, and tobacco.
