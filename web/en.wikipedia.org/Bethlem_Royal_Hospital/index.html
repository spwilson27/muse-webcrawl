Bethlem Royal Hospital

Bethlem Royal Hospital, also known as St Mary Bethlehem, Bethlehem Hospital and Bedlam, is a psychiatric hospital in London. Founded in 1247 as a priory, it was one of the first institutions in Europe devoted to the care of people with mental illness, and its name gave the English language the word "bedlam", meaning uproar and confusion.

History

The hospital began at Bishopsgate in the City of London. It began admitting people with mental illness in the later Middle Ages and came under the control of the City of London in the 16th century. In 1676 it moved to a grand building at Moorfields designed by Robert Hooke, where public visiting of the patients — long a notorious spectacle — continued until 1770. The hospital moved again to St George's Fields, Southwark, in 1815, and finally in 1930 to Monks Orchard near Beckenham in south-east London, its present site.

Today

Bethlem is now part of the South London and Maudsley NHS Foundation Trust and provides specialist mental health services. Its archives and the Bethlem Museum of the Mind, opened in 2015, document the long history of mental health care; the museum holds artworks by former patients including Richard Dadd and Louis Wain.
