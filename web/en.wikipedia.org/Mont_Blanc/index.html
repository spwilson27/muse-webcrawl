Mont Blanc (French: Mont Blanc [mɔ̃ blɑ̃]; Italian: Monte Bianco, both meaning "white mountain") is the highest mountain in the Alps and Western Europe, rising 4,808.7 m (15,777 ft) above sea level, and the highest in Europe outside the Caucasus Mountains. It is ranked 11th in the world in topographic prominence.

The mountain stands between the regions of Aosta Valley, Italy, and Haute-Savoie, France. The location of the summit of Mont Blanc is on the watershed line between the valleys of Ferret and Veny in Italy and the valleys of Montjoie and Arve in France, on the border between the two countries. The Mont Blanc tunnel connects Chamonix, France to Courmayeur, Italy.

Climbing history

The first recorded ascent of Mont Blanc (then thought to be the highest mountain in Europe) was on 8 August 1786 by Jacques Balmat and the doctor Michel Paccard. This climb traditionally marks the start of modern mountaineering. The first woman to reach the summit was Marie Paradis in 1808.

Tourism

Nowadays, the summit is ascended by an average of 20,000 mountaineer-tourists each year. The mountain is also a major centre for skiing, hiking, and the Ultra-Trail du Mont-Blanc race. The Aiguille du Midi cable car carries visitors to 3,842 m.
