RMS Queen Mary

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


RMS Queen Mary in service

RMS Queen Mary is a retired British ocean liner that sailed primarily on the North Atlantic Ocean from 1936 to 1967 for the Cunard-White Star Line and was built by John Brown & Company in Clydebank, Scotland. Together with RMS Queen Elizabeth, she provided an express liner service between Southampton, Cherbourg and New York City.

Of 80,774 gross register tons, Queen Mary was one of the largest and fastest liners of her era, several times winning the Blue Riband for the fastest Atlantic crossing. During the Second World War she served as a troopship, carrying Allied soldiers — including a record 16,683 people on a single voyage in 1943, still the most ever carried aboard one vessel.

Construction and launch

Construction began in December 1930 but was suspended during the Great Depression; work resumed in 1934 after the British government provided a loan conditional on the merger of Cunard and White Star Line. The ship was launched on 26 September 1934 by Queen Mary, after whom she was named — a departure from the tradition that Cunard ships' names ended in "ia". Her maiden voyage began on 27 May 1936.

War service

Requisitioned in 1940, the liner was painted grey — earning the nickname "the Grey Ghost" — and converted to carry troops, principally between New York, Britain, Australia and the Middle East. Her speed made her effectively immune to U-boat attack, and she was a prized target: Adolf Hitler reportedly offered a bounty for her sinking. In October 1942 she accidentally rammed and sank her escort, the cruiser HMS Curacoa, with the loss of 337 men.

Post-war service and retirement

After refitting, Queen Mary resumed transatlantic service in 1947 alongside Queen Elizabeth, dominating the route until jet airliners eroded passenger numbers in the late 1950s. She made her final voyage in 1967, sailing to Long Beach, California, where she remains moored as a floating hotel, museum and event venue.
