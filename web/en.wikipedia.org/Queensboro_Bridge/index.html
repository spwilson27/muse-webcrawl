The Queensboro Bridge, officially the Ed Koch Queensboro Bridge since 2011, is a cantilever bridge over the East River in New York City. Completed in 1909, it connects Long Island City in Queens with Manhattan, passing through Roosevelt Island. It is also known as the 59th Street Bridge, a name popularized by the Simon & Garfunkel song "The 59th Street Bridge Song (Feelin' Groovy)".

Design and construction

Designed under Commissioner Gustav Lindenthal with architect Henry Hornbostel, the bridge is a double-decked cantilever structure with a total length of about 3,725 feet (1,135 m). It was originally built to carry elevated railway trains, streetcars, vehicles, and pedestrians. It opened to the public on June 18, 1909.

Configuration

Today the bridge carries ten lanes of traffic on two levels, along with a walkway and a bikeway on the northern side. It is a heavily used toll-free crossing operated by the New York City Department of Transportation.

Renaming and restoration

In 2011 the bridge was renamed in honour of former mayor Ed Koch. Major rehabilitation projects have addressed its roadway decks, cables, and steelwork.
