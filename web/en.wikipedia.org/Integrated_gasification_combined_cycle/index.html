Integrated gasification combined cycle

An integrated gasification combined cycle (IGCC) is a technology using a high-pressure gasifier to turn coal and other carbon-based fuels into pressurised gas — synthesis gas (syngas). The syngas is cleaned and burned in a gas turbine whose exhaust heat drives a steam turbine, giving the higher efficiency of a combined cycle while gas cleanup occurs before combustion.

Process

Coal is reacted with oxygen and steam at high temperature and pressure to produce syngas composed mainly of carbon monoxide and hydrogen. Particulates, sulfur compounds and other pollutants are removed from the concentrated, pressurised gas stream — a cheaper and more effective cleanup than scrubbing dilute flue gas. The configuration also makes pre-combustion carbon capture comparatively feasible.

Deployment

Demonstration and commercial IGCC plants have operated in the United States (such as Edwardsport and Kemper County, the latter converted to natural gas), Europe (Puertollano in Spain and Buggenum in the Netherlands) and Japan. High capital costs and competition from cheap natural gas have limited wider adoption.
