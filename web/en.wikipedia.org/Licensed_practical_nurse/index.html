Licensed practical nurse

A licensed practical nurse (LPN), known in some jurisdictions as a licensed vocational nurse (LVN) or registered practical nurse, is a nurse who provides basic nursing care under the direction of registered nurses and physicians. In the United States, LPNs are licensed by state boards of nursing after completing an approved practical nursing programme and passing the NCLEX-PN examination.

Role

LPNs take vital signs, administer many medications, collect samples, provide wound care and personal care, monitor patients and report changes to supervising staff. They work in hospitals, nursing homes, clinics, home health and physicians' offices. Their scope of practice is narrower than that of registered nurses and varies by state; some tasks, such as certain intravenous therapies and patient assessment responsibilities, may be restricted.

Education

Practical nursing programmes typically last about one year at community colleges or technical schools. Many LPNs later bridge to registered nursing through LPN-to-RN programmes.
