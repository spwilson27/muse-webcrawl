Twinjet
Engines: two jet engines
Examples: Boeing 777, Airbus A320, Boeing 737
Key regulation: ETOPS

A twinjet or twin-engine jet is a jet aircraft powered by two engines. It is the most common configuration for modern commercial airliners, business jets and many military aircraft, offering a balance of performance, efficiency and safety.

Design

Engines are usually mounted under the wings in pods, on the rear fuselage, or in one case (the McDonnell Douglas DC-10's trijet layout being separate) within the tail. A twinjet must be able to fly safely on one engine, so its engines are more powerful than a comparable four-engine aircraft's.

ETOPS

The relaxation of rules restricting twinjets from flying far from diversion airports — codified as ETOPS (Extended-range Twin-engine Operations) — allowed twins to dominate long-haul routes once reserved for trijets and quadjets, thanks to the reliability of modern turbofans.
