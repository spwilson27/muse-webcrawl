American wire gauge

American Wire Gauge (AWG) is a logarithmic stepped standardised wire gauge system used since 1857, predominantly in North America, for the diameters of round, solid, nonferrous, electrically conducting wire. Dimensions of the wires are given in ASTM standard B 258. It is also known as the Brown & Sharpe wire gauge, after the manufacturing firm that developed it.

Properties

The system is counterintuitive: as the gauge number increases, the wire diameter decreases. The ratio of successive diameters is constant (the 39th root of 92, approximately 1.12293), so each step of six gauge numbers approximately doubles or halves the diameter, and each step of three numbers doubles or halves the cross-sectional area—and hence halves or doubles the resistance of a given length. Sizes range from 4/0 (0000), the largest at 11.68 mm diameter, down through 40 AWG and finer.

Use

AWG sizes are specified throughout North American electrical codes and practice: typical examples are 14 AWG and 12 AWG copper conductors for household branch circuits, 10 AWG for electric dryers, and fine gauges such as 24 AWG for telephone and network cabling. Stranded wire is specified by the total cross-section of its strands, and AWG also applies to body jewellery and piercing sizes.
