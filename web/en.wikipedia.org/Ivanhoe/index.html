Ivanhoe is a historical novel by Sir Walter Scott, first published in 1819. Set in 12th-century England during the reign of Richard I, it follows the Saxon knight Wilfred of Ivanhoe and helped popularise interest in medieval romance and chivalry in the 19th century.

Plot

Disinherited by his father Cedric for his loyalty to the Norman king Richard, Ivanhoe returns secretly from the Crusades and competes, disguised, in a great tournament at Ashby-de-la-Zouch. The story interweaves the conflict between Saxons and Normans, the captivity of King Richard, the villainy of Prince John and the Templars, and the characters of Rebecca, a Jewish healer, and Robin Hood (as Locksley).

Influence

Ivanhoe was an enormous success and is credited with reviving popular interest in the Middle Ages. It also reshaped the legend of Robin Hood, establishing the familiar version of him as a heroic Saxon outlaw opposing Norman oppression. The novel has been adapted many times for film, television, and opera.
