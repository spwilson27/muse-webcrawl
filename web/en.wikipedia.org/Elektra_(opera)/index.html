Elektra (opera)

Composer: Richard Strauss

Librettist: Hugo von Hofmannsthal

Language: German

Premiere: 25 January 1909, Dresden

Elektra, Op. 58, is a one-act opera by Richard Strauss with a German libretto by Hugo von Hofmannsthal, adapted from his 1903 play of the same name. Premiered at the Dresden Court Opera on 25 January 1909, it is a cornerstone of operatic modernism, famous for its ferocious dissonance and intense psychological drama.

Plot

Elektra, daughter of the murdered king Agamemnon, lives in degradation at the court of Mycenae, consumed by the desire to avenge her father on his killers — her mother Klytämnestra and Klytämnestra's lover Aegisth. She fails to persuade her gentle sister Chrysothemis to join the deed. Haunted and guilt-ridden, Klytämnestra seeks Elektra's counsel; when false news arrives that Elektra's exiled brother Orest is dead, Elektra resolves to act alone. The stranger who brought the news reveals himself as Orest, enters the palace, and murders Klytämnestra and Aegisth. Elektra, ecstatic, dances herself to death.

Music

Strauss wrote for an enormous orchestra of over 100 players. The score pushes tonality to its limits, using polytonality and grinding dissonance to depict madness and violence, while motifs are associated with Elektra, Agamemnon, Orest and the act of revenge. The role of Elektra is among the most demanding in the soprano repertoire, requiring the singer to remain on stage, singing almost continuously, for the full 100 minutes.

Reception

Initially notorious for its "cacophony," Elektra quickly entered the international repertoire. It was Strauss and Hofmannsthal's first operatic collaboration; their next, Der Rosenkavalier (1911), moved deliberately toward a warmer, more lyrical style.
