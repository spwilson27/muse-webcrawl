Joseon

Capital: Hanseong (Seoul)
Official language: Korean; Classical Chinese (literary)
Government: Absolute monarchy
Established: 5 August 1392
Disestablished: 12 October 1897 (proclamation of the Korean Empire)
Religion: Neo-Confucianism (state ideology)

Joseon (Korean: 조선; also romanised as Chosŏn) was a Korean dynastic kingdom that lasted for approximately five centuries, from 1392 to 1897. It was founded by Yi Seong-gye (King Taejo) following the overthrow of the Goryeo dynasty, and was the last dynastic kingdom of Korea, succeeded briefly by the Korean Empire before Japanese annexation in 1910.

History

General Yi Seong-gye seized power in 1388 and founded the new dynasty in 1392, moving the capital to Hanseong (modern Seoul) and adopting Neo-Confucianism as the state ideology in place of Buddhism. Under King Sejong the Great (r. 1418–1450) the kingdom flourished: the Korean alphabet, Hangul, was promulgated in 1443–1446, and advances were made in science, printing, agriculture and law. The dynasty endured the devastating Japanese invasions of 1592–1598 (the Imjin War), repelled with the help of Ming China and Admiral Yi Sun-sin's naval victories, and Manchu invasions in 1627 and 1636.

Government and society

Joseon was a highly centralised Confucian monarchy governed through a bureaucracy staffed by the scholarly yangban aristocracy, recruited largely through the gwageo civil service examinations. Society was formally stratified into yangban, commoners and the hereditary lowborn. Court politics were marked by factional struggles among literati parties, and the meticulous court records — the Veritable Records (sillok) and the daily diaries of the Royal Secretariat — survive as some of the most complete dynastic archives in the world.

Later years

In the nineteenth century, weakening royal authority, peasant rebellions such as the Donghak uprising, and the competing pressures of China, Japan and the Western powers destabilised the kingdom. King Gojong proclaimed the Korean Empire in 1897, but Japan made Korea a protectorate in 1905 and annexed it in 1910, ending over five centuries of the Yi dynasty's rule. Joseon's art, ceramics (noted white porcelain), architecture and Confucian institutions left a deep imprint on modern Korean culture.
