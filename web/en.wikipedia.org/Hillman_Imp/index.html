Hillman Imp

Hillman Imp
Manufacturer: Rootes Group (later Chrysler UK)
Production: 1963–1976
Layout: rear-engine, rear-wheel drive
Engine: 875 cc all-alloy inline-four

The Hillman Imp is a small economy car manufactured under the Hillman marque by the Rootes Group (later Chrysler UK) from 1963 to 1976. It was Britain's first mass-produced car with an aluminium engine and the first with the engine in the rear, mounted in a compact monocoque body with a folding rear seat and opening rear window.

Development

The Imp was Rootes' answer to the BMC Mini, designed under engineers Mike Parkes and Tim Fry. To build it, Rootes constructed a new factory at Linwood in Scotland under government pressure to create employment there. Its 875 cc Coventry Climax-derived engine was canted at 45 degrees, and the car earned praise for its handling and performance in rallying and racing.

Reception and legacy

Early cars suffered reliability problems, and production difficulties at Linwood hurt the model; sales never matched the Mini's, though derivatives such as the Singer Chamois and Sunbeam Stiletto were also sold, and about 440,000 Imps were built. The Linwood plant's troubles became a symbol of British industrial-relations problems, and the factory closed in 1981.
