The World Trade Organization (WTO) is an intergovernmental organisation that regulates and facilitates international trade. Governments use it to negotiate trade agreements and settle trade disputes. It officially commenced operations on 1 January 1995 under the Marrakesh Agreement, replacing the General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade (GATT) that had been established in 1948.

Functions

The WTO administers the multilateral trading system built on principles of non-discrimination — most-favoured-nation treatment and national treatment — and progressive liberalisation through negotiated reductions in tariffs and other barriers. Its agreements cover goods (GATT), services (GATS) and intellectual property (TRIPS). A distinctive feature is its dispute settlement system, long described as the "crown jewel" of the organisation, though its Appellate Body has been unable to hear appeals since 2019 due to blocked appointments.

Structure and criticism

Decisions are made by consensus among all members at biennial Ministerial Conferences; the Secretariat in Geneva is headed by a Director-General. The WTO has been a focus of anti-globalisation protest, most famously at the 1999 Seattle ministerial, and faces criticism over development imbalances and stalled negotiating rounds such as the Doha Round, launched in 2001.
