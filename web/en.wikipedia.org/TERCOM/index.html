TERCOM

TERCOM (Terrain Contour Matching) is a navigation system used primarily on cruise missiles. It allows a missile to navigate at low altitude over long distances without GPS or external guidance by comparing the terrain below it with pre-loaded reference maps.

Principle

A radar altimeter measures the missile's height above the ground continuously along its flight path. The sequence of elevation readings forms a terrain profile, which the guidance computer correlates with a stored digital map of the planned route. When the measured profile matches a known position on the map, the system updates its estimate of the missile's location and corrects the inertial navigation system, which drifts over time.

Use

TERCOM was developed in the United States in the 1970s and first deployed on missiles such as the Tomahawk and the AGM-86 ALCM. Its accuracy depends on the distinctiveness of the terrain: it works best over land with varied relief and poorly over water, ice caps or deserts, where other techniques such as DSMAC (scene-matching) or satellite navigation take over. Maps were historically derived from satellite reconnaissance imagery.
