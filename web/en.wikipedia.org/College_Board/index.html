The College Board is an American nonprofit organization formed in 1900 as the College Entrance Examination Board (CEEB) to expand access to higher education. It develops and administers standardized tests and curricula used by K–12 and post-secondary institutions in the United States and internationally. The College Board's membership includes more than 6,000 schools, colleges, universities, and other educational organizations.

Programs

The College Board is best known for the SAT, a college admissions test first administered in 1926, and for the Advanced Placement (AP) Program, launched in 1955, which offers college-level courses and examinations to high school students. Other programs include the PSAT/NMSQT, the CLEP program for college credit by examination, the Accuplacer placement tests, and the CSS Profile used by many colleges for institutional financial aid. The PSAT is also the qualifying test for the National Merit Scholarship Program.

In 2016 the SAT was redesigned to align with school curricula, and in 2024 the test transitioned to a shorter, adaptive digital format. The College Board also provides college-search and planning services such as BigFuture.

Criticism

The College Board has been criticized for its effective monopoly over admissions testing and AP coursework, its fees, the security and scoring of examinations (including past incidents of score errors and test leaks), and questions about whether its nonprofit status matches its revenues and executive compensation. Debates over test-optional admissions, especially following the COVID-19 pandemic, have also affected the organization, although many universities have reinstated testing requirements.
