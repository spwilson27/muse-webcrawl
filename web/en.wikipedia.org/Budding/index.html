Budding

Budding is a form of asexual reproduction in which a new organism develops from an outgrowth or bud due to cell division at one particular site. The small bulb-like projection coming out from a parent organism is called a bud. The new organism remains attached as it grows, separating from the parent only when it is mature, leaving scar tissue behind.

In microorganisms

Budding is characteristic of a few unicellular organisms. Certain bacteria, such as Caulobacter and Hyphomicrobium, reproduce by budding. Many yeasts, including baker's yeast (Saccharomyces cerevisiae), multiply by an unequal division process in which a small daughter cell is pinched off from the mother cell.

In animals

Some species of invertebrates reproduce by budding. The freshwater polyp Hydra forms buds in its body wall that develop into miniature adults and break away when mature. Sponges and some corals also produce buds or gemmules.

In horticulture

In plant propagation, budding is a form of grafting in which a single bud of a desired variety is inserted into the bark of a rootstock plant. It is widely used for fruit trees and roses because it is economical with scion material and heals quickly.
