Magazine were an English rock band formed in 1977 in Manchester by singer Howard Devoto after he left the punk band Buzzcocks, which he had co-founded. Widely regarded as one of the first post-punk bands, Magazine combined the energy of punk with keyboards, art-rock ambition, and Devoto's literate, sardonic lyrics.

History

The band's debut single "Shot by Both Sides", co-written with Pete Shelley, was released in January 1978 and reached the UK Top 50. Their debut album Real Life (1978), featuring guitarist John McGeoch and bassist Barry Adamson, was acclaimed for its atmospheric, keyboard-heavy sound. Follow-ups Secondhand Daylight (1979) and The Correct Use of Soap (1980), produced by Martin Hannett, consolidated their reputation; McGeoch then left to join Siouxsie and the Banshees. A fourth album, Magic, Murder and the Weather (1981), preceded the band's split later that year.

Legacy and reunion

Magazine's influence extended well beyond their modest commercial success, shaping post-punk, goth, and later alternative rock. The band reformed in 2009 for live shows and released a fifth studio album, No Thyself, in 2011.
