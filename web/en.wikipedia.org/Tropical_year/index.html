Tropical year

Mean length365.24219 days (epoch J2000)
In hours365 d 5 h 48 min 45 s (approx.)
Defined byCycle of the seasons; return of the Sun to the same equinox or solstice

A tropical year, also known as a solar year, an astronomical year or an equinoctial year, is the time that the Sun takes to return to the same position in the sky — as seen from Earth — completing a full cycle of the seasons. For example, it is the time from one March equinox to the next, or from one summer solstice to the next.

Length

The mean tropical year at the start of the year 2000 was 365.24219 days of 86,400 SI seconds. Because the Earth's axial precession and orbital parameters change slowly, the tropical year's length differs slightly depending on which point of the orbit is used as the reference, and it is gradually shortening by a fraction of a second per century. The tropical year is about 20 minutes shorter than the sidereal year — the time taken for the Sun to return to the same position relative to the fixed stars — because of the precession of the equinoxes.

Calendars

Civil calendars aim to track the tropical year so that dates stay in step with the seasons. The Gregorian calendar has a mean year of 365.2425 days, close to the March-equinox year of about 365.24237 days, producing an error of roughly one day in several thousand years. The older Julian calendar's 365.25-day year drifted by about one day every 128 years, eventually motivating the Gregorian reform of 1582.
