Barnard 68 is a dark absorption nebula — a dense molecular cloud of cold gas and dust — located about 400 to 500 light-years away in the constellation Ophiuchus. It is one of the dark nebulae catalogued by the American astronomer E. E. Barnard in his 1919 catalogue of dark markings in the sky. Because its dust completely blocks the visible light of the stars behind it, Barnard 68 appears as an almost perfectly round, inky-black patch — often described as a "hole in the sky" — against the rich star fields of the Milky Way.

Properties

The cloud is a classic example of a Bok globule: an isolated, compact, cold cloud roughly half a light-year across with a mass of about two Suns and an internal temperature of only about 8–16 kelvin. Infrared and millimetre observations can peer through its outer layers, revealing thousands of background stars and mapping its density structure, which closely matches the profile of a cloud in hydrostatic (Bonnor–Ebert) equilibrium between gravity and gas pressure.

Fate

Studies of molecular-line emission show subtle asymmetries suggesting that Barnard 68 may be interacting or colliding with a smaller nearby cloud (Barnard 68 is near the globule B69/B70 region of the Pipe Nebula complex). Such a collision could tip the balance toward gravitational collapse, making Barnard 68 a candidate site for future low-mass star formation — one of the nearest and best-studied examples of the immediate precursors of stars.
