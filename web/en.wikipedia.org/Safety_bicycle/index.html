Safety bicycle

The safety bicycle is the design of bicycle that displaced the dangerous penny-farthing (ordinary) in the 1880s and remains the basic form of the modern bicycle: two wheels of roughly equal size, a chain driving the rear wheel, and a low frame that lets the rider put both feet on the ground.

Development

Key steps included John Kemp Starley's Rover safety bicycle of 1885, which combined a rear chain drive with a steerable front wheel, and John Boyd Dunlop's pneumatic tyre of 1888, which greatly improved comfort. Because the rider sat low between the wheels, falls were less hazardous than from the high ordinary — hence the name.

Impact

The safety design, mass-produced and affordable, triggered the 1890s bicycle boom, expanded personal mobility — notably for women, for whom the drop-frame version was marketed — and spurred improvements to roads, ball bearings, tyres and chain manufacture that fed the early automobile and aviation industries.
