Kami

Traditional countYaoyorozu — "eight million" (i.e. countless)

Kami (Japanese: 神) are the spirits, deities, and sacred powers venerated in the Japanese religion of Shinto. The word resists precise translation — rendered variously as "god", "spirit", or "that which is above" — and kami may be natural phenomena and places, ancestral spirits, mythological deities, or any entity or quality regarded as awe-inspiring and worthy of reverence.

Nature of kami

Classical Shinto texts such as the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki describe multitudes of kami: the traditional phrase yaoyorozu no kami, "eight million kami", signifies an uncountable host. Kami inhabit mountains, rivers, trees, rocks, waterfalls, and animals, and include the sun goddess Amaterasu, ancestress of the imperial house; Inari, kami of rice and prosperity, whose shrines number in the tens of thousands; and deified human beings such as the war kami Hachiman and the scholar Sugawara no Michizane, enshrined as Tenjin. Kami are not conceived as omnipotent or morally perfect: they can err, grow angry, and require pacification and offerings.

Worship

Kami are enshrined in shrines (jinja), where the deity is believed to reside in a sacred object, the shintai or goshintai — often a mirror, sword, or stone — kept hidden in the inner sanctuary (honden). Worship consists of purification, offerings of food, sake, and branches of the sakaki tree, prayers (norito), and festivals (matsuri) in which the kami is carried through the community in a portable shrine (mikoshi). Priests (kannushi) and shrine maidens (miko) conduct the rites.

Concepts and scholarship

The eighteenth-century scholar Motoori Norinaga defined kami as anything whatsoever that is extraordinary, awe-inspiring, or possessed of superior power — including emperors, heroes, and even frightening natural forces. Modern scholars relate the concept to musubi (growth and creation) and makoto (sincerity), and note the kami's dual aspects: a gentle nigimitama and a rough aramitama, whose balance worship seeks to maintain.
