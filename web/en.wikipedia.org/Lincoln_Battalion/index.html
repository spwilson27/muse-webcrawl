The Lincoln Battalion was a battalion of American volunteers who fought for the Spanish Republic during the Spanish Civil War (1936–1939). It formed part of the XV International Brigade, which also included British, Canadian, and other battalions. Roughly 2,800 Americans served in Spain, most of them in the Lincoln Battalion and the associated Washington Battalion, with which it later merged.

History

The first American volunteers arrived in Spain in early 1937 and were organised at Albacete, the International Brigades' base. The battalion first saw action at the Battle of Jarama in February 1937, where it suffered heavy casualties. It later fought at Brunete, Quinto, Belchite, Teruel, and finally in the Ebro offensive of 1938, before the International Brigades were withdrawn in the autumn of that year.

Composition

The volunteers were drawn largely from the American left, including members and sympathisers of the Communist Party USA, though many were unaffiliated. The unit was notable for being racially integrated at a time when the United States armed forces were segregated; Oliver Law, an African American, briefly commanded the battalion in 1937.

Legacy

Survivors formed the Veterans of the Abraham Lincoln Brigade, and the unit remains a celebrated example of international solidarity in American labour and left-wing history. The volunteers were often labelled "premature anti-fascists" during the McCarthy era.
