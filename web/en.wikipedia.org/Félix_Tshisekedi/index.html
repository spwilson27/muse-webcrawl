Félix Tshisekedi

Félix Tshisekedi
Born: 13 June 1963, Léopoldville (Kinshasa)
Office: President of the Democratic Republic of the Congo (since 2019)
Party: Union for Democracy and Social Progress (UDPS)

Félix Antoine Tshisekedi Tshilombo (born 13 June 1963) is a Congolese politician who has served as the President of the Democratic Republic of the Congo since January 2019. He is the son of Étienne Tshisekedi, the veteran opposition leader and founder of the Union for Democracy and Social Progress (UDPS), whom he succeeded as party leader in 2018.

Early life

Tshisekedi was born in Léopoldville (now Kinshasa) and spent much of his early life in exile in Belgium, where his father had relocated after falling out with the Mobutu regime. He studied marketing and communication in Brussels and was active in the UDPS diaspora before returning to the Congo.

Presidency

In the December 2018 general election, the electoral commission declared Tshisekedi the winner over Martin Fayulu, in results that were disputed by Fayulu's camp and questioned by observer groups; the Constitutional Court upheld the outcome, producing the first transfer of power by election in the country's history as he succeeded Joseph Kabila. Initially governing in coalition with Kabila's alliance, Tshisekedi broke with it in late 2020 and assembled a new parliamentary majority, the Sacred Union. His government has faced the ongoing conflict with the M23 rebel movement in the east, supported — according to the Congolese government and UN experts — by Rwanda. He was re-elected in the December 2023 elections for a second term.

Tshisekedi chaired the African Union for 2021–2022. His administration has emphasised anti-corruption prosecutions, free primary education, and renegotiation of mining contracts, particularly with Chinese firms.
