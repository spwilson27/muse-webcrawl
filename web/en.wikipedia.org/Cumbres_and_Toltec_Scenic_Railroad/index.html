Cumbres and Toltec Scenic Railroad

The Cumbres and Toltec Scenic Railroad is a 3 ft (914 mm) narrow-gauge heritage railroad that operates seasonal trains between Antonito, Colorado, and Chama, New Mexico, in the United States. At 64 miles (103 km), it is the longest and highest narrow-gauge steam railroad in North America, crossing the 10,015-foot (3,053 m) Cumbres Pass and traversing the Toltec Gorge of the Los Pinos River.

History

The line was built in 1880 by the Denver and Rio Grande Railroad as part of its San Juan extension, serving mining camps and the silver country of the San Juan Mountains. It operated as a freight and passenger line — the "Chili Line" era routes and the San Juan Express passed over it — until the Rio Grande sought to abandon it in the late 1960s. In 1970 the states of Colorado and New Mexico jointly purchased the most scenic portion, from Antonito to Chama, along with locomotives and rolling stock, and began operating it as a tourist railroad.

Route and operations

Trains climb steep grades of up to 4% to Cumbres Pass, cross Cascade Creek Trestle — at 137 feet the highest on the line — and run along the rim of the 800-foot-deep Toltec Gorge, with a lunch stop at the remote station of Osier, Colorado. The railroad operates historic Baldwin steam locomotives, including K-27, K-36, and K-37 class Mikados, and maintains shops in Antonito and Chama. It is jointly owned by the two states and administered by an interstate commission, with operations supported by the Friends of the Cumbres & Toltec Scenic Railroad volunteer organization.

The railroad was designated a National Historic Landmark in 2012 as the Cumbres and Toltec Scenic Railroad National Historic District. It has appeared in numerous films, including Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade.
