Religion in Indonesia

The Istiqlal Mosque in Jakarta faces the city's Catholic cathedral.

Religion in Indonesia is characterised by the dominance of Islam — Indonesia has the world's largest Muslim population — within a state that formally recognises six religions and constitutionally guarantees freedom of worship. The first principle of the state philosophy Pancasila is belief in "the one and almighty God" (Ketuhanan Yang Maha Esa).

Demographics

According to the 2010 census, about 87% of Indonesians identified as Muslim (overwhelmingly Sunni, of the Shafi'i school), about 7% as Protestant, 3% as Roman Catholic, about 1.7% as Hindu — concentrated in Bali — and under 1% each as Buddhist and Confucian, with smaller shares following indigenous and other faiths. Hinduism arrived from India in the first millennium CE and shaped the Majapahit empire; Islam spread from the thirteenth century through trade along the coasts; Christianity came with Portuguese and Dutch colonisation.

State recognition

The Ministry of Religious Affairs officially recognises Islam, Protestantism, Catholicism, Hinduism, Buddhism and Confucianism; since a 2017 Constitutional Court ruling, adherents of indigenous faiths (aliran kepercayaan) may list that status on identity cards. Blasphemy laws and the requirement of monotheistic belief marginalise atheists and some minority groups; Ahmadiyya and Shia communities have faced discrimination and local violence, and church construction permits have been a recurrent flashpoint.
