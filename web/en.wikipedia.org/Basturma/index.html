TypeCured meat
RegionAnatolia, Armenia, Middle East
Main ingredientsBeef (or other meat), fenugreek paste, spices

Basturma (also spelled pastirma, pastırma or bastırma) is a highly seasoned, air-dried cured beef that originated in Anatolia. It is known throughout Turkey, Armenia, Azerbaijan, Georgia, the Levant, Egypt and the Balkans, and is closely related to the Romanian pastramă, from which the American pastrami is generally considered to descend.

Preparation

The meat, usually beef from the fillet, ribeye or leg, is salted and pressed to remove moisture. After drying for several days, it is coated with a thick paste called çemen, made from ground fenugreek seeds, garlic, hot paprika and other spices mixed with water. The coated meat is then hung to air-dry for ten days to two weeks until the paste hardens into an aromatic crust.

Culinary uses

Basturma is eaten sliced very thin, often as a meze or appetizer. In Turkish and Armenian cooking it is also fried with eggs, added to bean stews, or used as a topping. Its strong fenugreek aroma is distinctive and permeates the meat.

History

Dried, spiced meats of this kind have a long history among the pastoral peoples of Central Asia and Anatolia, where air-drying was a practical means of preserving meat. The name is related to the Turkish bastırmak, meaning "to press", referring to the pressing stage of production.
