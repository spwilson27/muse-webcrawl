Spanish–American War
Date: April–August 1898
Location: Cuba, Puerto Rico, Philippines, Guam
Result: American victory; Treaty of Paris (1898)

The Spanish–American War was an armed conflict between Spain and the United States in 1898. Hostilities began in the aftermath of the explosion of the USS Maine in Havana Harbor and the American demand for Spain's withdrawal from Cuba, where a war of independence was underway.

Course of the war

The US Navy won decisive victories at Manila Bay (1 May 1898) under Commodore Dewey and at Santiago de Cuba in July. American ground forces, including Theodore Roosevelt's Rough Riders, landed in Cuba and then Puerto Rico. An armistice was signed in August after barely ten weeks of fighting.

Treaty of Paris and consequences

By the Treaty of Paris (1898), Spain recognised Cuban independence and ceded Puerto Rico, Guam and the Philippines to the United States for 0 million. The war marked the end of the Spanish Empire in the Americas and the emergence of the United States as a world power, while the status of the Philippines led directly to the Philippine–American War.
