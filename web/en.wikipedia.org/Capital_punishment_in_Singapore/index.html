Capital punishment in Singapore

Capital punishment in Singapore
StatusLegal and in use
MethodLong-drop hanging
SiteChangi Prison
OffencesMurder, drug trafficking, firearms offences, kidnapping, treason

Capital punishment is a legal penalty in Singapore, carried out by long-drop hanging at Changi Prison, traditionally at dawn on a Friday. Singapore has one of the highest per-capita execution rates in the world, and the penalty applies most frequently to drug trafficking and murder.

Statutes

Under the Misuse of Drugs Act, trafficking specified quantities of drugs — for example more than 15 grams of diamorphine (heroin) or 500 grams of cannabis — attracts a presumptive or mandatory death penalty. Amendments in 2012 gave judges discretion in certain courier cases and for offenders with diminished responsibility. The Penal Code provides the death penalty for murder and certain firearms offences.

Debate

The government defends capital punishment as a necessary deterrent that keeps Singapore safe. Human rights organisations such as Amnesty International criticise the mandatory death penalty for drug offences and campaign for abolition.
