The Compacts of Free Association (COFA) are international agreements establishing and governing the relationships of free association between the United States and the three Pacific island sovereign states of the Federated States of Micronesia (FSM), the Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI), and the Republic of Palau. As a result, these countries are sometimes also known as the Freely Associated States (FAS).

Background

The islands had been administered by the United States after the Second World War as part of the Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands, a United Nations trusteeship. The compacts ended that arrangement, granting the island states full sovereignty while the United States retained full authority and responsibility for their security and defense, including the right to deny military access by third countries ("strategic denial").

Provisions

In exchange, the United States provides economic assistance, access to many U.S. domestic programs and services (including the U.S. Postal Service and Federal Aviation Administration), and the right of FAS citizens to live, work, and study in the United States without a visa. The U.S. military also leases the Ronald Reagan Ballistic Missile Defense Test Site on Kwajalein Atoll in the Marshall Islands. The economic provisions of the FSM and Marshall Islands compacts, originally set for 15 years and extended in 2003 for 20 years, were renewed again in agreements signed in 2023 and funded by the U.S. Congress in March 2024, providing roughly $7 billion over 20 years across the three states.
