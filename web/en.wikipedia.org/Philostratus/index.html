Philostratus

Philostratus (Greek: Φιλόστρατος; c. 170 – 247/250 AD) was the name borne by several related Greek sophists and writers of the Roman imperial period, members of a family from Lemnos. The works attributed to "Philostratus" include some of the most distinctive prose of the movement known as the Second Sophistic.

The Philostrati and their works

Philostratus the Elder (Flavius Philostratus, c. 170–247) is credited with the Lives of the Sophists, a collective biography of Greek rhetoricians; the Life of Apollonius of Tyana, a long and novelistic account of the first-century Pythagorean sage Apollonius, commissioned by the empress Julia Domna and long controversial for its miraculous episodes and its use as a pagan counterpoint to the Gospels; and the Heroicus, a dialogue on the heroes of the Trojan War. The Imagines, a set of descriptions of paintings in a Neapolitan gallery that became a founding text of art description (ekphrasis), is attributed to Philostratus the Elder or to his grandson Philostratus the Younger, who also wrote a second series of Imagines. The gymnastic treatise Gymnasticus and a collection of fictional Love Letters complete the corpus.

Reputation

The Philostrati wrote in a self-consciously classical Attic Greek at the courtly centre of the Severan empire, and their mixture of biography, fiction, religion and rhetorical display has made them key witnesses to Greek culture under Rome. The authorship questions among the several Philostrati occupied scholars from the Byzantine Suda onward and are still not fully resolved.
