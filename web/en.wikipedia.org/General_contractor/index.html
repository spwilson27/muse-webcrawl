General contractor

A general contractor, main contractor or prime contractor is responsible for the day-to-day oversight of a construction site, the management of vendors and trades, and the communication of information to all involved parties throughout the course of a building project. The general contractor typically contracts directly with the owner and in turn hires subcontractors to perform specialised portions of the work.

Responsibilities

General contractors provide all materials, labour, equipment, and services necessary for the construction of the project. Their duties include applying for building permits, securing the property, providing temporary utilities on site, managing site personnel, surveying and engineering, disposing of construction waste, monitoring schedules and cash flows, and maintaining accurate records. The general contractor is also typically responsible for site safety and for the quality of the finished work.

Procurement

General contractors are commonly selected through competitive bidding, negotiation, or design–build arrangements. In a traditional design–bid–build project, the contractor bids on completed architectural drawings; in construction management and design–build delivery, the contractor is involved earlier and may assume design responsibilities as well.

Relations with subcontractors

Most of the physical work on a large project is performed by subcontractors — firms specialising in trades such as electrical work, plumbing, structural steel, or concrete. The general contractor coordinates their sequencing, verifies their progress for payment, and bears contractual responsibility to the owner for their performance.

Licensing

Requirements differ by country and, in the United States, by state: many jurisdictions require general contractors to be licensed, bonded, and insured, particularly for residential work. Unlicensed contracting is an offence in several jurisdictions.
