Eerste Divisie

The Eerste Divisie (Dutch pronunciation: [ˈeːrstə diˈvizi]; English: First Division) is the second-highest division in the Dutch football league system, ranking below the Eredivisie and above the Tweede Divisie. Founded in 1956, it is organised by the KNVB. The league has been sponsored by Keuken Kampioen since 2018 and is therefore officially known as the Keuken Kampioen Divisie.

History

The Eerste Divisie was established in 1956 when professional football in the Netherlands was reorganised into a national pyramid. Initially the league consisted of two parallel divisions (Eerste Divisie A and B) with 32 clubs in total; this was reduced to a single division in later years. For decades the league operated largely without relegation, since the amateur tiers below it were separated from the professional leagues. A system of promotion and relegation between the Eerste Divisie and the amateur Topklasse was introduced only in 2009–10, and the introduction of the Tweede Divisie in 2016 completed the pyramid.

Several well-known clubs have spent seasons in the division, including ADO Den Haag, NAC Breda, SC Cambuur and De Graafschap. The league also hosts the reserve ("Jong") teams of Ajax, PSV, AZ and FC Utrecht, which are ineligible for promotion.

Competition format

Twenty teams compete in a double round-robin format, playing 38 matches each. The season is divided into periods, and the winners of each period qualify for the promotion play-offs, joined by the highest-finishing teams that have not yet qualified. The champion — and, since 2023, the runner-up — is promoted directly to the Eredivisie, while play-off winners can also earn promotion against Eredivisie clubs fighting relegation.

Champions

The most successful clubs in the division's history include FC Volendam, FC Den Bosch and Heracles Almelo, each of which has won the title multiple times.
