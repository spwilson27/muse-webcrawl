Conglomerate (company)

A conglomerate is a type of multi-industry company that consists of several different, often unrelated, subsidiary businesses operating under a single corporate group. A conglomerate usually has a parent company that owns controlling stakes in smaller companies, each of which conducts business independently. Conglomerates are frequently multinational and among the largest corporations in the world.

History

Conglomerates became prominent in the United States during the 1960s, when low interest rates and a series of legal and tax considerations made it profitable to acquire companies in unrelated industries. Firms such as International Telephone and Telegraph (ITT), Litton Industries, and Gulf+Western built empires spanning insurance, hotels, manufacturing, and entertainment. The conglomerate boom ended with the market decline of the late 1960s, and during the 1980s many conglomerates were broken up as corporate raiders targeted their undervalued parts.

Advantages and disadvantages

Supporters argue that conglomerates can allocate capital efficiently across divisions, smooth earnings through diversification, and apply professional management to acquired businesses. Critics point to the "conglomerate discount": diversified firms often trade at a lower valuation than the sum of their parts, because internal capital markets can subsidize weak divisions and investors cannot easily evaluate disparate businesses. In the late twentieth century, the prevailing management doctrine shifted toward focus on "core competencies," prompting many conglomerates to divest unrelated units.

Examples

Well-known conglomerates include Berkshire Hathaway, which owns businesses ranging from insurance and railroads to candy and batteries; General Electric, historically a sprawling industrial group that split into separate aerospace, healthcare, and energy companies in the 2020s; and large Asian groups such as Japan's keiretsu, South Korea's chaebol (for example Samsung and Hyundai), and India's Tata Group and Reliance Industries.
