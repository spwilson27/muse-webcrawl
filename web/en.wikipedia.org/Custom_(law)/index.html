Custom (law)

A custom in law is the established pattern of behaviour within a particular social setting that can be objectively verified and, when recognised by a legal system, may acquire the force of law. Customary law exists where a certain legal practice is observed and the relevant actors consider it to be law (opinio juris).

Customary international law

In international law, custom is one of the principal sources of law recognised by the Statute of the International Court of Justice, described as "evidence of a general practice accepted as law". A customary rule requires both consistent state practice and the belief that the practice is legally obligatory.

Domestic legal systems

Within national systems, custom has historically been an important source of law, particularly in the English common law, where local customs could be recognised if they had existed immemorially, were certain, reasonable, and continuously observed. In many jurisdictions, customary law remains significant in areas such as indigenous land rights, family law, and trade usage, where contracts are often interpreted against the background of established commercial customs.
