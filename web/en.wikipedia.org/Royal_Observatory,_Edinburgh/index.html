The Royal Observatory, Edinburgh (ROE) is an astronomical institution located on Blackford Hill in Edinburgh, Scotland. The observatory has been at its present site since 1896 and is administered by the Science and Technology Facilities Council. It is home to the UK Astronomy Technology Centre (UK ATC) and the Institute for Astronomy of the University of Edinburgh.

History

The origins of the observatory go back to 1776, when Professor Thomas Short founded a private observatory. The University of Edinburgh later became involved, and in 1822 an observatory was established on Calton Hill, becoming the Royal Observatory under the aegis of the Astronomer Royal for Scotland, a post created in 1834. Light pollution and cramped conditions on Calton Hill led to a move to Blackford Hill in 1896, funded by a donation from James Ludovic Lindsay, 26th Earl of Crawford, who also donated his library and instruments.

Work

The observatory's early work included time-keeping services for the city — the famous time ball on Calton Hill and the time gun at Edinburgh Castle were synchronised from the observatory. In the twentieth century it became a centre for photographic astronomy and later for the development of astronomical instrumentation and digital sky surveys. The UK Astronomy Technology Centre designs and builds instruments for major telescopes, including contributions to the James Webb Space Telescope's MIRI instrument and to the European Extremely Large Telescope.

The Institute for Astronomy, part of the University of Edinburgh's School of Physics and Astronomy, carries out research in cosmology, galaxy formation, and exoplanets. The ROE also operates a visitor centre and has been involved in wide-field survey data archiving through the Wide Field Astronomy Unit.
