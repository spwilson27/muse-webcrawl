Karmapa

The Karmapa is the head of the Karma Kagyu, one of the four major schools (the Kagyu school) of Tibetan Buddhism. The Karmapa lineage is the oldest tulku lineage in Tibetan Buddhism, dating to Düsum Khyenpa (1110–1193), and is traditionally said to embody the activity of all the Buddhas; its name literally means "the one who carries out Buddha-activity".

History

Düsum Khyenpa, a disciple of Gampopa, founded monasteries including Tsurphu, which became the seat of the Karmapas. Before his death he is said to have left a prediction letter enabling his followers to find his reincarnation, Karma Pakshi (1204–1283) — making the Karmapa line the first recognised reincarnation lineage in Tibet, predating the Dalai Lama line. Successive Karmapas served as teachers to Mongol and Chinese emperors, and the 5th Karmapa, Deshin Shekpa, met the Yongle Emperor of Ming China in 1407.

The Black Crown

The Karmapa is associated with the Black Crown, said to have been woven by dakinis from their hair and materialised into a physical crown by the Yongle Emperor so that others could see it. The crown ceremony, in which the Karmapa holds the crown above his head, is believed to awaken devotion and compassion in those who witness it.

Recent history

The 16th Karmapa, Rangjung Rigpe Dorje (1924–1981), fled Tibet in 1959 and established his seat at Rumtek Monastery in Sikkim, India; he played a major role in bringing Tibetan Buddhism to the West. The identity of the 17th Karmapa became the subject of a well-known controversy, with two main candidates recognised by different groups of Karma Kagyu teachers: Ogyen Trinley Dorje, recognised by the 14th Dalai Lama and based in India, and Trinley Thaye Dorje, based in Europe and India. The two met in France in 2018 and issued a joint statement expressing their wish to heal the division.
