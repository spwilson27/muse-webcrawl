Established: 1943
Location: Los Alamos, New Mexico, United States
Type: National security laboratory
First director: J. Robert Oppenheimer
Operator: Triad National Security, LLC, for the U.S. Department of Energy / NNSA
[Image placeholder: Aerial view of Los Alamos National Laboratory on the Pajarito Plateau]

Los Alamos National Laboratory is one of the sixteen research and development laboratories of the United States Department of Energy. Located near Santa Fe, New Mexico, it was founded in 1943 as the secret "Site Y" of the Manhattan Project, where the first atomic bombs were designed and built under the scientific direction of J. Robert Oppenheimer.

History

The laboratory was established on a remote mesa in northern New Mexico, on the site of the Los Alamos Ranch School. Its wartime staff included many of the century's leading physicists. The first nuclear device, using the laboratory's implosion design, was detonated at the Trinity test on 16 July 1945. After the war the laboratory continued nuclear weapons design, sharing that role with Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory from 1952.

Mission

Today Los Alamos works on nuclear stockpile stewardship, nuclear nonproliferation, supercomputing, materials science, bioscience and energy research. It employs roughly 14,000 people and operates some of the world's most powerful supercomputers.
