Neglected tropical diseases

Neglected tropical diseases (NTDs) are a diverse group of some 20 conditions, recognized by the World Health Organization, that are prevalent mainly among impoverished communities in tropical areas of Africa, Asia, and the Americas. They affect more than a billion people and are "neglected" because they attract little research funding relative to their burden.

The diseases

The WHO list includes dengue and chikungunya, rabies, trachoma, yaws, leprosy, Chagas disease, human African trypanosomiasis (sleeping sickness), leishmaniasis, lymphatic filariasis, onchocerciasis (river blindness), schistosomiasis, soil-transmitted helminthiases, Buruli ulcer, echinococcosis, foodborne trematodiases, mycetoma, scabies, snakebite envenoming, and taeniasis/cysticercosis.

Control

Control relies heavily on mass drug administration of donated medicines, vector control, clean water and sanitation, and veterinary public health. The 2012 London Declaration and the WHO road map for 2021–2030 set targets for eliminating or eradicating several NTDs; dracunculiasis (Guinea-worm disease) has been brought to the brink of eradication, and dozens of countries have eliminated at least one NTD.
