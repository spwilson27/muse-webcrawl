Elaine de Kooning

Elaine Marie Catherine de Kooning
Born: 12 March 1918, New York City
Died: 1 February 1989, Southampton, New York
Known for: Painting, art criticism
Movement: Abstract Expressionism

Elaine de Kooning (born Elaine Marie Catherine Fried; 12 March 1918 – 1 February 1989) was an American Abstract Expressionist painter and a prominent art writer and teacher. She was a central figure of the New York School and the wife of the painter Willem de Kooning.

De Kooning was renowned for her vigorous gestural portraits.

Life and career

Born in Flatbush, Brooklyn, Elaine Fried studied at the Leonardo da Vinci Art School and the American Artists School, where she met Willem de Kooning; they married in 1943. She became an editorial associate of ARTnews in 1948 and wrote influential essays championing Abstract Expressionism, profiling artists such as Arshile Gorky and Franz Kline. She signed her work "EdK" partly to avoid being dismissed as merely her famous husband's wife.

Work

De Kooning is best known for her energetic portraits of men—among them President John F. Kennedy, whom she painted from life studies in 1962–63 for the Truman Library commission—and for series inspired by bullfights, basketball players, and the cave paintings of Lascaux and Altamira, which she visited late in life. Her style combined the gestural brushwork of action painting with a firm grounding in drawing and observation.

Teaching and legacy

She taught at numerous institutions including Yale, Carnegie Mellon, and the University of Georgia, and was an important mentor to younger artists. A major retrospective of her portraits was held at the National Portrait Gallery in Washington, D.C., in 2015.
