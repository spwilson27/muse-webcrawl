The Mini–Mental State Examination (MMSE) or Folstein test is a 30-point questionnaire widely used in clinical and research settings to measure cognitive impairment. It was introduced in 1975 by Marshal Folstein and colleagues as a brief, standardized method for grading patients' cognitive state.

Administration

The test takes about 5–10 minutes and samples functions including arithmetic, memory and orientation. Examinees are asked the date and location, repeat and later recall three words, count backward by sevens, name objects, repeat a phrase, follow commands, write a sentence and copy intersecting pentagons.

Scoring

Scores range from 0 to 30. A score of 24 or higher is generally considered normal; lower scores suggest mild (19–23), moderate (10–18) or severe (≤9) cognitive impairment. Scores are influenced by age and education, and the test is less sensitive to mild cognitive impairment and frontal-lobe dysfunction than alternatives such as the Montreal Cognitive Assessment.

Copyright

Although long treated as a public clinical tool, the MMSE is copyrighted and officially published versions are distributed commercially, a situation that has drawn criticism and encouraged use of alternative instruments.
