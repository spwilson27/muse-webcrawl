Green Bay is a city in the US state of Wisconsin, the county seat of Brown County, situated at the mouth of the Fox River where it flows into the bay of Green Bay, an arm of Lake Michigan. It is the third-largest city in Wisconsin by population and famously the smallest metropolitan market with a major-league professional sports franchise in the United States.

History

The area was inhabited by the Menominee and Ho-Chunk peoples before Europeans arrived. In 1634 the French explorer Jean Nicolet landed near Green Bay, claiming the region for France; a fur-trading post followed and the settlement, called La Baye, grew into one of the oldest European settlements in the Midwest. Green Bay passed to Britain after the Seven Years' War and to the United States after the American Revolution, though British influence in the fur trade lingered until after the War of 1812. Fort Howard anchored the American presence.

Economy

Green Bay's economy was built on shipping, lumber and, above all, paper — the Fox River valley became one of the world's leading paper-producing districts, earning the city the nickname "Toilet Paper Capital of the World". The Port of Green Bay handles coal, limestone, cement and project cargo. Meatpacking is also historically significant and gave the local football team its name.

Sports

The city is synonymous with the Green Bay Packers of the National Football League, founded in 1919 and owned by a publicly held nonprofit corporation of fans. The Packers play at Lambeau Field, one of the most storied stadiums in American sport, and the city is often called "Titletown" for the team's record of championships under coaches such as Curly Lambeau and Vince Lombardi.

Geography

Green Bay sits on low ground at the southern end of the bay, about 180 km north of Milwaukee. Its climate is humid continental, with cold snowy winters influenced by the lake and warm summers.
