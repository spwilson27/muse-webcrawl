Emperor Meiji

Emperor Meiji (November 3, 1852 – July 30, 1912), born Mutsuhito, was the 122nd emperor of Japan according to the traditional order of succession. He reigned from 1867 until his death and presided over the Meiji Restoration, which transformed Japan from an isolated feudal society into a modern industrialised world power.

Succeeding his father, Emperor Kōmei, amid the collapse of the Tokugawa shogunate, the young emperor became the symbol of the reformist government that abolished the domains, created a conscript army, promulgated the Meiji Constitution of 1889 and promoted rapid Westernisation under the slogan fukoku kyōhei ("rich country, strong army").

During his reign Japan won wars against Qing China (1894–95) and Imperial Russia (1904–05), annexed Korea (1910) and emerged as a great power. After his death he was enshrined at the Meiji Shrine in Tokyo, and the era name Meiji ("enlightened rule") became his posthumous name.
