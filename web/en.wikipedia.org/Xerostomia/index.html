Xerostomia is the subjective sensation of oral dryness.

Xerostomia, also known as dry mouth, is the subjective sensation of oral dryness, which may or may not be accompanied by objectively reduced salivary flow (hyposalivation). It is a symptom rather than a disease in itself, and it becomes more common with age, largely because older adults take more medications that suppress salivation.

Causes

Hundreds of medications can cause dry mouth, including many antidepressants, antihypertensives, antihistamines and anticholinergic drugs. Autoimmune destruction of the salivary glands occurs in Sjögren syndrome. Radiation therapy for head and neck cancers frequently damages the major salivary glands permanently. Other causes include dehydration, diabetes, mouth breathing, smoking and anxiety.

Effects

Saliva protects the teeth and soft tissues, aids digestion and speech, and controls oral microbes. Its absence raises the risk of dental caries, oral candidiasis, gum disease, cracked lips, difficulty wearing dentures, and problems chewing, tasting and swallowing.

Diagnosis and treatment

Assessment may include measuring unstimulated and stimulated salivary flow (sialometry) and reviewing medications. Management focuses on treating the underlying cause where possible, frequent sips of water, saliva substitutes and stimulants such as sugar-free gum or lozenges, meticulous oral hygiene with fluoride, and, in some cases, sialogogue drugs such as pilocarpine or cevimeline.
