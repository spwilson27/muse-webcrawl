Federal Security Service (FSB)

Formed: 3 April 1995
Preceding agency: Federal Counterintelligence Service (FSK)
Headquarters: Lubyanka, Moscow, Russia
Director: Alexander Bortnikov (since 2008)

The Federal Security Service of the Russian Federation (Russian: Федеральная служба безопасности, FSB) is the principal security agency of Russia and the main domestic successor to the Soviet Union's KGB. Its headquarters are in the Lubyanka Building in Moscow.

Role and responsibilities

The FSB's responsibilities include internal security and counterintelligence, border security, counter-terrorism, surveillance and combating serious crime. It incorporates the Border Guard Service and provides information security functions. The service reports directly to the President of Russia. Vladimir Putin, a former KGB officer, served as FSB director in 1998–1999 before becoming prime minister and then president.

History

The FSB descends from the Cheka through the NKVD and KGB. After the KGB was broken up in 1991, a succession of agencies — the Ministry of Security, then the Federal Counterintelligence Service (FSK) — preceded the FSB's creation in 1995. Its powers have expanded substantially since 2000, including roles in the Chechen conflicts and, later, in Russia's wars against Ukraine.

Criticism

The FSB has been accused by journalists and human rights groups of involvement in assassinations, suppression of dissent and pervasive surveillance; such allegations have figured in cases including the 1999 apartment bombings controversy and the poisonings of Alexander Litvinenko and Alexei Navalny. The agency is sanctioned by several Western governments.
