Light


Light is electromagnetic radiation within the portion of the spectrum visible to the human eye, roughly 380–750 nanometres in wavelength. It travels in vacuum at 299,792,458 m/s.

Light or visible light is electromagnetic radiation that can be perceived by the human eye. Visible light is usually defined as having wavelengths in the range of 400–700 nanometres, corresponding to frequencies of 750–420 terahertz.

Properties

Light exhibits wave–particle duality: it propagates as an electromagnetic wave and interacts in discrete quanta called photons. In vacuum it travels at the speed of light, c = 299,792,458 metres per second. Its behaviour is described by reflection, refraction, diffraction, interference and polarisation, the subject matter of optics.

History of study

Newton argued light consists of corpuscles; Huygens proposed a wave theory, confirmed by Young's double-slit experiment. Maxwell showed light is an electromagnetic wave, and Einstein's explanation of the photoelectric effect (1905) established its quantum nature.
