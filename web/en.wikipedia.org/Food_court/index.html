Food court

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A food court is generally an indoor plaza or common area within a facility that is contiguous with the counters of multiple food vendors and provides a common area for self-serve dining. Food courts may be found in shopping malls, airports, railway stations, universities, hospitals, and other large public venues.

Description

Food courts consist of a number of vendors at food stalls or service counters offering prepared, usually fast, food. Orders are taken at the counter, the food is served on trays, and diners carry it to communal seating in the central area. Dishes, cutlery, and tray return are typically managed centrally by the venue operator. In some regions, particularly in Southeast Asia, outdoor or semi-outdoor equivalents known as hawker centres or food centres serve a similar function.

History

The concept developed with the post-war shopping mall in North America. The food court at the Paramus Park mall in New Jersey, which opened in March 1974, is often cited as one of the first successful mall food courts in the United States, following an earlier example at the Plymouth Meeting Mall in Pennsylvania (opened 1968). By the 1980s food courts had become a standard feature of mall design, intended to keep shoppers in the mall longer and to serve the employees of attached office complexes. Airport terminals adopted the format extensively from the 1990s onward, mixing fast-food outlets with local restaurants and bars.

Evolution

As traditional malls declined in the 2010s, operators increasingly replaced conventional food courts with upscale "food halls", featuring local chefs, craft beverages, and communal design aimed at attracting diners as a destination in itself.
