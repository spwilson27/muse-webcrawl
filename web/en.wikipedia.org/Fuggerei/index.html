The Fuggerei is the world's oldest public housing complex still in use. It is a walled enclave within the city of Augsburg, Bavaria, founded in 1521 by the extraordinarily wealthy banker Jakob Fugger as a place where the needy citizens of Augsburg could live at nominal cost. It comprises 67 houses with 147 apartments, a church, and administrative buildings, enclosed by walls with gates that are still locked each night.

Conditions and rent

The rent has famously remained unchanged for five centuries: one Rhenish guilder per year, equivalent today to about €0.88, plus a small charge for heating and maintenance. In return, residents are expected to pray three times daily for the Fugger family, as specified in the founding charter. Tenants must be Catholic, have lived in Augsburg for at least two years, and be poor without being destitute; they originally were expected to work towards independence. About 150 people live in the Fuggerei today.

History

The complex survived the Thirty Years' War and was about three-quarters destroyed by Allied bombing in February 1944, after which it was rebuilt in its original style. It is still administered by the Fugger foundation (the Fürstlich und Gräflich Fuggersche Stiftungs-Administration), funded by the original endowment, forestry income, and admission fees paid by some 200,000 visitors a year. Franz Mozart, great-grandfather of Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart, was among its residents.
