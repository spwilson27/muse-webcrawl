Edward Everett

Edward Everett (11 April 1794 – 15 January 1865) was an American politician, statesman, educator, and orator from Massachusetts. He served as US Representative, Senator, Governor of Massachusetts, Minister to Great Britain, Secretary of State, and president of Harvard University. Today he is chiefly remembered as the headline orator who spoke for two hours at Gettysburg on 19 November 1863, immediately before Abraham Lincoln delivered the Gettysburg Address.

Everett, the most celebrated American orator of his era.

Early life and academia

A child prodigy, Everett graduated from Harvard at seventeen, became a Unitarian minister, and then studied in Germany, earning a doctorate at Göttingen — among the first Americans to do so. He became Harvard's first professor of Greek literature and edited the North American Review.

Political career

Everett served ten years in Congress (1825–35), then four terms as Governor of Massachusetts. He was Minister to Britain (1841–45) and succeeded Daniel Webster as Secretary of State under President Fillmore. In 1860 he ran for vice president on the Constitutional Union ticket with John Bell.

Gettysburg

Chosen as the principal speaker for the dedication of the Soldiers' National Cemetery, Everett delivered a learned two-hour oration tracing the battle in detail; Lincoln's two-minute address, which followed, overshadowed it. Everett wrote to Lincoln the next day that he wished he had come "as near to the central idea of the occasion in two hours" as Lincoln had "in two minutes". During the Civil War he raised funds and spoke tirelessly for the Union cause, helping to purchase and preserve Washington's Mount Vernon.
