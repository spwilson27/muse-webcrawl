Caster angle

The caster angle or castor angle is the angular displacement of the steering axis from the vertical axis of a steered wheel in a car, motorcycle, bicycle, or other vehicle, measured in the longitudinal direction. It is the angle between the pivot line (the line through the steering axis) and a vertical line through the wheel centre, viewed from the side of the vehicle.

Effects

Positive caster, where the steering axis leans so that its top is behind the wheel contact patch, creates a self-aligning torque that helps the wheels return to the straight-ahead position and improves directional stability at speed. Excessive caster increases steering effort, while negative caster makes steering light but can feel unstable. The effect is analogous to the fork rake on a bicycle and to the trailing wheels of a shopping cart trolley, which are named casters.

Adjustment

Caster is one of the angles checked during wheel alignment, alongside camber and toe. On many cars it is fixed by the suspension design; on others it can be adjusted with shims or eccentric bolts. Typical values for passenger cars are between 2 and 7 degrees of positive caster.
