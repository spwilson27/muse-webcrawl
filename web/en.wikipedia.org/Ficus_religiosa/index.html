Ficus religiosa

Ficus religiosa, commonly known as the sacred fig, peepal, pipal or ashvattha, is a species of fig native to the Indian subcontinent and Indochina. It is a large, fast-growing deciduous tree of the mulberry family (Moraceae), reaching 30 metres in height with a trunk up to 3 metres in diameter. Its distinctive leaves are heart-shaped with an elongated, tapering drip tip.

Religious significance

The species is sacred in Hinduism, Buddhism and Jainism. In Buddhism it is revered above all as the kind of tree under which Siddhartha Gautama attained enlightenment at Bodh Gaya; that tree became known as the Bodhi tree, and cuttings from its descendants — notably the Sri Maha Bodhi in Anuradhapura, Sri Lanka, planted in the 3rd century BCE — are among the oldest historically documented trees in the world. Hindus venerate the peepal as a dwelling place of deities, and it is commonly planted beside temples.

Description and ecology

The tree often begins life as an epiphyte, germinating in the crevice of another tree or a building and sending roots down to the soil; in this it resembles its relative the banyan, though it forms fewer aerial prop roots. Like other figs, it depends on a specific fig wasp for pollination of its small, paired fruits. The leaves move readily in the slightest breeze because of their long, flexible stalks.

Uses

Bark, leaves and figs of Ficus religiosa are used in Ayurvedic medicine for a variety of ailments, and the tree is widely planted as an ornamental shade tree in tropical Asia.
