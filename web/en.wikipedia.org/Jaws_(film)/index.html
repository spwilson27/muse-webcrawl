Jaws (film)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Jaws is a 1975 American thriller film directed by Steven Spielberg, based on the 1974 novel by Peter Benchley. It stars Roy Scheider as police chief Martin Brody, who, with the help of a marine biologist (Richard Dreyfuss) and a professional shark hunter (Robert Shaw), hunts a man-eating great white shark that attacks beachgoers at a summer resort town in New England.

Shot mostly on location at Martha's Vineyard in Massachusetts from May to October 1974, Jaws was the first major motion picture to be shot on the ocean and consequently had a troubled production, going over budget and schedule. As the art department's mechanical sharks often malfunctioned, Spielberg decided to mostly suggest the shark's presence, employing a foreboding, minimal theme created by composer John Williams to indicate its impending appearances. Spielberg and others have likened this suggestive approach to that of director Alfred Hitchcock.

Plot

During a beach party at night on Amity Island, young Chrissie Watkins goes skinny dipping and is attacked and killed by an unseen force. Police chief Martin Brody, new to the island, wants to close the beaches, but Mayor Larry Vaughn overrules him, fearing a loss of tourist revenue. After more attacks — including the death of a young boy, Alex Kintner — the town offers a bounty, and shark hunter Quint offers to kill the shark for $10,000. Brody, Quint, and marine biologist Matt Hooper set out on Quint's boat, the Orca. After a long pursuit, during which Quint recounts his experience as a survivor of the USS Indianapolis sinking, Quint is killed by the shark. Brody finally kills the shark by shooting a pressurised scuba tank lodged in its mouth, and he and Hooper paddle back to shore on wreckage.

Cast

  • Robert Shaw as Quint

Release and reception

Universal Pictures released Jaws to over 450 screens in North America on June 20, 1975, in an exceptionally wide release accompanied by an extensive television advertising campaign. The film was a critical and commercial success, and is often cited as the first summer blockbuster. It grossed more than $470 million worldwide on a budget of about $9 million, and was the highest-grossing film of all time until Star Wars surpassed it two years later. It won several awards, including Academy Awards for Best Film Editing, Best Original Dramatic Score, and Best Sound; it was also nominated for Best Picture.

Legacy

Jaws established the modern Hollywood business model of high-concept films released widely in summer with heavy marketing. It was followed by three sequels — Jaws 2 (1978), Jaws 3-D (1983), and Jaws: The Revenge (1987) — none involving Spielberg or Benchley. In 2001, it was selected by the Library of Congress for preservation in the National Film Registry.
