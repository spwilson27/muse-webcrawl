The Nicaraguan Revolution (Spanish: Revolución Nicaragüense) was the process by which the Sandinista National Liberation Front (FSLN) overthrew the dictatorship of the Somoza family, which had ruled Nicaragua since 1936, and the subsequent conflict between the revolutionary government and the US-backed counter-revolutionary Contras during the 1980s. The revolution was a defining episode of the late Cold War in Latin America.

Background

The FSLN, founded in 1961 and named for Augusto César Sandino, the guerrilla leader who resisted the US occupation of Nicaragua in the 1920s–30s, waged a long insurgency against Anastasio Somoza Debayle. Outrage grew after the regime's corrupt handling of relief for the 1972 Managua earthquake and the January 1978 assassination of newspaper editor Pedro Joaquín Chamorro. A broad opposition coalition—including business groups, the church, and urban workers—joined mass uprisings in 1978–79. On 19 July 1979, Sandinista forces entered Managua as Somoza fled the country.

The revolutionary government

The FSLN-led Junta of National Reconstruction launched sweeping programmes: a literacy crusade in 1980 that sharply reduced illiteracy, land reform redistributing Somoza-family estates, expanded healthcare, and nationalisation of banking and much industry, within a formally mixed economy and political pluralism. Daniel Ortega became the junta's coordinator and won the presidency in the 1984 elections.

The Contra war

From 1981 the United States, under President Ronald Reagan, financed, armed, and trained the Contras—counter-revolutionary forces based in Honduras and Costa Rica—while mining Nicaragua's harbours, an action the International Court of Justice ruled unlawful in 1986. The US Congress's attempts to restrict aid produced the Iran–Contra affair. The war devastated the economy, killed tens of thousands, and pushed the government toward conscription and austerity. Exhausted by war and economic collapse, voters turned the FSLN out in the February 1990 election, won by Violeta Chamorro's UNO coalition, bringing the revolutionary period to a close.
