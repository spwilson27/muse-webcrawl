Upper Geyser Basin

The Upper Geyser Basin is a geyser basin in Yellowstone National Park in Wyoming, United States. It contains the largest concentration of geysers in the world — nearly one quarter of all the geysers in the park — spread along the Firehole River over an area of roughly one square mile (2.6 km²). The basin is home to Old Faithful, the most famous geyser in the park.

Features

Five of the park's predictable geysers are found in the basin: Old Faithful, Grand Geyser (the tallest predictable geyser in the world, at about 200 feet / 61 m), Riverside Geyser, Daisy Geyser and Castle Geyser. Other notable geysers include Beehive, Giant, Giantess, Lion, Plume and Aurum. The basin also contains colourful hot springs, including Morning Glory Pool, Chromatic Pool, Beauty Pool and the springs of Biscuit Basin, a sub-basin across the Firehole River.

Geology

The basin lies within the Yellowstone Caldera, created by a supervolcanic eruption 640,000 years ago. Heat from the underlying magma chamber warms groundwater that circulates through rhyolite lava flows; silica dissolved from the rock is deposited as geyserite (siliceous sinter) around vents, creating the constrictions that allow geysers to erupt. A boardwalk system and the Old Faithful Inn, opened in 1904, serve the heavy visitor traffic to the area.

History

Members of the Washburn–Langford–Doane Expedition of 1870 explored the basin and named Old Faithful for its regular eruptions. The area became part of Yellowstone, the world's first national park, when it was established in 1872. The 1959 Hebgen Lake earthquake and subsequent seismic activity altered the behaviour of several features in the basin.
