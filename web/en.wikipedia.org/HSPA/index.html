High Speed Packet Access

High Speed Packet Access (HSPA) is an amalgamation of two mobile protocols, High Speed Downlink Packet Access (HSDPA) and High Speed Uplink Packet Access (HSUPA), that extends and improves the performance of existing 3G mobile telecommunication networks using the WCDMA protocols. HSPA was standardised by the 3GPP in Releases 5 and 6 of UMTS.

Performance

HSDPA introduced a shared high-speed downlink channel with adaptive modulation (up to 16-QAM), fast scheduling and hybrid ARQ, initially offering peak rates of 14.4 Mbit/s. HSUPA brought corresponding uplink improvements up to 5.76 Mbit/s. Later evolutions known as HSPA+ (Evolved HSPA) added 64-QAM, MIMO and dual-carrier operation, raising theoretical downlink peaks to 42 Mbit/s and beyond.

Deployment

HSPA networks were deployed worldwide during the late 2000s and were commonly marketed as "3.5G". They formed the main mobile broadband technology before the rollout of LTE in the 2010s.
