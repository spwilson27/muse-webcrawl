Mustafa Kemal Ataturk

Mustafa Kemal Atatürk, first President of Turkey

Mustafa Kemal Atatürk (c. 1881 – 10 November 1938) was a Turkish field marshal, revolutionary statesman, and the founding father of the Republic of Turkey, serving as its first president from 1923 until his death. (This article's title follows the spelling "Ataturk" given in the source URL; the standard Turkish spelling is Atatürk, meaning "Father of the Turks", a surname granted to him by parliament in 1934.)

Military career

An Ottoman officer educated at military schools in Monastir and Istanbul, Mustafa Kemal distinguished himself commanding the defence against the Allied landings at Gallipoli in 1915 and later on the Caucasus and Palestine fronts. After the Ottoman defeat in the First World War, he led the Turkish National Movement in the War of Independence (1919–1922), defeating Greek forces and forcing the renegotiation of the peace settlement at Lausanne.

Presidency and reforms

The Republic of Turkey was proclaimed on 29 October 1923 with Mustafa Kemal as president. His reforms — known as Atatürk's reforms or Kemalism — abolished the sultanate and caliphate, secularised law and education, adopted the Latin alphabet for Turkish (1928), introduced European civil and penal codes, extended political rights to women (suffrage in 1934), and promoted industrialisation and national identity. He died in Istanbul in 1938 and is buried at the Anıtkabir mausoleum in Ankara.
