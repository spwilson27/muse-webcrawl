Sport in Finland

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Ice hockey is Finland's most popular spectator sport

Sport in Finland is an important part of Finnish culture and national identity. The most popular spectator sports are ice hockey and Formula One racing, while by participation the top activities include walking, cycling, skiing, and floorball. Finland has punched above its weight at the Olympic Games, especially in long-distance running, cross-country skiing, ski jumping, and wrestling, earning the country the nickname of a sports superpower relative to its population of about 5.5 million.

History and stars

Early heroes include runner Paavo Nurmi, the "Flying Finn", who won nine Olympic gold medals in the 1920s. Athletics remains prominent, with javelin throw a signature event (Tero Pitkämäki, and earlier Tiina Lillak and Seppo Räty). Finland hosts the Rally Finland, a fast gravel round of the World Rally Championship, and produced famous drivers such as Kimi Räikkönen and rally champions Juha Kankkunen and Marcus Grönholm.

National sports

Pesäpallo, a Finnish variant of baseball invented in the 1920s, is considered the national sport. Finland's men's ice hockey team (the Leijonat, "Lions") won its first Olympic gold in 2022.
