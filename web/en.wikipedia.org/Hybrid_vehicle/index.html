Hybrid vehicle

Hybrid vehicle
Type: Vehicle powertrain technology
Propulsion: Internal combustion engine + electric motor
First mass-produced: Toyota Prius (1997)

A hybrid vehicle is one that uses two or more distinct types of power to move the vehicle. The term most commonly refers to hybrid electric vehicles (HEVs), which combine a conventional internal combustion engine system with an electric propulsion system. The presence of an electric powertrain is intended to achieve either better fuel economy than a conventional vehicle or better performance.

History

Hybrid designs date to the earliest days of the automobile; Ferdinand Porsche's Lohner-Porsche Mixte of 1900 combined a petrol engine with electric hub motors. Interest revived in the 1990s, and the Toyota Prius, launched in Japan in 1997, became the first mass-produced hybrid car, followed by the Honda Insight. Sales expanded worldwide in the 2000s as fuel prices rose and emissions rules tightened.

Types

Mild hybrids use a small motor-generator that assists the engine and enables stop-start operation but cannot propel the car alone. Full hybrids can drive short distances on electric power alone. Plug-in hybrids (PHEVs) carry larger batteries that can be recharged from the electrical grid, allowing substantial electric-only range. Series hybrids use the engine only as a generator, while parallel hybrids allow both power sources to drive the wheels.

Technology

Hybrid systems recover braking energy through regenerative braking, storing it in a nickel-metal hydride or lithium-ion battery. A power control unit manages the flow of energy between engine, motor, and battery, choosing the most efficient combination for conditions. Many designs use an Atkinson-cycle engine optimised for efficiency rather than peak power.

Benefits and criticism

Advocates cite reduced fuel consumption and tailpipe emissions, especially in urban driving. Critics note the extra cost, weight, and complexity of dual powertrains, and question the lifecycle environmental cost of battery production. See also Electric vehicle and Plug-in hybrid.
