Cultural property

Cultural property includes the physical, or "tangible," cultural heritage, such as artworks, buildings, books, documents, and other artifacts. These objects are considered especially important because of their cultural, historical, artistic, scientific, or religious significance to a group of people or a nation.

The protection of cultural property in times of armed conflict is governed by the Hague Convention for the Protection of Cultural Property in the Event of Armed Conflict of 1954, the first international treaty devoted exclusively to this subject. The 1970 UNESCO Convention addresses the illicit import, export, and transfer of ownership of cultural property, and the 1995 UNIDROIT Convention provides rules for the restitution of stolen or illegally exported cultural objects.

Emblem

The distinctive emblem of the 1954 Hague Convention — a blue shield, known as the Blue Shield — marks cultural property under special protection and serves as the cultural equivalent of the Red Cross emblem used for medical personnel and facilities.

Debates

Disputes over cultural property frequently concern the repatriation of objects acquired during colonial rule or wartime looting, such as the Parthenon marbles held by the British Museum and the Benin Bronzes held by various Western museums. Defenders of retention argue for "universal museums" that preserve and display heritage for a global audience, while advocates of restitution argue that cultural property belongs with its communities and nations of origin.
