Lazarus of Bethany

Lazarus of Bethany (Latinised from Greek Lazaros, from Hebrew Eleazar, "God has helped") is a figure in the Gospel of John whom Jesus raises from the dead four days after his burial — the Raising of Lazarus, one of the most dramatic miracles attributed to Jesus. He is the brother of Mary and Martha and lived in Bethany near Jerusalem.

The miracle

In John 11, Jesus, told that Lazarus is ill, delays, then comes to the tomb, weeps — the shortest verse in English Bibles, "Jesus wept" — and calls "Lazarus, come out!" The raising precipitates the Sanhedrin's plot against Jesus in John's narrative. Lazarus also appears reclining at table in John 12, where chief priests plot to kill him too as living evidence.

Veneration and traditions

Lazarus is venerated as a saint; Orthodox tradition celebrates Lazarus Saturday before Palm Sunday, and later legends made him bishop of Kition in Cyprus (where his reputed tomb lies in Larnaca) or, in western tradition, bishop of Marseille. His name gives us "lazar house" and the medical "Lazarus sign".
