Brownian motion

Brownian motion is the random motion of particles suspended in a medium — a liquid or a gas — resulting from their collision with the fast-moving molecules of the medium. It is named after botanist Robert Brown, who in 1827 observed pollen grains jiggling under a microscope.

Albert Einstein's 1905 paper on Brownian motion provided decisive empirical evidence for the existence of atoms and molecules, deriving the relation between the diffusion coefficient and molecular dimensions. Jean Perrin's experiments confirmed Einstein's predictions.

Mathematically, Brownian motion is modelled by the Wiener process, a continuous-time stochastic process fundamental to probability theory, mathematical finance (the Black–Scholes model), and statistical physics.
