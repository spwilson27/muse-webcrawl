Le Mans Prototype

A Le Mans Prototype (LMP) is a type of sports prototype racing car built specifically for endurance racing, most famously the 24 Hours of Le Mans, from which the class takes its name. Prototypes are purpose-built racing machines not derived from road cars, with enclosed wheels and, in modern form, closed cockpits. For decades they formed the top class of the FIA World Endurance Championship and the IMSA SportsCar Championship.

Classes

LMP1 was the premier category, contested by factory hybrids such as the Audi R18, Porsche 919 Hybrid and Toyota TS050, producing over 1,000 hp with electric hybrid systems. It was replaced at the top level by the Le Mans Hypercar regulations from 2021. LMP2 is a pro-am class using standardised chassis from suppliers such as Oreca and a spec Gibson V8 engine; it remains a mainstay of Le Mans, the European Le Mans Series and IMSA. LMP3, introduced in 2015, is an entry-level class with smaller Nissan- or Toyota-based engines.

Technical features

Prototypes use carbon-fibre monocoque chassis, mid-mounted engines and sophisticated aerodynamics generating high downforce. Balance of Performance measures and cost caps in the lower classes keep competition close.

History

The prototype tradition stretches back through Group C of the 1980s to the Ford GT40 and Porsche 917 era. The LMP name was formalised by the Automobile Club de l'Ouest in the 1990s, succeeding GTP and Group C categories, and Audi's diesel and hybrid cars dominated the 2000s and 2010s.
