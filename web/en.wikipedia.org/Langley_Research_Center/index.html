Langley Research Center

Langley Research Center (LaRC or NASA Langley), located in Hampton, Virginia, is the oldest of NASA's field centers. The center and its partners work in aeronautics, Earth science, and space exploration research. Established in 1917 by the National Advisory Committee for Aeronautics (NACA) as the Langley Memorial Aeronautical Laboratory, it was named for aviation pioneer Samuel Pierpont Langley.

History

Langley began operations in 1917 and became a centerpiece of American aeronautical research, home to a series of wind tunnels that tested nearly every American military aircraft of the Second World War era. In 1958, when NACA became NASA, Langley hosted the Space Task Group that directed Project Mercury, America's first human spaceflight program, before the group relocated to Houston.

Research

Today Langley focuses on aeronautics research (including advanced air mobility and flight safety), atmospheric science and Earth observation (including lidar instruments flown on satellites), and systems analysis for human and robotic space exploration. Its facilities include more than 40 wind tunnels, the largest being part of the National Transonic Facility constructed in the 1980s.

Notable people

Langley's human computers included Katherine Johnson, Dorothy Vaughan, and Mary Jackson, whose work on trajectory calculations and engineering was popularized by the book and film Hidden Figures. The center employed roughly 3,400 civil servants and contractors in recent decades.
