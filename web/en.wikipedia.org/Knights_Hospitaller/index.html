The Knights Hospitaller, formally the Order of Knights of the Hospital of Saint John of Jerusalem, is a Catholic military and religious order founded in Jerusalem around 1099, in the aftermath of the First Crusade. Originally established to care for sick and injured pilgrims at the Hospital of Saint John in Jerusalem, the order evolved into one of the most powerful military orders of the medieval world, alongside the Knights Templar and the Teutonic Order.

History

The order traces its origins to a hospital founded by merchants from Amalfi in the Muristan quarter of Jerusalem in the 1070s, dedicated to Saint John the Baptist. Under its first master, Blessed Gerard Thom, the hospice expanded and was formally recognised by Pope Paschal II in the bull Pie postulatio voluntatis (1113). Gerard's successor, Raymond du Puy, transformed the brotherhood into a military order, organising it into classes of knights, sergeants and chaplains and giving it a rule modelled partly on that of Saint Augustine.

The Hospitallers built and garrisoned some of the most formidable castles in the Crusader states, including Krak des Chevaliers in Syria and Margat. After the fall of Acre in 1291 ended the Crusader presence in the Levant, the order withdrew first to Cyprus and then conquered the island of Rhodes (1306–1310), where it established an independent state and a powerful navy that patrolled the eastern Mediterranean.

Rhodes and Malta

The Knights held Rhodes for over two centuries, repelling major Ottoman sieges in 1480 before finally surrendering the island to Suleiman the Magnificent in 1522 after a six-month siege. In 1530 the Holy Roman Emperor Charles V granted them the islands of Malta, Gozo and the North African port of Tripoli. From Malta the order's galleys waged a long corsair war against Ottoman and Barbary shipping. Its most famous episode was the Great Siege of Malta in 1565, when a few hundred knights and several thousand Maltese and Spanish troops under Grand Master Jean Parisot de Valette withstood an Ottoman invasion force.

The order's naval power peaked at the Battle of Lepanto in 1571. Thereafter it settled into the role of a small sovereign state. In 1798 Napoleon Bonaparte seized Malta on his way to Egypt, ending the knights' rule there. The order eventually re-established its headquarters in Rome in 1834.

The modern order

Today the Sovereign Military Order of Malta, as it is now styled, is a sovereign subject of international law, maintains diplomatic relations with over one hundred states, and has permanent observer status at the United Nations. It has returned to its original hospitaller vocation, running medical, humanitarian and disaster-relief programmes worldwide through its relief agency, Malteser International.

Organisation

Traditionally the order was divided into eight langues (tongues) — Provence, Auvergne, France, Italy, Aragon, England, Germany and Castile — each responsible for a section of the defences of Rhodes and Malta. Knights were required to prove noble ancestry, and governance rested with the Grand Master and the Sovereign Council.
