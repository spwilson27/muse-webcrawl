Bombay Beach, California

Location: Imperial County, California, United States

Shore: Eastern shore of the Salton Sea

Elevation: about 72 m (236 ft) below sea level

Population: a few hundred residents (census-designated place)

Bombay Beach is a census-designated place in Imperial County, California, on the eastern shore of the Salton Sea. Once a popular resort destination in the 1950s and 1960s, it declined as the Salton Sea grew increasingly saline and polluted, suffering floods, fish die-offs, and algal blooms. Much of the town was abandoned, leaving surreal ruins — trailers, boats, and structures encrusted in salt along the shore.

History

During the Salton Sea's resort heyday, Bombay Beach attracted visitors for swimming, waterskiing, and fishing, and celebrities visited nearby resorts. Rising salinity killed most fish species except tilapia, and periodic flooding in the 1970s submerged parts of the town behind protective berms. Many residents left, and property values collapsed.

Artistic revival

Since the 2010s, Bombay Beach has attracted artists, photographers, and tourists drawn to its post-apocalyptic landscape. The Bombay Beach Biennale, a festival of art, music, and philosophy founded in the mid-2010s, installed permanent artworks around the town, including an opera house, a cinema, and sculptural pieces on the beach. The town has appeared in films, television, and video games as an emblem of decay and resilience in the desert.
