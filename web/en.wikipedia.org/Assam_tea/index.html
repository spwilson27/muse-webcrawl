Assam tea

Assam tea is a black tea named after the region of its production, Assam, in India. It is manufactured specifically from the plant Camellia sinensis var. assamica. Assam's tropical climate, low altitude and abundant rainfall give the tea its characteristic full-bodied, brisk and malty flavour, and the state is the world's largest tea-growing region.

History

Tea plants were found growing wild in Assam by Robert Bruce in 1823, and commercial cultivation began under the British in the 1830s through the Assam Company. The industry expanded rapidly, supplied by labour recruited from across India, and Assam teas became a mainstay of British blends.

Production

Assam gardens produce two harvests, or "flushes": a spring first flush and a later second flush, the latter prized for its "tippy" golden leaves and sweeter, maltier liquor. Most Assam tea is processed by the CTC (crush, tear, curl) method for use in tea bags and blends such as English and Irish breakfast teas.

Geographical indication

"Assam tea" is a registered geographical indication in India, and the Tea Board of India certifies authentic produce with the Assam orthodox logo. The region produces more than half of India's total tea output.
