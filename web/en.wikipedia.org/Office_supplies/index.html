Office supplies

Office supplies are consumables and equipment regularly used in offices by businesses and other organizations, by individuals engaged in written communications, record keeping, or bookkeeping, and for the cleaning or replenishment of office equipment. The term covers a wide range of materials including paper products, data storage media, ink and toner, writing instruments, and small machines such as staplers, hole punches, and paper shredders.

History

The modern office-supply industry grew out of the stationery trade of the nineteenth century, when the spread of typewriters, carbon paper, and standardized paper sizes created mass demand for standardized consumables. Mail-order catalogues and later retail chains, such as Staples (founded 1986) and Office Depot, turned office supplies into a large retail sector. The rise of e-commerce and the paperless office have since reshaped the market toward computer accessories, printer consumables, and ergonomic furniture.

Categories

Common categories include writing and correction supplies (pens, pencils, markers, correction fluid), paper and pads, filing and storage (folders, binders, archive boxes), desk accessories (staplers, scissors, tape dispensers), mailing and shipping supplies, and technology consumables such as ink cartridges, toner, batteries, and storage media.
