Single rope technique

A caver climbing a pitch using single rope technique.

Single rope technique (SRT) is a method used in caving and in tree climbing to descend and ascend a single fixed rope. It replaced older methods such as ladders and the body rappel for vertical cave pitches, because the rope can be carried easily and rigged from a single anchor line rather than requiring the doubled rope or ladder systems used previously.

Equipment and systems

Descent is usually made with a friction device such as a rack, bobbin descender or stop descender, which can be locked off mid-pitch. Ascent uses mechanical ascenders (handled and chest ascenders such as the Croll) combined with a footloop, in systems such as the frog system common in Europe, or the ropewalker and Mitchell systems used in the United States. A cow's-tail lanyard provides safety attachment at rebelays and deviations, where the rope is redirected to avoid rub points, waterfalls or loose rock.

History

SRT was developed in the United States and Europe during the 1960s and 1970s as cavers pushed deeper systems such as those in the TAG region (Tennessee, Alabama, Georgia), and it became essential for exploring very deep caves, enabling pitches of hundreds of metres to be descended and re-ascended. The technique has since been adapted for arboriculture, rope access work and canyoning, where it is often called single rope technique as distinct from doubled-rope methods.
