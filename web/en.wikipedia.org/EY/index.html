Ernst & Young, doing business as EY since 2013, is a British multinational professional services partnership. It is one of the "Big Four" accounting firms, alongside Deloitte, PwC and KPMG, providing assurance (audit), tax, consulting, and strategy and transactions services to clients in more than 150 countries, with nearly 400,000 employees.

EY is one of the Big Four accounting firms.

History

EY's roots go back to 19th-century accounting firms founded by Arthur Young in Chicago and Alwin C. Ernst in Cleveland. Their firms merged with others over the decades, and in 1989 Ernst & Whinney merged with Arthur Young & Co. to form Ernst & Young. The firm rebranded globally as EY in 2013. Like its peers, EY operates as a network of legally separate member firms coordinated by a global entity, EY Global Limited.

Notable events

EY was the auditor of Lehman Brothers and faced civil fraud charges by the New York Attorney General over the bank's "Repo 105" accounting; it settled for $99 million in 2015. It was also auditor of Wirecard, whose 2020 collapse in Germany raised questions over EY's audits. In 2022–23 EY's global leadership pursued, then abandoned, a plan ("Project Everest") to split its audit and consulting businesses.
