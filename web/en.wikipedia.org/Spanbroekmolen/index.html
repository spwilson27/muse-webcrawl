Spanbroekmolen
Type: Windmill memorial
Location: Heuvelland, West Flanders, Belgium
Coordinates: near Kemmel

Spanbroekmolen is a former windmill site near the village of Kemmel in the municipality of Heuvelland, West Flanders, Belgium. The site is best known for its association with the First World War: the ground beneath and around the mill was the scene of intense mine warfare during the Battle of Messines in June 1917, and today the Spanbroekmolen crater — also known as the "Pool of Peace" — survives as a large water-filled mine crater preserved as a war memorial.

History

Before the war, Spanbroekmolen was a working corn mill standing on a modest ridge between Kemmel and Wijtschate (Wytschaete), overlooking the Ypres Salient. When the front line stabilised in late 1914, the mill stood just behind the German lines. German forces fortified the position, and the mill itself was destroyed in the fighting.

Battle of Messines

As part of the preparations for the Battle of Messines, British tunnelling companies of the Royal Engineers dug a deep mine beneath the German strongpoint at Spanbroekmolen. The mine was charged with approximately 41,000 kg (91,000 lb) of ammonal explosive. After delays — including a German camouflet explosion in March 1917 that damaged the gallery and killed several tunnellers — the mine was successfully re-driven and fired at 3:10 a.m. on 7 June 1917, in the opening minutes of the battle, along with 18 other mines along the Messines–Wytschaete ridge. The explosion obliterated the German position and left a crater roughly 76 metres (250 ft) in diameter.

Pool of Peace

After the war the crater filled with water and was preserved. The site was later acquired through the efforts of Lord Wakefield and associated with the Toc H movement, coming to be known as the "Pool of Peace". It remains a quiet memorial surrounded by farmland, located near other Messines sites such as the crater at Kruisstraat and the New Zealand Memorial at Messines Ridge. The nearby Spanbroekmolen British Cemetery contains the graves of soldiers killed in the area.
