Melodic death metal

Melodic death metal (also referred to as melodeath) is a subgenre of death metal that employs highly melodic guitar riffs, often borrowed from traditional heavy metal, and harmonized lead guitar lines. The genre emerged in the early to mid-1990s, primarily in Gothenburg, Sweden — giving rise to the term "Gothenburg metal" — with bands such as At the Gates, Dark Tranquillity and In Flames regarded as its pioneers.

Characteristics

Melodic death metal retains the speed, aggression and harsh vocals of death metal — typically high-pitched screams or mid-range growls rather than the low guttural style of many American bands — but adds prominent melody: twin-guitar harmonies reminiscent of Iron Maiden and other New Wave of British Heavy Metal acts, melodic solos, and sometimes keyboards and clean-sung choruses. Song structures tend to be more verse-chorus oriented than in traditional death metal.

History

The British band Carcass helped lay the groundwork with Heartwork (1993), while in Gothenburg, In Flames' The Jester Race (1996), Dark Tranquillity's The Gallery (1995) and At the Gates' Slaughter of the Soul (1995) defined the sound. Later acts such as Arch Enemy, Soilwork and Finland's Children of Bodom and Insomnium carried the style internationally. The genre strongly influenced American metalcore and the New Wave of American Heavy Metal in the 2000s.
