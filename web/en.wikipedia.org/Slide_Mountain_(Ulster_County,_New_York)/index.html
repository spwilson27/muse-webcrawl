Slide Mountain (Ulster County, New York)


Slide Mountain, the highest peak in the Catskills

Slide Mountain is the highest peak in the Catskill Mountains of the U.S. state of New York. It is located in the town of Shandaken in Ulster County. While the 4,180-foot (1,270 m) contour line on topographic maps is the accepted elevation, sources have variously listed its height as 4,180 feet or more, with some estimates as high as 4,204 feet (1,281 m).

Although it is not part of the Adirondacks or any other high peaks region, Slide is one of only two peaks in the state high enough to require a 2,000-foot (610 m) prominence to be considered separate mountains (the other being Hunter Mountain). It is considered one of the Catskill High Peaks, a list similar to those for other mountain ranges of the Northeast United States.

Geography

The mountain takes the form of a long ridge stretching northeast to southwest, with a broad, flat summit area. The headwaters of the East Branch of the Neversink River, part of the Delaware River watershed, rise on its eastern slopes. The West Branch of the Neversink rises on the western slopes. The mountain lies within the Slide Mountain Wilderness Area, the largest wilderness area in the Catskill Park, managed by the New York State Department of Environmental Conservation.

History

The mountain's name is thought to derive from a substantial landslide that occurred on its north face in the early 19th century. Naturalist John Burroughs, who lived nearby, popularized the mountain in his essays and often walked its slopes. In 1885, a survey determined Slide was the highest peak in the range, settling a long-standing dispute with Kaaterskill High Peak. A plaque at the summit commemorates Burroughs.

Hiking

Slide Mountain is a popular destination for peakbaggers. The most direct approach is from the trailhead on County Route 47 (Oliverea Road) via the Wittenberg-Cornell-Slide Trail, sometimes called the Burroughs Range Trail. The round trip is roughly 5.4 miles (8.7 km) with about 1,780 feet (540 m) of elevation gain. Climbers must ascend it to qualify for membership in the Catskill Mountain 3500 Club.
