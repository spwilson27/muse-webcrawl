The Mediterranean diet is a way of eating based on the traditional dietary patterns of the countries bordering the Mediterranean Sea, particularly Greece, southern Italy, and Spain as observed in the mid-20th century. It emphasizes plant foods — vegetables, fruits, legumes, whole grains, nuts and seeds — uses olive oil as the principal source of fat, includes moderate amounts of fish, dairy, and poultry, and limits red meat and sweets. In 2010 UNESCO inscribed the Mediterranean diet on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

History of the concept

The concept was popularized by the American physiologist Ancel Keys, whose Seven Countries Study, begun in 1958, associated the eating habits of Crete and other Mediterranean populations with low rates of coronary heart disease despite relatively high fat intake. Keys and his wife Margaret promoted the pattern in their bestselling book How to Eat Well and Stay Well the Mediterranean Way (1975). The diet pyramid developed in the 1990s by Oldways, the Harvard School of Public Health, and the World Health Organization helped codify the pattern for the public.

Health evidence

The Mediterranean diet has been studied in numerous observational studies and randomized trials. The Lyon Diet Heart Study reported reduced cardiac death and nonfatal myocardial infarction in patients assigned to a Mediterranean-style diet after a first heart attack. The large Spanish PREDIMED trial found that a Mediterranean diet supplemented with extra-virgin olive oil or nuts reduced the incidence of major cardiovascular events in high-risk persons by about 30 percent. Evidence also suggests benefits for type 2 diabetes, metabolic syndrome, and cognitive aging, though researchers debate how much reflects the diet itself versus associated lifestyles.

Components

Typical features include daily consumption of vegetables, fruits, whole grains, beans, and extra-virgin olive oil; fish and seafood at least twice a week; moderate poultry, eggs, cheese, and yogurt; rare red meat and sweets; water as the main beverage; and wine in moderation with meals for those who drink. Meals are traditionally shared and accompanied by physical activity as part of the overall Mediterranean lifestyle.
