Mountain warfare

Mountain warfare or alpine warfare is warfare conducted in mountains or similarly rough terrain. It is one of the most demanding forms of combat: operations are constrained by steep slopes, extreme weather, thin air at altitude, poor roads, and the difficulty of supplying and evacuating troops. Control of high ground offers observation and defensive advantage, but movement is channelled through predictable passes and valleys.

Challenges

Altitude above about 2,500–3,000 m causes altitude sickness and reduces physical performance; cold, wind, avalanches and rockfall add non-combat casualties. Vehicles are largely confined to valleys, so artillery, ammunition and food must often be moved by pack animal, porter or helicopter. Weather changes rapidly, communications are hampered by terrain, and engineering — roads, ropeways, tunnels — becomes critical.

History

Famous mountain campaigns include Hannibal's crossing of the Alps in 218 BC, fighting in the Italian Alps in the First World War (the "White War" between Austria-Hungary and Italy), the battles of Monte Cassino and the Vosges in the Second World War, the Soviet–Afghan War, and the 1999 Kargil War between India and Pakistan, fought at extreme altitude in the Himalayas. Specialist troops include Germany's Gebirgsjäger, France's Chasseurs alpins, Italy's Alpini and the U.S. 10th Mountain Division.
