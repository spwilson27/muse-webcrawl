Yehud Medinata

Yehud Medinata
Achaemenid province (Judah)
Period: c. 538 – 332 BCE
Capital: Jerusalem
Languages: Aramaic, Hebrew

Yehud Medinata (Aramaic: 𐡉𐡄𐡃 𐡌𐡃𐡉𐡍𐡕𐡀, "Province of Judah") was a self-governing Jewish province of the Achaemenid Persian Empire, established after Cyrus the Great conquered Babylon in 539 BCE and permitted exiled peoples, including the Judahites, to return home.

History

The province, far smaller than the old Kingdom of Judah, comprised the hills around Jerusalem — roughly 1,500–2,000 square kilometres at most — and was administered from Mizpah at first, then Jerusalem. Returned exiles under Sheshbazzar and Zerubbabel rebuilt the Temple, dedicated in 515 BCE as the Second Temple. In the mid-5th century BCE the missions of Ezra and Nehemiah — attested in the biblical books bearing their names — rebuilt Jerusalem's walls and reformed the community around Torah observance.

Society and evidence

Yehud's population was modest, perhaps 20,000–30,000. It minted small silver coins inscribed yhd in Aramaic script, among the earliest coins of the southern Levant, and left distinctive stamp impressions on jar handles. Together with the Elephantine papyri from a related Jewish garrison in Egypt, the Yehud material illuminates the formation of Judaism in the Persian period — when the Torah reached something close to its final form and Jerusalem's temple priesthood dominated communal life.

The province ended with Alexander the Great's conquest (332 BCE), passing into the Hellenistic world as Judaea.
