The Fletcher class was a class of destroyers built by the United States during World War II. The class of 175 ships was the largest destroyer class ordered, and one of the most successful. Designed in 1939 as naval treaties lapsed, the Fletchers were bigger and more capable than their predecessors, with a flush deck, five 5-inch/38 dual-purpose guns, ten torpedo tubes in two quintuple mounts, and machinery giving 60,000 shp and about 35 knots.

Service

The lead ship, USS Fletcher (DD-445), was commissioned on 30 June 1942. Fletchers fought throughout the Pacific — in the Solomons, at the Battle of Surigao Strait, and as radar pickets off Okinawa, where they bore the brunt of kamikaze attacks. Nineteen were lost in the war. Members of the class such as USS Johnston and USS Heermann won fame at the Battle off Samar in October 1944.

Postwar

Many Fletchers served in the Korean War; some were modernised under FRAM programmes, while dozens were transferred to allied navies — including Germany, Japan, Italy, Spain, Brazil, and Mexico — some remaining in service into the 2000s. Four survive as museum ships in the United States: The Sullivans in Buffalo, Kidd in Baton Rouge, Cassin Young in Boston, and Fletcher class representative Velos in Greece represents the class abroad.
