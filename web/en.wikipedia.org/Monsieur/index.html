Monsieur

Monsieur

Pronunciation: /məˈsjɜːr/ (French: [məsjø])
Abbreviation: M.
Plural: Messieurs (MM.)
Female equivalent: Madame

Monsieur (pronounced [məsjø]) is an honorific title that is used in the French language to address or refer to a man, equivalent to the English "Mister" or "Sir". The word derives from the possessive adjective mon ("my") and sieur, an archaic form of seigneur ("lord"). Its standard abbreviation is M., and the plural is Messieurs, abbreviated MM.

Usage

In contemporary French, monsieur is the customary polite form of address for an adult man, used in conversation, correspondence, and official contexts, often before a surname or a professional title (for example, "Monsieur le Président"). Historically, under the French monarchy, "Monsieur" was also the specific style of the eldest brother of the King of France, most famously Philippe I, Duke of Orléans, brother of Louis XIV, who was universally known simply as "Monsieur".

In other languages

The term has been borrowed into various other languages and contexts, sometimes as a marker of Frenchness or refinement. In English literary usage it may appear when quoting French speech or referring to French gentlemen.
