Padé approximant
FieldNumerical analysis, complex analysis
Introduced byHenri Padé (1892 thesis)

In mathematics, a Padé approximant is the "best" approximation of a function near a point by a rational function of given order. The technique was developed around 1890 by the French mathematician Henri Padé in his doctoral thesis, advised by Charles Hermite.

Definition

Given a function f and two integers m ≥ 0 and n ≥ 1, the Padé approximant of order [m/n] is the rational function whose power series expansion agrees with that of f to the highest possible order — through the term of degree m + n. It can be computed by solving a linear system derived from the Taylor coefficients of f, and is expressible as a ratio of determinants.

Properties and applications

Padé approximants frequently converge where Taylor series diverge, and they can capture poles and other singular behaviour that polynomials cannot. They are organized in the Padé table, of which the Taylor series forms the first column. Applications include numerical evaluation of special functions, summation of divergent series, solving differential equations, and stability analysis in control theory, where they give rational approximations of time delays.

For the exponential function, the diagonal Padé approximants are related to continued fractions and are used in the scaling-and-squaring method for computing matrix exponentials.
