Chamber (firearms)

In firearms, the chamber is the cavity at the back end of the barrel where a cartridge is inserted before firing. When the weapon is discharged, the cartridge case expands to seal the chamber (obturation), and the projectile is driven down the barrel while the chamber and breech contain the pressure of the burning propellant.


Cartridge seated in a chamber

Design

Chambers are machined to dimensions specific to each cartridge type, standardised by bodies such as SAAMI and the CIP; firing a cartridge in a chamber of the wrong dimensions is dangerous. Key dimensions include headspace — the distance controlling how the cartridge seats — and the leade or throat where the rifling begins. Revolvers have multiple chambers in a rotating cylinder, one aligned with the barrel at a time, whereas most other firearms have a single chamber integral with the barrel.

Chambering

"Chambering" also denotes the act of loading a round into the chamber, and, in gun design, the cartridge for which a firearm is made — as in "a rifle chambered in .308 Winchester". Special chamber features include fluting to ease extraction in some blowback designs.
