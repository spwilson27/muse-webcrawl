United States National Security Council

The United States National Security Council (NSC) is the principal forum used by the President of the United States for deliberating national security, military and foreign policy matters with senior advisers and cabinet officials. It was established by the National Security Act of 1947 and sits within the Executive Office of the President.

Membership

The Council is chaired by the President. Its statutory members include the Vice President, the Secretaries of State, Defense, Energy and the Treasury, and the Assistant to the President for National Security Affairs (the National Security Advisor), who runs the NSC staff. The Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff and the Director of National Intelligence serve as statutory advisers. Meetings are often held in the White House Situation Room.

Role and history

The NSC's influence has varied by administration, depending on the President's style and the standing of the National Security Advisor; prominent advisors have included Henry Kissinger, Zbigniew Brzezinski and Brent Scowcroft. The Council gained notoriety during the Iran–Contra affair in the 1980s, when members of its staff were found to have run covert operations, prompting reforms to keep the staff in a coordinating rather than operational role.
