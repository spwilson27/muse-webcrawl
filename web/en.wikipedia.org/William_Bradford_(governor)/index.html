William Bradford (governor)

William Bradford (c. 19 March 1590 – 9 May 1657) was an English Puritan Separatist who emigrated to Plymouth Colony in New England aboard the Mayflower in 1620 and served as the colony's governor for most of the period between 1622 and 1656. His journal, Of Plymouth Plantation, is the principal contemporary history of the colony's early years.

Early life

Born in Austerfield, Yorkshire, Bradford joined the Separatist congregation at Scrooby as a young man. When the congregation fled persecution to the Netherlands, he settled in Leiden, working as a cloth maker. In 1620 he sailed with the group, later called the Pilgrims, aboard the Mayflower; his wife Dorothy died in an apparent accident shortly after arrival in America.

Governor of Plymouth Colony

After the first governor, John Carver, died in April 1621, Bradford was elected in his place and re-elected almost thirty times. He presided over the colony's dealings with the Wampanoag under Massasoit — a peace that lasted for decades — the division of land, the paying off of the colony's London debts, and the harvest feast of 1621 later celebrated as the first Thanksgiving.

Of Plymouth Plantation

Between 1630 and 1651 Bradford wrote his history of the colony, including the famous passenger list of the Mayflower. The manuscript disappeared during the American Revolutionary War, was rediscovered in the library of the Bishop of London at Fulham Palace in the 1850s, and was published in 1856; Massachusetts formally received it back in 1897.
