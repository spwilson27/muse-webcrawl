Winchester Cathedral is a Church of England cathedral in Winchester, Hampshire, and the seat of the Bishop of Winchester. With an overall length of about 170 metres it is the longest medieval cathedral in Europe, and its architecture spans from Norman to Perpendicular Gothic.

History

A minster church stood on the site from 642. The present cathedral was begun in 1079 under Bishop Walkelin in the Norman style; its massive transepts and crypt survive from this phase. The tower collapsed in 1107 and was rebuilt. Later bishops, notably William of Wykeham, remodelled the nave in the Perpendicular style in the late 14th century.

People and treasures

The cathedral holds the tombs and mortuary chests of Anglo-Saxon and Norman kings, including bones attributed to Cnut and William Rufus. Jane Austen is buried in the north aisle; she died in Winchester in 1817. It also preserves the 12th-century Winchester Bible, one of the finest illuminated Bibles in England. In the early 20th century the building was famously saved from collapse by the diver William Walker, who underpinned its waterlogged foundations between 1906 and 1911.
