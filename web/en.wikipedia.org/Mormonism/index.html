Mormonism is the religious tradition and theology of the Latter Day Saint movement of Restorationist Christianity started by Joseph Smith in Western New York in the 1820s and 1830s. The movement is today represented principally by the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (LDS Church), headquartered in Salt Lake City, Utah, along with smaller denominations such as Community of Christ and various fundamentalist groups.

History

Smith reported that in 1820 he received a theophany (the First Vision) and that in 1827 an angel named Moroni delivered to him a set of golden plates, which he translated and published in 1830 as the Book of Mormon. He organized the Church of Christ that same year. Persecution drove the Saints from New York to Ohio, Missouri, and Illinois, where the city of Nauvoo was founded. Smith and his brother Hyrum were killed by a mob in 1844, precipitating a succession crisis. The largest body followed Brigham Young west to the Salt Lake Valley in 1847.

Beliefs

Mormons regard the Book of Mormon as another testament of Jesus Christ alongside the Bible, believe in continuing revelation through a living prophet, and hold distinctive doctrines including a pre-mortal existence, an embodied God the Father, exaltation, temple ordinances such as baptism for the dead and eternal marriage, and a health code known as the Word of Wisdom. Most other Christian churches regard Mormon teaching as outside the historic Christian tradition, while Mormons describe themselves as Christians restoring the original church.

Demographics and culture

The LDS Church reports more than 17 million members worldwide, with a strong presence in the Intermountain West of the United States. Mormon culture emphasizes family life, missionary service by young adults, genealogical research, food storage, and education through institutions such as Brigham Young University.
