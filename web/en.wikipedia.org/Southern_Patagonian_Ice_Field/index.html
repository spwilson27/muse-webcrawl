Southern Patagonian Ice Field

The Southern Patagonian Ice Field (Spanish: Campo de Hielo Patagónico Sur) is the second largest contiguous extrapolar ice field in the world. It is the bigger of two remnant parts of the Patagonian Ice Sheet, which covered all of southern Chile during the last glacial period. It lies in the Southern Andes, straddling the border between Argentina and Chile, and extends roughly 350 kilometres (220 miles) from north to south, covering about 16,800 square kilometres.

Geography

Dozens of outlet glaciers flow from the ice field, including the Perito Moreno, Upsala, and Viedma glaciers on the Argentine side flowing toward Lago Argentino, and the Pío XI (Brüggen) Glacier — one of the few advancing glaciers in the world — on the Chilean side. Much of the ice field is protected within Los Glaciares National Park (Argentina, a UNESCO World Heritage Site) and Bernardo O'Higgins National Park (Chile).

Border dispute and climate

A section of the international border across the ice field remained undefined for decades after the 1881 treaty; a 1998 agreement largely settled the line, though one sector's final demarcation has been slow. Like most temperate ice fields, the Southern Patagonian Ice Field is losing mass under regional warming, contributing measurably to global sea-level rise; a few glaciers, notably Perito Moreno and Pío XI, have been roughly stable or advancing.
