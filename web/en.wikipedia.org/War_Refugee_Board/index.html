War Refugee Board

The War Refugee Board, established by President Franklin D. Roosevelt on 22 January 1944, was a United States government agency created to aid civilian victims of the Axis powers, and in practice to rescue Jews from the Nazi Holocaust. Though created late in the war, it is credited with saving the lives of tens of thousands — by some estimates up to 200,000 — Jews.

Background

For much of the war, American policy restricted immigration, and rescue efforts were hampered by bureaucratic obstruction, particularly within the State Department. In January 1944, Treasury Secretary Henry Morgenthau Jr. presented Roosevelt with a document bluntly titled “Report to the Secretary on the Acquiescence of This Government in the Murder of the Jews”, prepared by Treasury officials including John Pehle. Days later Roosevelt signed Executive Order 9417 creating the Board.

Activities

Directed by Pehle, the Board worked with Jewish organisations such as the Joint Distribution Committee and the World Jewish Congress, as well as neutral governments and diplomats. It funded rescue operations in Romania, Hungary and elsewhere, pressed neutral countries to accept refugees, and supported the work of Raoul Wallenberg, the Swedish diplomat whose protective passports saved thousands of Budapest Jews. The Board also helped establish the Fort Ontario Emergency Refugee Shelter in Oswego, New York, the only refugee camp in the United States during the war, and promoted the dissemination of the Vrba–Wetzler report on Auschwitz.

Assessment

Historians regard the Board as a belated but effective intervention, demonstrating that more substantial rescue had been possible earlier. It was wound down in September 1945 after the end of the war in Europe.
