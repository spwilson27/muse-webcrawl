Ionian Sea

TypeMarginal sea of the Mediterranean
LocationBetween Italy, Albania and Greece
Max. depth5,267 m (17,280 ft) — Calypso Deep
IslandsIonian Islands (Corfu, Kefalonia, Zakynthos, Lefkada, Ithaca, Paxos, Kythira)
Connects viaStrait of Otranto (to the Adriatic)

The Ionian Sea is an elongated bay of the Mediterranean Sea. It is connected to the Adriatic Sea to the north by the Strait of Otranto, and is bounded by Southern Italy (including Calabria, Sicily and the Salento peninsula) to the west, and by Albania and Greece — including the Ionian Islands — to the east.

Geography

The sea stretches from the Strait of Messina and the eastern coast of Sicily in the southwest to the entrance of the Gulf of Corinth in the east. Its deepest point, the Calypso Deep in the Hellenic Trench southwest of Pylos, at about 5,267 metres, is the deepest point in the Mediterranean Sea. Important gulfs include the Gulf of Taranto in Italy and the Ambracian Gulf and Gulf of Patras in Greece.

Geology

The Ionian basin overlies one of the oldest pieces of oceanic crust in the Mediterranean and sits near the boundary where the African Plate subducts beneath the Aegean Sea Plate along the Hellenic arc — a zone of significant seismic activity that has produced strong earthquakes around Kefalonia and Zakynthos.

History and transport

In antiquity the sea was the main route between Greece and the Greek colonies of Magna Graecia in Italy. The Battle of Actium (31 BC) was fought near its eastern shore. Today ferry routes link Italy (Bari, Brindisi, Ancona, Venice) with Greece (Igoumenitsa, Patras, Corfu), and the Ionian Islands are major tourist destinations.
