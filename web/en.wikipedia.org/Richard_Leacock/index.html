Born: 18 July 1921, London, England
Died: 23 March 2011, Paris, France
Occupation: Documentary filmmaker

Richard Leacock

Richard Leacock (18 July 1921 – 23 March 2011) was a British-born documentary filmmaker who became a leading figure in the American Direct Cinema movement, pioneering lightweight synchronised-sound filming that let documentaries observe events as they unfolded.

Early work

Leacock shot his first film at age 14 in the Canary Islands and worked as a cameraman on Robert Flaherty's Louisiana Story (1948). During the Second World War he served as a combat cameraman. In the 1950s he worked for television and with Drew Associates.

Direct Cinema

Frustrated by bulky equipment, Leacock helped develop portable 16mm cameras with sync sound, enabling classics such as Primary (1960), about the Kennedy–Humphrey Wisconsin primary, and Crisis (1963). With D. A. Pennebaker and others he formed Leacock-Pennebaker, making films including Monterey Pop (1968). His own directorial works include Happy Mother's Day (1963) and A Stravinsky Portrait (1966).

Later life

From 1969 he taught at MIT, founding its film/video section, and championed small-format video. He later lived in Paris and made autobiographical films, including Les oeufs à la coque de Richard Leacock (1991).
