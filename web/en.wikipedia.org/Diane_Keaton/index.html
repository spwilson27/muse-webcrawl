Diane Keaton (born Diane Hall; January 5, 1946 – October 11, 2025) was an American actress known for her collaborations with Woody Allen and for roles spanning comedy and drama over a career of more than five decades.

Career

Keaton rose to prominence playing Kay Adams-Corleone in Francis Ford Coppola's The Godfather (1972) and its sequels. She won the Academy Award for Best Actress for her role as the title character in Woody Allen's Annie Hall (1977), whose menswear-inspired fashion made her a style icon. She received further Oscar nominations for Reds (1981), Marvin's Room (1996), and Something's Gotta Give (2003).

Other work

Beyond acting, Keaton directed the documentary Heaven (1987) and the feature Hanging Up (2000), and published memoirs including Then Again (2011). She was also known for her work in architecture and historic home preservation in Los Angeles.
