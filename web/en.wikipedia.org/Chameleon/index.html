Chameleon

Chameleons (family Chamaeleonidae) are a distinctive clade of Old World lizards, with about 200 species known. They are famous for their ability to change skin colour, their independently mobile eyes, their extremely long, fast-extending tongues, and their grasping zygodactylous feet and prehensile tails.

Characteristics

Chameleons range from a few centimetres long (the tiny Brookesia leaf chameleons) to about 70 cm in the largest species. Their eyes move independently and provide a full 360-degree field of view. The ballistic tongue, powered by an accelerator muscle and elastic collagen, can exceed the body length and strike prey in hundredths of a second.

Colour change

Colour change is driven by specialised skin cells — chromatophores and iridophores containing guanine nanocrystals — and serves camouflage, communication and temperature regulation rather than simple background matching alone. Males often display bright patterns to rivals and mates.

Distribution

Roughly half of all species live on Madagascar; others occur across mainland Africa, with a few in southern Europe, the Middle East and South Asia, and introduced populations in Hawaii and Florida. Most are arboreal insectivores of rainforest and savanna habitats.
