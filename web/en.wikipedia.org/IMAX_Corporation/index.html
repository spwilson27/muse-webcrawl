IMAX Corporation is a Canadian theatre company which designs and manufactures IMAX cameras and projection systems as well as performing film development, production, post-production and distribution to IMAX-affiliated theatres worldwide. It was founded in Canada in 1967 as Multiscreen Corporation. As of 2023, there are around 1,700 IMAX theatres in 87 countries.

History

IMAX traces its origins to Expo 67 in Montreal, where filmmakers Graeme Ferguson, Roman Kroitor and Robert Kerr produced the multi-screen film In the Labyrinth. Their company developed a new single-projector large-format system using 70mm film running horizontally — the 15/70 format — that could fill a much larger screen than conventional cinema. The first permanent IMAX theatre opened in 1971 at Ontario Place in Toronto, showing the film North of Superior.

For its first decades IMAX theatres were mostly in museums and science centres, showing purpose-made documentaries. From the late 1990s, the company expanded into commercial multiplexes, using its Digital Media Remastering (DMR) process to blow up Hollywood features, and later digital and laser projection systems. Blockbusters shot partly with IMAX cameras — including Christopher Nolan's The Dark Knight (2008) and later films — established IMAX as a premium theatrical format.

Technology

Classic IMAX film uses 70mm stock transported horizontally, giving a frame about ten times the area of standard 35mm film. IMAX with Laser projection uses dual 4K laser projectors with brighter images and expanded colour. IMAX theatres typically feature steeply raked seating and very large, slightly curved screens — the largest IMAX screen, in Leonberg, Germany, is about 38.8 metres wide.
