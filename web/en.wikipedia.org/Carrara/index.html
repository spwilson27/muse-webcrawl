Carrara is a city and comune in Tuscany, central Italy, in the province of Massa and Carrara. It lies at the foot of the Apuan Alps, close to the Ligurian Sea. The city is world-famous for its white marble, quarried in the surrounding mountains since Roman times and used in countless celebrated buildings and sculptures.

History

The area around Carrara was settled in ancient times, with the marble quarries exploited systematically from the Roman era onward. During the Middle Ages the town came under the rule of various powers, including Pisa, Genoa, and Milan. From the sixteenth century it was ruled by the Cybo-Malaspina family, under whom the duchy of Massa and Carrara took shape. Carrara later became part of the Duchy of Modena and then of unified Italy in the nineteenth century.

Carrara marble

Carrara marble is a white or blue-grey metamorphic rock prized for sculpture and building decoration. Michelangelo travelled to Carrara personally to select blocks for works such as the David and the Pietà. Roman monuments including the Pantheon's portico and Trajan's Column made use of Carrara stone, and marble from the Apuan quarries continues to be exported worldwide.

Main sights

  • Carrara Cathedral (Duomo), begun in the eleventh century, with a Romanesque-Gothic façade in white and dark marble.
  • The Palazzo Cybo-Malaspina, now home to the Academy of Fine Arts.
  • The marble quarries of the Apuan Alps, including the Fantiscritti and Colonnata basins.

Economy

Marble extraction and processing remain central to the local economy, alongside tourism and an annual international marble technology fair. The nearby port of Marina di Carrara ships stone around the world.
