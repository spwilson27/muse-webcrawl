MeaningWithin the peritoneal cavity
ContrastRetroperitoneal — behind the peritoneum, covered only on the anterior surface

Intraperitoneal means situated or occurring within the peritoneal cavity, the potential space between the parietal and visceral layers of the peritoneum in the abdomen. Abdominal organs that are almost completely invested by visceral peritoneum and suspended by a mesentery are described as intraperitoneal, in contrast to retroperitoneal organs, which lie behind the peritoneum and are covered by it only on their front surface.

Intraperitoneal organs are suspended within the peritoneal cavity by mesenteries carrying their vessels and nerves.

Classification of organs

Fully intraperitoneal organs include the stomach, the first part of the duodenum, the jejunum and ileum, the transverse and sigmoid colon, the liver and gallbladder, the spleen, the tail of the pancreas, and, in the female pelvis, the uterus, Fallopian tubes, and ovaries. Retroperitoneal organs include the kidneys, adrenal glands, most of the pancreas and duodenum, the ascending and descending colon, the aorta, and the inferior vena cava. Some organs, such as the bladder and lower rectum, are subperitoneal. The distinction matters surgically: intraperitoneal organs are mobile and reached directly on opening the cavity, whereas retroperitoneal structures require dissection through or behind the peritoneum and their injuries may not cause generalised peritonitis.

Clinical uses of the term

  • Intraperitoneal injection — a common route for administering substances to laboratory animals; the large absorptive surface of the peritoneum gives rapid uptake into the portal and systemic circulations.
  • Intraperitoneal chemotherapy — instillation of anti-cancer drugs directly into the cavity, used notably in ovarian cancer and peritoneal carcinomatosis, sometimes heated (HIPEC).
  • Peritoneal dialysis — exchange of fluid across the peritoneum in kidney failure.
  • Intraperitoneal bleeding or rupture — for example rupture of an intraperitoneal bladder, which spills urine into the cavity, versus extraperitoneal rupture confined outside it.
