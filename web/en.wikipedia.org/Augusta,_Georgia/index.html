Augusta, Georgia

State: Georgia, United States
Founded: 1736
Known for: Masters Tournament; Fort Eisenhower

Augusta is a consolidated city-county on the central eastern border of the US state of Georgia, on the Savannah River across from South Carolina. Founded in 1736 by James Oglethorpe, it is one of Georgia's oldest cities.

History

Augusta served as Georgia's state capital from 1785 to 1795 and grew as a centre of the cotton trade, aided by the Augusta Canal. During the Civil War it housed major Confederate powder works.

Sport and economy

The city is internationally known for Augusta National Golf Club, home of the Masters Tournament each spring. Major employers include Fort Eisenhower (formerly Fort Gordon), a centre for US Army cyber and signals training, and the medical district anchored by Augusta University.
