Shanghai Tower

Shanghai Tower, the tallest building in China, in the Lujiazui district of Pudong.

The Shanghai Tower (Chinese: 上海中心大厦; pinyin: Shànghǎi Zhōngxīn Dàshà) is a 128-storey, 632-metre (2,073 ft) megatall skyscraper in Lujiazui, Pudong, Shanghai. It is the tallest building in China and was the world's second-tallest building upon completion, behind Dubai's Burj Khalifa. Construction began in 2008 and the building topped out in 2013; it opened in stages from 2015–2016.

Designed by the American firm Gensler, with Jun Xia as lead architect, the tower twists approximately 120 degrees as it rises, a form intended to reduce wind loads by about 24 percent. The building consists of nine cylindrical sections stacked on one another, enclosed by a triangular outer glass façade that creates a double skin. Between the inner and outer skins are atrium "sky lobbies" at intervals, functioning as public squares within the tower.

Features

The tower houses offices, retail, a hotel (the J Hotel, among the world's highest), and observation decks near the top, including one with a tuned mass damper visible to visitors. Its high-speed elevators, supplied by Mitsubishi, were among the fastest in the world. The building achieved LEED Platinum certification, with features including rainwater recycling, a combined cooling/heating/power system, and the insulating double façade.

Shanghai Tower completes a trio of supertall buildings in Lujiazui alongside the Jin Mao Tower (1999) and the Shanghai World Financial Center (2008).
