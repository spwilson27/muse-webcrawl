Tax haven

Tax haven
CharacteristicsVery low or zero tax rates; financial secrecy; light regulation
ExamplesCayman Islands, Bermuda, British Virgin Islands, Jersey, Luxembourg, Singapore

A tax haven is a country or jurisdiction offering foreign individuals and businesses minimal or zero tax liability in a politically and economically stable environment, often combined with limited disclosure of financial information. Tax havens are a central, and controversial, feature of the offshore financial system.

Characteristics

Common features include low or nominal taxes on foreign-source income, strong bank secrecy or corporate confidentiality, ease of incorporating shell companies and trusts, and a large financial-services sector relative to the local economy. Academics and the OECD distinguish havens by their use for tax avoidance and evasion, base erosion and profit shifting by multinationals, and secrecy.

Scale and impact

Estimates of wealth held offshore run to many trillions of dollars. Researchers such as Gabriel Zucman have estimated that around 8–10% of global household financial wealth is held in tax havens, costing governments substantial revenue. High-profile leaks — including the Panama Papers (2016) and Paradise Papers (2017) — exposed the use of offshore structures by politicians, celebrities, and corporations.

Countermeasures

International responses include the OECD's Common Reporting Standard for automatic exchange of tax information, the United States' FATCA law, anti-avoidance rules such as controlled foreign corporation regimes, and the 2021 agreement by over 130 jurisdictions on a 15% global minimum corporate tax.
