Bashkir language


Bashkir written in the Cyrillic script.

Bashkir (Latin script: Başqort tele, Cyrillic: Башҡорт теле) is a Turkic language belonging to the Kipchak branch. It is co-official with Russian in Bashkortostan, a republic of Russia, and is spoken by roughly 1.2–1.5 million people, mainly ethnic Bashkirs in Bashkortostan and neighboring regions including Tatarstan, Chelyabinsk, Orenburg, Tyumen, Sverdlovsk and Kurgan oblasts and Perm Krai.

Classification and history

Bashkir is closely related to Tatar, with which it shares many features, though the languages are distinguished by characteristic sound changes, such as Bashkir s corresponding to Tatar ç in some words and distinct vowel developments. Literary Bashkir developed in the early 20th century; before that, Bashkir writers used the Volga Turki literary language.

Writing system

Bashkir was historically written in the Arabic script. A Latin-based alphabet (Yañalif) was adopted in 1930, and the Cyrillic alphabet replaced it in 1940, with additional letters for sounds specific to Bashkir, including Ғғ, Ҙҙ, Ҡҡ, Ңң, Ҫҫ, Һһ, Әә, Өө, and Үү.

Grammar

Like other Turkic languages, Bashkir is agglutinative, has vowel harmony, lacks grammatical gender, and typically follows subject–object–verb word order. It has six cases for nouns and a rich system of verbal suffixes expressing tense, aspect, mood, and voice.
