Laetoli

The 3.66-million-year-old hominin footprint trails at Laetoli, Tanzania

Laetoli is a prehistoric site in Enduleni ward, Ngorongoro District, in Arusha Region, Tanzania, famous for its ancient hominin footprints preserved in volcanic ash. Located about 45 km south of Olduvai Gorge, the site provides some of the earliest evidence of bipedal locomotion in human ancestors.

The footprint trails

In 1978, a team led by Mary Leakey excavated a trail of footprints (Site G) made about 3.66 million years ago, during the Pliocene. Volcanic ash from the nearby Sadiman volcano had been wetted by rain into a surface like wet cement; animals and hominins walked across it, and a further fall of ash buried and preserved the prints. The main trail consists of two parallel tracks, made by two individuals walking side by side — one apparently stepping in the other's footprints — with a third individual possibly crossing later. The prints show a fully bipedal, heel-to-toe gait with a developed arch and non-divergent big toe, and are generally attributed to Australopithecus afarensis.

Additional trails reported in 2015 (Site S), including a notably tall individual nicknamed "Chewie", extended the evidence of size variation within the species.

Fossils

The site also yielded the first fossils assigned in 1978 to Australopithecus afarensis, including the type specimen LH 4, a mandible recovered by Mary Leakey's team in the 1970s, along with abundant animal fossils. To protect the fragile footprints, the Site G trail was reburied in the 1990s after full documentation with casts and photogrammetry.
