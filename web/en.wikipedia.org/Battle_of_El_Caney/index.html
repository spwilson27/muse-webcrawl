Battle of El Caney

Battle of El Caney
Date: 1 July 1898
Location: El Caney, Cuba
Result: American victory
Part of the Spanish–American War

The Battle of El Caney was fought on 1 July 1898 during the Spanish–American War. United States forces under Major General William Rufus Shafter attacked the Spanish-held village of El Caney, northeast of Santiago de Cuba, intending to secure the flank before the main assault on the San Juan Heights.

The battle

The Spanish position, held by about 520 men under General Joaquín Vara del Rey, centred on a small stone blockhouse fort called El Viso and a ring of trenches and barbed wire. Brigadier General Henry W. Lawton's division of some 6,600 American troops expected to take the village in a couple of hours, but the defenders, armed with smokeless-powder Mauser rifles, held out for most of the day. American artillery and Gatling guns pounded El Viso, and the position was finally stormed late in the afternoon. Vara del Rey and two of his sons were killed; American casualties numbered about 440.

Aftermath

The stubborn defence delayed Lawton's division, which arrived at the San Juan Heights too late to support the main attack there as planned. Nonetheless, the fall of El Caney and San Juan on the same day tightened the American grip on Santiago, which surrendered on 17 July. The Spanish stand at El Caney was widely admired even by American observers.
