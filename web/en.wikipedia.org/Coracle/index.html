Coracle

A coracle is a small, rounded, lightweight boat of traditional design, used on rivers principally in Wales, but also in parts of the West Country and Ireland, and in similar forms in parts of Asia. The word derives from the Welsh cwrwgl.

Construction

The vessel is made of a framework of split and interwoven willow rods tied with willow bark, covered traditionally with animal hides or calico sealed with tar or pitch to make it waterproof. The result is a shallow, bowl-shaped craft light enough to be carried on one person's back.

Use

Coracles are propelled with a single paddle used over the bow in a figure-of-eight motion, drawing the boat forward. They have been used for centuries for net fishing — traditionally working in pairs with a net strung between two coracles — and for transport on rivers such as the Tywi, Teifi and Dee.

Similar hide boats, such as the quffa of Iraq and the parisal of India, developed independently on other continents.
