Platform as a service

Platform as a service (PaaS) or application platform as a service (aPaaS) is a category of cloud computing services that allows customers to provision, instantiate, run, and manage a modular bundle comprising a computing platform and one or more applications, without the complexity of building and maintaining the infrastructure typically associated with developing and launching an application. PaaS offerings may include hosting, server software such as a web server, database management, storage, networking, and development tools.

Characteristics

In a PaaS model, the provider operates the hardware, operating systems, and runtime environment, while the customer deploys and controls their own applications and data. Platforms commonly provide automatic scaling, managed databases, continuous deployment from source control, and usage-based billing. PaaS sits between infrastructure as a service (IaaS), which rents raw computing resources, and software as a service (SaaS), which delivers finished applications.

History and examples

Early influential PaaS offerings included Google App Engine (launched 2008), Microsoft Azure, Heroku, and later platforms such as Cloud Foundry, OpenShift, and AWS Elastic Beanstalk. The model later influenced container platforms and serverless/function-as-a-service offerings, which further abstract the runtime.

Advantages and disadvantages

PaaS can accelerate development and reduce operational overhead, since developers need not patch servers or manage middleware. Downsides include reduced control over the underlying environment, possible vendor lock-in, and constraints on runtime versions, networking, and persistence that may not suit all workloads.
