START II

START II (Strategic Arms Reduction Treaty II) was a bilateral treaty between the United States and Russia on the further reduction and limitation of strategic offensive arms. Signed by Presidents George H. W. Bush and Boris Yeltsin in Moscow on 3 January 1993, it would have banned the use of multiple independently targetable reentry vehicles (MIRVs) on intercontinental ballistic missiles (ICBMs) — the weapons regarded as most destabilising in a first strike. The treaty never entered into force.

Bush and Yeltsin sign START II at the Kremlin, January 1993.

Provisions

START II required each side to reduce deployed strategic warheads to 3,000–3,500, with no more than 1,700–1,750 on submarine-launched ballistic missiles, and to eliminate all MIRVed ICBMs — including the Soviet SS-18 "heavy ICBMs" and the US Peacekeeper. A second phase, to be completed by 2003, would have removed MIRVs entirely from the remaining force.

Ratification and collapse

The US Senate ratified START II in January 1996. The Russian State Duma ratified it only in April 2000, and then linked its entry into force to US compliance with the Anti-Ballistic Missile Treaty. When the United States withdrew from the ABM Treaty in June 2002 to pursue missile defence, Russia declared itself no longer bound by START II. The two countries instead concluded the simpler Strategic Offensive Reductions Treaty (SORT, the "Moscow Treaty") in 2002, and later New START in 2010.
