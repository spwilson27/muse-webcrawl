Intercontinental ballistic missile

An intercontinental ballistic missile (ICBM) is a ballistic missile with a range greater than 5,500 kilometres (3,400 mi), designed primarily for nuclear weapons delivery. ICBMs are distinguished from shorter-range ballistic missiles by their ability to cross between continents; they form the land-based leg of the nuclear triads of the United States, Russia and China, and are also possessed by France (historically), the United Kingdom (historically, land-based), India and North Korea.

A Minuteman III ICBM test launch. Silo-based ICBMs can reach targets on other continents in about 30 minutes.

GuidanceInertial, with stellar or satellite updates on some types
WarheadsOne or more nuclear re-entry vehicles (MIRV)
BasingSilos, road-mobile and rail-mobile launchers

Flight phases

An ICBM flight has three phases. In the boost phase (3–5 minutes), rocket motors lift the missile out of the atmosphere to altitudes above 1,000 km. In the midcourse phase (about 20 minutes), the warheads and decoys coast along a ballistic trajectory through space toward their targets; a post-boost vehicle ("bus") can release multiple independently targetable re-entry vehicles (MIRVs). In the terminal phase (under 2 minutes), the re-entry vehicles plunge through the atmosphere at hypersonic speed to their targets.

History

The Soviet R-7 Semyorka, which also launched Sputnik in 1957, was the first ICBM, followed by the American Atlas and Titan. Early missiles were liquid-fuelled, inaccurate and kept above ground; later generations moved into hardened silos and adopted solid fuel for rapid launch. The U.S. Minuteman (1962) pioneered solid-fuel silo basing, while the Soviet Union fielded heavy missiles such as the SS-18. Strategic arms control treaties from SALT through New START have limited numbers of deployed ICBMs and their warheads. Mobile systems — the Russian Topol and Yars, China's DF-41 — complicate targeting and reflect a shift toward survivability.

Operators and modernisation

The United States maintains about 400 Minuteman III missiles and is developing the Sentinel (LGM-35) replacement. Russia fields the largest ICBM force, including the RS-28 Sarmat. China has rapidly expanded its silo fields and mobile launchers. India tests the Agni-V and Agni-VI class missiles, and North Korea has flight-tested the Hwasong series since 2017.
