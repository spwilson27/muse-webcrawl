Chhena

Chhena (also spelled chhana) is a fresh, unripened curd cheese originating in the Indian subcontinent, made by curdling hot milk with an acidic food such as lemon juice or vinegar and draining the whey. It is a cornerstone of Bengali and Odia confectionery.

Preparation

Milk — cow's milk gives a soft chhena, while buffalo milk yields a firmer curd — is brought to the boil, the acid is stirred in, and the curds are collected in muslin and hung to drain. The result is a moist, crumbly cheese that is kneaded until smooth for sweets.

Uses

Chhena is the base for many famous sweets of eastern India, including rasgulla, sandesh, rasmalai, cham cham and pantua. It is similar to paneer but is not pressed, giving it a softer, more granular texture.
