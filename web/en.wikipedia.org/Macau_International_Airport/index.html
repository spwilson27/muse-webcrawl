Macau International Airport (IATA: MFM, ICAO: VMMC) is the international airport serving Macau, a special administrative region of China. Located at the eastern end of Taipa Island, it opened in December 1995, four years before the transfer of sovereignty over Macau from Portugal to China, and remains the territory's only airport.

Facilities

The airport has an unusual layout imposed by the territory's limited land. Its single concrete runway, 16/34, is built on a narrow strip of reclaimed land in the sea and is connected to the apron and terminal on Taipa by two causeway taxiways. The runway is 3,360 metres long and can handle wide-body aircraft such as the Boeing 747, although apron congestion limits operations. The passenger terminal has an annual design capacity of about six million passengers, which was exceeded in peak years before the COVID-19 pandemic.

History and role

Before the airport opened, Macau depended on Hong Kong's airport and ferry links; a helipad at the Outer Harbour Ferry Terminal still connects to Hong Kong and Shenzhen by helicopter. Since opening, the airport has become an important gateway for visitors to Macau's casino resort industry and a transfer point for passengers travelling between mainland China and Taiwan during periods when direct cross-strait flights were restricted. Air Macau, the territory's flag carrier, is based there, and low-cost carriers connect Macau with destinations across East and Southeast Asia.

Expansion

Plans approved in the 2020s provide for land reclamation around the airport to enlarge the apron, taxiways and terminal facilities, with the aim of raising capacity and easing the constraints of the original sea-runway design. The airport is served by city buses, taxis and the Taipa line of the Macau Light Rapid Transit. See also the article on urban transport systems for comparison.
