The Association for the Advancement of Creative Musicians (AACM) is a non-profit organisation and musicians' collective founded in Chicago in 1965. Dedicated to nurturing original, experimental music rooted in the African-American tradition, it became one of the most influential collectives in the history of jazz and improvised music.

History

The AACM was founded by pianist Muhal Richard Abrams together with Jodie Christian, Steve McCall, Phil Cohran and others, growing out of Abrams's Experimental Band. Frustrated by the economics of the commercial music business, its members committed to self-determination: presenting their own concerts, composing original work, and teaching. The AACM School of Music, founded in 1969, has trained generations of young musicians free of charge.

Music and influence

AACM artists pioneered approaches that fused free jazz with theatre, African instrumentation, electronics, and extended composition. Members and associates have included the Art Ensemble of Chicago, Anthony Braxton, Henry Threadgill, Leroy Jenkins, and George Lewis, whose book A Power Stronger Than Itself chronicles the association. A New York chapter was established in 1983, and the organisation celebrated its 50th anniversary in 2015.
