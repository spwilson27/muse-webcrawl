Young's inequality for convolution

In mathematics, Young's inequality for convolution is a mathematical inequality about convolution, named after William Henry Young. It gives a bound on the norm of the convolution of two functions in terms of the norms of the individual functions.

Statement

If f belongs to Lp space and g belongs to Lq space, with exponents satisfying 1/p + 1/q = 1 + 1/r, then the convolution f ∗ g belongs to Lr and its norm satisfies ||f ∗ g||r ≤ ||f||p ||g||q. An important special case is that the convolution of two square-integrable functions is bounded and continuous.

Generalizations

The inequality extends to locally compact groups equipped with Haar measure, and the optimal constant for the Euclidean case was determined later by Beckner and by Brascamp and Lieb, giving a sharpened version of the inequality.
