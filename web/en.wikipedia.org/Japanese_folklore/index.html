Japanese folklore

Japanese folklore encompasses the folktales, legends, beliefs and supernatural beings of Japan, shaped by Shinto and Buddhist traditions and by regional oral storytelling. Well-known tales include Momotarō (the Peach Boy), Urashima Tarō, The Tale of the Bamboo Cutter and the grateful crane of Tsuru no Ongaeshi.

Supernatural beings

The spirit world of folklore is populated by kami (deities and spirits), yōkai (strange apparitions and monsters), and yūrei (ghosts). Famous yōkai include the kappa, a water creature fond of cucumbers; the tengu, long-nosed mountain goblins; the shape-shifting fox (kitsune) and raccoon dog (tanuki); and the oni, horned ogres. The scholar-collector Kunio Yanagita documented regional legends in works such as Tōno Monogatari (1910), and Lafcadio Hearn introduced Japanese ghost stories to Western readers in Kwaidan.

Folktale heroes and motifs

Heroes such as Momotarō, born from a giant peach, and Issun-bōshi, the one-inch boy, embody courage and cleverness. Common motifs include animal gratitude, magical transformation, and the passage of time in the otherworld, as in Urashima Tarō's visit to the Dragon Palace. Folklore continues to inspire manga, anime and video games, from GeGeGe no Kitarō to Studio Ghibli films.
