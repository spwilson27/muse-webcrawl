Dischord Records

Dischord Records is an American independent record label based in Washington, D.C., specializing in the underground punk music of the Washington, D.C. region. The label was co-founded in 1980 by Ian MacKaye and Jeff Nelson, frontmen of the hardcore punk band Minor Threat, initially to release their previous band the Teen Idles' record Minor Disturbance when no label would issue it.

History and ethos

Dischord became the centre of the Washington, D.C. hardcore scene, releasing records by Government Issue, Scream, Marginal Man, and later central bands of post-hardcore and emo such as Rites of Spring, Embrace, Fugazi, Jawbox, Shudder to Think, and Q and Not U. MacKaye and Nelson famously operated on a strict do-it-yourself ethic: no contracts, handshake agreements, 50/50 profit splits with artists, low record and concert prices, and refusal to sell out to major labels or merchandising.

The label is closely associated with the "straight edge" ethos and with the 1985 "Revolution Summer", when D.C. bands pushed punk toward more melodic and socially conscious forms, helping birth the genre later called emo.

Fugazi and beyond

MacKaye's band Fugazi (1987–2003) became the label's best-selling act, demonstrating that a band could tour the world and sell hundreds of thousands of records while keeping ticket prices at five dollars and eschewing major-label distribution. Dischord continues to release and reissue records, maintain an extensive mail-order catalogue, and document D.C. punk history, and it remains entirely independent and owned by its founders.
