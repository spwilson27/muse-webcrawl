William M. "Trip" Hawkins III (born December 28, 1953) is an American entrepreneur and the founder of Electronic Arts, The 3DO Company, and Digital Chocolate.

Early life and career

Hawkins was a strategy and board-game enthusiast as a youth, designing his own games as a teenager. He graduated from Harvard University cum laude with a self-designed major in Strategy and Applied Game Theory, and later earned an MBA from Stanford University. He joined Apple Computer in 1978, when the company had only about fifty employees, and helped grow its office-product strategy; by the time he left in 1982, Apple had over 4,000 employees.

Electronic Arts

In 1982 Hawkins founded Amazin' Software, soon renamed Electronic Arts (EA). He championed the idea of treating game designers as "software artists", presenting them like rock stars in EA's advertising and packaging. Under Hawkins, EA pioneered direct distribution to retailers and a creator-focused publishing model, releasing influential early titles such as M.U.L.E. and Pinball Construction Set. He served as EA's chairman and CEO until 1991.

The 3DO Company

Hawkins left EA to found the 3DO Company, which licensed the design of the 3DO Interactive Multiplayer console to manufacturers such as Panasonic and GoldStar. Launched in 1993 at a high price of $699, the console failed to compete with cheaper rivals, and 3DO pivoted to software before going bankrupt in 2003.

Later ventures

In 2003 Hawkins founded Digital Chocolate, a developer of games for mobile phones and social platforms, serving as its CEO until 2012. He was inducted into the Academy of Interactive Arts & Sciences Hall of Fame in 2005.
