Home Office

The Home Office (HO) is a ministerial department of the Government of the United Kingdom, responsible for immigration, security, and law and order. It is headed by the Secretary of State for the Home Department — the Home Secretary, one of the Great Offices of State. The department's remit covers policing in England and Wales, borders and visas, passports, counter-terrorism, drugs policy, and crime prevention. Its headquarters are at 2 Marsham Street in Westminster, London.

History

The Home Office was created on 27 March 1782, when the old Southern Department was reorganised: its domestic responsibilities became the Home Office under the first Home Secretary, the Earl of Shelburne, while foreign affairs went to the new Foreign Office. The early Home Office oversaw a miscellany of domestic business — the militia, royal petitions, and public order — and accumulated functions through the 19th century: the Metropolitan Police (from 1829), factory inspection, prisons, and the registration of births and deaths. Over time many duties were hived off to new departments (health, education, labour), leaving the department focused on crime, policing, and immigration. The Home Secretary also retains a constitutional role in relation to the Channel Islands and the Isle of Man.

Responsibilities

The department's core responsibilities include: policing policy and funding in England and Wales and oversight of His Majesty's Inspectorate of Constabulary; counter-terrorism strategy (CONTEST) and oversight connected with the Security Service (MI5), which is formally accountable to the Home Secretary; immigration and nationality, including the visa system, asylum, and citizenship through UK Visas and Immigration; border security through Border Force; passports through HM Passport Office; fire and rescue services in England; drugs and alcohol policy; and the prevention of crime, extremism, and serious and organised crime, working with the National Crime Agency. Notable controversies of recent decades include the Windrush scandal (revealed in 2018), in which British subjects were wrongly detained and deported, leading to the resignation of Home Secretary Amber Rudd.

Organisation and reputation

The Home Office is one of the largest Whitehall departments, employing tens of thousands of staff across the core department and its agencies. It has a reputation as a "graveyard" for ministers: in 2006 Home Secretary John Reid famously described parts of it as "not fit for purpose" during a crisis over foreign prisoners, prompting a split in 2007 that moved prisons and probation to the new Ministry of Justice. Home Secretaries are among the most senior cabinet figures — holders have included Robert Peel (founder of the modern police), Winston Churchill, Roy Jenkins, Theresa May (the longest-serving of modern times, 2010–16), and Priti Patel.
