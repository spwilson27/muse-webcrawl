Special collections

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Rare books in a special collections reading room

In library and archival science, special collections are libraries or library units that house materials requiring special treatment because of their rarity, fragility, uniqueness, or value, or because they form a distinct, deeply researched collection on a subject. Special collections typically include rare books, manuscripts, archives, maps, prints, photographs, and ephemera.

Management

Unlike general circulating collections, special collections materials are usually non-circulating and consulted in supervised reading rooms, with controlled temperature, humidity, and security. Librarians and archivists in these units acquire, catalogue (often with rare-book standards such as DCRM), preserve, and provide reference and instruction services, and increasingly digitise holdings for wider access.

Examples

Well-known special collections include the British Library's treasures, the Folger Shakespeare Library, and university rare-book libraries such as Harvard's Houghton Library and Yale's Beinecke Library.
