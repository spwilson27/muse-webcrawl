Oregon is a state in the Pacific Northwest region of the United States. It is bordered by Washington to the north, Idaho to the east, Nevada and California to the south, and the Pacific Ocean to the west. Oregon's capital is Salem; its most populous city is Portland.

Geography

Oregon has a diverse landscape, from the rainy Pacific coast and the temperate rainforests of the Coast Range, through the fertile Willamette Valley and the volcanic Cascade Range — including Mount Hood, the state's highest point — to the dry high desert of eastern Oregon. The Columbia River forms most of its northern border, and the Columbia River Gorge is a noted scenic corridor. Crater Lake, in the south-central Cascades, is the deepest lake in the United States.

History

Indigenous peoples inhabited the region for thousands of years. The Lewis and Clark Expedition reached the Pacific at the Columbia's mouth in 1805, and the jointly occupied Oregon Country was the destination of the Oregon Trail migration in the 1840s. The Oregon Treaty of 1846 settled the boundary with British North America at the 49th parallel; Oregon Territory followed in 1848, and statehood came in 1859.

Economy

Timber, fishing and agriculture were historically dominant. Oregon remains a major producer of timber, hazelnuts, wine and grass seed. The Portland metropolitan area hosts the "Silicon Forest" cluster of technology companies, and Nike, headquartered near Beaverton, is the state's largest company. Oregon levies no sales tax.
