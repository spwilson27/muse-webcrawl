Braconidae

The Braconidae are a family of parasitoid wasps in the order Hymenoptera. With roughly 20,000 described species in over 1,000 genera — and true diversity estimated at many times that — they form the second-largest family in the Hymenoptera after their sister family, the Ichneumonidae. Braconids are found worldwide and are among the most important natural enemies of insect pests.

Biology

Most braconids are parasitoids of the larval stages of Lepidoptera, Coleoptera, Diptera and other insects. The female wasp lays eggs in or on the host with her ovipositor; the larvae develop inside the living host (endoparasitism) or externally (ectoparasitism), eventually killing it. Many species inject polydnaviruses along with their eggs, which suppress the host's immune system and regulate its development — one of the most remarkable symbioses known in insects. Well-known genera include Cotesia, Aphidius and Bracon.

Ecological and economic importance

Because so many braconids attack crop pests such as caterpillars, aphids and beetle larvae, they are widely used in biological control programmes. Cotesia congregata, a parasitoid of the tobacco hornworm, is also a model organism in education and research.

Classification

The family is divided into dozens of subfamilies, including Braconinae, Microgastrinae, Aphidiinae, Opiinae and Alysiinae. Molecular studies continue to revise the internal classification of this enormous group.
