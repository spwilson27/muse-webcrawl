Biocentrism

Biocentrism (from Greek βίος bios, "life" and κέντρον kentron, "center"), in a political and ecological sense, as well as in a spiritual sense, is an ethical point of view that extends inherent value to all living things. It is an understanding of how the earth works, particularly as it relates to biodiversity. It stands in contrast to anthropocentrism, which centers on the value of humans only.

Biocentrism extends intrinsic value to all living organisms.

In environmental ethics

Biocentric ethics holds that every living organism has moral standing in its own right, independent of its usefulness to humans. Philosophers such as Paul W. Taylor, in Respect for Nature (1986), developed a systematic biocentric egalitarianism in which all organisms are "teleological centers of life" pursuing their own good. Albert Schweitzer's ethic of "reverence for life" is an earlier expression of the same outlook.

Biocentrism in cosmology

The term has also been used by physician and scientist Robert Lanza for a speculative theory, presented in his 2009 book Biocentrism: How Life and Consciousness are the Keys to Understanding the True Nature of the Universe, proposing that life and consciousness are fundamental to the universe and create reality rather than merely arising within it. This version of biocentrism is regarded by mainstream physicists and philosophers as untestable and has been widely criticized as a misreading of quantum mechanics.

Related concepts

Biocentrism is closely related to ecocentrism, which extends value to whole ecosystems and species, and to deep ecology, which advocates a radical restructuring of human societies in light of the intrinsic value of nature.
