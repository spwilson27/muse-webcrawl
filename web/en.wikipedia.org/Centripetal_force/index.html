Centripetal force

Overview
FormulaF = mv²/r
DirectionToward the centre of curvature of the path
Term coined byIsaac Newton (from Latin centrum and petere, "to seek")

A centripetal force (from Latin centrum, "center" and petere, "to seek") is a force that makes a body follow a curved path. It always acts orthogonal to the motion of the body and towards the fixed point of the instantaneous center of curvature of the path. Isaac Newton described it as "a force by which bodies are drawn or impelled, or in any way tend, towards a point as to a centre". In Newtonian mechanics, gravity provides the centripetal force causing astronomical orbits.

Formula

For a body of mass m moving at tangential speed v along a path of radius of curvature r, the magnitude of the centripetal acceleration and force are:

a = v²/r,    F = ma = mv²/r

Common examples of centripetal force include the tension in a rope swinging a tethered ball, friction between tyres and road for a turning car, the normal force on a roller coaster loop, and the gravitational attraction keeping a satellite in orbit. Centripetal force should not be confused with the centrifugal force, a fictitious force that appears in rotating reference frames.
