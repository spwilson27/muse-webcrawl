Russell–Einstein Manifesto

The Russell–Einstein Manifesto was a statement issued in London on 9 July 1955 by the philosopher Bertrand Russell, in the midst of the Cold War. It highlighted the dangers posed by nuclear weapons and called for world leaders to seek peaceful resolutions to international conflict. It was signed by eleven eminent scientists and intellectuals, most prominently Albert Einstein, who signed it on 11 April 1955, a week before his death — his last public act.

Contents

The manifesto argued that a war fought with hydrogen bombs might end the human race, and that no side could hope for victory in such a conflict. It appealed to governments to acknowledge publicly that war could no longer serve their aims, and to scientists to meet in conference to assess the perils. Its most quoted passage reads:

"Remember your humanity, and forget the rest. If you can do so, the way lies open to a new Paradise; if you cannot, there lies before you the risk of universal death."

The signatories were Max Born, Percy W. Bridgman, Albert Einstein, Leopold Infeld, Frédéric Joliot-Curie, Herman J. Muller, Linus Pauling, Cecil F. Powell, Józef Rotblat, Bertrand Russell and Hideki Yukawa — a group including ten Nobel laureates.

Legacy

The manifesto directly led to the first Pugwash Conference, held in July 1957 at Pugwash, Nova Scotia, with support from industrialist Cyrus Eaton. The Pugwash movement of scientists became an influential back channel during the Cold War and, together with its long-serving president Józef Rotblat — the only signatory to have worked on the Manhattan Project and left it on moral grounds — received the Nobel Peace Prize in 1995. The manifesto is regarded as a founding document of the modern movement for nuclear disarmament.
