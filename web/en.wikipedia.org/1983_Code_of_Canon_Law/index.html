The 1983 Code of Canon Law (Latin: Codex Iuris Canonici) is the current codification of canon law for the Latin Church of the Catholic Church. It was promulgated by Pope John Paul II on 25 January 1983 and took effect on the first Sunday of Advent that year.

Background

The code replaced the 1917 Code of Canon Law, the first comprehensive codification of Latin canon law. Its revision, announced by Pope John XXIII alongside the Second Vatican Council in 1959, sought to express the Council's ecclesiology — including the Church as the People of God and greater roles for the laity — in juridical form.

Structure

The code contains 1,752 canons arranged in seven books: general norms; the People of God; the teaching office of the Church; the sanctifying office; temporal goods; sanctions; and processes.
