Kübler-Ross model

The Kübler-Ross model, commonly known as the five stages of grief, was introduced by Swiss psychiatrist Elisabeth Kübler-Ross in her 1969 book On Death and Dying, based on her work with terminally ill patients. The stages are denial, anger, bargaining, depression and acceptance — often abbreviated DABDA.

Application and criticism

The model was later applied more broadly to grief following the loss of a loved one and to other major life changes, and it became widely known in popular culture and in counselling and management training. Kübler-Ross herself emphasised that the stages are not a rigid sequence and that not everyone experiences all of them. Empirical research has found limited support for a fixed stage sequence, and grief researchers generally favour more flexible models of bereavement.
