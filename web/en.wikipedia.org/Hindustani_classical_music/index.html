Hindustani classical music

Hindustani classical music is the classical music tradition of the northern regions of the Indian subcontinent, distinguished from the Carnatic tradition of the south since roughly the 12th–16th centuries. It is built on two fundamental elements: the raga, a melodic framework for improvisation and composition, and the tala, a rhythmic cycle. Performances centre on improvisation within these frameworks, traditionally transmitted orally from teacher (guru) to disciple (shishya).

History

The tradition descends from the ancient music codified in texts such as the Natya Shastra and the Brihaddeshi, and from the devotional dhrupad sung in temples and courts. From the Delhi Sultanate and Mughal periods onward, Persian and Central Asian influences reshaped northern practice; the legendary court musician Tansen, at Akbar's court, is credited with many ragas still performed. Over the centuries regional courts nurtured distinct stylistic schools, the gharanas, and in the early 20th century the scholars Vishnu Narayan Bhatkhande and Vishnu Digambar Paluskar systematised raga classification, notation and public music education.

Forms and instruments

The most ancient vocal form is dhrupad, austere and meditative, accompanied by the pakhawaj drum. The predominant modern vocal form is khayal, more elaborate and improvisatory, usually accompanied by tabla and harmonium or sarangi; lighter forms include thumri, dadra, tappa and the sung poetry of the ghazal. Leading instruments include the sitar, sarod, bansuri (bamboo flute), shehnai, violin, santoor and the tabla pair. A performance typically begins with a slow, unmetered alap exploring the raga, followed by a composition (bandish) set to a tala cycle, with improvisation accelerating toward a climax.

Raga and tala

Hundreds of ragas exist, each defined by its ascending and descending scales, characteristic phrases, and strong and weak notes; many are traditionally associated with particular times of day or seasons. Common talas include teental (16 beats), ektal (12), jhaptal (10) and keherwa (8). Bhatkhande grouped ragas into ten parent scales called thaats.
