Admittance

In electrical engineering, admittance is a measure of how easily a circuit or device will allow a current to flow. It is defined as the reciprocal of impedance, analogous to how conductance and resistance are related for direct current. The SI unit of admittance is the siemens (symbol S). It was coined by Oliver Heaviside in 1887.

Definition

Admittance Y is the complex ratio of current to voltage: Y = I/V = 1/Z, where Z is impedance. Its real part is the conductance G, a measure of how easily current passes, and its imaginary part is the susceptance B: Y = G + jB. The magnitudes are measured in siemens. For a simple resistor, admittance is just 1/R; for a capacitor it is jωC and for an inductor 1/(jωL), where ω is angular frequency.

Applications

Admittance representations are convenient for analysing parallel circuits, where admittances simply add, and appear in microwave engineering through the admittance (immittance) Smith chart, in power systems as bus admittance matrices used in load-flow studies, and in acoustics and mechanics as analogues relating force-like and flow-like quantities.
