E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial is an adventure video game developed and published by Atari, Inc. for the Atari 2600, based on Steven Spielberg's 1982 film of the same name. It was designed by Howard Scott Warshaw, who had previously made the successful Raiders of the Lost Ark adaptation. E.T. guides the alien around screens and pits to assemble a telephone to "phone home" while avoiding scientists and FBI agents.

Development and failure

Atari paid a then-record sum (reportedly $20–25 million) for the licence and, to meet the Christmas season, gave Warshaw roughly five and a half weeks to produce the game. Although it sold about 1.5 million copies and was among the best-selling Atari 2600 cartridges, Atari had manufactured millions more; returns and unsold stock contributed heavily to Atari's losses and the parent company's write-downs during the North American video game crash of 1983.

Legacy and the burial

E.T. became a byword for rushed licensed games and is frequently listed among the worst video games of all time — a reputation recent reassessments have softened by blaming schedule and management. In September 1983 Atari dumped unsold cartridges and hardware in a landfill at Alamogordo, New Mexico. Long treated as urban legend, the burial was confirmed in April 2014 when an excavation recovered E.T. cartridges and other titles, documented in the film Atari: Game Over (2014).
