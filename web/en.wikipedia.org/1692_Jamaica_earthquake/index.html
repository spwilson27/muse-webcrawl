1692 Jamaica earthquake
Date: 7 June 1692, 11:43 local time
Location: Port Royal, Jamaica
Magnitude: ~7.5 (estimated)
Deaths: ~5,000 (including subsequent disease)

The 1692 Jamaica earthquake struck Port Royal, Jamaica, on 7 June 1692. A large portion of the town — then one of the richest and most notorious ports in the English Americas, famed as a haven for privateers and pirates — sank below the sea in minutes due to liquefaction of the sand spit on which it stood.

Event

Three shocks were felt around midday. The sand beneath Port Royal liquefied and some 13 hectares (33 acres) of the town, with houses, warehouses and wharves, slid into Kingston Harbour. About 2,000 people died immediately, and thousands more in the following weeks from injury and disease. A tsunami was reported, and the frigate HMS Swan was carried over rooftops by the surge.

Aftermath

Survivors founded Kingston across the harbour. Port Royal was partially rebuilt but declined as a commercial centre. The sunken ruins are preserved underwater today and are among the most important maritime archaeological sites in the Americas. Contemporaries widely interpreted the disaster as divine judgement on the "wickedest city on Earth."
