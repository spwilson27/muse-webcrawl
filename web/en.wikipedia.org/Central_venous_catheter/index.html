Central venous catheter

A central venous catheter (CVC), also known as a central line, is a catheter placed into a large vein — commonly the internal jugular, subclavian, or femoral vein — with its tip positioned in a central vein such as the superior vena cava. It allows administration of fluids and medications, measurement of central venous pressure, and blood sampling.

Uses

Central lines are used when peripheral access is inadequate or when patients need long-term or irritating infusions, such as chemotherapy, parenteral nutrition, or vasopressors; they are also used for haemodialysis and plasmapheresis. Types include non-tunnelled catheters for short-term critical care, tunnelled catheters and implanted ports for long-term therapy, and peripherally inserted central catheters (PICCs).

Complications

Insertion risks include arterial puncture, pneumothorax, bleeding, and malposition; longer-term risks include bloodstream infection, thrombosis, and vein stenosis. Sterile technique, ultrasound guidance, and catheter care bundles have substantially reduced complication rates. The Seldinger technique is the standard insertion method.
