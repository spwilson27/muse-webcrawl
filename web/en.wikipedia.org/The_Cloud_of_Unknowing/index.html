The Cloud of Unknowing


A page from a medieval manuscript of The Cloud of Unknowing.

The Cloud of Unknowing (Middle English: The Cloude of Unknowyng) is an anonymous work of Christian mysticism written in Middle English in the latter half of the 14th century. The text is a spiritual guide on contemplative prayer, in which the author counsels a young student to seek God not through knowledge or intellection but through intense contemplation and love, placing a "cloud of forgetting" between himself and all created things, and piercing the "cloud of unknowing" between himself and God with the "sharp dart of longing love".

Background

The unknown author is thought to have been a monk, possibly a Carthusian, writing in the East Midlands of England. The work draws on the apophatic (negative) theology of the 6th-century writer Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite, especially Mystical Theology, which the same author translated into Middle English. The Carthusians were instrumental in copying and preserving the text through the period of the English Reformation.

Teachings

The Cloud consists of 75 short chapters. It insists that God cannot be grasped by the intellect: "He may well be loved, but not thought. By love He can be caught and held, but by thinking never." The contemplative is instructed to beat upon the cloud of unknowing with the dart of longing love and to refuse to give up. The book belongs to the same flourishing of English vernacular mysticism that produced Julian of Norwich and Walter Hilton.

Later influence

Modern interest in the Cloud grew in the 20th century through translations and paraphrases by Evelyn Underhill and others. Its method informed the modern practice of Centering Prayer promoted by Thomas Keating and others, and it remains one of the best-known medieval English spiritual texts. The companion work by the same author, The Book of Privy Counselling, continues its instruction.
