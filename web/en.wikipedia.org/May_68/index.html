May 68

May 68 refers to the period of civil unrest in France in May–June 1968, beginning with student protests in Paris and escalating into a general strike involving some ten million workers that brought the country close to a standstill and shook the government of President Charles de Gaulle.

Course of events

Protests over university conditions and police repression at Nanterre and the Sorbonne led to the "Night of the Barricades" on 10–11 May. Unions called a general strike; factories and universities were occupied across France. De Gaulle briefly left for a French military base in Germany, dissolved the National Assembly, and called elections.

Outcome

The Grenelle agreements raised wages; Gaullists won the June 1968 elections decisively, but de Gaulle resigned in 1969 after losing a referendum. Demands ranged from higher pay to libertarian slogans such as "Sous les pavés, la plage" ("Under the paving stones, the beach").

Legacy

May 68 became a symbol of 1960s counterculture and protest movements worldwide, with lasting effects on French culture, education, feminism, and politics, debated ever since between celebrants and critics.
