San Marino Grand Prix

The San Marino Grand Prix was a Formula One motor race held at the Autodromo Enzo e Dino Ferrari in Imola, Italy, from 1981 to 2006. Although named after the nearby Republic of San Marino, the race took place in Italy; the title allowed Italy to stage two Grands Prix per year, the other being the Italian Grand Prix at Monza.

History

A non-championship race was held at Imola in 1980 as the Italian Grand Prix, and from 1981 the circuit hosted the San Marino Grand Prix as a World Championship round. Michael Schumacher was the most successful driver at the event with seven victories, while Williams and Ferrari won the race eight times each as constructors. Nelson Piquet won the inaugural 1981 edition.

The 1994 race

The 1994 San Marino Grand Prix is remembered as one of the darkest weekends in Formula One history. During the event, Austrian driver Roland Ratzenberger was killed in qualifying, and three-time world champion Ayrton Senna died in a crash at the Tamburello corner during the race itself. The tragedies prompted sweeping safety reforms in the sport, and the Imola circuit was extensively modified with chicanes.

End and legacy

The race was dropped from the calendar after the 2006 edition, won by Schumacher. Formula One returned to Imola in 2020 under a new name, the Emilia Romagna Grand Prix.
