Autoroutes of Quebec

Quebec autoroutes are marked with red, white, and blue shield signs.

The Autoroutes of Quebec are a series of controlled-access highways located in the Canadian province of Quebec, forming the core of the province's road network. The network is maintained by Transports Québec. The first autoroute, the Laurentian Autoroute (A-15), opened in 1958 north of Montreal; the network expanded rapidly in the 1960s and 1970s, notably ahead of Expo 67.

Numbering and features

Autoroutes are numbered with an "A-" prefix. Main routes generally bear even or multiple-of-five numbers, odd numbers run predominantly north–south, and three-digit numbers starting with 4, 6, 7, or 9 denote spurs and bypasses around Montreal and Quebec City. Speed limits are typically 100 km/h in rural areas and 70 km/h in urban sections. With the exception of a short section of A-25 and Highway 30, Quebec autoroutes are toll-free; earlier tolls were abolished in 1985.

Major routes

Among the principal autoroutes are A-20, the Trans-Canada Highway corridor between Ontario and the Bas-Saint-Laurent; A-40, linking Montreal with Quebec City via Trois-Rivières; A-15, from the United States border to the Laurentians; and A-10, running from Montreal toward Sherbrooke and the Eastern Townships.
