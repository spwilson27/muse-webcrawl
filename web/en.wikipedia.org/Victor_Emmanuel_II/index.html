Victor Emmanuel II

Victor Emmanuel II (Italian: Vittorio Emanuele II; 14 March 1820 – 9 January 1878) was King of Sardinia (also known as Piedmont-Sardinia) from 23 March 1849 until 17 March 1861, when he assumed the title of King of Italy and became the first king of a united Italy since the 6th century, a title he held until his death in 1878. Borrowing from the old Latin title Pater Patriae of the Roman emperors, Italians gave him the epithet of "Father of the Fatherland" (Padre della Patria).

Road to unification

Victor Emmanuel ascended the Sardinian throne after his father Charles Albert abdicated following defeat by Austria at Novara in 1849. Unlike other Italian rulers, he preserved the liberal Statuto Albertino constitution, earning Piedmont a reputation as a constitutional beacon. With his prime minister, the Count of Cavour, he allied with France against Austria in 1859, annexed Lombardy, and in 1860–61 absorbed the central duchies, the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies (conquered by Garibaldi's Expedition of the Thousand), and most of the Papal States.

On 17 March 1861 parliament proclaimed him King of Italy. Venice was annexed in 1866 and Rome in 1870, becoming the capital in 1871. Victor Emmanuel died in Rome in 1878 and is commemorated by the colossal Vittoriano monument (Altar of the Fatherland) in the capital.
