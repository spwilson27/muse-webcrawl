Prospero Colonna

Born1452, Civita Lavinia, Papal States
Died28 December 1523, Milan
AllegiancePapal States; Kingdom of Naples; Spain; Holy Roman Empire
RankCondottiero, commander

Prospero Colonna (1452–1523) was an Italian condottiero of the noble Colonna family who fought in the Italian Wars, chiefly in the service of Spain and the Holy Roman Empire. Nicknamed il Cunctator — "the Delayer" — after the Roman general Fabius Maximus, he was renowned for a cautious, methodical style of warfare that avoided pitched battle until conditions favoured him, an approach that brought him some of the most consequential victories of the wars.

Career

Colonna first campaigned in defence of the Papal States and Naples against Charles VIII of France in 1494–95, and spent a period in French captivity. Thereafter he served Ferdinand II of Aragon alongside the Gran Capitán, Gonzalo Fernández de Córdoba, sharing in the Spanish victories at Cerignola and the Garigliano in 1503 that secured Naples for Spain. In the 1520s, commanding Imperial forces in Lombardy with the Marquis of Pescara, he outmanoeuvred the French, winning the Battle of Bicocca in 1522 — a landmark demonstration of entrenched arquebusiers and artillery — and driving the French from the Duchy of Milan.

Legacy

Contemporaries ranked Colonna among the foremost captains of his age. His delay at Bicocca gave the Spanish word bicoca the sense of a cheaply won prize, and his campaigns are studied as exemplars of Fabian strategy in the age of pike and shot. He died at Milan in December 1523, still in command.
