Belmopan

Belmopan is the capital city of Belize. Its population was about 20,000 as of 2024, making it one of the smallest national capitals in the world by population. Belmopan is located in the Cayo District at an altitude of 76 metres (250 feet), some 80 kilometres (50 miles) inland from the Caribbean coast and the former capital, Belize City. The city was constructed after Hurricane Hattie devastated Belize City in 1961, and the government moved there in 1970. Its National Assembly Building is designed to resemble a Pre-Columbian Maya temple.

History

After Hurricane Hattie struck Belize City on 31 October 1961, destroying much of the low-lying coastal capital, the colonial government of British Honduras decided to build a new capital on safer, higher ground. The name Belmopan combines "Belize" with "Mopan", a local river and the name of a Maya people of the region. Construction, by the British firm Norman and Dawbarn, began in 1967 with a ring-road plan of government buildings in modernist style. The government officially moved in 1970, although many foreign embassies long remained in Belize City.

Geography and climate

Belmopan lies just north of the Belize River valley, beneath the northern foothills of the Maya Mountains. It has a tropical monsoon climate with a wet season from June to November. Nearby attractions include the Guanacaste National Park, the Belize Zoo, and the Blue Hole National Park with its inland sinkhole and cave systems.

Economy and culture

Government employment dominates the economy, supplemented by the University of Belize campus and regional agriculture. The city hosts the George Price Centre for Peace and Development, named after Belize's first prime minister, and serves as a transport hub on the George Price (Western) Highway.
