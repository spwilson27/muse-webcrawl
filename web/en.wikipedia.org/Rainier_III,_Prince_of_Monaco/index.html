Rainier III, who reigned over Monaco for nearly 56 years.

Rainier III (Rainier Louis Henri Maxence Bertrand Grimaldi; 31 May 1923 – 6 April 2005) was Prince of Monaco from 1949 until his death in 2005. His reign of almost 56 years made him one of the longest-reigning European monarchs of the 20th century. He is credited with transforming Monaco's economy from one based chiefly on gambling into a centre of finance, tourism and commerce, and with securing the principality's place in international organisations, including membership of the United Nations in 1993.

Early life and accession

Rainier was the son of Princess Charlotte and Prince Pierre. Educated in England, Switzerland and at Sciences Po in Paris, he served as an artillery officer in the French Army during the Second World War. He succeeded his grandfather, Louis II, in May 1949, his mother having renounced her rights.

Marriage and reign

In April 1956 Rainier married the American film star Grace Kelly, in what the press called "the wedding of the century"; they had three children. Princess Grace died after a car accident in 1982. Rainier promulgated a new constitution in 1962, oversaw extensive land reclamation (including the Fontvieille district), modernised the Monte Carlo casino business, and steered Monaco through a 1962–63 fiscal dispute with France. Failing health led him to delegate duties to his son, Hereditary Prince Albert, shortly before his death in April 2005, when Albert succeeded him as Albert II.
