Habanero


Habanero peppers

The habanero is a hot variety of chili pepper of the species Capsicum chinense. Unripe habaneros are green and colour as they mature; common colours are orange and red, though white, brown, yellow and pink varieties exist. Typically a ripe habanero is 2–6 centimetres (0.8–2.4 in) long and lantern-shaped. Habanero chilis are intensely hot, rated 100,000–350,000 Scoville units, and noted for a fruity, citrus-like flavour beneath the heat.

Origin and name

Like other C. chinense varieties, the habanero originated in the Amazon basin and spread through the Caribbean and Mexico. Its name means "from Havana" (La Habana), Cuba, a centre of the pepper trade, although the pepper itself is most closely associated with Mexico's Yucatán Peninsula, one of the largest producing regions. Despite its botanical name, chinense is a misnomer arising from a mistaken belief the species came from China.

Cultivation and use

Habaneros thrive in hot climates with morning sun and slightly acidic soil. They are a signature ingredient of Yucatecan cuisine, used in salsas such as xnipec, and widely used in hot sauces. Breeders have produced milder "habanada" varieties retaining the aroma with almost no heat, while the habanero family includes exceptionally hot descendants and relatives such as the Scotch bonnet, Red Savina (once certified the world's hottest at 577,000 SHU), and, by further breeding, peppers in the Carolina Reaper lineage.
