Chandragupta I was a king of the Gupta dynasty who ruled in northern India during the early 4th century CE. He is regarded as the effective founder of the Gupta Empire: he was the first Gupta ruler to take the imperial title Maharajadhiraja ("great king of kings"), and the Gupta era, beginning in 319–320 CE, is generally attributed to his accession.

Marriage alliance

Chandragupta's marriage to Kumaradevi, a princess of the Licchavi clan of Vaishali, greatly strengthened Gupta power and prestige; the alliance is commemorated on gold coins showing the king and queen, issued jointly or by their son in their honour. The marriage likely brought territory in the strategic Ganges valley.

Reign

From his base around Prayaga and Magadha, Chandragupta extended Gupta rule over much of the middle Ganges plain, including parts of present-day Bihar and Uttar Pradesh. He was succeeded by his son Samudragupta, under whom the empire expanded dramatically.
