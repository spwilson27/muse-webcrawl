Rabaul
Country: Papua New Guinea
Province: East New Britain
Location: Gazelle Peninsula, New Britain island

Rabaul is a township in East New Britain province, on the island of New Britain in Papua New Guinea. Built inside the flooded caldera of the Rabaul volcano on Simpson Harbour, it was the capital of the province and a major port until a volcanic eruption in 1994 devastated the town and forced the relocation of the provincial capital to nearby Kokopo.

History

Rabaul was founded under German colonial rule in 1910 as the capital of German New Guinea, then became the capital of the Australian-administered Territory of New Guinea. In the Second World War, Japanese forces captured it in January 1942 and developed it into a major base; Allied forces bypassed it, leaving it isolated until Japan's surrender. A 1937 eruption of the Vulcan cone had already killed hundreds.

1994 eruption

On 19 September 1994, simultaneous eruptions of the Tavurvur and Vulcan cones buried much of the town under heavy ashfall. Thanks to evacuation, deaths were few, but Rabaul's port and airport functions shifted to Kokopo. Tavurvur has erupted repeatedly since, and the town remains partly inhabited amid ongoing volcanic risk.
