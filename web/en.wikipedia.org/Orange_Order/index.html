Orange Order

Loyal Orange Institution
Founded: 1795, County Armagh, Ireland
Type: Protestant fraternal society
Headquarters: Belfast, Northern Ireland
Purpose: Defence of Protestantism and the British union

The Loyal Orange Institution, commonly known as the Orange Order, is an international Protestant fraternal order based in Northern Ireland and primarily associated with Ulster Protestants. It also has lodges in England, Scotland, Wales, the Republic of Ireland, the Commonwealth and the United States. The grand lodge is in Belfast.

History

The Order was founded in 1795 following sectarian conflict in County Armagh, including the "Battle of the Diamond", between Protestant Peep o' Day Boys and Catholic Defenders. It takes its name from the Protestant king William of Orange, who defeated the Catholic king James II at the Battle of the Boyne in 1690. The Order's members wear orange sashes and take part in marches, most prominently the Twelfth of July celebrations marking William's victory.

Beliefs and activities

The Order describes itself as defending Protestant religious and civil liberty and the British constitution. Historically it has been closely associated with unionism — opposition to Irish home rule and to Irish reunification. Orange marches through mainly Catholic areas have at times been a source of tension and violence, notably the long-running Drumcree conflict in Portadown, and parade routes are regulated by the Parades Commission.

Structure

Members belong to local lodges grouped into district, county and grand lodges. The Grand Orange Lodge of Ireland is the governing body. Membership has traditionally required being a professing Protestant, and members may not marry Catholics, though enforcement of the rules has varied. The Royal Black Institution is a related senior order.
