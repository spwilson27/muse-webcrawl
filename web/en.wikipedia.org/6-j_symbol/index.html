6-j symbol

The Wigner 6-j symbol is a mathematical quantity in quantum mechanics, introduced by Eugene Wigner, that governs the recoupling of three angular momenta. Where the 3-j symbol and Clebsch–Gordan coefficients couple two angular momenta, the 6-j symbol expresses the transformation between different coupling schemes for three: for example, coupling j1 and j2 first and then adding j3, versus coupling j2 and j3 first.

Definition

The 6-j symbol is written as a 2×3 array in braces, { j1 j2 j3 ; j4 j5 j6 }. It can be defined as a sum over a product of four 3-j symbols, or equivalently through the Racah formula as a sum involving factorials of combinations of its arguments. It is nonzero only when each of its four triads (j1, j2, j3), (j1, j5, j6), (j4, j2, j6), and (j4, j5, j3) satisfies the triangle inequalities and has an integer sum.

Symmetry and applications

The 6-j symbol has an unusually large symmetry group: it is invariant under any permutation of its columns and under swapping upper and lower entries in any two columns, together with the Regge symmetries giving a total of 144 symmetries. It appears throughout atomic and nuclear spectroscopy in the evaluation of matrix elements of tensor operators, in the theory of spin networks in quantum gravity, and as the fusion (F-) matrix in the theory of anyons and topological quantum computation.
