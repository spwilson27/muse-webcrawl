California Condor

The California condor (Gymnogyps californianus) is a New World vulture and the largest North American land bird. It became extinct in the wild in 1987 when all remaining wild individuals were captured, but has since been reintroduced to northern Arizona and southern Utah (including Zion and Grand Canyon national parks), the coastal mountains of central and southern California, and northern Baja California in Mexico.

Description

Adults are black with white underwing patches and a bare head varying from yellowish to bright red. The wingspan reaches 3 metres (9.8 ft) and weight up to 12 kg. Condors scavenge carrion, soar for hours, and may live 60 years or more.

Conservation

Poaching, habitat loss, and lead poisoning from ammunition in carrion drove the decline to just 27 birds. Captive breeding at the San Diego and Los Angeles zoos, led by the US Fish and Wildlife Service, began releases in 1992. In 2019 the 1,000th chick hatched since recovery began; the total population now exceeds 550, over half free-flying.

Threats

Lead poisoning remains the principal cause of death, prompting California's ban on lead ammunition in condor range; microtrash ingested by chicks and habitat pressures also persist.
