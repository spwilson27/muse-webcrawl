Diagoras of Rhodes

Diagoras of Rhodes was an ancient Greek boxer of the 5th century BC, celebrated as one of the most famous athletes of antiquity. He won the boxing contest at the Olympic Games of 464 BC and victories at the Isthmian, Nemean, and Pythian games, and was immortalised in Pindar's Seventh Olympian Ode.

Family of champions

Diagoras founded a remarkable sporting dynasty: his sons Akousilaos and Damagetos won Olympic boxing and pankration victories on the same day in 448 BC, his son Dorieus won repeated Olympic pankration titles, and his grandsons by his daughter Kallipateira also became Olympic champions. According to a famous anecdote preserved by later writers, when his two sons won on the same day and carried their aged father around the stadium on their shoulders, a spectator cried out, "Die now, Diagoras — you cannot ascend to Olympus besides", and Diagoras died in that moment of supreme happiness.

Legacy

A statue of Diagoras by Kallikles stood at Olympia. The modern Rhodes international airport is named Diagoras Airport in his honour.
