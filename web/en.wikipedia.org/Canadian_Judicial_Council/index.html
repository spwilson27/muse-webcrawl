Canadian Judicial Council

The Canadian Judicial Council (CJC; French: Conseil canadien de la magistrature) is the national council of the judiciary of Canada. It was established in 1971 under the Judges Act to promote efficiency and uniformity and to improve the quality of judicial service in the superior courts of Canada.

Formed1971
HeadquartersOttawa, Ontario
ChairChief Justice of Canada (ex officio)
MembershipChief justices and associate chief justices of federally appointed superior courts

Functions

The Council is composed of the chief justices and associate chief justices of the federal and provincial superior courts, and is chaired by the Chief Justice of Canada. Its best-known public function is reviewing complaints about the conduct of federally appointed judges; it can investigate a complaint and, in serious cases, recommend to the Minister of Justice that a judge be removed from office by Parliament. It also develops policies and model guidelines, including the Ethical Principles for Judges, and provides continuing education for judges through the National Judicial Institute.
