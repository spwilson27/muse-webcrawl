The Hmong are an ethnic group native to the mountainous regions of southwest China — where they are part of the Miao peoples — who migrated over the last several centuries into Vietnam, Laos, Thailand and Myanmar. A large diaspora formed in the United States, France and other countries after the Vietnam War era.

History

Hmong oral history and Chinese records trace centuries of conflict and migration from China's Guizhou, Sichuan and Yunnan provinces under pressure from Han expansion. In Laos, Hmong communities settled highlands around Xieng Khouang and Luang Prabang. During the Laotian civil war, many Hmong fought with the CIA-backed "Secret Army" of General Vang Pao against the Pathet Lao and North Vietnam; after 1975, tens of thousands fled to Thai refugee camps such as Ban Vinai and resettled abroad, especially in Minnesota, Wisconsin and California.

Culture

Hmong society is organised into patrilineal clans (around 18 surnames). Culture is noted for intricate embroidery and story cloths (paj ntaub), silver jewellery, New Year celebrations, oral literature, and musical instruments such as the qeej mouth organ. Religious practice traditionally combines animism and shamanism (ua neeb), while many Hmong are now Christian and some Buddhist. Dialects include White Hmong and Green (Mong Njua), written in the Romanized Popular Alphabet developed in the 1950s.

Issues

Hmong in Laos have faced persecution allegations linked to wartime allegiances, while diaspora communities have negotiated integration, education and preservation of language. Hmong Americans have achieved growing political representation, and Hmong New Year festivals are major public events in St. Paul, Minnesota.
