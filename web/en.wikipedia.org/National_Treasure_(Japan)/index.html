National Treasure (Japan)

A National Treasure (国宝, kokuhō) is the most precious of Japan's tangible cultural properties, as designated by the Agency for Cultural Affairs under the Law for the Protection of Cultural Properties. The designation recognizes objects of outstanding historical, artistic, and academic value for the nation.

Designation system

Japan's system for protecting cultural properties developed from the late 19th century, beginning with measures to preserve shrine and temple treasures during the Meiji era. Under the 1950 Law for the Protection of Cultural Properties, tangible cultural properties judged especially important are designated Important Cultural Properties; among these, the most exceptional are further designated as National Treasures.

Categories

National Treasures are classified into categories including buildings and structures (such as castles, shrines, and temples), paintings, sculptures, crafts (including swords and lacquerware), calligraphic works and classical books, ancient documents, archaeological materials, and historical materials. Famous examples include Himeji Castle, the main hall of Tōdai-ji, and numerous celebrated Buddhist statues and painted screens.

Preservation

Designated properties receive state protection; alterations, export, and transfer of ownership are restricted, and owners may receive subsidies for repair and preservation. As of recent counts, more than a thousand properties hold National Treasure status.
