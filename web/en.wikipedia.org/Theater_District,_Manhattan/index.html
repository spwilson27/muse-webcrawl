Theater District, Manhattan

LocationMidtown Manhattan, New York City, New York, United States
BoundariesRoughly West 40th Street to West 54th Street, Sixth Avenue to Eighth Avenue
Notable venues41 Broadway theatres

The Theater District is a neighborhood in Midtown Manhattan in New York City where most Broadway theatres are located, as well as many other theaters, movie theaters, restaurants, hotels, and other places of entertainment. It is bounded by West 40th Street on the south, West 54th Street on the north, Sixth Avenue on the east and Eighth Avenue on the west, and includes Times Square.

Broadway theatres

The district contains 41 professional theatres with 500 or more seats, which are collectively known as "Broadway." Productions staged in these venues are considered the highest level of commercial theater in the English-speaking world, together with London's West End. Broadway shows draw tens of millions of attendees annually and gross well over a billion dollars per season.

History

New York's theater scene migrated uptown over the nineteenth century, following the northward movement of the city's population. The area around Longacre Square — renamed Times Square in 1904 after The New York Times — became the center of theatrical activity with the opening of theatres such as the New Amsterdam (1903) and the arrival of producers including Oscar Hammerstein I. The district also became the center of the American entertainment industry more broadly, housing the Brill Building's music publishers and the offices of theatrical agents.

The district today

The Theater District remains one of New York City's principal tourist draws. In addition to the Broadway houses it includes Off-Broadway and Off-Off-Broadway venues, the studios of Broadway-related businesses, Duffy Square, Shubert Alley, and Sardi's restaurant, long a gathering place for theatre people.
