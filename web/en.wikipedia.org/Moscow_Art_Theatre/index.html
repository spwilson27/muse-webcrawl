Moscow Art Theatre

The Moscow Art Theatre (Russian: Московский Художественный театр) is a theatre company in Moscow, Russia, founded in 1898 by the theatre practitioner Konstantin Stanislavski together with the playwright and director Vladimir Nemirovich-Danchenko. It is regarded as one of the most influential theatres of the 20th century.

Founding and early years

Stanislavski and Nemirovich-Danchenko agreed to found the company after a famous meeting in 1897 at which they discussed reforming the Russian stage: they sought an ensemble-based theatre devoted to naturalistic staging and serious repertoire, in reaction against the melodramatic conventions of the day. The company's early fame rested on its productions of the plays of Anton Chekhov — The Seagull (1898), Uncle Vanya, Three Sisters, and The Cherry Orchard — and the seagull became the theatre's emblem. It also staged the work of Maxim Gorky, Henrik Ibsen, and Gerhart Hauptmann.

The Stanislavski system

At the theatre Stanislavski developed his influential approach to acting, the "system", which emphasized psychological truth, the actor's analysis of a character's objectives, and disciplined ensemble work. The method spread worldwide through touring productions in Europe and the United States in the 1920s and shaped actor training, including the American Method acting tradition.

Later history

The theatre survived the Russian Revolution and the Soviet era, receiving state honours; in 1987 the company split into two troupes. The Moscow Art Theatre school, founded in 1943, remains a leading drama school.
