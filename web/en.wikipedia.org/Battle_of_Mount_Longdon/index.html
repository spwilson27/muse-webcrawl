Battle of Mount Longdon

The Battle of Mount Longdon was fought between British and Argentine forces on 11–12 June 1982 during the Falklands War. The 3rd Battalion, the Parachute Regiment (3 PARA) attacked the Argentine 7th Infantry Regiment holding the rocky heights of Mount Longdon, west of Stanley, in one of the hardest-fought infantry actions of the war.

Background

After the landings at San Carlos and the advance across East Falkland, British forces closed on the Argentine defensive ring around Stanley. Mount Longdon was a key position anchoring the northern defences. 3 PARA, under Lieutenant Colonel Hew Pike, planned a silent night attack, but the assault was detected after a paratrooper triggered a mine, and the battle became a prolonged firefight among the rocks.

The battle

Fighting at close quarters for bunkers and trenches continued for about ten hours. Corporal Ian McKay was killed clearing a bunker position and was posthumously awarded the Victoria Cross. By dawn on 12 June the summit was in British hands. British losses were 23 killed and 47 wounded — the Parachute Regiment's heaviest casualties of the war; Argentine losses were around 31–50 killed.

Aftermath

The capture of Mount Longdon, together with Two Sisters and Mount Harriet taken the same night, broke the outer Argentine defensive line and led within two days to the Argentine surrender at Stanley on 14 June 1982.
