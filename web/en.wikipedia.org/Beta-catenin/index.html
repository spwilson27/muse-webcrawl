Beta-catenin

Beta-catenin (β-catenin) is a protein that in humans is encoded by the CTNNB1 gene. It has two principal functions: it is a subunit of the cadherin protein complex that mediates cell–cell adhesion at adherens junctions, and it is the central intracellular signal transducer of the canonical Wnt signaling pathway, one of the key pathways regulating embryonic development, stem cell maintenance, and tissue homeostasis.

Structure and adhesion function

Beta-catenin is an armadillo repeat protein, built from twelve armadillo repeats forming a superhelical scaffold that binds many partners. At the cell membrane it binds the cytoplasmic tail of classical cadherins (such as E-cadherin) and, through alpha-catenin, links the adhesion complex to the actin cytoskeleton.

Role in Wnt signaling

In the absence of Wnt ligands, cytoplasmic beta-catenin is captured by a "destruction complex" containing APC, axin, casein kinase 1, and GSK3β; phosphorylation marks it for ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation, keeping cytosolic levels low. When Wnt binds Frizzled and LRP5/6 receptors, the destruction complex is inhibited, beta-catenin accumulates, enters the nucleus, and partners with TCF/LEF transcription factors to activate target genes such as MYC and CCND1 (cyclin D1).

Clinical significance

Deregulation of beta-catenin is a hallmark of many cancers. Loss-of-function mutations in APC — the cause of familial adenomatous polyposis — and stabilizing mutations in CTNNB1 itself drive colorectal cancer, hepatocellular carcinoma, desmoid tumours, and other malignancies. Germline CTNNB1 mutations also cause a neurodevelopmental disorder with intellectual disability, spasticity, and visual defects. Beta-catenin signalling is implicated in hair follicle cycling, bone formation, and fibrotic diseases.
