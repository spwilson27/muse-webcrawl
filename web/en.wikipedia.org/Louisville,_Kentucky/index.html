Louisville, Kentucky

Louisville is the largest city in the U.S. state of Kentucky, situated on the Ohio River at the Falls of the Ohio, just across from Indiana. Founded in 1778 by George Rogers Clark and named for King Louis XVI of France, it is the seat of Jefferson County; since 2003 the city and county have had a merged government.

Economy and culture

Louisville is a major shipping and logistics hub, home to UPS Worldport, and known for bourbon production, the Louisville Slugger baseball bat, and the Kentucky Derby, the first leg of the American Triple Crown, held each May at Churchill Downs since 1875. Famous natives include boxer Muhammad Ali, honoured at the Muhammad Ali Center.
