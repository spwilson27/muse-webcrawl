Assistant professor

Assistant professor is an academic rank used in universities or colleges in the United States, Canada, and some other countries. In the North American system, it is typically the entry-level position for faculty on the tenure track, below associate professor and full professor.

Role and tenure track

Assistant professors are expected to teach undergraduate and graduate courses, conduct research and publish in their field, and perform service to the institution and profession. After a probationary period — commonly six or seven years — they undergo a tenure review; successful candidates are promoted to associate professor with tenure, while unsuccessful candidates are typically given a terminal year.

Comparison with other systems

The rank is roughly comparable to a lecturer in the traditional British system, though usage varies as British institutions have adopted North American titles. In various European and Commonwealth systems the title may denote a non-tenure-track or part-time teaching post, or the first step of a different career ladder.
