Access to justice

Access to justice
Field: Law, human rights
Barriers: Cost, complexity, language, distance
Mechanisms: Legal aid, pro bono, court reform

Access to justice is a principle in the rule of law that describes how citizens should be able to access the legal system on equal terms. It encompasses the ability to obtain legal advice and representation, to bring claims before courts, and to receive fair and timely remedies.

Barriers

Common barriers include the high cost of litigation, court fees, complex procedures, language and literacy obstacles, geographic distance from courts, and lack of legal awareness. These barriers disproportionately affect poor and marginalized groups.

Responses

Legal aid programs, pro bono services, small claims courts, alternative dispute resolution, simplified procedures, and legal technology are used to improve access. International frameworks, including Sustainable Development Goal 16, recognize access to justice for all as a development priority.
