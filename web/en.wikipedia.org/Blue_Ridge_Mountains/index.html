The Blue Ridge Mountains are a physiographic province of the larger Appalachian range in the eastern United States. The mountain range is located in the Eastern United States and extends 550 miles southwest from southern Pennsylvania through Maryland, West Virginia, Virginia, North Carolina, South Carolina, Tennessee, and Georgia. This province consists of northern and southern physiographic regions, which divide near the Roanoke River gap. To the west of the Blue Ridge, between it and the bulk of the Appalachians, lies the Great Appalachian Valley.

Geography

The Blue Ridge contains the highest mountains in eastern North America south of Baffin Island. About 125 peaks rise above 5,000 feet (1,500 m); the highest is Mount Mitchell in North Carolina at 6,684 feet (2,037 m), the highest point east of the Mississippi River. The mountains are noted for having a bluish colour when seen from a distance, caused by isoprene released into the atmosphere by trees, contributing to the characteristic haze and colour.

Scenic routes and parks

The Blue Ridge Parkway, a 469-mile scenic highway, runs along the ridge crest from Shenandoah National Park in Virginia to Great Smoky Mountains National Park in North Carolina and Tennessee, both of which lie largely within the province. Skyline Drive traverses Shenandoah's length.

Ecology and culture

The Blue Ridge's oak-hickory and cove hardwood forests, spruce-fir stands at high elevations, and extraordinary salamander diversity make it a biodiversity hotspot. The region is central to Appalachian folk culture and music and is celebrated in the song "Take Me Home, Country Roads" and the folk standard "Blue Ridge Mountain Blues."
