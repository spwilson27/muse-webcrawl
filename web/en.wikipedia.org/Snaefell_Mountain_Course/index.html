Snaefell Mountain Course

Racing on the Mountain Course near the Bungalow

The Snaefell Mountain Course is a road racing circuit on the Isle of Man used for the Isle of Man TT and the Manx Grand Prix. At 37.73 miles (60.72 km), it is the longest circuit still used in international motorcycle racing, and it is made up entirely of public roads that are closed for the events. The course starts and finishes at the TT Grandstand in Douglas and climbs to about 422 metres (1,385 ft) near the mountain road section below Snaefell, the island's highest peak.

Course description

From Douglas the course heads west through Crosby and Glen Vine to Ballacraine, then north through Kirk Michael and Ballaugh — famous for its hump-backed bridge — to Ramsey. From Ramsey the road climbs the mountain section past the Gooseneck, the Bungalow and the Verandah to the Bungalow and Brandywell, the highest point, before descending through Creg-ny-Baa, Signpost Corner and Governor's Bridge back to the Grandstand. Landmarks such as Bray Hill, Quarterbridge, Braddan Bridge, Ginger Hall and the 32nd Milestone make up a layout riders are expected to memorise in fine detail.

Records and safety

The outright lap record stands at over 136 mph average speed, set by Peter Hickman in 2023, meaning a full lap takes under 17 minutes. The Mountain Course is regarded as one of the most demanding and dangerous circuits in motorsport: its narrow roads are lined by walls, houses and trees, and more than 260 competitors have been killed in TT and Manx Grand Prix racing since 1907. Its status is a continuing subject of debate, though the event remains a cornerstone of the Isle of Man's culture and economy.
