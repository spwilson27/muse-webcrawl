Alyattes

Alyattes (reigned c. 610–560 BC) was the fourth king of Lydia from the Mermnad dynasty, father of Croesus, and one of the most powerful rulers of western Anatolia in the early 6th century BC.

Reign

Alyattes continued Lydia's expansion against the Greek cities of the Ionian coast, capturing Smyrna, and fought a long war against the Medes under Cyaxares. That war ended after the famous "Battle of the Eclipse" in 585 BC, when a solar eclipse — said to have been predicted by Thales of Miletus — frightened both armies into making peace, fixing the Halys River as the border between Lydia and Media. Herodotus credits Alyattes with early experiments in coinage; Lydia in this era produced some of the first coins, made of electrum. His enormous tomb mound near Sardis was among the largest in the ancient world.
