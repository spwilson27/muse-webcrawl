Miracles of Jesus

The miracles of Jesus are proposed miraculous deeds attributed to Jesus in Christian and Islamic texts. The majority are faith healings, exorcisms, resurrections, and control over nature. In the Synoptic Gospels (Mark, Matthew, and Luke), Jesus refuses to give a miraculous sign to prove his authority, but in the Gospel of John, miracles are presented as "signs" (semeia) indicating divine glory. The word used in the Gospels is dynameis, "powers" or "mighty works".

Christians generally believe these miracles were historical events; other accounts, following David Hume, argue that evidence for miracles can never outweigh the uniformity of natural law. Most non-Christian scholars regard the accounts as theological narratives rather than history, though the healings and exorcisms are often connected to the historical Jesus's reputation as a healer and exorcist.

Categories

Healings

The largest group: Jesus heals a leper, a paralytic lowered through a roof, a woman with a haemorrhage, two blind men, a man with a withered hand, the centurion's servant (at a distance), Peter's mother-in-law, the blind Bartimaeus, and the ten lepers, among others.

Exorcisms

Jesus casts out demons in Capernaum, from the Gerasene demoniac (who calls himself "Legion"), from the Syrophoenician woman's daughter, and from a boy with seizures, among other episodes.

Nature miracles

These include turning water into wine at the wedding at Cana, the miraculous catch of fish, the feeding of the five thousand and of the four thousand, walking on water, calming the storm, the coin in the fish's mouth, and the cursing of the fig tree.

Raisings of the dead

Three individuals are raised: the daughter of Jairus, the widow's son at Nain, and Lazarus of Bethany, whose four-day entombment precedes the most fully narrated raising (John 11).

In Islam

The Quran also attributes miracles to Jesus (Isa), including speaking from the cradle, fashioning a bird from clay and breathing life into it, healing the blind and the leper, and raising the dead — all by God's permission.
