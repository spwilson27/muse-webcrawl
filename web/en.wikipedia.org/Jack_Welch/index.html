Jack Welch

John Francis Welch Jr. (1935 – 2020) was an American business executive who served as chairman and chief executive of General Electric (GE) from 1981 to 2001, a tenure during which the company's market value rose from about $12 billion to roughly $600 billion.

Career at GE

Welch joined GE in 1960 as a chemical engineer and became its youngest chairman at 45. He streamlined the conglomerate under a rule that each business be first or second in its market or be sold, cut hundreds of thousands of jobs — earning the nickname "Neutron Jack" — and expanded into financial services through GE Capital. Fortune magazine named him "Manager of the Century" in 1999.

Later life and criticism

After retiring, Welch wrote bestselling management books with Suzy Welch and founded an online MBA institute. Later assessments questioned the durability of his model, as GE's finance-heavy strategy unravelled after 2008 and his forced-ranking personnel system fell out of favour.
