Heiligenschein

Heiligenschein (German for "halo" or "saint's shining light") is an optical phenomenon in which a bright region appears around the shadow of the viewer's head. It occurs when the sun is low and the observer looks over a surface covered with dew drops, such as wet grass in the early morning.

Mechanism

The effect is a form of retroreflection. Each spherical dew drop acts as a lens, focusing sunlight onto the leaf surface behind it; the illuminated spot then reflects light back through the drop, which focuses it again toward the sun — and toward an observer whose head blocks the direct sunlight. The returned light is concentrated near the antisolar point, which is exactly where the shadow of the observer's head falls, so each person sees a glow only around their own shadow.

Related phenomena

The heiligenschein is related to the glory, the Brocken spectre, and the opposition surge observed on dry surfaces and in astronomy. A similar brightening of the lunar surface at full moon helps explain why the Moon brightens disproportionately near full phase. Astronauts and surveyors have also reported the effect on the dusty lunar surface around their shadows.
