Somoza family

The Somoza family ruled Nicaragua, directly and through puppet presidents, from 1937 until their overthrow in 1979. Their nearly half-century dynastic dictatorship left a profound mark on Nicaraguan politics, and the family name remained a loaded symbol long after the regime's fall.

Members

Anastasio Somoza García

Anastasio "Tacho" Somoza García (1896–1956) founded the dynasty. As head of the National Guard (Guardia Nacional), he ordered the killing of Augusto César Sandino in 1934, seized the presidency in 1937, and ruled as a strongman backed by the United States. He accumulated vast personal wealth and land. He was assassinated in 1956 by the poet Rigoberto López Pérez.

Luis Somoza Debayle

Luis Somoza Debayle (1922–1967), the elder son, succeeded his father as president (1957–1963) and continued to dominate politics afterward through the National Guard and the Liberal Nationalist Party.

Anastasio Somoza Debayle

Anastasio "Tachito" Somoza Debayle (1925–1980), the younger son, trained at West Point, commanded the National Guard and served as president from 1967 to 1972 and again from 1974. His corrupt response to the 1972 Managua earthquake and the repression that followed fueled the insurrection of the Sandinista National Liberation Front (FSLN). He fled Nicaragua in July 1979, was granted asylum in Paraguay, and was assassinated in Asunción in 1980.

The regime

The dynasty rested on the National Guard, U.S. support, and a family business empire that by the 1970s controlled a large share of Nicaragua's economy. Opposition grew through the 1970s, especially after the assassination of newspaper editor Pedro Joaquín Chamorro in 1978 touched off a general insurrection.

After 1979

Members of the family went into exile and much of their property was confiscated by the Sandinista government. Later generations, including relatives of the Debayle line, have occasionally figured in Nicaraguan public life, but the dynasty itself never returned to power.
