Pugwash Conferences on Science and World Affairs

The Pugwash Conferences on Science and World Affairs is an international organization that brings together scholars and public figures to work for the reduction of armed conflict and to seek cooperative solutions for global problems, particularly nuclear disarmament. It takes its name from the village of Pugwash, Nova Scotia, Canada, where the first conference was held in 1957.

History

The conferences grew out of the Russell–Einstein Manifesto of 1955, which called on scientists to assess the dangers of weapons of mass destruction. Józef Rotblat was a central founding figure and long-serving secretary-general. Pugwash played back-channel roles during the Cold War, contributing to treaties including the Partial Test Ban Treaty and the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty.

Recognition

In 1995, the organization and Rotblat jointly received the Nobel Peace Prize "for their efforts to diminish the part played by nuclear arms in international politics and, in the longer run, to eliminate such arms."
