Seawall

A seawall is a form of coastal defence constructed where the sea meets the land, designed to protect human habitation and infrastructure from waves, storm surges and coastal erosion. Seawalls are typically built of concrete, stone or steel, in vertical, curved or stepped profiles that absorb or reflect wave energy.

Design and effects

Curved and stepped faces throw wave water back on itself, reducing overtopping, while smooth vertical walls are cheaper but reflect energy strongly, which can scour the beach at their base. Although seawalls protect the land behind them, they can accelerate erosion of the beach in front and transfer erosion problems along the coast, which is why modern coastal management often combines them with beach nourishment or prefers softer defences such as dunes and wetlands.

Examples

Famous examples include the seawalls of Galveston, Texas, raised after the devastating 1900 hurricane, the Stanley Park seawall in Vancouver, and the extensive sea defences of the Netherlands, where much of the country lies below sea level.
