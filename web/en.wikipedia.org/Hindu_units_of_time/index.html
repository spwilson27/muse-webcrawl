Hindu units of time

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
The nested cycles of Hindu cosmology

Hindu units of time are described in Hindu texts ranging from microseconds to trillions of years, including cycles of cosmic time that repeat eternally. The system, recorded in works such as the Manusmṛti, Mahābhārata and the Purāṇas, measures everything from the smallest moment (truṭi) to the lifespan of the god Brahmā.

Cosmic cycles

Larger units structure Hindu cosmology. A yuga cycle (mahāyuga) of 4,320,000 years comprises the Kṛta (Satya), Tretā, Dvāpara and Kali yugas in the ratio 4:3:2:1; the present age is the Kali Yuga, traditionally begun in 3102 BCE. One thousand yuga cycles form a kalpa or day of Brahmā (4.32 billion years), followed by an equal night. Brahmā's lifespan of 100 such years equals about 311 trillion human years, after which the universe is dissolved and recreated.
