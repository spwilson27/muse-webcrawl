Multisport race


Examples: Triathlon, duathlon, aquathlon, adventure racing
Governing bodies: World Triathlon and national federations

A multisport race is an endurance competition that combines two or more sporting disciplines into a single continuous event, with athletes moving directly from one discipline to the next through a transition area and the clock running throughout.

Common formats

  • Triathlon — swimming, cycling, and running, in that order; the best-known multisport format and an Olympic sport since 2000.
  • Duathlon — running, cycling, and running again.
  • Aquathlon — swimming and running.
  • Aquabike — swimming and cycling only.
  • Winter triathlon — running, mountain biking, and cross-country skiing on snow.
  • Adventure racing — multi-day team events combining trekking, mountain biking, paddling, navigation, and other disciplines.
  • Quadrathlon and ö till ö (swimrun) — further combinations of disciplines, the latter alternating swimming and running across islands.

History

Multisport events trace to French races of the 1920s and the first modern triathlon in San Diego in 1974. The Ironman format, begun in Hawaii in 1978, popularized long-distance multisport racing worldwide, and the sport's formats have since diversified across distances and disciplines.

Transitions and equipment

Speed in the timed transition zones ("the fourth discipline") can determine results. Athletes use discipline-specific equipment staged in advance, and drafting rules, wetsuit regulations, and penalties vary by format and governing body.
