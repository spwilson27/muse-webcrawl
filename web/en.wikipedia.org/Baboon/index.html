Baboon

Baboons are primates of the genus Papio, among the largest non-hominoid primates. Six species are commonly recognised: the hamadryas, Guinea, olive, yellow, Kinda and chacma baboons. They are native to Africa and the Arabian Peninsula.

Anatomy and behaviour

Baboons are ground-dwelling monkeys with long, dog-like muzzles, heavy jaws with large canine teeth, close-set eyes and thick fur; males are considerably larger than females and in some species sport brightly coloured faces and rumps. They live in troops ranging from a handful to several hundred animals, organised by complex dominance hierarchies maintained through grooming, alliances and aggression. Baboons are omnivorous, eating grasses, seeds, fruit, insects and occasionally young antelope.

Social life

Baboon troops have been studied intensively in the wild — notably the long-running Amboseli project in Kenya — revealing sophisticated social knowledge, reconciliation behaviour and stress physiology linked to rank. Hamadryas baboons differ in forming multi-level societies built on one-male units. In ancient Egypt the hamadryas baboon was sacred, associated with the god Babi and with Thoth.
