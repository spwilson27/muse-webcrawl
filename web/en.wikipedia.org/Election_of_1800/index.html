1800 United States presidential election

The 1800 United States presidential election was the fourth quadrennial presidential election, held from October 31 to December 3, 1800. In what is now called the Revolution of 1800, Vice President Thomas Jefferson of the Democratic-Republican Party defeated incumbent President John Adams of the Federalist Party. The election was a political realignment that ushered in a generation of Democratic-Republican leadership and the first peaceful transfer of power between parties in American history.

Campaign

The rematch between Adams and Jefferson was bitterly fought, with both sides trading personal attacks. The Federalists were divided by Adams's peace negotiations with France and by his feud with Alexander Hamilton, and they were damaged by public anger over the Alien and Sedition Acts and new taxes. Jefferson's supporters accused Adams of monarchism; Federalists warned Jefferson was a dangerous radical and atheist.

The electoral tie and the House decision

Under the original Constitution, electors cast two undifferentiated votes for president. Every Democratic-Republican elector voted for both Jefferson and his running mate Aaron Burr, producing a 73–73 tie and throwing the election to the lame-duck, Federalist-controlled House of Representatives. After 35 deadlocked ballots over a week in February 1801, the House chose Jefferson on the 36th ballot, partly because Hamilton lobbied against Burr, whom he considered unprincipled.

Consequences

The crisis led directly to the Twelfth Amendment, ratified in 1804, which required electors to cast separate ballots for president and vice president. The election began the "Virginia dynasty" and the long decline of the Federalist Party.
