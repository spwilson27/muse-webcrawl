Paul Ginsparg (born November 1, 1955) is an American physicist best known as the founder of the arXiv preprint repository, which revolutionised the way scientists share research results. He is a professor of physics and of information science at Cornell University.

Early life and physics career

Ginsparg was raised on Long Island, New York, and attended Syosset High School. He earned his bachelor's degree in physics from Harvard University in 1977 and his PhD from Cornell University in 1981, with a dissertation on quantum field theory. As a physicist he worked on string theory and quantum gravity; with Vladimir Gribov he developed the Ginsparg–Wilson relation, a 1982 result on lattice fermions that remains important in lattice gauge theory.

arXiv

In August 1991, while at Los Alamos National Laboratory, Ginsparg set up an automated e-mail and FTP server (xxx.lanl.gov) so that string theorists could exchange preprints instantly rather than mailing paper copies. The archive, which moved to the web and became arXiv.org, expanded from high-energy physics into astrophysics, mathematics, computer science, statistics, economics, and many other fields. It now hosts more than two million articles and receives hundreds of thousands of submissions and millions of downloads; posting a paper on arXiv before formal peer review became standard practice across much of science.

Ginsparg moved with the archive to Cornell in 2001 and continued to oversee its development. He has written and spoken widely on scholarly communication, open access, and the future of scientific publishing. In 2002 he received a MacArthur "Genius" Fellowship in recognition of his work.
