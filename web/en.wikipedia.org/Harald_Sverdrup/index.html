Harald Ulrik Sverdrup (15 November 1888 – 21 August 1957) was a Norwegian oceanographer and meteorologist who made fundamental contributions to the theory of ocean circulation. The unit of volume transport in oceanography, the sverdrup (1 Sv = 106 m3/s), is named after him.

Career

Sverdrup studied at the University of Oslo and worked under Vilhelm Bjerknes in Leipzig on atmospheric dynamics before turning to the ocean. He was the chief scientist on Roald Amundsen's Maud expedition (1918–1925), which drifted across the Arctic Ocean, and published extensive analyses of Arctic tides and currents. In 1936 he became director of the Scripps Institution of Oceanography in California, transforming it into a major research centre; during the Second World War he helped develop wave-forecasting methods used in amphibious landings.

Scientific contributions

His 1942 textbook The Oceans: Their Physics, Chemistry and General Biology, written with Martin Johnson and Richard Fleming, was the first comprehensive modern synthesis of oceanography. In 1947 he derived the relation now called the Sverdrup balance, showing how the curl of the wind stress drives the depth-integrated meridional transport of the ocean's interior — a cornerstone of the theory later completed by Henry Stommel and Walter Munk. Sverdrup also lent his name to a theory of phytoplankton spring blooms based on the concept of critical depth. See also physical oceanography.
