Regimental sergeant major

Regimental sergeant major (RSM) is an appointment held by the senior warrant officer of a regiment or battalion in the British Army and in the armies of many Commonwealth nations. The holder is usually a warrant officer class 1 (WO1). The RSM is the senior soldier of the unit and a key adviser to the commanding officer on matters affecting the discipline, welfare, and professional standards of the soldiers.

Role

The RSM is traditionally responsible for drill, dress, and discipline within the unit, for ceremonial parades, and for the professional development of junior non-commissioned officers. On parade the RSM carries a pace stick, used historically to set the correct spacing between ranks and the length of pace. The appointment is one of the most prestigious available to a soldier who has risen through the ranks.

Although the RSM is formally subordinate to all commissioned officers, in practice the appointment commands considerable authority; officers are traditionally expected to address the RSM as "sir" or "ma'am" in some settings, and the commanding officer relies on the RSM as the unit's link to the rank and file.

Equivalents

In the Royal Navy the approximate equivalent is the warrant officer of the naval service or a ship's coxswain appointment; in the Royal Air Force, station warrant officer. In the United States Army, the comparable role at battalion and higher level is the command sergeant major. Commonwealth armies such as those of Canada, Australia, and New Zealand retain the RSM appointment along similar lines.
