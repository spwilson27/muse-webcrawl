Nairobi is the capital and largest city of Kenya. Its name derives from the Maasai phrase Enkare Nairobi, "cool water", referring to the Nairobi River. Founded in 1899 as a supply depot on the Uganda Railway, it became the capital of British East Africa in 1907 and of independent Kenya in 1963.

Geography and wildlife

Nairobi lies at about 1,795 metres above sea level in the south-central highlands, giving it a mild subtropical highland climate. Uniquely among world capitals, it contains a major national park: Nairobi National Park, established in 1946, where lions, rhinos, giraffes, and other savanna wildlife roam against a backdrop of the city skyline. The city is also a centre for conservation organisations.

Economy and international role

Nairobi is East Africa's principal financial, commercial, and technology hub — its tech sector is nicknamed "Silicon Savannah" — and hosts the Nairobi Securities Exchange and regional headquarters of many multinational companies. It is one of four major United Nations duty stations, housing the UN Environment Programme (UNEP) and UN-Habitat headquarters, and the only UN headquarters in the developing world.

Transport and society

The city is served by Jomo Kenyatta International Airport and the standard-gauge railway to Mombasa, opened in 2017. Rapid growth has produced large informal settlements such as Kibera alongside leafy suburbs, and persistent challenges in traffic, housing, and services.
