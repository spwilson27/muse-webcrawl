Jay Sterner Hammond (21 July 1922 – 2 August 2005) was an American politician of the Republican Party who served as the fourth governor of Alaska from 1974 to 1982. A bush pilot, commercial fisherman, and guide, Hammond is best remembered as the father of the Alaska Permanent Fund, which converts the state's oil wealth into a permanent savings account paying annual dividends to residents.

Early life and career

Born in Troy, New York, Hammond studied at Penn State and served as a Marine Corps pilot in the Pacific during the Second World War. He moved to Alaska after the war, working as a bush pilot, trapper, and commercial fisherman on Bristol Bay, homesteading at Lake Clark. Appalled by outside exploitation of Alaska's fisheries, he entered politics, serving in the territorial and state legislatures and as manager of the Bristol Bay Borough, championing local control of resources.

Governor

Elected governor in 1974, Hammond governed as a fiscally conservative environmentalist during the construction of the Trans-Alaska Pipeline and the Prudhoe Bay oil boom. His signature achievement was the Alaska Permanent Fund: he insisted that a share of the state's non-renewable oil revenues be constitutionally saved (voters approved the fund amendment in 1976) and later championed the Permanent Fund Dividend, under which every resident receives an equal annual payment — first issued in 1982. He also fought the proposed Rampart Dam, supported subsistence rights, and presided over the politically fraught selection of state lands under the Alaska Statehood Act.

Later life

After leaving office Hammond returned to Port Alsworth, wrote his memoir Tales of a Bush Rat Governor (1994), and remained an influential commentator, later spending down his own fortune to campaign against proposals he believed betrayed the Permanent Fund's original design. Mount Hammond and other Alaskan places bear his name; he is routinely ranked among the state's most consequential governors.
