Texas Ranger Division

The Texas Ranger Division, commonly called the Texas Rangers, is a state law enforcement agency of Texas, part of the Texas Department of Public Safety. Founded in 1823 by Stephen F. Austin as a ranging company to protect settlers, it is one of the oldest state-level law enforcement bodies in the United States.

History

Rangers served as scouts and fighters in the Texas Revolution and the Mexican–American War, patrolled the frontier, and pursued outlaws in the Old West. Their record includes both celebrated exploits and notorious abuses, particularly violence against Mexican Americans during the border troubles of the 1910s. In 1935 the Rangers were merged into the newly created Department of Public Safety.

Modern role

Today roughly 160 commissioned Rangers investigate major crimes, including murders, public corruption, and unsolved cases, and support local agencies across Texas. They wear a distinctive five-pointed star badge, traditionally said to be cut from a Mexican silver coin.
