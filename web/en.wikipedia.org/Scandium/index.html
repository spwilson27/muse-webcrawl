Scandium

SymbolSc
Atomic number21
Atomic mass44.956
Melting point1,541 °C
CategoryTransition metal

Scandium is a chemical element with the symbol Sc and atomic number 21. It is a silvery-white, moderately soft transition metal — the first of the transition series — and is sometimes classed with the rare-earth elements. Its existence as "eka-boron" was predicted by Dmitri Mendeleev in 1871, and it was discovered spectroscopically by Lars Fredrik Nilson in 1879 from Scandinavian minerals, hence its name.

Occurrence and production

Scandium is widely dispersed in trace amounts but concentrated ores are rare; it is obtained mainly as a by-product of uranium, titanium and rare-earth processing, and annual world production is only on the order of tens of tonnes.

Applications

The main use is in aluminium–scandium alloys, which gain strength and weldability and are used in aerospace components, high-end bicycle frames and sports equipment. Scandium iodide is added to metal-halide lamps to produce a daylight-like spectrum for film and stadium lighting, and the isotope scandium-46 serves as a radioactive tracer in oil refining.
