Tuxtla Gutiérrez is the capital and the largest city of the Mexican southeastern state of Chiapas. It is the seat of the municipality of the same name and the state's main centre of commerce, government and transport. The city lies in the central valley of Chiapas, on the Grijalva River basin, at an elevation of roughly 500 metres.

History

The area was originally settled by the Zoque people; the name Tuxtla derives from the Nahuatl Tochtlan, "place of rabbits". The modern city grew from a colonial-era village and was declared the capital of Chiapas in the late nineteenth century, ending a long alternation of the seat of government with San Cristóbal de las Casas. Its surname honours Joaquín Miguel Gutiérrez, a nineteenth-century Chiapas liberal politician.

Features

Tuxtla is a gateway to nearby natural attractions, most famously the Sumidero Canyon National Park. The city hosts the Miguel Álvarez del Toro Zoo (ZooMAT), noted for exhibiting native fauna of Chiapas, and serves as a hub for travel into the state's highlands and Lacandon jungle.
