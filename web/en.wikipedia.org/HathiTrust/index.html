HathiTrust preserves and provides access to millions of digitized volumes.

HathiTrust is a partnership of academic and research institutions offering a collection of millions of titles digitized from libraries around the world, together with long-term preservation of the digital files. Founded in 2008 by the institutions of the Committee on Institutional Cooperation (now the Big Ten Academic Alliance) and the University of California system, it is administered by the University of Michigan. "Hathi" is the Hindi word for elephant, an animal famed for its long memory.

Collection and services

The HathiTrust Digital Library holds more than 17 million digitized volumes, much of the content originating from the Google Books library scanning project and the Internet Archive. Public-domain works are freely readable online in full; in-copyright works are searchable, with full access available to users with print disabilities and through programs such as the Emergency Temporary Access Service operated during the COVID-19 pandemic. Key services include a bibliographic catalog, full-text search across the corpus, the HathiTrust Research Center (which supports computational analysis of the collection), and a shared print program coordinating retention of physical copies.

Copyright and litigation

HathiTrust was a defendant in Authors Guild v. HathiTrust (2012–2014), in which the U.S. courts held that mass digitization for full-text search and accessibility for print-disabled readers was fair use — a landmark ruling for digital libraries, companion to the later Authors Guild v. Google decision. Its Copyright Review Project has reviewed the status of millions of volumes to open public-domain works.

Governance

HathiTrust is a membership organization with more than 200 member libraries, governed by a Board of Governors and funded by member fees based on holdings and use.
