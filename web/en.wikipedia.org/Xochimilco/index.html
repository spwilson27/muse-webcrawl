Xochimilco

Xochimilco (Nahuatl: Xōchimīlco, "flower field place") is one of the 16 boroughs (demarcaciones territoriales) of Mexico City, lying about 17 km (11 mi) southeast of the city centre. It is famous for its network of canals and artificial floating gardens called chinampas, remnants of the lake system of the pre-Hispanic Valley of Mexico, and was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1987 together with the Historic Centre of Mexico City.

Chinampas and canals

Chinampas are raised agricultural beds built in the shallow lake by layering mud, vegetation, and lake sediment within woven reed enclosures, fixed in place by ahuejote willows planted at the corners. The technique, refined by the Xochimilca and later the Aztecs, produced exceptionally fertile plots yielding multiple harvests a year and supplied much of Tenochtitlan's food. A small number of working chinampas survive, still farmed for flowers and vegetables.

Today visitors ride flat-bottomed, brightly painted boats called trajineras along the canals, poled by trajineros; floating vendors and mariachi bands serve the boats, making Xochimilco one of Mexico City's most popular weekend destinations. The canals are also a refuge for the axolotl, the critically endangered Mexican salamander native to the lake system, and for migratory birds.

The borough

The borough retains a semi-rural character distinct from the rest of the capital, with village centres (pueblos) each centred on a colonial-era church, local patron-saint festivals, and markets known for plants and flowers. The Museo Dolores Olmedo, housed in a former hacienda, holds major collections of Diego Rivera and Frida Kahlo (the collection has since been relocated to Chapultepec's Lago/Algo gallery).

Environmental issues

Water extraction, urbanization, declining canal water quality, and invasive species such as carp and tilapia threaten the chinampa ecosystem and the axolotl, prompting conservation refuges built by local farmers and the National Autonomous University of Mexico.
