The 7.62×51mm NATO is a rimless, bottlenecked rifle cartridge adopted by NATO in 1954 as the standard for military rifles and machine guns. Developed from the experimental US T65 cartridge, it is dimensionally almost identical to the commercial .308 Winchester, which was introduced slightly earlier.

History

After the Second World War the United States sought a shorter cartridge matching the ballistics of the .30-06 Springfield, using new powders. The resulting 7.62×51mm became NATO's first standard rifle round, chambered in weapons such as the M14, FN FAL, Heckler & Koch G3 and the M60 and FN MAG machine guns. Although NATO later adopted the smaller 5.56×45mm for assault rifles, 7.62×51mm remains standard for sniper rifles, designated marksman rifles, general-purpose machine guns and miniguns.

Performance

Standard ball loadings fire a bullet of about 9.5–10.5 g at roughly 850 m/s, giving effective ranges beyond 800 metres in machine-gun and precision roles. Numerous variants exist, including tracer, armour-piercing, blank and match grades.
