Möbius transformation

Also called: Fractional linear transformation
Named after: August Ferdinand Möbius
Field: Complex analysis, geometry

In mathematics, a Möbius transformation is a rational function of the form f(z) = (az + b)/(cz + d), where the coefficients are complex numbers and ad − bc ≠ 0. It defines a conformal map of the Riemann sphere to itself.

Properties

Möbius transformations map circles and lines to circles and lines, preserve angles, and form a group under composition isomorphic to the projective linear group PGL(2, ℂ). Every such transformation is a composition of translations, rotations, dilations and inversion.

Applications

They are used in complex analysis, hyperbolic geometry — where they give the isometries of models of the hyperbolic plane — and in physics, where the Lorentz transformations act on the celestial sphere as Möbius transformations.
