Cosmos: A Spacetime Odyssey is a 2014 American science documentary television series, a follow-up to Carl Sagan's landmark 1980 series Cosmos: A Personal Voyage. Hosted by astrophysicist Neil deGrasse Tyson, the series explores astronomy, space, time, evolution, and the history of scientific discovery, using updated visual effects, animation, and the "Ship of the Imagination" device carried over from the original.

Development

The series was developed by Ann Druyan, Sagan's widow and co-writer of the original series, together with Steven Soter. Seth MacFarlane was instrumental in bringing the project to Fox and served as an executive producer. The score was composed by Alan Silvestri, and the visual effects made extensive use of modern CGI to depict cosmic phenomena and historical settings.

Episodes

The thirteen episodes include "Standing Up in the Milky Way", "Some of the Things That Molecules Do", "When Knowledge Conquered Fear", "A Sky Full of Ghosts", "Hiding in the Light", "Deeper, Deeper, Deeper Still", "The Clean Room", "Sisters of the Sun", "The Electric Boy", "The Immortals", "The World Set Free", and "Unafraid of the Dark". Topics range from the cosmic calendar and natural selection to the life of Clair Patterson and the dangers of lead poisoning.

Reception

The series premiered to an audience of millions across multiple networks and won four Primetime Emmy Awards, including Outstanding Writing for Nonfiction Programming and Outstanding Original Main Title Theme Music. A further season, Cosmos: Possible Worlds, premiered in 2020.
