Great Expectations

AuthorCharles Dickens
Published1861 (serialised 1860–61 in All the Year Round)
GenreBildungsroman
LanguageEnglish

Great Expectations is the thirteenth novel by Charles Dickens and his penultimate completed novel. It depicts the education of an orphan nicknamed Pip. The novel was first published as a serial in Dickens's weekly periodical All the Year Round from 1 December 1860 to August 1861, and in October 1861 Chapman and Hall published the novel in three volumes.

Plot summary

Pip, a young orphan raised by his harsh sister and her gentle blacksmith husband Joe Gargery in the Kent marshes, is terrified into helping the escaped convict Abel Magwitch. Later, the eccentric, reclusive Miss Havisham — who lives frozen in the decayed splendour of Satis House, still wearing the wedding dress of her youth — employs Pip as a companion for her beautiful adopted daughter Estella, whom Pip loves despite her coldness.

An anonymous fortune transforms Pip into a London gentleman with "great expectations"; he assumes his benefactor is Miss Havisham, but learns it is Magwitch, prospering in Australia and repaying the boy who once fed him. Pip's snobbery, his shame at Joe's manners, the collapse of his fortune, and his redemption through loyalty and suffering form the moral arc of this Bildungsroman.

Themes and reception

The novel explores class, ambition, guilt, and the corrupting power of wealth and false gentility. Dickens revised his original, bleaker ending at the suggestion of Edward Bulwer-Lytton to imply a possible reconciliation between Pip and Estella. Great Expectations is now widely regarded as one of Dickens's finest novels and has been adapted many times for film, television and the stage, including David Lean's celebrated 1946 film.
