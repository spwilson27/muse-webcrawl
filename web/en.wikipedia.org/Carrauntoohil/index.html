Carrauntoohil (Irish: Corrán Tuathail, meaning "Tuathal's sickle") is the highest mountain in Ireland, at 1,038.6 metres (3,407 feet). It is on the Iveragh Peninsula in the Reeks District in County Kerry, close to the centre of the MacGillycuddy's Reeks mountain range.

Carrauntoohil, the highest point on the island of Ireland.

Geography

Carrauntoohil is part of the MacGillycuddy's Reeks, Ireland's highest mountain range, composed of Devonian Old Red Sandstone. Three major ridges meet at the summit: the north-east ridge (Cnoc na Péiste), the south-east ridge (Cnoc na Toinne), and the west ridge. Between them lies the Hag's Glen, holding the lakes Lough Gouragh and Lough Callee.

Climbing

The most popular ascent is via the Hag's Glen and the steep gully known as the Devil's Ladder, a route of about 12.5 km. More challenging alternatives include the Brother O'Shea's Gully route and ridge traverses along the Reeks, such as the Coomloughra Horseshoe linking Carrauntoohil with Ireland's second and third highest peaks. A large metal cross, first erected in 1976 and replaced after storm damage, marks the summit. The mountain is privately owned farmland, and climbers are advised to respect access arrangements and rapidly changing Atlantic weather.

Name and prominence

The name derives from the Irish Corrán Tuathail. Carrauntoohil is the only Irish peak over 1,000 m and has a topographic prominence of over 900 m, qualifying it as a Marilyn and among the most prominent peaks in Ireland and Britain.
