Erhu (二胡)
ClassificationBowed string instrument (spike fiddle)
RangeAbout 3 octaves (D4–D7 typical)
Strings2, traditionally silk, now steel

The erhu (二胡; pinyin: èrhú) is a two-stringed bowed musical instrument, more specifically a spike fiddle, which may also be called a southern fiddle, and is sometimes known in the Western world as the Chinese violin or a Chinese two-stringed fiddle. It is the most popular of the huqin family of traditional Chinese bowed string instruments. It is used as a solo instrument as well as in small ensembles and large orchestras.

Construction

The erhu has a long thin neck and a small hexagonal or octagonal sound box covered on the front with snakeskin (now often synthetic), which gives the instrument its distinctive resonant, expressive tone. Unlike the violin, the erhu has no fingerboard; the player stops the strings by pressing them with the fingertips while suspended in the air. The two strings (tuned a fifth apart, typically D4–A4) are traditionally silk, though steel strings are now common, and the bow's hair passes between the two strings.

History and repertoire

Introduced to China from Central Asia by the tenth century (the huqin family appears in the Tang dynasty), the erhu rose to concert prominence in the twentieth century, notably through the virtuoso Liu Tianhua, who composed its first solo études. Its characteristic sound is often described as resembling the human voice. Standard solo pieces include Erquan Yingyue ("Moon Reflected on Second Spring," by Abing) and Sanmenxia Changxiangqu ("Rhapsody of the Sanmen Gorge").
