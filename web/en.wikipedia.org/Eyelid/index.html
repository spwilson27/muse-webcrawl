Eyelid

The eyelids protect and lubricate the eye.

An eyelid is a thin fold of skin that covers and protects the eye. Humans have an upper and a lower eyelid for each eye; the upper lid is the larger and more mobile, raised by the levator palpebrae superioris muscle and closed by the orbicularis oculi. Blinking—occurring roughly 15–20 times per minute—spreads tears across the cornea, clearing debris and keeping the surface moist.

Anatomy

The eyelid consists of skin, muscle, the fibrous tarsal plate, and conjunctiva lining its inner surface. Along the lid margins sit the eyelashes and the openings of the meibomian glands, which secrete the oily layer of the tear film. The medial canthus contains the lacrimal puncta, which drain tears into the nasolacrimal duct. In many East Asian people the upper lid lacks a crease (a "single eyelid"), while others have a supratarsal fold ("double eyelid"); an epicanthic fold covers the inner corner in some populations. Many other vertebrates possess a third eyelid, the nictitating membrane.

Disorders

Common eyelid conditions include styes (infected lash follicles), chalazia (blocked meibomian glands), blepharitis (lid-margin inflammation), ptosis (drooping of the upper lid), entropion and ectropion (inward or outward turning of the margin), and basal cell carcinoma, the most frequent malignant eyelid tumour.
