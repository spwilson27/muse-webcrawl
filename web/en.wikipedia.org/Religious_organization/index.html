Religious organization

A religious organization is an institution established to support, administer, or practise a religion. Such organisations include churches, mosques, synagogues, temples, monasteries, denominations, and missionary and charitable bodies affiliated with a faith.

Sociological types

Sociologists of religion commonly classify religious organisations along a church–sect continuum: churches are large, established bodies integrated with society; denominations are mainstream organisations that accept pluralism; sects are smaller, more demanding groups in tension with their surroundings; and cults or new religious movements form around novel beliefs or charismatic leaders.

Legal status

Religious organisations often enjoy specific legal recognition, such as tax-exempt status and protection of religious freedom, and in many countries they operate extensive networks of schools, hospitals, and charities.
