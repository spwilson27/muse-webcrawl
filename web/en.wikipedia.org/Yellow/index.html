Yellow

Yellow is the colour between green and orange on the spectrum of visible light, evoked by light with a dominant wavelength of roughly 570–590 nanometres. It is one of the subtractive primary colours used in printing (with cyan and magenta), and in the additive RGB system yellow is produced by combining red and green light. Because of its high visibility, yellow is used worldwide for warning signs, taxis and safety equipment.

In nature and pigments

Yellow ochre, a clay coloured by hydrated iron oxide, was among the first pigments used by humans, appearing in prehistoric cave art. Plants and animals display yellow through carotenoid pigments — in autumn leaves, egg yolks and bananas — while flowers such as sunflowers and daffodils use it to attract pollinators. Bright yellows in frogs and insects often advertise toxicity (aposematism). Historical pigments included the brilliant but poisonous orpiment and chrome yellow; modern yellows are typically organic azo compounds or cadmium yellow.

History and symbolism

In imperial China, yellow was the colour of the emperor and the centre of the cosmos. In Europe it acquired mixed meanings: gold-adjacent associations with wealth and sunlight, but also, in medieval and early modern usage, marks of exclusion — Judas was often depicted in yellow, and yellow badges were imposed on stigmatised groups, a practice culminating in the Nazi yellow star. In modern political symbolism yellow is associated variously with liberalism, libertarianism and pro-democracy movements such as Hong Kong's Umbrella Movement (2014). "Yellow journalism" denotes sensationalist reporting, and yellow is the colour of the leader's jersey in the Tour de France.

Colour coordinates

Web yellow is defined as hex #FFFF00, RGB (255, 255, 0). Process yellow in CMYK printing is (0, 0, 100, 0), an approximation not reproducible exactly in RGB. Shades and tints between green and orange include chartreuse, amber, gold and cream.
