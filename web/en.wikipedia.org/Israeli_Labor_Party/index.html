Israeli Labor Party

Labor dominated Israeli politics for the state's first three decades.

The Israeli Labor Party (Hebrew: מפלגת העבודה הישראלית, Mifleget HaAvoda HaYisraelit), commonly known as HaAvoda, is a social democratic political party in Israel. Formed in 1968 by the merger of Mapai, Ahdut HaAvoda and Rafi, it led every Israeli government from the founding of the state in 1948 until 1977.

History

Under David Ben-Gurion, Levi Eshkol and Golda Meir, Labor (and its predecessor Mapai) built the institutions of the new state, including the Histadrut labour federation and the kibbutz movement. After electoral defeat in 1977, the party returned to power under Yitzhak Rabin and Shimon Peres, signing the Oslo Accords with the PLO (1993–95) and the peace treaty with Jordan (1994). Rabin was assassinated in 1995. Ehud Barak led Labor to its last outright victory in 1999; the party subsequently declined sharply, overtaken by Kadima, Likud and centrist parties.

Ideology

Labor has historically advocated a two-state solution to the Israeli–Palestinian conflict, a mixed social-market economy, and secular Zionism, though its platform shifted toward the centre from the 1990s onward.
