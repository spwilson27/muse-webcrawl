The Vedas
Texts:Rigveda, Yajurveda, Samaveda, Atharvaveda
Language:Vedic Sanskrit
Composed:c. 1500–500 BCE (approx.)
Classification:Shruti ("what is heard")

The Vedas are a large body of religious texts originating in ancient India, composed in Vedic Sanskrit and forming the oldest layer of Sanskrit literature and the oldest scriptures of Hinduism. Hindus regard the Vedas as śruti — "that which is heard", revealed rather than authored.

A Rigveda manuscript page.

The four Vedas

  • Rigveda — 1,028 hymns to the gods (Agni, Indra, Soma, etc.), the oldest text.
  • Yajurveda — prose mantras and ritual formulas for sacrifices.
  • Samaveda — melodies and chants drawn largely from the Rigveda.
  • Atharvaveda — spells, charms, and practical hymns for everyday life.

Structure

Each Veda comprises four parts: the Samhitas (mantras), the Brahmanas (ritual commentary), the Aranyakas ("forest texts"), and the Upanishads — philosophical texts that became the basis of Vedanta.

Transmission

The Vedas were preserved for well over a millennium by exacting oral recitation techniques before being written down; UNESCO has recognized Vedic chanting as a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity.
