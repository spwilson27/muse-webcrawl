Khosrow II

Khosrow II (Middle Persian: Xusrō; c. 570 – 28 February 628), commonly known as Khosrow Parviz ("Khosrow the Victorious"), was the last great Sasanian King of Kings of Iran, reigning from 590 to 628. A son of Hormizd IV and grandson of Khosrow I, his reign saw the Sasanian Empire reach its greatest territorial extent — and then collapse into ruin.

Rise to power

Khosrow was proclaimed king during the revolt of the general Bahram Chobin against his father in 590. Defeated by Bahram, Khosrow fled to the Byzantine Empire, and the emperor Maurice restored him to the throne in 591 with military support, receiving territory in Armenia and the Caucasus in return. Khosrow eliminated rivals and consolidated his rule over the following decade.

The great war with Byzantium

When Maurice was murdered in 602 by the usurper Phocas, Khosrow declared war, ostensibly to avenge his benefactor. Persian armies conquered Syria, Palestine (taking Jerusalem and the True Cross in 614), Egypt, and much of Anatolia, reaching the shores of the Bosporus opposite Constantinople by 626. But the emperor Heraclius rallied the Byzantines, campaigned deep into Mesopotamia, and crushed the Sasanian army at the Battle of Nineveh in December 627.

Downfall and legacy

With Heraclius advancing on the capital Ctesiphon, Khosrow was deposed in a coup led by his son Kavad II, imprisoned, and executed in February 628. The catastrophic war exhausted both empires and left them vulnerable to the Arab Muslim conquests that began within a decade. In Persian literature, Khosrow's romance with the Armenian princess Shirin became one of the great love stories of the tradition, retold by Ferdowsi and Nizami.
