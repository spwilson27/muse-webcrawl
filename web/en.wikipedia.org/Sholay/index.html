Sholay

Sholay (Hindi: शोले, "Embers") is a 1975 Indian Hindi-language action-adventure film directed by Ramesh Sippy, written by the duo Salim–Javed, and produced by G. P. Sippy. An archetypal "curry Western" and the definitive masala film, it stars Dharmendra and Amitabh Bachchan as two small-time criminals, Veeru and Jai, hired by a retired police officer, Thakur Baldev Singh (Sanjeev Kumar), to capture the sadistic bandit Gabbar Singh (Amjad Khan), who has terrorised the village of Ramgarh. Hema Malini and Jaya Bhaduri co-star.

Production and plot

The film was shot over two and a half years in the rocky terrain of Ramanagara, Karnataka, initially in 70mm widescreen with stereophonic sound — a first for Indian cinema. Drawing on the Westerns of Sergio Leone and Sam Peckinpah, Kurosawa's Seven Samurai, and earlier Indian dacoit films, it interleaves action set pieces — a train robbery, shoot-outs in the ravines — with comedy (the jailer Asrani, the chatterbox Basanti), romance, and R. D. Burman's songs, including "Yeh Dosti" and "Mehbooba Mehbooba". In the climax, Jai sacrifices his life holding off Gabbar's men so Veeru can escape. The censors required Gabbar to be captured by the police rather than killed by the Thakur; Sippy's original ending was restored in the 204-minute director's cut decades later.

Reception and legacy

Though initial reviews were lukewarm, Sholay became a phenomenon: it ran for over five years in a single Bombay theatre (Minerva), broke box-office records across India and the Soviet Union, and is often cited as the highest-grossing Indian film of all time when adjusted for inflation. Its dialogue entered everyday speech — Gabbar's "Kitne aadmi the?" ("How many men were there?") and "Jo darr gaya, samjho marr gaya" are among Hindi cinema's most quoted lines — and Amjad Khan's Gabbar Singh is routinely ranked the greatest villain in Indian film history. The BBC and Sky polls and the BFI have repeatedly named it among the greatest Indian films ever made; it has been re-released in 3D (2014) and restored for its 50th anniversary. "Sholay" has become shorthand in Indian popular culture for the classic masala blockbuster.
