Standard of care

FieldLaw (tort), medicine, professional regulation
ConceptThe level of care a reasonably competent practitioner would provide
Used inNegligence and malpractice claims

The standard of care is the level of caution, skill and treatment that a reasonably competent person or professional in the same field would exercise under similar circumstances. It is a central legal concept in negligence law: a defendant breaches their duty of care when their conduct falls below the applicable standard.

In general negligence

For ordinary activities, the standard is that of the "reasonable person". It is adjusted for children (compared with children of similar age and experience), and for physical disabilities, while professionals and those with superior skill are held to the standard of a competent member of their profession.

In medicine

In medical malpractice, the standard of care is generally what a reasonably competent practitioner in the same specialty, with similar training, would have done in the same situation. It is typically established at trial through expert testimony, clinical guidelines and the literature. English law historically applied the Bolam test — conduct accepted as proper by a responsible body of medical opinion — as qualified by Bolitho, requiring that opinion to withstand logical analysis. Locality rules, which judged doctors against practice in their own community, have largely given way to national standards.

Other contexts

Standards of care also apply to lawyers, accountants, engineers, financial advisers and corporate directors (often framed as fiduciary duties), and in fields such as childcare and construction.
