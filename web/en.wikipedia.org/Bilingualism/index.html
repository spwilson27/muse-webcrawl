Bilingualism

Bilingualism is the regular use of two languages by an individual or a community. A bilingual person can communicate in two languages, though levels of fluency may vary considerably between the two. Bilingualism is widespread: estimates suggest that more than half of the world's population uses more than one language in everyday life.

Types

Researchers distinguish simultaneous bilingualism, in which a child learns two languages from birth, from sequential bilingualism, in which a second language is learned after the first is established. Balanced bilinguals have roughly equal proficiency in both languages, while dominant bilinguals are more proficient in one.

Cognitive aspects

Bilingual speakers must manage competition between their languages, which has led to research on possible cognitive effects, including executive control and delayed onset of dementia symptoms. Bilinguals also engage in code-switching, alternating between languages within a conversation or sentence.

Societal bilingualism

Many countries are officially bilingual or multilingual, including Canada (English and French) and Belgium. Societal bilingualism is maintained through education policy, media, and everyday community practice.
