Arboriculture

Arboriculture is the cultivation, management, and study of individual trees, shrubs, vines, and other perennial woody plants. The science and practice of arboriculture includes planting, pruning, fertilizing, cabling and bracing, risk assessment, and the diagnosis and treatment of diseases and pests affecting individual trees rather than whole forests.

Arboriculture is distinct from both forestry, which manages stands of trees for timber or ecosystem services, and horticulture more generally, in that it focuses on the health, structure, and safety of single specimen trees, especially those in urban and suburban landscapes.

History

The care of individual trees dates back to ancient Egypt, China, and Rome, where shade and fruit trees were planted and tended in gardens and along roads. Modern arboriculture developed in the nineteenth century, when writers such as John Davey, often called the "father of tree surgery," began to systematize pruning and cavity treatment. The profession professionalized in the twentieth century with the founding of bodies such as the International Society of Arboriculture (ISA) in 1924 and the development of national standards for tree care.

Practices

Core practices include structural pruning to develop a sound branch framework, removal of dead or hazardous limbs, soil management and mulching to improve root conditions, and integrated pest management. Arborists also install lightning protection systems in valuable trees and use cabling and bracing to support weak branch unions.

Tree risk assessment

Because large trees near people and property can pose a hazard, arborists assess the likelihood of failure and its consequences. Methods range from visual tree assessment to resistograph drilling and sonic tomography, which map internal decay. Risk assessment informs decisions about mitigation, monitoring, or removal.

Professional standards

In North America, the ANSI A300 standards govern pruning, fertilization, and support systems, while the ISA offers the Certified Arborist credential and best management practices. Comparable standards exist in Europe, Australia, and elsewhere.
