Second

The second (symbol: s) is the base unit of time in the International System of Units (SI). Since 1967 it has been defined as the duration of 9,192,631,770 periods of the radiation corresponding to the transition between the two hyperfine levels of the ground state of the caesium-133 atom. Sixty seconds make a minute and 3,600 seconds an hour.

History

The second originated as 1/86,400 of the mean solar day: hours were divided into minutes (pars minuta prima, "first small part") and minutes into seconds (pars minuta secunda, "second small part") in medieval Latin astronomy, following Babylonian sexagesimal division. Pendulum clocks of the 17th century first made the second measurable. In 1956 the second was defined as a fraction of the tropical year (the ephemeris second); in 1967 the atomic definition based on caesium was adopted, and in 2019 it was restated in terms of the fixed numerical value of the caesium frequency, ΔνCs = 9,192,631,770 Hz.

Use and multiples

The second is subdivided decimally into milliseconds, microseconds, nanoseconds and smaller units used in physics and computing, and scaled up into kiloseconds and megaseconds. Because the Earth's rotation is irregular and slowing, leap seconds are occasionally added to Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) to keep it within 0.9 s of astronomical time; international bodies have resolved to discontinue leap seconds by 2035.
