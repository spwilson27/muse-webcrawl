Current collector

A current collector is a device used in electric vehicles and railway systems to collect electric current from an external power supply, such as an overhead catenary wire or a third rail, and deliver it to the traction equipment. The term also applies to the electrode substrates in batteries and electrochemical cells.

Current collector
Types: Pantograph, trolley pole, collector shoe, bow collector
Used on: Electric trains, trams, trolleybuses
Power sources: Overhead line, third rail

Railway current collection

The pantograph — a spring- or air-loaded articulated frame pressing a carbon contact strip against the overhead wire — is the most common collector on modern electric trains. Trams historically used trolley poles with a grooved wheel or sliding shoe, while many metro and suburban systems use collector shoes sliding on an energised third rail.

Requirements

Collectors must maintain continuous, low-resistance contact at speed without excessive wear to either the strip or the wire, and must accommodate vertical movement from track irregularities and wire height variation. Loss of contact causes arcing, which damages both surfaces.

In batteries

In electrochemistry, current collectors are the conductive foils — typically aluminium for cathodes and copper for anodes in lithium-ion cells — that gather current from the electrode coatings and conduct it to the cell terminals.
