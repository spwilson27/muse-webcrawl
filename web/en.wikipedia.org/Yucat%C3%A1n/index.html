Yucatán is a state of Mexico occupying the northwestern part of the Yucatán Peninsula. Its capital and largest city is Mérida. The state is known for its Maya heritage, colonial cities, limestone cenotes, and henequen haciendas.

History

The region was a heartland of the ancient Maya civilisation, home to cities such as Chichén Itzá and Uxmal. Spanish conquest in the sixteenth century was followed by colonial rule; in the nineteenth century Yucatán twice declared independence from Mexico, and the long Caste War began in 1847. A boom in henequen (sisal) fibre made Mérida wealthy around 1900.

Geography and culture

The state sits on a flat limestone platform with no surface rivers; water collects in cenotes, natural sinkholes central to Maya religion and modern tourism. Yucatecan culture blends Maya and Spanish influences in language (Yucatec Maya is widely spoken), cuisine such as cochinita pibil, and music including jarana.
