Technology journalism

Technology journalists cover products, companies, and the social effects of technology.

Technology journalism is the use of journalistic media to report on technology-related subjects, including consumer electronics, software, the internet, scientific research commercialization, and the business and regulation of the technology industry. It ranges from enthusiast product coverage to investigative reporting on issues such as privacy, artificial intelligence, and platform power.

History

Technology coverage grew with the personal computer revolution of the late 1970s and 1980s, spawning specialist magazines such as Byte, PC Magazine, and later Wired (founded 1993), alongside technology desks at newspapers. The commercial web of the 1990s and the blog era of the 2000s produced influential online outlets such as Ars Technica, TechCrunch, Engadget, and The Verge, and turned product launches — especially by companies such as Apple — into major media events.

Practice and criticism

Technology journalism includes product reviews, how-to guides, industry analysis, and coverage of research. Critics have noted tensions in the field: reliance on advertising from the companies covered, access journalism around embargoed launches, hype cycles around new technologies, and historically narrow demographics in the profession. Since the 2010s, scrutiny of large technology firms' social impact has become a central theme, sometimes labelled the "techlash".
