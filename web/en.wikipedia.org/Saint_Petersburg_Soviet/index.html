Saint Petersburg Soviet

The Saint Petersburg Soviet of Workers' Deputies was a workers' council (soviet) formed in October 1905 during the Russian Revolution of 1905. It coordinated the general strike in the imperial capital and is regarded as the prototype of the soviets that became central to the Russian Revolution of 1917.

Formation

The Soviet emerged from strike committees during the October general strike, which began among printers and railway workers and spread across the empire. On 13 October 1905 (Old Style), striking workers convened at the Technological Institute in Saint Petersburg and elected deputies — roughly one per 500 workers — from the city's factories. About 400–500 deputies represented some 200,000 workers at its height.

Leadership and activity

Its first chairman was the lawyer Georgy Nosar (under the name Khrustalev). After Nosar's arrest, leadership passed to a presidium in which Leon Trotsky, recently returned from exile, played a leading role. The Soviet published its own newspaper, Izvestiya, organised strike funds, militia detachments and food supplies, and negotiated with the government of Sergei Witte. Its "Financial Manifesto" of 2 December 1905 called on citizens to refuse taxes and withdraw bank deposits, aiming to bankrupt the tsarist state.

Suppression

On 3 December 1905, troops surrounded the building where the Soviet sat and arrested its deputies, including Trotsky. The uprising that followed in Moscow was crushed within weeks, ending the Soviet's existence after fifty days.

Legacy

In February 1917 the model was revived as the Petrograd Soviet of Workers' and Soldiers' Deputies, whose "dual power" rivalry with the Provisional Government shaped the course of the revolution.
