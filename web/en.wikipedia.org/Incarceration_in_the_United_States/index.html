Incarceration in the United States

Incarceration in the United States is one of the main forms of punishment and rehabilitation for the commission of felony and other offences. The United States has historically had one of the highest incarceration rates in the world, with roughly two million people held in prisons, jails and other correctional facilities at its peak.

History

The prison population grew rapidly from the 1970s onward, driven by longer sentences, mandatory minimums, the war on drugs and changes in parole policy. Growth slowed after about 2009, and the total has since declined modestly.

Facilities

People are held in federal and state prisons, local jails, juvenile facilities, immigration detention and other institutions. A small share are run by private companies under contract.

Criticism and reform

Critics point to racial disparities, the social and economic costs of mass incarceration and high recidivism rates; reform proposals range from changes in drug sentencing to expanded alternatives to custody.
