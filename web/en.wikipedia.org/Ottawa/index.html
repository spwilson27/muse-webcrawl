Ottawa

Ottawa is the capital city of Canada, located in the province of Ontario at the confluence of the Ottawa, Gatineau, and Rideau rivers. With a population of about one million, it is Canada's fourth-largest city and, together with Gatineau across the river in Quebec, forms the National Capital Region.

History

Founded in 1826 as Bytown, a settlement for workers building the Rideau Canal under Colonel John By, the town was renamed Ottawa in 1855. Queen Victoria chose it as the capital of the Province of Canada in 1857, a compromise between Toronto and Montreal, and it became the capital of the Dominion of Canada at Confederation in 1867.

Government and economy

Ottawa is home to Parliament Hill, the Supreme Court, and most federal government departments, which are major employers alongside a large technology sector nicknamed "Silicon Valley North." The city is officially bilingual, reflecting its position on the Ontario–Quebec border.
