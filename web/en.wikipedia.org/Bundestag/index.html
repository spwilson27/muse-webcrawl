The Bundestag (German: [ˈbʊndəstaːk], "Federal Diet") is the federal parliament of Germany and the only federal representative body directly elected by the German people. Together with the Bundesrat, which represents the state governments, it forms the German legislature. It has met in the Reichstag building in Berlin since 1999, having previously sat in Bonn.

Functions

The Bundestag enacts federal legislation, elects the Federal Chancellor, approves the federal budget, oversees the executive, and authorises deployments of the Bundeswehr abroad. Its members (MdB) are elected for four-year terms by a mixed-member proportional system: voters cast one vote for a constituency candidate and a second for a party list, with the second vote determining each party's overall share, subject to a 5% threshold.

Composition and history

The first Bundestag was elected in 1949 for the new Federal Republic. Its size varied for decades because of overhang and balance seats — reaching 736 members after the 2021 election, then the second-largest elected legislature in the world. Electoral reform fixed the size at 630 seats from the 2025 election. Sessions are presided over by the President of the Bundestag, traditionally a member of the largest parliamentary group.
