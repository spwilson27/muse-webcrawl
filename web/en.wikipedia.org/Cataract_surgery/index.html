Cataract surgery, also called lens replacement surgery, is the removal of the natural lens of the eye (the "crystalline lens") that has developed an opacification — a cataract — and its replacement with an artificial intraocular lens (IOL). It is one of the most common, safest, and most effective surgical procedures performed worldwide, typically done on an outpatient basis under local anaesthesia.

History

The oldest technique, "couching" — dislocating the lens into the vitreous — was practised in antiquity, including by the Indian surgeon Sushruta. In 1747 Jacques Daviel performed the first planned extracapsular extraction. In 1949 Harold Ridley implanted the first intraocular lens in London, inspired by observing that fragments of aircraft-canopy acrylic in pilots' eyes were well tolerated. Charles Kelman introduced phacoemulsification (ultrasonic fragmentation of the lens) in 1967, enabling small-incision surgery.

Techniques

Phacoemulsification

The modern standard in most developed countries: through a small corneal incision (about 2–3 mm), an ultrasonic probe emulsifies the lens nucleus, which is aspirated; a foldable IOL is then inserted into the remaining capsular bag.

Extracapsular and manual small-incision surgery

In extracapsular cataract extraction (ECCE), the lens nucleus is removed in one piece through a larger incision. Manual small-incision cataract surgery (MSICS) is a low-cost, sutureless variant widely used in high-volume settings in the developing world, with outcomes approaching phacoemulsification.

Intracapsular extraction

Removal of the lens and capsule together — now rarely used because of higher complication rates.

Intraocular lenses

IOLs may be monofocal (one focus distance), multifocal, toric (correcting astigmatism), or accommodating. Lens power is calculated pre-operatively from eye measurements (biometry) to target a desired refractive outcome.

Risks and complications

Serious complications are uncommon. They include posterior capsule opacification (the most common late issue, treated with YAG laser capsulotomy), endophthalmitis (intraocular infection), retinal detachment, corneal oedema, and refractive surprise.
