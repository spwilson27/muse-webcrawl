Politics of Italy

The politics of Italy take place within the framework of a unitary parliamentary republic, in which the president of Italy is the head of state and the president of the Council of Ministers (the prime minister) is the head of government. Executive power is exercised by the government, legislative power is vested in both the government and the two houses of Parliament, and the judiciary is independent of the executive and the legislature.

Italy has been a democratic republic since 2 June 1946, when the monarchy was abolished by popular referendum and a constituent assembly drafted the Constitution of Italy, which entered into force on 1 January 1948.

Institutions

The Parliament is perfectly bicameral, consisting of the Chamber of Deputies (400 members since 2022) and the Senate of the Republic (200 elected members plus life senators), both elected for five-year terms. The President of the Republic is elected by Parliament and regional delegates for a seven-year term, appoints the prime minister, and can dissolve Parliament. The Constitutional Court (Corte Costituzionale) reviews the constitutionality of laws. Italy is divided into 20 regions, five with special autonomous statutes, as well as provinces and municipalities (comuni).

Political history

The post-war "First Republic" (1948–1994) was dominated by the Christian Democracy party, governing in coalition while the large Italian Communist Party remained in permanent opposition. The Tangentopoli corruption scandals of the early 1990s destroyed the traditional parties and ushered in the "Second Republic," characterized by competition between centre-right coalitions led for two decades by Silvio Berlusconi and centre-left alliances. The Five Star Movement's breakthrough in 2013 and 2018 ushered in a more fragmented era. Following the 2022 general election, Giorgia Meloni of Brothers of Italy became prime minister, leading a right-wing coalition; Sergio Mattarella has served as president since 2015.

Features

Italian politics is known for frequent government turnover (more than 65 governments since 1946), coalition bargaining, and recurring debate over electoral reform. The electoral system since 2017 (the Rosatellum) combines proportional representation with single-member districts.
