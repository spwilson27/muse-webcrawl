Emamectin

Emamectin is an insecticide derived from abamectin, a fermentation product of the bacterium Streptomyces avermitilis. It is widely used as the benzoate salt, emamectin benzoate, against lepidopteran pests of vegetables, cotton, maize and other crops.

Emamectin acts on the nervous system of insects by binding to glutamate-gated chloride channels and gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) receptors, causing paralysis and death. It is translaminar, moving into leaf tissue, which gives it extended residual activity against leaf-feeding larvae.

In aquaculture, emamectin benzoate is used in feed to control sea lice infestations in farmed salmon, though concerns have been raised about its effects on non-target marine crustaceans and the development of resistance.
