Hungary (Hungarian: Magyarország) is a landlocked country in Central Europe, bordered by Austria, Slovakia, Ukraine, Romania, Serbia, Croatia, and Slovenia. Spanning much of the Carpathian Basin, it is dominated by the Great Hungarian Plain and crossed by the Danube. Its capital and largest city is Budapest.

History

The Hungarian state was founded around 1000 by King Stephen I, who Christianised the Magyar tribes that had settled the basin in the late ninth century. Hungary suffered Mongol invasion in 1241–42 and, after the Battle of Mohács in 1526, was divided for 150 years between the Ottoman Empire, the Habsburgs, and Transylvania. Reunified under Habsburg rule, it gained equal status in Austria-Hungary in 1867, but lost two-thirds of its territory under the Treaty of Trianon (1920). Hungary was an Axis power in the Second World War, then a Soviet satellite until the failed revolution of 1956 and the transition to democracy in 1989.

Geography and culture

The country is known for Lake Balaton, Central Europe's largest lake, its thermal baths, and the Hortobágy puszta. Hungarian, a Uralic language unrelated to its Indo-European neighbours, anchors a culture rich in folk music, literature, and cuisine (goulash, paprika). Hungary joined NATO in 1999 and the European Union in 2004.

Economy

Hungary has a high-income mixed economy with strong automotive, electronics, and pharmaceutical sectors, and a large services sector centred on Budapest.
