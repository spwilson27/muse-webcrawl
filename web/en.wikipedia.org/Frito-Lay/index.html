Frito-Lay

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Frito-Lay, Inc. is an American food company that manufactures, markets, and distributes corn chips, potato chips, and other snack foods. It is a division of PepsiCo, formed by the 1965 merger of the Frito Company and the H.W. Lay Company. Its brands include Lay's, Fritos, Doritos, Cheetos, Tostitos, Ruffles, and SunChips, and the company is the largest snack-food maker in the United States.

History

In 1932, San Antonio businessman Charles Elmer Doolin bought a corn chip recipe and business for $100 and founded the Frito Company, selling Fritos corn chips during the Great Depression. Meanwhile, Herman W. Lay, who had begun distributing potato chips in the American South in the 1930s, acquired the Atlanta-based Barrett Food Company in 1938 and renamed it H.W. Lay & Co. The two companies merged in 1961 to form Frito-Lay, Inc., which combined five factories and a national distribution network. In 1965 Frito-Lay merged with the Pepsi-Cola Company to create PepsiCo.

Products and innovation

Frito-Lay introduced Doritos in 1966, the first nationally distributed tortilla chip, and developed Tostitos (1979) and SunChips (1991). Its direct-store-delivery system, in which company drivers stock and rotate supermarket shelves, is widely studied as a logistics model. The company has also invested in baked and reduced-fat lines and in removing artificial flavours and colours from many products.

Operations

Frito-Lay North America operates dozens of manufacturing plants and thousands of delivery routes, and snack foods account for a substantial share of PepsiCo's revenue. Outside North America, PepsiCo sells many of the same brands through local divisions.
