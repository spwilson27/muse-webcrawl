Transport for London

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Transport for London (TfL) is a local government body responsible for most of the transport network in London, United Kingdom. Created in 2000 as part of the Greater London Authority, it took over functions from London Regional Transport and other predecessor bodies. TfL's role spans London Underground, London Buses, the Docklands Light Railway, London Overground, the Elizabeth line, London Trams, river services, cycling infrastructure, and the city's principal road network.

History

TfL was established under the Greater London Authority Act 1999 and began operating on 3 July 2000. It assumed control of London Underground in 2003 after the collapse of the public–private partnership maintenance arrangements was progressively unwound. The Oyster card smart ticketing system launched in 2003, followed by contactless payment acceptance from 2014.

Services

TfL manages or franchises: the Tube network of 272 stations; a bus network carrying around 2 billion passenger journeys a year before the COVID-19 pandemic; suburban rail concessions (London Overground and the Elizabeth line); the Docklands Light Railway; and Santander Cycles bike hire. It also licenses taxis and private hire vehicles, operates the congestion charge (introduced 2003) and the Ultra Low Emission Zone (introduced 2019).

Governance and funding

TfL is controlled by a board chaired by the Mayor of London. Its funding comes from fares, government grants, borrowing, and road user charges. Its planning role influences transit-oriented development around stations across the capital.
