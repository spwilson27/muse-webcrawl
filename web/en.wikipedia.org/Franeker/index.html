Franeker (West Frisian: Frjentsjer) is a city in the province of Friesland in the north of the Netherlands, and one of the eleven Frisian cities. Since 2018 it has been part of the municipality of Waadhoeke. It lies roughly 20 km west of Leeuwarden, on the Van Harinxma Canal.

History

Franeker developed around a defensive stronghold and received city rights in 1414. From 1585 to 1811 it was home to the University of Franeker, the second university founded in the Netherlands, which attracted students and scholars from across Europe before being closed under Napoleon. The town's star attraction is the Royal Eise Eisinga Planetarium, built between 1774 and 1781 by the wool-comber and amateur astronomer Eise Eisinga in the ceiling of his house — the oldest working planetarium in the world, inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2023.

Culture

Franeker is a centre of the Frisian handball game kaatsen; the PC tournament, the most important kaatsen competition, has been held in the town since 1854. Notable natives include the astronomer Jan Hendrik Oort, after whom the Oort cloud is named, and the painter Anna Maria Behn. The town retains a well-preserved historic centre with canals and monumental houses from its university era.
