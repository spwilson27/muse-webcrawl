War memorial

A war memorial is a building, monument, statue, or other edifice to celebrate a war or victory, or (predominating in modern times) to commemorate those who died or were injured in a war. Memorials range from triumphal arches and national monuments to simple plaques in churches listing the names of local men and women who did not return.

History

Commemoration of war is ancient — the Romans raised triumphal arches and columns for victories — but the modern practice of memorializing ordinary soldiers by name developed in the 19th century and expanded enormously after the First World War. In Britain and the Commonwealth, nearly every village erected a memorial listing its dead; in London, the Cenotaph in Whitehall became the focus of national remembrance. After the Second World War, names were usually added to the existing memorials rather than new ones built.

Symbolism and forms

Common forms include obelisks, crosses, cenotaphs (empty tombs), statues of soldiers, memorial halls and gardens. Many memorials incorporate symbolic elements: the poppy in Commonwealth countries, the eternal flame, and the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier, first established after 1918 to honor unidentified dead. Famous examples include the Arc de Triomphe in Paris, the Menin Gate at Ypres, and the Vietnam Veterans Memorial in Washington, D.C., whose inscribed names of over 58,000 dead marked a shift toward somber, non-triumphal commemoration.
