Product liability

Product liability is the area of law in which manufacturers, distributors, and sellers are held responsible for injuries caused by defective products placed into the stream of commerce.

Types of defect

Claims typically allege (1) manufacturing defects, (2) design defects, or (3) failure to warn (marketing defects). In the United States, the Restatement (Second) of Torts §402A established strict liability for defective products, so plaintiffs need not prove negligence; negligence and breach of warranty theories also persist.

Development

Landmarks include MacPherson v. Buick (1916), abolishing the privity bar, and Greenman v. Yuba Power Products (1963). The European Union's Product Liability Directive (1985) imposes a similar strict-liability regime. Defenses include product misuse, state of the art, and statutes of repose.
