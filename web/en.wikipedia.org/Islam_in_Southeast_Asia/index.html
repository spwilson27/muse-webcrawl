Islam in Southeast Asia

Southeast Asia is home to some of the world's largest Muslim populations.

Islam in Southeast Asia is the second most widely practised religion in the region after Buddhism, and the majority faith in Indonesia, Malaysia and Brunei. Indonesia is the most populous Muslim-majority country in the world, with well over 200 million adherents. Islam arrived in the maritime world of Southeast Asia from the 7th century onward, spread largely by Arab, Persian, Indian and later Chinese Muslim traders, and was consolidated by local rulers who converted from the 13th century.

History

Muslim merchants sailing the monsoon trade routes between Arabia, India and China established communities in ports such as Barus on Sumatra's west coast as early as the 7th century. The first clearly attested Muslim kingdom in the region was Samudera Pasai in northern Sumatra, whose ruler Malik Ibrahim converted in the late 13th century; Marco Polo recorded a Muslim town there in 1292. From these ports, Islam spread through intermarriage, Sufi teaching and royal patronage to Java, the Malay Peninsula, Borneo, Sulawesi and the southern Philippines. The Sultanate of Malacca, founded around 1400, became a major centre of Islamic learning and trade, and its conversion encouraged neighbouring ports to adopt the faith.

Diversity and practice

Southeast Asian Islam is predominantly Sunni and follows the Shafi'i school of jurisprudence, though it historically absorbed local customs, producing distinctive syntheses such as the abangan tradition of Java and the pondok and pesantren school networks. The Nahdlatul Ulama and Muhammadiyah organisations in Indonesia are among the largest independent Muslim organisations in the world.

Muslim minorities

Significant Muslim minorities live in Thailand's deep south (the Malay-speaking Pattani region), the Philippines (notably the Bangsamoro autonomous region in Mindanao), Myanmar (including the Rohingya), Vietnam, Cambodia and Singapore, where they form an important part of religious life.
