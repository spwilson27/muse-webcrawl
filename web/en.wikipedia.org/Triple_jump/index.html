Triple jump

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The triple jump, sometimes referred to as the hop, step and jump or the hop, skip and jump, is a track and field event similar to the long jump. The competitor runs down a runway and performs three distinct phases: a hop, landing on the same foot; a step, landing on the opposite foot; and a jump into a sand-filled pit. The combined distance of the three phases is measured.

History

Jumping events with multiple bounds existed in the ancient Olympic Games and in Irish and Scottish folk games. The triple jump has been part of the modern Olympic Games since their revival in 1896 for men; the women's event was added at the 1996 Atlanta Games.

Records

The men's world record is 18.29 metres, set by Jonathan Edwards of Great Britain at the 1995 World Championships in Gothenburg — a mark that has stood for decades. The women's world record is 15.74 metres, set indoors by Yulimar Rojas of Venezuela in 2022; she also holds the outdoor record of 15.67 metres from the Tokyo Olympics of 2021.

Technique

Athletes sprint down the runway at near maximum speed and take off from a board set 13 metres (men) or 11 metres (women) from the pit in major competition. The hop phase should be flat and active, the step phase maintained with an upright posture, and the final jump driven upward before a two-footed landing in the sand. Fouls occur when the athlete steps beyond the take-off board or leaves the pit closer to the board than the landing mark.
