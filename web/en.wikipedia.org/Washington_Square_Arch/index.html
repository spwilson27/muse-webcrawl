Washington Square Arch

Location: Washington Square Park, Manhattan, New York City
Completed: 1892
Architect: Stanford White
Material: Tuckahoe marble

The Washington Square Arch is a marble triumphal arch in Washington Square Park, in the Greenwich Village neighborhood of Lower Manhattan, New York City. It commemorates the centennial of George Washington's inauguration as the first President of the United States in 1789.

History

In 1889 a temporary wooden arch, designed by architect Stanford White, was erected over Fifth Avenue just north of Washington Square to celebrate the inauguration centennial. It proved so popular that funds were raised to build a permanent marble version, also designed by White of the firm McKim, Mead & White. The permanent arch was dedicated in 1892.

Description

The arch stands about 77 feet (23 m) tall and is built of Tuckahoe marble. Its piers carry sculpture, including statues of Washington in military and civilian dress added in the 1910s, sculpted by Hermon A. MacNeil and Alexander Stirling Calder. The arch marks the traditional start of Fifth Avenue and is one of New York's best-known landmarks, closely associated with New York University, whose buildings surround the park.
