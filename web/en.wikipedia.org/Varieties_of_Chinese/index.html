Varieties of Chinese

The varieties of Chinese form a family of related languages and dialects spoken across China and by overseas communities. Although traditionally called "dialects" (方言, fāngyán) in Chinese, many are mutually unintelligible in spoken form and would be considered separate languages by linguistic criteria; they are united by a shared written tradition based on Literary Chinese and now by the promoted standard language.

Classification

Linguists usually distinguish seven to ten major groups: Mandarin (including the basis of Standard Chinese, Putonghua), Wu (including Shanghainese), Yue (including Cantonese), Min (including Hokkien and Teochew), Hakka, Gan, and Xiang, with Jin, Huizhou, and Pinghua sometimes separated as well. The groups differ markedly in phonology — southern varieties preserve many final consonants and have more tones — and in vocabulary and grammar.

Standard Chinese

Standard Chinese, based on the Beijing dialect of Mandarin, is the official language of China and Taiwan and one of the official languages of Singapore. Written vernacular Chinese, developed in the early 20th century, is largely based on Mandarin grammar and vocabulary, though Cantonese has its own informal written form.
