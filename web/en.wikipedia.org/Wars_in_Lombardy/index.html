Wars in Lombardy

Condottieri warfare in the Po Valley, 1423–1454.

The Wars in Lombardy were a series of conflicts fought between 1423 and 1454 in northern Italy, principally between the Duchy of Milan under the Visconti and the Republic of Venice, which was expanding onto the Italian mainland (terraferma) and was usually allied with Florence. The long struggle ended with the Peace of Lodi in 1454, which established a balance of power among the Italian states that endured for forty years.

Course of the wars

The wars began when Milanese forces seized Forlì in 1423, alarming Florence into alliance with Venice. Fought in successive campaigns by famous condottieri — Francesco Bussone da Carmagnola for Venice, and Francesco Sforza and Niccolò Piccinino for Milan — the fighting ebbed and flowed across Lombardy. Venice won the Battle of Maclodio (1427) and acquired Brescia and Bergamo; the Battle of Delebio (1432) saw a Venetian defeat in the Valtellina, and the naval Battle of Ponza (1435) affected the wider struggle for Naples.

The Ambrosian Republic and Sforza

When Duke Filippo Maria Visconti died without a male heir in 1447, Milan proclaimed the Golden Ambrosian Republic. Francesco Sforza, the duke's son-in-law and greatest captain, first defended and then conquered the city, entering Milan in 1450 as its new duke. Exhausted by three decades of war, Sforza, Venice, and Florence concluded the Peace of Lodi on 9 April 1454, recognising Sforza's rule and fixing the Milanese–Venetian frontier roughly along the Adda river. The settlement was soon underwritten by the Italic League, a mutual-defence pact of the peninsula's major powers.
