Lithium (medication)

Lithium, principally as lithium carbonate and lithium citrate, is a mood-stabilising medication used mainly in the treatment of bipolar disorder, for acute mania and long-term prevention of manic and depressive episodes. It also reduces the risk of suicide in mood disorders.

Medical use

Lithium was introduced into psychiatry after John Cade's 1949 observations in Australia and was widely adopted in the 1970s. It has a narrow therapeutic index: blood levels must be monitored, typically targeting 0.6–1.0 mmol/L for maintenance, because excess causes toxicity affecting the nervous system, kidneys and gut.

Side effects and monitoring

Common effects include tremor, thirst and increased urination, weight gain and cognitive dulling. Long-term use can cause hypothyroidism and reduced kidney concentrating ability, so renal and thyroid function are checked regularly. Lithium is teratogenic to a modest degree, notably Ebstein's anomaly of the heart. Its mechanism is incompletely understood but involves inositol signalling and neuroprotective pathways.
