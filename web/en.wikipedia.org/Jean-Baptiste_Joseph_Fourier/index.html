Jean-Baptiste Joseph Fourier

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Jean-Baptiste Joseph Fourier (21 March 1768 – 16 May 1830) was a French mathematician and physicist born in Auxerre and best known for initiating the investigation of Fourier series, which eventually developed into Fourier analysis and harmonic analysis, and their applications to problems of heat transfer and vibrations. The Fourier transform and Fourier's law of conduction are also named in his honour. Fourier is also generally credited with the discovery of the greenhouse effect.

Biography

Fourier was born in Auxerre (now in the Yonne département of France), the son of a tailor. He was orphaned at the age of nine. Fourier was recommended to the Bishop of Auxerre and, through this introduction, he was educated by the Benedictine monks of the Convent of Saint-Mark. He later studied at the École Normale in Paris and at the École Polytechnique, where he became a teacher. During the French Revolution he was briefly imprisoned under the Terror.

In 1798, Fourier accompanied Napoleon Bonaparte on his Egyptian expedition as scientific adviser and was appointed secretary of the Institut d'Égypte. After returning to France in 1801, Napoleon appointed him Prefect (Governor) of the Department of Isère in Grenoble, where he oversaw road construction and other projects. It was while at Grenoble that he began his experiments on the propagation of heat.

Analytical theory of heat

In 1822, Fourier published his treatise Théorie analytique de la chaleur (The Analytical Theory of Heat), in which he showed that the conduction of heat in solid bodies could be analysed in terms of an infinite mathematical series now called the Fourier series. His work introduced the representation of a function as a sum of trigonometric functions and laid foundations for the modern theory of heat conduction, expressed in Fourier's law, and for harmonic analysis generally. He was elected to the Académie des Sciences in 1817 and to the Académie française in 1826.

Greenhouse effect

Fourier is credited with an early recognition, in papers of 1824 and 1827, that the Earth's atmosphere acts to raise the planet's temperature by insulating it, an insight later developed into the concept of the greenhouse effect. He compared the atmosphere's role to the glass cover of a vessel heated by sunlight, drawing on the experiments of Horace-Bénédict de Saussure.

Legacy

Fourier's name is one of the 72 names inscribed on the Eiffel Tower. The Fourier transform, Fourier series, Fourier analysis, and Fourier's law of heat conduction all bear his name, and he is regarded as one of the founders of mathematical physics.
