Rugby is a market town in eastern Warwickshire, England, close to the River Avon and the borders with Northamptonshire and Leicestershire. It is the second-largest town in Warwickshire. Rugby is most famous as the birthplace of rugby football, which is said to have originated at Rugby School.

History

The settlement appears in the Domesday Book as Rocheberie. Rugby School was founded in 1567 as a free grammar school and became one of England's famous public schools; tradition credits its pupil William Webb Ellis with inventing rugby football in 1823 by picking up the ball and running with it during a game of football.

Industry and growth

The town grew rapidly in the 19th century as a railway junction on the West Coast Main Line, and became a centre for electrical engineering: the British Thomson-Houston works, later part of GEC and Alstom, employed thousands. Frank Whittle developed the jet engine while based in Rugby and nearby Lutterworth in the 1930s.

Transport and culture

Rugby railway station is served by intercity trains between London Euston and the West Midlands, North West and Scotland. The town is close to the M1, M6 and A14 roads. The Webb Ellis Rugby Football Museum and a statue of William Webb Ellis mark the town's sporting heritage.
