Celtic nations

The Celtic nations are a cultural area and collection of territories in Northern and Western Europe where Celtic languages and cultural traits have survived. The term "nation" is used in its original sense to mean a people who share a common identity and culture and are identified by a traditional territory.

The six territories generally recognised as Celtic nations are Brittany (Breizh), Cornwall (Kernow), Ireland (Éire), the Isle of Man (Mannin or Ellan Vannin), Scotland (Alba), and Wales (Cymru). In each of these six nations a Celtic language is spoken to some extent: Brittonic languages are spoken in Brittany, Cornwall, and Wales, while Goidelic languages are spoken in Ireland, Scotland, and the Isle of Man.

Terminology

The term "Celtic nations" as a political grouping became current during the 19th and 20th centuries, when pan-Celtic movements sought to link the cultural and linguistic revivals of the various Celtic-speaking peoples. Organisations such as the Celtic League have promoted cooperation between the six nations and campaigned for their political and cultural rights.

Other territories

Regions such as Galicia and Asturias in northern Spain have sometimes been associated with the Celtic nations because of their Celtic musical and cultural traditions, although no Celtic language has been spoken there in historical times. The Celtic Congress has admitted Galician participation, but the Celtic League requires a living Celtic language, a criterion Galicia does not meet.
