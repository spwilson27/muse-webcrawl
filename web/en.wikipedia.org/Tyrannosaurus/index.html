Tyrannosaurus

Temporal rangeLate Cretaceous, 69–66 million years ago
SpeciesT. rex (type), T. mcraeensis
LengthUp to ~12.3 m
Mass~8–9 tonnes (largest specimens)
LocationWestern North America

Tyrannosaurus is a genus of large theropod dinosaur that lived in western North America during the final years of the Cretaceous Period, about 69 to 66 million years ago. Its best-known species, Tyrannosaurus rex — "tyrant lizard king" — is one of the largest known terrestrial predators and the most famous dinosaur in popular culture.

Description

T. rex had a massive skull up to 1.5 metres long, balanced by a long muscular tail, with greatly reduced two-fingered forelimbs. Its banana-sized serrated teeth were driven by one of the strongest bite forces of any land animal, capable of crushing bone. Famous specimens include "Sue", among the most complete skeletons known, and "Scotty", among the largest.

Biology and behaviour

Growth studies show tyrannosaurs underwent a dramatic adolescent growth spurt. Debate continues over whether T. rex was purely an active predator or partly a scavenger, over its speed, senses of smell and vision, and whether juveniles bore feather-like coverings, as found in earlier tyrannosauroids such as Yutyrannus.

Extinction and fame

Tyrannosaurus was among the last non-avian dinosaurs, disappearing in the Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event 66 million years ago. Since its description by Henry Fairfield Osborn in 1905 it has become a cultural icon of prehistoric life.
