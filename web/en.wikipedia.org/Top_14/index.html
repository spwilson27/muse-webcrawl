The Top 14 is a professional rugby union club competition in France run by the Ligue Nationale de Rugby (LNR). The league is at the top of the French rugby union league system, operating promotion and relegation with the Pro D2. Through its history the championship has been the premier domestic club competition in French rugby, and the modern Top 14 — formed for the 2005–06 season — is widely regarded as one of the strongest and best-attended club leagues in world rugby.

History

The French championship was first contested in 1892, when Racing Club de France defeated Stade Français in the first final. For much of the 20th century the championship was amateur and organised directly by the French federation. Professionalism arrived in 1995, and after several restructurings — the competition was known as the Top 16 in the early 2000s — the league settled on 14 clubs for the 2005–06 season. Salary caps, minimum stadium standards and collectively sold television rights subsequently made the Top 14 the richest domestic league in the sport, attracting leading players from around the world.

Format

The fourteen clubs play a double round-robin regular season of 26 rounds. The top six advance to the playoffs: the first- and second-placed teams receive byes to the semi-finals, while third through sixth contest a preliminary round (the barrages). Semi-finals are played at a neutral venue and the final is traditionally held in Paris, most often at the Stade de France. The bottom team is relegated automatically to Pro D2, while the 13th-placed team plays an access match against the Pro D2 runner-up. The top eight clubs qualify for the following season's European Rugby Champions Cup.

Clubs and records

Stade Toulousain is the most successful club in the competition's history with 23 championship titles, and has also supplied many of the league's most celebrated players. Other historically powerful clubs include Stade Français, AS Béziers, SU Agen, ASM Clermont Auvergne, RC Toulon, Racing 92 and Montpellier. League attendances and budgets — the largest clubs operate with budgets exceeding €40 million — are the highest in club rugby.
