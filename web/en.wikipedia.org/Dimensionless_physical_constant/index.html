Dimensionless physical constant

A dimensionless physical constant is a physical constant that is dimensionless, i.e. a pure number having no units attached and a numerical value that is independent of whatever system of units is used. Perhaps the best-known example is the fine-structure constant, α, which has the approximate value 1/137.036. Unlike dimensionful constants such as the speed of light or Planck's constant — whose numerical values depend on the choice of units — dimensionless constants are genuine features of nature that any correct physical theory must explain or take as free parameters.

Examples

  • Fine-structure constant α ≈ 1/137.036 — measures the strength of the electromagnetic interaction.
  • Proton-to-electron mass ratio μ ≈ 1836.153 — the ratio of the rest masses of the proton and electron.
  • Strong coupling constant αs ≈ 0.118 (at the Z boson mass scale) — measures the strength of the strong nuclear force; unlike α it "runs" with energy scale.
  • Weinberg angle sin²θW ≈ 0.231 — mixes the electromagnetic and weak interactions in electroweak theory.
  • Cabibbo angle and CKM matrix elements — dimensionless parameters describing quark flavour mixing.
  • Feigenbaum constants δ ≈ 4.669 and α ≈ 2.503 — universal numbers arising in chaos theory and period-doubling bifurcations.
  • Euler's number e and π are mathematical rather than physical constants, but appear throughout physical law.

Role in physics

The Standard Model of particle physics contains roughly two dozen free parameters, most of them dimensionless (particle mass ratios, coupling constants and mixing angles). Their values cannot currently be derived from the theory itself and must be measured. The apparent "fine-tuning" of some dimensionless constants — small changes to which would preclude atoms, stars or chemistry — figures prominently in discussions of the anthropic principle and multiverse hypotheses.

Because dimensionless constants do not depend on human conventions, physicists regard searches for their possible variation over cosmic time (for example, through observations of distant quasars or the Oklo natural nuclear reactor) as meaningful tests of fundamental physics. By contrast, claims about a "changing speed of light" are meaningless without specifying a dimensionless quantity that changed.

Counting constants

Physicists disagree about how many fundamental dimensionless constants exist, since the count depends on the theoretical framework. John Baez and others have enumerated 26 dimensionless parameters for the Standard Model together with gravity, while additional parameters (such as neutrino masses and mixing) extend the list. Many physicists hope a future unified theory will predict some or all of these numbers.
