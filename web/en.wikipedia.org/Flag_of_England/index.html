The flag of England is the national flag of England, a constituent country of the United Kingdom. It consists of the red cross of Saint George — a vertical and horizontal red band meeting at the center — on a white field. Saint George has been the patron saint of England since the reign of Edward III in the 14th century, and his cross was widely used by English soldiers and ships from the late medieval period.

History

The red cross on white was originally associated with the Republic of Genoa, whose ships English vessels sought to emulate for protection in the Mediterranean; by the late 13th century it was firmly used as an English emblem. The cross appeared on the uniforms of English archers, on royal standards, and at sea. When England and Scotland united, the cross of St George was combined with the Scottish saltire of St Andrew to form the Union Flag in 1606, but the St George's Cross continued — and continues — as England's own flag.

Use

The flag is flown by English institutions, by the Church of England, and by English sports teams; it is ubiquitous at England football matches, having largely displaced the Union Flag among England supporters since the 1990s. Its feast-day association is St George's Day, 23 April. Within the Union Flag, the red cross representing England and Wales is given precedence over the Scottish and Irish saltires.
