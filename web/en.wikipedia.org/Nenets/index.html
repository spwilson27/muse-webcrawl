The Nenets (Nenets: ненэй ненэче, nenəj nenəče; Russian: ненцы, nentsy), also known as the Samoyed people, are a Samoyedic indigenous people of Siberia living in the far north of Russia, principally in the Yamalo-Nenets and Nenets autonomous okrugs and the Taymyr Peninsula. They are the largest of the Samoyedic peoples, numbering roughly 45,000.

Culture and livelihood

Traditionally the Nenets are nomadic reindeer herders, migrating with enormous herds — collectively the largest domesticated reindeer herds in the world — between winter pastures in the forest-tundra and summer pastures near the Arctic coast. Reindeer provide transport, clothing, shelter (the conical chum tent covered in reindeer skins), and food. Many Nenets also fish and hunt marine mammals along the coast. The two main groups, Tundra Nenets and Forest Nenets, speak related but distinct varieties of the Nenets languages.

History

The Nenets resisted Russian expansion for centuries before being incorporated into the Tsarist and then Soviet states. In the Soviet period they faced collectivisation, forced settlement, and the removal of children to boarding schools, which eroded language transmission. Since the 1990s, large-scale natural gas development on the Yamal Peninsula — home to some of the world's largest gas reserves — has brought new wealth but also pressure on grazing lands and migration routes, a central concern for Nenets communities today.
