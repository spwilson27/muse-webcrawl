Netscape Navigator

Netscape Navigator was a proprietary web browser, and the flagship product of the Netscape Communications Corporation. First released in December 1994 as version 1.0, it was the dominant web browser of the mid-1990s, holding a usage share above 80% at its peak before losing the first browser war to Microsoft's Internet Explorer.

History

Navigator was developed by a team led by Marc Andreessen, co-author of the earlier Mosaic browser, and introduced features that shaped the web, including cookies, frames, and — in version 2.0 — JavaScript and the plug-in architecture. Netscape's spectacular 1995 initial public offering is often credited with igniting the dot-com boom.

Decline and legacy

Bundling of Internet Explorer with Windows eroded Navigator's share, and AOL acquired Netscape in 1999. In 1998 Netscape open-sourced its code, seeding the Mozilla project that later produced Firefox. The last version, Netscape Navigator 9, appeared in 2007, and AOL ended support in March 2008.
