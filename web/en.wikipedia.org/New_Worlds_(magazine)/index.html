New Worlds (magazine)

New Worlds was a British science fiction magazine that began as a fanzine in 1936 and was published professionally from 1946. Under editor John Carnell it was a respectable but conventional magazine; under Michael Moorcock, who became editor in 1964, it became the flagship of the New Wave movement in science fiction.

The New Wave

Moorcock encouraged experimental, literary work by writers such as J. G. Ballard, Brian Aldiss, Thomas M. Disch and M. John Harrison, emphasising style, inner space and contemporary concerns over traditional hard science fiction. The magazine received an Arts Council grant but also controversy, including an obscenity-related distribution dispute.

Later history

Regular monthly publication ended in the early 1970s; the title continued irregularly as a paperback anthology series and later specials, exerting influence far beyond its modest circulation.
