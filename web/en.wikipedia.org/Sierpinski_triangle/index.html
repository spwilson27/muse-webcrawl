Sierpinski triangle

The Sierpinski triangle (also spelled Sierpiński, first described by Wacław Sierpiński in 1915) is a fractal with the overall shape of an equilateral triangle, subdivided recursively into smaller equilateral triangles. Originally constructed as a curve, it is one of the basic examples of self-similar sets — a mathematically generated pattern that is reproducible at any magnification or reduction.

Construction

Start with an equilateral triangle. Subdivide it into four congruent equilateral triangles and remove the central one. Repeat the step with each of the three remaining triangles, and continue indefinitely. In the limit, the resulting set has zero area but infinite perimeter in the idealised model, and its Hausdorff dimension is log 3 / log 2 ≈ 1.585.

Equivalent constructions

The same pattern arises from Pascal's triangle by shading the odd binomial coefficients, from the "chaos game" (repeatedly moving a point halfway toward a randomly chosen vertex of a triangle), and from the cellular automaton Rule 60. It is closely related to the Sierpinski carpet and the Menger sponge in three dimensions.

History and uses

Patterns resembling the triangle appear in medieval church mosaics and Cosmati pavements centuries before Sierpiński's description. The fractal has been used in art, in multiband fractal antennas for mobile telephones, and as a standard introductory example in the study of fractals and iterated function systems.
