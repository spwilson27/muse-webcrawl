History of the World Wide Web

World Wide Web
InventorTim Berners-Lee (CERN)
ProposalMarch 1989
First websiteinfo.cern.ch (1991)
Public domain release30 April 1993

The World Wide Web was invented by British scientist Tim Berners-Lee at CERN in 1989 as a system of interlinked hypertext documents accessed over the Internet. Building on ideas from Vannevar Bush's Memex and Ted Nelson's hypertext, Berners-Lee wrote the first web browser and server on a NeXT computer, defining HTML, HTTP and the URL.

Growth in the 1990s

CERN released the Web into the public domain in 1993. The graphical Mosaic browser (1993) and Netscape Navigator (1994) made the Web accessible to the public, sparking a rapid commercial expansion and the dot-com boom. Search engines, e-commerce and the founding of the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C, 1994) accompanied growth from a handful of sites to millions.

Web 2.0 and beyond

After the dot-com bust of 2000–2001, interactive "Web 2.0" platforms — Wikipedia, YouTube, Facebook and Twitter — made users creators. Smartphones after 2007 shifted access to mobile apps and responsive design, while HTML5, social media, cloud services and streaming reshaped the Web in the 2010s and 2020s.
