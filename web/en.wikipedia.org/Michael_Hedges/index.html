Michael Hedges

Michael Alden Hedges (December 31, 1953 – December 2, 1997) was an American acoustic guitarist and composer, widely regarded as one of the most innovative and influential fingerstyle guitarists of the twentieth century. He pioneered a percussive, two-handed tapping style on steel-string acoustic guitar and helped launch the Windham Hill Records label's guitar catalog.

Early life and education

Hedges was born in Sacramento, California, and raised in Enid, Oklahoma. He studied composition and electronic music at the Peabody Conservatory in Baltimore, Maryland, where he was exposed to avant-garde techniques that shaped his experimental approach to the guitar.

Style and technique

Hedges treated the guitar as a small orchestra, combining bass lines, chords, melodies and percussion on a single instrument. He used altered tunings, slapped and tapped harmonics, hammer-ons with both hands, and struck the guitar body for rhythmic effect. He frequently performed on a harp guitar and on his custom "Saville" instruments. His 1984 album Aerial Boundaries is considered a landmark of modern fingerstyle guitar.

Career

Discovered by Windham Hill founder Will Ackerman, Hedges released Breakfast in the Field (1981), Aerial Boundaries (1984), Watching My Life Go By (1985), Live on the Double Planet (1987), Taproot (1990) and The Road to Return (1994). He collaborated with bassist Michael Manring and toured extensively.

Death and legacy

Hedges died in a car accident on a rain-slicked road near Mendocino, California, in December 1997. The posthumous compilation Torched appeared in 1999. His techniques influenced generations of fingerstyle players, including Andy McKee and the "candyrat" generation of percussive guitarists.
