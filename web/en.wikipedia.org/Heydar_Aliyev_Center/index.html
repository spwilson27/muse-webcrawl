Heydar Aliyev Center

The Heydar Aliyev Center is a 57,500 m² (619,000 sq ft) building complex in Baku, Azerbaijan, designed by Iraqi-British architect Zaha Hadid and noted for its distinctive architecture and flowing, curved style that eschews sharp angles. The center is named after Heydar Aliyev (1923–2003), leader of Soviet-era Azerbaijan from 1969 to 1982 and president of Azerbaijan from 1993 to 2003.

Design

The building emerges from the surrounding plaza as a single continuous surface, its folds and undulations wrapping the distinct programmatic elements — an auditorium, a museum devoted to Heydar Aliyev, exhibition spaces, and a library — into one fluid form. Hadid described the design as fluid form emerging from the folding of the landscape's natural topography. The structure is supported by a concrete frame combined with a space frame, faced with glass-fiber-reinforced concrete and polyester panels that give the building its smooth, seamless white appearance.

History

An international competition in 2007 selected Zaha Hadid Architects for the project. The center opened on 10 May 2012, the 89th anniversary of Heydar Aliyev's birth, with a ceremony attended by President Ilham Aliyev. In 2014 the building won the Design of the Year Award from London's Design Museum, making Hadid the first woman to receive the prize. The building has become an internationally recognized landmark of modern Baku, appearing in documentaries on extreme engineering.

Interior

The auditorium seats about 1,000 people and its oak-clad interior continues the exterior's fluid geometry. The museum presents the life of Heydar Aliyev through photographs, films, and personal effects, while temporary exhibitions have included works by Andy Warhol and other major artists.
