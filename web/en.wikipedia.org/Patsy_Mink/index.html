Patsy Takemoto Mink (December 6, 1927 – September 28, 2002) was an American attorney and politician from the U.S. state of Hawaii. A member of the Democratic Party, she served in the United States House of Representatives for 24 years, representing Hawaii's at-large and 2nd congressional districts. In 1965 she became the first woman of color and the first Asian-American woman elected to Congress.

Mink is best known as a co-author and champion of Title IX of the Education Amendments of 1972, the landmark federal law prohibiting sex discrimination in education programs receiving federal funding. In recognition of her role, the law was renamed the Patsy T. Mink Equal Opportunity in Education Act after her death. She was also the first Asian-American to seek the Democratic presidential nomination, running in the 1972 Oregon primary.

Early life and education

Mink was born Patsy Matsu Takemoto in Paia, on the island of Maui, the daughter of Japanese immigrant parents. She graduated from Maui High School as valedictorian in 1944. She attended the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa and Wilson College in Pennsylvania, earning a bachelor's degree in zoology and chemistry in 1948. After being rejected by numerous medical schools—a rejection she attributed to sex discrimination—she applied to law school and was admitted to the University of Chicago Law School, earning her Juris Doctor in 1951.

After law school, Mink and her husband John Mink returned to Hawaii, where she became the first Japanese-American woman admitted to the Hawaii bar. Finding private law firms unwilling to hire her, she founded her own practice and lectured at the University of Hawaiʻi.

Political career

Mink helped found the Young Democrats of Hawaii and entered politics in the territorial legislature, serving in the Hawaii territorial House of Representatives from 1956 to 1958 and the territorial Senate from 1958 to 1959, followed by the state Senate from 1962 to 1964. When Hawaii gained a second House seat in 1964, she won election to the U.S. House of Representatives, serving from 1965 to 1977.

An outspoken liberal, Mink opposed the Vietnam War, supported the Great Society programs, and championed early childhood education, bilingual education, and women's rights. She co-founded the Congressional Caucus for Women's Issues in 1977. In 1976 she left the House to run unsuccessfully for the U.S. Senate, then served as Assistant Secretary of State for Oceans and International Environmental and Scientific Affairs under President Jimmy Carter from 1977 to 1980.

Mink returned to the House in 1990, winning a special election, and served until her death in 2002. In her later tenure she remained a leading voice on education, civil rights, and labor issues, and she opposed the USA PATRIOT Act and the 2002 authorization for the Iraq War. She died of viral pneumonia in Honolulu on September 28, 2002, and posthumously won re-election that November. In 2014 she was awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom.
