Shadow cabinet

A shadow cabinet is a feature of the Westminster system of government. It consists of a senior group of opposition spokespeople who, under the leadership of the Leader of the Opposition, form an alternative cabinet to that of the government, and whose members shadow or mirror the positions of each individual member of the Cabinet.

Function

Shadow ministers scrutinize their corresponding government ministers, develop alternative policies, and present the opposition as a government-in-waiting. In the United Kingdom, members of the shadow cabinet receive additional salary and are expected to observe collective discipline, voting with the party line. If the opposition wins the next general election, its shadow ministers commonly take the corresponding posts in government.

Usage

The institution is used in the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, and other parliamentary systems. The term is also applied informally in some other democracies to opposition policy teams, though without official status.
