Co-axial escapement

The co-axial escapement is a type of modern watch escapement invented by English watchmaker George Daniels in the 1970s. It is notable for reducing sliding friction compared with the traditional Swiss lever escapement, allowing longer service intervals and stable rate over time.

Design

The escapement uses two escape wheels mounted co-axially on the same arbor, together with an intermediate wheel and a pallet fork with additional stones. Impulse is delivered largely through direct radial impulses rather than sliding across oil-lubricated pallet faces.

Adoption

Daniels patented the design in 1980 and sought a manufacturer for years. The Swiss company Omega adopted it, introducing the first mass-produced co-axial movement, calibre 2500, in 1999. Co-axial escapements have since become a signature technology across Omega's range, later combined with silicon balance springs and anti-magnetic materials.
