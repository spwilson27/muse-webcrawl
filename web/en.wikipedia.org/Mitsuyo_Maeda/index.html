Mitsuyo Maeda

Mitsuyo Maeda (前田 光世)

Born: 18 November 1878, Aomori, Japan

Died: 28 November 1941 (aged 63), Belém, Brazil

Style: Judo, catch wrestling

Known for: Bringing judo/jiu-jitsu to Brazil; teacher of the Gracie family

Mitsuyo Maeda (前田 光世, Maeda Mitsuyo, born 18 November 1878 – 28 November 1941), a Brazilian naturalized Japanese judōka and prizefighter, is considered one of the founders of Brazilian jiu-jitsu, along with Antônio "Satake" Soshihiro, Luiz França, Oswaldo Fadda and the Gracie brothers. Maeda was also a promoter of Japanese emigration to Brazil.

Maeda won the nickname Conde Koma (Portuguese for "Count Combat") while in Spain in 1908, and was known as the "toughest man who ever lived" in his era.

Life in Japan

Maeda was born in Funazawa Village, Hirosaki, Aomori, Japan. As a teenager he practised sumo and later the classical jujutsu style Tenshin Shinyo-ryu before moving to Tokyo, where he joined Jigoro Kano's Kodokan and studied judo, becoming one of its most talented students.

Travels and prizefighting

In 1904 Maeda travelled to the United States with Tsunejiro Tomita to demonstrate judo, then spent years travelling through the Americas and Europe — including Cuba, Mexico, and Spain — fighting challenge matches against boxers, wrestlers and other martial artists, and teaching judo. His style blended Kodokan judo with catch wrestling techniques he learned abroad, emphasizing ground fighting.

Brazil

Maeda arrived in Brazil in 1914 and eventually settled in Belém. He helped establish a Japanese immigrant colony and taught judo. Among his students was Carlos Gracie, the eldest son of his friend Gastão Gracie, who had helped Maeda settle in Brazil. Carlos passed the teachings to his brothers, who adapted them into what became Brazilian jiu-jitsu (Gracie jiu-jitsu). Maeda also taught Luiz França, whose lineage produced another major branch of Brazilian jiu-jitsu.

Maeda died in Belém in 1941 of kidney disease.
