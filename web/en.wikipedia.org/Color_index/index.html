Color index

In astronomy, the color index is a simple numerical expression that determines the color of an object, which in the case of a star gives its temperature. It is the difference in magnitude of the object measured at two different wavelengths, typically blue (B) and visual (V), written B−V.

Definition

By convention, the color index of A0 main-sequence stars such as Vega is defined as zero. Hot blue stars have negative B−V values, while cool red stars have large positive values, up to about +2.0 for M-type stars. Interstellar reddening increases the observed color index, an effect corrected using the color excess E(B−V).

Systems

The Johnson–Morgan UBV system uses U (ultraviolet), B, and V passbands; other systems include Cousins, Sloan, and Strömgren photometry. Color indices are plotted on color–magnitude and Hertzsprung–Russell diagrams.

Other uses

In geology and oceanography, color indices describe rock composition and water properties, and the term also appears in printing and soil science (Munsell system).
