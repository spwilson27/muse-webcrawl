Church of the Holy Sepulchre

The Church of the Holy Sepulchre is a church in the Christian Quarter of the Old City of Jerusalem. According to traditions dating back to the fourth century, it contains the two holiest sites in Christianity: the site where Jesus was crucified, at a place known as Calvary or Golgotha, and Jesus's empty tomb, where he was buried and resurrected. The church has been a major Christian pilgrimage destination since its creation and is administered jointly by several Christian denominations under arrangements known as the Status Quo.

History

The emperor Constantine ordered a church built on the site, identified by his mother Helena, after demolishing the Roman temple of Venus there; the basilica was consecrated in 335. The Persian invasion of 614 and a fire damaged the church, and in 1009 the Fatimid caliph al-Hakim ordered its near-total destruction. Rebuilt by the Byzantine emperor Constantine IX Monomachos, the complex was enlarged by the Crusaders, who consecrated the present church in 1149. Fires, earthquakes, and Ottoman-era repairs reshaped it repeatedly; a major restoration of the Aedicule (the shrine enclosing the tomb) was completed in 2017.

The site

Within the church lie the Stone of Unction, where Jesus's body is said to have been prepared for burial; the Aedicule containing the tomb chamber; the rock of Calvary, reached by a stairway and divided between Greek Orthodox and Catholic altars; and numerous chapels, including those of St Helena, where the True Cross was said to be found. A wooden ladder on a ledge above the entrance — the "immovable ladder" — has remained in place since at least the eighteenth century as a symbol of the Status Quo, under which Greek Orthodox, Roman Catholic (Franciscan), Armenian Apostolic, Coptic, Ethiopian, and Syriac communities share rights regulated to the smallest detail.

Pilgrimage

The Via Dolorosa, the route traditionally followed by Jesus to the crucifixion, ends at the church, whose stations include the final five Stations of the Cross. The church is the climax of Holy Week observances in Jerusalem, including the ceremony of the Holy Fire on Holy Saturday.
