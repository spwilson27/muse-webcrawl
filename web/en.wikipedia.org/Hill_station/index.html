Hill station

A hill station is a town located at a higher elevation than the nearby plain or valley, most often in Asia, founded by European colonialists seeking relief from summer heat and disease in the lowlands. The term derives from British India, where administrators, soldiers and their families retreated to settlements built in the hills and mountains — the government of British India itself moved seasonally to Shimla (Simla), which served as the summer capital from 1864.

Notable hill stations

Famous examples in India include Shimla, Darjeeling, Mussoorie, Nainital, Ooty (Udhagamandalam), Kodaikanal, Munnar and Srinagar; in Pakistan, Murree; in Sri Lanka, Nuwara Eliya; in Malaysia, the Cameron Highlands and Fraser's Hill; in Vietnam, Da Lat; in Indonesia, Bogor; and in the Philippines, Baguio, which served as the American colonial summer capital. Many were connected by narrow-gauge mountain railways, three of which in India are UNESCO World Heritage Sites. After independence, hill stations remained popular domestic tourist destinations, though many now face environmental pressures from tourism, construction and water scarcity.
