Mothlight is an American experimental short film directed by Stan Brakhage, released in 1963. It is a landmark of cameraless filmmaking: the film was not photographed with a camera at all, but assembled by collage directly onto the film strip.

Production and technique

Brakhage pressed moth wings, insect parts, grass, flowers and other organic debris between two strips of clear 16 mm Mylar editing tape, then contact-printed the result onto film stock so that the objects themselves became the images. As he put it, what he had before him was "a kind of Moth Light" — a rendering of "what a moth might see." Brakhage's marriage and finances were under strain at the time, and he described the film as emerging from that crisis, a work made literally from dead things gathered around a porch light.

Reception and legacy

Running about four minutes in silent projection, Mothlight is one of the most celebrated works of American avant-garde cinema and one of Brakhage's most famous films. It is frequently cited as a defining example of "direct film" or cameraless animation, alongside Len Lye's and Norman McLaren's drawn and scratched films, and is taught widely in film studies. Brakhage returned to the technique in later works such as The Garden of Earthly Delights (1981).
