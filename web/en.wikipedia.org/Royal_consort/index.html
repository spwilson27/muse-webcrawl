A royal consort is the spouse of a reigning monarch. Consorts of monarchs in the United Kingdom and its predecessors have no constitutional status or power but many have had significant influence over their reigning spouse. In most monarchies the female consort is styled a queen consort, while a male consort is usually styled a prince consort, though some monarchies grant the style of king consort.

Titles and styles

A queen consort is not to be confused with a queen regnant, who reigns in her own right. Historically, kings were almost always heads of state, so their wives were queens consort; where a woman reigns, her husband's title varies — in Britain, Prince Philip, Duke of Edinburgh, was never king consort, while in Denmark and Spain male consorts have sometimes held the title of king.

Role

Consorts typically support the monarch in ceremonial and state duties, undertake patronage of charities and organisations, and accompany the sovereign on state visits. Some consorts have exercised political power behind the scenes, and a consort may act as regent if the sovereign is a minor or incapacitated.

Coronation

In many European monarchies the consort is crowned alongside the sovereign in a coronation ceremony, though usually with a simpler rite. Queen Camilla was crowned alongside Charles III in 2023.
