Titin

Titin (also known as connectin) is a protein that in humans is encoded by the TTN gene. It is the largest known protein, with a molecular mass of about 3–4 megadaltons and a length of over 1 micrometre in its longest isoform, and its full chemical name is sometimes cited as the longest word in English.

Function

Titin molecules span half the length of a muscle sarcomere, from the Z-disc to the M-line, in striated muscle. They act as molecular springs that provide passive elasticity, keep thick filaments centred in the sarcomere, and serve as a scaffold for numerous signalling and binding proteins. Elastic stretching of titin's spring-like regions is a major determinant of the passive stiffness of resting muscle.

Clinical significance

Mutations in TTN, particularly truncating variants, are a major cause of dilated cardiomyopathy, and other variants cause skeletal myopathies such as tibial muscular dystrophy. The gene contains 363 exons, more than any other single gene.
