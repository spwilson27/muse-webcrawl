Olympus Corporation

IndustryMedical devices, optics, precision instruments

Olympus Corporation is a Japanese manufacturer of optics and reprography products, headquartered in Shinjuku, Tokyo. Founded in 1919 as a microscope and thermometer maker, Olympus became a world leader in medical endoscopy and was long famous for its cameras before exiting that business.

History

The company took its name from Mount Olympus, home of the Greek gods. It produced Japan's first practical gastrocamera in 1950 and built a dominant share — around 70 percent — of the global gastrointestinal endoscope market. Its camera division produced celebrated lines including the compact half-frame PEN series (1959), the OM system of SLRs designed by Yoshihisa Maitani, and early digital cameras.

2011 accounting scandal

In 2011 Olympus became the centre of one of Japan's largest corporate scandals when its newly appointed British CEO, Michael Woodford, was dismissed after questioning suspicious payments used to hide investment losses dating back to the 1990s — a loss-hiding scheme totalling over a billion dollars. The company's share price collapsed, executives were prosecuted, and Olympus paid fines and restructured its governance.

Refocus on medical technology

In 2020–2021 Olympus sold its camera (Imaging) division to Japan Industrial Partners, which continues the products under the OM System brand. Olympus thereafter concentrated on medical technology — endoscopy, therapeutic devices, and surgical products — which accounts for the great majority of its revenue.
