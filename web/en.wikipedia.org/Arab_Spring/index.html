Arab Spring
DateDecember 2010 – 2012 (with later waves)
LocationMiddle East and North Africa
CausesAuthoritarianism, corruption, unemployment, rising food prices, political repression

The Arab Spring (Arabic: الربيع العربي, ar-rabīʻ al-ʻarabī) was a series of anti-government protests, uprisings and armed rebellions that spread across much of the Arab world in the early 2010s. It began in Tunisia in December 2010 in response to corruption and economic stagnation, when street vendor Mohamed Bouazizi set himself on fire after being humiliated by officials. The Tunisian revolution forced President Zine El Abidine Ben Ali to flee in January 2011, and protests spread rapidly to Egypt, Libya, Yemen, Syria, Bahrain and beyond.

Course of events

In Egypt, mass protests centred on Cairo's Tahrir Square forced the resignation of President Hosni Mubarak on 11 February 2011 after thirty years in power. In Libya, protests escalated into civil war; a NATO-led intervention authorised by the UN Security Council helped rebels overthrow Muammar Gaddafi, who was killed in October 2011. In Yemen, President Ali Abdullah Saleh agreed to transfer power. In Syria, the initially peaceful protests against Bashar al-Assad were met with violent repression and spiralled into a catastrophic civil war. Gulf monarchies and other states responded with varying mixtures of repression (notably Bahrain, where a Saudi-led force helped suppress the uprising) and economic concessions.

Causes and methods

Analysts cite a common background of authoritarian rule, corruption, youth unemployment, rising living costs and decades of political frustration. Social media and satellite television played prominent roles in organising and publicising protests, and the movements were notably driven in their early phases by young, loosely organised demonstrators demanding dignity, jobs and political representation.

Aftermath

Outcomes diverged sharply. Tunisia achieved a fragile democratic transition with a new constitution in 2014, though it later experienced democratic backsliding. Egypt's elected Muslim Brotherhood government under Mohamed Morsi was overthrown by the military in 2013, restoring authoritarian rule under Abdel Fattah el-Sisi. Libya, Syria and Yemen descended into prolonged civil wars with massive casualties and refugee flows, and the chaos in Syria and Iraq enabled the rise of the Islamic State (ISIS) in 2014. The failure of most uprisings to deliver stable democracy led commentators to speak of an "Arab Winter." A second wave of protests in 2019 toppled long-time rulers in Sudan and Algeria.
