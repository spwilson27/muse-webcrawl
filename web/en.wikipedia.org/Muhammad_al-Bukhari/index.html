Muhammad ibn Ismail al-Bukhari (810–870 CE), commonly known as Imam al-Bukhari, was a Persian Islamic scholar from Bukhara who compiled the hadith collection known as Sahih al-Bukhari, regarded by Sunni Muslims as the most authentic (sahih) of all hadith compilations. His work systematised the science of hadith criticism and remains foundational to Sunni Islam.

Life

Al-Bukhari was born in Bukhara, in present-day Uzbekistan, in 810 CE. His father, Ismail, was himself a scholar of hadith who had studied with Malik ibn Anas. Orphaned young, al-Bukhari began studying hadith in childhood and is said to have memorised tens of thousands of traditions. At sixteen he performed the Hajj with his mother and brother and remained in Mecca and Medina to study, beginning travels that took him across the Abbasid world — to Baghdad, Basra, Kufa, Damascus, Egypt, and Nishapur — collecting traditions from more than a thousand teachers.

Sahih al-Bukhari

Over some sixteen years al-Bukhari sifted a vast body of reported traditions, applying stringent criteria to the chain of transmission (isnad) of each hadith: narrators had to be upright, possess accurate memory, and have demonstrably met those they transmitted from. The resulting collection contains roughly 7,500 narrations (about 2,600 without repetition), arranged by legal and theological topic. It is the first book in the canonical Six Books (Kutub al-Sittah) of Sunni hadith.

Other works and legacy

Al-Bukhari also wrote Al-Adab al-Mufrad, a collection on manners and ethics, and Al-Tarikh al-Kabir, a biographical dictionary of hadith transmitters. Late in life he settled near Samarkand after disputes in Bukhara, and died in the village of Khartank in 870 CE. His mausoleum near Samarkand became a major pilgrimage site, and his name remains synonymous with rigour in hadith authentication.
