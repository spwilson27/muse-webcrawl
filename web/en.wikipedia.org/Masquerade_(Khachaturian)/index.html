Masquerade (Khachaturian)

Masquerade is an orchestral suite by Aram Khachaturian, drawn from the incidental music he composed in 1941 for a production of Mikhail Lermontov's play of the same name at the Vakhtangov Theatre in Moscow.

The suite

In 1944 Khachaturian extracted five movements into a suite: Waltz, Nocturne, Mazurka, Romance and Galop. The opening Waltz is the most famous part of the work and one of the composer's best-known pieces, frequently used in film and concert programmes. Its sweeping, melancholy theme was reportedly composed after Khachaturian struggled to find a melody that suited the play's tragic heroine, Nina.

The play and music

Lermontov's drama Masquerade (1835) tells of Yevgeny Arbenin, who, deceived by appearances at a masked ball, poisons his innocent wife. Khachaturian's score was written for the last pre-war production at the Vakhtangov Theatre, which premiered on 21 June 1941, the eve of the German invasion of the Soviet Union.
