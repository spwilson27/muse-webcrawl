United Launch Alliance

United Launch Alliance, LLC (ULA) is an American aerospace manufacturer and launch service provider formed in December 2006 as a joint venture between Lockheed Martin and Boeing. For nearly two decades it was the principal launch contractor for the United States Department of Defense and NASA, flying the Atlas V and Delta IV rockets inherited from its parent companies, and since 2024 it has operated the next-generation Vulcan Centaur.

Formation

ULA combined the Delta (Boeing) and Atlas (Lockheed Martin) launch vehicle programs after the U.S. government concluded that neither business was profitable alone and that merging them would preserve assured access to space for national security payloads. The venture consolidated production at Decatur, Alabama, and headquarters in Colorado. For many years it held an effective monopoly on U.S. military launches, supported by an annual capability subsidy, until SpaceX was certified for national security missions in 2015 and began winning competed contracts.

Vehicles and missions

The Atlas V, powered on its first stage by the Russian-built RD-180 engine, carried payloads including NASA's Perseverance rover, the OSIRIS-REx asteroid mission, and Boeing's Starliner crew capsule. The Delta IV, including the three-core Delta IV Heavy, launched large reconnaissance satellites and NASA's Parker Solar Probe; it retired in April 2024. ULA's Vulcan Centaur, powered by Blue Origin BE-4 engines, first flew in January 2024 and became the company's sole vehicle for future national security and commercial missions.

Recent developments

ULA has sought to compete with SpaceX on price through Vulcan and partial reusability concepts such as engine recovery (SMART reuse). In 2023–2024, reports indicated the parent companies explored selling the joint venture. ULA won a major share of the U.S. Space Force's National Security Space Launch Phase 3 contracts alongside SpaceX and, later, Blue Origin.
