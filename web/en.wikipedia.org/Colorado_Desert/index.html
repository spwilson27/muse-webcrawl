Colorado Desert

The Colorado Desert is a part of the larger Sonoran Desert located in Southern California and northern Baja California. Despite its name, it lies mostly in California and is not related to the state of Colorado; it takes its name from the Colorado River, which forms its eastern boundary. The desert covers roughly 7 million acres (about 28,000 km²) and includes the Coachella and Imperial valleys.

Geography and climate

The region is bounded by the San Bernardino Mountains and the Transverse Ranges to the northwest, the Peninsular Ranges to the west, and the Colorado River to the east. It contains the Salton Sea, a large saline lake lying about 236 feet (72 m) below sea level, the lowest area of the desert. The climate is hot and arid, with summer temperatures frequently exceeding 110°F (43°C) and less than 5 inches (130 mm) of rain annually, most of it falling in winter.

Ecology and land use

Creosote bush, ocotillo, cholla cactus and desert fan palm oases characterise the flora. The desert includes much of Joshua Tree National Park's lower elevations and the Anza-Borrego Desert State Park. Extensive irrigated agriculture in the Imperial Valley, supplied by Colorado River water via the All-American Canal, contrasts with the surrounding barren landscape.
