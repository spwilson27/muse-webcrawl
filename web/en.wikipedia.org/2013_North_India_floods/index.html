The 2013 North India floods were a natural disaster in June 2013, when exceptionally heavy monsoon rainfall — far above normal for the season — caused devastating floods and landslides across the Himalayan state of Uttarakhand and parts of neighbouring states and Nepal.

Disaster

Between 14 and 17 June 2013, cloudbursts and torrential rain swelled rivers including the Mandakini, Alaknanda and Bhagirathi. A glacial lake above the pilgrim town of Kedarnath breached, sending a wall of water, mud and boulders through the town and destroying much of it. Thousands of Hindu pilgrims on the Char Dham yatra were stranded in mountain valleys as roads and bridges were swept away.

Rescue and toll

The Indian Armed Forces mounted one of the largest rescue operations in their history, airlifting and evacuating well over 100,000 people. The official death toll, including those missing and presumed dead, was around 5,700. Debate followed over the role of unregulated construction, tourism development and hydroelectric projects in fragile mountain terrain in worsening the disaster.
