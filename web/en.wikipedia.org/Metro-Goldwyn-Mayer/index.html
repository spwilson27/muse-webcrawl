Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer

Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer Studios Inc. (MGM) is an American media company involved in the production and distribution of film and television programs. Founded on April 17, 1924, from the merger of Metro Pictures, Goldwyn Pictures and Louis B. Mayer Pictures, it was for decades the most powerful studio of Hollywood's Golden Age, identified by its roaring lion mascot, Leo, and the motto Ars Gratia Artis ("Art for Art's Sake").

Golden Age

Under production chief Irving Thalberg and studio head Louis B. Mayer, MGM boasted "more stars than there are in heaven" — including Greta Garbo, Clark Gable, Joan Crawford, Spencer Tracy, Katharine Hepburn and Judy Garland — and released such landmarks as Grand Hotel (1932), The Wizard of Oz and Gone with the Wind (both 1939), and later An American in Paris (1951), Singin' in the Rain (1952) and Ben-Hur (1959), which won a record eleven Academy Awards.

Decline and changing ownership

From the 1950s, antitrust divestiture of its theatre chain, television competition and the end of the studio system eroded MGM's power. The studio passed through a succession of owners, including Kirk Kerkorian (who built the MGM Grand casino in Las Vegas), Ted Turner (whose 1986 purchase left MGM's pre-1986 library with Turner/Warner Bros.), and various consortiums. MGM survived a pre-packaged bankruptcy in 2010 and continued as a production company, home of the James Bond and Rocky/Creed franchises.

Amazon era

In 2022 Amazon completed its acquisition of MGM for $8.45 billion, folding the studio and its library of some 4,000 films and 17,000 television episodes into what became Amazon MGM Studios.
