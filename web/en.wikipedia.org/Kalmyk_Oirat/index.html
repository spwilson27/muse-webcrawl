Kalmyk Oirat

Kalmyk Oirat (Kalmyk: Хальмг өөрд, Xaľmg öörd) is a Mongolic language variety spoken by the Kalmyk people of the Republic of Kalmykia in the European part of Russia, on the northwestern shore of the Caspian Sea. It is generally regarded either as a dialect of the Oirat language or as a language in its own right; Ethnologue treats Kalmyk Oirat as a distinct language.

History

The Kalmyks descend from Oirat Mongols who migrated westward from Dzungaria to the lower Volga steppe in the early 17th century. Their speech diverged from the Oirat varieties of Mongolia and Xinjiang under the influence of Russian and Turkic languages. In 1648 the lama Zaya Pandita devised the "clear script" (todo bichig), an adaptation of the classical Mongolian script designed to represent Oirat sounds precisely, and it remained the Kalmyk literary script for centuries.

Status

In the Soviet period the language was written first in a Latin-based alphabet (from 1930) and then in Cyrillic, which is still official in Kalmykia today. The Kalmyks were deported to Siberia in 1943 and only allowed to return in 1957, a trauma that accelerated language loss. Kalmyk Oirat is now classified as endangered: Russian dominates education and public life, and fluent speakers are concentrated among older generations, though revival efforts include teaching in schools and media broadcasting.
