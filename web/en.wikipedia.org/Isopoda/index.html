Isopoda

Woodlice are the best-known terrestrial isopods.

Isopoda is an order of crustaceans that includes woodlice and their relatives. Typically they are flattened from top to bottom, with two pairs of antennae, seven pairs of jointed limbs on the thorax, and five pairs of branching appendages on the abdomen used in respiration. More than 10,000 species are known.

Diversity and habitat

Isopods live in the sea, in fresh water and on land. Marine forms range from tiny parasites of fish and other crustaceans to the giant isopods (Bathynomus) of the deep ocean floor, which can exceed 30 cm in length. Freshwater species include the water slaters (Asellus). On land, the woodlice (suborder Oniscidea) are among the very few crustacean groups fully adapted to terrestrial life, feeding mainly on decaying plant material.

Biology

Females brood their eggs in a pouch (marsupium) beneath the thorax. Many parasitic isopods have complex life cycles and dramatically modified bodies; the tongue-biting louse Cymothoa exigua famously replaces the tongue of its fish host. Isopods first appear in the fossil record in the Carboniferous period, over 300 million years ago.
