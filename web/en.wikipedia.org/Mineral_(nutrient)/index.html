Mineral (nutrient)

In the context of nutrition, a mineral is a chemical element required as an essential nutrient by organisms to perform functions necessary for life. Minerals originate in the Earth and cannot be made by living organisms; plants obtain them from soil and animals from plants or other food sources.

Essential minerals

The macrominerals, needed in larger amounts, are calcium, phosphorus, potassium, sodium, chloride, magnesium, and sulfur. Trace minerals required in small amounts include iron, zinc, copper, manganese, iodine, selenium, cobalt, and molybdenum. Five elements — carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen, and sulfur — are mainly obtained from organic food rather than counted among dietary minerals.

Functions

Minerals serve structural roles (calcium and phosphorus in bones and teeth), act as electrolytes (sodium, potassium, chloride), and function as cofactors for enzymes (zinc, copper, selenium). Deficiencies can cause conditions such as iron-deficiency anaemia, goitre from lack of iodine, and rickets partly from calcium imbalance, while excess intake of some minerals is also toxic.

Dietary sources

Minerals are obtained from a varied diet: dairy products for calcium, red meat and legumes for iron, seafood for iodine, and nuts, seeds and whole grains for magnesium and zinc. They are also sold as dietary supplements.
