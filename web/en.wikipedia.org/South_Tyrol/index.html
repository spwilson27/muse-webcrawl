South Tyrol

South Tyrol (Alto Adige / Südtirol)
Country: Italy
Capital: Bolzano (Bozen)
Area: 7,400 km²
Population: about 530,000
Languages: German (majority), Italian, Ladin

South Tyrol (German: Südtirol; Italian: Alto Adige) is an autonomous province in northern Italy, the northernmost province of the country, bordering Austria and Switzerland. Together with Trentino it forms the autonomous region of Trentino-Alto Adige/Südtirol. Its capital is Bolzano. A majority of the population — about 62 percent — speaks German, about 23 percent Italian, and about 4 percent Ladin, a Rhaeto-Romance language preserved in the Val Gardena and Val Badia.

History

Part of the Austrian County of Tyrol for centuries, South Tyrol was annexed by Italy after the First World War under the 1919 Treaty of Saint-Germain, despite its overwhelmingly German-speaking population. Fascist Italy pursued forced Italianisation; the 1939 "Option Agreement" between Mussolini and Hitler forced German speakers to choose between emigrating to the Reich or assimilation. After 1945 the Gruber–De Gasperi Agreement (1946) promised autonomy, which was only meaningfully delivered by the Second Autonomy Statute of 1972 after years of tension and a bombing campaign by South Tyrolean activists. Austria formally declared the dispute closed at the United Nations in 1992.

Autonomy and economy

The province retains roughly 90 percent of taxes raised locally and administers schools, health, transport and culture separately for each language group, with public jobs allocated by ethnic proportion. South Tyrol is one of Italy's wealthiest provinces, with an economy based on tourism (the Dolomites, a UNESCO World Heritage Site), high-value agriculture (apples and wine), and small manufacturing. Ötzi the Iceman, the famous glacier mummy, is displayed in Bolzano's South Tyrol Museum of Archaeology.
