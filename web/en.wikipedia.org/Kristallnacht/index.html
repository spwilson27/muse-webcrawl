Kristallnacht (German for "Crystal Night"), also known as the Night of Broken Glass, was a pogrom against Jews carried out by Nazi paramilitary forces and civilians throughout Nazi Germany on 9–10 November 1938. The name refers to the shattered glass from the windows of Jewish-owned shops, synagogues and homes that littered the streets.

Background and pretext

Anti-Jewish persecution had escalated since 1933 through boycotts, the Nuremberg Laws (1935) and forced emigration. The immediate pretext was the assassination of German diplomat Ernst vom Rath in Paris by Herschel Grynszpan, a 17-year-old Polish Jew whose family had been expelled from Germany. Nazi propaganda minister Joseph Goebbels used the killing to incite nationwide violence, presented as spontaneous public anger.

The pogrom

During the night, SA and SS units and party activists burned or damaged more than 1,000 synagogues, vandalized and looted some 7,500 Jewish businesses, and desecrated cemeteries and schools. At least 91 Jews were murdered, and about 30,000 Jewish men were arrested and sent to Dachau, Buchenwald and Sachsenhausen concentration camps, where hundreds died. The Jewish community was then fined one billion Reichsmarks as "atonement" for the damage.

Significance

Kristallnacht marked a decisive turning point: the shift from legal discrimination and economic exclusion to open, organized violence, foreshadowing the Holocaust. It accelerated Jewish emigration — including the Kindertransport of children to Britain — and drew widespread international condemnation, though few countries substantially loosened immigration quotas.
