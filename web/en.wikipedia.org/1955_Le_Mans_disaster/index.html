1955 Le Mans disaster

Date11 June 1955
VenueCircuit de la Sarthe, Le Mans, France
Event24 Hours of Le Mans
Deaths84 (83 spectators and driver Pierre Levegh)

The 1955 Le Mans disaster occurred during the 24 Hours of Le Mans motor race at the Circuit de la Sarthe in Le Mans, France, on 11 June 1955. A major crash involving the Mercedes-Benz 300 SLR of French driver Pierre Levegh sent large pieces of debris, including the engine block and the entire car, into a packed grandstand area, killing Levegh and 83 spectators. It remains the deadliest accident in motor racing history.

The crash

At the end of the 35th lap, Mike Hawthorn's Jaguar D-Type cut across to the pits, slowing abruptly. Lance Macklin's Austin-Healey swerved to avoid him, moving into the path of Levegh's much faster Mercedes. The Mercedes struck the rear of the Austin-Healey and was launched into the air, tumbling into the crowd. Its magnesium bodywork ignited and burned fiercely; attempts to douse the flames with water intensified the fire.

Aftermath

Organizers controversially continued the race, fearing that dispersing the crowd would block ambulances. Jaguar's Hawthorn and Ivor Bueb won. Mercedes withdrew its remaining cars in the small hours and withdrew from motor racing entirely at the end of the 1955 season, not returning to the top level for decades.

The disaster led to sweeping safety reforms at circuits worldwide. Switzerland banned circuit motor racing — a prohibition that lasted, with narrow exceptions, until 2022 — and several countries temporarily suspended racing while facilities were improved.
