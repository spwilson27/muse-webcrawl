1900 Galveston hurricane
Date: 8 September 1900
Location: Galveston, Texas
Category: 4 (estimated)
Deaths: 6,000–12,000 (often cited ~8,000)
Damage: ~$34 million (1900 USD)

The 1900 Galveston hurricane, also known as the Great Galveston Storm, made landfall on the city of Galveston, Texas, on 8 September 1900. It remains the deadliest natural disaster in United States history and one of the deadliest Atlantic hurricanes on record.

The storm

The hurricane, estimated at Category 4 strength with winds around 145 mph, drove a storm surge of up to 15 feet over Galveston Island, whose highest point was only about 9 feet above sea level. More than 3,600 buildings were destroyed. US Weather Bureau forecaster Isaac Cline became a central figure in accounts of the storm's warnings.

Aftermath

Between 6,000 and 12,000 people died. Galveston, then one of Texas's leading ports, rebuilt behind a massive seawall (begun 1902) and raised the grade of the entire city by as much as 17 feet — an extraordinary engineering project. Commerce nevertheless shifted decisively to Houston, and Galveston never regained its former prominence.
