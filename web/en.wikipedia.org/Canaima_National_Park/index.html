Canaima National Park

LocationBolívar State, Venezuela
Area~30,000 km²
Established12 June 1962
UNESCOWorld Heritage Site (1994)
Key featuresAngel Falls, tepuis, Gran Sabana

Canaima National Park (Spanish: Parque Nacional Canaima) is a vast protected area in Bolívar State, southeastern Venezuela, near the borders with Brazil and Guyana. Covering about 30,000 square kilometres — roughly the size of Belgium — it is one of the largest national parks in the world. The park protects part of the Gran Sabana and the ancient sandstone plateaus known as tepuis, and was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1994.

Geography

The park's landscape belongs to the Guiana Shield, one of the oldest geological formations on Earth. Its most famous features are the tepuis — sheer-walled, flat-topped mountains such as Roraima and Auyán-tepui, whose summits harbour unique ecosystems with many endemic species. From the cliffs of Auyán-tepui plunges Angel Falls (Salto Ángel), the world's tallest uninterrupted waterfall, with a total height of 979 metres. The western sector is centred on Canaima Lagoon, with its pink-sand beaches and falls such as Salto Hacha; the eastern sector contains the rolling grasslands of the Gran Sabana and waterfalls including Salto Kamá and Salto Kawi.

People and wildlife

The park is the homeland of the Pemón Indigenous people, whose communities, such as Kamarata and Uruyén, provide much of the park's tourism infrastructure; access is primarily by light aircraft and dugout canoe (curiara). Fauna includes giant anteaters, jaguars, ocelots, giant otters, and a rich bird life, while tepui summits support fragile assemblages of carnivorous plants and endemic frogs found nowhere else.
