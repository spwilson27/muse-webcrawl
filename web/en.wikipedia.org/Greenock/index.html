Greenock (Scots: Greenock; Scottish Gaelic: Grianaig) is a town in Inverclyde, Scotland, located on the south bank of the Firth of Clyde about 40 kilometres west of Glasgow. Historically part of Renfrewshire, it grew from a small fishing village into a major port and shipbuilding centre.

History

Greenock received burgh status in the 17th century and developed rapidly in the 18th as a port for the tobacco, sugar and West Indies trades; the world's first Custom House quay and a succession of harbours made it the principal port of the Clyde before Glasgow's rise. Sugar refining and shipbuilding became dominant industries, and the town's yards, including Scotts of Greenock, built large numbers of merchant and naval vessels. The engineer James Watt, improver of the steam engine, was born in Greenock in 1736 and is commemorated by the Watt Institution and a statue in the town.

Greenock suffered heavy bombing in the Blitz of May 1941. After the Second World War the decline of shipbuilding and sugar refining hit the town hard, though IBM established a major manufacturing plant in 1951 that operated for decades, and the Ocean Terminal container port continues to handle cargo and visiting cruise ships.

Landmarks

Prominent landmarks include the Municipal Buildings with their 75-metre Victoria Tower, the Italianate Custom House (now a museum), and Lyle Hill, whose "Free French" cross memorial overlooks the Tail of the Bank anchorage in the Clyde.

Transport

Greenock is served by the A8 and M8 corridor to Glasgow and by several railway stations on the Inverclyde Line. Ferries and shipping use the Clydeport Ocean Terminal.
