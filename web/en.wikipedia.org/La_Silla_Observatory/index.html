La Silla Observatory is an astronomical observatory in Chile with telescopes operated by the European Southern Observatory (ESO). Located on Cerro La Silla at an altitude of about 2,400 metres in the southern part of the Atacama Desert, some 160 kilometres north of La Serena, it was ESO's first observatory in Chile, inaugurated in 1969.

Telescopes

The site hosts a suite of telescopes of up to 3.6 metres in diameter. The ESO 3.6 m Telescope began operations in 1977 and is home to the HARPS spectrograph, one of the most successful planet-hunting instruments, which measures radial velocities with extremely high precision. The New Technology Telescope (NTT), commissioned in 1989, pioneered active optics, in which the shape of the primary mirror is continuously adjusted — technology later essential to very large telescopes. National telescopes, such as the Danish 1.54-metre and the MPG/ESO 2.2-metre, also operate at the site.

Discoveries

La Silla instruments have been used to discover numerous exoplanets, to study supernovae and gamma-ray bursts, and to carry out large surveys of the southern sky. The observatory enjoys around 300 clear nights per year, making it one of the premier observing sites on Earth.
