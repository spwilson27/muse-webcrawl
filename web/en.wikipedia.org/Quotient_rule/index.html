Quotient rule

Differentiating a quotient of functions

In calculus, the quotient rule is a method of finding the derivative of a function that is the ratio of two differentiable functions. If f(x) = g(x)/h(x), where both g and h are differentiable and h(x) ≠ 0, then:

f′(x) = [ g′(x)h(x) − g(x)h′(x) ] / [ h(x) ]2

Examples

The derivative of (4x − 2)/(x2 + 1) is [4(x2 + 1) − (4x − 2)(2x)] / (x2 + 1)2, which simplifies to (−4x2 + 4x + 4)/(x2 + 1)2. The quotient rule also yields the derivative of the tangent function from those of sine and cosine.

Proofs

The rule can be proved from the definition of the derivative using limits, derived from the product rule by writing g/h as g · h−1, or obtained from the chain rule and implicit differentiation. A common mnemonic is "low d-high minus high d-low, over low low".

Generalisations

The quotient rule is a special case of the differentiation rules for products of several functions and has analogues in the calculus of differential forms and in noncommutative settings.
