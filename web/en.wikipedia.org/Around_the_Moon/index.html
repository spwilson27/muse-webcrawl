Around the Moon

Author: Jules Verne

Original title: Autour de la Lune

Published: 1870

Preceded by: From the Earth to the Moon

Around the Moon (French: Autour de la Lune, 1870) is a science fiction novel by Jules Verne and the sequel to From the Earth to the Moon (1865). It continues the story of the Baltimore Gun Club's attempt to send a projectile around the Moon.

Plot summary

The projectile, carrying Impey Barbicane, Captain Nicholl and Michel Ardan, is deflected by a passing asteroid and enters orbit around the Moon rather than landing on it. The travellers observe the lunar surface, speculate about its far side and its possible atmosphere, and endure a close approach in which they glimpse the dark side illuminated by earthlight. A rocket firing shifts their course, and after orbiting the Moon they return to Earth, splashing down in the Pacific Ocean, where they are recovered by a United States Navy vessel.

Themes

The novel is notable for its detailed, if sometimes speculative, treatment of orbital mechanics, weightlessness and life support, anticipating problems that real spaceflight would later face.
