Cultural appropriation

Cultural appropriation is the adoption of an element or elements of one culture or identity by members of another culture or identity in a manner perceived as inappropriate, exploitative, or disrespectful. The concept is most often applied when members of a dominant culture take from a disadvantaged or marginalized culture, for example in fashion, music, art, sports mascots, religious symbols, and food.

Debate

Critics of appropriation argue that it can strip sacred or significant practices of their meaning, reinforce stereotypes, and allow the appropriating group to profit from the creativity of a group that faces discrimination for the same practices. Defenders of cultural borrowing respond that cultures have always developed through exchange and imitation, that influence is not a finite resource, and that distinguishing harmful appropriation from benign appreciation or cultural assimilation is difficult.

Examples discussed

Public controversies have involved Native American headdresses worn as festival fashion, non-Black performers adopting Black hairstyles and musical styles, the use of Indigenous names and imagery by sports teams, and the commercialization of yoga and other spiritual practices. Universities, festivals, and brands have sometimes changed practices in response to such criticism.

Related concepts

Cultural appropriation is contrasted with cultural appreciation, acculturation, and transculturation, and is situated within wider academic discussions of colonialism, power, and intellectual property.
