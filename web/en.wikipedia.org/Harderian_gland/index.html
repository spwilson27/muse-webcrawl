Harderian gland

The Harderian gland is an exocrine gland found in the orbit (eye socket) of most terrestrial vertebrates that possess a nictitating membrane (third eyelid), including many mammals, birds, and reptiles. It is named after the Swiss anatomist Johann Jacob Harder, who described it in deer in 1694. Humans and most higher primates lack a functional Harderian gland.

Function

The gland secretes fluids that lubricate and protect the eye and nictitating membrane. In rodents, its secretion is rich in lipids and in porphyrins, which give the tears a reddish colour under stress or illness (chromodacryorrhea, or "red tears", in rats). Proposed additional roles include pheromone production and social communication, photoprotection, thermoregulation in some species, and a contribution to the orbital immune system.

Structure and variation

Harderian glands vary greatly across taxa in size, lobular structure, and secretory product, which may be mucous, serous, or lipid-rich. In laboratory rodents they are among the largest orbital structures and are frequently studied in toxicology and ophthalmology. Birds possess a well-developed Harderian gland important in local immunity of the eye and upper respiratory tract.
