Sine and cosine

In mathematics, sine and cosine are trigonometric functions of an angle. The sine and cosine of an acute angle are defined in the context of a right triangle: for the specified angle, its sine is the ratio of the length of the side opposite that angle to the length of the hypotenuse, and its cosine is the ratio of the length of the adjacent leg to the hypotenuse. For an angle θ they are denoted sin θ and cos θ.

Unit circle definition

More generally, sine and cosine can be defined for any real value in terms of the coordinates of a point on the unit circle: for an angle θ measured from the positive x-axis, cos θ is the x-coordinate and sin θ the y-coordinate of the corresponding point on the circle. This extends the functions to all real numbers, where they are periodic with period 2π. They satisfy the fundamental identity sin²θ + cos²θ = 1.

Properties and calculus

Sine is an odd function and cosine an even function. Their derivatives are d/dx sin x = cos x and d/dx cos x = −sin x, and each is the sum of an everywhere-convergent power series: sin x = x − x³/3! + x⁵/5! − …, cos x = 1 − x²/2! + x⁴/4! − …. By Euler's formula, eiθ = cos θ + i sin θ, tying the functions to complex exponentials. The graphs of both are sinusoids, cosine being sine shifted by π/2.

History

The functions developed from the study of chords in Greek astronomy and half-chords in Indian mathematics; the word "sine" descends, through a chain of translations and a famous misreading, from the Sanskrit jyā (chord) via Arabic jība to Latin sinus (bay or fold). Indian and Islamic astronomers computed the first sine tables, and the functions became central to European mathematics through works of astronomy and navigation.

Applications

Sine and cosine model periodic phenomena throughout science and engineering — waves, oscillations, alternating current, sound and light — and are fundamental to Fourier analysis, which decomposes signals into sinusoidal components.
