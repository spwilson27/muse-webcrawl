Paris–Roubaix

Paris–Roubaix
Type: One-day road cycling race
Region: Northern France
First held: 1896
Nickname: The Hell of the North

Paris–Roubaix is a one-day professional road cycling race held annually in northern France, and one of the five Monuments of professional cycling. It is famous for its rough terrain, including long sectors of cobblestones (pavé), which give the race its nickname, the "Hell of the North".

History

The race was first run in 1896, created by manufacturers Théodore Vienne and Maurice Perez to publicize the new velodrome in Roubaix. It was originally promoted by the newspaper Le Vélo and its editor Paul Rousseau. The race has been run nearly every year since, except during the two World Wars.

Route

The route typically covers around 250–260 kilometres from Compiègne (or earlier from Paris) to the Roubaix velodrome. Over 50 kilometres of the course are cobbled farm roads, divided into numbered sectors of varying difficulty. The most famous sectors include the Trouée d'Arenberg in the Forest of Arenberg and the Carrefour de l'Arbre.

Winners

The record for most victories is shared by Roger De Vlaeminck and Tom Boonen, each with four wins. The winner traditionally receives a cobblestone as a trophy.
