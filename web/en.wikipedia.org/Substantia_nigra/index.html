Substantia nigra

Section of the midbrain; the substantia nigra appears as a dark band owing to neuromelanin.

The substantia nigra (Latin for "black substance") is a basal ganglia structure located in the midbrain that plays an important role in reward and movement. It is named for its dark appearance in unstained tissue, which results from high levels of neuromelanin in its dopaminergic neurons.

Structure

The substantia nigra is divided into two parts: the pars compacta, which contains densely packed dopaminergic neurons projecting mainly to the striatum via the nigrostriatal pathway, and the pars reticulata, a looser region of mostly GABAergic neurons that, together with the internal globus pallidus, forms one of the main output nuclei of the basal ganglia.

Function and clinical significance

Dopamine released from pars compacta neurons modulates the direct and indirect pathways of the basal ganglia, facilitating movement and contributing to reward-related learning. Progressive loss of these neurons is the pathological hallmark of Parkinson's disease, producing tremor, rigidity and bradykinesia; the resulting depigmentation of the substantia nigra is visible at autopsy. The neurotoxin MPP+, formed from MPTP, selectively damages these neurons and has been used to model the disease in animals.
