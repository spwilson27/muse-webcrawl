Automotive design
FieldIndustrial design / transportation design
CoversExterior styling, interior design, colour and trim
ToolsSketching, clay modelling, CAD/CAS, digital rendering

Automotive design is the process of developing the appearance — and to some extent the ergonomics — of motor vehicles, including cars, trucks and motorcycles. Most aspects fall to designers trained in industrial or transportation design working within manufacturers' studios.

Process

Design programmes begin with package and concept sketches, progress through digital modelling and full-size clay models evaluated in clinics, and conclude with production surfacing refined jointly with engineers. Designers specialise in exterior styling, interior (cabin) design, and colour, materials and finish.

History

Early cars were styled by coachbuilders; the first dedicated styling department was General Motors' Art and Color Section, established in 1927 under Harley Earl, who introduced the annual model change. Influential designers include Giorgetto Giugiaro, Marcello Gandini, Battista "Pinin" Farina and J Mays. Modern studios balance brand identity, aerodynamics, safety regulation and the packaging demands of electric powertrains.
