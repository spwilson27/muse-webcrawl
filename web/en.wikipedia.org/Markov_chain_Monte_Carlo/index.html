Markov chain Monte Carlo

Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) methods comprise a class of algorithms for sampling from a probability distribution. By constructing a Markov chain whose stationary distribution is the desired one, the state of the chain after many steps can be used as a sample, allowing integrals and expectations to be estimated for distributions too complex for direct sampling.

Algorithms

The canonical algorithm is Metropolis–Hastings (1953; generalised 1970), which accepts proposed moves with a probability correcting for asymmetries in the proposal. Gibbs sampling, a special case, cycles through conditional distributions and is widely used in Bayesian statistics. Hamiltonian Monte Carlo uses gradient information and auxiliary momentum variables to explore distributions far more efficiently in high dimensions.

Use in Bayesian statistics

MCMC revolutionised applied Bayesian inference from the 1990s onward: posterior distributions of complex models in genetics, epidemiology, econometrics and machine learning can be approximated without closed-form solutions. Software such as BUGS, JAGS and Stan automates implementation.

Convergence

Practitioners discard an initial "burn-in", thin or summarise samples, and diagnose convergence by comparing multiple chains (e.g. the Gelman–Rubin statistic) and checking effective sample sizes, since samples are correlated rather than independent.
