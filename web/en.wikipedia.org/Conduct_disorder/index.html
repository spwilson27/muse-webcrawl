Conduct disorder

Conduct disorder (CD) is a mental disorder diagnosed in childhood or adolescence that presents itself through a repetitive and persistent pattern of behaviour that includes theft, lies, physical violence, or other serious violations of the basic rights of others or major age-appropriate social norms. It is estimated to affect between roughly 1% and 10% of children and adolescents, and is more common in boys than girls.

Diagnosis

Under the DSM-5, diagnosis requires a pattern of at least three of fifteen criteria in the previous twelve months, grouped into aggression to people and animals, destruction of property, deceitfulness or theft, and serious violations of rules. A specifier "with limited prosocial emotions" identifies children showing callous-unemotional traits. Conduct disorder differs from oppositional defiant disorder in the severity of the behaviours involved.

Causes and treatment

Risk factors include genetic vulnerability, harsh or inconsistent parenting, poverty, exposure to violence, and comorbid conditions such as ADHD. Treatments with the best evidence are psychosocial, especially parent management training and multisystemic therapy; no medication is specifically approved for conduct disorder itself. Childhood-onset conduct disorder is a risk factor for antisocial personality disorder in adulthood.
