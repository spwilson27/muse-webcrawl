Kaiserstuhl

Kaiserstuhl
Location: Baden-Württemberg, Germany
Highest point: Totenkopf, 557 m
Origin: Volcanic

The Kaiserstuhl (literally "emperor's chair") is a cluster of hills of volcanic origin in the Upper Rhine Plain of southwestern Germany, in the state of Baden-Württemberg, northwest of Freiburg im Breisgau. Its highest peak is the Totenkopf at 557 metres. The name is traditionally linked to King Otto III, who is said to have held court in the area in the 10th century.

Geology and climate

The Kaiserstuhl formed from volcanic activity roughly 19 to 16 million years ago, during the formation of the Upper Rhine Graben. Its volcanic rocks are partly mantled by thick deposits of loess. Thanks to its position in one of Germany's warmest and sunniest regions, with a climate sometimes described as almost Mediterranean, the hills support distinctive flora and fauna, including orchids, green lizards, and bee-eaters.

Viticulture

The Kaiserstuhl is best known as a wine-growing district of the Baden wine region. Terraced vineyards on loess and volcanic soils, covering much of the hills' surface, produce noted Pinot Noir (Spätburgunder) and white wines. Villages such as Ihringen, Oberrotweil, and Burkheim are centres of the local wine trade, and large-scale land consolidation in the 20th century reshaped many slopes into broad terraces.
