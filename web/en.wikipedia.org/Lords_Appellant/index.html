Lords Appellant

The Lords Appellant were a group of English nobles who opposed King Richard II in the late 1380s. In 1387–88 they accused several of the king's favourites of treason in the Merciless Parliament, securing the execution or exile of figures around the court and temporarily curbing Richard's authority.

The leading Appellants were Thomas of Woodstock, Duke of Gloucester; Richard Fitzalan, Earl of Arundel; Thomas Beauchamp, Earl of Warwick; Henry Bolingbroke, Earl of Derby (later Henry IV); and Thomas Mowbray, Earl of Nottingham. Their ascendancy was short-lived: Richard regained control by 1389 and took revenge a decade later — Gloucester died in custody in 1397, Arundel was executed, and Warwick was exiled.

The episode is a major episode in the political crisis of Richard II's reign, foreshadowing his deposition in 1399.
