Electronic control unit

Electronic control unit
AbbreviationECU
TypeEmbedded control system
ApplicationsVehicles, aircraft, industrial machinery

An electronic control unit (ECU) is an embedded system that controls one or more electrical systems or subsystems in a machine. The term is most familiar in the automotive industry, where a modern vehicle may contain dozens of ECUs networked together.

Automotive ECUs

Common automotive ECUs include the engine control unit (or module), which manages fuel injection, ignition timing and emissions; the transmission control unit; the brake control module for anti-lock braking and stability control; airbag and body control modules; and infotainment systems. ECUs communicate over networks such as the CAN bus, LIN and automotive Ethernet.

Design

ECUs are built around microcontrollers with software stored in flash memory, and they must withstand harsh temperature, vibration and electrical conditions. Development follows functional-safety standards such as ISO 26262, and diagnostics follow the OBD-II standard for emissions-related faults.

Other applications

Aircraft use full-authority digital engine controls (FADEC), and industrial, agricultural and medical equipment all rely on ECUs for automation and safety functions.
