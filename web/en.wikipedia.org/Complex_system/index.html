Complex system

A complex system is a system composed of many components which may interact with each other. Examples include the global climate, the human brain, ecosystems, economies, living cells, and the World Wide Web. Because of the nonlinear interactions between components, complex systems typically exhibit emergent properties — collective behaviors that cannot be easily inferred from the properties of the individual parts.

Characteristics

Complex systems commonly feature many interacting elements, feedback loops, adaptation, self-organization, nonlinearity, and sensitivity to initial conditions. Their behavior often cannot be captured by simple reductionist models, so researchers employ simulation, network theory, agent-based modeling, and statistical mechanics. Related concepts include chaos theory, fractals, power laws, and criticality.

Study of complex systems

The modern study of complexity drew on cybernetics, systems theory, and nonlinear dynamics, and was institutionalized with the founding of the Santa Fe Institute in 1984. Contributors include Ilya Prigogine (dissipative structures), Murray Gell-Mann, and John Holland (complex adaptive systems). Nobel Prizes have recognized work in the area, including the 2021 Physics prize for Giorgio Parisi's work on complex physical systems.

Applications

Complexity science informs fields as diverse as epidemiology, finance, ecology, neuroscience, urban planning, and climate science. Understanding resilience, tipping points, and contagion in networks is a practical goal: for example, modelling how failures cascade in power grids or how diseases spread through contact networks.
