Do Not Track

Do Not Track (DNT) is a formerly proposed HTTP header field designed to allow internet users to opt out of tracking by websites, including analytics services, advertising networks, and social platforms. It was proposed in 2009 by consumer advocates and technology researchers.

History

The header, sent as DNT: 1, was first implemented in Mozilla Firefox in 2011, followed by other browsers including Internet Explorer, Safari, and Chrome. Compliance was voluntary, and most advertising networks and websites ignored the signal, undermining its effectiveness as a privacy tool.

Deprecation

The World Wide Web Consortium's Tracking Protection Working Group closed in 2019 without finalizing a compliance standard. Apple removed DNT support from Safari in 2019, citing its potential use in browser fingerprinting, and other browsers followed. The Global Privacy Control signal emerged later as a successor with legal backing in some jurisdictions.
