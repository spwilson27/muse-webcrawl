Bohol

Bohol, officially the Province of Bohol, is an island province of the Philippines located in the Central Visayas region, consisting of the island itself and 75 minor surrounding islands. Its capital and largest city is Tagbilaran. With a land area of about 4,821 square kilometres and a population of roughly 1.39 million (2020 census), it is the tenth-largest island of the Philippines. Bohol is a popular tourist destination, famous for the Chocolate Hills, the Philippine tarsier, and the beaches of Panglao Island.

Geography

The island lies southeast of Cebu across the Cebu Strait and northwest of Leyte. Its most famous landforms are the Chocolate Hills, more than 1,200 (by some counts over 1,700) uniform conical limestone hills around the towns of Carmen, Batuan, and Sagbayan; in the dry season their grass turns brown, giving them their name. The Loboc River, hanging bridges, and centuries-old coral-stone churches are other features. In 2023 Bohol was designated the Philippines' first UNESCO Global Geopark.

Wildlife

Bohol is one of the few homes of the Philippine tarsier (Carlito syrichta), one of the world's smallest primates, maintained in sanctuaries near Corella and Loboc. The island's forests and marine sanctuaries host dolphins, whale sharks, and diverse coral reefs; Pamilacan Island is known for dolphin- and whale-watching tours.

History

In 1565 the Spanish explorer Miguel López de Legazpi and the Boholano chieftain Datu Sikatuna concluded a blood compact (sandugo) on the island, commemorated by a shrine in Tagbilaran and an annual festival. Bohol was the site of two notable anti-colonial uprisings: the Tamblot revolt (1621–22) and the Dagohoy rebellion, led by Francisco Dagohoy from 1744 to 1829—the longest revolt in Philippine history. Bohol became a separate province in 1854. On 15 October 2013, a magnitude 7.2 earthquake struck the island, killing over 200 people and severely damaging many of its heritage churches, most of which have since been restored.

Tourism

Panglao Island, connected by bridges to Tagbilaran, is the tourism centre, with Alona Beach and the Bohol–Panglao International Airport, opened in 2018. Attractions include the Hinagdanan Cave, dive sites around Balicasag Island, river cruises on the Loboc, and the man-made mahogany forest in Bilar.
