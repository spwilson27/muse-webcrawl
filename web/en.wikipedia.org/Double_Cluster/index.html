Double Cluster

Double Cluster
Constellation: Perseus
Components: NGC 869 and NGC 884
Distance: ~7,500 light-years
Also known as: Caldwell 14; h and χ Persei

The Double Cluster (also designated Caldwell 14) is the common name for the naked-eye open clusters NGC 869 and NGC 884, which lie close together in the constellation Perseus. Known since antiquity — Hipparchus recorded them around 130 BCE — they appear as a hazy patch in the Milky Way under dark skies and resolve into hundreds of stars through binoculars or a small telescope.

Properties

Both clusters lie about 7,500 light-years away and are relatively young, roughly 12–13 million years old. Each contains hundreds of hot blue-white stars along with some red supergiants; their combined mass is estimated at several thousand solar masses. The clusters are approaching Earth at modest speeds (blueshifted spectra).

Observation

The Double Cluster is a favorite target for amateur astronomers in the northern autumn sky, located between Perseus and Cassiopeia.
