Committee of Imperial Defence

The Committee of Imperial Defence was an important part of the Government of the United Kingdom and the British Empire from the beginning of the 20th century until 1939. It was responsible for coordinating the defence of the Empire across the armed services and government departments and for researching military strategy in peacetime.

History

The committee was established in 1902 by Prime Minister Arthur Balfour, who chaired its first meeting in December of that year, following the lessons of the Second Boer War. It was an advisory body without executive power: the Prime Minister was its only permanent member, and ministers, service chiefs, and officials attended as summoned. Maurice Hankey, appointed assistant secretary in 1908 and secretary from 1912, built its influential permanent secretariat.

During the interwar period the committee oversaw plans for a possible war, including the strategic assumption that Singapore would be the Empire's main eastern stronghold. It ceased to meet in 1939 with the outbreak of the Second World War, when its functions passed to war cabinet machinery; its post-war successor bodies included the Chiefs of Staff Committee system and, eventually, modern defence councils.
