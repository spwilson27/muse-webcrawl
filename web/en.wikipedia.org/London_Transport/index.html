London Transport (brand)

London Transport
Founded1933 (London Passenger Transport Board)
ServicesUnderground, buses, trams
SuccessorTransport for London (2000)
SymbolThe roundel

London Transport was the public brand under which most transport services in London, England, were provided from 1933 until 2000, through a succession of public bodies beginning with the London Passenger Transport Board (LPTB). It unified the Underground, buses, trams and trolleybuses under a single authority.

Design legacy

Under its commercial manager Frank Pick, London Transport became famous for integrated design: the roundel symbol, Edward Johnston's typeface, Harry Beck's diagrammatic Tube map of 1933, and a celebrated poster programme. Its architecture and publicity set standards for corporate identity worldwide.

Successive bodies

The LPTB became the London Transport Executive under nationalisation in 1948, then the London Transport Board (1963), the London Transport Executive under the Greater London Council (1970), and London Regional Transport (1984). In 2000 its functions passed to the newly created Transport for London, though the London Transport name survives in the London Transport Museum in Covent Garden.
