Artaxerxes I (Old Persian: Artaxšaçā; Greek: Ἀρταξέρξης) was the fifth King of Kings of the Achaemenid Empire, reigning from 465 to 424 BC. He was a son of Xerxes I and succeeded his father after Xerxes was assassinated. His Greek epithet Macrocheir ("Longhand") reportedly referred to his right hand being longer than his left.

Reign

Artaxerxes faced a major revolt in Egypt led by Inaros, supported by Athens, which he suppressed by 454 BC. His relations with the Greek world dominated his reign: Athens and Persia eventually reached an accommodation often called the Peace of Callias (c. 449 BC), ending the Greco-Persian Wars.

Biblical connections

Artaxerxes is traditionally identified with the Persian king in the biblical books of Ezra and Nehemiah, who authorized Ezra's mission to Jerusalem and Nehemiah's rebuilding of the city walls.

Death and succession

Artaxerxes died in December 424 BC at Susa. His death triggered a succession crisis: his son Xerxes II was murdered after a brief reign, followed by Sogdianus, before Darius II secured the throne.
