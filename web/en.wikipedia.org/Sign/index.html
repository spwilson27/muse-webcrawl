Sign

A sign is an object, quality, event, or entity whose presence or occurrence indicates the probable presence or occurrence of something else. In the broadest sense, anything that communicates a meaning that is not the sign itself to the interpreter of the sign may be considered a sign. The study of signs and sign processes is called semiotics.

The philosopher Charles Sanders Peirce defined a sign as "something which stands to somebody for something in some respect or capacity." Peirce classified signs into three categories: icons, which signify by resemblance (such as a portrait); indices, which signify by causal or physical connection (such as smoke indicating fire); and symbols, which signify by convention (such as words).

In linguistics

The Swiss linguist Ferdinand de Saussure proposed a dyadic model of the linguistic sign composed of a signifier (the form, such as a sound pattern) and a signified (the concept), whose relationship is arbitrary and established by social convention. This model underlies much of structuralism and modern linguistics.

Signs in everyday life

Signs are ubiquitous: traffic signs regulate movement, signage directs people through buildings, logos identify brands, gestures convey messages, and symptoms serve physicians as signs of disease. Signage systems in public space are studied in fields such as wayfinding and information design.

Religious and cultural signs

In religion, signs may be understood as manifestations of the divine, portents, or miracles. Semiotics also examines ritual objects, sacred imagery, and textual signs within cultural systems.
