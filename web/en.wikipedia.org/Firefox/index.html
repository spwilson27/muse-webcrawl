Mozilla Firefox, or simply Firefox, is a free and open source web browser developed by the Mozilla Foundation and its subsidiary, the Mozilla Corporation. It uses the Gecko rendering engine to display web pages, implementing current and anticipated web standards. Firefox is available for Windows, macOS, Linux, and mobile operating systems.

History

Firefox was created in 2002 by members of the Mozilla community as a standalone browser spun off from the Mozilla Application Suite, under the codename "Phoenix", and was renamed Firebird and then Firefox before its 1.0 release on 9 November 2004. It grew rapidly as an alternative to Microsoft's Internet Explorer, reaching a peak usage share of around 30 percent in the late 2000s. Its share later declined amid competition from Google Chrome, but Firefox remains the principal browser independent of the Chromium engine and is developed with an emphasis on privacy, including features such as Enhanced Tracking Protection. In 2017 the "Quantum" release (version 57) introduced a rewritten engine with substantial performance improvements.

Features

Features include tabbed browsing, a customisable interface, a large library of extensions, spell-checking, an integrated PDF viewer, private browsing, sync across devices, and developer tools. The name "Firefox" refers to the red panda, which is also the browser's mascot; the logo shows a stylised fox-or-panda encircling a globe.
