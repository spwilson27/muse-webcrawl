Paris during the Belle Époque.

The Belle Époque (French for "Beautiful Era") is the period of French and European history, conventionally dated from the end of the Franco-Prussian War in 1871 to the outbreak of the First World War in 1914. Named in retrospect for its contrast with the horrors of the war, it was characterised by optimism, peace, economic prosperity and flourishing arts and sciences.

Culture and innovation

Paris hosted the great Expositions Universelles of 1889 (for which the Eiffel Tower was built) and 1900. Impressionism gave way to Post-Impressionism, Art Nouveau and the early avant-gardes; cinema, the automobile, electricity and the telephone transformed daily life. Cafés, cabarets such as the Moulin Rouge, and department stores epitomised the era's urban pleasures.

Society

Behind the glamour lay colonial expansion, labour unrest and political tensions—including the Dreyfus affair in France—but relative peace among the great powers made the period seem, in memory, a golden age abruptly ended in 1914. Britain's parallel period is often called the Edwardian era.
