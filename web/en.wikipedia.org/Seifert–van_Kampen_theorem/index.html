Seifert–van Kampen theorem

In mathematics, the Seifert–van Kampen theorem of algebraic topology (named after Herbert Seifert and Egbert van Kampen) states that the fundamental group of a topological space can be computed from the fundamental groups of two overlapping open subsets, together with the fundamental group of their intersection.

Statement

If a space X is the union of path-connected open sets A and B whose intersection is also path-connected, then the fundamental group of X is the pushout (free product with amalgamation) of the fundamental groups of A and B over that of the intersection. Intuitively, loops in X are assembled from loops in the two pieces, with loops in the overlap identified in both.

Applications

The theorem is the standard tool for computing fundamental groups of spaces built from simpler pieces. It yields, for example, the fundamental group of a wedge sum (a free product), of the torus (the abelian group Z × Z), and of surfaces and knot complements.
