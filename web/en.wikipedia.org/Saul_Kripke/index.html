Saul Aaron Kripke (1940–2022) was an American philosopher and logician, one of the most influential figures in analytic philosophy of the 20th century. A child prodigy, he published completeness results for modal logic while still a teenager.

Saul Kripke, philosopher and logician

Work

Kripke's semantics for modal logic — possible-worlds semantics using accessibility relations between worlds — made quantified modal logic rigorous and launched the modern field. His 1972 lectures published as Naming and Necessity argued that proper names are rigid designators, referring to the same object in every possible world, and revived essentialism and the notion of a posteriori necessities, using examples such as "water is H2O".

He also contributed a theory of truth intended to handle the liar paradox, a famous interpretation of Wittgenstein in Wittgenstein on Rules and Private Language (1982) — the "Kripkenstein" paradox about rule-following — and work on recursion theory. He taught at Princeton University and later at the CUNY Graduate Center.
