Cloud gaming

Cloud gaming, sometimes called gaming on demand or game streaming, is a type of online gaming in which video games are executed on remote servers and the resulting video is streamed directly to the player's device over the internet. The player's controller inputs are sent upstream to the server, which renders each frame and returns it as a compressed video stream, allowing games to run on hardware — such as phones, tablets, smart televisions, or low-power laptops — that could not run them locally.

History

Early demonstrations of the technology appeared in the 2000s, and the startup OnLive launched one of the first consumer services in 2010, followed by Gaikai, which was acquired by Sony and became the basis of PlayStation Now. As broadband speeds and video compression improved, major platform holders entered the market: Nvidia's GeForce Now, Microsoft's Xbox Cloud Gaming, and Amazon Luna. Google's Stadia, launched in 2019, shut down in January 2023, illustrating the commercial risks of the model. Cloud gaming has also become a feature bundled with subscription services and sold through smart-TV applications.

Technology

Performance depends on end-to-end latency: the time for input to reach the server, for the game to render a frame, and for the encoded video — typically using codecs such as H.264, H.265, or AV1 — to return to the client. Providers mitigate delay with geographically distributed edge data centres, predictive input techniques, and adaptive resolution and frame rates that respond to changing network conditions. Bandwidth requirements are typically in the range of 10–45 Mbit/s for 720p to 4K streaming.

Advantages and disadvantages

Cloud gaming removes the cost of high-end hardware, eliminates installation and patching, and enables instant trials across devices. Against this, image quality can suffer under congestion, latency makes fast competitive games harder to play, ownership and preservation are dependent on the provider's catalogue, and caps on data usage may limit play. Publishers have also raised questions about revenue sharing and platform control.
