Scat singing

Scat singing is vocal improvisation with wordless vocables, nonsense syllables, or without words at all — in effect, using the voice as a musical instrument. It is a hallmark technique of jazz singing.

History

Scat has roots in West African musical traditions, ragtime, and New Orleans jazz. Louis Armstrong's 1926 recording of "Heebie Jeebies" — legend says he dropped the lyric sheet and improvised syllables — popularised the style. Earlier recordings by performers such as Don Redman and Cliff Edwards also featured scat passages.

Notable practitioners

Ella Fitzgerald is widely regarded as the pre-eminent scat singer, turning extended improvised choruses into a virtuoso art. Other celebrated exponents include Cab Calloway, whose "hi-de-ho" call-and-response became a signature, Mel Tormé, Anita O'Day, Sarah Vaughan, Betty Carter, Bobby McFerrin, and Al Jarreau. In bebop, scat syllables often mimicked the phrasing of horn solos.

Technique

Skilled scat singers select syllables for their percussive attack and smooth articulation, improvising melodies with the harmonic and rhythmic sophistication of instrumentalists. Though often playful, scat is considered one of the most demanding forms of vocal jazz performance.
