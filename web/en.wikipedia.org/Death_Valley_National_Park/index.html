Death Valley National Park is an American national park that straddles the California–Nevada border, east of the Sierra Nevada. It includes Death Valley, the northern section of Panamint Valley, and parts of several mountain ranges. It is the largest national park in the contiguous United States and the hottest, driest, and lowest of all the national parks in the United States.

Climate and records

Death Valley is famous for extreme heat. On 10 July 1913, a temperature of 134 °F (56.7 °C) was recorded at Furnace Creek, the highest reliably measured air temperature on Earth. The valley is a rain-shadow desert: Pacific moisture is wrung out by successive mountain ranges before reaching it, and average annual rainfall is only about 2.2 inches (56 mm). Badwater Basin, at 282 feet below sea level, is the lowest point in North America.

Geology and landscape

The valley is a classic example of Basin and Range topography, a downdropped block (graben) between the uplifted Panamint Range to the west and the Amargosa Range to the east. Landmarks include the salt flats of Badwater, the multicoloured sediments of Artists Palette, the sliding stones of Racetrack Playa, the sand dunes near Stovepipe Wells, and Zabriskie Point. Telescope Peak rises more than 11,000 feet almost directly above the valley floor.

History

The Timbisha Shoshone have lived in the valley for at least a millennium. The name "Death Valley" dates to the California Gold Rush, when a party of emigrants was trapped there in the winter of 1849–50; legend records a departing traveller saying "Goodbye, Death Valley." Borax mining followed in the 1880s, with twenty-mule teams hauling borax to market. President Herbert Hoover proclaimed Death Valley a national monument in 1933, and the California Desert Protection Act of 1994 redesignated it a national park and greatly expanded its area.

Ecology

Despite its aridity, the park supports more than 1,000 species of plants and animals including bighorn sheep, coyotes, and the Death Valley pupfish, which survives in isolated springs. After wet winters, wildflowers carpet the valley floor in brief "superblooms."
