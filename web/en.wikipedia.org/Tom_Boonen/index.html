Tom Boonen

Tom Boonen (born 15 October 1980) is a Belgian former professional road racing cyclist, widely regarded as one of the greatest classics specialists and sprinters of his generation. Nicknamed "Tommeke" and the "King of the Cobbles", he won the UCI World Road Race Championships in 2005, the Tour of Flanders three times and Paris–Roubaix four times — equalling Roger De Vlaeminck's record in the "Hell of the North" — as well as six stages and the points classification of the Tour de France.

Career

Boonen, from Mol in the Flemish Kempen region, turned professional with US Postal in 2002 and immediately finished third in Paris–Roubaix. Moving to Quick-Step under Patrick Lefevere, he became the successor to Johan Museeuw as leader of Belgium's dominant classics team. In 2005 he achieved the rare Flanders–Roubaix double and won the world championship road race in Madrid. His rivalry with Swiss rider Fabian Cancellara defined the cobbled classics of the era, and in 2012 he again won both monuments in the same spring, along with E3 Harelbeke and Gent–Wevelgem. He also won the Tour of Qatar seven times, three Belgian national titles and stages in most major stage races.

Setbacks and retirement

Boonen's career was interrupted by injuries — notably a fractured skull's after-effects, knee problems and a heavy crash at the 2015 Tour of Abu Dhabi — and by controversy: he tested positive for cocaine in out-of-competition tests in 2008 and 2009 and was briefly suspended and excluded from the 2009 Tour de France. A national idol in Flanders, he retired after Paris–Roubaix in April 2017, finishing 13th in his final race after an attacking farewell ride.

Legacy

With more than 120 professional victories, Boonen stands among the most successful one-day racers in history, frequently compared with Eddy Merckx and Museeuw in Belgian cycling culture. After racing he pursued motorsport as an amateur racing driver.
