George Brinton McClellan (December 3, 1826 – October 29, 1885) was an American military officer, engineer, and politician who served as a major general during the American Civil War. A graduate of the United States Military Academy at West Point, he organized the Army of the Potomac in 1861 and served as general-in-chief of the Union Army for several months.

Early life and career

McClellan was born in Philadelphia, the son of a prominent surgeon. He entered West Point at age 15 and graduated second in the class of 1846. He served with distinction in the Mexican–American War and afterwards worked as a railroad engineer and executive, becoming president of the Ohio and Mississippi Railroad.

Civil War

At the outbreak of the Civil War, McClellan was appointed to command Ohio volunteers and won a series of small victories in western Virginia. After the Union defeat at the First Battle of Bull Run, President Abraham Lincoln placed him in command of the Army of the Potomac. McClellan proved a superb organizer and trainer, but his caution in battle frustrated Lincoln. His Peninsula Campaign of 1862 failed to capture Richmond, and after the Battle of Antietam in September 1862 he was relieved of command for failing to pursue Robert E. Lee's army.

Later life

McClellan was the Democratic nominee for president in 1864, losing to Lincoln. He later served as Governor of New Jersey from 1878 to 1881. He died in 1885 and was buried in Riverview Cemetery in Trenton.

Legacy

Historians remain divided over McClellan: praised for his organizational gifts and the devotion of his men, he is criticized for excessive caution and for overestimating enemy strength. His memoirs, McClellan's Own Story, were published posthumously in 1887.
