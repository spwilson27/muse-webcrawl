Mount Vesuvius

Mount Vesuvius (Italian: Monte Vesuvio) is a somma–stratovolcano located on the Gulf of Naples in Campania, Italy, about 9 km (5.6 mi) east of Naples and a short distance from the shore. It is one of several volcanoes forming the Campanian volcanic arc. Vesuvius consists of a large cone partially encircled by the steep rim of a summit caldera, the Monte Somma, caused by the collapse of an earlier and originally much higher structure.

Eruption of 79 AD

Vesuvius is best known for its eruption in 79 AD, which buried the Roman cities of Pompeii, Herculaneum, Oplontis and Stabiae under metres of volcanic ash and pumice. The eruption, described by Pliny the Younger, gave its name to the "Plinian" style of eruption.

Later eruptions and monitoring

Vesuvius has erupted many times since, notably in 1631, 1794, 1906 and 1944; the 1944 eruption destroyed the villages of San Sebastiano al Vesuvio, Massa di Somma and Ottaviano. It is regarded as one of the world's most dangerous volcanoes because some 3,000,000 people live in the surrounding area. The Vesuvius Observatory, founded in 1841, monitors the volcano continuously, and an evacuation plan exists for the "red zone" closest to the crater.
