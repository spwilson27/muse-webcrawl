Pompeii

Pompeii was an ancient city located in what is now the comune of Pompei near Naples in the Campania region of Italy. Pompeii, along with Herculaneum and many villas in the surrounding area (e.g. at Boscoreale, Stabiae), was buried under 4 to 6 m (13 to 20 ft) of volcanic ash and pumice in the eruption of Mount Vesuvius in 79 AD.

History

Founded by the Osci in the 7th or 6th century BC, Pompeii came under Greek, Etruscan and then Samnite influence before becoming a Roman colony in 80 BC after Sulla's siege. By the time of its destruction the city had an estimated population of 11,000 and a complex water system, amphitheatre, gymnasium and port. A severe earthquake in 62 AD caused extensive damage that was still being repaired when the eruption struck.

Destruction and rediscovery

On the traditional date of 24 August 79 AD, Vesuvius erupted, burying Pompeii under lapilli and ash and killing an estimated 2,000 of its inhabitants. The city was lost for about 1,500 years until its accidental rediscovery in 1599; systematic excavation began in 1748 under Charles III of Spain. Giuseppe Fiorelli's 19th-century excavations introduced the technique of pouring plaster into voids in the ash to recreate the forms of victims.

Today

The archaeological site is one of Italy's most popular tourist attractions, drawing around 2.5 million visitors a year, and forms part of the larger Vesuvius National Park. Ongoing conservation addresses weathering, vandalism and the pressure of tourism.
