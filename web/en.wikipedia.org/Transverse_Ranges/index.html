Transverse Ranges

The Transverse Ranges are a group of mountain ranges in Southern California that trend east–west rather than in the general northwest–southeast direction of most California ranges. The ranges extend from Point Conception in Santa Barbara County eastward to the San Bernardino Mountains and the Little San Bernardino Mountains, and include the Santa Ynez, Santa Susana, Santa Monica, San Gabriel, and San Rafael mountains, as well as the Channel Islands' northern group, which are a submerged continuation of the Santa Monica Mountains.

Geology

The Transverse Ranges result from compression along the "Big Bend" of the San Andreas Fault, where the fault's strike changes and the Pacific Plate's motion squeezes the crust. Uplift has been rapid: Mount San Gorgonio reaches 3,506 m (11,503 ft), the highest peak in Southern California. The ranges are bounded by major faults including the San Andreas, San Gabriel, and Santa Ynez faults and form the northern boundary of the Los Angeles Basin.

Climate and ecology

The mountains intercept Pacific moisture, producing chaparral on lower slopes, oak woodland, and coniferous forest at higher elevations. They form a barrier celebrated for scenery and recreation but also a zone of severe wildfire and debris-flow hazard where they face the urban foothills.
