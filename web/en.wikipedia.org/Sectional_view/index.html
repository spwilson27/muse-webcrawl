Sectional view

A sectional view (cutaway) drawn through the centre plane of an object, with hatched material where the cutting plane passes.

A sectional view, also called a section or cutaway view, is a drawing in technical drawing and engineering graphics that shows the interior of an object as if it had been sliced open along an imaginary cutting plane. Sectional views reveal hidden features that would otherwise be drawn with dashed hidden lines in an ordinary orthographic projection, and are essential for communicating the internal geometry of complex parts and assemblies.

Cutting plane

The position and direction of the cut are indicated on a parent view by a cutting-plane line, drawn as a thick chain line with arrows showing the direction of sight. Letters at the ends of the line identify the resulting section, which is labelled, for example, "Section A–A". The cutting plane may be a single flat plane, or it may be offset to pass through several features of interest, in which case the offsets are drawn at right angles and do not appear in the sectional view itself.

Hatching

Material that is actually cut by the cutting plane is shown with section lining, or hatching: thin parallel lines, normally at 45 degrees, spaced according to the size of the area. Adjacent parts in an assembly are hatched at different angles or spacings so that they can be distinguished. By convention, thin features such as ribs, webs, shafts, bolts and spokes are not section-lined when the cutting plane passes along their length, to avoid giving a false impression of thickness or solidity.

Types of section

A full section is produced when the cutting plane passes completely through the object. A half section shows one quarter of a symmetrical object removed, displaying the interior on one half and the exterior on the other. A broken-out section removes only a local region bounded by a freehand break line. A revolved section shows the cross-section of a bar or arm rotated into the plane of the drawing, while a removed section places that cross-section elsewhere on the sheet. An auxiliary section is projected onto a plane other than one of the principal planes.

Applications

Sectional views are used throughout mechanical, architectural and geological drawing. Architects use building sections to show floor heights and internal arrangements, and geologists draw cross-sections through rock strata. In computer-aided design, section views are generated automatically by slicing the solid model with a chosen plane.
