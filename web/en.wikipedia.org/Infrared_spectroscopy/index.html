Infrared spectroscopy

Typical range4000–400 cm⁻¹ (mid-infrared)

Infrared spectroscopy (IR spectroscopy, or vibrational spectroscopy) is a technique that measures the interaction of infrared radiation with matter by absorption, emission or reflection. It is used to study and identify chemical substances and functional groups: molecules absorb infrared light at frequencies matching the vibrations of their bonds, so an IR spectrum acts as a molecular "fingerprint".

Principles

A molecular vibration absorbs infrared radiation only if it produces a change in the molecule's dipole moment (the IR selection rule). Vibrations are classed as stretching (symmetric and asymmetric) and bending modes (scissoring, rocking, wagging, twisting). The mid-infrared region, conventionally 4000–400 cm⁻¹, contains the fundamental vibrations of most organic functional groups: broad O–H stretching near 3300 cm⁻¹, C–H stretching near 2900 cm⁻¹, and the sharp carbonyl (C=O) band near 1710 cm⁻¹ are textbook examples. The region below ~1500 cm⁻¹, the fingerprint region, is a complex pattern characteristic of the whole molecule.

Instrumentation

Modern instruments are almost exclusively Fourier-transform infrared (FTIR) spectrometers, which use a Michelson interferometer to record an interferogram of all frequencies simultaneously and convert it to a spectrum by Fourier transformation, gaining speed, sensitivity and wavelength accuracy over older dispersive instruments. Samples may be measured in transmission (as films, solutions, or KBr pellets), by attenuated total reflectance (ATR), where the sample contacts an internal-reflection crystal, or by diffuse reflectance.

Applications

IR spectroscopy is routine in organic and analytical chemistry for identifying compounds and functional groups, and is used in polymer science, pharmaceuticals (quality control and counterfeit detection), forensics, art conservation, environmental monitoring of gases, and remote sensing. Near-infrared spectroscopy extends the method to rapid, non-destructive analysis of food, agriculture and biological tissue.
