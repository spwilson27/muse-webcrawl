Tel Shiloh

Tel Shiloh (Hebrew: תל שילה; Arabic: Khirbet Seilun) is an archaeological site in the West Bank, identified with the biblical city of Shiloh. According to the Hebrew Bible, Shiloh was the central sanctuary of the Israelite tribes before the construction of the Temple in Jerusalem: the Tabernacle and the Ark of the Covenant were housed there, and the prophet Samuel was raised at its shrine under the priest Eli.

Biblical significance

The Books of Joshua, Judges, and Samuel describe Shiloh as the place where the land was divided among the tribes by lot, where an annual festival was held, and where Hannah prayed for a son and dedicated the young Samuel to the sanctuary. The Bible implies the shrine was destroyed, traditionally associated with a Philistine victory in the 11th century BC.

Archaeology

The mound covers about 80 dunams (8 hectares) and was first excavated by a Danish expedition in the 1920s and 1930s, and later by Israeli teams under Israel Finkelstein in the 1980s and by the Associates for Biblical Research from 2017 onward. Finds include Middle Bronze Age fortifications, Iron Age storage rooms and silos, a Byzantine church, and an early mosque. A flat, rock-cut area on the northern side of the tell has been proposed as the platform where the Tabernacle stood, though this identification is debated.

Modern site

The site lies adjacent to the Israeli settlement of Shilo, established in 1978, and is developed as a visitor attraction presenting the biblical history of Shiloh, with excavated remains, a visitor center, and reconstructed features.
