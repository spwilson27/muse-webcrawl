Maidan Nezalezhnosti

Location: Khreshchatyk, Kyiv, Ukraine
Name meaning: Independence Square
Landmark: Independence Monument (2001)

Maidan Nezalezhnosti (Ukrainian: Майдан Незалежності, literally "Independence Square") is the central square of Kyiv, the capital of Ukraine. Located on Khreshchatyk Street in the Shevchenko Raion, it has been the traditional site of political rallies and mass protests since Ukrainian independence in 1991, and the word "Maidan" itself has come to signify protest movements in Ukraine.

History

The square took shape in the 19th century and bore a succession of names, including Soviet names honouring Kalinin and the October Revolution, before receiving its present name after independence. It was largely rebuilt in 2001, gaining the Independence Monument — a column topped by the figure of the goddess Berehynia — as well as fountains, underground shopping centres, and a glass-domed conservatory.

Protests

The Maidan was the focal point of the 2004 Orange Revolution and, most dramatically, of the Euromaidan protests of 2013–14, during which demonstrators occupied the square for months; over a hundred protesters, remembered as the Heavenly Hundred, were killed in February 2014. Memorials to them line the adjacent Instytutska Street. The square remains the symbolic heart of Ukrainian civic life and a venue for national commemorations, including during the Russo-Ukrainian war.
