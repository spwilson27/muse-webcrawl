Trinity College is a constituent college of the University of Cambridge. Founded in 1546 by Henry VIII, who merged Michaelhouse and King's Hall, it is the largest of the Cambridge colleges by number of students and is widely regarded as among the wealthiest, with a substantial endowment and extensive landholdings.

History and buildings

The college's Great Court, the largest enclosed court in Europe, was built under Thomas Nevile, Master from 1593. The Wren Library, designed by Christopher Wren and completed in 1695, houses treasures including Isaac Newton's annotated copy of the Principia and A. A. Milne's original Winnie-the-Pooh manuscripts. The college chapel contains statues of famous members including Newton, Francis Bacon and Lord Tennyson.

Members and achievements

Trinity members have won 34 Nobel Prizes — more than any other Cambridge college — and alumni include Isaac Newton, Ernest Rutherford, Niels Bohr, Bertrand Russell, Ludwig Wittgenstein, Srinivasa Ramanujan and six British prime ministers. The college is also famous for the Trinity Mathematical Society and for its performances in the Mathematical Tripos.

Traditions

The Great Court Run, attempted when the college clock strikes noon, challenges runners to complete a circuit of the Great Court (about 367 metres) in the time taken for the clock to strike twelve — roughly 43 seconds — a feat re-created in the film Chariots of Fire. An apple tree outside the main gate is said to descend from the tree that inspired Newton's theory of gravity.
