The energy sector of the United States is the largest or second-largest in the world by most measures. The United States is the world's largest producer of petroleum and natural gas, a leading producer of coal and nuclear power, and a rapidly growing producer of wind and solar electricity. Since 2019 the country has been a net exporter of total energy, after decades of net imports.

Overview

Total primary energy consumption is roughly 100 quadrillion British thermal units ("quads") per year. Petroleum supplies the largest share, mainly for transportation; natural gas is the leading fuel for electricity generation and heating; coal's share has fallen sharply since its 2005 peak as gas and renewables displaced it.

History

Coal powered nineteenth-century industrialisation, and the Drake Well of 1859 in Pennsylvania launched the modern oil industry. The twentieth century saw the rise of giant oil companies, rural electrification, the interstate highway system and large-scale nuclear power after the 1950s. The oil shocks of 1973 and 1979 spurred efficiency standards and the creation of the Department of Energy in 1977. The shale revolution of the 2000s–2010s, driven by hydraulic fracturing and horizontal drilling, transformed the United States into the world's top oil and gas producer.

Electricity

Electricity generation totals over 4,000 terawatt-hours annually. Natural gas provides roughly two-fifths, followed by coal, nuclear (about a fifth, from the world's largest reactor fleet), wind, hydroelectricity and fast-growing solar. The grid is divided into three main interconnections — Eastern, Western and ERCOT (Texas) — overseen by regional transmission organisations and the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission.

Renewables and policy

Wind power is concentrated in the Great Plains and Texas; solar growth has been led by California and the Southwest. Federal policy has alternated between fossil-fuel promotion and decarbonisation drives, including the clean-energy incentives of the Inflation Reduction Act of 2022. Energy-related carbon dioxide emissions peaked in the mid-2000s and have since declined gradually.
