Parallel universes

Parallel universes are hypothetical self-contained planes of existence that coexist with one's own universe. The sum of all potential parallel universes is sometimes called the multiverse. Although the term originates in science fiction, related ideas arise in several interpretations of physics.

In physics

The many-worlds interpretation of quantum mechanics, proposed by Hugh Everett in 1957, implies that all possible outcomes of quantum measurements are realised in separate, branching universes. Cosmological models involving eternal inflation similarly suggest distant regions of space with differing physical conditions. Physicist Max Tegmark has proposed a classification of four levels of parallel universes, from distant copies of our Hubble volume to ensembles governed by entirely different mathematical structures.

In fiction

Parallel universes are a staple of science fiction and fantasy, used to explore alternative histories and versions of familiar characters. Notable examples appear in comics, television series such as Sliders and Fringe, and numerous novels. The concept is closely related to the idea of alternate history and time travel stories.
