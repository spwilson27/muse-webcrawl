Aretas IV Philopatris

Aretas IV Philopatris (Nabataean Aramaic: Ḥāritat) was the King of the Nabataeans from roughly 9 BC to AD 40. He was the most powerful of the Nabataean kings, ruling a kingdom centred on Petra in modern-day Jordan that prospered from the caravan trade in frankincense, myrrh, and other luxury goods.

Reign

Aretas rose to power after the death of Obodas III. His accession was initially not recognised by the Roman emperor Augustus, but he was later confirmed in his rule. He took the epithet Philopatris, meaning "lover of his country". Under Aretas IV, Nabataea reached its greatest extent and prosperity, and much of the monumental architecture of Petra, including many of its famous rock-cut tombs and temples, dates to his reign.

Family and biblical references

Aretas's daughter Phasaelis married Herod Antipas, tetrarch of Galilee. Herod later divorced her in order to marry Herodias, an episode connected in the Gospels with the imprisonment and execution of John the Baptist. Phasaelis fled to her father, and tensions contributed to a border war in which Aretas's forces defeated Herod's army around AD 36. Aretas is also mentioned in the New Testament's Second Epistle to the Corinthians, where Paul the Apostle recounts escaping from Damascus when the ethnarch under King Aretas guarded the city in order to arrest him.

Succession

Aretas IV was succeeded by his son Malichus II, who ruled with his wife Shaqilath II.
