Sailing at the Summer Olympics


Image placeholder: Sailing at the Summer Olympics

Sailing (formerly known as yachting) has been a sport at the Summer Olympic Games since 1900 in Paris, missing only the 1904 Games. Early regattas used tonnage-based classes of large boats; over the twentieth century the programme shifted toward smaller one-design dinghies and keelboats. Sailing is one of the few Olympic sports in which men and women competed together in open classes for much of its history, before separate men's and women's events and, later, mixed events were introduced.

Classes and format

Modern Olympic sailing is contested in one-design classes, in which all boats are built to identical specifications. Classes at the Paris 2024 Games included the ILCA 7 and ILCA 6 (formerly Laser), 470 (mixed), 49er and 49erFX skiffs, Nacra 17 mixed foiling catamaran, Formula Kite, and the iQFoil windsurfer. Regattas consist of a series of fleet races followed by a double-points medal race for the top ten boats.

Notable sailors

Great Britain is the most successful nation in Olympic sailing history. The Danish sailor Paul Elvstrøm won four consecutive gold medals (1948–1960), and Britain's Ben Ainslie won four golds and one silver between 1996 and 2012, making him the most decorated Olympic sailor. Brazil's Robert Scheidt and Torben Grael are also among the sport's most successful Olympians.
