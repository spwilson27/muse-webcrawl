Lee Harvey Oswald (October 18, 1939 – November 24, 1963) was a U.S. Marine veteran who assassinated John F. Kennedy, the 35th president of the United States, on November 22, 1963, in Dallas, Texas, firing from the Texas School Book Depository as the presidential motorcade passed through Dealey Plaza. He also killed Dallas police officer J. D. Tippit shortly afterwards, before being arrested at the Texas Theatre.

Life

Oswald served in the U.S. Marine Corps from 1956 to 1959, then defected to the Soviet Union, living in Minsk until 1962, when he returned to the United States with his Soviet wife, Marina. In April 1963 he attempted to assassinate retired general Edwin Walker in Dallas. He travelled to Mexico City in the autumn of 1963, visiting the Cuban and Soviet consulates, and took a job at the Texas School Book Depository in October.

Death and investigations

Two days after the assassination, while being transferred through the basement of Dallas police headquarters, Oswald was shot on live television by nightclub owner Jack Ruby, and died at Parkland Memorial Hospital. The Warren Commission (1964) concluded that Oswald acted alone; later investigations, including the House Select Committee on Assassinations (1979), generally agreed Oswald fired the shots, though the latter suggested a probable conspiracy on acoustic evidence later disputed. Oswald's guilt and possible accomplices remain subjects of extensive debate and conspiracy theories.
