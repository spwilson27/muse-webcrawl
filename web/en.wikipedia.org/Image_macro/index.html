Image macro

A typical image macro with superimposed caption text.

An image macro is a piece of digital media featuring a picture or artwork with superimposed text, often humorous in nature. Image macros are one of the most common forms of internet memes. The term derives from early web forums, where a "macro" was a stored snippet of text that could be quickly posted, and users began attaching images with embedded captions.

Style and history

The classic format places bold white text, frequently in the Impact typeface with a black outline, at the top and bottom of the image: the top line sets up a situation and the bottom line delivers a punchline. Early examples circulated on forums such as Something Awful and 4chan in the mid-2000s; the lolcat phenomenon, including the "I Can Has Cheezburger?" cat photograph, popularised the genre around 2007. Advice animal memes, featuring stock characters such as Courage Wolf or the Socially Awkward Penguin against a color-wheel background, followed in the late 2000s.

Later developments

As social media platforms grew, image macros diversified into reaction images, screenshots with annotation, and multi-panel formats, and dedicated generator websites made them trivial to produce. Critics within internet culture sometimes distinguished the older image macro format from newer, more free-form memes, but the format remains a staple of online humor.
