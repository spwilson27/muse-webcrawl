Television producer

A television producer is a person who oversees one or more aspects of video production on a television program. Some producers take more of an executive role, conceiving a series and pitching it to networks; others are involved more in day-to-day operations, integrating segments into a show. Producers form their own subgroup within the Writers Guild of America and the Producers Guild of America.

Types of producers

Television credits form a hierarchy. The executive producer bears ultimate responsibility for a series; in scripted television the showrunner — usually the creator and head writer — holds this role. Below are co-executive producers, supervising producers, producers, co-producers, and associate producers, ranks that often follow a writer's seniority. Segment producers, field producers, and line producers handle logistics, budgets, and specific pieces of content, especially in news and talk shows.

Responsibilities

Depending on the title, a producer may hire writers and cast, approve scripts and budgets, supervise production and post-production, liaise with the network, and shape the creative direction of the program.
