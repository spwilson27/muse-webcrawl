Differentiable manifold

In mathematics, a differentiable manifold (or smooth manifold when the structure is infinitely differentiable) is a type of manifold locally similar to a vector space in a way that permits calculus to be done on it. Each point has a neighbourhood homeomorphic to Euclidean space via charts, and the transition maps between overlapping charts are required to be differentiable, so that notions such as tangent vectors, derivatives and differential equations are well defined regardless of coordinates.

An atlas of charts gives a manifold its differentiable structure.

Definitions

A differentiable structure of class Ck is an atlas of charts whose transition functions are k times continuously differentiable; C∞ structures are smooth manifolds. At each point the manifold has a tangent space, and the collection of all tangent spaces forms the tangent bundle. Functions between manifolds are differentiable if their coordinate expressions are.

Key results and structures

The inverse function theorem and implicit function theorem carry over to manifolds; Whitney's embedding theorem states every smooth manifold can be embedded in Euclidean space. Extra structures placed on differentiable manifolds — Riemannian metrics, symplectic forms, complex structures — give rise to Riemannian, symplectic and complex geometry, and underpin modern physics: spacetime in general relativity is a smooth Lorentzian manifold, and phase spaces in mechanics are symplectic manifolds.
