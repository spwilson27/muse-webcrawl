Six Goswamis of Vrindavan

The Six Goswamis of Vrindavan were a group of devotional teachers (gurus) and ascetics in the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition of Hinduism, who lived in India during the 15th and 16th centuries. They are regarded as the chief disciples and emissaries of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, who instructed them to settle in Vrindavan, rediscover its lost holy places associated with the pastimes of Krishna, build temples, and systematise the theology of the movement in Sanskrit literature.

The six were:

  • Rupa Goswami — author of Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu and the principal theologian of the group;
  • Sanatana Goswami — his elder brother, author of Hari-bhakti-vilasa and Brihad-bhagavatamrita;
  • Jiva Goswami — their nephew, author of the Sat-sandarbhas, a systematic philosophical exposition;
  • Raghunatha Bhatta Goswami;
  • Gopala Bhatta Goswami — co-author of Hari-bhakti-vilasa;
  • Raghunatha Dasa Goswami — noted for extreme renunciation and devotional poetry.

Legacy

The Goswamis established major temples in Vrindavan, including Madana-Mohana, Govindaji and Gopinatha, and their writings remain the foundational theological texts of Gaudiya Vaishnavism, later carried worldwide by movements such as ISKCON.
