New Delhi is the capital of India and a district of the National Capital Territory of Delhi. Designed by British architects Edwin Lutyens and Herbert Baker, it serves as the seat of all three branches of the Government of India, hosting the Rashtrapati Bhavan (the official residence of the President), the Parliament House, and the Supreme Court.

History

At the Delhi Durbar of 1911, King George V announced the transfer of British India's capital from Calcutta to Delhi. A new imperial city was planned south of the old city (Shahjahanabad) on Raisina Hill, centred on a grand ceremonial axis (Kingsway, now Kartavya Path) running from the Viceroy's House to the war memorial arch, India Gate. New Delhi was formally inaugurated on 13 February 1931. After independence in 1947 it became the capital of the Republic of India; its plan of wide tree-lined avenues, hexagonal roundabouts and bungalow zones remains distinctive, though the metropolitan region has grown into one of the world's largest urban agglomerations, of over 30 million people.

Government and landmarks

New Delhi houses the central government ministries in and around the Central Secretariat, the diplomatic enclave of Chanakyapuri, and major landmarks including India Gate, Rashtrapati Bhavan, Connaught Place, Humayun's Tomb (in the adjoining district), and the Lotus Temple. The city has hosted major events including the 2010 Commonwealth Games (across Delhi) and the 2023 G20 summit.

Climate and environment

New Delhi has a humid subtropical climate with a monsoon season, extreme summer heat (regularly above 40 °C in May–June) and cool winters. Severe seasonal air pollution, particularly in winter from crop-residue burning, traffic and dust, makes Delhi one of the world's most polluted capitals.
