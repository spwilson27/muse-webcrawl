Name change

Name change is the legal act of changing one's name to something other than the name given at birth, adoption or marriage. Procedures vary widely by jurisdiction, from simple usage-based change in some common-law countries to formal court orders or statutory declarations in others.

Reasons

People change their names for many reasons: marriage and divorce; religious conversion; gender transition; adoption; dislike of a birth name; professional or stage names; anglicisation or de-anglicisation of immigrant names; escaping stigma or debt (though changes to defraud are generally prohibited); and political or cultural statements.

By jurisdiction

In much of the United States, a name may be changed by a court order obtained through a relatively simple petition, and in some states by mere usage. In the United Kingdom, there is no single legal authority for names; a change is evidenced by a deed poll enrolled or unenrolled, and official documents are updated on its basis. In Germany and many civil-law countries, name changes are restricted and require a court or administrative decision for an important reason. Japan requires permission of a family court. Many jurisdictions restrict names that are misleading, offensive, or include titles or numbers.
