Snæfellsjökull


Snæfellsjökull viewed from the north

Snæfellsjökull (Icelandic for "snow-fell glacier") is a 700,000-year-old glacier-capped stratovolcano in western Iceland. It is situated on the westernmost part of the Snæfellsnes peninsula and rises to 1,446 metres (4,744 ft) above sea level. The mountain is one of the most famous landmarks of Iceland, visible on clear days from Reykjavík, about 120 km (75 mi) away across Faxaflói bay. On 2 August 2012, the summit ice cap shrank as Iceland experienced a record-breaking heatwave, and for the first time in recorded history the summit was ice-free, though the glacier has since partially recovered.

Geology

Snæfellsjökull is a stratovolcano built of alternating layers of lava and tephra from eruptions over the last several hundred thousand years. Its last eruption is thought to have occurred around 200 AD, producing the Búðahraun lava field. The volcano is the westernmost part of the Snæfellsnes volcanic belt. The summit crater is about 2.5 km (1.6 mi) across and is covered by the Snæfellsjökull glacier; two small peaks flank the crater rim, the higher of which is the summit.

In culture

The mountain is the setting of Jules Verne's 1864 novel Journey to the Centre of the Earth, in which the protagonists descend into the earth through a chimney in the crater. It is also prominent in Icelandic literature, including the Laxdæla saga and Halldór Laxness's novel Under the Glacier (Kristnihald undir Jökli). Some people regard the mountain as a site of special spiritual or mystical energy, and it attracts visitors for that reason.

Snæfellsjökull National Park

The mountain and its surroundings lie within Snæfellsjökull National Park, established in 2001, the only Icelandic national park that extends to the coast. The park covers about 170 km² (66 sq mi) and protects lava fields, coastal cliffs with seabird colonies, and sites of historical interest such as the fishing station remains at Dritvík.
