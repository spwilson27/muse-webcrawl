Operation Trident was an offensive operation launched by the Indian Navy on Pakistan's port city of Karachi during the Indo-Pakistani War of 1971 on the night of 4–5 December 1971. Three Soviet-built Osa-class missile boats of the 25th Killer Squadron, escorted by anti-submarine corvettes, fired Styx anti-ship missiles in the first use of such weapons in the region, sinking the Pakistani destroyer PNS Khaibar, the minesweeper PNS Muhafiz and the merchant vessel MV Venus Challenger, and setting Karachi's Kemari oil storage tanks ablaze — all without Indian losses.

Background

Karachi was the headquarters of the Pakistan Navy and handled nearly all of Pakistan's maritime trade. After hostilities opened on 3 December, the Indian Western Naval Command under Vice Admiral Sourendra Nath Kohli sent the missile boats, towed part of the way to conserve their short range, against the port.

The attack

On the night of 4 December, INS Nipat, INS Nirghat and INS Veer approached Karachi and launched their missiles. Nirghat hit the destroyer Khaibar, Veer sank the minesweeper Muhafiz, and Nipat damaged the freighter Venus Challenger and struck the Kemari fuel farm. The attack sowed such confusion that a Pakistani aircraft fired on its own frigate PNS Zulfiqar in a friendly-fire incident.

Aftermath and legacy

A follow-up raid, Operation Python, on the night of 8–9 December inflicted further damage on Karachi's shipping and fuel reserves, effectively bottling up the Pakistan Navy for the rest of the war. The Indian Navy commemorates 4 December as Navy Day, and the operation is remembered as a landmark demonstration of missile warfare at sea.
