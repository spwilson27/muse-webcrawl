Jamaica is a neighbourhood in the New York City borough of Queens. It is a major commercial and transportation hub, centred on Jamaica Avenue and the Jamaica station complex, one of the busiest rail junctions in the United States.

History

The area was settled by the Dutch in 1656 as Rustdorp and later renamed Jamaica — a name derived from the Lenape word "Yameco" for beaver, not from the Caribbean island. It served as the county seat of Queens County and was a village of the town of Jamaica until consolidation with New York City in 1898.

Transportation

Jamaica station serves the Long Island Rail Road, the New York City Subway (E, J, and Z trains), and the AirTrain JFK to John F. Kennedy International Airport, making the area a key transfer point.

Landmarks

Notable sites include the King Manor Museum, home of Founding Father Rufus King; the Jamaica Performing Arts Center; and York College of the City University of New York, located in the neighbourhood since 1986.
