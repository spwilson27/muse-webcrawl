Order of Merit

The badge of the Order of Merit: a cross with the motto "For Merit" and a central medallion.

The Order of Merit (French: Ordre du Mérite) is a British and Commonwealth order of merit recognising distinguished service in the armed forces, science, art, literature, or the promotion of culture. Established in 1902 by Edward VII, admission is a personal gift of the monarch and is limited to a maximum of 24 living recipients from the Commonwealth realms, plus a small number of honorary members.

History

Edward VII created the order consciously modelling it on the Prussian Pour le Mérite. Early members included Lord Roberts, Lord Kelvin, and Florence Nightingale — the first woman admitted, in 1907. Members are entitled to use the post-nominal letters OM.

Composition

The order has a single class, and members wear a badge depicting a cross with a blue enamel circle bearing the words "For Merit" surrounding the royal cypher. There is also a military division, whose badge differs by crossed swords. Notable past members include Winston Churchill, T. S. Eliot, Bertrand Russell, Mother Teresa (honorary), and Nelson Mandela (honorary).

Precedence

In the United Kingdom the order ranks below the Order of the Companions of Honour in the order of precedence, though its very small membership makes appointment one of the most prestigious honours in the sovereign's personal gift.
