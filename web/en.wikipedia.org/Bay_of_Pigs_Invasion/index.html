Bay of Pigs Invasion

Date17–19 April 1961
LocationBahía de Cochinos (Bay of Pigs), Cuba
ResultCuban government victory
BelligerentsCuban Revolutionary Armed Forces vs. Brigade 2506 (CIA-backed Cuban exiles)

The Bay of Pigs Invasion (Spanish: Invasión de Bahía de Cochinos) was a failed military landing operation on the southwestern coast of Cuba in April 1961 by the United States Central Intelligence Agency (CIA)-sponsored paramilitary group Brigade 2506, composed mainly of Cuban exiles opposed to Fidel Castro's revolutionary government. The invasion was intended to trigger an uprising and overthrow Castro, but Cuban forces defeated the invaders within three days.

Background

After the Cuban Revolution brought Castro to power in 1959, relations with the United States deteriorated sharply. President Dwight D. Eisenhower authorised a covert programme in March 1960 to train Cuban exiles for an invasion. The plan was inherited and approved, in modified form, by President John F. Kennedy, who insisted the operation appear wholly Cuban and denied direct US air support.

The invasion

On 15 April 1961, B-26 bombers painted in Cuban markings attacked Cuban airfields but failed to destroy Castro's small air force. In the early hours of 17 April, about 1,400 exiles landed at Playa Girón and Playa Larga in the Bay of Pigs. Cuban T-33 jets and Sea Fury aircraft sank the supply ships Houston and Río Escondido, stranding the brigade without ammunition resupply. Castro personally directed the counterattack, and by 19 April the surviving invaders had surrendered or fled.

Aftermath

About 1,200 brigade members were captured; 1,179 were tried and sentenced to 30 years' imprisonment. In December 1962 they were ransomed for some $53 million in food and medicine. The fiasco was a major embarrassment for the Kennedy administration, strengthened Castro's position, pushed Cuba closer to the Soviet Union, and set the stage for the Cuban Missile Crisis of October 1962.
