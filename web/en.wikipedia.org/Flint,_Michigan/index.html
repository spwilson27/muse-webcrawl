Flint, Michigan

Flint is the largest city and seat of Genesee County, Michigan, United States. Located along the Flint River, 66 miles (106 km) northwest of Detroit, it had a population of 81,252 at the 2020 census, down from a peak of nearly 200,000 in 1960. Flint is best known as the birthplace of General Motors and the site of the 1936–37 Flint sit-down strike, a pivotal event in the history of the United Auto Workers and American organized labour.

History

The area was a trading point on the Saginaw Trail; a fur trading post was established in 1819 by Jacob Smith, and the city was incorporated in 1855. In the late nineteenth century Flint became a centre of carriage manufacturing — the "Vehicle City" — and William C. Durant built Buick and then founded General Motors there in 1908. The city's population boomed with the automobile industry, and during the Second World War its plants produced tanks and munitions. From the 1980s, GM's workforce in Flint collapsed from some 80,000 jobs to a few thousand, bringing population loss, poverty and fiscal distress; the city was under state emergency management for several periods between 2002 and 2015.

Water crisis

In April 2014, under a state-appointed emergency manager, Flint switched its water source from Lake Huron (supplied by Detroit) to the Flint River without proper corrosion-control treatment. The corrosive water leached lead from aging pipes into drinking water, exposing thousands of residents, including children, to elevated lead levels. The Flint water crisis became a national scandal, prompting criminal charges against officials, a return to the Detroit water supply in October 2015, a settlement of over $600 million for residents, and a court-ordered program to replace all lead service lines.

Geography and culture

Flint lies in the Flint/Tri-Cities region of mid-Michigan. Cultural institutions include the Flint Institute of Arts, the Sloan Museum, the Capitol Theatre and the University of Michigan–Flint. The city is the subject of Michael Moore's documentary Roger & Me (1989), about the effects of GM plant closures.
