North America

North America is a continent in the Northern and Western Hemispheres. It is bordered to the north by the Arctic Ocean, to the east by the Atlantic Ocean, to the southeast by South America and the Caribbean Sea, and to the west and south by the Pacific Ocean. It is the third-largest continent by area and the fourth most populous.

Geography

The continent includes the countries of Canada, the United States and Mexico, the nations of Central America, and the island states of the Caribbean. Greenland, the world's largest island, is geographically part of North America though politically an autonomous territory of Denmark. The continent's landmass is dominated by the Canadian Shield, the Great Plains, the Rocky Mountains in the west and the Appalachian Mountains in the east. Its only land connection to South America is the Isthmus of Panama.

History

North America was first peopled by migration across the Beringia land bridge during the last ice age, at least 15,000 years ago, giving rise to the many Indigenous peoples of the Americas. Norse explorers reached Newfoundland around 1000 CE (see Norse colonization of North America), but sustained contact began with Columbus's voyages from 1492 and subsequent European colonization by Spain, France, England and others, which devastated Indigenous populations through disease and conquest and reshaped the continent through the transatlantic slave trade.

Demographics and economy

The most widely spoken languages are English, Spanish and French. The United States has the world's largest national economy, and together the continent's NAFTA/USMCA members form one of the largest trading blocs. Major metropolitan areas include Mexico City, New York City, Los Angeles, Chicago and Toronto.
