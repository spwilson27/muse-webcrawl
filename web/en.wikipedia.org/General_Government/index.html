General Government

General Government
German: Generalgouvernement
Existed: 1939–1945
Capital: Kraków
Governor-General: Hans Frank
Status: German occupation administration in Poland

The General Government (German: Generalgouvernement für die besetzten polnischen Gebiete) was a German zone of occupation in Poland during the Second World War, established after the German and Soviet invasion of Poland in 1939. Unlike the western Polish territories annexed directly to the Reich, it was administered as a separate entity under Governor-General Hans Frank, headquartered at Wawel Castle in Kraków.

Administration and occupation policy

The territory was initially divided into four districts — Kraków, Lublin, Radom, and Warsaw — with Galicia added after the invasion of the Soviet Union in 1941. German policy treated the General Government as a reservoir of forced labour and a dumping ground for Poles and Jews expelled from annexed areas. Polish higher education and cultural institutions were suppressed, and the intelligentsia were targeted for murder in operations such as AB-Aktion.

The Holocaust

Jews in the General Government were confined to ghettos and then, under Operation Reinhard (1942–43), murdered in the extermination camps at Bełżec, Sobibór, and Treblinka; Auschwitz lay just outside its border in annexed territory. Nearly all of the roughly two million Jews in the territory were killed. The administration ceased to exist as the Red Army advanced in 1944–45. Hans Frank was convicted at Nuremberg and executed in 1946.
