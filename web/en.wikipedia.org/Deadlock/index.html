Deadlock

Diagram of two processes in deadlock, each waiting for a resource held by the other

In concurrent computing, a deadlock is a state in which each member of a group of processes is waiting for another member to take action, such as sending a message or releasing a lock, making progress impossible. Deadlocks are a common problem in multiprocessing systems, parallel computing, and distributed systems.

Conditions

The Coffman conditions, described by Edward G. Coffman Jr. in 1971, must all hold simultaneously for deadlock to occur: mutual exclusion, hold and wait, no preemption, and circular wait. Removing any one of these conditions prevents deadlock.

Handling

Strategies include deadlock prevention (structuring the system so a Coffman condition cannot hold), deadlock avoidance (such as the banker's algorithm), deadlock detection and recovery, and simply ignoring the problem, as most general-purpose operating systems do.

Livelock

A livelock is similar to a deadlock, except that the states of the processes involved constantly change with regard to one another, none progressing.
