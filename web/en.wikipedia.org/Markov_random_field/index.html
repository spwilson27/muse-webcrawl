Markov random field

In the domain of physics and probability, a Markov random field (MRF), also called a Markov network or undirected graphical model, is a set of random variables having a Markov property described by an undirected graph. It differs from a Bayesian network in that the graph has no direction: a variable is conditionally independent of all others given its neighbours in the graph.

Definition

Given an undirected graph G = (V, E) and a set of random variables indexed by V, the variables form a Markov random field if they satisfy the local Markov property: each variable is conditionally independent of every other variable given its neighbours. By the Hammersley–Clifford theorem, under positivity conditions the joint distribution can be factored into a product of non-negative potential functions over the maximal cliques of the graph.

Applications

Markov random fields are widely used in image processing and computer vision, for tasks such as image restoration, segmentation and stereo matching, where neighbouring pixels are encouraged to take similar labels. In statistical physics, the Ising model of ferromagnetism is a classic special case of a Markov random field on a lattice.

Relation to other models

Conditional random fields are discriminative variants of MRFs used for structured prediction. Factor graphs give an equivalent representation of the same factorisation.
