Operations research (British usage: operational research), often shortened to the initialism OR, is a discipline that deals with the development and application of analytical methods to improve decision-making, known in the United States as management science. The term operational analysis is used in the British (and some British Commonwealth) military as an intrinsic part of capability development, management and assurance.

Operations research employs techniques from other mathematical sciences, such as modelling, statistics, and optimisation, to arrive at optimal or near-optimal solutions to decision-making problems.

History

The field crystallised during the Second World War, when British scientists — notably Patrick Blackett's "circus" — applied quantitative methods to problems such as radar deployment, convoy sizing and anti-submarine search patterns, in work originally termed "operational research". After the war the methods spread to industry and logistics. George Dantzig's simplex algorithm for linear programming (1947) became a foundational tool.

Methods and applications

  • Optimisation — linear, integer and nonlinear programming; network flows.
  • Simulation — Monte Carlo and discrete-event models of complex systems.
  • Queueing theory — service systems, call centres, traffic.
  • Decision analysis and game theory.
  • Applications: supply chains, scheduling (airlines, railways, hospitals), revenue management, finance and defence analysis.
