Deucalion

Deucalion (Ancient Greek: Δευκαλίων) is a figure in Greek mythology, the son of the Titan Prometheus and husband of Pyrrha. He is the hero of the Greek flood myth: warned by his father that Zeus intended to destroy the corrupt Bronze Age of humanity with a deluge, Deucalion built a chest (an ark) in which he and Pyrrha survived nine days of flooding, coming to rest on Mount Parnassus. The pair then repopulated the earth and became ancestors of the Hellenic peoples.

The flood

The story is told by Apollodorus, Hyginus and most famously Ovid in Book 1 of the Metamorphoses. After the waters receded, Deucalion consulted the oracle of Themis at Delphi, who instructed the couple to "throw the bones of your mother behind you". Understanding "mother" as Mother Earth and her bones as stones, Deucalion and Pyrrha cast stones over their shoulders: those thrown by Deucalion became men and those by Pyrrha women. The myth plays on the Greek similarity of laos (people) and laas (stone).

Descendants

Deucalion and Pyrrha's son Hellen became the eponymous ancestor of the Hellenes, and through Hellen's sons Dorus, Aeolus and Xuthus, of the Dorians, Aeolians, Achaeans and Ionians. Deucalion was king in Phthia in Thessaly and was buried, according to tradition, at Athens. The Greek flood story belongs to a family of ancient Near Eastern and Mediterranean deluge myths that includes the Mesopotamian Atrahasis and Utnapishtim and the biblical Noah.
