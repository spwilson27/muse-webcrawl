Lucas critique

FieldMacroeconomics
Proposed byRobert Lucas Jr.
Year1976

The Lucas critique, formulated by Robert Lucas Jr. in his 1976 paper "Econometric Policy Evaluation: A Critique", argues that it is naive to predict the effects of a change in economic policy entirely on the basis of relationships observed in historical data, especially highly aggregated historical data.

Argument

Lucas argued that the parameters of econometric models are not structural: because agents form expectations about the future, a change in policy regime will change how agents behave, and therefore change the very relationships the model estimated. Sound policy evaluation must instead rest on "deep parameters" such as preferences and technology, which are invariant to policy.

Impact

The critique transformed macroeconomic modelling, encouraging the development of microfounded models with rational expectations, including real business cycle and New Keynesian DSGE models.
