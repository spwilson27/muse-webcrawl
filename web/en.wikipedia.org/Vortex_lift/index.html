Vortex lift

Vortex lift is a method of generating lift in which a wing produces rotating vortices that energise the airflow over its upper surface, delaying stall and producing additional lift at high angles of attack. It is characteristic of slender delta wings and highly swept leading edges.

Principle

On a sharply swept delta wing, the flow separates at the leading edge and rolls up into two stable vortices that sit above the wing. The low pressure within the vortex cores increases lift beyond that predicted by conventional attached-flow theory. As angle of attack rises, vortex lift grows until the vortices break down, typically well beyond the stall angle of a conventional wing.

Applications

Vortex lift enabled the low-speed performance of the Concorde supersonic airliner, whose ogee delta wing relied on it for takeoff and landing. Fighter aircraft exploit the same effect with leading-edge extensions (LERX), which generate powerful vortices improving manoeuvrability at high angles of attack.
