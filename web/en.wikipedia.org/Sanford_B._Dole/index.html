Sanford Ballard Dole (23 April 1844 – 9 June 1926) was a Hawaiian lawyer and jurist who led the overthrow of the Hawaiian monarchy in 1893 and became the only president of the Republic of Hawaii, then the first territorial governor of Hawaii after annexation by the United States.

Early life

Dole was born in Honolulu to American Protestant missionary parents. Educated at Punahou School and Williams College, he studied law in Boston and returned to Hawaii in 1872, building a practice in Honolulu and an early interest in conservation — he helped found the Hawaiian Audubon Society and was known as an amateur ornithologist. He served in the legislature of the Kingdom of Hawaii in the 1880s and was appointed to its supreme court in 1887.

Overthrow and presidency

Dole supported the 1887 "Bayonet Constitution" limiting the monarchy and, after Queen Liliʻuokalani moved to restore royal authority, headed the Committee of Safety that deposed her in January 1893. He became president of the Provisional Government and then, when the United States declined immediate annexation, president of the Republic of Hawaii on 4 July 1894. His government suppressed the royalist counter-rebellion of 1895 and negotiated the annexation treaty completed under President McKinley in 1898.

Later career

Appointed the first governor of the Territory of Hawaii in 1900, Dole served until 1903 and afterwards sat as a U.S. district judge (1903–1915). His younger cousin James Dole founded the Hawaiian Pineapple Company, maker of the Dole food brand. Dole is a polarising figure in Hawaii: credited by supporters with orderly government and land reform, and criticised by Native Hawaiian sovereignty advocates for his central role in ending the monarchy.
