The 1967 Tour de France was the 54th edition of the Tour de France, one of cycling's Grand Tours. It took place between 29 June and 23 July 1967, over 22 stages covering a total distance of 4,779 kilometres (2,970 mi). The race was won by Frenchman Roger Pingeon, riding for the French national team, ahead of Spanish climber Julio Jiménez and Italian Franco Balmamion.

The race is remembered above all for the death of English rider Tom Simpson, who collapsed and died on the slopes of Mont Ventoux during stage 13 on 13 July 1967, in one of the most infamous tragedies in the history of professional cycling. The 1967 edition was also the first Tour to be held with the prologue time trial format experimented with in later editions, and it marked the return of the race to national teams, which were used from 1967 until 1968 before trade teams returned.

Teams and riders

As had been customary in the 1960s, the 1967 Tour was contested by national teams. Thirteen teams of ten riders started the race, making an initial peloton of 130 cyclists. The French entered multiple squads, and the field included the dominant riders of the era: five-time winner Jacques Anquetil did not take part, having chosen to focus on other goals, which left the contest wide open among Pingeon, Raymond Poulidor, Lucien Aimar, Felice Gimondi, and the Spanish climbing contingent.

Route and stages

The race started in Angers in western France — the first time the Grand Départ had been held there — and proceeded in a clockwise direction, visiting the Pyrenees before crossing to the Alps via Mont Ventoux. The route included celebrated climbs such as the Col du Tourmalet, the Col d'Aubisque, Mont Ventoux and the Col du Galibier. The race finished, as was tradition before 1968's change, at the Parc des Princes velodrome in Paris.

Race overview

Roger Pingeon took the yellow jersey with a long solo breakaway on stage 5 to Jambes, and although he briefly lost the lead, he regained it decisively in the mountains. His advantage was built on consistency in the Pyrenees and a strong ride over Mont Ventoux's stage, and he sealed the victory with a solid final time trial into Paris. Jan Janssen won the points classification (green jersey), a prelude to his overall victory the following year. Julio Jiménez won the mountains classification for the third and final time in his career.

Death of Tom Simpson

The barren upper slopes of Mont Ventoux, where Tom Simpson collapsed during stage 13.

On 13 July 1967, during the 13th stage from Marseille to Carpentras, which crossed Mont Ventoux, British rider Tom Simpson collapsed about two kilometres from the summit of the mountain in extreme heat. Despite being helped back onto his bicycle by spectators, he collapsed again and was pronounced dead. Amphetamines and alcohol were found in his system, and the combination of drugs, heat, dehydration and exhaustion was blamed for his death. His last words are popularly reported as "Put me back on my bike." Simpson's death led to the introduction of mandatory drug testing in the Tour de France from the 1968 edition onwards, and a memorial now stands near the spot where he fell.
