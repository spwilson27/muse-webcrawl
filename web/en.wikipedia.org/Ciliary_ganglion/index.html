Ciliary ganglion

Ciliary ganglion
LocationPosterior orbit, between the optic nerve and the lateral rectus muscle
InnervatesSphincter pupillae and ciliary muscle of the eye
SystemParasympathetic nervous system

The ciliary ganglion is a small parasympathetic ganglion of the head, about the size of a pinhead (1–2 mm), located in the posterior orbit between the optic nerve and the lateral rectus muscle, just lateral to the ophthalmic artery. It is one of four parasympathetic ganglia of the head and neck.

Roots and branches

Three roots enter the ganglion. The parasympathetic (motor) root carries preganglionic fibres from the Edinger–Westphal nucleus, travelling with the oculomotor nerve (CN III) via its branch to the inferior oblique muscle; these are the only fibres that synapse in the ganglion. A sensory root from the nasociliary nerve (V1) and a sympathetic root from the internal carotid plexus pass through without synapsing.

Postganglionic parasympathetic fibres leave the ganglion in six to ten short ciliary nerves, which pierce the sclera around the optic nerve to supply the sphincter pupillae (producing pupillary constriction, or miosis) and the ciliary muscle (producing accommodation of the lens for near vision).

Clinical significance

Damage to the ciliary ganglion or short ciliary nerves can produce a tonic pupil (Adie's pupil), which is dilated, reacts poorly to light but slowly to near effort, and shows denervation supersensitivity to dilute pilocarpine.
