Newspeak

Newspeak is the fictional language of Oceania, the totalitarian superstate in George Orwell's dystopian novel Nineteen Eighty-Four (1949). It is a controlled language created by the Party to limit freedom of thought — concepts such as free will, individualism and opposition to the regime — by eliminating words that express them. Orwell described its principles in the appendix "The Principles of Newspeak"; the language was to fully supplant Oldspeak (standard English) by 2050.

Design

Newspeak deliberately shrinks the vocabulary of English. Words are stripped of secondary meanings: for example, bad is replaced by the antonym-derived ungood, intensified as plusungood and doubleplusungood. Regularised grammar removes irregular forms, and many words serve as both noun and verb. The vocabulary is divided into three classes:

A vocabulary — everyday words for eating, working, travelling and the like, greatly reduced in number and stripped of ambiguity.
B vocabulary — words deliberately constructed for political purposes, often abbreviations of compound phrases, such as Ingsoc (English Socialism), Minipax (Ministry of Peace) and doublethink.
C vocabulary — scientific and technical terms.

Purpose

The goal of Newspeak is to make heretical thought — "thoughtcrime" — literally impossible, on the theory that thought is dependent on words: if there is no word for freedom in the sense of intellectual liberty, the concept itself cannot be entertained. Each new edition of the Newspeak dictionary is thinner than the last as more words are abolished. The character Syme, a lexicographer working on the Eleventh Edition, enthusiastically explains this aim to Winston Smith before himself being vaporised.

Influence

The word "Newspeak" has entered general usage for bureaucratic or political language that is evasive, euphemistic or designed to obscure meaning. Orwell drew partly on his experience with wartime propaganda and on proposals for simplified English such as C. K. Ogden's Basic English, which he had earlier supported but came to see as open to abuse.
