The Haynes Automobile Company was an American automobile manufacturer based in Kokomo, Indiana, active in production from the 1890s until 1925. Its founder, Elwood Haynes, built one of the first successful gasoline-powered automobiles in the United States — the Haynes "Pioneer", first driven on 4 July 1894 — and the company that grew from it was among the pioneering firms of the American motor industry.

Origins

Elwood Haynes, a metallurgist who also invented the stainless-steel alloy Stellite, designed his first car in 1893–94 with machinists Elmer and Edgar Apperson. On Independence Day 1894 he drove the single-cylinder Pioneer about a mile and a half out of Kokomo at roughly seven miles per hour. The Haynes-Apperson Company was incorporated in 1898 to manufacture cars; after the Apperson brothers left in 1901 to form their own company, Haynes reorganised the business, which took the Haynes Automobile Company name in 1905.

Production years

Haynes cars acquired a reputation for advanced engineering, including the use of aluminium alloys, and the company marketed a range of touring cars and runabouts. It promoted itself aggressively with the slogan claiming priority in American motoring. Models included six- and twelve-cylinder cars; the Haynes V-12 of the later 1910s was a notable offering, and the firm produced the "Light Six" and "Light Twelve" series during and after the First World War.

Decline and closure

Like many independent manufacturers, Haynes struggled in the competitive 1920s market dominated by larger volume producers. The company entered receivership in 1924 and ceased production in 1925. Elwood Haynes died the same year; his Kokomo home is now the Elwood Haynes Museum, and the Pioneer is held by the Smithsonian Institution.
