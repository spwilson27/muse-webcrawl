Spanish treasure fleet

The Spanish treasure fleet (Spanish: Flota de Indias) was a convoy system used by the Spanish Empire from 1566 to 1790 to transport silver, gold, and other valuable goods from Spanish America to Spain, and manufactured goods in the opposite direction.

Organization

Two fleets sailed annually: the Flota de Nueva España to Veracruz in Mexico, and the Galeones de Tierra Firme to ports on the South American mainland such as Cartagena and Nombre de Dios (later Portobelo). Silver from Potosí and Zacatecas, gold, emeralds, pearls, spices, indigo, and cochineal were loaded for return. The fleets rendezvoused at Havana before sailing for Spain, arriving at Seville (later Cádiz).

Defence and losses

The convoy system was generally successful: entire fleets were captured only twice, by the Dutch under Piet Pieterszoon Hein in 1628 and by the English in 1656–57. More treasure was lost to hurricanes, notably the fleets of 1622, 1715, and 1733, wrecked off Florida and Cuba.

Decline

As Spain's maritime power waned and trade regulations relaxed, the system declined in the 18th century; the last treasure fleet sailed in 1790. The Manila galleon across the Pacific formed a connected branch of the same global trade network.
