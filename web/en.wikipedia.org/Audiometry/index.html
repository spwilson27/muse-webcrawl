Audiometry

Audiometry is a branch of audiology and the science of measuring hearing acuity for variations in sound intensity and pitch, involving thresholds and differing frequencies. Typically, audiometric tests determine a subject's hearing levels with the help of an audiometer, but may also measure ability to discriminate between different sound intensities, recognise pitch, or distinguish speech from background noise.

Pure tone audiometry

The most common test, pure tone audiometry, presents tones at standard frequencies (usually 250 Hz to 8,000 Hz) through headphones, and the softest level at which each tone is heard is plotted on an audiogram. Air-conduction thresholds test the whole hearing pathway, while bone-conduction thresholds, measured with a vibrator on the mastoid, bypass the outer and middle ear and help distinguish conductive from sensorineural hearing loss.

Other tests

Speech audiometry measures the ability to recognise spoken words at various intensities. Tympanometry assesses middle-ear function, and objective measures such as otoacoustic emissions and auditory brainstem response testing are used for infants and patients who cannot respond behaviourally.

Applications

Audiometry is used in newborn hearing screening, occupational health monitoring for noise-exposed workers, diagnosis of hearing disorders, and the fitting of hearing aids and cochlear implants.
