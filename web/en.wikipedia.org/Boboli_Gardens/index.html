Boboli Gardens

The Boboli Gardens (Italian: Giardino di Boboli) form a historic park in Florence, Italy, behind the Pitti Palace. Created for the Medici family from the mid-16th century, they are among the finest examples of the Italian Renaissance garden and cover about 4.5 hectares.

History

The gardens were designed initially by Niccolò Tribolo and continued by Bartolomeo Ammanati and Bernardo Buontalenti for Eleonora di Toledo, wife of Cosimo I de' Medici. They were progressively enlarged through the 17th century.

Features

Notable features include the amphitheatre, the Neptune Fountain, the Buontalenti Grotto, the Isolotto with its Oceanus fountain, and extensive collections of Roman and Renaissance statuary. The gardens form part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing for the Medici Villas and Gardens.
