No Child Left Behind Act

EnactedJanuary 8, 2002
Signed byPresident George W. Bush
Replaced byEvery Student Succeeds Act (2015)

The No Child Left Behind Act of 2001 (NCLB) was a United States federal law that reauthorized the Elementary and Secondary Education Act and expanded federal involvement in public education. It was signed into law by President George W. Bush on January 8, 2002, with broad bipartisan support.

Provisions

NCLB required states receiving federal education funding to administer annual standardized tests in reading and mathematics, to measure "adequate yearly progress" for all student subgroups, and to intervene in schools that repeatedly failed to meet targets. It also set requirements for teacher qualifications and mandated reporting of school performance to parents, including options to transfer out of persistently low-performing schools.

Criticism and replacement

Critics argued that the law's proficiency targets were unrealistic, that it narrowed curricula toward tested subjects, and that federal funding was insufficient. Beginning in 2011 the Obama administration granted states waivers from some requirements. In December 2015 NCLB was replaced by the Every Student Succeeds Act, which returned greater accountability authority to the states.
