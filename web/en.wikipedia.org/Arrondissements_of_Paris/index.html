Arrondissements of Paris

The arrondissements of Paris are the twenty administrative districts into which the city of Paris, France, is divided. Numbered 1 to 20, they spiral outward clockwise from the centre of the city in a pattern often compared to a snail shell (l'escargot de Paris). Each arrondissement has its own mayor and council (elected since 1983) subordinate to the Mayor of Paris, and is further divided into four quartiers.

History

Paris was first divided into twelve arrondissements in 1795. The present twenty date from 1860, when Napoleon III and Baron Haussmann annexed neighbouring communes, expanding the city to its modern limits and renumbering the districts in the now-familiar spiral.

Character

Parisians commonly refer to districts by number, each carrying distinct associations: the 1st contains the Louvre and Palais Royal; the 4th includes the Marais and Notre-Dame; the 5th is the Latin Quarter; the 6th Saint-Germain-des-Prés; the 7th the Eiffel Tower and Les Invalides; the 8th the Champs-Élysées; the 16th is famously affluent; the 18th includes Montmartre; and the outer eastern districts (11th, 19th, 20th) are known for working-class and immigrant communities and the Père Lachaise Cemetery. The last two digits of Parisian postal codes (75001–75020) correspond to the arrondissement.
