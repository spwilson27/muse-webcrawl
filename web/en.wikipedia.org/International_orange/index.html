International orange is a colour used in the aerospace industry to set objects apart from their surroundings, similar to safety orange but deeper and with a more reddish tone. It is also the colour of the famous Golden Gate Bridge and of the pressure suits worn by NASA astronauts.

The Golden Gate Bridge's colour is a variant of international orange chosen for visibility in San Francisco fog.

Variants

There are three principal shades referred to as international orange. The aerospace shade, defined by US Federal Standard 595 (FS 12197), is used to mark towers, test aircraft, and parts of rockets — elements of the Space Shuttle's external tank and solid rocket boosters were painted in it. The engineering shade is the colour of the Golden Gate Bridge; the bridge's consulting architect, Irving Morrow, selected the warm orange vermilion both for visibility in fog and to complement the setting, and the colour was adopted permanently instead of the originally planned grey or black-and-yellow striping. A third, more fluorescent shade appears in military and safety applications.

Uses

  • Astronaut suits — NASA's Advanced Crew Escape Suit, worn during launch and re-entry, is international orange, chosen for visibility against sea and sky in a rescue; it is nicknamed the "pumpkin suit."
  • Flight testing — experimental and chase aircraft often carry international orange panels for conspicuity.
  • Structures — besides the Golden Gate Bridge, the Tokyo Tower is painted in international orange and white to comply with Japanese aviation regulations.
  • Maritime and safety marking — buoys, cables, and high-visibility equipment.

Distinction from safety orange

Safety orange (blaze orange) is a lighter, more yellow-orange shade standardised for hunting safety, roadwork, and high-visibility clothing. International orange is darker and redder; the two are frequently confused, and paint sold as "international orange" for the Golden Gate Bridge differs measurably from the aerospace specification.
