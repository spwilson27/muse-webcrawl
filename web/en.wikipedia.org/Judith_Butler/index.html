Judith Butler (born 1956) is an American philosopher and gender theorist whose work has influenced political philosophy, ethics, literary theory and feminist and queer theory. Butler is a professor in the Graduate School and the Department of Comparative Literature at the University of California, Berkeley.

Life and education

Butler was raised in Cleveland, Ohio, in a Jewish family, studied philosophy at Bennington College and Yale University, receiving a doctorate from Yale in 1984 with a dissertation on desire in Hegel, later published as Subjects of Desire (1987). Butler taught at Wesleyan, George Washington University and Johns Hopkins before joining Berkeley in 1993. Butler identifies as non-binary and uses they/them pronouns.

Gender Trouble and performativity

Butler's best-known work, Gender Trouble: Feminism and the Subversion of Identity (1990), argued that gender is not an expression of an inner essence but is performative — constituted through repeated stylized acts, gestures and speech that create the illusion of a stable gendered self. The book questioned the category of "women" as the unproblematic subject of feminism and became a founding text of queer theory. Bodies That Matter (1993) extended the argument to the materiality of sex.

Later work

Later books address vulnerability, war and nonviolence, including Precarious Life (2004), Frames of War (2009), Notes Toward a Performative Theory of Assembly (2015) and The Force of Nonviolence (2020). Butler has also written extensively on the Israel–Palestine conflict and has been a prominent public intellectual and activist, receiving honours including election to the American Academy of Arts and Sciences.
