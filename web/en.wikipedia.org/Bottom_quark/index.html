Bottom quark

Symbol: b
Charge: −1/3 e
Mass: ~4.18 GeV/c²
Generation: Third
Discovered: 1977 (Fermilab)

The bottom quark, also known as the beauty quark, is a third-generation elementary particle. It carries an electric charge of −1/3 e and a bare mass of about 4.18 GeV/c², rather heavy compared with other quarks. All quarks, including the bottom quark, carry colour charge and interact via the strong interaction.

History

The bottom quark was predicted in 1973 by Makoto Kobayashi and Toshihide Maskawa to explain CP violation. It was discovered in 1977 at Fermilab by a team led by Leon Lederman, through observation of the upsilon (ϒ) meson, a bound state of a bottom quark and its antiquark.

Properties and hadrons

Bottom quarks are produced in high-energy collisions and decay relatively slowly — with lifetimes around a picosecond — mostly into charm or up quarks via the weak interaction. Hadrons containing bottom quarks include the B mesons and the Lambda-b baryon, whose decays are studied in flavour physics experiments such as BaBar, Belle and LHCb.
