The Waal is a major distributary branch of the river Rhine flowing through the Netherlands. It is the largest branch, carrying roughly two-thirds of the Rhine's discharge into the country, and is a major waterway for shipping between the port of Rotterdam and Germany.

Course

The Waal begins where the Rhine splits at the village of Pannerden into the Waal and the Pannerdensch Kanaal (which leads to the Nederrijn). From there the river flows westward past the cities of Nijmegen, Tiel and Zaltbommel. At Woudrichem and Gorinchem it merges with the Afgedamde Maas and continues as the Boven Merwede toward Rotterdam and the North Sea.

The river is extensively managed with dikes, groynes and floodplains, and it forms the southern boundary of the Betuwe region, with the Nederrijn and Lek to the north.

History and significance

In Roman times the Waal (Latin: Vahalis) marked part of the northern frontier (limes) of the empire. Nijmegen, the oldest city in the Netherlands, grew up on its southern bank. During the Second World War the river was a major obstacle in Operation Market Garden (1944), when Allied forces fought to capture the bridges at Nijmegen and Arnhem.

The Waal is one of the busiest inland shipping routes in Europe. Large bridges span it at Nijmegen (the Waalbrug and De Oversteek), Tiel, Zaltbommel (Martinus Nijhoff Bridge) and Gorinchem.
