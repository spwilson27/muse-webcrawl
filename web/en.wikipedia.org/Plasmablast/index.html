Plasmablast

A plasmablast is a short-lived, proliferating antibody-secreting cell that arises from activated B lymphocytes during an immune response. Plasmablasts are an intermediate stage between activated B cells and mature, non-dividing plasma cells.

Development

After encountering antigen, B cells in lymph nodes and other secondary lymphoid organs differentiate into plasmablasts, which enter the blood and migrate to the bone marrow or mucosal tissues. There some mature into long-lived plasma cells that sustain antibody production for years, and which are identified by markers such as CD38 and CD138, with reduced surface immunoglobulin and MHC class II.

Significance

Blood plasmablast numbers rise sharply about a week after infection or vaccination and are used as a marker of an active humoral immune response.
