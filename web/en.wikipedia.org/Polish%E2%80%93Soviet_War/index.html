Polish–Soviet War

Date1919 – 18 March 1921
LocationCentral and Eastern Europe
BelligerentsPoland (with Ukrainian allies) vs Russian SFSR and Ukrainian SSR
ResultPeace of Riga; Polish victory

The Polish–Soviet War (1919–1921) was an armed conflict between the Second Polish Republic and Soviet Russia over the borderlands of Ukraine and Belarus, in the aftermath of the First World War and the Russian Revolution.

Course of the war

Fighting escalated in 1920 when Polish forces under Józef Piłsudski, allied with Symon Petliura's Ukraine, advanced on Kyiv in April. A Soviet counteroffensive under Mikhail Tukhachevsky drove deep into Poland and threatened Warsaw. In August 1920 the Polish victory at the Battle of Warsaw — remembered as the "Miracle on the Vistula" — reversed the tide, and subsequent Polish victories at Komarów and on the Niemen forced the Soviets to sue for peace.

Peace of Riga

The Peace of Riga (18 March 1921) fixed a Polish-Soviet border well east of the Curzon Line, dividing Ukrainian and Belarusian lands between Poland and the Soviet republics. The outcome secured Poland's independence and halted the westward spread of the Russian Revolution for a generation.
