List of federal subjects of Russia

The Russian Federation is a federal state composed of 89 federal subjects (Russian: субъекты Федерации, subyekty Federatsii), each of which is a constituent entity of the federation. These subjects are grouped into several types: republics, krais (territories), oblasts (provinces), federal cities, one autonomous oblast, and autonomous okrugs (districts). Each subject has its own head of government and legislature and is represented in the Federation Council, the upper house of the Federal Assembly.

Types of federal subjects

TypeNumberDescription
Republics24Nominally autonomous; each has its own constitution, language, and legislature. Examples include Tatarstan, Bashkortostan, Sakha (Yakutia), Dagestan, and Chechnya.
Krais9Territories, legally similar to oblasts. Examples include Krasnodar Krai, Krasnoyarsk Krai, Primorsky Krai, and Altai Krai.
Oblasts48Provinces; the most common type of subject. Examples include Moscow Oblast, Leningrad Oblast, Novosibirsk Oblast, and Sverdlovsk Oblast.
Federal cities3Moscow, Saint Petersburg, and Sevastopol are cities that function as separate federal subjects.
Autonomous oblast1The Jewish Autonomous Oblast, the only subject of this type.
Autonomous okrugs4Districts with substantial ethnic autonomy, including Chukotka, Khanty-Mansi, Yamalo-Nenets, and Nenets Autonomous Okrugs.

Selected subjects

The largest federal subject by area is the Sakha Republic (Yakutia), covering about 3.1 million square kilometres in the Russian Far East — larger than Argentina. The most populous subject is the city of Moscow, with over 13 million residents, followed by Moscow Oblast and Saint Petersburg. The smallest subject by area is the federal city of Sevastopol.

Federal districts

Since 2000, the federal subjects have also been grouped into eight federal districts — Central, Northwestern, Southern, North Caucasian, Volga, Ural, Siberian, and Far Eastern — each overseen by a presidential plenipotentiary envoy. The federal districts are administrative groupings and are not themselves federal subjects.

History

The current structure evolved from the administrative divisions of the Russian Soviet Federative Socialist Republic within the Soviet Union. In the 2000s, several autonomous okrugs were merged into neighbouring krais and oblasts, reducing the number of subjects from 89 to 83; subsequent changes, including the accession of Crimea and Sevastopol in 2014 and claimed annexations in 2022, brought the claimed total back to 89, though several of these are not internationally recognised.
