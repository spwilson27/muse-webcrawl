Learned Hand formula

The Learned Hand formula (or Hand test, calculus of negligence) is an algebraic formula used in tort law to determine whether a party breached a duty of care. It was articulated by Judge Learned Hand in United States v. Carroll Towing Co. (1947).

Hand stated that liability depends on three variables: B, the burden of adequate precautions; P, the probability that the harm occurs; and L, the gravity of the resulting injury. Negligence consists in failing to take a precaution where B < P × L — that is, where the burden of prevention is less than the probability of loss multiplied by its magnitude.

The formula became a cornerstone of the law-and-economics approach to negligence, framing reasonable care as an efficiency comparison, though courts apply it as a guide rather than a precise calculation.
