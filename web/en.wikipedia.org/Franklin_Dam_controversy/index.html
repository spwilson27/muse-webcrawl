Franklin Dam controversy

The Franklin Dam controversy was a major Australian environmental dispute of the early 1980s over a proposed hydroelectric dam on the Gordon River in Tasmania, just below its junction with the Franklin River. The dam would have flooded parts of the Franklin River valley within the newly inscribed Tasmanian Wilderness World Heritage Area. The campaign to stop it became one of the most significant environmental protests in Australian history.

The blockade

The Tasmanian Hydro-Electric Commission's Gordon-below-Franklin scheme was opposed by the Tasmanian Wilderness Society, led by Bob Brown. After the area was listed as a World Heritage site in December 1982, protesters launched the Franklin River Blockade, a peaceful occupation of the dam site in which more than 1,400 people were arrested over the summer of 1982–83. The campaign, aided by striking photographs of the river by Peter Dombrovskis — notably "Morning Mist, Rock Island Bend" — became a national issue in the 1983 federal election.

Resolution

The Australian Labor Party under Bob Hawke won the 1983 election promising to stop the dam, and passed the World Heritage Properties Conservation Act. Tasmania challenged the law, but in the Tasmanian Dam Case (1983) the High Court of Australia upheld the Commonwealth's power to legislate to implement its international treaty obligations. The dam was never built, and the campaign helped establish the Australian Greens movement.
