Compass

TypeNavigational instrument
UsesDetermining direction relative to Earth's magnetic poles

A compass is a device that shows the cardinal directions used for navigation and geographic orientation. It commonly consists of a magnetized needle or other element, such as a compass card, that can pivot to align itself with magnetic north.

History

The magnetic compass was invented in China during the Han dynasty and was later used for navigation by the Song dynasty. It spread to Europe and the Islamic world, transforming maritime travel by allowing sailors to determine direction when the sky was overcast.

Types

Modern compasses include magnetic compasses, gyrocompasses, solid-state electronic compasses, and GPS receivers that derive heading from satellite signals. Hikers commonly use baseplate and lensatic compasses with topographic maps.

Use

To use a compass with a map, the user accounts for magnetic declination, the angle between magnetic north and true north, which varies by location and over time.
