Mannlicher

Mannlicher refers to firearms designed by the Austrian engineer Ferdinand Ritter von Mannlicher (1848–1904), or built on his patents, principally at the Steyr works in Austria. Mannlicher was a prolific designer of repeating rifles whose innovations — the en-bloc clip loading system and the straight-pull bolt action — were widely adopted in military service.

Designs

Mannlicher's en-bloc clip, patented in the 1880s, allowed a full clip of cartridges to be inserted into the magazine as a unit; it was used in the Austrian Mannlicher M1886 and M1895 rifles, the German Gewehr 88, the French Berthier, the Italian Carcano and, in adapted form, the American M1 Garand. His straight-pull bolt, in which the bolt handle is pulled directly back without rotation, armed Austria-Hungary's infantry with the Steyr-Mannlicher M1895, noted for its speed of operation. Mannlicher also designed early semi-automatic pistols (the M1894 and the Steyr Mannlicher M1901) and the rotating-spool magazine used in the Mannlicher–Schönauer sporting rifle (1903).

Legacy

The name survives in the Steyr Mannlicher company (now Steyr Arms) and its sporting and military rifles. Mannlicher rifles served through both World Wars in numerous armies and remain common as surplus and hunting arms.
