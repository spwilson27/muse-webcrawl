Noshaq

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Noshaq (also spelled Noshaq, Nowshak, or Noshak) is the highest mountain in Afghanistan and the second-highest independent peak of the Hindu Kush range, after Tirich Mir in Pakistan. It is situated on the border between Afghanistan's Badakhshan Province and Pakistan's Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (Chitral District), forming part of the Wakhan Corridor region.

Geography

Noshaq's massif includes several summits, of which the main peak rises to 7,492 metres. The mountain rises above the Wakhan Corridor to the north and the Tirich Gol valley to the south. Climbing routes approach from both sides: the Afghan route ascends from the Qala Panja area in the Wakhan, while the Pakistani route begins from the Shogram valley in Chitral.

Climbing history

The mountain was first climbed in 1960 by a Japanese expedition whose members Toshiaki Sakai and Goro Iwatsubo reached the summit via the western ridge from Afghanistan. The first winter ascent was achieved in 1973 by a Polish expedition (Tadeusz Piotrowski and Andrzej Zawada), a landmark in winter mountaineering. Noshaq's Afghan base camp became a popular objective for expeditions in the 2000s as one of the more accessible 7,000-metre peaks, and a number of Afghan climbers have trained on the mountain, including the first Afghan ascentionists.
