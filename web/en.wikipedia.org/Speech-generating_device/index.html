Speech-generating device

A speech-generating device (SPD) is an electronic augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) system used by people who are unable to produce sufficient spoken language to meet their communication needs. Speech-generating devices produce synthesised or digitised speech output, allowing the user to "speak" by selecting words, letters, or symbols on a display.

History

Early speech-generating devices of the 1970s and 1980s were bulky keyboard-based machines with limited vocabularies. Advances in speech synthesis, portable computing, and touchscreens have since produced lightweight devices and tablet applications with large vocabularies and natural-sounding voices.

Types

Devices range from dedicated hardware built solely for communication to general-purpose tablets running AAC software. Access methods include direct selection by touch, switch scanning, head pointers, and eye-gaze tracking for users with severe physical disabilities.

Users

Users include people with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), cerebral palsy, autism, stroke, and traumatic brain injury. Physicist Stephen Hawking famously communicated with a speech-generating device for much of his later life.
