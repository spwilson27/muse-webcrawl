Donald Trump

Donald John Trump (born June 14, 1946) is an American politician, businessman, and media personality who served as the 45th president of the United States from 2017 to 2021 and, having won the 2024 election, became the 47th president in January 2025. A member of the Republican Party, he is the second president in American history (after Grover Cleveland) to serve two non-consecutive terms.

Early life and business career

Trump was born in Queens, New York City, the fourth child of real-estate developer Fred Trump and Mary Anne MacLeod Trump. He attended the Kew-Forest School, the New York Military Academy, Fordham University, and the Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania, graduating in 1968 with a degree in economics. He then joined his father's real-estate business, taking control of it in 1971 and renaming it The Trump Organization.

He expanded into Manhattan real estate, building or renovating skyscrapers, hotels, casinos, and golf courses, including Trump Tower (opened 1983) and several Atlantic City casinos. Many ventures faced financial difficulty; his casino businesses went through bankruptcy proceedings in the 1990s and 2000s, while he personally avoided bankruptcy. From 2004 to 2015 he co-produced and hosted the reality television series The Apprentice, which greatly increased his public fame. He also licensed his name widely and co-authored books including The Art of the Deal (1987).

First presidency (2017–2021)

Trump won the 2016 presidential election against Hillary Clinton, winning the Electoral College while losing the national popular vote. His administration enacted a major tax cut (the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017), pursued deregulation, restricted immigration, appointed three justices to the Supreme Court, renegotiated trade agreements, recognised Jerusalem as Israel's capital, and withdrew the United States from the Paris climate agreement and the Iran nuclear deal.

He was impeached by the House of Representatives in 2019 on charges of abuse of power and obstruction of Congress, and acquitted by the Senate in 2020. His final year in office was dominated by the COVID-19 pandemic. He lost the 2020 election to Joe Biden. After the January 6, 2021 attack on the U.S. Capitol by his supporters, he was impeached a second time, for incitement of insurrection, and again acquitted after leaving office.

Between presidencies

Trump remained the dominant figure in the Republican Party. In 2023–2024 he faced several criminal indictments and civil judgments; in May 2024 a New York jury convicted him on 34 felony counts of falsifying business records, making him the first former U.S. president convicted of a felony. He survived an assassination attempt at a campaign rally in Butler, Pennsylvania, in July 2024.

Second presidency (2025–)

Trump defeated Vice President Kamala Harris in the 2024 election, winning both the Electoral College and the popular vote, and was inaugurated as the 47th president on January 20, 2025. His second administration moved quickly on immigration enforcement, tariffs, federal workforce reductions, and pardons for many January 6 defendants.
