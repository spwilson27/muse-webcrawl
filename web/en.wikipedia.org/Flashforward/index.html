Flashforward

A flashforward (sometimes called a prolepsis) is a scene in a narrative that takes the narrative forward in time from the current point in the story. Flashforwards are often used to represent events expected, projected, or imagined to occur in the future. They can also reveal significant parts of the story that have not yet occurred, but soon will in the chronology of the narrative. A flashforward is the counterpart of a flashback, which moves a story back in time.

Use in literature and film

Prolepsis appears in classical literature — for example, prophecies in epic poetry — and is common in modern fiction and television. The television series FlashForward (2009–10) was built entirely around the device, in which the whole world's population experiences a vision of their future. Shows such as Lost and Breaking Bad used flashforwards structurally, opening episodes or seasons with scenes from later in the timeline.

Related devices

A flashforward differs from foreshadowing, which merely hints at future events, and from a "flash-sideways", which depicts an alternate timeline.
