Jacob Gedleyihlekisa Zuma (born 12 April 1942) is a South African politician who served as the fourth president of South Africa from 2009 to 2018. A veteran of the African National Congress (ANC) and its armed wing uMkhonto we Sizwe, he was imprisoned on Robben Island for ten years under apartheid and later lived in exile.

Political career

After the unbanning of the ANC, Zuma rose through provincial and national government, serving as Deputy President under Thabo Mbeki from 1999 until 2005, when he was dismissed amid a corruption scandal surrounding his financial adviser. He nonetheless defeated Mbeki for the ANC presidency in 2007 and led the party to victory in the 2009 general election.

Presidency and controversies

Zuma's presidency was marked by economic stagnation and persistent corruption allegations, including public spending on upgrades to his Nkandla homestead and claims of "state capture" by associates such as the Gupta family. A judicial commission of inquiry (the Zondo Commission) later documented extensive corruption during his tenure. Facing mounting pressure, he resigned in February 2018 and was succeeded by Cyril Ramaphosa.

In 2021 Zuma was sentenced to 15 months in prison for contempt of court after refusing to testify before the commission; his arrest sparked widespread unrest in KwaZulu-Natal and Gauteng. Expelled from the ANC in 2024, he led the new uMkhonto weSizwe Party in that year's election.
