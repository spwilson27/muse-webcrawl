Theory of planned behavior

The theory of planned behavior (TPB) is a psychological theory proposed by Icek Ajzen in 1985 that links beliefs and behavior. It holds that a person's intention to perform a behavior is the most important immediate determinant of whether they will actually perform it, and that intention is shaped by three factors.

Components

The three determinants of intention are: attitude toward the behavior — the person's positive or negative evaluation of performing it; subjective norms — perceived social pressure from important others; and perceived behavioral control — the person's sense of how easy or difficult the behavior is, which Ajzen modelled on Bandura's concept of self-efficacy.

Applications and criticism

The theory grew out of the earlier theory of reasoned action developed by Ajzen and Martin Fishbein, adding perceived control to account for behaviors not fully under volitional control. TPB has been applied to hundreds of studies in health, marketing, environmental behavior and technology adoption, though critics dispute whether intention reliably translates into action.
