Mac App Store

Developer: Apple Inc.

Initial release: 6 January 2011

Operating system: macOS

Type: Digital distribution, software update

License: Proprietary

The Mac App Store is a digital distribution platform for macOS applications, developed and maintained by Apple Inc. It was announced on 20 October 2010 at Apple's "Back to the Mac" event and launched on 6 January 2011 as part of the Mac OS X 10.6.6 update, modelled on the App Store for iOS devices that had debuted in 2008.

History

The store opened with more than 1,000 applications, including Apple's own iWork and iLife programs sold individually for the first time. Within its first day it recorded over one million downloads. In 2012, with OS X Mountain Lion, the Mac App Store also became the channel for operating-system upgrades. A major redesign arrived with macOS Mojave in 2018, adding editorial content, curated collections and dedicated category tabs. In 2020 the introduction of Apple silicon Macs allowed compatible iPhone and iPad apps to run on the Mac and be distributed through the same storefront.

Use by developers

Applications sold through the Mac App Store must pass an Apple review process and, since 2012, must implement sandboxing for security. Apple takes a commission on sales — historically 30 per cent, reduced to 15 per cent for developers in the App Store Small Business Program from 2021. Developers may also distribute Mac software independently of the store, a freedom that distinguishes the Mac platform from iOS; this dual model has prompted long-running debate about curation, security and the reach of Apple's rules.

Features

The store handles purchases through the user's Apple ID, manages installation, licensing and automatic updates, and syncs purchases across machines signed in to the same account. It distributes free and paid applications, offers in-app purchases and subscriptions, and since macOS Catalina has also served as the home of Apple Arcade titles for the Mac.
