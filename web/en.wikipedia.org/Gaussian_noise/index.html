Gaussian noise

Gaussian noise, named after Carl Friedrich Gauss, is a type of signal noise that has a probability density function (pdf) equal to that of the normal distribution (also known as the Gaussian distribution). In other words, the values that the noise can take are Gaussian-distributed.

Properties

A Gaussian random variable z has pdf

pG(z) = (1/(σ√(2π))) · e−(z−μ)²/(2σ²)

where μ is the mean value and σ the standard deviation. The special case of Gaussian white noise has values at any pair of times statistically independent (and identically distributed). In testing channels and modelling systems, Gaussian noise is used as additive white noise to generate additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN).

Causes and occurrence

Gaussian noise arises naturally from many physical processes. The thermal noise (Johnson–Nyquist noise) in electronic circuits is approximately Gaussian, and the central limit theorem ensures that the sum of many independent small random effects tends toward a Gaussian distribution. In digital image processing, Gaussian noise can be reduced using a spatial filter such as a Gaussian blur, though when smoothing an image the result may blur fine detail.
