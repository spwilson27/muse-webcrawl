Jacques Villeneuve

Jacques Joseph Charles Villeneuve (born April 9, 1971) is a Canadian racing driver who competed in Formula One from 1996 to 2006, winning the Formula One World Drivers' Championship in 1997 with Williams. He also won the Indianapolis 500 and the CART Indy Car World Series in 1995, making him one of only a handful of drivers to hold all three titles. He is the son of Formula One driver Gilles Villeneuve, who was killed in a crash in 1982.

Villeneuve won the 1997 World Championship in only his second Formula One season.

Early career and America

Born in Saint-Jean-sur-Richelieu, Quebec, Villeneuve began racing in Italy before moving to North America, where he finished third in the 1993 Toyota Atlantic championship. In Indy cars he was CART Rookie of the Year in 1994; in 1995 he won four races including the Indianapolis 500 — recovering from a two-lap penalty — and took the CART championship, earning a Formula One test and a contract with Williams.

Formula One

Villeneuve announced himself by taking pole position and nearly winning on his Grand Prix debut in Melbourne in 1996; he won four races that year and finished runner-up to team-mate Damon Hill. In 1997 he won seven races and the championship in a famous final-round clash at Jerez, where Michael Schumacher's attempt to force him off the road backfired and led to Schumacher's exclusion from the standings. After a winless 1998, Villeneuve co-founded and drove for the BAR team (1999–2003) with modest results, then raced for Renault, Sauber and BMW Sauber before leaving Formula One midway through 2006.

Later career

Villeneuve subsequently raced in the 24 Hours of Le Mans (finishing second in 2008), NASCAR, the World Rallycross Championship, Formula E and the NASCAR Whelen Euro Series, and has worked as a television commentator. He is an Officer of the National Order of Quebec.
