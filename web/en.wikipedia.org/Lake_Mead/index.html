Lake Mead

Location: Nevada–Arizona border, United States
Formed by: Hoover Dam (completed 1936)
Primary inflow: Colorado River
Capacity: about 26 million acre-feet at full pool — the largest US reservoir by capacity

Lake Mead is a reservoir formed by the Hoover Dam on the Colorado River, located in the states of Nevada and Arizona, about 24 miles (39 km) east of Las Vegas. It is the largest reservoir in the United States in terms of water capacity and supplies water to Arizona, California, Nevada, and parts of Mexico, serving roughly 20 million people and large areas of farmland.

History

The lake was created as the Boulder Canyon Project impounded the Colorado between 1931 and 1936; it is named after Elwood Mead, commissioner of the US Bureau of Reclamation during construction. Several towns, most notably St. Thomas, Nevada, were submerged as the reservoir filled, and Lake Mead National Recreation Area — the first of its kind — was established around its shores in 1936.

Water levels and drought

Since 2000, prolonged drought and increasing demand across the Colorado River Basin have driven a dramatic decline in the lake's surface elevation — from near full pool in 1983 to record lows in 2022, exposing a prominent white "bathtub ring" of mineral deposits, long-submerged structures, boats, and even human remains from decades past. The shortfall prompted the first federally declared water shortage on the Colorado River in 2021, triggering cuts principally to Arizona and Nevada. Modest recoveries followed wetter winters, but long-term water security remains a central policy concern for the American Southwest.
