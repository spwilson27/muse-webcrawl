Noordoostpolder

Noordoostpolder (Dutch for "northeast polder") is a polder and municipality in the province of Flevoland in the central Netherlands. It is the oldest of the polders reclaimed from the IJsselmeer as part of the Zuiderzee Works: the surrounding dikes were completed in 1940 and the land was drained in 1942, during the German occupation of the Netherlands.

Geography and history

The polder lies several metres below sea level and is bordered by the IJsselmeer, the Ketelmeer and the older land of Overijssel and Friesland. The former islands of Urk and Schokland are now embedded in the polder's landscape; Schokland, a symbol of the Dutch struggle against the sea, is a UNESCO World Heritage site. The main town is Emmeloord, laid out at the polder's centre with ten smaller villages arranged around it, each within cycling distance.

Two pumping stations, the Leemans and Wortman gemaal installations (the latter diesel-powered), keep the polder dry. The land is predominantly agricultural, noted for seed potatoes, onions and tulips.
