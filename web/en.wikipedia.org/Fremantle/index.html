Fremantle

Fremantle is a port city in Western Australia, located at the mouth of the Swan River in the metropolitan area of Perth, the state capital. Fremantle Harbour serves as the port of Perth and was established in 1829 as part of the Swan River Colony. The city is known for its well-preserved Victorian and Edwardian streetscapes, maritime history, and vibrant arts and café culture. Locals often refer to it as "Freo".

History

The area was inhabited for thousands of years by the Whadjuk people of the Noongar nation, who called the place Walyalup. The first European settlement was established in 1829 when Captain James Stirling founded the Swan River Colony. Fremantle was named after Captain Charles Fremantle, who claimed possession of the west coast of Australia for Britain in May 1829. Convict labour arrived in 1850, and convicts built many of the city's landmark limestone buildings, including the Fremantle Prison, which operated from the 1850s until 1991 and is now a World Heritage-listed site.

The discovery of gold in Western Australia in the 1890s brought prosperity, and engineer C. Y. O'Connor oversaw the construction of a modern harbour in the Swan River estuary, opened in 1897. During the Second World War, Fremantle was a major Allied submarine base.

Attractions

Notable sites include the Fremantle Markets (established 1897), the Round House (1831), the oldest surviving building in Western Australia; the Western Australian Maritime Museum; and the Fremantle Arts Centre. The city hosted the America's Cup yacht race defence in 1987, which spurred major restoration of its historic West End. Fremantle is also home to the Fremantle Dockers Australian rules football club.

Geography and climate

Fremantle lies about 19 kilometres southwest of Perth's central business district. It has a Mediterranean climate with hot, dry summers moderated by the sea breeze known locally as the "Fremantle Doctor", and mild, wet winters.
