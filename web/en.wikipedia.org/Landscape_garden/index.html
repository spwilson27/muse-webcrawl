Landscape garden

Origin: England, early 18th century
Key figures: William Kent, Capability Brown
Features: Serpentine lakes, ha-has, clumped trees, follies

The landscape garden, also called the English landscape garden, is a style of park and garden design that originated in England in the early 18th century as a reaction against the formal, geometric French garden. It presents an idealised, naturalistic view of nature.

Characteristics

Landscape gardens feature sweeping lawns, serpentine lakes, clumps of trees, and temples, grottoes, and artificial ruins placed as eye-catchers. The sunken fence or ha-ha kept livestock out while preserving an unbroken view. Pioneered by William Kent and Charles Bridgeman, the style reached its height with Lancelot "Capability" Brown, who reshaped estates such as Stowe, Blenheim, and Stourhead (the latter developed by Henry Hoare II).

Influence

The style spread across Europe — to the jardin anglais in France, German parks such as Wörlitz, and beyond — and shaped public park design worldwide, including New York's Central Park.
