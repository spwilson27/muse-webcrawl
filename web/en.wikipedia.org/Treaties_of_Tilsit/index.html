Treaties of Tilsit

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Treaties of Tilsit, also collectively known as the Peace of Tilsit, were two agreements signed by Napoleon I of France in the town of Tilsit in July 1807 in the aftermath of his victory at Friedland. The first treaty, on 7 July, was between Napoleon and Tsar Alexander I of Russia, who had met on a raft on the River Neman. The second, on 9 July, imposed harsh terms on Prussia.

Terms

Russia accepted French domination in western Europe, joined the Continental System against Britain, and ceded the Ionian Islands to France; in return Napoleon promised support regarding Moldavia and Wallachia. Prussia lost roughly half its territory and population: lands west of the Elbe went to the new Kingdom of Westphalia, Polish territories became the Duchy of Warsaw, and Prussia paid a heavy indemnity and accepted French occupation until it was paid.

Consequences

Tilsit marked the zenith of Napoleon's empire, dividing Europe into French and Russian spheres. The humiliation of Prussia spurred the Stein–Hardenberg reforms. The Franco-Russian alliance frayed over the Continental System and Poland, collapsing into war with Napoleon's invasion of Russia in 1812. Like other Napoleonic settlements, Tilsit is often contrasted with the earlier Treaties of Nijmegen that had secured France's eastern frontier a century before.
