Castle

A castle is a type of fortified structure built during the Middle Ages predominantly by the nobility or royalty and by military orders. Scholars usually consider a castle to be the private fortified residence of a lord or noble, distinguishing it from a palace, which is not fortified, and from a fortress, which was not always a residence.

History

Castles originated in the 9th and 10th centuries in Europe, following the fall of the Carolingian Empire, which resulted in its territory being divided among individual lords and princes. Early castles were often built of earth and timber — the motte-and-bailey design — but were later rebuilt in stone. Over the following centuries, castle design evolved with the introduction of concentric defences, machicolations, and purpose-built artillery fortifications.

From the late Middle Ages, gunpowder artillery rendered tall medieval curtain walls vulnerable, and castles gradually lost their military function. Many were converted into stately residences or fell into ruin, while some continued in use as administrative centres and prisons.

Features

Typical features of a castle include a keep (donjon), curtain walls, towers, a gatehouse, a moat, battlements with crenellations, arrowslits, and a great hall. Castles were sited to command important routes, river crossings, or harbours, and served as centres of administration and symbols of authority as well as defensive strongholds.

Social role

Beyond defence, castles controlled the surrounding territory, housed the lord's household and garrison, and served as centres of justice and revenue collection. Their distribution and scale reflect the feudal structure of medieval society.
