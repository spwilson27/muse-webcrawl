Fort-de-France

Fort-de-France bay, one of the major harbours of the Caribbean.

Fort-de-France (Martinican Creole: Fodfwans) is the capital city of Martinique, an overseas department and region of France in the Caribbean. It is also one of the major ports of the Caribbean. With a population of around 75,000 in the commune and roughly 150,000 in the metropolitan area, it is the largest city in the French West Indies.

History

The site was first fortified by the French in 1638, when Jacques Dyel du Parquet built Fort Saint-Louis on the bay; a settlement called Fort-Royal grew beside it. The town was made capital of Martinique in the 1680s, displacing Saint-Pierre in administrative importance after the governorship moved there. It was renamed Fort-de-France in the nineteenth century. British forces captured the island several times, but it was returned to France. In 1902, after the eruption of Mount Pelée destroyed Saint-Pierre, Fort-de-France grew rapidly as the island's undisputed centre. The poet and politician Aimé Césaire, co-founder of the négritude movement, served as its mayor from 1945 to 2001.

Geography and landmarks

The city climbs steep hills above a broad bay on Martinique's west coast. Notable buildings include the Schoelcher Library, an ornate iron-and-glass structure designed for the 1889 Paris exhibition and shipped to Martinique; Saint-Louis Cathedral, rebuilt several times after fires and earthquakes, most recently in the 1890s; Fort Saint-Louis, still an active French naval base; and La Savane park, which once held a statue of Joséphine de Beauharnais, born on the island — the statue was beheaded in 1991 and toppled in 2020.

Transport and economy

Martinique Aimé Césaire International Airport lies just south of the city in Le Lamentin. Ferries connect Fort-de-France with Guadeloupe, Dominica and Saint Lucia, and cruise ships call at the port. The economy centres on administration, commerce, tourism and the port.
