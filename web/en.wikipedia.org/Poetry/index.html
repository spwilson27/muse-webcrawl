Poetry

Poetry (from the Greek word poiesis, "making") is a form of literary art that uses aesthetic and often rhythmic qualities of language to evoke meanings in addition to, or in place of, literal or surface-level meanings. Poetry is one of the oldest forms of literature, predating literacy itself: the earliest surviving works, such as the Sumerian Epic of Gilgamesh and the Vedas, were composed to be sung or recited and passed down orally.

Poetic forms and devices include metre, rhyme, stanza structure, alliteration, assonance, and figurative language such as metaphor and simile. Genres include the epic, lyric, dramatic, satirical, and narrative poem; specific forms include the sonnet, haiku, villanelle, ghazal, ballad, ode, and elegy.

History

Poetry emerged in pre-literate societies as a mnemonic and ceremonial art. Ancient traditions include Mesopotamian epics, Egyptian hymns, Chinese Shijing anthologies, Greek epic and lyric (Homer, Sappho, Pindar), Sanskrit epic, and the Hebrew psalms. In the medieval period, troubadour lyric, Persian ghazals, Japanese tanka, and Dante's Divine Comedy flourished. The Renaissance revived classical forms and produced Shakespeare's sonnets; Romantic poets of the late 18th and 19th centuries emphasized emotion and nature; modernist poets of the early 20th century, such as T. S. Eliot and Ezra Pound, broke with traditional forms, and free verse became dominant in much contemporary poetry.

Elements

  • Prosody — the study of metre, rhythm, and intonation.
  • Meter — a regular pattern of stressed and unstressed syllables, such as iambic pentameter.
  • Rhyme and form — recurring sound patterns and fixed structures such as the 14-line sonnet.
  • Imagery and figurative language — devices that create vivid mental pictures and layered meanings.
  • Sound devices — alliteration, onomatopoeia, and euphony.

Poetry in the modern world

Poetry continues in print, performance, and digital media. Spoken word and slam poetry have brought performance poetry to wide audiences, and poetry remains an important part of ceremonies, songs, and protest movements around the world.
