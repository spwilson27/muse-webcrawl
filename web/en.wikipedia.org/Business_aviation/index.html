Business aviation

Business aviation
Sector of general aviation
Typical aircraft: Business jets, turboprops
Trade bodies: NBAA, EBAA

Business aviation is the use of general aviation aircraft for business purposes, typically by companies and individuals to transport personnel and goods on their own schedules rather than those of scheduled airlines.

Operations

Business aircraft are operated privately by their owners, by corporate flight departments, through fractional ownership schemes, charter operators and jet-card programmes. The fleet ranges from piston aircraft and turboprops to light, midsize and large-cabin business jets such as the Gulfstream, Bombardier Global and Dassault Falcon families.

Rationale

Advocates argue that business aviation saves executive time, reaches thousands of smaller airports not served by airlines, and improves productivity en route. Critics point to its cost and high per-passenger carbon emissions, and the sector receives scrutiny over tax treatment and empty repositioning flights.

Scale

The United States operates the majority of the world's business jets. Industry associations such as the National Business Aviation Association (NBAA) represent the sector, and major manufacturers include Gulfstream, Bombardier, Dassault, Textron Aviation and Embraer.
