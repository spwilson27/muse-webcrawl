William Spencer Vickrey (21 June 1914 – 11 October 1996) was a Canadian-American professor of economics at Columbia University. He won the 1996 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences, shared with James Mirrlees, for fundamental contributions to the economic theory of incentives under asymmetric information. He died of a heart attack only three days after the prize was announced, before the award ceremony.

Contributions

Vickrey's 1961 paper "Counterspeculation, Auctions, and Competitive Sealed Tenders" founded modern auction theory, introducing the analysis of what is now called the Vickrey (second-price sealed-bid) auction, in which the highest bidder wins but pays the second-highest bid — making truthful bidding the optimal strategy. The framework underlies mechanism design and is used in modern spectrum and online advertising auctions.

He also pioneered the economics of congestion pricing — proposing, as early as the 1950s, time-varying fares for the New York subway and tolls for roads to reflect peak demand — and worked on marginal-cost pricing for public utilities and on optimal income taxation, the subject of his 1945 Columbia dissertation and the line of work Mirrlees later completed.
