Jón Sigurðsson (17 June 1811 – 7 December 1879) was the leader of the 19th-century Icelandic independence movement and Iceland's foremost national hero. A historian and philologist based for most of his life in Copenhagen, he argued Iceland's case for autonomy from Denmark on scholarly, legal and economic grounds, and presided over the restored Icelandic parliament, the Althing.

Early life and scholarship

Jón was born at Hrafnseyri in the Westfjords, the son of a pastor. He studied at the Latin school at Bessastaðir and from 1833 at the University of Copenhagen, where he immersed himself in Icelandic manuscripts at the Arnamagnæan Collection and edited texts for the Royal Society of Northern Antiquaries. He became an authority on Icelandic history, literature and economics, and published extensively, including the journal Ný félagsrit, in which he set out proposals for Iceland's economic development and free trade.

Independence movement

Elected to the Althing when it was re-established in 1845, Jón became its president in most sessions until his death. At the National Assembly of 1851 he led the Icelandic delegates in rejecting the Danish government's constitutional proposal with the famous words "Vér mótmaelum allir" ("We all protest"). His strategy — patient, legalistic and grounded in the historical rights established by the Old Covenant of 1262 — shaped the movement: Iceland received a constitution and legislative power in home affairs in 1874, home rule in 1904, and sovereignty in personal union with Denmark in 1918, before full republic status in 1944.

Death and legacy

Jón died in Copenhagen on 7 December 1879; his remains were brought to Iceland and he is buried in Reykjavík's Hólavallagarður cemetery. His birthday, 17 June, is Iceland's National Day — chosen also because the Republic of Iceland was proclaimed on that date in 1944. His portrait appeared on the Icelandic 500 krónur banknote, and the statue of him by Einar Jónsson stands before the Althing house in Reykjavík.
