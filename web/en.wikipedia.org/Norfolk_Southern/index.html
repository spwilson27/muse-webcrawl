Norfolk Southern Railway

[Image placeholder: Norfolk Southern Railway]

The Norfolk Southern Railway (reporting mark NS) is a Class I freight railroad in the United States, one of the country's largest. With headquarters in Atlanta, Georgia, Norfolk Southern operates approximately 19,300 route miles (about 31,100 km) of track in 22 eastern states and the District of Columbia, serving every major container port in the eastern United States.

History

The railroad was formed in 1982 through the merger of the Norfolk and Western Railway and the Southern Railway, two profitable carriers. In 1998–1999 Norfolk Southern and CSX Transportation divided Conrail between them, with Norfolk Southern acquiring about 58 percent of Conrail's assets, including most of the former Pennsylvania Railroad main lines. The enlarged system began operations on June 1, 1999.

Operations

Norfolk Southern moves freight including coal, automobiles, chemicals, intermodal containers and general merchandise. It is the largest originator of coal traffic in the eastern United States and a major transporter of automotive parts and finished vehicles.

Corporate affairs

The Norfolk Southern Corporation moved its headquarters from Norfolk, Virginia, to Atlanta in 2021. In February 2023 a Norfolk Southern train derailed in East Palestine, Ohio, spilling hazardous materials and drawing national attention to rail safety.
