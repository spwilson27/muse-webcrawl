Panthera onca augusta, commonly called the Pleistocene North American jaguar, is an extinct subspecies of the jaguar (Panthera onca) that lived in North America during the Pleistocene epoch. Fossils attributed to it have been found widely across the United States, including sites in California (such as the La Brea Tar Pits), Florida, Tennessee and Pennsylvania, dating from roughly 1.5 million to 11,000 years ago.

Description

P. o. augusta was notably larger than the modern jaguar — estimates suggest it was about 15–25% larger, with proportionally longer limbs and a somewhat less robust build than surviving jaguars. It is thought to have been the ancestral North American form from which jaguars spread into South America after the formation of the Isthmus of Panama, in the Great American Interchange. As the climate changed at the end of the Pleistocene and large prey declined, the northern jaguar populations grew smaller and their range contracted southward; the subspecies became extinct around the end of the last Ice Age, while jaguars survived in Central and South America.
