Samuel Jones Tilden (February 9, 1814 – August 4, 1886) was an American politician who served as the 25th governor of New York and was the Democratic nominee in the disputed 1876 United States presidential election. A corporate lawyer who amassed a large fortune representing railroads, Tilden built his political reputation as a reformer who prosecuted the corrupt Tweed Ring of Tammany Hall and broke the Canal Ring as governor.

Early life and career

Tilden was born in New Lebanon, New York, and briefly attended Yale University before studying law. Admitted to the bar in 1841, he became one of New York City's most successful corporate attorneys. He served a single term in the New York State Assembly in 1846 and helped organize the "Barnburner" free-soil faction of the Democratic Party. During the Civil War he remained a War Democrat and a manager of the party's state organization.

Reformer and governor

As chairman of the New York Democratic Party, Tilden led the campaign against William M. "Boss" Tweed, obtaining the evidence that convicted Tweed of corruption. Elected governor in 1874, he attacked the Canal Ring, a bipartisan group profiting from state canal contracts, and cut state taxes. His record made him a national figure and secured him the Democratic nomination for president in 1876.

The election of 1876

Tilden won the popular vote over Republican Rutherford B. Hayes by roughly 250,000 votes and appeared to have won the Electoral College. Disputed returns from Florida, Louisiana and South Carolina left the result unresolved. A congressional Electoral Commission awarded all disputed electors to Hayes by an 8–7 vote, and the Compromise of 1877 — in which Republicans agreed to end Reconstruction — settled the crisis. Tilden accepted the outcome, declining to contest it further. He remains the only presidential candidate to win an outright majority of the popular vote (50.9 percent) yet lose the election.

Later life and legacy

Tilden declined the Democratic nomination in 1880 on health grounds. His estate funded the establishment of the New York Public Library through the Tilden Trust, combined with the Astor and Lenox collections. He died in 1886 at his estate, Greystone, in Yonkers.
