The Morgan dollar is a United States dollar coin minted from 1878 to 1904, in 1921, and as a commemorative issue since 2021. Named for its designer, Mint engraver George T. Morgan, it depicts a profile of Liberty modelled on Philadelphia schoolteacher Anna Willess Williams on the obverse and an American bald eagle on the reverse.

History

The coin was authorised by the Bland–Allison Act of 1878, passed over President Hayes's veto, which required the Treasury to buy millions of ounces of silver each month — a concession to Western mining interests after the demonetisation of silver in 1873, the so-called "Crime of '73". Morgan's design won out over that of Chief Engraver William Barber. Production continued under the Sherman Silver Purchase Act until its repeal in 1893 ended large-scale minting; striking ceased in 1904 as bullion stocks ran out. The Pittman Act of 1918 melted over 270 million silver dollars; in 1921 the Morgan design was struck for one final year before the Peace dollar replaced it.

Collecting

Morgan dollars are among the most collected US coins. Carson City mint issues, the rare 1893-S, and the error-laden early San Francisco issues command high prices, while common-date coins in mint condition remain affordable. In 2021 the US Mint issued Morgan dollars again for the design's centennial.
