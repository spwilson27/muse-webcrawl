The Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands (TTPI) was a United Nations trust territory in Micronesia administered by the United States from 1947 to 1994. Comprising the former German colonies seized by Japan in 1914 and governed by Japan as the South Seas Mandate under the League of Nations, the territory's islands were taken by US forces in 1944 during World War II. The UN Security Council ratified the trusteeship on 2 April 1947; uniquely, it was designated a "strategic" trust, overseen by the Security Council rather than the General Assembly.

Administration

The territory encompassed more than 2,100 islands scattered over some 3 million square miles of the western Pacific, though the land area totalled only about 700 square miles. It was administered from Saipan through six (later seven) districts: the Marianas, Marshall Islands, Palau, Ponape (Pohnpei), Truk (Chuuk), Yap, and Kosrae. The U.S. Navy administered it until 1951, when the Department of the Interior took over. A Congress of Micronesia was established in 1965 as a territory-wide legislature.

Dissolution

Beginning in the 1970s the districts chose separate political futures. The Northern Mariana Islands became a U.S. commonwealth in 1978 (fully implemented 1986). The Marshall Islands and the Federated States of Micronesia (Chuuk, Yap, Pohnpei, Kosrae) entered Compacts of Free Association with the United States in 1986, and the UN formally ended the trusteeship for them in 1990. The last district, Palau, became independent in free association on 1 October 1994, whereupon the trusteeship was terminated — Palau being the last UN trust territory to gain independence.
