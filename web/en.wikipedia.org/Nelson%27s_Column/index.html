Nelson's Column

Nelson's Column
Location: Trafalgar Square, London, England
Completed: 1843
Height: 51.6 m (169 ft) overall
Architect: William Railton
Statue by: Edward Hodges Baily

Nelson's Column is a monument in Trafalgar Square in the City of Westminster, Central London, built to commemorate Vice-Admiral Horatio Nelson, who died at the Battle of Trafalgar in 1805. The monument was constructed between 1840 and 1843 to a design by architect William Railton at a cost of £47,000 (equivalent to several million pounds today), funded largely by public subscription and donations, including from the Tsar of Russia.

Description

The monument consists of a fluted Corinthian column of Dartmoor granite — built by the Canal Granite Company — standing on a stepped granite base, surmounted by a 5.5 m (18 ft) statue of Nelson carved from Craigleith sandstone by sculptor Edward Hodges Baily. The column's capital is decorated with bronze acanthus leaves cast from captured French cannon. At the base, four bronze relief panels cast from captured guns depict Nelson's major victories: Cape St Vincent, the Nile, Copenhagen, and his death at Trafalgar. Four monumental bronze lions, designed by Sir Edwin Landseer, were added at the corners of the plinth in 1867.

History and cultural role

The column has been a focal point of celebration and protest for more than a century and a half, and it hosts London's official Christmas tree (a gift from Norway since 1947) and New Year celebrations each year. The statue famously had its ceremonial role in Admiralty tradition; sailors once dined on the column before its completion. Major cleaning and restoration took place in 2006 for the bicentenary of Trafalgar. Nelson's Column remains one of London's most recognizable landmarks.
