Amantine Lucile Aurore Dupin, Baroness Dudevant (1 July 1804 – 8 June 1876), best known by her pen name George Sand, was a French novelist, memoirist, and journalist. One of the most popular writers in Europe in her lifetime, she stood as a central figure of French Romanticism and was known for her unconventional life — wearing men's clothing, smoking cigars, and conducting celebrated love affairs.

Life

Raised largely at Nohant in Berry by her grandmother, Aurore Dupin married Baron Casimir Dudevant in 1822 and had two children before separating from him and moving to Paris in 1831. Her first independent novel, Indiana (1832), published under the name George Sand, protested the constraints of marriage and made her famous. She had relationships with the writers Alfred de Musset and Prosper Mérimée, and a long, complex partnership with Frédéric Chopin from 1838 to 1847, much of it spent at Nohant and in Majorca, chronicled in A Winter in Majorca.

Works

Sand wrote more than 70 novels, along with plays, essays, and a vast correspondence. Early works such as Valentine (1832) and Lélia (1833) treated love and social convention; her later "pastoral" novels — La Mare au diable (1846), François le Champi (1848), and La Petite Fadette (1849) — idealised peasant life in Berry and remain French classics. Her autobiography, Histoire de ma vie (1854–55), is a major document of nineteenth-century literary life.
