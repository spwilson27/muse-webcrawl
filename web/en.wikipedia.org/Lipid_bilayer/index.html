Lipid bilayer

The lipid bilayer is a thin polar membrane made of two layers of lipid molecules, mainly phospholipids, that forms a continuous barrier around all cells and many organelles.

Hydrophilic heads face the aqueous environments inside and outside the cell while hydrophobic tails point inward, making the bilayer selectively permeable: small nonpolar molecules pass freely, while ions and large polar molecules require transport proteins. The fluid mosaic model describes proteins embedded in this fluid two-dimensional structure.
