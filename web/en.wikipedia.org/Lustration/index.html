Lustration

Lustration
Meaning: vetting and exclusion of former regime collaborators from public office
Where applied: post-communist Central and Eastern Europe; Iraq; Ukraine (2014)

Lustration is the process of vetting public officials and barring from positions of responsibility those who collaborated with a previous authoritarian regime, particularly its secret police. The term derives from a Roman ritual of purification.

Post-communist Europe

After 1989, several Central and Eastern European states adopted lustration laws. Czechoslovakia passed a far-reaching law in 1991; Poland, Hungary, Lithuania and others adopted varying screening systems, often administered through institutes of national remembrance that manage secret-police files.

Criticism

Critics argue that lustration can rest on unreliable files, punish association rather than proven wrongdoing, and be abused for political revenge; supporters regard it as essential to democratic consolidation and public trust.

Other cases

Related processes include de-Ba'athification in Iraq after 2003 and Ukraine's 2014 lustration law directed at officials of the Yanukovych era.
