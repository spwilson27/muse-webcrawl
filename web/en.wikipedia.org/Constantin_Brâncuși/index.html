Constantin Brâncuși

Constantin Brâncuși (19 February 1876 – 16 March 1957) was a Romanian sculptor, painter and photographer who made his career in France. Considered a pioneer of modernism, one of the most influential sculptors of the 20th century, Brâncuși is often called the patriarch of modern sculpture. He is known for his abstract sculptures that evoke rather than replicate forms, such as Bird in Space, The Kiss, and Sleeping Muse.

Early life

Brâncuși grew up in the village of Hobița, Gorj, close to Târgu Jiu, in the Romanian region of Oltenia. He trained at the School of Arts and Crafts in Craiova and the National School of Fine Arts in Bucharest, before traveling largely on foot to Paris in 1904. He briefly worked in Auguste Rodin's studio but left, remarking that nothing grows beneath tall trees.

Work

Brâncuși's art emphasizes clean geometrical lines that balance forms inherent in his materials with the symbolic allusions of representational art. He sought the essence of things, reducing heads, birds, and fish to smooth, ovoid or streamlined shapes, and he insisted on carving directly in stone or wood ("direct carving"). His Bird in Space series, begun in 1923, became the subject of a famous 1927–28 United States customs trial, Brâncuși v. United States, which established that abstract works could legally be art.

Târgu Jiu ensemble

In 1938 he completed the monumental ensemble at Târgu Jiu, Romania, commemorating heroes of the First World War: the Table of Silence, the Gate of the Kiss, and the 29.3-metre Endless Column. His Paris studio was bequeathed to the French state and is reconstructed beside the Centre Pompidou.
