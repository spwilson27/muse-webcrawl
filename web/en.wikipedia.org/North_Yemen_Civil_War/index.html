North Yemen Civil War

[Image placeholder: North Yemen Civil War]

The North Yemen Civil War was fought in the Yemen Arab Republic (North Yemen) from 1962 to 1970 between republican forces backed by Egypt and royalist tribes loyal to the deposed Imam Muhammad al-Badr, supported by Saudi Arabia and Jordan.

Background

In September 1962, days after Imam Ahmad's death, army officers led by Abdullah al-Sallal proclaimed the Yemen Arab Republic. Al-Badr escaped to the northern highlands and rallied royalist tribes. Egypt's president Gamal Abdel Nasser committed tens of thousands of troops — eventually some 70,000 — to support the republic, while Saudi Arabia supplied the royalists.

Course of the war

The conflict became a proxy war sometimes called Nasser's Vietnam. Egyptian forces used heavy weaponry and, on occasion, chemical weapons against royalist villages. The royalist siege of Sanaa in 1967–68 failed. After Egypt withdrew its troops following the Six-Day War of 1967, negotiations led to a settlement in 1970 in which the republic survived and some royalists joined the government.
