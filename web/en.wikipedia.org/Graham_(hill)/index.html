Graham (hill)

A Graham is a hill in Scotland (and some definitions extend to England and Wales) with a height between 600 and 762 metres (2,000 and 2,500 feet) and a prominence of at least 150 metres. The list of Grahams was compiled by hill-list author Fiona Torbet (née Graham) and published in 1992, bridging the gap between the Corbetts (2,500–3,000 ft) and lower hills.

Elevation range: 600–762 m (2,000–2,500 ft)

Prominence: At least 150 m

Number: 219 (Scotland, original list)

Named after: Fiona Graham (Torbet)

The list

Fiona Graham published her list in The Great Outdoors magazine in 1992, covering Scottish hills of 2,000–2,500 feet with a drop of at least 150 metres on all sides. The original list contained 222 hills; subsequent resurveying reduced the number. In 2022, following a major re-survey by the Database of British and Irish Hills community and discussion with the Torbet family, the metric threshold was formally adopted (600 m) and the list revised. Hillwalkers who climb all the Grahams are known as "Grahamists".

Context among hill lists

The Grahams form part of a hierarchy of British hill classifications popular with peak baggers: Munros (over 3,000 ft in Scotland), Corbetts (2,500–3,000 ft), Grahams (600–762 m), and Marilyns (any hill with 150 m prominence). The Grahams are notable because the 150-metre prominence rule was applied consistently, a criterion that later influenced other lists.
