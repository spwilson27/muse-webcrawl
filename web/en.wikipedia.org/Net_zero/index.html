Net zero

Net zero refers to a state in which greenhouse gases going into the atmosphere are balanced by removal out of the atmosphere. Under the Paris Agreement, limiting warming to 1.5 °C requires global carbon dioxide emissions to reach net zero around 2050, with deep cuts in other greenhouse gases.

GoalBalance GHG emissions and removals

Target date (CO₂)Around 2050 for 1.5 °C

Definitions

Net zero differs from carbon neutrality in covering all greenhouse gases and emphasising deep reductions first, with residual emissions offset by removals such as reforestation or direct air capture. Standards such as the Science Based Targets initiative restrict reliance on offsets.

Pledges

Many countries, cities and companies have adopted net-zero targets, covering most global emissions, though policies often lag pledges — a gap criticised as greenwashing when plans lack interim targets or credible detail.

Pathways

Key measures include clean electricity, electrification of transport and heating, energy efficiency, methane cuts, and protecting carbon sinks.
