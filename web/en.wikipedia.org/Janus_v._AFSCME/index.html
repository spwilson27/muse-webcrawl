Janus v. AFSCME

Janus v. AFSCME, Council 31
DecidedJune 27, 2018
Citation585 U.S. 878 (2018)
HoldingCompulsory public-sector union agency fees violate the First Amendment
OverruledAbood v. Detroit Bd. of Ed. (1977)

Janus v. American Federation of State, County, and Municipal Employees, Council 31 is a landmark 2018 decision of the Supreme Court of the United States holding that public-sector unions may not charge "agency" or "fair share" fees to non-members whom they are required to represent. The 5–4 decision, written by Justice Samuel Alito, overruled Abood v. Detroit Board of Education (1977).

Background

Mark Janus, an Illinois state child-support specialist, objected to paying agency fees to AFSCME Council 31 on the ground that public-sector collective bargaining is inherently political speech, so compelled fees subsidised speech he opposed. Illinois law followed Abood in permitting such fees for non-members covered by a union contract.

Decision

The Court held that requiring public employees to subsidise a union's bargaining and advocacy violates the First Amendment's prohibition on compelled speech and association, and that Abood was wrongly decided. The ruling affected agency-fee laws in more than twenty states.

Reactions

Labour unions and their supporters warned of revenue losses and a "free rider" problem; supporters of the decision hailed it as restoring workers' constitutional rights. Public-sector union membership declined modestly following the decision but less than some forecasts predicted.
