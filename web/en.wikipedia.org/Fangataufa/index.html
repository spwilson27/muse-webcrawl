Fangataufa (or Fangatafoa) is a small, low island in the Tuamotu group of French Polynesia. Together with the nearby atoll of Moruroa, it was the site of French nuclear weapons testing between 1966 and 1996. The atoll is located about 40 kilometres southeast of Moruroa and roughly 1,200 kilometres east of Tahiti.

Nuclear testing

France conducted 193 nuclear tests in French Polynesia, split between Fangataufa and Moruroa. Fangataufa hosted some of the largest tests, including the first French thermonuclear (hydrogen bomb) test, code-named Canopus, on 24 August 1968, which yielded about 2.6 megatons. After France moved its tests underground in 1975, shafts were drilled into the basalt beneath the atolls. The final test series took place in 1995–96, and the test site was dismantled after France signed the Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty in 1996.

Geography

Fangataufa is a small atoll with a land area of about 5 square kilometres enclosing a lagoon. It has been uninhabited except for military personnel during the testing era, and access remains restricted by the French military.
