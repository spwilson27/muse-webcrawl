Congo Basin

The Congo Basin is the sedimentary basin of the Congo River in west equatorial Africa. It contains the second largest rainforest in the world after the Amazon, covering roughly 3.7 million square kilometres across six countries: Cameroon, the Central African Republic, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, the Republic of the Congo, Equatorial Guinea and Gabon.

Geography

The basin is a vast, shallow depression ringed by plateaus and mountain ranges, including the Mitumba Mountains to the east. The Congo River and its tributaries — among them the Ubangi, Sangha, Kasai and Aruwimi — drain the basin westward to the Atlantic Ocean. Much of the interior is swamp forest and seasonally flooded forest, and the region is one of the least deforested large tropical forest blocks on Earth.

Biodiversity

The basin is home to exceptional biodiversity, including African forest elephants, lowland gorillas, chimpanzees, bonobos, okapis and forest buffalo. More than 10,000 plant species and around 400 mammal species have been recorded. Peatlands in the central basin, mapped in detail in the 2010s, store an estimated 29 billion tonnes of carbon.

Climate and threats

The climate is hot and humid year-round, with two rainy seasons near the equator. Threats include commercial logging, mining, bushmeat hunting and expanding agriculture, though deforestation rates remain lower than in the Amazon or Southeast Asia. The basin's forests are a major regulator of rainfall across central Africa and a globally significant carbon sink.
