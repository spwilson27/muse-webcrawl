Lord Rayleigh

John William Strutt, 3rd Baron Rayleigh (12 November 1842 – 30 June 1919) was an English physicist who, with William Ramsay, discovered the element argon, for which he received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1904. He made fundamental contributions to optics, acoustics, and fluid dynamics.

Work

Rayleigh explained why the sky is blue through the phenomenon now called Rayleigh scattering — the preferential scattering of short-wavelength light by particles much smaller than its wavelength. His precise measurements of gas densities revealed that nitrogen extracted from air was denser than nitrogen prepared chemically, leading to the discovery of argon in 1894. His two-volume Theory of Sound (1877–78) remains a classic of acoustics, and his work on surface waves produced the concept of Rayleigh waves, the ground-roll vibrations that travel through solids during earthquakes. The rayleigh unit of photon flux, the Rayleigh criterion for optical resolution, and Rayleigh flow in fluid dynamics are among the many concepts bearing his name.

Career

Educated at Trinity College, Cambridge, Rayleigh succeeded James Clerk Maxwell as Cavendish Professor of Physics in 1879, served as president of the Royal Society (1905–1908), and became chancellor of the University of Cambridge.
