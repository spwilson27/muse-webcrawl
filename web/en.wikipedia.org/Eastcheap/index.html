Eastcheap

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Eastcheap is a street in the City of London, running from the junction of Gracechurch Street and Fenchurch Street westward to Monument station and King William Street. It lies within the wards of Billingsgate and Bridge, close to London Bridge and the Monument to the Great Fire of London.

History

The name derives from the Old English ceap, meaning a market: Eastcheap was the eastern of two principal markets of medieval London, the other being Westcheap, now Cheapside. In the Middle Ages the street was lined with butchers' shops and taverns; the Boar's Head Inn on Eastcheap features in Shakespeare's Henry IV plays as the haunt of Falstaff and Prince Hal. The street was rebuilt after the Great Fire of 1666.

Notable buildings

At 16 Eastcheap stands a notable Victorian Gothic building of 1862, originally a spice warehouse, remarkable for its sculpted camel and crocodile motifs referencing the trades of its first occupants. Number 33–35 Eastcheap, known as "The Jampot", is an early work in the City associated with the architect Robert Lewis Roumieu, with a strikingly polychromatic façade. The street also contains the church of St Margaret Pattens at its eastern end and St Mary-at-Hill nearby.
