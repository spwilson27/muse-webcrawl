Avon Gorge

LocationBristol, England
RiverRiver Avon
Lengthabout 1.5 miles (2.5 km)
Spanned byClifton Suspension Bridge
DesignationSite of Special Scientific Interest

The Avon Gorge is a 1.5-mile (2.5 km) long gorge on the River Avon in Bristol, England. The gorge runs south to north through a limestone ridge 1.5 miles (2.4 km) west of Bristol city centre, and forms the boundary between the unitary authorities of North Somerset and Bristol.

Features

The gorge is spanned by Isambard Kingdom Brunel's Clifton Suspension Bridge, completed in 1864 and now an emblem of the city. On the Leigh Woods side, the gorge is flanked by a National Nature Reserve managed by the National Trust, while the Portway road and the Severn Beach railway line run along its eastern bank.

Ecology

The gorge's steep limestone cliffs and woodlands support rare plants, including endemic whitebeams found nowhere else, and nesting peregrine falcons. Its biological and geological importance is recognised by Site of Special Scientific Interest status covering some 155 hectares.

Origin

Geologists debate the gorge's origin; a leading theory holds that it was cut by glacial meltwater during the Ice Ages when the Avon's normal course was blocked.
