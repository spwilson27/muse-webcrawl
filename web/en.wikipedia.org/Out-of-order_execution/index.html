Out-of-order execution

Out-of-order execution is a technique used in high-performance central processing units in which instructions are executed in an order determined by the availability of input data and execution units, rather than strictly in the order they appear in the program.

Principles

Instructions are fetched and decoded in order, placed in reservation stations or a reorder buffer, and dispatched to execution units as their operands become ready. Results are retired in program order to preserve precise exceptions and the illusion of sequential execution. The approach was pioneered by the CDC 6600's scoreboard (1964) and developed in the IBM System/360 Model 91 with Tomasulo's algorithm (1967).

Modern processors

Out-of-order execution, combined with register renaming, branch prediction, and speculative execution, is standard in desktop and server CPUs; its speculation mechanisms were shown in 2018 to enable the Spectre class of side-channel attacks.
