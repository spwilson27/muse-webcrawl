Operation Cartwheel

Operation Cartwheel
Date: 30 June 1943 – 1944
Location: South West Pacific and Pacific Ocean Areas
Result: Allied victory; Rabaul isolated

Operation Cartwheel (1943–1944) was a major military operation for the Allies in the Pacific theatre of World War II. Cartwheel was an operation aimed at neutralising the major Japanese base at Rabaul on the island of New Britain. The operation was directed by the Supreme Allied Commander in the South West Pacific Area (SWPA), General Douglas MacArthur, whose forces advanced along the northeast coast of New Guinea and occupied nearby islands. Allied forces in the Pacific Ocean Areas, under Admiral Chester W. Nimitz, advanced through the Solomon Islands toward Bougainville.

Strategy

Cartwheel combined thirteen subordinate operations in a twin-pronged advance designed to cut off and isolate Rabaul rather than capture it, consistent with the Allied strategy of "island hopping" past strong points. The Japanese base, garrisoned by some 100,000 troops, was bombed into impotence and bypassed, with its garrison left to "wither on the vine" until Japan's surrender in 1945.

Course

Major steps included the landings at Woodlark and Kiriwina, the capture of Lae and Salamaua, the advance through the Huon Peninsula, the landings on New Britain at Arawe and Cape Gloucester, the seizure of the Admiralty Islands, and the landings at Hollandia. By mid-1944 Rabaul was completely isolated and the operation's objectives were achieved.
