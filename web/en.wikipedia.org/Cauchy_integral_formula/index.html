Cauchy integral formula

In mathematics, Cauchy's integral formula, named after Augustin-Louis Cauchy, is a central statement in complex analysis. It expresses the fact that a holomorphic function defined on a disc is completely determined by its values on the boundary of the disc, and it provides integral formulas for all derivatives of the function.

f(a) = (1/2πi) ∮C f(z)/(z − a) dz

Here C is a positively oriented, simple closed contour enclosing the point a, and f is holomorphic on and inside C.

Consequences

The formula implies that holomorphic functions are infinitely differentiable and analytic — each derivative is likewise given by a contour integral — which yields Cauchy's estimates on derivatives, Liouville's theorem (a bounded entire function is constant), and a proof of the fundamental theorem of algebra.

Generalisation

The formula extends to the Cauchy–Pompeiu formula for smooth (not necessarily holomorphic) functions, and to several complex variables via the Bochner–Martinelli formula. It is closely related to the residue theorem, of which it is a special case.
