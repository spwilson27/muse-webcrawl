Piero Dusio

Piero Dusio (13 October 1899 – 7 November 1975) was an Italian footballer, racing driver, and businessman, best known as the founder of the Cisitalia automobile company. As a young man he played football for Juventus, making a handful of appearances before an injury ended his playing career.

Cisitalia

Dusio built a textile fortune and in 1944 founded Cisitalia in Turin. Its single-seater D46, designed by Dante Giacosa using Fiat components, was a success in postwar voiturette racing. The Cisitalia 202 coupé of 1947, styled by Pinin Farina, became a landmark of automotive design; an example is held by the Museum of Modern Art in New York.

Later years

Ambitious projects, including a four-wheel-drive Grand Prix car designed with Ferdinand Porsche's bureau, overextended the company, and Cisitalia fell into financial difficulty. Dusio moved to Argentina in the early 1950s, where he pursued automotive ventures backed by the Perón government. He died in Buenos Aires in 1975.
