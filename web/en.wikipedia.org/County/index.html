A county is a geographic region of a country used for administrative or other purposes in some nations. The term derives from the Old French conté, denoting the jurisdiction of a count. In England the equivalent unit, the shire, dates to the Anglo-Saxon period; counties remain ceremonial and, in modified form, administrative units in the United Kingdom and Ireland.

United States

In the United States, counties are the primary administrative divisions of most states: 3,143 counties and county equivalents, including parishes in Louisiana and boroughs in Alaska. County governments typically maintain courts, sheriffs, jails, land records, and roads, and their powers vary widely by state. The most populous is Los Angeles County, California, with about ten million residents; the largest by area is San Bernardino County, California.

Elsewhere

Canada, Australia, New Zealand, and several European and Asian countries use counties or county-level divisions (such as China's xian) for local administration and statistics, though their functions differ greatly from country to country.
