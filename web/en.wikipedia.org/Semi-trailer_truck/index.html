Semi-trailer truck

A semi-trailer truck, also known as a semi-truck, tractor-trailer or articulated lorry, is a combination vehicle consisting of a powered tractor unit towing one or more semi-trailers. The semi-trailer has no front axle; much of its weight is supported by the tractor through a fifth-wheel coupling rather than by its own wheels alone — hence "semi".

Design and use

The tractor couples to the trailer's kingpin on a pivoting fifth wheel, allowing articulation in turns. Trailer types include box (dry van), refrigerated, flatbed, tanker and car carrier bodies. In North America a typical combination is about 21 metres (70 ft) long with a gross weight up to 36,300 kg (80,000 lb). Longer and heavier combinations, such as Australian road trains and European modular combinations, operate under special rules in some jurisdictions.

Economic role

Semi-trailer trucks carry the majority of overland freight by value in most developed economies and underpin just-in-time supply chains. The industry faces recurring driver shortages, tightening emissions rules and experiments with battery-electric and hydrogen tractors and with automated driving for highway haulage.
