Krampus

Krampus
RegionAlpine folklore: Austria, southern Germany, northern Italy, Slovenia, Hungary
FeastKrampusnacht, 5 December (eve of St Nicholas Day)
RoleHorned companion of Saint Nicholas who punishes naughty children

Krampus is a horned, anthropomorphic figure in the folklore of Alpine and Central European countries, described as half-goat and half-demon, who accompanies Saint Nicholas and punishes children who have misbehaved — in contrast to Nicholas, who rewards the well-behaved with gifts.

Description and customs

Krampus is usually depicted with shaggy fur, goat horns, a long pointed tongue, fangs, cloven hooves, chains and bells, and a bundle of birch branches (ruten) used to swat children; in some tales he carries a basket to cart away especially wicked children. On Krampusnacht, 5 December, men dressed as Krampus take part in parades known as Krampuslauf ("Krampus run"), particularly in Austria and Bavaria, sometimes chasing spectators. Fearsome greeting cards called Krampuskarten were exchanged from the 19th century.

Origins

The figure's roots are debated: scholars have linked Krampus to pre-Christian Alpine traditions and pagan winter rituals, while the name derives from the German krampen, "claw". The tradition was periodically suppressed — by the Catholic Church, and in Austria under Austrofascism in the 1930s — but revived strongly after the Second World War.

Modern popularity

Krampus has spread into global popular culture, appearing in films (including the 2015 horror-comedy Krampus), television and festivals in North America, where Krampus runs are now held in many cities each December.
