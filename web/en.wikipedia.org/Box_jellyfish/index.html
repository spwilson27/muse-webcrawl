Box jellyfish

Class: Cubozoa

Phylum: Cnidaria

Species: about 50 described

Distribution: tropical and subtropical Indo-Pacific and Atlantic waters

Box jellyfish (class Cubozoa) are cnidarian invertebrates distinguished by their cube-shaped medusae. They possess a remarkably well-developed sensory and nervous system for jellyfish, including 24 eyes — some with lenses, retinas, and corneas — arranged on four rhopalia, and they are strong, active swimmers capable of directed movement rather than mere drifting.

Venom and danger to humans

Several species are among the most venomous marine animals known. The Australian box jellyfish Chironex fleckeri can deliver an extremely painful sting that may cause cardiac arrest and death within minutes; other dangerous species include the Irukandji jellyfish (Carukia barnesi and relatives), whose tiny medusae cause Irukandji syndrome. Stinger nets, protective suits, and vinegar treatment of stings are used in northern Australia, where fatalities have been documented since the late 19th century.

Biology

Like other jellyfish, cubozoans alternate between polyp and medusa stages; their polyps can bud off directly into new polyps and metamorphose wholly into a single medusa. Box jellyfish feed on small fish and crustaceans, stunned by nematocysts on their trailing tentacles, which in Chironex may reach three metres long.
