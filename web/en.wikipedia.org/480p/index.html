480p

480p is the shorthand name for a family of video display resolutions. The p stands for progressive scan, i.e. non-interlaced. The 480 denotes a vertical resolution of 480 pixels, usually with a horizontal resolution of 640 pixels and a 4:3 aspect ratio (480 × 4⁄3 = 640) or a horizontal resolution of 854 pixels (or thereabouts) and a 16:9 aspect ratio (480 × 16⁄9 ≈ 854). Since the pixel count is vertically aligned a frame height of 480 pixels in progressive scan gives the resolution its name.

480p was used for many early plasma televisions and for the standard-definition television (SDTV) formats. Standard-definition digital television broadcasts in both the United States (ATSC) and in other countries have widely used 480p, and it is the native resolution of the DVD-Video format when played back with progressive scan.

480p24, 480p30 and 480p60

Common frame rates for 480p include 24, 30 and 60 frames per second. 480p24 is often used for film-based content converted with a progressive transfer, while 480p60 was promoted in the late 1990s and early 2000s as "enhanced-definition television" (EDTV), offering smoother motion than interlaced 480i.
