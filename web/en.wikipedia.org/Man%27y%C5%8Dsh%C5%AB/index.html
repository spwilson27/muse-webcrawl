Man'yōshū

Compiledc. 759 CE
LanguageOld Japanese
Contents20 volumes, about 4,500 poems

The Man'yōshū (万葉集, "Collection of Ten Thousand Leaves") is the oldest existing collection of Japanese poetry (waka), compiled sometime after 759 CE during the Nara period. It contains about 4,500 poems, some dating back to the 4th or 5th centuries, written by emperors, nobles, frontier guards, and commoners.

Script

The poems are written in man'yōgana, an early system using Chinese characters for their phonetic values to write Japanese — an important source for the historical study of the Japanese language and its dialects.

Significance

The Man'yōshū is regarded as one of the foundational works of Japanese literature; its name was chosen in 2019 as the source of the Japanese era name Reiwa, taken from a poem in the collection about plum blossoms.
