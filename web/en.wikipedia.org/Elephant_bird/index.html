Elephant bird

Elephant birds are extinct flightless birds belonging to the family Aepyornithidae, which were native to Madagascar. They are thought to have become extinct around 1000–1200 CE, probably as a result of human activity. Elephant birds comprised the genera Mullerornis, Vorombe, and Aepyornis.

Aepyornis maximus is commonly thought to have been the largest known bird, but a 2018 study found that Vorombe titan reached weights of up to 732 kilograms (1,614 lb) and was the largest bird ever to have lived. The bones of elephant birds are still found in Madagascar, and their enormous eggs — the largest single cells known — remain remarkably intact in some cases; a single egg was equivalent in volume to roughly 150 chicken eggs.

Habitat and behaviour

Elephant birds lived across Madagascar, with different species adapted to the island's varied environments, from rainforest to arid scrubland. They were herbivorous, probably feeding on fruits, leaves, and seeds, and they likely played a role in seed dispersal for Malagasy plants.

Extinction

The birds survived until around a thousand years ago. Proposed causes of their extinction include hunting by humans, habitat destruction by slash-and-burn agriculture, and disease introduced by domestic fowl. The extinction of the elephant birds forms part of the broader late Holocene extinction of Madagascar's megafauna following human settlement of the island roughly 2,000 years ago.

The elephant bird is often cited as a possible basis for the legend of the roc, the giant bird of Arabian and Persian folklore described by Marco Polo.
