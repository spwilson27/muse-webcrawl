The House of Braganza is a Portuguese noble dynasty that supplied the kings of Portugal from 1640 to 1910 and the emperors of Brazil from 1822 to 1889. It originated as a cadet line of the House of Aviz, founded by Afonso, the first Duke of Braganza, in 1442, and became one of the richest and most powerful noble houses in Europe.

Monarchy in Portugal

In 1640, amid revolt against Spanish Habsburg rule, the Duke of Braganza was acclaimed King John IV, beginning the Portuguese Restoration War. His descendants ruled through the era of Brazilian gold, the 1755 Lisbon earthquake under the Marquis of Pombal's ministry, the Peninsular War — when the court fled to Rio de Janeiro in 1808 — and 19th-century liberal civil wars between Miguelists and constitutionalists.

Brazilian branch

Prince Pedro, heir to the Portuguese throne, declared Brazilian independence in 1822 and became Emperor Pedro I of Brazil. His son Pedro II reigned until the republic was proclaimed in 1889. In Portugal, the dynasty (in its Braganza-Saxe-Coburg and Gotha line) ended with Manuel II after the 1910 revolution. Duarte Pio, Duke of Braganza, is the current head of the house and pretender to the Portuguese throne.
