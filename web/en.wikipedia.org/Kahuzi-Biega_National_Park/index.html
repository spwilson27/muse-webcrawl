Kahuzi-Biega National Park is a protected area near Bukavu in eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo, in South Kivu province near the western shore of Lake Kivu and the Rwandan border. It takes its name from two extinct volcanoes, Mount Kahuzi (3,308 m) and Mount Biega (2,790 m), and covers about 6,000 square kilometres of mountain and lowland rainforest.

Wildlife

The park is one of the last refuges of the eastern lowland gorilla (Grauer's gorilla), the largest gorilla subspecies, and was central to the early study and habituation of the species by the Belgian conservationist Adrien Deschryver, who helped establish the park in 1970. Its forests also hold eastern chimpanzees, forest elephants, bongo, owl-faced monkeys, and a rich endemic bird fauna; the Albertine Rift setting makes it one of Africa's most biodiverse protected areas, with habitats ranging from swamp and bamboo forest to subalpine heath.

Conservation challenges

Kahuzi-Biega was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1980 and placed on the List of World Heritage in Danger in 1997. The Congo wars brought refugee camps, militia occupation, mining for coltan and other minerals, poaching, and deforestation into the park; surveys estimated that the highland gorilla population fell by roughly half during the conflict years. Park rangers have been killed defending the area, and conservation groups continue to work with local communities, including the Indigenous Batwa, whose eviction during the park's expansion remains a subject of controversy and redress efforts.
