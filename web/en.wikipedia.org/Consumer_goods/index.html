Consumer goods

Consumer goods are products bought for consumption by the average consumer, as opposed to capital goods used in the production of other goods. They are the end result of production and manufacturing, and are what a consumer sees on store shelves.

Classification

Consumer goods are commonly divided into durable goods (such as cars, appliances, and furniture, which last for years), nondurable goods (such as food, fuel, and toiletries, consumed quickly), and services. Marketing classifies them further into convenience goods (bought frequently with little deliberation), shopping goods, specialty goods, and unsought goods. Fast-moving consumer goods (FMCG) are nondurables sold quickly at relatively low cost.

Economics

Spending on consumer goods is the largest component of GDP in most developed economies, and measures such as consumer confidence indices track households' willingness to buy them. Regulation of consumer goods focuses on safety, labeling, and protection against fraud.
