Laughter

Laughter is a physical and vocal reaction — typically rhythmic contractions of the diaphragm accompanied by characteristic sounds — usually expressing amusement, joy or mirth. It is found in humans and, in analogous forms, in other primates and social animals.

Psychology and physiology

Laughter is largely involuntary and socially contagious; people are far more likely to laugh in company than alone. It is triggered by humour, tickling, surprise and social bonding, and is associated with reduced stress hormones and release of endorphins. The study of laughter is called gelotology.

Social function

Laughter signals friendliness, defuses tension and strengthens group cohesion. Pathological laughter can occur with certain neurological conditions.
