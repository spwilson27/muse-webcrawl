Kuno National Park is a national park and wildlife sanctuary in Madhya Pradesh, India. It derives its name from the Kuno River, a tributary of the Chambal. It was initially established as a wildlife sanctuary in 1981 and received national park status in 2018.

History

The area was originally selected as the second home for the Asiatic lion, to establish a population separate from Gujarat's Gir Forest; despite a Supreme Court order in 2013 directing translocation, the lions were never moved. Instead, Kuno became the site of Project Cheetah: on 17 September 2022, eight African cheetahs from Namibia were released into the park, followed by twelve from South Africa in February 2023, marking the return of the cheetah to India some 70 years after the Asiatic cheetah was declared extinct there in 1952. The project has seen both births of cubs in India and the deaths of several introduced adults and cubs, prompting debate among conservationists.

Flora and fauna

The park's dry deciduous forest, dominated by kardhai, salai and khair trees, supports leopards, striped hyenas, Indian wolves, sloth bears, chital, sambar, nilgai and four-horned antelope, along with more than 120 bird species.
