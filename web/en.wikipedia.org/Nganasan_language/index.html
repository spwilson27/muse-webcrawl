Nganasan language

Nganasan is a Samoyedic language of the Uralic family spoken by the Nganasan people of the Taymyr Peninsula in Siberia, Russia. It is the northernmost of the Samoyedic languages and is severely endangered, with only a small and ageing population of fluent speakers.

Features

Nganasan is an agglutinative language with rich consonant gradation and a complex system of vowel harmony. Unlike most Samoyedic languages it developed a distinctive set of phonological alternations. It has two main dialect groupings, Avam and Vadeyev.

Status

Intergenerational transmission largely ceased during the Soviet period as Nganasan communities were settled in villages such as Ust-Avam and Volochanka and Russian became dominant. Documentation projects and community teaching efforts seek to record and revive the language, but UNESCO classifies it as severely endangered.
