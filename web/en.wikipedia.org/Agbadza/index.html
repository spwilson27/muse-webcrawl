Agbadza


Ewe musicians performing Agbadza drumming

Agbadza is an Ewe music and war dance of the Ewe people of Ghana, Togo and Benin, originating from the very ancient war dance called Atrikpui and considered one of the oldest musical forms of the Ewe. It is performed mainly at funerals and other social occasions, and has become one of the most recognised recreational dances of the Ewe.

Performance

An Agbadza ensemble is built around a lead drum (atsimevu), supporting drums including the sogo and kidi, the gankeke double bell, the axatse gourd rattle, and handclapping, accompanied by call-and-response singing. The music is organised around a recurring bell pattern in compound time. Dancers perform in pairs or individually in the centre of a circle, with characteristic movements of the arms and torso imitating the flight of birds — a reference to ancestral and migration traditions.
