Interpersonal relationship

An interpersonal relationship is a social association, connection, or affiliation between two or more persons. They vary in level of intimacy and sharing, and may be centred on something(s) in common. Interpersonal relationships are formed in the context of social, cultural and other influences. The study of interpersonal relationships involves several branches of the social sciences, including such disciplines as communication studies, psychology, anthropology, social work, sociology, and mathematics.

Types

Relationships include kinship relations such as family and marriage, friendships, work and professional relationships, romantic relationships, and relationships with communities, clubs, and neighbours. They may be regulated by law, custom, or mutual agreement, and form a basic building block of social groups and society as a whole.

Development

Relationships typically proceed through stages. Social penetration theory describes how relationships develop through gradual self-disclosure, from superficial exchanges to intimate sharing. Attraction is influenced by proximity, similarity, physical attractiveness, and reciprocal liking.

Relationships may be maintained through communication, shared activities, and mutual support, or may deteriorate through conflict, neglect, or betrayal. Research by John Gottman identified patterns such as criticism, contempt, defensiveness, and stonewalling as predictors of relationship dissolution.

Flourishing relationships

Positive psychology research links close relationships to health, happiness, and longevity. Emotional intimacy, trust, responsiveness, and shared meaning are associated with satisfaction, while loneliness is associated with numerous adverse health outcomes.
