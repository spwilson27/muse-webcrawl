Honda Super Cub

The Honda Super Cub is a Honda underbone motorcycle with a four-stroke single-cylinder engine ranging in displacement from 49 to 124 cc. In production since 1958, cumulative worldwide production surpassed 100 million units in 2017, making it the most produced motor vehicle in history.

Development

Conceived by Soichiro Honda and Takeo Fujisawa after a 1956 tour of Europe, the Super Cub combined the step-through convenience of a scooter with the durability of a motorcycle. Its pressed-steel monocoque frame, leg shield, enclosed chain, and automatic centrifugal clutch made it easy to ride one-handed — a feature aimed at delivery riders such as soba noodle couriers in Japan.

"You meet the nicest people on a Honda"

Honda's 1963 American advertising campaign, created by Grey Advertising, deliberately distanced the friendly little Cub from outlaw biker imagery and is credited with transforming the image of motorcycling in the United States. The Beach Boys' song "Little Honda" grew out of the campaign.

Legacy

The Super Cub remains in production in modernised forms, including the C125, and is a cultural icon across Asia, where variants serve as family transport, taxis, and delivery vehicles.
