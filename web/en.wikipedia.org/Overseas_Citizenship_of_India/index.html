Overseas Citizenship of India

Overseas Citizen of India

Issued by: Government of India

Established: 2005 (Citizenship (Amendment) Act, 2003)

Status: Permanent residency-style immigration status

Overseas Citizenship of India (OCI) is an immigration status authorised by the Citizenship Act, 1955, permitting a foreign citizen of Indian origin to live and work in India indefinitely. Despite its name, OCI is not citizenship: India does not permit dual citizenship, and OCI cardholders have no political rights — they cannot vote, stand for election, or hold constitutional office in India. The status was introduced in response to persistent demands from the Indian diaspora for something comparable to dual citizenship.

History

The Citizenship (Amendment) Act of 2003 inserted provisions for Overseas Citizenship into the Citizenship Act 1955, and the scheme was formally launched in 2005. A predecessor scheme, the Person of Indian Origin (PIO) Card, offered lesser benefits; in 2015 the PIO scheme was merged into OCI, and all PIO cardholders were deemed OCI cardholders. Registrations grew steadily, numbering in the millions within a decade, with the largest populations in the United States, the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia and the Gulf states.

Eligibility

A foreign national is eligible if they were eligible to become a citizen of India on 26 January 1950, were a citizen of India on or after that date, or belonged to a territory that became part of India after 15 August 1947; their children, grandchildren and great-grandchildren, and certain spouses, may also qualify. Citizens of Pakistan and Bangladesh — and those whose parents or grandparents were citizens of those countries — are ineligible, as are serving or retired foreign military personnel in most cases.

Rights and restrictions

OCI cardholders receive a lifelong, multiple-entry visa for India and exemption from reporting to police authorities. They enjoy parity with Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) in most economic, financial and educational matters, including admission quotas and domestic airfares. However, they cannot purchase agricultural or plantation land, adopt certain benefits, or work in restricted government roles without special permission, and they require permits to visit protected and restricted areas and to undertake research, missionary work, mountaineering or journalism in India. OCI status can be cancelled on grounds including fraud, disaffection toward the Constitution, or imprisonment for two years or more.

OCI cardholder document

Registered OCI holders receive a card booklet resembling a passport (in maroon after a 2015 redesign) together with a lifelong visa stamp (the "U" visa sticker) in their foreign passport. Rules require the card to be reissued at certain ages, and mismatched passport numbers have been a recurring practical difficulty for holders, prompting periodic relaxations.
