Longtermism

Longtermism
Key figuresNick Bostrom, Toby Ord, William MacAskill, Hilary Greaves
Key worksThe Precipice (2020); What We Owe the Future (2022)
Associated withEffective altruism

Longtermism is an ethical view that positively influencing the long-term future — over centuries, millennia or longer — is a key moral priority of the present generation. Its proponents argue that because the number of people who could exist in the future may be enormous, the expected value of safeguarding humanity's long-term potential, especially by reducing existential risks, could outweigh other moral considerations.

Ideas

The view draws on work by philosophers including Derek Parfit and Nick Bostrom, whose writings on existential risk and the astronomical value of the future laid early foundations. Toby Ord's The Precipice (2020) and William MacAskill's What We Owe the Future (2022) popularised strong longtermism: the claim that long-run effects dominate the moral assessment of our actions.

Criticism

Critics argue that longtermism can neglect pressing present-day harms, that predictions over very long timescales are unreliable, and that aggregating over vast hypothetical future populations leads to implausible conclusions. Supporters respond that concern for the future need not exclude duties to people alive today.
