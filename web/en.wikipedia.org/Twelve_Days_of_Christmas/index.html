Twelve Days of Christmas

The Twelve Days of Christmas, also known as Twelvetide, is a festive Christian season celebrating the Nativity of Jesus. In many Western Church traditions, the twelve days begin on Christmas Day (25 December) and run to the evening of 5 January, known as Twelfth Night, which precedes the feast of Epiphany on 6 January. In other traditions the count begins on Boxing Day (26 December) and ends on 6 January.

Observance

Historically, Twelvetide was a season of feasting, mumming, wassailing and celebrations, with Twelfth Night a particular occasion for revelry, cake and the crowning of a mock king or queen. The season contrasts with the penitential period of Advent that precedes it.

The carol

The period is best known today through the English Christmas carol "The Twelve Days of Christmas", a cumulative song first published in the late eighteenth century, in which a lover gives increasingly grand gifts on each day: a partridge in a pear tree, two turtle doves, three French hens, and so on up to twelve drummers drumming. Counting all repetitions, the song's gifts total 364 — nearly one for each day of the year.
