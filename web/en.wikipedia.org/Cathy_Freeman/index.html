Cathy Freeman

Cathy Freeman
Born: February 16, 1973, Mackay, Queensland
Sport: Track and field (sprints)
Honours: Olympic gold, 400 m, Sydney 2000; World champion 1997, 1999

Catherine Astrid Salome Freeman (born 16 February 1973) is an Australian former sprinter who specialised in the 400 metres. An Aboriginal Australian of the Kuku Yalanji people, she became a national symbol when she won the 400 m gold medal at the 2000 Sydney Olympics, having lit the Olympic flame at the opening ceremony.

Career

Freeman first gained attention at the 1990 Commonwealth Games, where at 16 she won gold in the 4 × 100 m relay. At the 1994 Commonwealth Games she celebrated her 200 m and 400 m victories carrying both the Australian and Aboriginal flags, an act that drew public debate. She won World Championship titles in 1997 and 1999, and silver at the 1996 Atlanta Olympics, before her defining Sydney victory in a personal best of 49.11 seconds. She retired in 2003.

Legacy

Freeman's Sydney win, watched by a stadium crowd of over 110,000 and a vast television audience, is remembered as a moment of national unity and a landmark for Indigenous Australians. She later founded the Cathy Freeman Foundation to support education for Indigenous children.
