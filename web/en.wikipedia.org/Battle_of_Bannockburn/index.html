Battle of Bannockburn

The Battle of Bannockburn was fought on 23–24 June 1314 between the army of King Robert the Bruce of Scotland and the army of King Edward II of England during the First War of Scottish Independence. It was one of the most significant Scottish victories of the war and a landmark in Scottish history.

Background

Edward Bruce, the king's brother, had besieged Stirling Castle, whose English governor agreed to surrender if not relieved by midsummer. Edward II marched north with a large army — perhaps 13,000–25,000 men, including a powerful force of heavy cavalry — to relieve the castle. Robert the Bruce, with perhaps 6,000–8,000 men, prepared positions south of Stirling, near the Bannock Burn, choosing ground that restricted cavalry.

The battle

On 23 June, English cavalry attempts to reach the castle and to outflank the Scots were repulsed; in a famous episode, Bruce himself killed the English knight Henry de Bohun in single combat. On 24 June the Scottish schiltrons — dense formations of spearmen — advanced downhill and drove the English army back into the boggy ground between the Bannock Burn and the Pelstream Burn, where it disintegrated. Edward II fled the field and barely escaped capture.

Aftermath

Stirling Castle surrendered, Scotland's independence was effectively secured on the battlefield, and Bruce's position as king was consolidated, though England did not formally recognise Scottish independence until the Treaty of Edinburgh–Northampton in 1328.
