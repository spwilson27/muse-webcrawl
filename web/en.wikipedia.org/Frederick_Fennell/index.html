Frederick Fennell

Frederick Fennell (July 2, 1914 – December 7, 2004) was an American conductor and one of the primary figures in promoting the wind ensemble as a serious artistic medium. In 1952, at the Eastman School of Music, he founded the Eastman Wind Ensemble, whose performances and recordings transformed the concert band world.

Career

Fennell studied at the Eastman School, where he became a faculty member and conductor. His concept of the wind ensemble — a flexible group with one player per part, modelled on the wind sections of the orchestra — contrasted with the large massed concert band. His Mercury Records "Living Presence" recordings with the Eastman Wind Ensemble, beginning in the 1950s, are considered landmarks for their repertoire and sound quality.

Later work

Fennell later conducted the Tokyo Kosei Wind Orchestra, whose recordings became influential worldwide, and guest-conducted widely, championing original works for winds by composers such as Percy Grainger, Gustav Holst, Vincent Persichetti and Karel Husa. He died in 2004 at age 90.
