The Lieber Code, formally General Orders No. 100, "Instructions for the Government of Armies of the United States in the Field," was a military code issued by President Abraham Lincoln on April 24, 1863, during the American Civil War. It was drafted principally by the legal scholar Francis Lieber.

Contents

The code of 157 articles governed the conduct of Union soldiers, addressing martial law, military necessity, the treatment of prisoners of war, partisans and spies, flags of truce, and the prohibition of torture and cruelty. It declared that military necessity permits measures indispensable to securing the ends of war but excludes acts that make peace unnecessarily difficult, and it recognized that enslaved people who came under Union protection became free.

Influence

The Lieber Code was the first modern codification of the laws of war and profoundly influenced later international humanitarian law, including the Hague Conventions of 1899 and 1907 and the Geneva Conventions. Its provisions were drawn upon in the trials following the Second World War and remain a foundational text in military legal education.
