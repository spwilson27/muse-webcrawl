microATX (sometimes stylised µATX, mATX) is a motherboard form factor standard introduced by Intel in December 1997. With a maximum size of 244 mm × 244 mm (9.6 in × 9.6 in), it is a smaller variant of the ATX form factor intended for lower-cost, compact desktop computers while retaining compatibility with ATX mounting points and power supplies.

Design

MicroATX preserves a subset of the ATX mounting hole positions, so microATX boards fit in full-size ATX cases, and it uses the same ATX power connectors, allowing standard PSU use. Compared with ATX, a microATX board typically provides fewer expansion slots — up to four in total, including usually one full-length PCIe x16 slot — and may have fewer memory slots and I/O ports, although many modern boards provide two or four DIMM slots and extensive connectivity.

Usage

The form factor became the mainstream choice for budget and mid-range desktop PCs, office systems, and compact home theatre PCs, balancing expandability against cost and size. Smaller variants including FlexATX and Mini-ITX target more compact builds, while entry-level server and workstation boards also use microATX.
