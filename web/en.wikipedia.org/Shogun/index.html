Shogun

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: the shogun Tokugawa Ieyasu]

A shogun (Japanese: 将軍, shōgun) was the military ruler of Japan during the period from 1185 to 1868. The full title was sei-i taishōgun (征夷大将軍), "Commander-in-Chief of the Expeditionary Force Against the Barbarians", originally granted to generals campaigning against the Emishi peoples of northern Japan.

From the establishment of the Kamakura shogunate by Minamoto no Yoritomo in 1192, the shogun became the de facto ruler of Japan while the emperor reigned as a figurehead in Kyoto. Successive shogunal governments — the Kamakura, Ashikaga (Muromachi), and Tokugawa shogunates — are collectively termed bakufu ("tent government").

Tokugawa shogunate

The Tokugawa shogunate, founded by Tokugawa Ieyasu after the Battle of Sekigahara in 1600 and formally established in 1603, governed from Edo (modern Tokyo) for over 260 years. Its systems of alternate attendance (sankin-kōtai) and maritime restrictions (sakoku) kept the daimyo under control and limited foreign contact. The last shogun, Tokugawa Yoshinobu, resigned in 1867, restoring imperial rule in the Meiji Restoration.
