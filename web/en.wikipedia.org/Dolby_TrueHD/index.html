Dolby TrueHD is a lossless multi-channel audio codec developed by Dolby Laboratories for home video, used primarily on Blu-ray Disc and formerly on HD DVD. It is built upon the Meridian Lossless Packing (MLP) technology originally developed by Meridian Audio for DVD-Audio, extended by Dolby to support more channels and higher bit rates for cinematic use.

Technical details

TrueHD delivers bit-for-bit identical audio to the studio master, supporting up to 24-bit/192 kHz resolution. On Blu-ray it can carry up to 16 discrete audio channels in theory, with practical implementations commonly offering 7.1 channels, and a maximum bitrate of about 18 Mbit/s. Metadata embedded in the stream supports dialogue normalization and dynamic range control, allowing playback devices to adjust levels for late-night listening.

History

Dolby TrueHD debuted in 2006 as a mandatory audio format for HD DVD and an optional format for Blu-ray. With the launch of Dolby Atmos in the home in 2014, TrueHD became the carrier for object-based Atmos soundtracks on Blu-ray and Ultra HD Blu-ray, with the height information encoded as objects alongside a conventional channel bed.

Comparison with DTS-HD Master Audio

Its principal competitor, DTS-HD Master Audio, is also lossless. A key practical difference is that TrueHD streams do not contain a lossy "core" track; Blu-ray players instead extract a separate Dolby Digital companion track for devices lacking TrueHD decoding, whereas DTS-HD embeds a DTS core within the same stream.
