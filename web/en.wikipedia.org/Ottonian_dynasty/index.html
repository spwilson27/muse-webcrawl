The Ottonian dynasty (also called the Liudolfings) was a Saxon dynasty of kings and emperors that ruled East Francia and then the Holy Roman Empire from 919 to 1024. Its rulers were Henry the Fowler, Otto I, Otto II, Otto III, and Henry II.

Rise

Henry the Fowler, Duke of Saxony, was elected king in 919 and consolidated the East Frankish kingdom. His son Otto I defeated the Magyars at the Battle of Lechfeld (955), took the Lombard crown of Italy, and was crowned emperor by the pope in 962, an event conventionally marking the founding of the Holy Roman Empire.

Rule

The Ottonians governed through an itinerant court, royal bishoprics, and close alliance with the Church — the "imperial church system" — and promoted a cultural revival known as the Ottonian Renaissance, notable for illuminated manuscripts and architecture. Otto III pursued ambitious plans centred on Rome; Henry II, the last of the line, was later canonised.

End

On Henry II's death without heirs in 1024 the throne passed to the Salian dynasty under Conrad II.
