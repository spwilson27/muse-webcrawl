Founding of Rome

Founding of Rome
Traditional date: 21 April 753 BC (ab urbe condita)
Legendary founder: Romulus
Location: Palatine Hill, on the Tiber
Main sources: Livy, Dionysius of Halicarnassus, Plutarch

The founding of Rome was a prehistoric event or process as understood by later Romans, who dated it traditionally to 21 April 753 BC and attributed it to Romulus, twin of Remus, who were said to have been suckled by a she-wolf as infants.

The legend

According to tradition, the twins, descendants of the Trojan hero Aeneas through the kings of Alba Longa, were exposed on the Tiber and rescued by the wolf. As adults they quarrelled over where to found their city; Romulus killed Remus and founded Rome on the Palatine Hill, becoming its first king.

Archaeology

Excavations show Iron Age huts on the Palatine from the 10th–8th centuries BC and a mid-8th-century wall consistent with a unified settlement emerging around the traditional date, although the coalescence of villages was gradual.

Dates

The Varronian chronology fixes the foundation at 753 BC; Roman years were sometimes counted ab urbe condita ("from the founding of the city"). The traditional date was celebrated as the Parilia festival and is still marked as Rome's birthday.
