Locus coeruleus

The locus coeruleus (Latin for "blue spot") is a nucleus in the pons of the brainstem involved with physiological responses to stress and panic. It is the principal site for brain synthesis of the neurotransmitter norepinephrine (noradrenaline), and its neurons project widely throughout the brain and spinal cord.

Anatomy

The locus coeruleus is located in the posterior wall of the rostral pons, beneath the floor of the fourth ventricle. Its blue-black colour comes from neuromelanin pigment in its neurons, which gives the nucleus its name. In humans it contains roughly 15,000 neurons on each side.

Function

Through its far-reaching noradrenergic projections, the locus coeruleus regulates arousal, wakefulness, attention, and the stress response. Its activity is high during wakefulness, decreases during sleep, and is nearly absent during REM sleep. Dysfunction of the system has been implicated in anxiety disorders, depression, and neurodegenerative diseases such as Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease, in which the locus coeruleus loses neurons early.
