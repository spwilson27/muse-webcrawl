Crowd

A crowd is a large group of people gathered together in a defined physical space, whether temporarily, as at a concert, sporting event or protest, or more loosely as a recurring social grouping. Crowds are a central subject of social psychology, sociology and public-safety planning.

Social psychology

Early theorists such as Gustave Le Bon argued that individuals in crowds lose self-awareness and rationality, a view later scholars criticised as overstated. Modern research emphasises shared social identity: crowd behaviour is usually guided by norms associated with the group identity its members share, rather than by mindless contagion.

Types

Sociologists distinguish casual crowds, conventional crowds gathered for a scheduled event, expressive crowds such as revellers or mourners, and acting crowds, which include mobs and riots. Protests, audiences and queues are all forms of crowd behaviour with distinct dynamics.

Safety

Very high densities can produce dangerous crowd crushes, in which compressive forces rather than trampling cause most injuries. Event planning, barrier design and real-time density monitoring are used to prevent such disasters.
