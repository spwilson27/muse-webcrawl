Satara district is a district of Maharashtra state in western India. Its administrative headquarters is the city of Satara. The district lies in the Deccan plateau, bounded by the Sahyadri (Western Ghats) to the west.

History

Satara was historically significant as a seat of the Maratha Chhatrapatis. After Shivaji's death, the Satara branch of the Bhonsle dynasty ruled from the city; Shahu I made it his capital in 1708. The region is closely associated with the forts of Pratapgad, Sajjangad, and Ajinkyatara, and with the 1649 battle at Pratapgad where Afzal Khan was killed.

Geography and economy

The Krishna River and its tributaries drain the district. The hill stations of Mahabaleshwar and Panchgani, among Maharashtra's most popular tourist destinations, lie in its northwest. The Kaas Plateau, famous for its seasonal wildflowers, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Agriculture, including sugarcane and strawberries, dominates the economy, and the Koyna Dam on the Koyna River is a major hydroelectric project.
