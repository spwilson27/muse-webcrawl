Car

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A car, or automobile, is a wheeled motor vehicle used for transportation. Most definitions specify that cars are designed to run primarily on roads, seat one to eight people, have four wheels, and mainly transport people rather than cargo. There are around 1.5 billion cars in operation worldwide.

History

The year 1886 is regarded as the birth year of the car, when German inventor Karl Benz patented his Benz Patent-Motorwagen. Early automobiles were expensive curiosities, but Henry Ford's Model T (1908–1927) and the moving assembly line brought car ownership to the mass market. Through the 20th century cars transformed cities, economies, and lifestyles, accompanied by vast road networks and industries in fuel, steel, and rubber.

Design and technology

A conventional car comprises a body and chassis, an internal combustion engine (or electric motors), transmission, steering, suspension, brakes, and wheels with pneumatic tyres. Most cars historically burned petrol or diesel; hybrid and battery-electric vehicles have grown rapidly since the 2010s, alongside advances in safety systems, driver assistance, and connectivity. Weight typically ranges from about 1,000 to 2,500 kg.

Impacts

Cars offer flexible personal mobility but contribute substantially to air pollution, greenhouse-gas emissions, road deaths (about 1.19 million annually worldwide), congestion, and urban sprawl. These effects motivate policies on emissions, electrification, public transport, cycling, and shared mobility.
