Leap of faith

A leap of faith, in its most commonly used meaning, is the act of believing in or accepting something not on the basis of reason. The phrase is commonly attributed to the Danish philosopher Søren Kierkegaard, although he himself did not use the exact term; he referred instead to a leap (Danish: Springet) in his works such as Concluding Unscientific Postscript.


Søren Kierkegaard

Kierkegaard's concept

For Kierkegaard, faith is not a conclusion reached by objective reasoning but a passionate commitment made in the face of objective uncertainty. In his pseudonymous writings, particularly under the name Johannes Climacus, he described religious faith as requiring a leap beyond reason: because the central claims of Christianity (such as the Incarnation) appear paradoxical or even absurd to reason, they can only be embraced through a subjective act of trust.

Common usage

In everyday usage, the phrase has broadened to mean any decision to trust or commit to something whose outcome cannot be guaranteed — starting a business, entering a relationship, or accepting a claim without proof. It is often contrasted with decisions based on evidence and probability.
