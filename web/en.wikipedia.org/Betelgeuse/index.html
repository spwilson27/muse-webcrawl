Betelgeuse

Betelgeuse is a red supergiant star in the constellation of Orion, usually the tenth-brightest star in the night sky and, after Rigel, the second-brightest in its constellation. It is a distinctly reddish, semiregular variable star whose apparent magnitude varies between 0.0 and +1.6. It is one of the largest known stars: if placed at the centre of the Solar System, its surface would lie beyond the asteroid belt and it would engulf the orbits of Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars, and possibly Jupiter.

Observational history

Betelgeuse's name derives from Arabic Yad al-Jawzā', "the hand of Orion". It was the first star (other than the Sun) whose angular diameter was measured, by Albert Michelson and Francis Pease in 1920 using interferometry. Its variability had been noted as early as 1836 by Sir John Herschel.

The Great Dimming

Beginning in October 2019, Betelgeuse dimmed dramatically, reaching a historic minimum in February 2020 and fuelling speculation that a supernova was imminent. Subsequent observations attributed the "Great Dimming" to a combination of a large cool spot on the star's surface and a cloud of dust ejected from the star crossing the line of sight. The star returned to normal brightness by mid-2020.

Future

Betelgeuse is expected to end its life in a Type II supernova explosion, probably within the next 100,000 years. When it does, it will shine as brightly as the half-Moon for weeks, but poses no threat to Earth.
