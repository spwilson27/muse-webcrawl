SD-WAN

TypeWide area network architecture
Based onSoftware-defined networking
Typical linksMPLS, broadband internet, LTE/5G

SD-WAN (software-defined wide area network) is an approach to managing wide area networks that applies software-defined networking principles to connections between enterprise branch offices, data centres and cloud services. An SD-WAN abstracts the underlying transport links — such as MPLS circuits, broadband internet and cellular connections — and routes traffic over them according to centrally managed policies.

Features

Typical SD-WAN products provide centralised orchestration, application-aware routing, dynamic path selection based on measured latency, jitter and packet loss, built-in encryption (usually IPsec tunnels), and zero-touch provisioning of branch appliances. Traffic for cloud applications can be sent directly to the internet from the branch ("local breakout") rather than backhauled through a central data centre.

Drivers and market

Adoption grew during the 2010s as businesses moved applications to cloud services such as Microsoft 365 and Salesforce, making expensive MPLS-only designs less attractive. SD-WAN is often combined with cloud-delivered security in the "secure access service edge" (SASE) model. Major vendors include Cisco, VMware (Broadcom), Fortinet, Palo Alto Networks and HPE Aruba, as well as telecommunications carriers offering managed services.
