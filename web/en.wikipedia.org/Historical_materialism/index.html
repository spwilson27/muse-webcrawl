Historical materialism

Historical materialism is a theory of history associated with Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels, holding that the material conditions of a society — its mode of production — fundamentally determine its organisation and development. In Marx's formulation, the economic "base" of productive forces and relations of production shapes the legal, political, and ideological "superstructure".

Core concepts

Societies are analysed as progressing through modes of production — commonly listed as tribal or primitive communism, slavery, feudalism, capitalism, and, in Marx's expectation, socialism and communism. Change occurs when developing productive forces come into conflict with existing relations of production, producing class struggle between those who own the means of production and those who do not. Marx summarised the approach in the preface to A Contribution to the Critique of Political Economy (1859): "It is not the consciousness of men that determines their being, but, on the contrary, their social being that determines their consciousness."

Influence and criticism

Historical materialism became the official doctrine of Marxist–Leninist states and a major influence on 20th-century historiography, sociology, and anthropology. Critics have charged it with economic determinism and teleology, while defenders and analytical Marxists have sought to restate it in more rigorous or less deterministic forms.
