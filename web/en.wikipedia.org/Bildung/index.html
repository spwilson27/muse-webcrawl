Bildung

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Bildung (German: [ˈbɪldʊŋ], "education", "formation") refers to the German tradition of self-cultivation, in which philosophy and education are linked in a manner that refers to a process of both personal and cultural maturation. During this process, the individual shapes their capacities and potentials through engagement with the cultural achievements of humanity, becoming a fully formed personality in the process.

Origins

The concept developed in the late 18th century, drawing on the ideas of Herder and Wilhelm von Humboldt, who saw education as the harmonious development of all human powers rather than mere training for a profession. Humboldt's university reforms made Bildung a guiding ideal of the German university.

Philosophical development

Hegel placed Bildung at the centre of his account of spirit's self-development, treating it as the alienation and recovery through which consciousness becomes universal. Later thinkers, including Gadamer, treated Bildung as a fundamental concept of the human sciences: the open-ended cultivation of judgment, taste, and receptivity to what is other.

Legacy

Bildung underlies the German ideal of the educated middle class (the Bildungsbürgertum) and influenced liberal-arts education internationally. The literary genre of the Bildungsroman — the novel of formation — narrates a protagonist's Bildung. In modern German, Bildung is also the ordinary word for education in general.
