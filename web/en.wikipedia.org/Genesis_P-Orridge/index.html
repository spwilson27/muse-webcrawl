Genesis P-Orridge

Born: Neil Andrew Megson, February 22, 1950, Manchester, England

Died: March 14, 2020 (aged 70), New York City, U.S.

Occupation: Musician, artist, writer, occultist

Known for: Throbbing Gristle, Psychic TV, industrial music

Genesis Breyer P-Orridge (born Neil Andrew Megson; February 22, 1950 – March 14, 2020) was an English singer-songwriter, musician, poet, performance artist, visual artist, and occultist who rose to notoriety as founder of the COUM Transmissions artistic collective and of the industrial music band Throbbing Gristle. As leader of the experimental band Psychic TV and founder of Thee Temple ov Psychick Youth network, P-Orridge was a foundational figure in industrial culture.

Career

After dropping out of the University of Hull, Megson adopted the name Genesis P-Orridge and co-founded COUM Transmissions in 1969, whose confrontational performances culminated in the notorious 1976 Prostitute exhibition at the Institute of Contemporary Arts in London. That year P-Orridge formed Throbbing Gristle with Cosey Fanni Tutti, Peter Christopherson, and Chris Carter; the band's label, Industrial Records, gave the genre its name with the slogan "industrial music for industrial people". When Throbbing Gristle split in 1981, P-Orridge formed Psychic TV, which explored psychedelic, acid house, and ritual music, and co-founded Thee Temple ov Psychick Youth, an occult network influenced by Aleister Crowley, William S. Burroughs, and Brion Gysin.

Pandrogyne project and later life

From 2003, P-Orridge and partner Lady Jaye (Jacqueline Breyer) pursued the "Pandrogyne" project, undergoing cosmetic surgeries and adopting matching appearances in an attempt to merge into a single entity; they married and both took the surname Breyer P-Orridge. After Lady Jaye's death in 2007, Genesis continued the project alone. P-Orridge continued performing with a re-formed Throbbing Gristle and with Psychic TV until being diagnosed with leukemia in 2017, and died in New York City in March 2020.
