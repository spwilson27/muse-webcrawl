Vasoactive intestinal peptide

Vasoactive intestinal peptide, also known as vasoactive intestinal polypeptide or VIP, is a peptide hormone that is vasoactive in the intestine. VIP is a peptide of 28 amino acid residues that belongs to a glucagon/secretin superfamily. It is produced in many tissues of vertebrates including the gut, pancreas, suprachiasmatic nucleus of the hypothalamus, and the brain.

Function

VIP has an effect on several tissues: it relaxes smooth muscle in the gastrointestinal tract and airways, stimulates secretion of water into pancreatic juice and bile, and causes vasodilation, lowering arterial blood pressure. It inhibits gastric acid secretion and absorption from the intestinal lumen. In the brain, VIP in the suprachiasmatic nucleus helps synchronise the circadian rhythm, and VIP acts broadly as a neurotransmitter and neuroprotective agent.

Clinical significance

Oversecretion of VIP can occur in a rare endocrine tumour called a VIPoma, causing the WDHA syndrome (watery diarrhoea, hypokalemia, and achlorhydria), sometimes called Verner–Morrison syndrome. VIP also has immunomodulatory and anti-inflammatory properties that have attracted research interest in autoimmune and inflammatory conditions.
