Activism

Activism consists of efforts to promote, impede, direct, or intervene in social, political, economic, or environmental reform with the desire to make changes in society toward a perceived greater good. Forms of activism range from writing letters and petitions, lobbying, boycotts, and strikes to demonstrations, sit-ins, civil disobedience, and, in the digital era, online campaigning and hacktivism.

History

Activism has a long history, from the abolitionist and suffrage movements of the 19th and early 20th centuries to the civil rights movement, anti-war protests, labour organising, and the environmental movement. Activists often work through non-governmental organisations, grassroots groups, and social movements, and influential strategies have been articulated by figures such as Mahatma Gandhi and Martin Luther King Jr., who advocated nonviolent resistance.

Methods and debate

Common tactics include raising public awareness, building coalitions, litigation, shareholder activism, and voter mobilisation. The rise of social media has enabled rapid, decentralised organising — sometimes labelled hashtag activism or, pejoratively, slacktivism — prompting debate about the depth of engagement such methods produce versus their reach and speed.
