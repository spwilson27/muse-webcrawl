Signal Iduna Park is a football stadium in Dortmund, North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany, also known by its original name, the Westfalenstadion. With a league capacity of 81,365 spectators, it is the largest stadium in Germany and one of the largest in Europe. It is the home ground of Bundesliga club Borussia Dortmund.

History

The stadium was built for the 1974 FIFA World Cup and opened on 2 April 1974 with a friendly between Borussia Dortmund and Schalke 04. It was financed largely by the city of Dortmund and lottery funds. The stadium hosted matches at the 1974 and 2006 World Cups, the 2001 UEFA Cup Final, and matches at UEFA Euro 2024. Naming rights were sold to the insurance company Signal Iduna in 2005.

Architecture

The stadium is famous for its steep stands and intense atmosphere. Its south stand, the Südtribüne, is the largest free-standing grandstand in Europe, holding 24,454 spectators, and is known as the "Yellow Wall" (Gelbe Wand) for the sea of black-and-yellow supporters who occupy it. Eight distinctive yellow pylons, 62 metres tall, support the roof structure added for the 2006 World Cup expansion.

Attendance

Borussia Dortmund regularly records the highest average attendance in European football, with the stadium often selling out its more than 80,000 places for Bundesliga matches.
