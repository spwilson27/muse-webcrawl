May Revolution

The May Revolution (Spanish: Revolución de Mayo) was a week-long series of events that took place from May 18 to 25, 1810, in Buenos Aires, then capital of the Viceroyalty of the Río de la Plata. It culminated in the removal of Viceroy Baltasar Hidalgo de Cisneros and the establishment of the Primera Junta, the first local government of what would become Argentina.

Background

The revolution was influenced by the Napoleonic Wars: Napoleon had deposed Ferdinand VII of Spain and installed Joseph Bonaparte, casting doubt on the legitimacy of colonial authorities appointed in the king's name. British invasions of the Río de la Plata (1806–1807) had also shown that local militias could defend the city without Spanish help.

Events

During the May Week, an open cabildo on 22 May voted to depose the viceroy. After a brief attempt to retain Cisneros in a ruling junta, popular pressure led on 25 May 1810 to the formation of the Primera Junta, headed by Cornelius Saavedra and including Mariano Moreno and Manuel Belgrano. Although loyalty to Ferdinand VII was formally maintained, the revolution began the Argentine War of Independence and is commemorated as Argentina's national day.
