Mount Saint Elias

Mount Saint Elias is the second-highest mountain in both Canada and the United States, standing 5,489 metres (18,009 ft) on the border between Alaska and Yukon in the Saint Elias Mountains. Remarkably, its summit lies only about 16 kilometres from tidewater at Icy Bay, giving it one of the greatest vertical reliefs on Earth — a rise comparable to Denali's but directly from the sea.

Exploration and climbing

The peak was sighted in 1741 by Vitus Bering's expedition on Saint Elias's feast day, which gave it its name. It resisted climbers for decades: the first ascent was achieved in 1897 by the expedition of Prince Luigi Amedeo, Duke of the Abruzzi, photographed by Vittorio Sella, after a gruelling approach over the Malaspina Glacier system. The mountain is climbed infrequently even today because of its remote position, huge scale and ferocious maritime weather; the second ascent did not occur until 1946.

Geography

The American side lies within Wrangell–St. Elias National Park and Preserve, the largest national park in the United States, and the Canadian side within Kluane National Park; together with neighbouring parks the area forms a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The Tlingit name for the mountain, Yasʼéitʼaa Shaa, means "mountain behind Icy Bay".
