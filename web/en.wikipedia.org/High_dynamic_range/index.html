High dynamic range

High dynamic range (HDR) is a technology for imaging and display that reproduces a greater range of luminance and colour than standard dynamic range (SDR). In photography and computer graphics, HDR techniques capture, store, or synthesise scenes whose brightest highlights and deepest shadows far exceed the capability of a single conventional exposure; in video, HDR formats allow displays to show brighter highlights, darker blacks, and a wider colour gamut.

HDR imaging

High-dynamic-range imaging (HDRI) originated in computer graphics in the 1990s: multiple photographs of the same scene at different exposures are merged into a single radiance map, which is then tone-mapped for display. File formats such as Radiance HDR and OpenEXR store these floating-point images. Modern smartphones apply computational HDR automatically, combining frames to extend dynamic range.

HDR video and displays

HDR video standards include HDR10, HDR10+, Dolby Vision, and Hybrid Log-Gamma (HLG), defined by the ITU-R BT.2100 recommendation. They use 10-bit (or higher) colour depth, wide colour gamut (BT.2020 primaries), and electro-optical transfer functions such as PQ (Perceptual Quantizer) capable of peak brightness of 1,000 nits or more — far beyond SDR's roughly 100 nits. HDR has become standard in premium televisions, streaming services, and games consoles.
