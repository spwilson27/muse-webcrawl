International Paralympic Committee

The International Paralympic Committee (IPC) is the international governing body of the Paralympic Movement. It is a non-profit organisation founded on 22 September 1989 in Düsseldorf, Germany, and headquartered in Bonn, Germany. The IPC organises the Paralympic Games, both summer and winter, and serves as the international federation for several para sports. Its membership comprises around 180 National Paralympic Committees, several international sports federations, and regional organisations.

History

The Paralympic movement traces its origins to the Stoke Mandeville Games, organised by Ludwig Guttmann in England in 1948 for veterans with spinal cord injuries. The first Paralympic Games recognised as such took place in Rome in 1960. For decades, international disability sport was run by separate impairment-specific organisations; the IPC was created in 1989 to unite them under one body. Since the Seoul 1988 Games, the Paralympics have been held in the same cities and venues as the Olympic Games, an arrangement formalised in agreements with the International Olympic Committee.

Functions

The IPC supervises the organisation of the Paralympic Games, governs classification — the system by which athletes are grouped according to how their impairments affect performance in each sport — and oversees anti-doping and eligibility rules. It acts as international federation for sports including para athletics, para swimming, para powerlifting, shooting para sport and para ice hockey. Its vision is to make for an inclusive world through para sport, and its symbol, the Agitos — three curved shapes in red, blue and green encircling a point — represents athletes moving together.

Presidents of the IPC have included Robert Steadward (1989–2001), Philip Craven (2001–2017) and Andrew Parsons, who took office in 2017.
