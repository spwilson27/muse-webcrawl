Christian meditation

Christian meditation is a form of prayer in which a structured attempt is made to become aware of, and reflect upon, the revelations of God. The term "meditation" appears in the Bible — for example, Joshua 1:8 and the Psalms speak of meditating on God's law and works — and Christian meditation has been practised since the early Church, both in monastic traditions and by laypeople.

Approaches

Christian meditation typically involves focused reflection on a passage of Scripture, an attribute of God, or a mystery of the faith, rather than the emptying of the mind associated with some Eastern practices. Monastic lectio divina — slow, prayerful reading of Scripture in the stages of reading, meditation, prayer, and contemplation — is the classic Western method. The rosary combines vocal prayer with meditation on episodes from the lives of Jesus and Mary. Ignatian meditation, from the Spiritual Exercises of Ignatius of Loyola, uses the imagination to enter Gospel scenes.

Contemplative traditions

Eastern Christianity developed hesychasm, centred on the repetition of the Jesus Prayer, while Western revival movements in the twentieth century popularised Centering Prayer (associated with Thomas Keating) and Christian meditation using a mantra such as "Maranatha", promoted by John Main and the World Community for Christian Meditation.

Views and debates

Most Christian traditions encourage meditation on Scripture. Some evangelical writers have cautioned against forms of meditation they regard as borrowed uncritically from other religions, while proponents respond that contemplative prayer has deep roots in historic Christianity.
