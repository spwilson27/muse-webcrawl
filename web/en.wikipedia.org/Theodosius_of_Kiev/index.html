Theodosius of Kiev

Theodosius of Kiev (died 3 May 1074), also known as Theodosius of the Caves, was an 11th-century monk and saint in Kievan Rus'. Together with Anthony of Kiev, he is considered a founder of the Kiev Monastery of the Caves (Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra) and of cenobitic monasticism in the Rus' lands.

Life

Born in Vasyliv near Kiev, Theodosius joined Anthony's cave hermitage as a young man despite his mother's opposition. When the community grew, he became its hegumen (abbot) around 1062 and organized it on the model of the Studion Monastery of Constantinople, emphasizing communal life, labour, charity and strict observance. He built the monastery's great church, founded hospitals and poorhouses, and did not hesitate to reprove princes for injustice.

Veneration

Theodosius was canonized by the Russian Orthodox Church, and his life, written by the monk Nestor, is one of the earliest works of Rus' hagiography. His relics rest in the Near Caves of the Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra. See also Kyiv Pechersk Lavra and Anthony of Kiev.
