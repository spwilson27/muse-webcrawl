Cushing, Oklahoma

Cushing is a city in Payne County, Oklahoma, United States, with a population of roughly 8,000. It is internationally known as a major crude oil storage and pipeline hub: Cushing is the delivery point for West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil futures traded on the New York Mercantile Exchange, earning it the nickname "Pipeline Crossroads of the World."

History

The town was laid out after the Land Run of 1891 and named for Marshall Cushing, private secretary to the Postmaster General. An oil boom began in 1912 with the Cushing–Drumright oil field, then one of the most productive in the United States. Pipelines and tank farms proliferated, and although field production declined, Cushing's position as a storage and trading hub grew.

Oil hub

Dozens of pipelines converge at Cushing, and storage capacity exceeds 90 million barrels in tanks around the city. Inventory levels reported weekly by the U.S. Energy Information Administration are closely watched by energy markets. The town sits on the geologically stable Cushing fault zone area — in 2016 a magnitude 5.0 earthquake near Cushing raised concerns about the tank farms.
