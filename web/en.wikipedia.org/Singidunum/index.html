Singidunum (Serbian: Сингидунум) was an ancient city that later became modern Belgrade, the capital of Serbia. It stood at the strategic confluence of the Sava and Danube rivers, on the hill where Belgrade Fortress (Kalemegdan) now stands.

History

The site was first settled by the Celtic tribe of the Scordisci in the 3rd century BC; the name Singidunum is thought to combine a tribal name (Singi) with the Celtic dunum ("fortification"). The Romans conquered the area in the 1st century BC, and Singidunum became an important military stronghold on the Danube limes, in the province of Moesia. The legion Legio IV Flavia Felix was stationed here from about 86 AD, and the settlement grew into a city with the usual Roman institutions, receiving colonial status in the 3rd century.

Singidunum was the birthplace of the emperor Jovian (r. 363–364). As a frontier city it was repeatedly attacked: by Huns in the 5th century, and later by Avars, Slavs, Bulgars, Hungarians and Byzantines. Under Byzantine rule it was known as a key Danubian fortress; in the early 7th century it fell to the Avars and Slavs. The Slavic name Beograd ("white city") is first recorded in 878.

Legacy

Remains of the Roman castrum lie beneath Belgrade Fortress and the Upper Town of Kalemegdan Park. The name Singidunum survives in historical and ceremonial use, for example in the University of Belgrade's older seals and in the modern Singidunum University.
