Ixodes

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ixodes is a genus of hard-bodied ticks (family Ixodidae), one of the largest genera of ticks with more than 200 described species. Members of the genus are found worldwide and are important vectors of diseases of humans and animals.

Biology

Ixodes species are three-host ticks: larva, nymph, and adult each feed on a different host. They lack eyes and ornate markings on the scutum, and the mouthparts project forward. Feeding can last several days, during which pathogens may be transmitted.

Medical importance

Ixodes scapularis (the black-legged or deer tick) and Ixodes pacificus transmit Lyme disease, babesiosis, and anaplasmosis in North America, while Ixodes ricinus (the castor bean tick) transmits Lyme disease and tick-borne encephalitis in Europe.
