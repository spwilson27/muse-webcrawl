High-tech architecture

High-tech architecture, also known as structural expressionism, is a type of late modernist architecture that emerged in the 1970s, incorporating elements of high-tech industry and technology into building design. High-tech architecture grew out of the modernist style, utilising new advances in technology and building materials. It emphasises transparency, exposed structure and services, and the honest expression of a building's technical elements.

Characteristics

Hallmarks of the style include externalised structure and building services (ducts, pipes, escalators) often colour-coded and displayed on the façade; prefabricated and industrial components; lightweight materials such as steel and glass; and flexible, unobstructed interior floors made possible by moving services to the perimeter. Leading practitioners include Renzo Piano, Richard Rogers, Norman Foster, Nicholas Grimshaw, and Michael Hopkins.

Notable examples

  • Centre Pompidou, Paris (Piano and Rogers, 1977)
  • Lloyd's Building, London (Richard Rogers, 1986)
  • Hongkong and Shanghai Bank Headquarters, Hong Kong (Foster, 1985)
  • Bank of China Tower, Hong Kong (I. M. Pei, 1990)
  • Sainsbury Centre for Visual Arts, Norwich (Foster, 1978)

In later decades the style evolved toward eco-tech and sustainable design, integrating energy efficiency and environmental responsiveness with the technological aesthetic.
