Yuan Shikai

Yuan Shikai
Born: 16 September 1859, Xiangcheng, Henan
Died: 6 June 1916, Beijing
Roles: Commander of the Beiyang Army; President of the Republic of China (1912–1916); self-proclaimed Hongxian Emperor (1915–1916)

Yuan Shikai (1859–1916) was a Chinese general and statesman who dominated China in the decade after the fall of the Qing dynasty, becoming the first official president of the Republic of China — and then briefly attempting to found his own dynasty.

Rise

After service in Korea, Yuan was given command of training China's first modernised force, the New Army, at Xiaozhan in the 1890s. Its successor, the Beiyang Army, was loyal to him personally. He backed the Empress Dowager Cixi in the 1898 coup against the Guangxu Emperor's reforms, became governor-general of Zhili, and pushed late-Qing modernisation in education, police, and industry. Forced into retirement after Cixi's death, he was recalled during the 1911 Revolution, negotiated the abdication of the boy emperor Puyi, and succeeded Sun Yat-sen as president in 1912.

Presidency and monarchy attempt

Yuan suppressed the Kuomintang's Second Revolution (1913), dissolved parliament, accepted most of Japan's humiliating Twenty-One Demands (1915), and in December 1915 proclaimed himself Hongxian ("Constitutional Abundance") Emperor. The announcement triggered the National Protection War: his own Beiyang generals defected, provinces declared independence, and Yuan abandoned the throne after 83 days. He died in June 1916 — of uremia, contemporaries said, hastened by disgrace — leaving China to fragment into the Warlord Era.

Assessment

Historians debate Yuan as a capable modernising strongman betrayed by imperial ambition versus a reactionary who strangled China's republican experiment.
