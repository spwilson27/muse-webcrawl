Record chart

Record charts rank the most popular recordings of the week, traditionally by sales and airplay.

A record chart, in the music industry, also called a music chart, is a ranking of recorded music according to certain criteria during a specified period. Many different recorded music charts are used in different countries and at different historical periods, and chart rankings are based on such data as sales (of physical media, downloads or streams), radio airplay, and jukebox or club play, sometimes in combination.

History

Trade papers began publishing popularity rankings in the early twentieth century, based initially on sheet-music sales and later on record sales and disc-jockey reports. In the United States, Billboard magazine launched its charts in the 1930s–40s and consolidated them into the Hot 100 singles chart in 1958; the Billboard 200 ranks albums. In Britain, the first singles chart appeared in the New Musical Express in 1952, and the Official Charts Company now compiles the UK Singles Chart and UK Albums Chart.

Methodology in the streaming era

Charts historically depended on reports from retailers and radio stations, which were vulnerable to manipulation and payola. Point-of-sale systems such as Nielsen SoundScan (from 1991) and Luminate greatly improved accuracy. Since the 2010s the emphasis has shifted to streaming: chart compilers count on-demand audio and video streams with conversion ratios that equate a set number of streams to one sale or download, and rules are periodically revised to handle phenomena such as album-equivalent units, passive playlist streams and chart manipulation through bundled merchandise or fan streaming campaigns.

Chart achievements and significance

Topping a major chart — a "number one" — is a widely reported mark of success, and chart statistics (weeks at number one, most entries, biggest jumps) form a large part of popular-music lore and reference works. Critics argue that chart methodology changes make historical comparisons misleading and that streaming-era charts favour catalog and superstar repertoire over new artists.
