Chronicle is a 2012 American found-footage superhero horror film directed by Josh Trank and with a screenplay by Max Landis, from a story by Trank and Landis. It stars Dane DeHaan, Alex Russell, and Michael B. Jordan as three Seattle high-school students — Andrew, Matt, and Steve — who gain telekinetic superpowers after exposure to a mysterious crystalline object in an underground cavern.

The introverted Andrew documents his life with a video camera, and the film is presented from the viewpoint of his and other characters' cameras. As the three master their abilities, Andrew's troubled home life and growing resentment push him toward violence, culminating in a destructive confrontation in downtown Seattle.

Plot

Andrew Detmer, a shy teenager abused by his alcoholic father and caring for his terminally ill mother, begins filming his life. At a party with his cousin Matt and the popular Steve, he discovers a hole in the woods containing a glowing crystalline object that grants them telekinetic powers, accompanied by nosebleeds. Over the following weeks they learn to move objects, fly, and influence their surroundings.

Andrew's powers grow fastest, but so does his instability: after killing a spider he begins to view himself as an "apex predator". Following a confrontation in which he causes an accident and later attacks his father, Andrew steals money for his mother's medication by robbing shops, injuring bystanders. Matt and Steve attempt to stop him, and the conflict ends in a battle around the Space Needle in which Steve is killed by lightning during flight and Andrew is ultimately killed by Matt with a spear from a statue.

Production

Trank conceived the idea while at film school, drawing on the manga and film Akira as influences. The film was shot largely in Cape Town, South Africa, and Seattle, with a small budget of $12 million. Its marketing campaign emphasised the found-footage style and the characters' flying sequences.

Reception

Chronicle was released on February 3, 2012, by 20th Century Fox. It grossed $126.6 million worldwide and received generally positive reviews, with praise for its premise, direction, and performances, particularly DeHaan's.
