2007–08 Writers Guild of America strike

Date5 November 2007 – 12 February 2008
LocationUnited States
PartiesWGA East & West vs. AMPTP
Duration100 days

The 2007–2008 Writers Guild of America strike, which began on 5 November 2007, was a labor conflict between the Writers Guild of America, East (WGAE) and West (WGAW), and the Alliance of Motion Picture and Television Producers (AMPTP). The strike, which lasted 100 days, disrupted film and television production throughout the United States.

Issues

The central dispute concerned writers' compensation for "new media": sales and streaming of films and television programmes over the Internet and other digital distribution, as well as residuals for DVD sales. The Guild sought a share of distributor's gross receipts in new media and jurisdiction over animation and reality programming.

Effects

Television production was heavily affected: many scripted series shortened their seasons or shut down, late-night talk shows went off the air or returned without writers, and the Golden Globe Awards ceremony was cancelled. The strike was estimated to have cost the Los Angeles economy over two billion dollars.

Resolution

A tentative agreement was reached on 8 February 2008, and the strike formally ended on 12 February 2008 after members voted to accept a new contract that included a formula for new-media residuals.
