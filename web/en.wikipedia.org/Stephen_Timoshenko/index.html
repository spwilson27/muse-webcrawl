Stephen Timoshenko

Stephen Prokofievich Timoshenko (1878–1972), founder of modern engineering mechanics.

Stephen Prokofievich Timoshenko (December 22, 1878 – May 29, 1972) was a Ukrainian-born American engineer regarded as the father of modern engineering mechanics. He developed the theory of elasticity, beam theory (the Timoshenko–Ehrenfest beam theory), and the theory of elastic stability, and wrote textbooks that trained generations of engineers.

Life

Timoshenko was born in Shpotivka in the Russian Empire (now Ukraine) and studied at the St Petersburg Institute of Ways of Communication. He taught in Kyiv and St Petersburg, emigrated via Yugoslavia after the Revolution, and in 1922 moved to the United States, where he worked for the Westinghouse Electric Corporation before holding professorships at the University of Michigan and Stanford University.

Contributions

His work covered the vibration and buckling of beams, plates and shells; the analysis of thermal stresses; and contact problems. The Timoshenko beam theory accounts for shear deformation and rotary inertia, refining the classical Euler–Bernoulli theory for short, thick beams. At Michigan he established the first American bachelor's and doctoral programmes in engineering mechanics.

Legacy

The American Society of Mechanical Engineers created the Timoshenko Medal in 1957 — Timoshenko was its first recipient — to honour distinguished achievement in applied mechanics. His books, including Strength of Materials and Theory of Elasticity (with J. N. Goodier), remain in print.
