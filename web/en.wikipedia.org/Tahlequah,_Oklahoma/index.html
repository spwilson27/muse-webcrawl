Tahlequah, Oklahoma

Tahlequah (TAL-ə-kwah; Cherokee: ᏓᎵᏆ, daliqua) is a city in Cherokee County, Oklahoma, United States, and its county seat. It is the capital of the Cherokee Nation and of the United Keetoowah Band of Cherokee Indians, and is home to Northeastern State University. Its population was about 16,000 at the 2020 census.

History

After the forced removal of the Cherokee along the Trail of Tears, the reunited Cherokee Nation chose the site in 1839 as its capital; a public meeting ground was laid out where the council ground and Capitol Square stood. Legend derives the name from the Cherokee word for "plains" or from a town in the old eastern homeland. The Cherokee Capitol building, completed in 1869 after Civil War devastation, is now a National Historic Landmark and houses the Cherokee Nation Supreme Court.

Culture and education

Tahlequah hosts the Cherokee National Holiday each Labor Day weekend and is a centre for Cherokee language revitalisation, including a language immersion school. Street signs are bilingual in English and Cherokee syllabary. Northeastern State University traces its origins to the Cherokee National Female Seminary of 1851. The nearby Illinois River is a popular floating and fishing destination.

Geography

The city lies in the foothills of the Ozark Mountains in northeastern Oklahoma, in "Green Country", near Lake Tenkiller. It is among the older municipalities in the state west of the Mississippi.
