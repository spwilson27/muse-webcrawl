Sokoto Caliphate

Founded1804
FounderUsman dan Fodio
CapitalSokoto (from 1809)
Ended1903 (British conquest)

The Sokoto Caliphate was an Islamic state in what is now northern Nigeria and neighbouring regions, founded by Usman dan Fodio during the Fulani jihad of 1804. At its height it was one of the largest states in nineteenth-century Africa, linking some thirty emirates and over ten million people.

History

Usman dan Fodio, a Fulani scholar and preacher, declared jihad against the Hausa kingdom of Gobir in 1804. His forces overthrew the Hausa states, and in 1809 he retired, dividing rule between his son Muhammad Bello (in the east, from Sokoto) and his brother Abdullahi (in the west, from Gwandu).

Government and legacy

The caliphate was organised as a confederation of emirates owing allegiance to the Sultan of Sokoto, who remains the spiritual leader of Nigerian Muslims. British forces under Frederick Lugard conquered the caliphate in 1903, but preserved its emirate structure within indirect rule in Northern Nigeria.
