IndieCade

IndieCade is an international festival and conference dedicated to independent video games, often described as the "Sundance of video games". Founded in 2005, IndieCade celebrates independent game development, showcasing innovative and experimental works from developers around the world and providing professional development, networking, and exhibition opportunities for creators outside the mainstream games industry.

History

IndieCade grew out of the independent games movement of the early 2000s. Its flagship festival was held for many years in Culver City, California, gathering thousands of attendees to play selected games in public spaces, attend talks, and take part in events such as the Night Games program of large-scale and experimental play. From 2013, IndieCade East was held in New York City in partnership with the Museum of the Moving Image, and international chapters and showcase events have appeared in Europe and elsewhere. In 2020 the festival moved substantially online.

Festival and awards

Hundreds of games are submitted each year and reviewed by a jury of developers, academics, journalists, and artists. Festival finalists and award winners gain significant visibility; past IndieCade selections have included influential independent titles such as Braid, Fez, Papers, Please, and numerous experimental and art games. Awards are given in categories celebrating visual design, audio, technology, narrative, and social impact.

Organization

IndieCade operates as a nonprofit organization. Beyond the festival, it runs year-round programming, developer support initiatives, and showcases at events such as E3 and Games for Change, and it has helped legitimize independent and experimental games within the wider industry and in academic study.
