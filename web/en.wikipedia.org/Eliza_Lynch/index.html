Eliza Lynch

Eliza Alice Lynch (19 November 1833 – 25 July 1886) was an Irish woman who became the partner of Francisco Solano López, president and dictator of Paraguay, and exerted considerable influence during the Paraguayan War (1864–1870), in which Paraguay fought the Triple Alliance of Brazil, Argentina, and Uruguay.

Born:19 November 1833, Charleville, County Cork, Ireland
Died:25 July 1886 (aged 52), Paris, France
Partner:Francisco Solano López

Early life

Lynch was born in Charleville, County Cork, Ireland, during the Great Famine, and emigrated with her family to Paris at the age of ten. She married a French army officer, from whom she separated, and in 1854 met the young Francisco Solano López, then visiting Paris as a Paraguayan envoy. She accompanied him back to Paraguay and became his companion, though the two never married.

Paraguay

In Asunción, Lynch became the most powerful woman in the country, introducing European fashions and culture and accumulating great wealth and land. During the Paraguayan War she accompanied López on campaign, organized nursing for the wounded, and by many accounts encouraged his resistance. In March 1870 she witnessed López's death at Cerro Corá and, according to tradition, buried him and their eldest son with her own hands.

Later life and legacy

After the war Lynch was deported; her property claims were rejected, and she died in Paris in 1886 in modest circumstances. Her body was returned to Paraguay in 1961, and she has since been re-evaluated as a national heroine; in 1970 her remains were placed in the National Pantheon of Heroes in Asunción.
