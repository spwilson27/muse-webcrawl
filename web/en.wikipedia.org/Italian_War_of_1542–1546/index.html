Italian War of 1542–1546

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Italian War of 1542–1546 was a conflict late in the Italian Wars, pitting Francis I of France and Suleiman I of the Ottoman Empire against the Holy Roman Emperor Charles V and Henry VIII of England. The course of the war saw extensive fighting in Italy, France, and the Low Countries, as well as attempted invasions of Spain and England. The conflict was inconclusive and ruinously expensive for the major participants.

Background

The Truce of Nice, which ended the Italian War of 1536–1538, provided little long-term relief: tensions between Charles V and Francis I continued, even though Henry VIII had shifted his position to ally with Charles by 1543. Francis I, allied once again with Suleiman I, launched a final invasion of Italian territory in early 1542. At the Battle of Ceresole (April 1544), French arms won a victory, but the French advance faltered.

Course of the war

In 1543, Francis I and Suleiman I cooperated in the joint siege of Nice, and the Ottoman fleet under Hayreddin Barbarossa wintered at Toulon. In 1544, Charles V and Henry VIII invaded northern France; Henry took Boulogne, while Charles's advance stalled and he made a separate Peace of Crépy with France. In 1545, a French attempt to invade England failed at the Battle of the Solent, where the Mary Rose sank. The war ended with the Treaty of Ardres in 1546, which restored the status quo ante bellum.
