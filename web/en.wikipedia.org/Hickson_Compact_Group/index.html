Hickson Compact Group

A Hickson Compact Group (HCG) is a collection of galaxies designated in astronomer Paul Hickson's 1982 catalogue Systematic Properties of Compact Groups of Galaxies. Hickson identified 100 groups using criteria applied to the Palomar Observatory Sky Survey: a group must contain at least four galaxies of comparable brightness, be isolated from its surroundings, and have high mean surface brightness (compactness).

Characteristics

Compact groups pack several galaxies into a volume not much larger than the Milky Way itself, so gravitational interactions, tidal tails, and mergers are common. They are natural laboratories for studying galaxy evolution, interaction-triggered star formation, and the build-up of intra-group light, though astronomers debate whether many are genuinely dense systems or chance alignments within looser groups.

Notable groups

The most famous example is HCG 92, better known as Stephan's Quintet in Pegasus. Others include HCG 31 and the Seyfert's Sextet (HCG 79). Observations from the Hubble Space Telescope and Chandra X-ray Observatory have revealed hot intergalactic gas and shock fronts in several groups. See also galaxy group.
