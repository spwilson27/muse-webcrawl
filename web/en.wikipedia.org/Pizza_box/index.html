Pizza box

A pizza box is a folding box made of corrugated cardboard in which hot pizzas are stored for take-out and home delivery. The box also serves as advertising space for the pizzeria that supplies it. A good pizza box must be rigid enough to stack, insulate the pizza to keep it warm, and vent steam so the crust does not become soggy.

History

Early pizza delivery used plain paper bags or thin boxes that collapsed under steam. The modern square corrugated box was developed in the United States in the 1960s; Domino's Pizza is credited with popularising a sturdy corrugated design that could be stacked without crushing the pizza inside. The small plastic tripod often placed in the centre — the "pizza saver" — was patented in 1985 by Carmela Vitale of Dix Hills, New York, to keep the lid from touching the cheese.

Design

Boxes are typically made from single-wall corrugated fibreboard with fluting that traps air for insulation. Small vents or perforations allow excess steam to escape. Standard sizes correspond to pizza diameters, commonly 10, 12, 14, 16, and 18 inches. The flat, square shape is efficient to store as flat blanks and quick to fold.

Environmental concerns

Grease and food residue contaminate cardboard pizza boxes, limiting their recyclability in many municipal systems, though clean portions can be recycled and the boxes are widely compostable. Reusable delivery containers have been trialled as an alternative.

Other uses

The term "pizza box" is also used in computing for a flat, wide rack-mounted or desktop computer enclosure roughly the size and shape of a pizza box.
