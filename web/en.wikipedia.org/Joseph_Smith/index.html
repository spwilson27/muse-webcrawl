Joseph Smith

Born: December 23, 1805, Sharon, Vermont, U.S.
Died: June 27, 1844 (aged 38), Carthage, Illinois, U.S.
Known for: Founder of the Latter Day Saint movement; translating the Book of Mormon
Successor: Disputed (succession crisis); Brigham Young led the largest faction

Joseph Smith Jr. (December 23, 1805 – June 27, 1844) was an American religious leader and the founder of Mormonism and the Latter Day Saint movement. When he was 24, Smith published the Book of Mormon, which he described as an English translation of an ancient record inscribed on golden plates revealed to him by an angel. During his lifetime he attracted tens of thousands of followers and founded a religion that continues to grow worldwide.

Early life

Smith was born in Sharon, Vermont, and grew up in western New York amid the religious revivalism of the Second Great Awakening in the "burned-over district." He said that in 1820, after praying about which church to join, he was visited by God the Father and Jesus Christ — an event known to believers as the First Vision. In 1823, he said, the angel Moroni directed him to a buried book of golden plates, which he was permitted to retrieve in 1827.

Book of Mormon and church founding

Working chiefly with Oliver Cowdery as scribe, Smith dictated the Book of Mormon, published in March 1830. On April 6, 1830, he formally organized the Church of Christ (later the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints) in Fayette, New York. Smith presented himself as a prophet in the biblical mold, receiving revelations later collected in the Doctrine and Covenants.

Ohio, Missouri, and Illinois

The church gathered at Kirtland, Ohio, where it built a temple, and in Missouri, which Smith identified as the site of Zion. Violent conflict with Missouri settlers culminated in the 1838 Mormon War and Governor Lilburn Boggs's "extermination order," driving the Saints from the state. Smith then founded Nauvoo, Illinois, which grew into one of the state's largest cities; he served as its mayor and commanded its militia, the Nauvoo Legion. In Nauvoo he introduced controversial doctrines including plural marriage, practised privately by Smith himself.

Death and succession

In 1844 Smith announced a candidacy for President of the United States. After the Nauvoo city council ordered the destruction of the Nauvoo Expositor, a newspaper critical of him, Smith was arrested and held at Carthage Jail. On June 27, 1844, an armed mob stormed the jail and killed Smith and his brother Hyrum. His death triggered a succession crisis; the largest body of followers accepted Brigham Young and migrated to Utah, while others followed Smith's son Joseph Smith III in what became the Reorganized Church (now Community of Christ).
