Llywelyn ap Gruffudd

Llywelyn ap Gruffudd (c. 1223 – 11 December 1282), often known as Llywelyn the Last (Welsh: Llywelyn Ein Llyw Olaf, "Llywelyn, Our Last Leader"), was the last Welsh prince of a native line recognised as Prince of Wales. The grandson of Llywelyn the Great, he united much of Wales under his rule before his death in battle during Edward I of England's conquest of Wales.

Rise to power

Llywelyn inherited a portion of Gwynedd after the death of his uncle Dafydd ap Llywelyn in 1246, but the kingdom was weakened by the Treaty of Woodstock (1247) with Henry III of England. From 1255, after defeating his brothers in battle at Bryn Derwin, Llywelyn consolidated control of Gwynedd and then expanded his authority across Wales, exploiting Henry III's difficulties during the Barons' Wars. By the Treaty of Montgomery (1267), Henry recognised Llywelyn as Prince of Wales, receiving his homage and a large cash payment, in return for acknowledging his overlordship of the Welsh magnates.

Conflict with Edward I

Relations with the new English king Edward I deteriorated after 1272. Llywelyn refused repeated summonses to do homage and proceeded with plans to marry Eleanor de Montfort, daughter of Edward's old enemy Simon de Montfort; Eleanor was captured at sea in 1275. In 1277 Edward invaded with a massive army, forced Llywelyn's submission by the Treaty of Aberconwy, and confined his rule to Gwynedd west of the Conwy.

Death

In 1282 Llywelyn's brother Dafydd began a revolt, and Llywelyn, though initially reluctant, joined it. During the campaign he was killed on 11 December 1282 near Builth Wells, in circumstances that remain somewhat obscure — traditionally while separated from his main army. His head was sent to London and displayed at the Tower of London. With his death, and the execution of Dafydd the following year, independent Welsh princely rule ended and Wales was annexed by the Statute of Rhuddlan (1284).
