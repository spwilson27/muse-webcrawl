Finger Lakes
Location: New York, United States
Number of lakes: 11 principal lakes
Largest: Seneca Lake
Known for: Wine production, gorges, tourism

The Finger Lakes are a group of eleven long, narrow, roughly north–south lakes in west-central New York State, named for their resemblance to fingers on a map. They were carved by glaciers during the last ice age, which deepened pre-existing river valleys; Seneca and Cayuga are among the deepest lakes in the United States.

The lakes

From west to east the principal lakes are Conesus, Hemlock, Canadice, Honeoye, Canandaigua, Keuka, Seneca, Cayuga, Owasco, Skaneateles and Otisco.

Economy and culture

The region is New York's largest wine-producing area, noted especially for Riesling, aided by the lakes' moderating effect on climate. Tourism centres on lake towns, state parks with dramatic gorges such as Watkins Glen, and institutions including Cornell University in Ithaca on Cayuga Lake.
