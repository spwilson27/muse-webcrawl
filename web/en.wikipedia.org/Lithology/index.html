Lithology

Lithology
Branch of: geology, petrology
Studies: macroscopic rock properties
Key data: composition, texture, color, grain size
Applications: stratigraphy, petroleum geology, well logging

The lithology of a rock unit is a description of its physical characteristics visible at outcrop, in hand specimen or core samples, or with low magnification microscopy. Such characteristics include colour, texture, grain size, and mineral or clast composition. Lithology may refer either to a detailed description of these characteristics, or to a summary of the gross physical character of a rock — as in "sandstone lithology" or "shale lithology."

Contrasting lithologies in a sedimentary rock sequence

Description and classification

Lithological description forms the foundation of stratigraphy. Each distinguishable rock type — a lithofacies when defined by appearance and depositional character — is recorded in stratigraphic columns and well logs. Common lithologies include sandstone, siltstone, mudstone and shale, conglomerate, limestone and dolomite among sedimentary rocks; granite, basalt, gabbro and rhyolite among igneous rocks; and slate, schist, gneiss and marble among metamorphic rocks.

In subsurface geology

In petroleum geology and hydrogeology, lithology is inferred from drill cuttings and geophysical well logs (gamma ray, resistivity, density and neutron logs). Lithological interpretation distinguishes reservoir rocks (typically porous sandstones and carbonates) from seals (shales and evaporites), and guides correlation between wells. "Mud logging" produces a continuous lithological record while drilling.

Lithostratigraphy

Lithostratigraphy organises rock bodies into named units — supergroups, groups, formations, members and beds — based solely on lithological character, independent of age. The formation is the fundamental unit: a mappable body of rock with distinctive lithology formed under conditions stable enough to give it a recognisable character.
