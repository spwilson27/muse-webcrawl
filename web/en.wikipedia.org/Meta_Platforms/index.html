Meta Platforms, Inc., doing business as Meta and formerly named Facebook, Inc., is an American multinational technology conglomerate based in Menlo Park, California. It owns and operates Facebook, Instagram, Threads, WhatsApp, and Messenger, among other products and services, and is one of the world's most valuable companies, counted among the "Big Five" American technology companies alongside Alphabet, Amazon, Apple, and Microsoft.


Meta headquarters, Menlo Park

History

Facebook was founded in 2004 by Mark Zuckerberg with fellow Harvard students Eduardo Saverin, Andrew McCollum, Dustin Moskovitz, and Chris Hughes. The social network expanded from universities to the general public in 2006 and held its initial public offering in May 2012. The company acquired Instagram (2012, about $1 billion), WhatsApp (2014, about $19 billion), and virtual-reality maker Oculus (2014). In October 2021 the parent company rebranded to Meta to signal its focus on building the "metaverse," while the social network retained the Facebook name. Reality Labs, the metaverse division, accumulated heavy losses even as the advertising business generated most revenue.

Products and business

Meta's "Family of Apps" reports roughly 4 billion monthly users across its services. Nearly all revenue derives from advertising sold largely through Facebook and Instagram. The company develops the Quest line of VR headsets, smart glasses with Ray-Ban, the Horizon virtual worlds, and large language models in the open-weight Llama family. Threads, launched in July 2023 as a text-based companion to Instagram, gained 100 million users within days.

Criticism

Meta has faced sustained scrutiny over data privacy (notably the Cambridge Analytica scandal of 2018), misinformation and election integrity, effects on teenagers' mental health revealed by the 2021 Facebook Files disclosures, antitrust suits from the U.S. Federal Trade Commission, and large fines under European privacy law, including a €1.2 billion GDPR fine in 2023.
