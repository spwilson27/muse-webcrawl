Library of Hadrian

The Library of Hadrian was created by the Roman Emperor Hadrian in AD 132 on the north side of the Acropolis of Athens, near the Roman Agora. According to Pausanias, the building had a hundred columns of Phrygian marble, pavilions with gilded ceilings, and rooms for books, lectures and reading.

Architecture

The complex measured about 122 by 82 metres, built around a central courtyard with a garden and pool, surrounded by colonnades. Books in the form of papyrus scrolls were stored in niches in the eastern rooms. The monumental western facade, with its projecting columns, is the best-preserved part today.

Later history

The library was damaged in the Herulian invasion of AD 267 and repaired in the 5th century; churches were later built within its courtyard, and in the Ottoman period it housed the seat of the voivode of Athens. Excavations have progressively uncovered the complex, which now stands in the Monastiraki district.
