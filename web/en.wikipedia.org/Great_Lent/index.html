Great Lent

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Great Lent (Greek: Μεγάλη Τεσσαρακοστή, "Great Forty Days") is the most important fasting season of the Eastern Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox, and Eastern Catholic churches. It prepares the faithful for Holy Week and Pascha (Easter) through prayer, fasting, and almsgiving, commemorating Christ's forty days of fasting in the desert.

Structure and dates

Great Lent begins on Clean Monday, seven weeks before Pascha, and runs through the Friday before Palm Sunday; together with Holy Week, which is reckoned separately, the full period of fasting lasts until the midnight service of Holy Saturday. Sundays of Lent have individual themes: the Triumph of Orthodoxy (first), St Gregory Palamas (second), the Veneration of the Cross (third), St John Climacus (fourth), and St Mary of Egypt (fifth). The weekdays are marked by the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts, and the Great Canon of St Andrew of Crete is sung in the first and fifth weeks.

Fasting practice

Traditional observance abstains from meat, dairy products, and eggs throughout the season, and from fish, wine, and oil on most weekdays, with relaxation on weekends and feasts. The fasting is accompanied by intensified prayer — including the Prayer of St Ephraim with prostrations — confession, and charitable works. The season opens with Forgiveness Vespers on the Sunday before Clean Monday, at which worshippers ask one another's forgiveness.
