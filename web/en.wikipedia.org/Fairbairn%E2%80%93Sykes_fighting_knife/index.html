Fairbairn–Sykes fighting knife

The Fairbairn–Sykes fighting knife is a double-edged fighting knife resembling a dagger or poignard, with a foil grip. It was designed by William Ewart Fairbairn and Eric Anthony Sykes in Shanghai in the 1930s and became famous during World War II.

TypeDagger
Place of originUnited Kingdom

Blade lengthapprox. 7 in (18 cm)

Design

The knife features a slender, stiletto-like blade optimized for thrusting, a wasp-waisted grip turned from brass or other metals, and a small S-shaped crossguard. Fairbairn and Sykes drew on their experience with the Shanghai Municipal Police and close-combat training to produce a weapon intended purely for fighting rather than utility.

Service

The F–S knife was standard issue to British Commandos and was also used by the Special Operations Executive, the Office of Strategic Services (whose OSS stiletto was closely modeled on it), airborne forces, and other special units. It remains an emblem of British commando forces and appears in the insignia of several units.
