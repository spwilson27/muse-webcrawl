The President of the Republic of Indonesia (Indonesian: Presiden Republik Indonesia) is the head of state and head of government of Indonesia, leading the executive branch and serving as commander-in-chief of the Indonesian National Armed Forces. The presidency was established on 18 August 1945, the day after the proclamation of Indonesian independence.

Powers and duties

Under the 1945 Constitution (as amended after 1998), the president holds executive authority, appoints and dismisses ministers, issues regulations, commands the armed forces, declares war and makes treaties with the approval of the People's Representative Council (DPR), grants clemency and amnesty, and proposes legislation. The president is assisted by a vice president, elected on the same ticket.

Election

Before 2004 presidents were elected by the People's Consultative Assembly (MPR). Constitutional reforms introduced direct elections: candidates run in pairs with a vice-presidential nominee, nominated by parties or coalitions meeting a threshold, and must win a majority with at least 20% of the vote in more than half of the provinces; otherwise a runoff is held. Terms last five years and a president may serve at most two terms.

History

Sukarno, the founding president, led Indonesia from 1945 through parliamentary democracy and his authoritarian "Guided Democracy" before being displaced after the upheavals of 1965–66. Suharto's New Order regime ruled from 1967/68 to 1998, when his resignation amid the Asian financial crisis opened the Reformasi era of democratization, constitutional amendment, and direct elections.

List of presidents

The presidents have been: (1) Sukarno (1945–1967), (2) Suharto (1967–1998), (3) B. J. Habibie (1998–1999), (4) Abdurrahman Wahid (1999–2001), (5) Megawati Sukarnoputri (2001–2004), (6) Susilo Bambang Yudhoyono (2004–2014), (7) Joko Widodo (2014–2024), and (8) Prabowo Subianto (from 20 October 2024).
