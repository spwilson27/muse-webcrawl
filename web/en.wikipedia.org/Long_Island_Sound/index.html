Long Island Sound is a tidal estuary of the Atlantic Ocean lying between the New York City metropolitan area and Long Island to the south and Connecticut to the north. It stretches about 110 miles from the East River in New York City eastward to the Race at its mouth near Fishers Island.

Geography and ecology

The Sound was formed as a glacial lake at the end of the last ice age and breached about 15,000 years ago. Fresh water enters mainly from the Connecticut, Housatonic and Thames rivers. It supports important fisheries for lobster (see Lobster), oysters, striped bass and menhaden, as well as extensive recreational boating and beaches along both shores.

Environment

Twentieth-century development around the Sound caused pollution and seasonal hypoxia in its western basin from excess nitrogen. Long-term restoration programmes by New York, Connecticut and the US Environmental Protection Agency have reduced nitrogen loads and improved water quality, though climate-driven warming remains a concern.
