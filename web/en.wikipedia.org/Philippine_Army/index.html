The Philippine Army is the main, oldest and largest branch of the Armed Forces of the Philippines, responsible for ground warfare. It traces its lineage to the revolutionary army of the Kataas-taasang, Kagalanggalangang Katipunan and the army of the First Philippine Republic, and in its modern form to the Philippine Commonwealth Army established in 1935.

History

The Army fought against Spain, then against the United States in the Philippine–American War, and was reconstituted under the National Defense Act of 1935 with American adviser Douglas MacArthur. Its divisions fought the Japanese invasion of 1941–42 at Bataan and Corregidor, and Filipino guerrilla units continued resistance during the occupation. After independence the Army conducted counterinsurgency campaigns against the Hukbalahap, the Moro separatist movements and the communist New People's Army, and contributed a battalion to the Korean War as part of PEFTOK.

Organization

The Army is organized into infantry divisions, an armor division, artillery, special forces and support units, and maintains a large reserve force.
