Pillarisation

Pillarisation (Dutch: verzuiling) is the vertical division of a society into separate "pillars" (Dutch: zuilen) according to religious or ideological affiliation, each with its own institutions — schools, universities, newspapers, broadcasting organisations, political parties, trade unions, hospitals and sports clubs.

The Netherlands

The classic case is the Netherlands, where society was organised from the late 19th century into Catholic, Protestant, socialist and liberal (or "general") pillars. Political elites cooperated across pillar lines in a system of consociational democracy, while ordinary citizens lived largely within their own pillar. From the 1960s the system rapidly eroded in a process of depillarisation (ontzuiling).

Elsewhere

Related forms of segmentation appeared in Belgium, Austria and Northern Ireland, and the concept is used more broadly for societies divided into self-contained communal blocs.
