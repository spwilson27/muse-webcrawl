Tag (metadata)

In information systems, a tag is a keyword or term assigned to a piece of information (such as a digital file, web page, or social media post). This kind of metadata helps describe an item and allows it to be found again by browsing or searching. Tags are usually chosen informally and personally by the item's creator or viewer, in contrast to formal classification schemes imposed by an institution.

A tag cloud, a common visualization of tag metadata

Tagging and folksonomies

The practice of collaborative tagging became widespread in the mid-2000s with social bookmarking services such as del.icio.us, photo-sharing sites such as Flickr, and later blogging and social media platforms. Thomas Vander Wal coined the term folksonomy for the resulting user-generated classification systems, which emerge from the aggregate choices of many taggers rather than a controlled vocabulary. Hashtags—tags prefixed with the # symbol, popularized on Twitter from 2007 onward—extended tagging into the text of posts themselves.

Uses

Tags support navigation and discovery, personal information management, and content recommendation. In software development, tags mark releases in version control; in digital music and photo libraries, metadata tags store artist, album, or subject information. Search engines and content platforms use tags as signals for indexing and ranking.

Comparison with classification

Unlike hierarchical taxonomies, tags are flat and non-exclusive: an item can carry any number of tags, and the same tag can be applied to very different items. This flexibility makes tagging cheap and adaptable but introduces synonymy (multiple tags for one concept), polysemy (one tag with several meanings), and inconsistency in spelling and granularity.
