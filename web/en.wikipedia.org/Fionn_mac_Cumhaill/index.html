Fionn mac Cumhaill (pronounced [fʲɪn̪ˠ mˠək ˈkuːwəl̪ʲ]; Old and Middle Irish: Find or Finn mac Umaill), often rendered in English as Finn MacCool or Finn MacCumhail, is a mythic hunter-warrior in Irish mythology, occurring also in the mythologies of Scotland and the Isle of Man. The stories of Fionn and his followers, the Fianna, form the Fenian Cycle (an Fhiannaíocht), much of it narrated in the voice of Fionn's son, the poet Oisín.

The Salmon of Knowledge

In the most famous story of his youth, the boy Fionn (born Deimne) was sent to study with the poet Finn Eces. The poet had spent seven years trying to catch the Salmon of Knowledge, a fish that contained all the knowledge of the world, living in the River Boyne. When he finally caught it, he instructed the boy to cook it while forbidding him to eat any. Fionn burned his thumb on the hot salmon and instinctively sucked it — thereby gaining the salmon's wisdom. Ever after, he could summon that knowledge by biting his thumb.

Leader of the Fianna

Fionn became leader of the Fianna, a band of roaming warriors in the service of the High King of Ireland, after proving himself by slaying Aillen, a fire-breathing being of the sídhe who burned Tara every Samhain. His adventures include the pursuit of Diarmuid and Gráinne, who eloped on the eve of Gráinne's wedding to the ageing Fionn, and tales of his hounds Bran and Sceólang. In folklore he is credited with building the Giant's Causeway in County Antrim as stepping-stones to Scotland.

Death and legacy

The circumstances of Fionn's death are variously told; in some traditions he did not die at all but sleeps in a cave beneath Dublin, ready to rise and defend Ireland in her hour of greatest need. His name survives in James Macpherson's Ossian poems and in countless Irish place names and legends.
