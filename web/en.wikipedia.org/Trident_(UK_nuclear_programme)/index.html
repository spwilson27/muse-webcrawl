Trident (UK nuclear programme)

Trident, also known as the Trident nuclear programme or Trident nuclear deterrent, covers the development, procurement and operation of the United Kingdom's nuclear weapons system based on submarine-launched ballistic missiles. It replaced the earlier Polaris system and has provided the UK's continuous at-sea deterrent since the 1990s.

System

The programme centres on four Vanguard-class ballistic missile submarines based at HMNB Clyde (Faslane) in Scotland, each armed with up to 16 US-designed Trident II D5 missiles maintained under a shared arrangement with the United States Navy; the warheads are designed and manufactured in the UK at the Atomic Weapons Establishment, Aldermaston. Since 1969, under Operation Relentless, at least one armed submarine has been on patrol at all times (Continuous At-Sea Deterrent, CASD).

History and renewal

The decision to acquire Trident was taken by the Thatcher government in 1980–82, initially selecting the Trident I C4 and then the more capable D5. The first Vanguard-class boat entered service in 1994. In July 2016 the House of Commons voted to renew the deterrent with four Dreadnought-class submarines, which began construction at Barrow-in-Furness and are expected to enter service from the early 2030s. The UK stockpile policy announced in 2021 raised the warhead cap to no more than 260.

Debate

Trident has been politically contentious since its inception, opposed by disarmament campaigners (such as CND) and by the Scottish National Party, which seeks removal of the submarines from Scotland; supporters argue it is the ultimate guarantee of national security and sustains skilled employment.
