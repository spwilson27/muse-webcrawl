Douglas Adams, author of The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy

Douglas Noel Adams (11 March 1952 – 11 May 2001) was an English author, humourist, and screenwriter, best known as the creator of The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy (HHGTTG). Originally a 1978 BBC radio comedy broadcast, The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy developed into a "trilogy" of five books that sold more than 15 million copies in his lifetime. It was also turned into a television series, several stage plays, comics, a video game, and a 2005 feature film.

Early life and education

Adams was born in Cambridge and educated at Brentwood School in Essex and St John's College, Cambridge, where he read English literature. At Cambridge he joined the Footlights revue group and met future collaborators. After university he worked as a writer for the BBC and as script editor for Doctor Who, writing episodes including "The Pirate Planet" and "City of Death" (with Graham Williams, under the pseudonym David Agnew).

The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy

The radio series premiered on BBC Radio 4 in March 1978. Its hero, Arthur Dent, is rescued from Earth moments before its demolition by a Vogon constructor fleet making way for a hyperspace bypass, and travels the galaxy with Ford Prefect, Zaphod Beeblebrox, Trillian and the chronically depressed robot Marvin. The series is famous for the number 42 — the "Answer to the Ultimate Question of Life, the Universe, and Everything" — and phrases such as "Don't Panic" and "mostly harmless." Adams's five novels are The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy (1979), The Restaurant at the End of the Universe (1980), Life, the Universe and Everything (1982), So Long, and Thanks for All the Fish (1984), and Mostly Harmless (1992).

Other work

Adams wrote the Dirk Gently novels Dirk Gently's Holistic Detective Agency (1987) and The Long Dark Tea-Time of the Soul (1988), co-wrote The Meaning of Liff (1983) with John Lloyd, and with Mark Carwardine wrote Last Chance to See (1990), a book and radio series about endangered species reflecting his passion for conservation. He co-founded the digital media company The Digital Village and designed the computer adventure game Starship Titanic. He was also an advocate of the Macintosh computer and a keen amateur guitarist.

Death and legacy

Adams died of a heart attack on 11 May 2001 in Montecito, California, aged 49. His unfinished work was published posthumously as The Salmon of Doubt (2002). Fans commemorate him annually on Towel Day, 25 May. Asteroid 18610 Arthurdent is named after his best-known character, and asteroid 25924 Douglasadams bears his name.
