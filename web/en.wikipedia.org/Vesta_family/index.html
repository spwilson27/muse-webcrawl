Vesta family

The Vesta family (FIN: 401) is a family of asteroids in the inner asteroid belt. It is one of the largest asteroid families, with over 15,000 known members, and is named after its largest member, 4 Vesta, which at about 530 kilometres in diameter is the second-most-massive object in the asteroid belt.

Origin

The family is believed to have formed from fragments ejected by an enormous impact on Vesta roughly one to two billion years ago, which excavated the Rheasilvia crater covering much of Vesta's southern hemisphere. Members are predominantly bright V-type asteroids with basaltic compositions resembling the HED meteorites (howardites, eucrites and diogenites), many of which are thought to be samples of Vesta delivered to Earth.

Interlopers

Because the family lies in a densely populated region of the belt, a number of unrelated asteroids share similar orbital elements and are considered interlopers. NASA's Dawn spacecraft orbited Vesta in 2011–2012, greatly improving knowledge of the parent body's geology.
