Hiking

Hiking is a long, vigorous walk, usually on trails or footpaths in the countryside. Walking for pleasure developed in the eighteenth century and arose because of changing attitudes to the landscape and nature. In the United States and the United Kingdom the term normally describes a day or overnight walk in natural surroundings, while related terms include trekking for multi-day journeys, bushwalking in Australia, and tramping in New Zealand. Overnight hiking with camping gear is commonly called backpacking.

History

Walking in wild landscapes for pleasure is largely a product of Romanticism: poets such as William Wordsworth, who walked some 175,000 miles in his lifetime by his own estimate, made pedestrian travel a literary ideal. In the nineteenth century, clubs such as the Alpine Club (1857) and the Sierra Club (1892) organized recreational walking and campaigned for access and conservation. Mass movements such as Germany's Wandervogel and Britain's ramblers — whose 1932 Kinder Scout trespass helped win public access rights — made hiking a mass pastime.

Equipment and safety

Hikers are advised to carry the "Ten Essentials": navigation, light, sun protection, first aid, knife, fire starter, emergency shelter, extra food, extra water, and extra clothing. Principal hazards include getting lost, hypothermia and heat illness, dehydration, lightning, stream crossings, and wildlife. The Leave No Trace principles — pack out litter, stay on durable surfaces, respect wildlife — guide low-impact practice.

Long-distance trails

Marked long-distance routes include the Appalachian Trail (about 2,200 miles) and Pacific Crest Trail (2,650 miles) in the United States, the pilgrimage network of the Camino de Santiago in Spain, the Tour du Mont Blanc in the Alps, and the Great Baikal and Shikoku Pilgrimage routes. Completing an entire trail in one journey is called thru-hiking; the Appalachian Trail Conservancy records roughly 3,000 attempts each year, with about one in four completing the trail.

Health

Hiking is a moderate-intensity aerobic exercise associated with cardiovascular fitness, improved mood, and reduced stress; uneven terrain also exercises balance and lower-body strength.
