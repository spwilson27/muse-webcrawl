Brunhes–Matuyama reversal

The Brunhes–Matuyama reversal was the most recent reversal of Earth's magnetic field, occurring approximately 781,000 years ago. It marks the boundary between the Matuyama Chron of reversed polarity and the present Brunhes Chron of normal polarity.

The reversal is named for geophysicists Bernard Brunhes, who discovered reversed magnetisation in lavas in 1906, and Motonori Matuyama, who showed that older rocks were reversely magnetised. The transition itself took several thousand years.

The boundary is a key global stratigraphic marker, recorded in deep-sea sediment cores and volcanic rocks worldwide, and is used to date Quaternary sediments and archaeological sites.
