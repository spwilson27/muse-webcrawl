Poet Laureate of the United Kingdom

Incumbent: Simon Armitage (since 2019)
Appointer: The monarch, on the advice of the Prime Minister
Term: 10 years (since 1999)
First holder: John Dryden (1668)

The Poet Laureate of the United Kingdom is an honorary position appointed by the monarch of the United Kingdom, traditionally on the advice of the prime minister. The role does not entail any specific duties, but there is an expectation that a holder will write verse for significant national occasions. The origins of the laureateship date back to 1616 when Ben Jonson received a pension from James I; the first official holder of the title was John Dryden, appointed in 1668.

History

Laureates were originally appointed for life; holders included William Wordsworth, Alfred, Lord Tennyson (the longest-serving, 1850–1892), and John Betjeman. Since Andrew Motion's appointment in 1999 the term has been fixed at ten years. Carol Ann Duffy, appointed in 2009, was the first woman and first Scot to hold the post; she was succeeded by Simon Armitage in 2019.

Remuneration

Traditionally the laureate received a stipend and a butt of sack (wine). Ted Hughes re-established the wine custom; today the honorarium is supplemented by payments for commissioned work, and the laureate receives a barrel of sherry.

List of poets laureate (selected)
