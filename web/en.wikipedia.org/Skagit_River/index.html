Skagit River

Skagit River Source: Allison Pass, British Columbia
Mouth: Skagit Bay, Puget Sound
Length: approx. 240 km (150 mi)
Dams: Gorge, Diablo, Ross

The Skagit River is a river in southwestern British Columbia and northwestern Washington in the United States that drains into Puget Sound. Approximately 240 kilometres long, it rises in the Canadian Cascades of British Columbia and flows southwest through the North Cascades of Washington, past the towns of Marblemount, Concrete and Sedro-Woolley, before splitting into forks and emptying into Skagit Bay near Mount Vernon.

Hydroelectric development

The upper river is impounded by the Skagit River Hydroelectric Project, a series of three dams — Gorge Dam, Diablo Dam and Ross Dam — owned by Seattle City Light and built between the 1920s and 1950s. Ross Dam creates Ross Lake, which extends into British Columbia. The project supplies a substantial share of Seattle's electricity and its facilities and the company town of Newhalem lie within the Ross Lake National Recreation Area, adjacent to North Cascades National Park.

Ecology

The Skagit is the largest and most biologically diverse river draining into Puget Sound. It supports all five species of Pacific salmon as well as steelhead and bull trout, and is the only river in Washington with healthy populations of all Pacific salmon species. Its estuary and the surrounding Skagit Valley farmland host large wintering populations of trumpeter and tundra swans and snow geese, and the river is famous for one of the largest winter concentrations of bald eagles in the contiguous United States, drawn by spawning chum salmon.
