Magtymguly Pyragy (born Magtymguly, son of the poet Döwletmämmet Azady) was an 18th-century Turkmen poet, philosopher, and spiritual teacher. He is revered as the founder of Turkmen classical literature and the national poet of Turkmenistan; his poems have been translated into many languages and are woven into Turkmen cultural life.

Monument to Magtymguly in Ashgabat.

Life and work

Magtymguly was born in the Gonbad-e Qabus region near the Kopet Dag mountains, in what is today Iran's Golestan province, and studied at madrasas in Khiva and Bukhara. He travelled widely in the Turkmen lands and beyond. Breaking with the courtly Persianate style of earlier Turkmen writing, he composed in a language close to spoken Turkmen, addressing ordinary people. Of the many poems attributed to him, around 800 are considered authentic; they treat themes of patriotism and Turkmen unity, morality, love, nature, and Sufi devotion, and many were set to music and performed by traditional dutar players (bagşy).

Legacy

Magtymguly's birthday is celebrated in Turkmenistan as part of the national Day of Revival, Unity and the Poetry of Magtymguly Pyragy. His image appears on Turkmen currency and monuments, and the 300th anniversary of his birth in 2024 was marked internationally, including by UNESCO.
