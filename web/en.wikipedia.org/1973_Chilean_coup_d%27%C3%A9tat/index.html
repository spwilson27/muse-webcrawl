The 1973 Chilean coup d'état was a military overthrow of the democratically elected socialist government of President Salvador Allende on 11 September 1973. Led by Army commander-in-chief Augusto Pinochet and the commanders of the navy, air force and Carabineros, the coup ended three years of the Popular Unity government and began a seventeen-year military dictatorship.

Background

Allende's programme of nationalisation and land reform, economic crisis and inflation, strikes, and fierce opposition in Congress produced escalating polarisation. The United States government, hostile to Allende's socialism, funded opposition groups and pressured Chile's economy; a first failed coup attempt, the Tanquetazo, came in June 1973. In August the Chamber of Deputies accused the government of constitutional violations, and Allende appointed Pinochet — thought loyal — as army commander.

The coup

On the morning of 11 September the navy seized Valparaíso and the armed forces demanded Allende's resignation. Hawker Hunter jets of the air force bombed the presidential palace, La Moneda, in Santiago. Allende refused to surrender and died in the palace during the assault, by suicide according to the official account and most later investigations. By nightfall the junta controlled the country.

Aftermath

The junta dissolved Congress, banned parties, and launched a campaign of repression in which thousands were executed, disappeared or imprisoned, and tens of thousands tortured, as documented by the Rettig and Valech commissions. Pinochet ruled until 1990, when Chile returned to democracy following the 1988 plebiscite.
