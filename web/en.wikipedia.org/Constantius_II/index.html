Constantius II

Constantius II (Latin: Flavius Julius Constantius; 7 August 317 – 3 November 361) was Roman emperor from 337 to 361. His reign saw constant warfare on the borders against the Sasanian Empire and Germanic peoples, while internally the Roman Empire struggled with the problem of the Christian church's Arian controversy.

Rise to power

The second son of Constantine the Great and Fausta, Constantius was elevated to caesar in 324. On his father's death in 337, he became emperor of the eastern provinces together with his brothers Constantine II and Constans, after a purge of other male relatives. Constantine II died in 340 and Constans in 350, leaving Constantius to defeat the usurper Magnentius at the battles of Mursa Major (351) and Mons Seleucus (353), becoming sole emperor.

Wars and administration

Constantius campaigned repeatedly against the Sasanian king Shapur II in Mesopotamia and appointed his cousins Gallus and then Julian as caesars to govern Gaul. In 357 Julian defeated the Alamanni at Strasbourg. Constantius celebrated a triumph in Rome in 357 and installed the great obelisk now in the Piazza San Giovanni in Laterano. He died of fever at Mopsucrene in Cilicia in 361 while moving to confront Julian, whom he named as his successor on his deathbed.

Religious policy

Constantius favoured the Arian (or "homoian") party in the church, exiling Nicene bishops such as Athanasius of Alexandria and Liberius of Rome, and convening councils, notably the twin councils of Rimini and Seleucia (359), to impose a compromise creed. His religious policies left the church deeply divided.
