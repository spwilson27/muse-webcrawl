Osteosarcoma


Type: Primary malignant bone tumour
Typical sites: Metaphyses of long bones, especially around the knee
Peak age: Adolescents and young adults

Osteosarcoma, also called osteogenic sarcoma, is a cancerous tumour arising in bone, in which the malignant cells produce immature bone (osteoid). It is the most common primary cancer of bone in children and adolescents, with a peak incidence during the teenage growth spurt, and usually develops in the metaphyses of long bones — most often the distal femur, proximal tibia and proximal humerus.

Signs, diagnosis and spread

The typical presentation is localised pain, often worse at night, followed by swelling or a palpable mass; occasionally the first sign is a pathological fracture. X-rays may show a mixed destructive and bone-forming lesion with a periosteal reaction such as the Codman triangle or a "sunburst" pattern, but diagnosis requires biopsy. Osteosarcoma spreads most often to the lungs. Risk is increased in hereditary retinoblastoma, Li–Fraumeni syndrome, Paget disease of bone and after radiation exposure.

Treatment and prognosis

Modern treatment combines multi-agent chemotherapy (commonly methotrexate, doxorubicin and cisplatin) with surgical removal of the tumour, increasingly by limb-salvage procedures rather than amputation. Five-year survival for localised disease treated with chemotherapy is roughly 60–70%, while metastatic disease at diagnosis carries a poorer prognosis.
