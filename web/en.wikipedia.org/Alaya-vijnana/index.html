Alaya-vijnana

Ālaya-vijñāna (Sanskrit: आलयविज्ञान, "storehouse consciousness") is a central concept in the Yogācāra school of Mahāyāna Buddhism. It is the eighth and foundational level of consciousness, a repository in which the karmic "seeds" (bīja) of all past experiences are stored, and from which the other, active forms of consciousness arise.

Doctrine

Yogācāra philosophy, systematised by Asaṅga and Vasubandhu in the 4th–5th centuries CE, distinguishes eight consciousnesses: the five sense consciousnesses, the mental consciousness (mano-vijñāna), the afflicted mentation (kliṣṭa-manas), and the ālaya-vijñāna. The ālaya receives impressions from experience — it is "perfumed" (vāsanā) by actions of body, speech and mind — and stores them as seeds that later ripen as new experiences, thereby explaining karmic continuity and memory without recourse to a permanent self (ātman).

Function in rebirth

Because the ālaya persists as a flowing continuum from moment to moment and from life to life, it accounts for the continuity of an individual's karmic history across rebirths. In Yogācāra soteriology, enlightenment involves the "overturning of the basis" (āśraya-parāvṛtti), in which the storehouse consciousness is transformed and ceases to function as a storehouse.

Interpretations

The concept has been compared with the unconscious in Western psychology, and in later traditions influenced by tathāgatagarbha thought it was sometimes identified with the buddha-nature, an identification debated within Buddhist scholasticism.
