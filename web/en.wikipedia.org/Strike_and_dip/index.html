Strike and dip

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Block diagram showing strike and dip of an inclined rock layer.

In geology, strike and dip describe the orientation of a planar feature such as a sedimentary bed, fault plane or joint. Together they fully specify the attitude of the plane in three-dimensional space.

Definitions

The strike is the compass bearing of a horizontal line on the plane — the line of intersection between the plane and a horizontal surface. The dip is the angle between the plane and the horizontal, measured in the vertical plane perpendicular to strike, together with the direction in which the plane descends. A bed might be recorded, for example, as striking 045° and dipping 30° to the southeast.

Measurement

Geologists measure strike and dip in the field with a geological compass-clinometer such as a Brunton compass. By convention, the "right-hand rule" variant records strike so that the plane dips to the right of the strike direction. On geological maps, strike and dip are shown by a T-shaped symbol: the long bar aligned with strike and the short tick pointing down-dip, labelled with the dip angle.

Apparent dip

A cross-section cut at an angle to the strike direction shows an apparent dip, always less than the true dip; the two are related by a simple trigonometric formula used when interpreting sections and borehole data.
