8.2 kiloyear event

The 8.2 kiloyear event was an abrupt episode of global cooling around 8,200 years before present, the largest climatic downturn of the Holocene epoch. Central Greenland temperatures fell by roughly 3 °C, and the disturbance lasted on the order of 150 years.

Mechanism

Scientists link the event to the catastrophic drainage of the proglacial lakes Agassiz and Ojibway after the collapse of the Laurentide Ice Sheet's Hudson Bay ice dome. The enormous freshwater influx into the North Atlantic slowed deep-water formation and the Atlantic meridional overturning circulation.

Global impact

Proxy records show cooler, drier conditions across the North Atlantic region, parts of Asia, and tropical Africa, with weakened monsoons. Archaeologists have suggested the event stressed early Neolithic farming societies and influenced migration and settlement in Southwest Asia and Europe.
