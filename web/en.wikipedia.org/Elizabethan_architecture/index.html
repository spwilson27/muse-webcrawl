Elizabethan architecture

Period: Reign of Elizabeth I, 1558–1603

Noted examples: Hardwick Hall, Burghley House, Longleat House

Elizabethan architecture refers to buildings of a certain medieval style constructed during the reign of Queen Elizabeth I of England (1558–1603). Historically, the era corresponds to the Cinquecento in Italy, the Early Renaissance in France, and the Plateresque style in Spain. In England the style blended late Gothic survivals with classical ornament drawn from continental pattern books.

Characteristics

The great "prodigy houses" of Elizabethan courtiers were designed to impress the queen on her progresses. They favored symmetry, large mullioned and transomed windows — "more glass than wall", as was said of Hardwick Hall — tall chimneys, strapwork decoration, heraldic display, and great halls giving way to long galleries. Towers, turrets, and fanciful gables enlivened the skyline, while classical orders were applied with more enthusiasm than archaeological accuracy.

Notable buildings

Principal examples include Longleat House in Wiltshire, Burghley House in Lincolnshire, Wollaton Hall in Nottingham, Montacute House in Somerset, and Hardwick Hall in Derbyshire, built for Bess of Hardwick. The style shaded into Jacobean architecture after 1603, before the fully classical work of Inigo Jones transformed English building.

Revival

The nineteenth century saw an Elizabethan Revival, and the style influenced country houses and public buildings in Britain and its former colonies.
