Far future in fiction

The far future has been used as a setting in many works of science fiction and fantasy. Stories set thousands, millions, or even billions of years ahead explore the evolution of humanity, the heat death of the universe, and the long-term fate of Earth and the Sun.

Early examples include H. G. Wells's The Time Machine (1895), which travels to AD 802,701 and beyond to a dying Earth. Olaf Stapledon's Last and First Men (1930) and Star Maker (1937) span billions of years. Later works include the Dying Earth subgenre pioneered by Jack Vance, Gene Wolfe's The Book of the New Sun, and Stephen Baxter's Xeelee Sequence.
