Dorset


Durdle Door, a natural limestone arch on the Dorset coast.

Dorset is a ceremonial county in South West England on the English Channel coast. It is bordered by Devon to the west, Somerset and Wiltshire to the north, and Hampshire to the east. The county town is Dorchester, and its largest town is Bournemouth. Dorset is known for its coastline, rural landscape, and literary associations.

Geography

The county covers about 2,653 square kilometres. Much of its coast forms part of the Jurassic Coast, a UNESCO World Heritage Site noted for its fossil-rich cliffs spanning 185 million years of geology. Inland, chalk downland, heath, and the Blackmore Vale dominate the landscape. Notable landmarks include Durdle Door, Lulworth Cove, and the chalk figure of the Cerne Abbas Giant.

History

Dorset was settled in prehistoric times; Maiden Castle near Dorchester is one of the largest Iron Age hill forts in Europe. The county played a role in the English Civil War and later in the story of the Tolpuddle Martyrs, agricultural labourers deported in 1834 for forming a trade union. Novelist Thomas Hardy set his fictional "Wessex" largely in Dorset, calling it "the Vale of the Little Dairies."
