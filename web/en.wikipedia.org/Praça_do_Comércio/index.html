Praça do Comércio

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
The square opens south onto the Tagus; the Arco da Rua Augusta closes its northern side.

The Praça do Comércio (Portuguese for "Commerce Square") is a grand waterfront square in Lisbon, Portugal, long known to English speakers as Terreiro do Paço ("Palace Yard"). It occupies the site of the Ribeira Palace, the royal residence from the early 16th century until the palace's destruction in the Lisbon earthquake of 1 November 1755. Rebuilt under the direction of the Marquis of Pombal as part of the reconstruction of the Baixa district, the square is one of the largest in Europe, measuring roughly 170 by 170 metres, and remains a symbolic gateway to the Portuguese capital.

Praça do Comércio

Location: Baixa, Lisbon, Portugal

Former use: Site of the Ribeira Palace

Rebuilt: After 1755, to designs by Eugénio dos Santos and Carlos Mardel

Key features: Arco da Rua Augusta; statue of King José I

Description

The square is lined on three sides by uniform yellow-painted arcaded buildings, built in the sober Pombaline style, which historically housed government ministries; the fourth side opens to the broad estuary of the Tagus. On the northern side, the triumphal Arco da Rua Augusta — designed in the 18th century, completed in 1873, and surmounted by allegorical figures including Glory crowning Genius and Valour — leads into the Rua Augusta, the main street of the rebuilt downtown. In the centre stands the bronze equestrian statue of King José I, inaugurated in 1775; it was designed by Joaquim Machado de Castro and was the first equestrian statue cast in Portugal.

History

The square witnessed one of Portugal's most traumatic political events: on 1 February 1908, King Carlos I and his heir, Luís Filipe, were assassinated here by republican gunmen as their carriage crossed the square. Two years later the monarchy fell. In the later 20th century the square served as a vast car park, but it has since been pedestrianised, with cafés (including the historic Martinho da Arcada, once frequented by the poet Fernando Pessoa), exhibitions, and the departure point for river ferries and trams. The equestrian statue and square remain a standard meeting point and backdrop for national celebrations, including New Year fireworks over the Tagus.
