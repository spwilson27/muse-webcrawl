Racah W-coefficient

Racah W-coefficient
Field: Quantum mechanics
Introduced by: Giulio Racah (1942)
Related to: 6-j symbol, angular momentum coupling

Racah W-coefficients were introduced by the Italian-Israeli physicist Giulio Racah in 1942 in his work on the theory of complex atomic spectra. They arise in the recoupling of three angular momenta in quantum mechanics: when three angular momentum operators are added together, the result can be expressed in different coupling schemes, and the W-coefficients are the unitary transformation coefficients connecting those schemes.

Definition and relation to 6-j symbols

The Racah coefficient W(j1 j2 J j3; J12 J23) is closely related to Wigner's 6-j symbol, from which it differs only by a phase factor and normalisation: W = (−1)sum { 6-j }. Racah derived a closed algebraic formula for the coefficient as a finite sum, involving the triangle conditions that the coupled angular momenta must satisfy for the coefficient to be non-zero.

Applications

Racah coefficients are fundamental tools in atomic and nuclear spectroscopy, where they simplify calculations of energy levels and transition rates in many-electron atoms and in the nuclear shell model. Racah's broader programme — using group theory and tensor operators in spectroscopy — became known as the "Racah algebra" and remains standard in theoretical physics.
