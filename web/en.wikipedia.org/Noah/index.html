Noah


Noah, in the biblical account, builds an ark to preserve life through the Flood.

Noah is a biblical patriarch and the protagonist of the flood narrative in the Book of Genesis (chapters 5–9). In the account, God, seeing the wickedness of humankind, resolves to destroy creation by flood, but spares Noah — described as "a righteous man, blameless among the people of his time" — instructing him to build an ark and bring into it his family and pairs of every animal. After the waters recede, God makes a covenant with Noah, symbolised by the rainbow, never again to destroy the earth by flood. Noah is also known as the father of Shem, Ham, and Japheth, from whom the nations of the earth are said to descend, and as the first viticulturist: he plants a vineyard and becomes drunk on its wine, in the episode involving the curse of Ham (or of Canaan).

In Genesis

Noah is the tenth and last of the antediluvian patriarchs, a descendant of Seth through Lamech, and is said to have lived 950 years. Genesis 5 places his birth 1,056 years after creation in the Masoretic chronology. The flood story itself appears to combine material from the Yahwist and Priestly sources, with doublets in numbers of animals, chronology, and divine names. In the covenant of Genesis 9, Noah's descendants are permitted to eat meat but forbidden to consume blood, and the rainbow is set in the clouds as the sign of the covenant.

Other traditions

Noah appears in the Quran as Nuh, one of the major prophets, who preached to a people who rejected him before the flood destroyed them. He is mentioned frequently in the New Testament and in later Jewish literature such as the Book of Jubilees and 1 Enoch, which elaborate his story. Flood heroes closely parallel to Noah appear in much older Mesopotamian literature — Ziusudra in Sumerian tradition, Atrahasis, and Utnapishtim in the Epic of Gilgamesh — leading scholars to see the biblical account as part of a wider ancient Near Eastern flood tradition.

Table of Nations and legacy

The "Table of Nations" in Genesis 10 traces the peoples known to the ancient Israelites to Noah's three sons: Shem (ancestor of the Semitic peoples), Ham (of Egypt and Canaan), and Japheth (of peoples to the north and west). The story of Noah and the ark has been retold continuously in art, literature, and popular culture, and Mount Ararat in Turkey is traditionally identified as the ark's resting place.
