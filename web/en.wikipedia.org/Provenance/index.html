Provenance

Documented provenance supports authentication and lawful title.

Provenance is the chronology of the ownership, custody or location of a historical object, work of art or archive. The word derives from the French provenir, "to come from". A well-documented provenance can confirm authenticity, increase an object's value, and establish that it was not looted or illegally exported; gaps in the record — especially for European art between 1933 and 1945 — may indicate spoliation during the Nazi era and grounds for restitution claims.

In art and antiquities

Provenance research assembles bills of sale, auction catalogues, exhibition histories, inscriptions and labels on the object itself, and archival correspondence. Forged provenance is a recognised technique of art fraud, used to give looted antiquities or fake paintings a respectable history. Museums increasingly publish provenance information online and employ dedicated researchers, a practice spurred by the 1998 Washington Conference Principles on Nazi-confiscated art.

Other uses

In archival science, provenance (the principle of respect des fonds) holds that records from a single creator should be kept together. In computing and science, data provenance records the origins and transformations of data, supporting reproducibility. In geology, provenance analysis infers the source terrain of sedimentary rocks from their mineral composition, and the term is used in the food and wine industries for documented origin.
