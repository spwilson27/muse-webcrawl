Caracol is a large ancient Maya archaeological site in the Cayo District of Belize, near the border with Guatemala. Once one of the most powerful Maya city-states, Caracol covered an area of some 200 square kilometres and supported a population that may have exceeded 100,000 at its peak — larger than that of present-day Belize City.

History

Occupied from the Middle Preclassic period, Caracol rose to prominence in the Classic period. In AD 562 its ruler, Lord Water, defeated the great city of Tikal in a "star war" — a victory that helped plunge Tikal into a century-long decline. Caracol reached its greatest extent in the 7th century under K'an II, whose inscriptions record further victories, including over Naranjo. The city was largely abandoned by about AD 1050.

The site

The centrepiece is Caana ("sky palace"), a 43-metre-high pyramid complex that remains one of the tallest human-made structures in Belize. The site is also noted for its vast system of sacbeob (raised causeways) radiating into residential and agricultural zones, extensive terraced fields, and numerous carved stelae and altars. Its ancient name, recorded in inscriptions, appears to have been Uxwitza ("three hill place").
