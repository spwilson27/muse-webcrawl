Krishnadevaraya, emperor of Vijayanagara.

Krishnadevaraya (17 January 1471 – 17 October 1529) was an emperor of the Vijayanagara Empire, also known as the Karnata Empire, reigning from 1509 until his death in 1529. He was the third monarch of the Tuluva dynasty, and is considered to be one of the greatest rulers in Indian history. He ruled the largest empire in India after the decline of the Delhi Sultanate. Presiding over the empire at its zenith, he is regarded as an icon by many Indians. Krishnadevaraya earned the titles Kannada Rajya Rama Ramana ("Lord of the Kannada Empire"), Andhra Bhoja ("Bhoja for Telugu Literature") and Mooru Rayara Ganda ("King of Three Kings").

Reign and conquests

Krishnadevaraya became the dominant ruler of the peninsula of India by defeating the sultans of Bijapur, Golconda, the Bahmani Sultanate and the Gajapati forces of Odisha, becoming one of the most powerful Hindu rulers in India. His campaigns included the recovery of the Raichur Doab from Bijapur (1520), the conquest of the Gajapati kingdom as far as Cuttack, and victories over the Qutb Shahi sultan of Golconda. Portuguese travellers such as Domingo Paes and Fernão Nunes visited his capital and left celebrated accounts of its wealth and of the king's person and administration.

Administration and culture

The emperor was advised by the able minister Timmarusu and was known for an efficient administration, irrigation works and patronage of trade. He was a great patron of literature in several languages. His own Telugu work, the Amuktamalyada, is a classic of Telugu poetry. Eight eminent Telugu poets — the Ashtadiggajas, including Allasani Peddana and Tenali Ramakrishna — adorned his court. He built and endowed many temples, adding the great gopurams and halls at Hampi's Virupaksha and Vittala temples, and commissioned the famous Hazara Rama temple. Portuguese chroniclers ranked his capital among the great cities of the world.

Death and succession

Krishnadevaraya died in 1529 and was succeeded by his half-brother Achyuta Deva Raya. The empire he left was at its territorial and cultural peak, though internal rivalries after his death weakened it before its defeat at the Battle of Talikota in 1565.
