Greenwich

Greenwich is a town in southeast London, England, within the Royal Borough of Greenwich, on the south bank of the River Thames. It is world famous as the location of the Royal Observatory and the prime meridian (0° longitude), from which Greenwich Mean Time takes its name. Greenwich's maritime history is reflected in the preserved tea clipper Cutty Sark, the Old Royal Naval College and the National Maritime Museum; the historic centre was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1997 as Maritime Greenwich.

History

Settled since the Bronze Age and recorded as Grenewic in the Saxon period, Greenwich became a royal residence under the Tudors: Henry VIII was born at the Palace of Placentia, as were Mary I and Elizabeth I. Charles II founded the Royal Observatory in 1675, commissioning John Flamsteed as the first Astronomer Royal. The International Meridian Conference of 1884 adopted the Greenwich meridian as the world's prime meridian. Greenwich Park, one of London's royal parks, offers celebrated views over the river to Canary Wharf. Queen Anne gave her name to the Queen's House, designed by Inigo Jones, the first Palladian building in England.
