Inspector general

An inspector general is an official responsible for conducting audits, investigations and inspections within a government agency, military organisation or other large institution, in order to detect fraud, waste, abuse and misconduct and to promote efficiency. Inspectors general are intended to operate with a measure of independence from the management of the bodies they oversee, typically reporting both to the head of the agency and to the legislature.

United States

The modern US federal inspector general system was created by the Inspector General Act of 1978, passed in the wake of Watergate-era scandals. The Act established Offices of Inspector General (OIGs) in major cabinet departments and agencies; there are now more than seventy federal OIGs. Most federal inspectors general are appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate, and can be removed by the President, who must notify Congress. Each OIG conducts independent audits and criminal or administrative investigations, issues semiannual reports to Congress, and operates hotlines for whistleblowers. The Council of the Inspectors General on Integrity and Efficiency (CIGIE) coordinates the community. State and local governments in the US have also adopted the model, as have many corporations and universities.

Military usage

In military contexts the title is older: an inspector general is a senior officer charged with inspecting units, investigating complaints and reporting on discipline, readiness and morale. The Inspector General of the United States Army traces its origins to the Continental Army, where Baron Friedrich Wilhelm von Steuben served as inspector general and standardised drill and training. Similar offices exist in many armed forces and police services, where "inspector general" may instead denote the chief of a police force, as in several Commonwealth and South Asian countries.

Other countries

Analogous oversight officials exist under various titles worldwide, including auditors general, ombudsmen and inspection services in France (such as the Inspection générale des finances). International organisations such as the United Nations also maintain offices of internal oversight headed by officials performing inspector-general functions.
