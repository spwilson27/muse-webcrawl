Gospel Music Workshop of America


The GMWA mass choir, assembled from workshop delegates.

The Gospel Music Workshop of America (GMWA) is an American organization dedicated to the preservation, teaching and performance of gospel music. Founded in 1967 in Detroit, Michigan, by the Reverend James Cleveland — one of the most influential figures in modern gospel — it grew into the largest gospel music convention in the world, drawing thousands of singers, musicians, songwriters and church music ministers to its annual meetings.

History and activities

Cleveland, himself a singer, pianist and composer known as the "King of Gospel", established the workshop to train church musicians and to give local choir directors access to new repertoire and professional instruction. Annual conventions, held in a different city each year, combine classes in voice, piano, organ, choir directing and music ministry with nightly musical services. Recordings by the GMWA Mass Choir and its chapters — notably songs such as "Jesus Is the Best Thing That Ever Happened to Me" — brought the workshop's sound to a wide audience.

Structure

The GMWA operates through local chapters in cities across the United States and abroad, organized into divisions including the Mass Choir, the National Mass Choir, youth and young adult divisions, and auxiliaries for ministers of music, ushers and other church workers. Since Cleveland's death in 1991 the organization has been led by a board of directors, with Bishop Albert L. Jamison Sr. serving as board chairman.
