Swaledale

Swaledale is one of the Yorkshire Dales in northern England, the valley of the River Swale in North Yorkshire. It lies largely within the Yorkshire Dales National Park and is noted for its wildflower meadows, dry-stone walls and field barns.

Geography

The River Swale, among the fastest-flowing in England, runs from the fells near Keld through Muker, Gunnerside and Richmond. Settlements include Reeth and the market town of Richmond at the dale's foot.

History and economy

Lead mining flourished in the 18th and 19th centuries, leaving extensive remains on the moors. Farming of Swaledale sheep — a hardy breed that takes its name from the valley — remains central, as does tourism on long-distance routes such as the Coast to Coast Walk.
