Toyota Motor Corporation is a Japanese multinational automotive manufacturer headquartered in Toyota City, Aichi, Japan. It is consistently the world's largest carmaker by vehicles sold, producing around ten million vehicles a year across the Toyota and Lexus brands, along with Daihatsu and Hino subsidiaries. Toyota is renowned for the Toyota Production System — the lean manufacturing philosophy of just-in-time production and continuous improvement (kaizen) that reshaped global industry — and for pioneering mass-market hybrid vehicles with the Prius.

History

Toyota grew out of Toyoda Automatic Loom Works, whose founder Sakichi Toyoda built Japan's loom industry. His son Kiichiro established an automobile department in 1933, producing the A1 prototype and the AA sedan in 1936; Toyota Motor Co. was incorporated in 1937. Post-war growth was built on small, economical cars and the guidance of engineers such as Taiichi Ohno and Eiji Toyoda, who developed the Toyota Production System through the 1950s and 1960s.

The Corolla (1966) became the best-selling nameplate in history, and the Camry anchored Toyota's success in North America, where the company built a large manufacturing base. In 1989 Toyota launched the luxury Lexus division, whose LS 400 reset industry quality benchmarks. The Prius (1997) was the first mass-produced hybrid car and sold in the millions. Toyota overtook General Motors as the world's largest automaker in 2008 and has held the sales crown for most years since.

Technology and strategy

Toyota popularised hybrid-electric vehicles and pursues a "multi-pathway" strategy combining hybrids, plug-in hybrids, battery-electric vehicles, and hydrogen fuel-cell cars such as the Mirai. It has invested heavily in solid-state batteries, autonomous driving through its Woven by Toyota subsidiary, and motorsport with Toyota Gazoo Racing, which has won the 24 Hours of Le Mans and World Rally Championship titles.
