The San Francisco Bay Area, commonly known as the Bay Area, is a region of Northern California surrounding the San Francisco, San Pablo and Suisun estuaries. The Association of Bay Area Governments defines it as the nine counties bordering the bay. Its largest cities are San Jose, San Francisco and Oakland, and the metropolitan region is home to roughly 7.8 million people.

Geography and subregions

The region is conventionally divided into the East Bay (Oakland, Berkeley, Richmond), the Peninsula, the South Bay (San Jose and Silicon Valley), the North Bay (Marin, Sonoma, Napa and Solano counties) and the city of San Francisco itself. Active faults, including the San Andreas and Hayward faults, cross the region, and its microclimates range from foggy coastal zones to hot inland valleys.

History

Indigenous peoples, including the Ohlone and Coast Miwok, inhabited the area for thousands of years before Spanish colonization began with the Portolá expedition of 1769 and the founding of Mission San Francisco de Asís in 1776. The California Gold Rush of 1849 transformed San Francisco into a major port. The 1906 earthquake and fire devastated the city, which rebuilt rapidly. After World War II the region became a center of the counterculture and, from the 1970s, of the technology industry.

Economy and transport

The Bay Area has one of the largest and most productive economies of any U.S. metropolitan region, anchored by Silicon Valley technology firms, biotechnology, finance and wine production in Napa and Sonoma. It is served by three international airports, the BART and Caltrain rail systems, and bridges including the Golden Gate Bridge and the San Francisco–Oakland Bay Bridge.
