Figure skating

Figure skating is a sport in which individuals, pairs, or groups perform on figure skates on ice. It was the first winter sport to be included in the Olympic Games, with its introduction at the 1908 Summer Olympics in London. The four Olympic disciplines are men's singles, women's singles, pair skating, and ice dance; synchronised skating is a non-Olympic discipline.

Elements

Skaters perform jumps (such as the Axel, Salchow, loop, flip, Lutz, and toe loop), spins, lifts, throw jumps, and step sequences, judged under the ISU Judging System introduced in 2004 after the judging scandal at the 2002 Winter Olympics. Each element receives a base value and a grade of execution, supplemented by program component scores for artistry and performance.

History

Modern figure skating was shaped by Jackson Haines in the 1860s, who introduced music, ballet, and free movement to the stiff English style. The sport is governed by the International Skating Union (ISU), founded in 1892. Celebrated skaters include Sonja Henie, Dick Button (first to land a double Axel and a triple jump), Katarina Witt, Michelle Kwan, and Yuzuru Hanyu, the first man to win two consecutive Olympic singles titles since Button.

Compulsory figures

The sport takes its name from the compulsory figures — patterns traced on the ice that once formed a major part of competition — which were eliminated after 1990 as judging difficulties and television appeal favoured free skating.
