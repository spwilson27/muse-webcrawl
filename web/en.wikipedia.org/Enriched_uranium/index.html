Enriched uranium

Enriched uranium is uranium in which the proportion of the fissile isotope uranium-235 has been increased above the 0.72% found in natural uranium through isotope separation.

Grades

Low-enriched uranium (LEU, under 20% U-235) fuels most commercial power reactors, typically at 3–5%. Highly enriched uranium (HEU, 20% or more, and weapons-grade at about 90%) is used in some research reactors, naval propulsion and nuclear weapons.

Enrichment methods

Historical methods include gaseous diffusion and electromagnetic separation; modern enrichment overwhelmingly uses gas centrifuges, with laser methods under development. Enrichment is a key proliferation concern because the same technology can produce reactor fuel or weapons material.
