Southern Baptist Convention

The Southern Baptist Convention (SBC) is a United States network of Baptist churches and the largest Protestant denomination in the country, with roughly 13 million members and more than 45,000 cooperating churches in recent counts. It is a fellowship of autonomous local churches that cooperate voluntarily in missions, education, and publishing; congregations are not governed by the Convention itself.

History

The SBC was founded in 1845 in Augusta, Georgia, when southern Baptists split from northern Baptists over whether slaveholders could serve as missionaries. It apologized for its origins in the defence of slavery in a 1995 resolution. The denomination grew rapidly in the postwar decades and became a central institution of evangelical Christianity. From 1979 a "conservative resurgence" shifted its agencies and seminaries toward stricter biblical inerrancy, prompting moderates to form rival bodies such as the Cooperative Baptist Fellowship.

Beliefs and agencies

Its doctrinal statement, the Baptist Faith and Message (revised 2000), affirms believer's baptism by immersion, congregational autonomy, and the authority of Scripture. The SBC supports the International Mission Board, the North American Mission Board, six seminaries, and the publishing and benefits work of Lifeway and GuideStone. In recent years it has faced internal debates over race, the role of women, and the handling of sexual abuse allegations, including a major independent investigation reported in 2022.
