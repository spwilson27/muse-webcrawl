Iberian pig

The Iberian pig (Spanish: cerdo ibérico) is a traditional breed of domestic pig native to the Iberian Peninsula. It is famed for the cured ham known as jamón ibérico, among the most prized hams in the world. The breed is characterised by its dark, often nearly black skin, slender legs and long snout.

Rearing

Iberian pigs are traditionally raised free-range in the dehesa, the oak parkland of southwestern Spain and Portugal. During the final fattening period, the montanera, the pigs feed extensively on acorns (bellotas) dropped by holm and cork oaks. This diet, rich in oleic acid, gives the fat its distinctive soft texture and nutty flavour, and produces meat heavily marbled with intramuscular fat.

Products

The finest grade, jamón ibérico de bellota, comes from purebred pigs finished on acorns and is aged for up to four years. The breed also yields fresh cuts and cured sausages such as lomo, chorizo and salchichón. Quality and labelling in Spain are regulated by law, with categories distinguished by breed purity and feeding regime.
