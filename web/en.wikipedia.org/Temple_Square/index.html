Temple Square

LocationDowntown Salt Lake City, Utah, United States
Area10 acres (4.0 ha) (main block)
OwnerThe Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints

Temple Square is a 10-acre (4-hectare) complex, owned by the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, in the center of Salt Lake City, Utah. The square, whose surrounding streets form the origin of Salt Lake City's street grid, is home to the Salt Lake Temple and is one of Utah's most popular tourist attractions, drawing millions of visitors each year.

History

Within days of the Mormon pioneers' arrival in the Salt Lake Valley in July 1847, Brigham Young designated the site for the Salt Lake Temple, whose construction spanned 1853 to 1893. The block was enclosed by an adobe-and-cobblestone wall in the 1850s.

Features

Buildings on the square include the Salt Lake Temple, the Salt Lake Tabernacle (home of the Tabernacle Choir at Temple Square), the Salt Lake Assembly Hall, the Seagull Monument, and two visitors' centers. The wider headquarters campus includes the Church Office Building, the Joseph Smith Memorial Building, the Church History Library, and the Conference Center across North Temple Street. The grounds are famous for their Christmas light displays.
