Samarco Mineração S.A. is a Brazilian mining company owned in equal shares by Vale and BHP. Founded in 1977, it mines iron ore in the state of Minas Gerais, transports the ore as slurry through pipelines to Espírito Santo, and processes it into iron ore pellets for the steel industry, historically ranking among the world's largest pellet exporters.

Operations

Samarco's system integrates mines and concentrators at Germano and Alegria in Minas Gerais with pelletising plants and the port terminal at Ponta Ubu in Espírito Santo. Slurry pipelines — among the longest of their kind when built — carry concentrate some 400 kilometres from the mines to the pellet plants, while a parallel line returns water. Before 2015 the company produced around 25–30 million tonnes of pellets per year.

Mariana dam disaster

On 5 November 2015, Samarco's Fundão tailings dam near Mariana collapsed, releasing tens of millions of cubic metres of mining waste. The mudflow destroyed the district of Bento Rodrigues, killed 19 people, and travelled down the Doce River to the Atlantic Ocean, in what is regarded as Brazil's worst environmental disaster. Samarco's operations were suspended, and its owners funded the Renova Foundation to carry out remediation and compensation. In 2018 Vale and BHP agreed to a settlement framework with Brazilian authorities, criminal and civil proceedings followed for years, and in October 2024 the companies and Brazil signed a reparations agreement valued at about R$170 billion.

Restart

Samarco resumed operations in December 2020 at reduced capacity, using dry stacking and filtration of tailings rather than conventional slurry dams, and has since been gradually ramping up production at its Germano and Ubu complexes.
