Languages of Ethiopia

The languages of Ethiopia include the official languages of the country together with a great diversity of indigenous and foreign tongues. Ethiopia is one of the most linguistically diverse countries in Africa: between 90 and 100 distinct languages are spoken there, most of them belonging to the Afroasiatic language family, with a substantial minority of Nilo-Saharan languages. Amharic, a Semitic language of the Ethiopian Semitic branch, serves as the working language of the federal government, while Oromo (Afaan Oromoo), a Cushitic language, is the most widely spoken first language in the country.

Overview

According to Ethnologue and Ethiopian census data, roughly 90 or more languages are indigenous to Ethiopia. Most belong to the Cushitic and Semitic branches of the Afroasiatic family; Omotic languages, once grouped under Cushitic, are now usually treated as a separate primary branch of Afroasiatic, largely because they are spoken almost exclusively in southwestern Ethiopia. Nilo-Saharan languages are concentrated along the western lowlands, in regions such as Gambela and Benishangul-Gumuz, where languages like Anuak and Nuer are spoken.

Writing systems

The Ge'ez script (fidel), an abugida in which each character represents a consonant–vowel syllable, is used for Amharic, Tigrinya and several other languages, and was historically the script of the classical Ge'ez language, which remains the liturgical language of the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church. Since 1991, many languages have adopted Latin-based alphabets: Oromo uses the Qubee alphabet, and Somali and Afar likewise use Latin orthographies.

Language policy

The 1995 Constitution of the Federal Democratic Republic of Ethiopia declares that all Ethiopian languages enjoy equal state recognition, with Amharic as the working language of the federal government. Regional states choose their own working languages, and primary education is widely provided in mother tongues. In 2020 the federal government announced plans to recognize additional working languages alongside Amharic, including Afaan Oromoo, Afar, Somali and Tigrinya.

Foreign languages

English is the most widely taught foreign language: it is a subject from primary school and the medium of instruction in secondary schools and universities. Arabic is spoken in some Muslim communities and border areas, and Italian retains a small legacy presence from the period of Italian occupation.
