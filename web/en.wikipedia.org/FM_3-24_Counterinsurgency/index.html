FM 3-24 Counterinsurgency

Author: U.S. Army and U.S. Marine Corps
Published: December 2006
Subject: Military doctrine

FM 3-24, formally Counterinsurgency, is a United States Army and Marine Corps field manual published in December 2006 that sets out doctrine for counterinsurgency operations. It was developed under the direction of General David Petraeus and Lieutenant General James Amos, with contributions from academics and practitioners, and drew heavily on the experiences of the wars in Iraq and Afghanistan.

Content

The manual emphasizes that counterinsurgency is primarily a political struggle in which the population is the center of gravity. Its best-known maxims include the paradoxes of counterinsurgency — for example, that sometimes the more force is used, the less effective it is, and that tactical success guarantees nothing. It stresses cultural awareness, intelligence-driven operations, and the "clear, hold, build" approach to securing areas.

Reception and influence

Unusually for a military manual, FM 3-24 was released publicly and became a bestseller, praised for its intellectual candor and criticized by some for underplaying the role of coercion. It shaped the 2007 "surge" strategy in Iraq. An updated version, incorporating lessons learned, was published in 2014.
