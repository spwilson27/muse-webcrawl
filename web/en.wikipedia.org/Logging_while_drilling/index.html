Logging while drilling

Logging while drilling (LWD) is a technique of conveying well logging tools into the well borehole downhole as part of the bottom hole assembly (BHA) during drilling, so that formation properties are measured while the well is being drilled rather than afterwards on wireline.

Measurements

LWD tools commonly measure gamma ray, resistivity, density and neutron porosity, sonic velocities, formation pressure and annular conditions, and can include imaging and nuclear magnetic resonance tools. Data are recorded in downhole memory and a subset is transmitted to surface in real time, historically by mud pulse telemetry and increasingly by electromagnetic or wired-pipe systems.

Advantages

Because measurements are made soon after the bit penetrates the formation — before drilling fluids invade deeply — LWD can give more representative readings, allows geosteering of horizontal wells within reservoir targets, and reduces rig time compared with separate wireline runs, though tools are expensive and data rates to surface are limited.

Related technique

Measurement while drilling (MWD) focuses on drilling mechanics and directional data such as inclination and azimuth, and the terms are often used together for combined services.
