Symbolic regression

Symbolic regression is a type of regression analysis that searches the space of mathematical expressions to find the model that best fits a given dataset, both in terms of accuracy and simplicity. Unlike conventional regression, which fits parameters of a predetermined model structure, symbolic regression makes no assumption about the form of the model: it builds expressions from elementary operators, functions, constants, and variables.

Method

The classical approach uses genetic programming, popularised by John Koza: candidate formulas are represented as expression trees and evolved by mutation and crossover under a fitness function balancing error and complexity (parsimony). Later systems such as Eureqa, PySR, and various deep-learning approaches rediscovered known physical laws from data in benchmark tests, for example recovering the equations of a double pendulum from motion-capture measurements.

Advantages and difficulties

Because the output is an explicit, human-readable equation, symbolic regression offers interpretability that black-box models lack, making it attractive for scientific discovery. Its search space is enormous, however, and the method is prone to overfitting and to producing bloated expressions unless complexity is penalised. Practical applications include empirical law discovery in physics, materials modelling, and control engineering.
