Keratitis

Keratitis is a condition in which the eye's cornea, the clear front part of the eye, becomes inflamed. The condition is often marked by moderate to intense pain and usually involves impaired eyesight that may be temporary or, in severe cases, permanent.

Causes

Keratitis may be infectious or non-infectious. Infectious keratitis can be caused by bacteria (commonly Pseudomonas aeruginosa in contact lens wearers), viruses (especially herpes simplex virus), fungi, and parasites such as Acanthamoeba. Non-infectious causes include injury, chemical burns, ultraviolet exposure (photokeratitis, as in snow blindness or arc eye), and contact lens overuse. Amoebic keratitis is a rare but serious infection strongly associated with poor contact lens hygiene.

Symptoms and treatment

Symptoms include eye pain, redness, blurred vision, sensitivity to light (photophobia), tearing, and a feeling of something in the eye. Because the cornea is essential to vision, keratitis is treated as a medical urgency: treatment depends on the cause and may involve antibacterial, antifungal or antiviral eye drops; corticosteroids are sometimes used with caution under specialist supervision. Severe scarring may require corneal transplantation. Untreated keratitis can lead to corneal ulceration, perforation, and blindness.
