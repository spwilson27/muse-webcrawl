General Atomics MQ-9 Reaper

MQ-9 Reaper
Type: unmanned combat aerial vehicle
Manufacturer: General Atomics Aeronautical Systems
First flight: 2 February 2001
Primary users: United States Air Force, Royal Air Force
Developed from: General Atomics MQ-1 Predator

The General Atomics MQ-9 Reaper (sometimes called the Predator B) is an unmanned aerial vehicle capable of remotely controlled or autonomous flight operations, developed for the United States Air Force as a hunter-killer platform for intelligence, surveillance, reconnaissance, and precision strike.

Design

The MQ-9 is much larger and more capable than its predecessor, the MQ-1 Predator. It is powered by a Honeywell TPE331-10 turboprop producing about 950 shaft horsepower, giving a maximum speed of roughly 240 knots and a service ceiling of about 50,000 feet, with endurance exceeding 27 hours. It can carry up to 3,800 pounds of stores on seven hardpoints, including AGM-114 Hellfire missiles, GBU-12 Paveway II laser-guided bombs, and GBU-38 JDAMs. A crew of a pilot and a sensor operator controls the aircraft from a ground control station via satellite link.

Operational history

Introduced in 2007, the Reaper has been flown extensively over Afghanistan, Iraq, Syria, Yemen, Libya, and Somalia, and is operated by air arms including the Royal Air Force, the French Air Force, and the Italian Air Force, as well as by NASA and US Customs and Border Protection in unarmed variants. Its long loiter time and persistent surveillance role have made it emblematic of remotely piloted warfare.
