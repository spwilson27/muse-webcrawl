Alpha Phi Alpha

Alpha Phi Alpha Fraternity, Inc. (ΑΦΑ) is the first intercollegiate historically African American fraternity. It was founded on December 4, 1906, at Cornell University in Ithaca, New York, by seven undergraduates known as the Seven Jewels.

Alpha Phi Alpha
Founded: 4 Dec 1906, Cornell University
Colours: Black and old gold
Motto: "First of All, Servants of All, We Shall Transcend All"

History

Founders Henry Arthur Callis, Charles Henry Chapman, Eugene Kinckle Jones, George Biddle Kelley, Nathaniel Allison Murray, Robert Harold Ogle, and Vertner Woodson Tandy established the fraternity as a study and support group that became a national organisation. Alpha Phi Alpha was the first of the Divine Nine organisations of the National Pan-Hellenic Council.

Programmes and members

National programmes include Project Alpha, A Voteless People Is a Hopeless People, and Go-to-High-School, Go-to-College. Notable members have included Martin Luther King Jr., Thurgood Marshall, W. E. B. Du Bois (honorary), Jesse Owens, and many members of Congress, judges, and university presidents. The fraternity led the campaign for the Martin Luther King Jr. Memorial in Washington, D.C.

Organisation

The fraternity has initiated over 290,000 members through undergraduate and alumni chapters in the United States and internationally, governed by a General President and general conventions.
