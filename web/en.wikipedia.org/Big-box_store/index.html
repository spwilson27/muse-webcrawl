Big-box store

A big-box store (also superstore, supercentre, or megastore) is a physically large retail establishment, usually more than 50,000 square feet (4,600 m²), characterised by a warehouse-like design, high-volume sales, wide product selection, and extensive surface parking. Examples include Walmart, Target, Costco, IKEA, and The Home Depot.

Development

The format emerged in the United States in the 1960s with discount department stores and grew with suburbanisation, car ownership, and efficient supply-chain logistics. "Category killer" chains specialise in one product category, such as electronics or home improvement, while warehouse clubs charge membership fees for bulk goods.

Criticism

Big-box stores are criticised for drawing trade from local independent retailers and town centres, for low-wage employment practices, traffic generation, and the environmental impact of large parking lots and single-storey construction. Some municipalities impose size caps or design requirements on them. Supporters emphasise low consumer prices, product range, and employment.

Abandoned stores

Vacated big-box buildings — "ghost boxes" — are often difficult to reuse because of their scale and specialised design, though some are converted into churches, libraries, call centres, or indoor recreation facilities.
