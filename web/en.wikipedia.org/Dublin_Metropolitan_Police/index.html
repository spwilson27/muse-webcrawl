Dublin Metropolitan Police

The Dublin Metropolitan Police (DMP) was the police force of Dublin, Ireland, from 1836 to 1925, when it was amalgamated into the new state's Garda Síochána. Unlike the Royal Irish Constabulary, which policed the rest of Ireland as an armed gendarmerie, the DMP was an unarmed, civilian-style force modelled on London's Metropolitan Police.

History

The force was established under the Dublin Police Act 1836, replacing earlier watch and police arrangements. Its uniformed division patrolled the city from stations across the metropolitan district, while its G Division of plain-clothes detectives, based at Exchange Court, became notorious for political surveillance of Irish nationalist organisations.

1913–1923

DMP constables were prominent in policing the 1913 Dublin Lock-out, when baton charges on Sackville Street killed and injured workers. During the Irish War of Independence, G Division detectives were targeted by the IRA; several were shot in 1919–1920. The unarmed uniformed branch largely remained neutral and was retained after independence.

Amalgamation

In 1925 the DMP merged with the Garda Síochána under the Police Forces Amalgamation Act, ending nearly ninety years of separate policing for the capital. Its records are preserved in the Garda Museum at Dublin Castle.
