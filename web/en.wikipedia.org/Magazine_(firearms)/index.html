Magazine (firearms)

A magazine is an ammunition storage and feeding device for a repeating firearm, either integral within the gun (internal or fixed magazine) or externally attached (detachable magazine). The magazine functions by holding several cartridges within it and allowing them to be fed into the firearm's chamber by a spring-loaded follower or other mechanism.

Types

Box magazines, detachable or fixed, stack cartridges in one or two columns and are the most common type in modern rifles and pistols. Tubular magazines, found under the barrel of many lever-action rifles and shotguns, hold cartridges end to end. Drum magazines store cartridges in a spiral for high capacity, while rotary, pan, and helical designs have also been used. A stripper clip (charger) is not itself a magazine but a device for loading one quickly; an en bloc clip, by contrast, is inserted into the magazine and forms part of the feeding system.

History

Repeating arms with magazines appeared in the 19th century with designs such as the Spencer and Henry rifles. The box magazine matured with bolt-action service rifles like the Lee–Enfield, and detachable box magazines became standard on the self-loading rifles and pistols of the 20th century.

Terminology

In common speech "clip" is often used for any magazine, a usage firearm enthusiasts regard as incorrect, since a clip merely holds cartridges for loading whereas a magazine feeds them into the action.
