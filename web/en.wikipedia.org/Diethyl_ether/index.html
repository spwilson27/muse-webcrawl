Diethyl ether

Diethyl ether
Formula(C2H5)2O
Molar mass74.12 g/mol
Boiling point34.6 °C
AppearanceColourless, highly volatile, flammable liquid

Diethyl ether, commonly known simply as ether, is an organic compound in the ether class with the formula (C2H5)2O. It is a colourless, highly volatile and extremely flammable liquid with a characteristic sweetish odour. Historically one of the first general anaesthetics, it remains a widely used laboratory solvent.

Diethyl ether: two ethyl groups joined by an oxygen atom.

Production

Ether is produced by the acid-catalysed dehydration of ethanol: two molecules of ethanol condense, typically over sulphuric acid at about 140 °C, eliminating water. It is also obtained as a by-product of ethanol production by vapour-phase hydration of ethylene.

Uses and history

On 16 October 1846, William T. G. Morton publicly demonstrated ether anaesthesia at Massachusetts General Hospital — "Ether Day" — transforming surgery. Diethyl ether has since been largely replaced by less flammable agents such as halothane and sevoflurane. In the laboratory it is a standard aprotic solvent for Grignard reactions and extractions, and it was once used as a starting fluid for engines. Ether forms explosive peroxides on storage in air and light, requiring careful handling.
