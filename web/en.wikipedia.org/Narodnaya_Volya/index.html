Narodnaya Volya

Narodnaya Volya (Russian: Народная воля, "People's Will") was a Russian revolutionary political organization of the late 19th century, founded in 1879 from the split of the populist (Narodnik) movement. It is best known for assassinating Tsar Alexander II in 1881.

Origins and programme

The populist group Zemlya i Volya ("Land and Liberty") split in 1879 over tactics. The faction that became Narodnaya Volya concluded that peaceful propaganda among the peasantry had failed and adopted political struggle — including terrorism — to force constitutional reform. Its programme demanded a constituent assembly, universal suffrage, freedom of speech and press, and transfer of land to peasant communes.

The assassination of Alexander II

The organisation's executive committee, including figures such as Andrei Zhelyabov and Sophia Perovskaya, made repeated attempts on the Tsar's life. On 13 March 1881 (1 March, old style), bombers led by Ignacy Hryniewiecki fatally wounded Alexander II in St Petersburg — on the very day he had been considering limited reforms. The leaders were arrested and five, including Perovskaya and Zhelyabov, were hanged.

Decline and legacy

Under Alexander III the organisation was crushed by police infiltration and arrests; a final attempt on Alexander III in 1887 involved Alexander Ulyanov, elder brother of Vladimir Lenin, who was executed. Narodnaya Volya's tactics of targeted political assassination shaped later revolutionary movements, including the Socialist Revolutionary Party's Combat Organization.
