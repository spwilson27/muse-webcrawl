REC (stylised as [•REC]) is a 2007 Spanish found-footage supernatural horror film directed by Jaume Balagueró and Paco Plaza. Presented as raw footage from a television crew's camera, the film follows reporter Ángela Vidal and her cameraman Pablo as they accompany a group of firefighters on a night call to an apartment building in Barcelona, where a terrifying infection traps the residents inside.

Plot

Ángela Vidal hosts a late-night television programme, Mientras usted duerme ("While You Sleep"), documenting the night shift of a Barcelona fire station. When the crew follows firefighters to an emergency call at a residential building, they find an elderly woman who attacks a police officer. Authorities seal the building under quarantine, and the trapped occupants discover that a rabies-like virus is spreading through bites, turning victims into violent, frenzied attackers. As the survivors dwindle, Ángela and Pablo reach the penthouse, where recordings and newspaper clippings reveal the infection's origin in a possessed girl named Tristana Medeiros, the subject of Vatican experiments. In the dark attic, night-vision footage captures the emaciated Medeiros attacking Pablo before dragging Ángela away as the camera falls.

Production

The film was produced by Filmax and shot in Barcelona with handheld digital cameras to achieve its documentary aesthetic. The directors kept much of the cast unaware of upcoming scares to elicit genuine reactions. The title refers to the record indicator on a video camera.

Release and reception

REC premiered at the Venice Film Festival in 2007 and was released in Spain that November. It received widespread critical acclaim for its intensity, realism and atmosphere, and is frequently cited among the finest found-footage horror films. It won two Goya Awards, including Best New Actress for Manuela Velasco.

Sequels and remake

The film spawned a franchise: REC 2 (2009), REC 3: Genesis (2012) and REC 4: Apocalypse (2014). An American remake, Quarantine, was released in 2008.
