RDS-1


Placeholder image: RDS-1

RDS-1 (Russian: РДС-1, Reaktivnyy Dvigatel' Spetsial'nyy) was the first nuclear bomb tested by the Soviet Union. It was detonated on 29 August 1949 at the Semipalatinsk Test Site in Kazakhstan, with a yield of about 22 kilotons.

Development

The weapon was developed under the direction of Igor Kurchatov at Arzamas-16 (KB-11), with Yulii Khariton as chief designer. The device was a plutonium implosion weapon closely modelled on the American Fat Man design, aided by intelligence from spies such as Klaus Fuchs.

Test

The test, codenamed "First Lightning" (Pervaya Molniya) in the USSR and "Joe-1" by the United States, was conducted from a tower. Its success ended the American atomic monopoly and accelerated the Cold War arms race. A second type, RDS-2, followed in 1951 with an improved design.
