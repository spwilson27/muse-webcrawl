The National Film Award for Best Actor, officially known as the Rajat Kamal Award for the Best Actor, is an honour presented annually at the National Film Awards of India, instituted in 1954 and administered by the Directorate of Film Festivals. The Best Actor category was introduced at the 15th National Film Awards in 1968, when Uttam Kumar won jointly for Antony Firingee and Chiriyakhana. Recipients are chosen by a jury appointed by the government and receive a Silver Lotus (Rajat Kamal) statuette and a cash prize.

Notable recipients

Amitabh Bachchan holds the record with four wins (Agneepath, Black, Paa, and Piku). Three-time winners include Kamal Haasan and Mammootty. Other celebrated recipients include Soumitra Chatterjee, Naseeruddin Shah, Om Puri, Mithun Chakraborty, Mohanlal, and Nana Patekar. In the 2020s, winners included Suriya and Ajay Devgn (shared, 2020), Allu Arjun (2021), and Rishab Shetty (2022).

The award recognises performances across all Indian languages, reflecting the multilingual character of Indian cinema; unlike the popular Filmfare Awards, the National Film Awards are state honours selected by jury rather than by public or industry vote.
