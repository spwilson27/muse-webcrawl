François Gérard Georges Nicolas Hollande (born 12 August 1954) is a French politician who served as President of France from 2012 to 2017. A member of the Socialist Party, he previously served as First Secretary of the party from 1997 to 2008, Mayor of Tulle from 2001 to 2008, and President of the General Council of Corrèze from 2008 to 2012. He also held the presidency of the ex officio position of Co-Prince of Andorra during his term.

Hollande rose to prominence under President François Mitterrand, serving as a special adviser at the Élysée Palace and later as a member of the Cour des comptes. He was elected to the National Assembly for Corrèze in 1988 and, after losing his seat in 1993, returned in 1997, the year he succeeded Lionel Jospin as First Secretary of the Socialist Party.

Presidency

Hollande defeated incumbent Nicolas Sarkozy in the 2012 presidential election, winning 51.6 per cent of the vote in the second round. Campaigning on a platform of growth over austerity, he introduced a 75 per cent top rate of income tax on earnings above €1 million (later struck down in its original form and restructured), legalised same-sex marriage in 2013, and pursued labour-market and pension reforms. In foreign policy he ordered the French intervention in Mali (Operation Serval, 2013) and French participation in strikes against the Islamic State in Iraq and Syria.

His presidency was marked by persistently high unemployment and, from November 2015, a state of emergency declared after the Paris terrorist attacks. Hollande hosted the 2015 United Nations Climate Change Conference, which produced the Paris Agreement. With approval ratings at historic lows, he announced on 1 December 2016 that he would not seek re-election, becoming the first president of the Fifth Republic to step aside after a single term. He was succeeded by Emmanuel Macron in May 2017.

Later career

After leaving office Hollande published the memoir Les leçons du pouvoir ("Lessons of Power", 2018) and remained an occasional commentator on French politics. In 2024 he was elected to the National Assembly for Corrèze, returning to parliament more than a decade after leaving it.
