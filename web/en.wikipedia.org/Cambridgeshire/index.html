Cambridgeshire is a ceremonial county in the East of England. It is bordered by Lincolnshire to the north, Norfolk to the north-east, Suffolk to the east, Essex and Hertfordshire to the south, and Bedfordshire and Northamptonshire to the west. The county town is the city of Cambridge.

Geography

Much of northern Cambridgeshire forms part of the Fens, a low-lying marshy region reclaimed over centuries by drainage; the area lies largely at or below sea level and contains Holme Fen, the lowest point in the United Kingdom at about 2.75 metres below sea level. The main rivers are the Great Ouse, the Nene and the Cam.

Settlements and economy

Besides Cambridge — world-famous for its university — the county includes the cathedral city of Peterborough, Ely with its great medieval cathedral, and market towns such as Huntingdon, St Ives, St Neots and Wisbech. Agriculture dominates the Fenland districts, while Cambridge anchors a major high-technology and research cluster nicknamed "Silicon Fen".

Local government

The ceremonial county comprises the administrative county of Cambridgeshire (with districts including Cambridge, East Cambridgeshire, Fenland, Huntingdonshire and South Cambridgeshire) and the unitary authority of Peterborough. The Cambridgeshire and Peterborough Combined Authority, established in 2017, is led by a directly elected mayor.
