Demosaicing

Demosaicing
Also calledCFA interpolation, debayering
InputSingle-channel mosaic from a color filter array
OutputFull-colour image (three channels per pixel)
Common patternBayer filter (RGGB)

Demosaicing (also called debayering or CFA interpolation) is a digital image processing algorithm used to reconstruct a full-colour image from the incomplete colour samples output by most digital camera sensors. Such sensors overlay a color filter array (CFA) — most commonly the Bayer filter patented by Bryce Bayer in 1976 — so that each photosite records only one of the three primary colours.

Goal and challenges

The algorithm must estimate the two missing colour values at every pixel. Simple approaches such as nearest-neighbour replication and bilinear interpolation are fast but produce artefacts at edges, including zippering, false colours and moiré. More advanced methods exploit correlations between colour channels and adapt to edge direction; modern approaches use machine learning.

Artefacts

Characteristic demosaicing errors include the maze (zipper) artefact along edges, false-colour fringes on high-frequency detail, and rainbow moiré on fine patterns such as fabrics. Cameras with anti-aliasing (optical low-pass) filters trade some sharpness to suppress these effects.

Context

Demosaicing is performed either in-camera when producing JPEG images or in raw-conversion software, where users may choose among algorithms. Alternative sensor designs, such as Foveon sensors that capture all three colours at each pixel, or three-sensor cameras with beam splitters, avoid the need for demosaicing.
