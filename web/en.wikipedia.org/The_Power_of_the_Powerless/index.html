The Power of the Powerless

AuthorVáclav Havel
Original titleMoc bezmocných
WrittenOctober 1978
LanguageCzech
SubjectDissent; post-totalitarianism

The Power of the Powerless (Czech: Moc bezmocných) is a celebrated political essay by the Czech dissident and playwright Václav Havel, written in October 1978 and circulated in samizdat. It became a manifesto of dissent in communist Central and Eastern Europe and a classic text on the nature of power and resistance.

Argument

Havel analyses the "post-totalitarian" system of late communism, in which domination is maintained less by terror than by ideology functioning as ritual. His famous opening example is a greengrocer who places the slogan "Workers of the world, unite!" in his shop window not from conviction but as a signal of compliance — "I obey, and therefore I have the right to be left in peace." The system's subjects thus become both its victims and its instruments, sustaining a world of "appearances" by "living within a lie."

Against this, Havel proposes "living within the truth": the greengrocer who one day refuses the sign, refuses ritual voting and speaks honestly commits no coup, but he threatens the system's foundation precisely because its power rests on universal pretence. Such acts build an independent "parallel polis" of culture and civic life outside official structures.

Context and influence

The essay was written for a planned (and never published) joint volume of Czech and Polish dissidents, following the founding of Charter 77, of which Havel was a spokesman; its circulation contributed to the police pressure that led to his imprisonment from 1979. It influenced dissident movements across the Soviet bloc, including Solidarity in Poland, and after the Velvet Revolution of 1989 — which carried Havel to the presidency of Czechoslovakia — it became a canonical text in the study of civil resistance, read in later protest movements far beyond Europe.
