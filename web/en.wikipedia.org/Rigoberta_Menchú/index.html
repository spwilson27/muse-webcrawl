Rigoberta Menchú

Rigoberta Menchú Tum (born 9 January 1959) is a K'iche' Guatemalan human rights activist, feminist, and Nobel Peace Prize laureate. Menchú has dedicated her life to publicising the rights of Guatemala's Indigenous peoples during and after the Guatemalan Civil War (1960–1996), and to promoting Indigenous rights internationally.

She received the Nobel Peace Prize in 1992 — the youngest person ever to receive it at the time, and the first Indigenous person. She is the subject of the testimonial biography Me llamo Rigoberta Menchú y así me nació la conciencia (1983), known in English as I, Rigoberta Menchú, based on interviews with anthropologist Elisabeth Burgos-Debray.

Early life and activism

Menchú was born to a poor Indigenous peasant family in Laj Chimel, a rural area in the El Quiché department of Guatemala. Her mother, father and younger brother were killed by the Guatemalan army during the civil war. She became active in the Committee for Peasant Unity (CUC) and in campaigns against human rights abuses by the Guatemalan armed forces, and was forced into exile in Mexico in 1981.

Political career and recognition

Menchú ran for President of Guatemala in 2007 and 2011, becoming the first Indigenous candidate to do so. She founded the political party Winaq. She has also received numerous other honours, including the Prince of Asturias Award, and honorary doctorates. The accuracy of some details in her testimonial biography was debated following criticism by anthropologist David Stoll in 1998, though the Nobel Committee affirmed her prize.
