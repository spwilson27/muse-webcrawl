Castillo San Cristóbal

Castillo San Cristóbal is a Spanish fort in San Juan, Puerto Rico. Built by Spain between 1765 and 1783 to protect the city of San Juan against land-based attacks, it is the largest fortification built by Spain in the Americas, covering some 27 acres (11 hectares) and rising about 150 feet (46 m) above the sea. Together with Castillo San Felipe del Morro, which guarded the harbour entrance, it formed the core of San Juan's defences.

Design

The fort was designed on the principle of defence in depth, with a series of interlocking bastions, tunnels, moats and a large main plaza. Its defences were repeatedly tested, notably during the British attack on San Juan in 1797 under Sir Ralph Abercromby, which the fort helped repel. During the Second World War, concrete pillboxes were added for coastal observation.

Today

Castillo San Cristóbal is administered by the United States National Park Service as part of the San Juan National Historic Site and was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1983. It is open to visitors and offers views over Old San Juan and the Atlantic coast.
