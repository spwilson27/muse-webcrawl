Video game content rating systems
Major bodies: ESRB (North America), PEGI (Europe), CERO (Japan), ACB (Australia), USK (Germany)
Purpose: advise on age suitability and content

A video game content rating system assigns video games an age or suitability rating, usually accompanied by content descriptors for violence, language, sexual content and other material. Most systems are industry-administered and voluntary, though some are backed by law restricting sales to minors.

History

Rating systems arose in the early 1990s after controversies over games such as Mortal Kombat and Night Trap prompted US congressional hearings. The industry responded by creating the Entertainment Software Rating Board (ESRB) in 1994, with ratings from E (Everyone) to AO (Adults Only). Europe adopted the PEGI system in 2003, using age bands 3, 7, 12, 16 and 18 with pictogram descriptors.

Comparison

Japan's CERO uses letter grades (A–Z); Australia's Classification Board is a government body whose "Refused Classification" outcome effectively bans a game from sale; Germany's USK ratings are legally binding. The International Age Rating Coalition (IARC) now provides a questionnaire-based process issuing ratings for several territories at once, widely used for digitally distributed games.
