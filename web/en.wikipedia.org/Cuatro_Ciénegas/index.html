Cuatro Ciénegas

Cuatro Ciénegas is a municipality in the Mexican state of Coahuila, and also the name of the desert valley in which it lies. The name means "four marshes". The region is renowned for its unusual ecosystem of spring-fed pools, known as pozas, in the middle of the Chihuahuan Desert, which host an exceptional number of endemic species.

Ecology

The Cuatro Ciénegas basin contains gypsum dunes, wetlands, and hundreds of turquoise pools fed by underground aquifers. Its waters are home to endemic fish, turtles such as the Coahuilan box turtle, snails, and living stromatolites — microbial structures resembling some of the earliest life forms on Earth. Because of this, scientists sometimes compare the basin's ecology to that of ancient seas or even to conditions on Mars.

Conservation

The valley was declared a federal area for the protection of flora and fauna in 1994 and is a UNESCO biosphere reserve. Groundwater extraction for alfalfa farming in surrounding areas threatens the water level of the pools, and several canals have already dried up, making conservation an ongoing concern.
