Max Weinreich (Yiddish: מאַקס ווײַנרײַך; 22 April 1894 – 29 January 1969) was a Lithuanian-Latvian-born Jewish linguist who specialised in Yiddish and was one of the founders of YIVO, the Yiddish Scientific Institute, in Vilna (Vilnius) in 1925. He is regarded as the founder of modern Yiddish linguistics and of the sociolinguistic study of Yiddish-speaking Ashkenazi society.

Life and career

Weinreich was born in Goldingen, Courland, studied in Libau (Liepāja) and St. Petersburg, and took his doctorate at the University of Marburg in 1923. In Vilna he directed YIVO's philological section and edited its scholarly publications, conducting fieldwork on Yiddish dialects and Jewish folklore. Caught in Denmark when the Second World War began, he moved to New York in 1940, where he re-established YIVO and also taught at the City College of New York.

Scholarship

Weinreich's magnum opus, the four-volume Geshikhte fun der yidisher shprakh (History of the Yiddish Language, published posthumously 1973; abridged English translation 1980/2008), reconstructed a thousand years of Yiddish development as a "fusion language" of Germanic, Hebrew-Aramaic, Romance, and Slavic components. His book Hitler's Professors (1946) documented the role of German scholarship in Nazi racial policy and antisemitic propaganda. He also produced landmark studies of Yiddish dialectology and Jewish naming practices.

The language-versus-dialect aphorism

Weinreich is famous for popularising the quip that "a language is a dialect with an army and navy" (a shprakh iz a dialekt mit an armey un flot), which he attributed to an unnamed Bronx high-school teacher, an auditor at his 1943–44 lecture series. The aphorism became a classic statement of the sociolinguistic view that the language/dialect distinction is social and political rather than purely structural. His son Uriel Weinreich was also a prominent linguist.
