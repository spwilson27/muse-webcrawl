Stari Ras (Serbian Cyrillic: Стари Рас, "Old Ras") was a medieval fortified city and one of the first capitals of the Serbian principality and kingdom of Raška, located near modern Novi Pazar in southwestern Serbia. Together with the nearby monastery of Sopoćani, it was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1979.

History

The fortress of Ras arose in the 8th or 9th century on the site of earlier late-antique and Byzantine defences, guarding the valley of the Raška river at a crossroads of Balkan trade routes. It became the seat of the Vukanović dynasty, the rulers of Raška. The surrounding region preserves some of the most important monuments of medieval Serbia: the Church of the Holy Apostles Peter and Paul (Petrova crkva), one of the oldest churches in Serbia, dating to the 9th–10th centuries; the monasteries of Đurđevi Stupovi (founded by Stefan Nemanja in 1171) and Sopoćani (founded by King Stefan Uroš I around 1260), famous for its frescoes; and the fortress of Gradina on the hill above the city.

Decline

As the Serbian state expanded and its political centre shifted northward in the 13th century, Ras declined in importance; the trading settlement that succeeded it grew around the Ottoman-founded town of Novi Pazar. The abandoned fortress fell into ruin, and its remains — ramparts, towers, and the foundations of dwellings — have been excavated since the mid-20th century.
