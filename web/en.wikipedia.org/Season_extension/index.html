Season extension

Season extension in agriculture is any method that lets crops be grown beyond their normal outdoor production season, or that brings harvest earlier or extends it later. Techniques range from simple covers and cold frames to heated greenhouses, and are used by market gardeners and home growers to supply produce for more of the year and to protect crops from frost.

Low tunnels (hoophouses) protect crops from frost

Techniques

  • Row covers of lightweight floating fabric ("fleece") protect against light frost and pests while admitting light and rain.
  • Cold frames and cloches — small glazed boxes — shelter individual plants or small beds.
  • Low tunnels and high tunnels (hoophouses/polytunnels) cover whole beds or blocks, raising temperatures and shielding from wind and rain; unheated high tunnels are a mainstay of season extension in temperate market gardening.
  • Greenhouses, heated or unheated, give the greatest control.
  • Mulches, especially plastic mulch, warm the soil; succession planting, choosing cold-hardy or fast-maturing varieties, and overwintering crops complement physical structures.

Benefits and limits

Extension can add several weeks at each end of the season, improve quality and continuity of supply, and raise incomes for local growers, with lower energy use than heated greenhouse production or long-distance transport. Limits include labour, capital costs, ventilation and disease management under cover, and plastic waste from films and mulches.
