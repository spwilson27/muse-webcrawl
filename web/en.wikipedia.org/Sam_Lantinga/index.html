Sam Lantinga

Sam Lantinga, creator of the SDL library.

Sam Lantinga is an American software engineer best known as the creator of Simple DirectMedia Layer (SDL), a widely used cross-platform library for games and multimedia applications. SDL provides low-level access to audio, keyboard, mouse, joystick and graphics hardware on many operating systems.

Career

Lantinga developed SDL in the late 1990s while working at Loki Software, which ported popular Windows games to Linux. SDL became the standard foundation for Linux game ports and later for indie and commercial games across platforms. After Loki's closure he worked at Blizzard Entertainment on World of Warcraft.

Valve and SDL 2

In 2012 Lantinga joined Valve, where he led the development of SDL 2.0, a major redesign released in 2013 that underpins much of Valve's Linux and Steam Deck ecosystem, including the Steam runtime. Ryan C. Gordon, a frequent collaborator, has often worked alongside him on Linux game technology.

Impact

Through SDL, Lantinga's work has influenced thousands of games and emulators; the library's permissive zlib licence and durable API design made it one of the most successful open-source game development tools.
