Gustav Fechner

Gustav Theodor Fechner
Born: 19 April 1801, Gross Särchen, Prussia
Died: 18 November 1887, Leipzig, Germany
Known for: Weber–Fechner law; psychophysics

Gustav Theodor Fechner (19 April 1801 – 18 November 1887) was a German physicist, philosopher and experimental psychologist. A professor at the University of Leipzig, he founded psychophysics, the quantitative study of the relationship between physical stimuli and the sensations they produce.

Psychophysics

In his Elemente der Psychophysik (1860), Fechner developed methods for measuring sensation and formalised the relationship now known as the Weber–Fechner law: sensation grows as the logarithm of stimulus intensity (S = k log I). Building on Ernst Heinrich Weber's observations about just-noticeable differences, Fechner sought to demonstrate that mind and body are lawfully related aspects of a single reality.

Other work

Fechner also wrote on aesthetics, colour afterimages (the Fechner effect), and philosophy, defending a panpsychist "day view" of the world against materialist "night views". The Fechner crater on the Moon is named in his honour.
