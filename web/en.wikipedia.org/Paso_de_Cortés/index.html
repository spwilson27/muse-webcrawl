Paso de Cortés
Elevation: about 3,700 m
Location: Mexico–Puebla border, Mexico

The Paso de Cortés is a high mountain pass in central Mexico, lying in the saddle between the volcanoes Popocatépetl and Iztaccíhuatl. It sits on the border of the states of México and Puebla within Iztaccíhuatl–Popocatépetl National Park.

History

The pass is named for the Spanish conquistador Hernán Cortés, whose expedition crossed it in November 1519 on the march from Veracruz to the Aztec capital Tenochtitlan. From the pass, the Spaniards and their allies beheld the Valley of Mexico spread out below.

Geography and access

At roughly 3,700 metres above sea level, the pass offers celebrated views of both volcanic peaks. A paved road from Amecameca reaches the pass, which serves as a starting point for climbers heading to Iztaccíhuatl (access to Popocatépetl is restricted because of its ongoing activity) and for visitors to the La Joya trailhead area.
