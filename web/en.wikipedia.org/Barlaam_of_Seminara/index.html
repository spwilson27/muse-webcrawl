Barlaam of Seminara

Barlaam of Seminara (c. 1290 – 1348), also known as Barlaam the Calabrian, was a southern Italian scholar, monk, and theologian. He is best known for his role in the Hesychast controversy, in which he opposed the mystical prayer practices defended by Gregory Palamas, and for teaching Greek to Petrarch.

Life

Born Bernardo Massari in Seminara, Calabria, he became a Basilian monk and took the name Barlaam. Around 1330 he traveled to Constantinople, where his learning in astronomy, mathematics, and philosophy won him imperial favor and an appointment as abbot. He wrote treatises on astronomy and on the computation of solar eclipses, and revised parts of Ptolemaic astronomy.

Hesychast controversy

Barlaam attacked the Hesychast monks of Mount Athos, mocking their claim that prayer could permit them to perceive the uncreated light of God, and criticizing their psychosomatic prayer technique. Gregory Palamas defended the monks, and the dispute became a major theological controversy in the Byzantine Empire. Councils at Constantinople in 1341 sided against Barlaam, who left for Italy, converted to Roman Catholicism, and became bishop of Gerace in 1342. His critique was formally condemned at the synod of 1351, after his death.

Legacy

In Italy, Barlaam taught Greek to Petrarch and influenced Giovanni Boccaccio, contributing to the transmission of Greek learning to the early Renaissance.
