Wichita, Kansas

Wichita
State: Kansas
County: Sedgwick (seat)
Population: about 397,000 (2020)
Nickname: "Air Capital of the World"
River: Arkansas River

Wichita is the largest city in the U.S. state of Kansas and the county seat of Sedgwick County. As of the 2020 census, the population of the city was 397,532. Located in south-central Kansas at the confluence of the Arkansas and Little Arkansas Rivers, Wichita began as a trading post on the Chisholm Trail in the 1860s and became a booming cattle town.

History

The city was incorporated in 1870 and grew as railroads arrived and cattle drives from Texas made it a railhead. In the 20th century it became a major centre of aircraft manufacturing — home to Cessna, Beechcraft and Learjet plants and a Boeing (later Spirit AeroSystems) facility — earning the nickname "Air Capital of the World". Wichita was also the site of a significant 1958 sit-in at a Dockum drugstore lunch counter, among the earliest successful sit-ins of the civil rights movement.

Landmarks and economy

Landmarks include the Keeper of the Plains, a 13.4-metre steel sculpture by Kiowa-Comanche artist Blackbear Bosin at the rivers' confluence, and Old Cowtown Museum. Wichita State University is the city's major university. Aviation manufacturing, health care and Koch Industries (headquartered in Wichita) anchor the economy.
