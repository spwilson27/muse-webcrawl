Bee Gees

The Bee Gees were a musical group formed in 1958 by brothers Barry, Robin and Maurice Gibb. Born on the Isle of Man and raised in Manchester and Australia, they became one of the best-selling music acts of all time.

Career

The trio first found success with harmony-rich pop in the 1960s, including "Massachusetts" and "To Love Somebody". In the mid-1970s they reinvented their sound with falsetto vocals and disco, providing much of the Saturday Night Fever soundtrack (1977), including "Stayin' Alive", "How Deep Is Your Love" and "Night Fever".

Legacy

The Bee Gees wrote many hits for other artists, were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1997, and have sold over 120 million records worldwide. Maurice died in 2003 and Robin in 2012. See Disco.
