Billboard 200

The Billboard 200 is a record chart ranking the 200 most popular music albums and EPs in the United States. Published weekly by Billboard magazine, it is based on consumption measured by Luminate, combining traditional album sales, track equivalent albums and streaming equivalent albums.

History

Billboard began an albums chart in 1945 with five positions. A regular 200-position chart was established in 1967, drawing on mono and stereo listings merged in 1968. In 1991 the chart switched to Nielsen SoundScan point-of-sale data, greatly increasing accuracy.

Methodology

Since December 2014, rankings use multi-metric consumption: one album sale equals one unit; ten individual track downloads equal one unit; and a set number of on-demand streams (originally 1,500) equal one unit, with later revisions distinguishing programmed and on-demand and paid versus ad-supported streams.

Records

The Beatles hold the record for most number-one albums. Long-running number ones include the West Side Story soundtrack, Michael Jackson's Thriller, Adele's 21 and Taylor Swift's albums. The first number one on the modern chart in 1967 was by the Monkees.
