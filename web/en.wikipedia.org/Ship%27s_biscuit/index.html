Ship's biscuit

Also called: Hardtack, sea biscuit
Ingredients: Flour, water, sometimes salt
Known for: Extreme hardness and long shelf life

A ship's biscuit, also known as hardtack, is a simple type of biscuit or cracker made from flour, water and sometimes salt. Inexpensive and extremely long-lasting, it was used to sustain sailors and soldiers on long journeys when fresh food was unavailable, from the age of sail into the twentieth century.

Preparation

The dough is baked hard — historically two or more times — to drive out moisture, producing a dense, dry biscuit that resists spoilage for years if kept dry. Sailors often soaked it in water, tea or stew, or broke it up, because in its dry state it was hard enough to break teeth.

Infestation and lore

Stored biscuits frequently became infested with weevils, giving rise to sailors' jokes about extra protein and traditions such as rapping the biscuit on the table to shake insects out. Surviving examples are displayed in maritime museums, and hardtack remained a military ration item through both world wars in some countries.
