Griffith Observatory

The Griffith Observatory is an observatory and planetarium in Los Angeles, California, on the south-facing slope of Mount Hollywood in Griffith Park. It commands a view of the Los Angeles Basin, including downtown Los Angeles to the southeast, Hollywood to the south, and the Pacific Ocean to the southwest. The observatory is a popular tourist attraction with an extensive array of space and science-related displays, and admission has been free since it opened in 1935, in accordance with the will of its benefactor, Griffith J. Griffith.

History

Griffith J. Griffith, a Welsh-American industrialist, donated the land for Griffith Park in 1896 and, in his 1919 will, left funds for an observatory, exhibit hall and planetarium, inspired in part by a visit to Mount Wilson Observatory where he looked through the 60-inch telescope. The building, designed by John C. Austin and Frederick M. Ashley, opened to the public on 14 May 1935. During World War II the planetarium trained pilots in celestial navigation; it later trained Apollo astronauts. The observatory closed in 2002 for a major renovation and expansion — largely excavated beneath the original building — and reopened in November 2006.

Exhibits and instruments

Exhibits include a Foucault pendulum in the central rotunda, beneath Hugo Ballin's murals of the history of science; the Wilder Hall of the Eye; the Leonard Nimoy Event Horizon theater; and the Samuel Oschin Planetarium. On the roof is a 12-inch Zeiss refracting telescope, which the observatory claims more people have looked through than any other telescope in the world, offered free to visitors on clear nights. Sunset views, the Hollywood Sign and the Astronomers Monument — honouring Hipparchus, Copernicus, Galileo, Kepler, Newton and Herschel — draw crowds daily.

In popular culture

The observatory is a celebrated film location, most famously in Rebel Without a Cause (1955), whose planetarium scenes are commemorated by a bust of James Dean on the grounds. It also appears prominently in La La Land (2016) and many other films and television programmes.
