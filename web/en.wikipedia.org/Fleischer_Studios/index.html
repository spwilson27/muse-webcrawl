Fleischer Studios

Fleischer Studios was an American animation studio founded in 1921 by brothers Max and Dave Fleischer, and based in New York City (later Miami). It was a major competitor to Walt Disney Productions in the 1920s and 1930s and is remembered for creating Betty Boop and Koko the Clown, for bringing Popeye the Sailor to the screen, and for the first animated Superman cartoons. Max Fleischer's invention of the rotoscope (patented 1917), which allowed animators to trace live-action film for realistic motion, was foundational to the studio's style.

History and innovations

The studio grew out of Max Fleischer's experiments during the First World War and the Out of the Inkwell series (1918–1929), in which Koko the Clown interacted with the live-action world. Technical innovations followed: the Fleischer Song Car-Tunes (1924–1927) introduced the "follow the bouncing ball" sing-along format and were among the first cartoons with synchronised sound, predating Disney's Steamboat Willie. In the sound era, the studio's Talkartoons launched Betty Boop (introduced 1930, a star by 1932), whose jazz-age flapper character and surreal, urban, often risqué cartoons — featuring music by Cab Calloway and Louis Armstrong — gave Fleischer a distinctly adult, New York flavour in contrast to Disney's wholesome style. Enforcement of the Hays Code after 1934 forced Betty to be toned down. The studio developed the "stereoptical" setback process, combining animated cels with three-dimensional miniature sets for depth.

Popeye, features, and decline

The Popeye series, licensed from King Features and launched in 1933 with a Betty Boop cartoon, became the studio's greatest success, by some measures surpassing Mickey Mouse in popularity by the late 1930s. Fleischer moved to Miami in 1938 and produced two animated features for Paramount: Gulliver's Travels (1939), a moderate success, and Mr. Bug Goes to Town (1941), which failed amid the studio's mounting debts. The lavish Superman serials (1941–1943) were critical triumphs but expensive. Paramount seized control of the studio in 1942, reorganising it as Famous Studios; Max and Dave Fleischer departed. The Fleischer Popeye and Superman cartoons remain landmarks of animation history.
