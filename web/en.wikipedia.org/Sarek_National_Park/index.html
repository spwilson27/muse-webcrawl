Sarek National Park (Swedish: Sareks nationalpark) is a national park in Jokkmokk Municipality, Lapland, in northern Sweden. Established in 1909, it is one of the oldest national parks in Europe. Sarek is a wild, mountainous area of high alpine peaks, deep valleys, and around one hundred glaciers, with virtually no tourist facilities — no marked trails, bridges, or cabins within the park — making it a destination reserved for experienced hikers. It forms part of the Laponia World Heritage Site, recognised for both its natural values and the continuing reindeer-herding culture of the Sámi people.

Geography

The park covers 1,970 square kilometres of the Scandinavian Mountains near the Arctic Circle. It contains six of Sweden's thirteen peaks over 2,000 metres, including Sarektjåkkå (2,089 m), the country's second-highest mountain. The Rapa River, fed by glaciers, forms a lush delta in the eastern part of the park, a classic landscape often photographed from the Skierfe cliff. Rainfall is among the highest in Sweden, and weather conditions can change rapidly even in summer.

Wildlife and access

Sarek is home to large predators including brown bear, wolverine, and lynx, as well as moose and herds of reindeer tended by Sámi herders, for whom the area remains working land. Access is from trailheads at Kvikkjokk, Ritsem, and Saltoluokta outside the park boundaries. Because there are no maintained trails or shelters, visitors must be entirely self-sufficient and capable of navigating trackless terrain and fording glacial rivers.
