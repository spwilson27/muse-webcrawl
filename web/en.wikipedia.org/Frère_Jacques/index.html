"Frère Jacques" is a French nursery rhyme of uncertain origin, traditionally sung as a round. In English it is known as "Brother John". The song addresses a sleeping friar — Brother Jacques — urging him to wake and ring the bells for matins: "Frère Jacques, Frère Jacques, / Dormez-vous ? Dormez-vous ? / Sonnez les matines ! Sonnez les matines ! / Ding, ding, dong."

Origins

The melody and words are first documented in the late eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, though the song may be older. Various identifications of "Jacques" have been proposed — including a Dominican friar or the composer Jean-Philippe Rameau's contemporary circles — but none is certain. Because its phrases repeat and its range is small, the tune is among the first songs taught to children in many countries, and versions exist in dozens of languages.

In classical music

Gustav Mahler used the melody, transposed into a minor key, as a funeral march in the third movement of his Symphony No. 1. The tune also appears in works by other composers and is widely quoted in popular culture.
