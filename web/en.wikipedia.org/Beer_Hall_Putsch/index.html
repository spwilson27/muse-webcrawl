Beer Hall Putsch

Date: 8–9 November 1923
Location: Munich, Bavaria, Germany
Result: Putsch suppressed; Hitler imprisoned

The Beer Hall Putsch, also known as the Munich Putsch, was a failed coup d'état by Adolf Hitler and the Nazi Party on 8–9 November 1923 in Munich, Germany. Inspired by Mussolini's March on Rome, Hitler and the decorated war hero Erich Ludendorff attempted to seize the Bavarian government and use it as a base to overthrow the Weimar Republic.

The putsch

On the evening of 8 November, Hitler and armed SA men burst into the Bürgerbräukeller beer hall, where Bavarian state commissioner Gustav Ritter von Kahr was addressing a meeting, and declared a "national revolution". Hostages were released after apparent agreement, but Kahr and the other leaders alerted the authorities overnight. The next day, some 2,000 Nazis marched toward the city centre and were stopped at the Odeonsplatz by police gunfire; sixteen Nazis and four policemen were killed, and Hitler fled, suffering a dislocated shoulder.

Aftermath

Hitler was arrested days later and tried for high treason in early 1924. The trial gave him a national platform. Convicted, he received a lenient five-year sentence in Landsberg Prison, of which he served less than nine months, dictating Mein Kampf during his confinement. The putsch's failure led him to pursue power through legal and electoral means thereafter; the Nazi Party later commemorated the sixteen dead putschists as martyrs.
