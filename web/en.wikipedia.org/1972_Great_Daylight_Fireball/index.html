1972 Great Daylight Fireball

Date10 August 1972
TypeEarth-grazing meteoroid
Closest approachAbout 57 km over Montana, USA

The Great Daylight Fireball of 1972 was an Earth-grazing meteoroid that passed within the Earth's atmosphere for about 100 seconds on 10 August 1972 before returning to space. It was visible in broad daylight over the western United States and Canada.

Path

The object, estimated at several metres to more than ten metres across, entered the atmosphere over Utah at about 15 km/s, descended to a minimum altitude of roughly 57 km over Montana, and then exited the atmosphere over Alberta, losing substantial mass and velocity. It was filmed by a tourist at Grand Teton National Park in Wyoming, and its passage was recorded by a US Air Force satellite.

Significance

The event was one of the best-documented Earth-grazing fireballs and provided early evidence of the aerobraking effect of the atmosphere on a natural body.
