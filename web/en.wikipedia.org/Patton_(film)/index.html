Patton (film)

Patton is a 1970 American epic biographical war film about General George S. Patton during World War II. It stars George C. Scott as Patton and Karl Malden as General Omar Bradley, and was directed by Franklin J. Schaffner from a screenplay by Francis Ford Coppola and Edmund H. North.

Plot

The film opens with Patton's famous speech to the Third Army, delivered before a giant American flag. It then follows his campaigns in North Africa and Sicily — including the controversial slapping of a shell-shocked soldier — his sidelining during the Normandy landings as a decoy, his command of the Third Army across France, the relief of Bastogne during the Battle of the Bulge, and his final months in occupied Germany.

Reception

Patton was a critical and commercial success, winning seven Academy Awards, including Best Picture, Best Director, and Best Actor. Scott famously declined his Oscar, calling the ceremony a "meat parade". The film is preserved in the National Film Registry and is regularly listed among the greatest war films.
