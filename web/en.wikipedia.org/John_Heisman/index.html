John William Heisman (October 23, 1869 – October 3, 1936) was a player and coach of American football and a leading innovator of the early game. The Heisman Trophy, awarded annually to the most outstanding player in college football, is named after him.

Playing and coaching career

Heisman played football at Brown and the University of Pennsylvania, then coached at Oberlin, Buchtel (Akron), Auburn, Clemson, Georgia Tech, Pennsylvania, Washington & Jefferson and Rice. At Georgia Tech (1904–1919) his Golden Tornado teams went on a 33-game unbeaten streak and won the 1917 national championship.

Record victory

In 1916 Heisman's Georgia Tech team defeated Cumberland University 222–0, still the most lopsided victory in American football history.

Innovations

Heisman popularised the legal forward pass, the spiral snap from center, the hidden-ball play and the "Heisman shift", and campaigned for dividing games into quarters and for a scoring system closer to the modern one. He also coached baseball and basketball and served as athletic director of the Downtown Athletic Club in New York.

Legacy

After his death in 1936, the Downtown Athletic Club's award for the best college football player was renamed the Heisman Trophy in his memory.
