Kutiyattam

Kutiyattam (Koodiyattam) is a form of Sanskrit theatre from Kerala, India, traditionally performed in temple theatres called koothambalams. Recognised by UNESCO as a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity, it is among the oldest living theatre traditions in the world, with a documented history of around 2,000 years.

Performance

Performances are elaborate, lasting many nights, with actors (Chakyar and Nambiar performers, and Nangiar women) using stylised hand gestures (mudras), facial expressions, and eye movements to enact Sanskrit plays such as those of Bhasa and Kalidasa. The mizhavu drum provides the rhythmic core. A single act may be expanded over several nights of detailed elaboration.

Modern era

In the 20th century, gurus such as Mani Madhava Chakyar brought Kutiyattam outside temple precincts, and institutions like Margi and Kalamandalam now train new performers.
