Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun and the second-smallest planet in the Solar System, larger only than Mercury. It is often called the "Red Planet" because iron oxide (rust) on its surface gives it a reddish appearance. Mars is a terrestrial planet with a thin atmosphere, polar ice caps of water and carbon dioxide, extinct volcanoes — including Olympus Mons, the tallest volcano in the Solar System — and the vast canyon system Valles Marineris.

Physical characteristics

Mars has about half Earth's diameter and only 11% of its mass, with surface gravity about 38% of Earth's. Its day (a sol) is slightly longer than an Earth day, and its axial tilt of 25.2° produces seasons similar to Earth's but nearly twice as long. Surface temperatures average about −63 °C.

Geology and water

Dried river valleys, lakebeds and minerals that form in water show that liquid water once flowed on Mars. Today water exists mainly as ice at the poles and underground. The rovers Spirit, Opportunity, Curiosity and Perseverance have explored the surface, and Perseverance is caching samples for a possible return to Earth.

Moons

Mars has two small, irregular moons, Phobos and Deimos, probably captured asteroids. Phobos is slowly spiralling inward and will eventually break up into a ring.

Exploration and culture

Robotic orbiters and landers from several space agencies have studied Mars since the 1960s. Named for the Roman god of war, the planet has been a fixture of astronomy, mythology and science fiction, and is the leading target for proposed human missions and discussions of past or present microbial life.
