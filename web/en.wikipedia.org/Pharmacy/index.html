Pharmacy

Pharmacy is the science and practice of discovering, producing, preparing, dispensing, reviewing and monitoring medications, aiming to ensure the safe, effective, and affordable use of medicines. It is a miscellaneous science as it links health sciences with pharmaceutical sciences and natural sciences. The professional practice is becoming more clinically oriented nowadays as most medicines are now manufactured by pharmaceutical industries, shifting pharmacists toward patient care, medication review, and counselling.

History

The earliest known compilation of medicinal substances was the Sushruta Samhita, an Indian Ayurvedic treatise. The first apothecary shops opened in Baghdad in the 8th century during the Islamic Golden Age, and the profession of pharmacy separated from that of medicine in the Islamic world before doing so in Europe. In 1240, Emperor Frederick II issued an edict formally separating the professions of physician and pharmacist in the Kingdom of Sicily. The mortar and pestle and the Bowl of Hygieia remain enduring symbols of the profession.

Practice areas

Pharmacists practise in community pharmacies, hospitals, clinics, the pharmaceutical industry, regulatory agencies, and academia. Clinical pharmacists work directly with physicians and patients to optimise medication therapy, while compounding pharmacists prepare customised formulations. Modern roles increasingly include vaccination, prescribing in some jurisdictions, chronic disease management, and pharmacovigilance.

Education and regulation

Pharmacists are university-educated and licensed; in many countries the Doctor of Pharmacy (PharmD) or a master's degree in pharmacy is the qualifying degree, followed by registration with a national regulator. Pharmacies themselves are also licensed premises subject to inspection and controlled-drug rules.
