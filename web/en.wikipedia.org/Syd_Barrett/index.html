Syd Barrett

Syd Barrett
Born: Roger Keith Barrett, 6 January 1946, Cambridge
Died: 7 July 2006, Cambridge
Occupation: Singer-songwriter, guitarist, painter
Known for: Co-founder of Pink Floyd

Roger Keith "Syd" Barrett (1946–2006) was an English musician who co-founded the rock band Pink Floyd in 1965 and was its original frontman and principal songwriter. His whimsical, psychedelic songs defined the band's early sound and made him a foundational figure of psychedelic rock before mental illness and heavy LSD use led to his departure in 1968.

Pink Floyd and solo work

Barrett wrote most of Pink Floyd's debut album The Piper at the Gates of Dawn (1967) and the hit singles "Arnold Layne" and "See Emily Play." As his behaviour became erratic, guitarist David Gilmour was brought in, and Barrett left the band in April 1968. He released two solo albums, The Madcap Laughs and Barrett (both 1970), produced with help from his former bandmates.

Later life and legacy

After a final attempted comeback in the mid-1970s, Barrett withdrew to Cambridge, living quietly as Roger Barrett, painting and gardening, and avoiding the music industry for three decades until his death from pancreatic cancer in 2006. Pink Floyd's 1975 album Wish You Were Here and its song "Shine On You Crazy Diamond" are tributes to him. His influence on psychedelic and alternative music remains extensive.
