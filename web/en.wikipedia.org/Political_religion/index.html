Political religion

Political religion is a concept describing belief systems — such as those of totalitarian movements — that function like religions, with sacred texts, rituals, prophets and promises of salvation, while centering on a political entity, leader or ideology rather than a deity.

Scholars including Eric Voegelin and Emilio Gentile applied the idea to Nazism, Fascism and Stalinism, analyzing leader cults, liturgical rallies and sacralization of politics. Related concepts include civil religion, which concerns shared national rituals and beliefs in democratic societies.
