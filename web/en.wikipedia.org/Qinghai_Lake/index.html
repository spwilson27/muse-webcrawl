Qinghai Lake

Qinghai Lake and rapeseed fields in summer

Qinghai Lake (Chinese: 青海湖; Tibetan: མཚོ་སྔོན་པོ་, Mtsho-sngon-po, "blue lake"; Mongolian: Köke nuur) is the largest lake in China and the largest alkaline (salt) lake in the country. It lies in an endorheic basin in Qinghai province, on the northeastern Tibetan Plateau, at an altitude of about 3,200 metres (10,500 ft).

Description

The lake has a surface area of roughly 4,300–4,500 km² (it shrank for decades during the 20th century before stabilising and partly recovering). It is fed by some two dozen rivers, principally the Buha River, and has no outlet; its salinity is lower than seawater but too high for most fish — its most notable species is the Qinghai Lake naked carp, an endemic fish of cultural and ecological importance. The lake's bird islands are a major breeding site for migratory birds, including bar-headed geese, and a Ramsar-listed wetland.

Culture and tourism

The lake is sacred in Tibetan Buddhism, and pilgrims traditionally circumambulate it. Since 2002 the Tour of Qinghai Lake, a professional multi-stage road cycling race, has been held around its shores each summer, one of the highest-altitude races in the world.
