National Eisteddfod of Wales

The National Eisteddfod of Wales (Welsh: Eisteddfod Genedlaethol Cymru) is one of the largest festivals of competitive music and poetry in the world, held annually in Wales, alternating between the north and the south of the country. It takes place during the first week of August and is conducted almost entirely through the medium of Welsh.

History

The first national eisteddfod in the modern sense was held at Aberdare in 1861, building on a long tradition of local eisteddfodau and the medieval gatherings of bards. The festival was reshaped in the late nineteenth century by the Gorsedd of the Bards, an institution founded by Iolo Morganwg in 1792, which still presides over the festival's main ceremonies.

Competitions and ceremonies

Competitors take part in a wide range of contests, including choral singing, solo singing, recitation, literature and composition. The festival's most prestigious literary honours are the Chair (awarded for a poem in free metre) and the Crown (for a poem in strict metre), each presented in dramatic ceremonies involving druids in flowing robes. The Prose Medal and the Daniel Owen Memorial Prize are also major awards.

The Maes

The festival site, known as the Maes, includes a large pavilion, exhibition spaces, stalls run by Welsh organisations and businesses, and a campsite. The Eisteddfod attracts around 150,000 visitors each year and is a cornerstone of Welsh-speaking cultural life.
