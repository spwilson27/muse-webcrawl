Leyland Motors Limited was a British vehicle manufacturer of trucks, buses, and trolleybuses. Founded in 1896 in Leyland, Lancashire, it grew into one of the world's largest commercial vehicle producers before becoming the core of the British Leyland Motor Corporation in 1968.

History

The company began as the Lancashire Steam Motor Company, building steam-powered lawn mowers and wagons. Renamed Leyland Motors in 1907, it produced petrol-engined trucks that served extensively in the First World War. Between the wars and after 1945, Leyland expanded through acquisitions, taking over Albion Motors, Scammell, and Standard-Triumph, and became a major exporter of buses, including thousands supplied to Cuba and other markets.

British Leyland and decline

In 1968 Leyland merged with British Motor Holdings to form the British Leyland Motor Corporation, a state-influenced giant that struggled with industrial disputes, ageing products, and foreign competition. The truck and bus operations passed through nationalization, were later privatized, and the Leyland Trucks business was sold to the Dutch company DAF in 1987; after DAF's collapse it became part of the American PACCAR group, which still builds DAF trucks at Leyland.

Legacy

The Leyland name survives in Leyland Trucks and in India's Ashok Leyland, founded in 1948 as a distributor and manufacturer of Leyland vehicles and now one of the world's largest bus and truck makers.
