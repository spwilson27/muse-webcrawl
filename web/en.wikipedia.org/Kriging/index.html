Kriging

One-dimensional data interpolation by kriging, with confidence intervals shown.

In statistics, originally in geostatistics, kriging or Kriging (also known as Gaussian process regression) is a method of interpolation based on Gaussian processes governed by prior covariances. Under suitable assumptions on the priors, kriging gives the best linear unbiased prediction (BLUP) at unsampled locations. Interpolating methods based on criteria other than smoothness need not yield the most likely intermediate values.

The method is widely used in the domain of spatial analysis and computer experiments. The technique is also known as Wiener–Kolmogorov prediction, after Norbert Wiener and Andrey Kolmogorov. The theoretical basis for the method was developed by the French mathematician Georges Matheron in 1960, based on the master's thesis of Danie G. Krige, the pioneering plotter of distance-weighted average gold grades at the Witwatersrand reef complex in South Africa. Krige sought to estimate the most likely distribution of gold based on samples from a few boreholes.

Principles

Kriging predicts the value of a function at a given point by computing a weighted average of the known values of the function in the neighbourhood of the point. The weights are chosen so that the prediction is unbiased and has minimum variance, and they depend on the spatial correlation structure of the data, modelled by a variogram or covariance function. Unlike simpler methods such as inverse-distance weighting, kriging accounts for the redundancy of clustered samples and provides an estimate of the prediction error (the kriging variance) alongside the prediction itself.

Types of kriging

Common variants include simple kriging (known constant mean), ordinary kriging (unknown constant mean, the most widely used), universal kriging (mean modelled as a trend surface), indicator kriging (for categorical or threshold-exceedance probabilities), and co-kriging (using auxiliary correlated variables). Kriging is mathematically equivalent to regression with radial basis functions and to splines under certain covariance choices, and is the spatial form of Gaussian process regression used widely in machine learning.

Applications

Kriging is used in mining and resource estimation, hydrology, soil science, meteorology, environmental monitoring, petroleum geology, and the design and analysis of computer experiments, where Gaussian process surrogate models guide optimisation (for example through expected-improvement criteria in Bayesian optimisation).
