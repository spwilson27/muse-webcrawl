À la carte

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

À la carte dining: each dish is listed and priced separately.

À la carte (French pronunciation: ​[a la kaʁt]) is a French-language phrase meaning "according to the menu", used in restaurant terminology in several related senses. In its primary sense, it refers to a menu of items priced and ordered separately, as opposed to a table d'hôte menu, in which a predetermined selection of courses is offered for a fixed price.

History

The phrase originates from French dining customs. The earliest known usage in English dates from 1816, in the period following the rise of the modern restaurant in Paris after the French Revolution. As restaurants developed, the à la carte format allowed diners greater freedom of choice than the communal or fixed offerings that had preceded it.

Usage

In a restaurant, à la carte items are priced individually; the total cost of a meal therefore depends on the dishes selected. À la carte ordering contrasts with set menus, tasting menus, buffets and prix fixe formats. The term has also passed into wider usage to describe any arrangement in which components are chosen and paid for individually, such as television channels, software features, airline extras or bundled services offered without a package.
