Sardine run

The sardine run is a seasonal phenomenon off the eastern coast of South Africa in which billions of southern African pilchards (Sardinops sagax, commonly called sardines) migrate northward along the coast of the Eastern Cape and KwaZulu-Natal during the austral winter, typically between May and July. It is one of the largest animal migrations on Earth by biomass, rivalling the wildebeest migrations of East Africa.

The migration

The run occurs when a tongue of cold water (below about 21°C) forms along the coast, allowing sardines from the Agulhas Bank spawning grounds to move northeast into waters normally too warm for them. Shoals can be many kilometres long and are visible from the air and from shore.

Predator feast

The sardines attract an extraordinary aggregation of predators: common dolphins herd the fish into dense "bait balls," joined by sharks, seals, Bryde's whales, and thousands of Cape gannets plunge-diving from above. The spectacle draws divers and tourists to towns such as Port St. Johns and Durban, and the arrival of the shoals supports local net fisheries. Some years the run fails to reach KwaZulu-Natal, an unpredictability linked to oceanographic conditions.
