Bernie Sanders 2016 presidential campaign

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The 2016 presidential campaign of Bernie Sanders, the junior United States senator from Vermont, was formally launched on May 26, 2015, in Burlington, Vermont. Sanders, an independent who described himself as a democratic socialist, sought the Democratic Party's nomination for President of the United States in the 2016 election.

Platform

Sanders's campaign focused on income and wealth inequality, campaign finance reform, and opposition to free trade agreements. Key proposals included a single-payer Medicare for All healthcare system, tuition-free public colleges funded by a tax on Wall Street speculation, a $15 federal minimum wage, breaking up large banks, and aggressive action on climate change. Rejecting contributions from super PACs, the campaign relied on small-dollar donations from millions of individual supporters, repeatedly breaking grassroots fundraising records.

Results

Though initially considered a long shot against former Secretary of State Hillary Clinton, Sanders won 23 primaries and caucuses, roughly 13 million votes, and 46 percent of pledged delegates. He was particularly strong among young voters and in caucus states, and won upset victories in Michigan and Indiana. On July 12, 2016, Sanders endorsed Clinton, and he spoke at the Democratic National Convention later that month urging unity behind her candidacy against Republican nominee Donald Trump.
