Endogenous retrovirus

Endogenous retroviruses (ERVs) are viral sequences in a genome derived from ancient retroviral infections of germ cells, inherited by descendants as part of their DNA. They make up roughly 8% of the human genome (HERVs).

Formation

When a retrovirus infects a germline cell, its proviral DNA can be passed on vertically. Over generations mutations disable most copies, leaving fossils of past infections shared among related species, providing evidence for evolution and viral history.

Effects

Some ERV genes have been co-opted, notably syncytins derived from envelope genes and essential for placental development. Others can influence gene expression and have been implicated, controversially or in model systems, in disease and in protection against related exogenous viruses. Reactivation of a porcine ERV is a concern in xenotransplantation.
