The East Midlands is one of the nine official regions of England, composed of the ceremonial counties of Derbyshire, Leicestershire, most of Lincolnshire, Northamptonshire, Nottinghamshire and Rutland. Its largest cities include Leicester, Nottingham, Derby, — Lincoln and Northampton. East Midlands Airport, near Castle Donington, is one of the United Kingdom's principal air freight hubs.

Geography

The region's landscape ranges from the uplands of the Peak District in the north-west of Derbyshire, through the limestone country of the Derbyshire Dales and the Sherwood Forest area of Nottinghamshire, to the flat fenlands of Lincolnshire on the North Sea coast. Major rivers include the Trent, the third-longest river in the United Kingdom, the Soar, the Derwent and the Witham. The highest point is Kinder Scout at 636 m.

History

Much of the region corresponds to the Five Boroughs of the Danelaw (Derby, Leicester, Lincoln, Nottingham and Stamford) established under Viking rule in the ninth century. During the Industrial Revolution the area became noted for hosiery and lace in Nottingham and Leicester, boot and shoe manufacture in Northamptonshire, and coal mining and iron founding in Derbyshire and Nottinghamshire.

Economy

Manufacturing remains significant: Rolls-Royce (aero-engines) and Toyota in Derby, and the rail engineering cluster around Derby, the largest in the UK. Distribution and logistics benefit from the region's central location at the junction of the M1 and A1 corridors. Leicester has a major clothing manufacturing sector, and the region also hosts the National Forest, a major environmental regeneration project spanning parts of Derbyshire, Leicestershire and Staffordshire.
