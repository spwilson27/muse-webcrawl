Slovenia

Lake Bled, one of Slovenia's best-known landmarks

Slovenia (Slovene: Slovenija), officially the Republic of Slovenia, is a country in Central Europe. It is bordered by Italy to the west, Austria to the north, Hungary to the northeast, Croatia to the south and southeast, and the Adriatic Sea to the southwest. Slovenia covers 20,271 square kilometres and has a population of about 2.1 million. Its capital and largest city is Ljubljana.

History

The territory of modern Slovenia was settled by Slavic peoples from the 6th century. Over the following centuries it came under Frankish, and then largely Habsburg, rule, remaining part of the Austrian lands for centuries. After the First World War, Slovenes joined the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes, later Yugoslavia. Following the Second World War, Slovenia became a constituent republic of the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia. On 25 June 1991, Slovenia declared independence, prevailing in the brief Ten-Day War against the Yugoslav People's Army. It joined the European Union and NATO in 2004 and adopted the euro in 2007, the first of the new member states to do so.

Geography

Slovenia sits at the crossroads of the Alps, the Dinaric Alps, the Pannonian Plain and the Mediterranean. Over 60% of its territory is covered by forest, making it one of the most forested countries in Europe. Its highest peak is Triglav (2,864 m), a national symbol depicted on the country's coat of arms and flag. The country is known for its karst terrain — the very word "karst" derives from the Slovenian Kras plateau — including the Postojna Cave and the Škocjan Caves, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Economy and culture

Slovenia has a developed, high-income economy with strong pharmaceutical, automotive and tourism industries. The Slovene language, written in the Latin alphabet, is central to national identity. Notable cultural figures include the poet France Prešeren, whose poem Zdravljica supplies the national anthem, and the architect Jože Plečnik, whose works reshape much of Ljubljana.
