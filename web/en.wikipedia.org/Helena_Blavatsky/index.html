Helena Blavatsky

Helena Petrovna Blavatsky (born von Hahn; 12 August 1831 – 8 May 1891) was a Russian occultist, philosopher, and author who co-founded the Theosophical Society in New York in 1875. She claimed to have travelled widely in Asia and to have been initiated by hidden Masters (Mahatmas) into an ancient esoteric wisdom, which she presented in her books Isis Unveiled (1877) and The Secret Doctrine (1888), the foundational texts of Theosophy.

Life

Born into an aristocratic family in Yekaterinoslav, in the Russian Empire (now Dnipro, Ukraine), Blavatsky married young, left her husband, and spent years travelling — by her account through Egypt, India, and Tibet, though the details are disputed. In 1873 she arrived in New York, where she met Henry Steel Olcott; together with William Quan Judge they founded the Theosophical Society. In 1879 Blavatsky and Olcott moved to India, establishing the Society's headquarters at Adyar, near Madras (Chennai), where the movement promoted a revival of interest in Hindu and Buddhist thought.

Controversy and influence

In 1885, the Society for Psychical Research's Hodgson Report concluded that the "Mahatma letters" attributed to her Masters were fraudulent — a judgement the SPR itself revisited a century later. Admirers regard Blavatsky as a pioneering figure who introduced Eastern religious concepts such as karma and reincarnation to a wide Western audience; through Theosophy her ideas influenced figures from Mahatma Gandhi to the development of later New Age movements. Critics have accused her of plagiarism and fabrication. She died in London in 1891.
