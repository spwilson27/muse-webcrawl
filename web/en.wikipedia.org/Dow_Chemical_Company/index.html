Dow Chemical Company

The Dow Chemical Company is an American multinational corporation headquartered in Midland, Michigan, United States. Founded in 1897 by chemist Herbert Henry Dow, the company is one of the largest chemical producers in the world, manufacturing plastics, chemicals, and agricultural products. Dow operates in approximately 160 countries and employs around 36,000 people. Since 2019 the business has traded as Dow Inc., following the separation of DowDuPont into three companies.

History

Herbert Henry Dow founded the company in 1897 to extract bromine from the brine deposits beneath Midland, Michigan, using an electrolytic process he had developed. The company expanded into bleach and other chemicals, famously undercutting a German bromine cartel in the early 1900s. During the twentieth century Dow diversified into plastics such as polystyrene and Saran, magnesium production, and consumer products. In 2001 Dow acquired Union Carbide, and in 2009 it acquired Rohm and Haas. A merger of equals with DuPont was completed in 2017, creating DowDuPont, which in 2019 split into Dow Inc. (materials science), DuPont de Nemours (specialty products) and Corteva (agriculture).

Products

Dow's businesses are organised into segments including packaging and specialty plastics, industrial intermediates and infrastructure, and performance materials and coatings. Its products include polyethylene and other plastics, silicones (through the former Dow Corning), polyurethanes, construction chemicals, and coatings materials.

Controversies

Dow has faced sustained criticism and litigation over environmental and health issues, including its manufacture of Agent Orange during the Vietnam War era, napalm, dioxin contamination around its Midland plant, and — through its acquisition of Union Carbide — responsibility associated with the 1984 Bhopal disaster in India, which Dow maintains rests with Union Carbide as a separate entity.
