Copyright term

Copyright term is the length of time copyright subsists in a work. After the term expires, the work enters the public domain and may be used freely. Terms vary between jurisdictions and between types of work.

Life-plus terms

For works by individual authors, the Berne Convention requires a minimum term of the author's life plus 50 years. Many countries, including the European Union member states and the United States, have extended this to life plus 70 years. Mexico is notable for a term of life plus 100 years.

Corporate and anonymous works

In the United States, works made for hire and anonymous or pseudonymous works are protected for 95 years from publication or 120 years from creation, whichever expires first.

Extensions and debate

Term extensions, such as the US Copyright Term Extension Act of 1998, have been controversial: supporters argue they harmonise international law and reward creators, while critics argue they delay the enrichment of the public domain with little benefit to most authors.
