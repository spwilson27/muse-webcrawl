Big Four of thrash metal

The "Big Four" is a nickname given to four American thrash metal bands widely regarded as the genre's most influential and commercially successful acts of the 1980s: Metallica, Slayer, Megadeth, and Anthrax. Together they are credited with developing and popularizing thrash metal, which combined the speed of hardcore punk with the technique and riffing of the new wave of British heavy metal.

The Big Four performed together at a historic series of concerts in 2010–2011.

The bands

  • Metallica (formed 1981, Los Angeles) — the best-selling of the four, with landmark albums including Master of Puppets (1986) and Metallica (1991).
  • Slayer (formed 1981, Huntington Park) — known for extreme speed and dark lyrical themes; Reign in Blood (1986) is a genre benchmark.
  • Megadeth (formed 1983, Los Angeles) — founded by Dave Mustaine after his departure from Metallica; noted for technical complexity on albums such as Rust in Peace (1990).
  • Anthrax (formed 1981, New York City) — the East Coast member of the group, blending thrash with hardcore and famously collaborating with Public Enemy.

Big Four concerts

In 2010 the four bands played together for the first time as part of the Sonisphere Festival in Europe, beginning on June 16, 2010 in Warsaw, Poland. The June 22, 2010 show in Sofia, Bulgaria was filmed and released as The Big Four: Live from Sofia, Bulgaria. Further shows followed in the United States, including Indio, California, and Yankee Stadium in New York in 2011.

Legacy

The Big Four designation is sometimes debated, with fans arguing for bands such as Exodus, Testament, or the German "Teutonic Big Four" (Kreator, Sodom, Destruction, Tankard), but the original four remain the term's standard referents.
