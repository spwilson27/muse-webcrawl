Jules Bastien-Lepage (1848 – 1884) was a French painter associated with the Naturalist movement, celebrated for his scenes of rural peasant life painted with near-photographic precision combined with plein-air freshness. Though he died at only 36, his influence on late 19th-century painting in France, Britain, Scandinavia and America was considerable.

Life and training

Born in the village of Damvillers in the Meuse, the son of a farmer, Bastien-Lepage studied at the École des Beaux-Arts in Paris under Alexandre Cabanel. He served in the Franco-Prussian War and, after an early success with portraiture, turned to the countryside around his native village, where he painted peasants working in the fields with unsentimental accuracy.

Major works

His breakthrough came at the Paris Salon with Hay Making (Les Foins, 1877), showing two exhausted harvesters resting at midday, and October (1878), depicting potato gatherers. His large Joan of Arc (1879), showing the saint listening to her voices in a garden, was a sensation and is now in the Metropolitan Museum of Art. Critics including Émile Zola championed him as the leader of Naturalism in painting.

Legacy

Bastien-Lepage died of a stomach illness in Paris in 1884. A monument by Auguste Rodin stands in Damvillers. His blend of academic drawing and observed rural life influenced artists such as the British Newlyn School and the American and Scandinavian naturalists of the 1880s.
