Operation Rah-e-Rast (Urdu: "the path of righteousness"), also known as the Second Battle of Swat, was a military operation conducted by the Pakistan Army against Taliban militants led by Maulana Fazlullah in the Swat Valley of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province, from May to July 2009.

Pakistani troops advancing in the Swat Valley, 2009.

Background

Fazlullah's Tehrik-e-Nifaz-e-Shariat-e-Mohammadi (TNSM) and its Taliban allies had progressively seized control of Swat from 2007, imposing strict rule, destroying schools and attacking security forces, despite peace deals in 2008 and February 2009. When the militants refused to disarm under the Nizam-e-Adl agreement and advanced into the neighbouring Buner district, within about 100 km of Islamabad, the government authorised a full military offensive.

The operation

Beginning in late April 2009 in Buner and Lower Dir, the army committed some 15,000 troops with artillery and air support against an estimated 4,000–5,000 militants. Mingora, the valley's main city, was cleared in heavy street fighting by the end of May, and by July the army declared Swat back under government control. The fighting displaced around two million people in one of the largest civilian displacements of the era; most were able to return within months. Fazlullah escaped to Afghanistan, where he later became leader of the Pakistani Taliban before being killed in a 2018 drone strike.

Aftermath

The operation was broadly supported by the Pakistani public and marked a turning point in national attitudes toward the Taliban. Militants nevertheless staged retaliatory attacks across Pakistan in late 2009, and a major offensive followed in South Waziristan (Operation Rah-e-Nijat).
