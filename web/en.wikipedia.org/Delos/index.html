Delos

Delos is a small island in the Cyclades of the Aegean Sea, near Mykonos, that was one of the most important religious and commercial centres of the ancient Greek world. In mythology it was the birthplace of the twin gods Apollo and Artemis, children of Leto.

History

Delos was a major sanctuary of Apollo from the Archaic period, and after the Persian Wars it became the treasury and meeting place of the Delian League in 478 BCE, before Athens transferred the treasury in 454 BCE. Declared a free port by Rome in 166 BCE, the island boomed as an entrepôt — notoriously including one of the Mediterranean's largest slave markets — and housed wealthy merchant communities from Italy, Phoenicia, Syria and Egypt. Sacked by Mithridates' forces in 88 BCE and by pirates in 69 BCE, it declined swiftly and was eventually abandoned.

Archaeology

The entire island is an archaeological site, inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1990. Surviving remains include the Sanctuary of Apollo, the Terrace of the Lions (marble lions dedicated by the people of Naxos around 600 BCE), richly mosaic-floored houses such as the House of Dionysus, an amphitheatre and the Sacred Lake. The island today has no permanent population beyond custodians and archaeologists.
