Agni (Sanskrit: अग्नि, "fire") is the Hindu god of fire and a guardian of humanity, revered as the mouth of the gods and the medium through which offerings are conveyed in Vedic sacrifice (yajna). He is one of the oldest and most important of the Vedic deities; the Rigveda contains more hymns addressed to Agni than to any other god except Indra, and the very first hymn of the Rigveda is dedicated to him.

Characteristics

Agni is depicted as red-bodied with two faces, seven arms, three legs and seven fiery tongues, riding a ram. He is said to be born threefold: in the sky as the sun, in the atmosphere as lightning, and on earth as fire kindled by friction. As the divine priest (hotar) and messenger, he carries oblations of ghee to the gods and brings the gods to the sacrifice.

Role in ritual

Fire offerings (homa, havan) remain central to Hindu ritual life, from daily worship to weddings — where Agni is the divine witness (agni sakshi) — and cremations. The sacred fires of the Vedic tradition include the householder's fire (garhapatya), the southern fire (dakshinagni) and the offering fire (ahavaniya).

Related concepts

In Ayurveda and yoga, agni also denotes the digestive and metabolic fire within the body. Agni gives his name to the southeast direction, of which he is guardian (dikpala), and appears in Buddhist and Jain traditions as well.
