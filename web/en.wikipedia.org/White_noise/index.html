White noise

In signal processing, white noise is a random signal having equal intensity at different frequencies, giving it a constant power spectral density. The term is used in physics, acoustics, telecommunications, and statistics. The name derives from white light, which contains all visible wavelengths at roughly equal intensity.

Properties

Ideal white noise has a power spectral density that is constant across all frequencies, implying infinite total power; in practice, signals are called white over a limited band. Successive samples of discrete white noise are statistically uncorrelated. Other "colours" of noise, such as pink noise (1/f) and brown noise, have spectra that fall off with frequency and sound deeper to the ear, because human hearing is more sensitive to higher frequencies: white noise therefore sounds like a bright hiss similar to a waterfall or television static.

Uses

White noise is used to mask distracting sounds and aid sleep or concentration, in sound masking systems for offices, and for privacy in speech-sensitive settings. In engineering it is used to test audio equipment, to characterise the frequency response of systems, and as a source of randomness in simulation and in the mathematical modelling of phenomena such as thermal (Johnson–Nyquist) noise in electronics.
