Marginal tax rate

Chart of income tax brackets and marginal rates (image placeholder).

In a tax system, the marginal tax rate is the rate of tax applied to the next unit of income — that is, the tax paid on an additional dollar of income divided by that additional dollar. It contrasts with the average (effective) tax rate, which is total tax divided by total income.

Progressive systems

Under a progressive income tax with brackets, higher marginal rates apply only to income falling within each higher bracket, not to the taxpayer's entire income. A common misconception is that moving into a higher bracket can reduce after-tax income; because the higher rate applies only to the increment, after-tax income always rises when gross income rises within a simple bracket structure.

Economic significance

Marginal rates are central to analyses of labour supply, tax planning and the design of transfers; economists also study the effective marginal tax rate, which accounts for the withdrawal of means-tested benefits as income increases.
