RNA-dependent RNA polymerase

RNA-dependent RNA polymerase (RdRp)
EC number: 2.7.7.48
Function: Synthesizes RNA from an RNA template
Found in: RNA viruses; eukaryotes (RNA silencing)

An RNA-dependent RNA polymerase (RdRp or RdR) is an enzyme that catalyzes the synthesis of RNA from an RNA template. RdRps are essential proteins encoded in the genomes of all RNA viruses that have no DNA stage, allowing them to replicate their genomes inside host cells. Examples include the polymerases of poliovirus (3Dpol), influenza virus, hepatitis C virus (NS5B), and SARS-CoV-2 (nsp12).

Structure

Viral RdRps share a characteristic "right hand" fold with palm, fingers, and thumb subdomains, and conserved sequence motifs (A–E) in the palm that coordinate metal ions for catalysis. Most viral RdRps lack proofreading activity, contributing to high mutation rates; coronaviruses are unusual in possessing a proofreading exonuclease (nsp14).

Medical importance

Because host cells of animals do not normally use RdRp for gene expression, viral polymerases are attractive drug targets: nucleoside analogues such as sofosbuvir (hepatitis C) and remdesivir (SARS-CoV-2) inhibit them. Eukaryotic cells also have RdRps involved in RNA interference and small-RNA amplification in plants, fungi, and nematodes.
