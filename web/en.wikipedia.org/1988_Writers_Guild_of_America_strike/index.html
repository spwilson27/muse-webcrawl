1988 Writers Guild of America strike

DateMarch 7 – August 7, 1988
Duration154 days (22 weeks)
UnionWriters Guild of America, East and West
CounterpartAlliance of Motion Picture and Television Producers (AMPTP)
Main issueResiduals for foreign and one-hour-program syndication

The 1988 Writers Guild of America strike was a strike action taken by members of the Writers Guild of America, East and the Writers Guild of America, West against the Alliance of Motion Picture and Television Producers. Lasting from March 7 to August 7, 1988 — 154 days, or 22 weeks — it remains the longest strike in the Guild's history.

Issues

The central dispute concerned residual payments: the studios sought to cut residuals for one-hour television shows syndicated in rerun packages and to tie foreign-market residuals to a sliding scale, arguing that the growing syndication and overseas markets were unprofitable at existing rates. Writers resisted what they saw as a rollback of hard-won gains.

Effects on the industry

The strike delayed the start of the 1988–89 television season, forced networks to run reruns, summer replacement series, and revived formats such as an updated Mission: Impossible filmed in Australia, and encouraged experimentation with unscripted programming. Late-night shows returned without writers, improvising monologues.

Settlement

The eventual settlement in August largely preserved the writers' position on foreign residuals while giving producers some relief on one-hour syndication. The strike is often cited as a turning point that nudged American television toward reality programming and reshaped the economics of scripted television.
