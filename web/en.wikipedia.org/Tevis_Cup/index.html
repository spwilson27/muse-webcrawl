Tevis Cup

The Tevis Cup — formally the Western States Trail Ride — is a 100-mile (160 km) endurance equestrian ride held annually in California, United States. Founded in 1955 by horseman Wendell Robie, the ride follows the Western States Trail from the Lake Tahoe area over the Sierra Nevada crest to Auburn, and must be completed within 24 hours. It is widely regarded as one of the most difficult endurance rides in the world and was instrumental in establishing the modern sport of endurance riding.

Course

The trail climbs from the start near Truckee, California, crossing the crest of the Sierra Nevada at Watson's Monument (about 9,400 ft / 2,865 m), then descends through a series of steep canyons — including the notorious Cougar Rock — before dropping to the American River crossings and the finish at the Gold Country Fairgrounds in Auburn. Riders face roughly 19,000 feet of cumulative descent and 15,000 feet of ascent, summer heat that can exceed 40 °C in the canyons, and mandatory veterinary checkpoints along the route.

Rules and awards

Horses are examined by veterinarians at stops along the trail and must be judged "fit to continue" to proceed. Riders who finish within 24 hours receive the silver Tevis Cup buckle; the Tevis Cup itself is awarded to the first finisher whose horse is in sound condition. The Haggin Cup is presented to the rider among the top ten finishers whose horse is judged in the best condition the morning after the ride.

Legacy

The ride inspired the creation of the American Endurance Ride Conference (AERC) in 1972 and, indirectly, the Western States Endurance Run, an ultramarathon foot race founded after Gordy Ainsleigh completed the trail on foot in 1974. Auburn styles itself the "Endurance Capital of the World" in recognition of both events.
