City

A modern city skyline.

A city is a human settlement of substantial size and significance. Although there is no single agreed definition, cities are generally distinguished from towns and villages by their population size, density, economic functions, infrastructure, and administrative or legal status. Cities are centres of commerce, government, education, culture and transport, and they typically provide specialised services to a surrounding hinterland.

Definitions

Definitions vary by country and era. Some jurisdictions grant city status by charter, cathedral, or royal decree; others use population thresholds that range from a few hundred inhabitants (in some Scandinavian countries) to tens of thousands. In Japan a settlement generally needs at least 50,000 inhabitants to be designated a city, while in the United Kingdom city status is granted by the monarch on the advice of ministers, often historically associated with the presence of a cathedral. Statisticians often use functional definitions based on built-up area and commuting patterns, giving rise to concepts such as the metropolitan area and the conurbation.

History

The first cities emerged in the Neolithic and early Bronze Age in fertile river valleys, including Mesopotamia (such as Uruk and Ur), the Nile Valley, the Indus Valley, and northern China. Their growth depended on agricultural surplus, which freed part of the population for crafts, trade, administration and priesthood. Cities such as Jericho and Çatalhöyük are among the earliest known urban settlements. Over subsequent millennia, cities grew as centres of empire, religion and trade; by 1800 only a small fraction of humanity lived in them, but the Industrial Revolution triggered explosive urban growth. In 2007 the global urban population surpassed the rural population for the first time, and the United Nations projects that roughly two-thirds of humanity will live in urban areas by 2050.

Government and planning

Cities are usually governed by a mayor and council and provide services such as policing, sanitation, water supply, public transport and land-use planning. Urban planning addresses zoning, infrastructure, housing and public space, while issues such as congestion, pollution, inequality and housing affordability remain central challenges of urban governance.
