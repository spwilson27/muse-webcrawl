Transbay Tube

LocationSan Francisco Bay, California
OpenedSeptember 16, 1974
Length3.6 miles (5.8 km)

The Transbay Tube is an underwater rail tunnel that carries Bay Area Rapid Transit (BART) trains under San Francisco Bay between San Francisco and Oakland, California. At 3.6 miles (5.8 km) long, it was the longest immersed-tube tunnel in the world when it opened in 1974.

Construction

The tube consists of 57 steel-and-concrete sections built in a shipyard, floated into position, and sunk onto a prepared bed on the bay floor. It lies at depths reaching about 135 feet below the surface and is designed to flex during earthquakes. Following the 1989 Loma Prieta earthquake, seismic retrofits added internal steel lining and other strengthening work.

Service

All four transbay BART lines (Richmond, Antioch, Dublin/Pleasanton, and Fremont services) share the tube's two bores, making it one of the most heavily used rail crossings in the United States and a frequent subject of capacity discussions, including proposals for a second crossing.
