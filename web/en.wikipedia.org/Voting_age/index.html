Voting age

A voting age is a minimum age established by law that a person must attain before they become eligible to vote in a public election. The most common voting age worldwide is 18 years, though ages range from 16 to 25 depending on the country and the type of election. Voting ages have generally fallen over time as part of the expansion of universal suffrage.

Most countries set the voting age at 18.

History

Through most of the 19th and early 20th centuries, where voting existed at all, the minimum age was typically 21, following the common-law age of majority. Czechoslovakia lowered its age to 18 in 1946, among the first to do so. A wave of reductions followed in the 1960s and 1970s: the United Kingdom lowered the age to 18 in 1969, and the United States followed with the Twenty-sixth Amendment in 1971, driven by the argument that young men old enough to be conscripted for the Vietnam War should be old enough to vote — "old enough to fight, old enough to vote."

Votes at 16

Since the late 20th century, a number of jurisdictions have extended voting rights to 16-year-olds. Austria became the first European Union country to adopt 16 as the general voting age in 2007. Brazil, Ecuador, Cuba, and Nicaragua also permit voting at 16 in national elections, and Scotland and Wales allow 16-year-olds to vote in devolved elections. Supporters argue that earlier enfranchisement builds lifelong voting habits; critics question maturity and political knowledge. Campaigns for a lower voting age remain active in many countries.
