Jay Conrad Levinson

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Jay Conrad Levinson (February 10, 1933 – October 10, 2013) was an American business writer, best known as the author of the 1984 book Guerrilla Marketing. He popularised the concept of guerrilla marketing — achieving conventional marketing goals with unconventional, low-cost, imaginative methods — and is often called the "father of guerrilla marketing".

Early life and career

Levinson was born in Detroit, Michigan, raised in Chicago, and served in the United States Army. He studied psychology at the University of Colorado. He worked in advertising in London and in the United States, serving as a creative director at J. Walter Thompson and Leo Burnett, where he contributed to campaigns for brands including Marlboro and the Pillsbury Doughboy era of American advertising.

Guerrilla Marketing

After leaving agency work, Levinson taught at the University of California, Berkeley, and wrote Guerrilla Marketing (1984), aimed at small businesses with limited budgets. The book became an international bestseller, translated into dozens of languages, and spawned a long-running series of more than twenty guerrilla marketing titles covering topics such as advertising, publicity, the internet, and specific professions. The central idea is that small businesses can compete with larger rivals through creativity, time, energy, and information rather than large spending.

Death

Levinson died on October 10, 2013, in San Rafael, California, at the age of 80.
