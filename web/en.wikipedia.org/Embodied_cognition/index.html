Embodied cognition

Embodied cognition is the concept that many features of cognition are shaped by aspects of an organism's entire body. It challenges the classical view, associated with cognitivism and computationalism, that the mind can be understood as abstract symbol manipulation largely independent of the body.

Proponents argue that sensory and motor systems are not mere input and output devices but are constitutive of conceptual processing: concepts are grounded in bodily states, gestures aid reasoning, and cognition extends into the environment through tools and artefacts. The approach draws on the phenomenology of Maurice Merleau-Ponty, the ecological psychology of James J. Gibson, and work by George Lakoff and Mark Johnson on conceptual metaphor.

Embodied approaches have influenced robotics (where situated, reactive machines such as Rodney Brooks's subsumption architecture robots replaced central planners), linguistics and the philosophy of mind. Critics note that some strong claims of embodiment are difficult to test, and that abstract thought may require representations that go beyond sensorimotor simulation.
