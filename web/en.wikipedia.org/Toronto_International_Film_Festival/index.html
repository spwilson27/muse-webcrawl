Toronto International Film Festival

The Toronto International Film Festival (TIFF) is one of the most prestigious and largest publicly attended film festivals in the world, held annually in September in Toronto, Ontario, Canada. Founded in 1976 as the Festival of Festivals, it screens several hundred films from dozens of countries and attracts hundreds of thousands of attendees, including a large contingent of film industry professionals and international media.

History

The festival was founded by Bill Marshall, Dusty Cohl and Henk Van der Kolk. It initially showcased films already shown at other festivals before developing into a key launching platform in its own right. Its timing in early autumn makes it an influential early indicator for the awards season, and many films premiered at Toronto have gone on to win Academy Awards. The festival's permanent home, TIFF Bell Lightbox, opened in 2010 in the city's entertainment district.

Programme and awards

TIFF has no competitive jury prize of the traditional kind; its most prominent honour is the People's Choice Award, voted by audiences, alongside awards for Canadian features, short films and the Platform competition. Programmes include Gala Presentations, Special Presentations, Midnight Madness, Wavelengths for experimental film, and Discovery for emerging directors.

Significance

The festival is widely regarded, alongside Cannes, Venice, Berlin and Sundance, as one of the most important events in world cinema, and it anchors Toronto's large film and television production industry.
