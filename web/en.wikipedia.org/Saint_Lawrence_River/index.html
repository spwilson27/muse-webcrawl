The Saint Lawrence River (French: Fleuve Saint-Laurent) is a large river in northeastern North America that drains the Great Lakes into the Atlantic Ocean. Flowing northeast from Lake Ontario past Montreal and Quebec City, it widens into the great estuary and Gulf of Saint Lawrence. Together with the Great Lakes, it forms one of the largest freshwater systems on Earth.

Geography

The river proper runs about 500 km from Kingston, Ontario, to the gulf; measured from the head of the St. Louis River in Minnesota, the whole system spans over 3,000 km. Major tributaries include the Ottawa, Saguenay and Richelieu rivers. The Thousand Islands archipelago lies at its head, and the Île d'Orléans divides the channel near Quebec City. The river's average discharge of roughly 16,800 m³/s at its mouth is among the world's largest.

History

Jacques Cartier explored the river in 1535, claiming the land for France; Samuel de Champlain founded Quebec City on its banks in 1608. The St. Lawrence became the principal artery of New France and of the fur trade. After the British conquest it remained central to the economy of Canada.

Seaway and ecology

The Saint Lawrence Seaway, opened in 1959, uses locks and canals to let ocean-going vessels reach the Great Lakes ports, bypassing rapids such as those at Lachine. The estuary supports rich marine life, including beluga whales, and migratory birds along its shores. Ice cover in winter historically closed navigation, though icebreakers now extend the season.
