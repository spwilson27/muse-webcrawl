Electron beam

An electron beam (or e-beam) is a stream of electrons moving in a common direction, usually in vacuum. Early studies used cathode rays produced in Crookes tubes, leading J. J. Thomson in 1897 to identify the electron as a particle.

Beams are generated by thermionic, photoelectric, or field emission from a cathode, then accelerated by electric fields and focused and steered by electric and magnetic lenses. Beam energy is commonly expressed in electronvolts.

Applications

Electron beams power cathode-ray tubes (historically used in televisions and oscilloscopes), electron microscopes, electron-beam lithography for semiconductors, electron-beam welding and machining, sterilization and materials processing, radiotherapy, and the electron guns of particle accelerators and free-electron lasers.
