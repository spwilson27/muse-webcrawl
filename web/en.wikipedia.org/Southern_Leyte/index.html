Southern Leyte is a province in the Philippines located in the Eastern Visayas region. It occupies the southern quarter of the island of Leyte, together with Limasawa Island in the south — traditionally held to be the site of the first Catholic Mass in the Philippines, celebrated in 1521 — and Panaon Island. Its capital is the city of Maasin.

History

The province was created in 1959, when the southern municipalities were separated from Leyte province. The territory saw fighting during the Second World War, and nearby Leyte Gulf was the site of one of the largest naval battles in history in October 1944. In February 2006, a devastating landslide at Guinsaugon, Saint Bernard, buried a village and killed over a thousand people, one of the deadliest landslides in Philippine history. In December 2021, Typhoon Rai (Odette) caused widespread destruction across the province.

Geography

Southern Leyte is mostly hilly and agricultural, planted with coconut and abaca. It faces the Bohol Sea and Surigao Strait and forms part of the gateway between the Visayas and Mindanao, with ferry ports at Liloan and San Ricardo serving routes to Surigao. Sogod Bay, between its two peninsulas, is noted for whale shark sightings and marine sanctuaries.

Economy and culture

Agriculture and fishing dominate the economy, with copra, abaca fibre and rice the main products. The province is predominantly Roman Catholic, and Limasawa draws pilgrims commemorating the Easter Sunday Mass of 31 March 1521, during Ferdinand Magellan's expedition. The Sinulog-style local festivals and Visayan (Cebuano) language and culture link it closely to Cebu and Bohol.

Municipalities

The province comprises one city (Maasin) and eighteen municipalities, including Sogod, Liloan, Saint Bernard, Hinunangan, Padre Burgos and Malitbog. The Agas-Agas Bridge in San Juan is among the tallest bridges in the Philippines.
