Panchavadyam (Malayalam: pañcavādyam, "five instruments") is a temple art form and orchestral ensemble of Kerala in southern India, performed for Hindu temple festivals. An ensemble normally comprises five kinds of instrument — the timila, maddalam, ilathalam (cymbals), idakka and kombu (trumpet) — although the shankha (conch) is also often sounded; the name counts the five principal instruments.

Performance

A panchavadyam performance unfolds in phases, structured by a rhythmic cycle (tala) whose tempo and density build progressively from a slow, sparse opening to a fast, thunderous climax lasting, in major festivals, several hours. The timila players — the lead section, sometimes numbering in the dozens — begin; the maddalam drummers, ilathalam players, idakka player and kombu blowers join in turn. Ensembles can exceed a hundred musicians arranged in rows before the temple, traditionally bare-chested men wearing white dhotis.

Context

Panchavadyam accompanies the processions of caparisoned elephants that are the centrepiece of Kerala's great pooram festivals. Its most celebrated single performance is the Madathil Varavu panchavadyam at the Thrissur Pooram, where vast crowds gather to hear leading masters. The form is closely related to other Kerala percussion ensembles — chenda melam (such as panchari melam), thayambaka and panchavadyam's own sibling traditions — but is distinguished by the absence of the chenda drum and the prominence of the hourglass-shaped timila.
