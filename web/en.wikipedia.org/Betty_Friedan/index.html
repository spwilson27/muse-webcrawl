Betty Friedan (born Bettye Naomi Goldstein; 4 February 1921 – 4 February 2006) was an American feminist writer and activist. A leading figure in the women's movement in the United States, her 1963 book The Feminine Mystique is often credited with sparking the second wave of American feminism in the 20th century.

Early life and education

Friedan was born in Peoria, Illinois, to Jewish immigrant parents. She graduated summa cum laude from Smith College in 1942 with a degree in psychology, and did graduate work at the University of California, Berkeley, before leaving to become a labour journalist in New York.

The Feminine Mystique

In 1957, preparing a questionnaire for her Smith classmates' 15th reunion, Friedan found widespread dissatisfaction among educated suburban housewives. Her resulting book, published in 1963, described this malaise as "the problem that has no name" and challenged the postwar ideology that women should find fulfilment exclusively through marriage and motherhood. It became a bestseller and a landmark of feminist literature.

Activism

In 1966 Friedan co-founded the National Organization for Women (NOW) and served as its first president. She helped organise the Women's Strike for Equality in 1970 and co-founded the National Association for the Repeal of Abortion Laws (later NARAL). She also co-founded the National Women's Political Caucus in 1971.

Later work

Her later books included The Second Stage (1981), The Fountain of Age (1993), which examined aging, and her memoir Life So Far (2000). Friedan died of congestive heart failure in Washington, D.C., on her 85th birthday.
