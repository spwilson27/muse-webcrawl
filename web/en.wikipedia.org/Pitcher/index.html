Pitcher

In baseball and softball, the pitcher is the player who throws ("pitches") the baseball from the pitcher's mound toward the catcher to begin each play, with the goal of retiring a batter, who attempts to either make contact with the pitched ball or draw a walk. The pitcher is often considered the most important player on the defensive side of the game, and as such is situated at the first position in the numbering system used to designate each player's defensive position.

Overview

The pitcher throws the ball in a particular style and speed to induce the batter to hit the ball into the air, hit it on the ground, or strike out. Along with the catcher, the pitcher forms the battery. Starting pitchers are usually expected to pitch deep into games, while relief pitchers specialize in entering the game later, including closers who pitch the final innings to preserve a lead.

Pitch types

Pitchers command a repertoire of pitches that differ in speed, movement and trajectory, including the four-seam fastball, curveball, slider, changeup, sinker, cutter, and the rare knuckleball. Effective pitching relies on deception—varying speed and location so the batter cannot predict the pitch.

Pitching strategy

The pitcher and catcher work together to choose pitches based on the batter's weaknesses, the count, the score, and the base runners. Pitchers must also field their position, cover first base on ground balls to the right side, and hold runners close to their bases to prevent stolen bases.

Roles

  • Starting pitcher – begins the game on the mound.
  • Relief pitcher – enters after the starter is removed.
  • Closer – a reliever who specializes in finishing games with a lead.
  • Setup man – pitches the inning before the closer.
