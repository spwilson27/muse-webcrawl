The Port of Abidjan is the principal seaport of Ivory Coast (Côte d'Ivoire) and one of the largest and busiest ports in West Africa. Located on the Ébrié Lagoon, it is connected to the Atlantic Ocean by the Vridi Canal, opened in 1950, which made Abidjan an ocean port despite its lagoon setting.

Trade

Abidjan handles the bulk of Ivory Coast's exports, including cocoa (of which the country is the world's largest producer), coffee, timber, and petroleum products. It also serves as a transshipment and transit port for landlocked neighbors such as Burkina Faso and Mali.

Facilities

The port complex includes container terminals in Treichville and Vridi, a fishing port, oil terminals, and a second container terminal at Côte d'Ivoire Terminal opened in 2022 to accommodate larger vessels. Annual traffic exceeds 20 million tonnes of cargo.
