Bar Council

The General Council of the Bar, known as the Bar Council, is the representative body for barristers in England and Wales. Founded in 1894, it promotes the Bar's interests, campaigns on access to justice, and represents the profession to government and the public.

Role

Since the Legal Services Act 2007, regulation of barristers is separated from representation: the independent Bar Standards Board regulates, while the Bar Council represents. The Council is led by a Chair elected annually, supported by committees on policy, remuneration, and equality and diversity.

Activities

The Bar Council provides training, ethical guidance, and services to some 17,000 practising barristers, organises the annual Bar Conference, and runs pro bono and social mobility initiatives such as the Bar Placement Scheme.

Related bodies

Barristers must belong to one of the four Inns of Court — Gray's Inn, Lincoln's Inn, Inner Temple and Middle Temple — which are responsible for Call to the Bar.
