Charles, Prince of Wales, was the title held by the eldest son of Queen Elizabeth II from 1958 until his accession to the throne as Charles III on 8 September 2022. Born at Buckingham Palace in 1948, he was created Prince of Wales and Earl of Chester at the age of nine and was formally invested at Caernarfon Castle in Wales in 1969, in a ceremony watched by a worldwide television audience.

Tenure as Prince of Wales

Charles was the longest-serving Prince of Wales in history, holding the title for more than 64 years, and the longest-serving heir apparent in British history. As Prince of Wales he undertook official duties on behalf of the Queen, made hundreds of overseas visits, and founded numerous charities, most notably The Prince's Trust (founded 1976), which has helped disadvantaged young people into work, education, and training.

Interests and initiatives

He became known for advocacy on the environment, organic farming and climate change, for his interest in traditional architecture (embodied in the model town of Poundbury in Dorset), and for his Duchy Originals organic food brand. His views were sometimes conveyed to ministers in the so-called "black spider memos," so named for his handwriting.

Personal life

His marriage to Lady Diana Spencer in 1981, described as "the wedding of the century," produced two sons, William and Harry, before its breakdown and divorce. Diana died in a car crash in Paris in 1997. In 2005 Charles married Camilla Parker Bowles. On his accession she became Queen Consort, and later Queen Camilla.
