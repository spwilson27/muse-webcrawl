Fluminense FC

Fluminense Football Club is a Brazilian sports club based in the neighbourhood of Laranjeiras, in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil. Founded on 21 July 1902 by Oscar Cox, a Brazilian of English descent, it is the oldest football club in Rio de Janeiro. Fluminense, widely known by the nickname Flu and as the Tricolor for its maroon, green and white colours, is one of the four big clubs of Rio football, alongside Flamengo, Vasco da Gama and Botafogo.

History

Fluminense dominated the early years of the Carioca Championship, and its opening of the Estádio das Laranjeiras in 1919 — where the Brazil national team played its first matches — is a landmark in Brazilian football history. The club has won the Brazilian national championship (Campeonato Brasileiro Série A) four times, in 1970, 1984, 2010 and 2012, and the Copa do Brasil in 2007. It has also won the Rio state championship more than 30 times.

The club's greatest international triumph came in 2023, when Fluminense won the Copa Libertadores for the first time, defeating Boca Juniors 2–1 after extra time in the final at the Maracanã, with goals from Germán Cano and John Kennedy. Fluminense then finished runner-up at the 2023 FIFA Club World Cup, losing the final to Manchester City. Its fiercest rivalry is the Fla–Flu derby against Flamengo, one of the most famous derbies in world football, named by journalist Mário Filho in 1925.

Stadium and supporters

Fluminense plays its major home matches at the Maracanã Stadium, which it shares with Flamengo, while the club's headquarters and historic stadium remain at Laranjeiras. Famous players include Castilho, Telê Santana (who later became a celebrated coach), Rivelino, Fred — the club's top scorer of the modern era — and Germán Cano.
