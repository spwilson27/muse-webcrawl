Capri is an island in Italy's Bay of Naples, in the region of Campania. Famous since Roman times for its rugged beauty, sea caves, and mild climate, it has been a fashionable resort since the 19th century and remains one of Italy's most celebrated tourist destinations.

History

The emperor Augustus prized the island, and his successor Tiberius built villas there, ruling the empire from Capri in his later years (AD 27–37); the ruins of his Villa Jovis still crown the island's eastern headland. In modern times Capri attracted artists, writers, and aristocrats, becoming a byword for Mediterranean elegance.

Sights

The Blue Grotto (Grotta Azzurra) is a sea cave where sunlight passing through an underwater opening bathes the water in an intense blue glow. Other landmarks include the Faraglioni sea stacks, the Gardens of Augustus, the chairlift to Monte Solaro from Anacapri, and the main square of Capri town, the Piazzetta. The island is connected by ferry and hydrofoil to Naples and Sorrento.
