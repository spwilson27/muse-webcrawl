Seven virtues

In Christian tradition, the seven virtues are a set of virtues held to be counterpoints to the seven deadly sins. They combine the four cardinal virtues of classical philosophy — prudence, justice, temperance, and fortitude — with the three theological virtues of faith, hope, and charity (love), drawn from the writings of Paul the Apostle.

The seven virtues were discussed by early Christian writers and became a standard part of medieval moral teaching. Lists of virtues opposed to particular sins include humility against pride, kindness against envy, abstinence against gluttony, chastity against lust, patience against wrath, liberality against greed, and diligence against sloth.

The concept appears throughout medieval literature and art, including Prudentius's Psychomachia, an allegorical poem describing the battle between the virtues and vices for the human soul.
