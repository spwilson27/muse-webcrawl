Antihypertensive drug

Classes of medicines used to lower blood pressure

Antihypertensives are a class of drugs used to treat hypertension (high blood pressure). Lowering blood pressure with medication reduces the risk of stroke, myocardial infarction, heart failure and kidney disease. Several drug classes are available, and treatment is usually started with one or a combination of first-line agents chosen according to the patient's age, comorbidities and tolerance.

Main classes

  • Thiazide and thiazide-like diuretics — e.g. hydrochlorothiazide, chlortalidone, indapamide; increase sodium and water excretion.
  • ACE inhibitors — e.g. lisinopril, ramipril; block conversion of angiotensin I to angiotensin II. A dry cough is a common side effect.
  • Angiotensin II receptor blockers (ARBs) — e.g. losartan, valsartan; block angiotensin II at its receptor, used when ACE inhibitors are not tolerated.
  • Calcium channel blockers — e.g. amlodipine (dihydropyridines) and diltiazem/verapamil (non-dihydropyridines); relax vascular smooth muscle.
  • Beta blockers — e.g. atenolol, metoprolol; reduce heart rate and cardiac output, now mainly used when there are other indications such as coronary disease or heart failure.
  • Other agents — alpha blockers (doxazosin), centrally acting drugs (methyldopa, clonidine), direct vasodilators (hydralazine, minoxidil), aldosterone antagonists (spironolactone, valuable in resistant hypertension).

Use in practice

Guidelines such as those of the ACC/AHA (2017) and ESC/ESH recommend the four first-line classes (thiazides, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, calcium channel blockers), frequently as fixed-dose combination pills, which improve adherence. Resistant hypertension — uncontrolled despite three drugs including a diuretic — prompts evaluation for secondary causes. In hypertensive emergencies, rapidly acting intravenous agents such as labetalol or nicardipine are used under close monitoring.
