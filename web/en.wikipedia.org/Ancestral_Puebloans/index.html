Ancestral Puebloans

The Ancestral Puebloans were an ancient Native American culture that spanned the present-day Four Corners region of the United States, comprising southeastern Utah, northeastern Arizona, northwestern New Mexico, and southwestern Colorado. They are believed to have developed, at least in part, from the Oshara tradition, which developed from the Picosa culture. The people and their archaeological culture are often referred to as Anasazi, a term once common but now discouraged because of its Navajo-language meaning ("ancient enemies").

Archaeology

The Ancestral Puebloan chronology is divided into periods: Basketmaker II and III (c. 100–750 CE), Pueblo I (750–900), Pueblo II (900–1150), Pueblo III (1150–1350), and Pueblo IV (1350–1600). The earliest periods are characterised by pit houses and atlatl hunting; pottery appears in Basketmaker III. The Pueblo II period saw the florescence of the Chaco system, with its great houses, great kivas, and an extensive network of roads radiating across the San Juan Basin.

Culture

The Ancestral Puebloans were skilled farmers of the "three sisters" — maize, beans, and squash — supplemented by hunting and wild plant gathering. They produced distinctive black-on-white pottery, woven cotton textiles, and turquoise jewellery traded over long distances. Their society appears to have been organised around clan and lineage groups, with ceremonial life centred on kivas, circular subterranean chambers entered by ladder.

Architecture

Their most famous legacy is their architecture: massive multi-storey masonry great houses such as Pueblo Bonito at Chaco Canyon, and the spectacular cliff dwellings of Mesa Verde — Cliff Palace, Long House, and Spruce Tree House — built into alcoves in the canyon walls during the 13th century.

Decline

The great centres were largely abandoned in the late 13th century, a migration long attributed to the "Great Drought" of 1276–1299, though warfare, resource depletion, and social upheaval also played roles. The descendants of the Ancestral Puebloans are the modern Pueblo peoples of New Mexico and Arizona, including the Hopi and Zuni.
