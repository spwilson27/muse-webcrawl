Quantum circuit

In quantum information theory, a quantum circuit is a model for quantum computation, similar in spirit to classical circuits, in which a computation is a sequence of quantum gates, measurements, initializations of qubits to known values, and possibly other actions.

Description

A circuit is drawn as horizontal lines representing qubits, read left to right in time. Gates such as the Hadamard, Pauli-X, phase and CNOT gates act on one or more qubits; the overall evolution is unitary until measurement collapses the state. Circuits are the standard way of specifying quantum algorithms such as the quantum Fourier transform.

Universal gate sets

Small sets of gates, for example {H, S, T, CNOT}, are universal: any unitary operation can be approximated to arbitrary accuracy from them, a result guaranteed by the Solovay–Kitaev theorem.
