Battle of Al Mansurah

Date8–11 February 1250
LocationAl Mansurah, Egypt
ResultEgyptian (Ayyubid/Mamluk) victory
BelligerentsCrusaders of Louis IX of France vs Ayyubid Egypt

The Battle of Al Mansurah was fought from 8 to 11 February 1250, between Crusaders led by Louis IX, King of France, and Egyptian forces under the Ayyubid sultanate, with the Bahri Mamluks playing a decisive role, during the Seventh Crusade. It was a decisive Egyptian victory that doomed the crusade.

Having captured Damietta in 1249, Louis advanced up the Nile toward Cairo and encamped before Al Mansurah, a town founded by Sultan al-Kamil on the east bank of the Nile's Damietta branch. On 8 February 1250, Crusader vanguard under Robert of Artois, the king's brother, crossed a ford revealed by a local Copt and charged into the town against orders, where the Mamluk commander Fakhr ad-Din had been killed in the first clash but the defenders regrouped: the Crusaders were ambushed in the narrow streets and almost entirely destroyed, Robert himself being killed.

Louis held the Crusader bridgehead against repeated Egyptian attacks, in which Greek fire was used extensively, but disease and supply shortage ravaged his camp. When the Egyptians cut his supply line from Damietta by moving ships overland into the Nile behind him, Louis attempted a retreat in April and was overwhelmed at the Battle of Fariskur, where he was captured. The crusade ended with Louis's release for a large ransom and the return of Damietta.
