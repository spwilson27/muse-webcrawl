Leicester

Leicester is a city and unitary authority in the East Midlands of England, the county town of Leicestershire, lying on the River Soar. One of the oldest cities in England, with a continuous history of around two millennia, it has a population of roughly 370,000, making it one of the largest cities in the Midlands. Leicester is notable for its ethnic diversity and for the discovery of the remains of King Richard III beneath a city car park in 2012.

History

The Roman town of Ratae Corieltauvorum was established around AD 47 on the Fosse Way; its Jewry Wall and bath complex still survive. In the Middle Ages Leicester became a borough of the earls of Leicester, including Simon de Montfort. The Battle of Bosworth (1485), where Richard III was killed, was fought nearby; in 2012 the king's skeleton was excavated from the former Greyfriars church and reburied in Leicester Cathedral in 2015. In the industrial era the city grew rich on hosiery, knitwear and shoes. The Great Central Railway and Thomas Cook, who ran his first excursion from Leicester in 1841, are part of its heritage.

Modern city

Post-war immigration, particularly from South Asia — including many who arrived after expulsion from Uganda in 1972 — made Leicester one of Britain's most multicultural cities, famous for its Diwali celebrations and the "Golden Mile" of Belgrave Road. In 2016 Leicester City Football Club won the Premier League at odds of 5,000–1, one of the greatest shocks in sporting history. The National Space Centre and two universities, the University of Leicester and De Montfort University, are major institutions.
