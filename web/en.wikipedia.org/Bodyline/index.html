Bodyline

Bodyline, also known as fast leg theory bowling, was a cricketing tactic devised by the English team for the 1932–33 Ashes series in Australia. It involved bowling fast, short-pitched deliveries at the batsman's body with a packed leg-side field, intending to force defensive deflections to waiting fielders.

The series

Conceived by captain Douglas Jardine to curb the prodigious scoring of Don Bradman, and executed by fast bowlers Harold Larwood and Bill Voce, the tactic helped England win the series 4–1 but caused serious injuries, crowd anger and a diplomatic crisis between Britain and Australia.

Legacy

The controversy led to changes in the Laws of Cricket restricting intimidatory bowling and leg-side field placements, and "Bodyline" remains a byword for unsporting play.
