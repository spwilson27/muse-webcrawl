Isaac Ingalls Stevens (March 25, 1818 – September 1, 1862) was an American military officer and politician who served as the first governor of Washington Territory (1853–1857), as a delegate to Congress from the territory, and as a Union general in the American Civil War, in which he was killed at the Battle of Chantilly.

Early career

Stevens graduated first in his class from the United States Military Academy at West Point in 1839 and served in the Corps of Engineers. He fought in the Mexican–American War, was wounded at Cerro Gordo, and wrote a campaign history of the Rio Grande and Mexico. He resigned his commission in 1853 to accept appointment as governor of the newly created Washington Territory.

Governor of Washington Territory

Travelling to his new post, Stevens also commanded the northern survey for a transcontinental railroad, exploring routes from St. Paul to Puget Sound. As governor and ex officio superintendent of Indian affairs, he negotiated a series of treaties in 1854–55 — including Medicine Creek, Point Elliott, Point No Point and Walla Walla — by which Native American tribes ceded most of their lands in exchange for reservations and reserved fishing rights. The treaties' fairness and Stevens's harsh methods were controversial and contributed to the Puget Sound War and Yakima War of 1855–58. He served as the territory's delegate to the US House of Representatives from 1857 to 1861.

Civil War and death

Stevens was commissioned a brigadier general of volunteers in 1861 and served in South Carolina and Virginia. On 1 September 1862, at the Battle of Chantilly, he seized his regiment's colours and led a charge in a thunderstorm; he was shot in the head and killed, and was posthumously promoted to major general.
