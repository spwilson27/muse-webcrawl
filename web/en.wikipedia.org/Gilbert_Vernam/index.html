Gilbert Sandford Vernam (3 April 1890 – 7 February 1960) was an American engineer at AT&T Bell Laboratories who is best known for inventing the stream cipher later known as the Vernam cipher, and for co-inventing the one-time pad.

The Vernam cipher

In 1917, while working on encryption for teleprinter (teletype) communications, Vernam devised a system in which a key recorded on a punched paper tape was combined character by character, using XOR (exclusive-or) logic on the Baudot code, with the plaintext to produce ciphertext. The patent (US 1,310,719) was filed in 1918 and granted in 1919. Army officer Joseph Mauborgne then proposed using a truly random key as long as the message — the one-time pad — which Claude Shannon later proved to be unbreakable when used correctly.

Vernam's electromechanical system was implemented in teleprinter networks and formed the basis for later automatic encryption equipment used by governments and militaries. He held numerous patents in communications and cryptography and worked on secure voice and switching systems at AT&T throughout his career.
