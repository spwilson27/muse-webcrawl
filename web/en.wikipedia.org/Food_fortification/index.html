Food fortification

Food fortification is the process of adding micronutrients (essential trace elements and vitamins) to food, in order to improve the nutritional quality of the food supply and provide a public health benefit with minimal risk to health. Fortification is distinguished from enrichment, which restores nutrients lost during processing. It is a proven, cost-effective strategy for preventing micronutrient deficiencies such as those causing goitre, neural tube defects, and anaemia.

Iodised salt, one of the earliest and most successful fortification programmes.

History and examples

Salt iodisation began in the 1920s in Switzerland and the United States to prevent goitre and congenital iodine deficiency, and is now practised in most countries. Vitamin D fortification of milk began in the 1930s and largely eliminated rickets in countries that adopted it. Flour fortification with iron and B vitamins became common during the Second World War; mandatory folic acid fortification of cereal grains, introduced in the United States and Canada in 1998, was followed by substantial reductions in neural tube defects such as spina bifida. Golden Rice is a genetically engineered rice producing beta-carotene, a precursor of vitamin A.

Methods and regulation

Fortification can be mass (applied to staple foods consumed by the general public), targeted (e.g. complementary foods for infants), or market-driven (voluntarily added by manufacturers). Effective programmes require the nutrient to be stable in the food vehicle, bioavailable, and safe across wide intake ranges. Bodies such as the World Health Organization and the Food and Agriculture Organization issue guidelines, and many countries mandate fortification of specific staples by law.

Criticism

Critics raise concerns about excess intake of some nutrients, the masking of vitamin B12 deficiency by high folic acid intake, and whether voluntary fortification disproportionately benefits already well-nourished consumers. Biofortification—breeding crops to contain more nutrients—is a complementary approach.
