Xerophyte

A xerophyte (from Greek ξηρός xeros "dry" + φυτόν phuton "plant") is a species of plant that has adaptations to survive in an environment with little liquid water, such as a desert or an ice- or snow-covered region in the Alps or the Arctic. Popular examples of xerophytes are cacti, pineapple, and some gymnosperms.

Cacti are classic xerophytes, storing water in thick stems and bearing spines instead of leaves.

Adaptations

Xerophytes reduce water loss through thick cuticles, sunken or sparse stomata, reduced leaf area (often with leaves modified into spines), and hairs that reflect light. Many store water in succulent tissues. Physiological adaptations include crassulacean acid metabolism (CAM), in which stomata open at night to fix CO2, minimizing daytime evaporation.

Classification

Ecologist Christen Raunkiær and others classified plants by water relations: xerophytes contrast with mesophytes of moderate environments and hydrophytes of wet habitats. Some xerophytes are drought-avoiders that complete their life cycle quickly after rains; others are drought-tolerant perennials.
