Central Asia–Center gas pipeline system

TypeNatural gas pipeline
LocationTurkmenistan, Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan, Russia
Began operation1967
Length~5,000 km (network)

The Central Asia–Center gas pipeline system is a network of natural gas pipelines built during the Soviet era to carry gas from the fields of Turkmenistan, Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan northward into the Russian gas transmission system. It remains one of the principal export routes for Central Asian gas.

History

Construction began in the 1960s, with the first line entering service in 1967. The system allowed the USSR to develop the large gas fields of the Karakum Desert, including the Dauletabad field in Turkmenistan, and to integrate Central Asian production into the union-wide supply network.

Route and capacity

The network consists of several parallel trunk lines running from Turkmenistan through Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan to junctions in Russia, where it connects with pipelines serving European markets. Its combined design capacity has been estimated at up to 90 billion cubic metres per year, though actual flows declined after the dissolution of the Soviet Union as Turkmenistan diversified its export routes.
