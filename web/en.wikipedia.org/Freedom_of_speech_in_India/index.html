Freedom of speech in India

The Supreme Court of India, guardian of Article 19 rights

Freedom of speech in India is guaranteed by Article 19(1)(a) of the Constitution of India, which gives all citizens the right "to freedom of speech and expression." Unlike the First Amendment in the United States, the Indian right is expressly subject to "reasonable restrictions" listed in Article 19(2).

Constitutional framework

Article 19(2) permits restrictions in the interests of the sovereignty and integrity of India, the security of the State, friendly relations with foreign states, public order, decency or morality, or in relation to contempt of court, defamation, or incitement to an offence. The First Amendment (1951), passed after early Supreme Court decisions in Romesh Thappar v. State of Madras and Brij Bhushan v. State of Delhi, expanded these grounds.

Landmark cases

The Supreme Court has held that the freedom includes the right to receive information (Bennett Coleman v. Union of India), that it protects artistic expression, and in Shreya Singhal v. Union of India (2015) it struck down Section 66A of the Information Technology Act, which criminalized "offensive" online messages, as unconstitutionally vague and overbroad. In K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India (2017) the Court recognized privacy as a fundamental right with implications for expression.

Contemporary issues

Debates continue over sedition law (Section 124A of the Penal Code, placed in abeyance by the Supreme Court in 2022), internet shutdowns — India has led the world in their frequency — film certification, and takedown rules for online intermediaries under the IT Rules of 2021.
