Henri Émile Benoît Matisse (31 December 1869 – 3 November 1954) was a French visual artist, known for his use of colour and his fluid, original draughtsmanship. Regarded, together with Pablo Picasso, as one of the defining figures of modern art, he worked as a painter, sculptor, printmaker and, in his final years, a creator of celebrated paper cut-outs.

Early life and Fauvism

Matisse was born in northern France and trained in law before turning to art after an attack of appendicitis led him to painting during his convalescence. He studied in Paris under Gustave Moreau and absorbed influences from Impressionism, Neo-Impressionism, Cézanne and Van Gogh. In 1905, exhibiting at the Salon d'Automne alongside André Derain and others, he burst into notoriety as the leader of the Fauves ("wild beasts"), whose paintings used intense, non-naturalistic colour. Works such as Woman with a Hat and The Joy of Life shocked critics and attracted patrons including Gertrude and Leo Stein and the Russian collector Sergei Shchukin.

Mature work

Commissioned by Shchukin, Matisse painted The Dance and Music (1909–10), monumental celebrations of rhythmic, simplified figures. His Red Room (Harmony in Red) of 1908 turned the interior into a flat expanse of patterned colour. After the First World War he settled in Nice, painting luminous interiors, odalisques and still lifes. Major later projects included the illustrations for the artist's book Jazz and the design of the Chapelle du Rosaire in Vence (1948–51), which he considered his masterpiece.

The cut-outs

Following surgery for cancer in 1941 that left him largely confined to bed and a wheelchair, Matisse developed his technique of "drawing with scissors": cutting painted paper into shapes and arranging them into compositions. These cut-outs, including The Snail (1953), the Blue Nude series (1952) and the images collected in Jazz (1947), are among his most admired works and brought his lifelong pursuit of colour and line to a radical simplicity.

Legacy

Matisse died in Nice in 1954. His work, spanning more than six decades, profoundly influenced modern painting through its expressive colour, decorative pattern and economy of line. Major holdings are in the Hermitage and Pushkin museums (the Shchukin collection), the Museum of Modern Art in New York, the Centre Pompidou, the Barnes Foundation and the Musée Matisse in Nice, housed in the villa where he lived.
