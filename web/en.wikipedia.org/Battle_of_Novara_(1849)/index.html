Battle of Novara (1849)

The Battle of Novara (also known as the Battle of Bicocca, after the nearby village) was fought on 23 March 1849 during the First Italian War of Independence. The Austrian army under Field Marshal Joseph Radetzky von Radetz decisively defeated the Piedmont-Sardinian army of King Charles Albert, ending the war and forcing Charles Albert to abdicate.

Background

After the armistice of Salasco ended the first campaign of 1848, Charles Albert of Sardinia denounced the truce in March 1849 and renewed the war against Austria, hoping to liberate Lombardy-Venetia. His army of some 85,000 men was nominally commanded by the Polish general Wojciech Chrzanowski, though command arrangements were confused. Radetzky, the 82-year-old Austrian field marshal, crossed the Ticino and manoeuvred rapidly against the disorganised Piedmontese.

The battle

After preliminary clashes at Mortara and Vigevano on 21 March, the main armies met at Novara on 23 March. The Piedmontese, about 54,000 men engaged, occupied strong positions around the Bicocca farm. Radetzky concentrated some 70,000 troops against them. Through the afternoon the Austrians fed in corps after corps, outflanked the Piedmontese right, and by evening broke their army in a series of determined attacks, capturing the Bicocca and driving the Piedmontese into Novara in disorder. Piedmontese casualties were about 5,800, Austrian roughly 4,100.

Aftermath

That night Charles Albert abdicated in favour of his son Victor Emmanuel II, who negotiated an armistice with Radetzky at Vignale. The subsequent Treaty of Milan imposed an indemnity on Piedmont but left its independence and constitution intact. Novara became enshrined in Italian memory as a national calamity, yet the survival of the Piedmontese constitutional monarchy allowed it to lead the successful unification of Italy a decade later.
