World language

A world language is a language that is spoken internationally and is learned and spoken by numerous people as a second language. A world language is not only characterized by the number of its speakers, but also by its geographical distribution, its use in international organizations, and its use in diplomatic relations and global media.

Characteristics

World languages typically function as lingua francas across large regions. They are used in international trade, science, aviation, and diplomacy. English is the most widely studied foreign language worldwide and serves as the principal global lingua franca of business and academia. Other languages with significant international reach include French, Spanish, Arabic, Russian, Mandarin Chinese, Portuguese, and Hindi.

History

Historically, languages such as Latin in Europe, Classical Chinese in East Asia, Arabic across the Islamic world, and French in European diplomacy served world-language roles within their spheres. The global expansion of European colonial empires spread several languages across continents, and the economic and technological influence of the United States in the 20th century consolidated the position of English.

Constructed world languages

Numerous constructed languages, most famously Esperanto, have been proposed as neutral international auxiliary languages, though none has achieved the global adoption of natural world languages.
