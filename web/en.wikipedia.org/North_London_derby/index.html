North London derby

The North London derby is the meeting of the association football clubs Arsenal F.C. and Tottenham Hotspur F.C., both based in North London, England. Fans of both clubs regard the other as their main rival, and the fixture is considered one of the fiercest derbies in English football and in world football generally.

Origins of the rivalry

Arsenal was founded in Woolwich, south-east London, in 1886 and moved north of the river to Highbury in Islington in 1913, close to Tottenham's home at White Hart Lane. The move into what Spurs considered their territory, followed by Arsenal's controversial election to the First Division in 1919 at Tottenham's expense, ignited the rivalry. The first competitive meeting had been a league match on 4 December 1909, shortly before Arsenal's relocation.

History

The derby has produced many famous occasions. Arsenal won the league title at White Hart Lane in 1971 and again in 2004, the latter during the unbeaten "Invincibles" season. Tottenham's victories include their 1983 and 1991 encounters and the 2008 League Cup semi-final, in which Spurs won 5–1. St Totteringham's Day is the name given by Arsenal fans to the day in the season when Tottenham can no longer finish above them in the league table; it was observed for 21 consecutive years until 2016, when Tottenham finished above Arsenal for the first time since 1995 and continued to do so for several seasons thereafter.

Records

Harry Kane is the fixture's all-time top scorer, having surpassed the previous records of Bobby Smith and Emmanuel Adebayor. The derby is typically played twice a season in the Premier League, with additional meetings in cup competitions. Both clubs have enjoyed periods of dominance, and the balance of victories across more than 200 competitive meetings remains relatively close.
