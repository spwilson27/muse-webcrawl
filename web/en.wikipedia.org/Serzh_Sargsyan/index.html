Serzh Sargsyan, third president of Armenia.

Serzh Azati Sargsyan (Armenian: Սերժ Ազատի Սարգսյան; born 30 June 1954) is an Armenian politician who served as the third President of Armenia from 2008 to 2018 and twice as Prime Minister, in 2007–2008 and briefly in April 2018. A veteran of the Nagorno-Karabakh movement and war, he held senior defence and security posts throughout the 1990s and 2000s before succeeding Robert Kocharyan as president.

Early life and Karabakh war

Sargsyan was born in Stepanakert in the Nagorno-Karabakh Autonomous Oblast. He graduated from Yerevan State University in philology and worked in Komsomol and Communist Party structures in Stepanakert. During the First Nagorno-Karabakh War he became a leading organiser of Karabakh Armenian forces, chairing the self-defence committee of the unrecognised Nagorno-Karabakh Republic. He subsequently served as Armenia's Minister of Defence (1993–1995), head of state security, Minister of Internal Affairs and National Security, and again Minister of Defence from 2000 to 2007.

Presidency

Sargsyan won the February 2008 presidential election amid opposition claims of fraud; the ensuing protests in Yerevan were suppressed on 1 March 2008 with the deaths of ten people. He was re-elected in 2013. His presidency saw the 2009 Zurich Protocols attempting normalisation with Turkey (never ratified), Armenia's 2013 decision to join the Russian-led Eurasian Economic Union rather than sign an EU Association Agreement, and a 2015 constitutional referendum converting Armenia into a parliamentary republic.

Resignation

When his second term ended in April 2018, Sargsyan — despite earlier pledges — was elected Prime Minister by parliament under the new system, retaining executive power. The move triggered mass protests led by Nikol Pashinyan, and on 23 April 2018, after eleven days of demonstrations, Sargsyan resigned, declaring that "Nikol was right. I was wrong." The episode, part of the 2018 Armenian "Velvet Revolution", ended the Republican Party's long dominance. Sargsyan later faced criminal charges relating to his time in office, which he denied.
