Ecliptic

The ecliptic is the plane of Earth's orbit around the Sun. From the perspective of an observer on Earth, it is the apparent path of the Sun across the celestial sphere over the course of a year; the Moon and the planets also travel near it, and eclipses occur when the Moon crosses it.

Relation to the equator

Because Earth's axis is tilted, the ecliptic is inclined to the celestial equator by the obliquity of the ecliptic, about 23.4°. The two points where the planes cross are the equinoxes; the points of greatest separation are the solstices. The tilt slowly varies between about 22.1° and 24.5° over a 41,000-year cycle, contributing to Milankovitch cycles of climate change.

The zodiac

The band of sky around the ecliptic is divided into the twelve signs of the zodiac, each spanning 30° of ecliptic longitude. The Moon and planets stay within this band because the Solar System formed from a flattened disc.

Ecliptic coordinates

In the ecliptic coordinate system, positions are given by ecliptic longitude (measured eastward from the vernal equinox) and ecliptic latitude. It is the natural frame for describing Solar System bodies, and the basis of the J2000 reference frame used in astronomy.
