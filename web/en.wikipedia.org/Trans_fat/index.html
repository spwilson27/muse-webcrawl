Trans fat

Cis and trans configurations of unsaturated fatty acids

Trans fat, also called trans-unsaturated fatty acids or trans fatty acids, is a type of unsaturated fat that occurs in foods. Small amounts occur naturally in the meat and milk of ruminants, but the main dietary source historically was industrially produced partially hydrogenated vegetable oils, in which some cis double bonds in the fatty acids are converted to the trans configuration. Trace concentrations are also produced by bacterial fermentation in the rumen (the source of vaccenic acid and conjugated linoleic acid in dairy products).

Health effects

Consumption of industrial trans fats is consistently associated with an increased risk of coronary heart disease: they raise LDL cholesterol, lower HDL cholesterol, and promote inflammation and endothelial dysfunction. Reviews by health authorities have concluded that there is no safe level of intake for artificial trans fat. The American scientist Fred Kummerow campaigned for decades for recognition of the hazard, and research from the 1990s onward led to labelling requirements and then to bans: Denmark limited industrially produced trans fats in 2003, the US Food and Drug Administration revoked the "generally recognised as safe" status of partially hydrogenated oils in 2015 (with compliance through 2018–2021), and the World Health Organization's REPLACE initiative (2018) called for global elimination of industrial trans fat.

Chemistry and food technology

Hydrogenation converts liquid vegetable oils into semi-solid fats desirable for margarine, shortening and frying because of their texture, shelf stability and low cost. In partial hydrogenation, hydrogenation is interrupted, leaving a mixture in which many remaining double bonds have isomerised from cis to trans, as in elaidic acid, the trans isomer of oleic acid. Fully hydrogenated oils contain almost no trans fat, and the industry has largely replaced partially hydrogenated oils with palm oil, interesterified fats and modified formulations since regulation tightened.
