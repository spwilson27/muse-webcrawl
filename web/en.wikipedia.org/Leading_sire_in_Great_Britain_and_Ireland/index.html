Leading sire in Great Britain and Ireland

The title of Leading sire in Great Britain and Ireland is awarded each year to the Thoroughbred stallion whose progeny have won the greatest total prize money in flat races in Great Britain and Ireland during the racing season. The championship has been recognised since the eighteenth century and is one of the most prestigious indicators of a stallion's commercial success at stud.

History

Early dominant sires included Herod, whose progeny earnings placed him at the head of the list many times in the late 1700s, and his son Highflyer. In the 19th century, stallions such as Stockwell and St. Simon — who headed the list nine times — set endurance records for the title. The twentieth century saw repeated championships for horses such as Nearco and Danehill, the latter helping to popularise the shuttle-stallion trade between hemispheres.

Modern era

In the twenty-first century the list has been dominated by Coolmore and Darley stallions. Sadler's Wells achieved a record 14 championships, and his son Galileo surpassed expectations with repeated titles through the 2010s and into the 2020s, his progeny's earnings driven by success in the Classics and major international races. Frankel, a son of Galileo, quickly joined the top ranks of the table soon after his first crops reached the racecourse.
