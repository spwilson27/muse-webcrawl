Deep Underground Neutrino Experiment

AcronymDUNE
Beam sourceFermilab, Illinois
Detector siteSanford Underground Research Facility, South Dakota
Baseline1,300 km

The Deep Underground Neutrino Experiment (DUNE) is a neutrino experiment under construction in the United States. It will send an intense beam of neutrinos from Fermilab near Chicago 1,300 kilometres through the Earth to huge detectors located 1.5 km underground at the Sanford Underground Research Facility in Lead, South Dakota, in the former Homestake gold mine.

Detectors

The far detector will consist of modules filled with tens of thousands of tonnes of liquid argon, using liquid-argon time-projection chamber technology to image neutrino interactions in fine detail. A near detector complex at Fermilab will characterise the beam before oscillation occurs.

Physics goals

DUNE aims to measure neutrino oscillation parameters precisely, determine the neutrino mass ordering, search for CP violation in the lepton sector (which may bear on the matter–antimatter asymmetry of the universe), detect neutrinos from supernovae, and search for proton decay. It is the flagship project of the US particle physics programme, with an international collaboration of over a thousand scientists.
