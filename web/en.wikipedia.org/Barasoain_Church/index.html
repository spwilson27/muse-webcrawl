Barasoain Church, formally the Our Lady of Mount Carmel Parish Church, is a Roman Catholic church in Malolos, Bulacan, Philippines. It is one of the most historically significant churches in the country, famed as the seat of the revolutionary Malolos Congress and the site of the proclamation of the First Philippine Republic and the ratification of the Malolos Constitution. Its image appeared on the Philippine ten-peso bill (and later on coins).

History

The parish was separated from the parish of Malolos in 1859, with Augustinian friars administering it. Earlier chapel structures of light materials were replaced over time; the present stone church and convent were completed in 1885 under Fr. Francisco Royo, in an eclectic style. The church caught fire during the revolutionary period and was later restored.

Role in the revolution

Barasoain gained national fame during the Philippine Revolution against Spain. On 15 September 1898 the Malolos Congress (the Revolutionary Congress) convened in the church, chaired by Pedro Paterno; Felipe Calderón drafted the constitution, and on 21 January 1899 the Congress ratified the Malolos Constitution. Two days later, on 23 January 1899, Emilio Aguinaldo was inaugurated in the church as President of the First Philippine Republic — the first republic in Asia.

Architecture and heritage

The church is of stone construction with a distinctive façade featuring a portico and a rounded pediment, and a four-storey bell tower. It houses a museum (the Barasoain Historical Landmark / Museo ng Republika ng 1899) maintained with the National Historical Commission of the Philippines, displaying artefacts of the First Republic. The church was declared a National Historical Landmark and remains an active parish and pilgrimage site.
