Data and information visualization

Data and information visualization (or data viz) is the graphical representation of data and information. By using visual elements such as charts, graphs and maps, data-visualization tools provide an accessible way to see and understand trends, outliers and patterns in data.

History

Early landmarks include William Playfair's statistical charts of the late 18th century, Charles Joseph Minard's flow map of Napoleon's Russian campaign (1869), and John Snow's cholera map of 1854. In the 20th century, Jacques Bertin's Sémiologie graphique (1967) systematised the use of visual variables, and Edward Tufte's The Visual Display of Quantitative Information (1983) popularised principles of graphical integrity and data-ink ratio.

Types

Common forms include bar charts, line charts, scatter plots, histograms, box plots, heat maps, tree maps, choropleth maps and network diagrams. Interactive visualization, developed from the 1980s onward, allows users to filter, zoom and query data directly, and forms a bridge between data visualization, human–computer interaction and visual analytics.

Applications

Visualization is used in scientific research, statistics, business intelligence, journalism and public policy communication. Modern tools range from spreadsheet charts to programming libraries such as D3.js and software such as Tableau.
