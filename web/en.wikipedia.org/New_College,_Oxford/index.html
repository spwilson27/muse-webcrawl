New College is a constituent college of the University of Oxford in the United Kingdom. Founded in 1379 by William of Wykeham, Bishop of Winchester, it is formally named the College of St Mary of Winchester in Oxford and is twinned with Winchester College, also founded by Wykeham.

History and buildings

New College was one of the first Oxford colleges to admit undergraduates and to organise teaching by tutorials. Its buildings, arranged around a great quadrangle, were a model for later colleges, and its chapel is noted for its medieval stained glass, Jacob Epstein's statue of Lazarus, and a famous choir. During the English Civil War, part of the city wall running through the college grounds was defended by Royalist forces.

People

Alumni include the film director and actor Richard Curtis, the novelist Kate Mosse, the broadcaster Rageh Omaar, and the physicist Heinz Wolff; fellows have included Isaiah Berlin and William Spooner, the latter lending his name to "spoonerisms".
