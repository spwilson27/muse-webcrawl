Paddy wagon

TypePolice vehicle
PurposePrisoner transport
Also known asPolice van, Black Maria

A paddy wagon is a colloquial term, primarily in North America, for a type of police vehicle used to transport prisoners, arrestees, or groups of people suspected of involvement in a crime. Similar vehicles are known in the United Kingdom and Australia as police vans, and historically in Britain as a "Black Maria".

History

The term originated in the United States in the early 20th century. Its etymology is often attributed to the large number of Irish immigrants ("Paddies") who served in American police forces, or, alternatively, to the perception that the wagons were frequently used to transport Irish-American arrestees. Enclosed horse-drawn wagons were first used for prisoner transport in the 19th century, later replaced by motorised vans and trucks.

Design

Modern prisoner transport vehicles typically feature a separate, secured compartment at the rear, often subdivided into cells, with limited visibility and reinforced doors. Larger versions, sometimes called prison buses, are used for moving prisoners between correctional facilities.
