The Conference of Rulers (Malay: Majlis Raja-Raja) is a council comprising the nine hereditary rulers of the Malay states in Malaysia, together with, for certain purposes, the governors (Yang di-Pertua Negeri) of Penang, Malacca, Sabah, and Sarawak. Established in its modern form under the Federation of Malaya Agreement of 1948 and entrenched in the Federal Constitution of 1957, it normally meets three times a year at the Istana Negara in Kuala Lumpur.

Functions

The Conference's best-known function is electing, from among the nine rulers, the Yang di-Pertuan Agong, Malaysia's federal head of state, and his deputy, for five-year terms, by secret ballot. The Conference also consents to laws affecting the privileges of the rulers, appoints members of certain bodies, agrees or disagrees to the extension of Islamic religious acts across the federation, and must be consulted on matters such as the appointment of the prime minister's key officers of state and proposed constitutional amendments touching the rulers' position, the special position of the Malays, and citizenship provisions.

Membership and proceedings

Only the nine rulers—the Yang di-Pertuan Besar of Negeri Sembilan, the Sultans of Selangor, Perlis (Raja), Terengganu, Kedah, Kelantan, Pahang, and Johor, and the Sultan of Perak—vote in royal elections; the four governors participate when the Conference discusses matters such as immigration or constitutional changes generally. The Keeper of the Rulers' Seal is the Conference's secretary and custodian of its official seal.

History

The Conference descends from the colonial-era durbars of the Federated Malay States, first held in 1897 at Kuala Kangsar under Resident-General Frank Swettenham, which brought the Malay rulers together in council. Its electoral role for a rotating constitutional monarchy is unique among the world's monarchies.
