Mnemosyne (Greek: Μνημοσύνη, "remembrance") is the personification of memory in Greek mythology. A daughter of the Titans Uranus (Sky) and Gaia (Earth), she is one of the twelve Titan offspring. Mnemosyne is best known as the mother of the nine Muses, whom she bore to Zeus after the two lay together for nine consecutive nights in Pieria; a year later she gave birth to the goddesses of the arts and sciences.

In myth and cult

Hesiod's Theogony opens with an invocation of the Muses, Mnemosyne's daughters, whose song grants forgetfulness of sorrows — a gift mirrored in their mother, who presides both over remembering and the relief of forgetting. In the underworld tradition of Greek eschatology, two springs mattered to the dead: Lethe, whose waters brought oblivion, and the spring of Mnemosyne, whose waters preserved memory. Gold funerary tablets buried with initiates (the "Orphic" lamellae) instruct the soul to avoid Lethe and drink instead from the lake of Memory, declaring "I am a child of Earth and starry Sky."

Worship

Mnemosyne had a modest cult: she was honoured at the oracle of Trophonius at Lebadeia in Boeotia, where consultants who had drunk from the spring of Forgetfulness were made to drink also from the spring of Memory, and was associated with a statue and throne at the site, as described by Pausanias.

Legacy

Her name survives in the English word "mnemonic" and related terms for memory aids. The asteroid 57 Mnemosyne, discovered in 1859, was named after her.
