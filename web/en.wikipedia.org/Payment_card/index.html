Payment card

A payment card is a card that can be used by a cardholder and accepted by a merchant to make a payment for a purchase or in payment of a debt. Payment cards include credit cards, debit cards, ATM cards, stored-value cards, fleet cards and gift cards. They are usually plastic and, increasingly, metal.

Features

Payment cards traditionally carry an embossed or printed card number, the cardholder's name, and an expiry date. Most modern cards have a magnetic stripe, an EMV chip, and contactless (NFC) capability for tap-to-pay transactions. Card numbers follow the ISO/IEC 7812 standard and include an issuer identification number and a Luhn check digit.

Types

A credit card draws on a revolving line of credit from the issuer. A debit card draws directly on funds in the cardholder's bank account. A prepaid card is loaded with funds in advance. Charge cards require the balance to be paid in full each month.

Processing

Transactions are authorised and settled through card networks such as Visa and Mastercard, involving the merchant's acquirer, the network, and the issuing bank.
