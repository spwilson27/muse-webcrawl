Hundred Days

The Hundred Days (French: les Cent-Jours) marked the period between Napoleon's return from eleven months of exile on the island of Elba to Paris on 20 March 1815 and the second restoration of King Louis XVIII on 8 July 1815. This period saw the final phase of the Napoleonic Wars, including the Waterloo campaign and the defeat of Napoleon by the Seventh Coalition, and it ended the First French Empire for the second and final time.

Napoleon's return

Napoleon escaped from Elba on 26 February 1815 and landed in southern France, marching to Paris as royal troops sent to arrest him defected to his side; Louis XVIII fled and Napoleon re-entered the capital on 20 March without a shot fired. The Congress of Vienna declared him an outlaw, and the Seventh Coalition — Britain, Prussia, Austria and Russia — mobilised against him.

The Waterloo campaign

Seeking to defeat the Allied and Prussian armies separately before they could combine, Napoleon invaded Belgium in June 1815. He defeated the Prussians at Ligny on 16 June and fought Wellington's army at Quatre Bras, but on 18 June he was decisively defeated at the Battle of Waterloo when the Prussians arrived on his flank.

Aftermath

Napoleon abdicated on 22 June. Allied troops occupied Paris, Louis XVIII was restored, and Napoleon surrendered to the British and was exiled to the remote island of Saint Helena, where he died in 1821. The Congress of Vienna's settlement reshaped Europe and began a century of relative great-power peace.
