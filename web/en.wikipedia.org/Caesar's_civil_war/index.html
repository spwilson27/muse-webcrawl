Caesar's Civil War

Caesar's Civil War (49–45 BC) was one of the last politico-military conflicts of the Roman Republic before its reorganization into the Roman Empire. It began as a series of political and military confrontations between Gaius Julius Caesar and the Optimates — the traditionalist senatorial elite led militarily by Gnaeus Pompeius Magnus (Pompey).

Background

The First Triumvirate of Caesar, Pompey, and Crassus had dominated Roman politics. After Crassus died at Carrhae in 53 BC and Pompey aligned with the Senate, demands that Caesar disband his army and stand trial drove the crisis.

The Rubicon

On 10 January 49 BC Caesar crossed the Rubicon River with Legio XIII, committing treason against the Republic and uttering, according to tradition, alea iacta est — the die is cast.

Campaigns

Caesar rapidly occupied Italy, defeated Pompeian forces in Spain at Ilerda, crossed to Greece and defeated Pompey at Pharsalus in 48 BC. Pompey fled to Egypt and was murdered. Caesar then campaigned in Egypt, Pontus (the swift victory at Zela — veni, vidi, vici), Africa (Thapsus, 46 BC), and finally Spain, where the last Pompeians fell at Munda in 45 BC.

Aftermath

Victorious, Caesar became dictator perpetuo, was assassinated on the Ides of March 44 BC, and the Republic descended into further civil wars ending with Augustus.
