CSS Virginia

Launched (as Merrimack): June 15, 1855

Commissioned (CSN): February 17, 1862

Fate: Burned by her crew, May 11, 1862

Type: Casemate ironclad ram

Armament: 10 guns

CSS Virginia was the first steam-powered ironclad warship built by the Confederate States Navy during the American Civil War. She was constructed as a casemate ironclad on the raised and rebuilt hull of the scuttled Union steam frigate USS Merrimack at the Gosport Navy Yard in Portsmouth, Virginia, in 1861–62.

Battle of Hampton Roads

On March 8, 1862, commanded by Flag Officer Franklin Buchanan, Virginia steamed into Hampton Roads and attacked the Union blockading squadron, ramming and sinking the sloop USS Cumberland and forcing the frigate USS Congress to surrender and burn, while Union shot largely bounced off her sloped iron armour. The next day, March 9, she engaged the Union ironclad USS Monitor in the first battle between ironclad warships. The fight ended in a tactical draw but marked a revolution in naval warfare, rendering wooden warships obsolete overnight.

Fate

Confined by her deep draft and unreliable engines, Virginia could neither escape to sea nor be taken up the shallow James River. When the Confederates evacuated Norfolk in May 1862, her crew ran her aground near Craney Island and burned her on May 11, 1862, to prevent capture. Parts of her wreck were later salvaged; artifacts and one of her anchors are preserved in museums, and her name is remembered in the term "Merrimac" often (and incorrectly) applied to the ship herself.
