Blink

Blinking is a rapid, semi-autonomic closure of the eyelids. It is an essential function of the eye that spreads tears across the ocular surface, keeping the cornea moist and clearing away small particles such as dust. A typical blink lasts about 100 to 400 milliseconds.

Mechanism

Blinking is controlled by the orbicularis oculi muscle, which contracts to close the eyelid, while the levator palpebrae superioris reopens it. Most spontaneous blinks originate in the globus pallidus of the basal ganglia, though reflex blinks are triggered through the trigeminal nerve in response to touch, bright light, or sudden approaching objects (the corneal reflex).

Rate

Humans blink on average around 15 to 20 times per minute, though the rate varies considerably: it falls when concentrating on visual tasks such as reading or using a computer screen, and rises during conversation or when the eyes are dry or irritated. Excessively reduced blink rates while using screens are associated with computer vision syndrome.

Functions beyond lubrication

Blinks also provide brief periods of darkness that help reset visual processing, and researchers have proposed that the timing of blinks is coordinated with attention and cognition — people tend to blink at natural breakpoints in speech, reading, and events.
