Africa Squadron

Active1843–1861
CountryUnited States
BranchUnited States Navy
RoleSuppression of the Atlantic slave trade

The Africa Squadron was a unit of the United States Navy that operated from 1843 to 1861 in the Atlantic Ocean off the coast of West Africa, tasked with suppressing the transatlantic slave trade under the Webster–Ashburton Treaty of 1842, which committed the United States to maintain a naval force of at least eighty guns off Africa.

Operations

Based at Porto Praya in the Cape Verde islands, the squadron's ships—including the sloops Portsmouth, Decatur, and Dale—cruised the coasts of Liberia, the Bight of Benin, and Angola. Hampered by slow sailing ships, unhealthy conditions, and the use of foreign flags by slavers, it captured around three dozen vessels over its existence.

End

The squadron was recalled at the outbreak of the American Civil War in 1861 to blockade Confederate ports, effectively ending the American anti-slavery patrol off Africa.
