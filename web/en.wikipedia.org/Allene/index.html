Allene

An allene is a compound in which one carbon atom has double bonds with each of its two adjacent carbon centres. Allenes are classified as cumulated dienes; the simplest member of the class, CH2=C=CH2, is itself commonly called "allene" and is known systematically as propadiene.

The central carbon of an allene is sp-hybridised, and the two π bonds are perpendicular to one another. As a result, the two CH2 planes at the ends of a parent allene lie at right angles. When each terminal carbon bears two different substituents, the molecule lacks a plane of symmetry and is chiral, exhibiting axial chirality — a property first predicted by van 't Hoff in 1875 and demonstrated experimentally decades later.

Synthesis and reactivity

Allenes can be prepared by isomerisation of propynes, by the reaction of propargyl electrophiles with organometallic reagents, and by sigmatropic rearrangements, among other methods. Their cumulated double bonds are reactive toward electrophiles, radicals, and transition-metal catalysts, and allenes are valuable intermediates in organic synthesis; many natural products, pharmaceuticals, and molecular materials contain allene units. The parent compound, propadiene, is a gas obtained industrially as a component of MAPP gas mixtures.
