Law officers of the Crown

The law officers of the Crown are the principal legal advisers to the sovereign and government in the United Kingdom and Commonwealth realms. For England and Wales the senior officers are the Attorney General and the Solicitor General, who head the Attorney General's Office, oversee the Crown Prosecution Service and the Serious Fraud Office, and represent the Crown in major litigation.

Other jurisdictions

Scotland has its own law officers — the Lord Advocate and Solicitor General for Scotland — heading the Crown Office and Procurator Fiscal Service; since devolution the Lord Advocate serves the Scottish Government, with a separate Advocate General advising the UK Government on Scots law. Northern Ireland has an Attorney General for Northern Ireland, and Wales a Counsel General. Crown Dependencies and overseas territories appoint their own attorneys general.

Role

Law officers advise ministers on legality, superintend prosecuting authorities (in England and Wales without directing individual cases, under the Shawcross convention), and may sit in Parliament or cabinet committees.
