Common opossum

The common opossum (Didelphis marsupialis), also called the southern opossum and locally known by names such as manicou, is a marsupial species living in the Neotropics. It is found from Mexico through Central America to Argentina, including several Caribbean islands, in habitats ranging from lowland rainforest to disturbed and suburban areas.

Description and behaviour

The common opossum is about the size of a domestic cat, with coarse grey-black fur, a white face with dark eye patches, large naked ears, and a long, naked, prehensile tail. It is a nocturnal, largely solitary omnivore, feeding on invertebrates, small vertebrates, fruit, and carrion; it is also an important predator of venomous snakes, to whose venom it is partially immune.

Like other didelphids, the female carries her altricial young in a pouch (marsupium). The species adapts readily to human-modified landscapes and is common throughout its range, though it is hunted for food in some regions.
