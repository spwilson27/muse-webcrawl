Fulfillment house

A fulfillment house (also called a fulfillment centre or third-party logistics provider, 3PL) is a company that provides outsourced warehousing, order processing, and shipping services on behalf of other businesses, typically mail-order and e-commerce retailers.

Services

Fulfillment houses receive and store a client's inventory, then, when an order arrives, pick the items from stock, pack them, apply shipping labels, and hand the parcel to a carrier. Many also handle invoicing, payment processing, call-centre customer service, returns management, and the insertion of marketing materials.

Business model

Clients are usually charged a combination of storage fees (per pallet or per cubic volume), pick-and-pack fees per order or item, and postage at cost or at a negotiated rate. The model allows small and medium-sized sellers to operate without owning warehouses or employing shipping staff, and to scale capacity up or down with demand.

History

Fulfillment houses grew out of the mail-order catalogue industry of the 20th century, where third parties handled the back-end logistics of coupon responses, prize promotions, and subscriptions. The rise of e-commerce from the late 1990s greatly expanded the sector, with large operators such as Amazon's Fulfillment by Amazon (FBA) service integrating warehousing directly into online marketplaces.
