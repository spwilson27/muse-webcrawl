Napier Commission

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Napier Commission, officially the Royal Commission of Inquiry into the Condition of Crofters and Cottars in the Highlands and Islands of Scotland, was a royal commission established in 1883 by the government of William Ewart Gladstone. It was chaired by Francis Napier, 10th Lord Napier, and was appointed in response to widespread agitation among crofters in the Scottish Highlands and Islands, a period of protest sometimes called the Crofters' War.

Background

Following the Highland Clearances, crofters lived in conditions of great insecurity: they held their small plots of land on short tenancies, with no security of tenure, no compensation for improvements, and rents they regarded as excessive. In the early 1880s crofters began organised resistance, including rent strikes and the seizure of land, notably in Skye, where the Battle of the Braes took place in 1882.

Inquiry and report

The commission travelled throughout the Highlands and Islands, taking evidence from crofters, landlords, and officials — some 775 witnesses in all. Its report, published in 1884, documented severe poverty and grievance, and recommended security of tenure, fair rents adjudicated by a land court, and compensation for improvements. Although the report was more moderate than the crofters' full demands, it gave official recognition to their complaints.

Legacy

The agitation continued, and the commission's findings paved the way for the Crofters' Holdings (Scotland) Act 1886, which granted security of tenure to crofters and established the Crofters Commission to fix fair rents. The Napier Commission remains a landmark in the history of Scottish land reform.
