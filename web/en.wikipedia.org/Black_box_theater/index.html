Black box theater

A black box theater configured with seating on three sides.

A black box theater is a simple performance space, typically a square room with black walls and a flat floor, that can be configured in many different seating and staging arrangements. The space is deliberately neutral and flexible, allowing directors and designers to reshape the relationship between performers and audience for each production.

Characteristics

Black box theaters usually seat between 50 and 250 audience members on movable risers or chairs. Because the walls are painted black and there is no fixed proscenium arch or permanent stage, productions can be staged in the round, in thrust, in traverse, or in conventional end-on formats. Lighting is typically provided by a grid above the space, and scenery tends to be minimal, placing the emphasis on performance and text.

History and use

The black box became popular in the 1960s and 1970s with the rise of experimental and avant-garde theater, which sought intimate, inexpensive spaces free of the conventions of large proscenium houses. Today black boxes are standard features of universities, schools, and theater companies, used for workshops, new play development, student productions, and experimental work, as well as small-scale professional productions.
