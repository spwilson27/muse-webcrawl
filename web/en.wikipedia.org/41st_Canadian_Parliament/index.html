The 41st Canadian Parliament was in session from June 2, 2011, to August 2, 2015, with its membership set by the 2011 federal election held on May 2, 2011. The Conservative Party under Prime Minister Stephen Harper won a majority government with 166 of 308 seats in the House of Commons.

Composition and milestones

The election produced a historic realignment: the New Democratic Party under Jack Layton won 103 seats, becoming the Official Opposition for the first time, while the Liberal Party fell to 34 seats, its worst result ever. The Bloc Québécois was reduced to four seats, and Green Party leader Elizabeth May won her party's first seat. Layton died in August 2011 and was succeeded as NDP leader by Thomas Mulcair in 2012; Justin Trudeau became Liberal leader in 2013.

Legislation

The Parliament enacted major omnibus budget bills, the Safe Streets and Communities Act, copyright modernization, and 2014 changes to election law (the Fair Elections Act) and citizenship rules. It also approved Canadian participation in combat missions in Libya (extending from the previous Parliament) and against ISIS in Iraq and Syria. A 2013 Senate expenses scandal overshadowed the government's final years. The Parliament was dissolved on August 2, 2015, triggering the longest Canadian election campaign of the modern era, which the Liberals won.
