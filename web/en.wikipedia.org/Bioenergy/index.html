Bioenergy

Bioenergy is a form of renewable energy derived from biological material — biomass — such as wood, agricultural crops and residues, animal wastes, and municipal refuse. Biomass can be burned directly for heat and electricity, or converted into liquid and gaseous fuels such as ethanol, biodiesel, and biogas. Bioenergy is the largest source of renewable energy worldwide, much of it in the traditional form of fuelwood used for cooking and heating.

Forms and conversion

The main conversion routes are direct combustion, which supplies most bioelectricity and district heat; anaerobic digestion, which produces methane-rich biogas from wet wastes; fermentation of sugars and starches into ethanol; transesterification of vegetable oils and animal fats into biodiesel; and thermochemical processes such as gasification and pyrolysis that yield syngas, bio-oil, and charcoal. Advanced biofuels from lignocellulosic feedstocks (wood, straw, grasses) are an active research area because they avoid direct competition with food crops.

Sources of biomass

Feedstocks include forestry and sawmill residues, dedicated energy crops such as miscanthus, switchgrass, and short-rotation willow or poplar, agricultural residues like straw and bagasse, energy crops grown for fuel (sugarcane, maize, oil palm, rapeseed), sewage, manure, and the organic fraction of municipal solid waste. Residues and wastes are generally considered the most sustainable sources.

Climate debate

Bioenergy is often treated as carbon neutral on the argument that growing plants reabsorb the carbon released when biomass is burned. This assumption is contested: burning wood can release more carbon per unit of energy than coal in the short term, and the "carbon debt" may take decades of regrowth to repay. Land-use change — clearing forests or grasslands for energy crops — can release large carbon stores and harm biodiversity, and crop-based biofuels raise "food versus fuel" concerns. Scientific assessments therefore distinguish sharply between sustainable residue-based bioenergy and systems that drive deforestation or displace food production.

Role in energy systems

Bioenergy supplies roughly a tenth of global primary energy, dominated by traditional uses in developing countries. Modern bioenergy contributes heat, power, and transport fuel, and is one of the few renewable options for aviation fuel and high-temperature industrial heat. Combined with carbon capture and storage (BECCS), bioenergy is one of the main proposed routes to negative emissions in climate-mitigation scenarios.
