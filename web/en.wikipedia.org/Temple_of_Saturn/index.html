Temple of Saturn

The Temple of Saturn (Latin: Templum Saturni or Aedes Saturni) was an ancient Roman temple to the god Saturn in the Roman Forum. Its ruins stand at the foot of the Capitoline Hill at the western end of the Forum. The temple housed the state treasury (aerarium) of Rome.

History

Tradition dates the first temple to 497 BC, early in the Roman Republic. The building was repeatedly rebuilt; the surviving structure derives mainly from a restoration of 42 BC by Munatius Plancus and a later rebuilding after a fire in the late 4th century AD, commemorated by an inscription on the architrave: "Senatus populusque Romanus incendio consumptum restituit" ("The Senate and People of Rome restored [the temple] consumed by fire").

Architecture

Eight Ionic columns of the front portico survive, standing on a high podium of travertine. In the treasury beneath and within the temple, Rome's gold and silver reserves and state archives were kept. The festival of Saturnalia, held in December, began at the temple.
