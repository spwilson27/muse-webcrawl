William III (William Henry; Dutch: Willem Hendrik; 4 November 1650 – 8 March 1702), also known as William of Orange, was the sovereign Prince of Orange, Stadtholder of Holland and other Dutch provinces, and, from 1689, King of England, Scotland and Ireland, ruling jointly with his wife Mary II until her death in 1694.

Glorious Revolution

William was the leading Protestant opponent of Louis XIV's France. Invited by English opponents of the Catholic James II, he landed at Torbay on 5 November 1688 with a Dutch army; James fled, and in 1689 Parliament offered the crown jointly to William and Mary, together with the Bill of Rights, which limited royal power and laid foundations of constitutional monarchy. His victory over James at the Battle of the Boyne in Ireland (1690) is still commemorated by the Orange Order.

Reign

William's reign was dominated by the Nine Years' War against France. At home, his government founded the Bank of England (1694), saw the beginnings of the national debt system, and passed the Act of Settlement 1701, which fixed the Protestant succession after his death. He died in 1702 from pneumonia following a fall from his horse, and was succeeded by Queen Anne. His struggle against France set the stage for the War of the Spanish Succession.
