Continuum hypothesis

The hypothesis asks whether any set is strictly larger than the integers yet smaller than the reals.

The continuum hypothesis (abbreviated CH) is a hypothesis about the possible sizes of infinite sets. It states: there is no set whose cardinality is strictly between that of the integers and the real numbers. In the mathematics of Georg Cantor, the cardinality of the continuum (the real numbers) is 2ℵ0, and the hypothesis asserts this equals ℵ1, the next cardinal after ℵ0. Cantor advanced the hypothesis in 1878 and spent years trying to prove it; David Hilbert placed it first on his list of 23 problems in 1900.

Independence

In 1940 Kurt Gödel showed that the negation of CH cannot be proved from the Zermelo–Fraenkel axioms with the Axiom of Choice (ZFC), by constructing the inner model L. In 1963 Paul Cohen completed the picture with his method of forcing, showing that CH itself cannot be proved from ZFC either. Thus CH is independent of the standard axioms of set theory, and its truth depends on the axioms one adopts.

The generalised hypothesis

The generalised continuum hypothesis (GCH) asserts that for every infinite cardinal there is no cardinal strictly between it and its power set. GCH is also independent of ZFC, though implied by the axiom of constructibility (V = L).
