Krishnaji Howlaji Ara (1914–1985), commonly known as K. H. Ara, was an Indian painter and a founding member of the Progressive Artists' Group in Bombay (Mumbai). He is regarded as one of the pioneers of modern Indian art, celebrated especially for his still lifes and his studies of the female nude.

Life and career

Ara was born in Bolarum, near Secunderabad, and ran away to Bombay as a teenager, where he worked at odd jobs — including as a car cleaner — while teaching himself to paint. He participated in the Salt Satyagraha of 1930 and was jailed for a time. Encouraged by critics such as Rudy von Leyden, he held his first solo exhibition in 1942. In 1947 he co-founded the Progressive Artists' Group with F. N. Souza, S. H. Raza, M. F. Husain, H. A. Gade, and S. K. Bakre, the circle that reshaped Indian painting after independence.

Work

Unlike several of his colleagues who left for Europe, Ara remained in India. He developed a distinctive practice centred on the still life — arrangements of flowers, fruit, bottles, and drapery rendered with sensuous colour and a vigorous paint surface — and on the nude, which he treated with a frankness unusual in Indian art of the time. His gouaches and oils of the 1950s are considered defining images of the Bombay school. Ara received the Padma Shri in 1976, and his work is held by the National Gallery of Modern Art, New Delhi, and other major collections.
