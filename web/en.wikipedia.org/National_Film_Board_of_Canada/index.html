National Film Board of Canada

Founded: 1939
Headquarters: Montreal, Quebec
Founder: John Grierson (first commissioner)
Output: documentaries, animation, interactive works

The National Film Board of Canada (NFB; French: Office national du film du Canada) is Canada's public film and digital media producer and distributor. Founded in 1939 under documentary pioneer John Grierson, it has produced more than 13,000 works and won over 5,000 awards, including 12 Academy Awards.

History

Created initially to produce wartime information and propaganda films such as the Canada Carries On series, the NFB moved its production centre to Montreal in the 1950s. Its French Unit and later Studio D (the women's studio, founded 1974) diversified its voices, while Challenge for Change used film to give marginalised communities a voice in the 1960s–70s.

Animation and innovation

The NFB is world-renowned for experimental animation: Norman McLaren pioneered drawn-on-film and pixilation techniques (Neighbours, 1952), and later animators produced Oscar-winners such as The Sand Castle, Bob's Birthday and Ryan. Documentaries like Pour la suite du monde and works by Alanis Obomsawin on Indigenous issues are landmarks of Canadian cinema. Today the NFB streams thousands of films free on its website and produces interactive digital works.
