Market (place)

A traditional marketplace, a gathering place for trade since antiquity.

A market, or marketplace, is a place where people regularly gather to buy and sell provisions, livestock and other goods. Historically, markets were held in the open spaces of towns and cities — market squares often giving the settlement its centre — and in many parts of the world they remain an important feature of daily life, from covered bazaars and souks to farmers' markets and street markets. Some markets operate daily; others are held weekly or seasonally, following traditions that may date back centuries.

History

Markets are among the oldest institutions of trade. They were central to the Greek agora and the Roman forum, and medieval European towns sought charters granting the right to hold markets and fairs, with market crosses marking the spot where oaths of fair dealing were sworn. In the Islamic world the covered bazaar or souk became an architectural complex of shops, caravanserais and mosques; the Grand Bazaar of Istanbul, founded in the fifteenth century, remains one of the world's largest covered markets. Colonial and industrial-era cities built permanent market halls of iron and glass, such as Les Halles in Paris.

Types

Markets range from wholesale markets supplying retailers (such as great produce and fish markets) to retail formats: farmers' markets selling directly from producers, flea markets for second-hand goods, craft markets, Christmas markets in German-speaking Europe, and the night markets of East and Southeast Asia, famous for street food. Wet markets in Asia sell fresh meat, seafood and produce. In West Africa, market women and periodic market rings organised trade across regions long before colonial rule.

Social and economic role

Beyond exchange, marketplaces are social institutions: places of news, gossip, courtship and political gathering, and in many cultures still the heart of the town. Economists distinguish the physical marketplace from the abstract "market" of economics — the totality of potential exchanges — but the physical market remains vital where informal trade provides livelihoods for large shares of the population. Although supermarkets and e-commerce have displaced markets in some regions, farmers' and street markets have also seen revivals as consumers seek local produce and street food culture.
