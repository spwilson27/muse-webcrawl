Monster group

In the area of abstract algebra known as group theory, the Monster group M (also known as the Fischer–Griess Monster, or the Friendly Giant) is the largest sporadic simple group, having order

The finite simple groups have been completely classified. Every such group belongs to one of 18 countably infinite families, or is one of 26 sporadic groups that do not follow such a systematic pattern. The Monster group contains 20 of the other sporadic groups as subquotients, and these 20 have been called the happy family by Robert Griess. The remaining six sporadic groups are called the pariahs.

History

The Monster was predicted by Bernd Fischer and Robert Griess in 1973, as a simple group containing certain other sporadic groups as centralizers of involutions. Griess constructed the Monster in 1980 as the automorphism group of the Griess algebra, a 196,884-dimensional commutative non-associative algebra. John Conway and Simon Norton conjectured a deep connection between the Monster and the modular j-function, known as monstrous moonshine, which was proved by Richard Borcherds in 1992 using string theory; Borcherds received the Fields Medal partly for this work.

Representations

The smallest faithful complex representation of the Monster has dimension 196,883 — one less than the dimension of the Griess algebra. The Monster can be realized as a Galois group and in other ways; it acts on a vertex operator algebra known as the moonshine module.
