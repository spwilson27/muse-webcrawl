Tlaxcala (Spanish pronunciation: [tla(k)sˈkala]), officially the Free and Sovereign State of Tlaxcala, is one of the 32 federal entities of Mexico. It is located in east-central Mexico, in the Altiplano region, bounded by the states of Puebla, Hidalgo, and the State of Mexico. It is the smallest state in the country by area. Its capital is the city of Tlaxcala de Xicohténcatl.

History

Before the Spanish conquest, Tlaxcala was an independent confederation of four republics — Tepeticpac, Ocotelolco, Tizatlán, and Quiahuixtlán — inhabited by Nahua people closely related to, but long hostile to, the Mexica (Aztecs) of Tenochtitlan. Encircled by the Aztec Empire, which imposed an embargo on goods such as salt and cotton and conducted "flower wars" against it, Tlaxcala never was conquered by the Mexica. When Hernán Cortés arrived in 1519, the Tlaxcalans first fought the Spanish and then, under leaders including Xicotencatl the Elder and Maxixcatzin, allied with them. Tlaxcalan warriors formed the bulk of the indigenous army that besieged and destroyed Tenochtitlan in 1521, and Tlaxcalans later assisted Spanish campaigns across Mesoamerica and the Philippines. In return, the Spanish Crown granted Tlaxcala special privileges: its nobility retained status, the province was governed by an indigenous council (cabildo), and Tlaxcalans were exempted from some taxes and the encomienda system. Modern Tlaxcalans have debated this legacy; some regard the alliance as a betrayal of indigenous peoples, others as a pragmatic act of self-preservation against Aztec domination.

Geography and economy

The state occupies a high plateau dominated by the volcano La Malinche (4,462 m). Its economy rests on agriculture (maize, barley, pulque maguey), textiles, and increasingly manufacturing and tourism. Notable sites include the pre-Hispanic ruins of Cacaxtla, famous for its vivid Maya-style murals, and Xochitécatl, the colonial churches of the capital, and the sanctuary of the Virgen de Ocotlán.

The name Tlaxcala derives from Nahuatl Tlaxcallān, "place of corn tortillas".
