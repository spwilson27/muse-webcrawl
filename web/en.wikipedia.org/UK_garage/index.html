UK garage

UK garage, abbreviated as UKG, is a genre of electronic dance music that developed in London in the mid-1990s. Emerging from the UK's jungle and rave scenes and the British remix culture of US garage house, the genre is characterised by shuffling, syncopated 2-step percussion, chopped and pitched-up vocal samples, and deep sub-bass. It spawned the related styles of 2-step garage, grime, dubstep and UK funky.

History

In the mid-1990s, DJs at London clubs such as the Paradise Garage-inspired nights began playing US garage house imports at faster tempos and pitching up imports to fit alongside jungle. British producers including Grant Nelson and the Dreem Teem developed a distinctive domestic sound. By 1999–2000, UK garage had crossed into the charts: Artful Dodger's "Re-Rewind" featuring Craig David reached number two, and acts such as MJ Cole ("Sincere") won mainstream recognition, with Craig David's Born to Do It a major album success.

2-step and aftermath

Around 2000, producers such as El-B and Horsepower Productions stripped the four-to-the-floor kick from the rhythm to create 2-step, emphasising shuffle and space. The darker edges of the scene fed into grime (around the pirate radio stations of east London) and dubstep (around Croydon's Big Apple Records). UK garage later influenced bassline house in the north of England and UK funky; periodic revivals in the 2010s and 2020s brought new producers such as PinkPantheress to prominence.

Characteristics

UK garage tracks typically run at around 130–135 beats per minute, with swung hi-hats, off-beat snares, time-stretched vocals and warping basslines. MCs frequently host club nights and pirate radio sets, an element inherited from jungle and sound system culture.
