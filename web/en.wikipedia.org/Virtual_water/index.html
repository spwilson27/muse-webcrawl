Virtual water

Virtual water (also called embedded water or hidden water) is the water consumed and polluted in the production of a good or service — the volume of fresh water used "virtually" along its whole supply chain. The concept was introduced in 1993 by the British geographer John Anthony Allan, who argued that water-scarce countries can relieve pressure on their own resources by importing water-intensive products, in effect importing virtual water. Allan received the Stockholm Water Prize in 2008 for the concept.

Virtual water content of products

Producing one kilogram of wheat requires roughly 1,300 litres of water, while a kilogram of beef can require around 15,000 litres, mostly for growing feed crops. A single cup of coffee embodies about 140 litres. Researchers distinguish "blue water" (surface and groundwater), "green water" (rainfall stored in soil) and "grey water" (water needed to dilute pollutants).

Trade and the water footprint

International trade in food and goods moves enormous volumes of virtual water: major exporters include the United States, Brazil, Argentina and Australia, while the Middle East, North Africa, Japan and much of Europe are net importers. The related concept of the water footprint, developed by Arjen Hoekstra and the Water Footprint Network, measures total virtual water use of consumers, businesses and nations.
