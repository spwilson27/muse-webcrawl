Takeaway food

Takeaway food (British English), also called takeout or to-go food (American English), is prepared food purchased at a restaurant or fast-food outlet for consumption elsewhere, either collected by the customer or delivered to their home or workplace. It is one of the oldest forms of commercial food service and a major sector of the modern food industry.

History

Ready-cooked food sold for consumption off the premises existed in antiquity: street stalls and cookshops served urban populations in ancient Rome (whose thermopolia sold hot food and drinks), China, and the Islamic world. In industrial-era Britain, fish and chips became an iconic working-class takeaway from the 1860s. The twentieth century added drive-through restaurants in the United States, pizza delivery, and the spread of Chinese, Indian, and kebab takeaways across Europe and beyond. From the 2010s, app-based delivery platforms transformed the sector, connecting customers to restaurants without their own delivery fleets.

Forms and packaging

Takeaway ranges from street food eaten in the street to full restaurant meals delivered by courier. Packaging has evolved from newspaper wrapping and waxed paper to foam, plastic, foil, and moulded-fibre containers — with growing regulatory and consumer pressure over single-use plastics and food waste. Temperature retention and food safety during transport are central practical concerns.

Economics and impact

Takeaway is a significant employer and a large share of restaurant revenue in many countries; the COVID-19 pandemic sharply accelerated delivery as dine-in service closed. Critics link heavy reliance on takeaway food to poor diet quality, packaging waste, and poor conditions for gig-economy delivery riders, while supporters point to convenience, affordability, and support for small independent businesses.
