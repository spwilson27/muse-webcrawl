Chandragupta II


Chandragupta II, Gupta emperor, reigned c. 375–415 CE.

Chandragupta II (reigned c. 375–415 CE), also known by his title Vikramaditya, was the third ruler of the Gupta Empire in India, and is regarded as one of its greatest emperors. The son of Samudragupta, he extended the empire through conquest and marriage alliances until it stretched from the Indus valley in the west to Bengal in the east. His reign is remembered as a high point of classical Indian art, literature and science, sometimes called a golden age.

Accession

According to inscriptional and literary evidence, Chandragupta succeeded his father Samudragupta around 375 CE. Some traditions suggest his elder brother Ramagupta may briefly have held the throne, but the details are disputed among historians.

Conquests

Chandragupta's most celebrated achievement was the defeat of the Western Kshatrapas of Malwa, Gujarat and Saurashtra, ending some four centuries of Saka rule in western India and giving the Guptas access to the lucrative ports of the Arabian Sea. He also consolidated Gupta authority over the Vakataka territories of the Deccan through the marriage of his daughter Prabhavatigupta to the Vakataka king Rudrasena II.

Court and culture

Later tradition associates Chandragupta's court at Ujjain and Pataliputra with the navaratnas, the "nine gems" of learning, among them the poet Kalidasa. The Chinese Buddhist pilgrim Faxian travelled through India during his reign and left an account of a prosperous and well-governed empire. The emperor himself was a Vaishnavite, as shown by his coins and inscriptions, but patronised Buddhism and Jainism as well.

Legacy

Chandragupta II was succeeded by his son Kumaragupta I. His titles, including Vikramaditya ("sun of valour"), became legendary and were adopted by later kings; the figure of Vikramaditya in Indian folklore is partly modelled on him.
