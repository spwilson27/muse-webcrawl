Downs

Chalk downland in southern England.

The downs are areas of open chalk grassland and rolling hills found mainly in southern England, taking their name from the Old English dūn, meaning hill. The principal ranges are the North Downs and South Downs, which form a great arc around the Weald, together with the Berkshire, Marlborough and Dorset downs.

Landscape and ecology

Downland developed over centuries of sheep grazing on thin chalk soils, producing a springy turf rich in wildflowers and butterflies, including orchids and chalkhill blues. Much traditional downland was lost to ploughing in the 20th century, and surviving fragments are protected as nature reserves and, in the South Downs, as a national park established in 2010.

Other uses

The term "downs" also denotes offshore anchorages, most famously the Downs off the Kent coast near Deal, a sheltered roadstead historically important to shipping in the English Channel.
