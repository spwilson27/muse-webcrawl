Belay device

A belay device is a mechanical piece of climbing equipment used to control a rope during a belay. It is designed to improve belay safety for both the climber and the belayer by allowing the belayer to manage the rope with minimal physical effort, and by multiplying the friction on the rope so that a fall can be arrested with modest holding force.

Function

Most belay devices function on the principle of friction: the rope is threaded through the device in a configuration that bends it sharply, so that when the belayer pulls on the brake strand the rope jams or is squeezed, letting the belayer stop a falling climber. Devices are almost always used together with a locking carabiner attached to the belayer's harness belay loop.

Types

Tubular devices

The classic tubular or "plate" device descends from the Sticht plate, introduced in the 1970s. Modern versions such as the ATC (Air Traffic Controller) consist of a tapered aluminium tube with slots for one or two ropes and a keeper cable. They can also be used for rappelling (abseiling).

Assisted-braking devices

Assisted-braking (sometimes called auto-locking) devices, exemplified by the Petzl GriGri introduced in 1991, contain an internal cam that pivots to pinch the rope under load, helping the belayer arrest a fall. Other designs achieve similar ends through geometry alone, using the carabiner to wedge the rope (so-called passive assisted braking).

Figure eight

The figure-eight descender, originally designed for rappelling, can also be used to belay, though it tends to twist the rope and is less favoured for belaying in modern practice.

Use in guide mode

Many tubular devices can be attached directly to an anchor in "guide mode," allowing a leader to belay one or two seconding climbers from above with an auto-blocking configuration.
