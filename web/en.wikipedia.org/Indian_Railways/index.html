Indian Railways

Indian Railways (IR) is the state-owned national railway system of India, operated by the Ministry of Railways. It manages the fourth-largest railway network in the world by size, with over 65,000 km of route and more than 7,000 stations, and is among the world's largest employers, with roughly 1.2 million employees. Before the COVID-19 pandemic it carried more than eight billion passengers a year, along with over a billion tonnes of freight.

History

The first passenger train ran in 1853 from Bombay (Mumbai) to Thane, a distance of 34 km, under the Great Indian Peninsula Railway. The network expanded rapidly under British rule through guaranteed-return private companies; the system was progressively nationalized and consolidated into Indian Railways after independence in 1947. Landmark achievements include the Konkan Railway (1998) through the Western Ghats and the ongoing construction of dedicated freight corridors and high-speed rail.

Network and services

The network is organized into zones and divisions and uses predominantly broad gauge (1,676 mm). Services range from suburban locals in Mumbai and Kolkata to long-distance expresses, luxury trains such as the Palace on Wheels, and premium trains including Rajdhani, Shatabdi, and the semi-high-speed Vande Bharat Express trains introduced in 2019. Metre- and narrow-gauge "toy trains" of the Darjeeling Himalayan and Nilgiri Mountain railways are UNESCO World Heritage Sites. Extensive electrification has converted most broad-gauge routes.
