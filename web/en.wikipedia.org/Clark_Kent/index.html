Clark Kent

Alter ego: Superman (Kal-El)
Created by: Jerry Siegel and Joe Shuster
First appearance: Action Comics #1 (1938)
Occupation: Journalist for the Daily Planet

Clark Joseph Kent is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by DC Comics. He is the civilian identity and alter ego of the superhero Superman, created by writer Jerry Siegel and artist Joe Shuster, and first appeared in Action Comics #1 in 1938.

Fictional biography

Born Kal-El on the planet Krypton, the infant was rocketed to Earth and found by Kansas farmers Jonathan and Martha Kent, who raised him in Smallville as their son Clark. To protect his loved ones and live an ordinary life, the adult Clark works as a reporter for the Daily Planet in Metropolis, adopting a mild-mannered, bespectacled persona—glasses, conservative suits, and a diffident manner—radically unlike his confident superhero identity. His colleagues include Lois Lane, Jimmy Olsen, and editor Perry White.

Characterization

Writers have varied the balance between the identities: early stories presented Clark as a disguise and Superman as the true self, while later versions, especially after the 1986 reboot by John Byrne, treated Clark as the authentic personality and Superman as the public role. The contrast between Kent and Superman is often read as a meditation on assimilation, identity, and the immigrant experience, reflecting the background of his creators.

Portrayals

Clark Kent has been played on screen by Kirk Alyn, George Reeves, Christopher Reeve, Dean Cain, Tom Welling (in Smallville, a series centred on the young Clark), Brandon Routh, Henry Cavill, and Tyler Hoechlin, among others.
