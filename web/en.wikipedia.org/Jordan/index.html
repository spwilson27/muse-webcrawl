Jordan

Official name: Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan
Capital (and largest city): Amman
Official language: Arabic
Monarch: Abdullah II
Area: 89,342 km²
Population: approx. 11.3 million
Currency: Jordanian dinar (JOD)

Jordan, officially the Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan, is a country in West Asia. It is situated at the crossroads of Asia, Africa and Europe, within the Levant. It is bordered by Saudi Arabia to the south and east, Iraq to the northeast, Syria to the north, Israel and Palestine (the West Bank) to the west, and the Red Sea to the southwest at the Gulf of Aqaba. The capital and largest city is Amman, named after the Ammonites who inhabited the area in biblical times.

History

The land of Jordan has been home to ancient kingdoms including Ammon, Moab and Edom, and later formed part of the Nabataean kingdom, whose capital Petra is now Jordan's most famous archaeological site, before coming under Roman, Byzantine and Islamic rule. After the First World War the Emirate of Transjordan was established under British mandate in 1921, ruled by the Hashemite Emir Abdullah. The kingdom gained independence in 1946. In the 1948 Arab–Israeli War Jordan occupied and annexed the West Bank and East Jerusalem, which it lost to Israel in the Six-Day War of 1967; Jordan renounced its claim to the West Bank in 1988 and signed a peace treaty with Israel in 1994, the second Arab state to do so.

Geography and demography

Jordan is mostly arid desert plateau, with the Jordan Valley and the Dead Sea — the lowest point on Earth's land surface — along its western border, and the Jordan River a vital but scarce water resource. The population, concentrated in the northwest around Amman, Zarqa and Irbid, includes large communities of Palestinian, Iraqi and Syrian refugees; Jordan is one of the most water-scarce countries in the world.

Economy and culture

Classified as an upper-middle-income economy, Jordan depends on phosphates and potash, tourism, foreign aid, remittances and growing services and technology sectors. Sites such as Petra, Jerash and Wadi Rum, and the baptism site on the Jordan River, draw visitors from around the world. The country is a constitutional monarchy in which King Abdullah II, who succeeded his father Hussein in 1999, retains wide executive and legislative authority.
