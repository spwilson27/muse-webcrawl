Pre-nominal letters

Pre-nominal letters are a title, salutation, or form of address placed before a person's name. Examples include academic and professional prefixes such as Dr (Doctor), Prof. (Professor), and Rev. (Reverend), as well as honorific titles of nobility and office. Pre-nominal letters contrast with post-nominal letters, which follow the name (for example, PhD, FRS, or QC).

Usage

In many European countries, titles of nobility historically functioned as pre-nominals, such as Graf in German-speaking countries or the British courtesy prefixes of peers. In British usage, styles such as "The Rt Hon." (The Right Honourable) precede the names of privy counsellors, and "Sir" and "Dame" precede the given names of knights and dames. Military and naval ranks, ecclesiastical titles, and judicial titles ("Judge", "Justice", "Lord Justice") are also used pre-nominally.

Academic titles

In some countries, notably Germany and Austria, academic degrees are customarily carried before the name in formal address, such as "Dr. med." or "Dipl.-Ing." In English-speaking countries, by contrast, academic qualifications are usually given as post-nominals, with only "Dr" or "Professor" used pre-nominally as a form of address.
