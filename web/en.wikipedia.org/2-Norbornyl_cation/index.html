2-Norbornyl cation

The 2-norbornyl cation is a carbocation derived from norbornane (bicyclo[2.2.1]heptane) by removal of a hydride or leaving group from the 2-position. It became one of the most famous and long-running controversies in physical organic chemistry: whether the cation has a classical structure with a localized positive charge, or a non-classical structure in which the charge is delocalized over a three-center two-electron bond.

Saul Winstein proposed the bridged, non-classical structure in 1949 to explain the unusually fast solvolysis of exo-norbornyl derivatives and the exclusive formation of exo products. H. C. Brown championed the classical interpretation for decades. Later spectroscopic studies, X-ray crystallography of a stable salt, and high-level computations confirmed the bridged, non-classical structure.
