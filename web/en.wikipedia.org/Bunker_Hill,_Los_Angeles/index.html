Bunker Hill, Los Angeles

Bunker Hill
Neighborhood of Los Angeles, California
Location: Downtown Los Angeles
Known for: Angels Flight railway, Victorian mansions (former), skyscrapers

Bunker Hill is a neighborhood in Downtown Los Angeles, California. Historically a steep hill crowned with grand Victorian mansions, it was entirely redeveloped in the second half of the 20th century into a district of office towers, cultural institutions and residential high-rises.

History

Named after the Battle of Bunker Hill, the hill was developed from the 1860s as one of the city's most fashionable residential districts. The Angels Flight funicular, opened in 1901, connected the hilltop homes with the commercial district below. By the mid-20th century the aging rooming houses were deemed blighted, and the Community Redevelopment Agency demolished nearly the entire neighborhood; residents, many of them elderly pensioners, were displaced.

Redevelopment

Redevelopment produced landmarks including the Museum of Contemporary Art, the Walt Disney Concert Hall, The Broad museum and numerous corporate skyscrapers. The area is served by Angels Flight, restored and reopened in the 1990s.

In popular culture

The lost Victorian neighborhood was a favorite setting of film noir and the novels of Raymond Chandler and John Fante, whose Ask the Dust is set partly on Bunker Hill.
