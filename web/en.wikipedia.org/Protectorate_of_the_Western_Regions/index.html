Protectorate of the Western Regions

The Protectorate administered Han China's interests in the Tarim Basin and beyond.

The Protectorate of the Western Regions (Chinese: 西域都護府; pinyin: Xīyù Dūhù Fǔ) was a military and administrative office established by the Han dynasty of China to govern the "Western Regions" — the oasis states of the Tarim Basin and, at times, territories farther west in Central Asia. It was first created in 60 BC under Emperor Xuan, following the campaigns of the general Zheng Ji against the Xiongnu and the submission of the kingdom of Jushi.

Headquartered first at Wulei (near modern Luntai) and later at Qiuci (Kucha), the Protector General supervised local kings, collected tribute, protected the routes of the Silk Road, and coordinated military colonies (tuntian) that supplied Han garrisons. The office represented the westernmost reach of regular Chinese administration in the Han period.

History

Han expansion into the region followed the missions of Zhang Qian in the second century BC and decades of rivalry with the Xiongnu for control of the city-states around the Taklamakan Desert. The formal protectorate of 60 BC is conventionally taken as the date when the Western Regions came under Han administration.

The office lapsed during the turmoil at the end of the Western Han and the usurpation of Wang Mang, then was restored under the Eastern Han, notably during the career of Ban Chao, who served in the region for three decades from AD 73 and brought most of the Tarim states back under Han suzerainty. His son Ban Yong continued the work. As Eastern Han power waned in the second century AD, effective control contracted and the protectorate was eventually abandoned.

The Tang dynasty revived the institution in a new form with the Anxi Protectorate (640 AD), which governed the region from Kucha until the late eighth century.
