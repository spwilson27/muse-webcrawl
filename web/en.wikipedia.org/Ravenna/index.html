Ravenna (Italian: [raˈvenna]) is a city in the Emilia-Romagna region of northern Italy, near the Adriatic Sea. It was the capital city of the Western Roman Empire during the 5th century, then of the Ostrogothic Kingdom, and later the centre of the Byzantine Exarchate of Ravenna, giving it an unmatched concentration of late antique art and architecture.

Early Christian and Byzantine monuments

Eight of Ravenna's early Christian monuments are inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site, famed for their mosaics: the Basilica of San Vitale ( consecrated 547), the Mausoleum of Galla Placidia, the Neonian and Arian baptisteries, the Basilica of Sant'Apollinare Nuovo and Sant'Apollinare in Classe, the Archbishop's Chapel, and the Mausoleum of Theodoric. The San Vitale mosaics of Justinian and Theodora are among the most celebrated works of Byzantine art.

Later history

The poet Dante Alighieri spent his last years in Ravenna, dying there in exile in 1321; his tomb remains a place of literary pilgrimage. Today the city is also a major port and agricultural centre, connected to the Adriatic by the Candiano Canal.
