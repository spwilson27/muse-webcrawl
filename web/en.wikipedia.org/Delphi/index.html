Delphi is a town on Mount Parnassus in the south of mainland Greece. In ancient times it was an important Greek religious sanctuary sacred to the god Apollo. It was the seat of the Pythia, the high priestess who delivered the oracle, and was regarded by the Greeks as the centre (omphalos) of the world.

The sanctuary was a pan-Hellenic religious centre where athletes from all over the Greek world competed in the Pythian Games, one of the four Panhellenic Games. The site was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1987.

The oracle

The Oracle of Delphi was consulted on matters ranging from colonization and warfare to private affairs. The Pythia, seated on a tripod over a chasm, entered a trance — ancient sources attribute her utterances to vapours rising from the earth — and her words were interpreted by priests into verse. Famous consultations include those of Croesus of Lydia and the Athenians before the Battle of Salamis ("wooden walls"). Modern geological study has suggested ethylene or other hydrocarbon gases may have issued from faults beneath the temple.

The sanctuary

The sanctuary complex included the Temple of Apollo, the theatre, the stadium, the tholos of Athena Pronaia, and treasuries built by Greek city-states (notably the well-preserved Treasury of the Athenians). The maxims "Know thyself" and "Nothing in excess" were inscribed at the temple entrance.

History

Delphi's influence grew from the 8th century BC. It was sacked and enriched by turns through the classical period, came under Roman patronage (notably from Hadrian), and declined as Christianity spread; the oracle was finally silenced in the late 4th century AD under the emperor Theodosius I. Excavations by the French School at Athens began in 1892.

The modern site

The modern town of Delphi lies adjacent to the archaeological site, which includes a museum housing finds such as the Charioteer of Delphi. The area is a major tourist destination.
