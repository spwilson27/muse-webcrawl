Alternative medicine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Alternative medicine refers to practices claimed to have the healing effects of medicine but whose effectiveness has not been established using the scientific method, or whose theory and practice are not part of biomedicine. Examples include homeopathy, naturopathy, chiropractic, acupuncture, Ayurveda, and various forms of energy healing. When used alongside conventional treatment, such practices are often called complementary medicine; the combination of conventional and alternative approaches is known as integrative medicine.

Criticism and regulation

Scientific researchers and medical bodies criticize many alternative therapies for lacking evidence beyond placebo effects, and warn that they can delay effective treatment or interact harmfully with drugs. Regulation varies widely by country and practice. In the United States, the National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH) funds research into these therapies.

Usage

Surveys find that a substantial minority of adults in Western countries use some form of complementary or alternative therapy each year, most often for chronic pain and musculoskeletal complaints.
