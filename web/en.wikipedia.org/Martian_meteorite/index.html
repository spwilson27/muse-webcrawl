Martian meteorite

A Martian meteorite is a rock that formed on Mars, was ejected from the planet by an impact event, traveled through space, and landed on Earth as a meteorite. More than 200 such meteorites are known, though they form only a small fraction of all meteorites found.

Groups and evidence

Most Martian meteorites belong to the SNC group — shergottites, nakhlites, and chassignites — named for the falls at Shergotty (India, 1865), Nakhla (Egypt, 1911), and Chassigny (France, 1815). Their Martian origin is established by young crystallization ages, isotopic signatures, and trapped gases matching the Martian atmosphere measured by the Viking landers.

Famous specimens

The Antarctic meteorite ALH84001, an orthopyroxenite about 4 billion years old, drew worldwide attention in 1996 when scientists reported structures interpreted as possible fossilized bacteria; the claim remains disputed. Other well-known Martian meteorites include EETA79001, which contains shock-melted pockets with Mars-like gas, and the "Black Beauty" breccia NWA 7034.

Significance

Until sample-return missions deliver material directly, Martian meteorites are the only samples of Mars available for laboratory study, giving insight into the planet's volcanism, atmosphere, and potential habitability.
