The Staatskapelle Berlin (German: [ˈʃtaːtskaˌpɛlə]) is a German orchestra and the resident orchestra of the Berlin State Opera (Staatsoper Unter den Linden). Tracing its origins to the electoral court chapel founded in 1570, it is one of the oldest orchestras in the world, and one of Germany's most distinguished ensembles.

History

The ensemble began as the court orchestra of the Electors of Brandenburg and became the orchestra of the Prussian royal opera house, opened on Unter den Linden in 1742. Its Kapellmeisters and conductors have included Gaspare Spontini, Giacomo Meyerbeer, Felix von Weingartner, Richard Strauss, Erich Kleiber, Otto Klemperer, Wilhelm Furtwängler and Herbert von Karajan (1941–1945). After the Second World War it became the principal opera orchestra of East Berlin, led by Franz Konwitschny and Otmar Suitner, while retaining its concert role.

The Barenboim era and after

Daniel Barenboim became artistic director and Generalmusikdirektor in 1992, shortly after reunification, and led the orchestra for three decades, establishing the annual Festtage festival and making extensive recordings of the core German repertoire — Beethoven, Brahms, Bruckner, Wagner and Strauss symphonies and operas. Barenboim stepped down for health reasons in January 2023, later becoming honorary conductor for life. Christian Thielemann succeeded him as Generalmusikdirektor in September 2024.

The orchestra performs both at the Staatsoper and in symphony concerts at venues including the Philharmonie and the Konzerthaus Berlin, and tours internationally. It has about 140 members and is legally supported by the State of Berlin.
