Platonic Academy

Plato (centre left) and Aristotle in Raphael's School of Athens.

The Platonic Academy (Greek: Ἀκαδημία) was a school founded by Plato in Athens around 387 BC. It was located in a grove sacred to the hero Academus, outside the city walls to the north-west, from which it took its name — the origin of the modern word "academy". The school continued, with interruptions, for nearly nine centuries until the emperor Justinian closed it in AD 529, making it a foundational institution in the history of Western philosophy and education.

Under Plato and his successors

Plato taught at the Academy for the last forty years of his life, and his dialogues are associated with its discussions. Members investigated mathematics, astronomy, dialectic, ethics, and political philosophy; over the entrance, according to tradition, was inscribed "Let no one ignorant of geometry enter". Plato's nephew Speusippus succeeded him as head (scholarch), followed by Xenocrates, Polemo, and Crates. Aristotle studied and taught at the Academy for twenty years before founding his own school, the Lyceum.

Sceptical and later phases

In the third century BC, under Arcesilaus, the Academy turned towards philosophical scepticism — the "Middle Academy" — arguing against Stoic claims to certain knowledge. Carneades in the second century BC was its most famous sceptical head. After the school was destroyed during Sulla's siege of Athens in 86 BC, Antiochus of Ascalon revived a more dogmatic Platonism. In late antiquity the Academy became the centre of Neoplatonism, led by figures such as Plutarch of Athens, Syrianus, and Proclus, whose commentaries on Plato shaped medieval thought.

Closure and legacy

In AD 529 Justinian's measures against pagan teaching ended the Athenian school; its last head, Damascius, emigrated briefly to Persia with several colleagues. In 1462, the Florentine Platonic Academy was informally revived around Marsilio Ficino under Medici patronage. The name "academy" has since been applied to learned societies and schools worldwide, including the Accademia dei Lincei and national academies of science.
