Animation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The illusion of movement from successive images

Animation is a filmmaking technique by which still images are manipulated to create moving images. In traditional animation, images are drawn or painted by hand on transparent celluloid sheets (cels) to be photographed and exhibited on film. Animation has been recognized as an artistic medium, specifically within the entertainment industry.

Many animations are computer animations made with computer-generated imagery (CGI). Stop motion animation, in particular claymation, has continued to exist alongside these other forms. Animation is contrasted with live-action film, although the two do not exist in isolation. Many filmmakers have produced films that are a hybrid of the two.

History

Precursors of animation include the phenakistiscope (1833), zoetrope (1834) and flip book. Émile Reynaud's Théâtre Optique presented projected animated films publicly in 1892. Early animated films include J. Stuart Blackton's Humorous Phases of Funny Faces (1906) and Winsor McCay's Gertie the Dinosaur (1914).

Walt Disney's Steamboat Willie (1928) popularised sound cartoons, and Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs (1937) was the first cel-animated feature in English and Technicolor. Japanese animation (anime) developed its own industry and global audience from the mid-20th century. Computer animation rose to prominence with Pixar's Toy Story (1995), the first fully CGI feature film.

Techniques

  • Traditional (2D) animation — drawn frame by frame, often "on twos" at 12 drawings per second.
  • Computer animation — 2D vector animation and 3D animation modelled, rigged, and rendered digitally, often with keyframes and interpolation.
  • Stop motion — physical objects, puppets or clay figures photographed one frame at a time.
  • Other forms — rotoscoping, cutout animation, sand animation, and motion capture-assisted work.

The twelve basic principles of animation, codified by Disney animators Ollie Johnston and Frank Thomas, include squash and stretch, anticipation, staging, timing, and appeal.
