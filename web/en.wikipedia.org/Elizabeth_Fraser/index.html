Elizabeth Fraser

Elizabeth Davidson Fraser (born 29 August 1963) is a Scottish singer, songwriter and musician. She was the vocalist and lyricist for the Scottish rock band Cocteau Twins, who achieved international critical praise for their ethereal, effects-laden sound and her distinctive soprano voice.

Fraser is noted for lyrics that range from straightforward English to glossolalia — words chosen for sound over literal meaning.

Cocteau Twins

Fraser formed Cocteau Twins in Grangemouth, Scotland, in 1979 with Robin Guthrie and Will Heggie. Across albums including Treasure (1984) and Heaven or Las Vegas (1990), her voice became the band's defining feature, soaring over Guthrie's processed guitars. The band disbanded in 1997.

Collaborations and solo work

Fraser contributed to the This Mortal Coil collective, singing the celebrated cover of Tim Buckley's "Song to the Siren" (1983). She collaborated with Massive Attack, notably on "Teardrop" (1998), and with artists including Peter Gabriel, The Future Sound of London, and Yann Tiersen. Her solo releases have been sparse; in 2022 she released the EP Sun's Signature with Damon Reece under the name Sun's Signature.
