Quark condensate

In quantum chromodynamics (QCD), the quark condensate is a nonzero vacuum expectation value of a quark–antiquark pair, ⟨q̄q⟩. Its existence signals the spontaneous breaking of chiral symmetry in the QCD vacuum and plays a central role in the low-energy structure of hadrons.

Overview

For light quarks the condensate has a value on the order of ⟨q̄q⟩ ≈ −(250 MeV)3 (in magnitude, up to conventions). Through the Gell-Mann–Oakes–Renner relation, the condensate relates the masses of the light quarks to the mass and decay constant of the pion, explaining why the pion is an unusually light pseudo-Nambu–Goldstone boson.

Significance

The quark condensate is an order parameter for chiral symmetry breaking, which generates most of the visible mass of the universe: the bulk of the proton's mass arises from QCD dynamics rather than from the Higgs-given masses of its quarks.
