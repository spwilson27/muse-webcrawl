Infimum and supremum

A set with its upper bounds, whose least element is the supremum.

In mathematics, the infimum (abbreviated inf; plural infima) of a subset S of a partially ordered set P is the greatest element of P that is less than or equal to each element of S, if such an element exists. The supremum (abbreviated sup; plural suprema) of S is the least element of P that is greater than or equal to each element of S. The supremum is also called the least upper bound, and the infimum the greatest lower bound.

Definitions

An upper bound of a subset S of a partially ordered set (P, ≤) is an element a of P such that x ≤ a for all x in S. The supremum of S, if it exists, is the least such upper bound. Infima are defined dually using lower bounds.

For example, the supremum of the open interval (0, 1) in the real numbers is 1, although 1 is not an element of the set. The set of rational numbers whose square is less than 2 has supremum √2 in the reals but no supremum within the rationals — the observation that motivates the completeness of the real numbers.

Least-upper-bound property

The real numbers have the least-upper-bound property: every nonempty subset of R that is bounded above has a supremum in R. This completeness property is equivalent (over the ordered-field axioms) to the other standard formulations of the completeness of the reals, such as the convergence of Cauchy sequences.

Relation to maxima and minima

If a set has a maximum element, that maximum is also its supremum; but a supremum need not belong to the set, whereas a maximum must. When the supremum belongs to the set it is also the maximum. Supremum and infimum generalize the notions of maximum and minimum to sets that lack them.
