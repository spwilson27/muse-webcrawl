The Transnistria conflict is a frozen conflict between Moldova and the unrecognised state of Transnistria (officially the Pridnestrovian Moldavian Republic, PMR), supported by Russia. Its most intense phase was the Transnistria War of 1992, fought after Transnistrian separatists, backed by the Russian 14th Guards Army, declared independence from Moldova as the Moldavian SSR dissolved.

Background

Transnistria is a narrow strip of land between the Dniester River and Ukraine. Unlike the rest of Moldova, where ethnic Romanians/Moldovans predominate, the region has large Russian and Ukrainian minorities and was industrialised during the Soviet era. Fears in 1989–1990 that Moldova might unify with Romania and adopt a Romanian-only language policy prompted separatist mobilisation in Tiraspol.

The 1992 war

Fighting escalated in March 1992, centred on Dubăsari and Bender (Tighina). In June 1992, Russian forces under General Alexander Lebed intervened decisively, and a ceasefire was signed on 21 July 1992 by Presidents Mircea Snegur and Boris Yeltsin. Roughly a thousand people had been killed.

Frozen conflict

A Joint Control Commission and a trilateral peacekeeping force oversee the ceasefire. Negotiations in the "5+2" format have failed to resolve the status question. Russia maintains troops and a large ammunition depot at Cobasna. Since 2022, Russia's invasion of Ukraine has heightened tensions around the region.
