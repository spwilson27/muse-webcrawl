Golden Age of Comic Books

The Golden Age of Comic Books describes an era of American comic books from 1938 to about 1956, during which modern comic books were first published and became enormously popular. A new medium and a new genre — the superhero — were created, and characters introduced in this period, including Superman, Batman, Captain America, Wonder Woman and Captain Marvel, remain cultural icons.

History

The Golden Age is conventionally dated from the debut of Superman in Action Comics #1 (cover-dated June 1938). Superman's success unleashed a flood of costumed heroes: Batman followed in 1939, and Timely Comics (later Marvel) introduced the Human Torch, the Sub-Mariner and, in 1941, Captain America. Wonder Woman debuted in 1941 as one of the first major female superheroes. During the Second World War, comics sold in huge numbers to children and servicemen, and heroes routinely fought the Axis powers on their covers.

Post-war decline

After the war, superhero sales faded and publishers diversified into romance, crime, horror and funny-animal comics — Archie Comics' teenage humour became a staple. In the early 1950s, psychiatrist Fredric Wertham's book Seduction of the Innocent (1954) blamed comics for juvenile delinquency, prompting US Senate hearings and the industry's self-censoring Comics Code Authority. EC Comics' celebrated horror and crime lines were effectively destroyed. The era is generally considered to end around 1956, when DC's revamped Flash heralded the Silver Age.

Collecting

Golden Age comics are among the most valuable in the hobby: copies of Action Comics #1 have sold for millions of dollars, driven by scarcity — wartime paper drives destroyed countless issues — and by the first appearances of enduring characters.
