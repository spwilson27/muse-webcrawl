Fruitarianism

Fruitarianism is a diet related to veganism that consists primarily of consuming fruits and possibly nuts and seeds, but without any animal products. Fruitarian diets may be adopted for ethical, religious, environmental, cultural, economic, and health reasons. Some fruitarians eat only plant material that can be gathered without harming the plant — in the botanical sense, fruits, nuts, and seeds — while others also include vegetables and grains in varying proportions.

Motivations and variations

Motivations include the belief that fruit is the natural diet of humans and their primate ancestors, ethical objections to killing plants as well as animals, and religious doctrines such as those of some Jain and Hindu traditions that seek to minimize harm to living beings. Notable advocates have included the 19th-century German nutritionist Gustav Schlickeysen and, briefly, Apple co-founder Steve Jobs, who experimented with a fruitarian diet in his youth.

Health considerations

Nutrition authorities generally consider a strict fruit-only diet deficient in protein, essential fatty acids, vitamin B12, vitamin D, calcium, iron, and zinc, and warn of risks including dental erosion from frequent fruit acids and sugars. Health professionals advise that anyone attempting such a diet do so with careful planning and supplementation.
