The Utrecht Hill Ridge (Dutch: Utrechtse Heuvelrug) is a ridge of low hills running roughly 50 kilometres from Huizen on the Gooimeer in the northwest to Rhenen on the Lower Rhine in the southeast, in the Dutch provinces of North Holland and Utrecht. It is one of the most prominent natural landscapes of the central Netherlands, largely covered by forest and heathland.

Geology

The ridge is a push moraine (stuwwal) formed during the Saalian glaciation about 150,000 years ago, when land ice advancing from the north pushed up sands and gravels deposited by the Rhine and Meuse. Its highest summit, the Amerongse Berg, reaches 69.2 metres above sea level — among the highest points in the country outside Limburg.

Nature and land use

The southern part of the ridge is protected as the Utrechtse Heuvelrug National Park, established in 2003 and extended northward in 2013, covering roughly 10,000 hectares of forest, heath and drift sands. The area is also known for its many country estates (buitenplaatsen), especially along the Langbroekerwetering, and for towns such as Amerongen, Doorn, Driebergen and Zeist. During the Second World War the ridge saw fighting around the Grebbeberg near Rhenen in May 1940.
