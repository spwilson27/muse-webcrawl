Diablo 630

The Diablo 630 is a daisy wheel printer introduced by the Diablo Data Systems division of Xerox in the late 1970s and marketed into the 1980s. It is remembered as one of the definitive letter-quality printers of the early personal-computer era.

Description

The printer used a removable metal or plastic daisy wheel, each petal carrying a single character, which a hammer struck against an inked ribbon and the paper. The 630 could print at up to about 40 characters per second with typewriter-quality output, supported proportional spacing and interchangeable typefaces, and connected to computers and word processors through parallel and serial interfaces.

Significance

The Diablo interface protocol became an informal industry standard, emulated by many competing printers. The 630 and its specifications were also influential in the development of the Diablo 630 emulation in later printers, and the printer was famously driven by early machines such as the Xerox Alto research systems. Daisy wheel printing was ultimately displaced by dot-matrix and then laser printers.
