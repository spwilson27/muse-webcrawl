Sean Kelly

John James "Sean" Kelly (born 24 May 1956) is an Irish former professional road racing cyclist, one of the most successful riders of the 1980s and widely regarded as one of the finest classics specialists of all time. From a farming background near Carrick-on-Suir, County Waterford, he turned professional in 1977 and won 193 races in an eighteen-year career.

Career highlights

Kelly won the Vuelta a España in 1988 and took the points classification at the Tour de France four times (1982, 1983, 1985 and 1989), finishing as high as fourth overall in the Tour in 1985. His true domain was the one-day classics and short stage races: he won Paris–Nice a record seven consecutive times (1982–1988), Milan–San Remo twice, Paris–Roubaix twice, Liège–Bastogne–Liège twice, the Tour of Lombardy three times, Gent–Wevelgem, the Tour of Flanders-equalling consistency without victory there, and the season-long Super Prestige Pernod competition five times. He was ranked world number one for much of the mid-1980s under the FICP rankings. His durability, sprinting speed after hard races, and tactical intelligence made him formidable in all but the highest mountains.

Style and reputation

Famously taciturn, Kelly was known for his toughness in bad weather and for descending fearlessly. He worked as a stonemason's labourer and on the family farm before cycling, and his rise from rural Ireland to the top of a sport dominated by continental Europeans made him a national hero alongside his contemporary Stephen Roche, with whom he formed an unlikely Irish golden generation in the 1980s peloton.

Later life

After retiring in 1994, Kelly became a well-known cycling commentator, principally with Eurosport, and has supported the development of Irish cycling, including the Sean Kelly Cycling Academy in Belgium and teams bearing his name. He remains closely associated with his home region, where the Sean Kelly Tour of Waterford sportive draws thousands of riders.
