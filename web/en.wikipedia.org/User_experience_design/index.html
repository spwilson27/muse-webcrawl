User experience design (UX design, UXD or UED) is the process of designing products, systems and services with a focus on the quality and thoughtfulness of the user experience. It encompasses user research, information architecture, interaction design, visual design and usability evaluation, with the goal of making products useful, usable and desirable.

History

The term "user experience" was popularised in the 1990s by cognitive scientist Don Norman, who joined Apple as Vice President of Advanced Technology and later co-founded the Nielsen Norman Group with Jakob Nielsen. Precursors include Henry Dreyfuss's Designing for People (1955), the human factors and ergonomics traditions, and the human–computer interaction research of the 1980s.

Elements

Jesse James Garrett's model, The Elements of User Experience (2000), describes five planes: strategy (user needs and business goals), scope (features and content requirements), structure (interaction design and information architecture), skeleton (interface, navigation and information design), and surface (sensory and visual design). Peter Morville's "UX honeycomb" lists seven facets: useful, usable, desirable, findable, accessible, credible and valuable.

Process

Common UX activities include stakeholder and user interviews, personas, journey mapping, competitive analysis, sketching and wireframing, interactive prototyping, A/B testing and moderated or unmoderated usability testing. UX designers commonly work alongside product managers, visual/UI designers and software engineers in agile teams.
