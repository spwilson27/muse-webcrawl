Spin-off (media)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A spin-off extends an existing fictional world

In media, a spin-off (or spinoff) is a radio programme, television programme, film, video game, or other narrative work derived from already existing works that focus on more details and different aspects from the original work (e.g. particular topics, characters, or events). A spin-off retains the setting of its parent work but shifts focus to a secondary character, a new ensemble, or another time period.

Types and examples

Classic television spin-offs include Frasier (from Cheers), Better Call Saul (from Breaking Bad), and the numerous Law & Order and Star Trek series. Sidequels and character-focused films extend franchises in cinema. Spin-offs differ from reboots (which restart continuity), remakes, and sequels (which continue the main storyline).

Reasons

Studios create spin-offs to exploit a successful property, retain audiences, and extend a fictional universe at lower risk than an entirely new work, though success is not guaranteed.
