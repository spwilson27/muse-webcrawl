The Iberian Peninsula (Spanish and Portuguese: Península Ibérica), also known simply as Iberia, is a peninsula in southwestern Europe, separated from France by the Pyrenees and bounded by the Atlantic Ocean, the Mediterranean Sea, and the Strait of Gibraltar. It comprises mainland Spain and Portugal, the microstate of Andorra, the British territory of Gibraltar, and a small strip of southern France. After the Scandinavian and Balkan peninsulas, it is the third-largest peninsula in Europe.

Geography

The peninsula is dominated by the Meseta, a high central plateau ringed and crossed by mountain ranges: the Pyrenees in the northeast, the Cantabrian Mountains in the north, the Sierra Morena in the south, and the Baetic System, whose Sierra Nevada contains Mulhacén, the peninsula's highest peak. Major rivers include the Ebro (the only great river flowing to the Mediterranean), the Tagus (the longest), the Douro, the Guadiana, and the Guadalquivir. The climate ranges from the wet Atlantic "Green Spain" to semi-arid conditions in the southeast.

History

Iberia was home to some of Europe's earliest human inhabitants (Atapuerca) and to celebrated prehistoric art at Altamira. Phoenicians, Greeks, and Carthaginians founded colonies before Rome conquered the peninsula as Hispania (218 BC–AD 472). Visigothic rule gave way in 711 to the Muslim conquest, producing al-Andalus and centuries of coexistence and conflict during the Reconquista, completed in 1492—the year Columbus sailed under Spanish sponsorship. The Iberian Union (1580–1640) briefly joined the Spanish and Portuguese crowns, whose empires spanned the globe. Today the peninsula's principal languages are Spanish, Portuguese, Catalan, Galician, and Basque, the last a pre-Indo-European isolate.
