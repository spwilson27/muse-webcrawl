Walt Disney World

The Walt Disney World Resort, also called Walt Disney World or Disney World, is an entertainment resort complex in Bay Lake and Lake Buena Vista, Florida, United States, near the cities of Orlando and Kissimmee. Opened on October 1, 1971, the resort is operated by Disney Experiences, a division of the Walt Disney Company. The property covers nearly 25,000 acres (39 sq mi; 101 km²), of which only about half has been developed.

Theme parks

Walt Disney World contains four theme parks: Magic Kingdom (opened 1971), Epcot (1982), Disney's Hollywood Studios (1989), and Disney's Animal Kingdom (1998). It also features two water parks, Typhoon Lagoon and Blizzard Beach, along with the Disney Springs shopping and entertainment district, dozens of resort hotels, golf courses, and the ESPN Wide World of Sports Complex.

History

The resort was conceived by Walt Disney in the 1960s as a supplement to Disneyland in California. Disney secretly acquired vast tracts of central Florida land through dummy corporations, and the Florida legislature created the Reedy Creek Improvement District to give the company quasi-governmental control over the land. Walt Disney died in 1966, before construction began; his brother Roy O. Disney oversaw the project and insisted it be named "Walt Disney World" in his honor. Magic Kingdom, modeled on Disneyland but on a grander scale, was the first park to open.

Attendance

Walt Disney World is the most visited vacation resort in the world, with average annual attendance of more than 58 million visitors across its parks.
