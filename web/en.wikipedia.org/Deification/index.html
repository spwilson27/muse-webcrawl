Deification

Deification is the treatment of a mortal as a god, or the process by which something or someone is made divine. The term covers the historical practice of worshipping rulers and heroes as gods, and theological doctrines — especially in Eastern Christianity — in which human beings are said to participate in the divine nature.

Historical cults

Pharaohs of ancient Egypt were regarded as living gods, and Greek heroes could receive cult after death. In Rome, the imperial cult deified emperors — Julius Caesar was formally consecrated as Divus Julius in 42 BC, and successors were routinely deified by the Senate after death. In East Asia, figures such as the deified general Guan Yu acquired temples and worship, and Japanese emperors were presented as divine until 1946.

Theosis

In Eastern Orthodox theology, theosis or divinization is the transformative process by which a Christian is made partaker of the divine nature through grace, summed up in Athanasius's dictum that God "was made man, that we men might become gods" — understood as participation in God's energies, not his essence, and not a change of creaturely status. Related concepts appear in some Western mystical traditions and in Latter-day Saint doctrine of exaltation.
