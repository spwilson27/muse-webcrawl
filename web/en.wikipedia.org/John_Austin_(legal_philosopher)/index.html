John Austin (legal philosopher)

John Austin
Born3 March 1790, Creeting Mill, Suffolk, England
Died1 December 1859, Weybridge, England
SchoolLegal positivism

John Austin (1790–1859) was an English legal theorist who founded the analytical tradition of jurisprudence and is regarded as a father of legal positivism.

Theory

In The Province of Jurisprudence Determined (1832), based on his lectures as the first professor of jurisprudence at University College London, Austin argued that law is properly the command of a sovereign, backed by sanctions, and that the existence of law is separate from its moral merit — "the existence of law is one thing; its merit or demerit is another."

Influence

Though his lectures drew few students and he resigned in 1834, his posthumously published works shaped legal education across the British world. Later scholars, above all H. L. A. Hart, both developed and criticised Austin's command theory.
