Heine–Borel theorem

In real analysis the Heine–Borel theorem, named after Eduard Heine and Émile Borel, states that a subset of Euclidean space is compact if and only if it is closed and bounded.

Statement

For a subset S of Rn, the following two statements are equivalent: (1) S is closed and bounded; (2) every open cover of S has a finite subcover, i.e. S is compact. In particular, a subset of the real line is compact if and only if it is a finite union of closed intervals.

The theorem was first proved by Heine in 1872 in the context of uniform continuity, and generalised by Borel in 1895. Its equivalence with other forms of completeness of the real numbers — such as the Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem and Cauchy completeness — makes it a central result in analysis. The "closed and bounded" characterisation does not hold in arbitrary metric spaces, where compactness implies but is not implied by closedness and boundedness.
