The Coca-Cola Company

The Coca-Cola Company is an American multinational corporation founded in 1892. It manufactures, sells and markets nonalcoholic beverage concentrates, syrups and alcoholic beverages, and operates a franchised distribution system dating from 1889 in which the company produces syrup concentrate, sold to bottlers worldwide. Its flagship product, Coca-Cola, was invented in 1886 by pharmacist John Stith Pemberton in Atlanta, Georgia; the company is headquartered in Atlanta today.

History

Pemberton's coca wine–inspired syrup was first sold at Jacobs' Pharmacy in May 1886; his bookkeeper Frank M. Robinson coined the name and the flowing Spencerian script logo. Businessman Asa Candler acquired the formula and incorporated The Coca-Cola Company in 1892, driving national expansion through bottling contracts beginning in 1899. Under Robert Woodruff, president from 1923, Coke became a global icon — put into the hands of American servicemen worldwide during the Second World War at five cents a bottle.

Products and business

Beyond Coke itself, the portfolio includes Sprite, Fanta, Schweppes (in some markets), Minute Maid juices, Dasani water, Powerade, and Costa Coffee (acquired 2019). The company sells concentrates and syrups to bottling partners while owning or holding stakes in some bottlers. Coca-Cola is consistently ranked among the world's most valuable brands and sells its products in more than 200 countries and territories. Its 1985 "New Coke" reformulation and swift reversal after consumer backlash remains a famous marketing case study.
