Hugo Preuss

Hugo Preuss
Born: 28 October 1860, Berlin
Died: 9 October 1925, Berlin
Occupation: Jurist, politician (DDP)
Known for: Principal drafter of the Weimar Constitution
Post: Reich Minister of the Interior (1919)

Hugo Preuss (German: Preuß; 1860–1925) was a German lawyer and liberal politician who is best known as the principal author of the constitution of the Weimar Republic of 1919. A leading theorist of municipal self-government and democratic constitutional law, he served as the first Reich Minister of the Interior of the republic.

Scholarly career

Preuss, born into an assimilated Jewish family in Berlin, studied law and became a lecturer at the University of Berlin, though an ordinary professorship was long denied him in part because of discrimination; he later taught at the Berlin Handelshochschule. His scholarship drew on Otto von Gierke's theory of associations (Genossenschaft) and on the Prussian reformer Heinrich Friedrich Karl vom Stein, arguing against the sovereign-state absolutism of the Obrigkeitsstaat in favour of self-governing communities and democratic legitimacy.

The Weimar Constitution

After the November Revolution of 1918, Friedrich Ebert appointed Preuss State Secretary of the Interior and entrusted him with drafting a constitution. His draft, presented in early 1919, proposed a unitary democratic republic with strong fundamental rights and proposed restructuring the Prussian-dominated state system. The Weimar National Assembly modified his draft — strengthening federalism and the presidency and adding a lengthy catalogue of rights — but the resulting Weimar Constitution retained his essential democratic framework. As minister he defended the constitution and resigned in June 1919 in protest at the Treaty of Versailles.

Later life

Preuss co-founded the German Democratic Party (DDP), sat in the Prussian state assembly, and continued writing against both monarchist reaction and the anti-democratic right until his death in 1925.
