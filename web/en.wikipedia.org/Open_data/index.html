Open data

Open data is data that is openly accessible, exploitable, editable and shared by anyone for any purpose, generally under an open licence. Goals of the open data movement include government transparency and accountability, scientific reproducibility, innovation and public participation.

The concept builds on open access, open source and open government. Tim Berners-Lee proposed a five-star deployment scheme linking datasets with the Linked Open Data cloud using URIs and RDF. Government portals such as data.gov (United States, launched 2009) and data.gov.uk publish thousands of datasets; scientific examples include GenBank and open astronomy archives.

Principles

Data should be available in machine-readable, non-proprietary formats, with provenance, at reasonable cost (preferably free), and without discrimination. Tensions include privacy, national security, commercial sensitivity and data quality. FAIR principles (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable) complement openness in research data management.
