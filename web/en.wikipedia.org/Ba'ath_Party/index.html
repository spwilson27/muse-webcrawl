Arab Socialist Ba'ath Party
Founded: 7 April 1947, Damascus
Founders: Michel Aflaq, Salah al-Din al-Bitar, Zaki al-Arsuzi
Ideology: Ba'athism — Arab nationalism, pan-Arabism, Arab socialism
Motto: "Unity, Freedom, Socialism"

The Arab Socialist Ba'ath Party (Arabic: حزب البعث العربي الاشتراكي, "Party of the Arab Resurrection") is a political party founded in Syria in 1947 by Michel Aflaq, Salah al-Din al-Bitar, and associates of Zaki al-Arsuzi. The party espoused Ba'athism, an ideology combining Arab nationalism, pan-Arabism, and Arab socialism, summed up in its slogan "Unity, Freedom, Socialism."

History

The party grew rapidly across the Arab world in the 1950s. It came to power in Syria and Iraq through coups in 1963. In 1966 the party split into rival Syrian-dominated and Iraqi-dominated factions, which thereafter competed bitterly; the Iraqi branch was deposed by the 2003 invasion of Iraq, and the Syrian branch ruled Syria until the fall of the Assad regime in December 2024.

Ideology

Ba'athism held that the Arab nation is a single, eternal entity that should be united in one state, and that socialism was the necessary means to achieve an Arab renaissance (ba'ath). In practice, the ruling Ba'athist regimes became authoritarian single-party states.
