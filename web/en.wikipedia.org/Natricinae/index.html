The Natricinae are a subfamily of colubrid snakes, comprising more than 200 species in over 30 genera. They include familiar snakes such as the grass snake of Europe, the garter snakes and watersnakes of North America, and the keelbacks of Asia. Some classifications elevate the group to full family rank as Natricidae.

Characteristics

Natricines are mostly small to medium-sized, semi-aquatic or terrestrial snakes that feed heavily on amphibians, fish, and earthworms. Many have strongly keeled dorsal scales, giving species such as the Asian keelbacks (Rhabdophis) their common name. Unlike most colubrids, many natricines are live-bearing (viviparous), including garter snakes and grass snakes' close relatives.

Venom and defence

Most natricines are harmless to humans, but the subfamily includes notable exceptions. The tiger keelback (Rhabdophis tigrinus) is both venomous and poisonous: it sequesters toxins from the toads it eats in special nuchal glands on its neck. Garter snakes likewise sequester tetrodotoxin from newt prey. Defensive behaviours include flattening the body, musking, and playing dead, as famously performed by the grass snake (Natrix natrix).

Genera

Representative genera include Natrix (grass snakes), Thamnophis (garter snakes), Nerodia (North American watersnakes), Rhabdophis (keelbacks), Storeria (brown snakes), and Regina (crayfish snakes).
