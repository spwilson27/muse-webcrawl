Double auction

Double auction
Field: Economics, market design
Examples: Stock exchanges, electricity markets

A double auction is a market institution in which both buyers and sellers submit bids and offers, and transactions occur when bids and asks cross. It is the basic structure of organized exchanges: in a continuous double auction, such as a stock market's order book, trades execute whenever a bid meets or exceeds an ask; in a call (batch) auction, orders accumulate and clear periodically at a single price.

Properties

Experimental economics, beginning with Vernon Smith's studies in the 1960s, showed that double auctions converge rapidly to the competitive equilibrium price even with few traders and private information. Theoretical work includes the result of Myerson and Satterthwaite that no bilateral trading mechanism can be fully efficient under asymmetric information, motivating designs that sacrifice some efficiency for incentive compatibility and budget balance.

Applications

Double auctions run securities and futures exchanges, wholesale electricity markets, and many online platforms for ads and cloud computing resources.
