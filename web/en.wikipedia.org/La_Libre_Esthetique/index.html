La Libre Esthétique

La Libre Esthétique was an artistic society in Brussels, Belgium, founded in 1893 by lawyer and patron Octave Maus to promote avant-garde art, succeeding the dissolved group Les XX.

Exhibitions

Its annual salons, held from 1894 until the First World War interrupted them in 1914, showed Belgian and international artists including neo-impressionists, symbolists and early modernists, alongside decorative arts, concerts and lectures, reflecting Maus's ideal of uniting the arts.

Legacy

La Libre Esthétique helped make Brussels a centre of Art Nouveau and modern art, exhibiting figures such as Paul Signac, Théo van Rysselberghe and Auguste Rodin.
