Venus

Venus
Distance from Sun: 0.72 AU
Orbital period: 225 days
Diameter: 12,104 km
Surface temperature: ~465 °C
Atmosphere: 96.5% CO2, 92 atm surface pressure

Venus is the second planet from the Sun and the closest planet to Earth in size and mass, often called Earth's "sister planet." It orbits the Sun every 224.7 Earth days and rotates extremely slowly — and retrograde — once every 243 Earth days, the slowest rotation of any planet, so that its solar day is longer than its year.

Atmosphere and climate

Venus is shrouded in a dense atmosphere of carbon dioxide with clouds of sulfuric acid. A runaway greenhouse effect makes it the hottest planet in the Solar System, with surface temperatures around 465 °C, hot enough to melt lead, and surface pressure about 92 times that of Earth at sea level. Venus is the brightest planet in Earth's sky, appearing as the "morning star" or "evening star."

Surface

Radar mapping by the Magellan orbiter revealed a surface of volcanic plains, highlands such as Ishtar Terra and Aphrodite Terra, and relatively few impact craters, indicating a surface resurfaced by volcanism roughly 300–600 million years ago. The tallest mountain, Maxwell Montes, rises about 11 km above the mean surface level.

Exploration

Venus was the first planet visited by a spacecraft (Mariner 2, 1962). The Soviet Venera programme achieved the first successful landing on another planet and returned the first surface photographs. Later missions include Venus Express and Japan's Akatsuki orbiter. In 2020, a reported detection of phosphine in the clouds sparked debate about possible aerial life, though the finding remains contested.
