Joey Ramone (born Jeffrey Ross Hyman; 19 May 1951 – 15 April 2001) was an American singer, best known as the lead vocalist of the punk rock band the Ramones. His image — tall and lanky, with long dark hair, sunglasses and leather jacket — became an icon of punk culture.

Early life

Hyman was born in Forest Hills, Queens, New York. He grew up listening to rock and roll, especially girl groups, the Beatles, the Who and Iggy Pop. He played drums in the glam band Sniper before co-founding the Ramones in 1974 with John Cummings (Johnny Ramone) and Douglas Colvin (Dee Dee Ramone); originally the drummer, he switched to lead vocals when Dee Dee proved unable to sing and play bass simultaneously.

Ramones

The Ramones pioneered punk rock with short, fast, melodic songs drawing on 1960s pop, surf music and bubblegum. Joey co-wrote and sang many of the band's best-known songs, including "I Wanna Be Sedated", "Sheena Is a Punk Rocker", "Rock 'n' Roll High School" and "Blitzkrieg Bop" (the last mainly by Tommy and Dee Dee). His melodic sensibility balanced Johnny Ramone's strict discipline; the two held opposing political views and were estranged for years after Johnny married Joey's former girlfriend Linda, the subject of "The KKK Took My Baby Away". The band toured relentlessly until disbanding in 1996 after 2,263 concerts.

Illness, death and legacy

Joey was diagnosed with lymphoma in 1995 and died of the disease on 15 April 2001, aged 49, reportedly listening to U2's "In a Little While". His solo album Don't Worry About Me was released posthumously in 2002, and a second, ...Ya Know?, in 2012. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame with the Ramones in 2002. Joey Ramone Place in the East Village, near the former CBGB, was named in his honour in 2003.
