During World War II, Romania first fought as a member of the Axis under the dictatorship of Ion Antonescu, contributing more troops to the German invasion of the Soviet Union than any other German ally, and then, after King Michael's coup of 23 August 1944, switched sides and fought with the Allies for the remainder of the war.

Road to the Axis

In 1940 Romania lost territory to the Soviet Union (Bessarabia and Northern Bukovina), Hungary (Northern Transylvania) and Bulgaria (Southern Dobruja). King Carol II abdicated, and General Ion Antonescu took power, at first alongside the fascist Iron Guard, which he suppressed in January 1941. Seeking to recover the lost eastern territories and dependent on German protection, Romania joined the Tripartite Pact in November 1940, and German troops entered the country.

War against the USSR

Romania joined Operation Barbarossa on 22 June 1941, recovering Bessarabia and Northern Bukovina and occupying a zone between the Dniester and Southern Bug ("Transnistria"), including Odessa, taken after a costly siege. Romanian armies fought at Stalingrad, where the Romanian Third and Fourth Armies were destroyed in November 1942, suffering catastrophic losses. Under the Antonescu regime, Romanian authorities were responsible for the deaths of between 280,000 and 380,000 Jews in Bessarabia, Bukovina and Transnistria, and for the deportation and deaths of thousands of Roma.

Switching sides

With the Red Army advancing into Romania in the Jassy–Kishinev Offensive, King Michael arrested Antonescu on 23 August 1944 and declared war on Germany. Romanian forces then fought alongside the Soviets in Transylvania, Hungary and Czechoslovakia. The 1947 Paris Peace Treaty restored Northern Transylvania to Romania but confirmed the loss of Bessarabia and Northern Bukovina, and Romania fell within the Soviet sphere, becoming a communist state.
