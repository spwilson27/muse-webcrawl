Radical centrism

Ideology overview
IdeologyCentrism
Related termsRadical middle, Third Way

Radical centrism, also called the radical centre or radical middle, is a political ideology and movement that advocates fundamental, systemic reform drawing on ideas from across the political spectrum rather than splitting the difference between left and right. Radical centrists typically argue that deep problems require bold solutions, but ones arrived at pragmatically and empirically.

Ideas

Recurring themes include skepticism of ideological purity, support for market mechanisms tempered by strong public institutions, investment in education and science, electoral and governance reform, and a willingness to combine policies normally associated with opposing camps — for example, carbon pricing paired with deregulatory simplification, or universal basic services paired with deregulated housing supply.

Relation to the Third Way

Radical centrism resembles the Third Way associated with politicians such as Bill Clinton and Tony Blair, but its proponents distinguish it as more reformist and less managerial, seeking structural changes rather than triangulation between existing party positions.

Criticism

Critics from both left and right question whether radical centrism is a coherent doctrine or a branding exercise, and note that "bold moderation" can understate real conflicts of interest in politics.
