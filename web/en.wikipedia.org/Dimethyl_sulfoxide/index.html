Dimethyl sulfoxide

Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) is an organosulfur compound with the formula (CH3)2SO. This colorless liquid is an important polar aprotic solvent that dissolves both polar and nonpolar compounds and is miscible in a wide range of organic solvents and water.

Properties

DMSO has a relatively high boiling point (189 °C) and melting point (18.5 °C). It is produced industrially as a byproduct of the kraft process for making paper from wood pulp, by oxidation of dimethyl sulfide.

Applications

In the laboratory DMSO is a common solvent and a mild oxidant, used in reactions such as the Swern oxidation. In biology it serves as a cryoprotectant for cells and as a vehicle that carries dissolved substances through skin and membranes. Medically, it is approved in some countries for treating interstitial cystitis, and it is widely used — sometimes controversially — as a topical remedy.

DMSO is of low toxicity, though it readily penetrates skin and can carry contaminants with it, and contact often produces a garlic-like taste in the mouth.
