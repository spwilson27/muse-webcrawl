Singapore Airlines (abbreviation: SIA or SQ) is the flag carrier of the Republic of Singapore, with its hub located at Singapore Changi Airport. Widely regarded as one of the world's leading airlines, it is known for its service, its "Singapore Girl" cabin crew branding, and for pioneering the use of very large and ultra-long-range aircraft. The airline is majority-owned by the Singapore government investment company Temasek Holdings.

History

The airline traces its origins to Malayan Airways, founded in 1947, which became Malaysia–Singapore Airlines before splitting in 1972 into Singapore Airlines and Malaysian Airline System. SIA expanded rapidly with Boeing 747s and Concordes (operated jointly with British Airways), and became the launch customer of the Airbus A380, entering it into service in October 2007 on the Singapore–Sydney route. It also pioneered ultra-long-haul non-stop flights, including Singapore–New York, among the longest scheduled passenger flights in the world.

Fleet and services

The fleet is composed entirely of wide-body aircraft, including the Airbus A350, A380, and Boeing 777 and 787 families, with an average age kept low by a policy of frequent renewal. The airline offers suites, first, business, premium economy and economy classes, the KrisFlyer frequent-flyer programme, and the KrisWorld in-flight entertainment system. Its subsidiaries have included the low-cost carrier Scoot and a cargo operation, and it holds a stake in India's Vistara-successor arrangements within the Air India group following the 2024 merger.

Reputation

Singapore Airlines has repeatedly been named among the world's best airlines in industry rankings, including multiple Skytrax "Airline of the Year" awards.
