Hazaras

The Hazaras (Persian: هزاره, romanized: Hazāra; Hazaragi: آزره, romanized: Āzrə) are an ethnic group and a native and one of the main ethnic groups of Afghanistan. They are one of the largest ethnic groups in Afghanistan, primarily residing in the highlands of central Afghanistan, in the region known as Hazarajat. Hazaras speak the Hazaragi dialect of Persian (Dari), and the majority follow Shia Islam, predominantly Twelver Shi'ism — a religious distinction from the mainly Sunni population of Afghanistan that has shaped their history.

Origins

The Hazaras' ethnogenesis is debated. Genetic and historical evidence points to substantial ancestry from Mongol and Turkic soldiers who settled in the region after the Mongol invasions of the 13th century, mixed with the indigenous Iranian-speaking population. The name Hazara is commonly derived from the Persian hazār ("thousand"), recalling the Mongol military unit of a thousand soldiers. Their East and Central Asian features, Turkic and Mongolian loanwords in Hazaragi, and clan names support this mixed origin.

History

The Hazaras maintained semi-independent tribal chiefdoms in the mountains of Hazarajat for centuries. Under the Afghan emir Abdur Rahman Khan in the 1890s, a brutal campaign conquered Hazarajat, killing or enslaving a large share of the population — estimates often run to more than half — and confiscating lands, an event remembered as a genocide by Hazaras. Through the 20th century Hazaras were largely excluded from political power and higher education, and many worked as laborers in Kabul.

During the Afghan civil wars, Hazaras coalesced politically under the Hezb-e Wahdat party led by Abdul Ali Mazari, and suffered further atrocities, including massacres by the Taliban in Mazar-i-Sharif (1998) and Bamyan, where the Taliban also destroyed the Buddhas of Bamiyan in 2001, in Hazara-majority territory. Since 2001 Hazaras have achieved notable educational and political participation, but Hazara civilians and Shia mosques and schools have continued to be targeted in terrorist attacks by IS-K and others, both in Afghanistan under the restored Taliban regime and in the large diaspora in Pakistan (notably Quetta), Iran, and the West.

Culture

Hazara culture blends Persian, Central Asian and Mongol elements, expressed in music (the dambura lute), cuisine, embroidery, and poetry in Hazaragi. Hazaras have a reputation for valuing education, including for girls, and figure prominently in Afghan sport, academia and civil society. The region of Bamyan, with its lakes at Band-e Amir (Afghanistan's first national park), is the Hazara heartland.
