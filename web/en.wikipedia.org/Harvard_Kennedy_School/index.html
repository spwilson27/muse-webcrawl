The Harvard Kennedy School (HKS), officially the John F. Kennedy School of Government, is the school of public policy and government of Harvard University in Cambridge, Massachusetts. Founded in 1936 as the Harvard School of Public Administration, it was renamed in 1966 in memory of President John F. Kennedy. The school offers master's degrees in public policy, public administration, and public administration in international development, as well as doctoral programs and executive education.

History

The school was founded with a grant from Lucius N. Littauer, an alumnus and paper manufacturer, whose gift of $2 million funded both the school and the Littauer Center building. Instruction began in 1937 with a small class of Littauer Fellows. After Kennedy's assassination, Harvard's Institute of Politics was founded in 1966 as a living memorial, and the school took the Kennedy name; its present campus on John F. Kennedy Street, designed around an internal forum space, opened in 1978. Deans have included Don K. Price, Richard Neustadt, Graham Allison (who led the school from 1977 to 1989 during its major expansion), Joseph Nye, David Ellwood, Douglas Elmendorf, and (from 2024) Jeremy Weinstein.

Academics and research centers

The flagship degree is the two-year Master in Public Policy (MPP); the school also offers the Master in Public Administration (MPA), a mid-career Mason Program for professionals from developing and transitional countries, and PhD programs jointly with other Harvard faculties. Its research centers include the Belfer Center for Science and International Affairs, the Center for International Development, the Mossavar-Rahmani Center for Business and Government, the Taubman Center for State and Local Government, the Carr Center for Human Rights Policy, the Shorenstein Center on Media, Politics and Public Policy, and the Ash Center for Democratic Governance and Innovation. The Institute of Politics hosts forums and fellows and runs the Harvard Public Opinion Project.

People

Alumni include numerous heads of government and state, cabinet ministers, legislators, and leaders of international organizations across dozens of countries. The school's motto, adapted from Kennedy's inaugural address — "Ask what you can do" — is inscribed at its entrance.
