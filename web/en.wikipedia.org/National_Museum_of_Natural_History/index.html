National Museum of Natural History

Established: 1910

Location: National Mall, Washington, D.C., United States

Part of: Smithsonian Institution

Collection: Over 145 million specimens and artefacts

The National Museum of Natural History (NMNH) is a natural history museum administered by the Smithsonian Institution, located on the National Mall in Washington, D.C., United States. It opened in 1910 and is one of the world's pre-eminent natural history museums, maintaining a collection of more than 145 million specimens of plants, animals, fossils, minerals, rocks, meteorites, human remains and cultural artefacts — the largest such collection in the world. Admission is free.

Collections and exhibits

The museum's rotunda is dominated by a mounted African elephant. Famous exhibits include the Hope Diamond in the Janet Annenberg Hooker Hall of Geology, Gems and Minerals; the Hall of Fossils (Deep Time), opened in 2019, featuring dinosaur skeletons including Tyrannosaurus rex; the Sant Ocean Hall; the Hall of Human Origins; and the O. Orkin Insect Zoo. The museum also holds the Neanderthal from Shanidar and extensive anthropological collections.

Research

Approximately 185 Smithsonian scientists work at the museum, the largest group of scientists dedicated to the study of natural and cultural history in the world. Research divisions cover anthropology, botany, entomology, invertebrate zoology, mineral sciences, paleobiology and vertebrate zoology. The museum supports global biodiversity research, including the Smithsonian's Museum Support Center in Maryland for collection storage.
