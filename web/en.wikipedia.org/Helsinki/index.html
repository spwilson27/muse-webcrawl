Helsinki's South Harbour and cathedral

Helsinki (Swedish: Helsingfors) is the capital and most populous city of Finland. Located on the shore of the Gulf of Finland, it is the seat of the Uusimaa region and, together with the neighbouring cities of Espoo, Vantaa and Kauniainen, forms the Helsinki metropolitan area, home to roughly a third of Finland's population.

History

Helsinki was founded in 1550 by King Gustav I of Sweden as a trading post to rival Tallinn across the gulf. After Finland passed to the Russian Empire in 1809, Grand Duke Alexander I moved the capital from Turku to Helsinki in 1812, and the city was rebuilt in a monumental neoclassical style designed largely by Carl Ludvig Engel, whose Senate Square and Helsinki Cathedral remain landmarks. Helsinki has been Finland's capital since independence in 1917.

Geography and climate

The city spreads across a peninsula and an archipelago of some 315 islands. It has a humid continental climate with long, dark winters and mild summers with nearly 19 hours of daylight around the solstice. The fortress of Suomenlinna, built across six islands in the 18th century, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Culture and economy

Helsinki hosted the 1952 Summer Olympics and was World Design Capital in 2012. It is home to the University of Helsinki, Aalto University, the Finnish National Opera, and a large technology and start-up sector; the city's port is among the busiest passenger ports in Europe, with ferry links to Stockholm and Tallinn.
