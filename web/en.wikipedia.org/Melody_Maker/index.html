Melody Maker was a British weekly music magazine, one of the world's earliest music weeklies, founded in 1926 initially as a magazine for dance-band musicians. It became one of the best-known British "inkies" alongside its long-time rival NME (New Musical Express), chronicling jazz, then rock and pop, progressive rock, punk's aftermath, dance music and Britpop over the course of the 20th century.

Originally focused on jazz and dance music, Melody Maker pivoted towards rock in the 1960s under writers such as Chris Welch and Richard Williams, championing progressive and album-oriented rock while the NME leaned towards pop and later punk. In the 1990s it covered indie and Britpop, but sales declined and in December 2000 IPC Media merged it into NME; the title effectively ceased independent publication, though aspects of its brand continued online.
