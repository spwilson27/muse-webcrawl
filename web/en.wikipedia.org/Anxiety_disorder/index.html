Anxiety disorders are a group of mental disorders characterised by significant and uncontrollable feelings of anxiety and fear such that a person's social, occupational and personal functioning are significantly impaired. Anxiety may cause physical and cognitive symptoms, such as restlessness, irritability, easy fatigability, difficulty concentrating, increased heart rate, chest pain, abdominal pain, and many others. Anxiety disorders are the most common of mental disorders and affect roughly 30% of adults at some point in their lives.

Types

Recognised anxiety disorders include generalised anxiety disorder (GAD), characterised by excessive, persistent worry about everyday matters; panic disorder, involving recurrent unexpected panic attacks; specific phobias, intense fears of particular objects or situations; social anxiety disorder, fear centred on social scrutiny; agoraphobia; and separation anxiety disorder. Under the DSM-5, obsessive–compulsive disorder and post-traumatic stress disorder — previously classed with anxiety disorders — are placed in their own categories but remain closely related.

Causes

The causes of anxiety disorders are thought to involve a combination of genetic and environmental factors. Anxiety disorders often run in families, suggesting a heritable component. Environmental contributors include childhood adversity, chronic stress, trauma, and the use of substances such as caffeine, alcohol and certain drugs, which can provoke or worsen anxiety. Medical conditions such as thyroid disorders can also produce anxiety symptoms.

Mechanisms

Anxiety disorders are associated with dysregulation of brain systems involved in threat detection, including the amygdala, and with imbalances in neurotransmitters such as serotonin, norepinephrine and gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA). The body's stress-response systems, including the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis, are also implicated.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is based on clinical interview and standardised criteria, after ruling out medical causes. Treatment commonly involves psychotherapy — especially cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT), which has strong evidence of effectiveness — and medication, including selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) and serotonin–norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors (SNRIs). Benzodiazepines may be used briefly in some cases but carry risks of dependence. Exercise, sleep hygiene and stress management are often recommended as adjuncts.

Epidemiology

Anxiety disorders are among the most prevalent mental health conditions worldwide, affecting an estimated 4% of the global population at any given time. They are roughly twice as common in women as in men and typically begin in childhood, adolescence or early adulthood. They frequently co-occur with depressive disorders and substance use disorders.
