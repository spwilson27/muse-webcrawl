N scale

Ratio1:148 (UK) to 1:160
Gauge9 mm (0.354 in)
Prototype gaugeStandard gauge, 1,435 mm
Introduced1960s (Arnold Rapido)

N scale is a popular model railway scale. Depending on the manufacturer and country, the scale ranges from 1:148 to 1:160, but in practice it is most commonly 1:160 with 9 mm track gauge representing standard gauge. The name derives from the gauge: Nine millimetres. N scale is the second most popular model railway scale worldwide, after HO scale.

History

Although trains and accessories of similar size existed earlier, modern N scale was launched by the German firm Arnold with its "Rapido" line in the early 1960s. Japanese manufacturers such as Kato and Tomix adopted the scale widely, modelling Japanese trains at 1:150 on the same 9 mm gauge.

Advantages

The small size allows modelers to fit long mainline runs, extensive yards, and large scenery in modest spaces, and to model long trains realistically. Modern N scale locomotives run smoothly at prototypically slow speeds, and digital command control (DCC) decoders are widely available for the scale.

Standards

Standards are maintained by the NMRA in North America and by NEM norms in Europe. British N gauge uses a 1:148 ratio because early mechanisms could not be made small enough for true 1:160 models of Britain's smaller loading gauge.
