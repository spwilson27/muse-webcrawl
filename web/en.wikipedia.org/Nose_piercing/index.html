Nose piercing

LocationNostril, septum, or bridge of the nose
JewelleryStuds, rings, captive bead rings, barbells
Healing time~2–6 months (nostril); longer for cartilage

Nose piercing is the piercing of the skin or cartilage of the nose, usually for wearing jewellery. Common placements are the nostril, the nasal septum, and the bridge. Nose piercing has a history stretching back thousands of years and appears in many cultures; it is mentioned in Middle Eastern traditions recorded in the Bible and has long been practised in South Asia, where nostril piercing is associated with marriage and Ayurvedic tradition.

Types

The nostril piercing is the most common, typically worn with a stud or small ring. The septum piercing passes through the thin strip of tissue at the front of the nasal septum (the "sweet spot") rather than the cartilage, and is often worn with a circular barbell. The bridge piercing passes through the skin at the top of the nose between the eyes. Other variants include high nostril, nasallang, and Austin bar piercings.

History and culture

Nose rings appear in ancient Middle Eastern and Indian contexts; in India, brides traditionally wear a nath, sometimes connected by a chain to the ear. In the West, nose piercing became associated with punk and alternative subcultures in the 1970s and 1980s before entering mainstream fashion in the 1990s and 2000s. Healing requires regular cleaning, and risks include infection, irritation bumps, and migration or rejection of jewellery.
