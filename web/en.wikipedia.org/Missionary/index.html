Missionary

Definition: Member of a religious group sent to promote its faith

A missionary is a member of a religious group who is sent into an area in order to promote its faith or provide services to people, such as education, literacy, social justice, health care, and economic development. The word "mission" originates with the Latin missio, the sending of Jesuits by Ignatius of Loyola in the 16th century, though missionary activity is far older.

By religion

Christian missions range from the apostle Paul and medieval friars to Catholic orders and Protestant societies that sent figures such as Robert Morrison to China, John Williams to the Pacific, and James Hudson Taylor inland China. Islam spread through trade and Sufi missionaries (da'wa); Buddhism sent emissaries under Ashoka and along the Silk Road; Hindu, Sikh, Baháʼí and other traditions also conduct missions.

Criticism and legacy

Missions built schools, hospitals, printing presses and first written forms of many languages, but were also entangled with colonialism, and their suppression of indigenous cultures is now widely criticised. Modern missions emphasise partnership, development and inculturation.
