Holographic principle

The holographic principle is a property of string theories and a supposed feature of quantum gravity stating that the description of a volume of space can be thought of as encoded on a lower-dimensional boundary to the region—such as a light-like boundary like a gravitational horizon. The principle was first proposed by Gerard 't Hooft in 1993 and given a precise string-theoretic interpretation by Leonard Susskind.

Motivation

The principle is inspired by black hole thermodynamics: the entropy of a black hole, given by the Bekenstein–Hawking formula, is proportional to the area of its event horizon rather than to its volume. This suggests that the maximum information content of any region scales with its surface area.

Realizations

The best-known concrete realization is the AdS/CFT correspondence, in which a gravitational theory in anti-de Sitter space is equivalent to a conformal field theory living on its boundary. Research in quantum information has connected holography to quantum error-correcting codes and entanglement geometry.
