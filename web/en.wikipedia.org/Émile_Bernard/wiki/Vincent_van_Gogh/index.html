Vincent van Gogh

Born30 March 1853, Zundert, Netherlands
Died29 July 1890, Auvers-sur-Oise, France
MovementPost-Impressionism
WorksThe Starry Night, Sunflowers, The Potato Eaters

Vincent Willem van Gogh (30 March 1853 – 29 July 1890) was a Dutch Post-Impressionist painter who is among the most famous and influential figures in the history of Western art. In just over a decade he created roughly 2,100 artworks, including about 860 oil paintings, most in the last two years of his life. His work is notable for its bold colours and dramatic, expressive brushwork.

Life

Born into an upper-middle-class family, van Gogh worked as an art dealer, teacher, and lay preacher before turning to art in 1880. His early Dutch work, such as The Potato Eaters (1885), used a dark palette. In Paris from 1886 he absorbed Impressionist and Japanese influences and his colour brightened; he formed friendships with artists including Émile Bernard and Paul Gauguin.

Moving to Arles in 1888, he painted the Sunflowers series and hoped to found an artists' colony; his stay with Gauguin ended in crisis and the notorious ear incident. Suffering recurrent psychotic episodes, he spent a year at the Saint-Paul asylum in Saint-Rémy, where he painted The Starry Night. He died aged 37 from a gunshot wound, generally accepted as self-inflicted, at Auvers-sur-Oise.

Legacy

Largely unrecognised in life and supported financially by his brother Theo — whose widow Johanna preserved his letters and paintings — van Gogh's reputation soared in the 20th century. His letters are a major literary source on modern art, and his works rank among the most expensive paintings ever sold. The Van Gogh Museum in Amsterdam holds the largest collection.
