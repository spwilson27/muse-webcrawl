Aerosol paint

Aerosol paint (commonly called spray paint) is paint that comes in a sealed, pressurized container and is released in an aerosol spray when a valve button is depressed. Aerosol painting is one form of spray painting; it leaves a smooth, even coat, unlike many traditional rolled and brushed paints. Standard-sized cans are lightweight, portable, inexpensive, and easy to store. Aerosol primer can be applied directly to bare metal and many plastics.

History

Edward Seymour, of Sycamore, Illinois, added paint to existing spray can technology at his wife Bonnie's suggestion in 1949. It was initially designed to demonstrate an aluminum paint he developed. His patent was granted in 1951. Most aerosol paints sold in the United States are made in accordance with the American Coatings Association's standards.

Applications

Aerosol paint is used commercially by sign painters and for painting machinery, vehicles, and buildings; by hobbyists for model making; and in street art and graffiti. Large areas can be covered quickly and evenly because the spray applies thin coats that dry fast.

Graffiti and street art

Graffiti artists prize aerosol paint for its speed, portability, and range of colors. Specialized caps produce lines of varying widths. Artists such as spray paint artists have developed elaborate techniques using stencils and layering. Because it is often used without permission on public property, possession of spray paint is regulated in some jurisdictions.

Health and environment

The propellants and solvents in aerosol paint release volatile organic compounds. Historically chlorofluorocarbons were used as propellants before being phased out under the Montreal Protocol; modern cans use hydrocarbons such as propane and butane.
