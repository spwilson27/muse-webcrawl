Murad I (Ottoman Turkish: مراد اول; Turkish: I. Murad, nicknamed Hüdavendigâr, "the God-devoted"; 29 June 1326 – 15 June 1389) was the third sultan of the Ottoman Empire, ruling from 1362 until his death. The son of Orhan, he transformed the Ottoman realm from a frontier principality into a transcontinental empire, conquering much of southeastern Europe.

Reign and conquests

Under Murad, Ottoman forces captured Adrianople (Edirne), which became the empire's European capital, and won decisive victories over Balkan coalitions at the Maritsa River (1371), making Serbia and Bulgaria his vassals. In Anatolia he annexed neighbouring Turkish beyliks through purchase, marriage alliances, and force. Murad organised the Janissary corps as a standing infantry recruited through the devshirme levy of Christian boys, and systematised the timar land-grant system that financed the Ottoman cavalry. He was the first Ottoman ruler to use the title of Sultan prominently.

Death at Kosovo

Murad was killed at the Battle of Kosovo on 15 June 1389, where Ottoman forces defeated a coalition led by the Serbian prince Lazar. According to tradition, a Serbian knight — in later legend named Miloš Obilić — gained access to the sultan's tent by feigning desertion and assassinated him, either during or after the battle. Murad's internal organs were buried at the field in Kosovo, where his tomb (türbe) still stands, while his body was interred in Bursa. His son Bayezid I succeeded him on the battlefield.
