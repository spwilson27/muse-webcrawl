Liber Linteus

The Liber Linteus Zagrabiensis (Latin for "Linen Book of Zagreb"), also known as the Liber Agramensis, is the longest known Etruscan text and the only surviving linen book. Most of it remains untranslated because of the limited knowledge of the Etruscan language, though enough is understood to show it is a ritual calendar.

History

The text was written around the 3rd century BC, probably near Perugia. The linen was later cut into strips and used as wrapping for a mummy in Ptolemaic Egypt. The mummy and manuscript were bought in Alexandria in 1848 by Mihajlo Barić and eventually came to the Archaeological Museum in Zagreb, Croatia.

Content

The surviving text contains some 1,200 words arranged in columns, prescribing offerings and ceremonies on particular dates for various gods. It is a primary source for Etruscan religion and language.
