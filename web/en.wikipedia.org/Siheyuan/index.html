Siheyuan

A siheyuan (Chinese: 四合院) is a traditional Chinese courtyard residence in which buildings are arranged on four sides around a central courtyard. Most characteristic of Beijing, siheyuan housed extended families for centuries and embody principles of Confucian hierarchy, privacy, and feng shui.

Layout

The compound is entered through a gate in the southeast corner. An outer courtyard with guest and service rooms leads through a decorated inner gate to the main courtyard, around which stand the principal hall on the north side (reserved for the family head), the eastern and western wing rooms for younger generations, and a southern building facing the main hall.

History

Courtyard houses date back over two thousand years, and the Beijing siheyuan took its mature form under the Ming and Qing dynasties. After 1949 many were subdivided among multiple families as dazayuan. Rapid redevelopment has demolished many, though notable examples, such as the former residences of Soong Ching-ling and Guo Moruo, are preserved as museums.
