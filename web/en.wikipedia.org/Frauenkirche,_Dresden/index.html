The Frauenkirche (Church of Our Lady) in Dresden is a Lutheran church and one of the city's most famous landmarks, noted for its great stone bell-shaped dome, the "Steinerne Glocke". Destroyed in the February 1945 bombing of Dresden, it stood as a ruin and war memorial for nearly fifty years before being rebuilt after German reunification.

Original church

Master carpenter George Bähr built the church between 1726 and 1743 for Dresden's Lutheran citizens, a striking assertion in Catholic-ruled Saxony. Its dome, 91 metres high overall including the lantern, rests on eight slender piers — an engineering tour de force in sandstone.

Destruction and reconstruction

The church survived the firestorm of 13–14 February 1945 but collapsed two days later from the heat. The East German authorities preserved the ruin as a memorial. After reunification, a citizens' initiative drove reconstruction (1994–2005), reusing thousands of catalogued original stones — still recognisably dark among the new sandstone. The new orb and cross atop the dome were made by a British silversmith, Alan Smith, whose father had flown in the raid. The church was reconsecrated on 30 October 2005 and is a symbol of reconciliation.
