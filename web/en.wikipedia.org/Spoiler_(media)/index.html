Spoiler (media)

A spoiler is an element of a disseminated summary or description of a media narrative that reveals significant plot elements, usually the climax or ending, presented in a way that may diminish the dramatic tension or surprise of experiencing the work for the first time.

Usage

The term derives from the verb "to spoil". Websites, magazines and discussion forums commonly preface such material with a "spoiler warning" or use spoiler tags, collapsible text or blurred passages so readers can choose whether to expose themselves to the revelation. The term was popularised in the early 1980s on the Usenet newsgroup for film discussion, and columnist Doug Kenney used "Spoiler" as the title of material in National Lampoon magazine.

Research

Psychological studies on spoilers have produced mixed findings; a widely cited 2011 study by Leavitt and Christenfeld suggested spoilers may not spoil — and can even enhance — enjoyment of stories, although subsequent research has questioned the generality of that result.
