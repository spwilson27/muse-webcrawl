German folklore

German folklore is the body of traditional stories, legends, customs and beliefs originating in the German-speaking regions of Europe. It draws on pre-Christian Germanic mythology, medieval Christian influence, and centuries of regional storytelling, and it was systematically collected in the 19th century as part of the Romantic interest in the folk culture of nations.

Collections

The most famous collectors were Jacob and Wilhelm Grimm, whose Children's and Household Tales (1812–1857) — including "Cinderella", "Snow White", "Hansel and Gretel", "Rumpelstiltskin" and "Rapunzel" — became classics of world literature. The Grimms also collected German legends and worked on German mythology and grammar, while Ludwig Bechstein and others published further regional tale collections.

Figures and beings

German folklore is populated by dwarfs and kobolds, the mischievous Till Eulenspiegel, water spirits such as the Lorelei of the Rhine, wild hunters, werewolves, and the witch-haunted Brocken mountain in the Harz. Legends attach to historical figures too: the Pied Piper of Hamelin, Doctor Faustus, the sleeping emperor Frederick Barbarossa in the Kyffhäuser mountain, and the rat-catcher and town sagas of the medieval period.

Customs

Folkloric traditions survive in seasonal customs such as Karneval (carnival), Walpurgis Night, Christmas markets and the figure of Knecht Ruprecht, Krampus in the Alpine south, and numerous local festivals, costume traditions and folk dances.
