Acute accent

The acute accent ( ´ ) is a diacritic used in many modern written languages with alphabets based on the Latin, Cyrillic, and Greek scripts. It is most commonly used in languages such as Spanish, French, Hungarian, Irish, Icelandic, Portuguese, Polish, and Vietnamese, among many others, where it can indicate stress, vowel length, vowel quality, or tone.

Uses

In Spanish, the acute marks word stress where it deviates from default rules, as in café or médico. In Hungarian it marks long vowels (á, é, í, ó, ő, ú, ű). In French it appears on e (é) to denote the close-mid vowel /e/. In Vietnamese it denotes the rising (sắc) tone. In transliteration of Greek, it originally marked the high pitch accent.

History

The acute accent derives from the apex used in Latin inscriptions and from the Greek oxeîa (ὀξεῖα) accent mark introduced by Hellenistic grammarians. Its name comes from Latin accentus acutus, "sharp accent".

Typography

In Unicode, precomposed characters exist for common letter–accent combinations, and a combining acute accent (U+0301) can follow any base letter. On many keyboards it is typed with a dead key.
