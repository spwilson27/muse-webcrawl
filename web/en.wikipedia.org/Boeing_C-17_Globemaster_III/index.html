Boeing C-17 Globemaster III

The Boeing C-17 Globemaster III is a large military transport aircraft that was developed for the United States Air Force (USAF) from the 1980s to the early 1990s by McDonnell Douglas, which merged with Boeing in 1997. The C-17 carries the name of two previous piston-engined military cargo aircraft, the Douglas C-74 Globemaster and the Douglas C-124 Globemaster II.

Design

The C-17 is powered by four Pratt & Whitney F117-PW-100 turbofan engines, a military version of the PW2000. Its externally blown flaps use engine exhaust to augment lift, allowing steep approaches and operations from austere runways as short as about 1,064 m (3,500 ft). It can carry up to 77.5 tonnes (170,900 lb) of cargo, including an M1 Abrams main battle tank, and its cargo hold is accessible via a large rear ramp for drive-on/drive-off loading and airdrop.

Operational history

The type first flew in September 1991 and entered service in 1995. It has been the USAF's premier strategic-tactical airlifter, supporting operations in Iraq, Afghanistan, and numerous humanitarian missions, including the August 2021 Kabul airlift. Export operators include the United Kingdom, Australia, Canada, India, Qatar, the United Arab Emirates, Kuwait, and the NATO Strategic Airlift Capability.

Production

Production at Long Beach, California ended in 2015 after 279 aircraft were built. The C-17 remains in widespread service and is expected to fly for decades to come.
