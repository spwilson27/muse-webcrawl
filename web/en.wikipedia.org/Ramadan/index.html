Ramadan (Arabic: رَمَضَان) is the ninth month of the Islamic lunar calendar and the holy month of fasting for Muslims. During Ramadan, adult Muslims fast from dawn to sunset — abstaining from food, drink and marital relations — as an act of worship commemorating the first revelation of the Qur'an to Muhammad. Fasting in Ramadan (sawm) is one of the Five Pillars of Islam.

Observance

The fast begins at dawn after the pre-dawn meal (suhoor) and ends at sunset with the evening meal (iftar), often shared communally. Children, the elderly, travellers, the ill, and pregnant or menstruating women are exempt, with missed fasts made up later or compensated by feeding the poor. The month emphasises prayer — including the nightly tarawih prayers — almsgiving (zakat and voluntary sadaqah), self-discipline and reflection. The last ten nights include Laylat al-Qadr, the Night of Decree, believed to be when the Qur'an's first verses were revealed and worth more than a thousand months of worship.

End of Ramadan

Because the Islamic calendar is lunar, Ramadan moves about 11 days earlier each Gregorian year, cycling through all seasons over roughly 33 years. The month ends with the sighting of the new crescent and the festival of Eid al-Fitr, marked by communal prayer, feasting and gifts of charity to the poor.
