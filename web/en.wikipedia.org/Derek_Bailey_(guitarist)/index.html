Derek Bailey (guitarist)

Derek Bailey (29 January 1930 – 25 December 2005) was an English avant-garde guitarist and a leading figure in the free improvisation movement. Working with a distinctive, radically unconventional technique, he treated the guitar as a source of sound to be explored in the moment rather than as an instrument for playing pre-composed parts.

Bailey in performance, exploring the guitar's full sonic range

Career

Born in Sheffield, Bailey began as a professional dance-band and session guitarist before turning to free jazz in the 1960s. In 1966 he joined drummer Tony Oxley and bassist Gavin Bryars in the Joseph Holbrooke Trio, then formed the Spontaneous Music Ensemble with John Stevens. In 1970 he co-founded Incus Records with Oxley and saxophonist Evan Parker, one of the first musician-run independent labels for improvised music.

Company and writings

From 1976 Bailey organized Company, a shifting ensemble and annual "Company Week" festival that paired improvisers from wildly different backgrounds — jazz musicians, classical players, rock musicians such as Bill Laswell, and even dancer Min Tanaka. His book Improvisation: Its Nature and Practice in Music (1980), later adapted for television, remains a standard text on the subject, distinguishing "idiomatic" from "non-idiomatic" improvisation — the latter being Bailey's own territory.

Style and legacy

Bailey's playing avoided melody, harmony, and regular rhythm in favour of harmonics, clusters, percussive attacks, volume-pedal swells, and prepared-guitar sounds. Though forbidding to some listeners, he influenced generations of improvisers and experimental guitarists worldwide. Diagnosed with motor neurone disease, he continued performing until near his death in London on Christmas Day 2005.
