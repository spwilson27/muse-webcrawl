Bribery

Bribery is the practice of offering, giving, receiving or soliciting something of value as a means of influencing the actions of an official or other person in a position of trust, in breach of their duty. It is a form of corruption, criminalised in virtually all jurisdictions, and conceptually distinguished from legitimate gifts or payments by the quid pro quo — the intent to induce improper performance of a function. Bribes may be money, gifts, favours, contracts, information or entertainment, and may be offered actively by the giver or solicited by the recipient; in most legal systems both sides commit an offence.

Legal treatment

Classical bribery targeted public officials, but modern statutes also cover commercial bribery between private parties. In the United States, 18 U.S.C. § 201 criminalises bribing federal officials, while the Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (1977) extends liability to bribes paid to foreign officials by companies with US connections. The United Kingdom's Bribery Act 2010 is among the broadest: it criminalises active and passive bribery, bribery of foreign officials, and creates a strict-liability corporate offence of failing to prevent bribery, subject to an "adequate procedures" defence. Internationally, the OECD Anti-Bribery Convention (1997) and the UN Convention against Corruption (2003) oblige signatories to criminalise transnational bribery.

Forms and settings

Bribery ranges from petty payments to petty officials (sometimes called facilitation or "grease" payments — permitted in narrow form under the FCPA but not under the UK Act) to grand corruption involving heads of state and billions in procurement contracts. Common settings include public procurement, customs, licensing, law enforcement, healthcare prescribing, construction permits and sport governance; the 2015 FIFA scandal, in which officials took bribes and kickbacks over media and hosting rights, is a prominent example. Economics research treats bribery as a tax-like distortion that misallocates resources toward the well-connected, discourages investment and erodes trust in institutions; Transparency International's Corruption Perceptions Index ranks countries by perceived public-sector corruption each year.

Defenses and boundaries

Boundaries with lawful conduct — campaign contributions, lobbying, hospitality, gifts within cultural norms — are set by intent and disclosure rules. Compliance programmes, whistleblower protections (such as the US Dodd-Frank bounty scheme), deferred prosecution agreements and debarment from public contracts are common enforcement tools. Notably, a payment made under extortion or duress may not be treated as bribery of the payer in some jurisdictions, since a defining feature of bribery is voluntary corrupt intent.
