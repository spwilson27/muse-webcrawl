Drop goal

A drop goal, also called a dropped goal or field goal in some codes, is a method of scoring points in rugby league and rugby union. A drop goal is scored when a player drop-kicks the ball through the opposing team's goal posts during open play — that is, kicks the ball after it bounces, rather than from a tee or from the hands.

Value

In rugby union a drop goal is worth three points. In rugby league it is generally worth one point, with a two-point value for drop goals kicked from beyond the 40-metre line under rules adopted in some competitions. The drop goal's value has changed over the history of both codes; it was once worth four points in rugby union before being reduced in 1948.

Famous drop goals

Perhaps the most famous drop goal in rugby history is Jonny Wilkinson's extra-time kick that won the 2003 Rugby World Cup final for England against Australia. In the 1995 World Cup semi-final, Joel Stransky's drop goal in extra time took South Africa past France on the way to their title. Drop goals are especially prized in close matches as a way to break deadlocks when defences prevent tries.

Related scores

The drop goal should be distinguished from the penalty goal and conversion, which are place-kicked, and from the goal from mark, which was abolished in the 20th century. In Australian rules football and Gaelic football, kicking a ball dropped from the hands is a normal technique rather than a distinct scoring play.
