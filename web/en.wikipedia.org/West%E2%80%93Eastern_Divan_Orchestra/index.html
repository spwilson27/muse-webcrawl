The West–Eastern Divan Orchestra is an orchestra of young musicians from Israel, Palestine and other Arab countries, founded in 1999 by conductor Daniel Barenboim and scholar Edward Said. It was created to promote coexistence and dialogue through music, taking its name from Goethe's poetry collection West–Eastern Divan, a work of mutual East–West understanding.

Activities

The orchestra meets each summer for rehearsals and discussions, traditionally in Seville, Spain, followed by an international concert tour. It has performed at major venues and festivals including the BBC Proms, the Salzburg Festival and Carnegie Hall, and in 2005 performed in Ramallah in the West Bank. Its members have also performed in both Israel and the Palestinian territories in related chamber projects.

Barenboim–Said Academy

The project led to the founding of the Barenboim–Said Academy in Berlin, opened in 2016, which trains young musicians from the Middle East and North Africa in music and the humanities. The orchestra's work has been documented in the film Knowledge Is the Beginning.

Reception

The orchestra has received awards for its contribution to intercultural understanding, and Barenboim has described it as a space where Israelis and Arabs could work together as equals, while acknowledging it cannot by itself bring peace.
