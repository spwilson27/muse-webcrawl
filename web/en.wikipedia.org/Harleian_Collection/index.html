Harleian Collection

The Harleian Collection is one of the main collections of manuscripts in the British Library in London. It was assembled by Robert Harley, 1st Earl of Oxford (1661–1724), and his son Edward Harley, 2nd Earl of Oxford (1689–1741), two of the greatest bibliophiles of eighteenth-century Britain.

Contents

The collection comprises more than 7,000 manuscripts, over 14,000 original legal documents, and some 500 rolls, ranging from early medieval illuminated manuscripts and Anglo-Saxon texts to state papers, heraldic and genealogical records, and the papers of antiquaries. Highlights include the Harley Psalter, richly illustrated medieval bestiaries and romances, and manuscripts collected from dissolved monasteries after the Reformation.

Acquisition by the nation

In 1753 the manuscripts were purchased from the Countess of Oxford and her daughter, the Duchess of Portland, for £10,000 under the British Museum Act, becoming one of the foundation collections of the British Museum, alongside the Sloane and Cotton collections. They passed to the British Library on its creation in 1973. The Harleys' printed books, by contrast, were sold to the bookseller Thomas Osborne and dispersed. The manuscripts are catalogued in the Catalogue of the Harleian Manuscripts and are identified by the shelfmark prefix "Harley".
