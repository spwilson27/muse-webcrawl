Nihon Hidankyo, formally the Japan Confederation of A- and H-Bomb Sufferers Organizations (Japanese: 日本原水爆被害者団体協議会), is a Japanese organisation formed by hibakusha — survivors of the 1945 atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki. Founded in 1956, it is the only nationwide organisation of atomic bomb survivors in Japan. Its twin aims are the prevention of nuclear war and the abolition of nuclear weapons, and improved state compensation and welfare for survivors. In 2024 Nihon Hidankyo was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize for its efforts to achieve a world free of nuclear weapons and for demonstrating through witness testimony that nuclear weapons must never be used again.

History

Nihon Hidankyo was established on August 10, 1956, in Nagasaki during the second World Conference against Atomic and Hydrogen Bombs, amid public outrage following the irradiation of the fishing vessel Lucky Dragon No. 5 by the 1954 Castle Bravo test at Bikini Atoll. Prefectural hibakusha associations across Japan affiliated into the confederation.

Activities

The organisation campaigns for state compensation on the grounds that the Japanese state, which waged the war, bears responsibility for survivors' suffering, and it presses for the signing and ratification of the Treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons. Its members give testimony around the world, send delegations to the United Nations and NPT review conferences, and support survivors of nuclear tests globally, including in the Marshall Islands, Kazakhstan, and elsewhere.

Nobel Peace Prize

On October 11, 2024, the Norwegian Nobel Committee announced that Nihon Hidankyo would receive the Nobel Peace Prize, praising the survivors' decades of testimony as a reminder of the catastrophic human cost of nuclear weapons and as a foundation of the "nuclear taboo." The award was presented in Oslo on December 10, 2024.
