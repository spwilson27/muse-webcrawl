Hohenzollern Castle

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Hohenzollern Castle, ancestral seat of the House of Hohenzollern, on Mount Hohenzollern.

Hohenzollern Castle (German: Burg Hohenzollern) is the ancestral seat of the imperial House of Hohenzollern. The third of three hilltop castles built on the site, it stands atop Mount Hohenzollern (855 m), near Hechingen in the Swabian Alps of Baden-Württemberg, Germany, about 50 kilometres south of Stuttgart.

History

The first castle on the mountain was built in the early eleventh century and was completely destroyed in 1423 after a ten-month siege by the imperial cities of Swabia. A second, larger castle was built from 1454 to 1461, serving as a refuge for the Swabian branch of the family, but it fell into ruin after the Thirty Years' War and was largely demolished by the early nineteenth century.

The present castle was built by Crown Prince Frederick William of Prussia, later King Frederick William IV, between 1850 and 1867 in the English Gothic Revival style, to designs associated with Friedrich August Stüler, as a family memorial rather than a working residence. It is considered a masterpiece of nineteenth-century military and romantic architecture in Germany.

The castle today

Still owned jointly by the Prussian and Swabian lines of the Hohenzollern family, the castle is open to the public and receives over 350,000 visitors a year. Its treasury displays items of Prussian royal regalia, including the Crown of Wilhelm II, personal effects of Frederick the Great, and the famous letter of George Washington thanking Baron von Steuben. The castle hosts concerts, exhibitions, a Christmas market and open-air cinema in summer.
