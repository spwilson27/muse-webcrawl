Hack and slash

Hack and slash, also known as hack and slay (H&S or HnS) or slash 'em up, is a term that refers to a type of gameplay that emphasises close-quarters melee combat, typically with bladed weapons. Originally used in tabletop role-playing games to describe a style of play focused on combat over storytelling, it is now chiefly associated with a subgenre of action video games in which the player fights large numbers of enemies with swordplay and combo-based attacks.

Origins in role-playing games

The term arose in the role-playing game community of the late 1970s and 1980s — it is often traced to Dungeons & Dragons circles — as a (sometimes pejorative) label for campaigns dominated by dungeon combat and monster-killing rather than plot or character development. It later described action-oriented computer RPGs, most famously Blizzard's Diablo series, where "hack and slash" came to denote loot-driven isometric dungeon crawling.

Video game genre

As a character-action genre, hack and slash games feature fast, stylised melee combat, crowd control against many opponents, juggling and combo systems, and stylish ranking of performance. Defining examples include Capcom's Devil May Cry series (2001–), Team Ninja's Ninja Gaiden, Sony's God of War, and PlatinumGames' Bayonetta, as well as "musou" crowd-brawlers such as Koei's Dynasty Warriors. The genre overlaps with beat 'em ups but generally stresses weapon-based combat, deeper move sets, and acrobatic character action.
