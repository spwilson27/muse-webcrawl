Berkshire is a historic and ceremonial county in South East England, west of London, often called the Royal County of Berkshire because of the presence of Windsor Castle. The county town is Reading, and other major towns include Slough, Bracknell, Maidenhead, Wokingham, and Newbury. Since 1998 the county has been administered by six unitary authorities and has no county council.

Geography

The River Thames flows along Berkshire's northern boundary, and the Berkshire Downs — chalk hills including the prehistoric Ridgeway and the Uffington White Horse — cross the west. The east of the county forms part of the London commuter belt and the "Silicon Corridor" along the M4 motorway, home to many technology companies.

History

Berkshire was an Anglo-Saxon shire and featured in the campaigns of Alfred the Great, including the Battle of Ashdown (871). Boundary changes in 1974 transferred the Vale of White Horse and Didcot area to Oxfordshire and Slough to Berkshire from Buckinghamshire. Notable places include Windsor Castle, Eton College, Ascot Racecourse, and the royal palace at Reading's former abbey site.
