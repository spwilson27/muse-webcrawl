Hans Haacke

Hans Haacke
Born: 12 August 1936, Cologne, Germany
Movement: Conceptual art, institutional critique

Hans Haacke (born 1936) is a German-born American artist and a leading figure of conceptual art and institutional critique, living in New York since 1965. His work examines the social, political, and economic systems in which art circulates.

Early work

Haacke began in the 1960s with "systems" works exploring physical and biological processes, such as Condensation Cube (1963–65), a sealed acrylic box in which water condenses in response to gallery conditions. He taught for many years at Cooper Union in New York.

Institutional critique

From about 1970 his work turned to exposing the finances and governance of museums and their sponsors. His 1971 solo exhibition at the Guggenheim Museum was cancelled six weeks before opening over works tracing Manhattan real-estate holdings, an act of censorship that became famous. Later works, such as MetroMobiltan (1985) and his 1993 Venice Biennale German pavilion installation (which shared the Golden Lion), addressed corporate sponsorship and national history. His polls of gallery visitors were early exercises in participatory art.
