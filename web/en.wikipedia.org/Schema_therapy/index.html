Schema therapy

Schema therapy blends cognitive, experiential and interpersonal techniques.

Schema therapy is an integrative psychotherapy developed by Jeffrey E. Young for treating personality disorders and chronic psychological problems that do not respond well to traditional cognitive behavioural therapy. It combines elements of cognitive therapy, behaviour therapy, object relations theory, attachment theory, and Gestalt therapy into a unified model.

Early maladaptive schemas

The central concept is the early maladaptive schema: a broad, pervasive pattern of memories, emotions, cognitions, and bodily sensations regarding oneself and one's relationships, developed during childhood or adolescence and elaborated throughout life. Young identifies 18 schemas grouped into five domains, including disconnection and rejection, impaired autonomy, impaired limits, other-directedness, and overvigilance and inhibition.

Schema modes

Schema modes are the moment-to-moment emotional states and coping responses that dominate at a given time. Therapists help patients recognise modes such as the Vulnerable Child, the Angry Child, the Detached Protector, and the Punitive Parent, and to strengthen the patient's Healthy Adult mode.

Techniques

Schema therapy places greater emphasis than standard cognitive therapy on the therapeutic relationship—particularly "limited reparenting"—and on experiential techniques such as imagery rescripting and chair work, in which patients revisit and rework memories of painful childhood experiences.

Evidence

Randomised trials have found schema therapy effective for borderline personality disorder and other personality disorders, with comparatively low dropout rates.
