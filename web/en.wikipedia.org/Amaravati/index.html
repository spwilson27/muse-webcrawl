Amaravati

Amaravati is a city in Andhra Pradesh, India, situated on the southern bank of the Krishna River in Guntur district. It is the legislative capital of Andhra Pradesh and was developed as a planned greenfield capital city after the state's bifurcation in 2014. The name is also borne by the ancient town nearby, famous for its great Buddhist stupa.

The ancient town

Historic Amaravati was an important centre of Buddhism for many centuries. The Amaravati Stupa (Mahachaitya), built in phases from around the 3rd century BCE under the Satavahanas and later dynasties, was once among the largest stupas in India, richly carved with limestone reliefs depicting scenes from the life of the Buddha. Its sculptures — many now in the British Museum and the Government Museum, Chennai — define the distinctive "Amaravati school" of art. The town is also a Hindu pilgrimage site, home to the ancient Amareswara temple dedicated to Shiva, one of the Pancharama Kshetras.

The new capital

After the creation of Telangana in 2014 left Hyderabad outside the residuary state, Andhra Pradesh chose the Amaravati region for a new capital. The master plan, prepared with Singaporean assistance, envisioned a sustainable city of 217 km². Construction of government complexes proceeded from 2015, though the project was scaled back and re-scoped amid changes of government; in 2024 the state reaffirmed Amaravati as its sole capital and revived development works.
