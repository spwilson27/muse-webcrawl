Relative humidity

Relative humidity (RH) is the ratio of the partial pressure of water vapor in air to the equilibrium (saturation) vapor pressure of water at the same temperature, expressed as a percentage. A relative humidity of 50% means the air contains half the water vapor needed for saturation at that temperature.

Definition and measurement

Because saturation vapor pressure rises steeply with temperature (roughly following the Clausius–Clapeyron relation), cooling air at constant moisture content raises its relative humidity until it reaches the dew point, at which condensation begins. RH is measured with a hygrometer; traditional instruments include hair-tension and wet-and-dry-bulb psychrometers, while modern sensors are typically capacitive polymer devices.

Significance

Relative humidity governs comfort and health — very dry air irritates skin and airways and increases transmission of some respiratory viruses, while humid air impedes sweat evaporation and raises the apparent temperature, summarised in the heat index. It also affects condensation and mould growth in buildings, static electricity, the preservation of documents and artefacts, and many industrial processes. In meteorology, high relative humidity near the surface is associated with cloud formation and precipitation.
