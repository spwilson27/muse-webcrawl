Vaux-le-Vicomte

The Château de Vaux-le-Vicomte is a French Baroque château located in Maincy, near Melun, southeast of Paris. Built between 1658 and 1661 for Nicolas Fouquet, finance minister to the young Louis XIV, it was the first great collaboration of architect Louis Le Vau, painter Charles Le Brun, and landscape gardener André Le Nôtre — the team that would later create Versailles.

History

Fouquet inaugurated the château with a lavish fête on 17 August 1661, attended by the king, with a play by Molière and fireworks. The extravagance provoked Louis XIV's suspicion and jealousy; within weeks Fouquet was arrested by d'Artagnan on charges of embezzlement and spent the rest of his life in prison. The king seized the château's treasures and employed its artists at Versailles.

The estate passed through several owners and was restored in the late 19th century by the Sommier family; it remains privately owned by their descendants (the Vogüé family) and is open to the public, famous for candlelit summer evenings. Vaux-le-Vicomte is considered the founding work of the French formal garden style.
