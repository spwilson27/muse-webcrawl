Real-time tactics

Real-time tactics (RTT) is a genre of tactical wargame played in real time rather than in turns. Unlike real-time strategy (RTS) games, RTT titles typically omit base building, construction, and resource gathering, instead giving the player a fixed set of units — sometimes with limited reinforcement — and emphasising manoeuvre, positioning, cover, and combined-arms tactics.

Characteristics

Common features include squads rather than individual soldiers as the unit of control, morale and suppression systems, line-of-sight and stealth, capture points, and mission-based campaigns. Because there is no economy to manage, the player's attention is on the battlefield decisions themselves.

Notable examples

Early examples include Ground Control (2000) and the Close Combat series. Other well-known RTT games and series include Company of Heroes (which retains some base elements), Men of War, the Total War battle layer, World in Conflict, Wargame and Steel Division, and the Desperados and Shadow Tactics stealth-tactics subgenre. The boundary between RTT and RTS is debated, and the label "RTT" gained currency largely through genre classifications in the late 1990s and 2000s.
