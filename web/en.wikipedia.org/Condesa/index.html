Condesa

Condesa or La Condesa is an area in the Cuauhtémoc borough of Mexico City, south of Zona Rosa and west of Colonia Roma. It is composed of three colonias or officially recognized neighborhoods: Colonia Condesa, Colonia Hipódromo and Colonia Hipódromo Condesa. The area is considered to be fashionable and popular with younger businesspeople, students and pet lovers.

The name comes from a countess (condesa in Spanish), María de la Campa y Cos, the Countess of Santiago de Calimaya, who owned land and a hacienda in the area in the colonial era.

History

The area began as lands belonging to haciendas in the colonial period. In the early 20th century, the Jockey Club built the Hipódromo de la Condesa racetrack in 1910, and the surrounding land was subdivided for housing. The racetrack hosted automobile races as well, but by 1927 the area began to be developed as a residential neighborhood.

During the 1930s and 1940s, the area became fashionable, and many Art Deco apartment buildings were constructed, including the landmark Edificio Condesa. The neighborhood attracted a cosmopolitan population, including a significant Jewish immigrant community.

Description

Condesa is noted for its Art Deco and Art Nouveau architecture, tree-lined avenues, and parks such as Parque México and Parque España. Avenida Ámsterdam follows the oval route of the old racetrack. The area is known for its cafés, restaurants, bookstores and nightlife, and it is home to a young, creative population.

Earthquake damage

The neighborhood suffered significant damage in both the 1985 Mexico City earthquake and the 2017 Puebla earthquake, owing in part to its position on the soft soils of the former lakebed of Lake Texcoco.
