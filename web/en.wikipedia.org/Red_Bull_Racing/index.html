Red Bull Racing

Red Bull Racing, currently competing as Oracle Red Bull Racing and also known simply as Red Bull or RBR, is a Formula One racing team, racing under an Austrian racing licence and based in the United Kingdom. It is one of two Formula One teams owned by conglomerate Red Bull GmbH, the other being RB Formula One Team (formerly AlphaTauri and Toro Rosso).

History

The team was formed when the energy drinks company Red Bull purchased the Jaguar Racing team from Ford in November 2004, making its debut at the 2005 Australian Grand Prix with drivers David Coulthard and Christian Klien. Adrian Newey joined as chief technical officer in 2006, and the team's first victory came at the 2009 Chinese Grand Prix with Sebastian Vettel.

First era of dominance (2010–2013)

From 2010 to 2013 Red Bull won four consecutive Constructors' and Drivers' Championships, all with Vettel, powered by Renault engines and exploiting aerodynamic innovations such as the blown diffuser. Mark Webber was Vettel's team-mate throughout this period.

Hybrid era and resurgence

Mercedes dominated the early turbo-hybrid era from 2014. Red Bull remained a front-running team with Daniel Ricciardo and, from 2016, Max Verstappen — who won on his debut for the team at the 2016 Spanish Grand Prix, aged 18. The team switched to Honda power units in 2019. Verstappen won the Drivers' Championship in 2021 in a controversial season finale in Abu Dhabi, then dominated in 2022 and 2023, when the RB19 became the most successful car in F1 history by win percentage and Verstappen won a record 19 of 22 races. Verstappen secured a fourth consecutive title in 2024, though McLaren took the Constructors' Championship.

Red Bull Powertrains and Ford partnership

After Honda announced its withdrawal, Red Bull established Red Bull Powertrains to take over engine production, and from 2026 will partner with Ford under the new engine regulations.
