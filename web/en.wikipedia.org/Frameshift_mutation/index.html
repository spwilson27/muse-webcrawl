Frameshift mutation

A frameshift mutation is a genetic mutation caused by the insertion or deletion (an indel) of a number of nucleotides in a DNA sequence that is not a multiple of three. Because the ribosome reads the genetic code in triplets (codons), an indel that is not divisible by three shifts the reading frame, changing every codon downstream of the mutation.

Mechanism and consequences

The shifted reading frame typically produces a completely different amino acid sequence and often encounters a premature stop codon, truncating the protein. Frameshift mutations therefore tend to have severe effects, frequently causing complete loss of protein function. Messenger RNAs bearing premature stop codons are often degraded by nonsense-mediated decay.

Examples in disease

Frameshift mutations underlie many genetic disorders. Examples include some forms of cystic fibrosis, Tay–Sachs disease, Crohn's disease susceptibility (a frameshift in NOD2), and hereditary cancers caused by mutations in mismatch repair or tumour suppressor genes. Duchenne muscular dystrophy typically results from deletions that disrupt the reading frame of the dystrophin gene, whereas in-frame deletions produce the milder Becker form.

Detection

Frameshifts are detected by DNA sequencing and are a common class of pathogenic variant identified in clinical genetic testing.
