The Game Awards


The Game Awards trophy, designed by Wētā Workshop.

The Game Awards is an annual awards ceremony honouring achievements in the video game industry. Established in 2014, the shows are produced and hosted by the Canadian game journalist Geoff Keighley, who had previously worked on its predecessor, the Spike Video Game Awards, for over a decade. In addition to the awards, the ceremony features premieres of upcoming games and new information about previously announced titles, along with musical performances and appearances by figures from the games industry and other media.

History

Keighley hosted the Spike VGX awards until Spike ended the show in 2013. In 2014 he invested his own money to launch The Game Awards as an independent, livestreamed event, first held at The AXIS in Las Vegas in December 2014, and later moved to the Microsoft Theater (now Peacock Theater) in Los Angeles. The ceremony is carried live on dozens of streaming platforms and has grown to an audience of well over one hundred million livestreams annually.

Format and voting

Winners are determined by a blended vote between an international jury of media outlets (weighted 90 per cent) and public fan voting (10 per cent). The top honour is Game of the Year, whose winners have included Dragon Age: Inquisition (2014), The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt (2015), Overwatch (2016), The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild (2017), God of War (2018), Sekiro: Shadows Die Twice (2019), The Last of Us Part II (2020), It Takes Two (2021), Elden Ring (2022), Baldur's Gate 3 (2023), and Astro Bot (2024). Categories also cover direction, narrative, art, score, performance, and esports.

Criticism

The show has been criticized for the dominance of trailers and advertisements over award presentations, for rapid-fire announcements of many honours offstage, and for Keighley's dual role as producer and industry promoter. It has nonetheless become the most prominent annual awards event in gaming and a major marketing platform.
