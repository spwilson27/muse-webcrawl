Nepheline syenite

Nepheline syenite is a holocrystalline, plutonic igneous rock composed chiefly of nepheline and alkali feldspar. It is the silica-undersaturated equivalent of syenite: because the magma from which it forms lacks sufficient silica to form feldspar alone, part of the alkali content crystallises as the feldspathoid mineral nepheline instead, and quartz is absent. Rocks transitional between nepheline syenite and syenite contain small amounts of both quartz and nepheline.

Composition and varieties

A typical nepheline syenite contains 50–70% alkali feldspar (perthite, orthoclase, or albite), 10–30% nepheline, and smaller amounts of mafic minerals such as aegirine, arfvedsonite, biotite, or hornblende, together with accessories like sodalite, cancrinite, zircon, and titanite. Named varieties include foyaite (from Fóia, Portugal), ditroite, miarolite, and the sodalite-rich decorative stone from localities such as the Kola Peninsula (Russia), Mont Saint-Hilaire (Canada), and the Ilímaussaq complex (Greenland). These alkaline complexes are famous among mineral collectors for their extraordinary diversity of rare minerals.

Occurrence and uses

Nepheline syenites form in alkaline magmatic provinces, often associated with continental rifting or mantle plumes. Commercially, low-iron nepheline syenite mined in Canada (Ontario) and Norway is an important industrial mineral used in glass and ceramics as a source of alumina and alkalis that lowers melting temperatures; it is also used as a filler in paints and plastics, and polished as a decorative building stone.
