Makerere University is Uganda's largest and oldest institution of higher learning, located on Makerere Hill in the capital, Kampala. Founded in 1922 as a technical school, it became a university college affiliated with the University of London in 1949, the flagship campus of the University of East Africa in 1963, and an independent national university in 1970.

History

Makerere educated many of the leaders of post-independence East Africa, including Julius Nyerere of Tanzania, Milton Obote of Uganda and Mwai Kibaki of Kenya, and writers such as Ngũgĩ wa Thiong'o. The university suffered decline during Uganda's political turmoil of the 1970s and 1980s but revived from the 1990s with expanded enrolment and private sponsorship. Its Main Building, the "Ivory Tower", was gutted by fire in September 2020 and subsequently rebuilt.

Academics

The university is organised into constituent colleges covering health sciences, engineering, agriculture, humanities, business and law, among others, and is consistently ranked among the top universities in Africa.
