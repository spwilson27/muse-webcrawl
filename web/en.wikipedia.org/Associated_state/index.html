Associated state

An associated state is the minor partner in a formal, free relationship between a political territory with a degree of statehood and a (usually larger) state, for which no other specific term, such as protectorate, is adopted. The details of such a free association are contained in a Compact of Free Association or an associated statehood act, and are specific to the countries involved.

Associated state
ExamplesCook Islands and Niue (with New Zealand); Marshall Islands, Micronesia, Palau (with the United States)
Governing instrumentCompact of Free Association or act of association

Current associated states

The Cook Islands and Niue are self-governing in free association with New Zealand: they conduct most of their own affairs, while New Zealand retains responsibilities for defence and some foreign affairs, and their people hold New Zealand citizenship. Under Compacts of Free Association with the United States, the Marshall Islands, the Federated States of Micronesia, and Palau are sovereign states in which the US provides defence, funding, and certain services, and whose citizens may live and work in the United States. These three are members of the United Nations, as the Cook Islands and Niue increasingly conduct independent diplomacy.

History

The concept emerged in decolonisation: the United Nations recognised "free association" as one of the ways a non-self-governing territory could reach a full measure of self-government (General Assembly Resolution 1541 (XV), 1960). In the Caribbean, the British West Indies Associated States (1967–1983) used associated statehood as a step toward independence for Antigua, Dominica, Grenada, Saint Kitts-Nevis, Saint Lucia and Saint Vincent. Puerto Rico's status as a commonwealth in association with the United States is sometimes described in comparable terms, though it remains an unincorporated US territory.

Characteristics

Associated states typically retain the right to terminate the relationship unilaterally and move to full independence. Unlike protectorates, the arrangement is voluntary and revocable; unlike federacies or commonwealth realms, the partner states retain separate international legal personality in varying degrees.
