Republic of Cyprus

Cyprus is an island country in the eastern Mediterranean Sea.

The Republic of Cyprus is an island country in the eastern Mediterranean Sea, south of Turkey and west of Syria and Lebanon. Its capital and largest city is Nicosia. The republic gained independence from the United Kingdom in 1960 under a constitution designed to balance its Greek Cypriot majority and Turkish Cypriot minority, with Archbishop Makarios III as its first president.

Division

Intercommunal violence in 1963–64 ended Turkish Cypriot participation in the government, and in 1974 a coup by Greek Cypriot nationalists seeking union with Greece prompted a Turkish military intervention. The island has remained divided since: the Republic of Cyprus exercises effective control over the southern two-thirds, while the north is administered by Turkish Cypriots, whose 1983 declaration of independence is recognised only by Turkey. A UN-patrolled buffer zone separates the two sides, and repeated reunification negotiations — most prominently the Annan Plan referendum of 2004, approved by Turkish Cypriots but rejected by Greek Cypriots — have not produced a settlement.

Economy and international position

Cyprus joined the European Union in 2004 and adopted the euro in 2008. Its economy is dominated by services, including tourism, shipping, and financial and professional services; a severe banking crisis in 2012–13 led to an international bailout and the resolution of its second-largest bank. The discovery of natural gas fields in its exclusive economic zone has added an energy dimension to its regional relations.
