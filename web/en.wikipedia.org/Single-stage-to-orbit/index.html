Single-stage-to-orbit

A single-stage-to-orbit (SSTO) vehicle reaches orbit from the surface of a body without jettisoning hardware such as engines, fuel tanks, or boosters. No SSTO vehicle has yet operated from Earth, though several have been designed and one — the McDonnell Douglas DC-X — demonstrated key technologies in suborbital flight.

Challenges

The rocket equation demands that an Earth SSTO devote roughly 90 percent or more of its lift-off mass to propellant, leaving a very small fraction for structure, engines, and payload. Achieving orbit with chemical rockets requires extremely light structures and efficient engines across the whole flight profile; aerospike engines and air-breathing concepts such as SABRE have been proposed to improve performance.

Example projects

Projects have included the DC-X experimental vehicle, Lockheed Martin's X-33 and VentureStar, and Reaction Engines' Skylon spaceplane concept. Lunar and Martian SSTO operation is far easier because of lower gravity and, on the Moon, no atmosphere; the Apollo Lunar Module's ascent stage was effectively an SSTO vehicle from the Moon.
