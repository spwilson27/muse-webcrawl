Zohar

Image placeholder: Title page of an early printed Zohar

The Zohar (Hebrew: זֹהַר, "Splendor" or "Radiance") is a foundational work in Jewish mystical thought known as Kabbalah. Written in Aramaic, it is a mystical commentary on the Torah in the form of discussions among rabbis including the second-century sage Shimon bar Yochai.

Authorship

The Zohar appeared in Spain in the late 13th century, circulated and published by Moses de León, who attributed it to Shimon bar Yochai. Most modern scholars hold that de León himself and his circle composed the bulk of it, while traditional Kabbalists maintain its ancient authorship.

Content

Its teachings center on the nature of God, the ten sefirot, the origin of evil, and the cosmic significance of the commandments. The Zohar profoundly shaped later Jewish mysticism, including Lurianic Kabbalah.
