Regional Internet registry

A Regional Internet registry (RIR) is an organization that manages the allocation and registration of Internet number resources — IP addresses (IPv4 and IPv6) and autonomous system numbers — within a defined region of the world. There are five RIRs, coordinated globally through the allocation hierarchy headed by the Internet Assigned Numbers Authority (IANA).

The five registries

The five RIRs are: ARIN (North America), RIPE NCC (Europe, the Middle East, and parts of Central Asia), APNIC (Asia-Pacific), LACNIC (Latin America and the Caribbean), and AFRINIC (Africa). Each operates as a membership-based, non-profit organization and develops its allocation policies through open community processes.

Functions

RIRs distribute number resources to Internet service providers and other organizations, maintain public registration databases (including WHOIS and RDAP services), and support reverse DNS delegation. They also coordinate through the Number Resource Organization (NRO) and contribute to global Internet governance.
