The South Africa national rugby union team, commonly known as the Springboks, represents South Africa in men's international rugby union. One of the sport's most successful teams, they have won the Rugby World Cup a record four times, and are renowned for a physical, forward-oriented style of play in their green and gold jerseys.

History

South Africa played its first test in 1891 against a touring British Isles side and quickly became, alongside New Zealand's All Blacks, one of rugby's dominant powers; the rivalry between the two remains the sport's most storied. During the apartheid era, the whites-only selection of the team made it a focus of protest, and South African sport was progressively isolated by international boycotts from the 1960s until readmission in 1992.

World Cup triumphs

Hosting the 1995 World Cup, the Springboks beat New Zealand 15–12 in the final at Ellis Park, a moment of national unity symbolised by President Nelson Mandela presenting the trophy wearing captain Francois Pienaar's jersey. Further titles followed in 2007 (beating England in Paris), 2019 (beating England in Yokohama, captained by Siya Kolisi, the team's first Black captain), and 2023 (edging New Zealand 12–11 in Paris), making South Africa the first nation to win four World Cups.

Competitions

The Springboks compete annually in The Rugby Championship against New Zealand, Australia, and Argentina, and have won the tournament (and its Tri Nations predecessor) several times. Notable players include Jonah Lomu-era rivals such as Joel Stransky, Bryan Habana, John Smit, Victor Matfield, and Eben Etzebeth.
