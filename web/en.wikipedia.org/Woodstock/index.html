Woodstock

Woodstock was a music festival held from 15 to 18 August 1969 on Max Yasgur's dairy farm in Bethel, New York, United States. Billed as "An Aquarian Exposition: 3 Days of Peace & Music," it attracted more than 400,000 people and became a defining moment of 1960s counterculture.

Dates15–18 August 1969
LocationBethel, New York, US
AttendanceOver 400,000
OrganisersRoberts, Rosenman, Kornfeld, Lang

The festival

Thirty-two acts performed, including Joan Baez, Santana, the Grateful Dead, Janis Joplin, Jefferson Airplane, The Who, and Jimi Hendrix, whose closing rendition of the U.S. national anthem became iconic. Massive crowds overwhelmed fences, turning the event into a free festival; despite rain, mud, and shortages, it remained remarkably peaceful.

Legacy

Woodstock symbolised the ideals and music of its generation and was documented in the Oscar-winning 1970 film Woodstock. Later anniversary festivals were held under the name in 1979, 1989, 1994, 1999, and 2009.
