Caribbean culture

The culture of the Caribbean is a rich blend shaped by Indigenous peoples, European colonisation, the forced migration of enslaved Africans, and later immigration from India, China, the Middle East, and elsewhere. This creolisation produced distinctive languages, music, religions, cuisines, and festivals across the islands and coastal mainland.

Language and literature

European languages (English, Spanish, French, Dutch) are official in most territories, but everyday speech often uses creole languages such as Jamaican Patois, Haitian Creole, and Papiamento. Caribbean literature achieved worldwide recognition with writers such as Derek Walcott and Saint-John Perse (Nobel laureates), V. S. Naipaul, Jamaica Kincaid, and Édouard Glissant.

Music and festivals

The Caribbean originated many influential music genres: reggae, dancehall, and dub from Jamaica; calypso and soca from Trinidad and Tobago; salsa (developed with Cuban and Puerto Rican roots); merengue and bachata from the Dominican Republic; zouk from the French Antilles; and Afro-Cuban jazz. Carnival — held before Lent in Trinidad and many islands, and exported to diaspora cities such as London (Notting Hill) and Toronto — is the region's signature festival, alongside Jamaica's Reggae Sumfest and Barbados's Crop Over.

Religion, food, and sport

Christianity predominates, alongside Afro-Caribbean religions including Rastafari, Santería, Vodou, and Obeah, and significant Hindu and Muslim communities in Trinidad, Guyana, and Suriname. Cuisine combines African, European, Indigenous, and South Asian elements: jerk chicken, callaloo, roti, doubles, ackee and saltfish, and rice and peas. Cricket is a unifying passion in the anglophone Caribbean — the West Indies team draws players from many nations — alongside football, athletics, and baseball in the Spanish-speaking islands.
