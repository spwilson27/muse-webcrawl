The Four Loves is a 1960 book by C. S. Lewis exploring the nature of love through the four Greek words for it: storge (affection), philia (friendship), eros (romantic love), and agape (charity, or God's love). The book grew out of a series of radio talks Lewis recorded for the Episcopal Radio-TV Foundation in the United States.

Contents

Lewis distinguishes "need-love" from "gift-love" and examines each of the four loves in turn. Affection is the humblest and most widespread love, found in families; friendship, he argues, is the least biological and most undervalued in the modern world; eros is treated with both celebration and caution; and charity is presented as the divine love that must order and redeem the three natural loves, which left to themselves risk becoming selfish or idolatrous.

Reception

The book is among Lewis's most popular works of Christian apologetics and has remained continuously in print, frequently cited in discussions of friendship and the theology of love.
