George Daniels (watchmaker)

George Daniels CBE
Born: 19 August 1926, Sunderland, England
Died: 21 October 2011, Isle of Man
Occupation: Horologist
Known for: Co-axial escapement; handmade watches

George Daniels, CBE (19 August 1926 – 21 October 2011) was an English horologist who was considered one of the greatest watchmakers of the twentieth century. Working essentially alone, he designed and built complete watches by hand — cases, dials, and movements — and invented the co-axial escapement, the first fundamentally new practical watch escapement since the eighteenth century, which was adopted commercially by Omega in 1999.

Life and training

Born into poverty in Sunderland and raised in London, Daniels left school at fourteen and taught himself watchmaking while working at a variety of jobs, including a wartime spell in the army. He supported himself by restoring antique watches — in particular those of Abraham-Louis Breguet, whose work he revered — while studying horology at evening classes and later at the City of London College and Hackney Technical College. He became a Fellow of the British Horological Institute.

The handmade watches

Beginning in 1969, Daniels made a small series of pocket watches entirely by hand, mastering all thirty-two crafts traditionally required. His watches incorporated tourbillons, minute repeaters, perpetual calendars, and equations of time, and are distinguished by their engine-turned (guilloché) dials and classically English finish. Only a few dozen were made; they are now among the most valuable modern mechanical watches, with examples selling at auction for large sums. In 2017 his "Space Traveller" watch sold for about £3.2 million, then a record for an English watch.

The co-axial escapement

Daniels's co-axial escapement, patented in the 1970s, transmits energy to the balance with very little sliding friction, so the movement needs almost no lubrication at the escapement and can run far longer between services. After years of rejection by the industry, the Swiss manufacturer Omega adopted the design in 1999, and it has since been fitted to millions of watches. Daniels's memoir All in Good Time and his practical manual Watchmaking remain standard references, and his charitable trust supports education in horology and engineering. His apprentice Roger W. Smith continues the tradition of English handmade watchmaking on the Isle of Man, where Daniels lived and worked.
