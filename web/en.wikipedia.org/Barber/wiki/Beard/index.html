Beard


A full beard.

A beard is the hair that grows on the chin, upper lip, cheeks, and neck of humans and some animals. In humans, usually pubescent or adult males are able to grow beards. Beard growth is linked to stimulation of hair follicles in the area by dihydrotestosterone, a hormone influenced by testosterone.

Biology

During puberty, rising androgen levels cause vellus hair follicles on the face to develop into terminal hair follicles, producing thicker, darker hair. Beard growth rate and density vary widely among individuals and populations and are largely hereditary. Beards grow through phases over several years and may change in color, often greying with age.

History and culture

Attitudes toward beards have shifted across history. In ancient Egypt, beards were associated with royalty and divinity, and pharaohs wore false beards. In classical Greece beards signified wisdom and virility, while Alexander the Great is credited with ordering his soldiers to shave so enemies could not grab their beards in combat. Beards carried religious significance in many traditions and were mandated or encouraged in some branches of Judaism, Islam, and Sikhism (where uncut hair, kesh, is observed). In the Victorian era beards became highly fashionable in Britain, partly influenced by soldiers returning from the Crimean War. The 20th century saw beards decline with the spread of the safety razor and military grooming rules, before revivals in the 1960s counterculture and again in the 2010s.

Styles and care

Beard styles include the full beard, goatee, chinstrap, sideburns, and Van Dyke. Grooming may involve trimming, washing, and oils or balms. The removal of beards is typically by shaving.
