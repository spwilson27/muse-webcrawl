Hairdresser


A hairdresser at work in a salon.

A hairdresser is a person whose occupation is to cut or style hair in order to change or maintain a person's image. This is achieved using a combination of hair coloring, haircutting, and hair texturing techniques. Most hairdressers are professionally licensed as either a hairdresser, a barber, or a cosmetologist.

History

Hair care is ancient: hairdressing was practiced in ancient Egypt and Rome, where elaborate styles signaled status. In 17th- and 18th-century France, professional hairdressers rose to prominence at court, creating towering coiffures for aristocratic women, and hairdressing became a recognized skilled trade. The modern salon emerged in the late 19th and 20th centuries, with figures such as Marcel Grateau (inventor of the Marcel wave) and, later, Vidal Sassoon popularizing new techniques and salon models.

Occupation

Hairdressers work in salons, barbershops, spas, hotels, and in film, television, and fashion. Services include cutting, coloring, chemical treatments such as perms and relaxers, styling for events, and extensions. Training typically combines vocational school or apprenticeship with licensing examinations covering sanitation, chemical safety, and technique. Many hairdressers rent chairs in salons or operate as self-employed stylists.

Distinctions

Barbers traditionally specialize in cutting men's hair and facial shaving, while hairdressers and cosmetologists generally cover a broader range of services for all clients; in many jurisdictions the licenses have converged.
