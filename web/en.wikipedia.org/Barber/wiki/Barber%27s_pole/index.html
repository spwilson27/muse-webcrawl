Barber's pole

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A barber's pole is a type of sign used by barbers to signify the place or shop where they perform their craft. The trade sign is, by a tradition dating back to the Middle Ages, a staff or pole with a helix of colored stripes (often red, white, and blue in the United States; commonly red and white elsewhere). The pole may be stationary or may revolve, often with the aid of an electric motor. A "barber's pole" with a helical stripe that appears to move upward or downward is also a classic example of an optical illusion known as the barberpole illusion.

Origin and symbolism

The pole derives from barbers' historical second trade as surgeons and bloodletters. According to the traditional account, the pole represented the staff a patient gripped during bloodletting to make the veins stand out, the stripes the bloodied and clean bandages — which were washed and hung on the pole to dry — with red for blood (or arterial blood), white for bandages, and, in America, blue sometimes explained by the colors of the flag or by venous blood. Brass basins at the top and bottom of some poles evoke the bowls used to hold leeches and catch blood.

History and regulation

Barbers and surgeons were formally separated in England in 1745, but the pole remained the barber's emblem. In the United States, manufactured poles by companies such as Koken and the William Marvy Company became fixtures of Main Street from the late 19th century onward, and motorized spinning models proliferated in the 20th. Some jurisdictions have restricted the symbol by law or licensing practice, reserving it to licensed barbershops (as opposed to salons), and a few municipalities regulate its use as signage.

In science and culture

The barberpole illusion — in which rotating stripes on a cylinder appear to translate along its axis — is a standard demonstration in perception science, and the term "barber pole" is borrowed in other fields, from genetics (barber-pole worm, a nematode parasite) to candy striping and aviation windsocks.
