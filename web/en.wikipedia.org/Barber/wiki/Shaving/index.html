Shaving


Shaving with a straight razor.

Shaving is the removal of hair, by using a razor or any other kind of bladed implement, to slice it down to the level of the skin or otherwise. Shaving is most commonly practiced by men to remove their facial hair and by women to remove their leg and underarm hair. A man is called clean-shaven if he has had his beard removed entirely.

History

Before the advent of razors, hair was sometimes removed using two shells to pull the hair out or using water and a sharp tool. Early razors were made of flint, bronze, and later iron and steel. Straight razors dominated for centuries until the safety razor was popularized in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, notably through Gillette's disposable-blade design. Electric razors were developed in the 20th century, offering a dry alternative.

Methods

Wet shaving uses water, shaving cream, gel, soap, or oil together with a straight razor, safety razor, or cartridge razor. Dry shaving typically uses an electric razor or clipper. Shaving is usually done in the direction of hair growth first, and sometimes against it for a closer result, at some risk of irritation and ingrown hairs.

Effects and problems

Common side effects include razor burn, cuts, and pseudofolliculitis barbae (ingrown hairs). Contrary to a popular belief, shaving does not cause hair to grow back thicker; the blunt tip of regrowth merely feels coarser.
