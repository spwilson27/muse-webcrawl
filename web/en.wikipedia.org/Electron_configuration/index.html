Electron configuration


Order in which atomic subshells are filled (Aufbau principle)

In atomic physics and quantum chemistry, the electron configuration is the distribution of electrons of an atom or molecule (or other physical structure) in atomic or molecular orbitals. For example, the electron configuration of the neon atom is 1s2 2s2 2p6, meaning that 2 electrons occupy the 1s subshell, 2 occupy the 2s subshell and 6 occupy the 2p subshell.

Shells and subshells

Electrons occupy shells identified by the principal quantum number n = 1, 2, 3, ... Each shell contains subshells labelled s, p, d and f, holding at most 2, 6, 10 and 14 electrons respectively. The ordering of filling follows the Aufbau principle: electrons fill the lowest-energy orbitals first, subject to the Pauli exclusion principle (no two electrons share all four quantum numbers) and Hund's rule (orbitals of equal energy fill singly before pairing).

Notation

Configurations are written as a sequence of subshell labels with superscripts giving the number of electrons in each. Noble-gas shorthand replaces the inner "core" configuration with the symbol of the preceding noble gas in brackets: iron, for example, is [Ar] 3d6 4s2. Well-known exceptions to the simple filling order occur at chromium ([Ar] 3d5 4s1) and copper ([Ar] 3d10 4s1), where half-filled and filled d subshells confer extra stability.

Relation to the periodic table

The block structure of the periodic table reflects electron configuration: the s-block, p-block, d-block (transition metals) and f-block (lanthanides and actinides) correspond to the subshell being filled. Elements in the same group share similar outer (valence) configurations, which explains their similar chemical behaviour. Valence electrons determine bonding, oxidation states and most chemical properties.
