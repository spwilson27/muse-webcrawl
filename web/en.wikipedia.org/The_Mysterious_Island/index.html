The Mysterious Island (French: L'Île mystérieuse) is a novel by French writer Jules Verne, published in 1874–1875. A "Robinsonade," it relates the adventures of five American castaways on an uncharted island in the South Pacific who build a thriving colony from almost nothing using their scientific knowledge. The novel is a crossover sequel to Verne's Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Seas (1870) and In Search of the Castaways (1867–68), revisiting Captain Nemo and the character Ayrton.

Plot summary

During the American Civil War, five Union prisoners escape besieged Richmond by balloon and are blown across the Pacific to a volcanic island they name Lincoln Island. Led by engineer Cyrus Smith (Cyrus Harding in some translations), with reporter Gideon Spilett, sailor Pencroff, young Herbert, servant Neb, and the dog Top, the colonists manufacture tools, pottery, glass, nitroglycerin, a telegraph, and eventually a ship. Mysterious interventions — a chest of supplies, the rescue of the marooned Ayrton from nearby Tabor Island, and the destruction of a pirate brig — are eventually explained by the dying Captain Nemo, whose submarine Nautilus lies in the island's caverns. Nemo dies as the island's volcano erupts; the castaways are rescued from a bare rock by the ship Duncan.

Themes

The novel celebrates engineering, chemistry, and rational mastery over nature, while the figure of Nemo adds meditation on exile, guilt, and vengeance. It is among the most technically detailed of Verne's works and a classic of survival literature.

Adaptations

The novel has been filmed repeatedly, including the 1961 Ray Harryhausen version with Herbert Lom as Nemo and the 2012 film Journey 2: The Mysterious Island. It also inspired the adventure game Return to Mysterious Island.
