Bran

Bran, also known as miller's bran, is the hard outer layers of cereal grain that are separated from the endosperm during milling. It consists of the combined aleurone and pericarp of the seed, and together with the germ is an integral part of whole grains; it is removed in the production of refined white flours.

Composition and nutrition

Bran is particularly rich in dietary fibre — wheat bran is roughly 40–50% fibre — and contains B vitamins, minerals such as iron, magnesium and zinc, and various antioxidants and phytochemicals. Rice bran additionally contains oil rich in gamma-oryzanol, while oat bran is notable for the soluble fibre beta-glucan, associated with cholesterol reduction. Bran also contains phytic acid, which can reduce mineral absorption.

Uses

Bran is used in breakfast cereals (bran flakes, raisin bran), muffins, and as an added ingredient in breads and baked goods to increase fibre. Historically, most bran went to animal feed, and it remains a major feed ingredient for livestock. Rice bran is pressed or solvent-extracted for cooking oil and stabilised by heat to prevent rancidity from its lipase enzymes.

Common brans

The most widely produced brans come from wheat, rice, oats, corn (maize) and rye. Wheat bran is the most common in Western diets and is sold as a supplement to relieve constipation and support digestive health.
