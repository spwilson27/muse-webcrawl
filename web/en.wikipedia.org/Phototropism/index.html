Phototropism

Phototropism is the growth of an organism in response to a light stimulus. Plant shoots typically exhibit positive phototropism, growing toward light, while roots often show negative phototropism, growing away from it. The response maximizes light capture for photosynthesis.

Mechanism

Blue light is detected by photoreceptor proteins called phototropins. According to the Cholodny–Went model, perception of directional light causes the hormone auxin to redistribute to the shaded side of a shoot tip. There, auxin stimulates cell elongation, so the shaded side grows faster and the shoot bends toward the light.

History

Charles Darwin and his son Francis studied phototropic bending in grass coleoptiles in The Power of Movement in Plants (1880), showing the tip senses light while bending occurs below. Frits Went later isolated the growth substance, auxin, from coleoptile tips. Phototropism is one of several plant tropisms, alongside gravitropism and thigmotropism.
