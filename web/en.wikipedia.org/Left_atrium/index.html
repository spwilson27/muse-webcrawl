Left atrium

Left atrium
System: Cardiovascular
Receives from: Pulmonary veins (four)
Drains to: Left ventricle via mitral valve
Appendage: Left atrial appendage

The left atrium is one of the four chambers of the human heart, located on the posterosuperior aspect of the heart. It receives oxygenated blood from the lungs through the four pulmonary veins and pumps it through the mitral valve into the left ventricle.

Structure

The chamber has thinner walls than the ventricles, since it pumps blood only a short distance. Its smooth-walled posterior part receives the pulmonary veins, while the trabeculated anterior part includes the muscular left atrial appendage, a common site of thrombus formation in atrial fibrillation. The interatrial septum bears the fossa ovalis, the remnant of the fetal foramen ovale.

Clinical significance

Left atrial enlargement is a marker of chronic pressure or volume overload and is associated with atrial fibrillation, mitral valve disease and heart failure. Assessment of left atrial size is a routine part of echocardiography.
