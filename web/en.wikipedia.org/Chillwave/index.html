Chillwave (originally considered a synonym for glo-fi) is a music microgenre that emerged in the late 2000s. It is characterized by a faded or dreamlike retro pop sound, escapist lyrics revolving around beach life and summer, and psychedelic or lo-fi aesthetics such as low-budget effects, distorted synthesizer pads, and simple musical structures. The term was coined in 2009 by Carles, the anonymous author of the blog Hipster Runoff, half-jokingly, to describe a wave of young American acts sharing a hazy, nostalgic sound.

Characteristics

Chillwave draws on the sounds of 1980s synth-pop, yacht rock, and muzak filtered through tape hiss and cheap recording equipment, often produced in bedrooms with laptops and samplers. Songs typically evoke childhood summers, VHS tapes, and coastal leisure — a "sonic nostalgia" for media half-remembered from childhood. Critics grouped it within the broader "hypnagogic pop" described by David Keenan in The Wire in 2009.

History

The genre crystallized in the summer of 2009, sometimes called the "Summer of Chillwave", with breakthrough releases including Washed Out's Life of Leisure EP ("Feel It All Around" later became the Portlandia theme), Neon Indian's Psychic Chasms, and Toro y Moi's early singles. Blogs and Pitchfork propelled the microgenre, which quickly attracted both acclaim and backlash as a manufactured internet trend. By the early 2010s the label had faded, though its hazy production style strongly influenced later internet genres such as vaporwave, future funk, and lo-fi hip-hop.
