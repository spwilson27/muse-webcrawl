Musée Rodin

Location: Hôtel Biron, 7th arrondissement, Paris, France
Established: 1919
Collection: Works of Auguste Rodin and Camille Claudel

The Musée Rodin is a museum in Paris dedicated to the sculptor Auguste Rodin, opened in 1919 in the Hôtel Biron, the elegant eighteenth-century mansion where Rodin lived and worked in his final years. A second site occupies Rodin's home at Meudon, the Villa des Brillants. The Paris museum and its sculpture garden are among the most visited art museums in France.

History

Rodin rented rooms in the Hôtel Biron from 1908, joining tenants such as Henri Matisse and Jean Cocteau. In 1916 he reached an agreement donating his entire collection — his sculptures, drawings, paintings and antiquities — to the French state, on condition that the Hôtel Biron become a museum of his work. Rodin died in 1917; the museum opened to the public two years later.

Collection

The collection holds some 6,600 sculptures, 8,000 drawings and thousands of photographs and objects. In the garden stand monumental bronzes including The Thinker, The Burghers of Calais, The Gates of Hell, and the monuments to Balzac and Victor Hugo. Inside are marble works such as The Kiss and The Cathedral, alongside a room devoted to Camille Claudel, Rodin's student, collaborator and lover, whose works include The Waltz and The Mature Age. The museum also administers the casting of posthumous bronzes from Rodin's original moulds, which Rodin bequeathed to the state along with the rights to reproduce his work.
