Ecclesiastical court
Also called: Court Christian
Law applied: Canon law
Jurisdiction: Church discipline, clergy, marriage, wills (historically)

An ecclesiastical court, also called a court Christian, is a court established by a church to adjudicate matters of canon law, church discipline and, historically, a wide range of civil matters involving clergy and laity.

History

In medieval Europe, ecclesiastical courts claimed jurisdiction over marriage, divorce, wills, defamation, perjury and offences by clergy, who enjoyed "benefit of clergy" exemption from secular courts. In England, a hierarchy ran from archdeacons' courts through diocesan consistory courts to the provincial Court of Arches, with appeals historically to Rome and, after the Reformation, to the monarch in council.

Modern role

Most civil jurisdiction was removed from church courts in the 19th century (in England, probate and matrimonial causes passed to the state in 1857). Today, Roman Catholic tribunals chiefly handle marriage annulments and clergy discipline; Anglican consistory courts deal mainly with faculty applications for works on church buildings, clergy discipline and related matters.
