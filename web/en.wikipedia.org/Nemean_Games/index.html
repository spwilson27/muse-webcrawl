Nemean Games

The Nemean Games were one of the four Panhellenic Games of ancient Greece, held at the sanctuary of Zeus at Nemea in the northeastern Peloponnese. Traditionally founded in 573 BCE, they took place every two years, in the second and fourth years of each Olympiad, alternating with the Isthmian Games.

Mythical origins

Two foundation myths were told. In one, the games began as funeral games for the infant Opheltes, who was killed by a serpent while his nurse Hypsipyle guided the Seven against Thebes to a spring; the seer Amphiaraus renamed the child Archemoros, "beginning of doom", and the judges wore black in mourning and awarded crowns of wild celery. In the other, Heracles instituted the games after slaying the Nemean Lion as the first of his twelve labours.

Programme

Events closely followed the Olympic model: foot races including the stadion, diaulos and dolichos; wrestling, boxing and the pankration; the pentathlon; boys' events; and, from the Hellenistic period, musical and poetic contests. Equestrian events were held in a hippodrome. Victors received a crown of fresh wild celery, and their triumphs were celebrated in victory odes — several of Pindar's Nemean Odes survive.

Site and revival

The sanctuary included the Temple of Zeus, treasuries, a bathhouse and a stadium seating about 40,000, reached by a vaulted tunnel that still survives. Control of the games rested first with Cleonae and later with Argos; activity faded in late antiquity. Since 1996, the Society for the Revival of the Nemean Games has organised modern revivals in which participants run barefoot in the ancient stadium.
