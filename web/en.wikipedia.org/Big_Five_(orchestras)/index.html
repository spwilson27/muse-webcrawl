Big Five (orchestras)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Big Five is a traditional grouping of the five American orchestras long regarded as standing at the forefront of musical excellence in the United States. The term originated in the 1950s and 1960s in music journalism.

History of the term

The grouping crystallised in the era of long-tenured music directors and major recording contracts: the Philadelphia Orchestra under Eugene Ormandy, Chicago under Georg Solti, Cleveland under George Szell, Boston under Charles Munch and Seiji Ozawa, and New York under Leonard Bernstein.

Modern usage

Commentators frequently note that the distinction has blurred, as orchestras such as the Los Angeles Philharmonic, San Francisco Symphony, and Minnesota Orchestra have achieved comparable artistic standing and larger budgets. Lists are sometimes expanded to a "Big Seven", but the traditional five remain the standard reference point.
