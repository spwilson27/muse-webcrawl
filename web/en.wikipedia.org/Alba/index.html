Alba

Alba (pronounced [ˈal̪ˠapə]) is the Scottish Gaelic name for Scotland. The term is also used in Irish (Alba) and Manx (Nalbin) for Scotland, and is cognate with the Welsh Yr Alban. Historically, Alba referred to the Pictish kingdom north of the Forth, and after the unification of the Picts and Scots in the 9th–10th centuries it became the Gaelic name of the Kingdom of Scotland ruled by the kings of Alba.

Etymology

The name derives from the ancient Brythonic root also found in "Albion", the oldest known name for the island of Great Britain, itself possibly from a word meaning "white" — a reference some connect to the white cliffs of the south coast. In Gaelic tradition the root later came to denote specifically the northern kingdom.

Kingdom of Alba

The Kingdom of Alba emerged around 900 AD from the union of the Pictish and Gaelic (Dál Riata) realms traditionally associated with Kenneth MacAlpin and his dynasty. Its kings, beginning with Constantine II, gradually extended authority over Strathclyde and Lothian. Malcolm II and David I continued the consolidation that produced medieval Scotland.

Modern usage

Alba survives in terms such as Alba gu bràth ("Scotland forever"), the name of a BBC Scottish Gaelic television channel (BBC Alba), and the Alba Party, a Scottish pro-independence political party founded in 2021.
