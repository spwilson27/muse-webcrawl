Muhammad Shah Rangeela (1702–1748) was the thirteenth Mughal emperor, reigning from 1719 to 1748. His long reign saw the acceleration of the Mughal Empire's decline, with regional powers such as the Marathas, the Nawabs of Bengal and Awadh, and the Nizam of Hyderabad asserting effective independence.

Reign

Raised to the throne by the Sayyid Brothers, Muhammad Shah later freed himself from their control with the help of Nizam-ul-Mulk. A patron of the arts fond of music, poetry and pleasure — earning him the epithet Rangeela ("the colourful") — he presided over a brilliant court while imperial authority shrank.

Invasion of Nader Shah

In 1739 the Persian ruler Nader Shah invaded India, defeated the Mughals at Karnal, and sacked Delhi, carrying off immense treasures including the Peacock Throne and the Koh-i-Noor diamond. The massacre and plunder of Delhi was a decisive blow from which the empire never recovered.
