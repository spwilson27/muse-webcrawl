Shigeru Miyamoto (Japanese: 宮本 茂, born November 16, 1952) is a Japanese video game designer, producer, and game director at Nintendo, widely regarded as one of the most influential designers in the history of video games. He created some of the medium's most acclaimed and best-selling franchises, including Mario, Donkey Kong, The Legend of Zelda, Star Fox, and Pikmin, and is often called the "father of modern video gaming."

Early life and joining Nintendo

Miyamoto was born in the town of Sonobe, Kyoto, and grew up exploring the hillsides, caves, and lakes around his rural home — experiences that later inspired the exploration-based design of The Legend of Zelda. He studied industrial design at the Kanazawa College of Art and joined Nintendo in 1977 as an artist, through a connection of his father to president Hiroshi Yamauchi. He was mentored early by hardware designer Gunpei Yokoi, with whom he collaborated on his first projects.

Career

His breakthrough came with the arcade game Donkey Kong (1981), one of the first games with a developed storyline and characters, introducing both Donkey Kong and the character who became Mario. Super Mario Bros. (1985), designed with Takashi Tezuka for the NES, defined the side-scrolling platformer and helped make Mario the face of video gaming. The Legend of Zelda (1986) pioneered open-world exploration and battery-backed saving.

Miyamoto went on to produce or direct landmark titles across every Nintendo platform, including Super Mario 64 (1996), which set the template for 3D platforming; The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time (1998), frequently cited as one of the greatest games ever made; and Wii Sports (2006), central to the Wii's mainstream success. As general manager of Nintendo EAD and later a senior executive, he supervised much of the company's software output while mentoring younger designers.

Honours

Miyamoto was the first person inducted into the Academy of Interactive Arts and Sciences Hall of Fame (1998) and has received a BAFTA Fellowship, the Game Developers Choice Lifetime Achievement Award, and Japan's Person of Cultural Merit honour (2019).
