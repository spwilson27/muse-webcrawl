The Game & Watch is a line of handheld electronic games developed, manufactured, released, and marketed by Nintendo from 1980 to 1991. Created by game designer Gunpei Yokoi, each unit features a single game to be played on an LCD screen, in addition to a clock, an alarm, or both — hence the name. They were Nintendo's first major success in video games and helped establish the handheld game market.

History

Yokoi conceived the idea while watching a bored businessman playing with an LCD calculator on a bullet train. Using inexpensive calculator-style LCD technology, he designed small devices that played one simple game and told the time. The first title, Ball, was released in 1980. The series grew through several hardware formats: Silver, Gold, Wide Screen, and the innovative Multi Screen models that folded open to reveal two screens — a design that foreshadowed the Nintendo DS by more than two decades. The 1982 Donkey Kong Multi Screen unit introduced the cross-shaped D-pad, one of the most influential input designs in gaming history.

Other notable titles include Manhole, Fire, Octopus, Parachute, Oil Panic, and adaptations of Nintendo's arcade hits Donkey Kong, Donkey Kong Jr., and Mario Bros. Later models included the vertical-clamshell and tabletop variants, and the final unit was Mario the Juggler in 1991.

Legacy

The Game & Watch line sold over 43 million units worldwide and made Nintendo a major player in electronic games before the Game Boy — also designed by Yokoi — launched in 1989. Nintendo has revisited the line with the Game & Watch Gallery compilations, Club Nintendo reissues of Ball, and special anniversary hardware: Game & Watch: Super Mario Bros. (2020) and Game & Watch: The Legend of Zelda (2021). Mr. Game & Watch, a character based on the generic stick-figure protagonist of the games, appears as a fighter in the Super Smash Bros. series.
