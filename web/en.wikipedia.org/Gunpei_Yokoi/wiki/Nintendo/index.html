Nintendo Co., Ltd. is a Japanese multinational video game company headquartered in Kyoto. It develops, publishes, and releases both video games and video game consoles. Founded in 1889 by craftsman Fusajiro Yamauchi as Nintendo Koppai to produce handmade hanafuda playing cards, the company successively moved into toys and then electronic games, becoming one of the most influential and valuable companies in the video game industry.

History

After decades as a playing-card manufacturer, Nintendo diversified in the 1960s under president Hiroshi Yamauchi into toys — including the Ultra Hand designed by engineer Gunpei Yokoi — and then into electronic entertainment. Yokoi's Game & Watch handhelds (1980) and the Family Computer / Nintendo Entertainment System (1983/1985), along with Shigeru Miyamoto's Donkey Kong (1981) and Super Mario Bros. (1985), helped revive the video game industry after the North American crash of 1983.

Subsequent hardware included the Game Boy (1989), Super Nintendo Entertainment System (1990), Nintendo 64 (1996), GameCube (2001), Nintendo DS (2004), Wii (2006) — a major mainstream phenomenon built around motion control — the Wii U (2012), and the hybrid Nintendo Switch (2017), one of the best-selling consoles of all time, succeeded by the Nintendo Switch 2 in 2025.

Franchises

Nintendo's intellectual properties are among the most recognisable in entertainment: Mario, The Legend of Zelda, Pokémon (co-owned with Game Freak and Creatures Inc. through The Pokémon Company), Donkey Kong, Metroid, Kirby, Star Fox, Fire Emblem, Animal Crossing, Splatoon, and Super Smash Bros. Characters such as Mario serve as mascots recognised worldwide.

Philosophy

Nintendo is known for emphasising novel gameplay experiences over raw hardware power — an approach that traces back to Yokoi's "lateral thinking with withered technology" and remains a hallmark of its console designs.
