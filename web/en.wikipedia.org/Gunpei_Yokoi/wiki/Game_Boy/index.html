The Game Boy is a handheld game console developed by Nintendo, launched in Japan in 1989. It was created by Gunpei Yokoi and his Nintendo R&D1 team, combining features of the NES home console with the portability of Yokoi's earlier Game & Watch line. Despite technically inferior hardware compared with rivals such as the Sega Game Gear and Atari Lynx, the Game Boy's long battery life, durability, and strong game library made it an enormous commercial success.

Design philosophy

Yokoi's guiding philosophy was "lateral thinking with withered technology" — using mature, cheap, well-understood components in novel ways. The Game Boy used a modest Sharp LR35902 processor and a 160 × 144 pixel monochrome LCD able to display four shades of grey-green, but it ran for 15–30 hours on four AA batteries, far outlasting its colour-screen competitors. Its controls — a D-pad, A and B buttons, Start and Select — were inherited from the Game & Watch and NES.

Launch and success

The console launched bundled with Tetris in North America, a pairing that proved decisive: the puzzle game appealed far beyond the usual gaming audience. The Game Boy line went on to sell 118.69 million units worldwide including the Game Boy Color (1998), which was backward compatible with the original library. Landmark titles included Super Mario Land, The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening, Kirby's Dream Land, and above all Pokémon Red, Green and Blue (1996/1998), which revived the platform late in its life and launched a global phenomenon.

Hardware and variants

The original model was followed by the smaller Game Boy Pocket (1996), the Japan-only Game Boy Light (1998) with a backlit screen, and the Game Boy Color (1998). Accessories included the Game Link Cable for multiplayer, the Game Boy Camera and Printer, and the Super Game Boy adaptor for playing cartridges on a television through the SNES. The line was succeeded by the Game Boy Advance in 2001, and the original Game Boy was discontinued in 2003.

Legacy

The Game Boy is credited with creating the modern handheld gaming market and remains one of the best-selling game systems of all time. Its success vindicated Yokoi's philosophy of affordable, focused hardware over raw power.
