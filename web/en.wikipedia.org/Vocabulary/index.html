Vocabulary

A vocabulary (also called a lexicon) is a set of words within a language. The word vocabulary can also refer to the words known to an individual person (their personal lexicon), or to the specialized terminology of a particular field or activity. Vocabulary development is a central part of language acquisition in children and of second-language learning in adults.

Types

Researchers distinguish between receptive vocabulary — words a person understands when heard or read — and expressive vocabulary — words a person can use in speech or writing. Receptive vocabulary is normally larger. Educators also distinguish oral vocabulary from print vocabulary.

Size and acquisition

A native English-speaking adult may know roughly 15,000–20,000 word families, while a five-year-old entering school typically knows several thousand words. Most vocabulary is acquired incidentally through reading, conversation and exposure, though direct instruction is also effective. The vocabulary of academic texts differs from everyday speech and is sometimes taught through lists such as the Academic Word List.
