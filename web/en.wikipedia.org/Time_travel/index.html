Time travel

Time travel is the hypothetical activity of traveling into the past or future. The concept is a recognised theme in philosophy, science fiction and popular culture, though time travel to the past is generally considered impossible in the prevailing understanding of physics, which requires travel faster than light or exotic spacetime geometries.

Physics

Special relativity permits a form of travel into the future: an object moving at relativistic speed, or in a stronger gravitational field, experiences less elapsed time than a stationary observer, as demonstrated by the twin paradox and by measurements with atomic clocks. General relativity admits theoretical solutions containing closed timelike curves, such as rotating cylinders and traversable wormholes, which would permit travel to the past, but these require exotic matter with negative energy density and may be prevented by quantum effects.

Paradoxes and fiction

Proposed backward time travel raises paradoxes such as the grandfather paradox, and resolutions including the Novikov self-consistency principle and the many-worlds interpretation. In fiction, time travel became popular after H. G. Wells's The Time Machine (1895) and remains a staple of books and films, from Back to the Future to Doctor Who.
