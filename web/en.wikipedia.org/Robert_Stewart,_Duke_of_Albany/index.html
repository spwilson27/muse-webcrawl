Robert Stewart, Duke of Albany (c. 1340 – 3 September 1420) was a Scottish prince and statesman, a member of the House of Stewart, and the third son of King Robert II of Scotland. Created Duke of Albany in 1398, he served as regent of Scotland during the reigns of his elder brother Robert III and his nephew James I, effectively ruling the kingdom for much of the period from 1388 until his death.

Early life

Robert Stewart was born around 1340, the third son of Robert Stewart, later King Robert II, and Elizabeth Mure. When his father ascended the throne in 1371, Robert became one of the most powerful magnates in the kingdom. He was created Earl of Fife in 1371 and Earl of Menteith through his marriage to Margaret Graham, Countess of Menteith. His brother John, Earl of Carrick, became heir to the throne and later reigned as Robert III.

Ambitious and capable, Robert accumulated extensive lands and influence in central Scotland, building Doune Castle as one of his principal residences.

Rise to power

In December 1388, the council of the realm removed Robert III's elder brother John from the guardianship of Scotland because of his infirmity following a horse-kicking injury, and appointed Robert Stewart, then Earl of Fife, as Guardian of the kingdom. Robert governed during the turbulent years of Anglo-Scottish warfare, including the period after the Battle of Otterburn (1388).

In 1398 Robert was created Duke of Albany, while his nephew David Stewart was created Duke of Rothesay — the first dukedoms created in Scotland. After Robert III proved unable to control his son David, Albany and the Douglas faction arrested the Duke of Rothesay in 1402; David died in captivity at Falkland Palace, in circumstances that raised suspicions of starvation. A general council absolved Albany of blame, but the episode shadowed his reputation.

Regency

When Robert III died in 1406, his younger son James had just been captured by the English on his way to France and was held prisoner in England. Albany was appointed Governor of Scotland and ruled in James's name for the next fourteen years. His regency was marked by the recovery of some royal authority, conflicts with the Lord of the Isles — including the bloody Battle of Harlaw in 1411 — and efforts to secure James's release, which some contemporaries believed Albany pursued without great urgency, since the king's return would end his own rule.

Albany also intervened in the Western Schism politics of the church and supported the University of St Andrews, founded in 1413, Scotland's first university.

Death and legacy

Robert Stewart died at Stirling Castle on 3 September 1420, aged about eighty, and was buried at Dunfermline Abbey. He was succeeded as Duke of Albany and governor by his son Murdoch Stewart. When James I finally returned to Scotland in 1424, he moved against the Albany Stewarts: Murdoch, his sons, and Albany's aged father-in-law the Earl of Lennox were executed in 1425, destroying the family's power.

Historians have long debated Albany's character: he was an able and durable politician in a violent age, though his ambition and the convenient deaths and captivities of rivals have led some to judge him harshly.

Family

Albany married twice: first Margaret Graham, Countess of Menteith, with whom he had several children including his heir Murdoch; and secondly Muriella Keith, with whom he had further children, including John Stewart, Earl of Buchan, a celebrated commander in France. His descendants through various lines remained prominent in Scottish and European affairs.
