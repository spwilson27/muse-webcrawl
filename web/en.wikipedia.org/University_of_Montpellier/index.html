University of Montpellier

Type: Public research university
Established: 1220 (medical school 1181)
Location: Montpellier, France
Students: ~50,000

The University of Montpellier (French: Université de Montpellier) is a public research university in Montpellier, in the south of France. Its medical school, founded around 1181 and formally recognised in the thirteenth century, is among the oldest in the world still in operation; the university itself was established by papal bull in 1220. Students and teachers there included Rabelais, Nostradamus and Petrarch.

History

Montpellier was a great medieval centre of medicine and law, open to Jewish, Muslim and Christian scholars. Like other French universities it was suppressed in the Revolution (1793) and reconstituted in the nineteenth century. Split into three universities in 1970, Montpellier 1 and 2 reunified in 2015 as the University of Montpellier, while Paul Valéry University Montpellier 3 remains separate.

Profile

The university is strong in medicine, pharmacy, law, sciences and sport sciences, and participates in the Montpellier University of Excellence (MUSE) initiative. The Jardin des Plantes, founded in 1593, is France's oldest botanical garden.
