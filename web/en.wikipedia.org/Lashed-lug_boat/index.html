Lashed-lug boat

Lashed-lug boats are built by the Austronesian peoples of Southeast Asia and Oceania using a distinctive shell-first technique: hull planks are edge-joined with dowels and lashed, through carved protruding lugs left on the inner plank surfaces, to ribs and thwarts with plant-fibre cordage, without nails.

Characteristics

The lashings give the hull flexibility in heavy seas and allow planks to be replaced. The technique appears in archaeological finds such as the 7th–8th century Butuan boats of the Philippines and the Punjulharjo boat of Java, and persists in craft such as the Philippine paraw and balangay.

Distribution

Lashed-lug construction spread with Austronesian expansion from Taiwan and the Philippines through Indonesia to Madagascar and the Pacific.
