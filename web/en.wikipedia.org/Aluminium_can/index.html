Aluminium can

An aluminium can (British English) or aluminum can (American English) is a metal container for storage of liquids such as soft drinks, alcohol, and food products, made primarily of aluminium. Aluminium cans are widely used for beverages worldwide; about 180 billion beverage cans are produced each year.

History

The first commercial aluminium beverage can was produced in 1958 by the Hawaii Brewing Corporation for its Primo beer. In 1959, Ermal Fraze invented the pop-top opening mechanism while working for the Aluminum Company of America (Alcoa). The two-piece drawn-and-ironed can, developed in the early 1960s, largely replaced the older three-piece soldered tinplate can. Aluminium cans gained popularity because they are lighter than steel, do not rust, chill quickly, and provide a good surface for printing.

Manufacture

Most aluminium beverage cans are made in two pieces. A circular blank of aluminium sheet is drawn into a cup, then redrawn and ironed through a series of dies to form the thin-walled body with an integral bottom. The top is flanged, filled, and seamed to a separate lid fitted with a scored opening and a stay-on tab. The walls of a modern can can be less than 0.1 mm thick, yet the filled can withstands substantial internal pressure from carbonation.

Recycling

Aluminium cans are among the most recycled consumer products in the world. Recycling a can saves about 95% of the energy needed to produce new aluminium from bauxite ore, and cans can be recycled indefinitely without loss of quality. Global beverage can recycling rates average around 69%, with some deposit-return countries exceeding 95%.
