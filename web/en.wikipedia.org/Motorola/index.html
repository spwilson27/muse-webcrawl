Motorola

[Image: The Motorola "batwing" logo, adopted in 1955]

Motorola, Inc. was an American multinational telecommunications company based in Schaumburg, Illinois. It was founded in Chicago in 1928 by Paul V. Galvin and his brother Joseph as the Galvin Manufacturing Corporation, and took the Motorola name - coined for its popular car radios, combining "motor" with "ola" (as in Victrola) - in 1947. Motorola became a pioneer of radio and electronics: it made early commercial car radios, supplied radio equipment in the Second World War (including the SCR-536 "Handie-Talkie"), and provided the transponder through which Neil Armstrong's first words from the Moon were transmitted in 1969.

Mobile communications and semiconductors

Motorola invented the first handheld mobile phone prototype, the DynaTAC, demonstrated by Martin Cooper in 1973 and commercialized in 1983; its MicroTAC (1989) and StarTAC (1996) were landmark designs, and the RAZR V3 (2004) sold over 130 million units. Its semiconductor division produced the 68000 microprocessor family used in early Macintosh, Amiga and Atari computers, and the PowerPC chips developed with IBM and Apple. The company also ran the Iridium satellite telephone venture and built the Six Sigma quality methodology, invented by Motorola engineers in 1986.

Breakup

After years of losses in the 2000s as handset market share collapsed, Motorola split on January 4, 2011, into two public companies: Motorola Mobility, covering handsets and home devices (acquired by Google in 2012, then sold to Lenovo in 2014), and Motorola Solutions, covering enterprise and public-safety communications, which continues today.
