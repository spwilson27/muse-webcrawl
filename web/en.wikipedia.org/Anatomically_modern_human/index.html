Anatomically modern human

The modern human skull is globular, with a small face tucked beneath the braincase.

The term anatomically modern humans (AMH) or anatomically modern Homo sapiens refers in paleoanthropology to individual members of the species Homo sapiens with an appearance consistent with the range of phenotypes in modern humans — distinguished from more archaic forms by a lightly built skeleton, a high rounded (globular) skull, a small face retracted under the braincase, and a prominent chin.

Origins

Fossils from Jebel Irhoud in Morocco, dated to about 300,000 years ago, are among the earliest widely accepted anatomically modern remains, pushing the origin of our species well back into the Middle Stone Age and suggesting a pan-African emergence rather than a single East African "cradle". The Omo Kibish remains from Ethiopia (at least ~195,000 years old) and the Herto skulls (~160,000 years old) document the subsequent development of the modern form.

Dispersal and admixture

Anatomically modern humans dispersed out of Africa in multiple waves, the major successful expansion occurring roughly 70,000–50,000 years ago. Genetic evidence shows that these migrating populations interbred to a small extent with archaic humans they encountered, including Neanderthals (contributing ~1–4% of the genomes of most non-African people) and Denisovans. Whether "behavioural modernity" — art, ornament, and complex tools — arose gradually in Africa or suddenly remains debated.
