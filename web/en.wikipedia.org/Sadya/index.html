Sadya

A traditional sadya served on a banana leaf.

Sadya (Malayalam: സദ്യ) is a traditional feast in the Indian state of Kerala, typically served on a banana leaf. It is a vegetarian meal consisting of a wide variety of dishes — often more than two dozen — served in a specific order and at specific positions on the leaf. The sadya is the centrepiece of celebrations such as the harvest festival of Onam and is also served at weddings, birthdays, and other festive occasions in Kerala.

Serving and etiquette

The banana leaf is laid with its tip pointing to the left of the seated guest. Dishes are placed in a prescribed arrangement: pickles, banana chips, and sarkara varatti (jaggery-coated banana chips) on the left; salt, banana, and papadam; then the curries and side dishes along the top. Guests traditionally sit cross-legged on the floor and eat with the right hand, without cutlery. Servers move along the rows offering each item in sequence, and there are customary rules about accepting or declining a dish and about folding the leaf after the meal to indicate satisfaction.

Dishes

A typical sadya includes boiled rice served with parippu (dal with ghee) and sambar; side dishes such as avial (mixed vegetables in coconut and yoghurt), thoran (stir-fried vegetables with coconut), olan (ash gourd and beans in coconut milk), kaalan, erissery, pachadi, kichadi, injipuli (ginger-tamarind chutney), naranga curry (lime pickle), and rasam. The meal concludes with payasam, a sweet pudding — varieties include palada pradhaman, ada pradhaman, and parippu payasam — often eaten mixed with banana and papadam.

Onam sadya

The Onam sadya (Onasadya) is the grandest form of the feast, traditionally said to comprise 26 or more dishes. Grand communal sadyas, such as the Vallasadya at Aranmula, serve thousands of devotees, while snake-boat crews are honoured with elaborate multi-course servings.
