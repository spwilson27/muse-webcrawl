Chronic beryllium disease

Chronic beryllium disease (CBD), or berylliosis, is a chronic allergic-type lung disease caused by exposure to beryllium and its compounds. It is an occupational disease affecting workers in industries such as aerospace, electronics, ceramics, nuclear weapons production and beryllium extraction, and is distinct from acute beryllium poisoning.

Mechanism and symptoms

In genetically susceptible people — many carry the HLA-DPB1 Glu69 variant — inhaled beryllium triggers an immune response that produces granulomas, principally in the lungs. Symptoms develop months to decades after exposure and include shortness of breath, cough, fatigue, weight loss, joint pain and skin lesions. The condition can resemble sarcoidosis and may progress to pulmonary fibrosis and right heart failure.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis combines an exposure history, the beryllium lymphocyte proliferation test (BeLPT) demonstrating sensitisation, lung biopsy showing non-caseating granulomas, and imaging. There is no cure; removal from exposure and corticosteroids such as prednisone are the mainstays of treatment. Prevention relies on workplace exposure limits and protective equipment.
