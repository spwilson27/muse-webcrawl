Margraviate of Brandenburg

The Margraviate of Brandenburg (German: Markgrafschaft Brandenburg) was a major principality of the Holy Roman Empire from 1157 to 1806. It played a pivotal role in the history of central Europe and of Germany, providing the core territory of the later Kingdom of Prussia. Its rulers, the margraves, were among the seven prince-electors entitled to choose the Holy Roman Emperor. The margraviate's capital was first Brandenburg an der Havel, later Berlin-Cölln.

Origins

The territory, settled by Slavic peoples such as the Hevelli and Sprevane, was conquered in stages by German kings from the tenth century. In 1157 the Ascanian count Albert the Bear retook Brandenburg from the Slavic prince Jaxa of Köpenick and styled himself the first margrave. German settlement (the Ostsiedlung) followed, with towns, monasteries and bishoprics founded across the margraviate, which expanded eastward into the Neumark.

Later medieval and Hohenzollern rule

After the Ascanians died out in 1320, Brandenburg passed through Wittelsbach and Luxembourg hands — including the false Waldemar episode — before Emperor Sigismund granted it in 1415 to Frederick of Hohenzollern, burgrave of Nuremberg. The Hohenzollerns were to rule Brandenburg for five hundred years. In 1618 the margrave inherited the Duchy of Prussia, forming Brandenburg-Prussia, and in 1701 the elector Frederick III crowned himself King in Prussia as Frederick I.

Dissolution

The margraviate retained its name within the Prussian monarchy until the Holy Roman Empire was dissolved in 1806, whereupon it became the Prussian Province of Brandenburg. Today its territory is divided mainly between the German states of Brandenburg and Berlin and western Poland.
