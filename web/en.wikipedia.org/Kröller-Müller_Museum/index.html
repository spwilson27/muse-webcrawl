Kröller-Müller Museum

Kröller-Müller Museum
Location: Otterlo, Netherlands
Established: 1938
Founder: Helene Kröller-Müller

The Kröller-Müller Museum (Dutch: Kröller-Müller Museum) is a national art museum and sculpture garden in Otterlo, in the Netherlands, located within De Hoge Veluwe National Park.

Collection

Founded by collector Helene Kröller-Müller, the museum holds the second-largest collection of works by Vincent van Gogh in the world, including The Potato Eaters, Café Terrace at Night and a version of Sunflowers. The collection also includes works by Piet Mondrian, Georges Seurat, Pablo Picasso, Odilon Redon and Juan Gris.

Building and sculpture garden

The museum building, designed by Henry van de Velde, opened in 1938; a later wing by Benthem Crouwel opened in 1977. Its sculpture garden of about 25 hectares displays works by Auguste Rodin, Henry Moore, Barbara Hepworth, Jean Dubuffet and others, and includes Jardin d'émail by Dubuffet.
