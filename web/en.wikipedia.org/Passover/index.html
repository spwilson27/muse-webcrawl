Passover

HebrewPesach (פֶּסַח)
Observed byJews and Samaritans
DatesBegins 15 Nisan, lasts 7 days (8 in the Diaspora)
SignificanceCommemorates the Exodus from slavery in ancient Egypt
Key observancesSeder meal, eating matzah, abstaining from chametz

Passover, also called Pesach, is a major Jewish holiday celebrating the Exodus of the Israelites from slavery in Egypt, as told in the Book of Exodus. According to the biblical narrative, God "passed over" the houses of the Israelites during the tenth plague — the death of the firstborn — before Pharaoh released them. Passover begins on the 15th day of the Hebrew month of Nisan, in the spring, and lasts seven days in Israel and eight days among most Jews in the Diaspora.

Observances

The best-known ritual is the Seder, a ceremonial meal held on the first night (first two nights in the Diaspora), at which the Exodus story is retold using the Haggadah, the Four Questions are asked — traditionally by the youngest child — four cups of wine are drunk, and symbolic foods are eaten: matzah (unleavened bread), maror (bitter herbs), charoset, and a shank bone.

Throughout the festival, observant Jews refrain from eating or owning chametz — leavened grain products — commemorating the haste of the departure from Egypt, in which the Israelites' bread had no time to rise. Homes are cleaned of chametz before the holiday, and many sell their chametz symbolically for its duration.

Origins and history

Historians and biblical scholars debate the festival's origins; it may combine an older pastoral spring rite involving a sacrificial lamb with an agricultural festival of unleavened bread, later fused and historicized as a commemoration of the Exodus. In Second Temple times, pilgrims brought the paschal lamb sacrifice to Jerusalem. After the Temple's destruction in 70 CE, the home-based Seder became the center of observance.

Samaritan Passover

The Samaritans continue to perform the Passover sacrifice on Mount Gerizim, roasting whole lambs as prescribed in the Torah, a rite that draws spectators each year.
