Cabaret Voltaire

The Cabaret Voltaire was a nightclub in Zurich, Switzerland, founded by Hugo Ball and his partner Emmy Hennings. It opened on 5 February 1916 at Spiegelgasse 1 and is famous as the birthplace of the Dada art movement. Located in the back room of the Holländische Meierei tavern in Zurich's old town — on the same street where Lenin was then living in exile — the cabaret operated for only about six months, but launched one of the most influential avant-garde movements of the 20th century.

History

Ball and Hennings, German émigrés who had fled the First World War, announced the cabaret as a centre for artistic entertainment, inviting young artists of Zurich to perform regardless of creed. Nightly programmes included music, dance, poetry readings and experimental performances by figures such as Tristan Tzara, Jean Arp, Sophie Taeuber, Marcel Janco and Richard Huelsenbeck. In June 1916 Ball performed his sound poem "Karawane" in a stiff paper costume — a defining moment of Dada. The name "Dada" itself was adopted by the group around this time, its origin disputed ever since (variously attributed to a dictionary stabbing, a child's word for hobbyhorse, or the Romanian "da, da" — "yes, yes").

Revival

The building later fell into disrepair; in 2002 it was occupied by artists protesting its threatened redevelopment, and after eviction the city of Zurich supported its reopening as a cultural centre in 2004. The present-day Cabaret Voltaire hosts exhibitions, performances and a bar, and maintains a library devoted to Dada.
