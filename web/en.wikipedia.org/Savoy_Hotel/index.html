Savoy Hotel

The Savoy Hotel is a luxury hotel located on the Strand in the City of Westminster, London, overlooking the River Thames. Opened on 6 August 1889, it was built by impresario Richard D'Oyly Carte with profits from his Gilbert and Sullivan operas.

History

The Savoy was among the first luxury hotels in Britain, with electric lights, lifts ("ascending rooms"), and en-suite bathrooms with constant hot water. César Ritz was its first manager and Auguste Escoffier its head chef; both were later dismissed for fraud, and went on to found the Ritz hotels. Claude Monet painted views of the Thames from his room at the Savoy. The hotel's American Bar is one of the world's most famous cocktail bars.

The hotel today

The Savoy closed for a major restoration in 2007 and reopened in October 2010 after a renovation reported to cost over £220 million. It is managed by Fairmont Hotels and Resorts. The hotel stands beside the Savoy Theatre (1881) and gave its name to Savoy Court, famously the only road in Britain where vehicles drive on the right.
