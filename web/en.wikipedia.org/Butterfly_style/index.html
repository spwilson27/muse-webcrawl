Butterfly style
Ice hockey goaltending technique
Pioneers: Glenn Hall, Tony Esposito; popularised by Patrick Roy
Key feature: Dropping to knees with pads flared

In ice hockey, butterfly style is a technique of goaltending distinguished by the goaltender dropping to their knees to cover the lower part of the net with their leg pads flared out to the sides, resembling butterfly wings.

Technique

When a shot is expected low, the goaltender drives both knees down so the pads lie flat along the ice, sealing the five-hole and covering the bottom of the net, while the glove and blocker protect the upper corners and the chest stays tall. Modern variants include the reactive butterfly and the hybrid style blending butterfly with stand-up play.

History

Glenn Hall was an early exponent in the 1950s and 60s, followed by Tony Esposito. The style was systematised and popularised by Patrick Roy under coach François Allaire in the 1980s and 1990s, aided by larger, lighter leg pads. It is now the dominant foundation of goaltending technique worldwide.

Criticism

Critics note that dropping early can expose the top of the net and that the style places heavy stress on goaltenders' hips and knees.
