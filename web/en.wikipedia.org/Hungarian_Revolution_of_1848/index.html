Hungarian Revolution of 1848

The Hungarian Revolution of 1848 was one of the European revolutions of 1848 and a defining event in Hungarian history. Beginning with the bloodless uprising of 15 March 1848 in Pest, it produced a liberal reform government and then a war of independence against the Austrian Empire, which was crushed in 1849 with Russian help.

The revolution began in Pest on 15 March 1848, now a national holiday.

Background

In the Reform Era, liberals such as István Széchenyi and Lajos Kossuth pressed for modernisation, abolition of serfdom and greater autonomy from Vienna. News of revolution in Paris and Vienna in March 1848 triggered action in Hungary.

The revolution

On 15 March, students and intellectuals led by the poet Sándor Petőfi proclaimed the Twelve Points in Pest and recited Petőfi's National Song; the crowd freed the political prisoner Mihály Táncsics. The Diet at Pressburg passed the April Laws — abolishing serfdom, establishing a responsible Hungarian ministry under Prime Minister Lajos Batthyány, and modernising the state — which Emperor Ferdinand sanctioned.

War of independence

Conflict with Vienna and with Hungary's own national minorities, encouraged by the court, turned reform into war. Croatian Ban Josip Jelačić invaded in September 1848; Kossuth organised the Honvéd army, which won notable victories in the spring campaign of 1849. Hungary declared independence and deposed the Habsburgs in April 1849, but Austria called in Russian troops, and the outnumbered Hungarians surrendered at Világos in August 1849.

Legacy

Reprisals followed, including the execution of Batthyány and the thirteen Martyrs of Arad. Yet the April Laws' abolition of serfdom endured, and the compromise of 1867 eventually granted Hungary equal status in Austria-Hungary. 15 March remains one of Hungary's national holidays.
