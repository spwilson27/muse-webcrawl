NORAD

North American Aerospace Defense Command
Founded: May 12, 1958
Headquarters: Peterson Space Force Base, Colorado
Countries: United States and Canada
Motto: "We Have the Watch"

The North American Aerospace Defense Command (NORAD) is a combined organization of the United States and Canada that provides aerospace warning, aerospace control, and maritime warning for North America. It is the world's only binational military command.

History

NORAD was established in 1957–58, during the Cold War, to coordinate the air defense of North America against Soviet bombers. Its combat operations center was famously housed inside Cheyenne Mountain near Colorado Springs, a hardened facility blasted out of granite and designed to survive a nuclear attack. Radar lines across the Canadian Arctic, including the Distant Early Warning Line and its successor the North Warning System, fed the command's warning network.

Mission

NORAD's tasks include detecting and warning of aircraft, missile, and space threats to North America, and exercising sovereign airspace control, including intercepting errant or hostile aircraft. Since the September 11 attacks, Operation Noble Eagle has kept fighters on alert for domestic air defense. Day-to-day operations are run from Peterson Space Force Base, with Cheyenne Mountain retained as an alternate command center. The commander of NORAD is simultaneously head of U.S. Northern Command, and the deputy commander is a Canadian officer.

NORAD Tracks Santa

Since 1955, following a misprinted telephone number in a department-store advertisement, NORAD and its predecessor have run the NORAD Tracks Santa program each Christmas Eve, reporting the progress of Santa Claus's sleigh to children worldwide. See also Cheyenne Mountain Complex.
