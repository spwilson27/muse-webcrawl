Baltimore clipper

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Baltimore clipper is a fast sailing ship — historically a topsail schooner or brigantine — that was built in the mid-Atlantic United States, particularly at Fells Point, Baltimore, Maryland, from the mid-18th century into the mid-19th century. Baltimore clippers were celebrated for their speed and weatherliness, and were widely used as privateers and blockade runners, and also — notoriously — in the slave trade and opium trade, where speed was at a premium.

Design

The type developed from the pilot boats and Bermuda sloops of the colonial Chesapeake. Baltimore clippers were characterized by a long, low, raked hull with a sharply V-shaped (deadrise) cross-section, raked stem and sternposts, tall masts raked aft, and a large sail plan relative to their tonnage. They were rigged most commonly as topsail schooners with fore-and-aft sails plus square topsails, a plan that was fast on all points of sail and could be handled by a relatively small crew.

Uses and fame

During the American Revolutionary War and especially the War of 1812, Baltimore clippers such as the Chasseur — nicknamed the "Pride of Baltimore" — were among the most successful American privateers, provoking Britain to tighten the blockade of the Chesapeake. Their combination of speed and handiness also made them favorites of slavers seeking to evade the British West Africa Squadron and the U.S. Navy, and examples such as the Dos Amigos were captured in that trade. The design influenced the great clipper ships of the mid-19th century.

Replicas

The type is commemorated by the replica topsail schooner Pride of Baltimore (1977), lost in a squall in 1986, and her replacement Pride of Baltimore II (1988), which serves as a sailing ambassador for the city and state.
