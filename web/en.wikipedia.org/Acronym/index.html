Acronym

An acronym is an abbreviation formed from the initial components of a phrase or word, usually individual letters or parts of words. Familiar examples include NASA (National Aeronautics and Space Administration), laser (light amplification by stimulated emission of radiation), and scuba (self-contained underwater breathing apparatus).

Terminology

There is disagreement among linguists and lexicographers about the precise definition. In a narrower sense, only abbreviations pronounced as words (such as NATO or radar) are acronyms, while those pronounced as a sequence of letters (such as FBI or ATM) are called initialisms. Many dictionaries now use "acronym" for both. Some acronyms, like laser and scuba, have become ordinary words through lexicalisation and are no longer capitalised.

Use

Acronyms are common in science, technology, government, medicine, and business, where they save space and time. They also proliferate in military and bureaucratic language, sometimes to the point of obscurity. Backronyms — phrases constructed after the fact to fit an existing word, such as "SOS" interpreted as "Save Our Souls" — are folk etymologies built on acronym-like forms.
