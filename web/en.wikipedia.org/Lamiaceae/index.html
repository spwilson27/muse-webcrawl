The Lamiaceae (also called Labiatae) are a family of flowering plants commonly known as the mint, deadnettle, or sage family. Many members are aromatic in all parts and include widely used culinary herbs such as basil, mint, rosemary, sage, savory, marjoram, oregano, thyme, and lavender.

Description

Plants in the family typically have square stems, opposite leaves, and bilaterally symmetrical, two-lipped (bilabiate) flowers — the feature that gave the older name Labiatae, from the Latin for "lipped". The fruit usually splits into four nutlets. The family has a cosmopolitan distribution with some 236 genera and more than 7,000 species; the largest genera include Salvia, Scutellaria, Stachys, and Plectranthus.

Uses

Beyond culinary herbs, the family supplies ornamentals such as coleus and bee balm, timber trees like teak (now placed in Lamiaceae), and sources of essential oils and traditional medicines, including patchouli and Chinese skullcap.
