Sailing (sport)

The sport of sailing involves a variety of competitive sailing formats that are sanctioned through various sailing federations and yacht clubs. Racing disciplines include match racing, fleet racing, and team racing; additionally there are speed trials and competitions for remote-controlled boats. Sailing has been part of the Olympic Games since 1900 (it was scheduled for the 1896 Games but cancelled due to weather), and is governed internationally by World Sailing, founded in 1907.

Racing formats

In fleet racing, many boats race on the same course, typically around a set of marks, with scoring by finishing position under a low-points system. Match racing pits two boats directly against each other, as in the America's Cup, while team racing involves two teams of usually three boats each, combining individual places to determine a winner. Offshore races, such as the Fastnet Race and the Ocean Race (formerly Volvo Ocean Race), cover long distances over open water, and handicap systems such as IRC and ORC allow dissimilar boats to compete together.

Classes and equipment

Competition uses classes ranging from small dinghies such as the Optimist, Laser (ILCA), and 470, to keelboats, multihulls, and foiling craft such as the Nacra 17 and the AC75 monohulls of the America's Cup. At the Olympic Games, classes have changed repeatedly to reflect new technology and to balance men's and women's events.
