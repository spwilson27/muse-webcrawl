Fretwork

Fretwork is an interlaced decorative design that is either carved in low relief on a solid background, or cut out with a fretsaw, coping saw, or scroll saw to form an openwork pattern. The term is also used in architecture for decorative openwork railings, screens, and veranda trim, especially the wooden "gingerbread" ornament of Victorian houses.

History

Geometric fret patterns — repetitive meanders and interlocking key patterns — are ancient, appearing in Greek, Chinese, Egyptian, and Islamic art. Pierced fretwork in wood became especially popular in the 19th century, when pattern books and, later, treadle scroll saws put the craft within reach of amateur woodworkers. Fretwork panels were used in furniture, clock cases, picture frames, shelves, and architectural details.

Technique

A design is transferred to the workpiece, holes are drilled at interior points, and the saw blade is threaded through each hole in turn to cut out the waste. Thin plywood or hardwoods are typical materials. Modern fretworkers often use a powered scroll saw, while CNC routers and laser cutters now produce fretwork in quantity.
