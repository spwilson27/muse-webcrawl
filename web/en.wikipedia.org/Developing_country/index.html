A developing country is a sovereign state with a less developed industrial base and a lower Human Development Index (HDI) relative to other countries. There is no universally agreed definition: the World Bank classifies economies by gross national income per capita into low, lower-middle, upper-middle, and high income, and in recent years has phased out the term "developing world" in its publications.

Measurement

Countries that are neither developed nor less developed are commonly described as developing. Development is measured with statistical indices such as income per capita, life expectancy, literacy, and access to services, combined in measures like the HDI. The United Nations designates 44 states (as of the mid-2020s) as least developed countries (LDCs), which receive special support measures.

Characteristics and debates

Developing countries typically have lower living standards, a higher share of employment in agriculture, faster population growth, and less developed infrastructure, though there is wide diversity among them — from emerging industrial powers such as China and India to fragile states. Critics note that the developed/developing binary can be misleading, and terms such as "Global South" are often used instead.
