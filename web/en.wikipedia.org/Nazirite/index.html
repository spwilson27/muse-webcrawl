Nazirite

In the Hebrew Bible, a Nazirite (Hebrew: נזיר, nazir, "consecrated" or "separated") is an individual who voluntarily takes a special vow described in Numbers 6:1–21. The vow sets the person apart as holy to God for a defined period, or in some cases for life.

The vow

During the period of the vow, the Nazirite must observe three principal restrictions:

  • Abstain from wine and all products of the grapevine, including grapes and raisins.
  • Refrain from cutting the hair of the head, letting the locks grow long.
  • Avoid contact with corpses, even those of close family members, because corpse impurity would break the vow.

If the vow is broken, for example by accidental contact with the dead, the Nazirite must shave the head, bring offerings, and begin the period anew. At the successful completion of the vow, the Nazirite shaves the consecrated hair at the entrance of the sanctuary and offers it on the altar together with prescribed sacrifices.

Biblical figures

The most famous Nazirite is Samson, whose uncut hair was the source of his strength and whose vow was announced before his birth (Judges 13). The prophet Samuel is also described as a lifelong Nazirite dedicated by his mother Hannah (1 Samuel 1). The Mishnah tractate Nazir is devoted to the laws of the vow, including the special category of the "Samson Nazirite."

Later practice

In the Second Temple period, Nazirite vows remained popular as an expression of piety; the New Testament describes the Apostle Paul participating in the completion rituals of men under such vows (Acts 21). After the destruction of the Temple in 70 CE, the sacrificial completion rite became impossible, and the practice largely lapsed, though it has been occasionally revived.
