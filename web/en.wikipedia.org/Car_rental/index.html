Car rental

A row of rental cars awaiting customers (placeholder)

A car rental, hire car or car hire agency is a company that rents automobiles for short periods of time to the public, generally ranging from a few hours to a few weeks. It is often organised with numerous local branches (which allow a user to return a vehicle to a different location), and is primarily located near airports or busy city areas, often complemented by a website allowing online reservations.

History

The earliest known example of cars offered for rent dates to 1906 in Germany. In the United States, Joe Saunders of Omaha, Nebraska, began renting a Model T Ford in 1916, founding the business that became Saunders Drive-It-Yourself. John Hertz built up a major rental operation in Chicago in the 1920s (later Hertz), and after the Second World War airport-based rental expanded rapidly with air travel; Avis (founded 1946 by Warren Avis) pioneered airport locations and the famous "We try harder" slogan. Today the industry is dominated globally by groups such as Enterprise Holdings (Enterprise, National, Alamo), Hertz, and Avis Budget Group, alongside regional firms and car-sharing services.

Business model and conditions

Rental companies buy or lease large fleets of new vehicles and rent them at daily or weekly rates; vehicles are later sold through dealership channels or auctions ("risk cars" and manufacturer-repurchase "program cars"). Renters typically must hold a driving licence, meet a minimum age (often 21–25, with young-driver surcharges), and provide a credit or debit card. Contracts include mileage terms, fuel policies, insurance and damage waivers (such as collision damage waiver), cross-border restrictions, and additional-driver fees. Prices vary sharply by season, location, and booking channel, and extra products — GPS, child seats, additional insurance — form a significant share of revenue.
