Ronald Linn Rivest (born May 6, 1947) is an American cryptographer and computer scientist, a professor at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) and a member of MIT's Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence Laboratory (CSAIL). He is best known as a co-inventor of the RSA public-key cryptosystem and as the designer of widely used symmetric ciphers and cryptographic hash functions.

Education and career

Rivest earned a bachelor's degree in mathematics from Yale University in 1969 and a Ph.D. in computer science from Stanford University in 1974. At MIT he co-founded the cryptography and information security group. With Adi Shamir and Leonard Adleman, he published the RSA algorithm in 1977 — the "R" in RSA — one of the first practical public-key cryptosystems, which became a foundation of secure communication on the internet. The three co-founded RSA Data Security to commercialize the technology.

Contributions

Rivest designed the RC family of symmetric encryption algorithms (RC2, RC4, RC5, and RC6) and the MD2, MD4, and MD5 message-digest algorithms; MD5 was for years one of the most widely deployed hash functions in the world. More recently he has worked on election security and verifiable voting systems, proposing methods such as the ThreeBallot voting protocol. He is also a co-author, with Thomas Cormen, Charles Leiserson, and Clifford Stein, of Introduction to Algorithms, the standard textbook in the field.

Awards

Rivest shared the 2002 ACM Turing Award with Shamir and Adleman for the "ingenious contribution for making public-key cryptography useful in practice". He is a member of the National Academy of Sciences and the National Academy of Engineering, and was inducted into the National Inventors Hall of Fame.
