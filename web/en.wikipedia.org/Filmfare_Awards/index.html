The Filmfare Awards are annual awards honouring artistic and technical excellence in Hindi-language films in India. Presented by Filmfare magazine of The Times Group since 1954, they are among the oldest and most prominent film awards in India, frequently compared with the Academy Awards in prestige within the Hindi film industry ("Bollywood"). The trophy, a statuette of a woman in a dance pose, is popularly known as "The Black Lady".

History

The awards were first held on 21 March 1954 at the Metro Theatre in Bombay, honouring films of 1953; Do Bigha Zamin won Best Film and Dilip Kumar won Best Actor for Daag. Originally called the Clare Awards, after Times of India critic Clare Mendonca, they were renamed for the magazine. Winners were initially chosen by readers' votes; a dual system later combined popular voting with a critics'/jury selection, and Filmfare also instituted separate Critics Awards for Best Film and performances. The ceremonies grew into major televised spectacles with stage performances.

Categories and records

Major categories include Best Film, Best Director, Best Actor and Actress (leading, supporting and critics' variants), Best Music Director, Best Lyricist and playback singing awards, plus technical categories for cinematography, editing, choreography and action. Dilip Kumar, Shah Rukh Khan and Amitabh Bachchan are among the most decorated performers; Gulzar, A. R. Rahman and others hold records in music categories. Related ceremonies include the Filmfare Awards South (for Telugu, Tamil, Kannada and Malayalam cinema, from 1954), and regional Marathi, Bengali, Punjabi and OTT (web series) editions.

Criticism

The awards have periodically faced criticism over the influence of popular voting, lobbying, and the perceived favouring of star families and commercially successful films, prompting boycott calls and debate about credibility — criticisms common to major popular film awards.
