Elizabeth Anne Holmes (born February 3, 1984) is an American former biotechnology entrepreneur who was convicted of fraud in connection with her blood-testing company Theranos. She founded the company in 2003 after dropping out of Stanford University and raised more than 00 million from venture capitalists and private investors, claiming its technology could run a wide range of laboratory tests from a few drops of blood.

Rise and fall of Theranos

At its peak in 2013–2014, Theranos was valued at about billion, and Holmes was widely celebrated as the world's youngest self-made female billionaire. In October 2015, investigative reporting by The Wall Street Journal revealed that the company's proprietary devices performed only a small fraction of the tests advertised, with most analyses run on standard machines from other manufacturers. Regulators subsequently banned Holmes from operating a laboratory, and Theranos dissolved in 2018.

Trial and imprisonment

In January 2022, a federal jury convicted Holmes on four counts of defrauding investors. She was sentenced to 11 years and 3 months in federal prison and began serving her sentence in May 2023.
