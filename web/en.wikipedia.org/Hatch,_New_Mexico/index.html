Hatch, New Mexico

Hatch is a village in Doña Ana County, New Mexico, United States. The population was about 1,600 at recent censuses. Hatch is best known as the self-proclaimed "Chile Capital of the World" for its production of New Mexico chile peppers, and hosts the annual Hatch Chile Festival each Labor Day weekend.

Geography

Hatch lies in the Hatch Valley along the Rio Grande in southern New Mexico, roughly 40 miles northwest of Las Cruces. Interstate 25 passes just east of the village. The surrounding irrigated farmland, watered from Elephant Butte and Caballo reservoirs upstream, supports chile, onion, pecan and cotton production in the fertile river valley.

History

The settlement grew around a station on the Atchison, Topeka and Santa Fe Railway in the early 1880s and was named for General Edward Hatch, a Union officer who commanded the military district of New Mexico. The community was originally called Santa Barbara; flooding from the Rio Grande periodically damaged the town before upstream dams moderated the river. Hatch was incorporated as a village in the 1920s.

Chile production

The Hatch Valley's hot days, cool nights and rich soil make it renowned for New Mexico chile, especially green chile harvested in late summer and roasted for distribution nationwide. Varieties developed nearby at New Mexico State University, such as 'NuMex Big Jim', were bred for the region. The Hatch Chile Festival, founded in 1971, draws tens of thousands of visitors each September for roasting, cooking contests and the crowning of a Chile Queen.

Transportation and education

New Mexico State Road 26 connects Hatch southwest to Deming, and State Road 187 follows the river valley. The village is served by Hatch Valley Public Schools. The nearby Spaceport America, the commercial spaceport east of the valley near Truth or Consequences, has brought additional attention to the area.
