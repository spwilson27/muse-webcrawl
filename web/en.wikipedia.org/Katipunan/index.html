The Katipunan, officially the Kataas-taasang, Kagalanggalangang Katipunan ng mga Anak ng Bayan ("Supreme and Venerable Association of the Children of the Nation", KKK), was a Philippine revolutionary society founded in 1892 by Andrés Bonifacio and others in Manila. Its aim was to gain independence from Spanish colonial rule through armed revolution, after the failure of peaceful reform movements such as José Rizal's La Liga Filipina.

Organisation

The secret society recruited through a triangle system, used Masonic-style rituals, grades (Katipon, Kawal, Bayani), passwords, and the newspaper Kalayaan. Members swore oaths in blood. Women were admitted to an auxiliary section; Bonifacio's wife Gregoria de Jesús guarded the society's documents.

Revolution

Discovered by the Spanish in August 1896, the Katipunan launched the Philippine Revolution with the "Cry of Pugad Lawin", tearing up their residence certificates (cedulas). Fighting spread across Luzon; in Cavite, Emilio Aguinaldo's faction won early victories. Rivalry between the Magdalo and Magdiwang councils led to the Tejeros Convention of 1897, Bonifacio's displacement, arrest, and execution. Aguinaldo later negotiated the Pact of Biak-na-Bato, dissolved the Katipunan, and went into exile, before returning in 1898 during the Spanish–American War to proclaim Philippine independence.
