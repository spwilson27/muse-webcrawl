Relief valve

A relief valve is a type of safety valve used to control or limit the pressure in a system; pressure might otherwise build up and create a process upset, equipment failure, explosion, or fire. The valve opens at a preset pressure to divert fluid from the protected system.

Types

The most common design is the spring-loaded pressure relief valve, in which the process pressure acts against a spring; when the set pressure is exceeded, the valve lifts and vents fluid, reseating when pressure falls. Pilot-operated relief valves use a small pilot valve to control the main valve and offer tighter shutoff near the set point.

Terminology

"Relief valve" is often used for liquid service, "safety valve" for gas or steam service, and "safety relief valve" for devices suitable for both. Relief valves are essential components of boilers, pressure vessels, hydraulic systems, and pipelines, and are governed by codes such as the ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code.
