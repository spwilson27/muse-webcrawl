Balling scale

The Balling scale is a hydrometer scale used to measure the concentration of dissolved solids — principally sugars — in aqueous solutions, expressed as the percentage by weight of sucrose in solution at a reference temperature (originally 17.5 °C). It is closely related to, and largely superseded by, the Brix scale.

History

The scale was developed by Bohemian chemist Karl Josef Napoleon Balling in 1843 for the brewing industry. Adolf Brix later recalculated the tables at 15.5 °C, giving the Brix scale, while Fritz Plato's corrections (published around 1900, referenced to 20 °C) produced the Plato scale widely used by professional brewers.

Use

Brewers and winemakers use such scales to estimate the original extract of wort or must, and hence potential alcohol content. Degrees Balling, Brix, and Plato are numerically close and are often used interchangeably in practice, particularly in homebrewing.
