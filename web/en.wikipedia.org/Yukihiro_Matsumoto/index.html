Yukihiro Matsumoto

Yukihiro Matsumoto (Japanese: 松本行弘, born 14 April 1965), also known as Matz, is a Japanese computer scientist and software programmer best known as the chief designer of the Ruby programming language and its original reference implementation, Matz's Ruby Interpreter (MRI).

Early life and education

Matsumoto was born in Tottori Prefecture, Japan. He taught himself programming while still in high school, and graduated with a degree in information science from the University of Tsukuba. He became interested in programming language design early, studying languages such as Lisp, Smalltalk, and Perl.

Ruby

Matsumoto began work on Ruby in February 1993, wanting a scripting language that was fully object-oriented, more powerful than Perl, and designed for programmer productivity and enjoyment — a philosophy often summarized as optimizing for programmer happiness. The first public release of Ruby (version 0.95) appeared in December 1995. Ruby gained international attention in the mid-2000s through the Ruby on Rails web framework.

Matsumoto remains Ruby's chief designer and "benevolent dictator," overseeing the language's development and its standard implementation. He has also worked on projects including the lightweight Ruby implementation mruby and the Streem programming language.

Career and recognition

Matsumoto has worked for Japanese companies including Network Applied Communication Laboratory (NaCl) in Matsue, Shimane Prefecture, where he is a fellow, and he served as chief architect of Ruby at Heroku and later Stripe. In 2011 he received the Award for the Advancement of Free Software from the Free Software Foundation. The Japanese government has also honored him for his contributions to open-source software. Matsumoto is a member of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints and served as a missionary in his youth.
