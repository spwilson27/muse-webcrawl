Political violence

Political violence encompasses violence by both state and non-state actors pursuing political goals.

Political violence is violence perpetrated by people or governments to achieve political goals. It includes acts by states against other states or against civilians and non-state actors, and acts by non-state actors such as insurgents, militias, and terrorist groups. Many types of political violence are covered under different concepts in the social sciences.

Forms

Forms of political violence include war, civil war, genocide, ethnic cleansing, state repression and political terror, torture, forced disappearance, insurgency and counterinsurgency, terrorism, assassination, rioting, and communal or electoral violence. Revolutions and coups d'état typically involve political violence as well.

Study

Scholars analyze the causes of political violence at several levels: individual grievances and ideology, group polarization and mobilization, and structural conditions such as state weakness, inequality, and ethnic demography. Theorists from Frantz Fanon, who argued that anti-colonial violence could be a "cleansing force", to Hannah Arendt, who sharply distinguished violence from power, have debated its legitimacy and consequences. Data projects such as the Uppsala Conflict Data Program systematically record armed conflicts and one-sided violence.
