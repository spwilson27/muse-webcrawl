Differential one-form

FieldDifferential geometry
DefinitionA section of the cotangent bundle; a linear functional on tangent vectors
GeneralizationDifferential forms of higher degree

In differential geometry, a differential one-form (or simply one-form) on a smooth manifold is a smooth section of the cotangent bundle: at each point it assigns a linear map from the tangent space to the real numbers. One-forms are the natural objects to integrate along curves.

Definition and examples

In local coordinates, a one-form is written α = f1 dx1 + … + fn dxn, where the fi are smooth functions. The differential df of a function is a one-form; in elementary calculus, expressions such as P dx + Q dy in line integrals are one-forms. Applied to a vector field, a one-form produces a function — geometrically, it measures how the vector crosses a stack of level hypersurfaces.

Operations

One-forms can be pulled back by smooth maps, combined with the exterior derivative d to produce a two-form, and related to vector fields via a Riemannian metric (the "musical isomorphisms"). A one-form is exact if it equals df for some function, and closed if its exterior derivative vanishes; the difference between the two is measured by de Rham cohomology. In physics, one-forms appear as work and heat forms in thermodynamics, potentials in electromagnetism, and momentum covectors in Hamiltonian mechanics.
