Cross of Gold speech

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Cross of Gold speech was a speech delivered by William Jennings Bryan, a former United States congressman from Nebraska, at the Democratic National Convention in Chicago on July 9, 1896. In the address, Bryan supported bimetallism or "free silver", which he believed would bring the nation prosperity. He decried the gold standard, concluding the speech with the famous line: "You shall not crucify mankind upon a cross of gold."

Background

The United States had been embroiled in a bitter debate over monetary policy since the 1870s. Farmers and debtors, hurt by deflation, demanded the free coinage of silver at a ratio of 16 to 1 with gold, which would expand the money supply. Eastern financial interests defended the gold standard. The Panic of 1893 intensified the conflict and split the Democratic Party.

The speech and its effect

Bryan, only 36 years old and little known nationally before the convention, spoke last in the debate over the party platform. His dramatic delivery, including holding his arms outstretched like a crucified figure at the peroration, electrified the delegates. The convention nominated him for president the next day, making him the youngest major-party nominee in American history. Although Bryan lost the general election to William McKinley, the address is widely regarded as one of the most famous political speeches in United States history and made Bryan the dominant figure in the Democratic Party for a generation.
