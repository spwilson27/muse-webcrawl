Mother goddess

A mother goddess is a goddess who represents motherhood, fertility, creation, or the Earth itself, or who embodies the bounty of nature. Mother goddesses appear in many religious traditions, ancient and modern, and are often associated with the earth, agriculture, and the cycle of life and death.

Examples

Ancient examples include the Sumerian Ninhursag, the Egyptian Isis, the Greek Demeter and Gaia, the Phrygian Cybele (the Magna Mater of Rome), the Hindu goddess Devi in her many forms, and the Chinese Mazu and Japanese Amaterasu in related maternal/protective roles. In Mesoamerica, figures such as Coatlicue played comparable roles. Some scholars have interpreted Upper Paleolithic "Venus figurines" as evidence of prehistoric mother-goddess worship, though this reading is debated.

Theories

In the 19th and early 20th centuries, theorists such as Johann Jakob Bachofen proposed that early human societies were matriarchal and centred on goddess worship. In the mid-20th century, archaeologist Marija Gimbutas argued for a goddess-centred "Old Europe". These universalizing theories are now treated cautiously by most specialists, who prefer to study particular goddesses in their local contexts. Mother goddesses nevertheless remain important in modern pagan and goddess-movement spirituality, and the concept of "Mother Earth" or "Mother Nature" is widespread in popular culture.
