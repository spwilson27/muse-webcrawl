Troponin C

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Troponin C is a protein that is a component of troponin, the calcium-sensitive regulatory complex attached to actin filaments in striated muscle. When calcium ions released by the sarcoplasmic reticulum bind to troponin C, it changes conformation and, through troponin I and troponin T, moves tropomyosin away from myosin-binding sites on actin, permitting cross-bridge cycling and muscle contraction.

Structure

Troponin C is a small dumbbell-shaped protein of the EF-hand family, with two globular domains joined by a central helix. The C-terminal domain contains two high-affinity sites that bind Ca²⁺ or Mg²⁺ and anchor the protein to troponin I. The N-terminal domain contains the low-affinity regulatory sites whose calcium binding triggers contraction in skeletal muscle.

Isoforms and clinical relevance

Humans express two isoforms: TNNC1, found in cardiac muscle and slow skeletal muscle, and TNNC2, found in fast skeletal muscle. Because the cardiac isoform is also present in slow skeletal fibres, it is not the subunit measured in blood tests for heart attack — that role belongs to troponin I and troponin T. Mutations in TNNC1 can cause hypertrophic and dilated cardiomyopathy, and the drug levosimendan acts in part as a calcium sensitiser by binding troponin C.
