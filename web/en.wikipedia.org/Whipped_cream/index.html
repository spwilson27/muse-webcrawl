Whipped cream

TypeDairy topping
Main ingredientsHeavy cream, sugar, vanilla
Food energy~250 kcal per 100 g

Whipped cream is heavy cream that has been beaten by a whisk, electric mixer, or other means until it is light and fluffy and able to hold soft or firm peaks. It is often sweetened and sometimes flavoured with vanilla, and is used as a topping for desserts such as pies, cakes, hot chocolate, and fruit.

Preparation

Cream with a fat content of roughly 30–36% or higher whips best, because the fat globules trap air bubbles beaten into the liquid and form a stable foam. The cream must be cold; warm cream will not whip properly. Sugar — usually powdered sugar — and flavourings are added near the end of whipping. Over-whipping separates the foam into butter and buttermilk.

History

Whipped cream has been known in European cuisine since at least the 16th century; a recipe appears in the writings of Cristoforo di Messisbugo in 1549. In French it is called crème fouettée or, when sweetened and flavoured with vanilla, crème Chantilly, after the Château de Chantilly where the chef François Vatel is said to have served it.

Pressurised cream

Since the 20th century, whipped cream has been sold in aerosol cans, in which cream is combined with nitrous oxide gas; when the valve is opened, the gas expands and whips the cream instantly. Such products are convenient but less stable than hand-whipped cream.
