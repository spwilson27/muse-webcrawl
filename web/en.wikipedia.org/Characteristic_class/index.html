Characteristic class

FieldAlgebraic topology, differential geometry
Associated toPrincipal bundles, vector bundles
ExamplesStiefel–Whitney, Chern, Pontryagin, Euler classes

A characteristic class is a way of associating to each principal bundle (or vector bundle) over a topological space a cohomology class of the base space, in such a way that isomorphic bundles receive the same class. Characteristic classes are global invariants that measure the extent to which a bundle is "twisted" — that is, how it differs from a simple product space.

Definition

Given a topological group G, a characteristic class for principal G-bundles assigns to each bundle over a base B a class in the cohomology of B, naturally with respect to pullback along continuous maps. By the theory of classifying spaces, such classes correspond to cohomology classes of the classifying space BG; for example, characteristic classes for real vector bundles live in the cohomology of the infinite Grassmannian.

Examples

The most important families are the Stiefel–Whitney classes of real vector bundles, valued in mod-2 cohomology and obstructing properties such as orientability and the existence of spin structures; the Chern classes of complex vector bundles, which generalize the notion of degree; the Pontryagin classes of real bundles, which are rational invariants related to Chern classes; and the Euler class, whose evaluation on the fundamental class gives the Euler characteristic.

Applications

Characteristic classes are fundamental tools in topology and geometry. They provide obstructions to the existence of nowhere-vanishing vector fields and parallelizations of manifolds, enter the formulas of the Hirzebruch signature theorem and the Atiyah–Singer index theorem through characteristic numbers, and, through the Chern–Weil construction, connect bundle topology to the curvature of connections. They also appear in mathematical physics, for instance in the quantization of magnetic charge and the theory of anomalies.
