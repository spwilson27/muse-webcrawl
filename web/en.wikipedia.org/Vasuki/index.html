Vasuki

Affiliation: Naga
Abode: Patala
Parents: Kashyapa and Kadru
Siblings: Shesha, Manasa, Takshaka

Vasuki (Sanskrit: वासुकि) is a serpent king in Hindu mythology, one of the nagas, the sons of the sage Kashyapa and Kadru. He is a devotee of Shiva and is depicted coiled around the god's neck; Shiva blessed him and wore him as an ornament after Vasuki sought refuge with him.

Churning of the ocean

Vasuki is most famous for his role in the Samudra Manthana, the churning of the Ocean of Milk: the devas and asuras wrapped him around Mount Mandara and used him as the churning rope, pulling him back and forth. From the strain, Vasuki is said to have exhaled the poison halahala, which Shiva drank. A temple to Vasuki stands at Nagalapuram, and he is also venerated in Jain and Buddhist traditions, where he appears among the eight great Naga kings.
