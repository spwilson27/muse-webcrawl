Andean civilizations

The Andean civilizations were a complex of indigenous societies that developed in the Andes mountains and Pacific coast of South America, from the Norte Chico (Caral) culture of the third millennium BC to the Inca Empire, conquered by Spain in the 1530s. They were among the world's few "pristine" civilisations, arising independently of outside influence.

Development

The Norte Chico civilisation built monumental platforms at Caral around 3000–1800 BC. Later cultures included Chavín, Paracas, Nazca (famous for the Nazca Lines), Moche (renowned for portrait pottery and the huacas of the north coast), Tiwanaku and Wari — the first Andean states to expand over large territories — and Chimú, builders of the great adobe city of Chan Chan.

The Inca Empire

The Inca expanded from Cuzco in the 15th century to rule the largest empire in the pre-Columbian Americas, administering millions of subjects through road networks, terraced agriculture, storehouses, and the knotted-string records called quipu — all without wheeled transport, alphabetic writing, or iron tools.

Legacy

Andean peoples domesticated the potato, quinoa, llama, and alpaca. After the Spanish conquest, Quechua and Aymara languages and many cultural practices survived and remain central to the region's identity.
