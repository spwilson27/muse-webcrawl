Teetotalism

Teetotalism is the practice or promotion of complete personal abstinence from the consumption of alcohol, specifically in alcoholic drinks. A person who practices teetotalism is called a teetotaler (US spelling) or teetotaller (UK spelling). The teetotal movement was first started in Preston, England, in the early 19th century, and grew within the wider temperance movement.

Origin of the term

The word is usually attributed to a meeting of the Preston Temperance Society in 1833, at which former fisherman Richard "Dicky" Turner, urging total abstinence, declared that "nothing but te-te-total will do." The term emphasizes total abstinence — "T-total" — as opposed to moderation pledges that permitted beer or wine.

Reasons for teetotalism

People abstain for reasons including religion (many Muslims, Mormons, Seventh-day Adventists, and some other faiths forbid alcohol), health, recovery from alcoholism, family circumstances, personal preference, or legal age. Organizations such as the Independent Order of Rechabites and the Woman's Christian Temperance Union promoted the pledge; Alcoholics Anonymous supports abstinence as the basis of recovery.
