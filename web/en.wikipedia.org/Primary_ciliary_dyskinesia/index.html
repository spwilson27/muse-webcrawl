Primary ciliary dyskinesia

Primary ciliary dyskinesia (PCD), also known as immotile cilia syndrome, is a rare inherited disorder characterized by defects in the action of cilia lining the respiratory tract, fallopian tubes, and sperm flagella. Because mucociliary clearance fails, affected individuals suffer chronic recurrent infections of the lungs, sinuses, and ears from infancy, which can progress to bronchiectasis.

Signs and genetics

PCD is usually inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern and can result from mutations in dozens of genes encoding ciliary structural proteins, including dynein arm components. Roughly half of patients have situs inversus—mirror-image arrangement of the internal organs—because embryonic nodal cilia that establish left-right asymmetry are also defective; PCD with situs inversus and sinusitis is known as Kartagener syndrome. Male infertility from immotile sperm and female subfertility are also common.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis combines clinical features with nasal nitric oxide measurement (typically very low in PCD), high-speed video analysis of ciliary beating, transmission electron microscopy of ciliary ultrastructure, and genetic testing. There is no cure; management centres on airway clearance physiotherapy, prompt antibiotic treatment of infections, and monitoring of lung function.
