Dolores Hidalgo
Country: Mexico
State: Guanajuato
Full name: Dolores Hidalgo Cuna de la Independencia Nacional
Known for: Grito de Dolores (1810)

Dolores Hidalgo, officially Dolores Hidalgo Cuna de la Independencia Nacional ("Cradle of National Independence"), is a city in the Mexican state of Guanajuato. It is famed as the site where the priest Miguel Hidalgo y Costilla issued the Grito de Dolores on 16 September 1810, the cry that began the Mexican War of Independence from Spain.

History

The town, originally known simply as Dolores, was a parish where Hidalgo served as priest. In the early hours of 16 September 1810, Hidalgo rang the church bell and called on his parishioners to take up arms against Spanish rule. The church, the Parroquia de Nuestra Señora de los Dolores, remains a central landmark. After independence the town was renamed in Hidalgo's honor.

Culture and economy

Dolores Hidalgo is known for its ceramics industry, particularly brightly painted Talavera-style pottery produced by local workshops, and for ice creams sold in the main plaza in unusual flavors, including avocado, mole, and tequila. Tourism centers on independence-related sites including Hidalgo's house, now a museum, and the independence museum.
