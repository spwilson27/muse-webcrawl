Isabella II (Spanish: Isabel II; 10 October 1830 – 9 April 1904) was Queen of Spain from 1833 until her deposition in 1868. The only surviving daughter of Ferdinand VII, she ascended the throne as an infant after her father's Pragmatic Sanction of 1830 set aside the Salic law, provoking the claim of her uncle Don Carlos and the First Carlist War.

Regency and majority

Her mother, Maria Christina of the Two Sicilies, ruled as regent until 1840, when General Baldomero Espartero forced her into exile and took over the regency. Isabella was declared of age at thirteen in 1843. In 1846 she was married, against her wishes, to her cousin Francis, Duke of Cádiz; the marriage was unhappy and rumours about the paternity of her children dogged the reign.

Reign

Isabella's reign was marked by political instability: liberal progresistas and conservative moderados alternated in power, punctuated by military pronunciamientos led by generals such as Narváez, Espartero and O'Donnell. The queen intervened personally in politics, favoured conservative and clerical interests, and relied heavily on court camarillas. Spain saw railway building, the 1851 Concordat with the Vatican, and colonial war in Morocco (1859–60).

Deposition and exile

Growing corruption, economic crisis, and the queen's personal scandals culminated in the Glorious Revolution of September 1868; after the Battle of Alcolea, Isabella fled to France. She formally abdicated in Paris in 1870 in favour of her son Alfonso, who was restored as Alfonso XII in 1874. Isabella lived mostly in Paris, visiting Spain occasionally, and died there in 1904. She was buried in the Royal Crypt of El Escorial.
