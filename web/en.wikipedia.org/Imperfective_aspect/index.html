Imperfective aspect

Imperfective aspect
[Image: Timeline diagram contrasting an ongoing (imperfective) event with a completed (perfective) one]
CategoryGrammatical aspect
NotationIPFV
OppositePerfective aspect

The imperfective (abbreviated IPFV) is a grammatical aspect that presents a situation as incomplete, ongoing, habitual, or internally structured, without reference to its endpoints. It contrasts with the perfective aspect, which presents a situation as a single completed whole.

Meaning and use

Imperfective forms typically express actions in progress at a reference time ("she was reading"), repeated or habitual actions ("he walks to work"), general truths, and background description in narrative. Because it views a situation from inside, the imperfective does not entail completion: "I was writing a letter" does not imply the letter was finished — a puzzle known as the imperfective paradox in formal semantics.

In particular languages

Slavic languages such as Russian grammaticalise the perfective/imperfective opposition in almost every verb, usually with distinct verb lexemes (pisat' "to write", imperfective, versus napisat', perfective). In French and Spanish, the past-tense contrast between the imperfect (imparfait/imperfecto) and the simple past reflects the same opposition. English expresses imperfective meanings mainly through the progressive ("is reading") and habitual constructions, while Mandarin Chinese marks ongoing action with particles such as 在 and 著.
