The Kepler Track is a 60-kilometre circular tramping track in Fiordland National Park, in the south-western corner of the South Island of New Zealand. It is one of the New Zealand Great Walks administered by the Department of Conservation, and usually takes three to four days to complete.

Route

The track begins near the control gates of Lake Te Anau and passes through beech forest along the shore before climbing above the bushline to Luxmore Hut, with a side trip available to the summit of Mount Luxmore (1,472 m) and to the Luxmore Caves. The route continues along exposed alpine ridges, descends via a long series of switchbacks to Iris Burn Hut, then follows the Iris Burn valley and the Waiau River past Moturau Hut on Lake Manapouri, returning to Te Anau.

History and events

Unlike most Fiordland tracks, which follow historic trade or travel routes, the Kepler Track was purpose-built and opened in 1988 to relieve pressure on the Milford and Routeburn tracks. It is named after the Kepler Mountains, themselves named for the astronomer Johannes Kepler. The track hosts the annual Kepler Challenge mountain run, in which competitors complete the full loop in a single day.
