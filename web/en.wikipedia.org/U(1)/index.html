U(1)

U(1) is the group of points on the unit circle
TypeLie group, abelian group
ElementsComplex numbers of modulus 1
Isomorphic toCircle group; SO(2)

In mathematics, the unitary group of degree 1, written U(1), is the group of all complex numbers with absolute value 1 under multiplication. It is the simplest non-trivial compact, connected Lie group, and is isomorphic to the circle group and to the rotation group SO(2).

Every element of U(1) can be written as eiθ for a real angle θ, so the group can be visualized as the unit circle in the complex plane, with group multiplication corresponding to addition of angles. U(1) is abelian (commutative), and it appears as the subgroup of 1×1 unitary matrices in the general unitary group U(n).

In physics

U(1) is of central importance in physics as a gauge group: quantum electrodynamics is a U(1) gauge theory, in which the U(1) symmetry corresponds to conservation of electric charge. In the Standard Model, the electroweak interaction is based on the gauge group SU(2) × U(1), whose spontaneous symmetry breaking gives mass to the W and Z bosons while leaving the photon massless. U(1) symmetries also appear throughout condensed matter and string theory.
