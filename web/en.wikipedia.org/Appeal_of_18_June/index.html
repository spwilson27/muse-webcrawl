The Appeal of 18 June (l'Appel du 18 juin) is a speech by French General Charles de Gaulle, broadcast by BBC radio from London on 18 June 1940. In it, de Gaulle — a junior minister who had fled France as it sought an armistice with Nazi Germany — rejected the armistice and called on French forces and citizens to continue the war alongside Britain.

Content and reception

De Gaulle declared that France had lost a battle but not the war, arguing that the conflict was a world war in which France could draw on its empire and the industrial power of Britain and eventually the United States. The broadcast was heard by few at the time — de Gaulle was then little known — but the speech was reprinted in newspapers and a related appeal was spread through the famous poster "À tous les Français". It is regarded as the founding act of the French Resistance and of Free France.

Legacy

The appeal is commemorated annually in France; the original poster and the memory of 18 June 1940 are central to Gaullist tradition. UNESCO inscribed the appeal's texts in the Memory of the World register, and the date is marked at the Mont-Valérien memorial and at ceremonies in London and Paris.
