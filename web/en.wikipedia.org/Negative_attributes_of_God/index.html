Negative attributes of God

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The doctrine of the negative attributes of God (via negativa) holds that God can be described only by stating what God is not, because any positive attribute drawn from created things would limit or misrepresent the divine essence. It is a central theme of apophatic theology in Judaism, Christianity, and Islam.

Maimonides

Moses Maimonides gave the doctrine its classic formulation in The Guide for the Perplexed (12th century). Positive attributes, he argued, imply composition in God and apply only equivocally; true statements must be negations of privations — God is "not weak" rather than "powerful" — or descriptions of God's actions rather than essence. Even existence and unity, as predicated of God, do not mean what they mean for creatures.

Other traditions

Similar reasoning appears in the Christian apophatic tradition from Pseudo-Dionysius through Meister Eckhart, and in Islamic kalām's stress on God's absolute transcendence (tanzīh). Critics such as Thomas Aquinas preferred the doctrine of analogy, holding that perfections can be predicated of God in a qualified, analogical sense. See also Apophatic theology and Maimonides.
