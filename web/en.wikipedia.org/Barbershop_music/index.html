Barbershop music

Barbershop music is a style of unaccompanied four-part close harmony singing, traditionally for four voices: lead (the melody), tenor, baritone and bass. It is characterised by consonant chords — notably the ringing "barbershop seventh" — and homophonic texture that makes overtones audible.

Origins

The style emerged in the United States in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, with roots in African American quartet singing in barbershops and social gatherings, later popularised by the recording industry and vaudeville.

Organisation

The Barbershop Harmony Society (founded 1938 as SPEBSQSA) organises competitions for quartets and choruses; Sweet Adelines International and Harmony, Incorporated serve women's ensembles; mixed harmony groups have grown rapidly in recent decades.

Repertoire

Classic repertoire draws on turn-of-the-century popular songs ("Sweet Adeline", "Let Me Call You Sweetheart"), arranged with extensive use of secondary dominants and swipes (gliding chord changes under sustained notes).
