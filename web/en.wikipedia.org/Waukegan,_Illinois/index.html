Waukegan is a city in northeastern Illinois, United States, and the county seat of Lake County. It lies on the western shore of Lake Michigan, roughly 40 miles (64 km) north of downtown Chicago, near the Wisconsin border. The name derives from a Potawatomi word, Waukegance, meaning "little fort." Waukegan is the ninth-largest city in Illinois and the fifth-largest city on the western shore of Lake Michigan.

History

The site was a French trading post known as Little Fort before being renamed Waukegan in 1849; it was incorporated as a city in 1859. Its harbour on Lake Michigan and position on the rail line between Chicago and Milwaukee made it an industrial centre for manufacturing, shipping and tanning. In the later 20th century the city faced industrial decline and environmental remediation challenges, including PCB contamination in Waukegan Harbor, which was designated a federal Superfund site.

Geography and transport

Waukegan sits atop a bluff above Lake Michigan, with a downtown marina and lakefront. It is served by Metra's Union Pacific North Line to Chicago, and by Waukegan Regional Airport (Waukegan National Airport). U.S. Route 41 and Interstate 94 pass nearby.

Notable people

Waukegan is famous as the hometown of science fiction author Ray Bradbury, who immortalised it as the fictional "Green Town" in works such as Dandelion Wine and Something Wicked This Way Comes. Comedian Jack Benny also grew up in Waukegan and frequently claimed it as his hometown in his act.
