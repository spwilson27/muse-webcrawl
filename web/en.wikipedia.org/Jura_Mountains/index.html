Jura Mountains

The Jura Mountains (French: Massif du Jura) are a sub-alpine mountain range a short distance north of the Western Alps and mainly demarcate a long part of the French–Swiss border. They extend for about 360 km (220 mi) from the vicinity of the Rhine near Basel in the northeast to the Rhône near Geneva in the southwest. The highest summit is the Crêt de la Neige at 1,720 m (5,643 ft).

Geology

The Jura is a classic fold-and-thrust belt of limestone formed when the collision of the African and European plates that built the Alps compressed and folded the sedimentary strata. Its arched, parallel ridges and valleys gave their name to the Jurassic Period, whose rocks were first systematically studied in these mountains by Alexander von Humboldt and others. The region is noted for karst features — caves, swallow holes and underground rivers — and for dinosaur tracks preserved in its quarries.

Economy and culture

The Jura is associated with watchmaking (in towns such as La Chaux-de-Fonds and Besançon), the production of Comté cheese, wine from Arbois and Château-Chalon, and cross-country skiing. The Swiss canton of Jura, created in 1979, takes its name from the range, as does the French department of the same name.
