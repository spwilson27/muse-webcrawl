Overpopulation

Overpopulation occurs when a population exceeds the carrying capacity of its environment, degrading resources and quality of life. Applied to humans, the concept concerns the balance between population size, consumption and the Earth's ecological limits.

History of concern

Thomas Malthus argued in 1798 that population grows geometrically while food supply grows arithmetically. In the 20th century, works such as Paul Ehrlich's The Population Bomb (1968) renewed fears, while agricultural advances and falling fertility rates moderated growth projections.

Present views

World population passed 8 billion in 2022; UN projections foresee a peak later this century. Debates focus less on numbers alone than on consumption patterns, technology, distribution, and the ecological footprint of affluent societies, as well as the challenges of ageing and shrinking populations in many countries.
