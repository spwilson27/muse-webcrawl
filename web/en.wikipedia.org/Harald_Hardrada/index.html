Harald Hardrada

Harald Hardrada (Harald Sigurdsson)
c. 1015 – 25 September 1066
King of Norway, 1046–1066

Harald Sigurdsson (c. 1015–1066), known as Harald Hardrada ("hard ruler"), was King of Norway from 1046 to 1066 and one of the last great Viking kings. His invasion of England ended with his death at the Battle of Stamford Bridge, an event often taken to mark the end of the Viking Age.

Exile and the Varangians

Wounded at 15 in the Battle of Stiklestad (1030), where his half-brother King Olaf fell, Harald fled east to Kievan Rus' and then to Constantinople, where he rose to lead the Byzantine Emperor's Varangian Guard. The sagas credit him with campaigns in Sicily, the Holy Land, and Bulgaria and with accumulating great wealth before returning to Scandinavia in 1045 after a spell of imprisonment and a daring escape.

King of Norway

Harald first shared the throne with his nephew Magnus the Good, then ruled alone from 1047. He founded Oslo, maintained a standing military force, and waged long, inconclusive wars against Denmark — hence his stern epithet. In 1066 he pressed a claim to the English crown, allied with Tostig Godwinson, won at Fulford, but was defeated and killed by Harold Godwinson at Stamford Bridge on 25 September 1066 — just days before Harold himself fell at Hastings.
