Ian Fleming

Ian Lancaster Fleming (28 May 1908 – 12 August 1964) was a British writer, best known for his postwar series of spy novels featuring James Bond. Fleming came from a wealthy family connected to the merchant bank Robert Fleming & Co. His father was the Member of Parliament Valentine Fleming, who was killed in the First World War.

Educated at Eton, Sandhurst and the universities of Munich and Geneva, Fleming worked as a journalist and stockbroker before, during the Second World War, serving in British Naval Intelligence. As assistant to the Director of Naval Intelligence, Rear Admiral John Godfrey, and later as a commander, he was involved in planning operations including Operation Goldeneye and the work of 30 Assault Unit, experiences that furnished material for his fiction.

The Bond books

Fleming wrote his first Bond novel, Casino Royale, at his Jamaican home, Goldeneye, in early 1952; it was published in 1953. He went on to write twelve Bond novels and two collections of short stories, including From Russia, with Love (1957), Goldfinger (1959) and Thunderball (1961). The books drew heavily on his wartime service and his later journalism for The Sunday Times, and they made distinctive the character of Bond: a blunt, romantically ruthless agent whose tastes in cars, food, drink and clothing mirrored Fleming's own.

Fleming also wrote the children's story Chitty-Chitty-Bang-Bang (1964) for his son Caspar, and two works of non-fiction, The Diamond Smugglers (1957) and Thrilling Cities (1963).

Death and legacy

A heavy smoker and drinker, Fleming suffered heart disease and died of a heart attack on 12 August 1964 in Canterbury, Kent, aged 56; his final Bond novel, The Man with the Golden Gun, was published posthumously. The Bond books have sold over 100 million copies worldwide, and the film series launched with Dr. No (1962) became one of the most successful franchises in cinema history. After Fleming's death the Bond series was continued by other authors, beginning with Kingsley Amis's Colonel Sun (1968).
