Ho Chi Minh Trail

Ho Chi Minh Trail
Vietnamese: Đường mòn Hồ Chí Minh
Location: Vietnam, Laos, Cambodia
Used: 1959–1975, Vietnam War
Length: network totalling thousands of km

The Ho Chi Minh Trail (Vietnamese: Đường Trường Sơn) was a military logistics network of roads and trails that ran from North Vietnam to South Vietnam through the kingdoms of Laos and Cambodia. It provided support, in the form of manpower and materiel, to the Viet Cong and the People's Army of Vietnam during the Vietnam War.

Development

Established from 1959 by Group 559, the trail expanded from footpaths to a vast network with truck roads, fuel pipelines, way stations and anti-aircraft defences, maintained despite intense US bombing, including Operations Barrel Roll and the secret bombing of Cambodia.

Significance

The trail sustained the Tet Offensive (1968) and the final 1975 offensive. US interdiction efforts, including Operation Igloo White sensors, failed to close it.

Legacy

Parts are now the Ho Chi Minh Highway in Vietnam; unexploded ordnance in Laos remains a hazard.
