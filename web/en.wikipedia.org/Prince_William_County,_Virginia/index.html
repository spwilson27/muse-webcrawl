Prince William County, Virginia

Prince William County
State: Virginia
County seat: Manassas
Named for: Prince William, Duke of Cumberland
Population: approximately 480,000 (2020 census era)

Prince William County is a county in the Commonwealth of Virginia, in the Washington metropolitan area. The county seat is the independent city of Manassas, which is administratively separate from the county. As of the 2020 census, the population was about 482,000, making it Virginia's second-most populous county.

History

The county was formed in 1731 from parts of Stafford and King George counties and named for Prince William, Duke of Cumberland, the third son of King George II. During the American Civil War, the county was the site of the First and Second Battles of Bull Run (Manassas), commemorated today at Manassas National Battlefield Park. For much of its history the county was rural and agricultural, but from the 1960s onward it suburbanised rapidly as part of the growth of metropolitan Washington, D.C.

Geography

The county lies along the Potomac River, with the Occoquan River forming part of its boundary. It includes Prince William Forest Park, a large unit of the National Park Service, and parts of the Quantico Marine Corps Base area in the south. Major communities include Woodbridge, Dale City, Lake Ridge, Gainesville, Haymarket and Dumfries — the oldest continuously chartered town in Virginia.

Economy and transport

The county is a major suburban employment and residential area, served by Interstate 95 and Interstate 66, Virginia Railway Express commuter trains, and numerous state routes. George Mason University's Science and Technology Campus is located near Manassas, and the county has a growing biotechnology and data-center sector.
