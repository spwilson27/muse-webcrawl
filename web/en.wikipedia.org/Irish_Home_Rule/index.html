Irish Home Rule

Irish Home Rule was a movement that campaigned for self-government for Ireland within the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland. Beginning in the 1870s under Isaac Butt and later Charles Stewart Parnell, it sought a devolved Irish parliament in Dublin controlling domestic affairs while Ireland remained part of the United Kingdom.

The Home Rule bills

Liberal prime minister William Ewart Gladstone introduced the First Home Rule Bill in 1886; it was defeated in the House of Commons and split the Liberal Party. A second bill passed the Commons in 1893 but was rejected by the House of Lords. Under John Redmond, the Irish Parliamentary Party held the balance of power after 1910, and the Parliament Act 1911 removed the Lords' absolute veto.

The Third Home Rule Bill and Ulster

The Government of Ireland Act 1914 (the Third Home Rule Bill) passed into law but was suspended for the duration of the First World War. Ulster unionists, led by Edward Carson, fiercely opposed it, signing the Ulster Covenant in 1912 and forming the Ulster Volunteer Force. The Easter Rising of 1916 and the rise of Sinn Féin transformed Irish politics, and the War of Independence (1919–1921) led instead to partition and the creation of the Irish Free State.

Legacy

The Government of Ireland Act 1920 created separate home rule parliaments for Northern Ireland and Southern Ireland; only the northern parliament functioned. The idea of devolution resurfaced in late twentieth-century British politics.
