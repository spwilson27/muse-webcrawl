Tureen

A decorative 18th-century soup tureen

A tureen is a serving dish for foods such as soups or stews, often shaped as a broad, deep oval vessel with fixed handles and a low domed cover with a knob or handle. It is a piece of tableware used for serving food at table, from which individual portions are ladled.

History

The tureen developed in late 17th- and early 18th-century France, at the court of Louis XIV, as part of elaborate formal dining services. Large soup tureens, sauce tureens and smaller tureens for vegetables and side dishes became standard elements of silver and porcelain services. French silversmiths and the great porcelain factories of Meissen, Sèvres and Chelsea produced highly decorative examples, often in the rococo style. The word derives from French terrine, ultimately from Latin terra, "earth", referring to earthenware.

Design

Tureens are commonly made of ceramic (porcelain, earthenware, stoneware), silver, or glass. A soup tureen typically rests on feet or a platter stand and has two handles for carrying; its lid often has a notch for a ladle. Smaller tureens are made for sauce, vegetables and other dishes.
