Tunisia

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Tunisia, officially the Republic of Tunisia, is the northernmost country in Africa. It is part of the Maghreb region of North Africa, bordered by Algeria to the west and southwest, Libya to the southeast, and the Mediterranean Sea to the north and east. Its capital and largest city is Tunis. Tunisia covers about 163,610 km² and has a population of roughly 12 million.

History

The region was home to the Phoenician city-state of Carthage, Rome's great rival, destroyed in 146 BC. It later formed part of the Roman, Vandal, Byzantine, Arab, Ottoman and French spheres. Tunisia gained independence from France in 1956 under Habib Bourguiba, who ruled until 1987, when Zine El Abidine Ben Ali took power. Ben Ali was overthrown in January 2011 in the Jasmine Revolution, the first uprising of the Arab Spring, and Tunisia subsequently adopted a democratic constitution in 2014; since 2021, President Kais Saied has consolidated power, prompting debate over the country's democratic backsliding.

Geography

The Atlas Mountains cross the northwest, giving way to a central plain and the semi-arid Sahel coast, while the south merges into the Sahara. The climate is Mediterranean in the north and desert in the south.

Economy and culture

Tunisia's economy rests on agriculture (olives, dates, citrus), phosphates, textiles, tourism and a growing services sector. Arabic is the official language, with French widely used in education and commerce. Sites such as the medina of Tunis, the amphitheatre of El Jem and the ruins of Carthage are UNESCO World Heritage Sites.
