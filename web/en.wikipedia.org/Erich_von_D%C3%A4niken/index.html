Erich von Däniken

Born: 14 April 1935, Zofingen, Switzerland
Occupation: Author
Known for: Ancient astronaut hypotheses

Erich Anton Paul von Däniken (born 14 April 1935) is a Swiss author of several pseudoscientific books on extraterrestrial influence on early human culture, including the best-selling Chariots of the Gods?, published in 1968. Von Däniken is one of the main figures responsible for popularizing the "paleo-contact" and ancient astronauts hypotheses.

Chariots of the Gods?

His first and most famous book argued that structures such as the Egyptian pyramids, Stonehenge, and the Nazca Lines were constructed with the help or inspiration of extraterrestrial visitors, and that many religious texts describe such contacts. The book sold millions of copies worldwide but was widely criticised by scientists and archaeologists as pseudoarchaeology.

Later work

Von Däniken wrote dozens of follow-up books, hosted television programmes, and founded the Mystery Park (later Jungfrau Park) theme park in Interlaken, Switzerland, devoted to his theories. In 1991 he received the Ig Nobel Prize in Literature. Archaeologists and historians continue to reject his interpretations, while his work remains popular with the general public.
