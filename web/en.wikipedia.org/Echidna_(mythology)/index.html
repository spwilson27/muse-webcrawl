In Greek mythology, Echidna (Greek: Ἔχιδνα, "She-Viper") was a monster, half-woman and half-snake, who lived alone in a cave. She was the mate of the fearsome Typhon and mother of many of the most famous monsters of Greek myth, earning her the title "Mother of All Monsters".

Genealogy and description

According to Hesiod's Theogony, Echidna was born in a cave to Phorcys and Ceto (or in other accounts to Tartarus and Gaia). Hesiod describes her as "half a nymph with glancing eyes and fair cheeks, and half again a huge snake, great and awful, with speckled skin", who ate raw flesh and was ageless and deathless, hidden in a place beneath the earth at Arima. Apollodorus likewise describes her as a viper-woman. Her consort Typhon was even more monstrous, with a hundred serpent heads.

Offspring

By Typhon, Echidna bore a brood of monsters that feature across Greek heroic myth: Orthrus, the two-headed hound of Geryon; Cerberus, the hound of Hades; the many-headed Lernaean Hydra; the fire-breathing Chimera; the Sphinx; the Nemean Lion; the Eagle that tormented Prometheus; the dragon Ladon, guardian of the golden apples; and the Crommyonian Sow. Many of these creatures were slain by the hero Heracles in the course of his labours.

Death

Although Hesiod calls Echidna immortal, later tradition held that she was killed by Argus Panoptes, the hundred-eyed giant, who found her asleep and slew her — a deed recorded in Apollodorus' Library. The egg-laying monotreme mammals of Australia, the echidnas, take their scientific name from this figure.
