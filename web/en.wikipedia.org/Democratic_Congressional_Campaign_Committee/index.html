Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee

The Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee (DCCC) is the national campaign committee of the Democratic Party dedicated to electing Democrats to the United States House of Representatives. It is one of the "Hill committees" of American politics; its Republican counterpart is the National Republican Congressional Committee (NRCC). The DCCC is chaired by a member of the House Democratic caucus appointed by the party's House leader, and its headquarters are in Washington, D.C.

Activities

The committee recruits candidates in competitive districts, provides fundraising support, polling, research, and media advice, and spends money directly on advertising in the most closely contested races. Through programmes such as "Red to Blue", it highlights priority challengers and open-seat candidates. The DCCC also defends vulnerable Democratic incumbents, designated as "Frontline" members. Because of campaign finance law, the committee coordinates only to a limited degree with the candidates it supports, but it may make unlimited independent expenditures.

History

The committee traces its origins to the 1860s, when the Democratic Party first organised congressional campaign work separately from its national committee. Its modern importance grew with the rising cost of House campaigns in the late 20th century. Under chairs such as Rahm Emanuel in the 2006 cycle, the DCCC was credited with an aggressive recruitment and fundraising strategy that helped Democrats regain the House majority, and it played a similar role in the 2018 midterm elections. The committee's spending decisions, and its involvement in primary elections, periodically attract controversy within the party.
