Loa loa

Loa loa
KingdomAnimalia
PhylumNematoda
FamilyOnchocercidae
DiseaseLoiasis

Loa loa is a parasitic filarial nematode (roundworm) that causes loiasis, also called African eye worm disease, in humans in the rainforest regions of West and Central Africa. It is transmitted by the bites of deer flies and mango flies of the genus Chrysops.

Life cycle

Infective larvae enter the skin when an infected fly feeds. The worms mature and migrate through subcutaneous tissues, sometimes visibly crossing the conjunctiva of the eye. Female worms release microfilariae that circulate in the blood during the day, matching the daytime biting habits of the fly vectors, which ingest them and complete the cycle.

Disease

Many infections are symptomless. Characteristic signs include Calabar swellings — transient, itchy, non-pitting swellings caused by allergic reactions to migrating worms — and the dramatic but usually harmless passage of an adult worm across the eye. Treatment is with diethylcarbamazine, but in people with very high microfilarial loads it can provoke severe reactions including encephalopathy, a major consideration in areas where loiasis overlaps with onchocerciasis control programmes.
