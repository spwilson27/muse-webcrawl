Baylor College of Medicine (BCM) is a private medical school and research institution located in the Texas Medical Center in Houston, Texas, United States. Consistently ranked among the top medical schools in the country for research and for the low cost of its tuition relative to peer private institutions, Baylor is known for its Human Genome Sequencing Center, its role in the Human Genome Project, and research programs in genetics, neuroscience, cancer biology and tropical medicine.

History

The school was founded in Dallas in 1900 as the University of Dallas Medical Department and became affiliated with Baylor University in Waco in 1903, taking the name Baylor University College of Medicine. In 1943 the M.D. Anderson Foundation invited the school to anchor the planned Texas Medical Center in Houston, and the college moved to Houston, occupying the Roy and Lillian Cullen Building in 1947. In 1969 it separated from Baylor University and became an independent institution under its present name, allowing it to receive public funding. Under the leadership of cardiologist Michael E. DeBakey, its first president and a pioneering cardiovascular surgeon, Baylor grew into a major research power.

Education and research

Baylor enrolls about 180 medical students per class in its M.D. program and also offers Ph.D. programs through its Graduate School of Biomedical Sciences, a School of Health Professions, and the National School of Tropical Medicine, founded in 2011. Its teaching hospitals include Baylor St. Luke's Medical Center, Texas Children's Hospital, Ben Taub Hospital, and the Michael E. DeBakey VA Medical Center. The college's Human Genome Sequencing Center, led for many years by Richard Gibbs, was one of the five main centres of the international Human Genome Project. In 2021, Baylor researchers Maria Elena Bottazzi and Peter Hotez and their team developed the patent-free COVID-19 vaccine CORBEVAX.

Rankings

U.S. News & World Report regularly places Baylor among the top 25 American medical schools for research and among the leaders in primary care, and its genetics, pediatrics and other specialty programs through affiliated hospitals are nationally ranked. The college is also noted for annual tuition well below that of most private medical schools.
