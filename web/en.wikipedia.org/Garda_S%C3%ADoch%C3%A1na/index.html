The Garda Síochána (Irish for “the Guardians of the Peace”) is the national police service of Ireland. Members are commonly called gardaí (singular garda). The service is generally unarmed in routine patrol work and is headed by the Garda Commissioner, appointed by the Government of Ireland.

History

After Irish independence, the Civic Guard was created in 1922 to replace the Royal Irish Constabulary in the Irish Free State and was soon renamed Garda Síochána. Specialised units later developed for traffic, detective work, public order, organised crime and national security.

Organisation

The force is organised into regions, divisions and districts, with headquarters at the Phoenix Park in Dublin. It works alongside the Police Service of Northern Ireland on cross-border crime and is overseen through Irish policing and oversight bodies.

Roles

Duties include crime prevention and investigation, road safety, community policing, immigration functions in some contexts, protection of state institutions and support during emergencies.
