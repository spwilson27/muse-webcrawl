Richard Oakes (May 22, 1942 – September 27, 1972) was a Mohawk Native American activist who became a prominent leader of the Red Power movement. He is best known as a spokesman and leader of the occupation of Alcatraz Island (1969–1971), in which a group calling itself Indians of All Tribes claimed the island under the terms of the 1868 Treaty of Fort Laramie and demanded its return as an Indian cultural and educational centre.

Richard Oakes, spokesman for Indians of All Tribes at Alcatraz.

Early life

Oakes was born on the St. Regis Mohawk Reservation (Akwesasne) in upstate New York. Like many Mohawk men he worked as a high-steel ironworker on bridges and skyscrapers, travelling widely, before moving to San Francisco, where he enrolled at San Francisco State University and helped develop one of the first Native American studies programmes, recruiting Native students and building alliances among the Bay Area's urban Indian community.

Alcatraz occupation

On November 20, 1969, Oakes led 89 Native Americans ashore on Alcatraz, beginning a 19-month occupation that drew national attention to federal Indian policy — termination and relocation — and galvanised a generation of Native activists. Oakes served as the occupation's charismatic spokesman until early 1970, when he left the island following the death of his stepdaughter. The occupation ended in June 1971 when federal officers removed the remaining occupiers; it is credited with helping shift US policy toward Indian self-determination, including the Indian Self-Determination and Education Assistance Act of 1975.

Later activism and death

Oakes continued organising, working on land and fishing-rights campaigns in California and the Pacific Northwest and supporting the Pit River Tribe's struggle. In September 1972 he was shot and killed near Annapolis, California, in an altercation with a YMCA camp manager, who was acquitted on grounds of self-defence — a verdict widely protested by Native communities.

Legacy

Oakes is remembered as one of the defining voices of the Red Power era; San Francisco State named its Richard Oakes Multicultural Center in his honour, and he was commemorated with a Google Doodle on his 80th birthday in 2022.
