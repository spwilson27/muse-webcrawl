Erg (landform)

The Erg Chebbi sand sea in Morocco

An erg (from Arabic ʿarq, "dune field") is a broad, flat area of desert covered with wind-swept sand with little or no vegetative cover. The term is applied especially to the great sand seas of the Sahara, such as the Grand Erg Oriental and Grand Erg Occidental in Algeria. More generally, erg is a geomorphological term for the largest type of dune field, typically covering areas of at least 125 square kilometres; smaller dune fields are simply called dune fields.

Formation

Ergs form where there is an abundant supply of loose sand, winds strong enough to transport it, and conditions that cause the sand to accumulate rather than pass through. Sand is moved by saltation and surface creep and piles up into dune forms — barchan, transverse, linear, and star dunes — whose shapes reflect wind regime and sand supply. Most of the world's ergs lie in the arid subtropics downwind of large source areas of sediment, such as dry lake beds and river floodplains.

Distribution

Active ergs are concentrated in the dry belts between roughly 20° and 40° latitude in both hemispheres. The Sahara contains the largest hot-desert sand seas; other major ergs occur in the Arabian Peninsula (including the Rub' al Khali, the largest continuous sand sea on Earth), Central Asia, Australia, southern Africa (the Kalahari and Namib), and North and South America. Sand seas also exist on other bodies: Mars, Venus, and Saturn's moon Titan have extensive dune fields.

Movement and age

Individual dunes migrate downwind while the erg as a whole may remain in place for tens of thousands of years, its sand recycled internally. Many ergs accumulated mainly during drier phases of the Quaternary, and their dunes record past wind patterns. Although only about 20 percent of the Sahara is covered by ergs, they are among its most iconic landscapes.
