William Powell Lear (26 June 1902 – 14 May 1978) was an American inventor and businessman. He founded the Lear Jet Corporation and is credited with more than 140 patents, including key developments in the car radio and the 8-track tape format.

Early career

Largely self-taught in electronics, Lear worked on radio equipment in the 1920s and helped develop the first practical car radio, sold as the Motorola. During the Second World War his company, Lear Inc., produced aircraft instruments and autopilots.

Learjet

In the early 1960s Lear founded a company in Wichita, Kansas, to build a small, fast business jet based on a Swiss ground-attack aircraft design. The Learjet 23, first flown in 1963, created the modern business jet market and became a symbol of corporate aviation.

Other inventions

Lear developed the Lear Stereo 8 system, the commercially successful 8-track tape cartridge, and pursued ideas ranging from steam-powered cars to aircraft navigation aids late in life.
