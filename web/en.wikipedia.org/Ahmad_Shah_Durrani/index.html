Ahmad Shah Durrani (c. 1720/1722 – 16 October 1772), born Ahmad Khan Abdali, was the founder of the Durrani Empire and is regarded as the founder of the modern state of Afghanistan. A commander in the army of Nader Shah of Persia, he was elected shah by a loya jirga at Kandahar in 1747 after Nader's assassination, taking the title Durr-i-Durran, "Pearl of Pearls," from which the name Durrani derives.

Empire building

From Kandahar, Ahmad Shah forged an empire stretching at its height from eastern Persia to northern India, and from the Amu Darya to the Arabian Sea. He annexed Herat, Kabul, Peshawar, Kashmir, and the Punjab, and campaigned nine times into India. His most famous victory was the Third Battle of Panipat (14 January 1761), where he crushed the Maratha Confederacy — one of the largest and bloodiest battles of the 18th century. In 1762 he destroyed the Harmandir Sahib (Golden Temple) at Amritsar in his conflict with the Sikhs, though Sikh power in Punjab recovered and grew thereafter.

Legacy

Ahmad Shah was also a poet in Pashto and Persian. He is honored in Afghanistan as Ahmad Shah Baba, the father of the nation; his mausoleum stands in Kandahar, next to the shrine of the Cloak of the Prophet. His empire fragmented among his successors, but the polity he created is the ancestor of modern Afghanistan.
