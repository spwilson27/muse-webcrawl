Spartoi

OriginGreek mythology; Thebes

In Greek mythology, the Spartoi (Greek: Σπαρτοί, "sown men") were armed warriors who sprang fully grown from the teeth of a dragon sown in the earth by Cadmus, the legendary founder of Thebes. They are a classic case of the "earthborn" (autochthonous) beings who emerge from the soil itself.

The myth

Sent by Delphi's oracle to found a city where a special cow would rest, Cadmus came to Boeotia and needed water for sacrifice. The spring was guarded by a dragon sacred to Ares, which killed his companions before Cadmus slew it. On Athena's advice, he sowed half the dragon's teeth in the ground (the other half later went to Colchis, where Jason faced the same task). Instantly, armed men sprang from the furrows and fell upon one another; Cadmus hurled a stone among them so that each believed himself attacked by another, and they fought until only five survived. These five — Echion, Udaeus, Chthonius, Hyperenor and Pelorus — submitted to Cadmus and helped him build the citadel of Thebes, the Cadmeia; the great Theban noble families later claimed descent from them.

Jason's Spartoi

The motif recurs in the story of Jason and the Argonauts: King Aeëtes of Colchis set Jason to yoke fire-breathing bulls, plough a field and sow the remaining dragon's teeth. The earthborn warriors who rose were destroyed by the same trick — a stone thrown among them turned them on each other while Jason's crew finished the fight.

Interpretation

The Spartoi embody the idea that Thebes' aristocracy was literally born from its own soil — autochthony conferring a claim to the land. The tale of men sprung from dragon's teeth also survives in the English idiom "to sow dragon's teeth", meaning to stir up strife or create future enemies.
