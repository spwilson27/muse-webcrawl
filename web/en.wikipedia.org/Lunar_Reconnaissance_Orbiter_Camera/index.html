Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter Camera

The Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter Camera (LROC) is the main optical imaging system aboard NASA's Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter (LRO), a robotic spacecraft orbiting the Moon that was launched in June 2009. Operated by Arizona State University, LROC has produced the most detailed maps of the lunar surface ever made, imaging more than a million square kilometres at resolutions fine enough to resolve features smaller than a metre across, including the hardware left behind at the Apollo landing sites.

Instruments

LROC consists of three cameras: a Wide Angle Camera (WAC) and two Narrow Angle Cameras (NACs). The WAC images the surface in seven colour bands at roughly 100 metres per pixel over a 90-kilometre-wide swath, while the two NACs together image a 5-kilometre-wide swath at about 0.5 metres per pixel. Because the two NACs are aimed side by side, they provide monochrome images of extraordinary detail that have revealed boulders, crater chains, lava tubes, and the tracks of astronauts and rovers.

Science

The camera system supports LRO's mapping mission: characterising polar illumination, searching for exposed water ice, documenting impact hazards, and certifying safe landing sites for future crewed and robotic missions. LROC images of the Apollo 12, 14, and 17 sites show descent stages, instrument packages, and astronaut footpaths as bright and dark streaks in the lunar dust.
