Financial services

Financial services are the economic services provided by the finance industry, which encompasses a broad range of businesses that manage money, including banks, credit-card companies, insurance companies, accountancy firms, consumer-finance companies, stock brokerages, investment funds, and payment processors. Financial services are a major component of the service sector in developed economies.

Sectors

The industry is usually grouped into banking (accepting deposits, lending, and payment services), insurance (underwriting risk for life, health, property, and liability cover), and investment services (asset management, securities brokerage, investment banking, and financial advice), together with supporting activities such as credit rating, market data, and financial technology ("fintech").

Significance

Financial services support the rest of the economy by intermediating between savers and borrowers, providing payment infrastructure, and pooling risk. Major financial centres include New York, London, Hong Kong, Singapore, and Tokyo. The sector is heavily regulated because of its systemic importance — the failure of large institutions can threaten the wider economy, as in the financial crisis of 2007–08 — and because of the need to protect consumers and combat money laundering.
