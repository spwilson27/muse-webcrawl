Quilombo

A quilombo (Portuguese pronunciation: [kiˈlõbu]) is a Brazilian hinterland settlement founded by people of African origin, including settled quilombolas, or descendants of enslaved Africans who escaped slavery. Most of the inhabitants of quilombos, called quilombolas, were escaped slaves (and in some cases, later, freed African Brazilians and others). A similar settlement exists in other Spanish-speaking countries of Latin America, called a palenque; its inhabitants are palenqueros. The word derives from the Kimbundu word kilombo.

Palmares

The most famous quilombo was Palmares, in the captaincy of Pernambuco (present-day Alagoas), which grew during the 17th century into a confederation of settlements with a population sometimes estimated in the tens of thousands. Under leaders such as Ganga Zumba and Zumbi, Palmares resisted Portuguese and Dutch military expeditions for decades before its destruction in 1694; Zumbi was killed in 1695, and 20 November is now commemorated in Brazil as Black Consciousness Day.

Modern quilombos

Thousands of quilombo communities persist in Brazil. The 1988 Constitution recognised the land rights of quilombola communities, granting them title to their traditional lands, though implementation has been slow and contested. Such settlements are recognised for their role in preserving African-Brazilian culture, religion, and agricultural practices.
