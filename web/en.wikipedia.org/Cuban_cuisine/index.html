Cuban cuisine is the cooking tradition of Cuba, a fusion of Spanish, African, indigenous Taíno and wider Caribbean influences, with additional Chinese and French touches. Built on rice and beans, pork, root vegetables and plantains, it relies on slow cooking and the seasoned base called sofrito — onion, bell pepper and garlic sautéed in oil — rather than on chilli heat.

Typical dishes

The best-known main courses include ropa vieja (shredded beef in a tomato-based sauce), lechón asado (mojo-marinated roast pork), picadillo (seasoned ground beef with olives and raisins), and moros y cristianos — black beans and white rice cooked together. Fried ripe plantains (maduros), green plantain chips and tostones, and yuca with garlic mojo are common accompaniments. The pressed Cuban sandwich of ham, roast pork, Swiss cheese, pickles and mustard, perfected in Florida's Cuban exile communities, became famous worldwide. Flan, arroz con leche and strong sweetened espresso (cafecito) round out the table.

Modern history

Food shortages after 1959 and during the 1990s "Special Period" reshaped home cooking on the island toward resourcefulness, while exile communities in Miami and elsewhere preserved and elaborated the pre-revolutionary repertoire. Since the 2010s, private restaurants (paladares) have driven a revival of creative Cuban cooking in Havana.
