Nitrogen oxide

Nitrogen oxide may refer to a binary compound of oxygen and nitrogen, or a mixture of such compounds. The principal oxides of nitrogen include nitric oxide (NO), nitrogen dioxide (NO2), nitrous oxide (N2O), dinitrogen trioxide (N2O3), dinitrogen tetroxide (N2O4), and dinitrogen pentoxide (N2O5).

NOx

In atmospheric chemistry and pollution regulation, the term NOx denotes the sum of nitric oxide and nitrogen dioxide. These gases are produced in high-temperature combustion in engines and power plants and contribute to smog, acid rain, and respiratory illness. NOx emissions are controlled by catalytic converters and selective catalytic reduction.

Individual compounds

Nitric oxide is a signalling molecule in biology and a precursor to nitrogen dioxide in air. Nitrous oxide, known as laughing gas, is used as an anaesthetic and is also a potent greenhouse gas. Nitrogen dioxide is a toxic reddish-brown gas with a pungent odour, in equilibrium with its dimer N2O4.
