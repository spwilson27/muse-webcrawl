Unsymmetrical dimethylhydrazine (UDMH; 1,1-dimethylhydrazine) is a chemical compound with the formula H2N–N(CH3)2. It is a colourless liquid with a sharp, fishy, ammonia-like odour, best known as a component of hypergolic rocket fuels. UDMH is stable and can be stored for long periods in fuel tanks, making it attractive for military missiles and spacecraft, but it is toxic and carcinogenic.

Uses

UDMH is commonly paired with the oxidiser nitrogen tetroxide or mixed oxides of nitrogen; the combination ignites spontaneously on contact. It fuels or has fuelled the Russian Proton rocket, Chinese Long March 2/3/4 vehicles, Indian PSLV and GSLV upper stages, and many intercontinental ballistic missiles, including the Titan II. A 50/50 mixture of UDMH and hydrazine is known as Aerozine 50, used in the Apollo Lunar Module. Mixtures with monomethylhydrazine and additives give fuels such as UH 25.

Production

UDMH is produced industrially by the catalytic hydrogenation of dimethylnitrosamine or by methylation of hydrazine. The compound is also formed in small amounts in certain foods and tobacco smoke.

Safety

UDMH is absorbed through the skin, is a suspected carcinogen, and breaks down in the environment into N-nitrosodimethylamine, a potent carcinogen. Spills from launches and crashes of UDMH-fuelled rockets, notably around the Baikonur Cosmodrome and the 1996 Long March 3B accident at Xichang, have caused environmental and public-health concern. Handling requires stringent protective measures.
