A taco (Spanish: [ˈtako]) is a traditional Mexican dish consisting of a small hand-sized corn or wheat tortilla topped with a filling, then folded around the filling and eaten by hand. Fillings range from seasoned meats and seafood to beans, cheese, and vegetables, typically garnished with salsa, onion, cilantro, guacamole, and lime.

History

The taco predates the Spanish arrival in Mexico: indigenous peoples of the Valley of Mexico filled tortillas with small fish and other foods. The modern word "taco" is thought to derive either from a term for the paper-wrapped gunpowder charges used by Mexican silver miners in the 18th century or from Nahuatl words for a plug or wad. Tacos were popularised in the United States by Mexican immigrants in the early 20th century, and the hard-shell, U-shaped version was patented in the 1940s and mass-marketed by chains such as Taco Bell, founded by Glen Bell in 1962.

Varieties

Tacos al pastor feature pork marinated in chiles and achiote, shaved from a vertical spit (a technique adapted from Lebanese shawarma) and served with pineapple. Other well-known styles include tacos de carnitas (slow-cooked pork), tacos de barbacoa (slow-cooked meat), Baja tacos (batter-fried fish with cabbage and crema, from Baja California), tacos de canasta ("basket tacos", steamed and sold from baskets), and the breakfast tacos of Texas. The taquería, or taco stand, is a fixture of Mexican street food culture.
