Cookbook

A cookbook or cookery book is a kitchen reference containing recipes. Cookbooks may be general, or may specialize in a particular cuisine or category of food. Recipes in cookbooks are organized in various ways: by course (appetizer, first course, main course, dessert), by main ingredient, by cooking technique, alphabetically, by region or country, and so on. They may include illustrations of finished dishes and preparation steps, discussions of cooking techniques, advice on kitchen equipment, ingredients, substitutions, historical and cultural notes, and so on.

Cookbooks may be written by individual authors, who may be chefs, cooking teachers, or other food writers; they may be written by collectives; or they may be anonymous. They may be addressed to home cooks, to professional restaurant cooks, to institutional cooks, or to more specialized audiences.

History

The oldest surviving collection of recipes comes from ancient Mesopotamia, in the form of clay tablets written in Akkadian cuneiform. In the Greco-Roman world, the work known as De re coquinaria, attributed to Apicius, collected Roman recipes. Medieval cookbooks include Le Viandier by Guillaume Tirel (Taillevent) and the English Forme of Cury (c. 1390), compiled by the master cooks of Richard II.

The invention of printing made cookbooks widely available. In the 19th century, writers such as Isabella Beeton, whose Book of Household Management (1861) became a standard reference, and Fannie Farmer, who introduced standardized level measurements in the Boston Cooking-School Cook Book (1896), shaped modern home cooking. In the 20th century, Julia Child's Mastering the Art of French Cooking (1961) and Irma Rombauer's Joy of Cooking (1931) became canonical.

Types

Reference cookbooks such as Larousse Gastronomique take an encyclopedic approach. Community cookbooks collect recipes from a community, school, or religious group, often for fundraising. Chef cookbooks present the dishes of a notable chef or restaurant. Single-subject cookbooks focus on one ingredient, technique, or cuisine.

Digital era

Since the late 20th century, recipes have increasingly been published on websites, blogs, and apps, though printed cookbooks remain popular and frequently appear on bestseller lists.
