The House of Habsburg, also known as the House of Austria, is one of the most prominent and important dynasties in European history. Originating in what is now Switzerland, the family rose to rule Austria from 1282 and, through strategic marriages, came to dominate the Holy Roman Empire, Spain and its colonial empire, Hungary, Bohemia, and numerous other territories. Their motto, attributed to the marriages arranged by Maximilian I, was: "Let others wage war; you, fortunate Austria, marry."

Rise to power

The family takes its name from Habsburg Castle in the Swiss canton of Aargau. Rudolf of Habsburg was elected King of the Romans in 1273 and secured Austria and Styria for his sons. From 1440 until the Empire's dissolution in 1806, the imperial throne was held almost continuously by Habsburgs.

Spanish and Austrian branches

Under Charles V (1500–1558), the Habsburg dominions reached their greatest extent, encompassing Spain, the Netherlands, much of Italy, Austria, and Spain's American and Asian colonies. On his abdication the inheritance split into a Spanish branch (extinct 1700) and an Austrian branch. The Austrian Habsburgs later ruled the Austrian Empire and Austria-Hungary until the monarchy's collapse at the end of the First World War in 1918.

Legacy

Centuries of dynastic inbreeding produced the distinctive "Habsburg jaw" (mandibular prognathism), most pronounced in Charles II of Spain. The family's patronage shaped European art, music, and architecture, and Habsburg descendants remain prominent in European public life.
