Gloria in excelsis Deo ("Glory to God in the highest") is a Christian hymn known as the Greater Doxology. It opens with the words sung by angels at the Nativity in the Gospel of Luke (Luke 2:14) and continues with an early liturgical expansion of praise and supplication addressed to God the Father and to Christ.

Text and history

The hymn originated in Greek-speaking Christianity, where a version appears in the fourth-century Apostolic Constitutions as a morning prayer. Translated into Latin, it entered the Roman liturgy and became a fixed part of the Ordinary of the Mass, sung on Sundays (outside penitential seasons) and feast days, following the Kyrie.

The text includes the acclamations Laudamus te ("We praise you") and the Qui tollis peccata mundi ("You who take away the sins of the world"), and ends with praise of Christ "with the Holy Spirit, in the glory of God the Father".

Musical settings

The Gloria has been set to music by countless composers as part of complete Mass settings — notably in J. S. Bach's Mass in B minor — and as a standalone work, most famously Antonio Vivaldi's Gloria in D major (RV 589). The refrain of the Christmas carol "Angels We Have Heard on High" is drawn from the hymn's opening line.
