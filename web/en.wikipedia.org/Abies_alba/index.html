Abies alba

Common name: European silver fir

Family: Pinaceae

Height: 40–50 m (exceptionally 60 m)

Native range: Mountains of Europe

Abies alba, the European silver fir or silver fir, is a fir native to the mountains of Europe, from the Pyrenees north to Normandy, east to the Alps and the Carpathians, and south to southern Italy and Serbia. It is a major component of montane forests and an important timber tree.

Description

The silver fir is a large evergreen conifer growing to 40–50 metres tall with a straight trunk and a conical crown. Its needles are flattened, dark green above with two whitish bands of stomata beneath — the silvery underside gives the tree its name. The bark of young trees is smooth and grey, becoming cracked into plates with age. Upright cylindrical cones, 9–17 cm long, disintegrate on the tree to release winged seeds.

Ecology and uses

Silver fir grows on mountain slopes typically between 300 and 1,950 metres elevation, often alongside Norway spruce and European beech, and it tolerates shade well in youth. It suffered notable decline ("Tannensterben") in parts of Central Europe during the late twentieth century, attributed in part to air pollution, but has since recovered in many areas. Its light, soft timber is used in construction, furniture and paper pulp, and the species is also grown as a Christmas tree.
