Make in India

The Make in India logo, a lion made of cogs and wheels.

Make in India is an initiative by the Government of India to create and encourage companies to manufacture their products in India and to incentivise dedicated investment in manufacturing. The policy approach was designed to create a conducive environment for investments, develop modern infrastructure, and open new sectors for foreign capital. The initiative targeted 25 economic sectors for job creation and skill enhancement, including automobiles, aviation, biotechnology, defence manufacturing, electronics, food processing, pharmaceuticals, railways, renewable energy, textiles and tourism.

History

Make in India was launched by Prime Minister Narendra Modi on 25 September 2014, shortly after his government took office, as part of a wider set of nation-building initiatives. The launch followed a workshop on the programme held in December 2014 involving secretaries of the Government of India and industry leaders. The initiative's emblem, a lion silhouette constructed from industrial gears, was intended to symbolise manufacturing strength and national pride.

Objectives and pillars

The initiative rests on four pillars: new processes (easing business regulation and improving India's ranking in ease-of-doing-business indices), new infrastructure (industrial corridors and smart cities), new sectors (opening defence, insurance and medical devices to greater foreign direct investment), and a new mindset (the government acting as facilitator rather than regulator). Programmes such as the Production Linked Incentive (PLI) schemes, announced from 2020 for sectors including electronics and pharmaceuticals, later became central instruments of the manufacturing push.

Outcomes and criticism

Supporters point to growth in electronics assembly, defence production and foreign direct investment inflows, and to India becoming a major manufacturer of mobile phones. Critics note that manufacturing's share of GDP did not rise as quickly as the initiative's stated ambition of 25 per cent, that job creation in labour-intensive sectors lagged, and that land acquisition, logistics costs and regulatory complexity remained obstacles. Make in India was complemented by related campaigns such as Digital India, Skill India, Startup India and the later emphasis on Atmanirbhar Bharat (self-reliant India).
