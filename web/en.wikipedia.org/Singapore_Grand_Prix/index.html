The Singapore Grand Prix is Formula One's original night race.

The Singapore Grand Prix is a motor race held on the Marina Bay Street Circuit in Singapore and forming part of the Formula One World Championship. When it joined the calendar in 2008 it became Formula One's first night race, run under floodlights against the backdrop of the city's skyline. It is regarded as one of the most physically demanding races of the season because of the heat, humidity, bumpy street surface, and the event's nearly two-hour duration.

History

A Grand Prix was first held in Singapore in 1961 at the Thomson Road circuit, then a Formula Libre event that ran until 1973. The modern race began on 28 September 2008, won by Fernando Alonso — a victory later tainted by the "Crashgate" scandal, in which Alonso's Renault teammate Nelson Piquet Jr. deliberately crashed to trigger a safety car.

Characteristics

The street circuit threads past landmarks such as the Anderson Bridge and the Padang. The race has an unusually high safety-car probability, and the tropical conditions push cockpit temperatures above 50 °C. A 2023 layout revision removed the floating-platform section, shortening the lap. Sebastian Vettel is the most successful driver at the event, with five victories.
