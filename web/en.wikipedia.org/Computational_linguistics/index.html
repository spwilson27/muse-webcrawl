Computational linguistics

Computational linguistics is an interdisciplinary field concerned with the computational modelling of natural language, both from a linguistic and a computational perspective. Historically rooted in efforts at machine translation in the 1950s, the field overlaps substantially with natural language processing (NLP), which emphasises practical applications.

Areas of study

Computational linguistics addresses the structure of language at every level: phonetics and phonology (speech recognition and synthesis), morphology, syntax (parsing and grammar formalisms), semantics, and discourse and pragmatics. Theoretical work investigates what formal and computational mechanisms can account for human language, while applied work builds systems for machine translation, information retrieval, question answering, and dialogue.

Approaches

Early systems were rule-based, encoding grammatical knowledge by hand. From the late 1980s, statistical methods trained on large corpora became dominant, and since the 2010s neural network models — culminating in large language models based on the transformer architecture — have transformed the field's capabilities and research questions.
