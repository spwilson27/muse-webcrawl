Charles, Duke of Guelders

Charles, Duke of Guelders (9 November 1467 – 30 June 1538), often called Charles of Egmond, was Duke of Guelders and Count of Zutphen from 1492 until his death. For almost half a century he resisted the attempts of the Habsburg rulers of the Netherlands to absorb his duchy, making Guelders the last substantially independent principality in the northern Low Countries.

Early life and accession

Charles was the son of Adolf of Egmond and Catherine of Bourbon. His family had lost the duchy to Charles the Bold of Burgundy, and Charles himself was captured and raised at the Burgundian court in Ghent. In 1492 the towns of Guelders ransomed him and proclaimed him duke, beginning his long struggle against Maximilian I and his Habsburg successors.

Wars with the Habsburgs

Supported at various times by France and by the formidable mercenary captain Maarten van Rossum, Charles waged repeated wars against Philip the Handsome, Margaret of Austria and Emperor Charles V. His forces raided deep into Holland and Brabant, and in 1517 his troops even briefly threatened Amsterdam. Under the Treaty of Gorinchem in 1528 he was recognised as duke but acknowledged Guelders as held under the Empire, with succession arrangements that ultimately failed to preserve independence.

Death and succession

Charles died at Arnhem in 1538, childless. He had arranged for William, Duke of Jülich-Cleves-Berg, to succeed him, and William held Guelders until forced to cede it to Charles V by the Treaty of Venlo in 1543, after which the duchy was incorporated into the Habsburg Netherlands.
