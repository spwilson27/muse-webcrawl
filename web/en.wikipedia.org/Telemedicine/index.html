Telemedicine

Telemedicine is the distribution of health-related services and information via electronic information and telecommunication technologies. It allows long-distance patient and clinician contact, care, advice, reminders, education, intervention, monitoring and remote admissions. The broader term telehealth also covers non-clinical services such as provider training and administrative meetings.

Types

Telemedicine is commonly divided into three modalities: store-and-forward, in which medical data is sent to a specialist for later review; remote monitoring, in which patients' vital signs and other data are collected at home; and real-time interactive services, such as video consultations between patient and provider.

History

Early telemedicine used radio and telephone; NASA pioneered remote physiological monitoring for astronauts in the 1960s. Use expanded dramatically during the COVID-19 pandemic, when regulators in many countries temporarily relaxed licensing and reimbursement restrictions.

Benefits and limitations

Benefits include improved access for rural and mobility-limited patients, reduced travel, and continuity of care. Limitations include the inability to perform hands-on physical examinations, technology and connectivity barriers, privacy and security concerns, and varying reimbursement and licensing rules across jurisdictions.
