Mary Shelley

Mary Wollstonecraft Shelley (née Godwin; 30 August 1797 – 1 February 1851) was an English novelist who wrote the Gothic novel Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus (1818), which is considered an early example of science fiction. She also edited and promoted the works of her husband, the Romantic poet and philosopher Percy Bysshe Shelley.

Life

Mary was the daughter of the philosopher William Godwin and the feminist writer Mary Wollstonecraft, who died shortly after her birth. In 1814 she began a relationship with Percy Bysshe Shelley, then still married; they wed in 1816 after his first wife's death. During the famous "Year Without a Summer" of 1816, spent near Geneva with Lord Byron, a ghost-story contest led her to conceive Frankenstein, published anonymously in 1818 when she was 20.

Percy Shelley drowned in Italy in 1822. Mary returned to England and devoted herself to raising their son, Percy Florence, and to her writing and editorial work, which was crucial to establishing her husband's poetic reputation.

Works

Besides Frankenstein, her novels include Valperga (1823), the apocalyptic The Last Man (1826), Perkin Warbeck (1830), Lodore (1835) and Falkner (1837). She wrote travel narratives, biographies for encyclopedias, and the edition of Percy Shelley's Poetical Works (1839). She died in London in 1851, probably of a brain tumour.
