Gutzon Borglum

Gutzon Borglum (1867–1941), designer and sculptor of Mount Rushmore.
Born25 March 1867, St. Charles, Idaho Territory
Died6 March 1941, Chicago, Illinois
Known forMount Rushmore; Stone Mountain carving

John Gutzon de la Mothe Borglum (25 March 1867 – 6 March 1941) was an American sculptor best known for creating the monumental presidential portraits on Mount Rushmore in South Dakota, one of the most famous sculptures in the United States.

Early life and training

Born in Idaho Territory to Danish immigrant parents, Borglum studied art in San Francisco, Paris (at the Académie Julian), and London. He first won recognition for sculptures of horses and Western subjects, and later for public monuments, including a bust of Abraham Lincoln now in the United States Capitol crypt.

Stone Mountain and Mount Rushmore

In 1915 Borglum was commissioned to carve a Confederate memorial on Stone Mountain, Georgia; he completed the head of Robert E. Lee before disputes ended his involvement in 1925, and his work there was later blasted away. That same year he began his great project in the Black Hills of South Dakota: the faces of George Washington, Thomas Jefferson, Theodore Roosevelt, and Abraham Lincoln, carved 60 feet (18 m) high into the granite of Mount Rushmore. Work continued from 1927 until 1941, employing about 400 workers using dynamite and honeycomb drilling; remarkably, there were no fatalities.

Death and legacy

Borglum died in Chicago in March 1941, months before the memorial's completion; his son Lincoln Borglum supervised the finishing work. His monumental style remains controversial, in part because of his documented involvement with the Ku Klux Klan during the Stone Mountain period and because Mount Rushmore was carved on land sacred to the Lakota Sioux.
