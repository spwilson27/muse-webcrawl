Peritonitis

Peritonitis is inflammation of the peritoneum, the serous membrane lining the abdominal cavity and covering the viscera. It is a serious, often life-threatening condition that usually requires emergency treatment.

Causes

Secondary peritonitis most often follows perforation of a hollow viscus — for example a perforated peptic ulcer, ruptured appendix or diverticular perforation — spilling bacteria and gut contents into the cavity. Primary (spontaneous bacterial) peritonitis occurs mainly in people with cirrhosis and ascites. Peritoneal dialysis can also be complicated by peritonitis.

Signs and symptoms

Typical features are severe abdominal pain with tenderness, guarding and rigidity ("board-like abdomen"), fever, nausea and ileus. Examination may show rebound tenderness and absent bowel sounds. Untreated, peritonitis progresses to sepsis and shock.

Treatment

Management comprises resuscitation, broad-spectrum antibiotics and urgent source control, usually surgery to repair the perforation and wash out the abdomen. Mortality remains substantial, especially in elderly and immunocompromised patients.
