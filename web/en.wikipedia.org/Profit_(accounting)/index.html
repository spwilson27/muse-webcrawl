Profit (accounting)

The income statement reports gross, operating, and net profit.

In accounting, profit is an entity's income distributed to its owners and a measure of profitability, calculated as revenue minus expenses over a period. Several levels of profit are reported on the income statement: gross profit (revenue minus cost of goods sold), operating profit (gross profit minus operating expenses such as wages, rent, and depreciation), and net profit or net income (after interest and taxes), often called the "bottom line."

Accounting vs economic profit

Accounting profit counts only explicit, recorded costs. Economic profit additionally subtracts implicit costs, including the opportunity cost of capital and the owner's labour, so economic profit is usually smaller. A firm can report a positive accounting profit while earning zero or negative economic profit. Profitability ratios derived from these figures, such as gross margin and net margin, are used by investors and managers to compare performance across firms and periods.

Taxation

Taxable profit is determined under tax law and often differs from accounting profit because of rules on depreciation, deductions, and timing. Corporate income tax is generally levied on a version of net profit adjusted for these statutory differences.
