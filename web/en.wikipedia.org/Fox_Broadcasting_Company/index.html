Fox Broadcasting Company

The Fox Broadcasting Company, commonly known simply as Fox, is an American commercial broadcast television network owned by Fox Corporation. Launched on October 9, 1986, as a competitor to the "Big Three" networks (ABC, CBS and NBC), Fox grew into the fourth major U.S. network, becoming known for programmes such as The Simpsons, The X-Files, American Idol and NFL football coverage.

History

Fox was formed after Rupert Murdoch's News Corporation purchased the Metromedia station group and the 20th Century Fox film studio in 1985. Under executives including Barry Diller and Jamie Kellner, the network expanded from late-night and a few nights of prime time to a full seven-night schedule by 1993, and gained major prominence after acquiring NFL broadcast rights that year. In 2019, following Disney's acquisition of 21st Century Fox's entertainment assets, the network became part of the newly formed Fox Corporation, which retained the broadcast network, Fox News and Fox Sports.

Programming

The network's signature series include the long-running animated sitcom The Simpsons (premiered 1989), Married... with Children, Beverly Hills, 90210, 24, House, Glee and Empire, alongside sports under the Fox Sports banner.
