Kitniyot

Kitniyot

Hebrewקטניות (legumes)
SubjectPassover dietary custom
Applies toAshkenazi Jewish practice

Kitniyot (Hebrew: קטניות, literally "legumes" or "small things") is a category of foods — including rice, corn, beans, lentils, peas, buckwheat, sesame and mustard — that Ashkenazi Jews traditionally refrain from eating during the Jewish holiday of Passover, even though they are not chametz (leavened grain) and are permitted to Sephardi and Mizrahi Jews.

Origin of the custom

The prohibition, a custom (minhag) rather than a biblical or Talmudic law, is first documented in medieval France and Germany. Rabbis gave several reasons: kitniyot could be ground into flour resembling chametz flour and cooked into similar dishes, leading to confusion; grains might be mixed into sacks of kitniyot; and some legumes swell when cooked like leaven. Medieval authorities including the Semak recorded it, and it became binding Ashkenazi practice.

Modern debate

Because the custom restricts the Passover diet heavily — especially for vegetarians — it is regularly debated. The Conservative movement’s Committee on Jewish Law and Standards ruled in 2015 that its affiliated communities may permit kitniyot, and many Israeli Ashkenazim have relaxed the custom, but Orthodox Ashkenazi practice generally maintains the prohibition while disputing the status of newer items such as quinoa and peanut and rapeseed (canola) oils.
