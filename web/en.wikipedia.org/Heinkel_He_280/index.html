The Heinkel He 280 was an early turbojet-powered fighter aircraft designed and produced by the German aircraft manufacturer Heinkel. It was the first jet fighter in the world to fly, and the first production-type combat aircraft anywhere to be designed for jet propulsion from the outset. Developed in secrecy during the early stages of the Second World War, the He 280 ultimately lost out to the rival Messerschmitt Me 262 and was cancelled in 1943 after only nine prototypes had been built.

Development

Heinkel had pioneered jet flight with the He 178, which on 27 August 1939 became the first aircraft to fly under turbojet power, using Hans von Ohain's engine. Building on this, the company began Project He 280 in late 1939 as a private venture. The design featured an elliptical wing with twin tail fins, tricycle landing gear (unusual for the time), and a compressed-air ejection seat—making it the first aircraft ever fitted with one. The pilot Erich Warsitz conducted early glide tests in September 1940 with the aircraft towed aloft by a He 111; the first powered flight under its own HeS 8 engines followed on 30 March 1941, flown by Fritz Schäfer.

Trials and competition

On 5 April 1941 the He 280 was demonstrated before officials including Ernst Udet and Erhard Milch, reaching about 780 km/h. A mock dogfight in 1942 saw the He 280 easily outpace a Focke-Wulf Fw 190, Germany's best piston fighter. However, development was hampered by the unreliability and slow maturation of the HeS 8 engine; plans to use the Junkers Jumo 004 or BMW 003 required substantial redesign. Meanwhile the Messerschmitt Me 262, though first flown with a piston engine in 1941, was favoured by the Reich Air Ministry for its greater development potential and Messerschmitt's production capacity.

Cancellation

On 27 March 1943 Milch cancelled the He 280 programme in favour of the Me 262. Only nine He 280s were built, and none saw operational service. Ernst Heinkel attributed the decision partly to political factors within the ministry. Despite its failure to enter service, the He 280 demonstrated the jet fighter concept and pioneered features—tricycle gear, ejection seat, twin-engine layout—that became standard.
