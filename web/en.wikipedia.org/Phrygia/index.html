Phrygia

In classical antiquity, Phrygia was a kingdom in the west-central part of Anatolia, in what is now Asian Turkey, centred on the Sangarios River. Its people, the Phrygians, are believed to have migrated into Anatolia from the Balkans around the end of the second millennium BC.

History

The Phrygian kingdom flourished in the 8th century BC under kings such as Midas, famed in Greek legend for his golden touch. Its capital was Gordium, associated with the Gordian Knot later cut by Alexander the Great. Around 690 BC the kingdom was devastated by Cimmerian invaders; Phrygia subsequently passed under Lydian, then Persian, Hellenistic, Roman and Byzantine rule.

Culture

The Phrygians spoke an Indo-European language written in an alphabet related to Greek. They worshipped the mother goddess Cybele and were noted for their music — the Phrygian mode preserves their name — and for the Phrygian cap, a soft conical hat later adopted as a symbol of liberty.
