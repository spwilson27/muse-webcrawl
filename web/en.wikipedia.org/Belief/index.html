Belief

Belief
FieldEpistemology, philosophy of mind, psychology
DefinitionA mental attitude that a proposition is true
Related conceptsKnowledge, faith, opinion, credence, justification

Beliefs guide reasoning and action.

A belief is a mental attitude in which a person takes a proposition or state of affairs to be true. Beliefs form a core subject of epistemology and the philosophy of mind: classical epistemology analysed knowledge as justified true belief, an account challenged by the Gettier problem. Beliefs may be conscious or dispositional, occurrent or tacit, and they guide action in combination with desires.

Formation and change

Beliefs arise from perception, memory, testimony, inference and socialisation. Psychology studies biases in belief formation—such as confirmation bias and motivated reasoning—and processes of belief revision, persuasion and delusion in clinical contexts.

Types

Philosophers distinguish core and peripheral beliefs, religious beliefs held on faith, and degrees of belief or credences, central to Bayesian epistemology. In religious studies, "belief" also denotes the doctrines professed by a faith community.
