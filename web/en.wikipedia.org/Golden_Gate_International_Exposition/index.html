Golden Gate International Exposition

Dates18 February – 29 October 1939; 25 May – 29 September 1940
LocationTreasure Island, San Francisco, California
Theme"Pageant of the Pacific"
AttendanceOver 17 million (both seasons)

The Golden Gate International Exposition (GGIE) was a world's fair held in San Francisco in 1939 and 1940 to celebrate the completion of the city's two great bridges, the San Francisco–Oakland Bay Bridge (1936) and the Golden Gate Bridge (1937). It took place on Treasure Island, an artificial island built in San Francisco Bay expressly for the fair.

The fair

The exposition's theme, "Pageant of the Pacific," celebrated the cultures and commerce of the Pacific Rim nations. Its architecture blended Art Deco with Mayan, Incan, and Southeast Asian motifs; the centrepiece was the 400-foot Tower of the Sun. At night the fairgrounds were lit with innovative indirect and coloured lighting, and attractions included the China Clipper flying boats of Pan American Airways, which used Treasure Island as their Pacific terminus.

Entertainment

The fair's midway, the Gayway, featured Sally Rand's Nude Ranch and other popular spectacles, while the fine arts exhibition — including works from Pacific cultures and Old Master paintings — drew large crowds. After a financially disappointing first season, the fair reopened in 1940 with a greater emphasis on entertainment.

Aftermath

Treasure Island was intended to become San Francisco's airport after the fair, but with the entry of the United States into World War II it was taken over by the U.S. Navy as Naval Station Treasure Island. Two hangars and the fair's administration building (now the Treasure Island Museum) survive from the exposition.
