Fulk, King of Jerusalem

Fulk (Latin: Fulco; c. 1089/92 – 13 November 1143), also known as Fulk the Younger, was Count of Anjou (as Fulk V) from 1109 to 1129 and King of Jerusalem from 1131 to his death. He was the son of Fulk IV, Count of Anjou, and Bertrade de Montfort.

Count of Anjou

As Fulk V of Anjou, he expanded Angevin power, subduing rivals in Maine and maintaining a careful balance between the kings of France and England. In 1128 his eldest son Geoffrey married Matilda, daughter of Henry I of England — a union from which the Plantagenet dynasty descended.

King of Jerusalem

In 1129 Fulk married Melisende, heiress of Baldwin II of Jerusalem, abdicated Anjou to Geoffrey, and travelled to the Levant. On Baldwin's death in 1131, Fulk and Melisende were crowned jointly. Fulk's attempts to sideline his wife and her supporters provoked a crisis in 1134, when Melisende's cousin Hugh II of Jaffa revolted; after the revolt failed, Fulk was reconciled with the queen, who thereafter shared fully in government.

Wars and diplomacy

Fulk campaigned against Zengi, the atabeg of Mosul and Aleppo, and strengthened the kingdom's southern defences against Fatimid Egypt, overseeing the construction or reinforcement of castles including Ibelin, Blanchegarde, and Beth Gibelin.

Death

Fulk died in a hunting accident near Acre in November 1143, when his horse stumbled and fell, crushing him. He was succeeded jointly by Melisende and their son Baldwin III.
