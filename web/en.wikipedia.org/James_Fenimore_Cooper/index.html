James Fenimore Cooper (15 September 1789 – 14 September 1851) was an American writer of the early 19th century, whose historical romances depicting frontier and Native American life helped create a distinctive American literature.

Life

Cooper grew up in Cooperstown, New York, founded by his father. Expelled from Yale, he served as a midshipman in the U.S. Navy before turning to writing with Precaution (1820) and the Revolutionary War spy novel The Spy (1821), which brought him fame.

The Leatherstocking Tales

His best-known works are the five Leatherstocking Tales featuring frontiersman Natty Bumppo: The Pioneers (1823), The Last of the Mohicans (1826), The Prairie (1827), The Pathfinder (1840), and The Deerslayer (1841). The Last of the Mohicans remains his most popular novel and has been adapted many times for film and television.

Other work and legacy

Cooper also wrote respected sea novels such as The Pilot (1824) and works of social and political commentary. Mark Twain later satirized his prose in "Fenimore Cooper's Literary Offences", yet Cooper's influence on the American frontier myth endures.
