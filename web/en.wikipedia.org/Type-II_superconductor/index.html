Type-II superconductor

The mixed (vortex) state between Hc1 and Hc2
ClassificationSuperconductivity
Ginzburg–Landau parameterκ > 1/√2
ExamplesNb-Ti, Nb3Sn, YBCO, most alloys

In superconductivity, a type-II superconductor is a superconductor that exhibits an intermediate phase of mixed ordinary and superconducting properties at intermediate magnetic fields and temperatures above its critical temperature. In this mixed state, the magnetic field partially penetrates the material in the form of quantized flux tubes called Abrikosov vortices.

Type-II behaviour occurs when the Ginzburg–Landau parameter κ exceeds 1/√2. Below the lower critical field Hc1, the material expels the field completely (the Meissner effect), as in a type-I superconductor. Between Hc1 and the upper critical field Hc2, flux penetrates in vortices while the bulk remains superconducting; above Hc2 superconductivity is destroyed.

Importance

Because type-II materials can remain superconducting in very high magnetic fields, they are used for nearly all practical superconducting magnets, including those in MRI scanners, particle accelerators, and fusion experiments. High-temperature superconductors such as YBCO are extreme type-II materials. Pinning of vortices by defects is essential: unpinned vortices move under current flow and dissipate energy, limiting the usable critical current.
