Green Bay, Wisconsin

Downtown Green Bay at the mouth of the Fox River.

Green Bay is a city in and the county seat of Brown County, Wisconsin, United States. Located at the head of Green Bay, an arm of Lake Michigan, at the mouth of the Fox River, it had a population of 107,395 at the 2020 census, making it the third-largest city in Wisconsin. It is best known internationally as the home of the Green Bay Packers of the National Football League, the smallest market in major American professional sports and the only community-owned franchise in the NFL.

History

French explorer Jean Nicolet landed near Green Bay in 1634, and the site became one of the oldest European settlements in the Midwest, a hub of the fur trade visited by missionaries and traders and fortified as Fort Howard under the French, British, and Americans successively. The city grew in the 19th century as a Great Lakes port and lumber and paper-mill centre; paper manufacturing remains a major industry, earning Green Bay the nickname "Toilet Paper Capital of the World". The Packers, founded in 1919 and admitted to the NFL in 1921, play at Lambeau Field and have won thirteen league championships including four Super Bowls.

Geography and climate

The city lies about 112 miles (180 km) north of Milwaukee, in a humid continental climate zone with cold, snowy winters and warm summers. The Port of Green Bay handles coal, cement, salt, and other bulk cargo, and the Fox River flows northeast through the city into the bay.
