NSFNET

The National Science Foundation Network (NSFNET) was a program of coordinated, evolving projects sponsored by the National Science Foundation (NSF) from 1985 to 1995 to promote advanced research and education networking in the United States. The NSFNET was a major contributor to the transformation of the Internet from a network serving a small community of researchers into a public, worldwide network.

History

NSFNET began as a 56 kbit/s backbone linking NSF's five supercomputer centres (Princeton, Pittsburgh, the University of Illinois, Cornell, and San Diego) in 1986. Upgraded to T1 (1.5 Mbit/s) in 1988 under a cooperative agreement with Merit Network and IBM/MCI, and to T3 (45 Mbit/s) in 1991, traffic doubled roughly every seven months. Regional networks connected hundreds of universities to the backbone.

Privatisation and legacy

NSFNET's Acceptable Use Policy barred commercial traffic, spurring the growth of early commercial providers. In 1995 the backbone was decommissioned and the Internet transitioned to a fully commercial structure built around network access points and private backbones. The program also funded CSNET's successors and helped establish networking in many other countries.
