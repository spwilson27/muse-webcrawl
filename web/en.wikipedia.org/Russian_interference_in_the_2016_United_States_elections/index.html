Russian interference in the 2016 United States elections

The report of Special Counsel Robert Mueller, released in 2019, documented Russia's interference campaign.

The Russian government interfered in the 2016 United States presidential election with the goals of harming the campaign of Hillary Clinton, boosting the candidacy of Donald Trump, and increasing political and social discord in the United States. According to the U.S. intelligence community, the operation — sometimes codenamed Project Lakhta — was ordered directly by Russian president Vladimir Putin.

Components of the campaign

The interference had two principal elements. The St. Petersburg-based Internet Research Agency conducted a disinformation and social-media campaign, creating fake American personas and groups that reached tens of millions of users, bought advertisements, and organized rallies. Separately, Russian military intelligence (the GRU) hacked computers of the Democratic National Committee and the Clinton campaign, releasing stolen emails through DCLeaks, Guccifer 2.0, and WikiLeaks. Russian actors also probed state election infrastructure.

Investigations and response

The FBI's Crossfire Hurricane investigation opened in July 2016; in May 2017 Robert Mueller was appointed special counsel. His 2019 report concluded that Russia interfered "in sweeping and systematic fashion," though it did not establish a criminal conspiracy between the Trump campaign and the Russian government. The investigation produced indictments of Russian nationals and entities, and several Trump associates were convicted of unrelated or process crimes. The United States imposed sanctions and expelled Russian diplomats in response, and the bipartisan Senate Intelligence Committee confirmed the intelligence community's findings.
