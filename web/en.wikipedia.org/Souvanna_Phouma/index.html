Prince Souvanna Phouma (1901–1984) was a Laotian statesman who served repeatedly as prime minister of the Kingdom of Laos between 1951 and 1975 and led the neutralist faction in the Laotian civil war. Educated in Hanoi and France as an engineer, he sought to keep Laos neutral between communist and Western blocs.

Career

A nephew of King Sisavang Vong, Souvanna joined the Lao Issa independence movement in 1945, then reconciled with the French-backed kingdom. As prime minister he negotiated at the 1954 Geneva Conference after the First Indochina War, and formed coalition governments including his half-brother Souphanouvong's Pathet Lao (1957, 1962). The 1962 International Agreement on the Neutrality of Laos, signed at Geneva, was his diplomatic peak.

Civil war years

Coups, the collapse of coalitions, and US and North Vietnamese intervention made neutrality untenable; his government depended increasingly on American aid while General Vang Pao's forces fought in the north. After the Paris settlement on Vietnam, a 1973 ceasefire produced a final coalition, but the Pathet Lao took full power in 1975, abolishing the monarchy. Uniquely, Souvanna was retained as an adviser to the new Lao People's Democratic Republic and lived quietly in Vientiane until his death in 1984.

Legacy

Souvanna Phouma is remembered as a courteous, Francophile moderate whose neutralism failed amid superpower rivalry — a symbol of Laos's thwarted attempt to avoid the fate of Vietnam.
