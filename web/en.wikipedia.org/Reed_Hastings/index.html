Wilmot Reed Hastings Jr. (born October 8, 1960) is an American businessman. He is the co-founder and executive chairman of Netflix, which he led as chief executive officer from its founding in 1997 until 2023. Hastings previously founded Pure Software and has been a prominent donor and advocate in education policy, particularly for charter schools.

Hastings co-founded Netflix in 1997 with Marc Randolph.

Early life and career

Hastings grew up in the Boston area and studied mathematics at Bowdoin College, graduating in 1983. After college he joined the Peace Corps and taught mathematics in Swaziland (now Eswatini). He later earned a master's degree in computer science from Stanford in 1988. In 1991 he founded Pure Software, a tools company for software developers, which went public and merged into Rational Software in 1997 in a deal worth about $750 million.

Netflix

Hastings and Marc Randolph founded Netflix in Scotts Valley, California, in 1997 as a DVD-by-mail rental service, reportedly inspired by a $40 late fee Hastings owed for a copy of Apollo 13. Netflix launched streaming in 2007 and original programming in 2013, growing into the world's largest subscription streaming service. Its management culture, described in the internal "Freedom and Responsibility" deck and Hastings's 2020 book No Rules Rules (with Erin Meyer), is widely studied. In January 2023 Hastings stepped down as co-CEO, becoming executive chairman.

Other activities

Hastings served on the board of Microsoft (2007–2012) and Facebook (2011–2019). He has donated tens of millions of dollars to education causes, including charter school networks and scholarship funds for historically Black colleges, and in 2024 donated $1 million in support of a political action committee. With his wife Patty Quillin he is a signatory of the Giving Pledge.
