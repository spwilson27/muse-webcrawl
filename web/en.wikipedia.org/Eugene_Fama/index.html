Eugene Francis Fama (born 14 February 1939) is an American economist, best known for his empirical work on portfolio theory, asset pricing and the efficient-market hypothesis. He has spent most of his career at the University of Chicago.

Research

Fama's 1970 paper "Efficient Capital Markets" gave the efficient-market hypothesis its classic statement: in a market where prices fully reflect available information, consistently beating the market is not to be expected. With Kenneth French he developed the Fama–French three-factor model (later extended to five factors), showing that size and value factors help explain stock returns beyond market risk. Their work provided the intellectual basis for index investing and for the strategies of Dimensional Fund Advisors, with which Fama is associated.

Nobel Prize

In 2013 Fama shared the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences with Lars Peter Hansen and Robert Shiller "for their empirical analysis of asset prices".
