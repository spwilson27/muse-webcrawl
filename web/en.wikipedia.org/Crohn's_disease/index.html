Crohn's disease

Crohn's disease is a type of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) that causes chronic inflammation of the gastrointestinal tract, most commonly the terminal ileum and colon, but it can affect any part from mouth to anus, often in discontinuous 'skip' lesions.

SymptomsAbdominal pain, diarrhoea, weight loss, fatigue, fever
ComplicationsStrictures, fistulae, abscesses, malnutrition
Named afterBurrill Bernard Crohn (described 1932)

Causes

Cause is multifactorial: genetic susceptibility (e.g., NOD2 variants), an abnormal immune response to gut microbiota, and environmental factors including smoking (which worsens Crohn's, unlike ulcerative colitis).

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis uses ileocolonoscopy with biopsies (transmural inflammation, sometimes granulomas), imaging and labs. There is no cure; treatment induces and maintains remission with corticosteroids, immunomodulators (azathioprine, methotrexate), biologics (anti-TNF, vedolizumab, ustekinumab, risankizumab) and surgery for complications.
