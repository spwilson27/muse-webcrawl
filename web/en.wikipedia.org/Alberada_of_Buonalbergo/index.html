Alberada of Buonalbergo (c. 1033 – c. 1122) was a Norman noblewoman of southern Italy, the first wife of Robert Guiscard, Duke of Apulia, and the mother of Bohemond I, prince of Taranto and Antioch and leader of the First Crusade.

Life

Alberada was the daughter of Gerard (Girard) of Buonalbergo, a Norman lord of the Ariano area in Campania. Around 1051 she married the rising Hauteville leader Robert Guiscard, then a minor lord based in Calabria; the marriage brought him a useful following of her father's knights — tradition credits her dowry with some 200 knights who helped Guiscard secure his position in the County of Apulia and Calabria.

About 1058 Guiscard had the marriage annulled on grounds of consanguinity so that he could make a far more advantageous match with Sikelgaita, sister of Prince Gisulf II of Salerno. The annulment had lasting consequences: it disinherited, in Guiscard's Italian succession planning, Alberada's son Bohemond in favour of Sikelgaita's son Roger Borsa — a slight that pushed Bohemond toward his spectacular career in Byzantium and the crusading East, where he carved out the Principality of Antioch after fighting at Dyrrhachium and on the First Crusade.

Alberada lived to a great age. Her tomb survives in the Hauteville abbey of the Holy Trinity at Venosa, bearing an inscription praising her as the wife of Guiscard and mother of the great Bohemond — the only tomb in the abbey never desecrated, reportedly because later generations revered her memory.
