Beetle

Beetles are insects that form the order Coleoptera, the largest order in the animal kingdom, with about 400,000 described species. Their front pair of wings is hardened into wing-cases, the elytra.

Anatomy

Beetles undergo complete metamorphosis, from egg to larva to pupa to adult. They range in size from less than a millimetre to over 17 centimetres.

Diversity and ecology

Beetles inhabit almost every habitat except polar regions and the open sea, feeding on plants, fungi, other animals and debris. They include ladybirds, scarabs, stag beetles, weevils and fireflies.

Human significance

Some beetles are agricultural pests, others are pollinators and decomposers, and scarabs held religious significance in ancient Egypt. See Insect.
