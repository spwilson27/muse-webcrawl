Penelope Devereux

Penelope Rich, Lady Rich, née Devereux (1563 – 7 July 1607), was an English noblewoman and courtier of the Elizabethan era. She is best remembered as "Stella", the inspiration for Sir Philip Sidney's sonnet sequence Astrophel and Stella, one of the foundational works of English Renaissance poetry.

Life

Penelope was the daughter of Walter Devereux, 1st Earl of Essex, and Lettice Knollys. Her father may have intended her to marry Philip Sidney, but after Essex's death she was married in 1581 to Robert Rich, later 1st Earl of Warwick—Sidney's Astrophel and Stella (written in the early 1580s, published 1591) laments the lost beloved "Stella", identified with her. She became a lady-in-waiting to Queen Elizabeth I but fell from favour for her role in her brother the Earl of Essex's rebellion in 1601.

Later years

Her unhappy marriage to Rich was effectively over by the 1590s; she lived openly with Charles Blount, Lord Mountjoy, whom she married in 1605 after her divorce—conduct that scandalised the Jacobean court. She died in 1607.
