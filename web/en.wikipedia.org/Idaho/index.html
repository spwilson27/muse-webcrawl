Idaho is a state in the Pacific Northwest and Mountain West regions of the United States. It borders Montana and Wyoming to the east, Utah and Nevada to the south, Oregon and Washington to the west, and the Canadian province of British Columbia to the north. Idaho's capital and largest city is Boise.

Geography

Idaho is among the most mountainous states, with the Rocky Mountains dominating its landscape and a narrow panhandle extending northward. The Snake River Plain crosses the south, and the Snake River forms Hells Canyon, the deepest river gorge in North America. The highest point is Borah Peak at 12,662 feet (3,859 m). Much of the state is public land, including the Frank Church–River of No Return Wilderness, one of the largest wilderness areas in the contiguous United States.

History

The region was home to peoples including the Shoshone, Nez Perce, and Coeur d'Alene for thousands of years. The Lewis and Clark Expedition crossed the area in 1805–1806. Idaho Territory was created in 1863 during mining booms that followed the discovery of gold; Idaho became the 43rd state on July 3, 1890.

Economy

Idaho is famous for its potatoes, producing roughly a third of the U.S. crop, but its economy also rests on agriculture broadly, food processing, lumber, mining, and a growing technology sector centred on Boise, home to Micron Technology. Tourism and outdoor recreation are increasingly important.

Demographics and politics

With about 1.8 million residents at the 2020 census, Idaho has been one of the fastest-growing states in percentage terms. Politically it is strongly Republican, though its politics include a notable libertarian and independent streak.
