Insectivore

Insectivore
DietInsects and other small invertebrates
ExamplesHedgehogs, shrews, moles, many bats, anteaters

An insectivore is a carnivore that eats primarily insects and similar arthropods. Although individually small, insects exist in enormous numbers and constitute a very large part of the animal biomass in almost all non-marine, non-polar environments. Animal groups that feed heavily on insects include many primitive mammals, birds, lizards, amphibians, fish, and other arthropods such as dragonflies and spiders.

Mammals

The name insectivore was once applied to a group of mammals comprising the hedgehogs, moonrats, moles, shrews, and solenodons, classified in the order Insectivora. Molecular studies have since shown that this grouping was polyphyletic; the golden moles and tenrecs are now placed in Afrosoricida, while the remaining forms belong to Eulipotyphla.

Other animals

Many lizards, frogs, and birds are primarily insectivorous. Among plants, carnivorous species such as the Venus flytrap supplement their nutrition by trapping insects. Large specialist feeders such as anteaters and pangolins consume vast numbers of ants and termites daily.
