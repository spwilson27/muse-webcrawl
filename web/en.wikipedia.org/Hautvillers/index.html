Hautvillers is a commune in the Marne department in north-eastern France, near Épernay in the Champagne wine region. The village is celebrated as the birthplace of champagne wine, owing to its association with the Benedictine monk Dom Pierre Pérignon.

History

The village grew around the Abbey of Saint-Pierre d'Hautvillers, founded in 650 by Saint Nivard, bishop of Reims. Dom Pérignon (1638–1715) served as cellarer of the abbey from 1668 until his death, and is credited — partly in legend — with improvements in blending wines from different vineyards, using cork stoppers, and making white wine from black grapes. He is buried in the abbey church, where his tombstone can be seen in front of the altar.

Wine and sights

Hautvillers is a premier cru village surrounded by vineyards on the slopes of the Montagne de Reims overlooking the Marne valley. Its streets are decorated with wrought-iron signs depicting the trades and champagne houses of the village, a tradition begun in the 1950s. The abbey church of Saint-Sindulphe, which houses Dom Pérignon's tomb and a museum, draws many visitors. In 2015 the Champagne hillsides, houses and cellars, including those around Hautvillers, were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
