Alternation of generations

Alternation of generations (also called metagenesis or heterogenesis) is the predominant type of life cycle in plants and algae. In it, a multicellular haploid sexual phase, the gametophyte, alternates with a multicellular diploid asexual phase, the sporophyte.

Alternation of generations
Phases: Gametophyte (n) ↔ Sporophyte (2n)
Key processes: Meiosis, fertilisation
Described by: Wilhelm Hofmeister (1851)

The cycle

A mature sporophyte produces haploid spores by meiosis. Spores germinate into gametophytes, which produce gametes by mitosis. Fusion of gametes (fertilisation) restores the diploid state, and the zygote grows into a new sporophyte. In bryophytes the gametophyte dominates; in ferns both phases are independent; in seed plants the gametophytes are highly reduced and retained within sporophyte tissues — the pollen grain and embryo sac.

Variation

Cycles are isomorphic when the two generations look alike (as in the sea lettuce Ulva) and heteromorphic when they differ greatly. Similar alternation occurs in some fungi and in animals such as cnidarians (polyp/medusa), though usage there varies.

Evolution

The sporophyte likely evolved by delaying meiosis after fertilisation, allowing a diploid phase to produce many spores. Dominance of the sporophyte in vascular plants correlates with larger size and terrestrial adaptation.
