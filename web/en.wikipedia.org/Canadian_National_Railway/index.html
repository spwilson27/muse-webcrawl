The Canadian National Railway Company (French: Compagnie des chemins de fer nationaux du Canada), known simply as CN, is a Canadian Class I freight railway headquartered in Montreal, Quebec, serving Canada and the Midwestern and Southern United States. It is the largest railway in Canada by both revenue and the physical size of its network, with roughly 20,000 route miles (32,000 km) of track spanning Canada from the Atlantic to the Pacific coast and south through the central United States to the Gulf of Mexico.

History

CN was created by the Government of Canada between 1918 and 1923 by amalgamating several railways that had fallen into bankruptcy or been nationalised — including the Canadian Northern Railway, the Intercolonial Railway, the National Transcontinental Railway and, in 1923, the Grand Trunk Railway. As a Crown corporation it operated not only freight and passenger trains but also hotels, a telegraph company, shipping lines and the CN Tower in Toronto. Its passenger services were transferred to the new Crown corporation Via Rail in 1978.

For much of the twentieth century CN was chronically unprofitable, but under president Paul Tellier in the early 1990s the company was restructured, and in 1995 the federal government privatised it through one of the largest public share offerings in Canadian history. The shares are listed on the Toronto and New York stock exchanges.

Expansion into the United States

Following privatisation, CN expanded southward by acquiring the Illinois Central Railroad in 1998, giving it a direct route from Chicago to New Orleans, and later the Wisconsin Central (2001), Great Lakes Transportation (2004), and the Elgin, Joliet and Eastern Railway (2009), which provides a bypass around Chicago. CN thus became one of only a handful of transcontinental North American railways and the sole railway connecting ports on the Atlantic, Pacific and Gulf coasts.

Operations

CN hauls a diversified mix of commodities: petroleum and chemicals, grain and fertilisers, forest products, metals and minerals, automobiles, coal and intermodal containers. The railway has been a prominent adopter of "precision scheduled railroading", the operating model introduced by chief executive E. Hunter Harrison in the late 1990s. CN remains one of the most efficient large railways in North America by operating ratio.
