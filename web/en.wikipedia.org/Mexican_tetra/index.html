The Mexican tetra (Astyanax mexicanus), also known as the blind cave fish in its cave-dwelling form, is a species of freshwater fish in the family Characidae, native to the Nearctic ecozone. It is famed among evolutionary biologists because it exists in two strikingly different forms: a sighted, pigmented surface form and a blind, unpigmented cave form.

Forms

Surface populations inhabit rivers and streams from Texas and New Mexico in the United States south through north-eastern Mexico. Cave populations — known from around 30 caves in the Sierra de El Abra region of San Luis Potosí and Tamaulipas — have independently colonised subterranean waters and convergently evolved loss of eyes and pigmentation, enhanced non-visual senses (including an expanded lateral line and taste buds), altered metabolism and sleep loss.

Research model

Because cave and surface forms remain interfertile despite their divergence, A. mexicanus is a powerful model organism for studying the genetic basis of regressive evolution, development of the eye, pigmentation pathways such as albinism, and metabolic adaptation. Hybrid crosses between different cave populations can partially restore eye development, demonstrating that different genes were lost in different caves — evidence for convergent evolution at the molecular level.

Aquarium trade

The blind cave form is sometimes sold in the aquarium trade as the "blind cave tetra". Both forms are hardy schooling fish growing to roughly 12 cm.
