Space Exploration Technologies Corp., commonly referred to as SpaceX, is an American space technology company founded in 2002 by Elon Musk with the goal of reducing space transportation costs and enabling the colonisation of Mars. SpaceX developed the Falcon launch vehicles, the Dragon spacecraft and the fully reusable Starship system, and operates Starlink, the largest satellite constellation ever deployed. It became the first private company to send a spacecraft to the International Space Station (2012) and to fly astronauts there (2020).

History

After early Falcon 1 failures, SpaceX reached orbit in 2008 and won a NASA Commercial Orbital Transportation Services contract that saved the company. Falcon 9 debuted in 2010; in December 2015 a Falcon 9 first stage landed successfully, and in 2017 a recovered booster flew again. Crew Dragon's Demo-2 mission in May 2020 returned crewed launches to US soil. SpaceX's launch cadence came to dominate the global market, flying more mass to orbit than all other providers combined.

Starship and Starlink

Starship — a fully reusable two-stage system comprising the Super Heavy booster and the Starship upper stage — is the largest rocket ever flown and is central to NASA's Artemis Moon programme. Starlink, SpaceX's broadband constellation of thousands of low-Earth-orbit satellites, funds much of the company's development and has become strategically important for communications in remote areas and conflict zones.
