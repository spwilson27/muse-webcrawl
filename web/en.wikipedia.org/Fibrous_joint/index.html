Fibrous joint

Fibrous joints are connections between bones formed by dense fibrous connective tissue. They generally permit little or no movement. There are three types of fibrous joints.

Types

Sutures are the interlocking joints between the bones of the skull, which may ossify with age. A syndesmosis joins bones with a sheet or band of ligamentous tissue, as between the tibia and fibula, allowing slight movement. A gomphosis is a peg-and-socket joint, found only between a tooth and its socket in the jaw.
