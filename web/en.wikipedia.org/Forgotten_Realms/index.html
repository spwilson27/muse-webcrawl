Forgotten Realms

Faerûn, the principal continent of the Forgotten Realms.

Forgotten Realms is a campaign setting for the Dungeons & Dragons (D&D) fantasy role-playing game. Commonly referred to by players and game designers alike as "The Realms", it was created by game designer Ed Greenwood around 1967 as a setting for his childhood stories. It became the most successful and longest-running D&D setting, and is set mainly on the continent of Faerûn on the planet Abeir-Toril.

Creative origins

Ed Greenwood began writing stories about the Realms as a child and later used it as the setting for his D&D campaigns, contributing articles to Dragon magazine from 1979. TSR acquired the setting and published the Forgotten Realms Campaign Set ("the grey box") in 1987. Greenwood's sage Elminster became the setting's signature narrator.

The world

The Realms is a high-fantasy world of competing city-states, ancient empires and meddling gods. Famous locations include the city of Waterdeep, the underdark metropolis of Menzoberranzan, Baldur's Gate, Neverwinter, the frozen Icewind Dale, and the magocracy of Thay. The setting's history is punctuated by cataclysms such as the Time of Troubles (1989), the Spellplague (2008, accompanying fourth edition) and the Second Sundering, which restored much of the earlier cosmology for fifth edition.

Novels and media

Hundreds of novels have been published in the setting, most notably R. A. Salvatore's books about the drow ranger Drizzt Do'Urden, beginning with The Crystal Shard (1988). Video games set in the Realms include the Baldur's Gate series, Icewind Dale, Neverwinter Nights, and Baldur's Gate 3 (2023). The 2023 film Dungeons & Dragons: Honor Among Thieves is also set in the Realms.

Reception

The Forgotten Realms is generally considered the flagship D&D setting and the default world for fifth edition products. Its depth of lore — tens of thousands of pages of sourcebooks and fiction — is both celebrated and criticized as intimidating for new players.
