WiMAX

WiMAX (Worldwide Interoperability for Microwave Access) is a family of wireless broadband communication standards based on the IEEE 802.16 set of standards. Promoted by the WiMAX Forum, it was designed to provide fixed and mobile broadband access over metropolitan-area distances, as an alternative to cable, DSL and, for mobile use, cellular networks.

History and technology

The original 802.16 standard (2001) served line-of-sight fixed links; 802.16-2004 added non-line-of-sight fixed access, and 802.16e (2005, "Mobile WiMAX") supported mobility using OFDMA. Peak rates of tens of megabits per second were achievable over ranges of several kilometres. Networks were deployed in many countries, and in the United States Sprint and Clearwire built a national mobile WiMAX network launched commercially in 2008.

Decline

WiMAX ultimately lost the mobile broadband market to LTE, which enjoyed broader support from mobile operators and handset makers. Sprint shut down its WiMAX network in 2015–2016, and WiMAX survives mainly in niche fixed-wireless, utility and aviation (AeroMACS) applications.
