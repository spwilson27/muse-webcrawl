Channapatna

Channapatna is a town in the Ramanagara district of Karnataka, India, located about 60 km southwest of Bengaluru on the Bengaluru–Mysuru highway. The town is famous throughout India for its wooden toys and lacquerware, which carry a Geographical Indication tag, and it is often called Gombegala Ooru, the "town of toys" in Kannada.

Toy craft

The lacquerware tradition of Channapatna is more than two centuries old and is associated with the patronage of Tipu Sultan, who is said to have invited artisans from Persia to train local craftsmen. Toys are traditionally turned on lathes from soft ivory wood (hale mara) and finished with natural vegetable dyes, producing brightly coloured dolls, rocking horses, spinning tops and puzzles. The craft supports thousands of artisans in the town and surrounding villages.

Geography and demographics

Channapatna lies at an average elevation of roughly 739 metres. According to census figures, the town's population is around 70,000, with Kannada as the principal language and Urdu also widely spoken.

Economy

Besides toys, the local economy includes sericulture and silk reeling, coconut products, and agriculture. The town is a stop on the railway line between Bengaluru and Mysuru.
