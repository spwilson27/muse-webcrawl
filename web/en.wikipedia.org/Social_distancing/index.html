Social distancing

Social distancing, also called physical distancing, is a set of non-pharmaceutical interventions intended to prevent the spread of a contagious disease by maintaining physical distance between people and reducing the number of times people come into close contact with each other. Measures include staying at least 1–2 metres (3–6 feet) apart, avoiding gatherings, closing schools and workplaces, and cancelling mass events.

History

Isolation of the sick is ancient, but systematic distancing measures were notably applied during the 1918 influenza pandemic, when cities such as St. Louis that acted early and layered measures fared better than those, like Philadelphia, that held mass gatherings. The approach was revived in pandemic plans in the 2000s and applied on an unprecedented global scale during the COVID-19 pandemic beginning in 2020.

Measures

Common measures include working from home, distance learning, limits on gathering sizes, closure of non-essential businesses, travel restrictions, and protective behaviours such as avoiding handshakes. Quarantine (separating those exposed) and isolation (separating those infected) are related but distinct interventions.

Effectiveness and costs

Modelling and observational studies associate distancing with reduced transmission and "flattening the curve" of infections so that health systems are not overwhelmed. The measures carry substantial economic and psychological costs, including lost income, interrupted education, and increased loneliness, which shaped debates over their duration and stringency.
