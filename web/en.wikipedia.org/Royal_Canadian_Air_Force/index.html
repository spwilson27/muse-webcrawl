Royal Canadian Air Force

The Royal Canadian Air Force (RCAF; French: Aviation royale canadienne) is the air and space force of Canada. Its role is to "provide the Canadian Armed Forces with relevant, responsive and effective airpower." The RCAF is one of three environmental commands within the unified Canadian Armed Forces. It was established in 1920 as the Canadian Air Force and granted the royal title in 1924.

History

Canadian aviators served with distinction in the First World War in British flying services; Canadians such as Billy Bishop were among the war's leading aces. An independent Canadian Air Force was formed in 1920 and became the Royal Canadian Air Force on 1 April 1924. In the Second World War the RCAF grew into the fourth-largest Allied air force, with over 215,000 personnel in uniform by 1944. Canada hosted the British Commonwealth Air Training Plan, which trained more than 130,000 Allied aircrew at bases across the country. RCAF squadrons fought in the Battle of Britain, the bombing campaign over Europe (No. 6 Group RCAF), the Battle of the Atlantic, and the campaigns in North Africa, Italy, and Northwest Europe.

Cold War and unification

During the Cold War, the RCAF contributed an air division to NATO forces in Europe, operated the CF-104 Starfighter and other types, and shared continental air defence with the United States through NORAD. In 1968 the RCAF was merged into the unified Canadian Forces, becoming Air Command (AIRCOM) in 1975; the historic name Royal Canadian Air Force was restored in 2011.

Modern role and equipment

Today's RCAF, headquartered at National Defence Headquarters in Ottawa, is commanded by the Commander of the Royal Canadian Air Force. It operates CF-18 Hornet fighters (with the F-35A Lightning II selected as their replacement), CP-140 Aurora patrol aircraft, CC-177 Globemaster III and CC-150 Polaris transports, CH-147F Chinook and other helicopters, and performs search and rescue across Canada's vast territory. Its motto is Per ardua ad astra — "Through adversity to the stars."

Structure

The RCAF is organised principally into 1 Canadian Air Division (operational command) and 2 Canadian Air Division (training), with wings at bases including Cold Lake, Bagotville, Trenton, Greenwood, Comox, and Winnipeg. In 2022 the RCAF also assumed responsibility for Canada's military space capabilities with the formation of 3 Canadian Space Division.
