Kangra district is the most populous district of the Indian state of Himachal Pradesh. Its headquarters is Dharamshala, the winter capital of the state and the seat of the exiled Tibetan government of the Dalai Lama at McLeod Ganj. The district occupies the Kangra Valley beneath the Dhauladhar range of the Himalayas.

History

Kangra was the capital of the ancient Trigarta kingdom, mentioned in the Mahābhārata, and of the Katoch dynasty, one of the oldest ruling houses in India. The massive Kangra Fort, built by the Katoch kings, was a coveted prize seized over the centuries by Mahmud of Ghazni, the Delhi Sultanate, the Mughals, the Sikh Empire of Ranjit Singh and, in 1846, the British. The region was also the seat of the celebrated Kangra school of miniature painting, which flourished in the 18th century. A devastating earthquake in 1905 killed some 20,000 people in the valley.

Geography and economy

The Beas River flows through the district, and the Pong Dam reservoir (Maharana Pratap Sagar) is an important Ramsar-listed wetland. Tea gardens around Kangra and Palampur produce the green and black Kangra tea, and tourism — to Dharamshala, McLeod Ganj, Palampur and the temple towns of Kangra, Baijnath and Jwalamukhi — is a major economic activity.

Places of worship

Kangra is home to several revered Shakti Peetha temples, including Jwalamukhi, Chamunda Devi, Brajeshwari Devi (Kangra) and Chintpurni nearby, drawing large numbers of pilgrims each year.
