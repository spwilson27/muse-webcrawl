Umbrella species

Umbrella species are species selected for making conservation-related decisions, typically because protecting these species indirectly protects the many other species that make up the ecological community of their habitat — the "umbrella effect".

Characteristics

Umbrella species usually have large habitat requirements and wide ranges, so that conserving their habitat shelters many co-occurring species. Examples often cited include the northern spotted owl, tigers, and giant pandas.

Criticism

Critics note that the umbrella effect is not guaranteed: other species in the habitat may have different needs, and reserves chosen for one charismatic species do not always capture the most biodiversity.
