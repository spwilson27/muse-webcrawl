Mary I of England

Mary I (18 February 1516 – 17 November 1558), also known as Mary Tudor, and as "Bloody Mary" by her Protestant opponents, was Queen of England and Ireland from July 1553 and Queen of Spain as the wife of Philip II from January 1556 until her death in 1558. She was the only surviving child of Henry VIII and his first wife, Catherine of Aragon, and the first undisputed queen regnant of England.

Early life

Mary was declared illegitimate after Henry annulled his marriage to Catherine in the course of the English Reformation, was separated from her mother, and served in the household of her half-sister Elizabeth before being restored to the succession by the Act of Succession of 1544. A devout Catholic, she resisted the Protestant reforms of Edward VI's reign and, on his death in 1553, defeated the attempt to place Lady Jane Grey on the throne, entering London to popular acclaim.

Reign

Mary restored Catholic worship, reconciled England with the papacy under Cardinal Reginald Pole, and in 1554 married Philip of Spain — a match that provoked Wyatt's rebellion and enduring fears of Spanish domination. From 1555 her government revived the heresy laws, under which about 280 Protestants were burned at the stake, earning her the posthumous epithet "Bloody Mary", popularised by John Foxe's Book of Martyrs. Her reign also saw administrative and naval reforms and the refoundation of religious houses, but ended in disappointment: a phantom pregnancy, Philip's absences, and the loss of Calais — England's last possession in France — in January 1558. She died that November, probably of influenza or ovarian cancer, and was succeeded by her half-sister Elizabeth I, who reversed her religious settlement.
