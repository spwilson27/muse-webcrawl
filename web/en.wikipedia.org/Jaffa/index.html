Jaffa

Jaffa (Hebrew: יָפוֹ, Yafo; Arabic: يَافَا, Yāfā) is an ancient port city, now the southern, older part of Tel Aviv–Yafo in Israel. One of the oldest working ports in the world, Jaffa has been inhabited for some 4,000 years and served for millennia as the maritime gateway to Jerusalem.

History

Jaffa appears in Egyptian records of the 15th century BCE and in the biblical account as the port from which Jonah set sail. It passed through Phoenician, Greek, Roman, Byzantine, Arab, Crusader, Mamluk and Ottoman hands. Napoleon besieged and captured the city in 1799. In the 19th and early 20th centuries Jaffa grew as a centre of trade famous for its oranges, and became a major Palestinian Arab city and cultural centre.

Under the 1947 UN Partition Plan Jaffa was to be an Arab enclave within the Jewish state. In the 1948 war the city was captured by Israeli forces and most of its Arab population fled or was expelled; Jaffa was united with Tel Aviv in 1950, forming the municipality of Tel Aviv–Yafo.

Landmarks

Old Jaffa's landmarks include the Ottoman clock tower, St Peter's Church, the harbour with its lighthouse, the flea market, galleries in the old stone lanes, and the Zodiac alleys. The Jaffa orange became a famous export brand of the citrus trade.
