Permian-Triassic extinction event

The Permian–Triassic extinction event, colloquially known as the Great Dying, was the Earth's most severe known extinction, occurring about 251.9 million years ago at the boundary between the Permian and Triassic periods. It marks the boundary between the Paleozoic and Mesozoic eras.

Permian–Triassic extinction
Date: c. 251.9 million years ago
Losses: ~81% of marine species, ~70% of terrestrial vertebrate species
Suspected cause: Siberian Traps volcanism

Scale

An estimated 81% of marine species and a large share of terrestrial life went extinct, including trilobites entirely and most synapsid and amphibian lineages. Recovery took millions of years — far longer than after other mass extinctions. It is the only known mass extinction of insects on a large scale.

Causes

The leading explanation is massive volcanism of the Siberian Traps flood basalts, releasing carbon dioxide and other gases that drove rapid global warming, ocean acidification and widespread ocean anoxia. Contributions from methane release, coal combustion by intruding magma, and other feedbacks are debated.
