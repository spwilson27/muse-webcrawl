Dinosaur National Monument

LocationColorado and Utah, United States
Area210,844 acres (853 km²)
Established4 October 1915
Governing bodyNational Park Service

Dinosaur National Monument is an American national monument located on the southeast flank of the Uinta Mountains on the border between Colorado and Utah, at the confluence of the Green and Yampa rivers.

Fossil beds

The monument is famous for the Carnegie Dinosaur Quarry, discovered by Earl Douglass in 1909, where a wall of rock preserves around 1,500 dinosaur bones from the Late Jurassic Morrison Formation, including Allosaurus, Diplodocus, Stegosaurus and Camarasaurus specimens. The Quarry Exhibit Hall encloses the fossil wall in place.

Landscape and history

Beyond the quarry, the monument protects deep river canyons, petroglyphs of the Fremont culture, and historic ranch sites. President Woodrow Wilson proclaimed the original 80-acre monument in 1915; Franklin D. Roosevelt expanded it greatly in 1938. A proposed dam at Echo Park in the 1950s was defeated in a landmark conservation campaign.
