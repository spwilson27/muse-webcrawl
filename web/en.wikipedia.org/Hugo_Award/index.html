Hugo Award

Hugo Award
Awarded for: Best science fiction or fantasy works
Presented by: World Science Fiction Society
First awarded: 1953
Venue: Worldcon (annual)
Named after: Hugo Gernsback

The Hugo Awards are an annual set of literary awards for the best science fiction or fantasy works and achievements of the previous year, given at the World Science Fiction Convention (Worldcon) and chosen by its members. Named after Hugo Gernsback, founder of the pioneering science fiction magazine Amazing Stories, the Hugo is widely regarded as the premier award in the genre.

History

The awards were first given in 1953 at the 11th Worldcon in Philadelphia, in seven categories, and have been awarded every year since 1955. The distinctive trophy — a stainless steel rocket ship on a base whose design varies annually — was introduced in the early years and standardised in its rocket form. Voting is by members of that year's Worldcon using a preferential (instant-runoff) ballot, following open nominations.

Categories

Categories include Best Novel, Best Novella, Best Novelette, Best Short Story, Best Series, Best Graphic Story, Best Dramatic Presentation (split into long and short forms), and awards for editors, artists and fan writers. Best Novel winners include works by Alfred Bester, Ursula K. Le Guin, Isaac Asimov, N. K. Jemisin — the first author to win for all three books of a trilogy — and many others. The related Astounding Award for Best New Writer is administered alongside but is not itself a Hugo.

Controversies

In the 2010s the awards were at the centre of the "Sad Puppies" and "Rabid Puppies" campaigns, in which organised slates sought to influence nominations; voters responded in part by selecting "No Award" in some categories in 2015, and nomination rules were changed (the "E Pluribus Hugo" system) to reduce slate dominance.
