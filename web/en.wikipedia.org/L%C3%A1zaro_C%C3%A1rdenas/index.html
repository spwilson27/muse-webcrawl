Lázaro Cárdenas

Lázaro Cárdenas del Río
Born21 May 1895, Jiquilpan, Michoacán, Mexico
Died19 October 1970, Mexico City
OfficePresident of Mexico, 1934–1940

Lázaro Cárdenas del Río (1895–1970) was a Mexican general and statesman who served as president of Mexico from 1934 to 1940. A veteran of the Mexican Revolution from Michoacán, he is remembered as one of Mexico's most popular presidents.

Presidency

Cárdenas carried out sweeping land reform, redistributing some 20 million hectares to ejidos (communal farms); promoted socialist education; supported organised labour; and broke the power of former president Plutarco Elías Calles, exiling him in 1936. On 18 March 1938 he expropriated the foreign-owned oil industry, creating Pemex — an act commemorated annually in Mexico. He also welcomed Spanish Republican refugees from the Spanish Civil War.

Legacy

His presidency consolidated the ruling party's corporatist structure while advancing the social promises of the Revolution. He later served as Secretary of Defense during the Second World War.
