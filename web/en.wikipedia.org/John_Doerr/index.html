John Doerr

[Image placeholder: portrait of John Doerr]

L. John Doerr (born June 29, 1951) is an American investor and venture capitalist at Kleiner Perkins in Menlo Park, California. He has directed venture funding to technology companies including Compaq, Netscape, Symantec, Sun Microsystems, Amazon, Google, and Twitter. Doerr was a leading advocate of the management system Objectives and Key Results (OKRs) and authored the best-selling book Measure What Matters (2018).

Doerr studied electrical engineering at Rice University and earned an MBA from Harvard Business School. He joined Intel in 1974 and became one of its top salespeople before joining Kleiner Perkins in 1980. There he backed many defining companies of the PC and internet eras; his 1999 investment in Google grew into one of the most successful venture investments ever.

Climate and philanthropy

From the mid-2000s Doerr promoted investment in clean technology, warning in a widely seen 2007 TED talk that climate change was the largest economic opportunity of the century. In 2022 he and his wife Ann gave US$1.1 billion to Stanford University to establish the Stanford Doerr School of Sustainability, one of the largest gifts ever to a university. His 2021 book Speed & Scale sets out a plan of OKRs for decarbonisation.

Doerr has served on public company boards and on President Obama's Economic Recovery Advisory Board.
