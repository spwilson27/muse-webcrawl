Fort William

Fort William, Scotland, beneath Ben Nevis at the southern end of the Great Glen.

Fort William is a name borne by several places and fortifications, most notably the town of Fort William in the Scottish Highlands, the Canadian fur-trade post at what is now Thunder Bay, Ontario, and Fort William in Kolkata, India. This article surveys the principal uses of the name.

Fort William, Scotland

Fort William (Scottish Gaelic: An Gearasdan, "The Garrison") is a town in Lochaber in the Scottish Highlands, on the shore of Loch Linnhe. It grew up around a fort built by English forces under General George Monck in 1654–55, during the Commonwealth period, to control the Highlands after the Civil War. The wooden fort was replaced by a stone fortress in 1690 and named after William of Orange (William III). Government troops held the fort during the Jacobite risings of 1715 and 1745; it was besieged unsuccessfully by Jacobite forces in 1746. The fort was demolished in the 19th century to make way for the railway. Today the town is a major centre for outdoor tourism, lying at the foot of Ben Nevis, the highest mountain in the British Isles, and at the start of the Great Glen and the Caledonian Canal.

Fort William, Ontario

Fort William was a North West Company fur-trade headquarters established in 1803 at the mouth of the Kaministiquia River on Lake Superior, named for William III. Renamed from Fort Kaministiquia, it became the company's great inland entrepôt where voyageurs exchanged trade goods and furs each summer. After the North West Company merged with the Hudson's Bay Company in 1821, the post declined. The city of Fort William, Ontario, incorporated in 1892, amalgamated with Port Arthur in 1970 to form Thunder Bay. A reconstructed Fort William Historical Park now stands near the original site.

Fort William, Kolkata

Fort William in Kolkata (Calcutta), India, was built by the British East India Company on the eastern bank of the Hooghly River. Named after King William III, the original fort was completed around 1706; after its capture by Siraj ud-Daulah in 1756—the event associated with the "Black Hole of Calcutta"—Robert Clive rebuilt a much larger octagonal fort, completed in 1781. It remains an Indian Army headquarters, the seat of the Eastern Command.

Other uses

Other Fort Williams include a fort in Newfoundland, a Hudson's Bay post in northern Canada, Fort William in Ghana (a British slave-trade fort at Anomabu), and Fort William, a 17th-century stronghold in Bengal. Several of these sites are preserved as historic monuments.
