The Indian Air Force (IAF) is the air arm of the Indian Armed Forces. Its primary mission is to secure Indian airspace and to conduct aerial warfare during armed conflict. It was officially established on 8 October 1932 as an auxiliary air force of the British Empire, and it adopted the prefix "Royal" during the Second World War; the prefix was dropped in 1950 when India became a republic.

The IAF is among the largest air forces in the world, with roughly 170,000 personnel and more than 1,600 aircraft in service, including multirole fighters such as the Sukhoi Su-30MKI, Dassault Rafale, and the indigenously developed HAL Tejas.

History

The force saw its first action in Waziristan and later in Burma during the Second World War. After independence it fought in the Indo-Pakistani wars of 1947–48, 1965, and 1971, and in the Kargil War of 1999, where high-altitude precision strikes played a prominent role. The IAF also conducts humanitarian assistance and disaster relief, and maintains India's strategic airlift and airborne early warning fleets.

Structure

The IAF is headed by the Chief of the Air Staff, an air chief marshal. It is organised into five operational commands — Western, Central, Eastern, Southern, and South Western — plus Training Command and Maintenance Command. Its fleet includes fighters, transport aircraft such as the C-17 Globemaster III, helicopters, UAVs, and the Garud Commando Force for special operations.
