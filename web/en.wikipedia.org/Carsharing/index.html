Carsharing

Carsharing is a model of car rental where people rent cars for short periods of time, often by the hour or minute, gaining access to a vehicle without the costs and responsibilities of ownership. It is distinct from traditional rental in its self-service access, distributed fleet, and pricing that includes fuel and insurance.

Models

In round-trip carsharing (such as Zipcar), vehicles are picked up from and returned to designated parking spots. In free-floating carsharing (such as the former car2go/Share Now), vehicles may be left anywhere in a home area. Peer-to-peer carsharing lets private owners rent out their own cars through a platform.

History and effects

One of the earliest schemes was the Sefage cooperative in Zurich in 1948; the modern industry grew from the 1980s and 1990s in Switzerland and Germany. Studies cited by operators suggest each shared car replaces several privately owned cars and that members drive less after joining, reducing emissions and parking demand, though free-floating schemes may also compete with public transport.
