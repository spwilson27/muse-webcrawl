Gerald Rudolph Ford Jr. (1913–2006) was the 38th president of the United States, serving from 1974 to 1977. He is the only president never elected to the presidency or vice presidency: he was appointed vice president under the Twenty-fifth Amendment after Spiro Agnew's resignation, and succeeded Richard Nixon when Nixon resigned over Watergate.

Early life and Congress

Born Leslie Lynch King Jr. and raised in Grand Rapids, Michigan, Ford starred on the University of Michigan football team and earned a law degree from Yale. After naval service in the Pacific he served 25 years in the House of Representatives, becoming House Minority Leader in 1965 and sitting on the Warren Commission.

Presidency

Ford declared that "our long national nightmare is over," but a month into his term he granted Nixon a full pardon, a decision widely blamed for his defeat in 1976 yet later praised as an act of healing. His administration contended with stagflation, the fall of Saigon in April 1975, and the Mayaguez incident. In the 1976 election, after fending off Ronald Reagan's primary challenge, Ford narrowly lost to Jimmy Carter.

Later life

Ford remained an elder statesman, and in 2001 received the Profile in Courage Award for the pardon. He died in 2006 aged 93, then the longest-lived U.S. president.
