The Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic (SADR; Spanish: República Árabe Saharaui Democrática) is a partially recognised state that claims the territory of Western Sahara, a former Spanish colony in North Africa. It is proclaimed and governed by the Polisario Front and is a member of the African Union.

History

Spain withdrew from Spanish Sahara in 1975–76 under the Madrid Accords, which partitioned the territory between Morocco and Mauritania despite an International Court of Justice advisory opinion finding no ties of territorial sovereignty that would override self-determination. The Polisario Front, founded in 1973 to fight Spanish rule, proclaimed the SADR at Bir Lehlou on 27 February 1976 and waged a guerrilla war against both occupying states. Mauritania withdrew in 1979; Morocco then extended its control and built a fortified sand wall, the berm, enclosing the western, economically useful portion of the territory. A UN-brokered ceasefire in 1991 promised a referendum on independence, which has never been held because of disputes over voter eligibility.

Government and territory

The SADR government administers the thinly populated "Free Zone" east of the berm — roughly a fifth to a quarter of the claimed territory — and the Sahrawi refugee camps near Tindouf in southwestern Algeria, where much of the population has lived since fleeing the war in 1975–76. Its constitution envisages a multiparty democracy after independence, but in practice the Polisario governs as a single movement; its president serves as head of state.

International status

Dozens of states have recognised the SADR at various times, though a number have withdrawn or frozen recognition. It has been a full member of the African Union since 1982, a step that led Morocco to leave the organisation (it rejoined in 2017 without the SADR being expelled). No UN member state recognises Moroccan sovereignty over Western Sahara de jure, and the UN lists the territory as a non-self-governing territory, although the United States recognised Moroccan sovereignty in 2020 and several states have since endorsed Morocco's autonomy plan. Fighting resumed at low intensity after the 1991 ceasefire broke down in November 2020.
