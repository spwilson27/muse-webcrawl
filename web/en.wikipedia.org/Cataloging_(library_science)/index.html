In library and information science, cataloging (or cataloguing) is the process of creating metadata describing information resources — books, journals, recordings, maps, digital files, and other materials — and organising that metadata into a catalogue so that users can find, identify, select, and obtain the resources.

Description and access points

Descriptive cataloging records attributes of an item such as title, statement of responsibility, edition, publisher, date, physical description, and series, following content standards such as RDA (Resource Description and Access), which succeeded the Anglo-American Cataloguing Rules (AACR2). Cataloguers also provide access points — authorised forms of names of persons, corporate bodies, and works — so that all works by an author or about a topic can be collocated in the catalogue.

Subject cataloging and classification

Subject cataloging assigns terms from controlled vocabularies, notably Library of Congress Subject Headings, and classification numbers from schemes such as the Dewey Decimal Classification or Library of Congress Classification, which determine shelf arrangement in physical collections.

Encoding and systems

Catalogue records were historically maintained on card catalogues; since the 1960s they have been encoded in the MARC (Machine-Readable Cataloging) format for computerised catalogues and shared through bibliographic utilities such as OCLC's WorldCat, which enable copy cataloging — the reuse of records created by other libraries. Newer models such as FRBR and its successor, the IFLA Library Reference Model, structure metadata around works, expressions, manifestations, and items, and linked-data formats such as BIBFRAME are intended to carry cataloguing into the web environment.
