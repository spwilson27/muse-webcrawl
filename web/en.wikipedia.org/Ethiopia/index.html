Ethiopia, officially the Federal Democratic Republic of Ethiopia, is a landlocked country located in the Horn of Africa region of East Africa. It shares borders with Eritrea, Djibouti, Somalia, Kenya, South Sudan and Sudan. Ethiopia has an area of 1,104,300 square kilometres and a population of over 120 million, making it the second-most populous nation in Africa after Nigeria.

Ethiopia is widely considered the birthplace of coffee and one of the oldest sites of early human ancestors. Founded on ancient kingdoms such as the Aksumite Empire, it is one of the few African countries never to have been formally colonised, having resisted European colonisation during the Scramble for Africa, though it was occupied by Italy from 1936 to 1941.

History

Fossils of early hominins, including "Lucy" (Australopithecus afarensis, c. 3.2 million years old), were found in the Afar region. The Kingdom of Aksum (c. 100–940 AD) was a major trading empire and adopted Christianity in the 4th century. After medieval Solomonic rule, Emperor Menelik II defeated Italy at the Battle of Adwa (1896), preserving independence. Haile Selassie ruled until the 1974 revolution brought the communist Derg to power; its fall in 1991 led to the present federal republic. The Tigray war (2020–2022) caused heavy loss of life before a peace agreement.

Geography

Ethiopia is dominated by a high central plateau (the Ethiopian Highlands) split by the Great Rift Valley, with the Danakil Depression — among the lowest and hottest places on Earth — in the northeast. Major rivers include the Blue Nile, which rises at Lake Tana. Coffee originates from the Kaffa region.

Politics

Ethiopia is a federal parliamentary republic; the president is head of state and the prime minister head of government. The country is divided into ethnically based regional states and chartered cities, including the capital, Addis Ababa, which also hosts the headquarters of the African Union.

Economy

Agriculture employs the majority of the population; coffee is the leading export. Recent decades saw rapid state-led growth in infrastructure, including the Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam on the Blue Nile. Services, manufacturing and the national carrier Ethiopian Airlines — Africa's largest airline — are also significant.

Culture

Ethiopia uses its own calendar (about seven to eight years behind the Gregorian) and time system. The Ge'ez script serves Amharic, Tigrinya and other languages. The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church is the largest religious body, alongside substantial Muslim and other communities. Cuisine centres on injera, a sourdough flatbread served with stews (wat).
