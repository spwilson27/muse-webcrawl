Holy Family

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Holy Family has been a favourite subject of Christian art since the Renaissance.

The Holy Family consists of the Child Jesus, the Virgin Mary and Saint Joseph. The subject became popular in Christian art from the 1490s, though earlier depictions of the three together do occur, and it also appears in the architecture of churches and shrines. The veneration of the Holy Family was formally recognised with the establishment of its feast, celebrated in the Catholic Church on the Sunday within the Octave of Christmas, or on 30 December when Christmas falls on a Sunday.

Depictions and devotion

In art, the Holy Family is usually shown as a domestic group at home in Nazareth, sometimes expanded with Saint Anne (Mary's mother) or the young John the Baptist, or in scenes such as the Flight into Egypt or the Presentation at the Temple. Artists including Michelangelo, Raphael, El Greco, Rembrandt and Murillo painted celebrated versions. Devotion to the Holy Family grew in the seventeenth century and was promoted strongly in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, when the family was proposed as a model for Christian families generally; religious congregations, schools and parishes worldwide bear its name, and the church of the Sagrada Família in Barcelona is dedicated in its honour.

Extended family

Accounts of Jesus' wider family come largely from apocryphal writings such as the Gospel of James, which describes Mary's parents Joachim and Anne. Debates about the "brothers of Jesus" mentioned in the Gospels shaped differing doctrines about Mary's perpetual virginity among Christian traditions.
