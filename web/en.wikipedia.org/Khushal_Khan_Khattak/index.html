Khushal Khan Khattak

Khushal Khan Khattak (1613 – 25 February 1689; Pashto: خوشال خان خټک), also known as Khushal Baba, was a Pashtun poet, tribal chief, and warrior from Akora Khattak in the Mughal Empire. He is regarded as the national poet of Afghanistan and a father of Pashto literature.

Life

Khushal was born in 1613 into the Khattak tribe. His grandfather Malik Akoray had cooperated with the Mughal emperor Akbar, and the Khattaks guarded the royal road from Peshawar to Attock. Khushal succeeded his father Shahbaz Khan as tribal chief and served the Mughals, fighting campaigns in the Hindu Kush. However, relations with the emperor Aurangzeb soured, and Khushal was imprisoned in the fortress of Gwalior in the Deccan for about two years.

Rebellion and poetry

Released and embittered, Khushal spent his later years trying — largely in vain — to unite the Pashtun tribes against Mughal rule. His poetry is famous for its martial spirit, calls for Pashtun unity and honour, praise of the sword, and contempt for those who submitted to Delhi. He wrote in Pashto and also composed works in Persian. His collected verse, the Kulliyat, runs to tens of thousands of lines and covers love, philosophy, religion, hunting, and tribal politics.

Death and legacy

Khushal died on 25 February 1689 at Dambara, in what is now Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan, and was buried near Akora Khattak; his tomb, inscribed with his wish that no Mughal dust disturb his rest, remains a place of pilgrimage. His descendants included his great-grandson Afzal Khan Khattak, author of the Tarikh-e-Morassa, a history of the Pashtuns. Peshawar's airport, schools, and the Khushal Khan Khattak University at Karak are named after him.
