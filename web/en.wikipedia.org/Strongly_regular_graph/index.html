Strongly regular graph

In graph theory, a strongly regular graph (SRG) is a regular graph with v vertices and degree k such that every two adjacent vertices have λ common neighbours and every two non-adjacent vertices have μ common neighbours. Such a graph is denoted srg(v, k, λ, μ). The concept was introduced by Raj Chandra Bose in 1963.

Examples

Examples include the Petersen graph, srg(10, 3, 0, 1); the 5-cycle, srg(5, 2, 0, 1); the Clebsch graph, srg(16, 5, 0, 2); and the Shrikhande graph, srg(16, 6, 2, 2), famous for sharing parameters with the 4×4 rook's graph while not being isomorphic to it. Paley graphs of prime-power order are strongly regular.

Properties

The adjacency matrix of a strongly regular graph has exactly three distinct eigenvalues, connecting the theory to linear algebra and design theory. The parameters must satisfy the feasibility condition (v − k − 1)μ = k(k − λ − 1). Strongly regular graphs are distance-regular graphs of diameter 2, and they arise from two-class association schemes, finite geometry, and block designs.
