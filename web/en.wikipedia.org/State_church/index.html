State church

A state church (or state religion) is a religious organisation or creed officially endorsed by the state. A state that has a state religion, while not necessarily theocratic, typically gives that religion special status in law and public life.

Examples

Current or historical examples include the Church of England (established in England), the Evangelical Lutheran churches of Denmark and Iceland, the Roman Catholic Church in Costa Rica, and Islam as the state religion of many Middle Eastern and North African countries. Buddhism has constitutional status in Sri Lanka and Cambodia.

Disestablishment

Many countries have disestablished their churches — the Church of Sweden separated from the state in 2000, and Norway loosened its ties in 2012 — reflecting the broader trend of separation of church and state in modern constitutions.
