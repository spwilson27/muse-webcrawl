Gudrun

Gudrun (Old Norse: Guðrún), known in Middle High German as Kudrun and in the Nibelungenlied as Kriemhild, is a major figure in the heroic legends of the Germanic peoples. She is the sister of Gunnar (Gunther) and the wife of the hero Sigurd (Siegfried), and her story of love, betrayal, and vengeance is central to the Völsung cycle in Norse mythology and to the Nibelungenlied.

In Norse tradition

In the Poetic Edda and the Völsunga saga, Gudrun Gjúkadóttir marries Sigurd after he rides through Brynhild's wall of flame. When Sigurd is murdered at the instigation of Brynhild and Gudrun's brothers, Gudrun's grief becomes the subject of several lays, including Guðrúnarkviða. She is later married, against her will, to Atli (Attila the Hun). After Atli kills her brothers Gunnar and Högni to seize the Nibelung treasure, Gudrun takes a terrible revenge: she kills her own sons by Atli, serves them to him at a feast, and burns his hall. In the later poems she incites her sons Hamdir and Sörli to avenge their half-sister Svanhild, a doomed expedition told in Hamðismál.

In German tradition

As Kriemhild in the Nibelungenlied (c. 1200), she is a Burgundian princess whose marriage to Siegfried and his murder by Hagen lead her, in the poem's second half, to marry Etzel (Attila) and engineer the destruction of the Burgundian court at his hall. A separate Middle High German epic, Kudrun (13th century), tells of a different heroine, Kudrun of the Hegelingen, whose abduction and steadfast endurance form a counterpoint to the vengeance of the Nibelungenlied.

Legacy

Gudrun/Kriemhild appears in Wagner's Ring cycle (as Gutrune), in Fritz Lang's film Die Nibelungen (1924), and in William Morris's epic The Story of Sigurd the Volsung (1876). The name Gudrun remains a common female given name in Iceland and Scandinavia.
