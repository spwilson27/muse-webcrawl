Rolls-Royce Motors was a British luxury car manufacturer, created as a separate division in 1973 when the car business of Rolls-Royce Limited was split from its aero-engine operations. The company built cars at Crewe, Cheshire, and was sold by its owner Vickers in 1998, an outcome that split the Rolls-Royce and Bentley marques between two German manufacturers.

History

Rolls-Royce Limited was nationalised in 1971 after the financial collapse caused by development of the RB211 jet engine. In 1973 the profitable car division was separated as Rolls-Royce Motors, while the aero business continued as Rolls-Royce (1971) Limited. Vickers bought Rolls-Royce Motors in 1980.

Under Vickers the company modernised its range, introducing the Silver Spirit in 1980 and later the Silver Seraph, the first entirely new Rolls-Royce in many years, launched in 1998 with a BMW-supplied V12 engine.

The 1998 sale

In 1998 Vickers agreed to sell Rolls-Royce Motors to Volkswagen AG for £430 million. However, the Rolls-Royce name and logo were licensed separately by Rolls-Royce plc, the aero-engine company, which granted them to BMW. From 2003, BMW's new subsidiary Rolls-Royce Motor Cars took over production of Rolls-Royce-branded cars at a new factory at Goodwood, while Volkswagen retained the Crewe plant and the Bentley marque, renaming the business Bentley Motors.

Models

Principal models of the period included the Silver Shadow II, Silver Spirit, Silver Spur, Corniche, Camargue, and the Silver Seraph, alongside the corresponding Bentley models such as the Mulsanne and Continental R.
