Guernica (Picasso)

Artist: Pablo Picasso
Year: 1937
Medium: Oil on canvas
Dimensions: 349.3 cm × 776.6 cm
Location: Museo Reina Sofía, Madrid

Guernica is a large 1937 oil painting by Spanish artist Pablo Picasso. It is one of his best-known works, regarded by many art critics as the most moving and powerful anti-war painting in history. It is exhibited in the Museo Reina Sofía in Madrid.

The grey, black, and white painting, on a canvas 3.49 metres (11 ft 5 in) tall and 7.77 metres (25 ft 6 in) across, portrays the suffering wrought by violence and chaos. Prominent in the composition are a gored horse, a bull, screaming women, a baby, a dead soldier, and flames.

Background and creation

Picasso painted Guernica at his home in Paris in response to the 26 April 1937 bombing of Guernica, a town in the Basque Country in northern Spain, by Nazi Germany's Condor Legion and Fascist Italy at the request of the Spanish Nationalists. Upon completion, Guernica was exhibited at the Spanish display at the 1937 Paris International Exposition, and then at other venues around the world. The touring exhibition was used to raise funds for Spanish war relief.

Interpretation

The scene is composed of writhing figures: a mother cradling her dead child, a bull standing impassively, a wounded horse, dismembered bodies, and observers at the edges. Scholars have debated the symbolism for decades; Picasso himself refused to explain the painting's imagery, saying the bull and horse stood for brutality, darkness, and the people. A famous anecdote tells of a German officer asking Picasso, "Did you do this?" and Picasso replying, "No, you did."

Later history

For safekeeping during World War II, the painting was held at the Museum of Modern Art in New York, where it remained until it could return to a democratic Spain. It arrived in Madrid in 1981 and moved to the Reina Sofía in 1992. A tapestry copy hangs at the United Nations headquarters in New York.
