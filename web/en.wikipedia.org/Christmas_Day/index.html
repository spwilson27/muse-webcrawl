Christmas Day is a holiday commemorating the birth of Jesus Christ, observed primarily on 25 December as a religious and cultural celebration among billions of people around the world. A feast central to the Christian liturgical year, it is preceded by the season of Advent and followed by the twelve days of Christmastide, which end with Epiphany.

History

The date of Jesus's birth is not stated in the Gospels. By the early-to-mid fourth century, the Western Christian church had placed Christmas on 25 December, a date later adopted in the East. Theories about the choice of date include the calculation of 25 March as the date of the Annunciation, nine months earlier, and the proximity of Roman festivals around the winter solstice, such as Saturnalia and the birthday of Sol Invictus. Some Eastern churches, using the Julian calendar, celebrate Christmas on the date corresponding to 7 January in the Gregorian calendar.

Observance and customs

Christmas Day is a public holiday in many countries. Customs vary widely and include attending church services, especially Midnight Mass on Christmas Eve; decorating Christmas trees and homes with lights; exchanging cards and gifts; singing carols; and sharing festive meals featuring dishes such as roast turkey or goose, Christmas pudding, and mince pies. The figure of Santa Claus (Father Christmas), derived from Saint Nicholas and other traditions, is said to deliver gifts to children on Christmas Eve.

In many countries, 26 December is also a holiday, known as Boxing Day in Commonwealth nations and Saint Stephen's Day elsewhere. Christmas customs blend Christian themes with older pre-Christian winter traditions and modern secular and commercial practices.

Economics

Christmas is a peak selling season for retailers in many nations, and the holiday has an enormous economic impact, with spending on gifts, food, decorations, and travel. The commercial importance of the season has grown since the 19th century and is sometimes criticized as a distraction from the religious meaning of the feast.
