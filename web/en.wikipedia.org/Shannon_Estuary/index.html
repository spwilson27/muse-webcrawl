Shannon Estuary

The Shannon Estuary, Ireland's largest estuary, seen from the air.

The Shannon Estuary (Irish: Inbhear na Sionainne) is the largest estuary in Ireland. It extends westward from the city of Limerick, where the River Shannon meets the tidal waters at the head of the estuary, for about 102 kilometres (63 mi) to the Atlantic Ocean between Loop Head in County Clare and Kerry Head in County Kerry. The estuary separates County Clare and County Kerry to the north and south respectively, with County Limerick along its north-eastern shores.

The estuary is an important commercial waterway: its deep, sheltered waters host the ports of Foynes and Limerick, the Moneypoint power station, and the Shannon Airport at its northern side, which grew from the transatlantic flying-boat base at Foynes. Tarbert and Killimer are linked by a car ferry across the estuary's lower reaches.

Ecology

The intertidal mudflats and saltmarshes of the estuary form a Special Area of Conservation and Special Protection Area, supporting internationally important numbers of wintering wildfowl and waders. The estuary is also home to Ireland's only resident population of bottlenose dolphins, numbering around 140 individuals, and dolphin-watching operates from Carrigaholt and Kilrush.

History

The estuary appears as "Senus" in Ptolemy's geography. In the mid-20th century it was central to transatlantic aviation: Foynes was the European terminus for flying boats in 1939–1945, and the modern Shannon Airport on the opposite shore became famous for pioneering duty-free shopping (the world's first airport duty-free shop opened there in 1947) and for the invention of Irish coffee, created at Foynes in 1943.
