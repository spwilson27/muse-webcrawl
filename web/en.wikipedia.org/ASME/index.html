The American Society of Mechanical Engineers (ASME) is an American professional association that, in its own words, "promotes the art, science, and practice of multidisciplinary engineering and allied sciences around the globe" via continuing education, training and professional development, codes and standards, research, conferences and publications, government relations, and other forms of outreach. Founded in 1880, ASME is one of the oldest and most influential standards-developing organisations in the world.

History

ASME was founded in 1880 by a group of leading industrialists and engineers, including Alexander Lyman Holley, Henry Rossiter Worthington and John Edison Sweet, partly in response to numerous steam boiler failures and explosions in the United States during the nineteenth century. The society's early work on standardisation of machine tools and screw threads led to its landmark Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code, first published in 1914–15, which greatly improved boiler safety.

Codes and standards

ASME develops and maintains more than 600 codes and standards covering pressure technology, piping, elevators and escalators, nuclear components, geometric dimensioning and tolerancing (the widely used ASME Y14.5 standard), and many other areas. Its Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code is referenced by regulation in much of North America and used worldwide. ASME standards are developed through a voluntary consensus process accredited by ANSI.

Activities

ASME publishes journals and conference proceedings through ASME Press and the ASME Digital Collection, organises technical conferences such as IMECE and Turbo Expo, administers the ASME Foundation's educational programmes, and designates Historic Mechanical Engineering Landmarks. The society also presents major awards, including the ASME Medal, and operates student sections and design competitions such as the Human Powered Vehicle Challenge.
