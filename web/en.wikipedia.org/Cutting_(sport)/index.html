Cutting (sport)

Cutting is a western-style equestrian sport that began within the ranching work of separating individual cattle from a herd. In competition, a horse and rider enter a herd of cattle, separate one animal, and then prevent it from returning to the herd for a set time, working largely through the horse's own movements with minimal rein input.

Competition

A cutting run lasts 2½ minutes. The rider "cuts" a cow from the herd with help from two herd holders and two turnback riders, then drops the reins; the horse must mirror the cow's movements to keep it separated. Judges score on the horse's accuracy, responsiveness, and degree of difficulty, with typical winning scores in the low-to-mid 70s out of 80 per judge's card. Penalties are assessed for losing a cow, helping the horse with the reins, or allowing the cow to rejoin the herd.

The cutting horse

Cutting horses, most often American Quarter Horses, are bred and trained for "cow sense" — an innate ability to read and react to cattle. Famous bloodlines descend from horses such as Doc Bar and Peppy San Badger. Training a finished cutting horse takes years, and elite horses compete for large purses in events run by the National Cutting Horse Association (NCHA), founded in 1946, including its Futurity for three-year-olds.
