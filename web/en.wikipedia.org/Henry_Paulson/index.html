Henry Paulson

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Henry Merritt Paulson Jr. (born March 28, 1946) is an American banker and financier who served as the 74th United States Secretary of the Treasury from 2006 to 2009 under President George W. Bush. As Treasury Secretary during the financial crisis of 2007–2008, he was one of the principal architects of the US government's response, including the $700 billion Troubled Asset Relief Program (TARP). Before joining the administration he was chairman and chief executive officer of Goldman Sachs.

Early life and career

Paulson was born in Palm Beach, Florida, and raised on a farm in Barrington Hills, Illinois. He graduated from Dartmouth College in 1968, where he played football and was a member of Phi Beta Kappa, and earned an MBA from Harvard Business School in 1970. After work at the Pentagon and in the Nixon White House (as an aide to John Ehrlichman), he joined Goldman Sachs in Chicago in 1974. He rose through investment banking, became co-head of the firm's investment banking division, and succeeded Jon Corzine as chairman and CEO in 1999, leading Goldman through its initial public offering that year. Known as "Hank" Paulson, he accumulated a fortune estimated at several hundred million dollars and was also a prominent conservationist, chairing The Nature Conservancy.

Secretary of the Treasury

Sworn in on July 10, 2006, Paulson initially focused on Social Security reform, trade, and relations with China through the Strategic Economic Dialogue. From 2007 the collapsing housing market dominated his tenure. He coordinated the conservatorship of Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac in September 2008; days later came the bankruptcy of Lehman Brothers, which he declined to rescue. Fearing systemic collapse, Paulson — together with Federal Reserve Chairman Ben Bernanke — asked Congress for emergency authority, producing the Emergency Economic Stabilization Act of 2008 and TARP, which was used to inject capital directly into banks. A famous episode of the crisis had Paulson kneeling before House Speaker Nancy Pelosi to plead for the bill's passage after its initial failure in the House.

Later work

After leaving office he published the memoir On the Brink (2010) and founded the Paulson Institute at the University of Chicago, focused on US–China relations and sustainable economic growth; his 2015 book Dealing with China became a bestseller in China. In 2016 he unexpectedly endorsed Hillary Clinton for president, criticising Donald Trump.
