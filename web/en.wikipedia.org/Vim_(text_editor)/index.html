Vim (text editor)

Vim (a contraction of "Vi IMproved") is a free and open-source, screen-based text editor program. It is an improved clone of Bill Joy's vi, written by Bram Moolenaar and first released publicly in 1991. Vim is a modal editor: it operates in distinct modes for inserting text and for issuing commands, which allows elaborate editing operations to be composed from keystrokes. It runs on Unix, Linux, macOS, Windows, and many other platforms, and is installed by default on most Unix-like systems.

Features

Beyond vi, Vim adds multiple windows and tabs, unlimited undo with persistent history, syntax highlighting for hundreds of languages, a built-in scripting language (Vim script) and interfaces to others, a plugin system, spell checking, code folding, and visual selection modes. Its modal grammar — operators combined with motions and text objects, such as diw ("delete inner word") — is credited with high editing efficiency once learned, though beginners face a famously steep learning curve, including the classic stumbling block of how to exit the program (:q!).

History and community

Moolenaar began Vim on the Amiga in 1988 as a clone of vi; version 1.14 appeared in 1991, and the editor was ported to Unix and beyond, growing through community contributions. Vim was released under a charityware licence encouraging donations to children in Uganda. After Moolenaar's death in 2023, development continued under community maintainers. The editor is a central combatant of the humorous "editor war" with Emacs, and its key bindings have been emulated in countless other programs; the Neovim fork, begun in 2014, modernises the codebase and has itself become widely used.
