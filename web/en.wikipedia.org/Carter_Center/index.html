The Carter Center is a nonprofit, non-governmental organisation founded in 1982 by former US president Jimmy Carter and former first lady Rosalynn Carter, in partnership with Emory University. Based in Atlanta, Georgia, the Center works to advance human rights and alleviate suffering in more than 80 countries, guided by the principle of addressing fundamental problems rather than duplicating the work of others. Its motto is "Waging peace. Fighting disease. Building hope."

Programmes

The Center is best known for observing elections — it has monitored more than a hundred elections in dozens of countries — and for its health programmes. Since 1986 it has led the international campaign to eradicate Guinea worm disease (dracunculiasis), reducing annual cases from an estimated 3.5 million to a handful, which would make it only the second human disease eradicated after smallpox. Other health work targets river blindness (onchocerciasis), trachoma, schistosomiasis, lymphatic filariasis and malaria. The Center also mediates conflicts, promotes democratic governance and human rights, and works on mental health policy through the programme Rosalynn Carter established.

Recognition

Jimmy Carter's work with the Center was recognised in 2002, when he received the Nobel Peace Prize for his decades of efforts in conflict resolution, democracy and human rights. The Carter Center complex in Atlanta also houses the Jimmy Carter Presidential Library and Museum.
