Strongheart

Strongheart (born Etzel von Oeringen; October 1, 1917 – June 24, 1929) was a male German Shepherd who was one of the early canine movie stars. After being trained in Germany as a police and war dog in the Imperial German Army during World War I, he was brought to the United States by husband-and-wife filmmakers Laurence Trimble and Jane Murfin, who had seen "the most magnificent specimen of a German shepherd" while touring European kennels.

Career

Trimble trained the dog and directed him in several films — The Silent Call (1921), Brawn of the North (1922), The Love Master (1924), and White Fang (1925) — the Jack London adaptation being widely considered his finest performance. Strongheart became one of the most popular film stars of his era, rivalled only by Rin Tin Tin, who entered films shortly afterwards.

Strongheart made enormous amounts of money for Warner Bros.-distributed productions and toured the country making personal appearances, drawing crowds of thousands. His popularity helped fuel the enormous vogue for German Shepherds as pets in the United States during the 1920s.

Personal life and death

Strongheart was mated with Lady Jule, another German Shepherd who appeared with him in The Love Master. Their offspring included a long line of famous show and film dogs. In 1929, while filming, Strongheart accidentally fell against a hot studio light and was burned; he died a few days later, on June 24, 1929, from complications of the resulting tumor and infection.

Legacy

Strongheart's grandson, Lightning, was a film star in the 1930s. A further descendant, Nemo, served in Vietnam and was reportedly the first sentry dog officially returned to the United States after military service. Strongheart received a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame in 1960, one of only three dogs so honoured, alongside Rin Tin Tin and Lassie.

In 2017, author Emily Arnold McCully published a picture book about Strongheart, and his story remains an important chapter in the history of animal actors.
