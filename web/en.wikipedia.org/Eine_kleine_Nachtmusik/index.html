Eine kleine Nachtmusik

Composer: Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart

Catalogue: K. 525

Composed: 10 August 1787, Vienna

Published: 1827 (posthumous)

Scoring: string ensemble (2 violins, viola, cello, double bass)

Movements: 4 (originally 5)

Eine kleine Nachtmusik ("A Little Night Music"), K. 525, is a serenade for strings in G major by Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart, completed in Vienna on 10 August 1787 while he was working on the opera Don Giovanni. It is one of the most famous and widely performed pieces of classical music, though the occasion for which it was written is unknown — Mozart entered it in his personal catalogue simply as "a little night music". The conventional translation "A Little Night Music" is a mild mistranslation: a more literal rendering is "a little serenade".

Movements

  1. Allegro vivace, G major, sonata form — opening with the famous rising Mannheim rocket theme.
  2. Romanze: Andante, C major — a gentle, lyrical slow movement.
  3. Menuetto: Allegretto, G major, with trio in D major.
  4. Rondo: Allegro, G major — a lively finale.

Mozart's catalogue entry lists five movements: between the first movement and the Romanze there was originally a second minuet, whose music is now lost. Scholars have speculated that it survives, adapted, in other works, but the serenade is universally performed in its four-movement form.

Publication and reception

The work was not published in Mozart's lifetime; his widow Constanze sold the manuscript in a bundle of scores in 1799, and it was first printed in 1827 by Johann André at Offenbach. Originally scored for a small string ensemble — one player per part in Mozart's time — it is now frequently performed by full string orchestras. The serenade's opening theme has become a byword for Mozart's music itself, quoted and parodied endlessly in film, advertising, and popular culture, including the 1979 play and 1984 film Amadeus.
