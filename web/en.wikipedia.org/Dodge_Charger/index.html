Dodge Charger

The Dodge Charger is a model of automobile marketed by Dodge (Stellantis) in several forms over seven main generations. The name first appeared on show cars in the 1960s and has been used for a mid-size muscle car, a personal luxury coupe, a subcompact hatchback, and a full-size four-door sedan.

History

The first production Charger (1966–1967) was a fastback coupe based on the Coronet. The second generation (1968–1970), with its famous "Coke bottle" styling, hidden headlights and available 426 Hemi V8, became an icon of the muscle-car era — further immortalized as the "General Lee" in the television series The Dukes of Hazzard and as the villain's car in the film Bullitt. Variants included the Charger R/T, the aerodynamic Charger 500 and the winged Charger Daytona built for NASCAR. Later 1970s Chargers shifted toward personal luxury; in the 1980s the name was applied to an unrelated front-wheel-drive hatchback derived from the Omni.

Modern era

The Charger name returned in 2006 on a rear-wheel-drive four-door sedan sharing its platform with the Chrysler 300 and Dodge Challenger. High-performance versions included the SRT8, the 707-horsepower Charger SRT Hellcat (2015) and the 797-horsepower Hellcat Redeye. The V8 sedan ended production in 2023; a new generation launched for 2024–2025 includes battery-electric Charger Daytona models and a twin-turbo inline-six "Sixpack" version.
