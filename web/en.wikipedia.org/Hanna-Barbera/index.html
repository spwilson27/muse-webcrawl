Hanna-Barbera

Hanna-Barbera was an American animation studio and production company founded on July 7, 1957, by William Hanna and Joseph Barbera, the creators of Tom and Jerry at MGM. For more than four decades it was the dominant producer of television animation, creating such series as The Flintstones, Yogi Bear, The Jetsons, Scooby-Doo, and The Smurfs.

History

When MGM closed its cartoon studio in 1957, Hanna and Barbera formed H-B Enterprises (soon Hanna-Barbera Productions) to produce animation for the new medium of television. To work within tiny TV budgets they perfected limited animation — fewer drawings, held poses, cycles, and strong dialogue and sound effects — beginning with The Ruff and Reddy Show (1957) and The Huckleberry Hound Show (1958), the first animated series to win an Emmy Award.

In 1960 The Flintstones became the first animated prime-time sitcom and a major hit, followed by Top Cat, The Jetsons, and Jonny Quest. The studio dominated Saturday-morning television in the 1960s and 1970s with franchises such as Scooby-Doo, Where Are You! (1969), and later produced The Smurfs (1981), one of the decade's biggest successes. Taft Broadcasting acquired the studio in 1966.

Later years and legacy

Turner Broadcasting purchased Hanna-Barbera in 1991, using its library to launch Cartoon Network in 1992. The studio's backlot unit produced new series under the Cartoon Network Studios banner. After William Hanna's death in 2001, Hanna-Barbera was absorbed into Warner Bros. Animation, and its name was retired, though Warner Bros. later revived the brand (including Hanna-Barbera Studios Europe). The studio's library of characters remains among the most recognisable in animation history.
