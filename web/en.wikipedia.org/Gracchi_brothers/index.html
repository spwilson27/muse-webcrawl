The Gracchi brothers, Tiberius Sempronius Gracchus (c. 163–133 BC) and Gaius Sempronius Gracchus (c. 154–121 BC), were Roman brothers and tribunes of the plebs who attempted sweeping social and political reforms in the late Roman Republic. Their careers are traditionally seen as the opening episode of the Republic's final century of crisis, often called the Roman Revolution.

The brothers were sons of Tiberius Sempronius Gracchus, a distinguished general and censor, and Cornelia Africana, daughter of Scipio Africanus, the victor over Hannibal. Raised in one of Rome's most prestigious families, they used the tribunate to champion land redistribution, subsidised grain, and the extension of citizenship.

Tiberius Gracchus

Elected tribune in 133 BC, Tiberius proposed the lex Sempronia agraria, which would enforce long-ignored limits on the amount of public land (ager publicus) any one citizen could occupy and redistribute the surplus to the poor. When a fellow tribune vetoed the bill, Tiberius had him deposed by the assembly — an unprecedented act. Tiberius was murdered by a mob of senators led by his cousin Scipio Nasica, along with about 300 of his supporters, the first major act of political bloodshed in Rome for centuries.

Gaius Gracchus

Tribune in 123 and 122 BC, Gaius revived and extended his brother's programme: a subsidised grain law (lex frumentaria), judicial reforms transferring juries from senators to equestrians, new colonies, roads, and a proposal to extend citizenship to Rome's Italian allies. His citizenship bill failed, his popularity collapsed, and in 121 BC, under the first senatus consultum ultimum, Gaius took his own life as his supporters were massacred on the Aventine.

Legacy

The Gracchi became symbols of popular reform and martyrdom. Later generations of populares invoked their example, while conservative tradition blamed them for introducing violence into Roman politics. Modern historians debate whether their reforms could have saved the Republic or merely accelerated its transformation.
