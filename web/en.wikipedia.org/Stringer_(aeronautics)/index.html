Stringer (aeronautics)

In aircraft fuselage structures, a stringer is a longitudinal member to which the skin is attached. Stringers run lengthwise along the fuselage (or along a wing) and, together with frames (circumferential members), form the primary structure in semi-monocoque construction. Stringers support the skin, transfer aerodynamic and pressurization loads to the frames, prevent the skin from buckling, and increase the bending stiffness of the structure.

Design

Stringers are typically made of aluminium alloy (increasingly of composite materials) and are manufactured by extrusion or forming sheet metal into cross-sections such as Z, L, T, J, hat, and blade shapes. They may be riveted, bonded, or integrally machined with the skin. In wings, similar longitudinal members are often called stringers on the skin panels, while the much larger main spanwise members are spars.

Related structures

A longeron is a main longitudinal member, typically heavier than a stringer, used particularly in truss-type structures and in some fuselage designs. In shipbuilding and civil engineering, analogous longitudinal stiffeners stiffen plates and shells against buckling.
