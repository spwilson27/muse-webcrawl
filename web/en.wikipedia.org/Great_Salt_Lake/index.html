The Great Salt Lake is the largest saltwater lake in the Western Hemisphere and the eighth-largest terminal lake in the world. It lies in the northern part of the U.S. state of Utah, west of the Wasatch Range and the city of Salt Lake City, which takes its name from the lake. The lake is the largest remnant of Lake Bonneville, a prehistoric pluvial lake that once covered much of western Utah.

Because the lake is endorheic (having no outlet besides evaporation), its water is substantially saltier than seawater. Its shallow depth and large surface area mean its level fluctuates dramatically with inflow; in November 2022 it reached its lowest recorded elevation, exposing lakebed and raising concerns about toxic dust, lost habitat and the collapse of its ecosystem. Heavy snowmelt in 2023–2024 raised the level by several feet.

Geography and hydrology

The lake is fed principally by the Bear, Weber and Jordan rivers. A rock-fill railway causeway built in 1959 by Southern Pacific (now Union Pacific) divides the lake into a north arm (Gunnison Bay) and a south arm (Gilbert Bay). Little water passes through the causeway, so the north arm is near salt saturation and coloured pink-red by halophilic algae and bacteria, while the south arm is less saline and blue-green. Islands include Antelope Island, Fremont Island and Stansbury Island.

Ecology

Despite its salinity, the lake supports a rich food web: brine shrimp (Artemia franciscana), harvested commercially as aquarium food, and brine flies feed millions of migratory birds — eared grebes, Wilson's phalaropes, American white pelicans and many others — making it one of the most important bird habitats in North America. No fish live in the lake itself.

Economy and recreation

Mineral extraction (salt, magnesium, potassium sulphate and other products) and brine shrimp harvesting are major industries. Nineteenth-century tourism featured grand resorts such as Saltair on the south shore; today Antelope Island State Park and Great Salt Lake State Park are the main visitor centres. The lake's density makes swimmers exceptionally buoyant.

Decline and dust concerns

Water diversions from the lake's tributaries for agriculture and urban use are the main driver of long-term decline. The exposed playa contains heavy metals and other contaminants from mining and industry, and wind-blown dust from the dry lakebed threatens the health of the Salt Lake City metropolitan population. Utah lawmakers responded with measures including a new state office for the lake, water-conservation programs and, in 2023, raising the berm at the causeway breach to favour the south arm's ecosystem.
