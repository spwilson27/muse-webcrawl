Salts Mill (sometimes written Salt's Mill) is a former textile mill in the village of Saltaire, near Shipley in West Yorkshire, England. Opened in 1853 by the industrialist Sir Titus Salt, it was at the time one of the largest factories in the world, and it formed the centrepiece of Salt's model industrial village. Since 1987 it has been redeveloped as a cultural and commercial complex, best known for its galleries of works by the artist David Hockney.

History

Titus Salt made his fortune spinning alpaca and mohair yarns in Bradford. Disturbed by the pollution and poor living conditions of the mill districts, he resolved to build a new factory and a complete planned village on a greenfield site beside the River Aire and the Leeds and Liverpool Canal. The mill opened on his fiftieth birthday, 20 September 1853, with a banquet for his workers inside its vast combing shed.

The mill complex was built in Italianate style from local sandstone. Its main spinning mill is over 500 feet long, and the site included engine houses, warehouses, a dyehouse and one of the tallest factory chimneys in Britain. Around it Salt built stone houses, schools, a hospital, an institute, churches and parks for his workforce — the village of Saltaire, recognised in 2001 as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Textile production declined in the twentieth century and the mill finally closed in 1986. The entrepreneur Jonathan Silver bought the complex in 1987 and transformed it into a centre for art, retail and technology businesses. The 1853 Gallery displays one of the largest collections of David Hockney's work, Silver having been a friend of the Bradford-born artist.

Present day

Today Salts Mill houses galleries, bookshops, antique and homeware stores, cafés and restaurants, and offices for technology firms, while remaining the visual anchor of the Saltaire World Heritage Site and a major visitor attraction in Yorkshire.
