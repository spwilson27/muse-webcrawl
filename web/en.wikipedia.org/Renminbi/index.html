Renminbi

The 100 yuan note of the fifth series of the renminbi.

The renminbi (Chinese: 人民币; pinyin: Rénmínbì, literally "People's Currency"; abbreviated RMB) is the official currency of the People's Republic of China. It is issued by the People's Bank of China, the country's central bank. The basic unit of the renminbi is the yuan (元), which is subdivided into ten jiao and one hundred fen. The yuan is represented by the symbol ¥ and the ISO currency code CNY; offshore yuan traded in centres such as Hong Kong is often designated CNH.

History

The renminbi was first issued in December 1948, shortly before the founding of the People's Republic, unifying the currencies of the Communist-controlled areas during the Chinese Civil War. For decades its exchange rate was administratively set, and a dual system of domestic and foreign-exchange certificates operated until the mid-1990s. China maintained a de facto peg to the US dollar for much of the period after 1994, moving to a managed float in 2005.

Internationalisation

From 2009 China promoted cross-border use of the renminbi in trade settlement, offshore bond issuance, and swap lines with other central banks. In 2015 the International Monetary Fund decided to include the renminbi in the basket of currencies making up the Special Drawing Right, effective October 2016, making it one of the world's principal reserve currencies. The currency nevertheless remains subject to capital controls, and its international share of payments and reserves is smaller than China's share of world trade. China has also piloted a central bank digital currency, the e-CNY.
