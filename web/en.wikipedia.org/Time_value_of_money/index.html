Compound growth of a sum invested today

The time value of money is the principle that a sum of money available today is worth more than the same sum at a future date, because money held now can be invested to earn a return, and because of inflation and risk. It is one of the foundational concepts of finance and underlies the valuation of bonds, shares, loans and investment projects.

Calculations

The future value (FV) of a present sum (PV) growing at interest rate i per period for n periods is FV = PV(1 + i)n. Reversing the operation — discounting — gives present value: PV = FV / (1 + i)n. Related formulas value annuities (level series of payments), perpetuities (PV = payment / i) and growing annuities. With continuous compounding, FV = PV·ert.

Applications

Businesses use discounted cash flow (DCF) analysis and net present value (NPV) to appraise investments, discounting expected future cash flows at a rate reflecting their risk. Lenders amortise loans using the same mathematics; bond prices are the present value of coupons and principal. Opportunity cost is integral to the concept: accepting money later means forgoing returns available now.

History

Early discussions appear in the work of the School of Salamanca, notably Martín de Azpilcueta in the 16th century, and compound-interest problems were treated by Fibonacci in 1202. The concept sharpened with the development of actuarial science and modern capital theory in the 19th and 20th centuries.
