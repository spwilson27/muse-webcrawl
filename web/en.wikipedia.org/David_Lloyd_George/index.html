David Lloyd George

David Lloyd George, 1st Earl Lloyd-George of Dwyfor (17 January 1863 – 26 March 1945) was Prime Minister of the United Kingdom from 1916 to 1922. A Liberal Party politician and the only Welshman to hold the office, he was a major figure in the introduction of the British welfare state and led the country through the First World War and its aftermath.

Early life and rise

Lloyd George was born in Manchester to Welsh parents and raised in Llanystumdwy, Caernarfonshire, by his widowed mother and his uncle, a shoemaker and Baptist preacher. He qualified as a solicitor and was elected Liberal MP for Caernarfon Boroughs in 1890, holding the seat for 55 years. He rose as a fiery Welsh nationalist and Nonconformist campaigner and opponent of the Boer War.

Chancellor and social reform

As President of the Board of Trade and then Chancellor of the Exchequer under Asquith, he introduced old-age pensions (1908) and the radical "People's Budget" of 1909, which taxed land and high incomes to fund social programmes and naval expansion; its rejection by the House of Lords provoked the constitutional crisis resolved by the Parliament Act 1911. The National Insurance Act 1911 laid foundations of the welfare state.

Prime Minister

As Minister of Munitions he tackled the shell crisis of 1915. In December 1916 he replaced Asquith at the head of a coalition government, driving the war effort through a small War Cabinet, convoy systems, and unified Allied command. After victory he was a principal at the Paris Peace Conference of 1919. His postwar coalition grappled with Irish independence — he negotiated the Anglo-Irish Treaty of 1921 — industrial unrest, and the Chanak Crisis, which helped trigger his fall in October 1922 after Conservatives withdrew from the coalition at the Carlton Club meeting.

Later life

Lloyd George led a diminished Liberal Party, promoted ambitious public-works proposals against unemployment, and remained an MP until his death shortly after being raised to the peerage as Earl Lloyd-George of Dwyfor in 1945. He remains the last Liberal prime minister of the United Kingdom.
