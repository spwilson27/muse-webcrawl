Dvija

Dvija (Sanskrit: द्विज, "twice-born") is a term in Hindu tradition for a member of one of the three upper varnas — Brahmin, Kshatriya and Vaishya — who has undergone the upanayana initiation ceremony and been invested with the sacred thread (yajnopavita). The initiation is regarded as a spiritual second birth, the first being physical birth.

The upanayana ceremony

In the upanayana, traditionally performed for boys at ages prescribed by the Dharmashastra texts (around 8 for Brahmins, 11 for Kshatriyas and 12 for Vaishyas, counted from conception in some traditions), the initiate is invested with a sacred thread worn over the left shoulder, is taught the Gayatri Mantra by his guru, and begins the first ashrama (stage of life), that of the celibate student (brahmacharya). The thread's strands and knots vary by tradition, and the rite marks eligibility to study the Veda and to perform certain rituals.

Historical and textual context

The concept is rooted in Vedic and post-Vedic literature and elaborated in the Grihya Sutras and Dharmashastras such as the Manusmriti, which specifies the ages, materials of the thread and the duties of the twice-born. The term is also applied metaphorically in Sanskrit literature to birds (born from the egg and then hatched) and to teeth (which erupt twice).

Modern practice

The upanayana continues to be widely performed, especially among Brahmin communities, though the period of Vedic studentship it once inaugurated is usually now symbolic. Some reform movements, notably the Arya Samaj, have advocated extending the rite beyond its traditional varna and gender boundaries, and ceremonies for girls are conducted in some communities.
