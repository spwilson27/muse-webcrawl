Date: 30 November 1939 – 13 March 1940
Location: Eastern Finland
Result: Moscow Peace Treaty; Finnish territorial concessions

Winter War

The Winter War (Finnish: talvisota) was a war between the Soviet Union and Finland, beginning with a Soviet invasion on 30 November 1939, three months after the outbreak of the Second World War, and ending 105 days later with the Moscow Peace Treaty on 13 March 1940.

Background

The Soviet Union demanded Finnish territory near Leningrad, offering land elsewhere in exchange. Finland refused, and after the staged shelling of the Soviet village of Mainila, the USSR attacked without a declaration of war. The League of Nations expelled the Soviet Union over the invasion.

Course of the war

Despite overwhelming Soviet superiority in men, tanks and aircraft, Finnish forces under Marshal Carl Gustaf Emil Mannerheim used knowledge of terrain, ski mobility, and motti tactics — encircling and destroying road-bound enemy columns piecemeal — to inflict disproportionate casualties, most famously at Suomussalmi and along the Raate Road. Finnish defences on the Karelian Isthmus (the Mannerheim Line) held for months before a massive Soviet offensive in February 1940 forced a negotiated peace.

Outcome

Finland ceded about 11% of its territory, including Viipuri, and evacuated some 430,000 Karelians, but preserved its independence. The Red Army's poor performance contributed to Hitler's decision to invade the Soviet Union in 1941; Finland fought the USSR again in the Continuation War (1941–1944).
