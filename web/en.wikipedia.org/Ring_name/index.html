A ring name is a type of stage name used by an athlete such as a professional wrestler, mixed martial artist or boxer whose real name is considered unattractive, dull, difficult to pronounce or spell, amusing for the wrong reasons, or projecting the wrong image. Since the advent of the Internet, it has become relatively easy to discover a fighter's real name, but performers continue to adopt ring names that match their character or gimmick.

Professional wrestling

In professional wrestling, ring names are nearly universal. Some are owned by the promotion rather than the performer — a wrestler departing a company may be unable to take a famous name with them, as happened in various disputes between WWE and its former talent. Others, such as "Stone Cold" Steve Austin or Dwayne "The Rock" Johnson, become so identified with the performer that they follow them into mainstream celebrity. Japanese and Mexican traditions add further conventions: luchadores in lucha libre treat their ring names and masks as near-sacred identities, sometimes legally changing their names to their ring names.

Boxing and martial arts

Boxers have long used colourful ring names, from "The Manassa Mauler" (Jack Dempsey) to "The Greatest" (Muhammad Ali, born Cassius Clay). In Japanese sumo, wrestlers compete under a shikona, a ring name that may be chosen by the wrestler or his stablemaster. Mixed martial artists frequently adopt nicknames announced alongside their real names.
