Assyrian Church of the East

Assyrian Church of the East

Classification: East Syriac Christianity

Head: Catholicos-Patriarch of the East

Liturgy: East Syriac Rite (Liturgy of Addai and Mari)

Historical centre: Mesopotamia (modern Iraq)

The Holy Apostolic Catholic Assyrian Church of the East is an Eastern Christian church that traces its origins to the See of Seleucia-Ctesiphon in Mesopotamia, traditionally founded by the apostles Thomas, Addai, and Mari. It follows the East Syriac tradition and the theology associated with Theodore of Mopsuestia and Nestorius, and was therefore long called the "Nestorian Church" by outsiders — a label the church itself rejects.

History

The Church of the East declared its independence from the western imperial church in the fifth century and, from its base in Persia, sent missionaries along the Silk Road. By the medieval period its dioceses stretched across Persia, Arabia, India, Central Asia, and China; a stele erected at Xi'an in 781 records the arrival of Christianity in Tang-dynasty China in 635. The church contracted sharply under the Mongol successor states and Timur's campaigns, and its remaining heartland became the Hakkari mountains and the Urmia plain.

Schisms and modern period

A schism in 1552 eventually produced a line of patriarchs in communion with Rome, the origin of the Chaldean Catholic Church. The Assyrian genocide during the First World War devastated the community; the patriarchate later moved into exile, and in 1968 a further split created the Ancient Church of the East. Today the church's membership is largely in the Assyrian diaspora, with its patriarchal seat returned to Erbil in the Kurdistan Region of Iraq in 2015.

Worship

The church's central eucharistic liturgy is the ancient Liturgy of Addai and Mari, notable for lacking an institution narrative in its oldest form. Worship is conducted in Syriac, and the church recognises seven sacraments, including the unusual sacrament of the Holy Leaven (malka).
