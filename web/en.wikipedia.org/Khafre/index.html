Khafre (also known by the Hellenised name Chephren) was an ancient Egyptian pharaoh of the Fourth Dynasty during the Old Kingdom. A son of Khufu, he reigned in the mid-26th century BC and is best known as the builder of the second-largest pyramid at Giza.

Reign

Khafre succeeded his elder brother Djedefre. The length of his reign is uncertain; the Turin King List credits him with around 26 years, while the later historian Manetho gives a much longer figure that most Egyptologists discount. Little is known of political events in his reign, which appears to have been prosperous and stable.

Pyramid complex

Khafre's pyramid at Giza stands 136.4 metres (448 ft) tall today — originally about 143.5 metres — and appears taller than Khufu's Great Pyramid because it sits on higher bedrock. It retains part of its smooth Tura limestone casing near the summit. The complex includes one of the best-preserved valley temples of the Old Kingdom, built of massive limestone and granite blocks, where Auguste Mariette in 1860 discovered the celebrated diorite statue of Khafre enthroned, guarded by the Horus falcon, now in the Egyptian Museum in Cairo.

The Great Sphinx

The Great Sphinx of Giza, carved from a single ridge of bedrock beside Khafre's causeway, is widely attributed to his reign, largely on the basis of its position within his pyramid complex and stylistic arguments — though the attribution remains debated and no contemporary inscription names its builder.
