Brown Corpus

A part-of-speech-tagged sample in the Brown Corpus format

The Brown University Standard Corpus of Present-Day American English, better known as the Brown Corpus, was the first million-word electronic corpus of English, compiled at Brown University by Henry Kučera and W. Nelson Francis and published in 1964. It contains about 1,014,000 words drawn from 500 texts published in the United States in 1961, sampled across fifteen genres of prose.

Design

The samples, each about 2,000 words, were distributed across categories including press reportage, editorials, reviews, religion, skills and hobbies, popular lore, belles lettres, learned writing, and several kinds of fiction. The design became the template for later corpora: the British LOB Corpus matched it exactly for 1978 British English, and the Freiburg updates (Frown and FLOB) repeated the sampling for 1990s texts, enabling diachronic comparison.

Tagging and use

The corpus is distributed in a part-of-speech-tagged form using an 87-tag tagset developed at Brown. Kučera and Francis's Computational Analysis of Present-Day American English (1967) provided the first large-scale frequency counts of English words and letter sequences — data famously used in psycholinguistics and, later, in natural language processing. The tagged Brown Corpus was a standard training and evaluation resource for early statistical taggers and parsers and remains bundled with toolkits such as NLTK.
