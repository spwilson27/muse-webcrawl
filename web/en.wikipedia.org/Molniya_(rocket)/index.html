Molniya (rocket)

The Molniya (Russian: Молния, "lightning"), GRAU index 8K78, was a Soviet and later Russian expendable carrier rocket derived from the R-7 Semyorka intercontinental ballistic missile family. Designed under Sergei Korolev at OKB-1, it was developed in the early 1960s to launch probes to the Moon, Venus and Mars and to place communications satellites into highly elliptical "Molniya orbits". Variants, above all the Molniya-M (8K78M), remained in service for nearly five decades.

Design

The Molniya used the R-7 core with its four strap-on boosters and added a more powerful upper stage (Block I) and a fourth stage (Block L) capable of restarting in orbit — essential for interplanetary missions and for injecting satellites into high-apogee Molniya orbits. The rocket stood about 43 metres tall and could deliver roughly 1,600 kg to trans-lunar trajectories or place payloads of about 2,000 kg into high elliptical orbits.

Operational history

The original 8K78 first flew in 1960 and launched many of the Soviet Union's early Luna, Venera and Mars probes, including Luna 9, the first spacecraft to soft-land on the Moon in 1966, and Venera 9 in 1975. The improved Molniya-M, introduced in 1964, became famous for launching the Molniya communications satellites that served the Soviet orbita television network, as well as Oko early-warning satellites. It flew 296 times with a high success rate. The final Molniya-M launch took place on 30 September 2010, after which its roles passed to Soyuz-2 rockets with Fregat upper stages.
