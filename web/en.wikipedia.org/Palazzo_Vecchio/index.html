Palazzo Vecchio

LocationPiazza della Signoria, Florence, Italy
ArchitectAttributed to Arnolfo di Cambio
Construction1299–1314
Current useTown hall and museum

The Palazzo Vecchio ("Old Palace") is the town hall of Florence, Italy. It overlooks the Piazza della Signoria, which holds a copy of Michelangelo's David statue, and is the seat of the city's municipal government as well as a museum. Built between 1299 and 1314, the fortress-like Romanesque palace is attributed to Arnolfo di Cambio.

History

Originally called the Palazzo della Signoria after the Signoria of Florence, the ruling body of the Republic of Florence, the palace became the residence of Cosimo I de' Medici in 1540. When the Medici moved across the Arno to the Palazzo Pitti in 1565, it became the "Palazzo Vecchio" — the old palace. Giorgio Vasari directed extensive redecoration for Cosimo, including the great Salone dei Cinquecento, whose walls were painted by Vasari and his workshop; Leonardo da Vinci's lost Battle of Anghiari once adorned the same hall.

Features

The palace's 94-metre Arnolfo Tower rises above the city and is one of Florence's symbols. Highlights include the Salone dei Cinquecento, the Studiolo of Francesco I, and the apartments of Eleonora of Toledo with frescoes by Bronzino.
