The Wolf Prize in Mathematics is awarded almost annually by the Wolf Foundation in Israel. It is one of the six Wolf Prizes established by the Foundation and has been awarded since 1978; the others are in Agriculture, Chemistry, Medicine, Physics, and Arts. The prize is considered one of the most prestigious in mathematics, often regarded alongside the Fields Medal and the Abel Prize.

Laureates

The first laureates, in 1978, were Israel Gelfand and Carl L. Siegel. Other recipients include André Weil, Shiing-Shen Chern, Paul Erdős, Misha Gromov, Robert Langlands, and Peter Sarnak. Until the Abel Prize began in 2003, the Wolf Prize was probably the closest equivalent to a "Nobel Prize in Mathematics".
