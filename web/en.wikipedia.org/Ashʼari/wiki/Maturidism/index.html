Maturidism

Maturidism (Arabic: الماتريدية, al-Māturīdiyya) is one of the main schools of Sunni Islamic theology (kalam), founded by Abu Mansur al-Maturidi (d. 944 CE) of Samarkand in Transoxiana. Together with Ash'arism, it is regarded as one of the two orthodox schools of Sunni kalam, and it became the predominant theological orientation of the Hanafi school of law, spreading widely through Central Asia, the Ottoman Empire, the Balkans, and South Asia.

Doctrine

Maturidi theology holds that reason can know some moral truths independently of revelation: human beings can recognise good and evil, and the existence of God, by rational reflection, although revelation remains necessary for the details of worship and law. Like the Ash'aris, Maturidis affirm God's attributes as real and eternal and hold the Qur'an to be God's uncreated speech, but they differ from Ash'aris on several points: they define faith (īmān) primarily as inner assent rather than including works; they teach that God does not impose obligations beyond human capacity; they hold that humans have a somewhat stronger role in originating their acts while God remains the creator of all things; and they maintain that God's wisdom means He acts for purposes, so that nothing He does is futile. On the question of the divine attributes, Maturidis additionally affirm the attribute of takwīn (bringing into being) as an eternal attribute distinct from God's power.

History

Al-Maturidi's principal work, the Kitāb al-Tawḥīd, together with his Qur'an commentary Taʾwīlāt al-Qurʾān, laid the school's foundations. The creed of Najm al-Din al-Nasafi (d. 1142), with its famous commentary by Sa'd al-Din al-Taftazani, became a standard summary of Maturidi doctrine. Under the Ottomans, Maturidism was the official theology of the empire's Hanafi establishment, and it continues to be taught across Turkey, Central Asia, and the Indian subcontinent, notably at Deobandi and Barelvi institutions.
