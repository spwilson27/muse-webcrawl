Diedc. 935/936, Baghdad

Abū al-Ḥasan ʿAlī ibn Ismāʿīl al-Ashʿarī (Arabic: أبو الحسن علي بن إسماعيل الأشعري; c. 873/874 – c. 935/936), commonly known as al-Ash'ari, was an Arab Muslim theologian and the eponymous founder of Ash'arism, the most prominent school of Sunni speculative theology (kalam). A descendant of the companion of Muhammad, Abu Musa al-Ash'ari, he was born in Basra and spent much of his life in Baghdad.

Life

Al-Ash'ari was educated in the Mu'tazilite school of kalam, studying under the leading Basran Mu'tazilite Abu 'Ali al-Jubba'i, who was also his stepfather. Around the age of forty, according to the traditional account, he experienced a crisis of conviction following a series of dreams and a famous debate with al-Jubba'i over the fate of three brothers (a believer, an unbeliever, and a child who died young) — a problem the Mu'tazilite doctrine of divine justice, he concluded, could not resolve satisfactorily. He publicly renounced Mu'tazilism from the pulpit of the Basra mosque and devoted the rest of his life to refuting his former school while defending traditional doctrine with rational argument.

Doctrine

Al-Ash'ari sought a middle path between the literalist traditionalism of the Hanbalis and the rationalism of the Mu'tazila. He affirmed the attributes of God (such as His hand, face and settling on the Throne) as real but "without asking how" (bi-lā kayf), held the Qur'an to be uncreated, and taught the doctrine of kasb ("acquisition"), according to which God creates human acts while human beings "acquire" them and are thus responsible for them. His atomistic occasionalism held that God continually recreates the world at every instant. His major works include Maqālāt al-Islāmiyyīn (a heresiographical survey of Islamic sects), Kitāb al-Lumaʿ, al-Ibāna ʿan uṣūl al-diyāna, and the Risāla ilā ahl al-thaghr.

Legacy

Ash'arism, developed by later followers such as al-Baqillani, al-Juwayni and al-Ghazali, became the dominant theological orientation of the Shafi'i and Maliki schools of Sunni Islam and remains influential today.
