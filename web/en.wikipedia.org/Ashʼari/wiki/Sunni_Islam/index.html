Sunni Islam (Arabic: أهل السنة والجماعة, Ahl as-Sunna wa'l-Jamā'a, "the people of the Sunnah and the community") is the largest branch of Islam, followed by roughly 85–90% of the world's Muslims. The name derives from sunna, the way and practice of the Islamic prophet Muhammad, whose example Sunnis hold to be authoritative alongside the Qur'an. Sunni Muslims accept the first four caliphs (Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman and Ali) as the legitimate successors (Rashidun) of Muhammad, in contrast to Shia Islam, which holds that Ali and his descendants were designated successors.

Beliefs and practice

Sunni doctrine is classically summarised in the six articles of faith: belief in God, His angels, His revealed books, His messengers, the Day of Judgement, and divine decree. Religious practice centres on the Five Pillars — the declaration of faith (shahada), the five daily prayers (salah), almsgiving (zakat), fasting during Ramadan (sawm), and the pilgrimage to Mecca (hajj). The Qur'an is regarded as the uncreated word of God, and the hadith collections — above all the Ṣaḥīḥ collections of al-Bukhari and Muslim, together with the four other books of the "Six Books" (al-Kutub al-Sitta) — record the Sunnah.

Law and theology

Sunni jurisprudence is organised into four surviving schools (madhhabs): the Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i and Hanbali, which differ in legal methodology while recognising one another as orthodox. In theology, the dominant schools of kalam are Ash'arism and Maturidism, alongside the traditionalist Athari creed associated with the Hanbalis. Sufism, the mystical dimension of Islam, was historically intertwined with Sunni life, and most Sufi orders situate themselves within Sunni orthodoxy.

History and distribution

Sunni identity crystallised in the 9th and 10th centuries as a self-conscious orthodoxy in response to sectarian disputes of the early caliphates. Sunni Islam is today the majority tradition across North Africa, the Middle East, Central and South Asia, Indonesia and much of sub-Saharan Africa, with adherents estimated at around 1.7 billion people.
