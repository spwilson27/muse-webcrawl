Cecil Sharp

Cecil James Sharp (22 November 1859 – 23 June 1924) was an English collector of folk songs, folk dances, and instrumental folk tunes, as well as a lecturer, teacher, composer, and musician. He was the foremost figure of the first folk-song revival in England and a founder of the movement to preserve traditional English music and dance, including morris dancing and English country dance.

Life and work

Sharp was born in Camberwell, London, educated at Uppingham and Clare College, Cambridge, and spent a decade in Australia before returning to England as a music teacher. In 1903, hearing the gardener John England sing "The Seeds of Love" in Hambridge, Somerset, prompted him to begin systematic collecting. Over the following years he noted thousands of songs from singers in rural England.

Appalachian collection

From 1916 to 1918, with his collaborator Maud Karpeles, Sharp travelled through the Appalachian Mountains of the United States, collecting over 1,600 versions of English ballads and songs that had survived in the region's isolated communities — material published in English Folk Songs from the Southern Appalachians (1932).

Legacy

Sharp founded the English Folk Dance Society in 1911 (later the English Folk Dance and Song Society). Its London headquarters, Cecil Sharp House, opened in 1930 and remains the home of the Vaughan Williams Memorial Library. His collecting methods and editorial choices have been debated, but his work preserved a tradition that might otherwise have been lost.
