Etruscan civilization

The Etruscan civilization was an ancient civilization created by the Etruscans, a people who inhabited Etruria in ancient Italy, centred on a territory corresponding roughly to Tuscany, western Umbria, and northern Lazio, with additional influences in the Po Valley and Campania. The civilization endured from about 900 BC until it was absorbed into the Roman Republic in the first century BC.

Origins

The origins of the Etruscans have been debated since antiquity: Herodotus claimed they migrated from Lydia in Anatolia, while Dionysius of Halicarnassus argued they were indigenous to Italy. The Etruscan language was not Indo-European and remains only partly understood, though it was written in an alphabet derived from the Greek alphabet of Euboea.

Society and culture

Etruscan society was organized into a league of city-states, including Veii, Tarquinia, Cerveteri, and Volsinii. The Etruscans were famed for their metalwork, painted tombs, terracotta sculpture, and religious practices centred on divination, including haruspicy (reading entrails) and the interpretation of lightning. Rome's last three kings were traditionally of Etruscan origin, and Rome inherited many institutions, symbols, and engineering techniques from Etruria, including the arch, gladiatorial games, and the fasces.

Decline

From the fifth century BC, Etruscan power waned under pressure from Rome, the Celts, and the Greek cities of southern Italy. One by one the Etruscan cities fell to Rome, and by the early first century BC Etruscan culture had been fully Romanized.
