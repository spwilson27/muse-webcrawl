Tripoli (Arabic: طرابلس, romanized: Ṭarābulus) is the largest city in northern Lebanon and the second-largest city in the country. Situated 81 km (50 mi) north of the capital Beirut, it is the capital of the North Governorate and the Tripoli District. Tripoli overlooks the eastern Mediterranean Sea, and it is the northernmost seaport in Lebanon. Offshore are a string of small islands, the largest of which, the Palm Islands, is a protected nature reserve.

History

The site has been inhabited since at least the Phoenician era; the name derives from the Greek Tripolis, "three cities", recalling the three districts founded by merchants from Tyre, Sidon, and Arwad. Tripoli was a centre of a Phoenician confederation before passing through Persian, Hellenistic, Roman, and early Islamic rule.

In 1109 the Crusaders captured the city after a long siege, and it became the capital of the County of Tripoli, one of the four Crusader states of the Levant. The citadel of Raymond de Saint-Gilles, much rebuilt in later centuries, dates from this era. In 1289 the Mamluk sultan Qalawun captured and razed the old city, building a new Tripoli inland around the citadel; many Mamluk mosques, madrasas, and hammams survive. The city later became part of the Ottoman Empire, known for its soap-making, textiles, and trade.

Modern city

Today Tripoli is known for its medieval old city with souks and the Great Mosque, for the Rashid Karami International Fair designed by Brazilian architect Oscar Niemeyer, and for traditional industries including olive-oil soap. The city has suffered from Lebanon's economic crises and from periodic clashes between the neighbourhoods of Bab al-Tabbaneh and Jabal Mohsen, and it played a prominent role in the 2019 Lebanese protests.
