Jacobus Arminius

Jacobus Arminius (1560 – 1609), the Latinized name of Jakob Hermanszoon, was a Dutch Reformed theologian whose views on predestination and free will gave rise to Arminianism, a major current within Protestant theology.

Born10 October 1560, Oudewater, Netherlands
Died19 October 1609, Leiden
OccupationTheologian, professor at Leiden
Known forArminianism

Life and teaching

Educated at Leiden and Geneva, Arminius became a pastor in Amsterdam and then professor of theology at Leiden in 1603. Questioning the strict Calvinist doctrine of unconditional election, he taught that God's predestination is based on foreknowledge of human faith, and that Christ died for all, though only believers are saved.

Controversy and legacy

His disputes with colleague Franciscus Gomarus polarised the Dutch church. After Arminius's death, his followers presented the Remonstrance of 1610; the Synod of Dort (1618–19) condemned their position, but Arminian ideas profoundly influenced later movements, notably Methodism through John Wesley.
