Burghley House

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Burghley House is a grand sixteenth-century English country house near Stamford, Lincolnshire. It is a leading example of the Elizabethan prodigy house, built between 1555 and 1587 for Sir William Cecil, later 1st Baron Burghley, who was Lord High Treasurer and chief minister to Queen Elizabeth I.

Architecture

The house is built of ashlar stone around a courtyard, in an early English Renaissance style drawing on French and Flemish models. Its skyline is animated by cupolas, obelisks, chimneys and the great staircase tower. The interiors were extensively remodelled in the late seventeenth century for the 5th Earl of Exeter, with painted ceilings and walls by Antonio Verrio, including the famous Heaven Room.

Collections and grounds

Burghley holds an important collection of Old Master paintings, Oriental ceramics and furniture, much of it assembled by the 5th Earl on his travels in Italy. The parkland was landscaped by Lancelot "Capability" Brown in the eighteenth century and includes a deer park and a lake. The house remains the family home of the Cecil family and is administered by a preservation trust.

In popular culture

Burghley has appeared in numerous films and television series, including Pride & Prejudice (2005), where it stood in for Rosings Park, and The Da Vinci Code. Its park hosts the Burghley Horse Trials, one of the world's leading three-day equestrian events, held annually since 1961.
