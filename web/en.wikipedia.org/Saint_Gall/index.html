Saint Gall

Bornc. 550, Ireland
Diedc. 646, Arbon, Switzerland
Venerated inRoman Catholic Church; Eastern Orthodox Church
Feast16 October

Saint Gall (c. 550 – c. 646) was an Irish missionary monk and a disciple of Saint Columbanus, remembered as the apostle of the region around Lake Constance. The Abbey and city of St. Gallen in Switzerland bear his name.

Life

Gall accompanied Columbanus from Bangor in Ireland to Francia and then toward Lombard Italy. Falling ill in the region of Bregenz, he remained behind and settled as a hermit in the Steinach valley, where a monastic community later grew around his cell. A famous legend tells how Gall ordered a bear to bring firewood and rewarded it with bread, commanding it to leave the valley thereafter.

Legacy

The Abbey of Saint Gall, founded in 719 on the site of his hermitage, became one of the great centres of learning of medieval Europe, famous for its library and its distinctive plainchant notation.
