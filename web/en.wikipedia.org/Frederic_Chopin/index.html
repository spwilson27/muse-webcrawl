Frederic Chopin

Frédéric François Chopin
Born: 1 March 1810, Żelazowa Wola, Duchy of Warsaw
Died: 17 October 1849, Paris, France
Occupation: Composer, pianist
Era: Romantic

Frédéric François Chopin (born Fryderyk Franciszek Chopin, 1 March 1810 – 17 October 1849) was a Polish composer and virtuoso pianist of the Romantic period who wrote primarily for solo piano. He has maintained worldwide renown as a leading musician of his era, one whose "poetic genius was based on a professional technique that was without equal in his generation".

Life

Chopin was born in Żelazowa Wola in the Duchy of Warsaw and grew up in Warsaw, which in 1815 became part of Congress Poland. A child prodigy, he completed his musical education and composed his earlier works in Warsaw before leaving Poland at the age of 20, less than a month before the outbreak of the November 1830 Uprising. At 21, he settled in Paris, where for the last 18 years of his life he gave only about 30 public performances, preferring the more intimate atmosphere of the salon. He supported himself by selling his compositions and by giving piano lessons, for which he was in high demand. Chopin formed a friendship with Franz Liszt and was admired by many of his contemporaries, including Robert Schumann. A brief and unhappy visit to Majorca with the French writer George Sand in 1838–39 would prove one of his most productive periods of composition. In his final years, he was financially supported by his admirer Jane Stirling, who also arranged for him to visit Scotland in 1848. For most of his life, Chopin was in poor health; he died in Paris in 1849 at the age of 39, probably of pericarditis aggravated by tuberculosis.

Works

All of Chopin's compositions include the piano. Most are for solo piano, though he also wrote two piano concertos, a few chamber pieces, and some 19 songs set to Polish lyrics. His piano writing is technically demanding and emphasises nuance and expressive depth. He invented the concept of the instrumental ballade. His major piano works also include mazurkas, waltzes, nocturnes, polonaises, études, impromptus, scherzos, preludes, and sonatas, some published only posthumously.

Legacy

Chopin is widely regarded as one of the greatest composers for the piano. The International Chopin Piano Competition, founded in 1927 and held in Warsaw, is among the most prestigious classical music competitions.
