A ZIP Code (an acronym for Zone Improvement Plan) is a system of postal codes used by the United States Postal Service (USPS). Introduced on 1 July 1963, the basic format consists of five digits; an extended ZIP+4 code, introduced in 1983, adds four further digits to identify a smaller delivery segment.

Structure

The first digit represents a group of states in a national area, the second and third digits a sectional center facility region, and the last two digits a specific post office or delivery zone. ZIP+4 codes refine this to blocks, buildings, or individual high-volume receivers. Codes were assigned to speed sorting as mail volumes grew; the mascot Mr. ZIP promoted adoption, which was voluntary for ordinary mailers but quickly became near-universal.

Uses

Beyond mail delivery, ZIP Codes are widely used for marketing, demographic and statistical analysis — the Census Bureau defines ZIP Code Tabulation Areas approximating them — as well as for sales-tax calculation, insurance rating and address verification in e-commerce.

Special codes

Certain codes are unique to single organisations or facilities, such as 12345 for General Electric in Schenectady and 90210 for Beverly Hills, California, which became famous through television. Military mail uses APO, FPO and DPO addresses with dedicated ZIP Codes.
