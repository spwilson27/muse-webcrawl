Learned Hand

Learned Hand
Born: 27 January 1872, Albany, New York
Died: 18 August 1961 (aged 89)
Office: Judge, U.S. Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit (1924–1961); Chief Judge (1939–1951)

Billings Learned Hand (January 27, 1872 – August 18, 1961) was an American jurist regarded as one of the most influential judges in United States history, despite never serving on the Supreme Court. He sat on the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York from 1909 and on the Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit from 1924 until his death, serving as its chief judge from 1939 to 1951.

Jurisprudence

Hand is celebrated for his lucid prose, judicial restraint and pragmatism. He formulated the "Hand formula" in United States v. Carroll Towing Co. (1947), expressing negligence in algebraic terms: a party is liable where the burden of precaution is less than the probability of harm multiplied by its gravity (B < PL). His opinions and speeches, including "The Spirit of Liberty" (1944), remain widely quoted.

Legacy

Hand was repeatedly passed over for the Supreme Court, partly for his political independence. Scholars rank him alongside Holmes and Brandeis in his influence on American law.
