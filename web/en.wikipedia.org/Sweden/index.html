Sweden

Sweden, formally the Kingdom of Sweden, is a Nordic country located on the Scandinavian Peninsula in Northern Europe. It borders Norway to the west and north, Finland to the east, and is connected to Denmark in the southwest by a bridge–tunnel across the Öresund. With a population of about 10.5 million, Sweden is the largest Nordic country by area (about 447,425 km²) and population. Its capital and largest city is Stockholm.

History

Sweden emerged as a unified kingdom in the Middle Ages. In the 17th century, the Swedish Empire was one of the great powers of Europe, controlling much of the Baltic region. After the Great Northern War (1700–1721), Sweden lost its great-power status and in 1809 lost Finland to Russia. Sweden has not participated in war since 1814, maintaining a long policy of neutrality and non-alignment, though it joined the European Union in 1995 and NATO in 2024. The modern welfare state was built largely under the Social Democrats during the 20th century.

Government and economy

Sweden is a constitutional monarchy and a parliamentary democracy; the monarch is head of state, while executive power rests with the government led by the prime minister, and legislative power with the Riksdag. Sweden has a highly developed mixed economy oriented to exports — engineering, telecommunications, automotive, and pharmaceuticals are major industries — and consistently ranks high in measures of quality of life, equality, and innovation.

Geography and culture

The country is largely covered by forests, with many lakes and a long Baltic coastline. Swedish is the official language. Sweden has produced notable figures such as Alfred Nobel, founder of the Nobel Prizes; filmmaker Ingmar Bergman; the band ABBA; and companies including IKEA, Volvo, Ericsson, Spotify, and H&M.
