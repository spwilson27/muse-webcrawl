Actaea, officially (120347) Salacia I Actaea, is a natural satellite of the large Kuiper belt object 120347 Salacia. It was discovered on 21 July 2006 from images taken by the Hubble Space Telescope and announced by Keith S. Noll and co-workers. In 2011 it was named after Actaea, one of the Nereids of Greek mythology, following the naming of Salacia after the Roman sea goddess.

Properties

Actaea is about 286 kilometres (178 mi) in diameter, roughly one third the size of Salacia (~846 km), and orbits its primary at a distance of about 5,619 km in 5.494 days on a nearly circular orbit. The pair is a trans-Neptunian binary system in the classical Kuiper belt (a cubewano). From the orbit and the system's total mass, Salacia's density was estimated at about 1.29 g/cm³, suggesting a composition of rock and water ice.
