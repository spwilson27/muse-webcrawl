Yitzhak Rabin (Hebrew: יִצְחָק רַבִּין; 1 March 1922 – 4 November 1995) was an Israeli statesman and general who served twice as Prime Minister of Israel. As Chief of Staff he led the Israel Defense Forces during the 1967 Six-Day War. In his second premiership he signed the Oslo Accords with the Palestine Liberation Organization and a peace treaty with Jordan, sharing the 1994 Nobel Peace Prize. He was assassinated in 1995 by a far-right Israeli extremist opposed to the peace process.

Military career

Rabin joined the Palmach in 1941 and fought in the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, commanding the Harel Brigade on the Jerusalem front. Rising through the IDF, he became Chief of Staff in 1964 and planned the air operation that opened the Six-Day War. He then served as ambassador to the United States (1968–1973).

First premiership and return

Becoming prime minister in 1974 after Golda Meir's resignation, Rabin authorised the 1976 Entebbe rescue operation but resigned in 1977 amid a financial scandal involving his wife. After years in opposition and as defence minister during the First Intifada, he led Labor back to power in 1992 promising peace and domestic change.

Peace process and assassination

Rabin signed the Oslo I Accord (1993), the famous White House handshake with Yasser Arafat under Bill Clinton, and the Israel–Jordan peace treaty (1994), followed by Oslo II in 1995. On 4 November 1995, leaving a peace rally in Tel Aviv, he was shot by Yigal Amir and died in hospital. His funeral drew world leaders; Kings of Israel Square was renamed Rabin Square.
