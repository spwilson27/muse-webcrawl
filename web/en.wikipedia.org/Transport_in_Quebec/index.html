Transport in Quebec

The St. Lawrence River and the autoroute network are the twin spines of transport in Quebec.

Transport in Quebec is shaped by the province's vast territory, its concentration of population along the St. Lawrence River, and its severe winters. Roads, railways, ports and airports link Montreal and Quebec City with the rest of Canada and the United States, while air and seasonal marine services reach the remote northern communities of Nunavik.

Roads

Quebec's highway network is administered by Transports Québec. The autoroute system, begun in the late 1950s, includes Autoroute 20 and the Trans-Canada Highway corridor along the south shore, Autoroute 40 on the north shore, and Autoroute 15 south to the U.S. border and north into the Laurentians. Numbered provincial routes extend the network to regional centres; numbered routes run to nearly 30,000 km when municipal roads are excluded. Ferries operated by the Société des traversiers du Québec cross the St. Lawrence at points such as Tadoussac–Baie-Sainte-Catherine and Lévis–Quebec City.

Rail

VIA Rail operates intercity passenger trains in the Quebec City–Windsor Corridor, including services through Montreal, Drummondville and Quebec City. Freight is dominated by Canadian National and Canadian Pacific, with short lines such as the Quebec Gatineau Railway. The Montreal Metro, opened in 1966, and the Réseau express métropolitain (REM) light-rail network, whose first branch opened in 2023, provide urban transit in the Montreal region; Exo runs commuter trains.

Water

The St. Lawrence Seaway, opened in 1959, allows ocean shipping to reach the Great Lakes. The Port of Montreal is Canada's second-busiest port and a major container terminal; Quebec City, Trois-Rivières, Sept-Îles and Port-Cartier handle bulk cargoes such as iron ore, grain and aluminium. Icebreaking keeps the lower river navigable in winter.

Air

Montréal–Trudeau International Airport is the province's principal international gateway, with Montréal–Mirabel now used mainly for cargo. Quebec City Jean Lesage International Airport serves the capital region, and a network of regional airports connects remote and northern communities, where air travel is often the only year-round option.
