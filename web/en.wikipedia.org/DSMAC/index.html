DSMAC

DSMAC, short for Digital Scene Matching Area Correlator, is a guidance system used in some cruise missiles, most notably later variants of the United States Tomahawk missile. It provides terminal guidance by comparing images of the terrain below the missile, captured by an onboard camera, with reference imagery stored in the missile's computer before launch.

Operation

Before a mission, satellite or reconnaissance photographs of the target area are processed into digital reference scenes. During flight, the missile's optical sensor takes pictures of the ground, and the correlator compares the live images with the stored scenes. When a match is found, the system determines the missile's position precisely and makes final course corrections toward the target. DSMAC is typically used together with TERCOM (Terrain Contour Matching), which uses a radar altimeter and stored elevation maps for mid-course navigation, and with satellite navigation on later models.

History

DSMAC was developed for the Tomahawk programme and was first used operationally during the 1991 Gulf War. It greatly improved the missile's accuracy compared with inertial and terrain-matching guidance alone, allowing it to strike specific aimpoints on buildings and other structures. Improved versions, sometimes called DSMAC II, offered faster processing and finer image resolution.
