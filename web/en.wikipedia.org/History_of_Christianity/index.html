History of Christianity

The history of Christianity concerns the Christian religion, the Church, and its influence from the 1st century to the present. It began in Roman Judaea with the ministry of Jesus of Nazareth and the belief of his followers that he had been crucified and raised from the dead.

Early Church to the fall of Rome

Christian communities spread through the Roman Empire despite periodic persecutions. In 313 the Edict of Milan legalised the faith under Constantine, and in 380 it became the state religion of Rome. Ecumenical councils defined doctrine on the Trinity and the nature of Christ, while monasticism arose in the Egyptian desert. The Church divided increasingly between the Greek East and Latin West.

Middle Ages, Reformation, and modern era

The East–West Schism of 1054 formalised the split between Eastern Orthodoxy and Roman Catholicism. Medieval Latin Christendom built cathedrals, founded universities and religious orders, and launched the Crusades. In the 16th century the Protestant Reformation of Martin Luther, John Calvin, and others fractured Western Christianity, prompting the Catholic Counter-Reformation. Missionary activity carried Christianity worldwide during the age of European colonisation; in the 20th and 21st centuries the faith's centre of gravity shifted towards Africa, Asia, and Latin America, alongside ecumenical movements and rapid Pentecostal growth.
