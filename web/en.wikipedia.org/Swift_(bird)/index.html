Swift (bird)

The swifts are a family, Apodidae, of highly aerial birds. They are superficially similar to swallows, to which they are not closely related, but are instead related to hummingbirds. Swifts are among the fastest birds in level flight and spend most of their lives in the air, feeding on insects they catch on the wing.

KingdomAnimalia
OrderApodiformes

SpeciesAbout 100

Description

Swifts have long, scythe-like wings, short legs and tiny feet unsuited to perching, reflected in the family name Apodidae, from the Greek for "footless". Their plumage is typically dark brown or black, sometimes with white markings. They are superb fliers: the common swift can remain airborne for ten months without landing, eating, drinking, mating and even sleeping on the wing.

Distribution and nesting

Swifts occur on all continents except Antarctica. Many species nest in crevices in cliffs, caves, hollow trees or buildings, gluing nesting material together with saliva. Some species, such as the edible-nest swiftlet of Southeast Asia, build nests almost entirely of solidified saliva, which are harvested for bird's nest soup. Many temperate species are long-distance migrants, wintering in Africa or southern Asia.

Speed

Swifts are frequently cited among the fastest animals on Earth. Needletails are reported to reach speeds above 160 km/h in level flight, and common swifts have been measured at over 110 km/h, making them among the fastest birds in powered flight.
