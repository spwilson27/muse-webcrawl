Black nationalism

Black nationalism is a type of racial nationalism that emphasizes the social, political, cultural, and economic self-determination of black people, primarily those of African descent living in Western countries. It arose in response to slavery, segregation, and colonialism, and has taken forms ranging from cultural revival to advocacy of separate nationhood.

History

Precursors include Martin Delany in the 19th century, who promoted emigration to Africa. In the early 20th century Marcus Garvey's Universal Negro Improvement Association built a mass movement around black pride, economic independence, and the Back-to-Africa idea. The Nation of Islam, founded in 1930, combined black separatism with a distinct theology.

Later developments

During the civil rights era, figures such as Malcolm X advanced black self-determination, while the Black Power movement popularized more radical variants. Cultural nationalists promoted Afrocentric education, art, and dress. Related currents emerged in Africa and the Caribbean alongside decolonization, including Négritude in the Francophone world.

Criticism

Critics, including integrationists, have argued that separatist strategies impede equality within multiracial societies, while supporters reply that group solidarity and independent institutions are prerequisites for genuine equality.
