Lever Brothers was a British manufacturing company founded in 1885 by brothers William Hesketh Lever, 1st Viscount Leverhulme (1851–1925) and James Darcy Lever (1854–1910). The company grew from a small grocery business in Wigan into one of the world's largest soap manufacturers, and in 1929 it merged with the Dutch margarine company Margarine Unie to form the multinational conglomerate Unilever.

History

The Lever brothers entered into partnership with their father's wholesale grocery business in Wigan in the 1870s. In 1885 they began manufacturing soap at a rented factory in Warrington, marketing it under the name Sunlight. Sunlight soap was among the first packaged, branded household products, and its success rested on advertising, consistent quality, and the use of vegetable oils (coconut and palm kernel oil) rather than tallow, producing a soap that lathered more freely.

Demand outgrew the Warrington works, and in 1888 the brothers began building a new factory and model village on marshland at Bromborough Pool on the Wirral, named Port Sunlight. Production there began in the 1890s. The village provided housing, schools, a hospital, a church, and recreational facilities for workers, and remains a notable example of industrial paternalism and early town planning.

Expansion

Lever Brothers expanded rapidly overseas, establishing factories and subsidiaries in the United States, Canada, Australia, South Africa, and across Europe. The American subsidiary, Lever Brothers Company, was founded in 1895 and built a plant in Cambridge, Massachusetts. The company diversified into related products, producing Lifebuoy disinfectant soap, Lux soap flakes (later Lux toilet soap), Vim scouring powder, and Rinso washing powder.

To secure supplies of palm oil, William Lever acquired concessions and plantations in the Belgian Congo (through the Huileries du Congo Belge, founded 1911) and in the Solomon Islands; the Congo operations were later criticized for their labour practices. Lever also bought the Niger Company in 1920, which became part of the United Africa Company.

Merger into Unilever

By the late 1920s Lever Brothers and Margarine Unie were competing in overlapping markets for oils and fats. On 2 September 1929 they signed an agreement to merge, creating Unilever, one of the first modern multinational corporations. The Lever Brothers name continued in use by Unilever subsidiaries for many years, and the American business operated as Lever Brothers Company until 1995.

Legacy

Port Sunlight survives as a conservation area and garden village, home to the Lady Lever Art Gallery, founded by William Lever in memory of his wife. Brands created by Lever Brothers, including Sunlight, Lifebuoy, and Lux, continued to be marketed by Unilever around the world.
