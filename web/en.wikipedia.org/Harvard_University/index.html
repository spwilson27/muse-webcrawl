Harvard University


Harvard Yard, Cambridge, Massachusetts

Harvard University is a private Ivy League research university in Cambridge, Massachusetts, United States. Founded in 1636 as Harvard College by the Great and General Court of the Massachusetts Bay Colony, it is the oldest institution of higher learning in the United States. Its influence, wealth, and rankings have made it one of the most prestigious universities in the world. Harvard's endowment, over $50 billion, is the largest of any academic institution.

History

The college was named for clergyman John Harvard, who bequeathed his library and half his estate to it in 1638. Initially training Congregational and Unitarian clergy, it gradually secularised through the 18th and 19th centuries. Under president Charles William Eliot (1869–1909) Harvard became a modern research university. Radcliffe College, its women's coordinate institution, merged fully in 1999. In 2023, the U.S. Supreme Court's decision in Students for Fair Admissions v. Harvard ended race-conscious affirmative action in college admissions nationwide.

Campus and organisation

Harvard's 209-acre main campus centres on Harvard Yard in Cambridge, with additional campuses across the Charles River in Boston's Longwood Medical Area and in Allston. The university comprises Harvard College and twelve graduate and professional schools, including the Law, Medical, Business, Divinity, and Kennedy schools. Harvard Library is the largest academic library system in the world, with some 20 million volumes.

People and reputation

Harvard alumni, faculty, and researchers include 161 Nobel laureates, more than any other institution, as well as eight U.S. presidents, numerous heads of state, and 14 Turing Award winners. Its motto is Veritas ("Truth"), and its colour is crimson. The university competes athletically as the Harvard Crimson in the Ivy League.
