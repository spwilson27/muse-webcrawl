Gravimeter

Gravimeter
MeasuresGravitational acceleration
Unitsm/s², Gal, mGal, µGal
TypesRelative (spring, superconducting); absolute (free-fall, atom interferometry)
UsesGeodesy, geophysics, resource exploration, volcanology

A gravimeter is an instrument used to measure the local gravitational field of the Earth (or another body). Because gravity varies with latitude, elevation, tides and the density of subsurface rock, precise gravimetry can reveal underground structures, making gravimeters essential tools in geodesy, geophysics and mineral and petroleum exploration.

Relative gravimeters

Most gravimeters are relative instruments: they measure differences in gravity between locations rather than its absolute value. The classic design is a highly sensitive spring balance carrying a fixed mass; the LaCoste-Romberg gravimeter uses a "zero-length" spring to achieve great sensitivity, while the Scintrex CG-series instruments are widely used in field surveys. Superconducting gravimeters, which levitate a niobium sphere in a persistent magnetic field, achieve extraordinary stability and are used to record Earth tides and long-period signals at fixed observatories.

Absolute gravimeters

Absolute gravimeters measure g directly, nowadays usually by timing the free fall of a corner-cube reflector in a vacuum with laser interferometry, as in the FG5 instrument, which reaches accuracies of a few microgals. Newer cold-atom (quantum) gravimeters use atom interferometry to measure acceleration and offer absolute readings without drift, suiting them to field and even mobile use.

Applications

Gravity surveys detect salt domes, ore bodies, voids and groundwater changes; repeated measurements monitor magma movement beneath volcanoes and aquifer depletion. Gravimetry contributes to defining the geoid, calibrating inertial navigation, and, from satellites such as GRACE and GOCE, mapping Earth's gravity field globally. Measurements are conventionally expressed in gals (1 Gal = 0.01 m/s²), with field anomalies typically measured in milligals.
