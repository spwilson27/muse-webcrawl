Graffiti

A large graffiti mural in a contemporary urban setting

Graffiti (singular graffito) is writing or drawings made on a wall or other surface, usually without permission and within public view. It has existed since ancient times, with examples dating back to ancient Egypt, ancient Greece, and the Roman Empire. In modern times, spray paint and marker pens have become the commonly used materials, and graffiti has developed into a complex art form closely associated with hip hop culture.

History

The term derives from the Italian word graffio ("a scratch"). Ancient graffiti survives at sites such as Pompeii, where inscriptions include everyday messages, declarations of love, and political slogans. Modern graffiti culture emerged in Philadelphia in the late 1960s and in New York City in the early 1970s, where writers such as TAKI 183 gained fame for tagging their names across the subway system. By the 1980s, graffiti had spread worldwide and begun to enter galleries.

Styles and techniques

Graffiti ranges from simple handwritten tags to elaborate pieces. Common forms include the tag (a stylized signature), the throw-up (large bubble letters, usually in two colors), and the piece (short for masterpiece, a large multicolored work). Wildstyle lettering features interlocking letters and arrows. Stencil graffiti, popularized by artists such as Banksy, uses pre-cut templates for rapid application.

Controversy and legality

Graffiti is often associated with vandalism and property damage, and many jurisdictions prosecute unauthorized marking of property. At the same time, commissioned murals and legal walls have gained acceptance, and some works are protected or sold for large sums. The debate over whether graffiti is art or crime remains central to its public reception.
