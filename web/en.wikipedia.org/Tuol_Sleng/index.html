Tuol Sleng (Khmer: "hill of the poisonous trees") was a secret prison — officially Security Prison 21 (S-21) — operated by the Khmer Rouge regime in Phnom Penh, Cambodia, between 1975 and 1979. The site was formerly the Tuol Svay Prey High School. It was one of at least 150–196 torture and execution centres established across the country during the Cambodian genocide.

Operation

Prisoners — including former soldiers and government officials of the Khmer Republic, professionals, intellectuals, ethnic minorities, and increasingly purged Khmer Rouge cadres and their families — were photographed, forced to give detailed autobiographies, and subjected to systematic torture to extract confessions of alleged counter-revolutionary plots. Most were then executed, many at the Choeung Ek extermination centre (the "Killing Fields") outside the city. Only a tiny number of prisoners survived, among them the artists Vann Nath and Bou Meng, and cell-maker Chum Mey.

Discovery and museum

The prison was discovered by Vietnamese forces in January 1979 after the fall of Phnom Penh. The extensive archives — including thousands of prisoner photographs and forced confessions — were preserved. The site became the Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum in 1980. Its commandant, Kaing Guek Eav, alias "Duch", was convicted of crimes against humanity by the Extraordinary Chambers in the Courts of Cambodia (ECCC) in 2010 and sentenced to life imprisonment; he died in 2020. Tuol Sleng's archives were inscribed in UNESCO's Memory of the World Register in 2009.
