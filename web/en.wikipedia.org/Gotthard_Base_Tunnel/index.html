Gotthard Base Tunnel


A multifunction station inside the tunnel.

The Gotthard Base Tunnel (GBT; German: Gotthard-Basistunnel) is a railway tunnel through the Alps in Switzerland. At 57.09 km (35.5 mi) it is the longest railway tunnel in the world, and the deepest, reaching a maximum depth of about 2,300 m below the surface. It runs between Erstfeld in the canton of Uri and Bodio in Ticino, forming the core of the New Railway Link through the Alps (NRLA) on the Rotterdam–Genoa corridor.

Construction and opening

Swiss voters approved the NRLA project in referendums in the 1990s, partly to shift transalpine freight from road to rail. Main excavation began in 1999–2003 from five access points; the final breakthrough came on 15 October 2010. The tunnel consists of two single-track tubes connected by cross passages every 325 m, with multifunction stations at Sedrun and Faido. Nine workers died during construction. The tunnel was officially opened on 1 June 2016 and entered regular service that December; the whole Gotthard axis, including the Ceneri Base Tunnel, was completed in 2020 at a cost of about CHF 12.2 billion for the GBT alone within a larger NRLA budget.

Operations

Passenger trains travel at up to 200–230 km/h, cutting Zurich–Milan journey times to under three hours, and up to 260 freight trains a day can pass, composed of heavier, longer trains than the old mountain route allowed. The tunnel lies almost level (maximum gradient 0.8%), eliminating the spirals and climbs of the 1882 Gotthard Tunnel line.
