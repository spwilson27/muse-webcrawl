Soca music

Soca music is a genre of music defined by Trinidadian musician Lord Shorty (Garfield Blackman), its inventor, as the "Soul of Calypso". It grew out of calypso in Trinidad and Tobago in the early 1970s and became closely associated with the music played at Carnival. Soca combines calypso with Indian musical instruments and rhythms, funk, soul, and later electronic dance music influences.

A soca performance during Trinidad Carnival

Origins

Lord Shorty began experimenting in the early 1970s, adding East Indian instruments such as the dholak, tabla, and dhantal to calypso, and speeding up its rhythm; his 1974 album Endless Vibration and the song "Indrani" (1973) are regarded as foundational. The name "soca" was coined by a journalist for Shorty's "soul of calypso". Shorty later developed a slower, spiritually oriented offshoot called rapso-influenced "sokah".

Styles and spread

Substyles include groovy soca (mid-tempo) and power soca (fast, high-energy, up to ~160 bpm), chutney soca mixing Indian Caribbean vocals, and parang soca at Christmas. Leading artists have included the Mighty Sparrow, Calypso Rose, Superblue, Machel Montano, Bunji Garlin, and Kes. Soca is the Carnival music of Barbados (Crop Over), Grenada, Saint Lucia, and the wider Caribbean diaspora.
