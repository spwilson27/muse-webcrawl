Al Jazeera Media Network

Al Jazeera Media Network is a Qatari state-funded international media conglomerate headquartered in Doha, Qatar. Launched on 1 November 1996, its flagship Al Jazeera Arabic channel transformed the Arab media landscape with relatively uncensored news and debate programming. The network expanded to include Al Jazeera English (2006), the Balkan and Turkish services, the digital platform AJ+, documentary and children's channels, and the sports network beIN Sports, which was spun off in 2014.

History and influence

Al Jazeera was founded with a grant from the Emir of Qatar, Sheikh Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani, hiring many journalists from the closed BBC Arabic Television service. It gained worldwide prominence after the September 11 attacks by broadcasting statements from al-Qaeda leaders and reporting from Taliban-controlled Afghanistan, and its coverage of the 2003 Iraq War and the 2011 Arab Spring made it one of the most influential news organizations in the Middle East. Its "the opinion and the other opinion" slogan signalled a break from state-censored Arab broadcasting.

Controversies

Critics, including neighbouring governments, accuse the network of reflecting Qatari foreign policy; demands to shut it down were among the conditions imposed during the 2017–2021 Qatar diplomatic crisis, which Al Jazeera survived. The network's journalists have also been targets: staff have been imprisoned in Egypt and, during the Gaza war that began in 2023, several Al Jazeera journalists were killed in Israeli strikes, prompting international protests.
