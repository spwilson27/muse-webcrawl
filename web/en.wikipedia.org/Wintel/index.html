Wintel

Wintel is a portmanteau of Windows and Intel, referring to personal computers running Microsoft Windows on Intel x86-compatible processors, or to the long-standing alliance between Microsoft and Intel that dominated the PC industry from the 1990s onward.

History

The partnership developed as the IBM PC and its clones standardised on Intel processors and Microsoft operating systems (first MS-DOS, then Windows). Through the 1990s, the two companies' coordinated product cycles — new versions of Windows requiring, and new Intel chips enabling, more processing power — underpinned rapid PC upgrades and gave the platform an overwhelming share of the desktop and laptop market. The term was widely used in industry journalism of the era, sometimes critically, to describe the pair's combined market power.

Competition and decline of the term

The strict Wintel alignment loosened over time. AMD became a major supplier of x86 processors for Windows machines, Microsoft ported Windows to ARM processors, and Intel chips powered Apple Macintosh computers between 2006 and 2020. With the growth of smartphones and tablets running other operating systems, the Wintel platform's dominance of personal computing diminished, though Windows on x86 remained standard in business and gaming PCs.
