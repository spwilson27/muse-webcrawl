Loch Linnhe

TypeSea loch (fjord)
LocationHighland, west coast of Scotland

Loch Linnhe is a sea loch on the west coast of Scotland, in the Highland council area. It runs northeastward from the Firth of Lorn for about 50 kilometres (including its upper arm, Loch Eil) to Fort William at its head, following the line of the Great Glen Fault. The Caledonian Canal uses the loch as its southwestern approach to the Great Glen.

Geography

The loch is a glacially deepened fjord. Near its mouth it is divided by the island of Lismore; the Lynn of Lorne and Lynn of Morvern flank that island. Settlements on its shores include Fort William, Corpach (with its aluminium works, paper mill, and canal sea lock), Onich, Ballachulish — near the mouth of Loch Leven, a side loch — and Port Appin. Ben Nevis, Britain's highest mountain, rises close to its head. Corran Narrows, crossed by a ferry, marks a marked constriction partway along the loch.

Nature and economy

The loch supports salmon farming and mussel fisheries and is noted for wildlife including seals, porpoises, and otters. The surrounding scenery — steep hills dropping to the water — makes it a signature landscape of the West Highlands and a popular cruising and kayaking area. Ballachulish was historically famous for its slate quarries.
