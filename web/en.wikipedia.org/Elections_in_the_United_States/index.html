Elections in the United States

Elections in the United States are held for government officials at the federal, state, and local levels. The United States has one of the most decentralized electoral systems in the world: elections are administered primarily by state and local governments under a framework set by state law and federal constitutional and statutory rules, resulting in hundreds of thousands of elected offices — the largest number of any country.

Federal elections

The President and Vice President are elected every four years through the Electoral College. All 435 members of the House of Representatives serve two-year terms, and Senators serve six-year staggered terms with roughly one third elected every two years. Federal elections occur on Election Day, the Tuesday after the first Monday of November; even years without a presidential contest are midterm elections.

State and local elections

States elect governors, legislatures, judges (in many states), and officials such as attorneys general and secretaries of state — the latter typically serving as chief election officers. Locally, voters choose mayors, councils, school boards, sheriffs, and decide ballot measures including initiatives, referendums, and recalls. Primary elections select party nominees, using closed, open, or top-two systems depending on the state.

Voting

Voter eligibility is generally limited to citizens aged 18 or older; registration is required in most states (North Dakota is the exception). Methods include in-person voting on paper ballots or machines, early voting, and mail voting, which is universal in some states such as Oregon, Washington, and Colorado. Turnout in presidential elections has recently been around 60 percent of eligible voters, and lower in midterms and local contests.

History and reform

Suffrage expanded through constitutional amendments: the 15th (race), 19th (sex), 24th (poll tax), and 26th (age 18). The Voting Rights Act of 1965 transformed enforcement, and modern debates concern voter identification, gerrymandering, campaign finance, and election security.
