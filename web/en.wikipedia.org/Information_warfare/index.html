Information warfare

Information warfare
DomainMilitary, political, cyber
Key elementsPropaganda, disinformation, electronic warfare, cyber operations, psychological operations
Related conceptsHybrid warfare, cyberwarfare

Information warfare (IW) is the use and management of information and communication technology in pursuit of a competitive advantage over an opponent. It involves the collection, distribution, and manipulation of information, including propaganda, disinformation, electronic warfare, and cyber operations, to influence adversaries' decision-making while protecting one's own information systems.

History

Although the term became prominent in the 1990s with the United States Department of Defense, the underlying practices are ancient: deception, espionage, and propaganda have accompanied warfare for millennia. Sun Tzu emphasised deception in The Art of War. In the twentieth century, radio broadcasts, leaflet drops, and psychological operations were central to both world wars and the Cold War.

The digital revolution transformed the field. The spread of the internet and social media allowed state and non-state actors to reach global audiences instantly, blurring the line between war and peace and between military and civilian targets.

Components

Commonly cited components include electronic warfare (jamming and spoofing signals), computer network operations, psychological operations (PSYOP), military deception, and operational security. Modern doctrines also include strategic communications and public diplomacy.

Notable examples

The 2007 cyberattacks on Estonia, the Stuxnet operation against Iranian nuclear facilities, and interference campaigns around elections in the 2010s are frequently cited as examples of information and cyber operations in practice. Analysts also discuss the role of information operations in the Russo-Ukrainian War.

Criticism and debate

Scholars debate whether information warfare is a distinct form of warfare or a supporting function of conventional operations. Others raise concerns about effects on civilian discourse, media freedom, and democratic institutions.
