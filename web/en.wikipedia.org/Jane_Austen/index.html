Jane Austen

Jane Austen (1775–1817) was an English novelist known for witty, ironic depictions of the landed gentry and the marriage market. Her works critique the dependence of women on marriage for status and security while remaining beloved romances.

Life

Born at Steventon, Hampshire, Austen was educated mainly at home, wrote juvenilia as a teenager, and lived at Chawton from 1809, where she prepared her major novels for publication.

Works

Her novels are Sense and Sensibility (1811), Pride and Prejudice (1813), Mansfield Park (1814), Emma (1815), and the posthumous Northanger Abbey and Persuasion (1817). She published anonymously in her lifetime.

Legacy

Austen's free indirect discourse and realism influenced the English novel; her works are constantly adapted for film and television.
