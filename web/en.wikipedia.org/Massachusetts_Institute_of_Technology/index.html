Massachusetts Institute of Technology

The Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) is a private research university in Cambridge, Massachusetts, founded in 1861. It is renowned for science, engineering, and technology education and research, with the motto Mens et Manus ("Mind and Hand").

History and campus

Founded by William Barton Rogers, MIT moved from Boston to its current Charles River campus in 1916, centred on the Great Dome. It grew through World War II radar and computing work at the Radiation Laboratory and Lincoln Laboratory, and played a central role in the space race and the development of computer science.

Academics

MIT has five schools (Architecture and Planning; Engineering; Humanities, Arts, and Social Sciences; Management; Science) and about 11,000 students, roughly half graduate. Admission is among the most selective in the world. Affiliates include over 100 Nobel laureates. Its endowment and research spending rank among the largest of any university.

Culture

MIT is known for its rigorous problem sets, hacker tradition of elaborate pranks, and entrepreneurship: alumni have founded companies with combined revenues rivalling a large national economy.
