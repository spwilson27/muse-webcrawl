Murchison meteorite

A fragment of the Murchison meteorite

The Murchison meteorite is a meteorite that fell in Australia in 1969 near Murchison, Victoria. It belongs to a class of meteorites rich in organic compounds called carbonaceous chondrites (specifically the CM group). More than 100 kilograms of material were recovered, making it one of the most studied meteorites of its type.

Fall and recovery

On 28 September 1969 at about 11 a.m., a bright fireball was observed over Victoria before the meteorite exploded and fragmented; debris fell over an area of more than 13 square kilometres. Because the fall was witnessed and the material quickly collected, the samples suffered minimal terrestrial contamination.

Organic chemistry

The Murchison meteorite is famous for its rich organic content: more than 70 different amino acids have been identified in it, many of which are rare or absent in terrestrial life, as well as nucleobases such as uracil and xanthine, carboxylic acids, and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons. In 2020, researchers reported detecting pre-solar grains within the meteorite dated to about 7 billion years old — the oldest solid material found on Earth. These findings support the hypothesis that interstellar chemistry could have supplied building blocks for the origin of life.
