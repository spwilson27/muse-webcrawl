Aconcagua

Elevation: 6,961 m (22,838 ft)
Location: Mendoza Province, Argentina
Range: Andes
First ascent: 1897, Matthias Zurbriggen

Aconcagua is a mountain in the Principal Cordillera of the Andes in Mendoza Province, Argentina. At 6,961 metres (22,838 ft) above sea level, it is the highest mountain in the Americas, the highest outside Asia, and the highest in both the Western and Southern Hemispheres. It is one of the Seven Summits.

Geology and climate

Unlike many Andean giants, Aconcagua is not a volcano, though it was long thought to be one; it is an uplifted mass of volcanic and sedimentary rock. The mountain lies in Aconcagua Provincial Park and is known for extreme weather: ferocious winds (the viento blanco, or white wind), severe cold, and very low humidity. Despite its altitude, the normal route is not technically difficult, and Aconcagua is sometimes called the highest "walk-up" mountain in the world — though altitude sickness, hypothermia, and storms make it dangerous, and deaths occur most seasons.

Climbing

The first recorded ascent was by the Swiss guide Matthias Zurbriggen in 1897, during an expedition led by Edward FitzGerald. The standard route ascends the Horcones Valley to base camp at Plaza de Mulas (4,370 m), where hundreds of climbers gather each season (November to February). An alternative, more demanding route, the Polish Glacier traverse, approaches from the Vacas Valley. The youngest and oldest climbers to summit have repeatedly drawn attention; notable records include a 9-year-old summiteer in 2013.

Name

The origin of the name is uncertain; it may derive from Quechua Akon Kahwak ("stone sentinel") or from Mapuche or Aymara terms for the far side of the Aconcagua River.
