Yaylag

TypeSeasonal mountain pasture
RegionCaucasus, Anatolia, Central Asia
Related termYayla (Turkish); jailoo (Kyrgyz)

Yaylag (from Turkic yay, "summer") refers to the high summer pastures used by semi-nomadic and transhumant herders in the Caucasus, Anatolia, and Central Asia. Families move their flocks of sheep, cattle, and horses from lowland winter quarters (qishlaq) up to cool mountain meadows for the summer months.

Practice

The migration to the yaylag typically begins in late spring. Herders live in tents or stone huts, produce cheese, butter, and yoghurt, and sell or store dairy products for the winter. The seasonal movement structures traditional calendars, festivals, and kinship obligations in communities from Azerbaijan and Dagestan to Turkey and Kyrgyzstan.

Modern changes

Sedentarisation policies in the Soviet era and modern land reforms reduced nomadic transhumance, but yaylag pasture use persists, now often with truck transport, and summer pasture festivals have become cultural and tourist events in several regions.
