Hanoi is the capital and second-largest city of Vietnam, located in the north on the Red River delta. Founded as Thang Long in 1010, it is one of Southeast Asia's oldest capitals, with a millennium of history as a political centre under Vietnamese dynasties, French colonial rule, and the Democratic Republic of Vietnam.

History

Emperor Ly Thai To moved the capital to the site in 1010, naming it Thang Long ("ascending dragon"). The name Hanoi ("city inside the river bend") dates from 1831. France made it capital of French Indochina in 1902, adding wide boulevards and villas beside the Old Quarter's 36 guild streets. Ho Chi Minh proclaimed the Democratic Republic of Vietnam here on 2 September 1945. After the First Indochina War and the Geneva division of 1954, Hanoi was capital of North Vietnam and endured heavy US bombing during the Vietnam War, until reunification in 1976 following the Paris Peace Accords.

Economy and reform

Since Vietnam's Đổi Mới reforms of 1986, Hanoi has grown rapidly, with new districts west of the old core, a metro system opened in 2021, and expanding manufacturing and services, while remaining the seat of government, universities and cultural institutions.

Culture and sights

Landmarks include Hoan Kiem Lake and Ngoc Son Temple, the Temple of Literature (1070), the One Pillar Pagoda, the Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum, the Imperial Citadel of Thang Long (UNESCO site), and French colonial buildings such as the Hanoi Opera House. Water puppetry, pho and bun cha are cultural signatures. Noi Bai International Airport serves the city, which connects by rail and road to Laos and southern Vietnam.
