West Falkland (Spanish: Isla Gran Malvina) is the second largest of the Falkland Islands in the South Atlantic. It is separated from East Falkland by the Falkland Sound. The island is hilly, with the Hornby Mountains along the sound, and its highest point is Mount Adam at about 700 metres.

Geography and wildlife

The landscape is largely moorland and grassland used for sheep farming. The coastline is indented with bays and inlets. West Falkland and its offshore islands support seabird colonies, sea lions and elephant seals; unlike East Falkland, it has fewer introduced predators in some areas, though foxes were introduced in the 1930s.

Settlements and economy

The largest settlement is Port Howard; other settlements include Fox Bay, Port Stephens, Hill Cove and Chartres. The population is under 200. The economy is based on sheep farming for wool, with growing tourism, especially cruise-ship and wildlife visits. During the 1982 Falklands War there were skirmishes on the island but no major land battle.
