Supernova nucleosynthesis

Supernova nucleosynthesis is the production of new chemical elements inside a supernova explosion. The intense heat, pressure, and neutron flux in the collapsing and exploding core of a massive star drive nuclear reactions that forge elements heavier than those produced during the star's normal lifetime, and the explosion disperses them into interstellar space.

Processes

Explosive burning stages in the star's shells produce elements such as silicon, sulfur, and iron-peak nuclei. The rapid neutron-capture process (r-process), long attributed mainly to supernovae and now also strongly associated with neutron-star mergers, builds many of the heaviest elements, including gold and uranium. Neutrino-driven winds may host additional capture processes.

Significance

Together with stellar nucleosynthesis and cosmic-ray spallation, supernova nucleosynthesis accounts for the cosmic abundances of the elements and enriches the interstellar medium from which later generations of stars and planets form.
