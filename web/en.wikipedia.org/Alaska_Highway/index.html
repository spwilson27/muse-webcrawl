Alaska Highway

Length: 2,232 km (1,387 mi)
Southern terminus: Dawson Creek, British Columbia
Northern terminus: Delta Junction, Alaska
Constructed: 1942

The Alaska Highway (French: Route de l'Alaska), also known as the Alcan Highway, was constructed during World War II to connect the contiguous United States to Alaska across Canada. It begins at the junction with several Canadian highways in Dawson Creek, British Columbia, and runs to Delta Junction, Alaska, via Whitehorse, Yukon.

Construction

Fearing a Japanese invasion of Alaska after the attack on Pearl Harbor, the United States and Canada agreed in early 1942 to build a supply road. Construction began in March 1942, with the US Army Corps of Engineers — including thousands of African American soldiers serving in segregated regiments — pushing the pioneer road through in about eight months. The highway was opened to military traffic in November 1942 and was ceremonially dedicated at Soldier's Summit on Kluane Lake.

Route

The highway runs northwest through British Columbia to Whitehorse, the Yukon capital, then continues through Kluane country and across the Alaska border to Delta Junction, where it meets the Richardson Highway to Fairbanks. Major communities along the route include Fort St. John, Fort Nelson, Watson Lake, Teslin, Haines Junction, and Tok.

Modern highway

Originally 2,700 km of rough gravel pioneer road, the highway has been progressively straightened and paved; its length is now about 2,232 km (1,387 mi). Historic mileposts are preserved along the route, with Mile 0 at Dawson Creek a popular starting point for travellers driving to Alaska.
