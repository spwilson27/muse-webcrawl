An aquarium (plural: aquaria or aquariums) is a vivarium of any size having at least one transparent side in which aquatic plants or animals are kept and displayed. The term also denotes a public institution housing such exhibits. Fishkeepers use aquaria to keep fish, invertebrates, amphibians, aquatic reptiles such as turtles, and aquatic plants; large public aquaria display sharks, rays, marine mammals, and entire reef or kelp-forest ecosystems.

History

The Romans kept fish in marble tanks, and ornamental goldfish were bred in China and Japan for centuries. The modern aquarium emerged in the 1850s, when improved glass and understanding of the "balanced aquarium" — plants oxygenating water for fish — made self-sustaining tanks possible; Philip Henry Gosse popularised the hobby in Britain and coined the word "aquarium" in its modern sense. London Zoo opened the first public aquarium building, the Fish House, in 1853. Twentieth-century innovations — electric filtration, heating, aeration, acrylic panels, and acrylic viewing tunnels — enabled the great oceanaria of today, such as the Georgia Aquarium (2005), Singapore's S.E.A. Aquarium, and China's Chimelong Ocean Kingdom.

Home aquaria

Home aquaria range from small bowls to thousands of litres. Successful keeping depends on managing the nitrogen cycle — bacteria convert toxic ammonia from waste into nitrite and then relatively harmless nitrate — through biological filtration, water changes, and stocking discipline. Popular categories include tropical freshwater community tanks, African cichlid setups, planted aquascapes in the style of Takashi Amano, brackish tanks, and saltwater reef tanks with corals maintained under intense lighting. The global ornamental-fish trade is a multibillion-dollar industry, raising both livelihood and sustainability questions.

Public aquaria and conservation

Major public aquaria function as zoos: they breed endangered species, rescue and rehabilitate marine animals, conduct research, and run education programmes, coordinated through bodies such as the Association of Zoos and Aquariums. They are also criticised, particularly over the keeping of cetaceans; several countries and cities have banned or restricted captive dolphin and whale displays, and some institutions have retired them.
