Oenology

Oenology (also enology; from Greek οἶνος, oinos, "wine") is the science and study of wine and winemaking. An oenologist is an expert in the science of wine and of the arts and techniques for making wine. Oenology is distinct from viticulture, which concerns the cultivation of grapes, though the two fields are closely related and are frequently taught together as "viticulture and oenology" or "viticulture and enology".

Scope

Oenology covers the chemistry and microbiology of fermentation, the management of yeast and bacteria, clarification and stabilisation, barrel ageing, blending, and quality control. Oenologists analyse sugar levels (Brix), acidity, and flavour compounds, decide harvest and pressing regimes in cooperation with viticulturists, and oversee bottling.

Education

Formal training in oenology is offered at institutions such as the University of California, Davis; the University of Bordeaux; the University of Adelaide; and Geisenheim University in Germany. Famous oenologists and consulting winemakers include Émile Peynaud, often called the father of modern oenology, and Michel Rolland.

History

Winemaking is ancient, but oenology as a science began in the 19th century with Louis Pasteur's studies of fermentation, which identified the role of yeast and spoilage microorganisms. The 20th century brought temperature-controlled fermentation, cultured yeasts, and stainless steel tanks, professionalising wine production worldwide.
