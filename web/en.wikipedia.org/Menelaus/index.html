Menelaus

In Greek mythology, Menelaus (Greek: Μενέλαος) was a king of Mycenaean Sparta, the husband of Helen of Troy, and a central figure in the Trojan War. The younger brother of Agamemnon, king of Mycenae and leader of the Greek expedition, Menelaus sought to recover Helen after her abduction by the Trojan prince Paris — the act that, in the myth, caused the war.

Mythology

Menelaus and Agamemnon were driven from Mycenae by their uncle Thyestes and took refuge in Sparta, where Menelaus married Helen, daughter of King Tyndareus, and inherited the Spartan throne. All of Helen's former suitors had sworn the Oath of Tyndareus to defend her marriage; when Paris abducted her, Menelaus invoked the oath and the Greek kings assembled at Aulis to sail against Troy.

In the Iliad and Odyssey

In the Iliad Menelaus fights a duel with Paris in Book 3, which nearly ends the war before the gods intervene. In the Odyssey, Telemachus visits Menelaus and Helen, restored together in Sparta, who recount their wanderings home from Troy — including Menelaus' detention in Egypt and his wrestling of the sea god Proteus for knowledge of the way home and of Odysseus' fate.

After the war

Unlike most Greek heroes, Menelaus was promised in the Odyssey that, as Zeus's son-in-law, he would not die but be transported to the Elysian Fields. His sanctuary, the Menelaion, stood on a hill near Sparta and received cult worship in historical times; an archaeological site there preserves a Mycenaean mansion beneath the later shrine.
