John Zápolya or Szapolyai (Hungarian: Szapolyai János or Zápolya János; Croatian: Ivan Zapolja; c. 1487 – 22 July 1540) was King of Hungary (as John I) from 1526 to 1540. His rule was disputed by Ferdinand I, later Holy Roman Emperor, in the first phase of the Ottoman–Habsburg wars and the division of Hungary. Before his elevation he was Voivode of Transylvania and one of the kingdom's richest magnates.

Early career

John was the son of Stephen Zápolya, palatine of Hungary, and Hedwig of Cieszyn. As voivode of Transylvania from 1510, he brutally suppressed the peasant revolt led by György Dózsa in 1514, and the punitive laws that followed (enshrined in Werbőczy's Tripartitum) bound the peasantry to the soil. His ambition for the crown was already marked: in 1505 the Diet of Rákos had resolved that no foreigner should be elected king, a measure aimed at supporting a native candidate such as Zápolya.

King of Hungary

After King Louis II was killed at the Battle of Mohács (29 August 1526) against the Ottomans of Suleiman the Magnificent, Zápolya — who had not reached the battle in time — was elected king by the Diet at Székesfehérvár and crowned on 11 November 1526. A rival Diet elected Ferdinand of Habsburg in December. Civil war followed; defeated by Ferdinand, John fled to Poland and in 1528 concluded an alliance with Suleiman, becoming the Sultan's vassal and recovering his kingdom with Ottoman support in 1529, though Buda itself fell briefly to Ferdinand's forces.

Treaty of Nagyvárad and death

Exhausted by war, the two kings signed the secret Treaty of Nagyvárad (Oradea) in 1538, recognising each other's territories and providing that John's lands would pass to Ferdinand after his death. John then married Isabella Jagiellon of Poland in 1539; their son, John Sigismund, was born days before John's death at Sebeș on 22 July 1540. John's supporters repudiated the treaty and elected the infant king, precipitating the Ottoman occupation of central Hungary and the tripartite division of the kingdom.
