Hyperinflation in the Weimar Republic
Period: 1921–1923 (peak November 1923)
Currency: Papiermark
Exchange rate at peak: US$1 = 4.2 trillion marks
Ended by: Rentenmark reform, November 1923

Hyperinflation in the Weimar Republic was a period of currency instability in Germany from 1921 to 1923, peaking in late 1923 when the papiermark became effectively worthless. Prices doubled within days at the height of the crisis; one US dollar exchanged for 4.2 trillion marks in November 1923.

Causes

Germany had financed the First World War largely by borrowing rather than taxation, expecting victory indemnities. Defeat instead brought reparations under the Treaty of Versailles fixed in 1921 at 132 billion gold marks. Successive Weimar governments ran large deficits and the Reichsbank under Rudolf von Havenstein printed money to meet obligations and buy foreign currency. When Germany defaulted on deliveries, French and Belgian troops occupied the Ruhr in January 1923; the government financed passive resistance there by printing still more money, triggering the final spiral.

Course and effects

Workers were paid twice a day and rushed to spend wages before they lost value; banknotes were used as fuel and wallpaper, and barter replaced money in many transactions. Savers, pensioners and bondholders were wiped out, while debtors — including indebted farmers, businesses and the state itself — saw their debts evaporate. The crisis fed political extremism, including the Nazi Beer Hall Putsch of November 1923.

Stabilisation

Under Finance Minister Hans Luther and Reichsbank president Hjalmar Schacht, the Rentenmark was introduced in November 1923, notionally backed by land and industrial assets, at one trillion old marks to one. Fiscal discipline, an end to Ruhr resistance, and the 1924 Dawes Plan restructuring reparations restored confidence and ushered in the “Golden Twenties”.

Legacy

The trauma of 1923 left a lasting German aversion to inflation that has influenced the Bundesbank, the European Central Bank and German fiscal culture.
