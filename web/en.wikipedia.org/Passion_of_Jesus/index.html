The Passion of Jesus or Passion of Christ is the short final period of the life of Jesus of Nazareth, from his triumphal entry into Jerusalem to his crucifixion and burial. It includes episodes such as the Last Supper, the agony in the garden of Gethsemane, the betrayal by Judas Iscariot, the trials before the Sanhedrin and Pontius Pilate, the carrying of the cross, and the crucifixion itself. The word "Passion" derives from the Latin passio, meaning suffering.

Gospel accounts

All four canonical Gospels devote a disproportionately large share of their narrative to the Passion. The accounts agree on the broad sequence — Last Supper, arrest, trials, crucifixion, burial — while differing in detail and emphasis. Mark's Gospel, the earliest, is sometimes described as "a Passion narrative with an extended introduction". John's Gospel adds distinctive episodes such as the washing of the disciples' feet and the long farewell discourses.

Sequence of events

  • The Last Supper and the institution of the Eucharist
  • The agony in Gethsemane and the arrest
  • The trial before the Sanhedrin and Peter's denial
  • The trial before Pontius Pilate and the release of Barabbas
  • The scourging, mocking, and carrying of the cross along the Via Dolorosa
  • The crucifixion at Golgotha, the sayings from the cross, death and burial

In Christian devotion

The Passion is commemorated during Holy Week, most solemnly on Good Friday. Devotional practices include the Stations of the Cross, the rosary's Sorrowful Mysteries, and Passion plays such as the Oberammergau Passion Play, performed since 1634. The events are also central to doctrines of atonement and redemption.

In art and music

The Passion has been a dominant subject of Western art, from medieval altarpieces to works by Giotto, Duccio and Grünewald (the Isenheim Altarpiece). In music, the settings by J. S. Bach — the St Matthew Passion and St John Passion — are celebrated masterpieces, and Passion settings were composed by Schütz, Handel, Penderecki and many others. Films include Mel Gibson's The Passion of the Christ (2004).
