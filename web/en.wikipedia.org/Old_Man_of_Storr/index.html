The Old Man of Storr is a large pinnacle of basalt rock on the Trotternish peninsula of the Isle of Skye in Scotland. Standing about 50 metres high on the eastern flank of the Storr (719 m), it is one of Scotland's most famous and photographed landmarks, visible for miles across the Sound of Raasay.

Geology and folklore

The Storr is part of the Trotternish ridge, where thick lava flows resting on weaker sedimentary rocks have repeatedly collapsed in landslips, leaving fantastical pinnacles and towers. The "Old Man" is a remnant spire of one such slip. Local folklore explains the pinnacle as a giant turned to stone or the thumb of a buried giant; the name Storr itself derives from Old Norse.

Climbing and tourism

The pinnacle was first climbed in 1955 by Don Whillans and James Barber. Today a well-trodden path from the A855 north of Portree climbs through the "Sanctuary" of pinnacles to viewpoints around the Old Man, making it one of Skye's most popular walks. The landscape has appeared in films including Ridley Scott's Prometheus (2012).
