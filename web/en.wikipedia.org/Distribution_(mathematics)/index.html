Distribution (mathematics)

Distributions, also known as Schwartz distributions or generalized functions, are objects that generalize the classical notion of functions in mathematical analysis. Distributions make it possible to differentiate functions whose derivatives do not exist in the classical sense, and every locally integrable function has a distributional derivative. The theory was systematized by Laurent Schwartz in the 1940s, work for which he received the Fields Medal in 1950.

Motivation

Physicists had long used the Dirac delta function — an object that is zero everywhere except at one point, yet integrates to one — to model point charges and impulses, even though no ordinary function has these properties. Distributions provide a rigorous framework for such objects: a distribution is not defined by its values at points but by how it acts on ordinary (test) functions.

Definition

Fix an open set in Euclidean space. A distribution is a continuous linear functional on the space of smooth, compactly supported test functions, equipped with a suitable topology. Every locally integrable function defines a distribution by integrating the product with a test function. The Dirac delta at a point is the distribution that evaluates a test function at that point. Distributions can be differentiated indefinitely by transferring the derivative (with its sign changes) onto the test function; they can also be multiplied by smooth functions and, under conditions, combined by convolution.

Applications

Distributions underpin the modern theory of linear partial differential equations: concepts such as fundamental solutions — distributions whose application of an operator gives the delta — are defined in this framework, and theorems of Malgrange and Ehrenpreis guarantee fundamental solutions for constant-coefficient operators. Distribution theory is also used in Fourier analysis, where the Fourier transform extends to tempered distributions, and in probability, signal processing and quantum field theory.

History

Precursors included Oliver Heaviside's operational calculus and Paul Dirac's delta function in quantum mechanics. Sergei Sobolev introduced generalized functions in 1936 in connection with partial differential equations, and Schwartz unified and completed the theory between 1944 and 1950, publishing his Théorie des distributions. Mikio Sato's hyperfunctions and other frameworks later generalized the idea further.
