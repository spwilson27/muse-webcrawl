The rainbow trout (Oncorhynchus mykiss) is a species of trout native to cold-water tributaries of the Pacific Ocean in North America and Asia. It takes its name from the broad reddish-pink band along its lateral line and its heavily spotted body. The anadromous form, which migrates to the sea, is called steelhead.

Biology

Rainbow trout inhabit clear, cool, well-oxygenated streams, rivers and lakes. They spawn in gravel redds in spring, and feed opportunistically on insect larvae, crustaceans, fish eggs and small fish. Colouration varies with habitat: lake and sea-run fish are silvery, while stream residents show the vivid band most strongly in the spawning season.

Distribution and human use

Since the 19th century the species has been introduced worldwide for sport fishing and aquaculture, and is now found on every continent except Antarctica. It is among the most widely farmed cold-water fish and a prized game fish, but introduced populations can harm native fish and amphibians, and the species is listed among the world's 100 worst invasive alien species. Some native steelhead populations are protected under endangered-species legislation.
