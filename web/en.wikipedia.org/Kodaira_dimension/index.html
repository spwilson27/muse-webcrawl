Kodaira dimension

In algebraic geometry, the Kodaira dimension κ(X) measures the size of the canonical model of a projective variety X. Introduced by Kunihiko Kodaira through the work of the Italian school and Shigeru Iitaka, it takes values from −∞ up to the dimension of the variety.

Definition

The Kodaira dimension is defined by the growth rate of the dimensions of the spaces of global sections of powers of the canonical bundle: it is the exponent governing how h⁰(X, mKX) grows as m increases, or −∞ if no pluricanonical sections exist.

Significance

The Kodaira dimension is the coarsest birational invariant of a variety and underlies the Enriques–Kodaira classification of surfaces and the minimal model program in higher dimensions.
