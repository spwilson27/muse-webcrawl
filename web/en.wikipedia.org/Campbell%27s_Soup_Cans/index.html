Year1962
MediumSynthetic polymer paint on canvas (32 canvases)

Campbell's Soup Cans (sometimes referred to as 32 Campbell's Soup Cans) is a work of art consisting of thirty-two canvases, each measuring 20 inches (51 cm) in height by 16 inches (41 cm) in width, created in 1962 by the American artist Andy Warhol. Each canvas depicts a can of Campbell's soup — one of each of the condensed soup varieties the company offered at the time.

Creation and exhibition

The canvases were first exhibited on 9 July 1962 at the Ferus Gallery in Los Angeles, arranged on shelves to mimic a grocery store display. The exhibition was a landmark moment in the emergence of Pop art, challenging distinctions between commercial imagery and fine art. Dealer Irving Blum purchased the set and later sold it; the complete group was acquired by the Museum of Modern Art in New York in 1996.

Significance

Warhol's serial, quasi-mechanical treatment of a supermarket product became one of the defining images of Pop art and of twentieth-century art generally. Warhol later continued the soup can motif in screenprints using a variety of colour schemes.
