National Cabinet

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The National Cabinet is an intergovernmental forum in Australia composed of the prime minister and the premiers and chief ministers of the states and territories. It was established on 13 March 2020 in response to the COVID-19 pandemic, replacing the Council of Australian Governments (COAG), which was formally abolished in May 2020.

Formation and role

National Cabinet was created to coordinate the national response to the pandemic, managing matters such as border restrictions, quarantine, health-system capacity, and vaccine rollout. Unlike COAG, it operates on cabinet conventions and by consensus rather than as a traditional intergovernmental council.

In 2020 the government announced that National Cabinet would continue permanently as the principal forum for federal–state relations, meeting regularly and focusing on priorities including jobs, health, energy, housing, and skills.

Status debate

The body's legal status has been debated. Although named a "cabinet", it is not a cabinet committee in the constitutional sense, and a 2021 Federal Court decision held that it was not a committee of the Commonwealth Cabinet, meaning its documents were not automatically exempt from freedom-of-information requests. Legislation to give its deliberations cabinet-in-confidence status was proposed but contested by transparency advocates and crossbench parliamentarians.
