Weimar

Weimar is a city in the German state of Thuringia, with a population of about 65,000. It lies on the Ilm river, south of the Ettersberg. Weimar is famous for its cultural heritage: it was the home of Goethe and Schiller, the cradle of the Bauhaus movement, and the place where the German National Assembly met in 1919 to draw up the constitution of the Weimar Republic. The Classical Weimar ensemble and the Bauhaus sites are UNESCO World Heritage Sites.

History

First mentioned in documents of 899, Weimar became the residence of the dukes of Saxe-Weimar. Under Duchess Anna Amalia and her son Carl August, the court attracted Wieland, Goethe, Herder and Schiller, making the small town the centre of German classicism; Franz Liszt later made it a musical capital. In 1919 the National Assembly fled revolutionary Berlin for Weimar, and Germany's first democratic constitution was adopted there. Walter Gropius founded the Bauhaus in Weimar in 1919 before it moved to Dessau.

Dark chapter

On the nearby Ettersberg the Nazi regime built the Buchenwald concentration camp in 1937, where more than 56,000 people died. The camp memorial is a major site of remembrance.

Culture

Weimar was a European Capital of Culture in 1999. Landmarks include Goethe's house and garden house, Schiller's house, the Duchess Anna Amalia Library (restored after a devastating 2004 fire), the Bauhaus Museum and the German National Theatre.
