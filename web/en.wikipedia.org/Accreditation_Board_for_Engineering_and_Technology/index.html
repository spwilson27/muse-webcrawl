Accreditation Board for Engineering and Technology

ABET, founded as the Engineers' Council for Professional Development in 1932 and known from 1980 as the Accreditation Board for Engineering and Technology, Inc., is a non-governmental organization that accredits post-secondary programs in engineering, engineering technology, computing and applied and natural science. Based in Baltimore, Maryland, it is the recognized US accreditor for college and university STEM programs.

History

Seven engineering societies founded the organization in 1932 to coordinate professional development and education. It began accrediting engineering programs in 1936. The current name was adopted in 1980; since 2005 the organization has branded itself simply as ABET. Its member societies include IEEE, ASME, ASCE and dozens of other professional bodies.

Accreditation process

Programs voluntarily request accreditation and are reviewed by volunteer program evaluators from academia and industry against published criteria covering student outcomes, curriculum, faculty, facilities and continuous improvement. ABET's four commissions accredit engineering (EAC), engineering technology (TAC), computing (CAC) and applied and natural science (ANSAC) programs. Graduation from an ABET-accredited program is a prerequisite for professional engineer licensure in many US states.

International activity

ABET is a founding signatory of the Washington Accord (1989), through which signatory bodies mutually recognize the substantial equivalence of accredited engineering degrees. It also accredits programs outside the United States directly and supports quality assurance development in many countries.
