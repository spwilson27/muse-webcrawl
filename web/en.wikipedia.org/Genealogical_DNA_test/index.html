Genealogical DNA test

A genealogical DNA test is a DNA-based genetic test used in genetic genealogy that looks at specific locations of a person's genome in order to find or verify ancestral genealogical relationships, or (with lower reliability) to estimate the ethnic mixture of an individual. Direct-to-consumer companies such as AncestryDNA, 23andMe, FamilyTreeDNA, and MyHeritage have tested tens of millions of customers since the tests became commercially available in the 2000s.

Types of test

There are three main types of genealogical DNA test. Autosomal DNA tests survey hundreds of thousands of single-nucleotide polymorphisms across the chromosomes inherited from both parents; they identify relatives within roughly the last five to seven generations and provide "ethnicity estimates" by comparison with reference populations. Y-DNA tests examine the Y chromosome, passed nearly unchanged from father to son, and so trace the direct paternal line and a man's paternal haplogroup. Mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) tests examine the mitochondria inherited from one's mother, tracing the direct maternal line over much longer timescales.

Uses

Genealogists use test results to confirm or refute documentary research, break through "brick walls" in family trees, and find previously unknown relatives; adoptees have used them to identify biological parents. Databases of results also underpin genetic genealogy investigations by law enforcement, most famously the 2018 identification of the Golden State Killer suspect through relatives in a public database.

Criticisms and privacy

The tests raise privacy concerns, since DNA data can reveal information about relatives who never consented and may be accessed by law enforcement or exposed in data breaches. Ethnicity estimates are also probabilistic and change as company reference panels and algorithms are updated, and unexpected results — such as misattributed parentage — are a common and sometimes distressing outcome.
