Pontiac's War

Date1763–1766
LocationGreat Lakes, Ohio Country, Illinois Country
ResultStalemate; Royal Proclamation of 1763
BelligerentsNative American confederacy vs. Great Britain

Pontiac's War (also called Pontiac's Rebellion or Pontiac's Conspiracy) was launched in 1763 by a loose confederation of Native American peoples against British military occupation of the Great Lakes region following the French and Indian War. It is named for Pontiac, an Odawa leader who played a central role.

Course of the war

Inspired in part by the teachings of the Lenape prophet Neolin and angered by British policies under Jeffrey Amherst, Native forces captured eight British forts in May and June 1763 and besieged Fort Detroit and Fort Pitt. British expeditions under Henry Bouquet relieved Fort Pitt after the Battle of Bushy Run.

Outcome

The war ended in a negotiated peace by 1766. The British government issued the Royal Proclamation of 1763, reserving lands west of the Appalachians for Native peoples — a measure that alienated American colonists and contributed to tensions leading to the American Revolution. The conflict is also notorious for an incident in which British officers proposed distributing smallpox-contaminated blankets.
