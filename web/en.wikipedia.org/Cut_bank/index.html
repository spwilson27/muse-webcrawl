Cut bank

A cut bank, also known as a river cliff or river-cut cliff, is the outside bank of a curve or meander in a water channel (stream), which is continually undergoing erosion. Cut banks are found in abundance along mature or meandering streams, adjacent to the channel's outer bend.

Formation

Water flowing around a bend moves fastest along the outside of the curve, where centrifugal effects and helicoidal flow direct the current against the bank. This erodes and undercuts the bank, sometimes causing slumping and collapse, while slower water on the inside of the bend deposits sediment to form a point bar. Over time this paired erosion and deposition causes the meander to migrate across its floodplain.

Ecology

The steep, freshly exposed faces of cut banks provide nesting habitat for birds such as bank swallows and kingfishers, and the deep water at their base shelters fish.
