Cozia Monastery

Cozia Monastery
Location: Călimănești, Vâlcea County, Romania
Denomination: Romanian Orthodox
Founded: 1387–1388
Founder: Mircea the Elder
Style: Byzantine-influenced Wallachian

Cozia Monastery (Romanian: Mănăstirea Cozia) is a Romanian Orthodox monastery near Călimănești in Vâlcea County, Oltenia, Romania, situated on the right bank of the Olt River at the entrance to the river's mountain defile. Founded in 1387–1388 by Mircea the Elder (Mircea cel Bătrân), prince of Wallachia, it is one of the oldest and most valuable monuments of medieval Romanian art and architecture, and the burial place of its founder.

Architecture

The main church, dedicated to the Holy Trinity, is a triconch-plan church built of stone and brick in a Byzantine-influenced style, with alternating bands of masonry and rich carved decoration around the windows and cornice. It served as a model for later Wallachian church building. The original frescoes of 1390–1391 survive only in the narthex; the pronaos paintings date from later restorations, including work commissioned under Prince Constantin Brâncoveanu in 1710, when the church also received an open porch in the Brâncovenesc style. The complex includes the smaller Bolnița chapel (1542–1543), with notable 16th-century frescoes, and defensive walls that gave the monastery the character of a fortress guarding the Olt valley road.

History

The monastery's name is thought to derive from a Pecheneg–Cuman word for "walnut grove." Mircea the Elder was buried here in 1418, and the monastery remained a major cultural centre: in the 18th century it housed a school of mural painters. The site was damaged by earthquakes and floods, by the construction of the railway and road along the Olt, and by a nearby hydroelectric plant in the communist period, but has been extensively restored and remains an active monastery and pilgrimage site.
