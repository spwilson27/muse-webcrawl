Shtetl

A shtetl (Yiddish: שטעטל, shtetl; plural shtetlekh) is a small town with a large Ashkenazi Jewish population that existed in Central and Eastern Europe before the Holocaust. Shtetls were mainly found in the areas that constituted the Pale of Settlement in the Russian Empire as well as in Congress Poland, Galicia in Austria-Hungary, Romania, and the Kingdom of Hungary.

In Yiddish, the word shtetl is a diminutive of shtot ("town"), meaning "little town". The shtetls were market towns where Jews, largely barred from owning land, worked as merchants, artisans, and tradespeople serving the surrounding peasant population.

History

Jewish communities began settling in Eastern Europe's small towns in the late medieval and early modern periods, encouraged by Polish-Lithuanian rulers seeking to develop commerce. By the 19th century, millions of Jews lived in thousands of shtetls across the Pale of Settlement.

Decline

Emigration to the Americas, the upheavals of the First World War and the Russian Revolution, and economic changes thinned shtetl populations. The Holocaust destroyed the shtetl world almost entirely: during the Second World War, Nazi Germany and its collaborators murdered the Jewish inhabitants of the shtetls or deported them to extermination camps.

Cultural legacy

The shtetl lives on in literature and popular culture, notably in the Yiddish stories of Sholem Aleichem, whose fictional shtetl of Kasrilevke inspired the musical Fiddler on the Roof, and in the paintings of Marc Chagall.
