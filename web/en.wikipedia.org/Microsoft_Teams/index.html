Microsoft Teams

Microsoft Teams is a business communication platform developed by Microsoft, part of the Microsoft 365 family of products. It combines chat, video meetings, file storage and application integration in a shared workspace, and competes with services such as Slack and Zoom.

History

Teams was announced in November 2016 and launched worldwide in March 2017, after Microsoft abandoned plans to acquire Slack. It succeeded Skype for Business as Microsoft's enterprise communications tool. Usage surged during the COVID-19 pandemic: daily active users grew from 20 million in late 2019 to 44 million in March 2020, 75 million a month later, and over 250 million monthly users by 2021.

Features

Users organize into "teams" containing channels for threaded conversations. Core features include one-to-one and group chat, voice and video calls, screen sharing, meetings with transcription and recording, collaborative editing of Office documents, and integration with hundreds of third-party apps and bots through the Teams app store. A consumer version was integrated into Windows 11.

Antitrust and unbundling

Following a 2020 complaint by Slack, the European Commission investigated Microsoft's bundling of Teams with Office. In 2023–2024 Microsoft began unbundling Teams from Microsoft 365 and Office 365 suites in the European Economic Area and then worldwide.
