Armagh

Armagh (from Irish Ard Mhacha, "Macha's height") is a city in Northern Ireland and the county town of County Armagh. It is the ecclesiastical capital of Ireland — the seat of the Archbishops of Armagh of both the Roman Catholic Church and the Church of Ireland — and has been a centre of Christian worship associated with Saint Patrick since the 5th century. The city has a population of roughly 16,000.

History

Nearby Navan Fort (Emain Macha) was the ceremonial capital of the kings of Ulster in the Iron Age. Tradition holds that Saint Patrick founded his church at Armagh in 445 AD, and the city became the pre-eminent church in Ireland, its archbishop recognised as Primate of All Ireland. Two cathedrals named for St Patrick stand on opposite hills: the medieval Church of Ireland cathedral, resting place of Brian Boru, who died after the Battle of Clontarf in 1014, and the twin-spired Roman Catholic cathedral completed in the 19th century.

Sights and culture

Armagh is noted for its Georgian architecture, including its Mall, the Robinson Library (founded 1771), and the Armagh Observatory and Planetarium, founded in 1790. The surrounding county is known as the "Orchard County" for its apple orchards, and the city hosts an annual apple blossom festival. Armagh was granted city status in 1994.
