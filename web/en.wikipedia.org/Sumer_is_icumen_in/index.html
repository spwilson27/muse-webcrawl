Sumer is icumen in

"Sumer is icumen in" ("Summer has arrived") is a traditional English medieval round, or rota, dating from the mid-13th century, and the oldest surviving piece of counterpoint in England. It is famous as the earliest known example of a six-part polyphonic composition and the oldest known musical canon.

The manuscript

The piece survives in a manuscript (British Library Harley MS 978) associated with Reading Abbey. The Middle English text celebrates spring: the cuckoo sings, seeds grow, meadows bloom, and animals leap and call. A Latin text, Perspice Christicola, is written below and can be sung to the same music.

Music

Four voices sing the canon at the unison over a two-voice repeating ground (the pes), producing a remarkably sophisticated texture for its period. The melody of the cuckoo's call — two descending notes — permeates the piece.

Legacy

"Sumer is icumen in" remains one of the most frequently performed and recorded of all medieval English songs, and a standard illustration of early polyphony in music histories.
