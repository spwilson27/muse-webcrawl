Itô calculus

A sample path of Brownian motion, the foundation of Itô calculus

Itô calculus, named after Japanese mathematician Kiyosi Itô, is a branch of mathematics that extends ordinary calculus to stochastic processes, particularly Brownian motion. Developed by Itô beginning in the 1940s, it provides the framework for stochastic integration and stochastic differential equations (SDEs).

The Itô integral

Unlike paths of ordinary calculus, Brownian paths are nowhere differentiable and have unbounded variation, so a new integral is required. The Itô integral is defined as a limit of sums in which the integrand is evaluated at the left endpoint of each subinterval, making it non-anticipative. It is a martingale and satisfies the Itô isometry, which relates the expected square of the integral to the integral of the expected square of the integrand.

Itô's lemma

The cornerstone of the subject is Itô's lemma, the stochastic chain rule. For a process dXt = μ dt + σ dWt and a smooth function f, it states:

df = (ft + μ fx + ½σ² fxx) dt + σ fx dWt

The extra second-order term (a consequence of Brownian motion's quadratic variation, (dW)² = dt) distinguishes Itô calculus from ordinary calculus.

Applications

Itô calculus underlies mathematical finance — the Black–Scholes option pricing model is derived via Itô's lemma — as well as stochastic control, filtering theory, population dynamics, and models in physics and engineering. The alternative Stratonovich integral, which obeys ordinary chain-rule form, is used in some physical applications.
