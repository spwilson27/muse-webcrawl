Noahs Ark

Noah's Ark is the boat in the Genesis flood narrative (Genesis chapters 6–9) through which God spares Noah, his family, and examples of all the world's animals from a global deluge intended to destroy the wickedness of humankind.

The narrative

According to Genesis, God instructs Noah to build an ark of gopher wood, three hundred cubits long, fifty cubits wide, and thirty cubits high, sealed with pitch inside and out. Noah brings aboard his sons Shem, Ham, and Japheth with their wives, and pairs of all animals (seven pairs of clean animals). Rain falls for forty days and nights and the waters prevail for 150 days; the ark comes to rest on the mountains of Ararat. Noah sends out a raven and then a dove to find dry land, and God sets a rainbow as the sign of a covenant never again to destroy the earth by flood.

Traditions and searches

The story has parallels in earlier Mesopotamian flood myths, such as those of Atrahasis and Utnapishtim in the Epic of Gilgamesh. It is retold in the Quran and has inspired centuries of commentary, art, children's retellings, and expeditions searching for remains of the ark on Mount Ararat and elsewhere.
