Maya religion

The traditional Maya religion of the Maya peoples of Guatemala, Belize, El Salvador, and Mexico is a southeastern variant of Mesoamerican religion, developed over millennia in interaction with other traditions.

Cosmos and deities

The Maya cosmos was layered: heavens above, the earth, and the underworld Xibalba below, connected by the world tree. Major deities included the creator Itzamna, the rain god Chaac, the maize god, Kukulkan (the feathered serpent), and the moon goddess Ix Chel. Kings acted as intermediaries with the divine, performing bloodletting and sacrifice.

Practice

Religion centered on calendrical cycles, temple pyramids, ballgames, offerings, and codices such as the Popol Vuh of the Kʼicheʼ Maya. After the Spanish conquest, traditional practice syncretized with Roman Catholicism and continues in highland communities today.
