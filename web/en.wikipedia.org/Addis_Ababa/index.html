Addis Ababa (Amharic: አዲስ አበባ, "new flower") is the capital and largest city of Ethiopia. Situated at an elevation of about 2,355 metres in the foothills of Mount Entoto, it serves as the political, economic and cultural centre of the country and is often called the "political capital of Africa" because it hosts the headquarters of the African Union and the United Nations Economic Commission for Africa.

History

The site was chosen by Empress Taytu Betul in 1886 for its hot springs, and Emperor Menelik II founded the city the same year, planting eucalyptus trees that still characterise it. Addis Ababa became capital of the Ethiopian Empire and, after the Italian occupation of 1936–1941 (when it was the capital of Italian East Africa), seat of Haile Selassie's government. In 1963 the Organisation of African Unity was founded there. The city grew rapidly under the Derg and after 1991, with a building boom, the Addis Ababa Light Rail (opened 2015, the first in sub-Saharan Africa), and major projects such as Unity Park and the Grand Palace reconstructions.

Geography and climate

The city sprawls across a plateau dissected by streams descending from Entoto. Its high elevation moderates the tropical latitude, giving a subtropical highland climate with a main rainy season (kiremt) from June to September and average daytime temperatures around 20–25 °C year-round.

Landmarks

Notable sites include the National Theatre, St George's Cathedral, the Lion of Judah Monument, Merkato — one of Africa's largest open-air markets — the National Museum (home of the fossil "Lucy", Australopithecus afarensis), Addis Ababa University, and the headquarters complex of the African Union, inaugurated in 2012.
