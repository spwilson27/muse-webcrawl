The Pearl River Delta (PRD; Chinese: 珠江三角洲) is a low-lying region surrounding the Pearl River estuary in the south of China's Guangdong province, where the Pearl River flows into the South China Sea. It is one of the most densely urbanised and economically dynamic regions in the world, and a major manufacturing and trading hub.

Geography

The delta is formed by the confluence of three main tributaries — the Xi (West), Bei (North) and Dong (East) rivers — which spread into a network of distributaries around the estuary. The region encompasses the prefecture-level cities of Guangzhou, Shenzhen, Zhuhai, Foshan, Dongguan, Zhongshan, Jiangmen and parts of Huizhou and Zhaoqing, together with the special administrative regions of Hong Kong and Macau at the mouth of the estuary.

Economy

Following China's reform and opening up in 1978, the Pearl River Delta became the spearhead of the country's export-led growth. The establishment of special economic zones in Shenzhen and Zhuhai in 1980 attracted foreign investment, particularly from Hong Kong, and the region became known as the "world's factory" for electronics, textiles, toys and consumer goods. In recent decades the economy has shifted toward high technology, finance and services, with Shenzhen emerging as a major centre for technology companies such as Huawei and Tencent.

Greater Bay Area

In 2017 the Chinese government unveiled the Guangdong–Hong Kong–Macau Greater Bay Area plan, aiming to integrate the nine mainland PRD cities with Hong Kong and Macau into a single economic and transport region. Landmark infrastructure includes the Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macau Bridge, opened in 2018, the world's longest sea-crossing bridge at 55 kilometres.
