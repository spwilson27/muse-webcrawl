The Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter (MRO) is a NASA spacecraft launched on 12 August 2005 to study the geology and climate of Mars from orbit and to serve as a high-data-rate communications relay for surface missions. It entered Mars orbit on 10 March 2006 and used aerobraking to reach its science orbit later that year.

Instruments

MRO carries the HiRISE camera, the most powerful telescopic camera sent to another planet, capable of resolving surface features about a metre across; the CTX context imager; the CRISM imaging spectrometer for mapping minerals; the SHARAD radar for probing subsurface ice and rock layers; and instruments for weather and atmospheric studies.

Findings and relay role

The orbiter has imaged recurring slope lineae, buried glaciers and candidate landing sites, and has returned more data than all previous interplanetary missions combined. It relayed telemetry for the Phoenix, Curiosity and Perseverance landings and continues to support surface operations.
