Retinopathy of prematurity

Retinopathy of prematurity (ROP), also called retrolental fibroplasia, is a disease of the developing retinal blood vessels in premature infants that can lead to retinal detachment and blindness if untreated.

At riskVery premature, low-birth-weight infants
Key factorAbnormal retinal vascular development; oxygen exposure

Pathophysiology

In premature infants the retinal vessels are incompletely developed. Relative hyperoxia (including supplemental oxygen) suppresses vascular growth factors, halting vessel growth; subsequent relative hypoxia then drives abnormal proliferation of vessels and fibrous tissue at the junction of vascularised and avascular retina, which can contract and detach the retina.

Screening and treatment

At-risk infants (typically born before ~31 weeks or under ~1500 g) undergo serial retinal examinations, classified by zone, stage and "plus disease". Most ROP regresses spontaneously; severe disease is treated with laser photocoagulation of the avascular retina or intravitreal anti-VEGF injections such as bevacizumab.

History

An epidemic of blindness in the 1940s–50s was traced to unmonitored oxygen therapy in incubators, making ROP a landmark case in neonatal care and clinical trial design.
