Ossetian language

Ossetian (Ирон ӕвзаг, Iron ævzag) is an Eastern Iranian language spoken in Ossetia, a region on both sides of the Greater Caucasus Mountains — North Ossetia–Alania in Russia and South Ossetia. It is the descendant of the language of the Alans and, more distantly, the Scythians.

Dialects and script

The two main dialects are Iron, on which the literary language is based, and Digor. Ossetian is written in the Cyrillic script; it earlier used Georgian, Arabic, and Latin alphabets at various periods. It has about 600,000 speakers.

Features

Unusual for an Indo-European language, Ossetian developed ejective consonants and a large agglutinative case system under the influence of neighbouring Caucasian languages. Its literature draws on the Nart sagas, the shared epic heritage of the North Caucasus.
