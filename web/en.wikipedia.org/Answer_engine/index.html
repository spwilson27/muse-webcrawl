Answer engine

An answer engine is a type of search system that provides a direct, concise answer to a user's question instead of, or in addition to, a list of links to documents that might contain the answer. Answer engines draw on techniques from information retrieval, natural language processing, and knowledge representation to interpret the intent of a query and synthesize a response.

History

The concept grew out of question-answering systems developed in artificial intelligence research. Early examples included WolframAlpha, launched in 2009, which computes answers from structured data, and Ask Jeeves (later Ask.com), which attempted natural-language question answering. Mainstream search engines such as Google later added featured snippets and knowledge panels that answer queries directly on the results page.

How it works

An answer engine typically parses the user's question, identifies the entities and relationships involved, queries one or more knowledge bases or indexes, and generates a natural-language response. Modern implementations increasingly rely on large language models, sometimes combined with retrieval-augmented generation to ground answers in retrieved sources.
