Andrea Doria (30 November 1466 – 25 November 1560) was a Genoese statesman, condottiere and admiral, the dominant figure of the Republic of Genoa in the 16th century and the leading naval commander of the Holy Roman Emperor Charles V in the Mediterranean.

Career

Orphaned young, Doria began as a soldier of fortune, serving popes and Italian princes before turning to the sea. As a galley captain he fought the Ottoman Turks and Barbary corsairs in the western Mediterranean. In 1522 he entered French service under Francis I, but, alienated by French interference in Genoese affairs, in 1528 he dramatically transferred his allegiance — and his fleet — to Charles V. That same year he expelled the French from Genoa and refounded the republic under a reformed, oligarchic constitution that endured until 1797.

Admiral of the Empire

For three decades Doria commanded imperial fleets against the Ottomans, campaigning at Coron, Patras and Tunis (1535), though his caution at the Battle of Preveza (1538) drew criticism. His power in Genoa survived the conspiracy of Gian Luigi Fieschi in 1547, in which his nephew and heir Giannettino was killed. Agnolo Bronzino's famous portrait depicts him heroically as Neptune, god of the sea. Doria remained active into his nineties, campaigning against the French in Corsica, and died in Genoa in 1560, days short of his 94th birthday.

Legacy

Doria is remembered as the liberator and second founder of Genoa. His name has been borne by Italian warships and by the ocean liner Andrea Doria, which sank in 1956 after colliding with the Stockholm off Nantucket — an unrelated modern namesake frequently confused with the admiral.
