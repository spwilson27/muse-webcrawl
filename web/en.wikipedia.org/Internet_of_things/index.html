Internet of things

Internet of things (IoT)
Concept coined1999, by Kevin Ashton
Core technologiesSensors, embedded systems, wireless networks, cloud computing
Connected devicesMore than 15 billion (early 2020s estimates)
Key domainsSmart home, industry, healthcare, transport, agriculture

The Internet of things (IoT) describes devices with sensors, processing ability, software, and other technologies that connect and exchange data with other devices and systems over the Internet or other communication networks. The field has evolved due to the convergence of multiple technologies, including ubiquitous computing, commodity sensors, and increasingly powerful embedded systems, as well as machine learning.

History

An early example of a networked appliance was a modified Coca-Cola vending machine at Carnegie Mellon University in 1982, which reported its inventory and whether newly loaded drinks were cold. The term "Internet of Things" was coined by Kevin Ashton of Procter & Gamble in 1999, in the context of radio-frequency identification (RFID) and supply-chain management. The concept broadened in the 2010s as cheap sensors, smartphones, and cloud platforms made mass deployment feasible.

Applications

Consumer

Smart-home devices include thermostats, lighting, voice assistants, security cameras, and connected appliances, often coordinated through hubs or standards such as Matter.

Industrial

The Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) applies sensors and automation in manufacturing, logistics, and energy, enabling predictive maintenance, asset tracking, and process optimisation. It is a central element of "Industry 4.0".

Infrastructure and cities

"Smart city" projects use connected sensors for traffic management, smart metering, waste collection, and environmental monitoring. In agriculture, soil and weather sensors support precision farming; in healthcare, wearable devices and remote patient monitoring transmit health data to providers.

Technology

IoT devices typically combine embedded processors, sensors and actuators, and connectivity via Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, cellular networks (including 5G), or low-power wide-area networks such as LoRaWAN and NB-IoT. Data is processed locally ("edge computing") or in cloud platforms. Common application protocols include MQTT, CoAP, and HTTP.

Security and privacy concerns

The rapid proliferation of poorly secured devices has created major risks. In 2016 the Mirai botnet, assembled from compromised cameras and routers, launched some of the largest distributed denial-of-service attacks then recorded. Critics also raise concerns about surveillance and the collection of intimate behavioural data, leading regulators to introduce baseline security requirements for consumer IoT products in several jurisdictions.
