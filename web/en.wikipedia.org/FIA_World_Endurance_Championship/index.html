FIA World Endurance Championship

The FIA World Endurance Championship, abbreviated as WEC, is a world championship for automobile endurance racing organised by the Automobile Club de l'Ouest (ACO) and sanctioned by the Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA). The championship is contested by sports prototypes and grand tourers competing in different classes, and features races of six, eight, and 24 hours' duration, crowned by the 24 Hours of Le Mans.

History

The WEC was created in 2012, replacing the ACO's Intercontinental Le Mans Cup, and revived the name last used by the World Sportscar Championship, which had folded in 1992. Audi dominated the early seasons before Porsche returned in 2014 and won three consecutive Le Mans and world titles (2015–2017). Toyota became the benchmark team in the subsequent period. A major rules reset in 2021 introduced the Hypercar class, uniting Le Mans Hypercars (LMH) and IMSA-derived LMDh cars, and drew manufacturers including Ferrari (whose 499P won Le Mans in 2023, 2024, and 2025), Porsche, Cadillac, BMW, Alpine, and Lamborghini. From 2024 the GT ranks were reorganised around the LMGT3 class.

Format

Seasons typically comprise eight rounds across Europe, Asia, the Middle East, and the Americas, including venues such as Spa-Francorchamps, Fuji, Bahrain, Imola, Interlagos, and the Circuit of the Americas. World titles are awarded to drivers and manufacturers in the top class, with trophies for other classes. Endurance races require multiple drivers per car, with minimum and maximum driving times, and race strategies revolve around tyre wear, fuel economy, and reliability over long distances.
