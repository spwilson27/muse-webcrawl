DNA barcode

A DNA barcode is a short, standardized segment of DNA sequence used to identify a species, analogous to the way a supermarket barcode identifies a product. DNA barcoding is the taxonomic method that uses such markers.

Markers

For animals the standard barcode is a roughly 648-base-pair region of the mitochondrial cytochrome c oxidase subunit I (COI) gene. Plants use combinations of plastid genes such as rbcL and matK, fungi use the internal transcribed spacer (ITS), and bacteria use 16S rRNA.

Applications

Barcoding is used to identify unknown specimens, detect food fraud and illegal wildlife trade, survey biodiversity, and identify species from environmental DNA. Large reference libraries such as the Barcode of Life Data System (BOLD) store millions of records.

History

The approach was proposed in 2003 by Paul Hebert and colleagues at the University of Guelph, leading to the Consortium for the Barcode of Life.
