Palm Islands

The Palm Islands consist of three artificial islands, Palm Jumeirah, Deira Island, and Palm Jebel Ali, on the coast of Dubai, United Arab Emirates. Only Palm Jumeirah has been completed. The islands were created by land reclamation using sand dredged from the floor of the Persian Gulf and are each shaped like a date palm tree enclosed by a crescent breakwater.

Construction

Construction began in 2001 under the direction of developer Nakheel, a government-owned company. The islands were built entirely from sand and rock: millions of cubic metres of sand were sprayed ("rainbowed") into place by dredgers guided by GPS, and vibro-compacted to reduce liquefaction risk. No concrete or steel forms the islands themselves, though rock breakwaters protect them. Palm Jumeirah added some 78 kilometres of coastline to Dubai.

Palm Jumeirah

The smallest of the three, Palm Jumeirah was the first to be completed, with residents moving in from 2006–2007. Its trunk, fronds, and crescent host villas, apartments, hotels, and resorts, including Atlantis, The Palm, opened in 2008 at the crescent's centre. The island is connected to the mainland by a bridge and a monorail.

Palm Jebel Ali and Deira Islands

Palm Jebel Ali, roughly 50% larger than Palm Jumeirah, was largely reclaimed before work stalled after the 2008 financial crisis; development was revived in the 2020s. The third island, Palm Deira, was scaled back and rebranded as Deira Islands (later Dubai Islands). Critics have raised concerns about the projects' environmental effects, including changes to marine currents, water circulation, and local ecosystems.
