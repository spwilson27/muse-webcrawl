Adverbial

An adverbial is a word, phrase, or clause that functions as a modifier of a verb, adjective, another adverb, or a whole sentence, typically expressing time, place, manner, cause, purpose, condition, or degree — answering questions such as when?, where?, how?, and why? Unlike arguments of a verb, adverbials are usually adjuncts: optional elements that can be omitted without making the sentence ungrammatical.

Forms

Adverbials may be single adverbs (She sang beautifully), prepositional phrases (He left in the morning), noun phrases (We met last week), infinitive phrases expressing purpose (She came to help), or adverbial clauses introduced by subordinating conjunctions such as because, although, when, and if.

Types

Traditional grammar distinguishes adverbials of time, place, manner, frequency, degree, reason, purpose, result, concession, and condition. Grammarians also distinguish adjuncts from sentence adverbials (disjuncts) such as frankly in "Frankly, I disagree", which comment on the whole utterance rather than modifying the verb.
