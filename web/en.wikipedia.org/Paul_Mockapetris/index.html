Paul Mockapetris

Paul V. Mockapetris (born November 18, 1948) is an American computer scientist and Internet pioneer who invented the Domain Name System (DNS) in 1983.

DNS

Working at the Information Sciences Institute of the University of Southern California, Mockapetris responded to the problem of maintaining a single hosts file for the growing ARPANET. His RFCs 882 and 883 (1983), later superseded by RFCs 1034 and 1035 (1987), specified a distributed, hierarchical naming database that maps domain names to IP addresses and other records. He also wrote the first DNS server implementation, JEEVES.

Later career

Mockapetris held research and executive roles at several networking companies and served as chairman of Nominum. He was inducted into the Internet Hall of Fame in 2012 and received the IEEE Internet Award.
