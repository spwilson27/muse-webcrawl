New Look (Christian Dior)

The "New Look" was the name given by Carmel Snow, editor-in-chief of Harper's Bazaar, to Christian Dior's first collection, presented in Paris on 12 February 1947. It revolutionised post-war women's fashion and re-established Paris as the centre of the fashion world.

The silhouette

The collection rejected the boxy, fabric-saving styles of the war years in favour of rounded shoulders, a cinched waist, padded hips and full, calf-length skirts made with lavish quantities of fabric. Its emblematic outfit, the "Bar suit", paired a cream shantung jacket with a pleated black wool skirt.

Reception

The New Look was an immediate commercial success and defined the dominant silhouette of the 1950s, though it also drew protests from those who saw its extravagant use of cloth as wasteful during postwar rationing and from some who objected to its return to corsetry.
