Gimmick (professional wrestling)

In professional wrestling, a gimmick generally refers to the fictional character, persona, or identity portrayed by a wrestler. The term is also used to describe a special stipulation added to a match, or a foreign object used by a wrestler, but its most common meaning is the in-ring character itself.

A wrestler's gimmick may include their ring name, costume, entrance music, catchphrases, mannerisms, and behaviour, all of which combine to distinguish them from other performers and to elicit a reaction from the audience. Gimmicks are typically aligned with the traditional morality structure of wrestling: heroic "face" (babyface) characters and villainous "heel" characters.

History

Exaggerated personas have existed in wrestling since its carnival origins, but gimmicks became central during the television era of the 1950s, when flamboyant characters such as Gorgeous George drew large audiences. In the 1980s and 1990s, promotions such as the World Wrestling Federation built their business around highly stylised characters, for example Hulk Hogan's patriotic hero, The Undertaker's undead mortician, and a wide range of occupational characters such as wrestlers presented as dentists, clowns, or garbage collectors.

The late 1990s "Attitude Era" shifted mainstream gimmicks toward more realistic, rebellious personas. In lucha libre, the mask itself is a core element of a wrestler's gimmick and identity.

Gimmick matches

The term also describes matches with special rules or props, such as steel cage matches, ladder matches, or tables matches, designed to settle feuds or add spectacle. Wrestlers closely associated with a particular stipulation are sometimes said to have a "gimmick match", such as The Undertaker's Hell in a Cell and casket matches.
