Ludwig Mies van der Rohe (27 March 1886 – 17 August 1969) was a German-American architect and furniture designer, a pioneer of modern architecture. Together with Le Corbusier, Walter Gropius and Frank Lloyd Wright he is regarded as one of the founding masters of modernism. His mature buildings — glass-and-steel structures of extreme clarity — embody his aphorisms "less is more" and "God is in the details".

Career in Germany

Mies directed the Weissenhof housing exhibition in Stuttgart (1927), designed the Barcelona Pavilion for the 1929 International Exposition and the Villa Tugendhat in Brno (1930). He was the last director of the Bauhaus, dissolving the school under Nazi pressure in 1933.

American career

Emigrating to the United States in 1937, Mies headed the architecture school at the Armour Institute (now Illinois Institute of Technology) in Chicago and designed its campus. His postwar masterpieces include the Farnsworth House (1951), the Lake Shore Drive apartments, Crown Hall, and the Seagram Building in New York (1958, with Philip Johnson), which defined the glass office tower.
