Charlie Munger

Charles Thomas Munger (January 1, 1924 – November 28, 2023) was an American businessman, investor, and philanthropist. He was vice chairman of Berkshire Hathaway, the conglomerate controlled by Warren Buffett, who described Munger as his closest partner and right-hand man.

Born in Omaha, Nebraska, Munger worked as a teenager at Buffett & Son, a grocery store owned by Warren Buffett's grandfather. He served in the U.S. Army Air Corps, studied at the University of Michigan and Caltech, and graduated magna cum laude from Harvard Law School in 1948.

Before joining Berkshire Hathaway, Munger ran his own investment partnership from 1962 to 1975, generating compound annual returns of 19.8%. He was chairman of Wesco Financial Corporation and publisher of the Daily Journal Corporation, and a director of Costco.

Munger was known for his multidisciplinary approach to investing, advocating a "latticework of mental models" drawn from psychology, economics, physics, biology and history. He emphasized patience, rationality, and avoiding stupidity rather than seeking brilliance.

He was a major philanthropist, giving to the University of Michigan, Stanford University, and architectural projects, often insisting on involvement in building design. He died in Santa Barbara, California, at age 99, just over a month before his 100th birthday.
