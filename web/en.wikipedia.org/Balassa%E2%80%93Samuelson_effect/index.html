Balassa–Samuelson effect

The Balassa–Samuelson effect, proposed independently by Béla Balassa and Paul Samuelson in 1964, explains why price levels tend to be higher in richer countries: higher productivity in the traded-goods sector raises wages across the whole economy, making non-traded goods and services relatively more expensive in wealthy nations.

Mechanism

If productivity grows faster in tradables than in non-tradables, competition for labor bids up wages economy-wide. Non-traded services, unable to match the productivity gains, must charge higher prices, raising the overall consumer price level and the real exchange rate.

Implications

The effect implies that exchange rates converted at purchasing power parity systematically show poorer countries as cheaper, and that fast-growing economies should expect real currency appreciation as they develop.
