Military intelligence

Military intelligence is the discipline and the organisations that collect, analyse and disseminate information about foreign adversaries, terrain, weather and other factors affecting military operations, in order to support commanders' decision-making. It spans the strategic level (national capabilities and intentions) down to the tactical (the enemy battalion on the next ridge).

The intelligence cycle

Intelligence work follows a cycle: direction (what the commander needs to know), collection, processing, analysis and production, and dissemination. Analysis is usually graded for source reliability and information credibility, and products are classified and distributed on a need-to-know basis.

Collection disciplines

Collection is organised into "INTs": HUMINT (human sources and interrogation), SIGINT (signals interception and codebreaking), IMINT/GEOINT (photographic, satellite and geospatial imagery), MASINT (technical signatures), OSINT (open sources) and TECHINT (exploitation of captured equipment). Modern forces fuse these in all-source analysis cells.

History

Scouts and spies are as old as war — Sun Tzu devoted a chapter of The Art of War to espionage. Organised staffs emerged in the 19th century; the World Wars industrialised the craft, from Room 40 and Bletchley Park's Ultra codebreaking to photo-reconnaissance and deception operations such as Operation Fortitude. Cold War agencies professionalised strategic intelligence, and in the 21st century drones, satellites and cyber operations have made persistent surveillance central to operations.

Organisations

Examples include the U.S. Defense Intelligence Agency and service intelligence branches, Britain's Defence Intelligence, Russia's GRU, and Israel's Aman. Counterintelligence is the defensive counterpart, protecting forces from enemy collection.
