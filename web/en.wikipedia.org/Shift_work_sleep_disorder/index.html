Shift work sleep disorder

Specialty: Sleep medicine
Symptoms: Insomnia, excessive sleepiness
Cause: Work schedule conflicting with circadian rhythm
Risk factors: Night shifts, rotating shifts

Shift work sleep disorder (SWSD) is a circadian rhythm sleep disorder characterised by insomnia and excessive sleepiness in people who work non-traditional hours, such as night shifts or rotating shifts. It arises when a person's work schedule forces them to be awake during their biological night, when the internal circadian clock promotes sleep.

Symptoms

People with SWSD typically have difficulty falling asleep or staying asleep during their daytime sleep period, and feel unrefreshed and drowsy during their waking hours. The disorder is associated with reduced performance, irritability, and increased risk of accidents, as well as longer-term health problems.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is based on the history of symptoms tied to the shift schedule, sometimes supported by sleep diaries or actigraphy. Symptoms must persist for at least three months and be linked to the work schedule rather than another sleep disorder.

Treatment

Management includes sleep hygiene measures, scheduled napping, bright light exposure during the shift and light avoidance on the commute home, and in some cases melatonin or wakefulness-promoting medication prescribed by a physician.
