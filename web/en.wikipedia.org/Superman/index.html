Superman

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Superman is a superhero created by writer Jerry Siegel and artist Joe Shuster, first appearing in Action Comics #1 in 1938, published by DC Comics. He is regarded as the archetype of the superhero genre and one of the most recognisable fictional characters in the world.

Fictional biography

Born Kal-El on the planet Krypton, Superman was rocketed to Earth as an infant by his parents before Krypton's destruction. Raised as Clark Kent by farmers Jonathan and Martha Kent in Smallville, Kansas, he develops extraordinary powers under Earth's yellow sun — super strength, flight, invulnerability, heat vision, and super senses. As an adult in Metropolis he works as a reporter for the Daily Planet alongside Lois Lane, and fights villains including his arch-nemesis, Lex Luthor. His principal weakness is kryptonite, radioactive debris from his home planet.

Creation and publication

Siegel and Shuster, high-school friends from Cleveland, sold Superman to DC's predecessor for $130; decades of litigation over ownership followed, eventually settling with the creators receiving credit and financial provision. The character's success launched the Golden Age of Comic Books and an immense media franchise spanning radio, television (including the 1950s series with George Reeves), and films, notably the 1978 Superman starring Christopher Reeve, Man of Steel (2013), and the 2025 Superman film launching a new DC Universe.

Cultural impact

Superman's costume — blue suit, red cape, and stylised "S" shield — and his motto "truth, justice, and a better tomorrow" (formerly "the American way") are cultural landmarks. Scholars read the character variously as an immigrant allegory, a New Deal-era champion of the oppressed, and a pop-culture embodiment of moral idealism.
