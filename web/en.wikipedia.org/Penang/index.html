Penang (Malay: Pulau Pinang) is a Malaysian state on the northwest coast of Peninsular Malaysia, consisting of Penang Island and a strip of mainland territory known as Seberang Perai. Its capital, George Town, is famed for its well-preserved colonial and shophouse architecture and was inscribed, together with Malacca, as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2008.

History

In 1786 Captain Francis Light of the British East India Company acquired Penang Island from the Sultan of Kedah, founding George Town as a free port and trading post. Penang became part of the Straits Settlements and attracted merchants and labourers from China, India, Arabia and Europe, creating the state's distinctive multicultural society — including the Peranakan (Straits Chinese) community. The island was occupied by Japan during the Second World War, joined the Federation of Malaya in 1948, and became part of Malaysia in 1963.

Economy and culture

From the 1970s the Bayan Lepas Free Industrial Zone made Penang a global centre of electronics manufacturing, earning it the nickname "Silicon Valley of the East." The state is equally celebrated for its food: Penang's street food — including char kway teow, nasi kandar, asam laksa and Hokkien mee — is widely regarded as among the best in Southeast Asia. Major landmarks include Penang Hill, Kek Lok Si Temple and the Penang Bridge linking island and mainland.
