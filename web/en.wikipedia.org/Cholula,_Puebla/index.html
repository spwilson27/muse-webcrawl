Cholula, Puebla

Cholula (Spanish: Cholula de Rivadavia) is a city and district in the Mexican state of Puebla, located in the Puebla Valley west of the state capital, Puebla de Zaragoza. It is best known for the Great Pyramid of Cholula, the largest pyramid by volume known to exist in the world today, topped by the church of Nuestra Señora de los Remedios.

The Great Pyramid of Cholula, with the church of Nuestra Señora de los Remedios on its summit

History

Cholula is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the Americas, with settlement dating to the Preclassic period. It was a major religious and commercial center of Mesoamerica, sacred to the god Quetzalcoatl, and a pilgrimage destination. In 1519, during the Spanish conquest, Hernán Cortés and his Tlaxcalan allies carried out a massacre of Cholulteca nobles and inhabitants — an event known as the Cholula massacre. In the colonial period, Cholula became famous for its many churches; popular tradition claims 365, one for each day of the year.

Great Pyramid

The Great Pyramid of Cholula (Tlachihualtepetl, "made-by-hand mountain") measures roughly 450 m by 450 m at its base and contains about 4.45 million cubic meters of material, making it the largest pyramid by volume in the world. It was built in several stages over many centuries, beginning around the 3rd century BCE. A network of tunnels dug by archaeologists runs through its interior, revealing earlier structures within.

Modern city

Modern Cholula consists of the municipalities of San Pedro Cholula and San Andrés Cholula. It is home to the Universidad de las Américas Puebla and a lively student population. The city was designated a Pueblo Mágico ("Magic Town") by the Mexican government in 2012.
