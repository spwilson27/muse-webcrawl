The Madras Presidency, officially the Presidency of Fort St. George, was an administrative subdivision (province) of British India in the south of the subcontinent. At its greatest extent it covered most of present-day Tamil Nadu, the Malabar region of North Kerala, the coastal Andhra region, southern Odisha, and parts of Karnataka, making it one of the three great presidencies of British India alongside Bombay and Bengal.

History

The English East India Company established a factory at Madraspatnam in 1639–40 under Francis Day and built Fort St. George there. The settlement was raised to a presidency in 1653 (and again in 1684 after a period as an agency), and expanded through the Carnatic Wars, the Anglo-Mysore Wars, and the defeat of the Marathas and Tipu Sultan, so that by the early nineteenth century the presidency embraced nearly the whole peninsula south of the Krishna River. It was governed by a Governor and council, later supplemented by legislative councils, and under the Government of India Act 1935 it became a province with an elected ministry; the first was led by C. Rajagopalachari of the Indian National Congress in 1937.

Administration and society

The presidency was divided into districts under Collectors, with the ryotwari system of land revenue settlement—assessment directly on the cultivator—as its characteristic land policy, developed by Thomas Munro. Its society was the setting for the Justice Party and the non-Brahmin and Self-Respect movements associated with Periyar, as well as early Congress nationalism. Madras city was a major port, educational centre (University of Madras, 1857), and seat of one of India's first high courts (1862).

After 1947

At independence the presidency became Madras Province and then Madras State of the Republic of India; linguistic reorganisation in 1953–56 detached the Telugu-speaking Andhra region and redistributed other districts, and in 1969 the state was renamed Tamil Nadu.
