Arrow (Israeli missile)

The Arrow (Hebrew: Ḥetz) is a family of anti-ballistic missiles designed to intercept and destroy ballistic missiles in the upper atmosphere and in space. It was developed by Israel Aerospace Industries in cooperation with the United States, which has provided substantial funding since the programme began in the 1980s, and forms the top layer of Israel's multi-tiered missile defence system.

Development and variants

The programme was launched after Israel joined the US Strategic Defense Initiative research effort; flight testing began in 1990. Arrow 2, which entered service in 2000, intercepts missiles within the atmosphere using a blast-fragmentation warhead. Arrow 3, declared operational in 2017, is an exo-atmospheric interceptor that destroys targets in space by direct hit-to-kill collision, engaging ballistic missiles at greater ranges and altitudes. Batteries are supported by the Green Pine radar family and a command-and-control system, and integrate with lower tiers such as David's Sling and Iron Dome.

Operational use

Arrow 3 made its first operational interception in November 2023, destroying a ballistic missile launched from Yemen — widely reported as the first engagement of its kind in space. The system was used extensively during the mass Iranian missile attacks on Israel in 2024. Germany's decision to purchase Arrow 3 for the European Sky Shield Initiative made it the system's first export customer.
