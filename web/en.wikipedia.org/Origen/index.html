Origen

Bornc. 185 AD, Alexandria, Egypt
Diedc. 253 AD, Tyre, Phoenice
OccupationScholar, theologian, exegete
Notable worksOn the First Principles, Hexapla, Contra Celsum

Origen of Alexandria (c. 185 – c. 253), also known as Origen Adamantius, was an early Christian scholar, ascetic and theologian, and one of the most influential figures of the early Church. Born in Alexandria, he headed its catechetical school as a young man and produced an enormous body of writings — ancient sources credit him with some 800 to 2,000 works — of which only a portion survives.

Life

Origen's father Leonides was martyred in the persecution of 202. Eusebius reports that the young Origen wished to seek martyrdom too and was prevented only when his mother hid his clothes. He studied under Clement of Alexandria and, reputedly, Ammonius Saccas. Funded by a wealthy patron, Ambrose, he travelled widely, was ordained a priest in Caesarea, and later settled there after a dispute with Bishop Demetrius of Alexandria. During the Decian persecution he was imprisoned and tortured, dying soon after, around 253.

Works

His Hexapla set the Hebrew Bible and several Greek translations in parallel columns, founding biblical textual criticism. De Principiis (On the First Principles) was the first systematic work of Christian theology. His apologetic Contra Celsum answers the pagan critic Celsus, and he wrote commentaries and homilies on most books of the Bible, pioneering allegorical exegesis.

Legacy and controversy

Origen profoundly shaped Eastern and Western theology, but speculative doctrines attributed to him — the pre-existence of souls and the universal restoration (apokatastasis) — provoked the Origenist controversies; teachings in his name were anathematised in the 6th century. Modern scholarship has largely rehabilitated him as a church father of the first rank, though he was never canonised in the West.
