Virgo (constellation)

Virgo is one of the constellations of the zodiac. Lying between Leo to the west and Libra to the east, it is the second-largest constellation in the sky, after Hydra. Its brightest star is Spica, which makes the constellation easy to locate. Virgo is associated in Greek mythology with Demeter, goddess of the harvest, and with Astraea, goddess of justice, and is usually depicted as a maiden holding a sheaf of wheat.

Notable features

The brightest star, Spica (Alpha Virginis), is a blue giant and one of the brightest stars in the night sky, about 250 light-years away. Other notable stars include Zavijava, Porrima, Auva, and Vindemiatrix. Virgo is especially notable for the Virgo Cluster of galaxies, a rich cluster of roughly 1,300 to 2,000 galaxies located in the constellation's northern part; the cluster includes the giant elliptical galaxy Messier 87 and the Sombrero Galaxy (Messier 104).

Deep-sky objects

Because the galactic plane does not pass through Virgo, the constellation offers an unobscured view into deep space, and it contains numerous bright galaxies visible in amateur telescopes. In 2015, Virgo's galaxy Messier 87 was the subject of the first image of a black hole's event horizon, taken by the Event Horizon Telescope.

Mythology

The Babylonians knew part of the constellation as "The Furrow", representing the goddess Shala's ear of grain. The figure of the maiden has been associated with many goddesses, including Ishtar, Isis, and Demeter, and with the Roman Ceres. The autumnal equinox point historically lay within Virgo.
