Divide and rule

Divide and rule (Latin: divide et impera) is a strategy of gaining and maintaining power by breaking up larger concentrations of power into smaller pieces that are individually weaker, and by encouraging rivalries among subjects or rivals so they cannot unite against the ruler.

History

The maxim is attributed variously to Philip II of Macedon and to Julius Caesar, and was advocated by Niccolò Machiavelli in The Art of War. In the modern era the phrase is most often associated with colonial governance — notably British rule in India, where policies are said to have fostered divisions between Hindu and Muslim communities, and with the indirect rule of native authorities in Africa.

Use

Beyond empire, the tactic appears in politics, labour relations and management: a dominant party may split opposition votes, or an employer may set groups of workers against one another. Its opposite is the strategy of unifying opponents, summed up in the labour slogan "united we stand, divided we fall".
