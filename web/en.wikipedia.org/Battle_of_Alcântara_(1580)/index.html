The Battle of Alcântara, fought on 25 August 1580, was the decisive engagement of the War of the Portuguese Succession. A Spanish army under Fernando Álvarez de Toledo, 3rd Duke of Alba, defeated the forces of the Portuguese claimant António, Prior of Crato, on the Alcântara stream just west of Lisbon.

Background

The death of King Henry of Portugal in January 1580 without an heir triggered a succession crisis. Philip II of Spain, whose claim came through his mother Isabella of Portugal, sent the Duke of Alba into Portugal with some 20,000 men, while a fleet under the Marquis of Santa Cruz supported the invasion along the coast. António had been acclaimed king by popular supporters but lacked the backing of much of the nobility and clergy.

Battle and aftermath

Alba's veteran tercios crossed the Alcântara stream and overwhelmed António's largely inexperienced troops; contemporary accounts put Portuguese losses in the thousands, against light Spanish casualties. Lisbon fell shortly afterwards. Philip was proclaimed King of Portugal as Philip I in 1581, beginning the Iberian Union, which lasted until the Portuguese Restoration War of 1640. António continued resistance from the Azores with French support until the Spanish victory at Ponta Delgada in 1582.
