Gudrun Ensslin

Gudrun Ensslin (15 August 1940 – 18 October 1977) was a German far-left militant and a founding member of the Red Army Faction (RAF), the West German terrorist group often called the Baader–Meinhof Group. Along with Andreas Baader and Ulrike Meinhof, she was one of the most prominent figures of the first generation of the RAF.

Early life

Ensslin was born in Bartholomä, Baden-Württemberg, the daughter of a Protestant pastor. She studied German literature, English and pedagogy, and was regarded as a gifted student. In the mid-1960s she became involved in the protest movements against the Vietnam War and the West German emergency laws, and worked with the publishing collective that produced literature for the extra-parliamentary opposition.

Radicalisation and the RAF

On 2 April 1968, Ensslin and Baader, together with two accomplices, set fire to two department stores in Frankfurt am Main in protest against the Vietnam War. They were arrested two days later and sentenced to three years in prison. After their release pending appeal in 1969, they fled abroad; on their return they went underground and, with Meinhof, founded the Red Army Faction in 1970. The group carried out bombings, bank robberies and kidnappings, including attacks on United States military installations in West Germany.

Arrest, trial and death

Ensslin was arrested in Hamburg on 7 June 1972. She was tried at Stammheim Prison in Stuttgart in one of the longest and most expensive trials in West German history and was sentenced to life imprisonment in April 1977. During the "German Autumn" of 1977, the RAF and Palestinian hijackers attempted to force the release of Ensslin and her fellow prisoners. On the night of 17–18 October 1977, after the hijacked Lufthansa Flight 181 was stormed in Mogadishu, Ensslin was found hanged in her cell; Baader and Jan-Carl Raspe also died that night, in what became known as the Stammheim "Night of Death". The official finding of suicide has remained controversial.
