Virtue signalling
Coinage popularised by: James Bartholomew, 2015
Type: pejorative neologism
Related: conspicuous consumption, moral grandstanding, slacktivism

Virtue signalling is a pejorative term for the expression of moral values or opinions primarily to enhance one's social status or reputation, rather than out of genuine conviction. The phrase was popularised by British journalist James Bartholomew in The Spectator in April 2015, and spread quickly through political commentary, especially on social media.

Usage and debate

The term is most often applied to public statements of progressive causes — hashtags, profile-picture overlays, corporate statements — alleged to cost the speaker nothing. Critics of the term argue it is a thought-terminating insult that dismisses sincerity without evidence, and that all public moral expression involves some signalling, honest or not. Psychologists study the neighbouring concept of "moral grandstanding": using moral talk to seek status. Bartholomew himself later noted the phrase had been broadened beyond his intended meaning.
