Mount Lu

Mount Lu (Chinese: 廬山; pinyin: Lúshān, "Hut Mountain"), also known as Kuanglu (匡廬), is a mountain in Jiujiang, Jiangxi province, China, on the southern shore of Poyang Lake and near the Yangtze River. One of the most renowned mountains in China, it has been a centre of religious life, education, and landscape poetry for well over a thousand years, and is inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site (Lushan National Park, 1996).

History

Mount Lu has been celebrated since the Han dynasty; the historian Sima Qian climbed it in 126 BC. The monk Huiyuan founded Donglin Temple on its slopes in 386 AD and gathered the Pure Land tradition's foundational community there. The White Deer Grotto Academy, restored by Zhu Xi in 1179–80, became one of China's most influential Confucian academies. In the 20th century the resort town of Guling on the mountain became a summer retreat for missionaries, Nationalist leaders, and later Communist Party conferences — the pivotal 1959 Lushan Conference was held there.

Cloud seas are among Mount Lu's celebrated sights.

Culture and religion

The mountain inspired thousands of poems, including works by Li Bai — whose "Viewing the Waterfall at Mount Lu" is among the most famous poems in Chinese — Tao Yuanming, who lived in retirement nearby, and Su Shi, who observed that one cannot know the true face of Mount Lu while standing within it.

Scenery

Famous sights include the Five Old Men Peaks, Sandie Spring (Three-Tier Spring) waterfall, Flower Path, and the Lushan Botanical Garden (founded 1934). The area became a UNESCO Global Geopark for its Quaternary glacial landforms.
