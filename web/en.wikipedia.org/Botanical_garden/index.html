Botanical garden

A botanical garden or botanic garden is a garden dedicated to the collection, cultivation, preservation, and display of a wide range of plants, usually labelled with their botanical names. Botanical gardens combine scientific research and conservation with public education and recreation, and often include specialist collections such as herb gardens, rock gardens, greenhouses for tropical and desert plants, and arboreta devoted to trees.

History

The first physic gardens for studying medicinal plants appeared in Renaissance Italy — Pisa (1543) and Padua (1545), the latter still extant — followed by university gardens at Leiden, Oxford (1621), and Paris. In the 18th and 19th centuries, gardens such as the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew became hubs of imperial plant exchange, acclimatising economically important species like rubber, tea, and cinchona (quinine) around the world.

Modern roles

Today there are roughly 1,800 botanical gardens worldwide, coordinated in part by Botanic Gardens Conservation International. Beyond display, they maintain seed banks, conduct taxonomic and ecological research, run ex-situ conservation of endangered species, and teach horticulture. Famous examples include Kew, the New York Botanical Garden, Singapore Botanic Gardens (a UNESCO World Heritage Site), and the Kirstenbosch National Botanical Garden in Cape Town.
