RCEP

Full nameRegional Comprehensive Economic Partnership
Signed15 November 2020
Effective1 January 2022
Members15 Asia-Pacific countries

The Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP) is a free trade agreement among 15 Asia-Pacific countries: the ten member states of ASEAN (Brunei, Cambodia, Indonesia, Laos, Malaysia, Myanmar, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, and Vietnam) plus China, Japan, South Korea, Australia, and New Zealand.

Scale

RCEP is the world's largest trade bloc, covering roughly 30 percent of the world's population and about 30 percent of global GDP. It is the first free trade agreement linking China, Japan, and South Korea together.

Negotiation and signing

Negotiations were launched in 2012. India participated in talks but withdrew in 2019, citing concerns over trade deficits and market access. The agreement was signed on 15 November 2020 at a virtual ASEAN summit hosted by Vietnam, and it entered into force on 1 January 2022 for the first ratifying countries.

Provisions

The agreement eliminates tariffs on about 90 percent of goods traded between members over time, harmonizes rules of origin, and covers services, investment, e-commerce, and intellectual property, though its commitments are generally less deep than those of the CPTPP.
