Tosca

Tosca
Composer: Giacomo Puccini
Librettists: Luigi Illica, Giuseppe Giacosa
Premiere: 14 January 1900, Teatro Costanzi, Rome
Based on: La Tosca by Victorien Sardou

Tosca is an opera in three acts by Giacomo Puccini to an Italian libretto by Luigi Illica and Giuseppe Giacosa. Based on Victorien Sardou's 1887 French-language dramatic play La Tosca, the opera premiered at the Teatro Costanzi in Rome on 14 January 1900 and has become one of the most frequently performed works in the operatic repertoire.

Synopsis

Set in Rome in June 1800, amid Napoleon's invasion of Italy, the story follows the singer Floria Tosca; her lover, the painter and republican Mario Cavaradossi; and the sadistic chief of police, Baron Scarpia. Scarpia, coveting Tosca, arrests and tortures Cavaradossi to force her to reveal the hiding place of an escaped political prisoner. Tosca agrees to Scarpia's bargain for a mock execution and safe-conduct, then stabs him to death. But Scarpia's treachery continues from beyond the grave: the firing squad uses real bullets, and Tosca, facing arrest, leaps from the parapet of the Castel Sant'Angelo.

Music

The score is celebrated for its through-composed intensity and famous arias, including Cavaradossi's "Recondita armonia" and "E lucevan le stelle" and Tosca's "Vissi d'arte". The opening chords associated with Scarpia are among the most recognisable in opera. Early critics were sometimes hostile to the opera's violence — one famously called it a "shabby little shocker" — but audiences embraced it immediately.
