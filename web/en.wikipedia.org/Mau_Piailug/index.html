Mau Piailug

Pius "Mau" Piailug
Born: 1932, Satawal, Caroline Islands
Died: 12 July 2010 (aged 78), Maui, Hawaii
Occupation: Master navigator
Known for: Navigating Hōkūleʻa from Hawaii to Tahiti, 1976

Pius "Mau" Piailug (1932 – 12 July 2010) was a Micronesian navigator from the island of Satawal in the Caroline Islands. A master of traditional wayfinding — navigation by stars, swell patterns, winds, and seabirds without instruments — he became internationally known for guiding the Hawaiian voyaging canoe Hōkūleʻa from Hawaii to Tahiti in 1976.

Background

Piailug was taught from infancy by his grandfather, a master navigator (palu), in a tradition in which such knowledge was tightly held. On Satawal, master navigators were formally initiated in the weriyeng school. By the 1970s, Piailug was one of the few remaining practitioners of the full traditional system in Micronesia.

Hōkūleʻa

The Polynesian Voyaging Society's double-hulled canoe Hōkūleʻa had no Hawaiian navigator trained in the old methods, so Piailug agreed to guide her maiden voyage. Departing Maui on 1 May 1976, he navigated some 2,500 nautical miles to Tahiti in just over a month without charts or instruments, proving that long-distance Polynesian voyaging by deliberate navigation was possible. He later trained Hawaiian navigator Nainoa Thompson, helping ignite the Hawaiian cultural renaissance and a Pacific-wide voyaging revival.

Legacy

Piailug received an honorary doctorate and numerous honours; a voyaging canoe named in his honour, and the master navigators he trained across the Pacific, continue his teaching.
