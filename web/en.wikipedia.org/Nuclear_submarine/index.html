Nuclear submarine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A nuclear submarine is a submarine powered by a nuclear reactor. The performance advantages of nuclear submarines over conventional (diesel-electric) submarines are considerable: a nuclear boat can operate submerged for months at a time at high speed, because its reactor requires no oxygen and generates abundant power, whereas a diesel-electric submarine must surface or snorkel frequently to recharge its batteries and is limited in submerged speed and endurance.

History

The world's first nuclear-powered vessel was USS Nautilus (SSN-571), commissioned by the US Navy in 1954 and first underway on nuclear power in January 1955. In 1958 Nautilus completed the first submerged transit of the North Pole. The Soviet Union's first nuclear submarine, K-3 Leninsky Komsomol, followed in 1958. Britain, France, China, and India subsequently developed their own nuclear submarine fleets.

Types

Nuclear submarines fall into several categories:

  • Ballistic missile submarines (SSBNs) — large boats carrying submarine-launched ballistic missiles with nuclear warheads, forming the sea-based leg of nuclear deterrent forces, such as the US Ohio class and the Russian Borei class.
  • Attack submarines (SSNs) — fast boats armed with torpedoes and cruise missiles, tasked with hunting other submarines and ships and striking land targets, such as the US Virginia class and the British Astute class.
  • Cruise missile submarines (SSGNs) — boats converted or built to carry large numbers of conventional cruise missiles.

Technology

Naval reactors are usually compact pressurised water reactors fuelled with enriched uranium. Their cores may last decades without refuelling. Nuclear submarines also generate their own oxygen and fresh water, so crew endurance and food are the main limits on patrol length — typically two to three months for deterrent patrols. Noise reduction ("quieting") is a central design concern, since a submarine's survival depends on remaining undetected by sonar.

Accidents and decommissioning

Several nuclear submarines have been lost, including USS Thresher (1963), USS Scorpion (1968), and the Russian Kursk (2000). Decommissioning involves the safe removal and long-term storage of reactor compartments, a costly process carried out in the United States at the Hanford site and in Russia and Britain at dedicated facilities.
