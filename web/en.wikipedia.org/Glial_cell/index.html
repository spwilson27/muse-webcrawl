Glial cell

Glial cells, or simply glia (from Greek for "glue"), are non-neuronal cells of the nervous system that support, nourish and protect neurons. Once thought to be mere passive scaffolding, they are now known to play active roles in brain development, synaptic function, and response to injury. In the human brain, glia are roughly comparable in number to neurons.

Types

In the central nervous system the main types are astrocytes, which regulate the chemical environment around synapses and help maintain the blood–brain barrier; oligodendrocytes, which produce the myelin sheaths that insulate axons and speed signal conduction; microglia, the resident immune cells of the brain; and ependymal cells, which line the ventricles. In the peripheral nervous system, Schwann cells form myelin and satellite cells support neuronal cell bodies.

Functions

Glia buffer ions and neurotransmitters, supply nutrients and oxygen, modulate synapse formation and pruning, and mount defensive responses to infection and damage. Disorders involving glia include multiple sclerosis, in which myelin is attacked, and gliomas, tumours arising from glial cells.
