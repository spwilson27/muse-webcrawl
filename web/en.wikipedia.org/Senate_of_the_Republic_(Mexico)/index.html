Senate of the Republic (Mexico)

The Senate of the Republic (Spanish: Senado de la República) is the upper house of Mexico's bicameral Congress of the Union, the lower house being the Chamber of Deputies. It is composed of 128 senators, who serve six-year terms.

Composition and elections

Of the 128 senators, 64 are elected in pairs (a two-person formula) from each of the 31 states and Mexico City by plurality, 32 are awarded to the first minority (the runner-up formula in each entity), and 32 are elected by proportional representation from national party lists. Senators were historically barred from immediate re-election, but a 2014 constitutional reform allows one consecutive re-election, first applicable to senators elected in 2018. The senatorial term coincides with the six-year presidential term (sexenio).

Powers

The Senate has exclusive powers in foreign policy: it ratifies treaties, approves diplomatic appointments, and authorises the deployment of troops abroad and the entry of foreign forces. It also ratifies the Attorney General, approves appointments to various autonomous bodies, declares the disappearance of powers of a state government and appoints provisional governors in such cases, and acts as a jury in impeachment proceedings against high officials.

History

A bicameral congress with a senate was first created under the Constitution of 1824, abolished in favour of a unicameral system in 1857, and restored by reform in 1874. The Constitution of 1917, promulgated after the Mexican Revolution, retained the Senate in essentially its modern form. Since 2011 the Senate has met in a purpose-built headquarters at the corner of Paseo de la Reforma and Insurgentes in Mexico City.
