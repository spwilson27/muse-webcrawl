Isla Vista, California

Isla Vista is an unincorporated community and census-designated place in Santa Barbara County, California, United States. It lies on the south-facing coastal plateau adjacent to the University of California, Santa Barbara (UCSB) campus, and most of its roughly 15,000 residents are students, giving it one of the highest population densities of any community in the United States.

History

The area was part of a Mexican land grant before subdivisions were laid out in the 1920s. Growth followed the relocation of the university campus in the 1950s. Isla Vista became nationally known during the Vietnam War era: in 1970, protests culminated in the burning of a Bank of America branch, an iconic event of student unrest. In May 2014 the community was the site of a mass killing in which six people were murdered, prompting national debate about misogyny and gun violence.

Geography and culture

Isla Vista is bounded by the Pacific Ocean and coastal bluffs to the south and the university to the east. Its compact grid, centred on Pardall Road and Del Playa Drive, is famously bicycle-oriented. The community is known for beach culture, Halloween celebrations that once drew tens of thousands, and a youthful, densely packed rental housing stock.
