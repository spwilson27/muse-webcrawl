Protected area

Protected areas or conservation areas are locations that receive protection because of their recognised natural or cultural values. Protected areas are those in which human presence or the exploitation of natural resources (e.g. firewood, non-timber forest products, water, ...) is limited. The term broadly includes marine protected areas and transboundary protected areas that span the borders of more than one country.

IUCN categories

The IUCN classifies protected areas into six categories by management objective: Ia strict nature reserve, Ib wilderness area, II national park, III natural monument, IV habitat/species management area, V protected landscape/seascape, and VI protected area with sustainable use of natural resources.

History

Although sacred groves and royal hunting reserves are ancient, the modern protected-area movement dates from the establishment of Yellowstone in 1872. Global commitments such as the Convention on Biological Diversity's "30 by 30" target (protecting 30% of land and sea by 2030, adopted at COP15 in 2022) have spurred rapid expansion of designated areas.

Effectiveness and criticism

Protected areas are a cornerstone of biodiversity conservation, but their effectiveness varies: some are "paper parks" lacking enforcement or funding. Debate continues over the rights of Indigenous peoples and local communities who may be displaced or restricted by strict protection, and over integrating conservation with sustainable livelihoods.
