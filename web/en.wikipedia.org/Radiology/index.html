Radiology is the medical discipline that uses medical imaging to diagnose and treat diseases within the bodies of humans and other animals. It began with Wilhelm Conrad Röntgen's discovery of X-rays in 1895 and now encompasses a wide range of modalities: projection radiography, computed tomography (CT), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), ultrasound, nuclear medicine including positron emission tomography (PET), fluoroscopy and mammography.

Branches

Diagnostic radiology interprets images to detect and characterise disease, from fractures and pneumonia to cancer staging. Interventional radiology performs minimally invasive, image-guided procedures — angioplasty, embolisation, biopsies, drainage and ablation — often replacing open surgery. Radiation oncology, historically part of radiology, uses ionising radiation to treat cancer and is now usually a separate specialty.

Practice

Radiologists are specialist physicians who complete residency training in imaging and image-guided procedures. Images are stored and viewed in digital PACS systems, and teleradiology allows remote interpretation. Radiation protection — following the ALARA principle ("as low as reasonably achievable") — is central to the safe use of ionising modalities. Artificial intelligence tools are increasingly used to triage studies and flag abnormalities.
