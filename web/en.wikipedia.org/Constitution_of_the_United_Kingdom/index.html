Constitution of the United Kingdom

The Palace of Westminster, seat of the Parliament of the United Kingdom.

The constitution of the United Kingdom is the body of laws, conventions, and principles that together govern the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland. Unlike most countries, the United Kingdom does not have a single codified constitutional document. Its constitution is instead described as uncodified, or partially written and wholly uncodified, drawing on statutes, common law, parliamentary conventions, and authoritative scholarly works.

Sources

Key statutory sources include Magna Carta (1215), the Petition of Right (1628), the Bill of Rights 1689, the Act of Settlement 1701, the Acts of Union 1707, the Parliament Acts 1911 and 1949, the Human Rights Act 1998, and the devolution statutes for Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland. Judicial decisions in the common law tradition and constitutional conventions, such as the rule that the monarch acts on ministerial advice, are equally important.

Key principles

Two principles are traditionally regarded as foundational. The first is parliamentary sovereignty: Parliament can make or unmake any law, and no Parliament can bind its successors. The second, articulated by the Victorian jurist A. V. Dicey, is the rule of law, under which every person is subject to the ordinary law administered by the ordinary courts. The separation of powers in the British system is partial rather than strict; the executive is drawn from and accountable to the legislature, though the creation of the Supreme Court of the United Kingdom in 2009 strengthened judicial independence.

Devolution and reform

Since the late 1990s the constitution has been reshaped by devolution to Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland, the removal of most hereditary peers from the House of Lords, the Human Rights Act, and the United Kingdom's membership of, and subsequent withdrawal from, the European Union. Proposals for a written constitution are periodically debated but have not been adopted.
