Moving-average model

In time series analysis, the moving-average (MA) model is a common approach for modeling univariate time series. It specifies that the output variable depends linearly on the current and various past values of a stochastic (imperfectly predictable) term — that is, on current and lagged random shocks or "innovations".

Definition

An MA model of order q, denoted MA(q), is defined as Xt = μ + εt + θ1εt−1 + ... + θqεt−q, where μ is the mean of the series, the θ's are parameters, and the ε's are white noise error terms. Unlike the autoregressive (AR) model, the MA model is always stationary.

Interpretation and fitting

The model represents a series as a weighted average of past forecast errors. MA models can be fitted by maximum likelihood or by moment methods; the autocorrelation function of an MA(q) process cuts off after lag q, which helps identify the order. Combined with AR terms it forms ARMA and ARIMA models, central to the Box–Jenkins methodology.
