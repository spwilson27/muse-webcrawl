Harmonic series

Harmonic partials on a vibrating string

FieldsMathematics, music (acoustics)

The term harmonic series is used in two closely related senses, in mathematics and in music. In both cases it concerns quantities that arise at integer multiples or integer fractions of a fundamental unit.

In mathematics

The harmonic series is the infinite series formed by summing the reciprocals of the positive integers:

1 + 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/4 + 1/5 + …

Although its terms tend to zero, the series diverges: its partial sums grow without bound, though very slowly, increasing roughly like the natural logarithm. The sums of its first n terms are called harmonic numbers. The series is the borderline case of the p-series, which converges for exponents greater than 1 and diverges otherwise. See the main article, Harmonic series (mathematics).

In music

In music and acoustics, a harmonic series is the sequence of tones — called partials, harmonics or overtones — whose frequencies are integer multiples of a fundamental frequency. A vibrating string or air column naturally resonates at these frequencies simultaneously, and their relative strengths determine the timbre of a note. The intervals between successive harmonics (octave, perfect fifth, perfect fourth, major third, and so on) underlie much of harmony and instrument design; brass players select notes from the series with the embouchure alone, and techniques such as overtone singing and string harmonics isolate individual members of the series.

Relationship between the two senses

The mathematical series takes its name from the musical one: the reciprocals 1, 1/2, 1/3, 1/4 … are the proportions of string lengths that produce the harmonic overtones of a fundamental pitch, a connection known since antiquity and studied by Pythagoras and later by Marin Mersenne and others.
