720p (also called HD ready) is a progressive HDTV signal format with 720 horizontal lines and an aspect ratio of 16:9, giving a display resolution of 1280 × 720 pixels. The "p" stands for progressive scan, in which all lines of each frame are drawn in sequence, as opposed to interlaced video.

Broadcasting

720p was adopted as a broadcast standard by networks such as ABC and Fox in the United States, partly because progressive scanning handles fast-moving sports well. Common frame rates include 24, 25, 30, 50, and 60 frames per second; 720p60 is widely used in North American broadcasting.

Comparison and usage

720p has 921,600 total pixels — about twice the resolution of standard-definition 480p and roughly half of 1080p. It became the default "HD" tier for streaming video, webcams, and game consoles such as early Xbox 360 and PlayStation 3 output, and remains common for bandwidth-efficient streaming.
