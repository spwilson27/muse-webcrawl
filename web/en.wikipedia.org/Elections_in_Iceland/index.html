Elections in Iceland

Elections in Iceland are held to elect the Althing (the national parliament), the President of Iceland, and local municipal councils. Iceland uses proportional representation for parliamentary elections and a plurality system for presidential elections.

Voting in Iceland is open to citizens aged 18 and over. Turnout is typically high, often above 80% in parliamentary elections.

Parliamentary elections

The Althing has 63 members, elected for four-year terms from six multi-member constituencies. Nine seats are compensatory, allocated to parties to improve overall proportionality. Parties must pass a 5% national threshold to qualify for compensatory seats.

Iceland has a multi-party system. Major parties have included the Independence Party, the Progressive Party, the Social Democratic Alliance, the Left-Green Movement, the Pirate Party, the Reform Party, and the People's Party. Coalition governments are the norm, as no single party has won an outright majority in recent decades.

Presidential elections

The President of Iceland is elected by direct popular vote for a four-year term, with no term limit. The election uses first-past-the-post voting: the candidate with the most votes wins. Presidential elections have often been uncontested, with the incumbent re-elected without opposition.

Municipal elections

Municipal councils are elected every four years, most recently alongside the parliamentary elections. The capital, Reykjavík, has the largest council, with 23 members.

Referendums

Iceland has held several national referendums, most notably on the Icesave debt dispute in 2010 and 2011, and on constitutional reform in 2012, when voters approved proposals from a Constitutional Council.
