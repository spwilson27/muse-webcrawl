The Chelyabinsk meteor was a superbolide that entered Earth's atmosphere over the southern Ural region of Russia on 15 February 2013 at about 09:20 YEKT. The asteroid, roughly 18–20 metres across and weighing about 12,000 tonnes, exploded in an airburst at an altitude of some 23–30 km, releasing energy estimated at 400–500 kilotons of TNT — about thirty times the yield of the Hiroshima bomb.

Effects

The shock wave shattered windows across Chelyabinsk and neighbouring towns, injuring around 1,500 people, mostly from flying glass, and damaging more than 7,000 buildings. The intense flash briefly outshone the Sun. Dashboard camera and CCTV footage of the event spread worldwide, making it the best-documented meteor airburst in history. A half-tonne fragment was later recovered from Lake Chebarkul.

Significance

The object approached from the direction of the Sun and was not detected before impact. Coming the same day as the close flyby of the much larger asteroid 367943 Duende (then 2012 DA14), the event spurred investment in planetary defence, near-Earth object surveys and the UN-endorsed International Asteroid Warning Network. It remains the largest recorded meteor event since Tunguska in 1908.
