Gwadar (Balochi and Urdu: گوادر) is a port city on the south-western Arabian Sea coast of Pakistan, in the province of Balochistan, near the border with Iran and some 120 km from the Strait of Hormuz region. The city sits on a distinctive hammerhead-shaped peninsula around a natural harbour. Gwadar has become the centrepiece of the China–Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC), with a Chinese-operated deep-sea port intended to give western China and landlocked Central Asia a shorter route to the sea.

History

Gwadar was long a fishing town within the Khanate of Kalat. Remarkably, it was an enclave of the Sultanate of Oman from 1783 until 1958, when Pakistan purchased it from Oman for about US$3 million. After surveys in the 1950s identified its potential as a deep-water port site, development proceeded slowly for decades.

Port development and CPEC

The first phase of Gwadar Port was built with Chinese assistance and inaugurated in 2007. Under CPEC (launched 2015), the port, a free zone, an international airport and road links such as the Makran Coastal Highway have been expanded. The port's operator is the China Overseas Port Holding Company.

Geography and local issues

Gwadar's arid climate, chronic water scarcity and the grievances of its largely Baloch fishing population — including protests over illegal trawling and the distribution of development benefits — remain significant challenges. The city also experiences seismic risk; the 1945 Makran earthquake and tsunami struck the coast nearby.
