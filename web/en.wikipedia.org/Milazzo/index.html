Milazzo

Milazzo (Sicilian: Milazzu)
Region: Sicily
Metropolitan city: Messina
Population: about 30,000
Ancient name: Mylae

Milazzo is a town in the Metropolitan City of Messina, Sicily, southern Italy. It occupies a strategic peninsula jutting into the Tyrrhenian Sea and is the main departure port for the Aeolian Islands.

History

The site was settled in the Neolithic era and became the Greek colony of Mylae, founded from Zancle (Messina) around 716 BC. Off its shores Rome won its first great naval victory, the Battle of Mylae in 260 BC during the First Punic War, using the corvus boarding device. In 36 BC the waters near Milazzo saw the Battle of Mylae between Octavian's fleet under Agrippa and Sextus Pompey. In July 1860 Giuseppe Garibaldi defeated Bourbon forces at the Battle of Milazzo during the Expedition of the Thousand, opening the way to Messina.

Sights and economy

The town is dominated by the Castello di Milazzo, one of the largest castle complexes in Sicily, begun by the Arabs and enlarged by Norman, Swabian, Aragonese and Spanish rulers on the site of the ancient acropolis. The peninsula ends at Capo Milazzo, a protected marine area with a sanctuary of St Anthony of Padua. Modern Milazzo is an industrial and commercial port with an oil refinery, steel and food-processing industries, and busy ferry traffic to the Aeolian Islands.
