Police car

A police car is a ground vehicle used by police forces for transportation during patrols and responses to incidents. Police cars are typically equipped with distinctive livery, emergency lights, sirens, radios, and increasingly in-car computers, cameras, and prisoner partitions.

History

The first police car is often credited to Akron, Ohio, in 1899 — an electrically powered wagon used to transport arrested persons. Motorized patrol expanded rapidly in the early 20th century as automobiles replaced foot and bicycle patrols. Purpose-built models such as the Ford Crown Victoria Police Interceptor became dominant in North America, later succeeded by SUV-based interceptors.

Types

Categories include marked patrol cars, unmarked cars, traffic enforcement vehicles, pursuit vehicles, K-9 units, and prisoner transport vehicles. Markings range from high-visibility Battenburg patterns in the United Kingdom to black-and-white schemes in the United States.

Equipment

Standard equipment may include light bars, sirens, push bumpers, spotlights, mobile data terminals, automatic number-plate recognition, and secure rear seating for detainees.
