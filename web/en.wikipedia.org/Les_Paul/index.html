Les Paul

Les Paul (born Lester William Polsfuss, 9 June 1915 – 12 August 2009) was an American jazz, country and blues guitarist, songwriter and inventor. He was a pioneer of the solid-body electric guitar and of multitrack recording techniques that shaped modern popular music.

Career

Performing with his wife Mary Ford in the 1950s, Paul scored hits such as "How High the Moon" and "Bye Bye Blues", showcasing his virtuoso speed, layered guitar parts and echo effects. His experiments with overdubbing — which he called "sound on sound" — began in the 1940s.

The Les Paul guitar

In 1952 Gibson introduced the Les Paul model, designed with Paul's input, which became one of the most iconic electric guitars ever made, played by generations of rock musicians. Paul was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1988.
