John Forbes Nash Jr. (June 13, 1928 – May 23, 2015) was an American mathematician who made fundamental contributions to game theory, differential geometry and the study of partial differential equations. His game-theory concept, the Nash equilibrium, became a cornerstone of modern economics. Nash shared the 1994 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences and received the 2015 Abel Prize. His life inspired Sylvia Nasar's biography A Beautiful Mind and the Academy Award-winning film of the same name.

Early work

Nash studied at the Carnegie Institute of Technology (now Carnegie Mellon University) and took his doctorate at Princeton University in 1950 with a 28-page dissertation on non-cooperative games. In it he defined the equilibrium concept that bears his name, showing that every finite game has at least one such equilibrium when mixed strategies are allowed. He also proved striking results in geometry, including the Nash embedding theorem for Riemannian manifolds.

Illness and recovery

Beginning in 1959 Nash developed paranoid schizophrenia, which for decades interrupted his career with hospitalisations and delusional episodes. From the 1980s onward he gradually recovered, returned to Princeton, and resumed research. He married Alicia Larde in 1957; they divorced during his illness but remarried in 2001.

Death

Nash and his wife were killed on May 23, 2015, when the taxi in which they were riding crashed on the New Jersey Turnpike, days after he received the Abel Prize in Oslo.
