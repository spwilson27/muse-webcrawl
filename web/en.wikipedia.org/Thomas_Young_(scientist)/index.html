Thomas Young (13 June 1773 – 10 May 1829) was an English polymath who made notable contributions to the fields of vision, light, solid mechanics, energy, physiology, language, musical harmony and Egyptology. He has been called "the last man who knew everything".

Wave theory of light

In 1801–1803 Young presented his double-slit experiment, showing that light passing through two narrow slits produces interference fringes — strong evidence that light behaves as a wave, contradicting Newton's corpuscular theory. His work was later vindicated by Fresnel and became central to physics. He also proposed that colour vision arises from three types of receptors in the eye (later the Young–Helmholtz theory).

Other contributions

Young introduced Young's modulus, a measure of the elasticity of materials, and gave the word "energy" much of its modern physical sense. He explained the accommodation of the eye's lens and the cause of astigmatism. In Egyptology, he made key early steps in deciphering the Rosetta Stone's demotic and hieroglyphic texts, work completed by Champollion. He also compiled comparisons of around 400 languages and worked on tides, life insurance and weights and measures.
