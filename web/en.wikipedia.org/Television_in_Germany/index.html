Television in Germany is characterized by a strong public-service system operating alongside large commercial broadcasters. Germany has one of Europe's largest television markets, with households receiving dozens of free-to-air channels via cable, satellite, terrestrial, and IPTV.

Public broadcasting

The ARD consortium of regional broadcasters runs the national channel Das Erste and regional "third programmes," while ZDF provides a second national channel. They are funded primarily by the Rundfunkbeitrag, a per-household broadcasting contribution, and governed under the constitutional principle of distance from the state. The flagship news program Tagesschau (20:00 on Das Erste) is Germany's most-watched newscast. Joint ventures include the cultural channels arte and 3sat and the children's channel KiKA.

History

Experimental broadcasts began in the 1930s; regular service resumed in West Germany in 1952 and in East Germany with Deutscher Fernsehfunk in 1956. Commercial television was licensed from 1984, with RTL and Sat.1 building large audiences. After reunification in 1990 the eastern broadcaster was dissolved and its services absorbed into the federal public system.

Commercial television and streaming

The RTL Group and ProSiebenSat.1 dominate private television, alongside channels such as VOX, Kabel Eins, and n-tv. Germany has a significant domestic production sector — long-running series like the crime procedural Tatort (since 1970) are institutions — and streaming services have expanded rapidly since the 2010s.
