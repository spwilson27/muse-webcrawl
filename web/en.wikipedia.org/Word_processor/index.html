Word processor

A word processor is software or a device that allows users to create, edit, format, store and print documents. Word processors replaced the typewriter in offices during the late 20th century and became one of the defining applications of the personal computer.

Features

Beyond basic text entry, word processors offer character and paragraph formatting, fonts, styles, tables, images, footnotes, indexes, mail merge, spell checking, grammar checking, change tracking and collaboration. Most present a "what you see is what you get" (WYSIWYG) display in which the screen closely matches the printed page.

History

The term originated with dedicated electronic typing systems of the 1960s and 1970s, such as IBM's MT/ST and the Wang word processors. Software word processors — Electric Pencil (1976), WordStar (1978), WordPerfect and Microsoft Word (1983) — drove PC adoption; Word became dominant in the 1990s. In the 2010s, cloud-based processors such as Google Docs brought real-time collaborative editing into the mainstream.
