Versailles

Versailles is a commune in the Yvelines department of the Île-de-France region, about 19 kilometres (12 miles) west of central Paris. It is world-famous for the Palace of Versailles, the principal royal residence of France from 1682, when Louis XIV moved the court there, until the start of the French Revolution in 1789. The city that grew around the palace has a population of about 84,000 and remains a major administrative centre and garrison town, as well as one of France's most visited destinations.

The palace and the court

What began as Louis XIII's hunting lodge (1623) was transformed by Louis XIV into Europe's grandest palace, with the Hall of Mirrors, the Royal Chapel, and gardens laid out by André Le Nôtre, all glorifying the Sun King. By gathering the great nobility at court under his eye, Louis neutralised their power in the provinces. Versailles remained the seat of government under Louis XV and Louis XVI until October 1789, when a Parisian crowd marched on the palace and forced the royal family back to Paris — a turning point of the Revolution.

Later history

Versailles repeatedly returned to centre stage in French history. The German Empire was proclaimed in the Hall of Mirrors in January 1871 after the Franco-Prussian War; the French government sat at Versailles during the Paris Commune and again kept offices there for decades. In June 1919 the palace hosted the signing of the Treaty of Versailles, formally ending the First World War between Germany and the Allies. Today the palace and park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1979, receive millions of visitors annually, and the French parliament still meets at Versailles in Congress to amend the constitution.

The modern city

The town was planned on a regular grid around the palace avenues, with the royal stables, the cathedral of Saint-Louis and the Potager du roi (the king's kitchen garden, still productive). Versailles is an affluent suburb of Paris, linked by RER C and suburban trains, home to the prefecture of Yvelines, the University of Versailles Saint-Quentin-en-Yvelines, and substantial military installations at Satory.
