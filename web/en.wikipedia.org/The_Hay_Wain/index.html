The Hay Wain

ArtistJohn Constable
Year1821
MediumOil on canvas
Dimensions130.2 cm × 185.4 cm
LocationNational Gallery, London

The Hay Wain — originally titled Landscape: Noon — is a painting by John Constable, completed in 1821, which depicts a rural scene on the River Stour between the English counties of Suffolk and Essex. It shows a wooden wagon (a "wain") carrying hay, drawn by horses, crossing the river in front of Flatford Mill, with the cottage of Willy Lott at the left.

History

The painting was one of Constable's "six-footers", a series of large-scale canvases of the Stour valley. It attracted little attention when exhibited at the Royal Academy in 1821, but caused a sensation at the Paris Salon of 1824, where it influenced French artists including Eugène Delacroix and helped shape the Barbizon school. King Charles X awarded Constable a gold medal for the work.

Legacy

The Hay Wain is now housed in the National Gallery in London and is regarded as one of the most famous and beloved British paintings; the scene's setting, now known as Constable Country, is preserved largely as painted.
