Quadruplex videotape

Quadruplex videotape (also called 2-inch Quad) was the first practical and commercially successful videotape recording format. Developed by Ampex and introduced in 1956, it used two-inch-wide tape and four rotating record/playback heads mounted on a transverse drum, each head recording a segment of the video signal across the tape's width — hence "quadruplex".

History and impact

The Ampex VRX-1000 (production model VR-1000) was demonstrated in April 1956 and revolutionized television broadcasting: networks could time-shift programming between time zones with picture quality close to live broadcasts, replacing kinescope film recordings. Quad machines cost around $50,000 and the tape was expensive, leading broadcasters to reuse tapes — a major cause of lost early television recordings. Editing was initially done by physically cutting the tape with a microscope-aided splicing block; electronic editing followed.

Decline

Helical-scan formats, especially 1-inch Type C in the late 1970s and later component digital formats, displaced Quad, which remained in some use into the 1980s. Surviving Quad tapes are now digitized by archives for preservation.
