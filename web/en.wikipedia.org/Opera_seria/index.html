Opera seria

Opera seria (Italian for "serious opera"), also called dramma per musica or melodramma serio, is a term referring to the noble and serious style of Italian opera that predominated in Europe from the 1710s to about 1770. The term itself was rarely used at the time and only attained common usage once opera seria became unfashionable and beginning to be viewed as a historical genre.

Structure

Opera seria was typically built around a libretto, very often by Pietro Metastasio, the most celebrated librettist of the age. The drama unfolded through alternating recitative, which advanced the plot, and da capo arias, in which a single emotion or "affect" was explored and the singer displayed vocal virtuosity. Subjects were drawn from classical antiquity and mythology, usually involving kings, heroes, and conflicts between duty and love.

Composers

Leading composers of opera seria included Alessandro Scarlatti, Leonardo Vinci, George Frideric Handel, Johann Adolph Hasse, and the young Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart, whose Idomeneo (1781) is often considered a high point of the form. Christoph Willibald Gluck's reform operas, beginning with Orfeo ed Euridice (1762), sought to subordinate vocal display to dramatic truth and reshaped the genre in its final decades.
