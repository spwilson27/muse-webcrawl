Daniel Bernoulli

Born8 February 1700, Groningen, Dutch Republic
Died27 March 1782, Basel, Switzerland
FieldsMathematics, physics, medicine

Daniel Bernoulli (1700–1782) was a Swiss mathematician and physicist, a member of the celebrated Bernoulli family of Basel. He is remembered above all for his applications of mathematics to mechanics, especially fluid mechanics, and for his pioneering work in probability and statistics.

Work

In his masterpiece Hydrodynamica (1738), Bernoulli laid out what is now called Bernoulli's principle: along a streamline in a steady flow, an increase in the speed of a fluid occurs simultaneously with a decrease in pressure or potential energy. The same book contains an early kinetic theory of gases, deriving gas pressure from the collisions of particles. Earlier, in St. Petersburg, he wrote Exercitationes quaedam mathematicae (1724) and introduced the idea of expected utility in his 1738 paper on the St. Petersburg paradox, published by the Imperial Academy.

Life

Son of Johann Bernoulli and nephew of Jacob Bernoulli, Daniel studied medicine and mathematics, held a chair at St. Petersburg alongside Leonhard Euler, and in 1733 returned to Basel, where he held chairs in anatomy, botany, and finally physics. Friction with his father — who published a rival hydraulics work backdated to pre-empt his son's — marred their relationship.
