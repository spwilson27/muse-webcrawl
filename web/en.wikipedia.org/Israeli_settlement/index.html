Israeli settlement

A settlement in the West Bank

Israeli settlements are civilian communities built by Israel in territories it captured in the Six-Day War of 1967 — principally the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, and the Golan Heights. Settlements were also established in the Sinai Peninsula and the Gaza Strip, but those were evacuated in 1982 and 2005 respectively.

History

The first settlement, Kfar Etzion, was re-established in September 1967. Settlement expanded under both Labour and Likud governments, with ideological movements such as Gush Emunim (founded 1974) playing a major role alongside state planning. The population grew from a few thousand in the 1970s to several hundred thousand by the 2020s, concentrated in the West Bank (excluding East Jerusalem) and in annexed East Jerusalem and the Golan Heights.

Legal status

The international community, the International Court of Justice (in advisory opinions of 2004 and 2024) and the UN Security Council (Resolution 2334, 2016) regard the settlements as a violation of the Fourth Geneva Convention's prohibition on transferring an occupying power's population into occupied territory. Israel disputes this position, arguing the territories are "disputed" rather than occupied, while distinguishing authorized settlements from unauthorized "outposts," which are illegal under Israeli law itself.

Political significance

Settlements are among the central issues of the Israeli–Palestinian conflict, affecting negotiations over borders, land swaps and a two-state solution. The 2005 disengagement removed all settlements from Gaza and four in the northern West Bank.
