Nakba

The Nakba (Arabic: النكبة, romanized: an-Nakba, lit. 'the catastrophe') is the ethnic cleansing of Palestinian Arabs through their violent displacement and dispossession of land, property, and belongings, along with the destruction of their society and the suppression of their culture, identity, political rights, and national aspirations. The term is used to describe both the events of 1948, in which over 700,000 Palestinian Arabs fled or were expelled from their homes during the 1948 Palestine war, and the ongoing persecution and displacement of Palestinians by Israel throughout the Palestinian territories.

Events of 1948

During the 1948 war, more than 400 Palestinian villages were depopulated, and Palestinian urban neighbourhoods in cities such as Haifa, Jaffa, Acre, Safad, and Lydda were emptied. Causes of the flight and expulsions remain debated among historians: they include direct expulsion by Zionist and later Israeli forces, flight amid fighting and fear of massacres such as Deir Yassin, and, in some cases, calls by Arab leaders — the weight of each factor varies by locality. Israel subsequently barred the refugees from returning, and the Absentees' Property Law transferred their property to the state.

Commemoration

Palestinians commemorate Nakba Day on 15 May, the day after Israeli Independence Day, with demonstrations and ceremonies in the West Bank, Gaza Strip, Israel, and the diaspora. The term was popularized by the Syrian historian Constantin Zureiq in his 1948 pamphlet Ma'na an-Nakba ("The Meaning of the Catastrophe").

Historiography

The historiography of 1948 was transformed in the late 1980s by Israel's "New Historians", such as Benny Morris, Ilan Pappé, and Avi Shlaim, who used declassified Israeli archives to document expulsions and massacres, challenging earlier narratives that Palestinians had left voluntarily or on Arab orders. Israeli law and public discourse continue to debate the term's use, including legislation restricting state funding to bodies that commemorate the Nakba.
