Volcano Islands

The Volcano Islands (Japanese: 火山列島, Kazan Rettō), also called the Iwo Islands, are a group of three volcanic islands in the western Pacific Ocean administered by Japan as part of the Ogasawara (Bonin) island group, some 1,200 km south of Tokyo. From north to south they are Kita Iwo Jima (North Iwo Island), Iwo Jima (Iōtō), and Minami Iwo Jima (South Iwo Island).

Geography and history

The islands are the emergent summits of submarine volcanoes on the Izu–Bonin–Mariana arc. They were uninhabited when claimed by Japan in the late nineteenth century; sulfur mining later brought settlers to Iwo Jima. During the Second World War the islands were fortified, and Iwo Jima was the scene of a famous battle between Japanese and United States forces in February–March 1945, commemorated by the flag-raising photograph on Mount Suribachi.

After the war the islands were administered by the United States until their return to Japan in 1968. Today civilians are excluded: Iwo Jima hosts a Japan Self-Defense Forces airbase, and the other two islands are uninhabited nature reserves. Volcanic uplift at Iwo Jima is among the fastest measured anywhere, and offshore eruptions near Minami Iwo Jima created the short-lived island of Fukutoku-Okanoba in 2021.
