Regulbium

Regulbium was a Roman fort and settlement in south-eastern Britain, located at modern Reculver in Kent, England. It guarded the northern entrance to the Wantsum Channel, the strait that once separated the Isle of Thanet from the mainland, and formed part of the chain of coastal defences later known as the Saxon Shore forts.

History

A small fort was first established around the early 3rd century AD, and a larger stone fort was built on the site by about AD 210–220. The fort was roughly square, defended by a thick stone wall with internal turrets, and enclosed an area of about 3 hectares. Inside stood a headquarters building (principia), a commanding officer's house, granaries, and a small bath house.

Later history

After the Roman withdrawal the site was occupied by an Anglo-Saxon monastery founded in 669, whose church incorporated Roman building material. Much of the fort was lost to coastal erosion; the twin towers of the medieval church remain a prominent landmark.
