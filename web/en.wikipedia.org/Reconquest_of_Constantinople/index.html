Reconquest of Constantinople

The reconquest of Constantinople was the recapture of the Byzantine capital from the Latin Empire on 25 July 1261 by the forces of the Empire of Nicaea, restoring the Byzantine Empire under Michael VIII Palaiologos.

Background

Constantinople had been seized in 1204 by the Fourth Crusade, which established the Latin Empire. The Nicaean emperor Michael VIII, having secured his western flank by the Treaty of Nymphaeum with Genoa (March 1261), sent a small force under Alexios Strategopoulos to scout the city.

The recapture

Learning that the Latin garrison and Venetian fleet were away raiding Daphnousia, Strategopoulos's roughly 800 men entered through a little-used passage near the Gate of the Spring on the night of 24–25 July, opened the gates, and set fire to Venetian quarters. The Latin emperor Baldwin II fled. Michael VIII entered the city on 15 August 1261 and was crowned again, founding the Palaiologos dynasty, which ruled until 1453.
