Port of Singapore

LocationSingapore, Straits of Malacca
OperatorPSA International; Jurong Port
AuthorityMaritime and Port Authority of Singapore
Container throughputapprox. 39 million TEU (2023)

The Port of Singapore comprises the collective group of marine facilities and terminals along Singapore's southern coast and on its offshore islands that handle the city-state's shipping trade. Strategically located on the Strait of Malacca, the main shipping lane between the Indian Ocean and the South China Sea, it is the world's busiest transshipment port and one of the largest container ports overall, second in container volume only to Shanghai in recent years.

Operations

The port is operated principally by PSA International, successor to the Port of Singapore Authority, with Jurong Port handling conventional and bulk cargo. Terminals at Pasir Panjang, Brani, Keppel, and Tanjong Pagar served the city-facing waterfront; operations are progressively consolidating at the new Tuas mega-port in the west, whose first berths opened in 2022 and which is planned to reach a capacity of 65 million TEU when fully completed in the 2040s.

Significance

More than 130,000 vessel calls a year connect Singapore to some 600 ports in 120 countries. The port is the world's top bunkering port by fuel sales and supports a maritime cluster — ship repair, offshore engineering, shipping finance and law — that underpins Singapore's role as a global entrepôt, a position it has held since its founding as a British trading post in 1819.
