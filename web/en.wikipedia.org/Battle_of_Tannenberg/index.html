Battle of Tannenberg

Battle of Tannenberg
Date23–30 August 1914
LocationNear Allenstein, East Prussia (present-day Poland)
ResultDecisive German victory
BelligerentsGerman Empire; Russian Empire
CommandersPaul von Hindenburg, Erich Ludendorff; Alexander Samsonov (suicide)

The Battle of Tannenberg was fought between 23 and 30 August 1914 in East Prussia, in the opening weeks of the First World War. The German Eighth Army under Paul von Hindenburg and his chief of staff Erich Ludendorff encircled and destroyed the Russian Second Army of General Alexander Samsonov.

Exploiting intercepted, uncoded Russian radio messages and the separation between Samsonov's Second Army and Rennenkampf's First Army to the north, the Germans concentrated against Samsonov in the forests and lake country around Allenstein. The Russian centre collapsed into a pocket where tens of thousands were killed or captured; Samsonov shot himself in the forest rather than report the disaster. German losses were comparatively light, and some 90,000 Russians were taken prisoner.

Legacy

The victory, deliberately named after the nearby village of Tannenberg to avenge the Teutonic Knights' defeat there in 1410, made Hindenburg a national hero and halted the Russian invasion of East Prussia. The Germans followed it with victory at the First Battle of the Masurian Lakes, though Russia's early offensive had achieved its Allied purpose of drawing German strength from the Western Front.
