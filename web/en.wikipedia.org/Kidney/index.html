Kidney

SystemUrinary system; endocrine system
ArteryRenal artery
VeinRenal vein
Functional unitNephron (about 1 million per kidney)

In humans, the kidneys are two reddish-brown bean-shaped organs located in the retroperitoneal space on either side of the spine, each about 11–12 cm long. They receive blood from the paired renal arteries and drain into the paired renal veins. The kidneys filter the blood, excreting waste products such as urea in the urine, and regulate fluid volume, electrolytes, acid–base balance and blood pressure.

Structure

Each kidney has an outer cortex and an inner medulla arranged in renal pyramids. The functional unit is the nephron, consisting of a glomerulus and a tubule system (proximal tubule, loop of Henle, distal tubule and collecting duct). Urine collects in the renal pelvis and passes via the ureter to the bladder.

Function

The kidneys filter roughly 180 litres of plasma per day, reabsorbing nearly all of it. Beyond excretion and fluid balance, they secrete hormones including erythropoietin, which stimulates red blood cell production; renin, central to the renin–angiotensin system regulating blood pressure; and calcitriol, the active form of vitamin D.

Clinical significance

Chronic kidney disease affects roughly 10% of the world's population. Loss of kidney function can be treated with dialysis or kidney transplantation; the first successful transplant was performed in 1954. Kidney stones, infections and glomerulonephritis are other common disorders. The medical specialty concerned with the kidney is nephrology.
