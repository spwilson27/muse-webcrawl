East Prussian Offensive

East Prussian Offensive
Date: 13 January – 25 April 1945
Location: East Prussia, Nazi Germany
Part of: Eastern Front, World War II
Result: Soviet victory

The East Prussian Offensive was a strategic offensive by the Soviet Red Army against the German Wehrmacht on the Eastern Front from 13 January to 25 April 1945. The operation overran the German province of East Prussia, capturing its capital, Königsberg, after a prolonged siege.

Background

By early 1945 the Red Army stood on the borders of East Prussia, the easternmost province of Germany. The offensive was conducted principally by the 2nd and 3rd Belorussian Fronts, commanded by Konstantin Rokossovsky and Ivan Chernyakhovsky (later Aleksandr Vasilevsky), against the German Army Group Centre, later renamed Army Group North.

Course of the offensive

Soviet forces broke through the German defences in January 1945, reaching the Vistula Lagoon and cutting off large German formations in the Heiligenbeil Pocket. German forces were evacuated by sea in large numbers. The siege of Königsberg ended with the surrender of the garrison on 9 April 1945, and organised resistance in the Samland peninsula ended later that month.

Aftermath

The conquest of East Prussia triggered a mass flight and expulsion of the German civilian population. After the war, northern East Prussia, including Königsberg (renamed Kaliningrad), was annexed by the Soviet Union, while the southern part passed to Poland.
