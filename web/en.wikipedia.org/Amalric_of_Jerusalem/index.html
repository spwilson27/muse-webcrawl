Amalric of Jerusalem

Amalric (French: Amaury; 1136 – 11 July 1174) was King of Jerusalem from 1163 to 1174, and Count of Jaffa and Ascalon before his accession. He was the second son of Queen Melisende and Count Fulk V of Anjou, and succeeded his elder brother Baldwin III.

Early life

Amalric was born in 1136. He was made Count of Jaffa in 1151, and after the capture of Ascalon from the Fatimids in 1153 that city was added to his county. As count, he campaigned with Baldwin III in Syria. His first marriage, to Agnes of Courtenay, was annulled on grounds of consanguinity as a condition of his accession to the throne in 1163, though their children, Baldwin and Sibylla, were declared legitimate.

Reign

Amalric's reign was dominated by competition with Nur al-Din for control of Egypt. He invaded Egypt five times between 1163 and 1169, at times in alliance with the Fatimid vizier Shawar, at others against him. In 1167 he married Maria Komnene, a great-niece of the Byzantine emperor Manuel I Komnenos, cementing an alliance with Byzantium. A joint Byzantine–Frankish attack on Damietta in 1169 failed, and Egypt fell to Nur al-Din's general Shirkuh and his nephew Saladin, uniting Egypt and Syria under Muslim rule and encircling the kingdom.

Death and legacy

Amalric died of dysentery on 11 July 1174, while preparing a campaign against Saladin and negotiating an alliance with William II of Sicily. He was succeeded by his son Baldwin IV, the "Leper King", who was a minor. Amalric is remembered as a capable and energetic ruler, a patron of scholarship; the historian William of Tyre was tutor to his son and wrote his history at Amalric's request.
