Student loan
Purpose: Finance higher education
Major systems: US, UK, Australia (HECS-HELP)

A student loan is a loan designed to help students pay for post-secondary education and associated fees, books, supplies and living expenses. Systems differ widely: government-backed loans, income-contingent repayment schemes and private lending all exist.

By country

In the United States, federal Direct Loans and private loans together exceed .6 trillion outstanding, generally not dischargeable in bankruptcy. The United Kingdom uses income-contingent loans repaid through payroll; Australia's HECS-HELP scheme pioneered income-contingent repayment. Other countries provide tuition largely free or through grants.

Effects

Debt burdens affect graduates' choices of career, home ownership and further study, and student debt cancellation is a recurring political issue in several countries.
