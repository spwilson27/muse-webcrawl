Law of Return

New immigrants (olim) arriving in Israel

The Law of Return (Hebrew: חוק השבות) is an Israeli law passed on 5 July 1950 that gives all Jews the right to immigrate to Israel and acquire Israeli citizenship. It is regarded as one of the foundational laws of the State of Israel, expressing the Zionist principle of the ingathering of the Jewish people.

Provisions

The law grants every Jew the right to come to Israel as an oleh (immigrant) and to receive citizenship, unless they are deemed a danger to public health or security, or have a criminal past likely to endanger public welfare. In 1970 the law was amended to extend the right to the children and grandchildren of Jews, and to their spouses, regardless of their own religious status.

Definition debates

The question "Who is a Jew?" has generated decades of litigation before the Supreme Court of Israel, including cases concerning converts and Messianic Jews. The resulting jurisprudence balances religious definitions of Jewish identity against the law's secular national purpose.
