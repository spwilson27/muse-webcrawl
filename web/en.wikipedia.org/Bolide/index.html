Bolide

A bolide is normally taken to mean an exceptionally bright meteor, frequently one that explodes in the atmosphere. The term has somewhat different technical definitions in astronomy and geology.

Definitions

Astronomers generally use "bolide" for a fireball reaching a magnitude of −14 or brighter, considerably brighter than the full moon — more than twice as bright as a standard superbolide threshold sometimes set at magnitude −17. The International Astronomical Union does not maintain a precise official definition. In geology, a bolide is simply a large extraterrestrial impactor, such as an asteroid or comet, that strikes a planet or moon without reference to how it appears in the atmosphere.

Superbolides and notable events

The best known modern example is the Chelyabinsk meteor of 15 February 2013, when an object about 18 m across exploded over Russia with an energy estimated near 500 kilotons of TNT, generating a shock wave that injured around 1,500 people, mostly from broken glass. Sensors maintained by the Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty Organization routinely detect bolide events worldwide, and NASA publishes fireball reports based on U.S. government sensor data.

Terminology

Related terms include the meteoroid (the object in space), the meteor (the visible phenomenon), the fireball (a bright meteor), and the meteorite (material surviving to the ground). A bolide that fragments in a terminal burst is sometimes said to undergo an airburst, as in the 1908 Tunguska event.
