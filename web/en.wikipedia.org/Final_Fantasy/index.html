Final Fantasy

Final Fantasy is a Japanese science fantasy media franchise created by Hironobu Sakaguchi and developed and owned by Square Enix (formerly Square). The franchise centres on a series of role-playing video games, each generally featuring an independent story with new characters and settings, united by recurring elements such as crystals, airships, chocobos, and moogles.

History

The first game was released in Japan in 1987 for the Nintendo Entertainment System. Sakaguchi, facing the possible failure of Square, conceived it as potentially his final project in the industry — hence the title — but its success saved the company and launched the series. The games moved from Nintendo platforms to the Sony PlayStation with Final Fantasy VII (1997), whose cinematic presentation and international success made the series a global phenomenon.

Main series

The numbered main series runs from Final Fantasy (1987) through Final Fantasy XVI (2023). Notable entries include Final Fantasy IV, which introduced the Active Time Battle system; Final Fantasy VI, praised for its ensemble cast; Final Fantasy VII, later remade beginning in 2020; Final Fantasy X, the first with voice acting; and the massively multiplayer Final Fantasy XIV, whose 2013 relaunch as A Realm Reborn turned an initially failed game into one of the most successful in the genre.

Common elements and music

Recurring features include a job or character-class system, summons of powerful beings, a character named Cid, and music strongly associated with composer Nobuo Uematsu, whose themes such as the "Prelude" and "Main Theme" recur across the series. The franchise has expanded into films, anime, novels, and merchandise, and the games have sold well over 185 million copies worldwide, making it one of the best-selling video game franchises of all time.
