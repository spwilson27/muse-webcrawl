Hindu philosophy

Hindu philosophy refers to the philosophical traditions that developed on the Indian subcontinent in dialogue with the Vedas. It is conventionally divided into six orthodox (astika) schools, or darshanas, which accept the authority of the Vedas, as distinct from the unorthodox (nastika) traditions such as Buddhism, Jainism, and Charvaka.

The six orthodox schools

The six darshanas are typically paired: Nyaya (logic and epistemology) with Vaisheshika (atomism and metaphysics); Samkhya (an enumerative dualism of purusha and prakriti) with Yoga (the discipline of meditation, systematised by Patanjali); and Mimamsa (Vedic exegesis and ritual) with Vedanta (the philosophy of the Upanishads). Vedanta itself divides into subschools — Advaita (non-dualism) of Adi Shankara, Vishishtadvaita of Ramanuja, and Dvaita (dualism) of Madhva among them — which differ on the relation between the self (atman) and ultimate reality (Brahman).

Central concepts

Recurring concerns across the schools include dharma (duty/order), karma (action and its fruits), samsara (the cycle of rebirth), moksha (liberation), and the nature of valid knowledge (pramana). Philosophical debate was carried on through sutra literature, commentaries, and formal disputation over more than two millennia.
