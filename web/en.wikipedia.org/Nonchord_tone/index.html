Nonchord tone

A nonchord tone (NCT), also called a non-harmonic tone, is a note in a piece of music which is not part of the implied or expressed chord set out by the harmonic framework. In contrast, a chord tone is a note that is a part of the functional chord. Nonchord tones are mostly dissonant and resolve to chord tones nearby.

Types

Theorists classify nonchord tones by how they are approached and left:

  • Passing tone — fills the gap between two chord tones by step.
  • Neighbour tone — steps away from a chord tone and returns to it.
  • Suspension — a chord tone held over into the next chord, then resolving down by step.
  • Anticipation — arrives at the next chord's tone early.
  • Appoggiatura — a leapt-to accented dissonance resolving by step.
  • Escape tone, pedal point and retardation complete the traditional list.

Use

Nonchord tones create melodic interest and controlled dissonance, and their analysis is a foundation of common-practice harmony teaching and of Schenkerian analysis.
