A cheesesteak (also known as a Philadelphia cheesesteak or Philly cheesesteak) is a sandwich made from thinly sliced pieces of beefsteak and melted cheese in a long hoagie roll. A popular regional fast food, it has its roots in the city of Philadelphia, Pennsylvania.

History

The cheesesteak is credited to Pat and Harry Olivieri, hot dog vendors in South Philadelphia who, in the early 1930s, began selling chopped grilled beef on a roll; the sandwich's popularity grew, and Pat opened Pat's King of Steaks. Cheese was added later — according to the family's account, by manager Joe Lorenza in the 1940s. Rival Geno's Steaks, opened across the street by Joey Vento in 1966, made the intersection of 9th Street and Passyunk Avenue the centre of a famous rivalry.

Preparation and ordering

The meat is typically rib-eye or top round, sliced thin, cooked quickly on a lightly oiled griddle and chopped with spatulas. Cheese options include Cheez Whiz (the most popular since the 1950s), American cheese, and provolone. Fried onions are the standard addition ("wit" in local parlance; "witout" means without). Other toppings include mushrooms, peppers, ketchup and mayonnaise. The roll — classically an Amoroso's roll — is essential: long, soft, with a slightly crisp crust.

Variations and spread

Variations include the pizza steak (with tomato sauce and mozzarella), the cheesesteak hoagie (with lettuce and tomato), chicken cheesesteaks, and mushroom or pepper steaks. The sandwich spread throughout the Philadelphia metropolitan area and beyond, and has been widely imitated — and debated — across the United States.
