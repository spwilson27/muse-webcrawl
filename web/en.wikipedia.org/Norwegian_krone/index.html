The krone (Norwegian: krone, plural kroner; code NOK) is the currency of Norway and its dependent territories. It is nominally subdivided into 100 øre, though øre coins were withdrawn in 2012 and cash payments are rounded to the nearest krone.

History

The krone was introduced in 1875, replacing the speciedaler when Norway joined the Scandinavian Monetary Union with Sweden and Denmark. The union's gold standard dissolved during the First World War. The krone was later pegged to sterling and then the US dollar before moving to a free float in 1992, with monetary policy targeting inflation under Norges Bank.

Coins and banknotes

Coins circulate in 1, 5, 10 and 20 kroner. The current banknote series (introduced 2017–2019) features maritime motifs — including a Viking ship, a lighthouse and a cod — designed around the theme "The Sea".

Exchange rate

The krone is one of the world's most traded currencies despite Norway's small population, and its value is influenced by oil and gas prices, interest-rate differentials and global risk sentiment.
