Layoff

A layoff or downsizing is the temporary suspension or permanent termination of employment of an employee or, more commonly, a group of employees (collective layoff) by an employer for business reasons, such as personnel management or downsizing (reducing the size of an organization). Originally, a layoff denoted a temporary interruption of work — as when a factory worker was "laid off" during a seasonal or economic downturn with the expectation of recall — but the term has come to be used more broadly for permanent job losses not caused by the employee's own misconduct or performance, in contrast to being fired.

Terminology

Related and euphemistic terms abound in corporate usage: redundancy (standard in British and Commonwealth English, where "being made redundant" means one's job, not the person, has ceased to exist), reduction in force (RIF), rightsizing, restructuring, rationalization, and workforce adjustment. A furlough is a temporary unpaid leave commonly used in the public sector. Scholars of management have criticized the proliferation of euphemisms as obscuring the human cost of job loss.

Causes and process

Layoffs occur because of recessions, loss of business, mergers and acquisitions, automation, offshoring, or strategic restructuring. In many jurisdictions the process is regulated: in the United States, the federal WARN Act of 1988 requires employers with 100 or more employees to give 60 days' notice of plant closings and mass layoffs, and many states have "mini-WARN" laws; in the European Union and United Kingdom, collective redundancy rules require consultation with employee representatives and notification to authorities, and selection must generally follow fair criteria. Collective bargaining agreements may impose seniority-based order ("last in, first out") and bumping rights.

Effects

Displaced workers typically suffer substantial and long-lasting earnings losses, and layoffs are associated with adverse health outcomes. Survivors in the organization may experience "survivor syndrome" — guilt, insecurity, and reduced commitment — and research on corporate downsizing finds that it often fails to deliver the expected improvements in profitability or share price, because of lost skills, rehiring costs, and effects on morale.
