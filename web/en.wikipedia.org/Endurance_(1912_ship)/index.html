Endurance (1912 ship)

Endurance
Launched: 1912 (as Polaris)
Type: Barquentine
Tonnage: 350 gross tons
Length: 144 ft (44 m)
Fate: Crushed by ice and sank, 21 November 1915

Endurance was the three-masted barquentine in which Sir Ernest Shackleton and a crew of 27 men sailed for the Antarctic on the Imperial Trans-Antarctic Expedition of 1914–1917. Built in Norway and launched in 1912 as Polaris, she was considered one of the strongest wooden ships ever constructed for polar work. She was bought by Shackleton, renamed after his family motto Fortitudine vincimus ("by endurance we conquer"), and became famous when she was trapped and crushed by ice in the Weddell Sea — and for the survival of her entire crew.

The expedition

Endurance left Plymouth in August 1914, called at Buenos Aires and the whaling station at Grytviken, South Georgia, and entered the Weddell Sea in December 1914, aiming for Vahsel Bay, from where Shackleton planned to cross the continent to the Ross Sea. Battling heavy pack ice, the ship became beset on 19 January 1915, frozen fast about 60 miles from her destination.

Drift and loss

For ten months the ship drifted with the ice, enduring the Antarctic winter as a floating station. In October 1915 the pressure of the ice began to crush her hull; the crew abandoned ship onto the floes on 27 October, salvaging boats, food, and equipment. Endurance finally sank on 21 November 1915. The men camped on the ice until April 1916, then sailed in open boats to Elephant Island — the start of Shackleton's famous boat journey to South Georgia and the eventual rescue of all hands in August 1916.

The wreck

The wreck of Endurance was located on 5 March 2022 by the Endurance22 expedition in the Weddell Sea at a depth of about 3,008 metres, remarkably intact, with the ship's name visible across the stern. It is protected as a historic site and monument under the Antarctic Treaty.
