Winemaking

Winemaking, or vinification, is the production of wine, starting with the selection of fruit and ending with bottling. It is one of the oldest food technologies, with evidence of wine production in the Caucasus around 6000 BC.

Process

Grapes are harvested, sorted and crushed. For white wines the juice is usually pressed from the skins before fermentation; for reds, juice ferments in contact with skins and seeds, extracting colour, tannin and flavour. Yeasts — ambient or cultured — convert sugars to alcohol and carbon dioxide. Many red and some white wines then undergo malolactic fermentation, softening acidity.

Maturation and bottling

Wine may be aged in stainless steel, concrete or oak barrels, racked off its lees, fined and filtered for clarity and stability, then bottled under cork or alternative closures. The winemaker's decisions at every step — harvest date, fermentation temperature, oak regime, blending — shape the style. The scientific study of wine and winemaking is called oenology.
