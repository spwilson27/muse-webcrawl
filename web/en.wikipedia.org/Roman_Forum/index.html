The Roman Forum (Latin: Forum Romanum) was the central public space of ancient Rome, situated in a low valley between the Palatine and Capitoline Hills. For more than a thousand years it was the centre of Roman public life: the site of elections, triumphal processions, public speeches, criminal trials, gladiatorial matches and commerce — in effect, the heart of the city and of the empire it ruled. (It is unrelated to the modern Diocese of Port-Louis in Mauritius.)

History

Originally a marshy burial ground between settlements, the valley was drained in the 7th century BC — traditionally by the Cloaca Maxima under the Tarquin kings — and progressively paved and monumentalised. During the Republic it filled with temples, basilicas (law courts), the Comitium assembly place, and the speaker's platform, the Rostra, decorated with the prows (rostra) of captured warships. Julius Caesar and Augustus rebuilt much of it in marble; later emperors added their own triumphal arches and the great Basilica of Maxentius. From the 7th century AD the area decayed — the last monument, the Column of Phocas, was erected in 608 — and its ruins were quarried for centuries, serving even as cattle pasture ("Campo Vaccino") before systematic excavation began in the 19th century.

Key structures

Surviving or identifiable remains include the Curia Julia (senate house), the Temples of Saturn, Castor and Pollux, Antoninus and Faustina, and the deified Julius Caesar; the House of the Vestals; the twin arches of Titus (c. AD 81), famous for its relief of the sack of Jerusalem, and of Septimius Severus (AD 203); and the Via Sacra, the Sacred Way along which the legions marched in triumph — the soldiers of the Roman legion having carried Roman power as far as Britain in the conquest of Britain.

Today

The Forum is now one of the world's most visited archaeological sites, administered together with the Colosseum and Palatine Hill. Its layered ruins remain the single most important physical testimony to the political and religious life of ancient Rome.
