Latch (electronics)


Illustration: Latch (electronics)

A latch is an electronic circuit that stores one bit of information and has two stable states. Latches are level-sensitive, in contrast to flip-flops, which are edge-triggered; both are fundamental building blocks of digital electronics and the basis of computer memory.

Types

The simplest latch, the SR (set–reset) latch, can be built from two cross-coupled NOR or NAND gates. Adding gating produces the gated SR latch and the D latch, which stores the value present at its data input while its enable signal is asserted and holds it transparently otherwise.

Applications

Latches are used in registers, caches and static RAM cells, and as temporary storage in pipelined processors. Unintended latches inferred in hardware description languages are a common source of design errors in digital synthesis.
