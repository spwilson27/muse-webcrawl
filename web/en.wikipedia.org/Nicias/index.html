Nicias (c. 470–413 BC)

Nicias (Greek: Νικίας; c. 470 BC – 413 BC) was an Athenian politician and general during the period of the Peloponnesian War. He was a member of the Athenian aristocracy and had inherited a large fortune from his father, derived in part from the silver mines of Laurion. Following the death of Pericles in 429 BC, he became the principal rival of Cleon and the leaders of the democratic party in the struggle for political power in Athens.

Career

Nicias was famously cautious, pious, and risk-averse, qualities on which his political reputation rested — and which contrasted with the ambition of his rival Alcibiades. He repeatedly served as strategos and led successful campaigns in the early war. In 421 BC he negotiated the peace that bore his name, the Peace of Nicias, which temporarily halted the war between Athens and Sparta, though it never took full effect.

Sicilian Expedition and death

Despite his own opposition to the plan, Nicias was appointed one of the commanders of Athens' great expedition against Syracuse in Sicily (415–413 BC). His caution, illness, and ill fortune — including his decision to delay the retreat after a lunar eclipse, which he took as an omen — contributed to the total destruction of the Athenian army and fleet. Captured after the final defeat, Nicias was executed by the Syracusans in 413 BC. Thucydides judged him, of all the Greeks of his time, the least deserving of such a fate.
