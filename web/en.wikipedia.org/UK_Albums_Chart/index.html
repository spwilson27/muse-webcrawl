UK Albums Chart

The UK Albums Chart ranks the week's best-selling albums in Britain.

The UK Albums Chart is a record chart ranking the best-selling albums in the United Kingdom. Compiled by the Official Charts Company (OCC) on behalf of the British music industry, it is based on physical sales, downloads and, since 2015, streaming-equivalent album units gathered from retailers and digital services across the country. The full chart lists 100 positions and is published each Friday, with the top 40 historically broadcast in chart shows.

History

The first UK albums chart appeared in the Record Mirror in July 1956, listing a top five headed by Frank Sinatra's Songs for Swingin' Lovers!. Competing charts from publications such as the NME and Melody Maker coexisted until the industry adopted a single authoritative chart; Gallup compiled it through the 1980s and early 1990s before Millward Brown and then the OCC/Kantar lineage took over.

Records

Queen's Greatest Hits (1981) is the best-selling album in UK chart history, the first to pass seven million UK sales. ABBA's Gold: Greatest Hits and The Beatles' Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band follow among the all-time leaders. The longest-running number-one album is the South Pacific soundtrack, which spent 115 weeks at the top in the late 1950s. In the streaming era the methodology converts track streams into album units at standard ratios, periodically revised to weight paid streams more heavily than free ones.
