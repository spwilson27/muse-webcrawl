ADME

ADME is an abbreviation in pharmacokinetics and pharmacology for "absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion", the four processes that describe how the body handles a drug or other xenobiotic compound. Together with toxicity (ADMET), these properties determine a drug's efficacy, dosing, and safety profile.

Absorption

Absorption is the movement of a drug from its site of administration into the bloodstream. It depends on the route (oral, intravenous, inhaled, transdermal, and others), the drug's solubility and formulation, and factors such as gut pH and first-pass metabolism in the liver, which together determine bioavailability.

Distribution

Distribution describes the reversible transfer of the drug between blood and tissues. It is influenced by blood flow, plasma protein binding, and barriers such as the blood–brain barrier; the apparent volume of distribution is a standard summary measure.

Metabolism

Metabolism, chiefly in the liver, chemically transforms drugs into more water-soluble metabolites, typically in Phase I reactions (often by cytochrome P450 enzymes) and Phase II conjugation reactions. Metabolites may be inactive, active, or occasionally toxic.

Excretion

Excretion is the removal of the drug and its metabolites from the body, primarily via urine through the kidneys and via bile into the feces. The rate of elimination determines a drug's half-life and dosing interval.
