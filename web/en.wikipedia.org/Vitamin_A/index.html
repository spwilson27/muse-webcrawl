Vitamin A

Vitamin A is a fat-soluble vitamin — a group of organic compounds including retinol, retinal, retinoic acid, and several provitamin carotenoids such as beta-carotene. It is an essential nutrient for humans, required for vision, immune function, reproduction, and embryonic development.

Function

In the visual cycle, retinal (11-cis-retinal) combines with opsin proteins to form the photopigments of rod and cone cells, such as rhodopsin. Retinoic acid acts as a hormone-like regulator of gene expression in cell differentiation, and the vitamin supports epithelial integrity and T-cell immunity.

Deficiency and excess

Vitamin A deficiency is a leading preventable cause of childhood blindness in developing countries and increases mortality from infections such as measles. Conversely, chronic excessive intake of preformed vitamin A causes hypervitaminosis A, with liver damage and teratogenic effects.
