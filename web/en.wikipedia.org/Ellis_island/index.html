Ellis Island

Ellis Island is a federally owned island in New York Harbor, situated within the U.S. states of New Jersey and New York. From January 1, 1892, to November 12, 1954, it was the busiest immigrant inspection and processing station in the United States. During this period, nearly 12 million immigrants arrived in the country through the port of New York and New Jersey.

History

The island, much of it created by land reclamation, was originally a site for a fort and later a naval magazine before the federal government made it the principal east-coast entry station, replacing Castle Garden in Manhattan. First- and second-class passengers were usually inspected aboard ship, while steerage passengers underwent medical and legal examinations in the island's great registry hall. About 2 percent of arrivals were excluded and returned to their ports of origin.

Today

The main building now houses the Ellis Island National Museum of Immigration, and the island forms part of the Statue of Liberty National Monument, accessible by ferry from Battery Park in Manhattan and Liberty State Park in New Jersey.
