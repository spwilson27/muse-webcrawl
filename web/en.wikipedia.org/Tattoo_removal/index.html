Tattoo removal

Tattoo removal is the process by which tattoos are deliberately faded or removed from the skin. With the surge in popularity of tattoos, there has also been an increase in the number of people seeking to remove them. Reasons for removal include regret, changes in personal taste or lifestyle, employment requirements, and the end of relationships commemorated in the design.

Laser removal

Laser tattoo removal is considered the most effective and safest method. Q-switched lasers, and more recently picosecond lasers, emit short pulses of intense light at wavelengths absorbed by tattoo pigments. The energy shatters ink particles into smaller fragments, which are then cleared by the body's immune system over subsequent weeks. Multiple sessions — commonly six to twelve, spaced several weeks apart — are usually required, and different wavelengths are needed for different ink colors: black responds most readily, while green, blue, and yellow inks are more resistant.

Other methods

Older or alternative methods include dermabrasion, chemical peels with trichloroacetic acid, salabrasion, surgical excision, and cryosurgery. These tend to carry higher risks of scarring and incomplete removal. Cover-up tattooing — placing a new design over the old one — is a non-medical alternative often combined with some laser fading.

Risks and effectiveness

Side effects of treatment can include pain, blistering, infection, temporary or permanent changes in skin pigmentation, and scarring. Complete removal cannot be guaranteed; amateur tattoos with shallow ink may clear fully, while dense professional work, some colors, and cosmetic tattoos containing iron oxides can be stubborn or may paradoxically darken. The Kirby-Desai scale is used to estimate the number of laser sessions a patient will require.
