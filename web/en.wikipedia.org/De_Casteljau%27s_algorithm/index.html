De Casteljau's algorithm

In the mathematical field of numerical analysis, De Casteljau's algorithm is a recursive method to evaluate polynomials in Bernstein form or Bézier curves, named after its inventor Paul de Casteljau. The algorithm can also be used to split a single Bézier curve into two Bézier curves at an arbitrary parameter value.

Although the algorithm is slower for most applications than the direct approach, it is numerically more stable.

Definition

Given a polynomial in Bernstein form of degree n with control points β0, …, βn, the algorithm repeatedly linearly interpolates between successive points. Defining βi(0) = βi, each level of recursion computes:

βi(k)(t) = (1 − t) βi(k−1)(t) + t βi+1(k−1)(t)

After n levels, the single remaining point β0(n)(t) is the value of the polynomial — or the point on the Bézier curve — at parameter t. The intermediate points along the "edges" of this triangular scheme give the control points of the two sub-curves into which the original curve is divided at t, which is how the algorithm performs subdivision.

Applications

De Casteljau developed the algorithm in 1959 while working at the French car manufacturer Citroën, designing smooth curves for car bodies; the work remained confidential company material for years. Independently, Pierre Bézier at Renault developed similar methods that were published and gave Bézier curves their name. De Casteljau's algorithm remains a standard tool in computer-aided geometric design and computer graphics for evaluating and splitting Bézier curves and surfaces, including B-spline generalisations.
