Catford

Catford is a district of south London, England, within the London Borough of Lewisham, about 6 miles (10 km) south-east of Charing Cross. Its name is thought to derive from a ford over the River Ravensbourne where wild cats were seen. Catford grew from a village into a suburb with the arrival of the railway in the mid-19th century.

Landmarks

The district's best-known landmark is the Catford Cat, a large fibreglass black cat perched over the entrance to the Catford Centre shopping precinct. The Broadway Theatre (1932) is an art deco venue, and the area retains substantial Victorian and Edwardian housing, much of it built by speculative developers such as the Corbett estate. Catford is served by two stations, Catford and Catford Bridge.

Regeneration

Catford town centre has been the subject of long-running regeneration plans by Lewisham Council, including redevelopment of the shopping centre and improvements around the stations and the Ravensbourne.
