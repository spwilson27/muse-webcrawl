The occult (from the Latin word occultus, "clandestine, hidden, secret") is a category of esoteric or supernatural beliefs and practices which generally fall outside the scope of organised religion and mainstream science. It encompasses a wide range of traditions including magic, alchemy, astrology, divination, spiritualism and Hermeticism.

History

Occult traditions draw on ancient sources: Hellenistic astrology and Hermetic texts, medieval grimoires and Kabbalah, and Renaissance natural magic as systematised by Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa in his Three Books of Occult Philosophy (1531–33). In the nineteenth century the French writer Éliphas Lévi popularised a synthesis of magic, tarot and Kabbalah, and the founding of the Theosophical Society (1875) by Helena Blavatsky spread occult ideas worldwide. The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn (founded 1888) organised ceremonial magic into an influential graded system; its former member Aleister Crowley went on to found the religion of Thelema.

Practices

Practices described as occult include astrology and horoscope casting, tarot and other forms of divination, ritual or ceremonial magic, alchemy, necromancy and mediumship, numerology, and the use of talismans and sigils. The related notion of "occult sciences" was historically applied to astrology, alchemy and natural magic, which stood alongside the natural sciences before the Scientific Revolution.

Modern occultism

Twentieth- and twenty-first-century occultism includes Wicca and modern pagan witchcraft, chaos magic, ceremonial orders derived from the Golden Dawn, and a large publishing and popular-culture presence. Academic study of Western esotericism has grown since the late twentieth century, treating occult movements as a serious subject of religious and intellectual history, while scientific evaluations regard occult claims of supernatural efficacy as unsupported.
