Preterm birth, also known as premature birth, is the birth of a baby at fewer than 37 weeks of gestational age, as opposed to a full-term birth at about 40 weeks. Categories include late preterm (34 to under 37 weeks), moderately preterm (32 to under 34), very preterm (28 to under 32), and extremely preterm (under 28 weeks). Preterm birth is the leading cause of death in children under five worldwide.

Causes and risk factors

About a third of preterm births follow preterm labor, a third follow premature rupture of membranes, and a third are medically induced (deliveries undertaken for conditions such as preeclampsia or fetal growth restriction). Infection and inflammation are implicated in many spontaneous cases. Other risk factors include a prior preterm birth, carrying twins or more, a short cervix, uterine or placental abnormalities, chronic diseases, smoking and substance use, extremes of maternal age, and low socioeconomic status. In many cases no cause is identified.

Signs and prevention

Warning signs of preterm labor include regular contractions, pelvic pressure, low backache, and changes in vaginal discharge. Preventive measures for high-risk women include progesterone supplementation and cervical cerclage for a short cervix, treatment of infections, and smoking cessation. When preterm delivery threatens, antenatal corticosteroids accelerate fetal lung maturity, and magnesium sulfate given around very preterm birth reduces the risk of cerebral palsy.

Management

Tocolytic medicines may delay delivery briefly, mainly to allow steroids to work or transfer to a specialist center. Preterm infants commonly need respiratory support, help with temperature control and feeding, and monitoring in a neonatal intensive care unit. Kangaroo mother care — sustained skin-to-skin contact — improves outcomes, especially in low-resource settings.

Outcomes

Survival rises steeply with gestational age: most babies born after 28 weeks survive in high-income settings, while survival at 22–24 weeks remains uncertain and carries high risks of disability. Complications include respiratory distress syndrome, bronchopulmonary dysplasia, necrotizing enterocolitis, intraventricular hemorrhage, retinopathy of prematurity, and longer-term neurodevelopmental problems.

Epidemiology

An estimated 13–15 million babies are born preterm each year — roughly one in ten births globally — with the highest rates in sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia. Rates in the United States are around 10%. Complications of preterm birth caused close to a million deaths annually in young children in the mid-2010s.
