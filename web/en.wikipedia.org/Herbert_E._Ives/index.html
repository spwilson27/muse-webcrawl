Herbert E. Ives

Herbert Eugene Ives

Born: 21 July 1882, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, US

Died: 13 November 1953 (aged 71), Montclair, New Jersey, US

Field: Physics, electrical engineering

Known for: Ives–Stilwell experiment; early television and facsimile transmission

Ives at Bell Telephone Laboratories

Herbert Eugene Ives (21 July 1882 – 13 November 1953) was an American physicist and inventor who spent most of his career at Bell Telephone Laboratories. He led AT&T's research on television and telephotography in the 1920s and 1930s and, with G. R. Stilwell, performed the Ives–Stilwell experiment (1938), a classic confirmation of the transverse Doppler effect predicted by special relativity.

Career

Ives studied at the University of Pennsylvania and Johns Hopkins University, where he took his doctorate in 1908. At Bell Labs he headed work on photo transmission: in 1924 his team transmitted wirephotos of the US presidential conventions, and on 7 April 1927 he presided over the first long-distance television demonstration in the United States, sending images of Secretary of Commerce Herbert Hoover from Washington to New York. He also developed systems for colour television and stereoscopic (3D) displays, and amassed over 100 patents.

Views on relativity

Despite producing one of the key experimental confirmations of relativistic time dilation, Ives rejected Einstein's theory of relativity on philosophical grounds, favouring a Lorentzian ether interpretation, and published papers critical of relativity into the 1950s — a stance generally regarded as a paradox of his career. He received the Rumford Prize and other honours, and served as president of the Optical Society of America.
