Chryselephantine sculpture

Chryselephantine sculpture is sculpture made of gold (chrysos) and ivory (elephas) over a wooden core, a prestigious technique of ancient Greek art.

Famous examples

The two most celebrated works were by Phidias: the Statue of Zeus at Olympia, one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, and the Athena Parthenos in the Parthenon at Athens, whose ivory face and hands and gold drapery used over a ton of gold. Neither survives, though small Roman copies and descriptions remain.

Technique

Ivory plates formed the flesh, gold sheets the clothing and armour, built around a supporting framework. The term is also applied to later luxury statuettes combining ivory and bronze or gold.
