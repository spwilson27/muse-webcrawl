Easter Island (Spanish: Isla de Pascua; Rapa Nui: Rapa Nui) is an island and special territory of Chile in the southeastern Pacific Ocean, at the southeasternmost point of the Polynesian Triangle in Oceania. The island is most famous for its nearly 1,000 extant monumental statues, called moai, which were created by the early Rapa Nui people. In 1995, UNESCO named Easter Island a World Heritage Site.

Easter Island is one of the most remote inhabited islands in the world, lying 3,512 km (2,182 mi) west of continental Chile and 2,075 km (1,289 mi) east of the Pitcairn Islands.

History

Polynesian voyagers settled Easter Island, most likely between AD 700 and 1200. Oral tradition describes the first chief, Hotu Matu'a, arriving with two great canoes. The settlers built a complex society that quarried the moai from the volcanic tuff of Rano Raraku and transported them — perhaps by "walking" them upright with ropes, as recent experimental work suggests — to ceremonial platforms (ahu) around the coast. Over time the island's palm forest disappeared, society was transformed, and the moai were toppled, a process once popularised as self-inflicted "ecocide", though the extent of population collapse before European contact is debated by scholars.

The island's Dutch namesake comes from Admiral Jacob Roggeveen, the first recorded European visitor, who arrived on Easter Sunday, 5 April 1722. Peruvian slave raids in the 1860s devastated the population, and in 1888 Chile annexed the island. Most land was leased as a sheep ranch for decades; the Rapa Nui received Chilean citizenship in 1966. Rapa Nui National Park, created in 1935, protects the archaeological sites, and since 2017 the island's marine protected area and local governance have strengthened indigenous control.

Moai and culture

The moai are monolithic human figures with oversized heads, carved mostly in the 13th–16th centuries; the largest erected was about 10 metres tall and weighed some 82 tonnes, and an unfinished giant in the quarry exceeds 21 metres. Many were fitted with coral eyes and topped with red scoria cylinders (pukao). The island is also known for the birdman cult (tangata manu) centred on Orongo village, and for rongorongo, wooden tablets bearing a still-undeciphered script — the only known indigenous writing system of Oceania before the 20th century.
