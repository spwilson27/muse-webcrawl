Ho Chi Minh

Hồ Chí Minh (19 May 1890 – 2 September 1969), born Nguyễn Sinh Cung and also known as Nguyễn Ái Quốc, was a Vietnamese revolutionary and statesman. He founded the Việt Minh independence movement, proclaimed the Democratic Republic of Vietnam in 1945, and served as its prime minister (1945–1955) and president (1945–1969), leading North Vietnam through the First Indochina War and much of the Vietnam War. Saigon, the former capital of South Vietnam, was renamed Ho Chi Minh City in his honor.

Early life and travels

Born in Nghệ An province, the son of an imperial court official dismissed for anti-colonial sympathies, Nguyễn Sinh Cung left Vietnam in 1911 aboard a French steamer, working as a cook's assistant and traveling to the United States, Britain, and France. In Paris as Nguyễn Ái Quốc ("Nguyễn the Patriot") he joined the French Socialist Party, co-founded the French Communist Party in 1920 after reading Lenin's theses on the colonial question, and petitioned the Versailles Conference for Vietnamese self-determination. He later worked for the Comintern in Moscow, Guangzhou, and Siam, training Vietnamese cadres.

Founding the revolution

In 1941 he returned to Vietnam and established the Việt Minh front at Pác Bó, adopting the name Hồ Chí Minh ("He Who Enlightens"). Exploiting Japan's surrender, the Việt Minh seized Hanoi in the August Revolution, and on 2 September 1945 Hồ proclaimed independence in Ba Đình Square, quoting the American Declaration of Independence. War with France followed; the Việt Minh victory at Điện Biên Phủ (1954) led to the Geneva Accords and partition at the 17th parallel, with Hồ's government in the North.

Presidency and legacy

Hồ's North Vietnam pursued land reform (marred by excesses he publicly acknowledged in 1956) and socialist construction while supporting the southern insurgency, turning the conflict into the Vietnam War against the United States. His political thought, "Ho Chi Minh Thought," is enshrined alongside Marxism–Leninism as the ideology of the Vietnamese Communist Party. He died in 1969, six years before reunification; his embalmed body rests in the mausoleum in Hanoi, and his image appears on Vietnamese currency.
