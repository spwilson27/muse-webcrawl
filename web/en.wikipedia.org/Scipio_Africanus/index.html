Publius Cornelius Scipio Africanus (236–183 BC) was a Roman general and statesman, best known as the commander who defeated Hannibal at the Battle of Zama in 202 BC, ending the Second Punic War and earning the agnomen Africanus. He is considered one of the greatest military commanders in history.

Early career

Scipio survived the catastrophic defeats of Ticinus, Trebia and Cannae, where he served as a young officer. After his father and uncle were killed in Spain, he was given command there in 210 BC — despite being in his mid-twenties — captured New Carthage, and by victory at Ilipa (206 BC) expelled the Carthaginians from Iberia.

Zama and later life

Elected consul for 205 BC, Scipio persuaded the Senate to let him invade Africa, forcing Hannibal's recall from Italy. At Zama he neutralised Hannibal's elephants and, with Masinissa's Numidian cavalry, won the decisive victory that made Rome master of the western Mediterranean. He later served against the Seleucid king Antiochus III in Greece and Asia, but political attacks by his enemies, including Cato the Elder, drove him into retirement at Liternum, where he died in 183 BC — the same year, legend says, as Hannibal.
