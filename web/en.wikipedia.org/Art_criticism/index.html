Art criticism

Art criticism is the discussion or evaluation of visual art. Art critics usually criticise art in the context of aesthetics or the theory of beauty. A goal of art criticism is the pursuit of a rational basis for art appreciation, but it is questionable whether such criticism can transcend prevailing socio-political circumstances.

History

Criticism of art emerged as a distinct practice in the eighteenth century, with writers such as Denis Diderot reviewing the Paris Salons. In the nineteenth century critics such as John Ruskin and Charles Baudelaire shaped public taste, and in the twentieth century Clement Greenberg's formalist defence of abstract expressionism and modernism proved highly influential. Later critics, drawing on feminism, poststructuralism, and postcolonial theory, challenged the canon and the critic's authority.

Methods

Approaches to criticism include description, formal analysis, interpretation, and judgement. Critics write for newspapers, magazines, journals, and exhibition catalogues, and their reviews can significantly affect artists' reputations and markets — though the critic's power has been debated since the rise of curators and the contemporary art market.
