GIC (Singapore)

GIC Private Limited, formerly the Government of Singapore Investment Corporation, is a sovereign wealth fund established by the Government of Singapore in 1981 to manage the country's foreign reserves. GIC invests internationally across equities, fixed income, real estate, private equity, and infrastructure, with the aim of preserving and enhancing the international purchasing power of the reserves over the long term.

History and mandate

Before 1981, Singapore's reserves were managed conservatively by the Monetary Authority of Singapore. Then–Deputy Prime Minister Goh Keng Swee drove the creation of a dedicated investment company that could take a longer view and invest in a broader range of assets abroad. GIC's mandate, set by the President and Ministry of Finance, is to achieve good long-term returns above global inflation within acceptable risk parameters. Its rolling 20-year real rate of return is its headline performance measure.

Operations

GIC is one of the world's largest sovereign wealth funds, with assets under management widely estimated in the hundreds of billions of US dollars (the exact figure is not officially disclosed, though GIC states it manages well over US$100 billion). It maintains offices in major financial centres including New York, London, San Francisco, São Paulo, Beijing, Tokyo, Seoul, and Mumbai. Notable investments have included stakes in UBS and Citigroup during the 2008 financial crisis, and large global real-estate portfolios. Alongside Temasek Holdings, which owns and manages assets on a commercial basis, GIC forms the investment arm of Singapore's reserve management framework, overseen in part by the elected President's "second key" over reserves.

Governance

GIC is wholly owned by the Government of Singapore. Its board has included former prime ministers — Lee Kuan Yew served as chairman, and Lee Hsien Loong became chairman in 2024 after stepping down as Prime Minister. GIC publishes an annual report disclosing its annualised 5-, 10- and 20-year nominal and real returns, portfolio distribution, and governance arrangements, though not individual holdings.
