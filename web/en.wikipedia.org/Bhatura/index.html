Bhatura

Bhatura (also spelled bhatoora) is a deep-fried leavened bread from the Indian subcontinent, originating in Punjabi cuisine. It is made from a dough of refined wheat flour (maida), yoghurt, and a leavening agent such as yeast or baking powder; when fried in hot oil, the rolled-out discs puff up into soft, balloon-like breads.

Serving

Bhatura is most famously paired with spicy chickpea curry as chole bhature, a popular breakfast and street food across North India, especially in Delhi and Punjab. The dish is typically served with pickled onions, green chillies, mango or lime pickle, and sometimes a glass of lassi. A smaller, unleavened cousin, the puri, is fried in a similar way but does not use leavened dough.

Variations

Stuffed versions exist, such as paneer bhatura, in which the dough is filled with crumbled cottage cheese before frying. In Delhi, the closely related bedmi puri–aloo combination is a rival breakfast staple.
