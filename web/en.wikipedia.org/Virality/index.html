Virality

Virality
FieldMedia studies, marketing, network science
Related metricsK-factor, shares, reach, engagement rate
PlatformsSocial media, video sharing, messaging apps

Virality is the tendency of content — such as videos, images, articles or memes — to spread rapidly and widely across the internet, typically through social sharing. The term borrows from epidemiology: content is said to "go viral" when each viewer shares it with more than one other person on average, producing exponential growth analogous to an infection with a reproduction number above one.

Mechanisms

Viral spread is driven by social network structure, platform recommendation algorithms, emotional arousal (awe, humour, anger and surprise all promote sharing), social currency, practical value and storytelling. In marketing, the growth rate of a product's user base through invitations is quantified by the K-factor; a K-factor above 1 indicates self-sustaining viral growth.

History

Chain letters and email forwards were early analogues; the concept entered media discourse in the 1990s through "viral marketing". The rise of YouTube, Facebook and Twitter in the 2000s produced archetypal viral phenomena such as the "Star Wars Kid", "Charlie Bit My Finger" and the ALS Ice Bucket Challenge, which raised large sums for charity. Short-video platforms later accelerated cycles of viral trends and sounds.

Effects and criticism

Virality can make unknown creators famous overnight, power crowdfunding and social movements, and give small brands global reach. It is also criticised for rewarding outrage and misinformation, enabling harassment, and being largely unpredictable — most content engineered to go viral does not, a phenomenon sometimes called "manufactured virality" failure. Researchers study virality using cascade models and network diffusion theory.
