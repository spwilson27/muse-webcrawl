M. F. Husain

Maqbool Fida Husain
Born 17 September 1915, Pandharpur, British India
Died 9 June 2011, London, United Kingdom
Known for: painting, film
Awards: Padma Shri (1955), Padma Bhushan (1973), Padma Vibhushan (1991)

Maqbool Fida Husain (17 September 1915 – 9 June 2011), better known as M. F. Husain, was an Indian artist known for his bold, vibrantly coloured narrative paintings in a modified Cubist style. Often called the "Picasso of India", he was among the most celebrated and internationally recognised Indian artists of the 20th century.

Career

Husain began his career painting cinema billboards in Bombay in the 1930s. In 1948 he was invited by F. N. Souza to join the Progressive Artists' Group, which sought to forge a modern Indian art after independence. His breakthrough came with the exhibition of his work in Zürich in 1951, followed by shows in the United States and Europe. Recurring motifs include horses, the goddess Mother Teresa figures, Indian epics such as the Mahabharata and Ramayana, and scenes of Indian rural and urban life.

He also made films, including Through the Eyes of a Painter (1967), which won the Golden Bear at the Berlin International Film Festival, and Gaja Gamini (2000) with Madhuri Dixit.

Controversy and exile

Husain's depictions of Hindu deities in the nude, painted in the 1970s but widely circulated in the 1990s, provoked protests, vandalism of exhibitions, and numerous legal cases. From 2006 he lived in self-imposed exile, principally in Dubai and London, and accepted Qatari citizenship in 2010. He continued painting prolifically until his death in London in 2011.
