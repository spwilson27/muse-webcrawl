Mont de piété

A mont de piété (from Italian monte di pietà, "mount of piety") is a charitable pawnbroking institution that lends money at low interest against pledged valuables, created to protect the poor from usurious moneylenders.

History

The first monti di pietà were founded in 15th-century Italy under Franciscan influence; the Monte di Pietà of Perugia dates to 1462. Despite early controversy over charging any interest, the model spread across Catholic Europe. In France the Paris Mont-de-Piété was established in 1777 and survives today as the Crédit Municipal de Paris.

Operation

Borrowers deposit an item of value and receive a loan for a fraction of its worth. If the loan and modest interest are not repaid within the term, the pledge is sold at auction; surpluses above the debt are returned to the borrower.
