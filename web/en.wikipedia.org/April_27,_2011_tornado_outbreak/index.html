The April 27, 2011 tornado outbreak was the deadliest and most intense day of the 2011 Super Outbreak, the largest tornado outbreak in United States history. On April 27 alone, 216 tornadoes touched down, including four rated EF5 on the Enhanced Fujita scale. The state of Alabama was hardest hit: powerful long-track tornadoes devastated Tuscaloosa, Birmingham suburbs, and communities across the Tennessee Valley, while violent tornadoes also struck Mississippi, Tennessee, Georgia, and Virginia.

Meteorological setup

A powerful spring storm system combined a strong jet stream, abundant Gulf moisture, extreme atmospheric instability, and intense wind shear across the Southeast. The Storm Prediction Center issued a rare high risk outlook, and a particularly dangerous situation (PDS) tornado watch covered much of the region. Supercell thunderstorms developed in successive waves through the day.

Major tornadoes

Notable tornadoes included the Tuscaloosa–Birmingham EF4, the Hackleburg–Phil Campbell EF5 in Alabama, the Philadelphia and Smithville EF5 tornadoes in Mississippi, and the Rainsville EF5 in Alabama. The outbreak sequence from April 25 to 28 killed 324 people and injured thousands, making it one of the deadliest tornado events in US history, and caused an estimated $11 billion in damage.

Aftermath

The disaster prompted reviews of tornado warning communication, safe room construction, and building practices in the Southeast. April 27, 2011 is often compared with the April 3, 1974 Super Outbreak.
