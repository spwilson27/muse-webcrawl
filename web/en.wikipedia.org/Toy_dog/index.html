Toy dog

Toy dog traditionally refers to a very small dog kept as a pet or companion, and in modern usage it denotes the toy group recognized by kennel clubs: the smallest breeds, bred down in size primarily for companionship rather than work. Toy breeds typically weigh from under 1 to about 7 kilograms (roughly 2 to 15 pounds) as adults.

History

Miniature companion dogs have been kept for millennia; small spaniel- and spitz-type lap dogs appear in Roman, Chinese, and pre-Columbian contexts, and tiny dogs were prized at royal courts in Europe and Asia as status symbols and comforters. Many toy breeds were created in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries by selectively breeding down larger sporting, terrier, and working breeds.

Breeds

Breeds commonly classed in the toy group include the Chihuahua (the world's smallest recognized breed), Pomeranian, Yorkshire Terrier, Maltese, Papillon, Shih Tzu, Pekingese, Cavalier King Charles Spaniel, Toy Poodle, Pug, Italian Greyhound, Japanese Chin, and Affenpinscher.

Care and health

Toy dogs are popular for apartment living and require relatively little exercise, though they remain active and trainable. Their small size brings characteristic concerns: fragility and injury from falls or rough handling, dental crowding, patellar luxation, hypoglycemia in tiny puppies, and in some breeds breathing or eye problems. Their long lifespans — frequently 14 years or more — are among the longest of any dogs.
