Buttocks

The buttocks are two rounded portions of the anatomy at the posterior of the pelvic region, comprising the gluteal muscles — principally the gluteus maximus — covered by skin and a layer of fat. In humans they enable an upright posture while sitting without resting weight on the feet.

Anatomy

The gluteus maximus, one of the largest muscles in the body, extends the hip and is essential to walking, running and climbing; beneath it lie the gluteus medius and minimus, which stabilise the pelvis. The buttocks are separated by the intergluteal cleft and bounded above by the iliac crests.

Evolution and culture

Prominent buttocks are a distinctive human trait linked to bipedal locomotion. They have carried social and aesthetic significance in many cultures, variously celebrated in art, fashion and dance, and in other contexts subject to norms of modesty.
