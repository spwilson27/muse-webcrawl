Semisimple Lie algebra

In mathematics, a semisimple Lie algebra is a Lie algebra that is a direct sum of simple Lie algebras, where a simple Lie algebra is a non-abelian Lie algebra whose only ideals are itself and the zero ideal. Equivalently, a finite-dimensional Lie algebra over a field of characteristic zero is semisimple if and only if its radical — its largest solvable ideal — is zero.

Characterisations

Élie Cartan's criterion states that a Lie algebra (in characteristic zero) is semisimple if and only if its Killing form is non-degenerate. Another characterisation comes from Weyl's theorem on complete reducibility: a Lie algebra is semisimple if and only if every finite-dimensional representation of it is completely reducible, i.e. a direct sum of irreducible representations. The Levi decomposition writes any Lie algebra as a semidirect sum of a semisimple part and its solvable radical.

Classification

The simple Lie algebras over the complex numbers were classified by Wilhelm Killing and Élie Cartan in the late 19th century. There are four infinite families — An (special linear algebras sln+1), Bn (odd orthogonal algebras so2n+1), Cn (symplectic algebras sp2n), and Dn (even orthogonal algebras so2n) — and five exceptional algebras: G2, F4, E6, E7, and E8. The classification is encoded combinatorially by root systems and Dynkin diagrams.

Structure theory

The structure of a semisimple Lie algebra is analysed by choosing a Cartan subalgebra (a maximal abelian subalgebra of semisimple elements). The algebra decomposes into the Cartan subalgebra plus one-dimensional root spaces, indexed by the roots, which form a root system in the dual space. Choosing simple roots yields a Cartan matrix and a Dynkin diagram that determine the algebra up to isomorphism. The representation theory is governed by highest weights: finite-dimensional irreducible representations are classified by dominant integral weights, with dimensions given by Weyl's dimension formula and characters by the Weyl character formula.
