Pilote was a French comics magazine founded in 1959 that became the flagship of Franco-Belgian comics. Its first issue, published on 29 October 1959, launched Asterix by René Goscinny and Albert Uderzo, which debuted with the serialisation of Asterix the Gaul.

History

The magazine was created by writers and artists including Goscinny, Uderzo, Jean-Michel Charlier and Jean Hébrard, with backing from Radio-Luxembourg. Besides Asterix, Pilote serialised such series as Charlier and Giraud's Blueberry, Goscinny and Morris's Lucky Luke (from 1968), Greg's Achille Talon and later work by Gotlib and Claire Bretécher. In the 1970s it shifted toward an older readership before ceasing regular publication in 1989.

Legacy

Pilote is credited with raising the artistic and literary standing of comics in France and nurturing a generation of creators who reshaped the medium.
