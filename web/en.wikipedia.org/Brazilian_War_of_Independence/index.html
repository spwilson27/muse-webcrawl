Brazilian War of Independence

The Brazilian War of Independence (1822–1825) was a series of political and military events that consolidated Brazil's separation from the United Kingdom of Portugal, Brazil and the Algarves. Although independence was proclaimed by Prince Regent Dom Pedro on 7 September 1822, Portuguese garrisons in several provinces resisted, and fighting continued until 1824.

Background

The Portuguese court had transferred to Rio de Janeiro in 1808 fleeing Napoleon's invasion. When King João VI returned to Lisbon in 1821, the Portuguese Cortes sought to reduce Brazil to its former colonial status, prompting Pedro to remain in Brazil and defy orders to return.

The war

Armed resistance centred on Bahia, Maranhão, Pará, and the Cisplatine Province. Key episodes included the Battle of Pirajá, the naval campaigns of Lord Cochrane, and the siege of Salvador, which fell to Brazilian forces on 2 July 1823. Portuguese troops evacuated Montevideo in 1824.

Outcome

Portugal formally recognized Brazilian independence by the Treaty of Rio de Janeiro in 1825, in exchange for compensation of two million pounds sterling. The war preserved Brazil's territorial unity under the Empire of Brazil, in contrast to the fragmentation of Spanish America.
