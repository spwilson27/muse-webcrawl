Fireworks are a class of low explosive pyrotechnic devices used for aesthetic and entertainment purposes. They are most commonly used in firework displays (also called a fireworks show or pyrotechnics), combining a large number of devices in an outdoor setting. Such displays are the focal point of many cultural and religious celebrations.

Fireworks take many forms to produce different effects: peony (spherical bursts of coloured stars), willow (long trailing stars), chrysanthemum (bursts with visible trails), roman candles, rockets, fountains, sparklers, and firecrackers. Colours are produced by incandescence and by the emission spectra of metal salts — strontium for red, barium for green, sodium for yellow, and copper compounds for blue.

History

Fireworks originated in China, where bamboo stalks that exploded when thrown on a fire were followed, after the invention of gunpowder, by true firecrackers and rockets by the Song dynasty. Knowledge of fireworks spread westward along trade routes and through warfare; by the Renaissance, Italian and German pyrotechnists were staging elaborate displays for courts, and the art was codified in works such as Vannoccio Biringuccio's De la pirotechnia (1540). Famous historic displays include the 1749 London display for which Handel composed Music for the Royal Fireworks.

Composition and construction

An aerial shell consists of a casing containing a bursting charge of black powder, small pellets called stars (which produce the coloured points of light), a time fuse, and a lifting charge fired from a mortar. Consumer fireworks are distinguished from professional display fireworks by size and explosive content, and are regulated by hazard classes in most countries. Pyrotechnic compositions combine an oxidiser (often potassium nitrate or perchlorate), a fuel, colour agents, and a binder such as dextrin.

Safety and regulation

Fireworks cause thousands of injuries annually, mostly burns and eye injuries, and can start fires and distress people and animals with their noise. Regulation varies widely: some jurisdictions ban consumer fireworks entirely, others license sales around particular festivals. Major accidents at fireworks factories and storage sites — such as Enschede (2000) and Tianjin (2015) — have prompted stricter storage rules. Environmental concerns include perchlorate contamination of water and air pollution spikes during festivals such as Diwali.

Cultural significance

Fireworks mark celebrations worldwide: Lunar New Year and Mid-Autumn Festival in China, Diwali in India, Guy Fawkes Night in the United Kingdom, Independence Day in the United States, New Year's Eve globally, and Bastille Day in France. Japan's summer hanabi festivals draw enormous crowds, and international competitions such as the Montreal International Fireworks Competition showcase national pyrotechnic teams.
