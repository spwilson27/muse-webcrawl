Caucasus Campaign

The Caucasus campaign was a series of military engagements between the Russian Empire and the Ottoman Empire during the First World War, fought in the Southern Caucasus and Eastern Anatolia from 1914 to 1918.

Course of the campaign

Hostilities opened with the Russian Bergmann Offensive in November 1914, which was checked by Ottoman forces. The Ottoman Third Army under Enver Pasha then launched a disastrous winter offensive at the Battle of Sarikamish (December 1914 – January 1915), in which tens of thousands of Ottoman soldiers froze to death in the Allahüekber Mountains.

Russian forces under General Nikolai Yudenich counterattacked, winning at Kara Killisse and Malazgirt (1915), capturing Erzurum in a celebrated winter assault in February 1916, and taking Trabzon and Erzincan later that year. The fighting took place amid the Armenian genocide, in which the Ottoman government deported and killed much of the region's Armenian population.

Collapse

The Russian revolutions of 1917 dissolved the Russian Caucasus Army. The Armistice of Erzincan (December 1917) ended hostilities, and the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk ceded territory to the Ottomans. Ottoman forces advanced into the South Caucasus in 1918 before the Armistice of Mudros ended the war.
