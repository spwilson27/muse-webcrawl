The Oculus Rift is a virtual reality headset developed by Oculus VR, a division of Meta Platforms (formerly Facebook). First released on 28 March 2016, it is widely credited with launching the modern consumer VR era. The Rift required a connection to a personal computer and used external infrared sensors for positional ("Constellation") tracking.

History

The Rift began as a Kickstarter project in August 2012 by Palmer Luckey, whose prototype combined a wide field of view with low-latency head tracking. The campaign raised about $2.4 million. Developer kits DK1 (2013) and DK2 (2014) followed. In March 2014 Facebook acquired Oculus VR for approximately $2 billion. The consumer version, CV1, shipped in March 2016 at a price of US$599.

Hardware

The CV1 used two OLED panels with a combined resolution of 2160×1200 refreshing at 90 Hz, integrated headphones, and three-axis rotational plus positional tracking. The Oculus Touch motion controllers, released in December 2016, added hand presence. The later Rift S (2019), co-developed with Lenovo, switched to inside-out "Insight" tracking and a single LCD panel.

Software and content

The Rift ran software from the Oculus Store and was compatible with SteamVR. Notable titles included Lucky's Tale, Robo Recall, Beat Saber and Lone Echo. The platform popularised room-scale VR in the home.

Legacy

Meta discontinued the Rift S in 2021, ending the Rift line in favour of the standalone Meta Quest headsets. The Rift's success revived industry and public interest in virtual reality after earlier failures in the 1990s.
