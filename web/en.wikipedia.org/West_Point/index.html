West Point

West Point
Location: Orange County, New York, USA
Site: West bank of the Hudson River
Established: 1778 (fortress); 1802 (academy)
Institution: United States Military Academy

West Point is a United States Army post and census-designated place on the west bank of the Hudson River in Orange County, New York, best known as the home of the United States Military Academy (USMA), often itself called "West Point".

History

The site's strategic value lies in its commanding position above a sharp bend in the Hudson. Fortifications were established in 1778 during the American Revolutionary War, and the Great Chain was stretched across the river to block British shipping. Benedict Arnold's plot to surrender the fortress to the British in 1780 remains the most famous episode of its early history. In 1802 President Thomas Jefferson signed legislation founding the Military Academy there.

The Academy

The academy educates cadets for commissioning as officers in the U.S. Army, offering a four-year undergraduate programme with an emphasis on engineering, leadership and military training. Its graduates, the "Long Gray Line", include Ulysses S. Grant, Robert E. Lee, Dwight D. Eisenhower and Douglas MacArthur. Much of the campus is a National Historic Landmark, noted for its Gothic Revival architecture.
