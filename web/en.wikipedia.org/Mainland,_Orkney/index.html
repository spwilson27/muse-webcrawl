Mainland, Orkney

Mainland
Largest island of Orkney, Scotland
Area: about 523 km²
Highest point: Mid Hill, 271 m
Main settlements: Kirkwall, Stromness

The Mainland is the largest island in the Orkney archipelago of Scotland. It contains the islands' two burghs, Kirkwall (the administrative centre) and Stromness, and much of Orkney's population and farmland.

Geography

The island is sometimes divided informally into the East and West Mainland, joined by a narrow neck at Kirkwall. Much of the land is fertile lowland used for grazing and crops, with heather moorland in the west. Lochs of Stenness and Harray lie in the west.

History and archaeology

Mainland has an exceptional concentration of Neolithic sites, including Skara Brae, Maeshowe, the Ring of Brodgar and the Stones of Stenness — together designated the Heart of Neolithic Orkney UNESCO World Heritage Site. Norse settlement from the 9th century left a strong Scandinavian legacy in place-names and culture.

Transport

Kirkwall Airport provides air links, and ferries connect the island with mainland Scotland and the other Orkney islands. Causeways known as the Churchill Barriers link the East Mainland to islands to the south.
