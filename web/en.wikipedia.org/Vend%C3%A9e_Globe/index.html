The Vendée Globe is a single-handed, non-stop, unassisted round-the-world yacht race held every four years. Founded by Philippe Jeantot, the first edition started in 1989. The race begins and ends at Les Sables-d'Olonne in the Vendée department of France, and is widely regarded as the ultimate test of solo ocean racing; it is sometimes called the "Everest of the seas".

Course

Competitors sail down the Atlantic, around the Cape of Good Hope, Cape Leeuwin, and Cape Horn, leaving Antarctica to starboard, before returning up the Atlantic. The nominal course distance is about 24,300 nautical miles, though sailed distances are longer. Skippers must complete the circumnavigation entirely alone and without stopping or receiving physical assistance.

History

Titouan Lamazou won the inaugural 1989–90 race in 109 days. French sailors have dominated the event. François Gabart set a record of 78 days in 2012–13, Armel Le Cléac'h improved it to 74 days in 2016–17, and Charlie Dalin completed the 2024–25 edition in just over 64 days. Ellen MacArthur's second place in 2000–01 brought the race a large international audience.

Boats

The race is sailed in IMOCA 60-foot monohulls, many now equipped with hydrofoils that lift the hull partially from the water, enabling speeds above 30 knots. Attrition is high: in some editions fewer than half the starters have finished.
