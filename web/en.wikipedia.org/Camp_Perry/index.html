Camp Perry

Camp Perry is a National Guard training facility located on the shore of Lake Erie in Port Clinton, Ohio. It is best known as the home of the National Rifle and Pistol Matches, the centrepiece of American competitive marksmanship since 1907.

History

The site, named for Commodore Oliver Hazard Perry, victor of the Battle of Lake Erie in 1813, was acquired by the State of Ohio beginning in 1906, after the National Matches outgrew their earlier home at Sea Girt, New Jersey. The first matches at Camp Perry were held in 1907. The camp served as a training base in both world wars and, during the Second World War, also held prisoners of war. Among its landmarks are the distinctive rows of small prisoner-built "huts" — simple cottages still used to house competitors — and the camp's ranges extending toward the lake.

The National Matches

Each summer the Civilian Marksmanship Program (CMP), the National Rifle Association, and the Ohio National Guard conduct the National Matches: several weeks of rifle and pistol championships including the President's Rifle Match, the National Trophy Individual and Team matches, and CMP Games events. Thousands of civilian and military shooters compete. Camp Perry's ranges — Viale, Rodriguez, Petrarca, and Young — are among the largest outdoor shooting facilities in the world.
