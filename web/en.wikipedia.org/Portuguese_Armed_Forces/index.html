The Portuguese Armed Forces (Portuguese: Forças Armadas Portuguesas) are the military of Portugal. They include the General Staff of the Armed Forces, the three service branches — the Portuguese Navy, the Portuguese Army and the Portuguese Air Force — and other paramilitary and joint bodies. The President of the Republic is the Commander-in-Chief.

History

Portugal's military history extends back to the founding of the kingdom in the 12th century. Portuguese forces played a central role in the Age of Discovery, the Peninsular War, the First World War, and the Portuguese Colonial War (1961–1974) in Africa. The Armed Forces Movement (MFA) carried out the Carnation Revolution of 25 April 1974, ending the Estado Novo regime.

Modern role

Portugal is a founding member of NATO, and its forces regularly participate in alliance operations, United Nations and European Union missions, and international peacekeeping, including deployments in Africa, the Balkans, Afghanistan and maritime patrol missions in the Atlantic and Mediterranean. Conscription was abolished in 2004, and the forces are now all-volunteer professionals.

Structure

  • Portuguese Navy (Marinha)
  • Portuguese Army (Exército)
  • Portuguese Air Force (Força Aérea)
  • National Republican Guard (gendarmerie, under the Ministry of Internal Administration in peacetime)
