Sustainable food system

A sustainable food system is a food system that delivers food security and nutrition for all in such a way that the economic, social, and environmental bases to generate food security and nutrition for future generations are not compromised. The concept encompasses the full chain of activities — production, processing, distribution, consumption, and waste management — and their interactions with natural resources, livelihoods, and health.

Dimensions

Sustainability in food systems is usually assessed along three dimensions: environmental (greenhouse-gas emissions, land and water use, biodiversity, soil health), social (equity, labour conditions, cultural acceptability, nutrition and public health), and economic (viability of livelihoods for farmers and other actors). Food systems account for roughly a third of global anthropogenic greenhouse-gas emissions, making their transformation a major theme of climate policy.

Approaches

Proposed pathways include agroecology, reducing food loss and waste, shifting diets toward more plant-rich patterns, improving fertilizer and water efficiency, and strengthening local and regional food networks. International bodies such as the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) and the High Level Panel of Experts on Food Security and Nutrition (HLPE) have developed frameworks for assessing and reforming food systems, and the 2021 UN Food Systems Summit placed the concept at the centre of global food policy debate.
