House of Trastámara

The House of Trastámara (Spanish: Casa de Trastámara) was a dynasty of Iberian origin that reigned in the Crowns of Castile and Aragon, as well as in Sicily, Naples, and Navarre, from the later Middle Ages until the early sixteenth century. The dynasty takes its name from the County of Trastámara in Galicia.

Origins

The line began with Henry of Trastámara, an illegitimate son of Alfonso XI of Castile, who seized the Castilian throne from his half-brother Peter the Cruel after the Castilian Civil War, reigning as Henry II from 1369. A cadet branch came to rule Aragon after the Compromise of Caspe (1412) elected Ferdinand of Antequera.

The Catholic Monarchs

The dynasty's most famous members are Ferdinand II of Aragon and Isabella I of Castile, whose marriage in 1469 united the two crowns dynastically and laid the groundwork for a unified Spain. Their reign saw the completion of the Reconquista with the conquest of Granada (1492) and Columbus's voyage to the Americas. The main line ended with Joanna of Castile; her son Charles I brought the Habsburgs to Spain.
