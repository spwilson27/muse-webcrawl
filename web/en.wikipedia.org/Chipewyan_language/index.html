Chipewyan language

Chipewyan
Native name: Dënesųłiné
Region: Northern Canada (Manitoba, Saskatchewan, Alberta, Northwest Territories)
Family: Athabaskan (Dene)
Status: Official in the Northwest Territories

Chipewyan (Dënesųłiné, 'people of the barrens') is an Athabaskan (Dene) language spoken by the Chipewyan people of northwestern Canada. It is one of the official languages of the Northwest Territories.

Distribution and speakers

Speakers live in communities across northern Manitoba, Saskatchewan and Alberta and in the Northwest Territories, including Fort Smith, Fort Chipewyan and La Loche. The number of speakers is in the thousands, though transmission to children varies by community and the language is considered vulnerable.

Features

Like other Athabaskan languages, Chipewyan is polysynthetic with complex verb morphology and a tonal contrast. It is written in Latin-based orthographies and, historically, in Canadian Aboriginal syllabics in some areas.
