Creatine

Formula: C4H9N3O2
Source: Synthesized in liver and kidneys; found in meat and fish
Role: Rapid ATP regeneration in muscle

Creatine is an organic compound with the formula (H2N)(HN)CN(CH3)CH2CO2H that facilitates recycling of adenosine triphosphate (ATP), the cell's energy currency, primarily in muscle and brain tissue. It is synthesized in the human body from the amino acids arginine, glycine, and methionine, and is also obtained from eating meat and fish.

Biochemistry

About two-thirds of the body's creatine store in skeletal muscle is held as phosphocreatine. During intense activity, the enzyme creatine kinase transfers phosphocreatine's phosphate group to ADP, regenerating ATP faster than oxidative metabolism can. Creatine spontaneously degrades to creatinine, which is excreted by the kidneys and measured in blood tests as an indicator of renal function.

Supplementation

Creatine monohydrate is one of the most widely used and best-studied sports supplements; research supports modest improvements in strength and high-intensity exercise performance, and it is not banned by major sporting bodies. It is generally regarded as safe for healthy adults, though claims and evidence for cognitive benefits are still being investigated, as are therapeutic uses in creatine deficiency syndromes and certain neuromuscular diseases.

History

Michel Eugène Chevreul isolated creatine from meat extract in 1832 and named it from the Greek kreas, flesh. Its role in muscle contraction was clarified in the 1920s with the discovery of phosphocreatine.
