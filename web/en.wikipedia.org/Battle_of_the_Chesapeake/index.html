The Battle of the Chesapeake, also known as the Battle of the Virginia Capes, was a crucial naval battle of the American Revolutionary War, fought on 5 September 1781 between a French fleet under the Comte de Grasse and a British fleet under Rear Admiral Sir Thomas Graves at the mouth of Chesapeake Bay.

De Grasse had sailed from the West Indies with 28 ships of the line and landed 3,000 French troops to reinforce the allied army confronting Lord Cornwallis at Yorktown, Virginia. Graves arrived from New York with 19 ships to relieve Cornwallis. The fleets engaged in a partial, somewhat confused line-ahead action; no ships were sunk on the day, but several British ships were badly damaged, and after days of manoeuvring Graves withdrew to New York, leaving the French in control of the bay — reinforced soon after by the Comte de Barras's squadron carrying the allied siege artillery.

Consequences

Though tactically inconclusive, the battle was strategically decisive: Cornwallis was cut off from relief or evacuation by sea, and after the siege of Yorktown he surrendered his entire army on 19 October 1781. The defeat effectively ended major British operations in America and led to the peace settlement recognising the independence of the United States.
