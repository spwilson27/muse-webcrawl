Mykonos (Greek: Μύκονος) is a Greek island, part of the Cyclades, lying between Tinos, Syros, Paros and Naxos. The island has an area of 85.5 square kilometres (33.0 sq mi) and rises to an elevation of 341 metres (1,119 feet) at its highest point. It spans 10 kilometres (6 miles) from north to south and 15 kilometres (9 miles) from east to west. There are 10,134 inhabitants according to the 2011 census, most of whom live in the largest town, Mykonos, also known as Chora (i.e. the Town in Greek, following the common practice in Greece when the name of the island itself is the same as the name of the principal town).

Mykonos's nickname is "The Island of the Winds", due to the very strong winds which usually blow on the island. Tourism is a major industry and Mykonos is well known for its vibrant nightlife, beaches, and as a gay-friendly destination.

History

Herodotus mentions Carians as the original inhabitants of the island. Ionians from Athens seem to have followed next in the early 11th century BC. The island, in early times, was closely connected with the nearby island of Delos, a major religious centre of the ancient Greek world; Mykonos served as a supply island and place of retreat for pilgrims.

In Greek mythology, Mykonos was named after its first ruler, Mykonos, the son or grandson of the god Apollo. The island is also said to have been the location of the Gigantomachy, the great battle between Zeus and the Giants, and where Heracles killed the invincible giants after luring them from the protection of Mount Olympus.

The island was ruled by the Ottoman Empire until 1830, when it became part of the newly independent Greek state, following a role in the Greek War of Independence; the local heroine Manto Mavrogenous is honoured with a statue in the main square of Chora.

Geography and climate

The island is composed mostly of granite and the terrain is very rocky with many areas eroded by the strong winds. High quality clay and baryte, a mineral used as a lubricant in oil drilling, were mined on the eastern side of Mykonos until the late 1900s. The climate is typically Mediterranean, with strong north winds (the meltemi) in summer.

Tourism

Tourism has dominated the local economy since the 1960s, when the island became a stop for wealthy travellers and artists. Landmarks include the row of 16th-century windmills at Kato Mili, the waterfront district of Little Venice (Alefkandra), and the church of Panagia Paraportiani, an asymmetrical complex of five churches. Popular beaches include Psarou, Paradise, Super Paradise, Elia and Agios Stefanos. The nearby island of Delos, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is reached by boat from Mykonos.

Transport

Mykonos Island National Airport is located 4 kilometres (2.5 mi) southeast of the town of Mykonos. Ferries and high-speed catamarans connect the island's old and new ports with Athens (Piraeus and Rafina) and other Cycladic islands.
