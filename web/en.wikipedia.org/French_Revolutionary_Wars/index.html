French Revolutionary Wars

The French Revolutionary Wars (French: Guerres de la Révolution française) were a series of sweeping military conflicts fought between 1792 and 1802, pitting the French Republic against successive coalitions of European monarchies. Characterized by mass conscription, ideological fervour and the rise of Napoleon Bonaparte, they reshaped the map of Europe and led directly into the Napoleonic Wars.

War of the First Coalition (1792–1797)

France declared war on Austria in April 1792. After early defeats, the victory at Valmy saved the Revolution; the levée en masse of 1793 raised armies of unprecedented size. France annexed the Austrian Netherlands and the Rhineland, while Bonaparte's Italian campaign of 1796–97 knocked Austria out by the Treaty of Campo-Formio.

War of the Second Coalition (1798–1802)

Bonaparte's expedition to Egypt (1798) provoked a new coalition of Britain, Austria, Russia and the Ottoman Empire. Returning to seize power in the Coup of 18 Brumaire (1799), Bonaparte defeated Austria at Marengo (1800); the Treaties of Lunéville (1801) and Amiens (1802) briefly ended the wars.

Significance

The wars spread revolutionary legal and administrative reforms across Europe, destroyed the Holy Roman Empire's old order, pioneered total war and citizen armies, and made Bonaparte master of France.
