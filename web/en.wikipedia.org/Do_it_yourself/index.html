Do it yourself (DIY) is the method of building, modifying, or repairing things by oneself without the direct aid of paid professionals or certified experts. Academic research has described DIY as behaviours in which individuals engage raw and semi-raw materials and components to produce, transform, or reconstruct material possessions, including those drawn from the natural environment. DIY ranges from home improvement and car repair to electronics, crafts, and self-publishing.

History

Self-reliant making is as old as craft itself, but the DIY movement in its modern form grew with suburbanisation after the Second World War, the rise of big-box hardware retailers, and mass-market magazines and television programmes such as This Old House (from 1979). The term "do it yourself" had entered common usage by the 1950s in connection with home-improvement projects undertaken to save money or for recreation.

Subcultures

Beyond home improvement, "DIY" names an ethic in punk rock — self-released records, zines, and all-ages shows organised outside the commercial music industry — and in the maker movement, hackerspaces, and open-source hardware communities. Self-publishing, independent video games, and home recording all draw on the same ethos of bypassing gatekeepers. Scholars have connected DIY to critiques of consumerism and to traditions of craft and appropriate technology.

Economics and safety

DIY can reduce costs but requires tools, skills, and time, and mistakes in areas such as electrical or structural work can be dangerous or illegal without certification. The home-improvement retail market built around DIY is worth hundreds of billions of dollars annually worldwide.
