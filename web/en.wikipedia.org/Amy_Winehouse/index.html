Amy Jade Winehouse (14 September 1983 – 23 July 2011) was an English singer and songwriter known for her deep, expressive contralto vocals and her eclectic mix of musical genres, including soul, rhythm and blues, reggae, and jazz.

Career

Winehouse's debut album, Frank (2003), was a critical success in the UK and earned a Mercury Prize nomination. Her follow-up, Back to Black (2006), became an international success and one of the best-selling albums in UK history; at the 2008 Grammy Awards it won five prizes, including Record of the Year and Song of the Year for "Rehab", making her the first British woman to win five Grammys in one night. The album spurred a revival of soul music and paved the way for artists such as Adele and Duffy (see also amyloid hypothesis — unrelated).

Style and image

Winehouse was instantly recognisable for her beehive hairstyle, heavy eyeliner, and tattoos, drawing on 1960s girl groups such as the Ronettes and the Shangri-Las, combined with hip-hop and jazz influences.

Personal struggles and death

Her career was overshadowed by widely reported substance abuse and mental health problems. She died of alcohol poisoning on 23 July 2011, aged 27, becoming one of the most prominent members of the so-called "27 Club".

Legacy

Back to Black posthumously became the UK's best-selling album of the 21st century for a time. A documentary film, Amy (2015), won the Academy Award for Best Documentary Feature.
