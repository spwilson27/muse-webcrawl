Green Line (Israel)

The Green Line or 1949 Armistice border is the demarcation line set out in the 1949 Armistice Agreements between the armies of Israel and those of its neighbors (Egypt, Lebanon, Jordan, and Syria) after the 1948 Arab–Israeli War. It served as the de facto border of the State of Israel from 1949 until the Six-Day War in 1967. The name comes from the green ink used to draw the line on the map during armistice talks on Rhodes.

History

The armistice agreements explicitly stated that the lines were not final political borders. The line separated Israel from the Jordanian-administered West Bank and the Egyptian-administered Gaza Strip, and divided Jerusalem into Israeli-held West Jerusalem and Jordanian-held East Jerusalem.

Status after 1967

After Israel captured the West Bank, Gaza Strip, Golan Heights, and Sinai Peninsula in 1967, the Green Line lost its function as a border, though it remains the internationally referenced line distinguishing Israel proper from the occupied territories. The Israeli West Bank barrier, built in the 2000s, follows a route that deviates from the Green Line in many places, which has been a subject of legal disputes, including before the Israeli Supreme Court and the International Court of Justice.
