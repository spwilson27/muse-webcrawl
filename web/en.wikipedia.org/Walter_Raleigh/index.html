Sir Walter Raleigh (c. 1552 – 1618) was an English statesman, soldier, writer, and explorer. A favourite of Elizabeth I, he helped establish English colonisation of North America, introduced (by tradition) the potato and tobacco to England and Ireland, and wrote poetry and a monumental History of the World before being executed under James I.

Elizabethan career

After fighting in the French Wars of Religion and in Ireland, Raleigh rose at court in the 1580s, receiving knighthood (1585), monopolies, and offices including Captain of the Guard. In 1584 he obtained a patent to colonise North America; the expeditions he sponsored founded the Roanoke Colony in present-day North Carolina — the "Lost Colony", whose fate remains unknown. He also fought against the Spanish Armada (1588) and led raids on Cádiz (1596) and the Azores (1597).

Fall and Guiana voyages

Raleigh secretly married Elizabeth Throckmorton, one of the Queen's maids of honour, in 1591, provoking a brief imprisonment. In 1595 he sailed to South America seeking the fabled El Dorado, publishing an embellished account, The Discoverie of Guiana. Under James I he was convicted of treason in 1603 and spent thirteen years in the Tower of London, where he wrote his History of the World (1614). Released for a final Guiana expedition in 1616, he was executed in 1618 after his men attacked a Spanish settlement, in breach of the terms of his release.

Legacy

Raleigh is one of the archetypal Elizabethan "sea dogs", and the capital of North Carolina bears his name. Famous anecdotes — spreading his cloak over a puddle for the Queen — are probably legendary, but his poems, including "The Nymph's Reply to the Shepherd", remain anthology pieces.
