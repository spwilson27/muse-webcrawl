Atil


Khazaria at its height, with Atil near the Caspian Sea

Atil, literally meaning "Big River" in Turkic languages, was the capital city of the Khazar Khaganate from the middle of the 8th century until the state's destruction in the late 10th century. It stood in the Volga delta, near the northwestern shore of the Caspian Sea, in what is now southern Russia.

Description

Contemporary Arabic and Persian geographers describe Atil as a great commercial city divided by the Volga River (which was itself called Atil in Turkic). The western part of the city contained the palace of the khagan, built of brick — a privilege reserved for the ruler — while the rest of the population lived in felt tents and clay houses. The eastern part, sometimes called Khazaran, was the commercial quarter, home to merchants, markets, mosques, churches, and synagogues reflecting the city's multi-religious character. Atil was administered by officials of different faiths, and disputes were judged according to the law of each community's own religion.

Trade

Situated at the crossroads of routes linking Northern Europe, the Islamic world, and the steppe, Atil grew rich on transit trade: furs, wax, honey and slaves came down the Volga from the forest peoples of the north, while silk, spices, glassware and silver dirhams arrived from the south and east. The city levied a tithe on goods passing through.

Destruction

Atil was sacked and destroyed by Sviatoslav I of Kiev during his campaign against the Khazars in around 968 or 969. The city never recovered its former importance, and its exact location remains uncertain; archaeological candidates have been proposed near Astrakhan, including the site at Samosdelka on the Volga delta, where some researchers believe remains of the Khazar capital have been found.
