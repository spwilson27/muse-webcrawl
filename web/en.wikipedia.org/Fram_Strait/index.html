Fram Strait

The Fram Strait, the principal gateway between the Arctic Ocean and the Nordic Seas.

The Fram Strait is the passage between Greenland and Svalbard that connects the Arctic Ocean with the Greenland Sea and the wider North Atlantic. It is the only deep-water connection between the Arctic Ocean and the rest of the world's oceans, reaching depths of roughly 2,500 metres at the Molloy Deep, and it is the main route by which sea ice and cold, fresh Arctic surface water leave the Arctic.

The strait is named after the Norwegian ship Fram, used by Fridtjof Nansen on his 1893–1896 expedition, during which the vessel drifted across the Arctic Ocean in the ice and exited through the strait, proving the existence of a transpolar drift current.

Oceanography

Two major current systems pass through the strait in opposite directions. The East Greenland Current flows southward along the Greenland side, carrying cold, low-salinity polar water and enormous quantities of sea ice out of the Arctic; it is the largest single exporter of Arctic sea ice. On the eastern side, the West Spitsbergen Current, the northernmost extension of the North Atlantic Current system, carries relatively warm, salty Atlantic water northward into the Arctic Ocean, where it subducts beneath the fresher surface layer.

Because so much of the Arctic's heat and freshwater exchange passes through this narrow gateway, the Fram Strait is heavily instrumented with moored arrays that monitor volume, heat and freshwater fluxes, and it is a key region for studying the effects of climate change on Arctic sea ice export.
