Quapaw is a town in Ottawa County, Oklahoma, United States. The population was 811 at the 2020 census. Located just south of the Kansas state line, it lies within the historic lead and zinc mining district of the Tri-State area and within the jurisdictional area of the Quapaw Nation, whose headquarters are nearby.

History

The community developed in the late 19th century on Quapaw tribal land; the town was incorporated in 1917, shortly before the Oklahoma side of the Tri-State mining district boomed for lead and zinc during the First World War era. Quapaw lay on the historic Route 66 alignment, and the boom faded as mining declined in the mid-20th century. Extensive mining waste left the wider area, including nearby Picher and the Tar Creek Superfund site, among the most contaminated in the United States; Quapaw itself sits on the margins of the Tar Creek area.

Geography

Quapaw is located in northeastern Oklahoma along U.S. Route 66 and the Spring River drainage, a few miles south of Baxter Springs, Kansas.

Notable people

The Quapaw Nation, a federally recognised tribe of the Quapaw people, takes its name from the same Dhegiha Siouan group historically associated with the lower Arkansas River.
