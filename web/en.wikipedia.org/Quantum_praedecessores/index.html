Quantum praedecessores

Quantum praedecessores was a papal bull issued by Pope Eugene III on 1 December 1145. It called for a new crusade — the Second Crusade — in response to the fall of the County of Edessa to the forces of Imad ad-Din Zengi in December 1144, one of the first Crusader states established after the First Crusade.

Contents and background

The bull was addressed to King Louis VII of France and his subjects. Recounting the successes of the First Crusade ("as much as our predecessors [did]"), Eugene granted the same remission of sins (indulgence) offered by Pope Urban II to the first crusaders, along with legal protections: crusaders' property was placed under the protection of the Church, interest on their debts was suspended, and they were granted immunity from lawsuits while on campaign.

Eugene also sought to persuade rulers to take the cross with promises of spiritual reward and remission of penance. Bernard of Clairvaux was commissioned to preach the crusade, most famously at Vézelay on 31 March 1146, where both Louis VII and Conrad III of Germany eventually took the cross.

Significance

Quantum praedecessores is considered the first papal crusading bull to survive in full and the model for later crusade bulls. Its combination of indulgence, legal privilege and organised preaching defined the institutional framework of crusading for centuries. The Second Crusade it launched (1147–1149) ended in failure before Damascus and generated lasting controversy, not least directed at Bernard himself.
