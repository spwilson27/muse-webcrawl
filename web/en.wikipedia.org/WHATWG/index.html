WHATWG

The Web Hypertext Application Technology Working Group (WHATWG) is a community of browser vendors and other interested parties that develops standards for the web platform, most importantly the HTML Living Standard and the DOM Standard. It was founded in 2004 by individuals from Apple, the Mozilla Foundation, and Opera Software in response to dissatisfaction with the World Wide Web Consortium's (W3C) direction, in particular its focus on XHTML 2.0 and lack of attention to HTML.

The WHATWG maintains the HTML Living Standard used by all major browsers.

History

The WHATWG's early work on "Web Applications 1.0" became the basis of HTML5. In 2007 the W3C adopted the WHATWG's work as the starting point for its own HTML5 effort, and the two organisations cooperated for a time, though their specifications diverged. In 2019 the W3C and WHATWG signed a memorandum of understanding under which the WHATWG maintains the canonical HTML and DOM standards and the W3C publishes review drafts, ending the split.

Unlike the W3C's versioned releases, WHATWG specifications are "living standards" — single documents updated continuously to match implemented behaviour. Its steering group consists of representatives of the major browser vendors: Apple, Google, Microsoft, and Mozilla. Other WHATWG standards include Fetch, URL, Encoding, Streams, and Fullscreen, which together define much of the modern web platform alongside specifications from the W3C and the IETF.
