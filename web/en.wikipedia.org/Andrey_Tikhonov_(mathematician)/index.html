Andrey Tikhonov (mathematician)

Andrey Nikolayevich Tikhonov (30 October 1906 – 7 October 1993) was a Soviet and Russian mathematician and geophysicist known for fundamental work in topology, functional analysis, mathematical physics, and ill-posed problems.

Contributions

In topology, Tikhonov introduced the Tychonoff product topology and proved Tychonoff's theorem (1930) that the product of compact spaces is compact, one of the most important results of general topology; completely regular spaces are accordingly called Tychonoff spaces. He is best known for the theory of regularisation of ill-posed problems — Tikhonov regularisation — a method of finding stable approximate solutions to problems such as inverting geophysical data, by adding a stabilising penalty term. He also worked on singularly perturbed differential equations and founded a major school of computational mathematics at Moscow State University, where he founded the Faculty of Computational Mathematics and Cybernetics in 1970.

Honours

Tikhonov received the Lenin Prize, two USSR State Prizes, and was twice named Hero of Socialist Labour.
