Head writer

A head writer is a person who leads the writing team of a television or radio series, most prominently in daytime soap operas, serial dramas and comedy shows. The head writer is responsible for the show's long-term storyline direction, creates or approves story arcs and episode breakdowns, and oversees the work of staff writers and script editors. In prime-time American television the equivalent creative role is usually held by the showrunner, often an executive producer, while in soap operas the head writer credit remains a distinct and prestigious position.

Responsibilities

Head writers set a series' narrative agenda months in advance, drafting long-term story projections ("bibles" and story outlines) that associate producers and network executives approve. They assign episodes to breakdown writers, who convert story arcs into scene-by-scene outlines, and to dialogue writers, who script individual episodes; the head writer or their deputy then edits the scripts for continuity, tone and character consistency. In soaps, which may produce 250 episodes a year, the head writer manages a layered hierarchy that keeps multiple interweaving plots moving simultaneously.

History

The role emerged with radio serials and early television soaps. Irna Phillips, creator of Guiding Light and As the World Turns, defined the genre's storytelling; Agnes Nixon (All My Children, One Life to Live) and William J. Bell (The Young and the Restless, The Bold and the Beautiful) became celebrated head writers whose tenures lasted decades. In variety and late-night comedy, "head writer" denotes the senior writer supervising monologue and sketch material — a post held at Saturday Night Live by figures including Tina Fey and Seth Meyers.

Recognition

Head writers of soap operas compete in the Daytime Emmy Award for Outstanding Drama Series Writing Team, and the Writers Guild of America negotiates their minimum terms. Long-serving head writers can become synonymous with their shows, and abrupt changes in the role are closely followed by fans because of the visible shifts in story direction that follow.
