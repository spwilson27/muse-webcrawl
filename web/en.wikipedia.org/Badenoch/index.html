Badenoch

Badenoch (Scottish Gaelic: Bàideanach) is a historic district of the Scottish Highlands centred on the upper valley of the River Spey (Strathspey) in the southeast of the Highland council area. Its principal settlements include Kingussie, Newtonmore, Laggan, Dalwhinnie, and Kincraig. Together with neighbouring Strathspey, it forms the Badenoch and Strathspey area, much of which lies within the Cairngorms National Park.

History

In the Middle Ages Badenoch was a lordship held by the Comyn family, whose power in the north made them rivals of the Bruce kingship; their stronghold at Ruthven was later replaced by Ruthven Barracks, built by the government after the Jacobite rising of 1715 and famously held against Jacobite attack in 1745 before falling the following year. The district was historically a stronghold of the Clan Macpherson, whose clan museum is at Newtonmore.

Geography

Badenoch stretches from the headwaters of the Spey near Loch Laggan southwest of the Monadhliath Mountains toward Grantown-on-Spey. The A9 trunk road and the Highland Main Line railway run through the strath, and Loch Insh and the Insh Marshes form an important wetland reserve. The area is popular for walking, skiing at the Cairngorms, and visiting the Highland Folk Museum at Newtonmore.
