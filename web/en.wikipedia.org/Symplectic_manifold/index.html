Symplectic manifold

In mathematics, a symplectic manifold is a smooth manifold M equipped with a differential 2-form ω, called the symplectic form, that is closed (its exterior derivative is zero) and nondegenerate (its pairing with tangent vectors defines an isomorphism between the tangent and cotangent bundles). Symplectic manifolds are the central objects of symplectic geometry.

Consequences of the definition

Nondegeneracy forces M to be even-dimensional, say 2n, and the top power ωn is nowhere vanishing, so every symplectic manifold is orientable and carries a canonical volume form. Darboux's theorem states that near any point there are coordinates (p1, …, pn, q1, …, qn) in which the form takes the constant standard shape, so symplectic geometry has no local invariants analogous to curvature.

Examples

The prototype is the phase space of a mechanical system: for a configuration space Q, the cotangent bundle T*Q has a canonical symplectic form, and Hamilton's equations describe the flow generated by an energy function via this form. Other examples include coadjoint orbits of Lie groups, complex projective spaces with the Fubini–Study form, and every Kähler manifold, whose Kähler form is symplectic. A compact manifold such as a sphere S2n admits a symplectic form only for n = 1, in contrast with the many exotic possibilities in dimension four.
