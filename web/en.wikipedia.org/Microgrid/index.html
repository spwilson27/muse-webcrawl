Microgrid

Microgrid
TypeLocalised electrical grid
ComponentsDistributed generation, storage, loads, controller
ModesGrid-connected and islanded

A microgrid is a local electrical grid with defined electrical boundaries, acting as a single controllable entity with respect to the main power grid. A microgrid can connect and disconnect from the grid, operating either in grid-connected mode or in "island mode", which enables it to function autonomously during outages.

Components

Typical microgrids combine distributed energy resources such as solar photovoltaic panels, small wind turbines, microturbines, fuel cells and combined heat and power units, with battery energy storage, controllable loads, and a supervisory controller that balances generation and demand and manages transitions between modes.

Applications

Microgrids improve resilience for critical facilities such as hospitals, military bases, universities and data centres, keeping them powered when the main grid fails. In remote communities, island nations and off-grid villages, microgrids based on renewables and storage can replace expensive diesel generation. They also help integrate higher shares of intermittent renewable energy at the local level.

Challenges

Challenges include protection and control complexity, stability in low-inertia islanded operation, interoperability standards, cybersecurity, and regulatory frameworks that were designed around centralised utilities. Nevertheless, falling costs of solar and batteries continue to drive rapid deployment worldwide.
