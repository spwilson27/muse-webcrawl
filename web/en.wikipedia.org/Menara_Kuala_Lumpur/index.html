KL Tower

KL Tower, also known as Menara Kuala Lumpur, is a 421-metre (1,381 ft) telecommunications tower in Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia. Completed in 1995, it is the seventh-tallest freestanding tower in the world and, together with the Petronas Twin Towers, a defining feature of the Kuala Lumpur skyline. It is distinct from the much taller skyscraper Merdeka 118.

Construction

Construction began in 1991 atop Bukit Nanas, one of the oldest permanent forest reserves in Malaysia; great care was taken to protect a century-old jelutong tree beside the site, including building a retaining wall around it. The tower was designed by the Malaysian firm Kumpulan Senireka Sdn Bhd. Its design incorporates Islamic motifs, including Muqarnas-style detailing in the lobby and Arabic calligraphy. Construction finished in 1995 and the tower was officially opened on 1 October 1996 by Prime Minister Mahathir Mohamad.

Facilities

The tower head houses an observation deck, a revolving restaurant (Atmosphere 360), event spaces and telecommunications equipment. Later additions include the Sky Deck open-air platform and the glass-floored Sky Box extending from the tower's edge. The tower is used annually for BASE jumping events and hosts a stair-climbing race.
