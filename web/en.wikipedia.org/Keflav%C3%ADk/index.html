Keflavík (Icelandic pronunciation: [ˈcʰɛplaˌviːk], meaning "driftwood bay") is a town in the Reykjanes Peninsula in southwest Iceland. It is part of the municipality of Reykjanesbær, formed in 1994 when Keflavík merged with the neighbouring towns of Njarðvík and Hafnir. Keflavík is best known internationally as the location of Keflavík International Airport, Iceland's main international gateway.

History

Keflavík developed as a fishing and trading post from the 16th century onward. During the Second World War and the Cold War, the United States operated Naval Air Station Keflavik at the airport, a major NATO installation guarding the GIUK gap; the base closed in 2006. The town's economy today rests on the airport, fishing, and tourism associated with the nearby Blue Lagoon and the volcanically active Reykjanes Peninsula.
