Pet

A pet, or companion animal, is an animal kept primarily for a person's company, entertainment or companionship rather than as a working animal, livestock or laboratory subject. The most popular pets are noted for their attractive appearance, intelligence and relatable personalities, and some are taken in on a purely altruistic basis (such as home-found strays) and accepted as pets regardless.

History

Pet keeping long predates industrialisation. The dog was the first domesticated species, accompanying humans for well over 14,000 years; a puppy buried alongside humans at Bonn–Oberkassel in Germany around 14,000 years ago is among the earliest direct evidence of an emotional bond. Cats were domesticated in the Near East roughly 9,000–10,000 years ago, and were buried with humans in Cyprus around 7500 BC. In ancient Egypt, Greece and Rome, animals including dogs, cats, birds and monkeys were kept as companions, and pet-keeping was widespread among Victorian elites, helping to found the modern pet industry and the first animal-welfare organisations.

Popular pets

Worldwide, dogs and cats are the most common pets, followed by freshwater fish, birds (such as budgerigars and canaries), and small mammals including rabbits, hamsters, guinea pigs and gerbils. Reptiles, amphibians and invertebrates such as tarantulas are also kept. Survey data in the United States regularly finds that a majority of households keep at least one pet.

Effects on owners

Pet ownership is associated in many studies with increased physical activity, reduced stress and social benefits, and animal-assisted therapy is used in hospitals, schools and care homes. The evidence for broad health effects is mixed, and pets can also transmit zoonotic diseases, cause allergies and injuries, and impose financial and emotional burdens, including grief at a pet's death.

Welfare and ethics

Keeping pets raises questions about animal welfare: appropriate housing, nutrition, breeding practices (including pedigree-related disorders), abandonment, overpopulation and shelter euthanasia. Many jurisdictions regulate pet ownership, restrict certain species, and require licensing or microchipping. The keeping of exotic animals is controversial because of welfare, safety and conservation concerns, and because released or escaped pets can become invasive species.
