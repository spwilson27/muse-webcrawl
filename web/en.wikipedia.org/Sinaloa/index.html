Sinaloa, officially the Estado Libre y Soberano de Sinaloa (Free and Sovereign State of Sinaloa), is one of the 31 states which, along with Mexico City, compose the Federal Entities of Mexico. It is divided into 18 municipalities, and its capital city is Culiacán Rosales. The state lies in northwestern Mexico, bounded by the Gulf of California to the west and the Sierra Madre Occidental to the east.

Geography

Sinaloa stretches along the eastern shore of the Gulf of California, bordering Sonora to the north, Chihuahua and Durango to the east, and Nayarit to the south. A fertile coastal plain, watered by rivers descending from the Sierra Madre Occidental — including the Fuerte, Sinaloa, and Culiacán rivers — gives way to foothills and high sierra in the east. The principal ports and resorts include Mazatlán, Topolobampo and Altata.

Economy

The state is one of Mexico's most important agricultural producers, often called the country's breadbasket, growing tomatoes, maize, wheat, beans, chickpeas and a wide range of vegetables, much of it for export. Fishing and shrimp farming along the coast, tourism centred on Mazatlán, and food processing are also significant. The state's name is also internationally associated with the Sinaloa Cartel, one of Mexico's most powerful drug trafficking organisations, founded in the state.

History

Before Spanish colonisation the region was inhabited by peoples including the Cahita-speaking groups of the coast. Colonised in the 16th and 17th centuries, Sinaloa formed part of the province of Sonora y Sinaloa until it became a separate state in 1830.
