Ciudad Juárez

Ciudad Juárez
Country: Mexico
State: Chihuahua
Founded: 1659
Population (metro): about 1.5 million

Ciudad Juárez, commonly referred to as Juárez, is the most populous city in the Mexican state of Chihuahua. It lies on the Rio Grande (Río Bravo) opposite El Paso, Texas, in the United States, with which it forms a major binational metropolitan area.

History

The city was founded in 1659 by Spanish Franciscan friars as a mission and settlement called El Paso del Norte, an important crossing point on the Camino Real de Tierra Adentro linking Mexico City with Santa Fe. After the Mexican–American War, the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo (1848) placed the Rio Grande as the international boundary, splitting the settlement; the northern part became El Paso, Texas.

In 1888 the city was renamed in honor of Benito Juárez, the Mexican president who had taken refuge there during the French intervention. During the Mexican Revolution, Juárez was the site of significant battles, including the capture of the city by Francisco I. Madero's forces in 1911.

Economy

Juárez is a major industrial center, known for its maquiladora factories producing automotive parts, electronics, and medical devices for export. Its bridges to El Paso — including the Bridge of the Americas and Paso del Norte — handle large volumes of trade and daily cross-border traffic.

Violence and recovery

The city drew international attention in the 1990s for a long series of murders of women (feminicides), and again in the late 2000s and early 2010s when cartel-related violence made it one of the most violent cities in the world. Homicide rates later fell substantially, though security remains a concern.
