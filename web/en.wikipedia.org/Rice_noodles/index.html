Rice noodles


Dried rice noodles

Rice noodles are noodles made from rice flour and water, originating in southern China and widespread throughout East and Southeast Asian cuisines. Because rice contains no gluten, the noodles rely on the gelatinisation of rice starch for their structure; corn starch or tapioca starch is sometimes added to improve chewiness and clarity. They range in form from very thin vermicelli to broad flat ribbons and are sold fresh or dried.

History

Rice noodles are generally traced to the Qin dynasty period of Chinese history, when northerners accustomed to wheat noodles moved south into rice-growing regions and adapted their noodle-making to rice. From China they spread through trade and migration across Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia, Myanmar and the Philippines, becoming embedded in each region's cooking.

Varieties

  • Mixian and mifen — Chinese rice noodles; mixian, from Yunnan, is the noodle of "crossing-the-bridge noodles".
  • Bánh phở — the flat Vietnamese noodle of phở.
  • Sen lek and sen yai — Thai medium and wide flat rice noodles used in pad thai and pad see ew.
  • Shahe fen (he fen / hor fun) — broad Cantonese rice ribbons, stir-fried in dishes such as beef chow fun.
  • Kway teow — flat rice noodles of Malaysia and Singapore, famous in char kway teow.
  • Rice vermicelli (mai fun, bún, bee hoon) — hair-thin noodles used in soups, salads and spring rolls.
  • Idiyappam — South Indian and Sri Lankan steamed rice-noodle cakes ("string hoppers").

Preparation

Dried rice noodles are usually soaked in warm water until pliable, then briefly blanched or stir-fried; overcooking quickly makes them mushy. Fresh noodles need only brief heating. Rice noodles are served in soups, stir-fries and salads, and are naturally gluten-free.
