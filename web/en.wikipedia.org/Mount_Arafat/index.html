Mount Arafat (Arabic: جَبَل عَرَفَات, romanized: Jabal ʿArafāt) is a small granite hill located about 20 km (12 mi) southeast of Mecca in the plain of Arafat in the Hejaz region of Saudi Arabia. It reaches about 70 m (230 ft) in height and is the place where the Islamic prophet Muhammad is said to have delivered his Farewell Sermon (Khutbat al-Wada') to the Muslims who had accompanied him for the Hajj towards the end of his life.

Religious significance

Muslims believe that Adam and Eve were reunited on the hill after falling to Earth and being separated, and that the hill is the place where they were forgiven, giving it the name Jabal ar-Rahmah, "Mount of Mercy". A pillar on the summit marks the spot.

The plain of Arafat surrounding the hill is the central site of the Hajj pilgrimage. On the ninth day of Dhu al-Hijjah, known as the Day of Arafah, pilgrims travel from Mina to Arafat and remain there from noon until sunset in a ritual called wuquf ("standing"), listening to a sermon and praying. The Hajj is considered invalid if the pilgrim does not spend the afternoon on Arafat.

Geology and geography

The hill is a granodiorite outcrop rising from a flat plain at roughly 300 m above sea level. The surrounding plain has been developed with roads, tents and facilities to accommodate the millions of pilgrims who gather each year, and it is served by the Al Mashaaer Al Mugaddassah Metro line during the Hajj season.
