Irish Free State

The Irish Free State (Irish: Saorstát Éireann) was the state established in December 1922 under the Anglo-Irish Treaty signed in December 1921. It comprised 26 of Ireland's 32 counties; the six north-eastern counties, as Northern Ireland, exercised their right to opt out. The Free State was a dominion of the British Empire with the British monarch as head of state, represented by a Governor-General.

Establishment and civil war

The Treaty split the Irish republican movement. Pro-Treaty forces under Michael Collins and Arthur Griffith formed a provisional government, while anti-Treaty republicans under Éamon de Valera rejected the oath of allegiance to the Crown. The resulting Irish Civil War (1922–1923) ended in victory for the Free State, though at the cost of Collins's life and deep political bitterness.

Government

The Executive Council was headed by W. T. Cosgrave until 1932, when de Valera's Fianna Fáil won power. De Valera proceeded to dismantle the Treaty's constraints: the oath was abolished, the Governor-Generalship was downgraded and then abolished, and the Economic War with Britain followed the withholding of land annuities.

The 1937 constitution

In 1937 the Free State was succeeded by Éire under a new constitution, Bunreacht na hÉireann, which created the office of President and removed nearly all references to the Crown. The Irish Free State thus formally ceased to exist on 29 December 1937.
