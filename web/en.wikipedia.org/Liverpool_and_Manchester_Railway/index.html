The Liverpool and Manchester Railway (L&MR) was the first inter-city railway in the world. It opened on 15 September 1830 between the Lancashire towns of Liverpool and Manchester in England. It was also the first railway to rely exclusively on steam power, to run a scheduled passenger service, and to be double-tracked throughout.

Origins

The railway was built to carry raw cotton from the port of Liverpool to the mills of Manchester and to return finished goods more cheaply and reliably than the canals. A company was formed in 1824; George Stephenson was appointed engineer, and the line received its Act of Parliament in 1826 after an earlier bill had failed.

Construction

The 31-mile line crossed the floating bog of Chat Moss, traversed the Sankey Valley on a nine-arched viaduct, and passed through the Olive Mount cutting and the Wapping Tunnel into Liverpool. In 1829 the company held the Rainhill Trials to select locomotives; the winner was Stephenson's Rocket.

Opening

The opening day was marred by the death of William Huskisson, MP for Liverpool, who was struck by Rocket at Parkside — the first widely reported railway fatality. Despite this, the line was an immediate commercial success and set the pattern for railway development worldwide. In 1845 the L&MR was absorbed into the Grand Junction Railway.
