Central Park Zoo

The Central Park Zoo is a small 6.5-acre (2.6 ha) zoo located in Central Park, in New York City. It is part of an integrated system of four zoos and one aquarium managed by the Wildlife Conservation Society (WCS), all of them accredited by the Association of Zoos and Aquariums (AZA).

History

The zoo began as an informal menagerie in the 1860s, when New Yorkers donated unwanted animals — including bears, monkeys and a sea lion — to the park. In 1864 the state legislature formally authorized a zoological garden, making it one of the oldest zoos in the United States. A formal zoo with cage-houses arranged around the Arsenal building opened in 1934 under Parks Commissioner Robert Moses.

By the 1980s the old barred cages were considered outdated. The zoo closed in 1983 and underwent a five-year, $35 million renovation led by the Central Park Conservancy and WCS. It reopened on August 8, 1988, with naturalistic, zoned habitats replacing the old cages.

Exhibits

The zoo is organized into three climate zones. The Tropic Zone is an indoor rainforest housing tropical birds, bats and reptiles. The Temperate Zone features red pandas, Japanese macaques (snow monkeys) and a sea lion pool at the center of the complex. The Polar Circle houses penguins (chinstrap, gentoo, king and macaroni) and formerly polar bears; its celebrated polar bear Gus, a media sensation in the 1990s, died in 2013. The adjacent Tisch Children's Zoo, reopened in 1997, provides hands-on animal exhibits for young visitors.

The zoo's entrance is marked by the Delacorte Clock, a musical clock installed in 1965 with bronze animal figures that parade and play nursery rhymes on the hour.

In popular culture

The Central Park Zoo is the home of the penguin characters in the animated Madagascar film franchise, and it appears in numerous films and books set in New York City, including the children's picture book Mr. Popper's Penguins associations and J.D. Salinger's The Catcher in the Rye.
