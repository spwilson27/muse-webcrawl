Crown Court
Jurisdiction: England and Wales
Established: 1972 (Courts Act 1971)
Handles: Indictable criminal offences, appeals from magistrates' courts

The Crown Court is the court of first instance for serious criminal cases in England and Wales. It sits in more than 70 locations and is part of the Senior Courts of England and Wales.

Jurisdiction

The Crown Court tries indictable offences — such as murder, rape, and robbery — before a judge and jury, hears appeals from magistrates' courts, and sentences offenders committed to it. Judges range from High Court judges for the gravest cases to circuit judges and recorders.

History

The court was created by the Courts Act 1971, replacing the old assizes and quarter sessions, and began operating in 1972. Its creation followed the Beeching Royal Commission's reforms of the English court system.
