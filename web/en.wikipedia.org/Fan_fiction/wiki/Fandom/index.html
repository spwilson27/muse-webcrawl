Fandom

A fandom is a subculture composed of fans characterized by a feeling of empathy and camaraderie with others who share a common interest. Fans typically are interested in even minor details of the object(s) of their fandom and spend a significant portion of their time and energy involved with their interest, often as a part of a social network with particular practices, differentiating fandom-affiliated people from those with only a casual interest.

History

Organized fandom is often traced to science fiction fandom of the 1930s, whose members published fanzines, founded clubs and held the first fan conventions, developing a culture and vocabulary ("fandom", "con", "filk") later adopted by other fan communities. Media fandom grew around television series such as Star Trek in the 1960s and 1970s, and anime, comics, music and gaming fandoms developed their own traditions. The internet dramatically expanded fandom from the 1990s onward, enabling global communities, online archives and social media fan cultures.

Activities

Fandom activities include attending conventions, cosplay, writing fan fiction, producing fan art and fan videos, collecting memorabilia, role-playing, maintaining wikis and engaging in online discussion. Many fandoms have their own names for members, such as "Trekkies", "Whovians", "Potterheads" and "Bronies".

Fan studies

The academic field of fan studies examines fandom's creativity, social organization and relationship with media producers. Scholars note that fandoms operate largely as gift economies and have been a space in which women and other marginalized groups create and share their own stories.
