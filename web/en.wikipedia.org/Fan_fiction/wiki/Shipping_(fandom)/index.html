Shipping (fandom)

Shipping is the desire by followers of a fandom for two or more people, either real-life people or fictional characters (in film, literature, television series, etc.), to be in a romantic or sexual relationship, and the activity of supporting or discussing such a pairing. It is considered a type of fan labour and fan fiction. A person who engages in shipping is a shipper, and the supported pairing is a ship.

Etymology

"Ship" is short for "relationship", and the verb form emerged in online fandom in the mid-1990s, notably among fans of The X-Files, who distinguished "relationshippers" — who wanted Mulder and Scully together — from "noromos" who did not. The term spread through internet fandoms and eventually into mainstream usage.

Practices

Ships are commonly referred to by portmanteau names blending the characters' names (for example, "Drarry" for Draco Malfoy/Harry Potter), or by notation such as "Character A/Character B". Shippers produce fan fiction, fan art, fan videos and meta essays about their pairings, and discuss evidence for them in the source text. A fan's preferred pairing is often called their OTP ("one true pairing"). Debates between supporters of rival ships are known as "ship wars".

Types

Pairings may be canon (confirmed in the work), near-canon, or entirely non-canonical. Shipping includes same-sex pairings (the basis of slash fiction), pairings of real people in real person fiction, and "crack ships" proposed humorously. Creators sometimes acknowledge or deliberately tease popular ships, a practice that can itself attract controversy, including accusations of queerbaiting.
