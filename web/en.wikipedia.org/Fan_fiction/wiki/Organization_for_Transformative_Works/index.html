The Organization for Transformative Works (OTW) is a nonprofit organization, founded by fans in 2007, that works to preserve and advocate for fanworks and fan culture. Its best-known project is Archive of Our Own (AO3), a nonprofit, open-source archive for fanfiction and other transformative works. The OTW is run by volunteers, many of them fans themselves, and is funded by donations.

Projects

  • Archive of Our Own (AO3) — an archive hosting millions of fanworks, launched in open beta in 2009;
  • Fanlore — a wiki documenting fandom history and culture;
  • Transformative Works and Cultures — a peer-reviewed academic journal of fan studies;
  • Open Doors — a project that rescues at-risk fan archives by importing them into AO3;
  • Legal advocacy — defending the position that fanworks are fair use, including testimony and exemption petitions regarding anti-circumvention rules.

History

The OTW was founded in 2007 amid fan concern over the commercialization of fandom, including the launch of the for-profit site FanLib, and over deletions of fan content on commercial platforms. Fans — including academics, authors and archivists — organized a nonprofit controlled by fans to host fanworks on fan-owned infrastructure. AO3 won the Hugo Award for Best Related Work in 2019.
