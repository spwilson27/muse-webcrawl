Mary Sue

A Mary Sue is a character archetype in fiction, usually a young woman, who is often portrayed as inexplicably competent across all domains, gifted with unique traits or possessions, and surrounded by characters who admire her. The term originates in fan fiction, where it described an original character inserted into a fictional universe who is unrealistically flawless and beloved, often understood as an idealized self-insert of the author. The male counterpart is sometimes called a Gary Stu or Marty Stu.

Origin

The name comes from "A Trekkie's Tale", a 1973 parody fan fiction story by Paula Smith published in the fanzine Menagerie. Its heroine, Lieutenant Mary Sue, aged fifteen and a half, was the youngest lieutenant in Starfleet history, won multiple awards, and saved the Enterprise crew, satirizing the idealized self-insert characters common in Star Trek fan fiction of the period.

Characteristics

Traits commonly attributed to a Mary Sue include exceptional beauty, unusual features such as exotic eyes or hair, multiple extraordinary talents, a tragic backstory, instant mastery of skills, and the admiration or romantic interest of major canonical characters, along with a lack of meaningful flaws or consequences. Online "litmus tests" have been created to score characters against these traits.

Criticism of the term

Writers and critics have argued that the term is applied inconsistently and often in a sexist way: competent female characters in original fiction are labelled Mary Sues far more readily than comparable male characters, which are treated as conventional heroes. The label is also criticized for discouraging young, often female, writers from writing wish-fulfilment fiction. Defenders respond that the concept still usefully identifies shallow characterization and lack of narrative tension.
