Slash fiction

Slash fiction (also known as "m/m slash" or slashfic) is a genre of fan fiction that focuses on romantic or sexual relationships between fictional characters of the same sex. While the term was originally restricted to stories in which otherwise presumably heterosexual male characters engage in homosexual activity, it is now used more generally for any same-sex pairing. The equivalent genre focusing on female characters is sometimes called femslash.

History

The genre began in Star Trek fandom in the 1970s with Kirk/Spock stories, published in fanzines; the name "slash" derives from the "/" used in pairing notation such as "K/S". Because of the controversial nature of the material, early slash circulated discreetly, often with age statements required for purchase. Slash spread to other media fandoms — Starsky & Hutch, The Professionals, later The X-Files, Harry Potter and anime fandoms — and flourished online from the 1990s.

Characteristics and analysis

Slash is written and read predominantly by women, a fact that has drawn extensive scholarly attention. Analyses suggest that slash allows exploration of romantic and erotic relationships between emotional equals, freed from conventional gender roles, and offers writers a space to examine intimacy, consent and identity. Critics debate whether slash is subversive or reinforces stereotypes, and how it relates to LGBTQ representation; related but distinct traditions include Japanese yaoi and boys' love.

Terminology

Pairings are written with a slash between names; works may be gen (no romance), het, slash or femslash. Ratings and warnings about content are customary. Slash about real people overlaps with real person fiction and raises additional ethical questions.
