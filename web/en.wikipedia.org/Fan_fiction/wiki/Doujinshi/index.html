Doujinshi

Doujinshi (同人誌), also romanized as dōjinshi, is the Japanese term for self-published printed works, such as magazines, manga, and novels. Part of a broader category of doujin (self-published) works, doujinshi are often derivative of existing anime, manga, video games or other media, produced by amateurs — though some professional artists participate as a side venture — and sold at events such as Comiket or through specialty shops.

Overview

Doujinshi are typically produced by "circles" (サークル), groups of one or more creators. Many are fan works that reimagine copyrighted characters, including parody and romance pairings not present in the source; others are entirely original. Print runs are usually small, and production is a hobbyist activity driven by enthusiasm rather than profit.

Distribution

The largest doujinshi fair, Comiket, held twice a year in Tokyo, attracts hundreds of thousands of visitors. Doujin shops such as Mandarake and Toranoana resell doujinshi, and digital distribution has grown in recent years. Many well-known professional manga artists, including groups such as CLAMP, began their careers producing doujinshi.

Legal status

Derivative doujinshi technically infringe copyright under Japanese law, but rights holders generally tolerate them because the doujin market promotes the underlying works and trains new creators; enforcement is rare except in exceptional cases. This informal tolerance is often contrasted with stricter enforcement in other countries.
