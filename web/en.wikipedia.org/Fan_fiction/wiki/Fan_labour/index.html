Fan labour

Fan labour (also fan labor and fan activity) is the productive and creative work carried out by fans, ranging from writing fan fiction, drawing fan art and making fan videos to maintaining wikis, translating works, moderating communities and organizing conventions. Fan studies scholars use the term to draw attention to the effort involved, and to its complicated relationship with commercial media industries, which benefit from unpaid fan activity while fans themselves generally operate within a gift economy.

Forms

  • Transformative works: fan fiction, fan art, fanvids, podfic, filk and fan games;
  • Curatorial labour: tagging, archiving, recommending and reviewing works;
  • Infrastructural labour: running archives, forums, wikis and conventions, often as volunteers;
  • Promotional labour: campaigning for shows, translating and publicising works across languages.

Gift economy and industry

Within fandom, fan labour is typically exchanged for recognition, feedback and community standing rather than money, and commercialising fan works has historically been taboo in many communities. At the same time, media companies increasingly rely on fan engagement for marketing, and debates continue over whether platforms and producers exploit fans' unpaid work. Some creators transition from fan labour to professional work; publishers and game studios also recruit from fandoms.

Scholarship

Henry Jenkins's Textual Poachers (1992) reframed fans as active producers rather than passive consumers. Later scholars have examined fan labour through the lenses of free labour in digital economies, gender (much fan labour is performed by women), and affective labour.
