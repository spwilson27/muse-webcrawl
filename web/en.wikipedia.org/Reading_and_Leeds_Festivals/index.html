Reading and Leeds Festivals

The Reading and Leeds Festivals are a pair of annual music festivals that take place in Reading and Leeds in England. The events take place simultaneously on the Friday, Saturday and Sunday of the August bank holiday weekend. The Reading Festival is held at Little John's Farm on Richfield Avenue in central Reading, near the Caversham Bridge. The Leeds event is held in Bramham Park, near Wetherby, the grounds of a historic house. Headliners and most supporting acts typically play at both sites, with Reading's Friday line-up becoming Leeds' Saturday line-up and vice versa.

History

The Reading Festival originated as the National Jazz Festival in 1961, held initially in Richmond, and moved to Reading in 1971. Through the 1970s and 1980s it became a major rock festival, and in 1999 a second site at Leeds was added after the original plan to expand at Reading proved impractical, replacing the V Festival's northern leg. Reading is the world's oldest popular music festival still in existence.

Music

Historically a rock and alternative festival, the line-up has broadened to include indie, metal, punk, hip hop and electronic acts across multiple stages, including the Main Stage, the NME/Radio 1 Stage, the Festival Republic Stage and the BBC Introducing Stage. Memorable performances include Nirvana's 1992 headline set at Reading, widely regarded as one of the greatest festival performances of all time.

Tickets and attendance

The combined daily capacity is around 200,000 across both sites. Weekend tickets include camping, and the festivals are notorious for selling out quickly, as well as for the muddy conditions and lively campsite atmosphere, including the traditional Sunday night/Monday morning revelry at Reading.
