Amenable group

In mathematics, an amenable group is a locally compact topological group carrying a kind of averaging operation on bounded functions that is invariant under translation by group elements. The original definition, in terms of a finitely additive invariant measure (or mean) on subsets of the group, was introduced by John von Neumann in 1929 under the German name "messbar" in response to the Banach–Tarski paradox.

Definition

A locally compact Hausdorff group G is amenable if the space of bounded continuous functions on G admits a left-invariant mean, that is, a linear functional that assigns to each function a value between its infimum and supremum and is unchanged when the function is translated by a group element.

Examples

Compact groups and abelian groups are amenable, as are solvable groups and groups of subexponential growth. The free group on two generators is not amenable. Amenability is preserved under many group constructions, including subgroups, quotients, extensions, and directed unions.

Properties

Amenable groups satisfy a fixed-point property for affine actions on compact convex sets, and they admit Følner sequences in the discrete case: finite subsets that are almost invariant under translation. Amenability plays a central role in ergodic theory, operator algebras, and geometric group theory.
