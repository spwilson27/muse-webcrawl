Pontiac, Michigan

Pontiac is a city in and the county seat of Oakland County in the U.S. state of Michigan, United States. As of the 2020 census, the city had a population of 61,606. A part of the Detroit metropolitan area, Pontiac lies about 20 miles (32 km) northwest of Detroit. It gave its name to the Pontiac automobile brand of General Motors.

History

The first settlers arrived in 1818, and the town was named for the Ottawa chief Pontiac, who had his headquarters at the nearby confluence of the Clinton River and Saginaw Trail. Pontiac was formally recognized by the state legislature in 1837 and incorporated as a city in 1861. In the nineteenth century it was a centre of carriage and wagon manufacturing; the transition to automobiles came naturally, and the Oakland Motor Car Company — renamed Pontiac Motor Division in 1926 — became one of General Motors' principal brands.

Economy and decline

Pontiac's factories produced cars and trucks, and GMC Truck operations were based in the city. From the 1970s onward the city suffered severe industrial decline and population loss; between 2009 and 2013 it was administered by a series of state-appointed emergency managers. The Pontiac Silverdome, opened in 1975 as home of the Detroit Lions, was demolished in 2017–2018 after the team moved to Detroit. In recent decades the city has seen redevelopment efforts and an influx of smaller businesses and healthcare institutions.

Geography and transport

Pontiac is traversed by Woodward Avenue (M-1), one of Michigan's oldest highways, and hosts the Woodward Dream Cruise each August. The Clinton River runs through the city. Amtrak's Wolverine trains stop at Pontiac station, the northern terminus of the service, and Oakland County International Airport lies just west of the city in Waterford Township.
