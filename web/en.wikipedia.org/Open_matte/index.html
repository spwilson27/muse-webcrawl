Open matte

Open matte is a filming technique that involves matting out the top and bottom of the film frame in the movie projector (known as a soft matte) for the widescreen theatrical release and then scanning the film without a matte (at Academy ratio) for a full-screen home video release. An open-matte film therefore has more picture information above and below the theatrical frame, rather than the cropped sides seen in pan and scan transfers.

The taller open-matte frame (outer area) versus the matted widescreen image.

Technique

When shooting with a soft matte, the director and cinematographer compose for the intended widescreen aspect ratio (typically 1.85:1) while the camera exposes the full 1.37:1 Academy frame. The extra area is expected to be masked in theatres. Because boom microphones, lighting equipment and crew could appear in the unprotected area, care is still usually taken to keep the full frame tidy.

Home video use

In the era of 4:3 televisions, open-matte scans provided fullscreen releases that filled the screen without losing the sides of the composition — the inverse of pan and scan. Directors have had mixed views: the added headroom can change the intended composition, and unintended elements occasionally become visible. With the shift to 16:9 televisions and streaming, open matte has become less common, though some films have been re-released in taller IMAX ratios using similar principles.

Related techniques

  • Pan and scan — cropping a widescreen image to fit a narrower screen.
  • Letterboxing — preserving the theatrical ratio with black bars.
  • Super 35 — a format that exploits the full film width to derive multiple ratios.
