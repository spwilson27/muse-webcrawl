The British Antarctic Territory (BAT) is a sector of Antarctica claimed by the United Kingdom as one of its 14 British Overseas Territories. It comprises the region south of 60°S latitude and between longitudes 20°W and 80°W, forming a wedge shape extending to the South Pole and including the Antarctic Peninsula, the South Orkney Islands, and the South Shetland Islands. The territory covers 1,709,400 km², making it by far the largest of the British Overseas Territories by area.

History

The United Kingdom first asserted sovereignty over parts of the region through letters patent in 1908 and 1917, administered as Dependencies of the Falkland Islands. The separate British Antarctic Territory was formally created on 3 March 1962. The claim is not recognized by Argentina and Chile, whose own Antarctic claims overlap it. Under the Antarctic Treaty, signed in 1959 and in force from 1961, all territorial claims in Antarctica are held in abeyance and the continent is reserved for peaceful scientific purposes; the territory is administered in accordance with the Treaty system.

Research and presence

The territory has no permanent population. The British Antarctic Survey operates staffed research stations including Rothera (the territory's main logistics hub, on Adelaide Island), Halley VI on the Brunt Ice Shelf, and Signy in the South Orkneys, as well as the historic bases at Port Lockroy — now a museum and post office run by the UK Antarctic Heritage Trust — and Faraday Station, transferred to Ukraine in 1996 and renamed Vernadsky, where scientists had documented the Antarctic ozone hole in the 1980s. The Royal Navy's ice patrol ship maintains a seasonal presence. The territory issues its own postage stamps and has a Commissioner resident in London.

Geography and wildlife

The territory includes some of Antarctica's most accessible and biologically rich coasts, with large colonies of penguins, seals, and seabirds, and marine areas of global conservation significance. Queen Elizabeth Land, in the southern part of the wedge, was named in 2012 to mark the Diamond Jubilee of Elizabeth II.
