Ronald Reagan Ballistic Missile Defense Test Site

LocationKwajalein Atoll, Marshall Islands
OperatorUnited States Army Space and Missile Defense Command
Established1960s (as Kwajalein Missile Range)
Named forRonald Reagan, 40th U.S. President

The Ronald Reagan Ballistic Missile Defense Test Site, commonly referred to as the Reagan Test Site (formerly the Kwajalein Missile Range), is a missile test range in the Marshall Islands in the Pacific Ocean operated by the United States Army. It encompasses the Kwajalein Atoll lagoon and several islands in the atoll, including Kwajalein, Roi-Namur, and Meck Island, and covers an area of roughly 1.9 million square kilometres including the surrounding ocean impact zones.

History

The United States took Kwajalein from Japanese forces in the Battle of Kwajalein in February 1944 and used it as a naval and air base. Missile testing began in the late 1950s and expanded in the 1960s, when the Army's Nike-Zeus anti-ballistic missile programme established radars and launch facilities on Kwajalein and Meck Island. The range supported successive generations of missile-defence programmes, from Safeguard and the Homing Overlay Experiment to present-day Ground-Based Midcourse Defense intercept tests, in which target missiles are frequently launched from Vandenberg Space Force Base or Kodiak, Alaska, and interceptors are fired from Meck Island. The site was renamed in 1999 in honour of President Ronald Reagan, who had initiated the Strategic Defense Initiative.

Facilities

The test site hosts some of the world's most sophisticated radar instrumentation, including the ALCOR, TRADEX, ALTAIR, and MMW radars on Roi-Namur, and the Kiernan Reentry Measurements Site (KREMS) complex, used to observe reentry vehicles with high precision. Because of its equatorial location and vast ocean range, Kwajalein also supports space surveillance and satellite launch operations; SpaceX conducted its early Falcon 1 launches from Omelek Island in the atoll between 2006 and 2009.

Local community

American personnel and contractors live mainly on Kwajalein Island, while most Marshallese workers live on the crowded island of Ebeye and commute by ferry. Use of the atoll is governed by the Compact of Free Association between the United States and the Republic of the Marshall Islands, under which the U.S. pays landowners for the use of the test site.
