3D scanner

3D scanner
TypeImaging / measurement device
OutputPoint cloud, polygon mesh
TechnologiesLaser triangulation, structured light, time-of-flight, photogrammetry, CT
ApplicationsReverse engineering, heritage preservation, medicine, quality control

A 3D scanner is a device that analyses a real-world object or environment to collect data on its shape and, in some cases, its appearance. The collected data — typically a point cloud of geometric samples — can then be used to construct digital three-dimensional models.

Technologies

Scanners work by several principles. Time-of-flight laser scanners measure the round-trip time of a laser pulse to build scenes at long range, while laser triangulation and structured-light systems project patterns onto nearby objects and infer depth from their deformation. Photogrammetry reconstructs geometry computationally from overlapping photographs, and industrial CT scanning can capture internal structures invisible to optical methods. Contact scanners, such as coordinate-measuring machines, physically probe the surface and are accurate but slow.

Applications

3D scanning is used for reverse engineering and quality inspection in manufacturing, documenting cultural heritage sites and artifacts, planning surgery and fitting prosthetics in medicine, creating assets for film and video games, and recording crime scenes and building interiors for virtual tours. Consumer depth sensors in phones and tablets have made casual 3D capture widely available.
