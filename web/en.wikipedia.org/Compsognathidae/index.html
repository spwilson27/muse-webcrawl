Compsognathus, the type genus of the family

Compsognathidae is a family of small carnivorous theropod dinosaurs from the Jurassic and Cretaceous periods. Compsognathids were among the smallest known dinosaurs, most being about one metre in length, with light, hollow bones, long legs adapted for running, and three-fingered hands.

Genera

The family is named for Compsognathus from the Late Jurassic Solnhofen limestone of Germany. Other genera commonly assigned to the family include Sinosauropteryx, Juravenator, Scipionyx, Huaxiagnathus, and Mirischia, though classifications vary and some analyses place these taxa as early coelurosaurs outside a strict Compsognathidae.

Feathers

Compsognathids provided some of the earliest evidence of feathers in dinosaurs: Sinosauropteryx, described in 1996 from China's Yixian Formation, was the first non-avian dinosaur found with filamentous integument — simple "protofeathers" — preserved around its body, and melanosome analysis later suggested a reddish-brown, banded tail. Juravenator preserves scaly skin with feather-like structures, complicating simple pictures of feather distribution.

Diet

Preserved gut contents show compsognathids preyed on small vertebrates: a lizard (Bavarisaurus) was found in the stomach of the German Compsognathus specimen, and Scipionyx preserves remains of fish and other small animals.
