The Rio Carnival (Portuguese: Carnaval do Rio de Janeiro) is a festival held every year in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil, in the days before Lent. It is considered the biggest carnival in the world, drawing some two million people per day onto the streets and filling the Sambadrome for the competitive samba-school parades.

History

The festival descends from the Portuguese entrudo, a rowdy pre-Lenten street celebration introduced in the colonial era. In the 19th century, masked balls and organised parades (the grandes sociedades) spread among the elite, while Afro-Brazilian communities in neighbourhoods such as Praça Onze developed the street traditions — cordões, ranchos and blocos — from which samba emerged.

Samba school parades

The centrepiece is the parade of the samba schools of the Special Group, held since 1984 in the Sambadrome designed by Oscar Niemeyer. Each school — associations rooted in working-class neighbourhoods, such as Mangueira, Beija-Flor, Portela and Salgueiro — presents an 65–75 minute parade on a chosen theme (enredo) with thousands of costumed dancers, drummers (bateria), elaborate floats, a flag couple and a standard-bearer. Judges score categories including percussion, harmony, floats, costumes and overall evolution; the lowest-ranked school is relegated and the winner of the second tier promoted.

Street carnival

Beyond the Sambadrome, hundreds of street bands and block parties (blocos) — among them the Cordão da Bola Preta, founded 1918, and Monobloco — parade through the city, free and open to all.
