Queretaro

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Querétaro is one of the 32 federal entities of Mexico, located in north-central Mexico in the Bajío region. Its capital and largest city is Santiago de Querétaro. Among Mexico's smallest states by area, Querétaro is one of its fastest growing, with an economy built on manufacturing, aerospace, automotive parts, and data centres.

Geography and history

The state ranges from the semi-arid plains around the capital to the lush mountains of the Sierra Gorda in the northeast, whose Franciscan missions are a UNESCO World Heritage Site. In the colonial era the region was a frontier between Spanish settlements and Chichimeca peoples; the city of Querétaro played a pivotal role in Mexican independence — the conspiracy that launched the 1810 movement was planned there — and in 1867 Emperor Maximilian was defeated and executed on the Cerro de las Campanas outside the city. Mexico's current constitution was promulgated in Querétaro in 1917.

Economy

Querétaro is a hub of Mexico's aerospace industry, home to plants of Bombardier and Safran and to the Universidad Aeronáutica en Querétaro, and hosts a large automotive and information-technology sector. The historic centre of Santiago de Querétaro, with its baroque churches and colonial aqueduct of 74 arches, is also a UNESCO World Heritage Site and a centre of tourism.
