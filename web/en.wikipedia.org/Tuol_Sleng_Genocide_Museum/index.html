The Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum (Khmer: សារមន្ទីរឧក្រិដ្ឋកម្មប្រល័យពូជសាសន៍ទួលស្លែង) is a museum in Phnom Penh chronicling the Cambodian genocide. The site is a former secondary school which was used as Security Prison 21 (S-21) by the Khmer Rouge regime from 1976 until 1979, one of at least 150 torture and execution centres run by the regime's internal security service, the Santebal.

S-21

Prisoners — officials of the fallen Lon Nol regime, soldiers, intellectuals, alleged spies, and eventually thousands of Khmer Rouge cadres accused in internal purges, together with their families — were photographed, forced to write detailed confessions, and tortured. Nearly all were then taken to the extermination camp at Choeung Ek, a few kilometres away, and killed. Of an estimated 14,000 to 20,000 people imprisoned at S-21, only a small number survived; the best-known survivors include the painter Vann Nath, whose memoirs and paintings document the prison, and Chum Mey.

The prison was run by Kaing Guek Eav, known as "Duch", who was later convicted of crimes against humanity by the Khmer Rouge Tribunal in 2010 and sentenced to life imprisonment.

Museum

Discovered by Vietnamese forces in January 1979, the site was opened as a museum in 1980. Its cells, torture implements and walls of prisoner identification photographs are preserved largely as found. The Tuol Sleng archives were inscribed on UNESCO's Memory of the World register in 2009.
