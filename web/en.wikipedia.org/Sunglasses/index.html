Sunglasses

Sunglasses are a form of protective eyewear designed primarily to prevent bright sunlight and high-energy visible light from damaging or discomforting the eyes. They can also function as a visual aid and fashion accessory, with lenses of various colours, polarisation, and levels of darkness.

History

Prehistoric and historic Inuit peoples wore flattened walrus-ivory "snow goggles" with narrow slits to reduce glare. In 12th-century China, flat panes of smoky quartz were used by judges to conceal their expressions in court. Modern sunglasses were popularised in the 1920s and 1930s, aided by Sam Foster's mass-produced Foster Grant glasses and by the development of aviator sunglasses for military pilots.

Protection

Quality sunglasses block ultraviolet radiation (UVA and UVB), which can contribute to cataracts and other eye conditions. Polarised lenses additionally cut reflected glare from water, snow, and roads. Standards such as the European CE categories grade lenses by visible-light transmission.

Styles

Iconic styles include aviators, wayfarers, round, and wraparound frames; sunglasses are also worn in sports and by public figures as part of their image.
