The United Nations Partition Plan for Palestine was a proposal by the United Nations, adopted as General Assembly Resolution 181 (II) on 29 November 1947, that recommended the partition of Mandatory Palestine at the end of the British Mandate. The plan proposed two states — one Jewish and one Arab — joined by an economic union, with Jerusalem and its environs placed under a special international regime as a corpus separatum administered by the United Nations.

Background

Palestine had been governed by Britain under a League of Nations Mandate since 1920. Jewish immigration and land purchases, Arab opposition, and escalating violence between the communities — and against the British — led Britain to refer the problem to the United Nations in February 1947. The UN General Assembly appointed the United Nations Special Committee on Palestine (UNSCOP), composed of representatives of eleven nations, to investigate and recommend a solution.

UNSCOP's majority report of September 1947 recommended partition into Jewish and Arab states linked by an economic union; a minority proposed a single federal state. The Jewish Agency accepted the majority plan, while Arab leaders rejected both proposals.

The plan

Resolution 181 (II) called for:

  • Termination of the British Mandate and withdrawal of British forces
  • Creation of independent Arab and Jewish states after a transition period
  • An economic union between the two states, with a common currency and shared infrastructure
  • Jerusalem, Bethlehem, and surrounding areas to form a corpus separatum under UN administration, demilitarized and neutral
  • Protection of religious and minority rights and of the Holy Places

Under the proposed boundaries, the Jewish state was allotted roughly 56 percent of the mandate territory (much of it the Negev desert), including the coastal plain and eastern Galilee; the Arab state received about 42 percent, including the West Bank hill country and the Gaza area; Jerusalem comprised the remainder. At the time, Jews owned a small fraction of the land but constituted about one-third of the population.

Vote

The resolution passed the General Assembly on 29 November 1947 by 33 votes to 13, with 10 abstentions. The United States and the Soviet Union both supported it. The United Kingdom abstained and declined to implement a settlement not accepted by both parties.

Reactions and aftermath

Jewish leaders, including David Ben-Gurion and the Jewish Agency, accepted the plan as the basis for a Jewish state, while the Arab Higher Committee and the Arab League rejected it, arguing that the UN had no authority to partition Palestine against the wishes of its majority. Civil war between the communities began the day after the vote.

The plan was never implemented. On 14 May 1948, the day the Mandate expired, Israel declared independence; neighbouring Arab states invaded, beginning the 1948 Arab–Israeli War. The war's armistice lines of 1949 left Israel in control of about 78 percent of Mandate Palestine, with the West Bank annexed by Jordan and the Gaza Strip administered by Egypt.
