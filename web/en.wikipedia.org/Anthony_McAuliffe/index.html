Anthony McAuliffe

General Anthony C. McAuliffe

Born: 2 July 1898, Washington, D.C., U.S.
Died: 10 August 1975, Chevy Chase, Maryland
Service: United States Army
Commands: 101st Airborne Division (acting); Seventh Army

Anthony Clement McAuliffe (2 July 1898 – 10 August 1975) was a United States Army general best known for his defence of Bastogne during the Battle of the Bulge in December 1944, and for his one-word reply to a German surrender demand: "NUTS!"

Bastogne

As acting commander of the 101st Airborne Division while Maxwell Taylor was in Washington, McAuliffe directed the defence of the road junction of Bastogne, Belgium, surrounded by German forces. On 22 December 1944, German envoys presented an ultimatum demanding surrender; McAuliffe's written answer — "To the German Commander: NUTS! The American Commander" — became one of the most famous messages of the Second World War. The division held until relieved by Patton's Third Army.

Later career

McAuliffe went on to command the 103rd Infantry Division and later the Seventh Army in Europe, retiring in 1956 as a four-star general. He is buried at Arlington National Cemetery. A square in Bastogne bears his name.
