Dubliners

AuthorJames Joyce
LanguageEnglish
GenreShort story collection
Published1914 (Grant Richards, London)

Dubliners is a collection of fifteen short stories by James Joyce, first published in 1914. The stories follow the lives of ordinary Dublin residents and form what Joyce called a "chapter of the moral history of my country", depicting the city as the centre of a spiritual and cultural paralysis he sought to expose.

Contents

The collection is arranged in a progression through life: childhood stories such as "The Sisters", "An Encounter" and "Araby"; adolescent stories including "Eveline" and "After the Race"; mature-life stories such as "Clay", "A Painful Case" and "A Little Cloud"; and stories of public life including "Ivy Day in the Committee Room" and "Grace". It closes with the novella-length "The Dead", widely regarded as one of the greatest short stories in English.

Publication history

Joyce struggled for nearly a decade to publish the book: the Dublin firm Maunsel & Co. set the text but destroyed the sheets in 1912, fearing libel and obscenity actions. Grant Richards, who had first accepted the manuscript in 1906, finally issued it in June 1914.

Themes and style

Central devices include the "epiphany", a sudden revelation of truth experienced by a character, and a scrupulously plain realist style Joyce called "scrupulous meanness". Themes of paralysis, emigration, religion, nationalism and death recur throughout.
