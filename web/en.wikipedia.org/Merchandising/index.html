Merchandising

In retail, merchandising is any practice which contributes to the sale of products to a retail consumer. At a retail in-store level, merchandising refers to the variety of products available for sale and the display of those products in such a way that it stimulates interest and entices customers to make a purchase. In entertainment, the term also refers to products licensed from a film, television series, game or other intellectual property.

Retail merchandising

Retail merchandising covers product assortment, pricing, shelf placement (governed by planograms), signage, lighting, window displays and point-of-purchase promotions. Visual merchandising is the discipline of presenting products and store layout to highlight features and benefits. Seasonal cycles dominate merchandising calendars, with goods rotated for holidays and seasons. Online, "e-merchandising" applies the same ideas to website search results, recommendations and category pages.

Licensed merchandise

Licensed merchandising grew with 20th-century mass media: studio characters appeared on toys, clothing and lunchboxes, and the practice expanded enormously after the success of Star Wars licensing in 1977, which demonstrated that merchandise could rival box-office revenue. In professional sports, merchandising of team apparel is a major income stream. The field involves licence agreements in which the property owner receives royalties on sales.
