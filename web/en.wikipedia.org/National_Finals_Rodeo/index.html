National Finals Rodeo

First held: 1959 (Dallas, Texas)

Venue: Thomas & Mack Center, Las Vegas, Nevada (since 1985)

Sanctioning body: Professional Rodeo Cowboys Association (PRCA)

Abbreviation: NFR

The National Finals Rodeo (NFR) is the premier championship event of the Professional Rodeo Cowboys Association (PRCA). Held annually since 1959, it is often called the "Super Bowl of rodeo." The NFR determines the world champion in each of rodeo's events, based on season money standings combined with earnings at the finals themselves.

Format

The top 15 money earners in each event qualify for the NFR, which is contested over ten performances (rounds). Events include bareback riding, steer wrestling, team roping, saddle bronc riding, tie-down roping, barrel racing, and bull riding, along with steer roping, which crowns its champion at a separate finals. Contestants earn money in each round and for the ten-round average; the competitor with the highest combined season total is crowned world champion, while the highest average finisher wins the NFR average title.

History and venue

The first National Finals Rodeo was held in Dallas, Texas, in 1959. The event moved to Los Angeles and then to Oklahoma City, where it remained from 1965 to 1984. In 1985 the NFR moved to the Thomas & Mack Center in Las Vegas, Nevada, where its December dates and large purses helped transform the event into a major spectacle; in 2020 it was temporarily held in Arlington, Texas, because of the COVID-19 pandemic before returning to Las Vegas.
