The Church of Iceland (Icelandic: Þjóðkirkjan, literally "the National Church") is the national church of Iceland. It is an Evangelical Lutheran church and a member of the Lutheran World Federation and the Porvoo Communion.

History

Christianity was adopted in Iceland by a decision of the Althing in the year 1000. The medieval church was Roman Catholic, with dioceses at Skálholt (founded 1056) and Hólar (1106). The Lutheran Reformation was imposed by the Danish crown in the sixteenth century, culminating in the execution of the last Catholic bishop, Jón Arason, in 1550.

The complete Icelandic Bible translation of Guðbrandur Þorláksson was published in 1584 and played a central role in preserving the Icelandic language.

Organization

The church is headed by the Bishop of Iceland, based in Reykjavík. The country is divided into parishes served by pastors, and the church is governed in part by an elected church assembly (kirkjuþing). Although it is the national church, Iceland's constitution guarantees freedom of religion, and the state also supports other registered religious organizations through a parish fee system.

Membership

Historically, nearly all Icelanders belonged to the Church of Iceland. Membership has declined in recent decades; by the 2020s roughly three-fifths of the population were registered members, with growing shares belonging to other denominations, the Icelandic Ethical Humanist Association, or no registered group.
