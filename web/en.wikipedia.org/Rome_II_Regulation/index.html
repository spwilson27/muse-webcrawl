Rome II Regulation

The Rome II Regulation (Regulation (EC) No 864/2007) is a European Union regulation governing the conflict of laws for non-contractual obligations — chiefly tort and delict — in civil and commercial matters. It determines which country's law applies when a damaging event has cross-border elements within the EU.

Main rules

The general rule in Article 4 applies the law of the country where the damage occurs (lex loci damni), unless both parties share a habitual residence elsewhere or the tort is manifestly more closely connected with another country. Special rules cover product liability, unfair competition, environmental damage, infringement of intellectual property and industrial action. Article 14 permits parties, in defined circumstances, to choose the applicable law.

Scope

Adopted on 11 July 2007 and applicable from 11 January 2009, Rome II complements the Rome I Regulation on contractual obligations. It excludes matters such as family law, defamation handled separately, and nuclear damage. Denmark is not bound by it. See also conflict of laws.
