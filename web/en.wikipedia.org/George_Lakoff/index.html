George Philip Lakoff (born May 24, 1941) is an American cognitive linguist and philosopher, best known for his thesis that human thought is largely metaphorical and that the metaphors we live by structure our perception and reasoning. He taught at the University of California, Berkeley, from 1972 until his retirement.

Conceptual metaphor

With Mark Johnson, Lakoff wrote Metaphors We Live By (1980), arguing that expressions such as "argument is war", "time is money", and "up is good" reflect systematic conceptual mappings from concrete source domains to abstract target domains. In Women, Fire, and Dangerous Things (1987) he attacked classical categorisation theory, developing an account based on prototypes and radial categories. His later work on the embodied mind, including Philosophy in the Flesh (1999) with Johnson, holds that abstract thought is grounded in the body's sensorimotor systems.

Political framing

Lakoff applied cognitive linguistics to politics in Moral Politics (1996), proposing that conservatives and liberals understand politics through opposing family-based metaphors — the "strict father" and "nurturant parent" models. His popular book Don't Think of an Elephant! (2004) urged progressives to attend to framing in political communication. His political writings have been influential but also criticised as partisan.
