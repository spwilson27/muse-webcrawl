Christmas Spectacular Starring the Radio City Rockettes

The Christmas Spectacular Starring the Radio City Rockettes is an annual Christmas stage musical presented at Radio City Music Hall in New York City. The 90-minute show features the precision dance troupe the Rockettes and combines singing, dancing and humorous sketches in a celebration of the holiday season.

History

The show has been staged since 1933, when the Music Hall's first Christmas production, choreographed by Russell Markert and produced by Leon Leonidoff, introduced two numbers that remain in the show today: the "Parade of the Wooden Soldiers" and the "Living Nativity". The Rockettes themselves were founded in 1925 in St. Louis as the Missouri Rockets and became the Radio City troupe in the 1930s.

Signature numbers

The production is best known for the Rockettes' perfectly synchronised high-kick chorus lines and their wooden-soldier routine, in which the dancers, dressed as toy soldiers, execute elaborate marching patterns and collapse in slow motion like dominoes. Other long-running set pieces include the "Dance of the Frost Fairies", a Raggedy Ann number introduced in the 1970s, and the finale's Living Nativity, complete with live camels, sheep and donkeys.

Audience

The Spectacular runs from November through early January with multiple performances daily at peak season, and has been seen by tens of millions of spectators. Touring versions have performed in other American cities. The 2020 season was cancelled because of the COVID-19 pandemic, the first interruption in the show's history.
