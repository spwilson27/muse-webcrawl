Pfizer–BioNTech COVID-19 vaccine

A vial of the Pfizer–BioNTech COVID-19 vaccine (Comirnaty).

The Pfizer–BioNTech COVID-19 vaccine, sold under the brand name Comirnaty, is an mRNA-based COVID-19 vaccine developed by the German biotechnology company BioNTech in partnership with the American pharmaceutical company Pfizer. Its international nonproprietary name is tozinameran. It was the first COVID-19 vaccine to receive emergency authorization in Western countries and the first mRNA vaccine ever authorized for general use in humans.

Technology

The vaccine delivers nucleoside-modified messenger RNA (modRNA) encoding a stabilized form of the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein, packaged in lipid nanoparticles. After intramuscular injection, cells temporarily produce the spike protein, prompting the immune system to generate antibodies and T-cell responses. The vaccine requires two primary doses given several weeks apart; booster formulations updated for Omicron variants were later authorized. Early distribution was complicated by a requirement for ultra-cold storage at around −70 °C, later relaxed as stability data accumulated.

Authorization and efficacy

DateMilestone
2 December 2020First emergency authorization, by the United Kingdom's MHRA
11 December 2020Emergency use authorization by the U.S. FDA
23 August 2021Full FDA approval for individuals aged 16 and older

In its pivotal phase III trial of more than 43,000 participants, the vaccine showed about 95% efficacy against symptomatic COVID-19 shortly after the second dose. Later real-world studies showed waning protection against infection over time and reduced effectiveness against newer variants, while protection against hospitalization and death remained substantially stronger, particularly after booster doses.

Safety

Common side effects are injection-site pain, fatigue, headache, muscle pain, chills, and fever, generally lasting a day or two. Rare but serious adverse events associated with the vaccine include anaphylaxis and myocarditis/pericarditis, the latter occurring most often in adolescent and young adult males after the second dose. Global pharmacovigilance systems continue to monitor its safety profile, and regulators have consistently concluded that the benefits outweigh the risks for authorized populations.
