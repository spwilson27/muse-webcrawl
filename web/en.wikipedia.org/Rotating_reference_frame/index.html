Rotating reference frame

A rotating frame of reference is a special case of a non-inertial reference frame that is rotating relative to an inertial reference frame. An everyday example of a rotating reference frame is the surface of the Earth.

Fictitious forces

Newton's laws of motion in a rotating frame acquire additional terms called fictitious (or pseudo) forces. For a frame rotating with angular velocity Ω, an object of mass m experiences the centrifugal force m(Ω × r) × Ω, directed outward from the axis; the Coriolis force −2m(Ω × v), which deflects moving objects; and, if the rotation rate changes, the Euler force −m(dΩ/dt × r).

Applications

The Coriolis effect in the Earth frame governs large-scale atmospheric and oceanic circulation, the rotation of cyclones, and the precession of a Foucault pendulum. Rotating-frame analysis is essential in engineering of centrifuges, turbines, and rotating spacecraft, and in describing spin systems in magnetic resonance.

Relation between frames

The time derivative of a vector in the inertial frame equals its derivative in the rotating frame plus Ω × (the vector). This transport theorem allows the equations of motion to be rewritten entirely in rotating coordinates at the price of the fictitious force terms.
