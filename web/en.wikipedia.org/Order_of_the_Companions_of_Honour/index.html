Order of the Companions of Honour

The Order of the Companions of Honour is an order of the Commonwealth realms, founded by King George V on 4 June 1917 as a reward for outstanding achievement in the arts, literature, music, science, politics, industry, and religion.

Membership

Membership is limited to 65 Companions at any one time, in addition to the sovereign and honorary members from outside the Commonwealth. Members are entitled to use the post-nominal letters CH. There is no knighthood or rank of precedence attached to the order.

Insignia

The badge is an oval medallion depicting an oak tree, a shield of the Royal Arms, and a knight on horseback, surmounted by the Imperial Crown, worn on a crimson ribbon. The motto is "In action faithful and in honour clear", from Alexander Pope's description of James Craggs the Younger.
