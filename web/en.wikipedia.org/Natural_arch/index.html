Natural arch

A natural arch, natural bridge, or (less commonly) rock arch is a natural rock formation where an arch has formed with an opening underneath. Natural arches commonly form where inland cliffs are subject to erosion from the sea, rivers, or weathering, or where rock strata of differing resistance erode at different rates.

Formation

On coasts, waves erode cracks at the base of headlands, enlarging caves until they break through to form arches, which may later collapse to leave sea stacks. Inland, water flowing through joints in sandstone can carve alcoves and fins; further erosion through a fin produces an arch, as exemplified by the many arches of Arches National Park and the Colorado Plateau in Utah. Frost weathering, wind abrasion, and chemical dissolution of limestone also play roles.

Famous examples

Well-known natural arches include Delicate Arch and Landscape Arch in Utah — the latter among the longest natural rock spans in the world — Durdle Door on England's Jurassic Coast, the Azure Window in Malta (collapsed in 2017), and the Pont d'Arc in France.
