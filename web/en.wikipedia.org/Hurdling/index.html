Hurdling

A hurdler clearing a barrier in the 110 metres hurdles

Hurdling is the act of jumping over an obstacle at a high speed or in a sprint. In the sport of athletics, hurdling forms a group of events in which competitors run and jump over a series of evenly spaced hurdles placed along a track. The standard hurdle races are the 110 metres hurdles for men, the 100 metres hurdles for women, and the 400 metres hurdles for both sexes; shorter 60 metres races are contested indoors.

History

Hurdle races developed in England in the mid-19th century, initially using heavy wooden barriers. The events were standardised around the turn of the 20th century, and the modern lightweight, L-shaped hurdle — designed to tip forward when struck — was developed in the 1930s, greatly reducing the danger of the event. The women's 100 metres hurdles replaced the earlier 80 metres event at the 1972 Olympics.

Technique

Efficient hurdling aims to minimise the disruption to sprinting rhythm. The athlete attacks the hurdle with the lead leg extended, the trail leg sweeping around to the side, and the opposite arm reaching forward. In the sprint hurdles, elite athletes take three strides between hurdles. Knocking over a hurdle is not penalised provided it is not done deliberately, since modern hurdles are weighted to fall when struck.

The 110 metres hurdles world record is held by Aries Merritt (12.80 seconds, 2012). The women's 100 metres hurdles record is held by Tobi Amusan (12.12 seconds, 2022). In the 400 metres hurdles, Karsten Warholm (45.94) and Sydney McLaughlin-Levrone hold the men's and women's world records respectively.
