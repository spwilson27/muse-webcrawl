The Khronos Group, Inc. is an American non-profit, member-driven consortium based in Beaverton, Oregon, that creates open, royalty-free standards for the authoring and acceleration of graphics, parallel computation, vision processing and machine learning across a wide range of platforms and devices. Founded in 2000, its members include most major hardware and software companies in the graphics industry.

Standards

Khronos maintains a large portfolio of application programming interfaces and file formats, including:

  • OpenGL and OpenGL ES — cross-platform graphics APIs (stewardship transferred to Khronos in 2006)
  • Vulkan — low-overhead, cross-platform graphics and compute API, released in 2016
  • OpenCL — framework for heterogeneous parallel computing
  • WebGL — JavaScript binding of OpenGL ES for browsers
  • glTF — file format for transmission of 3D scenes and models, sometimes called "the JPEG of 3D"
  • OpenXR — standard for augmented and virtual reality platforms
  • SPIR-V — intermediate representation for GPU shaders and kernels
  • SYCL, OpenVX, COLLADA and others

Organisation

Members join at Promoter, Contributor or Associate/Academic levels; working groups draft specifications that are ratified by the promoter board and published royalty-free. Khronos also runs conformance test suites and an adopters programme so implementers can use the standards' trademarks. The consortium works alongside other bodies such as the W3C on web standards.

History

Khronos was founded in 2000 by companies including 3Dlabs, ATI, Discrete, Evans & Sutherland, Intel, NVIDIA, Silicon Graphics and Sun Microsystems, initially to promote OpenML and other media standards. It grew through stewardship of OpenGL ES for the booming mobile market and consolidated its central role with the release of Vulkan and glTF in the 2010s.
