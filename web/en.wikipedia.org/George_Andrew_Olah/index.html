George Andrew Olah

George Andrew Olah
Born: 22 May 1927, Budapest, Hungary
Died: 8 March 2017, Beverly Hills, California, U.S.
Field: Organic chemistry
Known for: Carbocation chemistry; superacids
Awards: Nobel Prize in Chemistry (1994)

George Andrew Olah (born Oláh András György; May 22, 1927 – March 8, 2017) was a Hungarian-American chemist. His research involved the generation and reactivity of carbocations via superacids. For this research, Olah was awarded the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1994 "for his contribution to carbocation chemistry." He later promoted the "methanol economy," a proposal to base future energy storage and fuel on methanol rather than fossil fuels.

Life and career

Olah was born in Budapest and studied at the Budapest University of Technology and Economics. After the Hungarian Revolution of 1956 he emigrated, first to England and Canada, where he worked at Dow Chemical, and then to the United States. He became a professor at Case Western Reserve University in Cleveland and in 1977 moved to the University of Southern California, where he founded the Loker Hydrocarbon Research Institute.

Carbocation chemistry

Carbocations — positively charged carbon intermediates central to organic reactions — were generally too short-lived to observe directly. Olah showed that in superacid media, such as the mixture of antimony pentafluoride and fluorosulfonic acid he dubbed "magic acid," carbocations persist long enough to be studied spectroscopically, especially by NMR. His work resolved the celebrated "nonclassical ion" controversy over the structure of the 2-norbornyl cation and transformed the understanding of hydrocarbon reactions, with practical applications in petroleum refining and isomerization. He also developed methods that make possible the direct conversion of methane into higher hydrocarbons and into methanol.

The methanol economy

In his later years Olah argued for a "methanol economy" in which carbon dioxide captured from industrial exhausts or the air is recycled with hydrogen into methanol, serving as a convenient liquid fuel, hydrogen carrier, and feedstock for the chemical industry. With collaborators he founded a company that built a commercial CO2-to-methanol plant in Iceland, the George Olah Renewable Methanol Plant, which began operating in 2012 using geothermal energy.
