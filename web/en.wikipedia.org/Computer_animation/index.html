Computer animation

Computer animation is the process used for digitally generating moving images, a subfield of computer graphics and animation. It includes both 2D and 3D techniques, ranging from simple vector motion to fully rendered photorealistic scenes in film and video games.

History

Early experiments date to the 1960s: Edward Zajac's 1963 Bell Labs film simulated a satellite orbiting Earth, and Ivan Sutherland's Sketchpad (1963) pioneered interactive graphics. The University of Utah became a centre of research in the 1970s. Westworld (1973) used early 2D digital imagery, and Futureworld (1976) showed a 3D animated face and hand. Pixar's Toy Story (1995) was the first fully computer-animated feature film.

Techniques

Keyframe animation

Animators set key poses at important frames; the computer interpolates intermediate frames ("inbetweening" or tweening), typically using spline curves.

Motion capture

Motion capture records the movement of real actors via markers and cameras, transferring the data to digital characters for realistic motion.

Procedural and simulation

Physics simulations animate cloth, hair, fluids, crowds and destruction. Procedural animation generates motion algorithmically, such as flocking behaviour.

Rigging and skinning

Characters are built as surface meshes bound to an internal skeleton of bones and joints (a rig), allowing animators to pose them with forward or inverse kinematics.

Rendering

Final images are produced by rendering, using rasterisation for real-time applications (games) or ray tracing and path tracing for film-quality output. Studios use render farms of thousands of processors.

Applications

Computer animation is used in feature films, television, video games, advertising, architectural visualisation, medical and scientific simulation, and virtual and augmented reality.
