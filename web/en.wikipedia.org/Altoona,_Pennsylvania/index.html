Altoona, Pennsylvania

Altoona is a city in Blair County, Pennsylvania, United States. Founded in 1849 by the Pennsylvania Railroad, it grew into one of the most important railroad towns in America, known for its locomotive shops and the nearby Horseshoe Curve.

History

The Pennsylvania Railroad established Altoona as a site for its repair and construction shops at the eastern base of the Allegheny Mountains. The Horseshoe Curve, completed in 1854, allowed trains to surmount the Allegheny Front and became a celebrated feat of engineering — and a target guarded during the Second World War. At their peak the Altoona Works employed many thousands and built thousands of locomotives.

Today

Altoona is home to the Railroaders Memorial Museum and the Altoona Curve minor-league baseball team. Rail operations remain part of the economy under Norfolk Southern, though on a far smaller scale than in the steam era.
