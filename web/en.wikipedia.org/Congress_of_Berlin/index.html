Congress of Berlin

The Congress of Berlin (13 June – 13 July 1878) was a meeting of the great powers of Europe — Germany, Austria-Hungary, France, the United Kingdom, Italy, Russia and the Ottoman Empire — convened to revise the Treaty of San Stefano that had ended the Russo-Turkish War of 1877–78 and to settle the affairs of the Balkans. It was presided over by the German Chancellor Otto von Bismarck, who styled himself an "honest broker".

The Treaty of Berlin

The resulting Treaty of Berlin recognised the full independence of Romania, Serbia and Montenegro, reduced the large Bulgaria created at San Stefano to an autonomous principality, and placed southern Bulgaria (Eastern Rumelia) under Ottoman sovereignty. Austria-Hungary received the right to occupy Bosnia and Herzegovina, Britain acquired Cyprus, and Russia retained southern Bessarabia and gains in the Caucasus.

Consequences

The settlement disappointed national aspirations across the Balkans — of Bulgarians, Serbs, Greeks and others — and the grievances it left behind festered into the Balkan Wars of 1912–13 and contributed to the tensions that produced the First World War.
