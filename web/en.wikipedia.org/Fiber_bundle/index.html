In mathematics, and particularly topology, a fiber bundle (or fibre bundle) is a space that is locally a product space but globally may have a different topological structure. Specifically, a fiber bundle consists of a total space E, a base space B, a fiber space F, and a continuous projection map π: E → B such that every point of B has a neighbourhood over which the bundle looks like the product of the neighbourhood with F. The preimage of a point of B is called the fiber over that point, and all fibers are homeomorphic to F.

Examples

The simplest bundle is the product space B × F itself, called a trivial bundle. The Möbius strip is a nontrivial line bundle over the circle: locally it looks like an interval times an arc of the circle, but globally the fiber is twisted, so it is not a product. The tangent bundle of a smooth manifold — whose fibers are the tangent spaces at each point — is a fundamental example in differential geometry, as are vector bundles more generally. The Hopf fibration presents the 3-sphere as a bundle of circles over the 2-sphere. A covering space is a fiber bundle whose fiber is a discrete space.

Sections and structure groups

A section of a bundle assigns to each point of the base a point in the fiber above it, continuously; vector fields are sections of the tangent bundle, and the existence of nowhere-zero sections is governed by characteristic classes such as the Euler characteristic. Bundles are often described by transition functions between local trivialisations, taking values in a structure group G — for vector bundles, the general linear group of the fiber. Principal bundles, in which the fiber is itself a group, are central to gauge theory in physics, where connections on bundles describe fundamental forces, and to the theory of characteristic classes.

History

Fiber bundle concepts emerged in the 1930s–1950s through the work of Hassler Whitney, who coined the term "fiber bundle" (initially "fiber space"), Heinz Hopf, Norman Steenrod — whose 1951 monograph The Topology of Fibre Bundles systematised the subject — and others. Bundles have become one of the basic structures of modern geometry and mathematical physics.
