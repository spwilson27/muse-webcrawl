Indication (medicine)

Indication
FieldMedicine, pharmacology
DefinitionA valid reason to use a certain test, medication, procedure, or surgery
OppositeContraindication

In medicine, an indication is a valid reason to use a certain test, medication, procedure, or surgery. The term is most often used for drugs: a drug indication states the disease or condition for which the drug has been approved by a regulatory authority such as the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) or the European Medicines Agency, based on evidence of safety and efficacy in clinical trials.

Drug indications and labelling

Approved indications appear in the official product label or summary of product characteristics. Prescribing a drug for an unapproved condition is known as off-label use; it is legal and common in many jurisdictions, though manufacturers may not promote such uses. Indications may be added, narrowed, or withdrawn as new evidence accumulates; a drug whose risks outweigh its benefits for an indication may have that indication removed.

Indications for procedures and tests

Procedures and diagnostic tests also have indications. For example, coronary artery bypass surgery is indicated for certain patterns of coronary artery disease, and screening mammography is indicated for defined age groups. Clinical practice guidelines typically enumerate indications to support appropriate use and reimbursement decisions.

Contraindication

The converse of an indication is a contraindication, a condition or factor that makes a treatment inadvisable. Contraindications may be absolute (the treatment must not be used) or relative (caution is required and benefits may still outweigh risks).
