Cape Agulhas is a rocky headland in the Overberg region of the Western Cape, South Africa. It is the geographic southern tip of the African continent and the conventional dividing line between the Atlantic and Indian Oceans. The nearby village of L'Agulhas is the southernmost settlement in Africa.

The Cape Agulhas Lighthouse, in service since 1849.

Geography

The cape lies about 170 kilometres southeast of Cape Town. By international convention, the meridian of 20° east longitude passing through the cape marks the boundary between the Atlantic and Indian Oceans, although oceanic currents shift around the point. The warm Agulhas Current flows south along the east coast and retroflects near the cape, while the cold Benguela Current moves northward along the west coast.

Name and history

Portuguese navigators rounded the cape in the late fifteenth century and named it Cabo das Agulhas — "Cape of Needles" — because compass needles showed no magnetic declination there: magnetic north coincided with true north. The shallow Agulhas Bank, sudden storms, and powerful currents made the coast a notorious graveyard of ships; more than a hundred wrecks are recorded, including the Arniston in 1815. The lighthouse, modelled on the Pharos of Alexandria, was lit in 1849.

Today

The cape falls within the Agulhas National Park, proclaimed in 1999, which protects coastal fynbos and wetlands. A monument and a large relief map of Africa mark the southernmost point for visitors.
