The 24 Hours of Spa is an endurance racing event for cars held annually since 1924 at the Circuit de Spa-Francorchamps in Belgium. It is currently sponsored by CrowdStrike and is contested by GT3-specification cars as the flagship round of the GT World Challenge Europe Endurance Cup and the Intercontinental GT Challenge.

History

The race was conceived by Jules de Their and Henri Langlois Van Ophem and first run in 1924, a year after the inaugural 24 Hours of Le Mans. Early editions were contested by touring cars on a longer version of the Francorchamps circuit. Through the middle decades of the twentieth century the event became a major round of the European Touring Car Championship, attracting factory teams from Alfa Romeo, BMW, Ford and others.

From 2001 the race was run for GT cars under the FIA GT Championship, and in 2011 it adopted GT3 regulations as part of the Blancpain Endurance Series. It has since grown into the largest GT3 race in the world, regularly drawing grids of more than 60 cars and overall entries from manufacturers including Audi, BMW, Ferrari, Lamborghini, Mercedes-AMG and Porsche.

Format

The race week includes practice, qualifying and a "Super Pole" shootout for the fastest twenty cars. The race itself begins on Saturday afternoon and runs through the night, finishing 24 hours later. Winning the event outright is considered one of the most prestigious achievements in GT racing.

Notable winners

Eric van de Poele holds the record with five overall victories. Manufacturer honours have been shared among several brands, with BMW, Audi and Ferrari among the most successful in the GT3 era.
