Ship

Types: Cargo, passenger, naval, fishing
Propulsion: Sail, steam, diesel, nuclear
Measured in: Tonnage, displacement

A ship is a large vessel that travels the world's oceans and other large bodies of water, generally distinguished from a boat by its size, shape and carrying capacity. Ships have supported exploration, trade, warfare and migration throughout history and remain the backbone of world commerce, carrying the great majority of international trade by volume.

History

Watercraft have existed since prehistory, evolving from rafts and dugout canoes to the planked sailing ships of antiquity, the great ocean-going sailing ships of the Age of Sail, and in the nineteenth century to steam-powered iron and steel vessels. The twentieth century brought diesel propulsion, containerisation and very large specialised ships such as supertankers and container ships.

Types

Merchant ships include container ships, bulk carriers, oil tankers, ferries and cruise ships. Naval ships include aircraft carriers, destroyers, frigates and submarines. Other categories include fishing vessels, research ships, tugs and icebreakers.

Architecture and operation

A ship's structure is built around its hull, with propulsion machinery, cargo spaces, accommodation and navigation equipment arranged within it. Ships are operated by a professional crew under a master (captain) and are subject to international regulation through bodies such as the International Maritime Organization.
