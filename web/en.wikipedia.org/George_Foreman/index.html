George Edward Foreman (1949–2025) was an American professional boxer, businessman, and minister. Nicknamed "Big George," he was a two-time world heavyweight champion and an Olympic gold medallist, whose comeback — regaining the title at age 45 — remains one of sport's most celebrated stories.

Early career and first title

Raised in Houston's Fifth Ward, Foreman took up boxing through the Job Corps and won the heavyweight gold medal at the 1968 Mexico City Olympics. Turning professional, he demolished the unbeaten Joe Frazier in Kingston, Jamaica, in January 1973, knocking him down six times in two rounds to win the world heavyweight title.

The Rumble in the Jungle

In October 1974, in Kinshasa, Zaire, Foreman defended the title against Muhammad Ali in the famous "Rumble in the Jungle." Ali's "rope-a-dope" tactics exhausted Foreman, who was knocked out in the eighth round — his first professional defeat. After a shocking loss to Jimmy Young in 1977 and a near-death experience, Foreman retired and became an ordained minister.

Comeback and business fame

Foreman returned to the ring in 1987, and in November 1994 knocked out Michael Moorer to regain the heavyweight championship at 45, the oldest boxer ever to win it. In retirement he became at least as famous as the spokesman for the George Foreman Grill, which sold over 100 million units; his endorsement deal reportedly earned him far more than his boxing career.
