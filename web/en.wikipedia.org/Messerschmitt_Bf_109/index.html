The Messerschmitt Bf 109 is a German World War II fighter aircraft that was, along with the Focke-Wulf Fw 190, the backbone of the Luftwaffe's fighter force. The Bf 109 first saw operational service in 1937 during the Spanish Civil War and was still in service at the end of World War II in 1945. It was one of the most advanced fighters when it first appeared, with an all-metal monocoque construction, a closed canopy, and retractable landing gear, and it was powered by a liquid-cooled, inverted-V12 aero engine.

Design and development

The aircraft was designed by Willy Messerschmitt and Robert Lusser at the Bayerische Flugzeugwerke in response to a 1934 requirement for a single-seat interceptor. The initial prototype was powered — Germany's suitable engines not yet being ready — by a British Rolls-Royce Kestrel. Design priorities were a small, light airframe wrapped around the most powerful engine available, with armament mounted in the wings and fuselage. Automatic leading-edge slats improved low-speed handling, but the narrow-track, outward-raked landing gear caused many take-off and landing accidents throughout the type's career.

Operational history

The Bf 109 was flown by the three top-scoring fighter aces of all time — Erich Hartmann (352 victories), Gerhard Barkhorn (301), and Günther Rall (275) — all of whom served with Jagdgeschwader 52 on the Eastern Front. It fought in the Battle of Britain, where its limited range over England was a decisive constraint, and in every other theatre where the Luftwaffe operated: North Africa, the Mediterranean, the defence of the Reich against Allied bombers, and the Eastern Front.

Variants

Successive variants ran from the early B, C, and D models through the Emil (E), the definitive Friedrich (F), the heavily produced Gustav (G — the most numerous variant, with the DB 605 engine), and the final Kurfürst (K). The G series alone accounted for roughly a third of all Bf 109 production. Postwar, the type was built in Czechoslovakia as the Avia S-199 and in Spain as the Hispano Aviación HA-1112 "Buchón", the latter flying with the Spanish Air Force into the 1960s.

With over 34,000 built between 1935 and 1945, the Bf 109 is the most-produced fighter aircraft in history.

Specifications (Bf 109G-6)

  • Crew: 1
  • Engine: Daimler-Benz DB 605A-1 inverted V12, 1,475 PS (1,085 kW)
  • Maximum speed: approx. 640 km/h (398 mph) at altitude
  • Range: approx. 850 km
  • Armament: 2 × 13 mm MG 131 machine guns; 1 × 20 mm MG 151/20 cannon firing through the propeller hub; optional underwing cannon gondolas
