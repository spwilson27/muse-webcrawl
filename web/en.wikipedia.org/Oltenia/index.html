Oltenia (Romanian pronunciation: [olˈteni.a]), also called Lesser Wallachia, is a geographical and historical region of Romania in western Wallachia. It lies between the Danube, the Southern Carpathians, and the Olt River, from which it takes its name.

Geography

Oltenia comprises the counties of Dolj, Gorj, Mehedinți, Olt, and Vâlcea. Its largest city and traditional capital is Craiova; other important towns include Târgu Jiu, Drobeta-Turnu Severin, Râmnicu Vâlcea, and Slatina. The region ranges from the Danube plain in the south to the Parâng and Retezat foothills in the north.

History

In antiquity the area was part of Dacia. Under the Ottoman vassal Wallachian state, Oltenia was governed by a Ban seated at Craiova. It was administered by Habsburg Austria between 1718 and 1739 as a result of the Treaty of Passarowitz. The revolutionary Tudor Vladimirescu began his 1821 uprising in Oltenia.
