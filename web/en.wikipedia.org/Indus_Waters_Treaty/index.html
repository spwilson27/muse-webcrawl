Indus Waters Treaty

The Indus Waters Treaty is a water-sharing agreement between India and Pakistan, brokered by the World Bank and signed in Karachi on 19 September 1960 by Indian prime minister Jawaharlal Nehru and Pakistani president Ayub Khan. Regarded as one of the most successful international water treaties, it has survived multiple wars and crises between the two countries, although India announced in 2025 that it was holding the treaty in abeyance following a militant attack in Kashmir.

Provisions

The treaty divides the six rivers of the Indus system between the two countries. The three "eastern rivers" — the Sutlej, Beas, and Ravi — are allocated to India for unrestricted use, while the three "western rivers" — the Indus, Jhelum, and Chenab — are allocated largely to Pakistan, with India permitted limited uses, including run-of-the-river hydroelectric projects subject to design constraints. The treaty established a Permanent Indus Commission, with a commissioner from each country, and a graded dispute-resolution mechanism culminating in a neutral expert or a court of arbitration.

History and disputes

The treaty resolved a dispute that began at partition in 1947, when headworks and canals of the undivided Punjab irrigation system ended up on opposite sides of the new border; in 1948 India briefly cut water supplies to Pakistani canals. Nine years of World Bank-mediated negotiation followed. Later disputes have centred on Indian hydroelectric projects on the western rivers, notably Baglihar (referred to a neutral expert, decided 2007) and Kishanganga (a court of arbitration ruled in 2013). India issued a notice in January 2023 seeking modification of the treaty, and in April 2025, after the Pahalgam attack in Indian-administered Kashmir, New Delhi announced the treaty would be held in abeyance — a step Pakistan said would be treated as an act of war.
