Württemberg

Württemberg is a historical region and former state in south-western Germany, centred on the Neckar valley with Stuttgart as its capital. It corresponds broadly to the eastern part of the modern state of Baden-Württemberg. The polity grew from a medieval county into a duchy (1495), an electorate (1803) and a kingdom (1806–1918), before becoming a free state of the Weimar Republic and eventually merging with Baden in 1952.

History

The House of Württemberg traces its origins to the late 11th century, taking its name from Wirtemberg Castle near Stuttgart. Count Eberhard I expanded the county considerably, and in 1495 Eberhard "the Bearded" was raised to duke at the Diet of Worms; he also founded the University of Tübingen in 1477. Württemberg adopted the Lutheran Reformation under Duke Ulrich in the 1530s and remained staunchly Protestant. The Thirty Years' War devastated the territory, and after 1648 the duchy slowly recovered, with Louisbourg (Ludwigsburg) built as a baroque rival residence to Stuttgart.

Under Duke (later King) Frederick, Württemberg gained extensive territory in the Napoleonic mediatisations, becoming an electorate in 1803 and a kingdom in 1806, and joined the German Confederation and, in 1871, the German Empire. King William II abdicated in November 1918, and Württemberg became the Free People's State of Württemberg within Weimar Germany.

After 1945

Following the Second World War the region was divided between the American occupation zone (Württemberg-Baden, with North Baden) and the French zone (Württemberg-Hohenzollern). A 1951 referendum approved merger, and on 25 April 1952 the three south-western states united as Baden-Württemberg.

Geography and culture

Württemberg includes the Swabian Jura (Schwäbische Alb), the Black Forest's eastern fringes, the fertile Neckar basin and wine-growing slopes around Heilbronn and Stuttgart. Its people speak Swabian dialects of Alemannic German. Regional specialities include Maultaschen, Spätzle and Trollinger wines. Major cities include Stuttgart, Ulm, Heilbronn, Reutlingen and Tübingen.
