Legionella

Legionella is a genus of pathogenic gram-negative bacteria that includes the species L. pneumophila, which causes Legionnaires' disease, a severe form of pneumonia, and the milder Pontiac fever. The genus is named after the 1976 outbreak at an American Legion convention in Philadelphia, where the bacterium was first identified.


Legionella pneumophila bacteria

Biology and ecology

Legionella bacteria live naturally in freshwater environments such as lakes and streams, where they parasitize amoebae. They proliferate in warm (25–45 °C) human-made water systems — cooling towers, hot tubs, showers, and plumbing — especially within biofilms. Infection occurs when people inhale aerosolized water droplets containing the bacteria; it is not generally spread person-to-person.

Species and disease

More than 50 species have been described, roughly half of which have been associated with human disease. L. pneumophila serogroup 1 accounts for the large majority of Legionnaires' disease cases. Diagnosis uses urinary antigen tests, culture, and PCR; treatment is with antibiotics such as macrolides or fluoroquinolones.

Prevention

Control relies on water management plans: maintaining hot water temperatures, disinfecting building water systems, and monitoring cooling towers, as formalized in standards such as ASHRAE Standard 188.
