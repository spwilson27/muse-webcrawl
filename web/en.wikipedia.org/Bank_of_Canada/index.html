Bank of Canada

The Bank of Canada (French: Banque du Canada) is a Crown corporation and Canada's central bank. Chartered in 1934 under the Bank of Canada Act, it is responsible for Canada's monetary policy and for promoting a safe and sound financial system within Canada. It is the sole issuer of Canadian banknotes.

Role and functions

The Bank's principal role, as defined in the Bank of Canada Act, is "to promote the economic and financial welfare of Canada." Its four areas of responsibility are monetary policy — keeping inflation low, stable, and predictable under a 2 percent inflation-control target — the financial system, currency, and funds management for the Government of Canada.

History

Before the Bank's creation, the federal Department of Finance performed central banking functions. The Bank opened its doors in March 1935, initially as a privately held institution, and was nationalized in 1938. Its head office occupies a landmark building on Wellington Street in Ottawa, opened in 1938 and expanded in the 1970s, and it also operates the Bank of Canada Museum.
