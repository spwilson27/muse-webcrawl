Food security

Food security
DefinitionAll people, at all times, have physical and economic access to sufficient, safe and nutritious food
PillarsAvailability, access, utilization, stability
Key agenciesFAO, WFP, IFAD

Food security is the state of having reliable access to a sufficient quantity of affordable, nutritious food. The measure of food access used in the United States was pioneered by the FAO. The availability of food is a matter of supply, while access concerns the demand and distribution of food. Household food security exists when all members, at all times, have access to enough food for an active, healthy life.

The concept of food security has evolved since the 1974 World Food Conference definition focused purely on food supply. The widely used definition from the 1996 World Food Summit states that food security exists "when all people, at all times, have physical and economic access to sufficient, safe and nutritious food that meets their dietary needs and food preferences for an active and healthy life."

Pillars

Food security rests on four pillars: availability (sufficient quantities of food of appropriate quality, supplied through domestic production or imports), access (the resources individuals have to obtain appropriate foods), utilization (the body's ability to make use of nutrients, depending on diet quality, clean water, sanitation and health care), and stability (access must not be at risk from sudden shocks or cyclical events). Agency and sustainability have been proposed as additional dimensions.

Measurement

Food security is measured by calorie intake per person, dietary diversity, and experiential scales such as the Food Insecurity Experience Scale (FIES) and the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC), which grades conditions from Phase 1 (minimal) to Phase 5 (famine/catastrophe). The Global Hunger Index ranks countries using undernourishment, child stunting, child wasting and child mortality.

Causes of food insecurity

Causes include poverty, armed conflict, climate change and extreme weather, economic shocks, food price spikes, crop failure, and disruptions to supply chains. Conflict is the largest single driver of acute food insecurity globally. Chronic food insecurity is closely linked to structural poverty and the work of economist Amartya Sen, who showed that famines often result from failures of access and entitlement rather than absolute food shortage.

Responses

Responses range from humanitarian food aid and social safety nets to investments in agricultural productivity, storage, infrastructure, climate adaptation and food sovereignty movements advocating local control over food systems. Sustainable Development Goal 2 ("Zero Hunger") aims to end hunger and achieve food security by 2030.
