RF power amplifier
Function: Amplifies radio-frequency signals to high power
Applications: Transmitters, radar, broadcasting
Devices: LDMOS, GaN, GaAs transistors; vacuum tubes

An RF power amplifier (RFPA) is a type of electronic amplifier that converts a low-power radio-frequency signal into a higher-power signal. Typically, RF power amplifiers are used to drive the antenna of a transmitter. Design goals include gain, power-output capability, efficiency, and linearity, with trade-offs between them depending on the application.

Applications

RF power amplifiers are found in broadcast transmitters (FM and television), cellular base stations and handsets, two-way radios, radar systems, satellite uplinks, and amateur radio equipment. In modulated systems such as those using OFDM, high linearity is required, often achieved with techniques like Doherty amplifier architectures or digital predistortion.

Technologies

Modern solid-state amplifiers use LDMOS, gallium nitride (GaN), and gallium arsenide (GaAs) transistors. Vacuum tubes such as klystrons and traveling-wave tubes remain in use for very high-power or high-frequency applications. Amplifier classes (A, AB, C, and switching classes such as E and F) describe the conduction behavior of the active device.
