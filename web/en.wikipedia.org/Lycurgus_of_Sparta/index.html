Lycurgus of Sparta

Lycurgus
Greek: Λυκοῦργος
Flourished: traditionally 9th–8th century BC
Known for: legendary lawgiver of Sparta

Lycurgus (Greek: Λυκοῦργος) was the legendary lawgiver of Sparta, credited by ancient sources with establishing the militarised social order, communal institutions and unwritten laws (the rhetra) that defined Spartan society.

Legend and sources

Plutarch's Life of Lycurgus is the fullest account, though Plutarch admits that nothing about him can be stated "without dispute". Tradition held that, as regent for his nephew, Lycurgus travelled to Crete, Ionia and Egypt before reforming Sparta with the sanction of the Delphic oracle.

Reforms attributed to him

The reforms ascribed to Lycurgus include the creation of the Gerousia (council of elders), the redistribution of land into equal lots, communal messes (syssitia), the harsh upbringing of boys in the agoge, and the discouragement of luxury and coined money.

Historicity

Modern scholars doubt whether Lycurgus was a single historical person; the "Lycurgan" system probably evolved over centuries and was retroactively attributed to one founder.
