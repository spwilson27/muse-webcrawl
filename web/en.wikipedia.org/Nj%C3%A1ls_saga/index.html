Njáls saga

Njáls saga (modern Icelandic pronunciation: [ˈnjauls ˌsaːɣa]), also known as The Story of Burnt Njáll, is a thirteenth-century Icelandic saga that describes events between 960 and 1020 AD. It is widely considered the greatest of the Sagas of Icelanders.

Synopsis

The saga follows the fortunes of Njáll Þorgeirsson, a wise lawyer famed for his foresight, and Gunnar Hámundarson, a peerless warrior. A feud involving their wives escalates through killings and legal battles until Njáll and his family are burned inside their home at Bergþórshvoll — the event that gives the saga its alternative title. The avenger Kári Sölmundarson then pursues the burners across Iceland and abroad.

Themes and authorship

The saga explores the tension between the old heroic code of honour and the new Christian ethic of forgiveness, and displays an unusually sophisticated understanding of Icelandic law. Its author is unknown; it was probably composed between about 1270 and 1290. The oldest surviving manuscript fragments date from around 1300.

Legacy

Njáls saga is the longest and most celebrated of the family sagas and has been translated many times. Sites associated with it in southern Iceland, such as Bergþórshvoll and Flosi's valley, remain places of literary pilgrimage.
