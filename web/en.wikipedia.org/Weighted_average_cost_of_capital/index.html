Weighted average cost of capital

Weighted average cost of capital
AbbreviationWACC
FieldCorporate finance
ComponentsCost of equity, after-tax cost of debt
Used asDiscount rate in DCF valuation

WACC weights the cost of each source of capital by its share of the firm's financing.

The weighted average cost of capital (WACC) is the rate that a company is expected to pay on average to all its security holders to finance its assets. The WACC is commonly referred to as the firm's cost of capital. Importantly, it is dictated by the external market and not by management.

Calculation

In general, WACC is calculated as the sum over each category of capital, proportionately weighted:

WACC = (E/V × Re) + (D/V × Rd × (1 − Tc))

where E is the market value of equity, D is the market value of debt, V = E + D, Re is the cost of equity, Rd is the cost of debt, and Tc is the corporate tax rate. The cost of equity is often estimated with the capital asset pricing model (CAPM), while the cost of debt is based on the yield to maturity of the firm's borrowing, adjusted for the tax deductibility of interest.

Use

WACC is widely used as the discount rate in discounted cash flow (DCF) valuation and as a hurdle rate for investment decisions: a project is expected to create value if its return exceeds the WACC. Because its inputs (especially the cost of equity) are estimates, the WACC is sensitive to assumptions about capital structure, beta, and market risk premium.
