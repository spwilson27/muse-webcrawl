George Eastman (July 12, 1854 – March 14, 1932) was an American entrepreneur who founded the Eastman Kodak Company and helped bring photography to the mainstream. He invented roll film in 1884/88, replacing cumbersome photographic plates, and in 1888 introduced the Kodak camera with the slogan "You press the button, we do the rest."

Early life and Kodak

Born in Waterville, New York, Eastman left school at fourteen to support his family and worked as a bank clerk in Rochester. Frustrated by the equipment needed for a planned photography trip, he experimented with dry-plate emulsions, patenting a plate-coating machine in 1879. The Eastman Dry Plate Company followed in 1881. His rolled transparent film and the simple box Kodak camera created the mass market for amateur photography; the inexpensive Brownie camera of 1900 put cameras in the hands of children.

Later ventures and philanthropy

Eastman built Kodak into the dominant photographic company of the twentieth century, supplying film stock for the motion picture industry. A major philanthropist, he gave away more than $100 million, much of it to the University of Rochester and MIT, largely anonymously under the name "Mr. Smith". He founded the Eastman School of Music and supported dental clinics and Tuskegee Institute. Suffering from a degenerative spinal condition, Eastman took his own life in 1932, leaving his Rochester mansion — now the George Eastman Museum — to the University of Rochester.
