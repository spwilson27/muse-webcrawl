Jerry Dammers


Jeremy David Hounsell Dammers (born 22 May 1955) is a British musician, founder of the Specials and the 2 Tone record label.

Jeremy David Hounsell Dammers (born 22 May 1955) is a British musician who was a founder, keyboard player, and primary songwriter of the Coventry ska revival band the Specials. He also founded the record label 2 Tone Records, which launched the late-1970s ska revival in Britain and released records by the Specials, the Selecter, Madness, the Beat, and others.

Early life

Dammers was born in Ootacamund, India, where his father, a clergyman, was posted, and grew up in Coventry, England. He studied art at Coventry Polytechnic (now Coventry University), where he began forming bands and developing the multiracial, mod-influenced aesthetic that became 2 Tone's signature black-and-white checkerboard imagery.

The Specials and 2 Tone

The Specials' debut album (1979), produced by Elvis Costello, combined ska rhythms with punk energy and pointed social commentary. Dammers wrote many of the band's best-known songs, including "Gangsters," "A Message to You, Rudy" (a cover), "Too Much Too Young," and the haunting hit "Ghost Town" (1981), which topped the UK chart as riots swept British cities. After most of the band left, Dammers continued as the Special AKA, scoring a major hit with "Free Nelson Mandela" (1984), a protest song that became an anthem of the anti-apartheid movement.

Later work

Dammers has worked as a DJ and bandleader; in the 2000s he formed the Sun Ra-inspired Spatial AKA Orchestra, performing cosmic jazz. He has remained a prominent anti-racism campaigner and was a leading figure in the UK branch of Artists Against Apartheid in the 1980s, helping organise major concerts calling for Mandela's release.
