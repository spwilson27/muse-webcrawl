Spintronics (spin transport electronics), also known as spinelectronics, is the study of the intrinsic spin of the electron and its associated magnetic moment, in addition to its fundamental electric charge, in solid-state devices. Unlike conventional electronics, which exploits only charge, spintronics aims to use spin states to store, carry and process information.

History

The field took off with the discovery of giant magnetoresistance (GMR) in 1988 by Albert Fert and Peter Grünberg, who shared the 2007 Nobel Prize in Physics. GMR read heads greatly increased hard-disk storage density in the 1990s. Subsequent milestones include tunnelling magnetoresistance, spin-transfer torque, and the spin Hall effect.

Devices

Spin valves and magnetic tunnel junctions sense or store data by the relative magnetisation of ferromagnetic layers; magnetoresistive random-access memory (MRAM) uses them as non-volatile memory. Proposed devices, such as the Datta–Das spin transistor, would switch currents by precessing electron spins rather than moving charge, promising lower power consumption. Spin qubits are also a leading platform in quantum information science.
