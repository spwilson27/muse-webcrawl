Pay-per-click


Sponsored results in a search engine

Pay-per-click (PPC) is an internet advertising model in which an advertiser pays a publisher a fee each time one of its advertisements is clicked. It is most commonly associated with search engine advertising, where advertisers bid on keywords so that their ads appear among the sponsored results when users search those terms; Google Ads is the largest PPC platform.

How it works

When a user enters a search query, the search engine runs an instantaneous auction among advertisers bidding on matching keywords. Ad position is usually determined by a combination of the bid and a quality measure — Google's Ad Rank multiplies the maximum cost-per-click bid by a Quality Score based on expected click-through rate, ad relevance, and landing-page experience. The advertiser pays only when a user actually clicks, and the price paid is typically just enough to beat the next advertiser's Ad Rank.

History

The PPC model emerged in the mid-1990s; GoTo.com (later Overture) launched a prominent paid-placement search engine in 1998, and Google introduced AdWords in 2000, switching to a PPC auction model in 2002. PPC became the dominant revenue model of the web, funding the search advertising industry.

Issues

Advertisers contend with click fraud — artificial clicks meant to exhaust a rival's budget or generate publisher revenue — and with ongoing debates about trademark bidding, ad blocking, and the privacy implications of behavioral targeting used alongside PPC campaigns.
