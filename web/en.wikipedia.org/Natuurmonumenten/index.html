Vereniging Natuurmonumenten (English: Society for the Preservation of Nature Monuments in the Netherlands) is a Dutch nature conservation organisation founded in 1905. It is one of the largest landowners in the Netherlands, managing more than 350 nature reserves covering over 110,000 hectares of woodland, heath, wetland, and coastal habitat.

History

The society was founded in response to a plan by the city of Amsterdam to use the Naardermeer lake as a rubbish dump. Biologist Jac. P. Thijsse and others campaigned to save the lake, and in 1906 Natuurmonumenten purchased the Naardermeer as its first reserve — a landmark in European conservation. The organisation pioneered the idea that citizens could collectively buy land to protect nature.

Reserves and activities

Well-known reserves include the Wadden Sea islands' marshes, the Veluwe forests and heathlands, the Oostvaardersplassen wetland, and the estate landscapes around 's-Graveland, where the society long had its headquarters. Natuurmonumenten opens most of its land to the public with walking and cycling routes, visitor centres, and ranger-led activities, and it campaigns on Dutch nature policy alongside partner organisations.

Membership

The association has several hundred thousand members and supporters and works with the twelve provincial landscape organisations (Provinciale Landschappen) that manage additional reserves in each province.
