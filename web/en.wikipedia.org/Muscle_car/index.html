Muscle car

Muscle car is a term for a category of high-performance American automobiles, typically rear-wheel-drive mid-size or full-size cars fitted with a large, powerful V8 engine. The classic muscle car era ran from the mid-1960s to the early 1970s, beginning conventionally with the 1964 Pontiac GTO.

Muscle car
Origin: United States
Classic era: 1964–1971/72
Layout: Front-engine, rear-wheel drive
Typical engine: large-displacement V8
Examples: Pontiac GTO, Dodge Charger, Ford Mustang (pony car), Chevrolet Chevelle SS

History

Precursors included the 1949 Oldsmobile Rocket 88 and the high-performance intermediates of the early 1960s. Pontiac's 1964 GTO package — a big 389 cu in V8 in the mid-size Tempest/Le Mans body — created the template and triggered a Detroit-wide horsepower war. Iconic models followed: Dodge Charger and Coronet R/T, Plymouth Road Runner and GTX, Chevrolet Chevelle SS and Camaro, Ford Torino and Mustang Mach 1, and specialty machines such as the Plymouth Superbird built for NASCAR homologation.

Decline and revival

Rising insurance premiums, tightening emissions rules, and the 1973 oil crisis ended the era; power ratings fell sharply through the 1970s. High-performance rear-drive American coupes returned in modern form from the 2000s, with cars like the Dodge Challenger and Charger Hellcat exceeding 700 horsepower, direct descendants of the muscle car formula. Australia produced its own muscle cars, such as the Ford Falcon GT and Holden Monaro.

Definitions

Enthusiasts distinguish "pure" muscle cars (intermediate bodies with big engines) from pony cars (the smaller Mustang and Camaro class) and full-size performance models, though the term is often used loosely for any powerful American V8 coupe.
