The Aṣṭādhyāyī ("Eight Chapters") is a celebrated Sanskrit treatise on grammar attributed to the ancient Indian scholar Pāṇini, composed around the 5th to 4th century BCE. It consists of approximately 3,959 aphoristic rules (sūtras) organized into eight chapters of four sections each, and provides a complete, generative description of the morphology and syntax of Sanskrit.

Method

Pāṇini's grammar is remarkable for its extreme economy and formal rigour. It employs a metalanguage of technical terms, markers (anubandhas), recursion and ordered rule application, deriving correct word forms from roots and affixes by a rule system modern scholars have compared to a Turing machine or to modern formal grammar in computer science. Supporting texts include the Śivasūtras, which organize the sounds of Sanskrit into an efficient sequence, and traditional lists of verbal roots and word classes.

Influence

The Aṣṭādhyāyī anchored the Indian grammatical tradition for more than two millennia, generating a vast commentarial literature beginning with Kātyāyana and Patañjali. When it became known to European scholars in the 19th century, it profoundly influenced the birth of modern linguistics, impressing figures such as Leonard Bloomfield with its precision.
