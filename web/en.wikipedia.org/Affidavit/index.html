Affidavit

An affidavit is a written statement of fact voluntarily made by an affiant or deponent under an oath or affirmation which is administered by a person who is authorized to do so by law. Such a statement is witnessed as to the authenticity of the affiant's signature by a taker of oaths, such as a notary public or commissioner of oaths. An affidavit is a type of verified statement or showing, or in legal parlance a deposition.

Uses

Affidavits are used in court proceedings to present evidence when live testimony is impractical, in applications for warrants and injunctions, and in many administrative and immigration matters. A false statement in an affidavit may constitute perjury.

History

The word derives from Medieval Latin affidavit, "he has declared on oath," the third-person perfect of affidare. The practice developed in English common law and spread throughout common-law jurisdictions.
