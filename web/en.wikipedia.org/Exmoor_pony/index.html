Exmoor pony

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Exmoor pony is a breed of pony native to the British Isles, where it has grazed on Exmoor, a moorland area in Devon and Somerset in south-west England, for centuries. It is one of the oldest and most primitive of the British moorland pony breeds, and some herds still run semi-feral on the moor.

Characteristics

Exmoor ponies are stocky and strongly built, standing about 11.3 to 12.3 hands high. Their coat is bay, brown, or dun with pangaré markings — a pale, mealy-coloured muzzle, rings around the eyes, and a light belly and flanks. They grow a thick, waterproof double winter coat, and have distinctive features including a "toad eye" with a fleshy hood over the eye, said to shed rain, and a short, thick "ice tail" with a fan of coarse hair at the top. The breed is noted for hardiness, stamina, and sure-footedness.

History and conservation

Ponies have been recorded on Exmoor since Domesday, and the breed society was founded in 1921 to preserve the free-living herds, identified by brands and annual gatherings. Numbers fell dangerously low during and after the Second World War, when the breed came close to extinction, and it remains listed as endangered by the Rare Breeds Survival Trust. Exmoors are used for riding, driving, and conservation grazing, and free-living herds have been introduced to nature reserves in Britain and elsewhere in Europe.
