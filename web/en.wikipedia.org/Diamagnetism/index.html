Diamagnetism

Diamagnetism is the property of materials that are repelled by a magnetic field; an applied magnetic field creates an induced magnetic field in them in the opposite direction, causing a repulsive force. It is a quantum mechanical effect present in all materials, but usually very weak compared with paramagnetism or ferromagnetism.

History

Diamagnetism was first observed by Anton Brugmans in 1778 and was named and studied by Michael Faraday in 1845, who found that many materials exhibit a weak repulsion by magnetic fields.

Properties

Diamagnetic materials have a relative magnetic permeability less than or equal to 1 and a negative magnetic susceptibility. Strong diamagnetic materials include bismuth, copper, water, and pyrolytic carbon. Superconductors exhibit perfect diamagnetism (the Meissner effect), expelling magnetic fields entirely.

Levitation

Because the effect, though weak, acts on all matter, diamagnetic levitation can be achieved in very strong magnetic fields; a living frog was famously levitated in a 16-tesla field at the University of Nijmegen in 1997.
