The Orinoco (Spanish: Río Orinoco) is one of the longest rivers in South America, flowing about 2,250 km in a great arc from the Guiana Highlands of Venezuela to its vast delta on the Atlantic Ocean. Roughly three quarters of its basin lies in Venezuela and the remainder in Colombia. It is the fourth-largest river in the world by discharge volume.

Course

The Orinoco rises in the Parima Mountains near the Brazil–Venezuela border. Its upper course includes the remarkable Casiquiare canal, a natural distributary that links the Orinoco to the Rio Negro and thus to the Amazon basin — the largest river bifurcation on Earth. Below the Atures and Maipures rapids near Puerto Ayacucho the river turns east, receiving great tributaries including the Meta, Apure, Ventuari and Caroní.

Delta and ecology

The Orinoco Delta, covering some 22,500 km², is a fan of distributaries through mangrove and swamp forest, home to the Warao people. The basin supports exceptional biodiversity, including the Orinoco crocodile, Amazon river dolphins and the giant otter. The Angel Falls, the world's tallest waterfall, lie on a tributary of the Caroní.

Economy

The river is a major transport artery; Ciudad Guayana, at the confluence with the Caroní, is an industrial centre built on the Guri hydroelectric dam, and the extra-heavy crude of the Orinoco Belt to the north is among the world's largest petroleum deposits.
