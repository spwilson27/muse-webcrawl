Tycho Brahe (born Tyge Ottesen Brahe; 1546–1601) was a Danish astronomer known for his comprehensive and unprecedentedly accurate astronomical observations, made without a telescope. His data enabled his assistant Johannes Kepler to discover the laws of planetary motion.

Life

Born into Danish nobility, Tycho lost the bridge of his nose in a student duel at Rostock in 1566 and wore a metal prosthesis thereafter. His observation of the "new star" of 1572 (now known as a supernova, SN 1572), which showed no measurable parallax and therefore lay beyond the Moon, shattered the Aristotelian doctrine of the unchanging heavens. A great comet in 1577 gave a similar result.

Uraniborg and observations

King Frederick II granted Tycho the island of Hven, where he built the observatory-palace Uraniborg and the underground Stjerneborg, equipping them with giant quadrants, sextants and armillary spheres of his own design. For over twenty years his team measured star and planet positions to within about one arcminute — five times better than previous catalogues.

System and death

Tycho rejected the Copernican system on observational and physical grounds and proposed his own geo-heliocentric model, in which the Sun and Moon orbit Earth while the other planets orbit the Sun. After falling out with Christian IV he left Denmark in 1597, becoming imperial astronomer at Prague, where Kepler joined him in 1600. He died there in 1601 after a banquet, traditionally from a burst bladder; modern analysis of exhumed remains (2010) found no evidence for the old suspicion of mercury poisoning.
