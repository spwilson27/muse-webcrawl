Decentralisation in Ukraine

Decentralisation in Ukraine is a programme of administrative and fiscal reform begun in 2014 that transferred substantial powers, budgets, and responsibilities from central and regional state administrations to newly formed local self-government bodies known as amalgamated territorial communities (hromadas).

Background

Ukraine inherited a highly centralised system of government from the Soviet period. Following the Revolution of Dignity in 2014, the new government identified decentralisation as a key reform to improve public services, curb corruption, and bring decision-making closer to citizens.

The reform

The reform proceeded in stages. Laws passed in 2014 and 2015 allowed villages, settlements, and towns to voluntarily amalgamate into larger hromadas with elected councils and mayors. Amendments to the Budget and Tax Codes gave local governments a share of personal income tax and other revenues, greatly increasing their budgets.

Completion in 2020

In 2020 the voluntary phase ended: the government defined 1,469 hromadas covering the whole country, and the number of raions (districts) was reduced from 490 to 136. Local elections in October 2020 were held on the new territorial basis.

Assessment

The reform is widely regarded as one of Ukraine's most successful post-2014 changes. Empowered local governments played a notable role in organising community resistance and humanitarian relief after Russia's full-scale invasion in 2022.
