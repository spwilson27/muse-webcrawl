Elinor Ostrom

Elinor Claire Ostrom (née Awan; 7 August 1933 – 12 June 2012) was an American political scientist and political economist whose work on the governance of common-pool resources challenged the conventional view that shared resources must be managed either by the state or by privatisation. In 2009 she shared the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences, becoming the first woman to win the prize in that category.

Education and career

Ostrom was born in Los Angeles, California, and earned her BA, MA and PhD in political science at the University of California, Los Angeles. In 1965 she joined the faculty of Indiana University, where with her husband Vincent Ostrom she co-founded the Workshop in Political Theory and Policy Analysis. Her early research examined water resource management in Southern California groundwater basins.

Governing the Commons

Her landmark book Governing the Commons (1990) analysed long-enduring irrigation systems, fisheries, forests and grazing commons around the world. She identified eight design principles characteristic of robust self-governing institutions, including clearly defined boundaries, monitoring, graduated sanctions, and accessible conflict-resolution mechanisms. Her findings offered an empirical counterpoint to the "tragedy of the commons" model.

Later work and legacy

Ostrom developed the Institutional Analysis and Development framework and advocated polycentric approaches to problems such as climate change, arguing that governance at multiple scales can outperform purely global or purely local arrangements. She died in Bloomington, Indiana, in 2012.
