Dick Durbin

Richard Joseph Durbin (born November 21, 1944) is an American lawyer and politician serving as the senior United States senator from Illinois, a seat he has held since 1997. A member of the Democratic Party, Durbin has served as Senate Democratic whip since 2005, the second-highest ranking position among Senate Democrats, and as chair of the Senate Judiciary Committee.

Early life and career

Durbin was born in East St. Louis, Illinois, to an Irish-American father and a Lithuanian-born mother. He graduated from Georgetown University and Georgetown University Law Center, worked as a legal counsel in the Illinois state government, and was first elected to the U.S. House of Representatives in 1982, where he served seven terms.

U.S. Senate

Durbin won election to the Senate in 1996, succeeding retiring senator Paul Simon, his longtime mentor. He has been a leading voice on immigration reform — he first introduced the DREAM Act in 2001 — as well as on tobacco regulation, gun control, and criminal justice reform. He voted against authorizing the Iraq War in 2002. In April 2025, Durbin announced that he would not seek re-election in 2026.
