Corfu (Greek: Κέρκυρα, Kérkyra) is a Greek island in the Ionian Sea, the second largest of the Ionian Islands, lying off the northwest coast of Greece near the Albanian border. Its principal city, also called Corfu, is home to the Ionian University, and the island's Old Town is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Corfu is a major tourist destination with a lush, green landscape unusual among Greek islands.

History

In antiquity Corfu was identified with Scheria, the island of the Phaeacians in Homer's Odyssey. Corinthian colonists founded Korkyra around 734 BC; its quarrel with Corinth helped trigger the Peloponnesian War. The island passed through Roman, Byzantine, and Norman hands before nearly four centuries of Venetian rule (1386–1797), during which it famously repelled repeated Ottoman sieges — the great siege of 1716 was broken with Imperial help under Count von der Schulenburg. After brief French and Russo-Ottoman periods, Corfu became part of the British protectorate of the United States of the Ionian Islands (1815–1864) before union with the Kingdom of Greece. In 1923 Mussolini's Italy briefly bombarded and occupied the island in the Corfu incident.

Geography and sights

The island is about 64 km long with a rugged mountainous north — Mount Pantokrator rises to 906 m — and rolling, olive-covered hills in the south. Notable sites include the Old and New Venetian fortresses of Corfu town, the Achilleion palace built for Empress Elisabeth of Austria, the monastery islet of Vlacherna with Pontikonisi (Mouse Island), and resorts such as Paleokastritsa and Sidari. The writer Gerald Durrell spent part of his childhood on Corfu, described in My Family and Other Animals.
