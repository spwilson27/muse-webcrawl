The Obie Awards (short for "Off-Broadway") are annual awards presented by the newspaper The Village Voice, together with the American Theatre Wing, honouring achievement in Off-Broadway and Off-Off-Broadway theatre in New York City.

History

The awards were created in 1955 by Village Voice theatre critic Jerry Tallmer, shortly after the paper's founding, to recognize the flourishing experimental theatre scene developing outside the commercial Broadway district. The first awards were given in 1956. Unlike the Tony Awards, which cover Broadway productions, the Obies celebrate work in smaller venues, and from 2014 the American Theatre Wing — co-presenter of the Tonys — joined the Voice in presenting them.

Categories and format

The Obies have no fixed categories and no nominees announced in advance; a panel of judges simply cites the season's most distinctive achievements in performance, direction, design, playwriting and sustained excellence. Special citations and grants are also given, including the Ross Wetzsteon Award for innovative theatre companies and awards for lifetime achievement and sustained excellence of performance. Because categories are created at the judges' discretion, the number and nature of awards vary from year to year.

Significance

Many artists who later achieved wide fame first gained recognition through the Obies, and the awards are regarded as the highest honour in Off-Broadway theatre. Past recipients include playwrights such as Edward Albee, Sam Shepard and Tony Kushner, and performers including Meryl Streep and many others early in their careers.
