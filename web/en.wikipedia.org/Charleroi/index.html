Charleroi

Charleroi is a city and municipality in the province of Hainaut, in the French-speaking region of Wallonia, Belgium. With a population of around 200,000 in the municipality and roughly half a million in its metropolitan area, it is one of the largest cities in Belgium. The city lies on the river Sambre, about 50 kilometres south of Brussels.

History

Charleroi grew from a fortress founded in 1666 by the Spanish Netherlands and named in honour of King Charles II of Spain. The fortress changed hands repeatedly in the wars of the late seventeenth and eighteenth centuries. In the nineteenth century the surrounding coal basin, iron and steel works, and glass industry turned Charleroi into one of the engines of Belgium's industrial revolution; the region was nicknamed the Pays Noir ("Black Country") for its coal mines and spoil heaps.

Economy and decline

The decline of coal mining and heavy steel in the later twentieth century hit Charleroi hard, leaving high unemployment and urban decay. Brussels South Charleroi Airport, a major base for low-cost airlines, is one of the region's largest employers, and the city has invested in regeneration, including the aeronautical and technology parks around Gosselies and a restructured city centre.

Transport and culture

Charleroi is served by a light metro network and by Charleroi-Central railway station. The city is home to the Royal Sporting Club Charleroi football club and to BPS22, a major museum of contemporary art housed in a former industrial hall.
