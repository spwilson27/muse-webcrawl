Rice flour

Rice flour (also rice powder) is a form of flour made from finely milled rice. It is distinct from rice starch, which is usually produced by steeping rice in lye. Rice flour is a common substitute for wheat flour and is widely used in the cuisines of China, Japan, Thailand, Vietnam and other parts of Southeast Asia.

Types

Rice flour may be made from white rice or brown rice. White rice flour is refined and polished, with the bran and germ removed, while brown rice flour retains them. Glutinous rice flour, made from glutinous rice, is used for chewy preparations such as mochi, despite containing no gluten; the name refers to its glue-like consistency when cooked.

Uses

Rice flour is used in rice noodles, rice paper wrappers, cakes, and as a thickening agent in soups and sauces. Because it contains no gluten, it is a staple of gluten-free diets and baking for people with coeliac disease. In Western baking it is often blended with other gluten-free flours to improve texture.

Production

Commercial rice flour is produced by dry or wet milling of broken rice grains. Wet milling yields a finer, less damaged starch that performs better in noodles and cakes.
