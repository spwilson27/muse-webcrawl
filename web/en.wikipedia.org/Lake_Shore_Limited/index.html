The Lake Shore Limited is a long-distance passenger train operated by Amtrak between New York City and Chicago via Albany and Buffalo, roughly following the historic route of the New York Central Railroad's famed 20th Century Limited. A separate section splits at Albany–Rensselaer to serve Boston, making it one of Amtrak's few trains with two eastern termini.

History

The train traces its ancestry to the New York Central's Lake Shore Limited, which began in 1897. Amtrak revived the name in 1971, initially running between New York and Chicago; the Boston section was added in 1975. The route passes along the Hudson River, the Erie Canal corridor, and the southern shore of Lake Erie, giving the train its name.

Route

Westbound trains leave Penn Station in New York, travel up the Empire Corridor to Albany, then continue through Syracuse, Rochester, and Buffalo before crossing into Ohio and stopping at Cleveland and Toledo. The train then runs through northern Indiana to Chicago Union Station. The full journey takes roughly 19 to 20 hours.

Equipment

The train typically uses Amfleet coaches, a Viewliner sleeping car or cars, a dining car, and a café car. East of Albany the New York section is hauled by a dual-mode P32AC-DM locomotive able to use third-rail power into Penn Station.
