Skanderbeg

Gjergj Kastrioti Skanderbeg Born: 6 May 1405, Principality of Kastrioti
Died: 17 January 1468 (aged 62), Lezhë
Role: Lord of Albania; leader of the League of Lezhë
Known for: Resistance to the Ottoman Empire, 1443–1468

Gjergj Kastrioti (6 May 1405 – 17 January 1468), commonly known as Skanderbeg, was an Albanian feudal lord and military commander who led a rebellion against the Ottoman Empire in what is today Albania, North Macedonia, Greece, Kosovo, Montenegro and Serbia. He is regarded as Albania's national hero.

Early life

Skanderbeg was born into the noble Kastrioti family. As a boy he was taken hostage to the Ottoman court, where he converted to Islam, received a military education and was given the name Iskender (after Alexander the Great) and the rank of bey — from which his name Skanderbeg derives. He distinguished himself in Ottoman service in campaigns in Anatolia and the Balkans.

Rebellion and the League of Lezhë

In November 1443, during the crusade of Varna, Skanderbeg deserted the Ottoman army with several hundred Albanian soldiers, seized the fortress of Krujë by a forged letter, and renounced Islam for Christianity. In March 1444 he convened the Albanian princes at Lezhë, forming the League of Lezhë, a confederation that resisted Ottoman expansion for decades. Using guerrilla tactics in Albania's mountainous terrain, his much smaller forces won victories against Ottoman armies at Torvioll (1444), Mokra, Otonetë and elsewhere, and withstood major sieges of Krujë in 1450 and 1466–67.

Death and legacy

Skanderbeg died of malaria at Lezhë in January 1468. His resistance delayed the Ottoman advance into western Europe and won him fame across Christendom; popes praised him as an "athlete of Christ". After his death Albanian resistance collapsed within a decade, but Skanderbeg became the central figure of Albanian national identity, celebrated in literature, in the Skanderbeg Monument in Tirana, and on the Albanian lek.
