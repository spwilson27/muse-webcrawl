Algorithmic art

Algorithmic art, also known as computer-generated art or generative art, is art created by means of an algorithm — a set of instructions executed by a computer that produces images, sounds, or other works with a degree of autonomy. The artist designs the system and its rules; the specific output may be unpredictable in detail.

History

Early practitioners in the 1960s included Frieder Nake, Georg Nees, and A. Michael Noll, who used mainframe computers and pen plotters to produce geometric drawings. Vera Molnár and Manfred Mohr developed plotter-based practices exploring order and randomness. The term "algorist" was adopted by a group of artists in the 1990s. Later, programming environments such as Processing (2001) made generative techniques widely accessible, and fractal art, evolutionary art, and — in the 2020s — AI-based generative models expanded the field, though debate continues about how far such systems involve artistic authorship.

Techniques

Common techniques include recursive subdivision, L-systems for plant-like growth, cellular automata, particle systems, random walks with aesthetic constraints, and iterative function systems for fractals. Randomness is often used but controlled through parameters and curation of outputs.
