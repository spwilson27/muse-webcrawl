Racing shell

Racing shell
Type: Rowing boat
Classes: Single scull (1x) to eight (8+)
Length (eight): about 17–18 m
Materials: Carbon-fibre composite (modern)

A racing shell is a long, narrow, lightweight boat designed for the sport of rowing. Shells are propelled by oars and steered, in larger boats, by a coxswain. Their extreme length-to-beam ratio minimizes drag, making them fast but unstable; racing eights can exceed 20 km/h over a 2,000-metre course.

Classes

Boats are classified by the number of rowers and by whether each rower uses one oar (sweep rowing) or two (sculling). Standard classes include the single scull (1x), double scull (2x), pair (2−), four (4−/4x), and eight (8+). A "+" indicates a coxed boat. The single scull is about 8.2 metres long and only about 27 cm wide at the waterline.

Construction and rigging

Modern shells are built from carbon-fibre and honeycomb composites; earlier boats were wood. Oars are mounted on outriggers ("riggers") that provide leverage, and sliding seats allow rowers to use their legs in the stroke. Minimum weights are regulated by World Rowing (for example, 96 kg for an eight).
