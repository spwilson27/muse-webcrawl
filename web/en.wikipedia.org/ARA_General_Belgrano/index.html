ARA General Belgrano (C-4) was an Argentine Navy light cruiser in service from 1951 until 1982. Originally ordered by the United States Navy as the Brooklyn-class cruiser USS Phoenix, she survived the attack on Pearl Harbor in 1941 and served in the Pacific Theater of World War II before being sold to Argentina. She is best known for being sunk by the Royal Navy submarine HMS Conqueror during the Falklands War, with the loss of 323 crew, almost half of all Argentine military deaths in the war.

Early career

As USS Phoenix (CL-46), the cruiser was moored at Pearl Harbor on 7 December 1941 and escaped undamaged. She escorted convoys and supported amphibious operations in the southwest Pacific, including at Cape Gloucester, the Admiralty Islands, and the Philippines. Sold to Argentina in October 1951 and renamed first 17 de Octubre and later General Belgrano after the Argentine independence hero Manuel Belgrano, she served as a flagship of the Argentine fleet and received Seacat surface-to-air missiles in the late 1960s.

Sinking

On 2 May 1982, during the Falklands War, the British nuclear-powered submarine HMS Conqueror, having received authorization following a change in the rules of engagement, fired three Mark 8 torpedoes at General Belgrano; two struck. The cruiser was operating outside the British-declared Total Exclusion Zone, southwest of the Falkland Islands, accompanied by two destroyers. The ship lost power, listed, and sank within about an hour. Of the 1,093 aboard, 323 were killed; the survivors endured up to two days in life rafts in heavy seas before rescue by Argentine and Chilean vessels.

Controversy and legacy

The sinking was the most controversial British action of the war because it occurred outside the exclusion zone and while Peruvian peace proposals were being discussed. The British government maintained that the cruiser group posed a threat to the task force regardless of its position and heading, and a 2003 disclosure by the ship's captain, Héctor Bonzo, confirmed that the group had orders to attack. The sinking effectively confined the Argentine surface fleet to port for the remainder of the war. In Argentina, General Belgrano is commemorated as a national symbol; its wreck, located in 2003, is treated as a war grave.
