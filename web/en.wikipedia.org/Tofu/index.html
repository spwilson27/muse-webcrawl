Tofu

Tofu
OriginChina (Han dynasty, traditionally attributed)
Main ingredientsSoy milk, coagulant (nigari or calcium sulfate)
Also known asBean curd; dòufǔ (Chinese), tōfu (Japanese), dubu (Korean)

Tofu (豆腐) is a food prepared by coagulating fresh soy milk, pressing the resulting curds into solid white blocks of varying softness—silken, soft, firm and extra-firm—and chilling them. It originated in China, where legend attributes its invention to the Han-dynasty prince Liu An (2nd century BC), and has been a staple of East and Southeast Asian cuisines for over a thousand years, spreading to Japan, Korea, Vietnam and beyond, in part through Buddhist vegetarian cooking.

Production and nutrition

Soybeans are soaked, ground and boiled to make soy milk, which is then curdled with salts such as magnesium chloride (nigari) or calcium sulfate; the curds are pressed into moulds. Tofu is low in calories, rich in protein containing all essential amino acids, and a source of iron, calcium (when calcium-set) and isoflavones. Its mild flavour absorbs the taste of marinades and sauces.

Varieties and dishes

Fresh tofu ranges from delicate silken tofu to chewy pressed and fermented forms such as stinky tofu and fermented bean curd. Famous dishes include mapo tofu from Sichuan, agedashi tofu in Japan, sundubu-jjigae in Korea, and taho in the Philippines. In Western cooking tofu is a principal meat substitute, distinct from the fermented soybean cake tempeh.
