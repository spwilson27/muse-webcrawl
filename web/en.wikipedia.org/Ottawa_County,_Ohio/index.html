Ottawa County, Ohio

Ottawa County is a county located in the northern part of the U.S. state of Ohio, along the southern shore of Lake Erie. As of the 2020 census, its population was about 40,000. The county seat and largest city is Port Clinton. The county was formed in 1840 and named for the Ottawa (Odawa) Native American tribe.

Geography

Ottawa County includes the Marblehead Peninsula and the Lake Erie Islands within its jurisdiction, among them South Bass Island (Put-in-Bay), Middle Bass Island, and Kelleys Island. Tourism and recreation around the islands and the lake shore are central to the local economy, as is the Davis–Besse Nuclear Power Station near Oak Harbor.

History

The county's Lake Erie waters saw Commodore Oliver Hazard Perry's victory in the Battle of Lake Erie in 1813, commemorated by Perry's Victory and International Peace Memorial at Put-in-Bay.
