The Gorner Glacier (German: Gornergletscher) is a valley glacier found on the west side of the Monte Rosa massif close to Zermatt in the canton of Valais, Switzerland. With a length of about 12.9 km and a surface area of roughly 48 square kilometres including its tributary system, its main tributary, the Grenzgletscher, is the second-largest glacial system in the Swiss Alps after the Aletsch Glacier.

Features

The Gorner Glacier is formed where several tributaries converge below the Gorner Gorge area; the Grenzgletscher, flowing from the Monte Rosa glaciers between the Breithorn, Castor, Pollux and Lyskamm, is its principal feeder. A distinctive feature is the Gornersee, an ice-marginal lake that forms each spring where the tributaries meet and empties suddenly during the summer. The glacier's meltwater drains through the Gornera river toward Zermatt.

Like most Alpine glaciers, the Gorner Glacier has retreated markedly since the end of the Little Ice Age, and its upper tributaries have separated, leaving debris-covered lower sections. The Gornergrat railway and the Monte Rosa Hut — a high-technology alpine refuge opened in 2009 — overlook the glacier, which is a popular sight from the Gornergrat viewpoint.
