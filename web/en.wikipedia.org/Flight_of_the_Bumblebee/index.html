Flight of the Bumblebee

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

"Flight of the Bumblebee" is an orchestral interlude written by Nikolai Rimsky-Korsakov for his opera The Tale of Tsar Saltan, composed in 1899–1900. In the opera, the music accompanies Prince Gvidon, transformed into a bumblebee, flying off to visit his father. The piece is famous for its frantic, chromatic runs and has become a showpiece for virtuoso soloists on virtually every instrument.

Music

The interlude is built almost entirely from continuous semiquaver (sixteenth-note) chromatic figures whirling around a few main notes, evoking the buzzing and darting flight of an insect. Although brief — about a minute and a half at full tempo — its relentless speed makes it a popular test of technical skill. Sergei Rachmaninoff made a well-known piano transcription, and arrangements exist for violin, flute, trumpet, guitar, and even electric guitar and human voice.

In popular culture

The piece's association with speed records and virtuosity has made it a staple of talent shows and film scores. It is the signature theme of The Green Hornet (in Al Hirt's trumpet version for the 1960s television series), and it has been used in countless cartoons and advertisements to signal frantic motion. Guitarists and pianists have competed to perform it at ever-faster tempi, and it appears in the Guinness record tradition of "fastest performance" attempts.
