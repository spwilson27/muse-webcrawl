Antagonist

An antagonist is a character in a story (or, in a broader sense, a person or force) who opposes the protagonist, the main character. The antagonist creates the obstacles, conflict and resistance that the protagonist must struggle against, and is one of the fundamental elements of narrative structure. The term derives from the Greek antagōnistēs, meaning "opponent" or "rival".

Role in narrative

The antagonist need not be a villain: it may be another character with contrary goals, a group, an institution, a force of nature, or even an aspect of the protagonist's own personality, as in stories of internal conflict. In classical Greek drama the antagonist was the second actor, the deuteragonist's counterpart, opposing the protagonist. A story's antagonist may change over the course of a narrative, and complex works may feature several antagonists or none in a conventional sense.

Types

  • Villain: an evil antagonist whose actions are morally wrong.
  • Rival: a competitor seeking the same goal as the protagonist.
  • Anti-villain: an antagonist with sympathetic or heroic qualities.
  • Force or environment: storms, disease, society or circumstance acting against the protagonist.

The antagonist is closely related to the concepts of conflict, villain and antihero.
