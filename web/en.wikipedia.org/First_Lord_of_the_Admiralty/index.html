The First Lord of the Admiralty, or formally the Office of the First Lord of the Admiralty, was the political head of the English and later British Royal Navy. The office was the senior member of the Board of Admiralty and, from the nineteenth century onward, almost invariably a member of the Cabinet. The office existed from 1628 until 1 April 1964, when the Admiralty was absorbed into the unified Ministry of Defence and the role passed to the Secretary of State for Defence.

Role and responsibilities

The First Lord was accountable to Parliament for the navy — its budget, readiness, shipbuilding programmes and strategy. Beneath him served the professional naval officers of the Board, headed by the First Sea Lord, who was the navy's senior serving officer and principal military adviser. The First Lord's official residence was Admiralty House in Whitehall.

Notable First Lords

Winston Churchill held the post twice: from 1911 to 1915, when he energetically modernised the fleet before the First World War but resigned after the Dardanelles failure, and again from September 1939 to May 1940, when he signalled the fleet the famous message "Winston is back" before becoming Prime Minister. Other prominent holders include Samuel Pepys (as Secretary to the Admiralty, its dominant administrator in the seventeenth century), Lord Barham during the Napoleonic Wars, and A. V. Alexander during the Second World War.

Abolition

The Defence reorganisation of 1964 merged the Admiralty, War Office and Air Ministry into a single Ministry of Defence. The last First Lord was the Earl of Jellicoe (1963–64); the ancient office of Lord High Admiral was thereafter assumed by the sovereign and later conferred on the Duke of Edinburgh.
