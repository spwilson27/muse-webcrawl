Shoko Asahara

Born: Chizuo Matsumoto, 2 March 1955
Died: 6 July 2018, Tokyo
Known for: Founder of Aum Shinrikyo
Conviction: Murder, terrorism (Tokyo subway sarin attack)

Shoko Asahara (麻原 彰晃; born Chizuo Matsumoto, 1955–2018) was the founder of the Japanese doomsday cult Aum Shinrikyo. Under his direction the cult carried out the 1995 Tokyo subway sarin attack, which killed 13 people and injured thousands, as well as the earlier Matsumoto sarin attack and several murders.

Early life and cult

Born partially blind into a poor family in Kumamoto Prefecture, Matsumoto studied acupuncture and Chinese medicine and ran a yoga school that developed into Aum Shinrikyo in the 1980s, blending Buddhist, Hindu and apocalyptic Christian ideas. He proclaimed himself a messianic figure and demanded absolute obedience from followers, some of whom were subjected to violence and confinement.

Crimes and trial

After the subway attack, Asahara was arrested at the cult's Mount Fuji compound. His trial, one of the longest in Japanese history, ended in 2004 with a death sentence for masterminding multiple murders. Appeals were exhausted in 2006, and he was executed by hanging on 6 July 2018, together with six other senior cult members.
