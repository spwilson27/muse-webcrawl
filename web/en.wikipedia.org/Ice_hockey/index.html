Ice hockey

Players: 6 per team on ice (incl. goaltender)
Equipment: Sticks, puck, skates, protective gear
Origin: Montreal, Canada, 1870s
Governing body: IIHF

Ice hockey is a team sport played on ice skates, usually on an ice rink with lines and markings, in which two teams of skaters use sticks to shoot a vulcanised rubber puck into the opposing goal. It evolved in 19th-century Montreal from stick-and-ball games, with the first organised indoor game played on 3 March 1875 and early rules published by McGill University students.

Gameplay

Each team fields five skaters and a goaltender. Games consist of three 20-minute periods. Body checking is permitted in men's senior play; offside and icing rules govern puck movement. Minor penalties send a player to the penalty box, giving the opponent a power play. The Stanley Cup, donated in 1893, is contested by the National Hockey League (NHL), founded 1917 and the sport's premier professional league.

International play

The International Ice Hockey Federation (1908) oversees world championships and Olympic hockey (men since 1920, women since 1998). Canada, Russia, Sweden, Finland, the United States, and Czechia are traditional powers. Women's hockey has grown rapidly, including the Professional Women's Hockey League launched in 2024.
