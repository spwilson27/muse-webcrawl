Blues scale

The term blues scale refers to several musical scales with specific characteristics of blues, jazz and rock music. The most common form is a hexatonic scale built from the minor pentatonic scale plus an added flattened fifth (♭5), the so-called “blue note”. In C, this gives C, E♭, F, G♭, G, B♭.

Forms

Other scales also called blues scales include a nine-note scale formed by combining the major and minor pentatonics, and various heptatonic versions. All share the characteristic pitch inflections — flattened thirds, fifths and sevenths — that singers and instrumentalists bend expressively in performance, so the written scale is an approximation of actual blues practice.

Usage

The blues scale is the foundation of improvisation in blues and much rock guitar playing, and it is widely used over dominant seventh chords and twelve-bar blues progressions. Because it clashes deliberately with parts of the underlying harmony, its use relies on resolution and phrasing as much as on strict chord-scale matching.
