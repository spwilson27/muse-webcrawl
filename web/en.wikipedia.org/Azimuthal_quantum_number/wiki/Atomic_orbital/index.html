Atomic orbital

An atomic orbital is a mathematical function describing the location and wave-like behaviour of an electron in an atom. In quantum mechanics an orbital is characterised by a set of quantum numbers: the principal quantum number n, the azimuthal (angular momentum) quantum number l, and the magnetic quantum number ml. The azimuthal quantum number determines the orbital's shape and its letter designation: l = 0 gives an s orbital (spherical), 1 a p orbital (dumbbell-shaped), 2 a d orbital, and 3 an f orbital.

Context: the azimuthal quantum number

This page is reached through the azimuthal quantum number topic: the value of l ranges from 0 to n − 1 for a given shell and fixes the magnitude of the electron's orbital angular momentum, √(l(l+1)) ħ. It also governs the number of angular nodes, which equals l. Each orbital can hold at most two electrons of opposite spin (the Pauli exclusion principle), so a subshell with quantum number l contains 2(2l+1) electron places across its 2l+1 orientations.

Models

Orbitals are usually drawn as surfaces enclosing a region — commonly 90% probability — within which the electron is likely to be found. They form the basis of electron configuration notation, the Aufbau principle and the structure of the periodic table, and they combine in molecular orbital theory and hybridisation models of chemical bonding.
