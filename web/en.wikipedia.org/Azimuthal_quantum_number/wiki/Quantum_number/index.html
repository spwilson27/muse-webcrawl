Quantum number

In quantum physics and chemistry, quantum numbers describe values of conserved quantities in the dynamics of a quantum system. Quantum numbers correspond to eigenvalues of operators that commute with the Hamiltonian and their values are discrete.

Electrons in atoms

Four quantum numbers can describe an electron in an atom completely: the principal quantum number n, the azimuthal quantum number l, the magnetic quantum number ml, and the spin quantum number s. Together they specify the unique quantum state of a single electron, consistent with the Pauli exclusion principle.

Other systems

Quantum numbers also arise in particle physics, where they include flavor quantum numbers such as isospin, strangeness, charm, bottomness and topness, and in molecular spectroscopy, where they describe rotational and vibrational states.
