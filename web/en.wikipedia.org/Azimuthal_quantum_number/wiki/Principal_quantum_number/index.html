Principal quantum number

In quantum mechanics, the principal quantum number (symbolized n) is one of five quantum numbers assigned to each electron in an atom to describe that electron's state. Its values are natural numbers starting from 1, making it a discrete variable.

Overview

Apart from the principal quantum number, the other quantum numbers for bound electrons are the azimuthal quantum number l, the magnetic quantum number ml, the spin quantum number s, and (for larger atoms) the projection of spin.

As n increases, the electron is found on average further from the nucleus and the energy of the electron increases. The principal quantum number corresponds to the electron shell: n = 1 is the innermost K shell, n = 2 the L shell, and so on.

Energy levels

For the hydrogen atom, the energy of an electron depends only on n, with En = −13.6 eV / n2. Each shell with principal quantum number n can hold at most 2n2 electrons.
