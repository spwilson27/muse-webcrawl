The Shawnee are an Indigenous people of North America whose language belongs to the Algonquian family. Their name derives from the Algonquian shaawanwa, meaning "southerner" or "people of the south". Historically centred in the Ohio River valley — in present-day Ohio, Kentucky, West Virginia, Indiana, and Pennsylvania — the Shawnee were known for their wide-ranging migrations and diaspora communities from Illinois to Alabama and from the Delaware River to the Savannah River. After forced removal in the 19th century, most Shawnee settled in what is now Oklahoma.

History

Early history and dispersal

Shawnee oral tradition and archaeological evidence connect them with the Fort Ancient culture of the Ohio Valley. During the upheavals of the 17th century, including the Beaver Wars, Shawnee communities dispersed widely across eastern North America — groups lived among the Creek in Alabama, in South Carolina on the Savannah River (whence the name "Savannah"), in Maryland and Delaware, and in Illinois — while many regrouped in the Ohio Country by the mid-1700s, in towns such as Chillicothe and Piqua. The Shawnee were important actors in the French and Indian War and in Lord Dunmore's War (1774), in which Chief Cornstalk's forces fought Virginia militia at the Battle of Point Pleasant; Cornstalk was murdered while under a flag of truce in 1777.

Resistance and removal

Under leaders including Blue Jacket, the Shawnee were a mainstay of the Western Confederacy that resisted American expansion after the Revolution until its defeat at Fallen Timbers (1794) and the Treaty of Greenville (1795). In the early 1800s the Shawnee brothers Tecumseh and Tenskwatawa (the Shawnee Prophet) built a pan-Indian confederacy centred at Prophetstown in Indiana, destroyed by William Henry Harrison's forces at Tippecanoe in 1811; Tecumseh was killed fighting alongside the British at the Battle of the Thames in 1813. Under pressure from treaties and the Indian Removal Act, the Ohio Shawnee ceded their remaining lands (notably Wapakoneta and Hog Creek) by the Treaty of Lewistown era settlements of 1831–1832 and removed west, eventually to Indian Territory (Oklahoma).

Modern tribal nations

Today there are three federally recognized Shawnee tribes, all headquartered in Oklahoma: the Shawnee Tribe (the former Loyal Shawnee, headquartered in Miami, Oklahoma), the Absentee Shawnee Tribe of Indians (near Shawnee, Oklahoma), and the Eastern Shawnee Tribe of Oklahoma (at Wyandotte/Seneca). A state-recognized group, the United Remnant Band, maintains a community in Ohio, where Shawnee cultural presence has revived. Total enrolled membership numbers in the several thousands, and language revitalization programmes work to sustain the Shawnee language, a Central Algonquian language closely related to Meskwaki and Kickapoo.

Culture

Traditional Shawnee society was organized into five divisions (Chillicothe, Thawikila, Mekoche, Pekowi, and Kispoko), each with assigned ceremonial and political roles, and into patrilineal name groups. Towns were led by civil chiefs, with war chiefs chosen for ability; women held significant authority over agriculture and town affairs. Ceremonies included the Bread Dance and other seasonal rituals tied to the agricultural cycle and to the creator figure Kokumthena ("Our Grandmother").
