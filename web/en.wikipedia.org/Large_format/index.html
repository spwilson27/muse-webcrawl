Large format

Large format photography refers to cameras using film or sensors of 4×5 inches (102×127 mm) or larger. The most common format is 4×5, with 5×7 and 8×10 also widely used. Most large format cameras are view cameras, with flexible bellows and movements that control focus and perspective.

Advantages

The large image area gives very high resolution, fine tonal gradation and shallow effective depth of field, while camera movements allow control of the plane of focus (Scheimpflug principle) and perspective correction valued in architecture, landscape and studio work. Photographers such as Ansel Adams popularised the format.

Practice

Sheet film is loaded in holders one sheet at a time, making the process slow and deliberate; images are composed upside-down on a ground glass screen.
