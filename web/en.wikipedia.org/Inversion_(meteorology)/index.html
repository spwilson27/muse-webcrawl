Inversion (meteorology)

In meteorology, an inversion is a deviation from the normal change of an atmospheric property with altitude. It almost always refers to a temperature inversion, in which a layer of warm air lies above cooler air near the surface, contrary to the usual decrease of temperature with height.

Causes

Surface inversions form on clear, calm nights when the ground cools by radiation faster than the air above it. Frontal inversions occur when warm air overrides a cooler air mass, and subsidence inversions develop when descending air in a high-pressure system warms adiabatically aloft.

Effects

Inversions act as a cap on convection, suppressing cloud formation and trapping pollutants near the ground, which can produce severe smog episodes such as those historically seen in Los Angeles and London. They also cause unusual sound propagation and mirages, and because they block vertical mixing they affect aviation, agriculture (frost pockets), and radio wave propagation.
