Nail (anatomy)

A nail is a claw-like plate of keratin found at the tips of the fingers and toes in most primates. Nails correspond to claws in other animals. Fingernails and toenails are made of a tough protective protein called alpha-keratin, a polymer also found in the hooves, hair and claws of vertebrates.

Structure

The nail consists of the nail plate, the nail matrix (the tissue under the cuticle from which the nail grows), the nail bed beneath the plate, the cuticle (eponychium), the nail folds, and the lunula — the pale crescent visible at the base of some nails. The free edge extends beyond the fingertip. Fingernails grow roughly four times faster than toenails, at about 3 mm per month; a fingernail regrows completely in about six months and a toenail in 12 to 18 months.

Function and health

Nails protect the sensitive fingertips, improve fine touch sensation by acting as a counterforce, and aid in grasping and manipulating small objects. Nail appearance can indicate health problems: clubbing, spoon-shaped nails (koilonychia), pitting, and discolouration may accompany systemic diseases. Common disorders include fungal infections (onychomycosis), ingrown toenails and paronychia.
