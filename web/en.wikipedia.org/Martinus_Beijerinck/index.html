Martinus Beijerinck

Martinus Willem Beijerinck (1851–1931) was a Dutch microbiologist and botanist, regarded as one of the founders of virology and of environmental microbiology.

Work

Working in Delft, where he founded the Delft school of microbiology, Beijerinck developed the enrichment culture technique, using selective media to isolate microbes with particular metabolisms. He was the first to isolate and describe nitrogen-fixing root-nodule bacteria (Rhizobium), free-living nitrogen fixers such as Azotobacter, and sulfate-reducing bacteria.

Discovery of viruses

In 1898, studying tobacco mosaic disease, Beijerinck showed the infectious agent passed through filters that retained bacteria and could diffuse through agar; he called it a contagium vivum fluidum — a living fluid contagion — coining the modern concept of a virus as an entity distinct from bacteria.

His laboratory in Delft trained generations of microbiologists, and the Beijerinckia genus of bacteria is named in his honor.
