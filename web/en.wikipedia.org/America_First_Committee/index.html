America First Committee

The America First Committee (AFC) was the foremost non-interventionist pressure group in the United States against American entry into the Second World War. Founded in September 1940 at Yale University, it claimed 800,000 paying members in 450 chapters, with its strongest support in the Midwest.

Founding and principles

The committee was founded by Yale law student R. Douglas Stuart Jr., together with other students including future president Gerald Ford and future Supreme Court justice Potter Stewart. Its principles held that the United States should build an impregnable defence, that no foreign power could successfully attack a prepared America, and that American democracy could be preserved only by keeping out of the European war. Prominent members included aviator Charles Lindbergh, its most famous spokesman, General Robert E. Wood of Sears-Roebuck, publisher William H. Regnery, and aircraft designer and senator Burton K. Wheeler.

Controversy and dissolution

The AFC was criticised by interventionists, including President Franklin D. Roosevelt's administration, and Lindbergh's September 1941 speech in Des Moines, naming "the British, the Jewish and the Roosevelt administration" as forces pushing America toward war, drew widespread charges of antisemitism and damaged the movement. The committee disbanded on 10 December 1941, three days after the Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor, and urged its members to support the war effort.
