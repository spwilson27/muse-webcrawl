Clebsch-Gordan coefficients

Clebsch–Gordan coefficients
FieldQuantum mechanics; representation theory
Named afterAlfred Clebsch, Paul Gordan
PurposeCoupling of angular momentum eigenstates
RelatedWigner 3-j symbol; Racah coefficients

In physics, the Clebsch–Gordan (C-G) coefficients are numbers that arise in angular momentum coupling in quantum mechanics. They appear as the expansion coefficients of total angular momentum eigenstates in an uncoupled tensor product basis; in other words, they express how two angular momenta combine into a state of definite total angular momentum.

Definition

Given two systems with angular momenta j1 and j2, the combined states |j m⟩ with j ranging from |j1 − j2| to j1 + j2 can be expanded in products |j1 m1⟩|j2 m2⟩ with m = m1 + m2. The expansion coefficients ⟨j1 m1 j2 m2 | j m⟩ are the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients. By the Condon–Shortley phase convention they are taken to be real, and they vanish unless the triangle condition on j1, j2, j is satisfied.

Properties

The coefficients obey orthogonality relations in both directions, reflecting the unitarity of the transformation between coupled and uncoupled bases. They are related to Wigner's 3-j symbols by a simple proportionality, and satisfy recursion relations that allow them to be computed starting from the highest-weight state j = j1 + j2, m = j, by applying the lowering operator J−.

Applications

Clebsch–Gordan coefficients are indispensable in atomic and nuclear physics: they govern the addition of orbital and spin angular momenta, the strengths of spectroscopic transitions, and selection rules. In particle physics the same mathematics, applied to isospin and to the rotation group SU(2) and its generalizations, underlies flavour symmetry classifications. The vector coupling coefficients were developed by Alfred Clebsch and Paul Gordan in the nineteenth-century invariant theory and imported into quantum mechanics in the 1920s and 1930s.
