A tripoint (or trijunction) is a geographical point at which the boundaries of three countries, states, or other administrative territories meet. There are roughly 157 international land tripoints in the world; a country with many neighbours, such as China (16) or Russia (14), has many tripoints.

Examples

Well-known tripoints include the Three Country Cairn where Norway, Sweden and Finland meet — the northernmost international tripoint in the world — and the tripoint of France, Germany and Switzerland near Basel. Rare points where four units meet are quadripoints; the Four Corners in the United States, where Utah, Colorado, Arizona and New Mexico meet, is the only true four-state point in the country and is marked by the Four Corners Monument.

Curiosities

Some tripoints lie in rivers or lakes, and a few disputed areas create would-be tripoints not internationally recognised. The tripoint of India, China and Myanmar, and points around Kashmir, remain unsettled. Boundaries converging at a single precise spot are often marked by monuments and are popular with geographical tourists.
