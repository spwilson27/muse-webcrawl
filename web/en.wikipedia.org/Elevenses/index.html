Elevenses

Elevenses is a short break taken at around 11:00 a.m. in many countries, in which tea or coffee is commonly consumed along with a light snack such as biscuits, cakes, or pastries. The custom is particularly associated with the United Kingdom and Commonwealth countries.

The word "elevenses" derives from the time of day at which the break is taken — eleven o'clock. The practice became widespread in Britain during the 19th century, when workers and office staff would pause mid-morning for refreshment between breakfast and lunch.

In popular culture

Elevenses features prominently in J. R. R. Tolkien's The Hobbit, in which Bilbo Baggins lists it among the hobbits' many daily meals: breakfast, second breakfast, elevenses, luncheon, afternoon tea, dinner, and supper. The concept was popularized for a wider audience by the film adaptation of The Lord of the Rings.

In A. A. Milne's Winnie-the-Pooh, the title character famously enjoys "a little something" at eleven o'clock, typically honey.

Similar customs

Comparable mid-morning breaks exist in many cultures, including the Spanish almuerzo, the Swedish fika, the German zweites Frühstück (second breakfast), and the North American coffee break.
