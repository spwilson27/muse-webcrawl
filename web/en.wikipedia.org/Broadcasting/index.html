Broadcasting

Broadcasting is the distribution of audio or video content to a dispersed audience via any electronic mass communications medium, usually radio waves through terrestrial radio or television stations, or via cable, satellite and the Internet. The term originally referred to the sowing of seeds by scattering them widely, and was adopted for radio transmissions in the early 20th century.

History

Broadcasting began with experimental radio transmissions, including Reginald Fessenden's early audio broadcasts in 1906 and the first scheduled radio services around 1920, such as station KDKA in Pittsburgh and the BBC, founded in 1922. Television broadcasting followed in the 1930s and became the dominant mass medium after the Second World War. Colour television spread in the 1950s and 1960s, cable and satellite expanded channel choice from the 1980s, and digital broadcasting replaced analogue transmission in most countries during the 2000s and 2010s.

Methods of broadcasting

Terrestrial broadcasting uses local transmitters and antennas. Cable and satellite systems deliver signals to subscribers over wired and orbital relays. Internet broadcasting, including streaming and podcasting, transmits media over packet-switched networks to computers and mobile devices.

Economics and regulation

Broadcasters are funded by advertising, subscription fees, licence fees or public taxation. Most countries license the radio spectrum, and many impose public-service obligations on broadcasters. Prominent broadcasting organisations include the BBC, NHK, PBS, CNN and Al Jazeera.
