Magellan's Cross

Location: Magallanes Street, Cebu City, Philippines
Material: Tindalo wood (outer casing)
Erected: 1521 (commemorating the arrival of Ferdinand Magellan)

Magellan's Cross (Spanish: Cruz de Magallanes) is a Christian cross planted by Portuguese and Spanish explorers led by Ferdinand Magellan upon their arrival in Cebu, Philippines, on 21 April 1521. It is one of the most famous historical landmarks of Cebu City and a symbol of the introduction of Christianity to the Philippines.

History

Magellan arrived in the Philippines in March 1521 during his expedition to circumnavigate the globe for Spain. After converting Rajah Humabon, the ruler of Cebu, and many of his subjects to Christianity, Magellan ordered a cross to be planted to mark the event. The original cross is believed to be encased inside the present tindalo wood cross, which is housed in a small octagonal pavilion, or kiosk, beside the Basilica Minore del Santo Niño.

Local belief held that chips of the original cross possessed miraculous healing powers, prompting devotees over the centuries to chip away pieces of it. To protect what remained, the cross was encased in the hollow tindalo cross visible today, a measure traditionally credited to the Augustinian friars.

The pavilion

The cross stands inside a stone pavilion built in the 19th century. The ceiling of the pavilion is decorated with paintings depicting the baptism of Rajah Humabon and the planting of the cross. Candles and offerings are frequently left by devotees at its base. The pavilion was damaged by the 2013 Bohol earthquake that also harmed the nearby basilica, and was subsequently repaired.

Significance

Along with the adjacent Magellan expedition sites and the Basilica del Santo Niño, the cross anchors Cebu's claim as the cradle of Christianity in the Far East. It is a regular stop on heritage tours of the city and featured prominently in the quincentennial commemorations of 2021.
