The Arab League (Arabic: الجامعة العربية), formally the League of Arab States, is a regional organisation of Arab states in the Middle East and North Africa. It was founded in Cairo on 22 March 1945, initially with six members — Egypt, Iraq, Transjordan, Lebanon, Saudi Arabia, and Syria — with Yemen joining shortly after. It now has 22 members. The League's stated aims are to coordinate members' policies, promote their common interests, safeguard their independence and sovereignty, and strengthen political, economic, cultural, and social ties among Arab peoples.

History

The League grew out of wartime discussions of Arab unity, encouraged by Britain's 1941 declaration of support for Arab union. The Alexandria Protocol of 1944 set its framework, and the Pact of the League of Arab States was signed the following year. Early decades were dominated by the Arab–Israeli conflict: member states fought Israel in 1948 and coordinated boycotts, while internal rivalries — between Hashemites, Egypt under Nasser, and Saudi Arabia — repeatedly frustrated collective action. After Egypt signed a separate peace with Israel in 1979, the headquarters moved to Tunis and Egypt was suspended; it returned in 1989 and Egypt rejoined. The League endorsed the Arab Peace Initiative in 2002, offering Israel normalisation in return for withdrawal from territories occupied in 1967 and a Palestinian state.

Structure

The supreme body is the Council of the League, in which each member has one vote; decisions bind only those states that accept them — a weakness often cited in explaining the League's limited effectiveness. Annual summits of heads of state have been held since 2001. A Secretary-General heads the Cairo secretariat; holders have included Amr Moussa (2001–2011) and Ahmed Aboul Gheit (from 2016). Specialised agencies cover areas from education and culture (ALECSO) to economics and aviation.

Recent decades

The League played a conspicuous role during the Arab Spring: in 2011 it suspended Libya and called for a no-fly zone — the political cover for international intervention — and suspended Syria over the Assad government's repression, readmitting it in 2023. It declared Hezbollah a terrorist organisation in 2016, reflecting Saudi–Iranian rivalry, and has struggled to present a unified response to conflicts in Yemen, Sudan, and Gaza. Observers generally judge it more effective as a forum and symbol of Arab identity than as an instrument of collective policy.
