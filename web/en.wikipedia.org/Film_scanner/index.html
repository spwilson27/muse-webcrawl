Film scanner

A film scanner is a device designed to scan photographic film directly into a computer without the use of any intermediate printmaking. It converts negatives and slides into digital images by passing light through the film and recording it with a CCD or CMOS sensor. Dedicated film scanners handle formats such as 35 mm and medium format, while flatbed scanners with transparency adapters can also scan film, usually at lower optical quality.

Characteristics

Film scanners are characterised by high optical resolution — commonly 2,700 to 5,000 pixels per inch for consumer and professional models — because film frames are small and require substantial enlargement. Equally important is dynamic range (Dmax), which determines how much shadow detail can be recovered from dense slides and negatives. Many scanners incorporate hardware-based dust and scratch removal, most famously Digital ICE, which uses an infrared channel to map surface defects (which are opaque to infrared while image dyes are transparent) and removes them in software; the technique does not work well on silver-based black-and-white film or Kodachrome.

Motion picture scanning

In cinema, high-end film scanners digitise motion picture negatives frame by frame at 2K, 4K or higher resolutions for digital intermediate workflows and restoration. Machine-transport scanners descended from the telecine, and newer laser and LED-based archival scanners are designed to handle fragile or shrunken film gently.
