Metacognition

Metacognition is "thinking about thinking": the awareness and regulation of one's own cognitive processes. The term is associated with the work of developmental psychologist John H. Flavell in the 1970s.

Components

Metacognition is commonly divided into metacognitive knowledge (what a person knows about their own memory, learning, and abilities) and metacognitive regulation (planning, monitoring, and evaluating one's performance). Failures of metacognition appear in illusions of competence, such as the Dunning–Kruger effect, in which poor performers overestimate their skill.

Education

Teaching metacognitive strategies — self-testing, spaced review, and reflecting on errors — improves learning outcomes. Metacognition also plays a role in psychotherapy, where metacognitive therapy targets unhelpful thinking styles, and in research on consciousness and artificial intelligence.
