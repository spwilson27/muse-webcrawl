The House of Medici was an Italian banking family and political dynasty that first consolidated power in the Republic of Florence under Cosimo de' Medici during the first half of the fifteenth century. The family originated in the Mugello region of Tuscany and rose to prominence through the Medici Bank, at its height the largest and most respected bank in Europe. The Medici ruled Florence, and later Tuscany, almost continuously from 1434 to 1737.

[Image: The Medici coat of arms — six balls (palle) on a gold shield]

Origins and banking

Giovanni di Bicci de' Medici founded the Medici Bank in 1397 and made the family fabulously wealthy through branches across Europe and the handling of papal finances. His son Cosimo the Elder used that wealth to become Florence's de facto ruler while preserving republican forms.

Rulers and patrons

Cosimo's grandson Lorenzo the Magnificent presided over the Florentine Renaissance at its height. The family's patronage supported artists and thinkers including Michelangelo, Botticelli, and Galileo. The Medici produced four popes and two queens of France, Catherine and Marie de' Medici. In 1569 Cosimo I was made the first Grand Duke of Tuscany.

Decline and extinction

The main line ended with the childless Gian Gastone in 1737; by the Treaty of Vienna the Grand Duchy passed to the House of Lorraine. Anna Maria Luisa de' Medici, the last Medici of the grand-ducal line, bequeathed the family's art collections to Florence on condition they remain there — the basis of the Uffizi and Palazzo Pitti museums.
