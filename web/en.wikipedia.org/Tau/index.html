Tau

Tau (Τ τ) is the nineteenth letter of the Greek alphabet.

Tau (uppercase Τ, lowercase τ; Greek: ταυ) is the nineteenth letter of the Greek alphabet. In the system of Greek numerals it has a value of 300. The letter is named after the Phoenician letter taw, meaning "mark" or "sign", from which it derives.

Uses in science and mathematics

The lowercase letter τ is used as a symbol for numerous concepts: the tau lepton, an elementary particle; torque in physics (as an alternative convention); the time constant of an exponentially decaying system; the golden ratio in some notations (as an alternative to φ); tau proteins, which stabilize microtubules in neurons and are implicated in Alzheimer's disease; and Kendall's tau rank correlation coefficient in statistics.

In mathematics, some writers have proposed τ as the circle constant equal to 2π (approximately 6.283), arguing that the circumference-to-radius ratio is more fundamental than the circumference-to-diameter ratio π. The proposal is marked by some enthusiasts on "Tau Day", 28 June.

Other uses

The tau cross, a T-shaped cross, is associated with Saint Anthony the Great and the Franciscan order; Saint Francis of Assisi used the tau as his signature. In the fictional Warhammer 40,000 universe, the T'au are an alien species and playable faction.
