Thebes (Greek: Θήβα, Thíva; Ancient Greek: Θῆβαι, Thēbai) is a town in Boeotia, Central Greece, and a former municipality. It is the largest town of Boeotia and was one of the most important cities of ancient Greece — a major rival of Athens and Sparta, and for a brief period in the 4th century BC the hegemonic power of the Greek world. The modern town is built over and around the acropolis, the Cadmea, of the ancient city.

Mythology

Thebes is the setting of some of the most famous Greek myths. Its legendary founder is Cadmus, the Phoenician prince who slew a dragon and sowed its teeth, from which sprang the Spartoi, armed men who became the ancestors of the Theban nobility. Thebes is the city of Oedipus, who solved the riddle of the Sphinx and unwittingly fulfilled the prophecy that he would kill his father and marry his mother; of Antigone; of the warring brothers Eteocles and Polynices and the expedition of the Seven Against Thebes; and the birthplace of Dionysus and of Heracles.

History

Archaeology shows a significant Mycenaean palace centre at Thebes; Linear B tablets found there attest its importance in the Bronze Age. In the classical period Thebes led the Boeotian confederacy. It sided with Persia in 480 BC, then with Sparta against Athens, then against Sparta: under the generals Pelopidas and Epaminondas, the Theban Sacred Band destroyed Spartan power at Leuctra (371 BC), making Thebes briefly the dominant city of Greece. Philip II of Macedon defeated Thebes and Athens at Chaeronea (338 BC); after a revolt in 335 BC, Alexander the Great destroyed the city, sparing only temples and the house of the poet Pindar. Thebes was later rebuilt and exists today as a modest agricultural and industrial town with an important archaeological museum.

Archaeology

Excavations on the Cadmea have uncovered the Mycenaean palace, frescoes, and the largest hoard of Near Eastern cylinder seals found in Greece. The Archaeological Museum of Thebes, reopened in 2016, displays finds from prehistory through the Byzantine period.
