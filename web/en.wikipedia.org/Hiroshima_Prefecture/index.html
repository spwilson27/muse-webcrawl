Hiroshima Prefecture (広島県, Hiroshima-ken) is a prefecture of Japan located in the Chūgoku region of Honshu. Its capital and largest city is Hiroshima. The prefecture faces the Seto Inland Sea and includes numerous islands, among them Miyajima (Itsukushima), famous for the floating torii gate of Itsukushima Shrine, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

The area corresponds to the former provinces of Aki and Bingo. The city of Hiroshima was the first city in the world to be attacked with an atomic weapon, on 6 August 1945; the Hiroshima Peace Memorial (Genbaku Dome) and Peace Memorial Park commemorate the event, and the prefecture has become internationally associated with peace advocacy.

Geography and economy

Hiroshima Prefecture consists of 14 cities and several districts. Its economy is anchored by heavy industry, shipbuilding, and automobiles — Mazda is headquartered in the prefecture — alongside oyster farming and sake brewing in Saijō. The Shimanami Kaidō expressway links its islands to Shikoku.
