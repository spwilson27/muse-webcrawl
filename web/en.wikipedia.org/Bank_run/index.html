Bank run

A bank run occurs when many clients withdraw their deposits from a bank simultaneously because they believe the bank may fail. Because banks lend out most deposits and hold only a fraction in reserve (fractional-reserve banking), a run can force even a solvent bank into illiquidity and failure.

Mechanism

Runs are self-fulfilling: early withdrawers are paid in full, so rational depositors rush to withdraw at the first sign of trouble. The Diamond–Dybvig model (1983) formalised this coordination problem.

History

Famous runs include those of the Great Depression (thousands of US bank failures, 1930–33), Northern Rock in the UK (2007 — the first British run in over a century), and Silicon Valley Bank (2023), which collapsed in two days after $42 billion in withdrawal requests.

Prevention

Deposit insurance (such as the FDIC, created 1933), central-bank lender-of-last-resort facilities, and bank holidays are designed to prevent panics.
