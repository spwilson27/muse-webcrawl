Hausdorff–Young inequality

The Hausdorff–Young inequality is a foundational result in Fourier analysis, established by William Henry Young for Fourier series and generalised by Felix Hausdorff to Fourier transforms. It bounds the size of the Fourier transform of a function in terms of the size of the function itself, in Lebesgue (Lp) norms.

Statement

For a function f in Lp with 1 ≤ p ≤ 2, its Fourier transform f̂ belongs to Lq, where q is the conjugate exponent satisfying 1/p + 1/q = 1, and the norm of f̂ in Lq is at most the norm of f in Lp (for suitably normalised measures). The inequality follows by interpolation between the endpoints p = 1 (where the transform is bounded) and p = 2 (the Plancherel theorem, where the transform is an isometry).

Sharp constants

In 1961 William Beckner computed the optimal constant in the inequality for certain cases, work that also produced the sharp form of the uncertainty principle in Fourier analysis. The optimal constant is less than 1 for 1 < p < 2, attained by Gaussian functions.

Applications

The inequality is used throughout harmonic analysis and partial differential equations wherever Fourier methods are applied, and it underlies estimates for dispersive equations and restriction theorems.
