Benedict Arnold

Benedict Arnold (1741–1801) was an American-born military officer who served during the American Revolutionary War. He fought with distinction for the Continental Army before defecting to the British in 1780, and his name became synonymous with treason in the United States.

Early career

Born in Norwich, Connecticut, Arnold was a merchant and militia officer. He helped capture Fort Ticonderoga in 1775, led an expedition to Quebec, and played a crucial role in the American victory at Saratoga in 1777, where he was badly wounded in the leg.

Defection

Feeling passed over for promotion and embittered by disputes, Arnold, as commander of West Point, plotted in 1780 to surrender the fort to the British. The plot was exposed when his contact Major John André was captured; Arnold fled to British lines and served as a British brigadier.

Later life

He moved to London, engaged in business ventures, and died there in 1801.
