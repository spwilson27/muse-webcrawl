Satoshi Ōmura (born 12 July 1935) is a Japanese biochemist known for his discovery and development of hundreds of pharmaceutical compounds derived from micro-organisms. He shared the 2015 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with William C. Campbell and Tu Youyou for work on therapies against infections caused by roundworm parasites.

Satoshi Ōmura, Nobel laureate in Physiology or Medicine

Career

Ōmura studied at the University of Yamanashi and Tokyo University of Science, and spent much of his career at the Kitasato Institute, becoming a leading figure in the search for bioactive compounds from soil micro-organisms. His group developed techniques for culturing rare actinomycetes and screening their metabolites.

Avermectin and ivermectin

In the 1970s Ōmura's team isolated Streptomyces avermitilis from a soil sample collected near a golf course at Kawana, Shizuoka. The compound produced by the microbe, avermectin, was developed with Merck into ivermectin, a drug that dramatically reduced the incidence of onchocerciasis (river blindness) and lymphatic filariasis in Africa and Latin America. A donation programme distributed the drug free of charge to hundreds of millions of people.
