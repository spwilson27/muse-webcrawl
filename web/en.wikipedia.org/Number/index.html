Number

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Subsets of the complex number system

A number is a mathematical object used to count, measure, and label. The most familiar examples are the natural numbers 1, 2, 3, and so on. Numbers can be represented in language by numerals and in writing by symbols called digits; for example, "5" is a numeral that represents the number five.

Classification

Over history the concept of number has been repeatedly extended. The natural numbers (counting numbers) were enlarged by the inclusion of zero and the negative integers, producing the integers. Ratios of integers give the rational numbers. The real numbers include both rational numbers and irrational numbers such as the square root of 2 and π. Extending further, the complex numbers add the imaginary unit i, satisfying i² = −1. Other extensions include the quaternions, p-adic numbers, and transfinite numbers.

History

Numerals developed independently in many cultures. The Hindu–Arabic numeral system, with its place-value notation and zero, spread from India through the Islamic world to Europe. Tally marks and counting tokens predate written numerals by millennia.

Main types

Key classes include natural numbers, integers, rational numbers, real numbers, complex numbers, algebraic and transcendental numbers, prime numbers, and cardinal and ordinal numbers used in set theory.
