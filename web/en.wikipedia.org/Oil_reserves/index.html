Oil reserves

Oil reserves denote the amount of crude oil that can be technically and economically extracted at the price of oil current at the time. Because the recoverability of oil varies with technology and price, reserve estimates are regularly revised. Proved reserves (1P) are those with a high degree of certainty of being recoverable; probable and possible reserves (combined as 3P) are progressively less certain.

Classification

Reserve categories commonly used include proved developed producing, proved developed non-producing, and proved undeveloped reserves, alongside probable and possible reserves. Bodies such as the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission and the Society of Petroleum Engineers set rules for reporting so that investors can compare companies' claims.

Largest reserves

Total world proved reserves are on the order of 1.7 trillion barrels. The largest national holdings (approximate, including extra-heavy oil and oil sands where reported) are:

OPEC members hold the majority of world proved reserves. Reported figures are politically and commercially sensitive: during the 1980s several OPEC countries sharply increased their stated reserves without announcing major discoveries, amid quota negotiations based partly on reserves.

Reserve life and debates

The reserves-to-production (R/P) ratio for the world has hovered around 40–50 years for decades, as new discoveries, enhanced recovery, and unconventional resources (tight oil, oil sands, extra-heavy oil) have replenished reserves roughly as fast as production. Debates over "peak oil" concern the timing of maximum production rather than the physical exhaustion of reserves.
