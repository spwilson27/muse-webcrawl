Out of the Past (released in the United Kingdom as Build My Gallows High) is a 1947 American film noir directed by Jacques Tourneur and starring Robert Mitchum, Jane Greer, and Kirk Douglas. Adapted by Daniel Mainwaring from his novel Build My Gallows High, it is widely regarded as one of the greatest films noir ever made.

Plot

Jeff Bailey, a former private investigator, runs a gas station in the small town of Bridgeport, California, hoping to escape his past. Years earlier, gambler Whit Sterling had hired him to find Kathie Moffat, who had shot Sterling and fled with $40,000. Jeff found Kathie in Acapulco and fell in love with her; when the past catches up — Sterling summons Jeff to Lake Tahoe and frames him in a scheme involving a murder and stolen tax records — Jeff must untangle a web of betrayal in which Kathie proves fatally treacherous. The story is told with an extended flashback structure, a classic noir device.

Reception and legacy

Critics praised Nicholas Musuraca's low-key cinematography and the fatalistic script; Jane Greer's Kathie is a definitive femme fatale, and Mitchum's performance made him the archetypal noir hero. The film was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry in 1991 as "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant." It was remade in 1984 as Against All Odds.
