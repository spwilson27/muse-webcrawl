Hampi

Hampi is a UNESCO World Heritage Site in the Vijayanagara district of Karnataka, India. It was the capital of the Vijayanagara Empire in the 14th–16th centuries and, at its peak, one of the largest and richest cities in the world.

History

The site, on the banks of the Tungabhadra River among granite boulder hills, has sacred associations with the Ramayana as Kishkindha. The empire's capital, Vijayanagara, flourished under rulers such as Krishnadevaraya (reigned 1509–1529), visited and described by Portuguese travellers Domingo Paes and Fernão Nunes. After the empire's defeat at the Battle of Talikota in 1565, the city was sacked and abandoned.

Monuments

The Group of Monuments at Hampi covers some 4,100 hectares with over 1,600 surviving structures: the Virupaksha Temple, still in worship; the Vittala Temple with its stone chariot and musical pillars; royal enclosures, elephant stables, the Lotus Mahal, and extensive fortifications, bazaars and irrigation works.

Tourism and heritage

Hampi is one of India's most visited heritage sites, protected by the Archaeological Survey of India and inscribed by UNESCO in 1986.
