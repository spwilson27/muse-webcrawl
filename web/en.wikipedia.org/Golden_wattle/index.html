Golden wattle

The golden wattle (Acacia pycnantha) is a tree of the family Fabaceae native to southeastern Australia. It grows to about 8 metres tall, with smooth bark, sickle-shaped phyllodes in place of true leaves, and profuse fragrant golden-yellow spherical flower heads that appear in late winter and spring.

The golden wattle is the floral emblem of Australia, proclaimed in 1988, and its green and gold colours inspired the national colours of Australian sporting teams. Wattle Day is celebrated on 1 September. The species has become invasive in parts of South Africa, New Zealand and elsewhere where it has been introduced, largely for tannin production and dune stabilisation.
