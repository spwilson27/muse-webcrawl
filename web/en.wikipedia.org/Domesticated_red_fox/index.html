Domesticated red fox

The domesticated red fox (Vulpes vulpes), often called the Russian domesticated fox or tame silver fox, is a form of the red fox bred for tameness in a famous long-running experiment begun in 1952 at Novosibirsk, Siberia, by Soviet geneticist Dmitry Belyaev and continued by Lyudmila Trut.

The experiment

Starting with silver foxes from fur farms, Belyaev selected each generation solely for tameness — breeding only the animals least fearful of and aggressive toward humans. Within a few dozen generations the foxes began wagging their tails, whining for attention and allowing handling.

Correlated changes

Remarkably, selection for behaviour produced physical changes resembling those in domestic dogs: floppy ears, curled tails, piebald coats, shortened snouts and altered reproductive cycles — a suite of traits known as the "domestication syndrome". The experiment is a landmark demonstration that selection on behaviour alone can drive rapid evolution of morphology and physiology, and the foxes are now also sold as exotic pets.
