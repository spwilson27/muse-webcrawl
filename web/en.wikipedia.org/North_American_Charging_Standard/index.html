North American Charging Standard

The North American Charging Standard (NACS), presently standardized as SAE J3400, is an electric vehicle (EV) charging connector system developed by Tesla. It has been used on all North American market Tesla vehicles since 2012 and was opened for use by other manufacturers in November 2022.

History

Tesla introduced its proprietary connector with the Model S in 2012 and deployed it across its Supercharger network in North America. On November 11, 2022, Tesla renamed the design the North American Charging Standard and published its specifications, inviting other automakers and charging network operators to adopt it.

In 2023 Ford and General Motors announced they would adopt NACS for their future EVs, triggering a cascade of similar announcements from nearly every major automaker selling vehicles in North America. In December 2023 SAE International published the design as standard J3400, formalizing it as an industry standard.

Technical description

The NACS connector is notably smaller than the Combined Charging System (CCS Type 1) connector because it uses the same two power pins for both AC charging and DC fast charging, communicating digitally with the vehicle. The standard supports charging rates up to 1 MW in its specification, although deployed Supercharger hardware typically delivers up to about 250 kW per vehicle.

Adoption

By 2024, manufacturers including Ford, GM, Rivian, Volvo, Polestar, Mercedes-Benz, Nissan, Honda, Hyundai, Kia, BMW, Toyota, and Volkswagen had announced plans to equip vehicles with NACS ports or to supply adapters, and major charging networks committed to adding NACS connectors.
