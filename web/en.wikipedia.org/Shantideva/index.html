Shantideva (Śāntideva)

Śāntideva (Sanskrit: शान्तिदेव; c. 685 – c. 763 CE) was an Indian Buddhist monk, philosopher, and poet associated with the great monastic university of Nalanda. A member of the Madhyamaka school, he is revered in Mahayana Buddhism — especially in Tibet — as a bodhisattva and as the author of two works preserved in Sanskrit: the Bodhisattvacaryāvatāra and the Śikṣāsamuccaya.

Life

Traditional biographies, largely legendary, relate that Śāntideva was born a prince in Saurashtra who renounced his throne after a vision of Mañjuśrī and became a monk at Nalanda. Nicknamed "Bhusuku" — from bhuñjāna (eating), supta (sleeping), and kuṭi-gata (going to the hut) — he was mocked as a monk who did nothing but eat, sleep, and wander off. Challenged to recite scripture before the assembly, he is said to have ascended the throne offered to him and recited the Bodhisattvacaryāvatāra, rising into the air at the famous verses of the ninth chapter before vanishing; later episodes credit him with miracles and debates in Magadha.

Works

The Bodhisattvacaryāvatāra ("Entering the Conduct of the Bodhisattvas"), in ten chapters and roughly a thousand verses, is one of the most beloved texts of Mahayana Buddhism. It describes the cultivation of bodhicitta (the awakening mind), the six perfections (pāramitās), meditation on the equality and exchange of self and others (notably in chapter 8), and a celebrated exposition of emptiness in chapter 9, the "Wisdom" chapter. Its dedication and confession verses are recited daily in Tibetan monasteries, and the Dalai Lama has called it a principal source of his own understanding.

The Śikṣāsamuccaya ("Compendium of Training") is a prose anthology of Mahayana sutra passages organised around the practice of the perfections, preserving quotations from many scriptures now lost in Sanskrit. A third work, the Sūtrasamuccaya, is sometimes attributed to him.
