Epidemic Intelligence Service

The Epidemic Intelligence Service (EIS) is a program of the United States Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) that trains physicians, scientists, veterinarians and other health professionals in applied epidemiology — the practice of investigating and controlling disease outbreaks. Established in 1951 by Alexander Langmuir, partly out of Cold War concern about biological warfare, it runs two-year fellowships based in Atlanta and in state and local health departments.

Work

EIS officers — popularly called "disease detectives" — are deployed to investigate outbreaks, conduct urgent public health research, and respond to natural disasters and emergencies. Famous investigations in which EIS officers took part include the identification of Legionnaires' disease in Philadelphia in 1976, the early epidemiology of AIDS, toxic shock syndrome, Ebola outbreaks in Africa, and the COVID-19 pandemic.

Training and alumni

The program's pedagogy is learning by doing: fellows are expected to complete a field investigation, an epidemiologic study, a surveillance system evaluation and scientific presentations during their service. Its alumni include many directors of the CDC, state epidemiologists and founders of analogous field epidemiology training programs worldwide.
