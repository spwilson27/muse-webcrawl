Downsampling (signal processing)


Downsampling reduces the sampling rate by keeping every M-th sample.

In digital signal processing, downsampling (also called decimation) is the process of reducing the sampling rate of a signal, usually by an integer factor M. Only every M-th sample is retained, discarding the rest. When performed on a discrete sequence, it lowers the data rate and the resolution of the signal.

Aliasing and filtering

Reducing the sample rate without first removing high-frequency content causes aliasing: components above the new Nyquist frequency fold into the lower band and distort the result. Practical decimators therefore apply a low-pass anti-aliasing filter with a cutoff at the new Nyquist rate before discarding samples. The combined structure is used in multirate systems such as audio sample-rate converters and software-defined radio.

Applications

Downsampling appears in image scaling, audio processing, data compression, and numerical simulation, and is the counterpart of upsampling, which increases the sampling rate by interpolation.
