Liga F is the highest level of league competition for women's football in Spain. Founded in 1988 as the Liga Nacional, it was previously known as the Primera División Femenina and Primera Iberdrola. In 2022 it became a fully professional league, governed by the Liga Profesional de Fútbol Femenino (LPFF), the first professional women's league in Spain.

History

The championship began in the 1988–89 season with nine teams. Early champions included Peña Barcelonista Barcilona and Añorga KKE. Atlético de Bilbao, Rayo Vallecano, Levante and Atlético de Madrid were prominent in later years, but from the 2010s FC Barcelona Femení became the dominant force, winning numerous consecutive titles and the UEFA Women's Champions League, and setting world-record attendances for women's club matches at Camp Nou.

Professionalisation

After a long campaign by players, including a strike in 2019 that produced the first collective bargaining agreement in Spanish women's football, the government approved professional status in 2021 and the new Liga F launched in September 2022 with centralised marketing and broadcast deals. Delays in the first season due to a referees' dispute drew international attention.

Format

Sixteen teams play each other home and away over 30 rounds. The top three qualify for the UEFA Women's Champions League, and the bottom two are relegated to the Primera Federación.
