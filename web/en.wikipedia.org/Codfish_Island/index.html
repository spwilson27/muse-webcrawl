Codfish Island / Whenua Hou, a predator-free sanctuary for the kākāpō

Codfish Island / Whenua Hou is a small island (14 km2 / 5.4 sq mi) located to the west of Stewart Island / Rakiura in southern New Zealand. It reaches a height of 250 m (820 ft) close to its south coast. The island is a nature reserve under the Department of Conservation; landing requires a permit, and it is kept free of introduced predators.

Conservation

The island is a major breeding site for the critically endangered kākāpō, the world's only flightless parrot; most of the surviving population lives on Whenua Hou and Anchor Island, and every individual bird is named and monitored by the Kākāpō Recovery Programme. The island's Māori name, Whenua Hou ("new land"), was added in the official dual name in recognition of its early history as the site of a mixed Māori–European sealing settlement in the 1820s–30s. Like other sanctuary islands and reserves — comparable in spirit to protected habitats on islands of the coconut crab's range — it is rodent-free, maintained through strict quarantine of all visitors and supplies.

Wildlife

Besides kākāpō, the island supports breeding populations of the yellow-eyed penguin, the Stewart Island robin, the South Island saddleback, and the Codfish Island fern bird. The endemic Codfish Island bat and large flightless weevils are also notable. Its forests include rimu, southern rātā and Hall's tōtara.
