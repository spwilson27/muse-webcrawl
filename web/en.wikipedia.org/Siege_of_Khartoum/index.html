Siege of Khartoum

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The siege of Khartoum (13 March 1884 – 26 January 1885) was the climactic episode of the Mahdist War in Sudan. Forces loyal to Muhammad Ahmad, the self-proclaimed Mahdi, besieged the Egyptian garrison of Khartoum, defended by Major-General Charles George Gordon, and stormed the city after a siege of 317 days, killing Gordon and much of the garrison — just two days before a British relief expedition arrived.

Background

The Mahdist revolt had swept through Sudan since 1881, destroying Egyptian forces including the army of Hicks Pasha in 1883. The British government of William Gladstone ordered the evacuation of Egyptian garrisons and sent Gordon — former Governor-General of Sudan — to Khartoum in early 1884 to organise the withdrawal. Instead, Gordon resolved to hold the city, becoming a popular hero in Britain and the focus of intense public pressure for a relief expedition.

The siege

Mahdist forces surrounded Khartoum in March 1884, cutting the telegraph and river communications. Gordon fortified the city, mounted guns on steamers, improvised mines and defences, and rationed food as supplies dwindled; the population suffered severe starvation. The relief expedition under Sir Garnet Wolseley advanced slowly up the Nile and across the desert. On the night of 25–26 January 1885 the Mahdists, taking advantage of the low Nile, stormed the weakened defences. Gordon was killed — the manner of his death became the stuff of legend — and thousands of soldiers and civilians were massacred.

Aftermath

The relief force reached Khartoum on 28 January, two days too late, and withdrew. The Mahdi died six months later; his successor, the Khalifa Abdallahi, ruled Sudan until the Anglo-Egyptian reconquest under Kitchener, completed at Omdurman in 1898. Gordon's death became a defining imperial legend in Victorian Britain.
