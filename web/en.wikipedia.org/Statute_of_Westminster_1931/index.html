Statute of Westminster 1931

The Statute of Westminster 1931 is an act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that sets the basis for the relationship between the Dominions of the British Empire and the Crown. It gave legislative effect to the political equality declared in the Balfour Declaration of 1926 and is regarded as a foundational step in the independence of Canada, Australia, New Zealand, South Africa, Ireland, and Newfoundland.

Provisions

The statute removed the power of the UK Parliament to legislate for a Dominion except at that Dominion's request and with its consent, freed Dominion parliaments from the Colonial Laws Validity Act 1865 (so their laws could no longer be struck down as repugnant to British law), and allowed Dominions to legislate extraterritorially.

Adoption

The statute applied immediately to Canada, South Africa, and the Irish Free State. Australia, New Zealand, and Newfoundland needed to adopt it locally: Australia did so in 1942 (retroactive to 1939), New Zealand in 1947, and Newfoundland never adopted it before joining Canada in 1949.

Legacy

Together with later measures such as the Canada Act 1982 and the Australia Acts 1986, the statute completed the transformation of the empire into the Commonwealth of Nations of equal, sovereign states sharing the Crown.
