Curriculum development

Curriculum development is the planned, purposeful process of designing what learners will study: selecting and organising content, learning experiences, teaching approaches, and assessment so that educational goals are achieved. It operates at national, school, and classroom levels and is a central concern of education policy.

Curriculum development
Field: Education
Key models: Tyler rationale, Taba model, backward design
Components: Aims, content, pedagogy, assessment

Models

Ralph Tyler's 1949 rationale posed four questions — purposes, experiences, organisation, and evaluation — that still structure curriculum work. Hilda Taba proposed a grassroots, teacher-led model beginning with diagnosing learners' needs. Later approaches include backward design (starting from desired outcomes and assessment) and competency-based frameworks.

Process

Development typically involves analysing social and learner needs, setting aims and standards, sequencing content, producing materials, piloting, training teachers, and evaluating outcomes for revision. Stakeholders include governments, subject experts, teachers, employers, and communities, whose competing values make curriculum inherently political.

Trends

Contemporary debates concern standardisation versus local autonomy, integration of technology, inclusive and culturally responsive curricula, and balancing knowledge-rich content with transferable skills.
