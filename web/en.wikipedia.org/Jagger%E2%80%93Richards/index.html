Jagger–Richards is the songwriting partnership of Mick Jagger and Keith Richards, the singer and guitarist of the Rolling Stones. Together they have written the great majority of the band's original catalogue, including "Satisfaction", "Paint It Black", "Jumpin' Jack Flash", "Sympathy for the Devil", "Gimme Shelter", "Brown Sugar", "Tumbling Dice", "Angie", "Miss You", and "Start Me Up". As producers they are credited under the pseudonym The Glimmer Twins.

Origins

Jagger and Richards had known each other since primary school in Dartford, Kent, and reconnected in 1961. In 1963 their manager, Andrew Loog Oldham, locked them in a kitchen and insisted they write songs, deliberately modelling them on Lennon–McCartney; the first results included "As Tears Go By" (a hit for Marianne Faithfull before the Stones recorded it). Initially the pair wrote ballads for others, but by 1965, with the riff-driven "(I Can't Get No) Satisfaction", they had found their own voice rooted in blues, R&B, and country.

Working method

Their collaboration has varied over six decades: Richards often contributes riffs, chord structures, and open-tuned guitar figures (famously on five-string open-G tuning), while Jagger frequently shapes melodies, lyrics, and the band's engagement with contemporary styles from disco to dance music. Some classics were largely one man's work — "Wild Horses" and "Angie" are chiefly Richards ballads; "Sympathy for the Devil" and "Brown Sugar" chiefly Jagger's — but all are jointly credited. Their partnership survived periods of estrangement in the 1980s, dubbed the "Third World War" by Richards, before reuniting for the Steel Wheels album and tour of 1989.

Legacy

Jagger–Richards ranks with the most commercially successful and durable songwriting teams in rock history; Rolling Stone magazine placed them among the greatest songwriters of all time. Their songs have been recorded by countless artists across genres.
