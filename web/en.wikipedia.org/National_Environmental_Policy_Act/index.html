National Environmental Policy Act

The National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA) is a United States environmental law enacted on 1 January 1970, signed by President Richard Nixon. Often called the "Magna Carta" of American environmental law, it requires federal agencies to assess the environmental effects of their proposed actions before making decisions.

Requirements

For "major federal actions significantly affecting the quality of the human environment," agencies must prepare an Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) analysing the effects of the proposed action, unavoidable adverse impacts, and alternatives. Smaller actions may be covered by a shorter Environmental Assessment (EA), leading to a Finding of No Significant Impact, or excluded categorically. NEPA also created the Council on Environmental Quality (CEQ) in the Executive Office of the President to oversee implementation.

Impact and debate

NEPA is procedural rather than substantive: it forces disclosure and public participation but does not forbid environmentally harmful decisions. Its processes have reshaped countless projects — highways, dams, pipelines, logging and energy development — and generated extensive litigation over the adequacy of analyses. Supporters credit it with democratising federal decisions; critics blame it for delay and cost, and Congress amended it in 2023 to impose page and time limits on reviews.
