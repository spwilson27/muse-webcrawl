Colonias are the neighbourhoods into which Mexico City is divided for administrative and postal purposes. The capital is formally organised into sixteen boroughs (alcaldías, formerly delegaciones), and each borough is in turn subdivided into colonias. There are more than 1,800 colonias in the city, ranging from historic districts founded in the colonial era to modern residential developments and informal settlements.

History

The term colonia came into widespread use during the late nineteenth century, when new residential districts were laid out beyond the old colonial core of the city during the Porfiriato. Early examples such as Colonia Juárez, Colonia Roma and Colonia Santa María la Ribera were planned suburbs with grid streets, parks and services, often marketed to the growing middle and upper classes. As the city expanded dramatically during the twentieth century, the colonia became the standard unit of urban subdivision.

Notable colonias

Some of the best-known colonias include Condesa, known for its Art Deco architecture and parkland; Roma, a cultural and gastronomic centre; Coyoacán's central colonias, which preserve a village-like character; Polanco, an upscale commercial district; and Doctores, one of the oldest working-class neighbourhoods. Each colonia typically has its own market, church, primary school and local festivals.

Administration

Colonias do not have their own governments; services such as water, paving and policing are provided by the borough and city governments. Residents may organise through neighbourhood committees (comités vecinales) that liaise with borough authorities. Postal addresses in Mexico City conventionally give the street, the colonia, the borough and the postal code.
