Dream

A dream is a succession of images, ideas, emotions, and sensations that usually occur involuntarily in the mind during certain stages of sleep. Humans spend about two hours dreaming per night, and each dream lasts around 5 to 20 minutes, although the dreamer may perceive the dream as lasting much longer.

Sleep stages

Dreams occur primarily during the rapid eye movement (REM) stage of sleep, when brain activity is high and resembles that of being awake. Dreams during non-REM sleep are generally less vivid and less frequently recalled. Mechanisms that prevent the body from acting out dreams include muscle atonia during REM.

Theories

The content and function of dreams have been a topic of scientific, philosophical, and religious interest throughout recorded history. Sigmund Freud proposed in The Interpretation of Dreams (1899) that dreams are disguised expressions of repressed wishes. Later theories include the activation-synthesis model of Hobson and McCarley, which attributes dream content to the brain's attempt to make sense of random neural activity, and memory-consolidation theories, which hold that dreaming helps process and integrate experiences.

Other phenomena

  • Lucid dreams — dreams in which the dreamer is aware of dreaming and may exert control.
  • Nightmares — unpleasant dreams causing fear or distress.
  • Recurring dreams — the same dream repeated over time.
  • Daydreams — waking fantasies, a related but distinct phenomenon.
