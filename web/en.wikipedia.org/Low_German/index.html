Low German

Low German (Plattdeutsch, Niederdeutsch) is a West Germanic language spoken in northern Germany, the eastern Netherlands, and by diaspora communities worldwide. It descends from Old Saxon and is distinguished from High German by the absence of the High German consonant shift.

History

Old Saxon was the language of the Saxons in the early Middle Ages. Middle Low German became the lingua franca of the Hanseatic League from the 13th to 16th centuries, used in trade around the North and Baltic Seas and influencing Scandinavian languages. After the decline of the Hanse, High German based on Luther's Bible translation displaced it in writing and administration.

Dialects

Major groups include Westphalian, Eastphalian, Northern Low Saxon, Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, Brandenburgisch, and East Pomeranian and Low Prussian, the latter two largely displaced after 1945. Dutch Low Saxon varieties are spoken in the northeastern Netherlands.

Status

Germany and the Netherlands recognize Low German as a regional language under the European Charter for Regional or Minority Languages. Estimates of speakers range from several hundred thousand fluent speakers to several million with passive knowledge. It lacks a single standardized orthography.
