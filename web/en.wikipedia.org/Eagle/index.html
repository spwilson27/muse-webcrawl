Eagles are large, powerfully built birds of prey in the family Accipitridae. The name is applied to some 60 species, most of them in Eurasia and Africa, with others in the Americas and Australia. Eagles are distinguished from other raptors by their larger size, heavy head and beak, and broad, long wings.

Description and hunting

Eagles have exceptionally keen eyesight — several times sharper than a human's — allowing them to spot prey from great heights. They hunt fish, mammals, birds and reptiles, seizing prey with strong talons; some species, such as the harpy eagle, take monkeys and sloths from the forest canopy, while sea eagles specialise in fish and waterbirds. The Philippine eagle and harpy eagle are among the largest flying birds.

Groups

Informal groupings include the booted or "true" eagles (genus Aquila and relatives), sea eagles and fish eagles (Haliaeetus), snake eagles, which feed largely on reptiles, and the giant forest eagles. Eagles build large stick nests, called eyries, in tall trees or on cliffs, and many species mate for life.

Cultural significance

The eagle has been a symbol of power, courage and freedom since antiquity, appearing in the heraldry and national emblems of many states. The bald eagle is the national bird of the United States; the golden eagle features on the flags and arms of countries from Mexico to Kazakhstan.
