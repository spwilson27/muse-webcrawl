Königswinter is a town in the Rhein-Sieg district of North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany, situated on the right bank of the Rhine, opposite the Mehlem district of Bonn and at the foot of the Siebengebirge hills. The town takes its name — "King's Winter" — from a legend that a Frankish king once wintered there.

Geography and landmarks

The town is dominated by the Drachenfels ("Dragon's Rock"), a 321-metre hill crowned by a ruined twelfth-century castle, in German legend the place where Siegfried slew the dragon Fafnir. Since 1883 the Drachenfels Railway, Germany's oldest rack railway, has carried tourists to the summit. Below stands Schloss Drachenburg, an ornate nineteenth-century villa-castle built in 1882–84. The Siebengebirge, one of Germany's oldest nature reserves (protected since the 1920s), surrounds the town.

History

First mentioned in documents in the early eleventh century, Königswinter developed around wine-growing, quarrying of trachyte stone, and later tourism fuelled by Rhine Romanticism — Lord Byron praised the Drachenfels in Childe Harold's Pilgrimage. In the late nineteenth century it became a fashionable resort. On the Petersberg, a nearby hill, stands the former federal guest house where the Petersberg Agreement was signed in 1949. The town today includes numerous incorporated villages and districts along the Rhine and in the hills.
