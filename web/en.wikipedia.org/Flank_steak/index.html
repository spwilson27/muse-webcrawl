Flank steak

Flank steak is a cut of beef steak taken from the flank, the abdominal muscles of the cow, located just in front of the rear quarter. It is a long, flat cut with pronounced longitudinal grain and is known for its intense beefy flavour despite being relatively lean and containing some connective tissue.

Characteristics

Flank steak is typically about 2.5 cm (1 inch) thick and can weigh roughly 450 to 900 grams (1 to 2 pounds). Because the muscle does a good deal of work during the animal's life, the cut is somewhat tougher than prime steaks from the rib or loin, but it rewards proper cooking and slicing. Cooks are advised to slice it thinly across the grain, which shortens the muscle fibres and greatly improves tenderness.

Preparation

Flank steak is often marinated before grilling or broiling, as acidic marinades help tenderise the surface. It is best cooked to medium-rare or medium; overcooking makes it tough. It is a classic choice for fajitas, London broil, and the French bistro dish bavette, and it is used in stir-fries where it is cut into thin strips.

Related cuts

Flank steak is sometimes confused with skirt steak, which comes from the plate section and is even more strongly flavoured and coarser in texture. In some markets, "flank steak" was historically a less prized cut sold cheaply, but rising demand has increased its price substantially.
