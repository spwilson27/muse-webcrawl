Swan House (Atlanta)

The Swan House, with its cascading front fountain, in Buckhead, Atlanta.

Swan House is a historic mansion in the Buckhead district of Atlanta, Georgia, United States. Built in 1928 for Edward H. Inman, heir to a cotton brokerage fortune, and his wife Emily MacDougald Inman, it was designed by architect Philip Trammell Shutze and blends classical revival and Italianate elements. The house takes its name from the swan motifs found throughout its interior decoration and furnishings.

Architecture

Shutze designed the stucco mansion with a grand double staircase at the rear and a cascading fountain down the front garden slope. The interiors feature coffered ceilings, elaborate plasterwork, and rooms decorated with swan carvings and furniture collected by the Inmans. The estate originally comprised 28 acres of gardens, lawns, and woodland.

History

Edward Inman died in 1931, only a few years after the house was completed; Emily Inman lived there until her death in 1965. In 1966 the Atlanta Historical Society (now the Atlanta History Center) purchased the house and surrounding land, opening it as a house museum. It is listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

In popular culture

Swan House served as a filming location for The Hunger Games: Catching Fire (2013) and Mockingjay films, where it portrayed President Snow's mansion in the Capitol.
