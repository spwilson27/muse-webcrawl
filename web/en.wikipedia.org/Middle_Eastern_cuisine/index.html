Middle Eastern cuisine

Middle Eastern cuisine comprises the culinary traditions of the countries of the Middle East, including the Levant, the Arabian Peninsula, Egypt, Mesopotamia, Anatolia, and Persia. Despite substantial regional variation, the cuisines share common ingredients and techniques shaped by the region's climate, trade history, and religious dietary rules: wheat, rice, lamb, chickpeas, lentils, olive oil, sesame, dates, yogurt, and aromatic spices feature throughout.

Common ingredients and dishes

Hummus (mashed chickpeas with tahini, lemon, and garlic), falafel (fried chickpea or fava bean balls), tabbouleh (bulgur and parsley salad), and pita flatbread are emblematic Levantine foods now eaten worldwide. Grilled meats on skewers — kebabs — and vertically roasted, shaved shawarma are central to the region's street food. Rice dishes include Persian polow and Arabian kabsa. Desserts feature filo pastry, nuts, and syrups, as in baklava and kunafa.

Regional traditions

Levantine

The cuisine of Syria, Lebanon, Jordan, and Palestine is noted for mezze culture, kibbeh (bulgur and minced meat), and liberal use of olive oil, lemon, and herbs.

Persian

Iranian cooking balances sweet and sour flavours, with saffron, barberries, pomegranate, and dried limes; rice prepared with a crisp golden crust (tahdig) is prized.

Arabian Peninsula

Gulf cuisine centres on rice-and-meat dishes such as kabsa and machboos, dates, camel products, and coffee ceremonies.

Egyptian

Egypt's largely vegetarian peasant dishes — koshari (rice, lentils, pasta), ful medames (fava beans), and ta'ameya (fava falafel) — reflect the Nile valley's agriculture.

Turkish

Turkish cuisine, heir to Ottoman palace cooking, bridges Balkan, Central Asian, and Middle Eastern traditions, with dishes such as imam bayildi, dolma, and Turkish delight.

Influences and spread

The medieval Arab agricultural revolution diffused crops such as citrus, sugarcane, and aubergine across the region and into Europe. Ottoman rule spread dishes across three continents, and twentieth-century migration popularised hummus, shawarma, and falafel in Western cities.
