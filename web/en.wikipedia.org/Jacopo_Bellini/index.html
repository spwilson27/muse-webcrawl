Jacopo Bellini

Bornc. 1400, Venice, Republic of Venice
Diedc. 1470–1471, Venice
OccupationPainter
ChildrenGentile Bellini; Giovanni Bellini

Jacopo Bellini (c. 1400 – c. 1470/71) was a Venetian painter of the early Renaissance, founder of one of the great painting dynasties of the Italian Renaissance. He was the father of Gentile Bellini and Giovanni Bellini and the father-in-law of Andrea Mantegna. Though few of his paintings survive, his two great sketchbooks of drawings — now in the Louvre and the British Museum — are among the most important documents of early Renaissance art.

Life and career

Jacopo trained in the workshop of Gentile da Fabriano, the leading master of the International Gothic style, and accompanied him to Florence, where he encountered the revolutionary new art of Brunelleschi, Masaccio and Donatello. Back in Venice he became the city's leading painter, receiving major commissions in Venice, Padua, Verona and Ferrara, including a celebrated (now lost) portrait of a lion for the Lion of Saint Mark. In 1441 he competed with Pisanello at Ferrara. He signed works as the father of Gentile and Giovanni, and his workshop trained both sons.

The sketchbooks

Jacopo's lasting fame rests on his two albums of drawings (begun around the 1430s–1450s), filled with daring experiments in linear perspective, elaborate classical architecture, landscapes and scenes from sacred and secular life. The drawings show him working out, on paper, problems of perspective construction and composition that his sons and Mantegna would realise in paint; the books remained in the Bellini workshop for generations as a reservoir of motifs.

Surviving paintings

Signed or attributed paintings include several Madonnas — among them the Madonna and Child in the Galleria dell'Accademia, Venice, and versions in the Louvre and the Uffizi — an Annunciation in Brescia, and a Crucifixion. His large narrative cycles for Venetian confraternities are lost, but their spirit was continued by Gentile's famous processional canvases.

Legacy

Through his sons and his workshop, Jacopo Bellini stands at the head of the Venetian Renaissance tradition that culminated in Giorgione and Titian.
