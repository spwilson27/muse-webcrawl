Viscometer

A viscometer is an instrument used to measure the viscosity of a fluid. For liquids with viscosities that vary with flow conditions, an instrument called a rheometer is used. Viscometers work with the sample in a steady flow state and are widely used in laboratories and industry for quality control of oils, paints, foods, pharmaceuticals, and polymers.

Types

Capillary (U-tube/Ostwald) viscometers measure the time for a fixed volume of liquid to flow through a narrow tube under gravity, giving kinematic viscosity. Falling-ball and falling-piston viscometers time a sphere moving through the fluid. Rotational viscometers (such as the Brookfield type) measure the torque required to rotate a spindle immersed in the sample and report dynamic viscosity; cone-and-plate and concentric-cylinder geometries suit different ranges. Vibrational and ultrasonic viscometers are used for inline process measurement.

Units and standards

The SI unit of dynamic viscosity is the pascal-second (Pa·s); the older poise and centipoise remain common. Kinematic viscosity is expressed in stokes or centistokes (mm²/s). Measurements are strongly temperature-dependent, so instruments are usually thermostated, and standard methods are defined by ASTM International and ISO.
