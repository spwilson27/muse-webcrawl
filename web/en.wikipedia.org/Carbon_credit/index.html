Carbon credit

A carbon credit is a generic term for any tradable certificate or permit representing the right to emit a set amount of carbon dioxide or an equivalent amount of another greenhouse gas (tCO2e). One carbon credit typically equals one metric tonne of carbon dioxide equivalent.

Emissions trading

Carbon credits are a central component of cap-and-trade systems, such as the European Union Emissions Trading System (EU ETS). A regulator sets a cap on total emissions and allocates or auctions allowances; companies that emit less than their allocation can sell surplus credits to those that emit more, creating a market price for carbon.

Carbon offsets

In voluntary markets, credits are generated by projects that reduce, avoid, or remove emissions — such as reforestation, renewable energy, methane capture, or direct air capture — and are purchased by companies or individuals to offset their own emissions. Standards bodies such as Verra and Gold Standard certify many such projects.

Criticism

Carbon credits have been criticised over questions of additionality (whether the reduction would have happened anyway), permanence, double counting, and the quality of some forestry and renewable-energy offsets. These concerns have driven efforts to tighten standards and increase transparency.
