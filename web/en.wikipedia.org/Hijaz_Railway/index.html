Hijaz Railway

Hijaz Railway
Route: Damascus to Medina
Length: 1,320 km (820 mi)
Gauge: 1,050 mm narrow gauge
Opened: 1908

The Hijaz Railway (also spelled Hejaz or Hedjaz) was a narrow-gauge railway built by the Ottoman Empire from Damascus in Syria to Medina in the Hijaz region of present-day Saudi Arabia, with a branch to Haifa on the Mediterranean. Completed in 1908, it was intended to serve Muslim pilgrims travelling to Mecca and to strengthen Ottoman control over the Arabian provinces.

Construction

Announced by Sultan Abdul Hamid II in 1900, the line was financed largely by donations from Muslims worldwide and built under the direction of the German engineer Heinrich August Meissner, with much of the labour provided by Ottoman soldiers. The main line reached Medina in 1908; the projected continuation to Mecca was never built.

First World War and after

During the First World War the railway was a strategic target of the Arab Revolt, whose raids — associated with T. E. Lawrence — repeatedly damaged the line. After the war the railway was divided among successor states; sections in Jordan and Syria remained in use, and heritage and phosphate trains still run on parts of the route in Jordan, while the Saudi section fell into disuse.
