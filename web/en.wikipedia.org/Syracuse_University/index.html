Syracuse University (informally "Cuse" or SU) is a private research university in Syracuse, New York, United States. Established in 1870 with roots in the Methodist Episcopal Church, the institution has been nonsectarian since 1873. The university has a total enrolment of more than 22,000 students and is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity".

History

Syracuse University traces its beginnings to Genesee College, founded in Lima, New York, by the Methodist Episcopal Church in 1850. When church leaders sought to move the college to a more central location, Syracuse won the contest, and the university was chartered in 1870. Its first building, the Hall of Languages (1873), still stands and houses the College of Arts and Sciences. The university was coeducational from the start, an unusual stance at the time. Syracuse significantly expanded after the Second World War under Chancellor William Pearson Tolley, welcoming returning veterans and building new schools.

Academics and schools

Syracuse comprises thirteen schools and colleges, including the Maxwell School of Citizenship and Public Affairs—long ranked among the top graduate schools of public administration in the United States—the S. I. Newhouse School of Public Communications, the School of Architecture, the Whitman School of Management, and the College of Law. The university is particularly noted for programs in public policy, communications, architecture, information studies, and sport management.

Campus and athletics

The main campus occupies about 700 acres on University Hill, east of downtown Syracuse, blending Romanesque Revival halls with modern buildings, plus satellite campuses abroad through SU Abroad centres such as Florence, London, and Madrid. Syracuse's athletic teams, the Orange, compete in NCAA Division I in the Atlantic Coast Conference. The men's basketball team, which plays in the JMA Wireless Dome (one of the largest on-campus basketball arenas in the country), won the national championship in 2003. Notable alumni include former U.S. President Joe Biden, who earned his law degree from Syracuse University College of Law in 1968.
