Eugenio Garza Sada (11 January 1892 – 17 September 1973) was a Mexican businessman and philanthropist who led the industrial conglomerate based in Monterrey descended from the Cervecería Cuauhtémoc brewery, founded by his father Isaac Garza. Trained as a civil engineer at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, he became a central figure of Mexican industry, overseeing the growth of companies in beer, glass, steel, cardboard and finance.

Tecnológico de Monterrey

In 1943 Garza Sada founded the Instituto Tecnológico y de Estudios Superiores de Monterrey (ITESM), a private university intended to supply Mexico with engineers and managers; it has grown into one of the largest university systems in Latin America. He promoted a business philosophy of human dignity, employee welfare and reinvestment, embodied in housing, savings and education schemes for workers.

Death

On 17 September 1973 he was killed during a botched kidnapping attempt by guerrillas of the Liga Comunista 23 de Septiembre, an event that shook the Mexican business establishment. A major avenue in Monterrey bears his name.
