The American Theater was a theater of operations during World War II including all continental American territory, and extending 200 miles (320 km) into the ocean from its coasts. It encompassed military operations within North and South America, the Aleutian Islands, Greenland, the Caribbean, and the Atlantic coastal approaches to the continent. Because of the great distances involved, combat was limited in scale compared with other theaters, but it included genuine battles and attacks on the American home front.

Operations

The largest land battle of the theater was the Aleutian Islands campaign (1942–1943), after Japanese forces occupied Attu and Kiska in Alaska; the Battle of Attu in May 1943 was the only battle of the war fought on incorporated American soil. In the Atlantic, German U-boats waged Operation Drumbeat against shipping off the U.S. East Coast in early 1942, sinking hundreds of vessels, while German saboteurs landed by submarine in Operation Pastorius (1942) and were captured.

Other incidents included the Japanese submarine shelling of Ellwood, California, and Fort Stevens, Oregon; the 1942 "Battle of Los Angeles" false-alarm air raid scare; Japanese Fu-Go balloon bombs launched in 1944–45, one of which killed six civilians in Oregon — the only deaths by enemy action on the U.S. mainland in the war; and the battle of the St. Lawrence in Canadian waters. U.S. forces also occupied Greenland and Iceland to secure the North Atlantic route.

Home front defense

The United States established the Eastern, Western, Southern, and Caribbean Defense Commands to guard against invasion and sabotage, raised coastal artillery and air defenses, organised civil defence, and conducted the mass internment of Japanese Americans under Executive Order 9066. The American Theater campaign medal recognised service in the theater.
