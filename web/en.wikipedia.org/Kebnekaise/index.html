Kebnekaise

Kebnekaise (Swedish: [kɛbnɛˈkâjsɛ]; from Sami Giebmegáisi, "cauldron crest") is the highest mountain in Sweden. The Kebnekaise massif lies in Lapland, about 150 kilometres north of the Arctic Circle and west of Kiruna near the border with Norway. It has two main summits: the glaciated southern peak (Sydtoppen) and the rocky northern peak (Nordtoppen, 2,096.8 m).

Changing height

For over a century the glacier-capped Sydtoppen was Sweden's highest point, measured at 2,111 metres in 1902. Because of glacier melting caused by climate change, the ice summit has been shrinking by roughly a metre per year. In August 2018 the northern, ice-free peak was measured higher than the southern one for the first time, making Nordtoppen Sweden's highest point; Sydtoppen has since been measured at around 2,094–2,096 metres, varying with season and snowfall.

Climbing

The mountain is a popular but serious mountaineering destination. The Swedish Tourist Association (STF) maintains the Kebnekaise mountain station at its foot, and guided ascents follow the Western Route (Västra leden) or the Eastern Route (Östra leden) across the Björlings glacier. The first recorded ascent was made in 1883 by Charles Rabot. The surrounding area is part of the Laponia region and is used for reindeer herding by Sami communities.
