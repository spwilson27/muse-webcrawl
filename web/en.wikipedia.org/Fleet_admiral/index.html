Fleet admiral (abbreviated FADM) is a five-star flag officer rank in the United States Navy, equivalent to General of the Army in the Army and Air Force. It is the highest rank attainable in the US Navy and is reserved for wartime appointments by Congress.

History

The rank was created in December 1944 during the Second World War to give senior American naval commanders parity with Allied field marshals and five-star officers. Only four officers have held it: William D. Leahy, Ernest J. King, Chester W. Nimitz, and William F. Halsey Jr. Leahy, as the senior officer, later received a date of rank making him the first and most senior fleet admiral.

Status

No officer has been appointed fleet admiral since 1946, and no officer currently holds the rank, though it has never been abolished and could in principle be revived by Congress. Fleet admirals remained on active duty for life with full pay under the original legislation.
