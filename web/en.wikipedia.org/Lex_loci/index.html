Lex loci

Lex loci (Latin for "the law of the place") is a shorthand expression used in conflict of laws for the law of the place with which a legal act or event is connected. It appears in a family of more specific phrases.

Forms

  • Lex loci actus — the law of the place where an act was done.
  • Lex loci celebrationis — the law of the place where a marriage was celebrated, often governing its formal validity.
  • Lex loci contractus — the law of the place where a contract was made.
  • Lex loci delicti (commissi) — the law of the place where a tort was committed.
  • Lex loci rei sitae (lex situs) — the law of the place where property is situated.
  • Lex loci solutionis — the law of the place where a contract is to be performed.

These connecting factors are used by choice-of-law rules to select the lex causae governing an issue with foreign elements.
