Industrial Light & Magic (ILM) is an American motion picture visual effects company founded by George Lucas in 1975 to create the effects for Star Wars (1977). Part of Lucasfilm, it is among the most influential visual effects studios in cinema history, credited with pioneering computer-generated imagery and many digital production techniques.

History

When Lucas found that 20th Century Fox's in-house effects department had been disbanded, he assembled a team in Van Nuys, California, under John Dykstra. The group developed the Dykstraflex motion-control camera system for Star Wars. ILM later relocated to Marin County and then to the Presidio of San Francisco.

Innovations and notable work

ILM milestones include the morphing in Willow (1988), the water pseudopod in The Abyss (1989), the liquid-metal T-1000 in Terminator 2: Judgment Day (1991), and the fully computer-generated dinosaurs of Jurassic Park (1993), which transformed the industry. The studio has won numerous Academy Awards for Best Visual Effects and has expanded into television (including the StageCraft virtual production system used on The Mandalorian) with studios in Vancouver, London, Singapore, Sydney, and Mumbai.
