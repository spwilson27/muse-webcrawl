Rioplatense Spanish

Rioplatense Spanish (Spanish: español rioplatense), also known as River Plate Spanish, is a variety of Spanish spoken mainly around the Río de la Plata basin of Argentina and Uruguay. It is distinguished by its use of voseo — the pronoun vos instead of tú for the second person singular — and by its characteristic intonation, influenced by the large wave of Italian immigration to the region in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

Features

The most recognisable phonetic feature of Rioplatense Spanish is the pronunciation of the letters ll and y as a voiced postalveolar fricative [ʒ] or affricate [dʒ] — a phenomenon known as žeísmo — and, increasingly among younger speakers in Buenos Aires, as the voiceless [ʃ] (šeísmo). The dialect also includes a large vocabulary drawn from lunfardo, the slang that developed in the working-class districts of Buenos Aires and Montevideo, with many words of Italian origin.

Geographic distribution

The variety is centred on Buenos Aires and Montevideo and is spoken across most of Argentina and Uruguay, with related voseo varieties in Paraguay and parts of Bolivia. Rioplatense Spanish dominates regional media, including the tango tradition and Argentine cinema.
