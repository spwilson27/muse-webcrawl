The Battle of Halbe (German: Kesselschlacht von Halbe, "cauldron battle of Halbe") was fought from 24 April to 1 May 1945 in the Spree Forest south-east of Berlin. The German Ninth Army under General Theodor Busse, encircled by Marshal Ivan Konev's 1st Ukrainian Front during the Battle of Berlin, fought a desperate breakout westward through the village of Halbe, hoping to link up with General Walther Wenck's Twelfth Army and reach the Elbe, where its soldiers could surrender to the Western Allies rather than the Soviets. Tens of thousands of soldiers and civilians died in one of the last major battles of the war in Europe.

Background

After the Battle of the Seelow Heights, Busse's Ninth Army fell back toward the Spree Forest and was cut off in a pocket around Halbe and Märkisch Buchholz, together with large numbers of refugees fleeing the Red Army. Hitler ordered the army to break out and relieve Berlin, an impossible task; Busse instead directed the breakout west, toward Wenck's Twelfth Army positions near Beelitz.

The breakout

Beginning on 25–26 April, massed German columns — troops of the Ninth Army, Waffen-SS remnants, Volkssturm, and civilian refugees — repeatedly assaulted the Soviet blocking positions around Halbe, suffering appalling losses to artillery, armour and air attack under constant shelling of the forest roads. The village of Halbe changed hands amid scenes of chaos. Between 28 April and 1 May, perhaps 25,000–30,000 soldiers, along with some thousands of civilians, broke through to Wenck's lines and the Elbe crossings; the remainder of the pocket was destroyed or captured. General Busse was among those who escaped.

Aftermath

Total German dead in the pocket are estimated at 30,000–40,000, including many civilians; Soviet losses were also heavy. The Waldfriedhof Halbe, established after the war in the forest, holds around 24,000–28,000 dead and is one of the largest war cemeteries in Germany. Because the battle occurred in the Soviet occupation zone and later East Germany, its memory was long suppressed, and mass graves in the Spree Forest continue to be investigated by war-graves organisations.
