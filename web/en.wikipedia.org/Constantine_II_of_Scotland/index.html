Constantine II of Scotland

Reign900–943
PredecessorDonald II
SuccessorMalcolm I
Died952, St Andrews

Constantine II (Gaelic: Constantín mac Áeda; died 952) was an early king of Scotland, then known as Alba, whose reign from 900 to 943 was among the longest in Scottish history. Son of Áed mac Cináeda, he consolidated the kingdom during decades of Viking pressure and shifting relations with the Anglo-Saxon kingdoms to the south.

Reign

Constantine fought repeated campaigns against Norse-Gaelic rulers of Dublin and York. In 918 his forces fought the battle of Corbridge against Ragnall ua Ímair. His alliance with the English against the Norse culminated in the great battle of Brunanburh in 937, where Constantine, allied with Olaf Guthfrithson of Dublin and Owain of Strathclyde, was defeated by Æthelstan of England — a battle celebrated in an Old English poem.

Church and kingdom

Constantine is credited with strengthening the Scottish church, working with the bishop Cellach of St Andrews in synods that bound the Gaelic church to observance of the faith. Gaelicisation of the former Pictish lands advanced under him, and the name Alba came to denote the whole kingdom.

Abdication and death

In 943 Constantine abdicated in favour of his cousin Malcolm I and retired to the monastery of St Andrews, where he became abbot of the Céli Dé and died in 952.
