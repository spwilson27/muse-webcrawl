Shares outstanding

Shares outstanding fluctuate with issuance and buybacks

Shares outstanding are all the shares of a corporation that have been authorised, issued, and purchased by and are held by investors, as distinct from treasury shares that the company itself holds. The number of shares outstanding is a key input in computing a company's market capitalisation (share price × shares outstanding) and its earnings per share. The figure appears on the balance sheet under shareholders' equity and is disclosed in quarterly and annual filings.

Basic and diluted shares

Companies report two versions of the count:

  • Basic shares outstanding: the actual shares currently held by investors.
  • Diluted shares outstanding: the basic count plus the shares that would be created if all convertible securities — stock options, warrants, convertible bonds, and convertible preferred shares — were exercised or converted.

Accounting standards (such as US GAAP and IFRS) require earnings per share to be reported on both bases, typically using the treasury stock method for options and warrants and the if-converted method for convertible securities. Because the number changes over a reporting period, financial statements use a weighted average number of shares outstanding for the period.

Changes in shares outstanding

The count rises when a company issues new shares — in an initial public offering, a seasoned (follow-on) offering, a stock split, the exercise of employee stock options, or a stock-based acquisition — and falls when the company repurchases its own shares in a buyback (the repurchased shares become treasury shares or are retired). A stock split multiplies the count without changing the company's total value, whereas buybacks genuinely return capital to remaining shareholders by concentrating their ownership.

Float

Closely related is the public float: the subset of shares outstanding that is freely traded, excluding restricted shares held by insiders, officers, and controlling shareholders. Index providers often weight index constituents by float-adjusted market capitalisation, so float, rather than total shares outstanding, determines a stock's index weight.

Authorised, issued, and outstanding

Authorised shares ≥ Issued shares ≥ Shares outstanding

Authorised shares are the maximum a company's charter permits; issued shares are those actually sold or granted; shares outstanding are issued shares minus treasury shares. For example, a company might be authorised to issue 1 billion shares, have issued 600 million, hold 50 million in treasury, and therefore have 550 million shares outstanding.
