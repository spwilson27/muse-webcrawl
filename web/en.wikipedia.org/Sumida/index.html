Sumida (墨田区, Sumida-ku) is one of the 23 special wards of Tokyo, Japan, located east of the Sumida River. It is home to the Tokyo Skytree, the tallest structure in Japan, and to the Ryōgoku Kokugikan, the centre of professional sumo wrestling.

Geography

Sumida lies in the low-lying eastern part of the capital, between the Sumida River to the west and the Arakawa River to the east. The ward is largely residential and historically industrial, with many small workshops and factories.

History

The ward was created in 1947 by the merger of the former Honjo and Mukojima wards. The area suffered heavy damage in the Great Kantō earthquake of 1923 and again in the firebombing of March 1945.

Landmarks

Besides the Skytree and the Kokugikan, Sumida contains the Edo-Tokyo Museum, the Sumida Hokusai Museum (dedicated to the ukiyo-e artist Katsushika Hokusai, born in the area), and the headquarters of the Asahi Breweries, whose distinctive golden "flame" sculpture overlooks the river.
