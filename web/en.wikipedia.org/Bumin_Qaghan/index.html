Bumin Qaghan (Old Turkic: 𐰉𐰆𐰢𐰣:𐰴𐰍𐰣, Bumïn qaγan; Chinese: 土門可汗, Tumen Khan; died 553) was the founder of the First Turkic Khaganate (also known as the Göktürk Khaganate), the first great nomadic empire of the Eurasian steppe to bear the name "Turk". As chief of the Ashina clan, Bumin led the Turks in revolt against their overlords, the Rouran Khaganate, in 552, destroying Rouran power and establishing an empire that stretched across Central Asia.

Background

The Ashina Turks were a subject people of the Rouran Khaganate, traditionally said to have worked as blacksmiths in the Altai Mountains region. Bumin, as their chief, sought to strengthen his position by requesting a marriage alliance with the Rouran khagan Anagui — a request that was contemptuously rejected, with Anagui reportedly insulting Bumin as a "blacksmith slave". This humiliation is traditionally given as a cause of the subsequent revolt.

Bumin instead allied himself with the Western Wei dynasty of northern China, marrying the Princess Changle in 551, and received Chinese support against the Rouran.

Revolt and founding of the Khaganate

In 552 Bumin defeated the Rouran forces; Anagui committed suicide, and the Rouran Khaganate collapsed as an effective power. Bumin proclaimed himself Illig Qaghan ("ruler of the people") of the new Turkic Khaganate. He established his court in the Ötüken region of present-day Mongolia, which remained the sacred heartland of Turkic power.

Bumin died within a year or so of his victory, in 553. He was succeeded in the east by his son Issik Qaghan, while his younger brother Istemi governed the western part of the empire as Yabgu, effectively laying the groundwork for the khaganate's later division into eastern and western halves.

Legacy

Although Bumin's reign was brief, his achievement was foundational: he created the first empire to use "Turk" as a political name, and the khaganate he founded came to dominate the steppe from Manchuria to the Black Sea, controlling much of the Silk Road trade between China and the west.
