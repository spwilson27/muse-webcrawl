Scientific nameDasypus novemcinctus
FamilyDasypodidae
RangeAmericas, from Argentina to the southern United States

Conservation statusLeast Concern (IUCN)

The nine-banded armadillo (Dasypus novemcinctus), also known as the nine-banded long-nosed armadillo or common long-nosed armadillo, is a mammal found in North, Central and South America, making it the most widespread of the armadillos. Its range has expanded steadily northward into the United States, where it is the state small mammal of Texas.

The species is named for the bands of scutes in the middle of its armoured shell, which allow flexibility when moving. Nine-banded armadillos almost always give birth to genetically identical quadruplets from a single fertilized egg, a trait called polyembryony. They are strong swimmers and can hold their breath for several minutes; when startled, they may leap straight up into the air.

Ecology and humans

Nocturnal and solitary, nine-banded armadillos dig burrows and forage by smell for insects, grubs and fallen fruit. They are one of few animals besides humans known to carry the bacterium that causes leprosy (Hansen's disease), and are used in medical research on the disease. In parts of Latin America they are hunted for food and their shells are fashioned into musical instruments and crafts.
