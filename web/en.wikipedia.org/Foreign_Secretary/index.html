Foreign Secretary

The Secretary of State for Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Affairs, commonly referred to as the Foreign Secretary, is a senior minister of the Crown in the Government of the United Kingdom. The Foreign Secretary is a member of the Cabinet and heads the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO), the government department responsible for protecting and promoting British interests worldwide.

History

The office was created in 1782 when the Southern and Northern Departments of government were reorganized into the Foreign Office and the Home Office. The first holder was Charles James Fox. For most of its history the department was known simply as the Foreign Office; in 1968 it merged with the Commonwealth Office to form the Foreign and Commonwealth Office, and in 2020 it absorbed the Department for International Development, becoming the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office.

Responsibilities

The Foreign Secretary is responsible for the work of the FCDO, including relations with foreign governments, British diplomacy, the overseas diplomatic network, consular services, and (since 2020) international development policy. The Foreign Secretary is regarded as one of the Great Offices of State, alongside the Prime Minister, the Chancellor of the Exchequer, and the Home Secretary.

Residence

The Foreign Secretary's official residence in London is 1 Carlton Gardens, near the Mall; Chevening House in Kent is also available for use by the officeholder.
