Chicken is the most common type of poultry in the world and is eaten in virtually every cuisine. Owing to the relative ease and low cost of raising chickens compared with cattle or pigs, chicken meat and eggs have become prevalent in numerous cuisines, and chicken is among the most-consumed meats globally.

History and production

Chickens were domesticated from the red junglefowl in Asia several thousand years ago. Modern broiler chickens, developed in the mid-20th century, reach slaughter weight in about six weeks. The United States, Brazil and China lead production. Birds are classed by age and size — broilers/fryers, roasters, capons and stewing hens — and sold whole or in cuts including breast, thigh, drumstick, wing and offal.

Cuisine and nutrition

Chicken is roasted, grilled, fried, steamed and stewed in dishes as diverse as fried chicken, chicken tikka masala, coq au vin, yakitori, arroz con pollo and Hainanese chicken rice. Lean breast meat is high in protein and low in fat compared with red meat, though skin and dark meat raise fat content. Undercooked chicken risks salmonella and campylobacter infection, so food safety guidance stresses cooking to a safe internal temperature and avoiding cross-contamination.
