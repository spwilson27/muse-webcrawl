Vicente Fox Quesada (born 2 July 1942) is a Mexican businessman and politician who served as the 62nd president of Mexico from 2000 to 2006. After campaigning as the candidate of the centre-right National Action Party (PAN), Fox became the first president not from the Institutional Revolutionary Party (PRI) since 1929, ending the PRI's 71-year hold on the presidency and completing Mexico's transition to competitive democracy.

Born in Mexico City and raised on the family ranch in San Francisco del Rincón, Guanajuato, Fox studied business administration at the Universidad Iberoamericana and rose through Coca-Cola's Mexican operations, becoming president of Coca-Cola Mexico before entering politics. He was elected to the Chamber of Deputies in 1988 and became governor of Guanajuato in 1995 after a disputed earlier campaign.

Presidency

Fox's 2000 victory was hailed as the culmination of gradual democratic reforms. His administration maintained macroeconomic stability and low inflation, expanded conditional cash-transfer programmes (Oportunidades), and created a transparency law and institute (IFAI) strengthening access to government information. However, his ambitious reform agenda — tax, energy, and labour reforms — was largely blocked by a Congress in which the PAN lacked a majority, and his relations with the United States cooled after Mexico opposed the 2003 invasion of Iraq; a hoped-for migration agreement with President George W. Bush was never achieved.

Fox's plainspoken, rancher persona and frequent verbal gaffes made him a distinctive figure. He left office with mixed approval ratings and was succeeded by fellow PAN member Felipe Calderón after the closely contested 2006 election.

Later life

After the presidency, Fox founded the Centro Fox study centre and library at his ranch, became an outspoken critic of drug prohibition and of U.S. president Donald Trump, and left the PAN in 2013. His later statements and feuds with the PAN leadership, and support for his wife Marta Sahagún's political ambitions, kept him in public debate.
