Lagrangian submanifold

In mathematics, a Lagrangian submanifold of a symplectic manifold (M, ω) is a submanifold L on which the symplectic form ω vanishes identically when restricted to L, and whose dimension is half that of M — the largest possible for such an isotropic submanifold.

Examples include any curve in a symplectic surface, the zero section and fibres of a cotangent bundle, and real loci of complex manifolds with anti-symplectic involutions. Lagrangian submanifolds are fundamental in classical mechanics, where configuration spaces arise as Lagrangians in phase space, and in modern geometry and physics: they are the objects of the Fukaya category in homological mirror symmetry, and correspondences between them generalise canonical transformations and Fourier integral operators.
