Burma
Country in Southeast Asia, officially Myanmar
Capital: Naypyidaw (largest city: Yangon/Rangoon)
Population: ~55 million

Burma is the former English name of the Southeast Asian country officially known since 1989 as Myanmar. The name Burma, derived from the majority Bamar ethnic group, remains in widespread informal use, and the choice of name has sometimes carried political connotations since the military government promoted "Myanmar".

History

Burma's early kingdoms included the Pyu city-states and the Pagan Empire, which unified the Irrawaddy valley in the 11th century. The Konbaung dynasty was defeated in three Anglo-Burmese wars, and the country became a British colony in 1886. Burma gained independence in 1948, but a 1962 military coup began decades of army rule, interrupted by a period of partial civilian government after 2011 and ended by the 2021 coup.

Geography

Burma is the largest country by area in mainland Southeast Asia, bordered by India, Bangladesh, China, Laos and Thailand. The Irrawaddy River is its principal waterway, and the country is rich in jade, gems, teak and natural gas.

People

The population is ethnically diverse: the Bamar form the majority, alongside Shan, Karen, Rakhine, Mon, Kachin, Chin and many other groups. Theravada Buddhism is the predominant religion.
