The Mennonites are a Christian denomination and a community of Anabaptist churches that trace their roots to the Radical Reformation of the 16th century. The movement is named after Menno Simons, a Dutch priest who joined the Anabaptists in 1536 and became their most influential leader, organising scattered congregations into a disciplined, peaceful church. Mennonites are known for their commitment to nonviolence and pacifism, refusal to swear oaths or hold public office in earlier centuries, plain dress in conservative groups, and strong mutual aid.

History

The Anabaptist movement began in Zurich in 1525 when Conrad Grebel, Felix Manz, and others rebaptised adult believers, rejecting infant baptism. Persecuted by both Catholic and Protestant authorities, Anabaptists spread through Switzerland, Germany, and the Netherlands. After the violent Münster Rebellion (1534–35), Menno Simons gathered the surviving peaceful Anabaptists, whose descendants became the Mennonites. The Dordrecht Confession of Faith (1632) summarised Dutch Mennonite doctrine.

Seeking freedom of religion, Mennonites migrated widely: to Prussia and then Russia (where "Russian Mennonites" formed prosperous colonies), to Pennsylvania from 1683 onward, and later to Canada, Mexico, Paraguay, Belize, and Bolivia. In the 1870s and 1920s, many Russian Mennonites left for North and South America as their military exemptions and autonomy were withdrawn.

Beliefs and practices

  • Believer's baptism — baptism upon confession of faith, usually by pouring.
  • Nonresistance — refusal of military service and personal violence, based on the Sermon on the Mount.
  • Church discipline — including the ban (shunning) in conservative communities.
  • Separation from the world — plain dress, simple living, and reluctance to swear oaths or hold political office, varying by group.

Mennonites range from "Old Order" groups — related to but distinct from the Amish (who split from the Mennonites under Jakob Ammann in 1693) — to fully modern denominations such as Mennonite Church USA and Mennonite Church Canada. The Mennonite World Conference links churches in more than 80 countries; today the largest populations are in Ethiopia, India, the Democratic Republic of Congo, and the United States. Mennonite relief and development work is coordinated by bodies such as the Mennonite Central Committee (founded 1920).
