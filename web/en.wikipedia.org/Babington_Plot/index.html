Babington Plot

The Babington Plot was a plan in 1586 to assassinate Queen Elizabeth I of England, a Protestant, and put Mary, Queen of Scots, a Catholic, on the English throne. The plot led directly to Mary's trial and execution.

The conspiracy

The plot was led by Anthony Babington and John Ballard, and developed from the earlier Throckmorton Plot. Letters between Babington and Mary were carried in a beer barrel to Chartley Hall, where Mary was held, but were secretly intercepted and deciphered by Thomas Phelippes for Elizabeth's spymaster, Francis Walsingham.

Exposure and trials

Walsingham allowed the correspondence to continue in order to gather evidence, including a postscript in Mary's reply of July 1586 approving the assassination. Babington and his fellow conspirators were arrested, tried for high treason, and executed in September 1586.

Consequences

Mary was tried by a commission of nobles and found guilty; she was executed at Fotheringhay Castle on 8 February 1587. The plot also hardened English policy toward Spain and contributed to the outbreak of open war culminating in the Spanish Armada of 1588. See also Mary, Queen of Scots.
