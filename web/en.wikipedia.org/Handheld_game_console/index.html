Handheld game console

A handheld game console is a small, portable self-contained video game console with a built-in screen and controls.

A handheld game console, or simply handheld console, is a small, portable, self-contained video game console with a built-in screen, game controls and speakers. Handheld game consoles are smaller than home video game consoles and contain the console, screen, speakers, and controls in one unit, allowing people to carry them and play them at any time and anywhere.

History

Early handheld electronic games emerged in the 1970s: Mattel's Auto Race (1976) used LED lights, and Milton Bradley's Microvision (1979) was the first handheld to use interchangeable cartridges. Nintendo's Game & Watch series (1980–1991), designed by Gunpei Yokoi and each containing a single game, sold tens of millions of units and introduced the D-pad control. The modern handheld era began with Nintendo's Game Boy (1989), which, bundled with Tetris, outlasted technically superior colour rivals such as the Atari Lynx and Sega Game Gear thanks to long battery life and a strong game library, ultimately selling about 118 million units (including the Game Boy Color).

Later generations

The Game Boy Advance (2001) continued Nintendo's dominance. Sony entered the market with the PlayStation Portable (PSP, 2004), emphasising multimedia capability, followed by the PlayStation Vita (2011). Nintendo's DS (2004), with dual screens and a touchscreen, became the best-selling handheld ever at about 154 million units, and its successor, the 3DS (2011), added glasses-free 3D. In 2017 Nintendo released the Switch, a hybrid that functions as both a home and handheld console, and it went on to become one of the best-selling consoles of all time. In the 2020s, handheld gaming PCs such as Valve's Steam Deck (2022) brought PC game libraries into portable form factors, while smartphones and tablets largely absorbed the casual handheld market.
