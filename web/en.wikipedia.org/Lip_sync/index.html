Lip sync

Lip sync (also spelled lip-sync, short for lip synchronization) is the practice of moving one's lips in time with prerecorded speech or song so as to give the impression of speaking or singing live. It is used in film and television production, in live musical performance, in drag performance, and in video applications; when done deceptively in contexts where live singing is expected, it can be controversial.

In film and music

In cinema, lip sync is essential to dubbing films into other languages and to musical production numbers, in which songs are recorded in a studio and mimed on set; animation likewise depends on matching characters' mouths to prerecorded dialogue. In pop music, performers have long sung to backing tracks on television programmes and at large events where choreography makes live singing difficult. The most notorious scandal involved the duo Milli Vanilli, whose 1990 Grammy Award was revoked after it emerged that the frontmen had not sung on their records at all. Ashlee Simpson's 2004 Saturday Night Live mishap, when the wrong backing vocal track played, became a widely cited example of the risks.

Other uses

Drag performers traditionally lip sync to famous recordings as the core of their acts, a format popularised in competitions such as "Lip Sync for Your Life" on RuPaul's Drag Race and the celebrity show Lip Sync Battle. Social-media lip syncing was the foundation of the app Musical.ly, which merged into TikTok in 2018. In audio engineering, "lip sync" also denotes the synchronisation error between sound and picture in audiovisual playback, measured in milliseconds.

Criticism

Audiences and critics often view lip syncing at ticketed concerts as a breach of trust: some jurisdictions have proposed or passed "truth in music" measures. Defenders note that heavy choreography, illness, or broadcast constraints may make fully live vocals impractical, and that a backing track supporting a live lead vocal is standard practice in major productions.
