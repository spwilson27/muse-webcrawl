I-novel

The I-novel (私小説, shishōsetsu, or watakushi shōsetsu) is a genre of Japanese literature in which the author narrates events from his or her own life in a confessional, seemingly unmediated form. It emerged in the early twentieth century and became one of the dominant modes of serious Japanese fiction.

Characteristics

I-novels are typically written in the first person and prize sincerity and authenticity over invention; the boundary between the author's real experience and the fiction is deliberately thin. Recurring concerns include the writer's own impoverished or dissolute daily life, illness, family conflict, and the act of writing itself.

History and influence

The genre is often traced to works such as Tayama Katai's Futon (1907). Later practitioners include Shiga Naoya, Dazai Osamu, and Kenzaburō Ōe. Critics have debated whether the form's introspection enriched Japanese literature or confined it to narrow autobiography.
