Polynomial ideal

A polynomial ideal is an ideal in a polynomial ring: given the ring k[x1, …, xn] of polynomials in n variables over a field k, an ideal I is a subset closed under addition and under multiplication by arbitrary polynomials, generated by some set of polynomials.

Fundamental results

The Hilbert basis theorem states that every ideal in k[x1, …, xn] is finitely generated. Through the correspondence between ideals and algebraic varieties, polynomial ideals are the algebraic side of algebraic geometry: the ideal of a variety consists of all polynomials vanishing on it, and Hilbert's Nullstellensatz characterises the ideals arising this way.

Computation

Gröbner bases, introduced by Bruno Buchberger in 1965, provide an effective method for computing with polynomial ideals, allowing problems such as ideal membership, elimination and solving systems of polynomial equations to be handled algorithmically. They are a cornerstone of computational algebra.
