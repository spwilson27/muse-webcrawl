Rendezvous (fur trade)

In North American history, a rendezvous was a large gathering of fur trappers and traders, most famously the Rocky Mountain Rendezvous held annually from 1825 to 1840. At these meetings, mountain men exchanged beaver pelts and other furs for supplies, and celebrated after a year in the wilderness.

Rocky Mountain Rendezvous

The system was pioneered by William H. Ashley of the Rocky Mountain Fur Company in 1825, when a supply train carried trade goods to a prearranged site in the mountains, eliminating the need for fixed trading posts. Sites included Henry's Fork of the Green River and locations in present-day Wyoming and Utah.

Decline

The rendezvous system ended as beaver populations declined and silk hats replaced beaver felt in fashion; the last rendezvous was held in 1840.
