The Weavers

The Weavers

Also known as: The Weaver ( folk group)

Formed: 1948, New York City

Members: Pete Seeger, Lee Hays, Fred Hellerman, Ronnie Gilbert

Genre: Folk

The Weavers were an American folk music quartet — Pete Seeger, Lee Hays, Fred Hellerman and Ronnie Gilbert — formed in New York in 1948. Descended from the Almanac Singers, they popularised folk music for a mass audience and helped ignite the American folk revival of the 1950s and 1960s.

Success and blacklisting

Signed to Decca Records, the Weavers had a huge hit in 1950 with Lead Belly's "Goodnight, Irene", which topped the charts, followed by "Tzena, Tzena, Tzena", "On Top of Old Smoky" and "Kisses Sweeter Than Wine". During the McCarthy era the group was blacklisted for its members' left-wing associations, lost its recording contract, and disbanded in 1952.

Legacy

A triumphant reunion concert at Carnegie Hall in 1955, released as a landmark live album, restored their reputation and inspired a generation of folk performers, including the Kingston Trio and Peter, Paul and Mary. The Weavers were inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame and influenced the protest-song tradition.
