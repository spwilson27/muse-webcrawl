Line of communication

A line of communication (or communications) is the route that connects an operating military unit with its supply base. Supplies and reinforcements are transported along it. An army's line of communication was vulnerable to interdiction or severing by an enemy force, either from raids or from direct attack, and an army whose lines of communication were cut off faced starvation, shortage of ammunition, and likely defeat.

History

The concept became central to military thought with the growth of standing armies in the early modern period. In the First World War, armies depended on railways and roads as their lines of communication, and the destruction of rail junctions was a major objective. In the Second World War, sea lines of communication (SLOC) were vital: the Battle of the Atlantic saw German U-boats attempt to sever Britain's maritime supply lines, while Allied convoys kept them open.

During the Cold War and after, air lines of communication (ALOC) grew in importance, with transport aircraft supplying forces beyond the range of surface transport. Modern militaries protect their lines of communication through route clearance, convoy escort, and redundant supply networks.

Related concepts

The term is also used in chess, where a line of communication is the file or diagonal connecting a piece to a square it must defend, and in general logistics for any transport route sustaining an organization.
