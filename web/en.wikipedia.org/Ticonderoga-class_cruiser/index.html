The Ticonderoga class of guided-missile cruisers is a class of warships of the United States Navy, first ordered and authorized in 1978. The class is built around the Aegis Combat System and the AN/SPY-1 phased-array radar, designed to defend carrier battle groups against massed air and missile attack. Twenty-seven ships were completed between 1983 and 1994.

Design

The cruisers use the hull form of the Spruance-class destroyers, enlarged to carry the Aegis system and its four fixed radar arrays mounted on the superstructure. The first five ships carried twin-arm Mk 26 missile launchers; from USS Bunker Hill (CG-52) onward, ships were fitted with the Mk 41 vertical launching system, with 122 cells able to fire Standard anti-aircraft missiles, Tomahawk cruise missiles, and anti-submarine rockets. The ships also carry two 5-inch guns, Harpoon anti-ship missiles, torpedo tubes, and close-in weapon systems, and can embark helicopters.

Service history

The lead ship, USS Ticonderoga (CG-47), was commissioned in 1983. The class saw action in the Gulf War and subsequent conflicts, launching Tomahawk strikes and providing air defence. USS Vincennes (CG-49) gained notoriety in 1988 when it shot down Iran Air Flight 655. Ships of the class also pioneered naval ballistic missile defence, with Aegis BMD-equipped cruisers conducting successful missile intercepts.

Retirement

The first five ships, lacking vertical launchers, were retired in 2004–2005. The remaining 22 cruisers have been progressively modernized, but the Navy has moved to retire the class in the 2020s as hulls age and maintenance costs rise, with several ships decommissioned from 2022 onward and the rest scheduled to follow by the end of the decade.
