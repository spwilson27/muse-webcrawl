Nuclear weapons of the United Kingdom

Last test26 November 1991

The United Kingdom possesses nuclear weapons and was the third country in the world to develop and test them, after the United States and the Soviet Union. It is one of the five nuclear-weapon states recognised by the Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons. Since 1998, the UK's deterrent has rested solely on submarine-launched ballistic missiles.

History

British scientists made crucial contributions to the wartime Manhattan Project, but post-war US legislation cut off cooperation, and the Attlee government decided in 1947 to build an independent British bomb. The first test, Operation Hurricane, took place in the Montebello Islands off Australia on 3 October 1952. A thermonuclear capability followed with the Operation Grapple tests at Christmas Island in 1957–58. The 1958 US–UK Mutual Defence Agreement restored close nuclear cooperation, which has shaped the programme ever since.

The deterrent

During the Cold War the RAF's V-bombers (Valiant, Victor and Vulcan) and later the Royal Navy's Resolution-class submarines carrying Polaris kept the deterrent. Since 1994 the force has consisted of four Vanguard-class submarines armed with US-supplied Trident II D5 missiles fitted with British-designed warheads, based at HMNB Clyde (Faslane), with warheads stored and maintained at Coulport and produced at the Atomic Weapons Establishment, Aldermaston. At least one submarine is on patrol at all times in a policy of continuous at-sea deterrence.

Successor programme

In 2016 Parliament voted to replace the Vanguard class with four Dreadnought-class submarines, planned to enter service from the early 2030s. The 2021 Integrated Review raised the cap on the stockpile from 180 to 260 warheads, ending the post-Cold War reductions.
