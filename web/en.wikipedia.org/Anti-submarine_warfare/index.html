Anti-submarine warfare

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


An anti-submarine weapon being launched

Anti-submarine warfare (ASW, or in the older form A/S) is a branch of underwater warfare that uses surface warships, aircraft, submarines, or other platforms, to find, track, and deter, damage, or destroy enemy submarines. Such operations are typically carried out to protect friendly shipping and naval vessels, support blockade, and defend against submarine-launched missiles.

History

ASW emerged as a distinct discipline in the First World War, when German U-boats threatened Allied shipping. Early measures — convoys, Q-ships, depth charges, hydrophones and the first active sonar (ASDIC) — were countered in the Second World War by wolfpack tactics, leading to an intense technological contest in the Battle of the Atlantic involving escort carriers, very-long-range patrol aircraft, centimetric radar, high-frequency direction finding (HF/DF), and ahead-thrown weapons such as Hedgehog and Squid.

During the Cold War, the threat of Soviet ballistic-missile and attack submarines drove the development of the SOSUS seabed listening network, nuclear-powered hunter-killer submarines, maritime patrol aircraft such as the P-3 Orion, and dipping sonar and sonobuoys.

Methods

Detection relies primarily on acoustics: passive sonar listens for radiated noise; active sonar transmits pings and listens for echoes. Non-acoustic sensors include magnetic anomaly detectors (MAD), radar for periscopes and snorkels, and satellite and signals intelligence. Once a submarine is localised, it can be attacked with depth charges, homing torpedoes, anti-submarine missiles, or naval mines.

Platforms

Modern ASW forces include specialised frigates and destroyers with towed-array sonars, attack submarines, fixed-wing maritime patrol aircraft (such as the P-8 Poseidon), and shipborne helicopters equipped with dipping sonar and lightweight torpedoes.
