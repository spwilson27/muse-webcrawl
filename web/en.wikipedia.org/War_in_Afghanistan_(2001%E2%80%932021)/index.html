War in Afghanistan (2001–2021)

Date7 October 2001 – 30 August 2021
LocationAfghanistan
ResultTaliban victory; fall of the Islamic Republic of Afghanistan

The War in Afghanistan was a conflict that began following the United States invasion of Afghanistan in response to the September 11 attacks. The US and its allies sought to dismantle al-Qaeda and remove the Taliban regime that harboured it, then spent two decades supporting the Islamic Republic of Afghanistan against a Taliban insurgency. It was the longest war in American history.

Invasion and aftermath

Operation Enduring Freedom began on 7 October 2001; Kabul fell within weeks and an interim government under Hamid Karzai was installed. NATO's ISAF mission expanded across the country, but the Taliban regrouped in Pakistan's border regions and waged a growing insurgency, despite a US troop "surge" in 2009–2011.

Withdrawal and collapse

Under the 2020 Doha Agreement the United States committed to withdraw. As foreign forces left in 2021, the Taliban launched a rapid offensive; Kabul fell on 15 August 2021, President Ashraf Ghani fled, and the last US troops departed on 30 August amid a chaotic evacuation.

Costs

The war killed well over 100,000 Afghans and about 2,400 US service members, and displaced millions. The United States spent an estimated $2 trillion on the conflict.
