Nobuo Fujita (1911 – 30 September 1997) was a warrant officer and pilot of the Imperial Japanese Navy Air Service who carried out the only wartime aircraft bombings of the contiguous United States during the Second World War. Flying a Yokosuka E14Y floatplane launched by catapult from the submarine I-25, Fujita made two raids in September 1942 intended to ignite the forests of Oregon with incendiary bombs.

The Lookout Air Raids

On 9 September 1942, Fujita dropped two 76-kilogram incendiary bombs near Mount Emily in Oregon, starting a small fire that was quickly suppressed by forest lookouts. A second raid followed on 29 September near Port Orford. The attacks caused negligible damage but were the only enemy air raids on the U.S. mainland during the war (the earlier shelling of Ellwood, California, and the bombing of Dutch Harbor in the Aleutian Islands aside). The story was largely kept from the American public at the time to avoid panic.

Post-war reconciliation

After the war Fujita ran a metal fabrication business in Japan. In 1962, invited by the community of Brookings, Oregon, he visited the town and presented it with his family's 400-year-old samurai sword as a gesture of peace and reconciliation. Initially fearful he might be prosecuted, he was warmly received, and he returned several times, planting trees at the bomb site. Brookings made him an honorary citizen; he died in 1997, and part of his ashes were scattered at the site he had bombed.
