Japanese submarine I-17

I-17

Class: B1-type submarine

Launched: 19 July 1939

Commissioned: 24 January 1941

Fate: Sunk 19 August 1943

I-17 was a B1 type submarine of the Imperial Japanese Navy that served in World War II. One of twenty boats in her class, she was notable for conducting the first shelling of the United States mainland by Axis forces during the war.

Service history

I-17 participated in the attack on Pearl Harbor in a supporting role, patrolling north of Oahu. In February 1942, under Commander Kozo Nishino, she surfaced off Santa Barbara, California, and shelled the Ellwood oil field on 23 February 1942 — the Bombardment of Ellwood — causing minor damage but significant alarm on the American West Coast. She later operated in the South Pacific, supporting operations around Guadalcanal and the Solomon Islands.

Loss

On 19 August 1943, I-17 was sunk off Nouméa, New Caledonia, by the American destroyer-minesweeper USS Tracy and a Royal New Zealand Air Force floatplane, after being located following an attack on a convoy. Most of her crew were lost, though a small number of survivors were recovered.
