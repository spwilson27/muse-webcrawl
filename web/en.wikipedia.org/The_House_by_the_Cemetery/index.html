The House by the Cemetery (Italian: Quella villa accanto al cimitero) is a 1981 Italian horror film directed by Lucio Fulci. It is the third instalment of Fulci's unofficial "Gates of Hell" trilogy, following City of the Living Dead (1980) and The Beyond (1981).

Plot

New York historian Norman Boyle moves with his wife Lucy and young son Bob into a house in the Boston area that belonged to his colleague Dr. Peterson, who hanged himself. The house's cellar conceals the tomb of Dr. Freudstein, a disgraced Victorian surgeon who sustains his decayed life by murdering victims and harvesting their cells.

Release and reception

The film is noted for its graphic gore and oneiric atmosphere. In the United Kingdom it became embroiled in the "video nasty" controversy of the 1980s and was heavily cut for years. It has since been reassessed as one of Fulci's most effective horror films.
