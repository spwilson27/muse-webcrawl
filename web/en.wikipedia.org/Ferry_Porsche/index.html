Ferry Porsche

Born: 19 September 1909, Wiener Neustadt, Austria-Hungary
Died: 27 March 1998, Zell am See, Austria
Occupation: Engineer, entrepreneur
Known for: Founder of the Porsche car marque; Porsche 356

Ferdinand Anton Ernst Porsche (19 September 1909 – 27 March 1998), known as Ferry Porsche, was an Austrian-German technical automobile designer and entrepreneur. He operated the company founded by his father, Ferdinand Porsche, and built it into the sports-car manufacturer Porsche AG. He is credited with creating the first true Porsche sports car, the 356.

Life and career

Ferry Porsche joined his father's Stuttgart design office in the 1930s and worked on projects including the Volkswagen Beetle and Auto Union racing cars. After the Second World War, while his father was imprisoned in France, Ferry ran the family's consultancy from Gmünd in Austria. In 1948, under his leadership the firm produced the Porsche 356, a lightweight sports car based partly on Volkswagen components, which established the Porsche name as a manufacturer in its own right.

Returning to Stuttgart-Zuffenhausen in 1950, he expanded the company and oversaw development of the 356's successor, the 911, designed under his son Ferdinand Alexander ("Butzi") Porsche and launched in 1963. Ferry remained honorary chairman after the company was converted into a public limited company in 1972, when family members withdrew from day-to-day management. He cultivated a company culture summarized in his belief that Porsche cars should be reliable, durable, and enjoyable in everyday use — "a Porsche should be a car that can be driven to the opera in the evening and to the race track the next morning."
