Orders in Council (1807)

The Royal Navy enforced the Orders in Council against neutral trade with France.

The Orders in Council of 1807 were a series of decrees issued by the British government during the Napoleonic Wars, by which Britain sought to restrict neutral trade with France and its allies in response to Napoleon's Continental System.

Background

In November 1806 Napoleon's Berlin Decree declared the British Isles under blockade and forbade all commerce with Britain. Britain replied in January 1807 with an Order in Council restricting neutral coastal trade with France, and — after the French Milan Decrees escalated the economic war — with the much broader Orders of November 1807.

Provisions

The November 1807 Orders declared all ports of France and its allies, and any port excluding British ships, to be under blockade. Neutral vessels were forbidden to trade with them directly; goods could only reach enemy ports after first calling at a British port, paying duties, and obtaining a licence. The Royal Navy enforced the system by seizing offending ships, including those of the still-neutral United States.

Consequences

The Orders were a major grievance behind the American Embargo Act of 1807 and, together with impressment, a principal cause of the War of 1812. Facing domestic economic distress and diplomatic pressure, Britain revoked the Orders in Council on 23 June 1812 — days after the United States had already declared war, news of the repeal not yet having crossed the Atlantic.
