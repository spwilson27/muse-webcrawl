NATO phonetic alphabet


Labelled image: the ICAO/NATO spelling alphabet

The NATO phonetic alphabet, also known as the ICAO phonetic alphabet or International Radiotelephony Spelling Alphabet, is a set of code words assigned to the 26 letters of the English alphabet so that critical combinations of letters and numbers can be pronounced and understood by those who exchange voice messages by radio or telephone, regardless of language differences or connection quality. It was developed in the 1950s through studies for the International Civil Aviation Organization and adopted by NATO in 1956.

History and usage

Earlier alphabets, such as the Allied Able–Baker alphabet of the Second World War, contained words unintelligible to non-native speakers. After extensive testing in many languages, the ICAO list was finalized in 1956; only a few letters (notably "Alfa" spelled with an F for non-English speakers, and "Juliett" with two Ts for French speakers) carry special spellings. The alphabet is used in civil aviation, maritime communication, the military, emergency services, and amateur radio.
