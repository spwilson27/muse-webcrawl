Standard terminal arrival route

A standard terminal arrival route (STAR) is a published flight procedure that an aircraft flying under instrument flight rules (IFR) follows when transitioning from the en-route phase of flight to the approach phase at its destination airport. STARs link a fix on an airway to an initial approach fix, sequencing arriving traffic in a predictable flow for air traffic control. In ICAO terminology the procedure is also called a "standard arrival route".

Function

A STAR specifies lateral waypoints, descent altitudes (often given as windows or "at or above/at or below" constraints) and speed limits — commonly 250 knots below 10,000 feet — so that arriving aircraft descend in an orderly stream and arrive at the approach fix at an appropriate altitude and speed. Modern RNAV STARs support continuous descent operations, in which the aircraft descends at near-idle thrust, saving fuel and reducing noise.

Clearance and use

STARs are normally filed in the flight plan and confirmed in the landing clearance; controllers may amend them with radar vectors, especially in busy terminal areas. Each STAR is named after the fix where it begins, with numbers and letters denoting version and runway transition. The departure counterpart is the standard instrument departure (SID); together with instrument approach procedures, SIDs and STARs structure traffic at every major airport.
