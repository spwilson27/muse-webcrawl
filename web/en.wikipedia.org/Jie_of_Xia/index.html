Jie of Xia

Jie (Chinese: 桀; traditionally r. 1728–1675 BCE) was the 17th and last ruler of the Xia dynasty of China. He is traditionally regarded as a tyrant and oppressor who brought about the collapse of the Xia through his extravagance and cruelty.

Tradition

Later Chinese histories depict Jie as a ruler of great physical strength but corrupt character, devoted to pleasure with his consort Mo Xi. Stories attribute to him extravagances such as a lake of wine and cruelty toward his ministers; the minister Guan Longfeng is said to have been executed for remonstrating with him. The oppressive rule provoked rebellion led by Tang of Shang, who defeated Jie at the Battle of Mingtiao (c. 1600 BCE by conventional chronology). Jie was exiled and died in Nanchao.

In Chinese political tradition Jie of Xia, together with Zhou of Shang, became the archetype of the depraved last ruler whose loss of the Mandate of Heaven justified dynastic overthrow. Modern scholars debate the historicity of the Xia dynasty and of Jie himself; the accounts were written many centuries after the events they describe.
