The Franciscans are a group of related mendicant religious orders of the Catholic Church, founded in 1209 by Saint Francis of Assisi. They include the Order of Friars Minor, the Order of Saint Clare (Poor Clares), and the Third Order of Saint Francis for laypeople and secular clergy, all following Francis's ideals of poverty, humility and preaching.

History

Francis received oral approval of his primitive rule from Pope Innocent III in 1209. Clare of Assisi founded the women's branch in 1212. The definitive rule, the Regula bullata, was confirmed by Honorius III in 1223. The order grew rapidly, sending missionaries across Europe and beyond, and produced theologians such as Bonaventure and Duns Scotus. Internal disputes over the strictness of poverty led to divisions between Conventuals and Observants, and the Capuchins split off in the 1520s.

Branches

The First Order today comprises the Friars Minor (OFM), Friars Minor Conventual (OFM Conv.) and Friars Minor Capuchin (OFM Cap.). The Second Order is the contemplative Poor Clares. The Third Order includes the Secular Franciscan Order and many congregations of sisters and brothers founded in the 19th century.

Legacy

Franciscans staffed missions in the Americas, Asia and Africa, founded universities, and remain prominent in education, healthcare and social work. The brown or grey habit and the Tau cross are their symbols; their greeting is "Pax et bonum" — peace and all good.
