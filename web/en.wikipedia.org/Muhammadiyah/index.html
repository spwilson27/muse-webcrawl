Muhammadiyah


Founded: 18 November 1912, Yogyakarta, Dutch East Indies
Founder: Ahmad Dahlan
Type: Islamic mass organization
Headquarters: Yogyakarta and Jakarta, Indonesia
Membership: Tens of millions (claimed)

Muhammadiyah is an Indonesian Islamic organization founded in 1912 by the Islamic scholar Ahmad Dahlan in Yogyakarta. It is one of the largest Islamic movements in Indonesia, alongside Nahdlatul Ulama, and is known for its modernist, reformist interpretation of Islam and its extensive network of schools, universities, hospitals, and orphanages.

History

Ahmad Dahlan, a merchant and religious teacher influenced by the reformist ideas of Muhammad Abduh, founded Muhammadiyah to purify Indonesian Islam of what he saw as syncretic practices and to promote education modeled partly on Dutch colonial schools. The organization spread rapidly through Java and the outer islands, founding modern schools that combined religious and secular subjects.

Doctrine

Muhammadiyah advocates a return to the Qur'an and the Sunnah and rejects practices it regards as innovations (bid'ah), superstition, or saint veneration. It emphasizes rational interpretation, social welfare, and education. Its decision-making body for religious questions, the Majelis Tarjih, issues collective rulings.

Social and educational work

The organization operates thousands of schools, dozens of universities (including the Universitas Muhammadiyah network), hospitals and clinics, and charitable institutions. Its women's wing, Aisyiyah, founded in 1917, is one of the oldest Muslim women's organizations in Indonesia. The youth wing, Pemuda Muhammadiyah, and disaster-relief agency MDMC are also prominent.

Role in Indonesia

Muhammadiyah generally avoids direct party politics, though its members have been influential in Indonesian public life, and the PAN political party was founded by figures associated with it. Leaders such as Amien Rais and Din Syamsuddin have been nationally prominent.
