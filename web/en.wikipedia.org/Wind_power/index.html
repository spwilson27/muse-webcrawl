Wind power is the use of wind energy to generate useful work, principally electricity via wind turbines. Humans have used wind for millennia — for sailing ships and windmills grinding grain and pumping water — but electricity generation began in the late 19th century and expanded rapidly from the 1970s oil crises onward.

Technology

A modern wind turbine converts the kinetic energy of moving air into electricity: wind turns a rotor of two or three blades, driving a generator in the nacelle. Most utility-scale turbines are horizontal-axis machines mounted on towers 80–120 m tall, rated from about 1 to over 15 MW for the largest offshore designs. Power output varies with the cube of wind speed, and turbines operate between cut-in (~3 m/s) and cut-out (~25 m/s) speeds. Installations are grouped in wind farms on land or offshore.

Growth and role

Wind is now one of the cheapest sources of new electricity in many regions and a key tool for decarbonising power systems. Cumulative global capacity passed 1 terawatt in 2023, with China the largest market. Variability is managed through forecasting, geographic spread, interconnection, and storage. Issues include visual and noise impacts, bird and bat collisions, and land use, though land between turbines can remain in agricultural use.
