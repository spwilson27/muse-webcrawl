Fundamental theorem of finitely generated abelian groups

The fundamental theorem of finitely generated abelian groups, also known as the fundamental theorem of finite abelian groups in the finite case, is a classification theorem in abstract algebra. It states that every finitely generated abelian group G is isomorphic to a direct sum of cyclic groups of a specific, essentially unique form.

Statement

Every finitely generated abelian group G is isomorphic to ℤʳ ⊕ ℤ/n₁ ⊕ ℤ/n₂ ⊕ ⋯ ⊕ ℤ/nₖ, where r ≥ 0 is the rank (the Betti number) and the integers nᵢ ≥ 2 satisfy the divisibility condition n₁ | n₂ | ⋯ | nₖ. The rank r and the invariant factors nᵢ are uniquely determined by G. The factors ℤ/nᵢ are called the invariant factors, and the torsion subgroup ℤ/n₁ ⊕ ⋯ ⊕ ℤ/nₖ records all elements of finite order.

Primary decomposition

An equivalent formulation decomposes the torsion part into its primary components: for each prime p, the p-part is a direct sum of cyclic p-groups ℤ/p^{eᵢ}. The exponents in this elementary-divisor form are also unique.

Examples

Any finite abelian group of order 12, for example, is isomorphic to either ℤ/12 or ℤ/2 ⊕ ℤ/6 — the only two groups given by the partitions of the prime factorisations of 12. The group ℤ ⊕ ℤ/4 ⊕ ℤ/2 is already in invariant-factor form only after re-ordering so that 2 divides 4.

History and generalisation

The finite case goes back to work by Frobenius and Stickelberger (1879). The theorem is a special case of the structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a principal ideal domain, of which ℤ (the integers) and polynomial rings are examples.
