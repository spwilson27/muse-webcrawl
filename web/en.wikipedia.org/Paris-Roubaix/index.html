Paris–Roubaix is a one-day professional men's cycling race in northern France, held each spring, usually in mid-April. First run in 1896, it is one of cycling's five Monuments and is famous for its rough terrain, in particular the many sectors of cobblestone roads (pavé) that give it the nickname "The Hell of the North". The race is organised by the Amaury Sport Organisation and finishes on the outdoor velodrome in Roubaix; the winner receives a cobblestone as a trophy.

History

The race was created by Roubaix textile manufacturers to publicise their new velodrome. It survived both world wars, which devastated the region it crosses — the source of the "Hell of the North" epithet, coined in 1919 for the ruined landscape rather than the cobbles themselves. Belgian riders Roger De Vlaeminck and Tom Boonen share the record of four victories each.

Route and cobbles

The modern start is in Compiègne, north of Paris. The flat course of around 257 km includes roughly 55 km of cobbles divided into sectors rated by difficulty. The most notorious are the Trouée d'Arenberg (the Arenberg forest trench, introduced in 1968), Mons-en-Pévèle and the Carrefour de l'Arbre. Mud, punctures and crashes are routine; riders finish coated in dust or mud. A women's race, Paris–Roubaix Femmes, was first held in 2021.

Recent winners

Recent editions have been dominated by Dutch rider Mathieu van der Poel, winner in 2023 and 2024 with long solo attacks, following victories by Sonny Colbrelli (2021) and Dylan van Baarle (2022).
