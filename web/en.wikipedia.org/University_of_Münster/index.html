The University of Münster (German: Universität Münster, known until 2023 as the Westfälische Wilhelms-Universität Münster, WWU) is a public research university in Münster, North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany. Founded in 1780, it is one of Germany's largest universities, with around 44,000 students and 15 faculties, and is known for the humanities, law, economics, medicine, and natural sciences.

History

The university traces its origins to the Münster Jesuit college of 1588 and to a university founded in 1780 by the prince-bishopric under Franz von Fürstenberg, with faculties of theology, philosophy, law, and medicine. It was downgraded to an academy during the Napoleonic era and restored as a full university in 1902 by Emperor Wilhelm II, whose name it bore until the university renamed itself University of Münster in 2023, citing Wilhelm II's militarism and antisemitism.

Organisation

The university's buildings are spread across the city, centred on the baroque Schloss, the former prince-bishop's palace. It has 15 faculties including Catholic and Protestant theology, law, economics, medicine, and mathematics and computer science. Its library (ULB Münster) holds major manuscript collections, including fragments studied by the Institut für neutestamentliche Textforschung, an internationally known centre for New Testament textual criticism.

Research

The university hosts Clusters of Excellence in mathematics ("Dynamics – Geometry – Structure") and religion and politics, and the Cells in Motion imaging centre. It is a member of the U15 group of German research universities.
