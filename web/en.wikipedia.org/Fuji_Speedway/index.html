Fuji Speedway is a motorsport race track standing in the foothills of Mount Fuji in Oyama, Shizuoka Prefecture, Japan. Built in the early 1960s and opened in 1965, it was originally intended to host NASCAR-style racing in Japan before being completed as a road course. It is famous for its very long main straight of about 1.5 km, one of the longest in world motorsport.

Formula One

Fuji hosted the first Formula One Japanese Grand Prix in 1976, the dramatic season finale in torrential rain at which James Hunt won the World Championship after Niki Lauda withdrew, judging the flooded track too dangerous. After a fatal accident in 1977 the Grand Prix moved away, returning briefly in 2007 and 2008 following a major renovation designed by Hermann Tilke, before moving permanently to Suzuka.

Other racing

The circuit is a regular venue for Japan's Super GT and Super Formula series and hosts the 6 Hours of Fuji round of the FIA World Endurance Championship. During the 2020 Tokyo Olympics (held in 2021), Fuji Speedway served as the finish venue for the road cycling races. Toyota, which bought the circuit in 2000, also operates extensive test and hospitality facilities on the site, in the shadow of Japan's most famous mountain.
