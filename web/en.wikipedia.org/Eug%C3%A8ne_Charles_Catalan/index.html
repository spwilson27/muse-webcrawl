Eugène Charles Catalan (30 May 1814 – 14 February 1894) was a French and Belgian mathematician who worked on continued fractions, descriptive geometry, number theory and combinatorics. He held a chair at the University of Liège.

Work

Catalan is remembered for the sequence of numbers now called Catalan numbers, which count many combinatorial structures and arise in the solution of polygon triangulation problems. In 1844 he stated the conjecture that the only consecutive powers greater than 1 are 8 and 9 — that is, the only solution of ap − bq = 1 with a, b ≥ 2 and p, q ≥ 2 is 3² − 2³ = 1. The statement, known as Catalan's conjecture, resisted proof for over 150 years until Preda Mihăilescu proved it in 2002; it is now called Mihăilescu's theorem.

He also introduced the Catalan surface, a ruled surface in geometry, and contributed to the theory of continued fractions and hypergeometric series.
