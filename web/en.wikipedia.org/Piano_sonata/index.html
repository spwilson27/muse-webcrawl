Piano sonata

A piano sonata is a sonata written for a solo piano. Piano sonatas are usually written in three or four movements, although some consist of a single movement (such as those by Scarlatti, Liszt and Scriabin) or two movements.

Classical form

The Classical piano sonata, developed by Haydn, Mozart and Beethoven, typically comprises a fast first movement in sonata form (exposition, development, recapitulation), a slow movement, a minuet or scherzo, and a lively finale, often a rondo. Beethoven's 32 piano sonatas, including the "Pathétique", "Moonlight", "Waldstein", "Appassionata" and "Hammerklavier", are regarded as the pinnacle of the repertoire.

Romantic and modern sonatas

Schubert, Chopin, Schumann, Liszt and Brahms expanded the form's scale and emotional range; Liszt's Sonata in B minor fused the movements into one continuous span. In the 20th century, composers such as Scriabin, Prokofiev, Bartók and Boulez reinterpreted the genre, while the solo sonatas of Domenico Scarlatti — over 550 short binary-form works for harpsichord — remain foundational.
