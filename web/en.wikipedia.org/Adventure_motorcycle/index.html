Adventure motorcycle

A large-displacement adventure motorcycle equipped for long-distance touring.

An adventure motorcycle, often called an adventure tourer or ADV bike, is a type of motorcycle designed for both paved and unpaved surfaces. Adventure motorcycles combine features of touring motorcycles — such as large fuel tanks, luggage capacity and comfortable ergonomics for long distances — with the long-travel suspension, upright riding position and ground clearance of off-road machines.

Characteristics

Typical characteristics include a tall seat height, spoked or cast wheels with dual-sport tyres (commonly a 21-inch front wheel on more off-road-oriented models, or 19-inch on road-biased models), engine protection such as skid plates and crash bars, and provisions for hard or soft luggage. Engines range from small single-cylinder units of around 300 cc to large twins exceeding 1,200 cc. Modern examples frequently include rider aids such as switchable ABS, traction control, riding modes and electronic suspension.

History

The category emerged from the popularity of rally raid racing, particularly the Paris–Dakar Rally, founded in 1979. BMW's R80G/S of 1980 is widely credited as the first purpose-built adventure motorcycle; its descendant, the R 1250 GS and later R 1300 GS, became the best-selling model in the segment. Other landmark models include the Honda Africa Twin, originally built as a Dakar replica, the KTM 950/990 and 1290 Super Adventure series, the Yamaha Ténéré line, the Suzuki V-Strom and the Triumph Tiger.

Usage

Adventure motorcycles are marketed for long-distance travel on mixed surfaces, including transcontinental expeditions. Television series and books documenting round-the-world journeys on adventure bikes — such as Long Way Round, in which Ewan McGregor and Charley Boorman rode from London to New York — substantially increased the popularity of the segment in the 2000s. Critics note that many large adventure bikes are heavy and rarely used off-road, prompting growth in a middleweight class of more manageable machines.
