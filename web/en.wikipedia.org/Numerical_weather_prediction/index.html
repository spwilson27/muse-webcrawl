Numerical weather prediction

Numerical weather prediction (NWP) uses mathematical models of the atmosphere and oceans to predict the weather based on current weather conditions. Though first attempted manually in the 1920s, it was not until the advent of computer simulation that numerical weather prediction achieved practical results.

History

Lewis Fry Richardson proposed numerical forecasting in 1922, imagining a "forecast factory" of human computers, but his trial calculation failed because of inadequate data and unstable methods. In 1950, a team led by Jule Charney and John von Neumann produced the first successful computerized forecast on the ENIAC. Operational forecasting began in the 1950s and 1960s, and skill has improved steadily with better models, observations, and computing power.

Method

Models divide the atmosphere into a three-dimensional grid and integrate the governing equations of fluid dynamics and thermodynamics forward in time. Observations from satellites, radar, aircraft, and surface stations are assimilated to form the initial state. Ensemble forecasting runs many simulations with slightly different initial conditions to estimate uncertainty. Major global models include the ECMWF's IFS, the US GFS, and the UK Met Office Unified Model.
