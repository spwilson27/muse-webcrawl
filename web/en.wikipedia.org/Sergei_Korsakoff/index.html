Sergei Korsakoff

Sergei Sergeyevich Korsakoff (Russian: Сергей Сергеевич Корсаков; 22 January 1854 – 1 May 1900) was a Russian neuropsychiatrist whose name is immortalised in Korsakoff syndrome, a chronic memory disorder associated with thiamine deficiency, often following prolonged alcohol misuse.

Life and career

Born in Gus-Khrustalny, Korsakoff studied medicine at Moscow University, graduating in 1875. He became one of the founders of Russian psychiatry, directing the psychiatric clinic of Moscow University and introducing humane, "no-restraint" treatment methods in Russian asylums. He was also an accomplished textbook author; his Course of Psychiatry (1893) was widely used.

Korsakoff's psychosis

In a series of papers in 1887–1891, Korsakoff described a distinctive psychosis characterised by profound anterograde amnesia, disorientation, and confabulation, occurring together with polyneuritis, which he called cerebropathia psychica toxaemica. His student Carl Wernicke described a related acute encephalopathy, and the combined condition is now often called Wernicke–Korsakoff syndrome.
