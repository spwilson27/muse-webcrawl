Achromatic lens

An achromatic lens or achromat is a lens that is designed to limit the effects of chromatic and spherical aberration. Achromatic lenses are corrected to bring two wavelengths (typically red and blue) into focus on the same plane. The most common type is the achromatic doublet, in which two individual lenses made from glasses with different amounts of dispersion are cemented together.

The first practical achromats were developed in the eighteenth century following the work of Chester Moore Hall and John Dollond, whose 1758 patent led to major improvements in refracting telescopes. An achromat typically pairs a positive element of crown glass with a negative element of flint glass, whose dispersions cancel while retaining net converging power.
