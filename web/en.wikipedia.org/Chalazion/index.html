Chalazion
SpecialtyOphthalmology
SymptomsPainless or mildly tender eyelid lump
CauseBlocked meibomian gland

A chalazion (from Greek chalaza, "hailstone") is a cyst in the eyelid caused by inflammation of a blocked meibomian (oil) gland. It usually occurs on the upper eyelid and presents as a firm, round lump within the tarsal plate. Unlike a stye (hordeolum), which is an acute bacterial infection of a gland and is usually painful, a chalazion is a chronic, sterile, granulomatous inflammation and is typically painless, though it may be tender in its early inflammatory phase.

Signs and symptoms

A chalazion develops gradually over several weeks as a painless swelling of the eyelid; large lesions can press on the cornea and induce astigmatism or blur vision. Eversion of the lid may reveal a red or grey granulomatous mass on the conjunctival surface. Chalazia are more common in adults than children and may recur, particularly in people with blepharitis, rosacea or seborrhoeic dermatitis.

Treatment

Many chalazia resolve spontaneously over weeks to months. First-line treatment is conservative: warm compresses applied several times daily and gentle lid massage to promote drainage, together with lid hygiene. Persistent or large lesions may be treated with an intralesional steroid injection or with surgical incision and curettage performed under local anaesthetic from the inner surface of the eyelid. Recurrent chalazia at the same site in an older adult should be biopsied to exclude sebaceous gland carcinoma, which can mimic a chalazion.
