Suryavamsha

Suryavamsha (Sanskrit: सूर्यवंश, "Solar dynasty") is a legendary dynasty of ancient India, said to descend from Surya, the sun god, through Vaivasvata Manu and King Ikshvaku. It is one of the two principal lineages of Hindu tradition, alongside the lunar Chandravamsha, and is celebrated in the epics and Puranas as the line of such figures as Harishchandra, Bhagiratha, Raghu and Rama.

Legendary history

According to the Puranic genealogies, the dynasty begins with the sun god Vivasvan, whose son Vaivasvata Manu survived a great flood and fathered Ikshvaku, founder of the royal line of Ayodhya. The lists of kings include the truth-telling Harishchandra; Sagara, whose descendants' digging brought the ocean (sagara) its name; Bhagiratha, who brought the goddess Ganga down from heaven; and Raghu, whose name the dynasty also bears (Raghuvamsha, as in Kalidasa's celebrated poem). Its most famous member is Rama, hero of the Ramayana and regarded as an avatar of Vishnu.

Historical claims

Numerous historical royal houses of India claimed Suryavamsha descent to legitimate their rule, among them the Sisodia Rajputs of Mewar — who styled themselves hereditary custodians of the line of Rama — as well as dynasties in Odisha (the Eastern Ganga and Gajapati rulers) and elsewhere. The kings of Thailand's Chakri dynasty likewise take the regnal name Rama, invoking the Ayodhya lineage.

In literature

The dynasty's history is narrated in the Vishnu Purana, the Bhagavata Purana and the epics, and is the subject of Kalidasa's Sanskrit mahakavya Raghuvamsha ("Dynasty of Raghu"), one of the most admired works of classical Indian poetry.
