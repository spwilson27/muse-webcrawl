Exponential decay

An exponential decay curve approaching zero.

A quantity is subject to exponential decay if it decreases at a rate proportional to its current value. Symbolically, the process is described by the equation dN/dt = −λN, where N is the quantity, t is time, and λ is a positive constant called the decay constant. The solution is N(t) = N0e−λt, where N0 is the initial value.

Half-life

The half-life is the time required for the quantity to fall to half its initial value: t1/2 = ln 2 / λ. After one half-life 50% remains; after two, 25%; after n half-lives, (1/2)n. The mean lifetime τ = 1/λ is the average time a decaying entity persists before decaying, and is related to half-life by τ = t1/2 / ln 2.

Applications

Exponential decay models radioactive decay of unstable isotopes, the discharge of a capacitor through a resistor, the cooling of an object toward ambient temperature (Newton's law of cooling, approximately), the elimination of many drugs from the body, and the attenuation of light or sound in absorbing media.

Decay by multiple processes

If a quantity decays by several independent processes simultaneously, the overall decay constant is the sum of the individual constants, and the observed half-life is shorter than that of any single process.
