Louisiana Creole

Louisiana Creole (Kouri-Vini) is a French-based creole language spoken in Louisiana. It developed in the French colonial period from contact between French settlers, enslaved Africans and Native American peoples.

History

The language took shape in the 18th century on plantations and in New Orleans. It is distinct from Cajun French, a dialect of French, though the two influenced each other and share vocabulary. Creole identity in Louisiana spans people of European, African and mixed ancestry.

Features

Louisiana Creole has a largely French-derived vocabulary with grammar shaped by African languages: preverbal tense-aspect markers, no grammatical gender, and pronouns such as mo (I) and li (he/she). Example: "Mo sòr manje" — "My sister is eating / ate."

Status

Once widely spoken, the language declined under 20th-century English-only schooling. Estimates of speakers range from a few thousand to around ten thousand, mostly older. Revitalisation efforts include classes, dictionaries and new media in Kouri-Vini.
