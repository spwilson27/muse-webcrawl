Kim (novel)

Author: Rudyard Kipling
Published: 1901
Setting: India, late 19th century

Kim is a novel by Nobel Prize-winning English author Rudyard Kipling, first published in book form in 1901. Set in India during the Great Game — the political rivalry between Britain and Russia in Central Asia — it follows the orphan Kimball O'Hara, "Kim", an Irish soldier's son raised as a street boy in Lahore.

Plot

Kim journeys with a Tibetan lama searching for a legendary River of the Arrow, while being recruited into British intelligence by Mahbub Ali and the ethnographer Colonel Creighton. Trained at St Xavier's school and as a spy, Kim helps thwart Russian agents in the Himalayas, torn between the lama's spiritual quest and the Game. The novel is celebrated for its vivid portrait of Indian life and is often ranked among the greatest English-language novels, though modern readings also examine its imperial outlook.
