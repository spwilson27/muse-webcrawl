The October Revolution, also known as the Bolshevik Revolution, was a revolution in Russia led by the Bolshevik Party of Vladimir Lenin that was a key moment in the Russian Revolution of 1917–1923. It took place on 25 October 1917 in the Julian calendar (7 November in the Gregorian calendar) and overthrew the Russian Provisional Government, transferring power to the soviets (workers' councils) dominated by the Bolsheviks.

Background

The February Revolution had forced Tsar Nicholas II to abdicate, leaving a Provisional Government under Alexander Kerensky ruling alongside the Petrograd Soviet in an unstable "dual power." The government continued Russia's involvement in the First World War despite catastrophic losses, postponed land reform, and lost authority after the failed June offensive and the Kornilov affair, during which the Bolsheviks — briefly suppressed after the July Days — rebuilt their strength as defenders of the revolution. By autumn, the Bolsheviks held majorities in the Petrograd and Moscow soviets; Lenin, from hiding, demanded an immediate seizure of power, and Leon Trotsky organised the insurrection through the Soviet's Military Revolutionary Committee.

The insurrection

On the night of 24–25 October (O.S.), Red Guards and loyal soldiers occupied the bridges, railway stations, telegraph offices, and power stations of Petrograd with almost no resistance. The cruiser Aurora fired a blank shot as the signal to storm the Winter Palace, where the Provisional Government's ministers were arrested in the early hours of 26 October; Kerensky had already fled. Casualties were remarkably few. Simultaneously, the Second All-Russian Congress of Soviets, meeting at Smolny, proclaimed the transfer of power and issued the Decree on Peace and the Decree on Land, abolishing private ownership of land.

Consolidation and civil war

Bolshevik control was initially confined largely to Petrograd and Moscow, where fighting was heavier. The new government, the Council of People's Commissars (Sovnarkom) chaired by Lenin, moved the capital to Moscow in March 1918 and signed the punitive Treaty of Brest-Litovsk with Germany. Elections to the Constituent Assembly in November returned a Socialist Revolutionary majority; the Bolsheviks dispersed the Assembly after a single day in January 1918. Opposition from "White" armies, foreign intervention, and internal revolts plunged Russia into a civil war lasting until 1922, from which the Bolsheviks emerged victorious, founding the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics in December 1922.

Legacy

The October Revolution was one of the most consequential events of the 20th century: it created the world's first self-proclaimed socialist state, inspired communist movements and revolutions worldwide, split the international socialist movement, and set the pattern of Cold War confrontation. In the Soviet Union, 7 November was celebrated as the Revolution's anniversary; John Reed's eyewitness account Ten Days That Shook the World remains a classic narration of the events.
