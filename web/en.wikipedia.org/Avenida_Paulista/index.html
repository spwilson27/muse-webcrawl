Avenida Paulista

Avenida Paulista (Paulista Avenue) is one of the most important avenues in São Paulo, Brazil. About 2.8 kilometres long, it runs along the ridge between the Jardins and Bela Vista districts and serves as a centre of finance, commerce, culture and public protest.

History

The avenue was inaugurated in 1891, developed by engineer Joaquim Eugênio de Lima, and named in honour of the people of São Paulo state (paulistas). It was initially a residential boulevard of coffee barons' mansions, of which the Casa das Rosas is a notable survivor. From the 1950s and especially after the 1960s, banks and corporate headquarters displaced the mansions and made it São Paulo's financial heart.

Landmarks

Landmarks include the São Paulo Museum of Art (MASP), a modernist landmark by Lina Bo Bardi suspended over a public plaza; Trianon Park; and numerous cultural centres and consulates. Since 2015 the avenue has been closed to motor traffic on Sundays and holidays, becoming a linear park for pedestrians and cyclists, and it is the traditional site of São Paulo's Pride parade and of large political demonstrations.
