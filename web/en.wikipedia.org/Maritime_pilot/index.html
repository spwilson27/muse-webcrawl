Maritime pilot

A maritime pilot, also known as a marine pilot or harbour pilot, is a mariner who manoeuvres ships through dangerous or congested waters, such as harbours or river mouths. Pilots are expert shiphandlers who possess detailed local knowledge of the waters where they operate.

Role

Pilotage is compulsory in most major ports. The pilot boards the vessel — often by pilot boat or helicopter, climbing a rope pilot ladder — and advises the master on navigation, currents, tides, berthing and tug use. Legally, the master retains command and responsibility in most jurisdictions, though some, such as the Panama Canal, give the pilot full navigational control.

Training

Pilots are typically master mariners with extensive sea experience who undergo further years of supervised training and licensing examinations specific to their district, covering local hydrography, traffic schemes and port procedures.

History

Pilotage is among the oldest maritime professions, mentioned in ancient Greek and Roman sources; medieval guilds such as the Cinque Ports pilots regulated the trade in Britain, and pilotage authorities remain statutory bodies in many countries.
