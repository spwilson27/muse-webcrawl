Secretary of State for Health and Social Care

The Secretary of State for Health and Social Care, also referred to as the Health Secretary, is a secretary of state in the Government of the United Kingdom, responsible for the work of the Department of Health and Social Care. The incumbent is a member of the Cabinet of the United Kingdom and reports directly to the Prime Minister.

The office oversees the National Health Service (NHS) in England, public health policy, and adult social care. It is one of the most senior and politically exposed posts in British government, not least because health spending is among the largest areas of public expenditure.

History

The post traces its origins to the Ministry of Health, created in 1919. In 1968 the ministry merged with the Ministry of Social Security to form the Department of Health and Social Security (DHSS), headed by the Secretary of State for Social Services. In 1988 the DHSS was split, creating the separate post of Secretary of State for Health. In January 2018, under Prime Minister Theresa May, the role was renamed Secretary of State for Health and Social Care to reflect the department's expanded remit over social care policy.

Responsibilities

The Health Secretary has overall responsibility for the work of the Department of Health and Social Care, including policy on the NHS, public health, and social care in England. Health is a devolved matter, so the governments of Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland run their own health services; the UK Health Secretary's direct remit covers England, though the department handles some UK-wide functions.

The secretary is supported by ministers of state and parliamentary under-secretaries, and works with arm's-length bodies such as NHS England, the UK Health Security Agency and the Care Quality Commission.

Notable holders

Early holders of the modern post included William Waldegrave, Virginia Bottomley and Stephen Dorrell. Later health secretaries have included Alan Milburn, Patricia Hewitt, Alan Johnson, Andrew Lansley — architect of the Health and Social Care Act 2012 — and Jeremy Hunt, the longest-serving holder of the office, who served from 2012 to 2018. Matt Hancock held the post during the early COVID-19 pandemic, succeeded by Sajid Javid and then Steve Barclay under Boris Johnson and Liz Truss. Victoria Atkins served under Rishi Sunak, and Wes Streeting was appointed in July 2024 under Keir Starmer.
