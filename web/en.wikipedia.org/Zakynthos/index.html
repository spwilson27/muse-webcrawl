Zakynthos

Zakynthos (Greek: Ζάκυνθος), also known by its Italian name Zante, is a Greek island in the Ionian Sea. It is the third largest of the Ionian Islands, covering an area of about 405 square kilometres (156 sq mi), with a population of around 41,000. The island's capital and largest town is Zakynthos City, on the southeast coast. Zakynthos is famous for its dramatic coastline — notably Navagio ("Shipwreck") Beach — and as an important nesting ground for loggerhead sea turtles.

Geography

Zakynthos lies in the Ionian Sea about 20 km from the northwest coast of the Peloponnese and south of Cephalonia. Its western coast is rugged and mountainous, rising to Mount Vrachionas (758 m), while the southeast is a fertile plain of vineyards, olive groves, and citrus orchards. The island's coastline is roughly 123 km long, indented with caves and bays, including the Blue Caves at the northern tip.

History

Inhabited since the Neolithic era, Zakynthos appears in Homer, who names it in the Catalogue of Ships in the Iliad. The island was later controlled successively by Athens, Macedon, and Rome. From 1185 it passed through Norman, Angevin, and other hands before a long period of Venetian rule (1484–1797), which left a deep mark on its culture and gave it the Italian name Zante and the epithet "the Flower of the Levant". After brief French, Russian-Ottoman, and British periods, Zakynthos was united with Greece in 1864. A catastrophic earthquake in 1953 destroyed most of the island's historic buildings, including much of Zakynthos City, which was rebuilt.

Tourism and wildlife

Tourism is the mainstay of the island's economy. Navagio Beach, where the wreck of the MV Panagiotis rests on white sand beneath sheer cliffs, is one of the most photographed places in Greece. Laganas Bay, on the south coast, is among the Mediterranean's most important nesting areas for the loggerhead sea turtle (Caretta caretta), protected since 1999 by the National Marine Park of Zakynthos. The island is also the birthplace of Dionysios Solomos, author of the Greek national anthem.
