Boeing 737

The Boeing 737 is an American narrow-body airliner produced by Boeing at its Renton factory in Washington. Developed to supplement the Boeing 727 on short and thin routes, the twinjet has a six-abreast economy cabin and retains much of the fuselage cross-section of Boeing's earlier 707 and 727 designs.

Envisioned in 1964, the initial 737-100 made its first flight in April 1967 and entered service in February 1968 with Lufthansa. The family has been produced in four generations: the Original series (737-100/-200), the Classic series (-300/-400/-500), the Next Generation series (-600/-700/-800/-900), and the 737 MAX series. More than 12,000 Boeing 737s had been delivered by the mid-2020s, making it the most produced commercial jet aircraft in history, competing primarily with the Airbus A320 family.

Design

To save development cost, Boeing retained the 707's fuselage cross-section, giving the 737 a wider cabin than its early competitors. The engines are mounted under the low wing; on later variants with larger turbofans, the engine nacelles have a distinctive flattened bottom to maintain ground clearance. The 737 retains conventional hydraulic flight controls, unlike the fly-by-wire systems of its Airbus competitors.

Generations

Original and Classic

The 737-200 was a stretched version that became the standard early model, popular with airlines serving short fields. The Classic series, launched in the 1980s with CFM56 turbofans, stretched the aircraft further and improved fuel efficiency.

Next Generation

The 737NG, introduced in 1997, brought a redesigned wing, glass cockpit and longer range. The -800 became the most popular variant, widely used by low-cost carriers such as Ryanair and Southwest Airlines.

737 MAX

The MAX, re-engined with CFM LEAP-1B turbofans, first flew in 2016. Following two fatal accidents in 2018 and 2019 (Lion Air Flight 610 and Ethiopian Airlines Flight 302), the type was grounded worldwide from March 2019 until late 2020, after Boeing revised the MCAS flight-control system and training requirements.
