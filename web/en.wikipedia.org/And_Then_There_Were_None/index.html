And Then There Were None

And Then There Were None is a mystery novel by the English writer Agatha Christie, first published in the United Kingdom in November 1939. It is the world's best-selling mystery novel and one of the best-selling books of all time, with over 100 million copies sold.

Plot

Ten strangers are lured to an island mansion off the Devon coast by a mysterious host, U. N. Owen. At dinner, a recorded voice accuses each guest of a past crime for which they escaped justice. One by one, the guests are murdered in ways that follow the verses of the nursery rhyme "Ten Little Soldier Boys," and the ten figurines on the dining table disappear one at a time. Cut off from the mainland, the survivors realise the killer must be one of them. A postscript and a message in a bottle found by a fishing boat reveal the murderer's identity and method.

Reception and adaptations

Christie described it as her most difficult book to write. The novel has been adapted many times for stage (by Christie herself, with a softened ending), film, radio, and television, including a noted 2015 BBC miniseries. Its original UK title, containing a racial slur, and the rhyme's wording have been changed in modern editions.
