Othniel

TribeJudah
RoleFirst judge of Israel (biblical)
FamilySon of Kenaz; wife Achsah

Othniel (Hebrew: עָתְנִיאֵל בֶּן־קְנַז, Otniel Ben Knaz) was the first of the biblical judges of Israel mentioned in the Book of Judges. He was a member of the tribe of Judah, the younger brother of Caleb, and married Caleb's daughter Achsah after capturing the city of Debir (Kiriath-sepher).

Narrative

According to Judges 3, after the Israelites had been oppressed for eight years by Chushan-Rishathaim, king of Aram-Naharaim, the spirit of the LORD came upon Othniel, who went to war, defeated the king, and delivered Israel. The land then had rest for forty years until Othniel's death.

Legacy

Othniel's account sets the pattern repeated through the Book of Judges: apostasy, oppression, a cry for help, and deliverance through a raised-up judge. The modern Israeli settlement of Otniel in the West Bank is named after him.
