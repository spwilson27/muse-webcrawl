Ovonramwen Nogbaisi (c. 1857 – 13 January 1914), also called Overami in British accounts, was the thirty-sixth Oba of the Kingdom of Benin, reigning from 1888 until his deposition by the British in 1897. His fall during the Benin Expedition of 1897 marked the effective end of the independent Benin Kingdom, one of the great states of pre-colonial West Africa.

Reign and conflict with Britain

Ovonramwen inherited a kingdom under growing pressure from British trade and imperial expansion. In 1892 he signed a treaty with the British consul Henry Galway that he believed regulated trade, but which Britain later claimed made Benin a protectorate. Disputes centred on the Oba's control of trade in palm produce and his restriction of access to Benin City. When Acting Consul-General James Robert Phillips led an unarmed — but provocative — expedition toward Benin City in January 1897, against the Oba's instruction to wait until a ritual period ended, the party was ambushed and nearly all its members killed.

Deposition and exile

Britain responded with a punitive expedition under Admiral Harry Rawson, which captured and burned Benin City in February 1897 and looted thousands of royal artworks — the famous Benin Bronzes — many of which remain in Western museums and are the subject of restitution claims. Ovonramwen surrendered in August 1897, was tried and deposed, and exiled to Calabar, where he died in 1914. His son Eweka II was permitted to restore the monarchy later that year under British rule.
