Global Game Jam

The Global Game Jam (GGJ) is an annual distributed game jam — and the largest game creation event in the world — in which participants at hundreds of physical and online sites in dozens of countries simultaneously create games over a single weekend, typically the last weekend of January. Founded in 2008 by Susan Gold and others and first held in January 2009, the event is run by the non-profit Global Game Jam, Inc.

Format

At the start of the event, a secret theme is revealed simultaneously worldwide (with region-specific start times to prevent spoilers). Participants form small teams — programmers, artists, designers, musicians, and writers — and design and build a complete, playable game in about 48 hours. Games are uploaded to the GGJ website under a Creative Commons licence, and the event emphasises collaboration, experimentation, and learning rather than competition: there are no winners or prizes. Additional optional "diversifiers" suggest constraints or accessibility features teams may adopt.

Scale

The jam has grown from 1,600 participants in 23 countries in 2009 to tens of thousands of participants at more than 800 sites in over 100 countries, producing thousands of games each year. It has been recognised by Guinness World Records as the largest game jam. The event went partly online during the COVID-19 pandemic and has since operated in a hybrid format.
