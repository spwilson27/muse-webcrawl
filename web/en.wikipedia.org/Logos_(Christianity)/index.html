Logos (Christianity)

In Christian theology, the Logos (Greek: Λόγος, "word, reason, discourse") is a name or title of Jesus Christ, seen as the pre-existent second person of the Trinity.

Gospel of John

The concept is anchored in the prologue of the Gospel of John: "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God… And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us" (John 1:1, 1:14). Here the Logos is both distinct from God and identified with God, and is the agent through whom all things were made.

Background

The term draws on Greek philosophy — in Heraclitus, the Stoics and Philo of Alexandria, the logos was the rational principle ordering the cosmos and mediating between God and the world — and on Jewish traditions about the personified Wisdom and Word of God, which early Christian writers combined in interpreting Christ.

Patristic development

Church Fathers such as Justin Martyr and Origen developed Logos theology (including the apologetic idea of the logos spermatikos, the "seed-bearing word" present in all humanity), and the doctrine underlies the Nicene affirmation that Christ is of one substance with the Father.
