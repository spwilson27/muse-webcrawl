Sebastianism

Sebastianism (Portuguese: Sebastianismo) is a Portuguese messianic belief that King Sebastian of Portugal, who disappeared in battle in 1578, would one day return to save Portugal in its darkest hour. The myth became a powerful strand of Portuguese national identity and later spread to Brazil, where it inspired millenarian movements.

Origins

Sebastian was killed at the Battle of Alcácer Quibir in Morocco in 1578, but his body was never conclusively identified. His death without an heir led to the Iberian Union under Spain in 1580, and many Portuguese refused to accept that the young king was dead, believing he would return on a foggy morning to restore the kingdom. Several impostors claiming to be Sebastian appeared in the following decades.

In Brazil

The belief travelled to colonial Brazil, where it animated popular movements such as the communities at Pedra Bonita in the 1830s and contributed to the atmosphere of the Canudos settlement destroyed in 1897. Elements of Sebastianism persist in Portuguese and Brazilian folklore, literature, and cordel poetry.
