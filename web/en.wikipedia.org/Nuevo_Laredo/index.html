Nuevo Laredo is a city in the Municipality of Nuevo Laredo in the Mexican state of Tamaulipas. It lies on the southern bank of the Rio Grande, opposite Laredo, Texas, in the United States. Together the two cities form the Laredo–Nuevo Laredo metropolitan area, one of the busiest inland ports on the Mexico–United States border.

History

Nuevo Laredo was founded in 1848, after the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo ended the Mexican–American War and moved the border to the Rio Grande. Families from the northern part of Laredo who wished to remain Mexican citizens crossed the river and established the new settlement, famously carrying with them even the remains of their ancestors from the old cemetery. The city grew with the arrival of the railroad in the 1880s, which linked Mexico City with the United States.

Economy

International trade dominates the local economy. Nuevo Laredo and Laredo together handle a very large share of the truck-borne trade between Mexico and the United States; the World Trade International Bridge, opened in 2000, is among the busiest commercial border crossings in North America. The city also hosts maquiladora manufacturing plants and a major customs and logistics sector.

Geography and climate

The city sits in the Rio Grande plains of northeastern Mexico, in a hot semi-arid climate zone, with very hot summers frequently exceeding 40 °C (104 °F) and mild winters. Several international bridges — including the Gateway to the Americas Bridge and the Juárez–Lincoln International Bridge — connect it with Laredo.
