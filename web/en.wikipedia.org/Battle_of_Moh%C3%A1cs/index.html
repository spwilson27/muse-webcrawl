The Battle of Mohács was fought on 29 August 1526 near Mohács, in the Kingdom of Hungary, between the forces of Hungary led by King Louis II and the invading Ottoman Empire led by Sultan Suleiman the Magnificent. The Ottoman victory led to the partition of Hungary for several centuries between the Ottoman Empire, the Habsburg monarchy, and the Principality of Transylvania.

Background

Suleiman invaded Hungary in 1526 after the expiry of a truce, taking Belgrade's frontier fortresses' successors and advancing up the Danube. The Hungarian army, some 26,000–30,000 men under the Archbishop Pál Tomori and King Louis II, met an Ottoman force roughly twice its size on the plain south of Mohács.

The battle

The battle lasted barely two hours. The Hungarian heavy cavalry charged the Ottoman centre but was shattered by Janissary musket fire and artillery, and the army collapsed. King Louis II died during the retreat, apparently drowning in a marshy stream, along with much of the Hungarian nobility and clergy.

Aftermath

With the king dead and the army annihilated, central Hungary fell under Ottoman rule for 150 years, the crown was disputed between Ferdinand of Habsburg and John Zápolya, and Mohács became a byword in Hungary for national catastrophe.
