Villages with fortified churches in Transylvania

The villages with fortified churches in Transylvania are a group of villages in Transylvania, Romania, built between the 13th and 16th centuries by the Transylvanian Saxons, German settlers invited by the Kings of Hungary. More than 150 well-preserved fortified churches survive of the nearly 300 originally built.

History

Repeated Mongol and later Ottoman invasions led Saxon communities to fortify their most important building — the church. Defences ranged from single towers and curtain walls with storage rooms, to full church-fortresses with multiple rings of walls, battlements, and provisions for a whole village to shelter inside. The surrounding villages were laid out with characteristic rows of solid houses along main streets and green spaces.

UNESCO listing

Seven villages — Biertan, Câlnic, Dârjiu, Prejmer, Saschiz, Valea Viilor, and Viscri — were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List (Biertan in 1993, the others in 1999) as outstanding examples of a cultural landscape and of defensive architecture. The church at Prejmer is considered the strongest church fortress in Transylvania, and Biertan was a bishop's seat for nearly 300 years.
