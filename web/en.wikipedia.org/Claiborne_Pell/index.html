Claiborne Pell

Claiborne de Borda Pell
Born: 22 November 1918, New York City
Died: 1 January 2009, Newport, Rhode Island
U.S. Senator from Rhode Island, 1961–1997

Claiborne de Borda Pell (November 22, 1918 – January 1, 2009) was an American politician and writer who served as a U.S. Senator from Rhode Island for six terms from 1961 to 1997. A Democrat, he is best remembered as the sponsor of the Pell Grants, the federal student aid programme he championed through the Higher Education Act amendments of 1972.

Early life and career

Pell was born into a wealthy and politically connected family in New York City and was educated at Princeton University. He served in the U.S. Coast Guard during the Second World War, later working in the State Department and as an investment adviser before entering politics. He won the Democratic nomination for the Senate in 1960 by defeating two former governors and went on to win the general election comfortably.

Senate career

Pell chaired the Senate Foreign Relations Committee from 1987 to 1995. He supported the creation of the National Endowment for the Arts and the National Endowment for the Humanities, the National Sea Grant College Program, and funding for public transportation; the Pell Grants — initially called Basic Educational Opportunity Grants — were renamed in his honour in 1980. The Claiborne Pell Bridge across Narragansett Bay also bears his name.

Retirement and death

Pell did not seek a seventh term in 1996 and retired to Newport, where he died of Parkinson's disease on New Year's Day 2009, aged 90.
