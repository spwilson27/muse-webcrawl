Japanese name

In modern times, Japanese names (Japanese: 日本の名前, Nihon no namae) usually consist of a family name (surname) followed by a given name, unlike in Western naming conventions where the given name comes first. Japanese names are typically written in kanji (Chinese characters), though some are written in hiragana or katakana. The pronunciation of a name is not always evident from its kanji characters, since many characters have multiple possible readings (on'yomi and kun'yomi).

Structure

Japanese law requires that a surname precede the given name, an order sometimes called "Eastern order." When Japanese names are written in the Latin alphabet, they were traditionally rendered in Eastern order as well, though Western order became common in the Meiji era; official Japanese government style since 2020 again recommends surname-first in Roman letters.

Family names

There are an estimated 100,000 or more distinct surnames in Japan — far more variety than in neighboring Korea or China. Common surnames include Satō (佐藤), Suzuki (鈴木), Takahashi (高橋), Tanaka (田中), Watanabe (渡辺), and Yamamoto (山本). Surnames were not used by commoners until the Meiji era, when in 1875 the government required all citizens to register a family name. Historically, married women traditionally adopted their husband's surname, a practice still required by Japanese civil law, which has been subject to ongoing legal and social debate.

Given names

Given names are chosen for the sound, meaning, and the appearance of their kanji. Popular elements include words for beauty, flowers, seasons, virtues, and aspirations. The characters usable in names are regulated: parents may choose from the jōyō kanji and the jinmeiyō kanji list maintained by the Ministry of Justice. Some given names are gendered strongly, while others are neutral.

Historical names

During the feudal period, aristocrats and samurai used clan names (uji) and court titles, and individuals might change names at milestones in life. Members of the imperial family do not have surnames. Japanese Christians and other historical figures are also known by baptismal names or art names.
