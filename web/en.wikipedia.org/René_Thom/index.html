René Thom
Born: 2 September 1923, Montbéliard, France
Died: 25 October 2002, Bures-sur-Yvette
Known for: Catastrophe theory; cobordism
Awards: Fields Medal (1958)

René Frédéric Thom (2 September 1923 – 25 October 2002) was a French mathematician. He made his reputation as a topologist, receiving the Fields Medal in 1958 for his work on cobordism theory, and later moved into applied mathematics, developing catastrophe theory.

Mathematics

Thom introduced fundamental concepts in differential topology, including the Thom space, the Thom isomorphism, characteristic classes, and transversality; the Thom conjecture on embedded surfaces was later proved using Seiberg–Witten theory.

Catastrophe theory

In his 1972 book Structural Stability and Morphogenesis, Thom classified the elementary catastrophes — the ways smoothly varying systems can undergo sudden discontinuous change — and promoted their application to biology and the social sciences. The theory's applications were popularised by Christopher Zeeman, though they also drew criticism for overreach.
