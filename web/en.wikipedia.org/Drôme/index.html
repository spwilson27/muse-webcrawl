Drôme (French pronunciation: [dʁoːm]) is a department in southeastern France, in the region of Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes. It takes its name from the Drôme River, a tributary of the Rhône. Its prefecture is Valence; its subprefectures are Die and Nyons.

The department was created on 4 March 1790 during the French Revolution, from part of the former province of Dauphiné. The landscape ranges from the Rhône valley in the west to the foothills of the Alps in the east, including the Vercors massif.

Economy

Drôme is known for agriculture, especially fruit production — it is one of France's leading producers of apricots, olives, and lavender — and for wine, including the Côtes du Rhône and the sparkling Clairette de Die. Tourism is important, with visitors drawn to the Vercors Regional Natural Park, the villages of the Drôme provençale, and the Palais idéal du Facteur Cheval at Hauterives.

History

The area was part of the Roman province of Gallia Narbonensis; Valence was the Roman colony of Valentia. In the Middle Ages it formed part of the Dauphiné, which passed to the French crown in 1349. During the Second World War, the Vercors plateau was a major centre of the French Resistance, and the maquis there suffered severe German reprisals in 1944.
