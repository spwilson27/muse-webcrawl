4D printing

4D printing refers to the process of printing, using conventional 3D printing techniques, an object whose shape or properties change over time — the "fourth dimension" — when exposed to a stimulus such as water, heat, light, or a magnetic field. The concept was introduced in a 2013 TED talk by Skylar Tibbits of the MIT Self-Assembly Lab.

Concept

4D printed objects are built from smart materials — including shape-memory polymers and hydrogels that swell at different rates — deposited in precise geometric arrangements. Because the materials respond differently to a stimulus, the printed structure folds, curls, expands, or assembles itself into a predetermined configuration without motors, wires, or manual intervention. Designers use computational models to predict how the printed geometry will evolve, effectively "programming" matter.

Applications

Research applications include self-assembling furniture and components, pipelines whose diameter adapts to flow, medical stents and implants that deploy inside the body, footwear and textiles that respond to the environment, and aerospace or infrastructure parts that repair or reconfigure themselves. The field remains largely at the research stage, with challenges including the limited range of printable smart materials, slow response times, and the difficulty of scaling and modelling complex transformations.
