Ellsworth Mountains

The Ellsworth Mountains are the highest mountain ranges in Antarctica, located on the western edge of the Ronne Ice Shelf in Marie Byrd Land. They stretch about 350 km (217 mi) long and 48 km (30 mi) wide in a north-to-south configuration. The range is made up of the northern Sentinel Range and the southern Heritage Range, separated by the Minnesota Glacier.

Highest peak:Vinson Massif
Elevation:4,892 m (16,050 ft)
Length:350 km (217 mi)
Location:Marie Byrd Land, Antarctica

Discovery and exploration

The mountains were discovered on 23 November 1935 by the American explorer Lincoln Ellsworth during a trans-Antarctic flight, and are named for him. They were mapped in detail by the United States Geological Survey from ground surveys and aerial photography during the 1960s.

The Sentinel Range includes Vinson Massif (4,892 m), the highest point on the Antarctic continent and one of the Seven Summits sought by mountaineers. Private expeditions are typically staged through the Union Glacier blue-ice runway.

Geology and climate

The ranges consist largely of folded sedimentary and low-grade metamorphic rocks of Paleozoic age. Temperatures in the mountains can fall below −50 °C, with an annual mean around −30 °C in the higher Sentinel Range, and katabatic winds are frequent.
