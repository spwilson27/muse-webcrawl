Université Laval

Type: Public research university
Established: 1852 (Séminaire de Québec, 1663)
Location: Quebec City, Quebec, Canada
Students: ~47,000

Université Laval is a public research university in Quebec City, Quebec, Canada. The university was founded by François de Montmorency-Laval, the first bishop of New France, as the Séminaire de Québec in 1663, making it the oldest institution of higher education in Canada and the first North American university to offer higher education in French. It received its royal charter from Queen Victoria in 1852.

Campus and academics

The main campus, Cité-Universitaire, covers about 1.2 square kilometres in the Sainte-Foy district and hosts most of the university's 17 faculties, including medicine, law, forestry, agriculture and a major business school (FSA ULaval). The Rouge et Or varsity teams are among the most successful in Canadian university sport, particularly in football.

Research

Laval is a member of the U15 Group of Canadian Research Universities and is noted for work in optics and photonics, Arctic science (through the Institut nordique du Québec), cardiology and infectious diseases.
