Zapruder film

The Zapruder film is a silent color motion picture sequence shot by Abraham Zapruder with an 8 mm home-movie camera in Dealey Plaza, Dallas, Texas, on 22 November 1963. Running about 26.6 seconds and comprising 486 frames, it captured the assassination of United States President John F. Kennedy and is regarded as the most complete visual record of the event, playing a central role in official investigations and in public discussion ever since.

Filming

Zapruder, a Dallas dress manufacturer, stood on a concrete abutment on the north side of Elm Street and filmed the presidential motorcade as it passed through Dealey Plaza. His film shows the limousine turning onto Elm Street, the President reacting to a shot, Governor John Connally also wounded, and the fatal head shot at frame 313, after which Jacqueline Kennedy climbed onto the trunk of the car. Because Zapruder's camera ran at about 18.3 frames per second, investigators could use the film frame numbers as a precise clock for timing the shots.

Aftermath and custody

Copies were made for the FBI and Secret Service the same day. Zapruder sold print rights and later the film itself to Time Inc., publisher of Life magazine, which published selected frames but did not allow the film's public screening for years; bootleg copies circulated from the late 1960s. The film was shown on American national television for the first time in 1975. The Zapruder family regained the copyright in 1978. In 1998 the United States government took title to the original film under the JFK Assassination Records Review Board process, with an arbitration panel awarding the family $16 million in 1999. The original is now held by the National Archives, while the Sixth Floor Museum at Dealey Plaza holds the copyright, donated by the family in 2000.

Investigations

The film was key evidence for the Warren Commission (1964) and the House Select Committee on Assassinations (1979), and its frames have been subjected to decades of analysis by journalists, scientists, and conspiracy theorists debating the number, timing, and origin of the shots.
