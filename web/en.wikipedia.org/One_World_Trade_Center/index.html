One World Trade Center, also known as One WTC and initially as the Freedom Tower, is the main building of the rebuilt World Trade Center complex in Lower Manhattan, New York City. Designed by David Childs of Skidmore, Owings & Merrill, it is the tallest building in the United States and the Western Hemisphere, and the seventh-tallest in the world.

The supertall structure has the same name as the North Tower of the original World Trade Center, which was destroyed in the terrorist attacks of September 11, 2001. The new skyscraper stands on the northwest corner of the 16-acre (6.5 ha) World Trade Center site.

History

Construction began on 27 April 2006. The building was topped out on 10 May 2013, when the final section of its spire was installed, giving it a symbolic height of 1,776 feet — a reference to the year of American independence. One World Trade Center opened on 3 November 2014, with the anchor tenant Condé Nast moving in. The One World Observatory, on floors 100–102, opened to the public on 29 May 2015.

Design and features

The tower's design is an elongated tapered octagon, its glass facade sheathed in prismatic glass that changes appearance with the light. Safety features exceed New York City building code requirements, including a reinforced concrete core, extra-wide pressurised stairwells, and dedicated firefighter lifts. The base is a 185-foot (56 m) square, nearly the same footprint as the original towers, and the building includes a 20-ton anchor of bedrock-adjacent foundation. It has approximately 2.6 million square feet of office space.
