Burr-Hamilton duel

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia (recreated from memory)

The Burr-Hamilton duel was a pistol duel fought on July 11, 1804, between sitting U.S. Vice President Aaron Burr and former Secretary of the Treasury Alexander Hamilton, at Weehawken, New Jersey. Hamilton was mortally wounded and died the following day; Burr was charged with murder in New York and New Jersey but was never tried, and the affair effectively ended his political career.

Background

Burr and Hamilton had been political rivals for years. Hamilton campaigned against Burr in the deadlocked presidential election of 1800, which was decided in the House of Representatives in favor of Thomas Jefferson, and again opposed Burr's candidacy for Governor of New York in 1804. After a newspaper published a letter reporting that Hamilton had expressed a "despicable opinion" of Burr at a dinner, Burr demanded an apology. Hamilton's evasive responses, defending the honor of his statements while refusing to recant, led Burr to challenge him to a duel.

The duel

The parties rowed across the Hudson River from New York to Weehawken in the early morning, the same site where Hamilton's son Philip had been killed in a duel three years earlier. Hamilton's second was Nathaniel Pendleton; Burr's was William Van Ness. Accounts differ on the sequence of shots: Hamilton fired first and missed (whether deliberately, "throwing away his shot," remains disputed), and Burr's shot struck Hamilton in the abdomen, causing internal injuries from which he died roughly 30 hours later.

Aftermath

Public reaction was overwhelmingly hostile to Burr, who fled south to avoid prosecution. Though he completed his term as vice president — presiding over the Senate impeachment trial of Samuel Chase while under indictment — his political career never recovered. The duel became one of the most famous episodes in early American political history and contributed to the decline of dueling in the northern United States.
