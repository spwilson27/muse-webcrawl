William Hanna

William Denby Hanna (July 14, 1910 – March 22, 2001) was an American animator and cartoon producer. With Joseph Barbera he created the Tom and Jerry cartoons at MGM and in 1957 co-founded Hanna-Barbera, the studio behind The Flintstones, Yogi Bear, Scooby-Doo, and The Jetsons.

Career

Hanna joined MGM's cartoon studio in 1937 and was paired with Barbera; their first Tom and Jerry short, Puss Gets the Boot (1940), launched a series that won seven Academy Awards. When MGM closed its animation unit in 1957, the pair founded their own company and pioneered limited animation for television, producing the first prime-time animated sitcom, The Flintstones (1960).

Legacy

Hanna-Barbera's characters became staples of American television for decades, and Hanna shared numerous Emmy Awards with Barbera.
