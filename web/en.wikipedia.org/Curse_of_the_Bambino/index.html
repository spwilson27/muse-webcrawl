Curse of the Bambino

The Curse of the Bambino was a famous sports-related superstition associated with the Boston Red Sox of Major League Baseball. According to the legend, the Red Sox were cursed after trading Babe Ruth — nicknamed "the Bambino" — to their rivals, the New York Yankees, following the 1919 season. The Red Sox, who had won five of the first fifteen World Series, including the 1918 title with Ruth, did not win another championship for 86 years, until 2004.

Origins

After the 1919 season, cash-strapped Red Sox owner Harry Frazee sold Ruth to the Yankees for a then-record sum (about $100,000, plus a loan), a deal widely reported at the time as financing Frazee's theatrical ventures, including the Broadway musical No, No, Nanette. Ruth went on to lead the Yankees to seven pennants and four World Series titles, transforming them into baseball's dominant franchise, while Boston endured decades of near-misses: losing World Series in 1946, 1967, 1975, and — most painfully, in a seventh game after being one strike from victory — 1986, as well as Bucky Dent's pennant-winning home run in a 1978 one-game playoff.

The term and the "reversal"

The phrase "Curse of the Bambino" was popularized by Boston Globe columnist Dan Shaughnessy's 1990 book of that name. The curse was said to be broken in 2004, when the Red Sox made an unprecedented comeback from a 3–0 deficit against the Yankees in the American League Championship Series and then swept the St. Louis Cardinals in the World Series — their first title in 86 years. Ritual "reversals" had long been attempted by fans, including placing a Red Sox cap atop Mount Everest and retrieving a piano said to be Ruth's from a pond near his former Massachusetts home.
