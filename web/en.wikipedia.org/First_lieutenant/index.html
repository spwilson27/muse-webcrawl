First lieutenant or 1st lieutenant is a commissioned officer military rank in many armed forces. In the United States Army, Air Force, Marine Corps, and Space Force, a first lieutenant is a junior commissioned officer (pay grade O-2) ranking above second lieutenant and below captain. The rank is typically held by officers with roughly two or more years of commissioned service.

United States

U.S. first lieutenants wear a single silver-colored bar as their insignia, earning them the informal description of "silver bar" officers, in contrast to the gold bar of a second lieutenant. Army and Marine first lieutenants commonly serve as platoon leaders with several years' experience, executive officers of companies, or staff officers at battalion level; Air Force and Space Force first lieutenants serve in operational and support roles with increased responsibility. Promotion from second lieutenant is normally automatic after about 18–24 months of successful service.

Other countries

Many other militaries use equivalent ranks translated as first lieutenant, such as the German Oberleutnant and the French lieutenant (in some systems the senior lieutenant grade). In the British Army the equivalent senior OF-1 rank is simply lieutenant, and the Royal Navy historically used "first lieutenant" as an appointment — the executive officer of a ship — rather than a substantive rank, a usage also found in the U.S. Navy, where "first lieutenant" denotes the officer in charge of deck operations aboard a vessel or at a shore command.

History

The term derives from the French lieu tenant, "place-holder," for an officer deputizing for a superior. As armies formalized rank structures in the 18th and 19th centuries, the lieutenant grade divided into first (senior) and second (junior) lieutenants, a distinction preserved in the continental and American systems.
