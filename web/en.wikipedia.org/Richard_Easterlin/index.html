Richard Ainley Easterlin (January 12, 1926 – December 16, 2024) was an American economist and demographer, professor at the University of Southern California, best known for the Easterlin paradox and the Easterlin hypothesis.

Contributions

The Easterlin paradox, set out in his 1974 paper "Does Economic Growth Improve the Human Lot?", observed that while richer people within a country tend to report greater happiness than poorer people, average reported happiness across countries and over time does not rise proportionally with income — a finding that launched the modern field of happiness economics. His relative income hypothesis proposed that people's material aspirations are shaped by their upbringing and reference groups, and he applied similar cohort-based reasoning to fertility and demographic cycles in works such as Birth and Fortune.
