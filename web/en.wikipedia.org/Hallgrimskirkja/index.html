Hallgrimskirkja

Hallgrimskirkja is a Lutheran parish church in Reykjavik, Iceland, and one of the tallest structures in the country.

Hallgrimskirkja (Icelandic pronunciation: [ˈhatl̥krimsˌcʰɪrca], "church of Hallgrímur") is a Lutheran (Church of Iceland) parish church in Reykjavik, Iceland. At 73 metres (239 ft) tall, it is the largest church in Iceland and among the tallest structures in the country. The church is named after the Icelandic poet and clergyman Hallgrímur Pétursson (1614–1674), author of the Hymns of the Passion. It is one of Reykjavik's best-known landmarks and is visible throughout the city.

Architecture

State architect Guðjón Samúelsson designed the church in 1937. He is said to have drawn inspiration from the trap rocks, mountains, and glaciers of Iceland's landscape: the sweeping concrete wings that rise on either side of the tower evoke the columnar basalt formations found around the country, such as those at Svartifoss waterfall. The interior is comparatively plain and Gothic in character, with high pointed-arch windows. In front of the church stands a statue of the explorer Leif Erikson, a gift from the United States in 1930 to commemorate the 1000th anniversary of the Icelandic parliament, the Althing — the statue predates the church itself.

Construction

Construction began in 1945 and took 41 years; the church was consecrated on 26 October 1986, the day before the 312th anniversary of Hallgrímur Pétursson's death. The crypt beneath the choir was consecrated in 1948, and the steeple and wings were completed in 1974. The building has attracted criticism for its long construction time and cost, but it is now widely regarded as an important symbol of Icelandic national identity.

Features

The church houses a large pipe organ built by the German organ builder Johannes Klais of Bonn, completed in 1992; it has 5,275 pipes, stands 15 metres tall, and weighs 25 tons. An observation platform in the tower, reached by lift, offers panoramic views over Reykjavik and the surrounding mountains. The church also functions as an observation tower and is used for concerts and cultural events in addition to regular worship.
