Controlled release

Controlled release is a term referring to a mechanism or technology used to regulate the rate at which an active substance is delivered to a target. In pharmaceutics, controlled-release dosage forms release a drug at a predetermined rate in order to maintain a constant drug concentration for a specific period of time with minimum side effects. Related terms include sustained release, extended release, and modified release.

Pharmaceutical formulations

Controlled-release medicines are commonly formulated as tablets or capsules containing the active ingredient embedded in an inert matrix, coated with a semi-permeable membrane, or delivered by an osmotic pump system. By slowing absorption, these formulations reduce the frequency of dosing, smooth peaks and troughs in blood concentration, and can improve patient adherence.

Other uses

Beyond medicine, controlled release is used in agriculture, where coated fertilisers and pesticides release their contents gradually in response to moisture or temperature, and in consumer products such as fragrances and cleaning agents using microencapsulation.
