M. V. Lomonosov Moscow State University (MSU) is a public research university in Moscow, Russia, and the oldest and largest university in the country. Founded in 1755 by the polymath Mikhail Lomonosov and statesman Ivan Shuvalov under a decree of Empress Elizabeth, it is widely regarded as Russia's most prestigious institution of higher education.

History

The university opened on 25 January 1755, a date still celebrated as Students' Day in Russia. Originally located near Red Square, it expanded in the Soviet era; its monumental main building on Sparrow Hills, completed in 1953, is one of the "Seven Sisters" Stalinist skyscrapers and, at 240 metres, remains among the tallest university buildings in the world.

Organisation and academics

MSU comprises more than 40 faculties and numerous research institutes, including major centres for physics, mathematics, chemistry and computational science. It operates supercomputing facilities, museums, a botanical garden and a scientific library of some ten million volumes. The university has produced many notable alumni, including Nobel laureates, Fields Medalists and heads of state and government.

Campus

The Sparrow Hills campus covers about 205 hectares overlooking the Moskva River. Beyond the main tower it includes the Shuvalov and Lomonosov academic buildings, the Fundamental Library, and extensive dormitories.
