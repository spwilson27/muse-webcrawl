Luoyang

The Longmen Grottoes south of Luoyang

Province: Henan, China

Population: ~7 million (2020 census)

Known for: Ancient capital; Longmen Grottoes; peonies

Luoyang (Chinese: 洛阳; pinyin: Luòyáng) is a prefecture-level city in western Henan province, China, situated on the central plain along the Luo River, a tributary of the Yellow River. One of the oldest continuously significant cities in China, it served as the capital of thirteen dynasties and states over more than 1,500 years, including the Eastern Zhou, Eastern Han, Cao Wei, Western Jin, Northern Wei, and Later Tang.

History

The area around Luoyang was settled in Neolithic times and became a ritual centre under the Zhou dynasty. In 25 AD Emperor Guangwu made Luoyang the capital of the restored Eastern Han dynasty, a status it kept for nearly two centuries; during this era Cai Lun's improvements to papermaking and the first Buddhist temple in China, the White Horse Temple (68 AD), marked the city. Luoyang was repeatedly devastated in warfare, notably by Dong Zhuo in 190 AD and during the War of the Eight Princes, yet was rebuilt on a grand scale under the Northern Wei after 493 AD.

Landmarks and culture

The Longmen Grottoes, a UNESCO World Heritage Site 12 km south of the city, contain tens of thousands of Buddhist statues carved into limestone cliffs between the Northern Wei and Tang dynasties. Other attractions include the White Horse Temple, the Luoyang Museum, and Guanlin Temple. Luoyang is famed as the "city of peonies"; its annual Peony Culture Festival each April draws large crowds. Since the 1950s the city has also been an industrial centre, home to tractor and machinery manufacture.
