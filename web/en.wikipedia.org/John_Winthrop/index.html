John Winthrop

John Winthrop (January 12, 1588 – March 26, 1649) was an English Puritan lawyer and one of the leading figures in founding the Massachusetts Bay Colony, the second major settlement in New England following Plymouth Colony. Winthrop led the first large wave of colonists from England in 1630 and served as governor for 12 of the colony's first 20 years. His writings and vision of the colony as a Puritan "city upon a hill" dominated New England colonial development, influencing the governments and religions of neighboring colonies.

Life in England

Winthrop was born in Edwardstone, Suffolk, into a wealthy landowning family. He studied at Trinity College, Cambridge, briefly practiced law, and served as a justice of the peace. As a convinced Puritan troubled by religious persecution under Charles I and economic hardship, he joined the Massachusetts Bay Company and was elected governor in 1629.

Massachusetts Bay Colony

Winthrop sailed on the Arbella in 1630, delivering en route the lay sermon "A Model of Christian Charity," famous for the phrase "city upon a hill." He founded Boston and governed the colony through controversies including the banishment of Roger Williams and Anne Hutchinson during the Antinomian Controversy. His journal is a major primary source for early New England history.

Legacy

Winthrop's son, John Winthrop the Younger, became governor of Connecticut. The elder Winthrop's vision shaped the self-understanding of New England and, through later quotation by American presidents, of the United States itself.
