Seismology

Seismology (from Greek seismós, "earthquake", and -logía, "study of") is the scientific study of earthquakes and the propagation of elastic waves through the Earth or other planetary bodies. A scientist who works in the field is a seismologist. Seismology also encompasses the study of earthquake environmental effects such as tsunamis, and of seismic sources caused by volcanic processes, tectonic movements, glaciers, ocean microseisms, and artificial events such as explosions.

Seismic waves

Earthquakes radiate energy as seismic waves. Body waves travel through the Earth's interior: compressional P-waves (primary) are the fastest, while shear S-waves (secondary) cannot pass through liquids — a fact that revealed the liquid nature of the Earth's outer core. Surface waves (Love and Rayleigh waves) travel along the surface and usually cause the most damage. By timing arrivals at seismograph stations, seismologists locate an earthquake's focus (hypocentre) and the point on the surface above it, the epicentre.

Instruments and scales

Waves are recorded by seismometers (seismographs); the earliest known device was Zhang Heng's seismoscope of 132 AD in China. Modern networks use broadband digital instruments. Earthquake size is measured by magnitude scales — historically the Richter scale, now usually the moment magnitude scale — while the intensity of shaking at a location is described by scales such as the modified Mercalli scale.

Structure of the Earth

Seismic waves are refracted and reflected by layers of differing density, allowing seismologists to image the Earth's interior much as ultrasound images a body. Key discoveries made this way include the crust–mantle boundary (the Moho, found by Andrija Mohorovičić in 1909), the liquid outer core (Richard Oldham, Beno Gutenberg) and the solid inner core (Inge Lehmann, 1936). Seismic tomography now produces three-dimensional models of the mantle used to understand convection and plate tectonics.

Applications

Beyond earthquake science and hazard assessment, seismology is used in hydrocarbon and mineral exploration (reflection seismology), in monitoring compliance with nuclear test-ban treaties, and in engineering design of earthquake-resistant structures. Related planetary seismology has placed seismometers on the Moon and Mars.
