Joseph of Arimathea

Joseph of Arimathea is a biblical figure who assumed responsibility for the burial of Jesus after his crucifixion. According to all four canonical Gospels, he was a wealthy disciple of Jesus, in Matthew's account, and a member of the council (the Sanhedrin) in Mark and Luke, who obtained permission from Pontius Pilate to take Jesus's body and laid it in his own new rock-cut tomb.

Later tradition

In medieval legend Joseph became a central figure. The apocryphal Gospel of Nicodemus expanded his story, telling of his imprisonment and miraculous release. Later romances made him the keeper of the Holy Grail, in which he was said to have collected Christ's blood at the cross, and credited him with bringing Christianity to Britain, founding the first church at Glastonbury and planting his staff there, where it blossomed as the Glastonbury Thorn.

Veneration

Joseph of Arimathea is venerated as a saint in the Roman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, and other churches; his feast is kept on 31 August in the Roman calendar and on 31 July in the Orthodox calendar. His hometown of Arimathea is usually identified with modern Ramla or with a site in Judea.
