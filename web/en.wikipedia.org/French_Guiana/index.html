French Guiana (French: Guyane, [ɡɥijan]) is an overseas department and region of France located on the northern coast of South America in the Guianas. It borders Brazil to the east and south and Suriname to the west. With an area of about 83,534 square kilometres, it is the second-largest region of France and the largest outermost region within the European Union. Its capital and largest city is Cayenne.

French Guiana is the only territory in mainland South America that is fully part of a European country, and as such it uses the euro and is governed under French law. It is best known internationally as the home of the Guiana Space Centre at Kourou, from which the European Space Agency launches many of its rockets.

History

The region was originally inhabited by various Indigenous peoples, including the Kalina, Arawak, Palikur and Wayampi. French colonisation began in the 17th century; Cayenne was founded in 1643. The territory became notorious in the 19th and 20th centuries for its penal colonies, most famously on Devil's Island (Île du Diable), where the wrongfully convicted Captain Alfred Dreyfus was held, and which operated until 1953. In 1946 French Guiana became an overseas department of France, ending its status as a colony.

Geography

French Guiana consists of a narrow coastal plain, where most of the population lives, and a vast interior of dense tropical rainforest forming part of the Amazonian forest and the Guiana Shield, one of the oldest geological formations on Earth. The climate is equatorial, hot and humid year-round. Roughly 90 percent of the territory is covered by forest, giving it exceptional biodiversity. Major rivers include the Maroni (forming the border with Suriname) and the Oyapock (forming the border with Brazil).

Politics

As an overseas department and region, French Guiana is an integral part of the French Republic. It elects two deputies to the National Assembly and two senators to the Senate. Local affairs are handled by the Territorial Collectivity of French Guiana (Collectivité territoriale de Guyane), created in 2016 by merging the former departmental and regional councils. In a 2010 referendum, voters rejected greater autonomy.

Economy

The economy depends heavily on the Guiana Space Centre at Kourou, which accounts for a large share of GDP and employment, and on subsidies from mainland France. Fishing (especially shrimp), gold mining, timber and rum are other significant activities. Unemployment and poverty rates are high compared with metropolitan France.

Demographics

The population of around 300,000 is ethnically diverse, including Creoles, metropolitan French, Indigenous peoples, Maroon communities (descendants of escaped enslaved Africans), and immigrant groups from Brazil, Suriname, Haiti, Laos (Hmong) and elsewhere. French is the official language, while French Guianese Creole is widely spoken.
