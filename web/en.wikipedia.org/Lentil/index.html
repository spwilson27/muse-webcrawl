Lentil

The lentil (Lens culinaris) is an edible legume grown for its lens-shaped seeds. It is an annual plant of the family Fabaceae, about 40 cm (16 in) tall, whose seeds grow in pods, usually with two seeds in each. Lentils are among the oldest domesticated crops, with remains found at Neolithic sites in the Near East dating back some 9,000–13,000 years.

Types and production

Lentils are classified by seed size and colour: large-seeded types include green and brown lentils, while small-seeded types include red (split) lentils, French green (Puy) lentils and black "beluga" lentils. Canada is the world's largest producer and exporter, followed by India, Australia and Turkey. In India, lentils (dal) are a dietary staple, cooked into soups and curries; in the Middle East and Mediterranean they appear in dishes such as mujadara and lentil soup. Unlike most legumes, lentils cook quickly without pre-soaking.

Nutrition

Lentils are rich in protein (about 25% by dry weight), dietary fibre, folate, iron and other B vitamins, and are low in fat. As a nitrogen-fixing legume, lentil crops also improve soil fertility in rotations with cereals.
