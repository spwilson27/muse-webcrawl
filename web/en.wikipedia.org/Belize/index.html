Belize (Belizean Creole: Bileez) is a country on the north-eastern coast of Central America, bordered by Mexico to the north, Guatemala to the west and south, and the Caribbean Sea to the east. It has an area of 22,966 km² (8,867 sq mi) and a population of about 397,000 (2022). Its capital is Belmopan and its largest city is the port of Belize City. Belize is the only Central American country whose official language is English, a legacy of its history as the British colony of British Honduras; it is a member of the Commonwealth realm and of the Caribbean Community (CARICOM).

History

The Maya civilisation flourished in the region for more than a millennium before European contact, leaving cities such as Caracol, Xunantunich, Lamanai, and Altun Ha. English and Scottish buccaneers and logwood cutters — the Baymen — settled the coast from the mid-17th century, cutting logwood and later mahogany with enslaved African labour. A British victory over a Spanish fleet at the Battle of St. George's Caye (1798) secured the settlement, which became the colony of British Honduras in 1862.

Guatemala has long claimed Belizean territory, a dispute that delayed independence; Belize became self-governing in 1964, took the name Belize in 1973, and achieved independence on 21 September 1981, with a British garrison remaining for defence. The territorial dispute with Guatemala was referred to the International Court of Justice following referendums in both countries in 2018–2019.

Geography

The northern half of Belize is a flat, swampy coastal plain; the south is dominated by the low Maya Mountains, whose highest point is Doyle's Delight (1,124 m). Offshore lies the Belize Barrier Reef, the largest barrier reef in the Northern Hemisphere and a UNESCO World Heritage Site, with some 450 cayes and the Great Blue Hole. About 60% of the land is forested, and Belize has an extensive protected-areas system, including the world's first jaguar preserve at Cockscomb Basin.

Demographics and culture

Belize is ethnically diverse: the largest groups are Mestizo (about 53%), Creole (about 26%), Maya (about 11%), and Garifuna (about 6%), alongside Mennonite, East Indian, Chinese, and other communities. English is official, Spanish is widely spoken, and Belizean Creole serves as a lingua franca. The culture blends Caribbean and Central American influences, expressed in music such as punta rock, and in Garifuna Settlement Day and Carnival celebrations.

Economy

Tourism is the largest earner, centred on the reef, cayes, and Maya ruins, followed by agriculture — sugar, citrus, and bananas historically dominated exports. Commercial oil was discovered in 2005 at Spanish Lookout. The Belize dollar is pegged at BZ$2 = US$1. Belize faces challenges from violent crime linked to drug trafficking and from vulnerability to hurricanes, which prompted the relocation of the capital from Belize City to Belmopan after Hurricane Hattie in 1961.
