Homotherium

HomotheriumFamily: Felidae (machairodont cats)
Lived: ~4 million – 12,000 years ago
Range: Eurasia, Africa, the Americas
Common name: scimitar-toothed cat

Homotherium is an extinct genus of machairodontine felids — the so-called scimitar-toothed cats — that ranged across North and South America, Europe, Asia and Africa during the Pliocene and Pleistocene, from about 4 million years ago until roughly 12,000 years ago.

Description

About the size of a large lion, Homotherium differed from the better-known Smilodon in having shorter, flattened, serrated upper canine teeth shaped like scimitars, and a sloping back reminiscent of a hyena, with front limbs longer than the hind limbs. Its build suggests an endurance runner adapted to open grasslands, pursuing prey such as young mammoths and other large herbivores, possibly in groups.

Fossil record

Notable remains come from Friesenhahn Cave in Texas, where bones of juvenile mammoths alongside Homotherium skeletons (including cubs) suggest the cats denned there and preyed on young proboscideans. The species Homotherium serum inhabited North America; Eurasian forms are often assigned to H. latidens. A humerus dredged from the North Sea showed the genus survived in northwestern Europe until about 28,000 years ago.

Extinction

Homotherium vanished near the end of the Pleistocene, part of the Quaternary extinction event that removed most large mammals, plausibly through a combination of climate change, prey decline and human pressure.
