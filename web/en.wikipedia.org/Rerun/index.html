Rerun
A rerun is a repeat broadcast of a television or radio programme after its initial airing.

A rerun or repeat is a re-airing of an episode of a radio or television program. The two types of reruns are those that occur during a hiatus and those that occur when a program is syndicated.

History

In the early days of television, most programming was broadcast live and could not easily be repeated. The development of kinescope recording and later videotape made it possible to preserve programs and air them again. The sitcom I Love Lucy is often credited with popularizing the rerun: because star Lucille Ball was pregnant during the second season, previously aired episodes were rebroadcast, and their strong ratings demonstrated that audiences would watch episodes they had already seen.

Variations

When a program is retired or cancelled, its episodes may be packaged for broadcast syndication, in which a network or syndicator sells the rights to air the series to other stations. Reruns shown in syndication are sometimes edited for time to fit more commercials, or to remove content considered unsuitable for certain time slots.

During the summer months, traditionally a period of lower viewership, networks have historically filled schedules with reruns of the season's episodes. This practice has declined somewhat with the rise of streaming media, summer original programming, and reality television.

In other media

The term is also applied to films shown again in theaters, and, metaphorically, to any repeated experience. In computing, to rerun a program simply means to execute it again.
