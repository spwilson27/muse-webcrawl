Netherlands Armed Forces

The Netherlands Armed Forces (Dutch: Nederlandse krijgsmacht) are the military forces of the Kingdom of the Netherlands. They consist of four service branches: the Royal Netherlands Navy (Koninklijke Marine), the Royal Netherlands Army (Koninklijke Landmacht), the Royal Netherlands Air Force (Koninklijke Luchtmacht), and the Royal Netherlands Marechaussee, a gendarmerie force performing military police and border duties. The armed forces fall under the Ministry of Defence, and the Commander of the Armed Forces is the highest-ranking military officer. The King is commander-in-chief in a largely ceremonial sense.

The Netherlands is a founding member of NATO (1949) and its forces are organised primarily for collective defence and international peacekeeping and crisis-management operations. Total active personnel number roughly 40,000–50,000 military personnel plus civilian staff. Conscription exists in law but call-up has been suspended since 1997, making the forces an all-volunteer professional military; in recent years all roles, including submarine service, have been open to women.

History

The Dutch military has roots in the army of the Dutch Republic and the naval tradition of the Dutch Golden Age, when the Netherlands was a leading maritime power. In the 20th century the armed forces fought in the Second World War — in Europe and in the Dutch East Indies — and the Korean War. During the Cold War the Netherlands fielded a large conscript army integrated into NATO's Northern Army Group. After 1991 the forces were restructured for expeditionary missions, contributing to operations in the Balkans, Iraq, Afghanistan (notably Task Force Uruzgan, 2006–2010) and Mali, as well as counter-piracy patrols off Somalia.

Modern equipment

The Navy operates frigates, patrol vessels, submarines of the Walrus class and the amphibious transport docks Rotterdam and Johan de Witt, together with the joint support ship Karel Doorman. The Air Force flies the F-35 Lightning II, having retired the F-16, alongside Apache and Chinook helicopters. The Army fields Leopard 2 tanks in a joint German-Dutch battalion, CV90 infantry fighting vehicles and PzH 2000 self-propelled howitzers. Dutch and German land forces are unusually deeply integrated, with Dutch brigades embedded in German divisions.
