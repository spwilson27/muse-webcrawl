The Ottawa River (French: Rivière des Outaouais; Algonquin: Kitchissippi, "Great River") is a major river in eastern Canada that forms, for much of its length, the boundary between the provinces of Ontario and Quebec. It flows 1,271 kilometres from its source in the Laurentian Plateau to join the St. Lawrence River at Montreal.

Course

From Lake Capimitchigama the river flows west and south through a series of lakes and rapids, past Mattawa and the city of Ottawa — Canada's capital, which sits on its south bank opposite Gatineau — before turning southeast to Montreal, where it meets the St. Lawrence near the Lake of Two Mountains.

History

For centuries the river was a principal route of the fur trade and later of the timber trade, with drives of squared logs floated down to Quebec City. It was named for the Odawa (Ottawa) First Nation, who controlled trade along it in the seventeenth century.

Hydroelectricity

Numerous dams, including those at Carillon and Chaudière Falls, generate hydroelectric power and regulate flow. The river is also used for recreation along its urban stretches.
