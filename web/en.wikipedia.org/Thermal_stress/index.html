Thermal stress

Thermal stress is a mechanical stress arising from changes in a material's temperature when its thermal expansion or contraction is constrained. If a material is free to expand, heating produces no stress; if constrained, the induced stress is proportional to the coefficient of thermal expansion, Young's modulus and the temperature change.

Examples

Thermal stresses are managed in engineering through expansion joints in bridges and pipelines, gaps in railway tracks, and material selection in components exposed to thermal cycling. Unmanaged thermal stress can cause cracking, as in thermal shock of glass, or buckling of rails in hot weather.
