Ruth (biblical figure)

Ruth gleaning in the fields of Boaz.

Ruth is the central figure of the Book of Ruth in the Hebrew Bible and the Old Testament. A Moabite woman, she married into an Israelite family from Bethlehem that had migrated to Moab during a famine. After the deaths of her husband, father-in-law and brother-in-law, Ruth refused to leave her mother-in-law Naomi, declaring: "Where you go I will go, and where you stay I will stay. Your people will be my people and your God my God."

Story

Returning with Naomi to Bethlehem, Ruth supported them by gleaning in the fields of Boaz, a wealthy relative of Naomi's late husband. Boaz treated her kindly and, acting as a kinsman-redeemer, married her. Their son Obed became the grandfather of King David, placing Ruth — a foreigner — in the genealogy of David and, in the New Testament Gospel of Matthew, in the genealogy of Jesus.

Themes

The narrative is celebrated for its portrayal of loyalty and loving-kindness (hesed), and Ruth is honoured in Judaism as a righteous convert; the Book of Ruth is traditionally read at the festival of Shavuot.
