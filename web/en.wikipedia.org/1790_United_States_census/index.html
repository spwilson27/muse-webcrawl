The 1790 United States census was the first census undertaken in the United States, conducted under the direction of Secretary of State Thomas Jefferson. Census day was August 2, 1790, and enumerators (assistant marshals) counted the population over a period of about nine months. The total population recorded was 3,929,214.

Data collected

The census recorded the name of the head of each household and counted free white males aged 16 and over, free white males under 16, free white females, all other free persons, and slaves. The results were used to apportion seats in the House of Representatives, expanding the House from 65 to 105 members.

Findings

Virginia was the most populous state, followed by Pennsylvania and North Carolina. Roughly 700,000 of those counted — about 18 percent of the population — were enslaved people. Several states' original schedules have been lost, including those for Delaware, Georgia, Kentucky, New Jersey, Tennessee, and Virginia, though Virginia's data was reconstructed from tax lists.
