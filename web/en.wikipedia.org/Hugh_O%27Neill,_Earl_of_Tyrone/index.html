Hugh O'Neill, 2nd Earl of Tyrone (Irish: Aodh Mór Ó Néill; c. 1550 – 20 July 1616) was an Irish Gaelic lord and key figure of late Tudor Ireland. Created Earl of Tyrone by the English crown yet inaugurated as The O'Neill in the Gaelic manner, he led the Irish confederacy in the Nine Years' War (1594–1603), the largest conflict of Elizabethan Ireland, before the Flight of the Earls in 1607.

Early life

A grandson of Conn O'Neill, 1st Earl of Tyrone, Hugh was raised partly in England and in the Pale, and served with English forces in Ireland before succeeding as head of the O'Neills of Tyrone. Balancing between the Dublin administration and his own power base in Ulster, he modernised his forces, importing arms and training men in pike-and-shot tactics.

Nine Years' War

Allied with Hugh Roe O'Donnell, O'Neill won major victories at Clontibret (1595) and the Yellow Ford (1598), where the English army suffered one of its worst defeats in Ireland. A Spanish expedition landing at Kinsale in 1601 ended in defeat in January 1602, and Lord Mountjoy's scorched-earth campaigns forced O'Neill's submission under the Treaty of Mellifont in 1603.

Flight and death

In September 1607 O'Neill and Rory O'Donnell sailed from Rathmullan into exile — the Flight of the Earls — opening the way to the Plantation of Ulster. O'Neill died in Rome in 1616.
