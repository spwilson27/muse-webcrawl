Overtone

An overtone is any natural frequency of a system above the fundamental frequency. In music, overtones are the partials present in a pitched sound above the fundamental; when the partials are integer multiples of the fundamental they are called harmonics.

Musical acoustics

The particular mixture and strength of overtones gives each instrument its characteristic timbre. A vibrating string or air column naturally resonates at its fundamental and at a series of higher modes; the relative amplitudes of these modes differ between, for example, a flute and an oboe playing the same note.

Harmonic series

The harmonic series (f, 2f, 3f, 4f, …) underlies much of Western harmony: the second harmonic is the octave, the third the perfect fifth above that, and so on. Some instruments, such as bells, produce inharmonic overtones.

Overtone singing

In overtone (throat) singing, practised in Tuva and Mongolia, a singer manipulates resonances of the vocal tract to amplify selected overtones as distinct pitches above a drone.
