Chronology of the universe

The expansion of the universe from the Big Bang to the present day.

The chronology of the universe describes the history and future of the universe according to Big Bang cosmology. The universe began about 13.8 billion years ago in an extremely hot, dense state and has been expanding and cooling ever since.

The very early universe

In the Planck epoch (up to 10−43 seconds after the Big Bang), the four fundamental forces may have been unified. Cosmic inflation, an exponential expansion lasting until roughly 10−32 seconds, smoothed and flattened the universe. As it cooled, quarks formed, then combined into protons and neutrons. In the first few minutes, Big Bang nucleosynthesis fused some of these into light nuclei — mostly helium-4, with traces of deuterium, helium-3 and lithium-7.

Recombination and the cosmic dark ages

About 380,000 years after the Big Bang, the universe cooled enough (~3,000 K) for electrons and nuclei to combine into neutral atoms. Radiation decoupled from matter, producing the cosmic microwave background observed today. The universe then entered the cosmic dark ages, lit only by the fading afterglow, until gravity gradually gathered gas into the first stars and galaxies several hundred million years later — the epoch of reionization, when starlight reionised the intergalactic medium.

Structure formation and the present era

Galaxies assembled hierarchically into clusters and filaments around dark-matter scaffolding. The Solar System formed about 4.6 billion years ago, 9.2 billion years after the Big Bang. Since roughly 5–6 billion years ago, the expansion of the universe has been accelerating under the influence of dark energy.

Future

Under standard models, accelerated expansion will continue indefinitely: stars will exhaust their fuel over trillions of years, leaving a cold, dark universe of stellar remnants — the "heat death" scenario.
