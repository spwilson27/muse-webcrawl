Presentation of Jesus at the Temple

The Presentation of Jesus at the Temple is an episode in the life of Jesus Christ described in chapter 2 of the Gospel of Luke. In accordance with Jewish law, Mary and Joseph brought the infant Jesus to the Temple in Jerusalem forty days after his birth to present him to the Lord and to offer the sacrifice prescribed for Mary's purification, as required by the Torah (Leviticus 12 and Exodus 13).

Biblical account

At the Temple the family was met by Simeon, a righteous man who had been promised he would not die before seeing the Messiah; taking the child in his arms, he uttered the canticle known as the Nunc dimittis, "Lord, now lettest thou thy servant depart in peace", and prophesied that the child was "a light to lighten the Gentiles". The prophetess Anna also recognised the child and gave thanks. Simeon foretold to Mary that a sword would pierce her own soul.

Feast

The event is commemorated as the Feast of the Presentation on 2 February, forty days after Christmas, also known as Candlemas from the blessing of candles and processions of light held that day. The feast is observed in the Roman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox, Anglican and some Lutheran churches; Orthodox tradition emphasises the Meeting of the Lord.
