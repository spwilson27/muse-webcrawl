Carnival is a festive season occurring before the Christian penitential season of Lent, typically in February or early March. It involves public celebrations — parades, masquerades, street parties, costumes, and feasting — that combine elements of circus, festival, and ritual inversion of everyday life. The season culminates on Shrove Tuesday, known in French as Mardi Gras ("Fat Tuesday"), the day before Ash Wednesday.

Origins

Carnival has roots in ancient pagan festivals such as Saturnalia and in the pre-Lenten customs of Catholic Europe, where the faithful used up rich foods such as meat, eggs, and butter before the Lenten fast — one suggested etymology of "carnival" is the Latin carne vale, "farewell to meat". Traditions of masked balls flourished in Venice from the Renaissance.

Celebrations worldwide

The largest carnival is that of Rio de Janeiro, centred on samba school parades in the Sambadrome. Others include the masked Carnival of Venice, Mardi Gras in New Orleans, the Trinidad and Tobago Carnival with its calypso and soca music and elaborate costumes, the Rhineland carnivals of Cologne and Düsseldorf (beginning on 11 November at 11:11), the carnival of Santa Cruz de Tenerife, and winter carnivals such as Quebec's. Through the African diaspora and European colonisation, carnival traditions spread widely across the Americas and the Caribbean.
