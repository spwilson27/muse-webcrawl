Privy Council of Japan

The Privy Council of Japan (Japanese: Sūmitsu-in) was an advisory council to the Emperor of Japan that operated from 1888 to 1947. Modeled in part on the British Privy Council, it was established to deliberate on the draft of the Meiji Constitution, and thereafter advised the Emperor on important matters of state, including constitutional questions, imperial ordinances, and treaties.

History

The council was created in April 1888 with Itō Hirobumi as its first president, resigning as prime minister to chair the secret deliberations on the draft constitution promulgated in 1889. Under the Meiji Constitution, the Emperor consulted the Privy Council on draft amendments, interpretations of the constitution and laws, emergency imperial ordinances, declarations of war and peace, and international treaties.

Influence

Because its members were appointed for life from senior statesmen, officials, and peers, the council became a conservative power centre, sometimes rivalling the elected government and the genrō (elder statesmen). It deliberated on matters such as the ratification of treaties and the Imperial Rescript decisions of 1945; Emperor Hirohito consulted its president during the decision to accept the Potsdam Declaration.

Abolition

The Privy Council was abolished when the postwar Constitution of Japan took effect on 3 May 1947, which removed the Emperor's governing prerogatives and created no successor advisory body of its kind.
