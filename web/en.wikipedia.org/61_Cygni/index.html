61 Cygni

61 Cygni is a binary star system in the constellation Cygnus, consisting of a pair of K-type dwarf stars, 61 Cygni A and B, orbiting each other with a period of about 659 years. Located about 11.4 light-years from Earth, it is notable as the first star (other than the Sun) to have its distance measured by stellar parallax.

History of observation

The system's exceptionally high proper motion — about 5 arcseconds per year — was noted by Giuseppe Piazzi in 1804, earning it the nickname "Piazzi's Flying Star." Friedrich Wilhelm Bessel chose it for parallax measurement because its rapid motion suggested it was nearby; in 1838 he published a parallax corresponding to a distance of roughly 10.4 light-years, one of the landmark measurements of 19th-century astronomy.

Properties

61 Cygni A is slightly larger and brighter than its companion; both are older than the Sun and show significant starspot activity. The system's space velocity and age make it of interest in studies of galactic kinematics.

Claims of companions

Twentieth-century astrometric studies periodically suggested low-mass companions or planets around the components, but these claims have not been confirmed and are generally attributed to instrumental error.
