Ku Klux Klan

The Ku Klux Klan (KKK) is the name of several American white supremacist terrorist and hate groups whose primary targets have been African Americans, Jews, Latinos, Asian Americans, Native Americans, and Catholics, as well as immigrants, leftists, homosexuals, Muslims, and atheists. The Klan has existed in three distinct eras.

First Klan (1865–1871)

The first Klan was founded in Pulaski, Tennessee, in late 1865 or early 1866 by Confederate veterans. It became a violent insurgency against Reconstruction-era governments in the South, using intimidation and murder against freedmen and their allies. Federal enforcement acts in 1870–1871 largely suppressed it.

Second Klan (1915–1944)

The second Klan was refounded in 1915 by William Joseph Simmons, inspired by the film The Birth of a Nation. It adopted the burning cross and mass rallies, grew into a powerful political movement in the 1920s with millions of members, and campaigned against immigration, Catholicism, and alcohol as well as racial minorities before collapsing amid scandals and internal strife.

Third Klan (1946–present)

After the Second World War, fragmented Klan groups opposed the civil rights movement with violence, including bombings and murders. Prosecutions, lawsuits (notably by the Southern Poverty Law Center), and infiltration have kept the modern Klan small and splintered, though it remains classified as a hate group.
