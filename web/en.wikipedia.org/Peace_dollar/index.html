Peace dollar

The Peace dollar is a United States dollar coin minted for circulation from 1921 to 1928 and again from 1934 to 1935, and as a bullion coin in 2021. Designed by Anthony de Francisci, it was issued to commemorate the end of the First World War and remains the last United States circulation dollar coin struck in 90% silver.

Peace dollar
Value: 1 U.S. dollar
Mass: 26.69 g
Composition: 90% silver, 10% copper
Years of minting: 1921–1928, 1934–1935, 2021–present (bullion)
Designer: Anthony de Francisci

Background

The Pittman Act of 1918 authorized the melting of millions of silver dollars and their later recoinage. Numismatists and peace advocates lobbied for a coin commemorating the war's end; Congress was not required to act because the Morgan dollar design had been in use for more than 25 years and could be changed by the Treasury alone.

Design

De Francisci's obverse shows a youthful Liberty wearing a radiant crown, modelled on his wife Teresa. The reverse depicts a bald eagle at rest on a mountain peak, clutching an olive branch, with the word PEACE below. The original design included a broken sword, removed after public objection that it symbolized defeat.

Mintage

The first coins, struck in high relief in late December 1921, are prized by collectors. Production continued until 1928, resumed briefly in 1934–1935, and the design was revived for bullion issues beginning in 2021.
