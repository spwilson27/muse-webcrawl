Edinburgh Castle

Edinburgh Castle is a historic castle in Edinburgh, Scotland. It stands on Castle Rock, a volcanic plug that has been occupied by humans since at least the Iron Age. The castle was a royal residence from the reign of David I in the 12th century until 1633, and it played a pivotal role in Scottish history, serving as both a fortress and a royal palace. It is Scotland's most visited paid tourist attraction.

History

The castle was captured and recaptured repeatedly during the Wars of Scottish Independence; in 1314 Thomas Randolph retook it in a daring night assault. Mary, Queen of Scots gave birth to James VI in the castle in 1566, and it endured the long "Lang Siege" of 1571–73 during the Marian civil war. It was besieged again in the Jacobite risings of 1689 and 1715 and held government prisoners during later wars.

Notable features

St Margaret's Chapel, built around 1130, is the oldest surviving building in Edinburgh. The Great Hall dates from the reign of James IV. The castle houses the Honours of Scotland (the Scottish crown jewels), the Stone of Scone (returned to Scotland in 1996), and the giant 15th-century bombard Mons Meg. The One O'Clock Gun is fired daily except Sundays.

Today

The castle hosts the Royal Edinburgh Military Tattoo each August and contains the National War Museum and the Scottish National War Memorial. It remains an active military station in part, while most of the site is managed as a visitor attraction.
