Visby-class corvette

The Visby class is a series of corvettes in use by the Swedish Navy, built by Kockums. It was the world's first operational class of stealth ships and is designed for low visibility in radar, infrared, magnetic, pressure, and acoustic signatures. The lead ship is named after Visby, the main city on the island of Gotland. Five ships were built.

Design

The hull is constructed of a carbon fibre reinforced plastic sandwich with a PVC core, giving low weight, high strength, and a non-magnetic signature. The faceted surfaces deflect radar energy away from its source, and the ship's combined diesel and gas turbine powerplant drives water jets instead of propellers, reducing underwater noise. The class displaces about 640 tonnes, is 72.7 m long, and can exceed 35 knots. The main gun is a Bofors 57 mm Mk 3 fitted with a stealth cupola; the ships were designed for the RBS-15 anti-ship missile, anti-submarine mortars, torpedoes, and mines, though delivery of some weapon fits was delayed by programme changes.

Ships

The class comprises HSwMS Visby (K31), Helsingborg (K32), Härnösand (K33), Nyköping (K34), and Karlstad (K35). The first ship was launched in 2000, but the class entered full operational service only in 2015 after prolonged outfitting and trials, largely because of the ambitious stealth technology and systems integration.

Mid-life upgrade

In 2021 Saab received a contract for a mid-life upgrade of the class, including installation of surface-to-air missile capability (Sea Ceptor), upgraded sensors, and extended service life into the 2040s.
