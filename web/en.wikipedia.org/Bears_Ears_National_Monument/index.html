Bears Ears National Monument

Bears Ears National Monument is a United States national monument in San Juan County in southeastern Utah, protecting a landscape of mesas, canyons, and twin buttes—the "Bears Ears"—sacred to several Native American tribes. It was established by President Barack Obama on 28 December 2016, reduced sharply by President Donald Trump in 2017, and restored and slightly expanded by President Joe Biden in 2021, after which it covered about 1.36 million acres (550,000 ha).

Landscape and heritage

The monument takes its name from two prominent buttes that rise above Cedar Mesa, a place of deep significance to the Hopi, Navajo, Ute Mountain Ute, Ute Indian Tribe, and Pueblo of Zuni, whose representatives formed the Bears Ears Inter-Tribal Coalition to advocate for protection. The region contains an extraordinary concentration of archaeological sites—cliff dwellings, rock imagery, granaries, and ceremonial sites of Ancestral Puebloan and other cultures—as well as paleontological resources and habitat on the Colorado Plateau.

Political controversy

Designation under the Antiquities Act was opposed by Utah's congressional delegation and many local officials, who favored state control and resource development, and supported by tribes and conservationists. Trump's 2017 proclamation cut the monument by about 85 percent into two units (Indian Creek and Shash Jáa) and opened removed lands to mining and drilling claims; Biden's October 2021 proclamation restored the original boundaries plus additional acreage and reinstated an intergovernmental cooperative agreement giving tribes a formal role in management by the Bureau of Land Management and the U.S. Forest Service.
