ResearcherFrederick Griffith
Year1928
OrganismStreptococcus pneumoniae
DiscoveryBacterial transformation

Griffith's experiment, reported in 1928 by Frederick Griffith, was the first experiment suggesting that bacteria are capable of transferring genetic information through a process known as transformation.

The experiment

Griffith worked with two strains of pneumococcus: a virulent smooth (S) strain and a harmless rough (R) strain. Heat-killed S bacteria alone did not harm mice, and live R bacteria alone did not either — but a mixture of heat-killed S and live R killed the mice, and living S bacteria were recovered from them. The dead S material had "transformed" the R bacteria into the virulent form.

Significance

Griffith concluded that some "transforming principle" carried hereditary information. In 1944 Avery, MacLeod, and McCarty identified that principle as DNA, paving the way for modern genetics.
