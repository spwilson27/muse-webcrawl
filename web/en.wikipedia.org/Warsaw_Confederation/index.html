Warsaw Confederation

The Warsaw Confederation, signed on 28 January 1573 by the Convocation Sejm in Warsaw, was a landmark act guaranteeing religious freedom in the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth. It committed the nobility to maintain peace among differing faiths (dissidentes de religione) and to ensure equal rights for religious dissenters.

Context

Formed during the interregnum after Sigismund II Augustus's death (1572) — the last Jagiellonian — a confederation of nobles sought to prevent civil strife and secure the first free election. Its terms were incorporated into the Henrician Articles sworn by every elected king.

Legacy

While much of Europe endured wars of religion, the Confederation made the Commonwealth a refuge — "a state without stakes," in Janusz Tazbir's phrase — for Catholics, Lutherans, Calvinists, Orthodox, Jews, Muslims and others, though tolerance narrowed during the Counter-Reformation and after the Deluge.
