Beat juggling

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Beat juggling is a turntablism technique in which a DJ manipulates two copies of the same record (or two records) on turntables to create new rhythmic patterns, extending breaks and recombining drum hits and short musical phrases in real time.

Technique

Using a crossfader and quick hand movements, the DJ alternates between the two turntables, repeating kick drums, snares and fills to build a live rhythm that differs from the recorded original. The technique grew out of the breakbeat DJing of Kool Herc and Grandmaster Flash and was developed further by DJs such as Grandmixer DXT, Cash Money and the Invisibl Skratch Piklz.

In competition

Beat juggling is a central element of DJ battles such as the DMC World DJ Championships, where routines are judged on technical difficulty, musicality and originality. Body tricks and increasingly complex patterns became hallmarks of competitive routines in the 1990s.
