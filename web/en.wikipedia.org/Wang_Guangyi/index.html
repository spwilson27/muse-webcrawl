Wang Guangyi

Wang Guangyi (Chinese: 王广义; born 1957 in Harbin, Heilongjiang) is a Chinese artist best known as one of the leading figures of the Political Pop movement that emerged in China in the late 1980s and early 1990s. His work juxtaposes the visual language of Cultural Revolution propaganda with the logos and imagery of Western consumer brands, creating ironic commentaries on ideology, commerce and globalisation.

Early life and education

Wang was born in Harbin in 1957, the son of a railway worker. During the Cultural Revolution he worked on the railways and in the countryside before China’s universities reopened. He was admitted to the Zhejiang Academy of Fine Arts (now the China Academy of Art) in Hangzhou, graduating in 1984.

Career

In the mid-1980s Wang was associated with the Northern Art Group, a collective of young artists in Harbin who sought to introduce rationalist and philosophical themes into Chinese art as part of the ’85 New Wave movement. His early paintings, such as the Frozen North Pole series, used muted palettes and stark compositions.

Wang achieved international prominence with his Great Criticism series (begun around 1990), which combined iconic images of workers, peasants and soldiers from Cultural Revolution posters with brand logos such as Coca-Cola, Marlboro, Chanel and Swatch. The series became one of the most recognisable bodies of work in contemporary Chinese art and a hallmark of Political Pop, alongside artists such as Li Shan and Yu Youhan.

Style and themes

Wang’s paintings typically employ flat areas of colour, bold outlines and stencilled text, mimicking both propaganda posters and advertising. By setting revolutionary slogans against consumer trademarks, he has said he aimed to expose how both systems mobilise images to shape belief and desire. Later series, including Materialist and Cold War Aesthetics, extended these concerns to religion, ideology and geopolitics.

Exhibitions and recognition

Wang’s work was included in the landmark 1993 exhibition China’s New Art, Post-1989 in Hong Kong and in the Venice Biennale. His paintings are held by major museums and command high prices at auction, reflecting the international market for contemporary Chinese art that developed in the 2000s.
