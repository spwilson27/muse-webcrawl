Compagnie Internationale des Wagons-Lits

The Compagnie Internationale des Wagons-Lits (CIWL), founded by the Belgian engineer Georges Nagelmackers in 1872, was a pioneering operator of sleeping cars and luxury train services across Europe. Inspired by Pullman cars in the United States, Nagelmackers built a network of through sleeping-car services linking European capitals.

The Orient Express

The company's most famous service was the Orient Express, launched in 1883 running between Paris and Constantinople (Istanbul), which became synonymous with luxury rail travel. CIWL also operated the Trans-Siberian services' sleeping cars, the Nord Express, the Sud Express and the Blue Train to the French Riviera, together with a chain of grand hotels.

Later history

After the Second World War, national railway companies progressively took over sleeping-car operations, and CIWL diversified into catering, travel services and hospitality. Its successor businesses became part of the Accor and Newrest groups; heritage CIWL carriages are preserved and operate in nostalgic luxury trains such as the Venice Simplon-Orient-Express.
