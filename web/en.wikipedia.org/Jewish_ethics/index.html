Jewish ethics

Jewish ethics is the Jewish theological and moral tradition, drawing on the Hebrew Bible, rabbinic literature (including the Talmud and Midrash), medieval philosophy and the responsa tradition. It addresses the conduct expected of human beings in relation to God, to other people, to animals and to society as a whole.


Ethical study in the Jewish tradition

Foundations

Central principles include tzedakah (righteousness, expressed in charitable giving), chesed (loving-kindness), tikkun olam ("repairing the world"), and pikuach nefesh, the principle that saving a human life overrides almost every other religious obligation. The commandment to love one's neighbour, and Hillel's formulation of the Golden Rule — "What is hateful to you, do not do to your fellow" — are foundational.

Musar literature

The medieval and early modern Musar ("ethics/discipline") literature, including works such as Bahya ibn Paquda's Duties of the Heart and Moshe Chaim Luzzatto's Mesillat Yesharim (Path of the Just), systematised the cultivation of character traits such as humility, patience and generosity. In the 19th century the Musar movement of Rabbi Israel Salanter turned these texts into a programme of ethical self-examination.

Contemporary issues

Modern Jewish ethics is applied to questions in medical ethics, business ethics, warfare, environmental responsibility and social justice, with different denominations weighing traditional sources and contemporary reasoning differently.
