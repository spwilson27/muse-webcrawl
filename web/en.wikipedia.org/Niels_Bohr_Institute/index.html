The Niels Bohr Institute (Danish: Niels Bohr Institutet) is a research institute of the University of Copenhagen. The research of the institute spans astronomy, geophysics, nanotechnology, particle physics, quantum mechanics and biophysics. It was founded in 1921 by the Danish theoretical physicist Niels Bohr, who had been on the staff of the University of Copenhagen since 1914 and had been lobbying for its creation since his appointment as professor in 1916.

History

During the 1920s and 1930s the institute was at the centre of the developing disciplines of atomic and quantum physics. Physicists from across Europe and beyond — including Werner Heisenberg, Wolfgang Pauli and George de Hevesy — visited and collaborated with Bohr, making Copenhagen a birthplace of quantum mechanics and of the "Copenhagen interpretation". Four researchers associated with the institute have won Nobel Prizes: Niels Bohr himself, his son Aage Bohr, Ben Mottelson, and Georges Charpak's collaborator network aside, the institute's staff have been central to Danish physics ever since.

Today the institute occupies several buildings, principally on Blegdamsvej in Copenhagen near Fælledparken, and incorporates research centres in cosmology, astrophysics and ice-core science (whose researchers drill cores in Greenland). It also runs the Rockefeller complex with related university institutes.
