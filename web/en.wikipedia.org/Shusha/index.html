Shusha (Azerbaijani: Şuşa) is a city in Azerbaijan, in the region of Nagorno-Karabakh. Founded in 1752 as the capital of the Karabakh Khanate, it was one of the cultural capitals of the Caucasus, known for its role in Azerbaijani music and poetry and for its Armenian heritage, and has been a focal point of the Nagorno-Karabakh conflict.

History

The fortress-city was built by Panah Ali Khan, founder of the Karabakh Khanate, on a strategic plateau defended by cliffs. In the 19th century Shusha was a major center of trade, silk production, and carpet weaving, and produced celebrated composers and performers of mugham, including Uzeyir Hajibeyli, who was born nearby. The city's Armenian and Azerbaijani populations clashed violently in 1905 and 1920, when much of the Armenian half was destroyed.

Modern conflict

During the First Nagorno-Karabakh War, Armenian forces captured Shusha in May 1992 and its Azerbaijani population fled. Azerbaijan recaptured the city in November 2020 during the Second Nagorno-Karabakh War, a turning point that preceded the ceasefire; Azerbaijan took full control of the region in 2023, after which the ethnic Armenian population departed. Azerbaijan has declared Shusha its cultural capital and hosts the Khari Bulbul music festival there.

Landmarks

Landmarks include the Shusha fortress walls, the Yukhari Govhar Agha Mosque, the Ghazanchetsots Cathedral, the Jidir Plain, and the mausoleum of the poet Vagif.
