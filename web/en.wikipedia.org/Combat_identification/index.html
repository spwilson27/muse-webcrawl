Combat identification

Combat identification is the process of attaining an accurate characterization of detected objects in the operational environment sufficient to support an engagement decision. Its central purpose is to distinguish friend, foe, and neutral in order to prevent fratricide — friendly fire incidents.

Methods

Techniques include identification friend or foe (IFF) transponders on aircraft and vehicles, blue force tracking systems using GPS, infrared strobes and glint tape visible through night-vision devices, thermal identification panels, markings and invasion stripes, and procedural measures such as recognition signals, phase lines, and rules of engagement.

Friendly fire problem

Studies of 20th- and 21st-century conflicts estimate friendly fire causes a significant share of combat casualties. Notable incidents drove investment in cooperative identification systems after the 1991 Gulf War and the 1994 Black Hawk shootdown.

Challenges

Non-cooperative targets, coalition interoperability, electronic warfare, and the speed of modern engagements complicate identification; doctrine therefore layers technical and procedural safeguards.
