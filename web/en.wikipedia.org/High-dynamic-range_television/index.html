High-dynamic-range television

High-dynamic-range television (HDR-TV) is a technology that improves the quality of television signals by increasing contrast ratio, peak brightness, black levels and colour range beyond those of standard-dynamic-range television. It is considered one of the major advances in picture quality since high-definition television.

Formats

Several HDR formats compete in the market. HDR10 is an open standard using static metadata and the PQ transfer function; it is supported by the Ultra HD Blu-ray format. Dolby Vision is a proprietary format supporting dynamic metadata and up to 12-bit colour. Hybrid Log-Gamma (HLG), developed by the BBC and NHK, is royalty-free and designed for broadcast compatibility with SDR displays. HDR10+, promoted by Samsung and others, adds dynamic metadata to an open framework.

Standards and adoption

HDR-TV is standardised in ITU-R Recommendation BT.2100, which defines image parameters for HDR television including the PQ and HLG transfer functions and the Rec. 2020 colour space. Streaming services, broadcasters and game consoles widely adopted HDR during the late 2010s, and most mid-range and premium televisions now support at least one HDR format.
