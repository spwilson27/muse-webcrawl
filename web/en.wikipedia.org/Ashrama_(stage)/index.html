Ashrama (stage)

An ashrama (Sanskrit: आश्रम) is a stage of life in the ashrama system of Hinduism, which divides life into four stages based on age and corresponding duties. Together with the concept of purushartha, the four aims of life, the ashrama system forms a central part of Hindu social and spiritual thought.

The four ashramas

The four stages are: Brahmacharya, the celibate student stage devoted to study under a guru; Grihastha, the householder stage of marriage, family, and work; Vanaprastha, gradual retirement and withdrawal from worldly affairs; and Sannyasa, complete renunciation devoted to liberation (moksha).

Origins and texts

The system is described in the Dharmashastra literature, including the Manusmriti and the Dharmasutras, which variously treat the stages as sequential phases of a single life or as alternative vocations. Earlier texts such as the Jabala Upanishad permitted renunciation directly from any stage.

Modern practice

In contemporary Hinduism the ashrama scheme functions largely as an ideal or framework rather than a universally followed programme, though the student and householder stages remain widely observed and the renunciate stage continues in monastic orders.
