Grace Brewster Murray Hopper (née Murray; 9 December 1906 – 1 January 1992) was an American computer scientist, mathematician, and United States Navy rear admiral. A pioneer of computer programming, she was one of the first programmers of the Harvard Mark I computer and developed the first compiler, the A-0 System, in 1952. She popularised the idea of machine-independent programming languages, leading to the development of COBOL, one of the first high-level programming languages.

Early life and education

Hopper was born in New York City and educated at Vassar College, graduating in mathematics and physics in 1928. She earned a master's degree (1930) and a PhD in mathematics (1934) from Yale University, then taught at Vassar until 1943, when she joined the US Naval Reserve during the Second World War.

Career

Assigned to the Bureau of Ships Computation Project at Harvard, Hopper worked on Howard Aiken's Mark I and authored its 561-page manual. After the war she joined the Eckert–Mauchly Computer Corporation, working on UNIVAC I, and created the A-0 compiler—initially dismissed by colleagues who believed computers could only do arithmetic. Her FLOW-MATIC language for business data processing directly influenced COBOL, whose design committee she advised. She also championed standardisation and validation of programming languages across the Navy.

Hopper is credited with popularising the term "debugging"; a famous 1947 incident at Harvard involved a moth found taped in the Mark II logbook, though the term predated her. She retired from the Navy in 1966, was repeatedly recalled to active duty, and finally retired in 1986 at age 79—the oldest serving officer in the US Navy—with the rank of rear admiral.

Legacy

Known affectionately as "Amazing Grace," Hopper received the National Medal of Technology in 1991 and, posthumously, the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2016. The annual Grace Hopper Celebration of Women in Computing, founded in 1994, is the world's largest gathering of women technologists. The US Navy destroyer USS Hopper is named for her.
