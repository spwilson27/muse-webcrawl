Geothermobarometry

Geothermobarometry
FieldMetamorphic petrology
MeasuresTemperature (geothermometry) and pressure (geobarometry) of rock equilibration
Common pairsGarnet–biotite, garnet–clinopyroxene, two-pyroxene

Geothermobarometry is the science of measuring the pressure and temperature history of a metamorphic or igneous rock by analysing the chemical compositions of its coexisting minerals. A geothermometer exploits a temperature-dependent exchange reaction, while a geobarometer uses a pressure-sensitive net-transfer reaction, usually one with a large change in molar volume.

Principles

Many mineral equilibria shift systematically with temperature or pressure. The iron–magnesium exchange between garnet and biotite, for example, depends strongly on temperature and only weakly on pressure, making it a classic thermometer. Reactions such as the equilibrium between garnet, plagioclase, aluminium silicate, and quartz (the GASP barometer) involve substantial volume changes and so track pressure. Compositions are measured with an electron microprobe and combined with thermodynamic calibrations derived from experiments.

Applications

By determining the conditions at which a rock equilibrated, geologists reconstruct pressure–temperature–time paths: how deeply a rock was buried during mountain building and how it returned to the surface. Geothermobarometry is central to understanding subduction zones, the growth of continental crust, and the exhumation of ultra-high-pressure terranes containing coesite and diamond.

Limitations

Results assume the minerals once equilibrated and were not reset by later diffusion or alteration; slow cooling can allow compositions to re-adjust, blurring the record of peak conditions.
