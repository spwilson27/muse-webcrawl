Uranus (mythology)

Uranus (Ancient Greek: Οὐρανός, Ouranós, meaning "sky" or "heaven") is a primordial Greek god personifying the sky and one of the Greek primordial deities. In Hesiod's Theogony, Uranus is the son and husband of Gaia, the personification of Earth. He was the father of the Titans, the Cyclopes, and the Hecatoncheires, and grandfather of the Olympian gods.

Mythology

According to Hesiod, Uranus imprisoned his monstrous children, the Hecatoncheires and Cyclopes, within Gaia's womb, causing her great pain. Gaia fashioned a great flint sickle and asked her children to punish their father. Only Cronus, the youngest Titan, was willing. When Uranus came to lie with Gaia, Cronus ambushed him and castrated him with the sickle. From the blood that fell on the earth sprang the Furies, the Giants, and the Meliae; the severed genitals, cast into the sea, produced foam from which Aphrodite arose.

Cronus then freed his siblings and took Uranus's throne, but Uranus and Gaia prophesied that Cronus would in turn be overthrown by his own son — a prophecy fulfilled by Zeus.

Planet

The planet Uranus, discovered in 1781, is the only planet named after a Greek rather than a Roman deity, continuing the sequence of Earth giving rise to Sky, Sky to Saturn (Cronus), and Saturn to Jupiter (Zeus).
