The German Football Association (German: Deutscher Fußball-Bund, DFB) is the governing body of football in Germany and one of the largest national sports federations in the world. It oversees the national teams, the DFB-Pokal cup competition, and, together with the DFL, the professional league system headed by the Bundesliga.

History

The DFB was founded in Leipzig in 1900 by representatives of 86 clubs. It joined FIFA as a founding member in 1904. Dissolved and refounded in the post-war years, it governed West German football until reunification in 1990, when the East German association merged into it. Germany's men's team has won the FIFA World Cup four times (1954, 1974, 1990, 2014) and the women's team twice (2003, 2007), successes administered under the DFB.

Structure

The association is organised through five regional and 21 state associations that run amateur and youth football. With more than seven million registered members, the DFB is the largest sports federation in Germany. It hosted the FIFA World Cup in 1974 and 2006 and UEFA Euro 2024.

Controversies

The DFB has faced scrutiny over the awarding of the 2006 World Cup — the so-called "Sommermärchen" affair involving allegations of slush funds — and senior officials resigned in 2015 amid the investigation.
