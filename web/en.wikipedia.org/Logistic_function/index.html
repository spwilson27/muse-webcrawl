Logistic function

A logistic function or logistic curve is a common S-shaped curve (sigmoid curve) described by the equation f(x) = L / (1 + e−k(x−x₀)), where L is the curve's maximum value (carrying capacity), k controls the steepness, and x₀ is the midpoint. The function was introduced by Pierre François Verhulst in the 1830s–1840s as a model of population growth.

History

Verhulst coined the name "logistic" (from the French logistique) in 1844–1845 papers on population growth. The curve was rediscovered in 1920 by Raymond Pearl and Lowell Reed, who promoted it as a law of population growth for the United States.

Applications

The logistic function models growth that is initially exponential but slows as it approaches a limit: population ecology with a carrying capacity, the spread of epidemics and innovations, and tumour growth. In statistics, its inverse (the logit) underlies logistic regression. In machine learning, the sigmoid serves as an activation function in neural networks, and the related Fermi–Dirac distribution arises in statistical mechanics.

Logistic differential equation

The curve is the solution of the differential equation dP/dt = rP(1 − P/K), whose growth rate declines linearly as P approaches the carrying capacity K. With time-varying or harvested populations, the dynamics can become more complex; the related logistic map is a classic example of chaos.
