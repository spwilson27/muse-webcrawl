Geodetic datum

A geodetic datum or geodetic system is a system of values used to measure locations on Earth, consisting of a reference ellipsoid, an origin and orientation for coordinate axes, and a set of fundamental stations whose coordinates have been determined. Datums are crucial for cartography, geodesy, surveying, and navigation, because the same physical point receives different latitude and longitude values in different datums.

Horizontal and vertical datums

A horizontal datum provides a frame for measuring positions across the Earth's surface, historically realized by fitting an ellipsoid to a region and tying it to survey control points. A vertical datum provides a reference surface for elevations, typically based on mean sea level at one or more tide gauges; heights in such datums are orthometric, measured along the direction of gravity relative to the geoid.

Examples

The World Geodetic System 1984 (WGS 84), maintained by the United States, is the datum used by GPS and is periodically refined. North America historically used NAD27 (based on the Clarke 1866 ellipsoid, anchored at Meades Ranch, Kansas) and now NAD83. Europe uses ETRS89; Great Britain's national mapping uses OSGB36. Modern datums are geocentric and increasingly time-dependent, accounting for plate tectonic motion of several centimetres per year.

Datum transformation

Converting coordinates between datums requires a datum transformation, commonly a seven-parameter Helmert transformation (three translations, three rotations, and a scale factor). Neglecting the datum can produce positional errors of hundreds of metres — a classic cause of errors when older maps are combined with GPS data.
