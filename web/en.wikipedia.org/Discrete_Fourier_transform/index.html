Discrete Fourier transform

Discrete Fourier transform
FieldSignal processing, mathematics
InputFinite sequence of complex numbers
Fast algorithmFast Fourier transform (FFT), O(N log N)

In mathematics, the discrete Fourier transform (DFT) converts a finite sequence of equally spaced samples of a function into a same-length sequence of equally spaced samples of the discrete-time Fourier transform, which is complex-valued. Equivalently, it decomposes a sequence into components of different frequencies. The DFT is one of the most important tools in digital signal processing.

Xk = Σn=0N−1 xn · e−i2πkn/N,   k = 0, …, N−1

Properties

The DFT is invertible (via the inverse DFT), linear, and periodic. It evaluates the Z-transform at N equally spaced points on the unit circle, and its convolution property underlies fast convolution algorithms. Because a direct computation costs O(N²) operations, practical use relies on fast Fourier transform algorithms such as the Cooley–Tukey algorithm, which reduce the cost to O(N log N) and were popularized in 1965.

Applications

Applications include spectral analysis of signals, audio and image compression (as a relative of the discrete cosine transform used in JPEG and MP3), solving partial differential equations, fast multiplication of large integers and polynomials, and data compression and filtering throughout science and engineering.
