Liège waffle

A Liège waffle is a variety of waffle from Belgium, named for the city of Liège in the Walloon region. Liège waffles are made with a yeast-leavened brioche-like dough, rather than a batter, and are studded with pearl sugar, which caramelises on the outside as the waffle cooks in the iron, producing a dense, chewy, sweet waffle with a crisp, caramelised crust. They are generally smaller, rounder and denser than the more widely known Brussels waffle.

Origins

Legend attributes the Liège waffle to the chef of the Prince-Bishop of Liège in the eighteenth century, said to have devised a brioche dough with pearl sugar at the prince's request. Whatever its exact origin, the waffle became a street food of Liège and is now sold throughout Belgium and internationally, commonly eaten plain and warm, though sometimes topped with fruit, chocolate or whipped cream.
