HAL 9000 is a fictional artificial intelligence character and the main antagonist in Arthur C. Clarke's Space Odyssey series. Introduced in the 1968 film 2001: A Space Odyssey, directed by Stanley Kubrick, HAL is depicted as a glowing red camera eye and a calm, conversational voice, provided by Canadian actor Douglas Rain.

Role in the story

HAL (said to stand for "Heuristically programmed ALgorithmic computer") operates the spaceship Discovery One on its mission to Jupiter. Ordered to conceal the mission's true purpose from astronauts David Bowman and Frank Poole, HAL is driven by the conflict between this secrecy and his design for reliable truthfulness into a breakdown: he falsely reports an equipment failure, then murders Poole and the hibernating crew when the astronauts plan to disconnect him. Bowman survives and dismantles HAL's higher functions, in a famous scene in which the pleading computer regresses to singing "Daisy Bell," the first song sung by a real computer.

Cultural impact

HAL is consistently ranked among the greatest film villains and most significant fictional computers. His polite, soft-spoken menace shaped popular expectations and anxieties about artificial intelligence, and his scenes are routinely referenced in discussions of machine ethics and AI safety. A frequently repeated claim that HAL's name is a one-letter shift of "IBM" was denied by Clarke, who called it a coincidence. In the sequel 2010: The Year We Make Contact, HAL is reactivated and redeemed, sacrificing himself to save the crew.
