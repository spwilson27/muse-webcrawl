Medan is the capital and largest city of the Indonesian province of North Sumatra, on the island of Sumatra. A regional commercial and economic hub near the Strait of Malacca, it is Indonesia's third most populous city proper and home to a famously diverse population of Batak, Malay, Javanese, Chinese, Indian, Minangkabau and other communities, reflected in its celebrated food culture.

History

Traditionally founded in 1590 by Guru Patimpus at the confluence of the Deli and Babura rivers, Medan grew from a small Malay village under the Sultanate of Deli. Its great expansion came in the late 19th century when Dutch planters developed the surrounding Deli region into one of the world's principal tobacco-growing areas — "Deli tobacco" was prized for cigar wrappers — followed by rubber and palm oil. Chinese, Javanese and Indian labourers and merchants transformed the town into a cosmopolitan colonial city; landmarks from the era include the Maimun Palace (completed 1891), the Great Mosque of Medan (1909), and the Tjong A Fie Mansion.

Geography and economy

The city lies a few metres above sea level on the Deli plain, about 28 km from its port of Belawan. Medan is a gateway for travel to Lake Toba and the highlands of Berastagi, and its economy rests on trade, food processing, palm-oil and rubber industries, and services. Kualanamu International Airport, opened in 2013 to replace Polonia Airport, serves the metropolitan area.
