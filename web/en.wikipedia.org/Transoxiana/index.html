Transoxiana

Transoxiana lay between the Amu Darya (Oxus) and Syr Darya (Jaxartes) rivers in Central Asia.

Transoxiana or Transoxania (Latin for "land beyond the Oxus") is the historical name for the region of Central Asia lying between the Amu Darya (the classical Oxus) and Syr Darya (Jaxartes) rivers, roughly corresponding to modern-day Uzbekistan, Tajikistan, southern Kazakhstan and northern Afghanistan. In Arabic sources it was called Mā warāʼ an-Nahr, "the land beyond the river" (that is, beyond the Oxus, from the perspective of Persia and Arabia).

History

In antiquity the region was inhabited by Iranian peoples, notably the Sogdians centred on Samarkand and the Bactrians to the south. It was conquered by Alexander the Great in the 4th century BC and later formed part of the Greco-Bactrian world, then fell under Kushan, Hephthalite, and Turkic rule. Its oasis cities grew rich on the Silk Road trade between China, Persia, and Europe.

Arab armies under Qutayba ibn Muslim conquered Transoxiana in the early 8th century AD, beginning its gradual Islamisation. Under the Persian Samanid dynasty (819–999), with its capital at Bukhara, the region became a flourishing centre of Islamic scholarship, Persian literature and science — the era of figures such as al-Khwarizmi's successors, the physician-philosopher Avicenna and the astronomer al-Biruni's contemporaries. Thereafter it passed to the Karakhanids, the Khwarazmian Empire, and in 1220 was devastated by the Mongol conquest of Genghis Khan.

In the late 14th century Transoxiana was the heartland of Timur (Tamerlane), who made Samarkand his imperial capital and adorned it with monumental architecture. His Timurid successors presided over a cultural renaissance in Samarkand and Herat. From the 16th century the region divided into Uzbek khanates — Bukhara, Khiva and Kokand — which were absorbed into the Russian Empire in the 19th century.

Geography

Agriculture in Transoxiana has always depended on irrigation from the two great rivers and their tributaries, such as the Zeravshan, which waters Samarkand and Bukhara before dissipating in the Kyzyl Kum desert.
