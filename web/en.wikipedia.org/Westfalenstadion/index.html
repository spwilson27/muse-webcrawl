Westfalenstadion (German pronunciation: [vɛstˈfaːlənˌʃtaːdi̯ɔn], lit. 'Westphalia Stadium') is a football stadium in Dortmund, North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany, which is the home of Borussia Dortmund. Officially called Signal Iduna Park for sponsorship reasons and BVB Stadion Dortmund in UEFA competitions, the name derives from the former Prussian province of Westphalia.

It is Germany's largest stadium and, with a league capacity of 81,365 spectators, one of the largest football stadiums in Europe. Its south stand, the Südtribüne, is the largest free-standing grandstand in Europe with a capacity of 24,454 standing spectators and is famous as the "Yellow Wall" (Gelbe Wand), formed by Dortmund supporters waving the club's black and yellow colours.

History

The stadium was built between 1971 and 1974 for the 1974 FIFA World Cup, replacing the Stadion Rote Erde as Dortmund's home ground. It originally held 54,000 spectators. It was expanded several times over the following decades: corner stands were added in the 1990s, and a third tier was built in preparation for the 2006 FIFA World Cup, during which the stadium hosted six matches including a semi-final.

The stadium's exterior is distinguished by eight bright yellow steel pylons, each 62 metres tall, which support the roof structure and have become a landmark of the Dortmund skyline. Average attendances at Borussia Dortmund home matches have regularly been among the highest in world football, frequently exceeding 80,000 per match.

International tournaments

The Westfalenstadion hosted matches at the 1974 and 2006 FIFA World Cups, UEFA Euro 1988, and UEFA Euro 2024, including a semi-final at Euro 2024. It also staged the second leg of the 1993 UEFA Cup Final and the 2001 UEFA Cup Final between Liverpool and Deportivo Alavés.
