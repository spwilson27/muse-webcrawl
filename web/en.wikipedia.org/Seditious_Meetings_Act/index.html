Seditious Meetings Act

The Seditious Meetings Acts were a series of British laws restricting public meetings and political assembly, enacted in response to fears of radicalism and revolution.

Seditious Meetings Act 1795

Passed by the government of William Pitt the Younger together with the Treasonable Practices Act — together known as the "Two Acts" or "Gagging Acts" — after an attack on the King's carriage in 1795, the act required notice to a magistrate for meetings of more than 50 people, empowered magistrates to disperse meetings, and regulated lecture rooms used for political debate.

Later acts

A further Seditious Meetings Act was passed in 1817 following the Spa Fields riots, and another in 1819 as part of the Six Acts that followed the Peterloo Massacre. The restrictions were later repealed, and the modern law of public assembly in the United Kingdom is governed principally by the Public Order Acts.
