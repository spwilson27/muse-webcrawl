In mathematics, Felix Klein's j-invariant, or j function, is a modular function of weight zero defined on the upper half-plane of complex numbers. It is the unique such function that is holomorphic away from a cusp at infinity and takes the values 1728 at i and 0 at the primitive cube root of unity e2πi/3. Two elliptic curves over the complex numbers are isomorphic if and only if they have the same j-invariant, so the j-invariant completely classifies elliptic curves over ℂ.

Definition

The j-invariant can be defined in terms of the Eisenstein series or the Dedekind eta function. One classical formula uses the modular discriminant Δ and the Eisenstein series E4:

j(τ) = 1728 · E4(τ)³ / Δ(τ)

Its q-expansion at the cusp (with q = e2πiτ) begins

j(τ) = 1/q + 744 + 196884q + 21493760q² + 864299970q³ + …

The coefficients are dimensions-related integers: famously, 196884 = 196883 + 1, where 196883 is the dimension of the smallest faithful representation of the Monster group — the starting point of monstrous moonshine.

Properties

The j-function is invariant under the modular group SL(2, ℤ) acting by Möbius transformations, and it generates the field of modular functions for the full modular group. It gives a bijection between isomorphism classes of elliptic curves over ℂ and complex numbers. The values j(i) = 1728 and j(e2πi/3) = 0 correspond to the curves with extra automorphisms (complex multiplication by the Gaussian and Eisenstein integers respectively).

The j-invariant of an elliptic curve

For an elliptic curve in Weierstrass form y² = x³ + ax + b, the j-invariant is

j = 1728 · (4a³) / (4a³ + 27b²)

defined whenever the discriminant is nonzero. Over general fields, equality of j-invariants classifies curves up to isomorphism over an algebraic closure.

Moonshine and other connections

The appearance of Monster group dimensions in the q-expansion, observed by John McKay and John Thompson, led to the moonshine conjectures, proved by Richard Borcherds using vertex operator algebras; the work contributed to Borcherds' Fields Medal in 1998. The j-function also appears in the theory of singular moduli and the class number problems studied by Heegner, Baker, and Stark.
