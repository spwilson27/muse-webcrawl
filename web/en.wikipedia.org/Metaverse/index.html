Metaverse

The metaverse is a loosely defined term for a network of persistent, shared, 3D virtual spaces linked into a perceived virtual universe, in which people, represented by avatars, can socialise, work, play, shop and create. The word was coined by Neal Stephenson in his 1992 science-fiction novel Snow Crash, and the concept was popularised by later fiction such as Ready Player One and by virtual worlds including Second Life, Roblox, Fortnite and VRChat.

History and hype

Precursors range from text-based MUDs and 1990s VRML experiments to massively multiplayer games. Interest surged in 2021 when Facebook, Inc. rebranded as Meta Platforms and announced heavy investment in virtual and augmented reality; many technology companies, brands and investors promoted metaverse platforms, virtual land and NFT-based assets. By 2023-2024 much of the hype subsided amid low user numbers on some platforms, criticism of the concept's vagueness, and a shift of industry attention toward artificial intelligence, though development of VR/AR hardware (such as Meta Quest and Apple Vision Pro) and game-based worlds continued.

Characteristics proposed

Definitions commonly cite persistence, synchrony, interoperability of identities and goods, a functioning virtual economy, and access through VR headsets as well as ordinary screens. Critics note that no existing platform meets the full vision, that interoperability standards are lacking, and that privacy, safety and governance questions remain unresolved.
