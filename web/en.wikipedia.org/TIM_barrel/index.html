TIM barrel

TIM barrel — a conserved protein fold consisting of eight α-helices and eight parallel β-strands that alternate along the peptide backbone. Named for triose-phosphate isomerase.

The TIM barrel, also known as an alpha/beta barrel, is a conserved protein fold consisting of eight alpha helices and eight parallel beta strands that alternate along the peptide backbone. The structure is named after triose-phosphate isomerase, a conserved metabolic enzyme. TIM barrels are now thought to be one of the oldest protein folds and are one of the most common folds in proteins: approximately 10% of all known enzymes adopt this fold.

Structure

In "TIM barrel" structures, the α and β regions form a pattern that folds into a sort of donut shape, topologically known as a torus. The parallel β-strands form the inner wall of this donut, whereas the α-helices form the outer wall. Each β-strand connects to the next adjacent strand in the barrel through a long right-handed loop that includes one of the helices, so the ribbon N-to-C coloring in the top view proceeds in rainbow order around the barrel.

Function and evolution

Although the barrel core exhibits remarkably low sequence variability, the active site in TIM barrel proteins is almost always at the C-terminal end of the β-strands, where loop regions can evolve new functions without disturbing the stable scaffold. The fold occurs in diverse enzyme families, including aldolases, enolases and isomerases, and is often cited as evidence for divergent evolution from a common ancestral half-barrel protein.
