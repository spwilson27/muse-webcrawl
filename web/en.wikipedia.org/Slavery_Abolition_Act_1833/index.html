Slavery Abolition Act 1833

The Slavery Abolition Act 1833 was an Act of Parliament of the United Kingdom which provided for the gradual abolition of slavery in most parts of the British Empire. It received royal assent on 28 August 1833 and took effect on 1 August 1834.

The Act freed enslaved children under six immediately, while others were redesignated as 'apprentices' bound to their former owners for several more years, a system that ended fully in 1838. Plantation owners were compensated with £20 million, a sum raised by the Treasury, while the formerly enslaved received no compensation.

Slavery was abolished under the Act in the Caribbean, Mauritius, and the Cape Colony, among other territories, though it did not initially apply to territories held by the East India Company, Ceylon, or Saint Helena.
