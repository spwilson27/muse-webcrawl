Wöhler curve

A Wöhler curve, also known as an S–N curve, is a graph used in fatigue analysis that plots the applied cyclic stress (S) against the number of cycles to failure (N) for a material or component. It is named after the German engineer August Wöhler, who conducted systematic fatigue experiments on railway axles between the 1850s and 1870s.

Description

S–N curves are obtained experimentally by subjecting specimens to constant-amplitude cyclic loading at various stress levels and recording the cycles until fracture. The horizontal axis (cycles) is usually plotted on a logarithmic scale. For many steels the curve flattens at low stress, revealing a fatigue limit (endurance limit) below which failure does not occur even for very large numbers of cycles; most non-ferrous alloys, such as aluminium, show no true limit and the curve continues to descend.

Use in design

Engineers use Wöhler curves together with rules such as Miner's rule for cumulative damage to predict the service life of components under cyclic loading, and to set safe stress levels. Because fatigue data scatter widely, design curves are often based on statistical reductions of test results.
