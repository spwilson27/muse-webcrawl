Coup d'état

A coup d'état (French for 'stroke of state'), often shortened to coup, is the illegal and overt seizure of a state by a small group, usually the military or other government elites. Unlike a revolution, which is typically carried out by a large number of people, a coup replaces existing leadership without necessarily changing fundamental social or economic policies.

History

The term comes from French and was popularised after Napoleon Bonaparte's coup of 18 Brumaire in 1799. Coups have occurred throughout history, from ancient Rome to modern Africa, Latin America, Asia and Europe. Cold War coups included those in Iran (1953), Guatemala (1954), Chile (1973) and many others.

Types and outcomes

Political scientists distinguish military coups, self-coups (autogolpe) where an incumbent suspends democracy, and palace coups within ruling families or parties. Successful coups often establish authoritarian regimes, though some lead to democratisation. Failed coups may trigger purges and civil war.
