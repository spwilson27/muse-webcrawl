Annapolis is the capital city of the U.S. state of Maryland. It is the county seat of Anne Arundel County and had a population of 40,812 at the 2020 census. Situated on the Severn River about 25 miles (40 km) south of Baltimore and about 30 miles (48 km) east of Washington, D.C., Annapolis is part of the Baltimore–Washington metropolitan area. The city is home to the United States Naval Academy, founded in 1845, and to St. John's College, founded in 1696 as King William's School.

History

The area was first settled by Puritans from Virginia in 1649, who established a settlement called Providence on the north shore of the Severn. The colonial capital was moved there from St. Mary's City in 1694–95, and the town was renamed Annapolis in honour of Princess Anne, later Queen Anne. It was incorporated as a city in 1708 and became a busy Atlantic port, including a significant role in the slave trade.

Annapolis briefly served as the capital of the United States: the Congress of the Confederation met in the Maryland State House from 26 November 1783 to 19 August 1784. It was there that General George Washington resigned his commission as commander-in-chief of the Continental Army on 23 December 1783, an event widely regarded as a landmark in the establishment of civilian control of the military. The Treaty of Paris, ending the Revolutionary War, was ratified in Annapolis on 14 January 1784.

Landmarks and culture

The Maryland State House, completed in 1779, is topped by the largest wooden dome in the United States built without nails, and it remains the oldest state capitol in continuous legislative use. The historic district preserves many 18th-century buildings, including the Hammond-Harwood House and the William Paca House. Annapolis has a long maritime heritage and is a centre for sailing and boating on the Chesapeake Bay, hosting the annual United States Sailboat Show.

Education

The United States Naval Academy occupies the site of Fort Severn at the mouth of the Severn River and educates officers for the Navy and Marine Corps. St. John's College, the third-oldest college in the United States, is known for its Great Books curriculum.
