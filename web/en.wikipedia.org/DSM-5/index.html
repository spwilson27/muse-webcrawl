DSM-5

The Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition (DSM-5), is the 2013 update to the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, the taxonomic and diagnostic tool published by the American Psychiatric Association (APA). In the United States, the DSM serves as the principal authority for psychiatric diagnoses.

Development and publication

Development of the DSM-5 began in the late 1990s, with work groups reviewing the literature and conducting field trials. It was published on May 18, 2013, superseding the DSM-IV-TR (2000). A text revision, the DSM-5-TR, was published in March 2022.

Changes from DSM-IV

Notable changes included the elimination of the multiaxial assessment system; the folding of Asperger syndrome, childhood disintegrative disorder and PDD-NOS into a single diagnosis of autism spectrum disorder; the renaming of "mental retardation" to intellectual disability; the removal of the bereavement exclusion from major depressive disorder; and the introduction of disruptive mood dysregulation disorder. Roman numerals were dropped in favour of Arabic numerals to facilitate future incremental updates.

Organization

The manual is organised into three sections: an introduction on using the manual, the diagnostic criteria and codes for twenty chapters of disorders, and a section on emerging measures, cultural formulation and conditions for further study.

Criticism

The DSM-5 attracted controversy over the expansion of some diagnostic categories, lowered thresholds for others, the secrecy of parts of the revision process, and the influence of pharmaceutical industry ties among panel members. The National Institute of Mental Health announced in 2013 that it would reorient its research away from DSM categories toward its own Research Domain Criteria (RDoC) framework.
