OpenAI is an American artificial intelligence research organisation founded in December 2015 with the stated mission of ensuring that artificial general intelligence (AGI) benefits all of humanity. It is best known for the GPT family of large language models and for ChatGPT, whose release in November 2022 sparked a global surge of interest and investment in generative AI. Other notable products include the DALL-E image generators, the Whisper speech-recognition system, the Sora video generator, and AI coding tools.

History

OpenAI was founded in San Francisco in 2015 by a group including Sam Altman, Elon Musk, Greg Brockman, Ilya Sutskever, and others, with pledges of about $1 billion in funding; it began as a nonprofit research lab and released early work such as the reinforcement-learning toolkit OpenAI Gym and the game-playing system OpenAI Five. In 2019 it created a "capped-profit" subsidiary to raise large-scale capital, accepting a multi-billion-dollar investment and cloud partnership from Microsoft. The transformer-based GPT series progressed from GPT-2 (2019) through GPT-3 (2020) to GPT-4 (2023) and subsequent multimodal and reasoning models.

ChatGPT and growth

ChatGPT, launched as a free research preview on 30 November 2022, reached an estimated 100 million users within about two months, making it one of the fastest-growing consumer applications ever. OpenAI followed with paid subscriptions, an API business, enterprise offerings, and app and agent features, and its valuation rose into the hundreds of billions of dollars, making it one of the world's most valuable private companies.

Governance controversies and structure

In November 2023, OpenAI's nonprofit board abruptly removed CEO Sam Altman, citing a loss of confidence; after five days of turmoil, employee and investor pressure, and a threat of mass resignation, Altman was reinstated and most of the board replaced. Several senior researchers later departed, some raising concerns about safety priorities. In 2025 the company completed a restructuring converting its for-profit arm into a public benefit corporation, with the nonprofit (renamed the OpenAI Foundation) retaining a major equity stake and control rights. OpenAI also faces ongoing copyright litigation, including a suit by The New York Times, and debate over its shift from open research toward commercial products.
