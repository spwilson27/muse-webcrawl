Land reclamation in Hong Kong has been practised since the nineteenth century to create flat land in a mountainous territory short of developable space. Roughly 68 square kilometres — about six per cent of Hong Kong's land area — has been reclaimed from the sea, housing a large share of the population and economy, particularly around Victoria Harbour.

History

Early schemes in the 1860s–1900s, such as the Praya Reclamation Scheme, extended the Central and Sheung Wan waterfront. Twentieth-century projects created land for new towns including Sha Tin and Tuen Mun, the Kwun Tong industrial area, and the entire Hong Kong International Airport platform at Chek Lap Kok, opened in 1998.

Debate

Reclamation narrowed Victoria Harbour dramatically, prompting public campaigns and the Protection of the Harbour Ordinance (1997), which established a presumption against harbour reclamation. Debate continues between development needs — notably the proposed Lantau Tomorrow Vision artificial islands — and environmental and heritage concerns.
