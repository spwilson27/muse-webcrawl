Saul Bass

Born8 May 1920, New York City, US
Died25 April 1996, Los Angeles, US
OccupationGraphic designer, filmmaker

Saul Bass (1920–1996) was an American graphic designer and Oscar-winning filmmaker, best known for his motion-picture title sequences, film posters, and corporate logos.

Title sequences

Bass revolutionised film title design beginning with Otto Preminger's The Man with the Golden Arm (1955), whose jagged paper-cut arm motif extended into a famous poster. He designed celebrated sequences for Alfred Hitchcock (Vertigo, North by Northwest, Psycho), Preminger (Anatomy of a Murder), Stanley Kubrick (Spartacus), and later Martin Scorsese (Goodfellas, Casino). He also directed the science-fiction film Phase IV (1974) and won an Academy Award for the short documentary Why Man Creates (1968).

Corporate identity

With his firm Saul Bass & Associates (later Bass/Yager), he designed enduring logos for AT&T (the bell and later globe marks), United Airlines, Continental Airlines, Minolta, and many others, often working with his wife Elaine Bass, a key collaborator on the title work.
