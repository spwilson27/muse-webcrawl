The 24-form tai chi, also called the Simplified form or Beijing form, is a short, standardized sequence of tai chi movements created in 1956 by a committee of tai chi masters commissioned by the Chinese Sports Committee, most prominently Li Tianji. It distills the traditional Yang-style long form into 24 movements that can be performed in about five minutes.

Purpose and structure

The form was designed to popularize tai chi as mass physical exercise in the People's Republic of China, making the art accessible to beginners and suitable for group practice in parks and workplaces. The sequence begins with "Commencing" and includes well-known movements such as "Part the Wild Horse's Mane," "Wave Hands Like Clouds," "Golden Rooster Stands on One Leg," and "Repulse Monkey," concluding with "Cross Hands" and the closing posture.

Popularity

The 24-form became the most widely practised tai chi routine in the world and a standard teaching form in China and internationally. It also serves as a competition routine in modern wushu taolu events and is frequently recommended in health contexts for balance, mobility, and gentle exercise for older adults.
