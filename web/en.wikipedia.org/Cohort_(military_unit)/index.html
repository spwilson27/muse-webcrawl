Cohort (military unit)

A cohort (Latin: cohors) was a standard tactical military unit of the Roman legion following the Marian reforms. A cohort consisted of about 480 soldiers, divided into six centuries of 80 men, each commanded by a centurion.

Organisation

A legion had ten cohorts. The first cohort was double-strength, with about 800 men in five double centuries, and included the legion's most senior centurion, the primus pilus, and the eagle standard (aquila). Cohorts two through ten were quingenary cohorts of six centuries.

History

The cohort replaced the maniple as the legion's main tactical subunit around the late 2nd century BC, giving Roman commanders greater flexibility on varied terrain.
