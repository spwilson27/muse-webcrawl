Chūshingura

Chūshingura (忠臣蔵, "Treasury of Loyal Retainers") is the title of the fictionalised accounts — in puppet theatre, kabuki, novels, film, and television — of the historical vendetta carried out by the forty-seven rōnin in early eighteenth-century Japan.

The historical events

In 1701, Asano Naganori, the daimyō of Akō, attacked the shogunal official Kira Yoshinaka in Edo Castle and was ordered to commit seppuku, while Kira went unpunished. Asano's retainers became rōnin (masterless samurai). Led by Ōishi Yoshio (fictionalised as Ōishi Kuranosuke), forty-seven of them waited nearly two years, then on a snowy night in early 1703 stormed Kira's mansion in Edo, killed him, and carried his head to Asano's grave at Sengaku-ji temple. The shogunate sentenced the rōnin to seppuku, and they are buried alongside their lord at Sengaku-ji, where their graves remain a place of pilgrimage.

The plays

Because direct dramatisation of contemporary political events was forbidden, plays relocated the story to an earlier period and changed names. The best-known version is the 1748 puppet play Kanadehon Chūshingura, written by Takeda Izumo, Miyoshi Shōraku, and Namiki Senryū, soon adapted for kabuki. Its eleven acts present the vendetta as the supreme example of the samurai virtues of loyalty (chūgi) and endurance.

Legacy

Chūshingura has been retold in countless adaptations — including films by Kenji Mizoguchi and Hiroshi Inagaki and the 2013 Hollywood film 47 Ronin — and remains central to Japanese ideas about loyalty, sacrifice, and honour, while also attracting debate about the morality of the vendetta itself.
