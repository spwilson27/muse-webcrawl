Baja California Sur ("South Lower California") is a state in northwestern Mexico, occupying the southern half of the Baja California Peninsula below the 28th parallel. Its capital and largest city is La Paz.

History

The region was home to the Pericú, Guaycura and Cochimí peoples. Jesuit missions were established from 1697. The southern territory was separated administratively at various times, and Baja California Sur became Mexico's second-youngest state on 8 October 1974, the same day as Quintana Roo.

Geography

The state is largely arid, crossed by the Sierra de la Giganta, and includes Pacific and Gulf coastlines as well as islands such as Isla Espíritu Santo. At its southern tip is the resort corridor of Los Cabos, including Cabo San Lucas.

Economy

Tourism, fishing, salt production at Guerrero Negro and government services dominate the economy. The lagoons of the Vizcaíno Biosphere Reserve are major gray whale calving grounds.
