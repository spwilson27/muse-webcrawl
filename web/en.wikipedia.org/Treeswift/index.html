Treeswift

The treeswifts, or crested swifts, are a family, Hemiprocnidae, of aerial birds closely related to the true swifts. The family contains a single genus, Hemiprocne, with four species. They are distributed from India and Southeast Asia through Indonesia to New Guinea and the Solomon Islands.

Description

Treeswifts are small to medium-sized, ranging from about 15 to 30 cm in length. Unlike true swifts, they have crests, long forked tails, soft plumage with glossy or iridescent patches, and the ability to perch — they regularly sit upright on exposed branches, which true swifts cannot do. Their wings are long and scythe-like, and they feed on the wing on insects. The hind toe is reversible in a way that permits gripping branches, and their nests are among the smallest of any bird relative to body size.

Species and behaviour

The four species are the crested treeswift (Hemiprocne coronata), grey-rumped treeswift (H. longipennis), whiskered treeswift (H. comata) and moustached treeswift (H. mystacea). They inhabit forest edges and clearings. The nest is a tiny cup of bark and feathers glued with saliva to the top of a horizontal branch, holding a single egg; the sitting bird perches across the branch and covers the nest, which is so small that the bird, rather than the nest, shelters the egg.
