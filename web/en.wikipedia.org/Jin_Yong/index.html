Jin Yong (金庸), pen name of Louis Cha Leung-yung (10 March 1924 – 30 October 2018), was a Hong Kong wuxia novelist and co-founder of the newspaper Ming Pao. With more than 100 million copies of his works sold worldwide (excluding pirated editions), he is regarded as the most influential modern Chinese martial-arts novelist, whose fifteen works of fiction, written between 1955 and 1972, reshaped the genre and have been adapted countless times for film and television.

Life

Cha was born into the scholarly Cha clan of Haining, Zhejiang. He became a journalist in Shanghai and then Hong Kong, writing film criticism and screenplays. In 1955 he published his first martial-arts serial, The Book and the Sword, under the pen name Jin Yong — formed by splitting the last character of his given name. In 1959 he co-founded Ming Pao, which he edited for over three decades, writing famous political editorials during the Cultural Revolution. He later played a role in Hong Kong's transition politics, serving on the committee that drafted the Basic Law.

Works

His novels are historical epics set against real dynasties: the Condor trilogy (The Legend of the Condor Heroes, The Return of the Condor Heroes, The Heaven Sword and Dragon Saber), Demi-Gods and Semi-Devils, The Smiling, Proud Wanderer and The Deer and the Cauldron, whose anti-hero Wei Xiaobao subverts the chivalric code. A couplet formed from the first characters of his fourteen novels is memorised by generations of readers. Cha revised his works twice, and the study of them — "Jinology" — is an academic field.

Death and legacy

Cha died in Hong Kong on 30 October 2018, aged 94, receiving tributes across the Chinese-speaking world. He received honorary doctorates, an OBE, and France's Légion d'honneur. His fictional jianghu — the rivers-and-lakes world of sects, manuals and honour — remains the definitive vision of the genre.
