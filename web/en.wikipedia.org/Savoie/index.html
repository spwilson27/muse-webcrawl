Savoie is a department in the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region of south-eastern France, named after the historic region of Savoy. Its prefecture is Chambéry. Together with Haute-Savoie it forms the French part of the former Duchy of Savoy.

History

Savoy was the ancestral territory of the House of Savoy, which later ruled Piedmont and eventually became kings of Italy. The region was annexed by France under the Treaty of Turin in 1860, confirmed by a plebiscite, and divided into the departments of Savoie and Haute-Savoie.

Geography and tourism

The department is largely mountainous, covering part of the French Alps, including the Vanoise National Park and the great ski areas of the Tarentaise — Courchevel, Méribel, Val d'Isère, and Tignes, collectively among the largest linked ski areas in the world. The region hosted events of the 1992 Winter Olympics centred on Albertville. Lake Bourget, France's largest natural lake, lies in the west. Savoyard cuisine (fondue, raclette, tartiflette) and wines such as Apremont are regional signatures.
