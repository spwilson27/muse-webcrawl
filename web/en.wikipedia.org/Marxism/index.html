Marxism

Marxism is a political philosophy and method of socioeconomic analysis that uses a materialist interpretation of historical development, known as historical materialism, to understand class relations and societal conflict. Developed by Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels in the mid-19th century, it holds that the mode of production — the economic base of society — shapes its political and ideological superstructure, and that history is driven by struggles between social classes.

Core concepts

Central ideas include the labour theory of value and surplus value, by which capitalists appropriate value produced by workers; alienation of labour under capitalism; the analysis of the bourgeoisie (owners of the means of production) and the proletariat (wage labourers); and the prediction that capitalism's internal contradictions would lead to its replacement by socialism and ultimately a classless, stateless communist society. Marx and Engels set out their programme in The Communist Manifesto (1848), and Marx developed his economics in Das Kapital (1867).

Schools and development

After Marx's death in 1883, Marxist thought diversified into numerous schools: orthodox Marxism, Leninism and the theory of the vanguard party, Trotskyism, Marxism–Leninism as adopted by the Soviet Union, Maoism, Western Marxism (Lukács, Gramsci and the concept of cultural hegemony), the Frankfurt School's critical theory, analytical Marxism, and various forms of democratic and libertarian socialism drawing on Marx.

Historical influence

Marxist parties and movements shaped much of 20th-century history: the Russian Revolution of 1917 founded the first state claiming Marxist ideology, followed by China, Cuba, Vietnam, and others. Marxist analysis also profoundly influenced academic disciplines including history, sociology, literary criticism, and economics. Critics dispute its economic predictions, its record in power, and its theories of value and history; supporters credit it with foundational critiques of capitalism, imperialism, and inequality that remain widely debated.
