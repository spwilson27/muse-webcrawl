Kamloops
Province: British Columbia, Canada
Population: approx. 100,000 (city)
Nickname: Tournament Capital of Canada

Kamloops is a city in south-central British Columbia, Canada, at the confluence of the North and South Thompson rivers near Kamloops Lake. With a population of roughly 100,000, it is a regional service and transportation centre for the Thompson-Nicola area. The name derives from the Secwépemc word "Tk'emlúps", meaning "meeting of the waters".

History

The area is the traditional territory of the Tk'emlúps te Secwépemc. Fur traders established posts here from 1811 onward, and the Hudson's Bay Company built Fort Kamloops in the 1840s. The city grew with the arrival of the Canadian Pacific Railway in 1885 and the cattle-ranching and fruit-growing economies of the surrounding valley. In 2021, the announcement of possible unmarked graves at the former Kamloops Indian Residential School drew international attention and mourning.

Geography and economy

Kamloops sits in a semi-arid valley of sagebrush and ponderosa pine, with hot summers and among the mildest winters in Canada's interior. Forestry, mining, ranching, and Thompson Rivers University anchor the economy. The city brands itself the Tournament Capital of Canada for its many sports facilities and hosted events, and is a noted mountain-biking and golf destination.
