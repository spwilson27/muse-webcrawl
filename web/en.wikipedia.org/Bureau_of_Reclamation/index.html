Bureau of Reclamation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Agency overview

Formed: June 17, 1902
Headquarters: Washington, D.C.
Parent agency: U.S. Department of the Interior

The United States Bureau of Reclamation, sometimes shortened to Reclamation, is a federal agency under the U.S. Department of the Interior, responsible for the oversight and operation of water resource management projects in the western United States. It is best known for building hundreds of dams, power plants, canals and other irrigation and water-supply works across the 17 western states.

History

The agency was created by the Reclamation Act of June 17, 1902, signed by President Theodore Roosevelt, initially as the United States Reclamation Service within the U.S. Geological Survey. It became an independent bureau in 1907. Its early mission was to reclaim arid western lands for agriculture through federally financed irrigation projects, with settlers repaying construction costs.

Major projects

Reclamation's best-known works include Hoover Dam on the Colorado River, Grand Coulee Dam on the Columbia River, Glen Canyon Dam, the Central Valley Project in California, and the Colorado–Big Thompson Project. Its 600 dams and reservoirs deliver water to roughly 31 million people and irrigate about 10 million acres of farmland.

Modern role

Since the late twentieth century the bureau's emphasis has shifted from new construction toward operating and maintaining existing facilities, water conservation, environmental restoration, and generating hydroelectric power — it is the second-largest producer of hydroelectric power in the United States.
