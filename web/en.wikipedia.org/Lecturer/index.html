Lecturer

A lecturer is an academic rank within many universities: broadly, a person who teaches at the tertiary level. The meaning of the title varies substantially between countries. In the United Kingdom and many Commonwealth systems, a lecturer is a full-time, often permanent ("tenured") academic appointment roughly equivalent to an assistant professor in North America, while in the United States "lecturer" typically denotes a teaching-focused, often non-tenure-track instructor. The word derives from the Latin lectura — a reading — recalling the medieval university practice of reading authoritative texts aloud to students.

United Kingdom and Commonwealth

In the UK, Ireland, Australia, New Zealand and South Africa, the standard academic career ladder runs: Lecturer → Senior Lecturer → Reader (or Associate Professor) → Professor. A lectureship is typically a permanent appointment combining teaching, research and administrative service, awarded after a probationary period; promotion depends on publication record, teaching and external standing. In Australia the equivalent grades are styled Level B (Lecturer) and Level C (Senior Lecturer) under national salary classifications.

United States and Canada

In North American usage, lecturer designates an instructor hired primarily to teach — sometimes full-time with a continuing appointment (e.g., "senior lecturer", or the University of California's Lecturer/Senior Lecturer/Professor of Teaching titles), often without research obligations or tenure. The main tenure-track ranks are assistant professor, associate professor and full professor. Confusion between the two systems is common: a British lecturer is a research-active faculty member, not an adjunct.

Other countries

In Germany the comparable junior ranks include Juniorprofessor and Privatdozent; Lektor chiefly denotes a language instructor. In France a maître de conférences is a permanent lecturer-grade civil servant; in Scandinavia, lektor (e.g., Danish and Norwegian) is a senior teaching rank. In many systems, guest lecturers, visiting lecturers and public lecturers are invited speakers without faculty rank.

Historical note

In medieval universities, lecturing was the central teaching act: a master read (legere) and glossed a prescribed text while students took notes. The rank survives in ecclesiastical usage too — a lector is one appointed to read scripture in services — and "lectureship" remains the name of endowed teaching posts in some institutions.
