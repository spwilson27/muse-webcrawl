Catarrhini

The Catarrhini (from Greek kata-, "down", and rhin-, "nose") are one of the two subdivisions of the simian primates, comprising the Old World monkeys, the apes and humans. They are native to Africa and Asia and are distinguished from the New World monkeys (Platyrrhini) by their narrow, downward-facing nostrils set close together.

Characteristics

Catarrhines have a dental formula of 2.1.2.3 (two incisors, one canine, two premolars and three molars in each quadrant), non-prehensile tails where tails are present (apes have none), opposable thumbs, and generally larger bodies and brains than platyrrhines. The parvorder divides into two superfamilies: Cercopithecoidea (Old World monkeys) and Hominoidea (apes, including gibbons, orangutans, gorillas, chimpanzees and humans).

Evolution

Early catarrhines such as Aegyptopithecus are known from Oligocene deposits of the Fayum in Egypt, dating to roughly 30 million years ago. The split between cercopithecoids and hominoids occurred in the late Oligocene or early Miocene.
