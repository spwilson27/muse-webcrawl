The Manchester Ship Canal is a 36-mile (58 km) inland waterway in the North West of England linking Manchester to the Irish Sea via the Mersey estuary. Opened in 1894, it was the largest navigation canal in the world at the time, and it allowed ocean-going ships to sail directly to the Port of Manchester, bypassing the Port of Liverpool and its dues.

History

The canal was promoted by Manchester businessmen led by Daniel Adamson and designed by Edward Leader Williams. Construction began in 1887 under contractor Thomas Walker, employing tens of thousands of navvies; after Walker's death and financial difficulties, Manchester Corporation stepped in with a loan. Queen Victoria performed the official opening on 21 May 1894. At Salford Quays the canal's docks made Manchester Britain's third-busiest port despite being 40 miles inland.

Decline and today

Traffic peaked in 1958 and declined with containerisation and larger ships; the Salford docks closed in 1982 and were redeveloped as Salford Quays, home to MediaCityUK. The canal remains in commercial use under the Peel Ports group, carrying bulk cargoes, and has been promoted as a low-carbon freight route.
