Dalmatian pelican

Scientific namePelecanus crispus
FamilyPelecanidae
Length160–183 cm
Wingspan290–351 cm
Mass7.25–15 kg
ConservationNear Threatened (IUCN)

The Dalmatian pelican (Pelecanus crispus) is the largest member of the pelican family and among the heaviest flying birds in the world. It breeds from southeastern Europe through Asia to China, in swamps and shallow lakes, and winters in ice-free areas around the Mediterranean and in Asia. It is distinguished from the great white pelican by its curly nape feathers, grey legs, and silvery-white plumage with black flight feathers visible mainly in flight.

Description

This huge bird measures 160 to 183 cm in length, with a wingspan of 290 to 351 cm and a weight of 7.25 to 15 kg. Its bill, 34–45 cm long, carries a bright orange pouch in the breeding season. In flight, the Dalmatian pelican shows black primaries and secondaries with a white leading edge from below, differing from the great white pelican's more extensive black. Its flight is slow and stately, alternating deep wingbeats with glides.

Behaviour and diet

Dalmatian pelicans feed mainly on fish, often cooperatively: groups form lines or semicircles to drive fish into shallow water, scooping them up in their pouches. A bird may consume over a kilogram of fish per day. They nest colonially on floating or island reed platforms, laying two eggs on average, and both parents incubate and feed the downy chicks.

Conservation

The species declined drastically during the 19th and 20th centuries from wetland drainage, persecution, disturbance, and collisions with power lines. Conservation measures in Europe — nest platforms, wardening, and power-line marking — helped colonies such as those at Lake Prespa (Greece/North Macedonia) and the Danube Delta (Romania) recover, and the IUCN downlisted it from Vulnerable to Near Threatened in 2017. Its global population is estimated at roughly 10,000–20,000 individuals. Avian influenza outbreaks and habitat loss remain serious threats.
