Running time: 136 minutes

The Matrix is a 1999 science fiction action film written and directed by the Wachowskis. It stars Keanu Reeves as Neo, a computer hacker who learns that the world he inhabits is a simulated reality, the Matrix, created by machines that enslave humanity as an energy source. Freed by the rebel Morpheus, Neo joins the human resistance and begins to realize his potential as "the One."

Plot

Neo, offered the choice between a blue pill (remaining in blissful ignorance) and a red pill (learning the truth), chooses the red pill and awakens in a pod in the machine-ruled real world of 2199. Aboard the hovercraft Nebuchadnezzar, he trains to bend the rules of the simulation and confronts the sentient Agents, particularly Agent Smith. Betrayed by crew member Cypher, Neo risks everything to rescue Morpheus and, after being killed, is resurrected by Trinity's love, returning with the ability to see and manipulate the Matrix's code.

Influences and themes

The film synthesizes cyberpunk, Hong Kong action cinema, anime, philosophy, and religious ideas drawn from Platonism, Gnosticism, Buddhism, and Descartes's skepticism. It popularized "bullet time," a visual effect allowing the camera to move around slow-motion action, and wire-fu choreography arranged by Yuen Woo-ping.

Reception and legacy

The Matrix won four Academy Awards, including Best Visual Effects and Best Film Editing, and grossed over $467 million worldwide. It launched a franchise of sequels — The Matrix Reloaded and The Matrix Revolutions (both 2003) and The Matrix Resurrections (2021) — plus the animated anthology The Animatrix. The film is preserved in the National Film Registry and its imagery and concepts, such as the red pill, have permeated popular culture and philosophy.
