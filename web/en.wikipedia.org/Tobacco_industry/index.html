The tobacco industry comprises the companies and organisations engaged in the growing, preparation, manufacture, marketing and distribution of tobacco and tobacco-derived products. It is dominated by a small number of multinational corporations — sometimes collectively called "Big Tobacco" — alongside the Chinese state monopoly China Tobacco, which produces roughly two-fifths of the world's cigarettes. More than five trillion cigarettes are sold globally each year.

History

Commercial tobacco production dates to the colonial plantations of the Americas. In the late 19th century, James Bonsack's cigarette-rolling machine (1881) and entrepreneurs such as James Buchanan Duke, whose American Tobacco Company dominated the US market until its dissolution under antitrust law in 1911, transformed cigarettes into a mass product. Mass advertising, distribution to soldiers in two world wars, and brands such as Camel, Lucky Strike and Marlboro made smoking near-universal among men in much of the world by the mid-20th century.

The health controversy

From the 1950s, epidemiological evidence linking smoking to lung cancer accumulated. Internal documents, later made public through litigation, showed that the industry publicly denied the evidence while privately acknowledging it — a 1969 Brown & Williamson memo famously described doubt as the industry's "product". In the United States, the industry faced the 1998 Tobacco Master Settlement Agreement, and in 2006 a federal court found the major companies liable under racketeering law for a decades-long scheme to defraud the public about smoking's dangers. The WHO Framework Convention on Tobacco Control (2003) sought to insulate policy-making from industry influence.

Structure today

The leading international companies are Philip Morris International (Marlboro), British American Tobacco (Dunhill, Lucky Strike, Pall Mall), Japan Tobacco and Imperial Brands; Altria is the major US-focused firm. China Tobacco operates largely within China. Facing declining cigarette volumes in high-income countries, the industry has invested heavily in e-cigarettes, heated tobacco products and nicotine pouches, promoted under the language of "harm reduction" — claims that public-health bodies treat with varying degrees of scepticism. Growth remains concentrated in low- and middle-income countries, where most of the world's roughly 1.3 billion tobacco users live.
