Cycas revoluta

Cycas revoluta, the sago palm, is a species of gymnosperm in the family Cycadaceae, native to southern Japan including the Ryukyu Islands. Despite its common name it is a cycad, not a true palm. It is one of the most widely cultivated cycads, grown as an ornamental plant in warm climates and as a houseplant elsewhere.

Description

The plant has a stout, rough trunk topped by a crown of glossy, dark green, feather-like fronds up to 1.5 metres long, each divided into many narrow leaflets with recurved (revolute) margins, the feature reflected in the species name. Plants are dioecious: males produce large upright cones, while females bear clusters of woolly megasporophylls with bright orange-red seeds. Growth is very slow; old specimens may reach several metres in height over many decades.

Cultivation and toxicity

Sago palm tolerates a range of soils with good drainage and is hardy to brief frosts. It is propagated from seed or from offsets ("pups") at the base of the trunk. All parts of the plant are poisonous, especially the seeds, which contain the toxin cycasin; ingestion by dogs and other pets can cause vomiting, liver failure, and death. The processed pith yields a form of sago starch in its native range, but it must be carefully prepared to remove toxins.
