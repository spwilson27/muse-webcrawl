The Memorial to Robert Gould Shaw and the Massachusetts Fifty-Fourth Regiment, commonly known as the Robert Gould Shaw Memorial, is a bronze relief sculpture by Augustus Saint-Gaudens located at the corner of Beacon and Park Streets on Boston Common. It commemorates Colonel Robert Gould Shaw and the 54th Massachusetts Infantry Regiment, one of the first African-American units in the Union Army during the American Civil War, whose soldiers fell with Shaw in the assault on Fort Wagner, South Carolina, in July 1863.

Creation

Saint-Gaudens worked on the memorial for 14 years, from 1883 until its unveiling on 31 May 1897. He modelled the marching soldiers individually, hiring African-American men as models and sculpting some forty heads, from which he selected sixteen for the final relief. Shaw is depicted on horseback, sword in hand, beside a flying figure representing the Angel of Death. The architectural setting, a granite stele, was designed by Charles Follen McKim of McKim, Mead & White.

Reception and legacy

The memorial is considered one of the finest works of American sculpture and the masterpiece of Saint-Gaudens. An inscription added on the reverse in 1982, drawn from Charles Ives's orchestral set Three Places in New England-era commemoration and a poem by James Russell Lowell, honours the soldiers by name. The monument forms part of Boston's Black Heritage Trail and is administered as part of Boston African American National Historic Site. It gained renewed fame through the 1989 film Glory.
