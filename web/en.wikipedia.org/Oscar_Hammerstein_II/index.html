Oscar Greeley Clendenning Hammerstein II (July 12, 1895 – August 23, 1960) was an American lyricist, librettist and theatrical producer. With composer Richard Rodgers he formed Rodgers and Hammerstein, the most successful partnership in American musical theatre, creating Oklahoma!, Carousel, South Pacific, The King and I and The Sound of Music.

Early career

Born into a theatrical family — his grandfather Oscar Hammerstein I was a famous opera impresario — Hammerstein studied at Columbia University and abandoned law for the theatre. In the 1920s he collaborated with composers including Rudolf Friml, Sigmund Romberg and Jerome Kern, with whom he wrote Show Boat (1927), a landmark in integrating song, story and serious themes, including racial prejudice.

Rodgers and Hammerstein

Beginning with Oklahoma! (1943), Hammerstein and Rodgers revolutionised the Broadway musical: songs advanced the plot, ballets expressed psychology, and serious subjects — racism in South Pacific, domestic abuse in Carousel — were addressed directly. Their shows won dozens of Tony Awards and a Pulitzer Prize for South Pacific. Hammerstein was also a mentor to Stephen Sondheim.

Legacy

Hammerstein died of stomach cancer in 1960, shortly after The Sound of Music opened. His humane, optimistic lyrics — "Oh, What a Beautiful Mornin'", "You'll Never Walk Alone", "Edelweiss" — remain part of the standard repertoire.
