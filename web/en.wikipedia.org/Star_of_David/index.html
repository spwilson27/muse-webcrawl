Star of David

The Star of David, a hexagram and symbol of Judaism.

The Star of David (Hebrew: Magen David, "Shield of David") is a generally recognized symbol of both Jewish identity and Judaism. Its shape is that of a hexagram—a compound of two equilateral triangles, one pointing up and one pointing down—which in geometric terms is a six-pointed star polygon, denoted {6/2}.

History

Hexagrams appear in many cultures and religions dating back thousands of years. The star's association with Judaism grew over the medieval period: it appeared on Jewish seals and synagogues, and by the 17th century the Jewish quarter of Vienna was marked with it. In 1897 the First Zionist Congress adopted the Star of David on the flag of the Zionist movement, which in 1948 became the flag of the State of Israel, with the blue star between two horizontal stripes.

Symbolism and interpretations

Various interpretations have been offered: the interlocking triangles represent the connection between God and humanity, or between the Jewish people and the divine; Kabbalistic tradition associates the symbol with cosmic and spiritual elements; and the name links it to the shield of King David, though there is no biblical evidence that David used such a device.

Other uses

During the Holocaust, Jews in Nazi-occupied Europe were forced to wear a yellow Star of David as a badge of identification. The star also appears in the emblem of the Magen David Adom, Israel's national emergency medical service, the Jewish analogue of the Red Cross.
