Thrombocytopenia

Thrombocytopenia is a condition characterised by an abnormally low level of platelets (thrombocytes) in the blood. A normal human platelet count ranges from 150,000 to 450,000 platelets per microlitre of blood; thrombocytopenia is generally defined as a count below 150,000 per microlitre. Because platelets are essential for blood clotting, a low count increases the risk of bleeding.

Signs and symptoms

Many cases are mild and asymptomatic, discovered incidentally on a routine blood count. As the platelet count falls, patients may develop petechiae (tiny red or purple spots on the skin), purpura, easy bruising, bleeding gums, nosebleeds, blood in the urine or stool, and unusually heavy menstrual bleeding. Severe thrombocytopenia can lead to dangerous internal bleeding, including intracranial haemorrhage.

Causes

Thrombocytopenia arises from one or more of three mechanisms: decreased platelet production in the bone marrow, increased platelet destruction or consumption, and sequestration of platelets in an enlarged spleen. Causes include immune thrombocytopenia (ITP), in which autoantibodies target platelets; drugs such as heparin (heparin-induced thrombocytopenia); bone marrow disorders including leukaemia and aplastic anaemia; chronic liver disease; infections; alcohol use; and inherited disorders such as Wiskott–Aldrich syndrome. Thrombotic microangiopathies such as thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP) and haemolytic uraemic syndrome combine thrombocytopenia with haemolytic anaemia.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis rests on a full blood count and examination of a peripheral blood smear; pseudothrombocytopenia caused by platelet clumping in EDTA-anticoagulated samples must be excluded. Bone marrow examination may be needed to assess production. Treatment depends on the cause and severity: it may involve stopping an offending drug, corticosteroids or other immunosuppression for ITP, platelet transfusion for active bleeding or before surgery, and disease-specific therapies such as plasma exchange in TTP.
