Livestock guardian dog

A livestock guardian dog (LGD) is a dog bred and trained to protect sheep, goats, cattle and other livestock from predators such as wolves, coyotes, bears and large cats. Unlike herding dogs, which move stock on command, guardian dogs live full-time with the flock or herd, bonding with the animals from puppyhood and treating them as their social group. When a predator approaches, the dog's size, deep bark and territorial behaviour usually deter it without a fight.

History

The use of large guardian dogs is thousands of years old, arising in the mountains of Europe and Asia where transhumant shepherds moved flocks over long distances. Ancient authors including Aristotle and Varro described white shepherd dogs kept with flocks. The practice spread with European settlement and remains widespread today, where it is valued as a non-lethal method of predator control that can reduce conflict between farmers and protected carnivores.

Breeds

Guardian breeds are typically large (45–70 kg or more), strongly built, and often white or light-coloured so shepherds can distinguish them from predators at a glance. Notable breeds include:

  • Great Pyrenees (Pyrenean Mountain Dog) — France and Spain
  • Maremma Sheepdog — Italy
  • Akbash and Kangal — Turkey
  • Šarplaninac — North Macedonia and Serbia
  • Caucasian Shepherd Dog — Caucasus
  • Tibetan Mastiff — Himalayas
  • Komondor and Kuvasz — Hungary

Behaviour and training

Effective guardians are attentive, trustworthy and protective: they stay with the stock, show no predatory behaviour toward it, and react to threats. Puppies are raised with livestock from about eight weeks of age so that the flock becomes their social focus. Poorly socialised or roaming guardians can themselves become a problem, so training and management are essential.
