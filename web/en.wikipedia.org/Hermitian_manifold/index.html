Hermitian manifold

In mathematics, a Hermitian manifold is a complex manifold equipped with a Hermitian metric: a smoothly varying positive-definite inner product on each complex tangent space that is invariant under the complex structure. Every complex manifold admits Hermitian metrics, making Hermitian geometry a natural generalization of Riemannian geometry to the complex setting.

Fundamental form

Associated to a Hermitian metric is its fundamental (1,1)-form ω. When ω is closed (dω = 0), the metric — and the manifold — is called Kähler. Kähler manifolds form a distinguished subclass with rich structure, including all smooth complex projective varieties and complex tori endowed with flat metrics.

Examples and theory

Notable non-Kähler Hermitian manifolds include Hopf manifolds and most complex surfaces of class VII. Hermitian geometry studies connections such as the Chern connection, curvature of Hermitian metrics, and special classes including balanced and Gauduchon metrics. Concepts are named for Charles Hermite by analogy with Hermitian matrices, which supply the pointwise linear algebra.
