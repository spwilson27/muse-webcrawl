Book of Joshua

Series: Former Prophets (Nevi'im)

Preceded by: Deuteronomy

Followed by: Judges

Chapters: 24

The Book of Joshua (Hebrew: סֵפֶר יְהוֹשֻׁעַ) is the sixth book of the Hebrew Bible and the first of the Former Prophets. It narrates the Israelites' conquest of Canaan under Joshua, successor to Moses, the division of the land among the twelve tribes, and Joshua's farewell addresses and death. Together with Deuteronomy and the books through Kings it forms what scholars call the Deuteronomistic History.

Contents

The book opens with God's commission to Joshua after Moses' death. Spies are sent to Jericho and sheltered by Rahab; the Israelites cross the Jordan on dry ground; and the city of Jericho falls after its walls collapse. After the setback at Ai caused by Achan's sin, campaigns in the south and north defeat coalitions of Canaanite kings; a famous episode describes the sun standing still over Gibeon. The second half of the book (chapters 13–22) lists tribal allotments, cities of refuge, and Levitical towns. The book closes with Joshua's covenant renewal ceremony at Shechem and his death at the age of 110.

Composition and historicity

Tradition ascribes the book to Joshua himself, but scholars date its composition and editing to the seventh and sixth centuries BCE, within the Deuteronomistic school. Archaeological evidence for a rapid military conquest of Canaan as described is contested; many sites named in the conquest narratives were unoccupied in the Late Bronze Age, and mainstream scholarship generally treats the book as theological history rather than a literal military record.
