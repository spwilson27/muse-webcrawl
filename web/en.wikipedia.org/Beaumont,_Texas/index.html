Beaumont, Texas

Beaumont is a city in the U.S. state of Texas and the county seat of Jefferson County, on the Neches River about 30 miles (48 km) west of the Louisiana border and 85 miles (137 km) east of Houston. With a population of about 115,000 at the 2020 census, it anchors the Beaumont–Port Arthur metropolitan area, part of the "Golden Triangle" industrial region of the Gulf Coast.

History

Settled in the 1820s–1830s as a farming and lumber community, Beaumont was incorporated in 1838. Its destiny changed on 10 January 1901, when the Lucas gusher blew in at Spindletop, just south of the city—an oil strike that launched the modern petroleum age, tripled Beaumont's population within months, and gave rise to companies that became Texaco and Gulf Oil. Refining and petrochemicals along the Neches and the nearby Port of Beaumont remain the backbone of the economy.

Geography and climate

Beaumont sits on flat coastal plain at about 16 feet (5 m) above sea level and has a humid subtropical climate with hot summers and frequent heavy rain. The city is vulnerable to Gulf hurricanes and flooding: Hurricane Rita (2005) and Hurricane Harvey (2017), whose rains destroyed the city's main water supply pumps, caused major damage.

Economy and culture

Major employers include refineries and chemical plants, the Port of Beaumont (among the busiest military embarkation ports in the United States), healthcare, and Lamar University, founded in 1923. The city is the birthplace of country singer George Jones and rock singer Janis Joplin grew up in nearby Port Arthur. Museums include the Art Museum of Southeast Texas, the Texas Energy Museum, and the Spindletop-Gladys City Boomtown Museum.
