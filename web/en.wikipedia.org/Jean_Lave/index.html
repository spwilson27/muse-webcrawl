Jean Lave

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Jean Lave (born 1934) is an American social anthropologist and learning theorist. She is a professor emerita at the University of California, Berkeley, and is known for her work on situated learning and communities of practice, developed with Etienne Wenger.

Work

Lave received her PhD in social anthropology from Harvard University in 1968. Her early fieldwork studied mathematical practice in everyday settings, notably among Vai and Gola tailors in Liberia and among grocery shoppers and Weight Watchers members in Orange County, California. In Cognition in Practice (1988), she argued that arithmetic competence is organised by the activity and setting in which it occurs, challenging assumptions that school-taught mathematics simply transfers to everyday life.

Situated learning and communities of practice

With Etienne Wenger, Lave wrote Situated Learning: Legitimate Peripheral Participation (1991), a highly influential work in education and anthropology. It proposed that learning is fundamentally a process of participation in a "community of practice": newcomers begin at the periphery of the community's activities — legitimately, but peripherally — and gradually move toward full participation as they master its practices, identities, and ways of speaking. Studies of midwives, tailors, quartermasters, butchers, and recovering alcoholics grounded the theory. The framework reshaped research on apprenticeship, workplace learning, and education, and informed Lave's later critiques of decontextualised schooling, including Learning and Everyday Life (2019).
