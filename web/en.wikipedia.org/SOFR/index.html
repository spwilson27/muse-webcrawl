Secured Overnight Financing Rate

The Secured Overnight Financing Rate (SOFR) is a secured overnight interest rate. SOFR is a reference rate (specifically, a "risk-free rate" or alternative reference rate) established as an alternative to LIBOR, which has been used extensively in interest rate derivatives and other financial instruments. In June 2023, LIBOR ceased after concerns had arisen about the integrity of the rate, which was found to have been manipulated by a number of lenders, and SOFR became the standard replacement in US dollar contracts.

Calculation

SOFR is calculated as a volume-weighted median of transaction-level tri-party repo data, Treasury General Collateral repo data, and agency GC repo transaction data, all collected by the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, which publishes the rate each business day at approximately 8:00 a.m. ET. Because it is based on actual transactions in the Treasury repurchase market — where banks and investors borrow or lend cash overnight using US Treasuries as collateral — SOFR reflects the cost of borrowing cash overnight collateralized by Treasury securities, with roughly $1 trillion in daily underlying transactions.

History

The Alternative Reference Rates Committee (ARRC), convened by the Federal Reserve, selected SOFR in June 2017 as its preferred alternative to USD LIBOR. Publication began on 2 April 2018. Unlike LIBOR, which was a forward-looking term rate based on bank submissions, SOFR is an overnight rate, so derivatives markets developed term SOFR rates derived from SOFR futures traded on the CME.
