Cross Bronx Expressway

Length: 6.5 mi (10.5 km)
Designations: I-95, I-295, US 1 (part)
Location: The Bronx, New York City

The Cross Bronx Expressway is a major freeway in the New York City borough of the Bronx, conceived and built under the direction of Robert Moses. It is a part of Interstate 95 (and for part of its route, Interstate 295 and US Route 1) and crosses the entire width of the Bronx, connecting the George Washington Bridge over the Hudson River with Long Island routes at the Throgs Neck and Bruckner interchanges. At 6.5 miles (10.5 km), it is one of the most heavily trafficked and congested roadways in the United States.

The Cross Bronx Expressway in the Bronx.

History

First proposed in the 1940s, the expressway was constructed in stages between 1948 and 1972. Building it required blasting through ridges, crossing valleys and rail lines, and demolishing thousands of apartments; its route through East Tremont displaced thousands of residents and is widely cited — notably in Robert Caro's biography The Power Broker — as a case study in the social costs of mid-century urban highway building. Community opposition, led in part by residents of East Tremont, fought unsuccessfully for an alternate route two blocks south.

Traffic and impact

The expressway carries very heavy through truck traffic between New England and points south and west, and is notorious for near-constant congestion; several studies have ranked segments of it among the slowest in the nation. The neighbourhoods alongside it experience high levels of diesel exhaust and elevated asthma rates, making the road a recurring focus of environmental-justice advocacy. Proposals over the years have ranged from capping sections of the expressway with parks to (in Moses-era plans long abandoned) additional parallel roadways.

Route

From west to east, the Cross Bronx runs from the Alexander Hamilton Bridge across the Harlem River, through the Bruckner Interchange area, and splits into the Throgs Neck Expressway (I-295) and the continuation of I-95 toward the New England Thruway.
