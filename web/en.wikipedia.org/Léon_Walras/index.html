Léon Walras

Marie-Esprit-Léon Walras
Born: 16 December 1834, Évreux, France
Died: 5 January 1910, Clarens, Switzerland
School: Lausanne school; marginalism
Known for: general equilibrium theory, Walras's law

Marie-Esprit-Léon Walras (1834–1910) was a French mathematical economist and Georgist, regarded as the founder of general equilibrium theory and a central figure of the marginal revolution.

Career

After unsuccessful ventures in journalism, banking and railway administration, Walras was appointed in 1870 to the new chair of political economy at the University of Lausanne, where he wrote his masterpiece, Éléments d'économie politique pure (1874–1877).

General equilibrium

Walras modelled the economy as a system of simultaneous equations in which prices adjust — by a process he called tâtonnement — until all markets clear. "Walras's law" states that the sum of excess demands across all markets is zero, so if all markets but one clear, the last must clear too.

Influence

His work founded the Lausanne school, continued by Vilfredo Pareto, and underlies modern neoclassical economics and the Arrow–Debreu model.
