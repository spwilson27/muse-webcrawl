Beatification

Beatification is recognition by the Catholic Church of a deceased person's entrance into Heaven and capacity to intercede on behalf of individuals who pray in their name. It is the third of the four major steps in the process of canonisation.

History

Bishops at first had authority to beatify, but reservation of the process to the Holy See developed during the Middle Ages. Pope Alexander III restricted the prerogative in the 12th century.

Process

A cause is normally opened after a waiting period, investigated by the diocese, and examined by the Congregation for the Causes of Saints. Proof of heroic virtue or martyrdom, and usually one verified miracle, is required.

Practice

A person who is beatified is given the title Blessed. Their feast day may be celebrated on a limited scale, whereas canonised saints are honoured universally. See Canonisation.
