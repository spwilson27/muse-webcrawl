North Holland (Dutch: Noord-Holland) is a province of the Netherlands in the northwestern part of the country, bordered by the North Sea to the west, the Wadden Sea and IJsselmeer to the northeast, and the provinces of Friesland, Flevoland, Utrecht and South Holland. Its capital is Haarlem, and its largest city is Amsterdam, the national capital, which lies within the province.

History

The province was created in 1840, when the province of Holland was split into North Holland and South Holland — a division prompted in part by the economic weight of Amsterdam. Historically the region combines the old Noorderkwartier of the County of Holland (West Friesland) with the Kennemerland, Waterland and Gooi districts.

Geography

North Holland is a largely low-lying peninsula between the North Sea and the IJsselmeer; much of its land consists of polders below sea level, protected by the dune belt along the coast. It includes the island of Texel, the largest of the Dutch Wadden Islands. The province contains Amsterdam, Haarlem, Alkmaar (famous for its cheese market), Zaandam and the Zaanstreek, Hilversum (the centre of Dutch broadcasting), and the ports of IJmuiden and Den Helder, the main base of the Royal Netherlands Navy. Schiphol Airport, one of Europe's busiest, lies in the municipality of Haarlemmermeer.
