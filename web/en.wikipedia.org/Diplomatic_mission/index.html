Diplomatic mission

A diplomatic mission is a group of people from a state or organisation present in another state to represent the sending state or organisation officially in the receiving or host state.

Types

The principal type is the embassy, headed by an ambassador and usually located in the host capital. Consulates, headed by consuls, handle administrative and citizen services in other cities. Commonwealth countries exchange high commissions rather than embassies. Permanent missions represent states at international organisations such as the United Nations.

Functions

Missions represent their state, protect its nationals, negotiate with the host government, observe and report conditions, and promote economic, cultural and scientific relations, as set out in the Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations.

Premises and immunity

Mission premises are inviolable: host-state agents may not enter without consent, and the mission's archives and correspondence are protected, though the premises remain the territory of the host state in law.
