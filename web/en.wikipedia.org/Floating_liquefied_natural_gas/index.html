Floating liquefied natural gas

Floating liquefied natural gas (FLNG) is a technology that enables the development of offshore natural gas resources. Floating above an offshore gas field, the FLNG facility produces, liquefies, stores and transfers liquefied natural gas (LNG) at sea before carriers ship it directly to markets. FLNG avoids the need for pipelines to shore and onshore liquefaction plants, making smaller or remote "stranded" gas fields economically developable and reducing the onshore environmental footprint.

Projects

The first FLNG unit to operate was PETRONAS's PFLNG Satu, which began production offshore Malaysia in 2016. Shell's Prelude FLNG, moored about 475 km north-east of Broome, Western Australia, is the largest floating facility ever constructed — 488 metres long and displacing about 600,000 tonnes when fully loaded — and produced its first LNG in 2019. Other units include PFLNG Dua, Gimi for the Greater Tortue Ahmeyim project off Mauritania and Senegal, and converted LNG carriers ("FLNG conversions") developed by Golar LNG.

Advantages and challenges

FLNG facilities can be built in shipyards under controlled conditions, relocated between fields, and removed at decommissioning. Challenges include maintaining liquefaction efficiency on a moving vessel, safe side-by-side offloading of LNG to carriers in open water, and fitting an entire gas plant onto a footprint far smaller than an equivalent onshore plant.
