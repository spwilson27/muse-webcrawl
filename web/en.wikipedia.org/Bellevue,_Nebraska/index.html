Bellevue is a city in Sarpy County, Nebraska, United States, and a southern suburb of Omaha. It lies on the west bank of the Missouri River. Bellevue is the oldest continuous town in Nebraska, tracing its origins to a fur-trading post established in the 1820s.

History

The area was a hub of the Missouri River fur trade; Manuel Lisa's post and later Lucien Fontenelle's post operated here, and the settlement (named belle vue, "beautiful view", for its river prospect) served as a gateway for emigrant trails. Bellevue was incorporated in 1855 and was an early rival to Omaha for regional primacy. Growth in the 20th century was driven by nearby Offutt Air Force Base, created from the Glenn L. Martin bomber plant during the Second World War.

Today

Offutt AFB, home to United States Strategic Command, is a major employer, and much of Bellevue's population is connected to the base. Landmarks include Fontenelle Forest, a large private nature reserve in the Loess Hills along the river, and the Sarpy County Museum. Bellevue is among Nebraska's largest cities and continues to grow with the Omaha metropolitan area. See also Omaha and Offutt Air Force Base.
