The Public Investment Fund (PIF; Arabic: صندوق الاستثمارات العامة) is the sovereign wealth fund of Saudi Arabia. It is among the largest sovereign wealth funds in the world, with total estimated assets of roughly US$900 billion. It was created in 1971 for the purpose of investing funds on behalf of the Government of Saudi Arabia.

The fund is controlled by Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, Saudi Arabia's de facto ruler since 2015. It is a central vehicle of the kingdom's Saudi Vision 2030 programme, which seeks to diversify the Saudi economy away from dependence on oil revenues.

History

The Public Investment Fund was established by Royal Decree in 1971 to provide financing support to projects of strategic significance to the national economy. For its first four decades it operated largely as a domestic holding company for government stakes in Saudi companies, including Saudi Basic Industries Corporation (SABIC) and the National Commercial Bank.

In March 2015, oversight of the PIF was transferred from the Ministry of Finance to the Council of Economic and Development Affairs, chaired by Mohammed bin Salman, who also became chairman of the fund's board. Yasir Al-Rumayyan was appointed governor of the fund.

Investments

The PIF has made a series of high-profile international investments, including a US$45 billion commitment to the SoftBank Vision Fund in 2017, stakes in Uber, Lucid Motors and other technology companies, and the acquisition of Newcastle United Football Club in 2021 through a consortium. Domestically it has launched "giga-projects" such as Neom, the Red Sea Project, Diriyah and Qiddiya.

In 2022, shares in the state oil company Saudi Aramco were transferred to the fund, significantly increasing its assets under management. The fund has stated an ambition to reach US$1 trillion in assets under management by 2025 and US$2 trillion by 2030.

Sports investments

The fund's sports investments include LIV Golf, the Newcastle United takeover, and sponsorship and event ventures that have drawn scrutiny under debates about "sportswashing" — the use of sport to improve a state's international image.
