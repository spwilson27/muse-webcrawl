Passive solar building design

In passive solar building design, windows, walls and floors are designed to collect, store, distribute and reject solar heat without mechanical systems, reducing energy use for heating and cooling.

Principles

Key elements are orientation (in the northern hemisphere, glazing facing south), aperture, thermal mass such as masonry floors that store daytime heat, distribution by conduction and convection, and control via overhangs and shading that block high summer sun while admitting low winter sun.

Techniques

  • Direct gain: sunlight enters living space and heats mass.
  • Indirect gain: Trombe walls and sunspaces buffer heat.
  • Isolated gain: attached greenhouses.
  • Passive cooling: cross-ventilation, stack effect, earth coupling and shading.

History

Ancient Greek, Roman and Chinese buildings used solar orientation; Socrates described south-facing houses. Modern passive solar design developed in the 20th century and underpins standards such as Passivhaus. It relates closely to vernacular architecture and responses to climate change.
