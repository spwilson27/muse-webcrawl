Capitulum

Capitulum
FieldBotany (also zoology, anatomy)
Family exemplarsAsteraceae
ComponentsFlorets on a receptacle, surrounded by involucral bracts

A capitulum (plural capitula) is a type of inflorescence in which many small flowers, called florets, are crowded together on a flattened or rounded receptacle so that the whole cluster resembles a single flower. It is characteristic of the family Asteraceae, which includes sunflowers, daisies, and dandelions, and is technically a pseudanthium ("false flower").

Structure

The receptacle is surrounded by protective bracts forming an involucre. In many Asteraceae, outer ray florets have strap-shaped petals that mimic the petals of a single flower, while central disc florets are tubular. This arrangement maximises pollinator attraction and seed production.

Other uses

In anatomy, a capitulum is a rounded bony prominence, such as the capitulum of the humerus. In zoology the term also denotes the body region of certain barnacles and mites.
