Node (physics)

A node is a point along a standing wave where the wave has minimum amplitude. For instance, in a vibrating guitar string, the ends of the string are nodes. By changing the effective length of the string by stopping it with a finger, the guitarist changes the effective length and hence the fundamental frequency of the note played.

Explanation

Standing waves form when two waves of the same frequency travel in opposite directions and interfere. Where they always meet out of phase, the displacement cancels — a node; where they meet in phase, the amplitude is maximal — an antinode. In a string fixed at both ends, the distance between adjacent nodes is half a wavelength. The concept extends to two- and three-dimensional systems: nodal lines in vibrating membranes (Chladni patterns) and nodal surfaces in atomic orbitals, where the probability of finding an electron is zero.
