United States Department of the Interior

The United States Department of the Interior (DOI) is an executive department of the U.S. federal government responsible for the conservation and management of federal lands and natural resources, and the administration of programs relating to Native Americans, Alaska Natives, Native Hawaiians, and territorial affairs. The department manages roughly 500 million acres — about one-fifth of the land of the United States — through agencies including the National Park Service and the Bureau of Land Management.

History

Proposed for decades as a "home department," the DOI was created in 1849, gathering the General Land Office, the Patent Office, the Bureau of Indian Affairs, and pensions from other departments. Caleb Blood Smith was the first secretary. Over time it shed functions (the Patent Office went to Commerce; the Bureau of Mines was abolished in 1996) while gaining the National Park Service (1916) and modern resource-management bureaus. The department historically bore heavy responsibility for the dispossession of Native lands — a legacy it has increasingly acknowledged; in 2021 Deb Haaland became the first Native American Cabinet secretary when she took office as Secretary of the Interior.

Bureaus

  • National Park Service — 430+ units including the national parks.
  • Bureau of Land Management — 245 million acres, mostly in the West and Alaska.
  • U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service — refuges and endangered species.
  • Bureau of Indian Affairs and Bureau of Indian Education.
  • Bureau of Reclamation — water projects in the West.
  • U.S. Geological Survey — science agency for land, water, and hazards.
  • Bureau of Ocean Energy Management and Bureau of Safety and Environmental Enforcement — offshore energy.

Responsibilities

The department balances conservation against energy development on public lands and offshore, manages recreation for hundreds of millions of visitors annually, fulfills trust obligations to tribal nations, and administers the U.S. territories of Guam, the U.S. Virgin Islands, American Samoa, the Northern Mariana Islands, and Puerto Rico-related affairs.
