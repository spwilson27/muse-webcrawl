First-person shooter

A first-person shooter (FPS) is a video game genre centred on gun and other weapon-based combat in a first-person perspective; the player experiences the action through the eyes of the protagonist and controls the player character in a three-dimensional space. The genre has been credited for launching computer and video game graphics technology, and with popularising multiplayer gaming and online communities.

History

Early experiments such as Maze War (1973) and Spasim (1974) pioneered the viewpoint. The genre took off with id Software's Wolfenstein 3D (1992), followed by Doom (1993), whose fast action, networked multiplayer and moddability defined the template — for years FPS games were simply called "Doom clones". Quake (1996) brought true 3D and online play; GoldenEye 007 (1997) proved the genre on consoles; Half-Life (1998) added seamless narrative; and the Halo and Call of Duty series made FPS games the dominant console genre of the 2000s.

Subgenres and influence

Variants include tactical shooters emphasising realism and teamwork, hero shooters built around distinct characters, immersive sims blending shooting with simulation, and battle royale games. Competitive FPS titles remain central to esports.
