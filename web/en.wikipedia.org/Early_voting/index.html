Early voting

Early voting, also called advance polling or pre-poll voting, is a voting process by which voters in a public election can vote before a scheduled election day. Early voting can take place remotely, such as via postal voting, or in person, available to voters that are usually unable to vote on election day, although the availability to all voters varies by jurisdiction.

In the United States

Most U.S. states offer in-person early voting at designated sites for a period before Election Day, and all states provide some form of absentee or mail voting. The share of voters casting ballots before Election Day has risen markedly in the 21st century, with a majority of votes in the 2020 presidential election cast early or by mail, driven partly by the COVID-19 pandemic.

Other countries

Advance polls are a standard feature of Canadian federal and provincial elections. Many European countries rely primarily on postal voting or proxy voting rather than in-person early voting, while Australia holds pre-poll voting centres in the weeks before election day, and a large and growing fraction of Australians now vote before polling day.

Debate

Supporters argue early voting increases turnout, reduces queues, and makes voting more convenient. Critics note that early voters may cast ballots before late-breaking campaign developments, and raise concerns about the security and administration of extended voting periods.
