Story of a Soul

Story of a Soul (French: Histoire d'une âme) is the autobiography of Thérèse of Lisieux, a French Discalced Carmelite nun who died at the age of 24. It was first published posthumously in 1898, a year after her death, in the form of a limited private printing of 2,000 copies intended for Carmelites and religious benefactors. The book became one of the most widely read spiritual works of the twentieth century and was central to Thérèse's rapid rise to popular sainthood; she was canonised in 1925.

Composition

The work consists of three distinct manuscripts written under obedience to her superiors — two of her sisters served as prioresses of the Lisieux Carmel. Manuscript A, written in 1895 at the request of Mother Agnes of Jesus (her sister Pauline), recounts her childhood; Manuscript B is a letter to her sister Marie of the Sacred Heart written in 1896 expressing her "little way"; Manuscript C, written in 1897 for Mother Marie de Gonzague during her final illness, describes her life in the convent. In them Thérèse sets out her "Little Way of spiritual childhood" — a path to holiness through trust, humility and small acts of love rather than great deeds.

Publication history

The first edition, edited heavily by Pauline, rearranged and corrected the manuscripts. Photographic reproductions of the original manuscripts were published in the 1950s, and a critical edition followed, allowing readers to see Thérèse's unedited text. The autobiography has been translated into dozens of languages, and an English translation by John Clarke, O.C.D., is widely used.

Influence

The book influenced numerous later Catholic writers and saints and popularised the image of Thérèse as the "Little Flower". It is read as a classic of modern Catholic spirituality.
