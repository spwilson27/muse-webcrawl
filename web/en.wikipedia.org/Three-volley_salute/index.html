Three-volley salute

The three-volley salute is a ceremonial act performed at military funerals and sometimes at other memorial events. A rifle party, usually consisting of an odd number of service members, fires three volleys of blank cartridges into the air in unison. It is performed as part of military funeral honors in the United States and in similar forms in other countries.

Origins

The custom is often traced to battlefield truces in which fighting was halted to clear the dead, and three musket volleys signalled that the dead had been cared for and the battle could resume. In the United States, the salute is rendered at the graveside service for veterans and service members, typically following the presentation of the flag and preceding or accompanying the playing of "Taps".

Confusion with the 21-gun salute

The three-volley salute is distinct from the 21-gun salute, which uses artillery or naval guns and is reserved for national flags, heads of state, and the highest military honors.
