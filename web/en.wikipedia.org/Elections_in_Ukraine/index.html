Elections in Ukraine are held to choose the President of Ukraine, the deputies of the Verkhovna Rada (parliament), and local councils and mayors. Elections are administered by the Central Election Commission of Ukraine and are generally held under universal, equal and direct suffrage by secret ballot.

Presidential elections

The President is elected for a five-year term in a two-round system: if no candidate wins an absolute majority in the first round, a runoff is held between the two leading candidates. Presidential elections since independence in 1991 have been closely watched as a measure of Ukraine's democratic development.

Parliamentary elections

The Verkhovna Rada has 450 seats. Electoral systems have varied over time, including mixed systems combining single-member districts with proportional party lists, and fully proportional representation with open lists under the 2019 Electoral Code.

Local elections

Local elections choose oblast, raion, city, town and village councils as well as mayors and village heads. Decentralisation reforms after 2014 gave greater weight to elections in amalgamated territorial communities (hromadas).
