Digital Light Processing

InventorLarry Hornbeck, Texas Instruments
Year1987 (DMD chip); first products 1996
Core deviceDigital micromirror device (DMD)

Digital Light Processing (DLP) is a set of chipsets based on optical micro-electro-mechanical technology that uses a digital micromirror device. Developed by Texas Instruments, DLP is used in video projectors, digital cinema projection, pico projectors, and 3D printing.

Technology

The DMD chip carries up to millions of microscopic mirrors, one per pixel, each tilting thousands of times per second toward or away from the light source. Grayscale is created by pulse-width modulation of the reflection; color is added by a spinning color wheel in single-chip systems or by separate chips for red, green, and blue in high-end cinema projectors.

Applications

DLP Cinema projectors enabled the rollout of digital cinema, meeting the Digital Cinema Initiatives specification. DLP competes with LCD and LCoS projection technologies and is noted for high contrast, reliability, and the absence of burn-in.
