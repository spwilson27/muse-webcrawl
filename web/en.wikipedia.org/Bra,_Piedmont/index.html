Bra is a town and comune in the province of Cuneo in the northwestern Italian region of Piedmont, situated about 50 kilometres southeast of Turin. It stands near the Langhe and Roero wine hills and is known for gastronomy and Baroque architecture.

History

The town traces its origins to the Roman settlement of Pollentia, founded nearby in the late 2nd century BC and the site of a battle in 402 AD. Bra itself grew in the Middle Ages and later came under the House of Savoy. Notable landmarks include the Baroque church of Sant'Andrea, designed in the circle of Bernini, and the Palazzo Mathis.

Slow Food

Bra is the birthplace of the Slow Food movement, founded by Carlo Petrini in 1986 to defend regional traditions and good, clean, and fair food. The movement's international headquarters and its University of Gastronomic Sciences (at nearby Pollenzo) are based here, and the town hosts the biennial "Cheese" festival celebrating artisanal dairy production. Bra is also known for its sausage, salsiccia di Bra, traditionally eaten raw.
