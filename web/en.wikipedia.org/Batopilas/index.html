Batopilas is a small town and the seat of the municipality of the same name in the southwestern part of the Mexican state of Chihuahua. Located deep in the Barrancas del Cobre (Copper Canyon) region at the bottom of a steep canyon along the Batopilas River, it was once one of the richest silver-mining towns in Mexico.

History

Silver was discovered in the area around 1632, and the town was formally founded in 1708. During its late-19th-century heyday, American mining magnate Alexander Shepherd — a former mayor of Washington, D.C. — ran the Batopilas Mining Company, building extensive works including a hydroelectric plant and an aqueduct. More than 300 mines operated in the district at its peak.

Geography and tourism

Batopilas sits at roughly 578 metres elevation in a subtropical canyon bottom, contrasting with the cool pine forests of the rim far above. It has been designated a Pueblo Mágico by Mexico's tourism authorities. Nearby attractions include the "Lost Cathedral" of Satevó, colonial haciendas and the canyon scenery of the Sierra Tarahumara.
