Jin–Song Wars

The Jin–Song Wars were a series of conflicts between the Jurchen-led Jin dynasty (1115–1234) and the Han Chinese-led Song dynasty in the 12th and 13th centuries in East Asia. The wars began in 1125 when the Jin, having destroyed the Liao dynasty with Song assistance, turned on their former allies, and they ended only with the Mongol conquest of both states.

Background

In 1120 the Song and Jin concluded the Alliance Conducted at the Sea, agreeing to jointly attack the Khitan Liao dynasty and divide its territory. After the Liao fell in 1125, disputes over the promised territories led the Jin to invade the Song.

Jingkang incident

Jin forces besieged the Song capital Kaifeng twice; in 1127 they captured the city, taking prisoner the emperors Qinzong and Huizong in the humiliation known as the Jingkang incident. The surviving Song court fled south and re-established the dynasty (the Southern Song) at Hangzhou.

Later campaigns

Fighting continued intermittently for decades. The Song general Yue Fei won notable victories before his recall and execution in 1142, after which the Treaty of Shaoxing fixed the border along the Huai River. Further Jin invasions in 1161 and 1206–1208 failed. In the 1230s the Song allied with the Mongols to destroy the Jin (1234), but the Mongols soon turned south, conquering the Song itself in 1279.
