Prakriti

Prakriti or Prakruti (Sanskrit: प्रकृति, prakṛti, "nature, primal matter") is a key concept in Hindu philosophy, particularly in the Sāṃkhya school, denoting the fundamental material substance of the universe from which all physical and mental phenomena evolve. It is contrasted with puruṣa, pure consciousness, the witness self.

In Sāṃkhya

Sāṃkhya teaches a dualism of prakriti (unconscious, active matter) and puruṣa (passive, conscious spirit). Prakriti is composed of three guṇas (qualities or strands): sattva (clarity, goodness), rajas (activity, passion), and tamas (inertia, darkness). In its unevolved state (mūlaprakṛti) the guṇas are in equilibrium; when disturbed, prakriti evolves into the twenty-three tattvas — intellect (buddhi), ego (ahaṃkāra), mind (manas), the senses, and the gross elements — producing the manifest world. Liberation (kaivalya) occurs when puruṣa discriminates itself from prakriti and ceases to misidentify with it.

Other contexts

In Ayurveda, prakriti refers to an individual's innate constitution, the unique balance of the three doṣas (vāta, pitta, kapha) present from birth. In some Hindu traditions, especially Śākta and devotional schools, Prakriti is personified as the Goddess (Devī/Śakti), the dynamic feminine energy of the divine, and in the Bhagavad Gītā Krishna describes prakriti as his lower nature (aparā prakṛti) through which he creates the cosmos.
