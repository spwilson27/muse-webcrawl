Empress Matilda (c. 1102 – 10 September 1167), also called Maud, was one of the claimants to the English throne during the civil war known as the Anarchy. The daughter and designated heir of King Henry I, she had been Holy Roman Empress as wife of Emperor Henry V. On her father's death in 1135, her cousin Stephen of Blois seized the crown, and Matilda's attempt to win it back produced nearly two decades of war. Though she never ruled England in her own right, her son succeeded as Henry II, founding the Plantagenet dynasty.

Matilda, daughter of Henry I and mother of Henry II

Early life and first marriage

Matilda was the daughter of Henry I of England and Matilda of Scotland. At age eight she was betrothed to the German king Henry V and was crowned queen of the Romans; she married him in 1114 and was later crowned empress in Rome. Henry V died in 1125, leaving her a childless widow at 23. Meanwhile, the drowning of her brother William Adelin in the White Ship disaster (1120) made Matilda her father's only legitimate child, and Henry I required his barons to swear oaths recognising her as heir.

Second marriage and succession crisis

In 1128 Matilda married Geoffrey of Anjou, a union that produced three sons, including the future Henry II. When Henry I died in 1135, Stephen of Blois crossed to England and had himself crowned, breaking the oaths. Matilda landed in England in 1139, beginning the civil war called the Anarchy.

The Anarchy

Matilda's fortunes peaked in 1141: Stephen was captured at the Battle of Lincoln, and she advanced to London in preparation for her coronation. Her imperious manner alienated the Londoners, who expelled her before the ceremony. After Stephen's release in exchange for her captured half-brother Robert of Gloucester, the war settled into stalemate. She famously escaped besieged Oxford Castle in 1142 by crossing the frozen Thames in white clothing. Matilda left England in 1148, but her son Henry continued the claim, and the Treaty of Wallingford (1153) recognised him as Stephen's heir.

Later life

Matilda spent her remaining years in Normandy, advising her son and acting as a patron of the Church. She died at Rouen in 1167 and was buried at Bec Abbey; her epitaph praised her as "great by birth, greater by marriage, greatest in her offspring".
