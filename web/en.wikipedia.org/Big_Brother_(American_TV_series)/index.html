Big Brother is a long-running American television reality show in which a group of people, called HouseGuests, live together in a custom-built house isolated from the outside world but continuously watched by television cameras. The series premiered on CBS on July 5, 2000, and is hosted by Julie Chen Moonves.

Format

Each week, HouseGuests compete for the title of Head of Household, who nominates two housemates for eviction. The Power of Veto competition can remove a nominee, and the remaining HouseGuests vote to evict. The final two are judged by a jury of evicted HouseGuests, and the winner receives a grand prize — originally $500,000, raised to $750,000 in later seasons.

Origins

The show is based on the Dutch series created by John de Mol, first broadcast in the Netherlands in 1999. The first American season used a public-vote format closer to the international version; from the second season it adopted the strategic, vote-based format that has defined the US edition since.

Spin-offs and streaming

The franchise includes Celebrity Big Brother, the online edition Big Brother: Over the Top, and additional competitions such as Big Brother Reindeer Games. Live feeds from the house cameras stream around the clock, a hallmark of the series since its debut.
