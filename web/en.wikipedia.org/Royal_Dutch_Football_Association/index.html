The Royal Dutch Football Association (Dutch: Koninklijke Nederlandse Voetbal Bond, KNVB) is the governing body of football in the Netherlands. It organises the national leagues — including the professional Eredivisie, Eerste Divisie and the KNVB Cup — the amateur game, futsal and the Netherlands national teams. With roughly 1.2 million members it is the largest sports federation in the country.

History

The association was founded on 8 December 1889 in The Hague by Pim Mulier and representatives of ten clubs as the Nederlandsche Voetbal- en Athletiekbond; it soon concentrated on football alone. In 1904 the KNVB was one of the founding members of FIFA in Paris, and in 1954 a founding member of UEFA. It received the designation "Royal" (Koninklijke) in 1929. Professionalism was legalised in 1954, leading to the creation of the Eredivisie in 1956.

National teams

The KNVB fields the men's national team — World Cup finalists in 1974, 1978 and 2010 and European champions in 1988 — and the women's national team, which won Euro 2017 on home soil and reached the 2019 World Cup final. Its youth development system, based at the KNVB Campus in Zeist, is internationally influential, emphasising technical training and a coherent playing philosophy through the age groups.

Organisation

The KNVB is structured as an association of associations: professional football is run through a dedicated section working with the league bodies, while amateur football is administered through district offices. It also governs refereeing, discipline, licensing of clubs and the registration of more than a million players at some 3,000 clubs.
