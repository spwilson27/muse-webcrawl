Certificate authority

Certificate authority
Role: Issues digital certificates
Standard: X.509
System: Public key infrastructure (PKI)

A certificate authority (CA) is an entity that stores, signs and issues digital certificates that bind a public key to an identity such as a website domain. CAs are trusted third parties at the heart of the web's public key infrastructure, enabling HTTPS.

How it works

An applicant generates a key pair and proves control of a domain or identity; the CA then signs a certificate. Browsers ship with lists of trusted root CAs and verify chains of trust. Certificates can be domain-validated, organisation-validated or extended-validation, and may be revoked if compromised.

Trust and incidents

The system depends on CA honesty and security; breaches such as the 2011 DigiNotar hack led to distrust and bankruptcy. Certificate Transparency logs and free automated CAs such as Let's Encrypt have since improved accountability. See Public key certificate and HTTPS.
