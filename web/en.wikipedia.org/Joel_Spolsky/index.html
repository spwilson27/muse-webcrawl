Joel Spolsky

[Image placeholder: portrait of Joel Spolsky]

Avram Joel Spolsky (born 1965) is a software engineer and writer. He is the author of Joel on Software, a blog on software development, and the creator of the Trello project management software. He was a program manager on the Microsoft Excel team between 1991 and 1994, and later founded Fog Creek Software (later renamed Glitch, Inc.), where he created FogBugz. He is also a co-founder and former CEO of Stack Overflow (later Stack Exchange).

Spolsky was born in Albuquerque, New Mexico, and grew up in Jerusalem, Israel, before returning to the United States for university. He graduated from Yale University with a bachelor's degree in computer science, then served as a paratrooper and kibbutz founder in Israel before joining Microsoft.

Joel on Software

Spolsky began writing Joel on Software in 2000. The essays — covering software management, design, and business — became widely read in the programming community. He popularised the "Joel Test", a twelve-point checklist for measuring the quality of a software team, and concepts such as "architecture astronauts". Several collections of the essays were published as books.

Stack Overflow and Trello

In 2008 Spolsky co-founded Stack Overflow with Jeff Atwood; the question-and-answer site grew into the Stack Exchange network and one of the most visited developer resources in the world. Trello, a visual project-management tool developed at Fog Creek, was released in 2011 and spun out as its own company in 2014; it was acquired by Atlassian in 2017. Stack Overflow was acquired by Prosus in 2021.
