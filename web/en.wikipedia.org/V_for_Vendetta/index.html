V for Vendetta

V for Vendetta is a British graphic novel written by Alan Moore and illustrated by David Lloyd (with additional art by Tony Weare). First published between 1982 and 1985 in the British anthology Warrior, the series was completed and collected by DC Comics in 1988–1989.

Set in a dystopian near-future United Kingdom ruled by the fascist Norsefire party after a nuclear war, the story follows V, an anarchist revolutionary wearing a Guy Fawkes mask, who wages a campaign of theatrical violence against the regime, and Evey Hammond, a young woman drawn into his mission. The Guy Fawkes mask from the story later became a widely used symbol of protest, notably adopted by the hacktivist group Anonymous.

Film adaptation

The graphic novel was adapted into a 2005 film directed by James McTeigue, written by the Wachowskis, and starring Hugo Weaving as V and Natalie Portman as Evey. The film, which reoriented some of the book's anarchist themes toward a critique of contemporary politics, was a commercial success and popularized the mask's iconic design.

Themes

The work explores themes of fascism, anarchism, identity, and the use of ideas as weapons, and is regarded as one of the landmark works of the graphic novel medium.
