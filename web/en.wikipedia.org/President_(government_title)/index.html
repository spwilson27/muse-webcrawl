President (government title)

A president is a leader of an organization, company, community, club, trade union, university or other group. In the context of government, the title President is commonly used for a head of state in most republics. The word derives from the Latin praesidens, "one who sits before", hence one who presides.

Presidents as heads of state

Most states that have a president as head of state are republics. The powers of presidents vary enormously according to the constitutional system. In presidential systems, such as the United States and much of Latin America, the president is both head of state and head of government, exercising executive power directly and typically being elected independently of the legislature. In parliamentary republics, such as Germany, India and Italy, the president is a largely ceremonial head of state while executive power rests with a prime minister. Semi-presidential systems, as in France and Russia, divide executive authority between a president and a prime minister.

Election and terms

Presidents may be directly elected by popular vote, chosen by an electoral college, or elected by the legislature. Terms commonly run from four to seven years, and many constitutions limit presidents to two terms. The office of president of the United States, established in 1789 with George Washington as its first holder, served as a model for many later republics. The French president of the Fifth Republic, elected for five years, is among the most powerful heads of state in a developed democracy.

Other uses of the title

Beyond national government, "president" is the title of the presiding officers of legislatures and courts in some countries, of leaders of subnational governments in some federations, and of the heads of supranational bodies such as the President of the European Commission and the President of the European Council.
