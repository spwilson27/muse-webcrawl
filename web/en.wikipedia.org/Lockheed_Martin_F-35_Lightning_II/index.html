Lockheed Martin F-35 Lightning II

F-35 Lightning II
Role: Stealth multirole fighter
Manufacturer: Lockheed Martin
First flight: 15 December 2006
Introduced: 2015 (F-35B, USMC)
Status: In service, in production
Number built: over 1,000 (by 2024)

The Lockheed Martin F-35 Lightning II is an American family of single-seat, single-engine, stealth multirole combat aircraft designed for air superiority and strike missions. It is produced in three variants — the conventional F-35A, the short-takeoff/vertical-landing F-35B, and the carrier-based F-35C — and is intended to serve as the cornerstone of NATO and allied air power into the 2080s.

Development

The F-35 grew out of the Joint Strike Fighter programme; Lockheed Martin's X-35 was selected over Boeing's X-32 in 2001. The first F-35 flew on 15 December 2006. Development was long and troubled, with software delays, structural redesigns and major cost growth; the programme's lifetime cost has been estimated at over $1.7 trillion, making it the most expensive military programme in history. Eight partner nations contributed to development, led by the United Kingdom.

Design

The F-35 combines low-observable shaping and radar-absorbent materials with an AN/APG-81 AESA radar and a distributed aperture system of infrared cameras giving the pilot a view projected through the airframe onto the helmet display. Its Pratt & Whitney F135 engine produces over 40,000 lbf of thrust; the F-35B adds a shaft-driven lift fan for vertical landing. Internal weapons bays preserve stealth, while external hardpoints allow a "beast mode" configuration for permissive environments.

Operational history

The US Marine Corps declared initial operational capability in July 2015, followed by the US Air Force (2016) and Navy (2019). Israel reported the first combat use of the F-35 ("Adir") in 2018. The type has been ordered by more than a dozen countries, including Britain, Italy, Australia, Japan, Norway and Switzerland, and more than 1,000 aircraft had been delivered by the mid-2020s.
