Appalachian Spring

Appalachian Spring is a ballet composed by Aaron Copland and choreographed by Martha Graham, premiered on 30 October 1944 at the Library of Congress in Washington, D.C. Copland's score, originally written for thirteen instruments, won the Pulitzer Prize for Music in 1945, and the orchestral suite drawn from it became one of the best-known works of American classical music.

Story and music

The ballet celebrates the pioneers of the American frontier in the 19th century: a young farmer and his bride build a farmhouse in the Pennsylvania hills, joined by a revivalist preacher, a pioneer woman and neighbours. The score famously incorporates the Shaker melody "Simple Gifts", presented in a set of variations near the work's climax.

Legacy

The title, suggested by Graham, comes from a line in Hart Crane's poem The Bridge and does not refer to a season in the Appalachians but to a spring (water source). The ballet's plain-spoken, open harmonies helped define a distinctly American sound in concert music.
