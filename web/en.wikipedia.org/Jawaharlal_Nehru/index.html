Jawaharlal Nehru

Jawaharlal Nehru (14 November 1889 – 27 May 1964) was an Indian anti-colonial nationalist, secular humanist, social democrat, statesman and author who was a central figure in India during the middle of the 20th century. He was the first Prime Minister of India, serving from 1947 until his death in 1964.

Early life

Born in Allahabad into a Kashmiri Pandit family, Nehru was educated in England at Harrow School and Trinity College, Cambridge, and trained as a barrister at the Inner Temple. On returning to India he practised law before joining the Indian National Congress under the mentorship of Mahatma Gandhi.

Independence movement

Nehru became a leader of the left wing of Congress, served repeatedly as Congress president, and was imprisoned several times by the British. He championed complete independence (Purna Swaraj), declared in 1929, and worked alongside Gandhi, Sardar Patel, and others in the freedom struggle.

Prime Minister

As prime minister, Nehru promoted parliamentary democracy, secularism, science and technology, and a mixed economy with five-year plans. Abroad he was a founder of the Non-Aligned Movement, pursuing an independent foreign policy during the Cold War. His tenure saw the establishment of the Indian Institutes of Technology, the Atomic Energy Commission, and the integration of princely states begun under Patel.

Works and legacy

Nehru wrote The Discovery of India, Glimpses of World History, and an autobiography, Toward Freedom. His birthday, 14 November, is celebrated in India as Children's Day. He was the father of Indira Gandhi and grandfather of Rajiv Gandhi, both later prime ministers.
