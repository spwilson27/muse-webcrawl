Pluot

Pluots are complex plum–apricot hybrids noted for their sweetness.

A pluot is a hybrid fruit produced by crossing a plum with an apricot, with the plum parentage predominating — typically around three-quarters plum and one-quarter apricot. The name is a portmanteau of "plum" and "apricot". Pluots are one of several "interspecific" Prunus hybrids developed in California in the late twentieth century; related crosses include the plucot (more evenly divided), the aprium (apricot-dominant), and the apriplum.

Development

Earlier plum–apricot crosses date to Luther Burbank and others in the early 1900s, but modern pluots were principally developed by plant breeder Floyd Zaiger of Zaiger's Genetics in Modesto, California, through decades of hand-pollination and selection — not genetic engineering. Zaiger trademarked the name "Pluot" and introduced leading varieties such as Dapple Dandy (sold as "Dinosaur Egg"), Flavor King, Flavor Queen, and Flavor Supreme in the 1990s. Because they are bred over many generations of backcrossing, pluots are more complex hybrids than the simple 50:50 plumcot.

Characteristics

Pluots resemble plums externally, with smooth skin in colours ranging from greenish-gold to deep purple and speckled ("dappled"), while the flesh may be pale, golden, or intensely red. They are noted for very high sugar content combined with complex flavour and relatively low acidity, and are eaten fresh. The fruit ripens in summer, and California is the main producing region. Nutritionally they provide vitamin C, vitamin A, potassium, and fibre comparable to other stone fruit.

Cultivation

Pluot trees require cross-pollination with compatible plum or pluot varieties and chill hours similar to plums. Orchards are concentrated in California's San Joaquin Valley. The varieties remain proprietary, licensed to growers through Zaiger's associated nursery operations, and the fruit commands premium prices in supermarkets.
