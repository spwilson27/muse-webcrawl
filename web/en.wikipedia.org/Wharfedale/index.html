Wharfedale is one of the Yorkshire Dales, a valley in northern England through which flows the River Wharfe. It extends from the river's source in the Langstrothdale fells eastward past Kettlewell, Grassington, Bolton Abbey, Ilkley, Otley and Wetherby to where the Wharfe joins the Ouse near Cawood. The upper valley, Upper Wharfedale, lies within the Yorkshire Dales National Park.

Wharfedale is noted for its limestone scenery, waterfalls such as those at Aysgarth (in neighbouring Wensleydale) and the Strid near Bolton Abbey — a narrow, notoriously dangerous chasm in the Wharfe — and for the ruins of Bolton Priory and Barden Tower. The spa town of Ilkley, beneath the gritstone edge of Ilkley Moor, became fashionable in the Victorian era for its hydropathic establishments.

History

There is evidence of prehistoric settlement on the limestone uplands above Grassington, and the valley carries a Roman road over the moors near Ilkley, where a Roman fort (Olicana) stood. Lead mining and quarrying were important industries in the upper dale in the 18th and 19th centuries; today the economy rests largely on agriculture and tourism. The name "Wharfedale" is also carried by a well-known British loudspeaker manufacturer founded in the dale's wider region in 1932.
