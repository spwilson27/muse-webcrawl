Battle of Okinawa

Battle of Okinawa
Date1 April – 22 June 1945
LocationOkinawa Island, Ryukyu Islands, Japan
ResultAllied victory
BelligerentsUnited States and Allies; Empire of Japan
CommandersSimon Bolivar Buckner Jr. (killed), Chester Nimitz; Mitsuru Ushijima

The Battle of Okinawa, codenamed Operation Iceberg, was fought from 1 April to 22 June 1945 on the island of Okinawa in the Ryukyu chain. It was the largest amphibious assault of the Pacific War and the last major battle of the Second World War.

American forces landed largely unopposed on 1 April, the Japanese 32nd Army under General Mitsuru Ushijima having withdrawn to fortified lines in the south, culminating in the Shuri Line. The fighting there was savage, conducted amid torrential rain and mud. Offshore, the US fleet endured massed kamikaze attacks, including the suicide sortie of the battleship Yamato. Ushijima and his staff committed suicide as organized resistance collapsed in late June.

Casualties and significance

More than 12,000 American servicemen were killed; Japanese military deaths exceeded 100,000, including conscripted Okinawans, and civilian deaths are estimated at between 40,000 and 150,000 — a very large share of the island's population. The ferocity of the battle and the scale of kamikaze attacks influenced Allied thinking about the prospective invasion of the Japanese home islands, and Okinawa became a major American base, remaining under US administration until 1972.
