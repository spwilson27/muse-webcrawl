Basler Fasnacht


Illuminated lanterns at the Morgestraich

The Basler Fasnacht is the carnival in Basel, Switzerland, and the largest carnival in the country. Unlike most carnivals, it begins after Ash Wednesday, on the Monday following it, at exactly 4:00 a.m. with the Morgestraich: all the lights of the old town are switched off, and hundreds of drummers and piccolo players in costume and elaborate masks begin to play in the darkness, accompanied only by painted lanterns.

The carnival lasts exactly 72 hours, ending on Thursday at 4:00 a.m., and is famed as the "three most beautiful days" (drey scheenschte Dääg) by the locals. Participants are organised in cliques, which march through the streets playing music and carrying large illuminated lanterns (Ladärne) painted with satirical motifs on current events. Unlike other carnivals, spectators do not dress up, and masked participants never reveal their identity; it is considered an honour-related taboo to be recognised.

Other highlights include the Cortège, the organised parade routes of Monday and Wednesday afternoons, the Guggenmusik brass concerts on Tuesday evening, and the Schnitzelbangg singers who perform satirical verses in Basel dialect in restaurants and cellars. In 2017, the Basel Fasnacht was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
