Hot pot

OriginChina
Main ingredientsSimmering broth, thinly sliced meats, vegetables, noodles, tofu, seafood
Regional namesHuoguo (火鍋), shabu-shabu (Japan), steamboat

Hot pot or hotpot is a Chinese cooking method prepared with a simmering pot of soup stock at the dining table, into which diners place a variety of raw ingredients to cook in the broth. Typical ingredients include thinly sliced meat, leaf vegetables, mushrooms, wontons, egg dumplings, tofu, and seafood. The cooked food is usually eaten with a dipping sauce.

Regional varieties

The spicy Sichuan hot pot uses a broth laden with Sichuan pepper and dried chillies, often served in a divided yuanyang pot that pairs the fiery broth with a mild one. Chongqing hot pot is famed for its tallow-based, intensely hot broth. Cantonese-style hot pot (daa bin lou) emphasises fresh seafood and light broths. In China's north, mutton hot pot cooked in a copper pot is traditional. Variants spread across East and Southeast Asia, including Japanese shabu-shabu and nabemono, Korean jeongol, Thai suki, and Vietnamese lẩu.

Dining style

Hot pot is a communal, social meal: diners cook their own food at the table, customising the meal with a sauce bar of ingredients such as sesame paste, soy sauce, garlic, coriander, and chilli oil.
