Northern Cyprus

Northern Cyprus (Turkish: Kuzey Kıbrıs)
Official name: Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus (claimed)
Capital: North Nicosia
Area: 3,355 km²
Population: about 400,000 (est.)
Currency: Turkish lira
Recognition: Recognised only by Turkey

Northern Cyprus, officially the Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus (TRNC), is a de facto state comprising the northeastern portion of the island of Cyprus. It is recognised only by Turkey, while the international community regards its territory as part of the Republic of Cyprus.

History

Intercommunal violence between Greek and Turkish Cypriots following independence in 1960 culminated in 1974, when a Greek Cypriot coup aiming at union with Greece prompted a Turkish military intervention. The island has been divided ever since along the UN-patrolled Green Line. In 1983 the Turkish Cypriot leadership unilaterally declared independence as the TRNC, a move condemned by UN Security Council Resolution 541 as legally invalid.

Status and politics

In 2004 Turkish Cypriots voted in favour of the Annan Plan for reunification, which Greek Cypriots rejected; the Republic of Cyprus then joined the European Union, with EU law suspended in the north. Negotiations toward a bizonal, bicommunal federation have repeatedly stalled, and recent Turkish Cypriot leadership has advocated a two-state solution. Northern Cyprus depends heavily on Turkey for economic support, defence, and transport links, as direct flights and trade are restricted by the embargo on its ports and airports.

Geography

The territory includes the Kyrenia mountain range along the north coast, part of the Mesaoria plain, and the Karpas Peninsula extending northeast. Major towns include North Nicosia, Famagusta (Gazimağusa), and Kyrenia (Girne).
