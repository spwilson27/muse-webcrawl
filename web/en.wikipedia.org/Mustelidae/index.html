Mustelidae

The Mustelidae (from Latin mustela, weasel) are a diverse family of carnivorous mammals, including weasels, otters, badgers, martens, ferrets, minks, and wolverines. With roughly 65 species in about 23 genera, it is the largest family in the order Carnivora and one of the most ecologically varied, occupying habitats from Arctic tundra to tropical forests and oceans.

Characteristics

Most mustelids have long, slender bodies with short legs, thick fur, and well-developed anal scent glands used for marking territory. Sizes range from the least weasel (as little as 25 g) to the sea otter (up to 45 kg). Many species are solitary, nocturnal hunters of small mammals, fish, or invertebrates; several, such as otters, are highly aquatic.

Classification

The family is divided into subfamilies including Guloninae (martens and wolverines), Helictidinae (ferret-badgers), Ictonychinae (grisons and polecats), Lutrinae (otters), Melinae (Eurasian badgers), Mellivorinae (the honey badger), Mustelinae (weasels, minks, ferrets), and Taxidiinae (the American badger).

Relationship with humans

Several mustelids have been prized for their fur — sable, ermine, and mink — and the domestic ferret, a domesticated European polecat, has been kept for rabbit hunting and as a pet for centuries. Some species, such as the sea otter, were hunted nearly to extinction before receiving protection.
