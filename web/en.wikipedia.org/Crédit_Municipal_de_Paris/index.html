Crédit Municipal de Paris
Founded: 1637 (as Mont-de-Piété)
Headquarters: 55 rue des Francs-Bourgeois, Paris
Type: Municipal pawnbroking and banking institution

The Crédit Municipal de Paris is a French municipal public institution providing pawnbroking loans and banking services. Founded in 1637 as the Mont-de-Piété de Paris — inspired by the Italian monti di pietà that lent against pledges to combat usury — it is the oldest financial institution in Paris, popularly known as "ma tante" ("my aunt").

Services

Its core activity is the prêt sur gage: an immediate loan granted against a deposited object of value — jewellery, watches, artworks, wine or instruments — which is returned when the loan is repaid, or sold at public auction if it is not. The institution also offers savings accounts, microcredit and debt-support services, and holds well-known auctions of unredeemed pledges.

History

Suppressed during the Revolution and re-established under Napoleon in 1804, the Mont-de-Piété was renamed Crédit Municipal in 1918. Its headquarters in the Marais district, on the rue des Francs-Bourgeois, has housed the institution since the 18th century.
