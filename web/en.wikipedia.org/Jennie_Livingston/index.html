Jennie Livingston (born February 24, 1962) is an American film director best known for the 1990 documentary Paris Is Burning, a landmark portrait of the drag ball culture of African-American and Latino gay and transgender communities in New York City during the late 1980s.

Early life and education

Livingston was born in Dallas, Texas, and grew up in Los Angeles. She studied photography and painting at Yale University, graduating in 1983.

Career

Livingston moved to New York and became involved in the downtown arts scene. With funding from public arts grants, she spent several years filming the balls—competitions in which "houses" (chosen families) competed in categories of dance, costume, and "realness". Paris Is Burning won the Grand Jury Prize at the Sundance Film Festival and became a defining document of ball culture, introducing voguing and house culture to wide audiences; in 2016 it was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry. Her later works include the short Who's the Top? (2005) and the documentary Earth Camp One. Livingston has also worked in television, including as a consulting producer on the drama series Pose, which drew on the world her film documented.

Legacy

Paris Is Burning remains a central text in queer cinema and documentary studies, celebrated for its intimacy with its subjects and debated for questions it raises about authorship and representation.
