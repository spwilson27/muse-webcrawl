Tolling (law)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Tolling
FieldCivil procedure, criminal procedure
EffectPauses or extends a limitations period
Related doctrinesEquitable tolling, discovery rule, fraudulent concealment, minority tolling

Tolling is a legal doctrine that pauses or delays the running of a statute of limitations — the period within which a lawsuit or prosecution must be brought. When a limitations period is tolled, the clock stops for the duration of the tolling event and resumes afterwards, so that the claimant's effective deadline is extended by the length of the pause. Tolling may be provided by statute or arise in equity where it would be unfair to let the period run.

Grounds for tolling

Common statutory grounds include the claimant's minority or mental incapacity, the defendant's absence from the jurisdiction or concealment of their whereabouts, imprisonment, and the pendency of another proceeding such as bankruptcy (where an automatic stay applies). The discovery rule, often treated as a form of tolling, delays accrual until the plaintiff discovers, or reasonably should have discovered, the injury and its cause — a rule of particular importance in medical malpractice and toxic-tort cases. Under the doctrine of fraudulent concealment, a defendant who actively hides a wrong cannot benefit from the limitations period expiring during the concealment.

Equitable tolling

In United States federal law, equitable tolling allows courts to extend deadlines where a litigant diligently pursued their rights but an extraordinary circumstance prevented timely filing; the Supreme Court applied the doctrine in cases such as Holland v. Florida (2010), involving gross attorney misconduct in a death-penalty case. Equitable tolling is granted sparingly and is distinct from equitable estoppel, which focuses on the defendant's misconduct inducing the delay. The filing of a class action tolls limitations for class members under the rule of American Pipe & Construction Co. v. Utah (1974) — a principle known as class-action tolling.

Tolling agreements

Parties may also agree privately to toll a limitations period — a tolling agreement or standstill agreement — typically while negotiating a settlement or awaiting the outcome of related proceedings, preserving claims without the cost of immediate litigation.
