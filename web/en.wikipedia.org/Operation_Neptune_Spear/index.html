Operation Neptune Spear was the United States special operations mission that killed Osama bin Laden, the founder and leader of the militant Islamist organization al-Qaeda, at his compound in Abbottabad, Pakistan, on 2 May 2011. The raid, authorized by President Barack Obama, was carried out by operators of the Naval Special Warfare Development Group (DEVGRU, popularly "SEAL Team Six") under CIA direction, ending a nearly decade-long manhunt that began after the September 11 attacks.

Intelligence and planning

The compound was identified after intelligence analysts tracked bin Laden's courier, Abu Ahmed al-Kuwaiti, to a large, unusually secure residence in the garrison town of Abbottabad. The CIA built a mock-up of the compound and operators rehearsed the assault for months, while the administration weighed options including a bombing strike before settling on a helicopter raid.

The raid

Shortly after midnight local time, two modified MH-60 Black Hawk helicopters carried the assault force from Afghanistan into Pakistan. One helicopter crashed on landing inside the compound but its crew and passengers survived and continued the mission; the crashed aircraft was destroyed in place. The SEALs cleared the buildings floor by floor, killing five people in total, including bin Laden, who was shot in an upstairs bedroom. No American personnel were killed. The force seized computers and documents, confirmed bin Laden's identity, and exfiltrated within about 40 minutes.

Aftermath

Bin Laden's body was flown to Afghanistan, identified through DNA comparison, and buried at sea from the aircraft carrier USS Carl Vinson in the North Arabian Sea. News of the killing sparked celebrations in the United States and worldwide reaction. The unilateral raid strained US–Pakistan relations and raised enduring questions about Pakistani awareness of bin Laden's presence.
