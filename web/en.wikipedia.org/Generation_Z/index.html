Generation Z (often shortened to Gen Z), also known as Zoomers, is the demographic cohort succeeding Millennials and preceding Generation Alpha. Researchers and popular media use the mid-to-late 1990s as starting birth years and the early 2010s as ending birth years, with the generation most commonly defined as people born from 1997 to 2012.

Terminology and definition

The name "Generation Z" follows the naming of Generation X and Generation Y (Millennials). The term "Zoomer" is a play on "boomer", combining the slang for a Baby Boomer with the "Z" of the generation's name. Other proposed names have included the iGeneration, Homeland Generation, and Post-Millennials.

Characteristics

Members of Generation Z are generally the children of Generation X and older Millennials. They are the first social generation to have grown up with access to the Internet and portable digital technology from a young age, and have been described as "digital natives".

Compared with previous generations, members of Generation Z in many developed countries tend to be well-behaved, abstentious, and risk-averse. They tend to live more slowly than their predecessors when they were their age, have lower rates of teenage pregnancy, and consume alcohol (but not necessarily other psychoactive drugs) less often.

Economic and political trends

Generation Z came of age during the Great Recession's aftermath, the COVID-19 pandemic, and rapid technological change. Political attitudes vary widely by country, but members of the generation have often expressed concern about climate change, student debt, and the cost of housing.
