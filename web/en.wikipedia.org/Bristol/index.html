Bristol is a city and unitary authority in South West England, straddling the River Avon near its confluence with the Severn Estuary. It received a royal charter in 1155 and was historically a county in its own right. With a population of around 470,000, it is the most populous city in southern England outside London.

History

The name derives from the Old English Brycgstow, "the place at the bridge". John Cabot sailed from Bristol in 1497 on the Matthew and reached North America, one of the earliest European voyages to the continent since the Norse. Bristol grew rich as an Atlantic port trading in wine, tobacco, sugar and — from the late 17th to early 19th centuries — enslaved Africans, a legacy that remains contested in the city; the statue of slave trader Edward Colston was toppled by protesters in June 2020 and thrown into the harbour.

In the 19th century the engineer Isambard Kingdom Brunel built the Great Western Railway to Bristol, the SS Great Western and SS Great Britain steamships, and the Clifton Suspension Bridge across the Avon Gorge (completed after his death in 1864). The port declined as ships grew larger, moving downstream to Avonmouth and Royal Portbury Dock. Bristol became a centre of the aerospace industry — Concorde was built at Filton — and of animation (Aardman Animations) and street art (Banksy is from the city).

Culture

Landmarks include Bristol Cathedral, St Mary Redcliffe church, the harbourside and M Shed museum, and the Clifton Suspension Bridge. The Bristol International Balloon Fiesta is held annually. The city has two Football League clubs, Bristol City and Bristol Rovers, and the University of Bristol and the University of the West of England.
