Anthony McLeod Kennedy (born 23 July 1936) is an American lawyer and jurist who served as an associate justice of the Supreme Court of the United States from 1988 until his retirement in 2018. Nominated by President Ronald Reagan, he was confirmed by the Senate 97–0. For most of his tenure he was the court's median justice and its principal "swing vote" in closely divided cases.

Early life and career

Kennedy was born in Sacramento, California, where his father was an influential lobbyist. He studied at Stanford University and Harvard Law School, practised law in California, and served as a judge on the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit from 1975 until his elevation to the Supreme Court, where he succeeded Lewis F. Powell Jr.

Supreme Court tenure

Kennedy is best known for his opinions on individual liberty and gay rights. He authored the majority opinions in Romer v. Evans (1996), Lawrence v. Texas (2003), United States v. Windsor (2013) and Obergefell v. Hodges (2015), the decision legalising same-sex marriage nationwide, and co-authored the plurality opinion in Planned Parenthood v. Casey (1992) that preserved the core of Roe v. Wade. His jurisprudence emphasised "liberty" and human dignity under the Due Process Clause, while he generally voted with conservatives on federalism, the death penalty and campaign finance (Citizens United).

Retirement

Kennedy announced his retirement on 27 June 2018, effective 31 July 2018. He was succeeded by Brett Kavanaugh. Since retiring he has spoken on civics education and the rule of law.
