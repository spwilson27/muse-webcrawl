Mass wasting

Definition: Downslope movement of rock and soil under gravity
Types: Falls, topples, slides, spreads, flows, creep
Triggers: Rainfall, earthquakes, erosion, excavation, saturation

Mass wasting, or mass movement, is the geomorphic process by which soil, regolith and rock move downslope under the force of gravity, without a transporting medium such as water, wind or ice doing the main work.

Classification

The Varnes classification distinguishes falls, topples, slides (rotational and translational), lateral spreads, flows (debris flows, mudflows, earthflows, avalanches) and creep, by material type and speed – from imperceptible creep to rock avalanches exceeding 100 km/h.

Causes and hazards

Slopes fail when shear stress exceeds shear strength, influenced by slope angle, water content, weathering, vegetation removal and seismic shaking. Landslides cause thousands of deaths annually and major infrastructure damage; hazard mapping, drainage, retaining structures and land-use planning mitigate risk.
