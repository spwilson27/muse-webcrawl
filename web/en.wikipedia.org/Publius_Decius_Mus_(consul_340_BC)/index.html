Publius Decius Mus (died 340 BC), son of Quintus Decius Mus, was a Roman consul in 340 BC, famous for his legendary self-sacrifice (devotio) at the Battle of Veseris during the Latin War, in which he dedicated himself and the enemy army to the gods of the underworld before charging into the Latin ranks to his death.

Career

Decius served as military tribune in 343 BC, where, according to Livy, he saved a Roman army trapped in a defile in Samnium. He was elected consul for 340 BC with Titus Manlius Torquatus as his colleague.

Devotio at Veseris

At the Battle of Veseris (340 BC), after a dream reportedly foretold that the army whose general died would win, Decius performed the devotio ritual recited by the pontifex maximus, veiled his head, and rode into the enemy lines, where he was killed. The Romans went on to win the battle. His son and grandson, both also named Publius Decius Mus, were said to have repeated the sacrifice in 295 BC and 279 BC respectively.
