Dose–response relationship

Dose–response relationship
FieldPharmacology, toxicology
Key parametersEC50, ED50, LD50, Emax, Hill coefficient
Typical formSigmoidal (log-dose vs. response)

The dose–response relationship describes the magnitude of the response of an organism to a stimulus — usually a drug, chemical, or radiation — as a function of exposure (dose) after a certain exposure time. When response is plotted against the logarithm of dose, the curve is typically sigmoidal (S-shaped), characterized by a threshold, a steep linear portion, and a plateau at the maximal effect (Emax).

E = Emax · Cn / (EC50n + Cn)

Key measures

The EC50 is the concentration producing half the maximal effect; the ED50, TD50, and LD50 are the doses effective, toxic, or lethal in 50 percent of a population, respectively. The ratio of toxic to effective dose defines the therapeutic index. The principle that "the dose makes the poison" is attributed to Paracelsus. Dose–response assessment is a foundation of drug development and of regulatory toxicology, including the derivation of safety limits such as the NOAEL (no-observed-adverse-effect level). Complications include hormesis — low-dose stimulation with high-dose inhibition — and non-monotonic responses seen with some endocrine disruptors.
