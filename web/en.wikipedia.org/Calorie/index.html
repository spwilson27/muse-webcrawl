Calorie

The calorie is a unit of energy that originated from the caloric theory of heat. The small calorie or gram calorie (symbol: cal) is defined as the energy needed to increase the temperature of one gram of water by one degree Celsius at a pressure of one atmosphere. The large calorie, food calorie, or kilocalorie (symbol: Cal, kcal) is the energy needed to raise the temperature of one kilogram of water by one degree Celsius, and equals 1,000 small calories.

Definitions and conversions

Because the specific heat of water varies slightly with temperature, several precise definitions exist; the thermochemical calorie is exactly 4.184 joules, making one kilocalorie equal to 4.184 kilojoules. The calorie is not part of the International System of Units (SI), which uses the joule, but food energy labelling in the United States and many other countries still uses the kilocalorie, shown simply as "Calories".

Nutrition

In nutrition, the kilocalorie measures the energy obtainable from food. Approximate energy values are 9 kcal per gram of fat, 4 kcal per gram of protein or carbohydrate, and 7 kcal per gram of alcohol. Daily human energy requirements are commonly around 2,000–2,500 kcal, varying with body size, age, sex and activity level.

History

The term was coined in the 1820s by Nicolas Clément in lectures on heat engines, and the kilocalorie was popularised in nutrition by Wilbur Atwater in the late 19th century.
