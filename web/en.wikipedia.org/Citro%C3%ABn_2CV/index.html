The Citroën 2CV (French: deux chevaux-vapeur, "two steam horses", referring to its tax horsepower rating) is a French economy car produced by Citroën from 1948 to 1990. More than 3.8 million were built, along with several hundred thousand of its derivatives such as the Dyane and Méhari.

Conceived before the Second World War under Pierre-Jules Boulanger, the design brief famously called for an "umbrella on four wheels" that could carry four peasants and 50 kg of produce — including a basket of eggs — across a ploughed field without breaking the eggs. Its features included a lightweight body, a small air-cooled flat-twin engine, front-wheel drive, and long-travel interconnected suspension giving a remarkably soft ride.

History

The car was unveiled at the Paris Salon in October 1948, initially to mixed reactions because of its spartan appearance, but it became one of France's most iconic cars and a symbol of minimalist, functional design. Production ended on 27 July 1990 at the Mangualde plant in Portugal.
