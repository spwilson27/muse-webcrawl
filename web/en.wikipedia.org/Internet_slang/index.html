Internet slang

Internet slang (also called Internet shorthand, cyber-slang, netspeak, digispeak or chatspeak) refers to the various kinds of slang and abbreviations used by Internet users in online communication, including chat rooms, forums, instant messaging and social media.

Origins

Much Internet slang originated in the constraints of early online communication: slow connections, message length limits and the effort of typing encouraged abbreviations such as ASL (age/sex/location), BRB (be right back) and LOL (laughing out loud). Bulletin board systems, Usenet and IRC developed their own conventions, and "leetspeak" (1337) deliberately substituted digits and symbols for letters, originally to evade word filters and to mark membership in hacker and gaming subcultures.

Forms

  • Initialisms and acronyms — LOL, OMG, BTW, FYI, IMO/IMHO, TL;DR.
  • Shortenings and vowel dropping — "txt spk" forms such as u for you, pls for please.
  • Emoticons and emoji — typographic faces like :-) and later standardized emoji used to convey tone.
  • Memes and catchphrases — rapidly spreading expressions, formats and hashtags.

Influence

Internet slang has spread offline: abbreviations like "LOL" are spoken aloud, and dictionaries now record many coinages that began online. Linguists study netspeak as a distinct register that combines features of writing and speech, and note that its forms change quickly as platforms and communities evolve.
