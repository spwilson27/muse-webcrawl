Cardiac electrophysiology

Cardiac electrophysiology is the branch of cardiology concerned with the electrical activities of the heart — the generation and conduction of the impulses that coordinate its beating — and with the diagnosis and treatment of rhythm disorders (arrhythmias).

The conduction system

Each heartbeat normally begins in the sinoatrial (SA) node, the heart's natural pacemaker. The impulse spreads across the atria, is delayed at the atrioventricular (AV) node, and then travels rapidly through the bundle of His, bundle branches, and Purkinje fibres to trigger ventricular contraction. This activity is recorded on the surface electrocardiogram (ECG). Abnormalities in impulse formation or conduction produce arrhythmias ranging from benign ectopic beats to life-threatening ventricular fibrillation.

Clinical electrophysiology

In an electrophysiology study, catheters with electrodes are threaded through a vein into the heart to record intracardiac signals and to provoke and map arrhythmias. Once an abnormal pathway or focus is identified, it can often be destroyed by catheter ablation using radiofrequency energy or cryotherapy — a curative treatment for many supraventricular tachycardias and an important option in atrial fibrillation. Electrophysiologists also implant and manage pacemakers, implantable cardioverter-defibrillators (ICDs), and cardiac resynchronisation devices.
