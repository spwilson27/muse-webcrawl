Atlanta Cyclorama


Detail of the Battle of Atlanta painting

The Atlanta Cyclorama is a cylindrical panoramic painting depicting the Battle of Atlanta of July 22, 1864, during the American Civil War. Measuring about 49 feet (15 m) high and 371 feet (113 m) in circumference, it is one of the largest oil paintings in the world and one of only two cycloramas in the United States (the other being the Gettysburg Cyclorama). Since 2019 it has been exhibited at the Atlanta History Center.

History

The painting was created in 1885–86 by a team of German and Austrian artists working for the American Panorama Company in Milwaukee, Wisconsin. Cycloramas were a popular form of immersive entertainment before cinema, and the Atlanta painting toured several cities before being purchased and donated to the city of Atlanta in the 1890s. From 1921 it was displayed in a purpose-built rotunda in Grant Park, beside Zoo Atlanta, where a three-dimensional diorama foreground of plaster figures, trees and earthworks completed the illusion for viewers standing on a central platform.

Restoration and move

In the 2010s the city of Atlanta arranged the painting's long-term loan to the Atlanta History Center. Conservators undertook an extensive restoration, including repairing a large section that had been cut away in the 1920s. A new purpose-built gallery, the Lloyd and Mary Ann Whitaker Cyclorama Building, opened in February 2019, where the restored painting is presented in its full cylindrical form for the first time in decades. The exhibition also addresses the painting's later life as an artefact of the "Lost Cause" interpretation of the Civil War.
