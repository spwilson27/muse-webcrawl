Spanish Armada


The Armada and English fireships, 1588

The Spanish Armada (Spanish: Grande y Felicísima Armada, "Great and Most Fortunate Navy") was a Spanish fleet of 130 ships that sailed from Lisbon in late May 1588 under the command of the Duke of Medina Sidonia, with the purpose of escorting an army from Flanders to invade England. The aim was to overthrow Queen Elizabeth I and Protestantism in England, stop English support for the Dutch Republic, and halt attacks by English privateers on Spanish interests in the Americas.

The campaign

The Armada reached the English Channel in July 1588 and fought a running series of engagements with the English fleet, commanded by Lord Howard of Effingham with Francis Drake and John Hawkins among his subordinates. The English, with more manoeuvrable ships and longer-ranged guns, harassed the Spanish formation but could not break it. The Armada anchored off Calais to await the Duke of Parma's invasion army, which was blockaded in port by Dutch flyboats.

On the night of 7–8 August, the English sent eight fireships into the anchorage, forcing the Spanish to cut their cables and scatter. In the ensuing Battle of Gravelines, the English inflicted heavy damage, and the wind drove the Armada into the North Sea, making a return through the Channel impossible. Medina Sidonia attempted to return to Spain by sailing around Scotland and Ireland, but autumn storms wrecked many ships on the Irish coast; perhaps a third of the fleet never returned, and thousands of men drowned or were killed ashore.

Significance

The defeat was a major propaganda triumph for Elizabethan England and a blow to Spanish prestige, though Spain rebuilt its navy and the Anglo-Spanish War continued until 1604. Revisionist historians note that the Armada's failure owed as much to weather, logistics, and the impossibility of coordinating with Parma as to English gunnery.
