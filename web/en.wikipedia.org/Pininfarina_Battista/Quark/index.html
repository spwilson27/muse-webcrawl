Quark

FlavoursUp, down, charm, strange, top, bottom
Charge+2/3 e or −1/3 e
Spin1/2

A quark is a type of elementary particle and a fundamental constituent of matter. Quarks combine to form composite particles called hadrons, the most stable of which are protons and neutrons, the components of atomic nuclei. All commonly observable matter is composed of up quarks, down quarks and electrons.

Properties

There are six types, or flavours, of quarks: up, down, charm, strange, top and bottom. Quarks carry fractional electric charge and colour charge, and are subject to all four fundamental interactions. Because of colour confinement, quarks are never observed directly in isolation; they are always found within hadrons, such as baryons (three quarks) and mesons (a quark and an antiquark).

History

The quark model was proposed independently by Murray Gell-Mann and George Zweig in 1964. Gell-Mann borrowed the word "quark" from James Joyce's novel Finnegans Wake ("Three quarks for Muster Mark!"). Deep inelastic scattering experiments at SLAC in the late 1960s provided the first direct evidence for quarks, and the top quark completed the set in 1995 at Fermilab.
