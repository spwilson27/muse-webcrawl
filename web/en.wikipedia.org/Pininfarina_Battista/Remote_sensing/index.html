Remote sensing

Remote sensing is the acquisition of information about an object or phenomenon without making physical contact with the object, in contrast to in-situ or on-site observation. The term is generally applied to Earth observation using sensors on aircraft or satellites.

Techniques

Remote sensing instruments are either passive, detecting natural radiation emitted or reflected by the target (such as optical and thermal imagers), or active, emitting energy and measuring the return (such as radar and lidar). Multispectral and hyperspectral sensors record reflected light in many wavelength bands, allowing land cover, vegetation health, water quality and mineralogy to be inferred from spectral signatures.

Applications

Applications include mapping and land-use planning, monitoring deforestation and crop condition, weather forecasting, oceanography, archaeology and disaster assessment. Remote sensing data are usually processed within geographic information systems (GIS).
