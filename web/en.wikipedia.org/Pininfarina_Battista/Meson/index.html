Meson

CompositionOne quark and one antiquark
StatisticsBosonic (integer spin)
ExamplesPion, kaon, eta, J/ψ, B meson

In particle physics, mesons are hadronic subatomic particles composed of an equal number of quarks and antiquarks, usually one of each, bound together by strong interactions. As bosons, mesons have integer spin. All known mesons are unstable.

Classification

Mesons are classified into families according to their quark content: pions contain up and down quarks; kaons contain a strange quark; charmed and bottom mesons contain charm or bottom quarks. The quark model organises mesons into nonets by spin, parity and flavour.

Role in physics

Mesons mediate the residual strong force between nucleons and are copiously produced in particle accelerators and in cosmic ray collisions. The study of B-meson decays at B-factories has been central to understanding CP violation.
