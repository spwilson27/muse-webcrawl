Chiral symmetry breaking

In particle physics, chiral symmetry breaking generally refers to the dynamical breaking of a chiral symmetry of a quantum field theory. The classic example is quantum chromodynamics (QCD), where the approximate chiral symmetry of the light quarks is spontaneously broken by the formation of a quark condensate in the vacuum.

Consequences

Spontaneous chiral symmetry breaking makes the pions light pseudo-Nambu–Goldstone bosons, explaining why the pion is far lighter than other hadrons. It also generates most of the mass of nucleons: the bulk of the mass of ordinary matter arises not from the Higgs mechanism but from QCD binding energy and the quark condensate.

Explicit breaking

The small nonzero masses of the up and down quarks break chiral symmetry explicitly, giving the pion its small but finite mass. The interplay of spontaneous and explicit breaking is described quantitatively by chiral perturbation theory.
