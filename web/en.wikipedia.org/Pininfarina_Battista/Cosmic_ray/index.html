Cosmic ray

Cosmic rays or astroparticles are high-energy particles, mainly originating outside the Solar System, that travel through space at nearly the speed of light. They consist primarily of protons and atomic nuclei, together with electrons, and arrive at Earth from all directions.

Composition and energy

About 89% of cosmic rays are protons, roughly 10% are helium nuclei (alpha particles), and about 1% are the nuclei of heavier elements, with a small component of electrons and positrons. Their energies span an enormous range, from about 10⁷ eV up to more than 10²⁰ eV for the rare ultra-high-energy cosmic rays.

Effects

When cosmic rays strike the atmosphere they produce showers of secondary particles, including muons that reach the ground. Cosmic rays are a source of background radiation, can cause errors in electronics, and contribute to the production of isotopes such as carbon-14 used in radiocarbon dating. They were discovered by Victor Hess in balloon experiments in 1912, for which he received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1936.
