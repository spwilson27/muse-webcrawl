Planet Labs PBC, commonly known as Planet, is an American Earth-imaging company based in San Francisco, California. Founded in 2010 by former NASA scientists Will Marshall, Robbie Schingler and Chris Boshuizen, it operates the largest fleet of Earth observation satellites in history—hundreds of small "Dove" CubeSats that photograph the entire landmass of the Earth every day.

Satellites and data

Planet's Dove satellites are built to the 3U CubeSat standard using consumer-grade electronics, allowing them to be manufactured and launched cheaply in "flocks". Their daily global imagery, at roughly 3–4 metres resolution, is complemented by a smaller number of SkySat satellites providing higher-resolution (about 0.5–0.7 m) imagery and video of specific sites. The company's premise—that daily imaging of the whole planet makes change visible and actionable—serves customers in agriculture, forestry, mapping, finance, journalism and defence, as well as researchers monitoring deforestation and climate.

History

The company's first Doves were launched in 2013–14, and a celebrated 2017 launch placed a record 88 satellites on a single Indian PSLV rocket. Planet acquired Google's Terra Bella (SkySat) business in 2017 and the German company BlackBridge (RapidEye) in 2015. Planet became a public benefit corporation and listed on the New York Stock Exchange in December 2021 through a merger with a special-purpose acquisition company. Its open-data and disaster-response programmes have made its imagery a frequent source for news coverage of conflicts, natural disasters and environmental change.
