CubeSat

TypeMiniaturised satellite standard
Unit size10 × 10 × 10 cm (1U)
DevelopedCalifornia Polytechnic State University and Stanford University, 1999

A CubeSat is a class of small satellites built to a standard form factor based on 10 cm cubes. One unit (1U) CubeSat has a volume of one litre and a mass of no more than about 2 kg; larger CubeSats are multiples of the unit, such as 3U, 6U and 12U configurations.

History and use

The standard was proposed in 1999 by Jordi Puig-Suari of Cal Poly and Bob Twiggs of Stanford University to give students affordable access to space. CubeSats are launched as secondary payloads and deployed from standardised dispensers such as the P-POD. Since then, thousands have flown, built by universities, space agencies and commercial companies for Earth observation, communications, technology demonstration and scientific research. NASA's MarCO CubeSats even flew to Mars in 2018.
