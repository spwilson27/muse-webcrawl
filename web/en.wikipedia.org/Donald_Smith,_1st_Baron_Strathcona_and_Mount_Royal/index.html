Donald Smith, 1st Baron Strathcona and Mount Royal

Donald Alexander Smith, 1st Baron Strathcona and Mount Royal (1820–1914) was a Scottish-born Canadian businessman, politician, and philanthropist who became one of the wealthiest men in the British Empire. He played a central role in building the Canadian Pacific Railway (CPR) and is famous for driving the Last Spike at Craigellachie, British Columbia, on November 7, 1885.

Career

Smith joined the Hudson's Bay Company in 1838, rising to become chief factor in Labrador and then commissioner of the company's Montreal department. He later served as a member of the Manitoba Legislative Assembly and of the Canadian Parliament, and was Canadian High Commissioner to the United Kingdom from 1896 until his death. He was a major financier of the Canadian Pacific Railway and invested in numerous banks, railways, and mines.

Philanthropy

Smith gave generously to McGill University, where he founded Royal Victoria College, and to hospitals and schools across Canada and Scotland. He was raised to the peerage as Baron Strathcona and Mount Royal in 1897.
