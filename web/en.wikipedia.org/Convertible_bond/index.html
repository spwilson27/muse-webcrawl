Convertible bond

In finance, a convertible bond, convertible note, or convertible debt (or simply convertible) is a type of bond that the holder can convert into a specified number of shares of common stock in the issuing company or cash of equal value. It is a hybrid security with debt- and equity-like features, generally carrying lower interest rates than comparable non-convertible debt because of the value of the conversion option.

TypeHybrid security
ComponentsBond plus equity conversion option
Conversion ratioShares received per bond

Structure

A convertible bond has a face value, a coupon, and a maturity date like an ordinary bond, together with a conversion price: the price per share at which the bond may be exchanged for stock. If the share price rises above the conversion price, holders can profit by converting; if it falls, they can simply hold the bond and receive interest and principal.

Valuation and uses

Convertibles are valued as a combination of a straight bond and a call option on the issuer's shares. Companies issue them to raise capital at lower interest cost and to defer issuing new shares; investors accept lower yields in exchange for downside protection plus equity upside. Variants include mandatory convertibles, which must convert at maturity, and exchangeable bonds, convertible into the shares of a different company. See also bond.
