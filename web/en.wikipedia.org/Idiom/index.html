Idiom

An idiom is a phrase or expression whose meaning cannot be deduced from the literal definitions of its words, but which has a figurative meaning known to native speakers. Examples in English include "kick the bucket" (to die), "raining cats and dogs" (heavy rain), and "break the ice" (to ease social awkwardness).

Characteristics

Idioms are typically fixed in form — changing words or grammar often destroys the figurative sense — and are deeply tied to culture and history. Many derive from obsolete practices, literature, the Bible, seafaring, farming, or sport.

Translation

Idioms rarely translate word-for-word; translators instead seek equivalent idioms or paraphrase the meaning. Idioms are a recognised challenge in language learning and machine translation.

Related figures

Idioms overlap with proverbs (complete sayings offering advice), phrasal verbs, slang, and dead metaphors whose figurative origin has been forgotten.
