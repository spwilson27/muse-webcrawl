Completeness of the real numbers

The real numbers form a continuum with no gaps

Intuitively, completeness implies that there are no "gaps" (in Dedekind's terminology) or "missing points" in the real number line. This contrasts with the rational numbers, whose corresponding number line has many "gaps" at irrational values. Completeness of the real numbers is a fundamental property underpinning real analysis and calculus.

Forms of completeness

Several equivalent formulations exist:

  • Least upper bound property: every nonempty subset of the real numbers that is bounded above has a least upper bound (supremum) in the real numbers.
  • Dedekind completeness: every Dedekind cut of the real numbers is realized by a real number.
  • Cauchy completeness: every Cauchy sequence of real numbers converges to a real number.
  • Nested interval property: every nested sequence of closed intervals whose lengths tend to zero contains exactly one point.
  • Monotone convergence: every bounded monotone sequence converges.

Construction

Completeness can be guaranteed by constructing the reals from the rationals, either by Dedekind cuts, as in Richard Dedekind's 1872 construction, or by completing the rationals under the Cauchy-sequence criterion, as in Georg Cantor's construction. Alternatively, completeness can be taken as an axiom characterizing the real numbers as the unique (up to isomorphism) complete ordered field.

Importance

The intermediate value theorem, the extreme value theorem, and the convergence of bounded monotone sequences all depend on completeness; each fails for the rational numbers. For instance, the set of rationals whose square is less than 2 is bounded above but has no rational least upper bound.
