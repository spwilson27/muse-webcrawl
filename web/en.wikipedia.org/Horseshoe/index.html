Horseshoe

A horseshoe is a fabricated product, normally made of metal, designed to protect a horse's hoof from wear. Shoes are attached on the palmar surface of the hooves, usually nailed through the insensitive hoof wall that is anatomically akin to the human toenail. The fitting and attachment of shoes is performed by a professional called a farrier.

Materials and design

Most horseshoes are made of steel, though aluminium shoes are used for racehorses and some performance horses because of their lighter weight. Shoes vary in shape and features: a groove (the crease) holds the nail heads, and additions such as calkins, studs, or borium improve traction. Rubber and plastic shoes also exist for therapeutic purposes.

Reasons for shoeing

Horses' hooves can wear down faster than they grow, especially on hard or abrasive surfaces and under the added weight of a rider or load. Shoes also correct gait and balance problems, provide grip, and protect injured or diseased hooves. Not all horses need shoes, and the practice of keeping horses barefoot is advocated by some.

Symbolism

The horseshoe is widely regarded as a symbol of good luck. Tradition holds that a horseshoe hung above a door wards off evil; opinions differ on whether it should hang with the ends pointing up (to hold the luck in) or down (to pour luck onto those below).
