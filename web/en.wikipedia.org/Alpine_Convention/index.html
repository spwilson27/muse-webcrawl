Alpine Convention

Signed: 7 November 1991, Salzburg
In force: 1995
Parties: 8 Alpine states and the European Union
Secretariat: Innsbruck and Bolzano

The Alpine Convention is an international treaty between the Alpine countries — Austria, France, Germany, Italy, Liechtenstein, Monaco, Slovenia, and Switzerland — together with the European Union, for the sustainable development and protection of the Alps. Signed in Salzburg in 1991 and in force since 1995, it was the first legally binding treaty for a transnational mountain region.

Framework and protocols

The framework convention is implemented through protocols covering nature conservation, mountain farming, spatial planning, mountain forests, tourism, soil conservation, energy, and transport. The transport protocol commits parties to refrain from building new large-capacity trans-Alpine roads. A Permanent Secretariat, established in 2003, has offices in Innsbruck and Bolzano, and the Alpine Conference meets every two years.

Scope and work

The convention area covers some 190,700 km² and about 14 million inhabitants. Its bodies coordinate work on climate adaptation, biodiversity corridors, and demographic change in mountain regions, working with observer organisations such as CIPRA, whose advocacy helped bring the treaty about.
