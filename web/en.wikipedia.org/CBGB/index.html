CBGB

CBGB was a music club at 315 Bowery in the East Village of Manhattan, New York City. Founded in 1973 by Hilly Kristal, its full name — CBGB & OMFUG — stood for "Country, BlueGrass, and Blues and Other Music For Uplifting Gormandizers." Intended for roots music, it instead became the birthplace and legendary showcase of American punk rock and new wave.

History

Kristal's policy of booking bands that played original material made CBGB the home stage of the Ramones, Television, Patti Smith, Blondie, Talking Heads, and the Dead Boys in the mid-1970s. In the 1980s the club's Sunday hardcore matinees featured Agnostic Front, the Cro-Mags, Murphy's Law, and Youth of Today. Its graffiti-covered walls and famously filthy bathroom became icons of underground culture.

Closure and legacy

After a rent dispute with its landlord, CBGB closed on October 15, 2006, with a final concert by Patti Smith; Kristal died the following year. The space later housed a John Varvatos boutique, which preserved some of the club's decor. CBGB was added to the National Register of Historic Places as part of the Bowery Historic District, and its name lives on in a festival, films, and merchandise worldwide.
