The Cotton Bowl Classic is an American college football bowl game that has been held annually in the Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex since January 1, 1937. The game was played at the Cotton Bowl stadium in Dallas until 2009, when it moved to AT&T Stadium in Arlington.

History

The Cotton Bowl was founded by Dallas oilman J. Curtis Sanford, who financed the first game out of his own pocket. Throughout the twentieth century it was considered one of the four major New Year's bowls alongside the Rose Bowl, Sugar Bowl, and Orange Bowl. It traditionally hosted the champion of the Southwest Conference.

Modern era

Since 2014 the Cotton Bowl has been one of the "New Year's Six" bowl games in the College Football Playoff system, hosting a national semifinal on a rotating basis. Its sponsor since 2021 is Goodyear, making its full name the Goodyear Cotton Bowl Classic.

Notable games

Memorable editions include the 1954 game, in which Rice's Dicky Moegle was tackled off the bench by Alabama's Tommy Lewis, and the 1979 game, where Notre Dame quarterback Joe Montana led a comeback in freezing, windy conditions that became known as the "Chicken Soup Game."
