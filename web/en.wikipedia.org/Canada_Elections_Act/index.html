Canada Elections Act

The Canada Elections Act (French: Loi électorale du Canada) is an Act of the Parliament of Canada which regulates the election of members of parliament to the House of Commons of Canada. It also prohibits MPs from switching parties after an election or from sitting as an independent if they were elected as a member of a party.

Administration

The Act is administered by Elections Canada, an independent, non-partisan agency headed by the Chief Electoral Officer. It sets out the rules governing voter eligibility, the National Register of Electors, nomination of candidates, campaign financing, third-party advertising, and the conduct of the vote itself.

Notable provisions

Among its provisions are limits on election expenses, partial reimbursement of parties' and candidates' expenses, rules requiring voter identification (expanded by the Fair Elections Act of 2014 and partly amended by the Elections Modernization Act of 2018, which restored the voter information card as valid ID and limited the writ period), and offences such as voting more than once or impersonating an election official.
