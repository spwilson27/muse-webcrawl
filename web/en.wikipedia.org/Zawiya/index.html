Zawiya

A zawiya (Arabic: زاوية, romanized: zāwiya, lit. "corner") is a building and institution associated with Sufis in the Islamic world, particularly in the Maghreb. It serves as a lodge, school and religious centre for a Sufi order (tariqa) and is often built around the tomb of the order's founder.

Functions

A zawiya typically combines the functions of a mosque, a madrasa for Qur'anic teaching, a hostel for travelling students and pilgrims, and a centre of charitable distribution. Zawiyas traditionally played an important social and political role in North and West Africa, mediating disputes, providing education and welfare, and organising resistance or accommodation toward central governments and, later, colonial powers.

Regional usage

In the Maghreb the term zawiya is the usual word for a Sufi lodge, corresponding to the khanaqah of the Persian and Turkish worlds and the tekke of the Ottoman Balkans and Anatolia. Famous examples include the zawiyas of Tlemcen, Fez and Tijaniyya centres in Senegal and Morocco. The Libyan city of Zawiya takes its name from such an institution.
