The United States Secret Service (USSS) is a federal law enforcement agency under the Department of Homeland Security with two distinct missions: conducting criminal investigations into financial crimes, and providing protection to current and former national leaders, visiting foreign heads of state, and major presidential candidates.

History

The Secret Service was created on 5 July 1865 within the Department of the Treasury to combat widespread counterfeiting of U.S. currency after the Civil War — by some estimates, one-third of currency in circulation was counterfeit. Its first chief was William P. Wood.

Following the assassination of President William McKinley in 1901, Congress requested that the Secret Service protect the president. Protection was extended over time to vice presidents, former presidents, presidential candidates, and visiting heads of state; a separate Uniformed Division (originating as the White House Police in 1922) guards the White House complex and foreign missions in Washington. In 2003, the agency was transferred from the Treasury Department to the new Department of Homeland Security, though proposals to return it to Treasury have periodically been debated.

Protective mission

The Secret Service protects the president, vice president, former presidents and their spouses, major candidates, and others designated by law or presidential directive, and secures National Special Security Events such as inaugurations, party conventions, and UN General Assembly sessions. Protective operations involve advance work, intelligence assessment, magnetometer screening, counter-sniper and counter-assault teams, and coordination with the military.

Investigative mission

The agency investigates counterfeiting of U.S. currency and securities, financial institution fraud, identity theft, computer and telecommunications fraud, and cybercrimes affecting financial infrastructure. It operates Electronic Crimes Task Forces and Financial Crimes Task Forces in field offices across the United States and abroad.

In popular culture

Agents protecting the president are famously depicted wearing sunglasses and earpieces, with the agency's vehicles — including the presidential state car, "The Beast" — managed in cooperation with the White House Military Office.
