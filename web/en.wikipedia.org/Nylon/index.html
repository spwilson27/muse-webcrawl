Nylon

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Nylon stockings, the fibre's famous first product

Nylon is a family of synthetic polymers with amide backbones, usually linking aliphatic or semi-aromatic groups. It is a thermoplastic silky material, generally made from petroleum, that can be melt-processed into fibres, films, or shapes. Nylon was the first commercially successful synthetic thermoplastic polymer.

History

Nylon was developed at DuPont by a research team headed by Wallace Carothers. Nylon 66 was first synthesised on 28 February 1935. DuPont announced the new fibre in 1938, and nylon stockings went on sale in the United States in May 1940, selling out within hours; during the Second World War production was diverted to parachutes, ropes, and other military uses.

Types and uses

The commonest variants are nylon 6 and nylon 66, named for the number of carbon atoms in their monomers. Nylons are strong, elastic, abrasion-resistant, and lustrous, and are easily dyed. Applications include textiles and hosiery, carpets, fishing line, toothbrush bristles, gears and machine parts, guitar strings, and engineering plastics; aramids such as Kevlar are chemically related polyamides.

Chemistry

Nylons form by condensation polymerisation: nylon 66 from hexamethylenediamine and adipic acid, nylon 6 from the ring-opening polymerisation of caprolactam. Hydrogen bonding between amide groups gives the material its strength and relatively high melting point.
