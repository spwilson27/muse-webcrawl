Lubyanka Building

The Lubyanka Building stands on Lubyanka Square in central Moscow and is the headquarters of the Russian Federal Security Service (FSB). Built in 1897–98 in a neo-baroque style as the offices of the Rossiya insurance company, it was seized after the 1917 Revolution.

Soviet era

Under the Soviet Union the building housed the headquarters and a notorious prison of the successive secret police organisations — the Cheka, GPU, NKVD and KGB. "Lubyanka" became a byword for political repression; many prisoners were interrogated, tortured or executed in its basement during the Great Terror. A statue of Cheka founder Felix Dzerzhinsky stood in the square until it was toppled in 1991.

Today

The building remains the FSB's headquarters and contains a museum of the security services. The Solovetsky Stone memorial to victims of repression stands nearby.
