Groundhog Day is a 1993 American fantasy romantic comedy-drama film directed by Harold Ramis from a screenplay by Danny Rubin and Ramis. It stars Bill Murray as Phil Connors, a cynical television weatherman who, while covering the annual Groundhog Day ceremony in Punxsutawney, Pennsylvania, becomes trapped in a time loop, reliving February 2 repeatedly until he changes his ways. Andie MacDowell plays his producer, Rita Hanson.

Plot

Phil initially exploits the loop for pleasure and profit, then despairs, before gradually using his unlimited time to learn skills, help the townspeople and better himself; only after becoming a genuinely generous person does he wake to February 3. Rubin conceived the story exploring how a person might change given infinite time; the length of Phil's loop has been variously estimated by commentators from ten years to millennia.

Production

Though set in Punxsutawney, the film was shot mainly in Woodstock, Illinois. The production marked tensions between Murray and Ramis that ended their long collaboration, though they reconciled near the end of Ramis's life.

Reception and legacy

The film was a box-office success and has grown enormously in stature: it appears on major critics' lists of the greatest films, was selected for the U.S. National Film Registry in 2006, and entered the language — "Groundhog Day" now commonly denotes an endlessly repeated situation. Religious and philosophical commentators have read it through Buddhist, Christian and existentialist lenses. It was adapted into a stage musical in 2016.
