Swabia

Swabia (German: Schwaben) is a cultural, historic and linguistic region in southwestern Germany, roughly corresponding to the medieval Duchy of Swabia. Today it spans much of Baden-Württemberg and the Bavarian administrative region also named Swabia, whose capital is Augsburg.

History

The region takes its name from the Suebi, a Germanic people. The medieval duchy was ruled by the Hohenstaufen dynasty, which also supplied Holy Roman Emperors. After its fragmentation in the 13th century, the area became a patchwork of free imperial cities, counties and ecclesiastical territories.

Culture

Swabian dialects of Alemannic German, dishes such as Maultaschen and Spätzle, and a proverbial reputation for thrift and industriousness define the region's identity. Major cities include Stuttgart, Augsburg, Ulm, Freiburg and Tübingen.
