Judaism is an Abrahamic, monotheistic, and ethnic religion comprising the collective religious, cultural, and legal tradition of the Jewish people. Rooted in the covenant between God and Abraham and the revelation of the Torah to Moses at Mount Sinai, it is among the oldest monotheistic religions, with a history stretching back more than three thousand years to the ancient Near East.

Beliefs and texts

Central tenets include belief in one God, the covenant with Israel, and a life governed by the commandments (mitzvot) of the Torah — the first five books of the Hebrew Bible (Tanakh). Rabbinic tradition, preserved in the Mishnah and Talmud, interprets and expands these laws. Core practices include Sabbath observance from Friday to Saturday sunset, dietary laws (kashrut), prayer, and festivals such as Passover, Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Sukkot, and Hanukkah.

History

Judaism developed from the religion of ancient Israel and Judah, through the Babylonian exile and the Second Temple period, into rabbinic Judaism after the Temple's destruction in 70 CE. The Jewish diaspora spread across Europe, North Africa, and the Middle East, enduring periods of flourishing and of persecution, culminating in the Holocaust, in which six million Jews were murdered. The State of Israel was established in 1948; today the largest Jewish populations are in Israel and the United States.

Movements

Modern Judaism includes Orthodox, Conservative, Reform, and Reconstructionist movements, differing in their approach to Jewish law and modernity, alongside secular and cultural forms of Jewish identity.
