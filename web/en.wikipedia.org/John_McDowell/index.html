John Henry McDowell (born 7 March 1942) is a South African philosopher, formerly a fellow of University College, Oxford, and now a professor at the University of Pittsburgh. Best known for his book Mind and World (1994), he is a prominent figure in contemporary philosophy of mind, epistemology and meta-ethics.

Life and career

McDowell was born in Boksburg, South Africa, educated at the University College of Rhodesia and Nyasaland, and won a Rhodes Scholarship to New College, Oxford. He taught at University College, Oxford, from 1966 to 1986 before moving to Pittsburgh.

Philosophy

In Mind and World, based on his John Locke Lectures, McDowell argues that experience itself has conceptual content, so that the world can directly justify beliefs — a position he calls "minimal empiricism". Influenced by Kant, Wittgenstein, Sellars and Aristotle, he defends a form of naturalised Platonism and "second nature" in ethics and mind.

Selected works

His essay collections Mind, Value, and Reality (1998) and Meaning, Knowledge, and Reality (1998) gather papers on virtue ethics, moral realism, singular thought and rule-following.
