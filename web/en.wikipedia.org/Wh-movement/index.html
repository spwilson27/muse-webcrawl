Wh-movement

Wh-movement
FieldSyntax (generative linguistics)
Named forInterrogative words beginning with "wh-"
PhenomenaQuestion formation, relative clauses, topicalization

Wh-movement (also known as wh-fronting, wh-extraction, or wh-raising) is the formation of sentence constructions involving interrogative words — words that begin with wh- in English, such as who, what, when, where and why — in which an interrogative phrase appears at the front of a clause while being interpreted as if it occupied another position within the sentence. For example, in "What did you see?", what is understood as the object of see, though it appears first.

The phenomenon occurs in question formation, relative clauses ("the book which I read") and certain focusing constructions. Languages differ widely: some, like English, front wh-phrases overtly; others, like Chinese and Japanese, leave them in situ; many use partial movement or multiple fronting.

Theoretical analysis

In generative grammar, wh-movement has been a central case study since the work of Noam Chomsky and Joseph Aoun. Within the principles-and-parameters and minimalist frameworks, the interrogative phrase is analysed as moving (or being copied) from its base-generated position to the specifier of the complementizer phrase (Spec,CP), leaving a trace or copy behind. The distribution of wh-movement led to the discovery of constraints such as Ross's island constraints and Subjacency — environments, such as complex noun phrases and embedded questions, out of which movement is impossible (*"What did John say that Mary bought a picture of?") — evidence that the phenomenon is governed by structural locality conditions rather than meaning alone.
