President of Egypt: 30 June 2012 – 3 July 2013
Born: 8 August 1951, El Adwah, Kingdom of Egypt
Died: 17 June 2019 (aged 67), Cairo
Party: Freedom and Justice Party (Muslim Brotherhood)

Mohamed Mohamed Morsi Eissa al-Ayyat (8 August 1951 – 17 June 2019) was an Egyptian politician and engineer who served as the fifth president of Egypt from June 2012 until his removal by the military in July 2013. A senior figure in the Muslim Brotherhood, he was the country's first democratically elected head of state, chosen in the 2012 presidential election held after the 2011 Egyptian revolution that ousted Hosni Mubarak.

Early life and career

Morsi was born in the Nile Delta village of El Adwah. He studied engineering at Cairo University and earned a PhD in materials science from the University of Southern California in 1982, afterwards teaching in the United States and at Zagazig University in Egypt. He served in the People's Assembly from 2000 to 2005 as an independent aligned with the then-banned Brotherhood, and was imprisoned several times under Mubarak, including during the 2011 uprising.

Presidency

After the Brotherhood's first-choice candidate was disqualified, Morsi ran as its substitute and narrowly defeated Ahmed Shafik in the run-off with about 52 percent of the vote. His year in office was turbulent: he asserted civilian control by retiring senior generals, brokered a Gaza ceasefire in November 2012, but then issued a constitutional declaration placing his decisions beyond judicial review, triggering mass protests. A controversial new constitution was approved by referendum amid deepening polarisation, economic deterioration, and fuel and electricity shortages.

Removal, imprisonment, and death

Following enormous protests on the anniversary of his inauguration, Defence Minister Abdel Fattah el-Sisi removed Morsi on 3 July 2013 in what supporters called a coup d'état. Morsi was detained, and his supporters' sit-ins were violently dispersed. He faced multiple trials and death and life sentences on charges including espionage and prison-breaking, which he and human-rights organisations denounced as politically motivated. On 17 June 2019 he collapsed and died during a court hearing in Cairo.
