Calabi–Yau manifold

Calabi–Yau manifold
FieldAlgebraic geometry, differential geometry, string theory
Named afterEugenio Calabi, Shing-Tung Yau
Key propertyVanishing first Chern class; Ricci-flat Kähler metric

A Calabi–Yau manifold, also known as a Calabi–Yau space, is a particular type of manifold which has properties, such as Ricci flatness, yielding applications in theoretical physics. Particularly in superstring theory, the extra dimensions of spacetime are sometimes conjectured to take the form of a six-dimensional Calabi–Yau manifold, which led to the idea of mirror symmetry.

Definitions

There are several inequivalent definitions in use. A common one describes a Calabi–Yau manifold as a compact Kähler manifold with a vanishing first Chern class. By a theorem of Shing-Tung Yau, who proved the Calabi conjecture in the 1970s, such a manifold admits a Ricci-flat Kähler metric.

Applications in string theory

In string theory, Calabi–Yau manifolds are used to compactify the six extra spatial dimensions. The topology of the chosen manifold determines features of the resulting four-dimensional physics, such as the number of generations of elementary particles.

Mirror symmetry

Mirror symmetry pairs a Calabi–Yau manifold with a "mirror" partner whose Hodge numbers are interchanged. This duality, first noticed around 1990, has produced striking results in enumerative geometry, including predictions of numbers of rational curves on quintic threefolds.
