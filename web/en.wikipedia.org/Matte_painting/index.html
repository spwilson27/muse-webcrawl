Matte painting

A matte painting is a painted representation of a landscape, set, or distant location that allows filmmakers to create the illusion of an environment that is not present at the filming location. Historically, matte painters and film technicians used various techniques to combine a matte-painted image with live-action footage, traditionally painting on large sheets of glass.

Traditional technique

In the classic glass-shot method, artists painted scenery on glass panels, leaving areas blank (the matte) through which the live action was photographed, or combined the painting and footage optically. The technique allowed vast castles, cityscapes and alien worlds to appear behind actors without costly construction or travel. Famous uses include the Emerald City in The Wizard of Oz, Xanadu in Citizen Kane, and the warehouse finale of Raiders of the Lost Ark, painted by Michael Pangrazio.

Digital era

From the 1990s, traditional matte painting was largely replaced by digital matte painting: artists use photo manipulation, 3D modelling and digital painting software, and environments can move in three dimensions through camera projection. One of the last major traditional paintings was made for Titanic (1997). Today matte painting survives as a department within visual effects studios, creating photorealistic environments for film, television and games.
