Pentecostalism

Pentecostalism is a Protestant Charismatic Christian movement that emphasizes direct personal experience of God through baptism with the Holy Spirit. The term is derived from Pentecost, the event described in the Acts of the Apostles when the Holy Spirit descended on the followers of Jesus, who began speaking in tongues.

Beliefs and practices

Pentecostals believe that faith must be powerfully experiential, emphasizing spiritual gifts including speaking in tongues (glossolalia), divine healing, and prophecy. Worship is often energetic, with expressive music, prayer, and testimony.

History

The modern movement began in the early 20th century, associated with Charles Parham's Topeka revival (1901) and William J. Seymour's Azusa Street Revival in Los Angeles (1906). Major denominations include the Assemblies of God, founded in 1914.

Growth

Pentecostalism has grown rapidly, especially in Latin America, Africa, and Asia, and Pentecostals and Charismatics together number hundreds of millions worldwide, making it one of the largest movements in global Christianity.
