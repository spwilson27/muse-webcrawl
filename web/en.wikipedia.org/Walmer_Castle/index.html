Walmer Castle is an artillery fort originally constructed by Henry VIII in Walmer, Kent, between 1539 and 1540. It formed part of the King's Device programme to protect against invasion from France and the Holy Roman Empire, and defended the strategically important Downs anchorage off the English coast. Comprising a keep and four circular bastions, the moated castle covered 0.61 hectares (1.5 acres) and had 39 positions for artillery. It cost the Crown a total of £27,092 to build the three castles of Walmer, Deal, and Sandown, which lay adjacent to one another along the coast.

History

Walmer Castle saw little action until the English Civil War in the 1640s, when it was held for Parliament and besieged by Royalist forces. From the 18th century, Walmer became the official residence of the Lord Warden of the Cinque Ports and was gradually transformed from a military fortification into a private house. Various prime ministers and politicians were appointed Lord Warden, including William Pitt the Younger, the Duke of Wellington, Lord Palmerston, and Winston Churchill.

The Duke of Wellington lived at the castle for 27 years and died there on 14 September 1852; the chair in which he died and other memorabilia are preserved in the castle. Queen Victoria was a regular visitor. The castle's gardens include a broadwalk created for Lady Hester Stanhope and extensive grounds maintained by English Heritage.

Architecture

The castle retains its original Tudor plan of a central keep surrounded by four rounded bastions, protected by a moat. The interiors largely reflect the domestic conversions of the 18th and 19th centuries, including the apartments of the Lord Warden.
