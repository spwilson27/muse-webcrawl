Surat (Gujarati: સુરત) is a city in the western Indian state of Gujarat. Located on the banks of the Tapti River, it is the second-most populous city in the state and one of the fastest-growing cities in India. The city is known for its diamond cutting and polishing industry — a large majority of the world's rough diamonds are cut and polished in Surat — and for its textile manufacturing, particularly synthetic fabrics, which have earned it the titles "Diamond City" and "Textile City of India".

History

Surat rose to prominence in the sixteenth century as a major port of the Mughal Empire and the principal embarkation point for pilgrims travelling to Mecca, earning it the name Bab al-Makkah, "the Gate of Mecca". In 1612, the English East India Company established its first factory in India at Surat, and the Dutch and French also maintained trading posts there. During the seventeenth century it was among the richest ports of Asia, exporting cotton textiles, silk, and spices.

The city's importance declined in the eighteenth century as the harbour silted and trade shifted to Bombay, and it was ceded to the British in 1800. In the twentieth century Surat revived as a centre of textile mills, and after the 1970s its diamond industry expanded rapidly, drawing migrant labour from across India. The city was severely affected by flooding in 1994, followed by an outbreak of pneumonic plague that attracted worldwide attention and prompted major reforms in municipal sanitation; Surat has since regularly ranked among India's cleanest cities.

Geography and climate

Surat lies on the left bank of the Tapti River, about 22 kilometres from its mouth in the Gulf of Khambhat of the Arabian Sea. The city has a tropical savanna climate with a monsoon season from June to September that brings heavy rain and occasional flooding of the Tapti, controlled in part by the Ukai Dam upstream.

Economy

The diamond industry dominates Surat's economy: thousands of cutting and polishing units process the greater part of the world's supply of diamonds by weight and value. The textile sector, centred on markets such as Ring Road and mills producing synthetic sarees and dress materials, supplies markets across India and abroad. The nearby industrial area of Hazira hosts major petrochemical, steel, fertiliser, and liquefied natural gas facilities, along with a major port. Surat has also been recognised under India's Smart Cities Mission and for its solar and urban-governance initiatives.

Transport and demographics

Surat is connected by national highways and by rail on the Mumbai–Ahmedabad corridor; the Mumbai–Ahmedabad high-speed rail line is planned to stop at Surat. The city is served by Surat International Airport. According to the 2011 census, the city population was about 4.47 million, predominantly Gujarati-speaking Hindus, with significant Jain, Muslim, and migrant communities.
