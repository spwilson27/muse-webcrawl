Assured tenancy

An assured tenancy is a type of residential tenancy in England and Wales created by the Housing Act 1988, which governs most tenancies granted by private landlords and housing associations since 15 January 1989. Its defining feature is security of tenure: the tenancy does not end merely because a fixed term expires or because the landlord serves a simple notice to quit. Instead, the landlord may recover possession only by obtaining a court order on one of the statutory grounds for possession set out in Schedule 2 to the 1988 Act, which include both mandatory grounds (such as two months' rent arrears under Ground 8) and discretionary grounds (such as other breaches of tenancy or suitable alternative accommodation being offered).

Assured shorthold tenancies

The Housing Act 1996 made the assured shorthold tenancy (AST) the default form of assured tenancy for new lettings from 28 February 1997. An AST gives the tenant much weaker security: after any initial fixed term (commonly six or twelve months), the landlord may seek possession by serving a notice under section 21 of the 1988 Act — the so-called "no-fault" eviction procedure — without proving any ground, subject to procedural requirements and deposit-protection rules. Because of this, most private-sector lettings in England are ASTs, while fully assured (non-shorthold) tenancies are now granted mainly by housing associations and some older private landlords.

Other features

Assured tenants have the right to refer excessive rents to a tribunal (the First-tier Tribunal) for assessment against market rents, the right to live in the property without interference ("quiet enjoyment"), and protection from unlawful eviction and harassment under the Protection from Eviction Act 1977. Certain lettings are excluded from the assured regime altogether, including tenancies at very low or very high rents, student lettings by specified institutions, holiday lets, and tenancies where the landlord lives in the same building (resident landlord) unless the accommodation is in a purpose-built block of flats.

Reform

Scotland replaced assured tenancies with the open-ended Private Residential Tenancy in 2017, and Wales introduced occupation contracts under the Renting Homes (Wales) Act 2016 in 2022. In England, legislation debated in the 2020s — the Renters' Reform Bill and its successor the Renters' Rights Bill — proposed abolishing section 21 notices and converting assured shorthold tenancies into periodic assured tenancies.
