Armagh Observatory

The Armagh Observatory is an astronomical research institute in Armagh, Northern Ireland. Founded in 1789 by Richard Robinson, Archbishop of Armagh, it is one of the longest continuously operating observatories in the United Kingdom and Ireland. Since 2016 it has formed part of the Armagh Observatory and Planetarium.

History

The observatory was established in 1789 and opened the following year. Its first director was the Reverend James Archibald Hamilton. Over the nineteenth century it became known for star catalogues and meteorological records; the Armagh catalogue of stars, compiled under Ernst Julius Öpik's era of work in the twentieth century, reflected a long tradition of positional astronomy. The observatory holds one of the longest continuous weather records in Ireland, with daily meteorological observations running since 1795.

Research

Modern research at Armagh covers stellar astrophysics, the solar system, and the history of astronomy. The observatory shares grounds with the Armagh Planetarium, founded by director Eric Lindsay, and together they form a major centre for astronomy education and outreach in Ireland.
