Pecking order

Pecking order originally refers to the dominance hierarchy among chickens, in which higher-ranking birds may peck at lower-ranking ones without retaliation. The term, translated from the German Hackordnung, was introduced by the Norwegian zoologist Thorleif Schjelderup-Ebbe in the 1920s. It has since been extended metaphorically to hierarchies in other animal groups and human organizations.

Pecking order
Type: Dominance hierarchy
First described: 1921 (Schjelderup-Ebbe)
Original subject: Domestic chickens

In chickens

Within a flock, hens establish a roughly linear ranking through pecking, threats and avoidance. The hierarchy determines priority of access to food, water and roosting places and tends to reduce ongoing fighting once established.

Wider use

Ethologists apply related concepts of dominance to many social species. In everyday speech, "pecking order" describes any ranked social or corporate hierarchy. In corporate finance, the pecking order theory describes firms' preference for internal financing over debt and equity.
