Durotriges

Maiden Castle, a great hillfort of the Durotriges

The Durotriges were one of the Celtic tribal groups living in Britain before and during the Roman conquest. Their territory corresponds roughly to modern Dorset, with parts of south Wiltshire and Somerset; their name survives in "Dorchester" (Durnovaria) and in the county name Dorset.

Territory and settlements

The Durotriges controlled a land of chalk downland defended by some of the most impressive hillforts in Britain, including Maiden Castle and Hod Hill. Their coastal trade is shown by the port at Hengistbury Head, which imported wine and pottery from Armorica (Brittany). Unlike many British tribes they appear to have been a loose confederation of smaller groups rather than a centralised kingdom, and they issued their own coinage.

Roman conquest

The tribe resisted the Roman invasion of AD 43: the Second Legion Augusta under the future emperor Vespasian campaigned through their territory, storming hillforts — excavations at Maiden Castle famously uncovered a "war cemetery" of defenders from this era. Under Roman rule the civitas of the Durotriges was administered from Durnovaria (Dorchester) and a second centre at Lindinis (Ilchester).

Burial customs

The Durotriges are archaeologically distinctive for their inhumation burials, often crouched bodies in oval pits accompanied by pottery, joints of meat and personal items — a rite different from the cremation burials common elsewhere in southern Britain.
