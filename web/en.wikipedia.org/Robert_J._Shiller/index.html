Robert James Shiller (born March 29, 1946) is an American economist, academic and author. He is Sterling Professor of Economics at Yale University and a fellow at the Yale School of Management's International Center for Finance. Shiller shared the 2013 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences with Eugene Fama and Lars Peter Hansen "for their empirical analysis of asset prices".

Research

Shiller's early work showed that stock prices fluctuate far more than subsequent dividends can justify — the "excess volatility" puzzle — challenging the efficient-market hypothesis and helping to found behavioural finance. With Karl Case he developed the Case–Shiller index of U.S. house prices, and his cyclically adjusted price-to-earnings ratio (CAPE) became a widely followed valuation gauge. In his bestseller Irrational Exuberance (2000) he warned of the dot-com bubble, and the updated 2005 edition warned of a housing bubble; both calls proved prescient. With George Akerlof he wrote Animal Spirits (2009) and Phishing for Phools (2015), and his 2019 book Narrative Economics explored how popular stories drive economic events.

Career

Shiller received his PhD from MIT in 1972 and has taught at Yale since 1982. He is a co-founder of the research firm MacroMarkets LLC and a long-time columnist; his online course and public lectures helped popularise financial literacy. He has served as vice president of the American Economic Association and president of the Eastern Economic Association.
