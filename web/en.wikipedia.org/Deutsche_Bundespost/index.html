Deutsche Bundespost

The Deutsche Bundespost (German Federal Post) was a German state-run postal and telecommunications authority, established in 1950 in the Federal Republic of Germany (West Germany) as the successor of the Deutsche Reichspost. It provided mail, parcel, telephone, telegraph, and postal banking services, and for decades was one of the largest employers in Germany.

History

The Bundespost operated as a special asset of the federal government administered by the Federal Ministry of Posts and Telecommunications. It held a monopoly on letter mail and telephone services in West Germany. Following German reunification in 1990, it absorbed the Deutsche Post of the GDR.

Division and privatization

The postal reform of 1989 (Postreform I) split the Bundespost into three enterprises: Deutsche Bundespost Postdienst (postal service), Deutsche Bundespost Telekom (telecommunications), and Deutsche Bundespost Postbank (postal bank). With the second postal reform in 1995, these became the joint-stock companies Deutsche Post AG, Deutsche Telekom AG, and Deutsche Postbank AG, which were subsequently privatised. The regulatory functions passed to a federal regulatory authority, now the Bundesnetzagentur.
