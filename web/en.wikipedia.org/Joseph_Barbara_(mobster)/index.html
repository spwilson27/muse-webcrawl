Joseph Mario Barbara (born Giuseppe Maria Barbara; August 9, 1905 – June 17, 1959), also known as "Joe the Barber", was an Italian-American mobster who became a powerful figure in the northeastern Pennsylvania Mafia, aligned with Russell Bufalino's organisation. He is best remembered as the host of the Apalachin meeting.

Life

Born in Sicily, Barbara immigrated to the United States as a teenager and settled in Endicott, New York. Suspected of several murders during the 1930s, he was never convicted, and built a legitimate front as a soft-drink distributor while exercising influence in the Southern Tier of New York. He purchased a 53-acre estate at Apalachin, near Binghamton.

Apalachin meeting

On November 14, 1957, more than sixty leading mafiosi from across the United States gathered at Barbara's estate. Alerted by an observant state trooper, police raided the meeting and detained dozens of men in expensive suits fleeing through the woods. The fiasco publicly confirmed the existence of a national organised-crime syndicate, something FBI director J. Edgar Hoover had long downplayed, and led to Barbara being subpoenaed and indicted. He died of a heart attack in 1959 before the legal consequences fully played out; his estate was later sold and the stone house demolished in 2007.
