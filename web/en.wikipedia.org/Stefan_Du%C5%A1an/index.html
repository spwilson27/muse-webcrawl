Stefan Dušan
c. 1308 – 20 December 1355
King of Serbia 1331–1346
Emperor of the Serbs and Greeks 1346–1355

Stefan Uroš IV Dušan (c. 1308 – 20 December 1355), known as Dušan the Mighty, was the King of Serbia from 8 September 1331 and Emperor of the Serbs, Greeks, and Bulgarians from 16 April 1346 until his death in 1355. He is regarded as the most powerful Serbian medieval ruler, conquering a large part of Southeast Europe and transforming Serbia into an empire.

Reign and conquests

Dušan came to power after deposing his father, Stefan Dečanski, following his victory over Bulgaria at the Battle of Velbazhd (1330). Exploiting the Byzantine civil war of 1341–1347, he conquered Macedonia, Albania, Epirus, Thessaly, and much of Greece. In 1346 he was crowned "Emperor and autocrat of the Serbs and Romans (Greeks)" at Skopje, his capital, and raised the Serbian archbishopric to a patriarchate.

Legacy

In 1349 (expanded in 1354) he promulgated Dušan's Code, one of the most important legal documents of medieval Serbia, regulating feudal society, the church, and the courts. His endowment, the Monastery of the Holy Archangels near Prizren, housed his tomb. Dušan died in 1355, possibly while marching on Constantinople; the empire, divided under his son Stefan Uroš V, fragmented within a generation, but Dušan remains a central figure in Serbian national memory.
