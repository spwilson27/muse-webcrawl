Steyr

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The main square and town hall of Steyr, Austria.

Steyr (German pronunciation: [ˈʃtaɪɐ]) is a city in the Austrian state of Upper Austria, situated at the confluence of the Steyr and Enns rivers. With a population of around 38,000, it is one of Austria's oldest and historically most important industrial towns, closely associated with ironworking and arms manufacture.

History

The settlement grew around the Styraburg, built by the Traungau margraves in the tenth century, and the town gave its name to the March of Styria and ultimately to the modern state of Styria. Steyr prospered as a centre of the iron trade, processing ore from the Erzberg, and became wealthy from the production of knives, scythes and firearms. Josef Werndl's Österreichische Waffenfabriksgesellschaft, founded in 1864, made Steyr famous for rifles such as the Mannlicher models and employed thousands.

Economy

Industrial firms remain central: Steyr Motors makes diesel engines, Steyr-Daimler-Puch's successors produced vehicles and bicycles, and BMW operates a major engine plant in the town. The arms firm Steyr Arms, maker of the Steyr AUG rifle, is based in the region.

Sights

The old town is among the best preserved in Austria, centred on the Stadtplatz with its rococo town hall and the gothic Bummerlhaus. Lamberg Castle and the parish church overlook the rivers.
