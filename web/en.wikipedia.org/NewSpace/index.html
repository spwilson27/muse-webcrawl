NewSpace

NewSpace, also called commercial spaceflight or entrepreneurial space, is a term describing the emergence of the private spaceflight industry and its spaceflight technologies, developed by companies working independently of governments and their traditional contractors.

Characteristics

NewSpace companies typically pursue lower costs through vertical integration, reusable launch vehicles, fixed-price contracts and commercial off-the-shelf technology, in contrast to the cost-plus contracting model of the traditional aerospace industry. Prominent examples include SpaceX, Blue Origin, Rocket Lab and Virgin Galactic, along with hundreds of smaller start-ups building small satellites, launch vehicles and space services.

History

The movement gained momentum in the 2000s with prizes such as the Ansari X Prize (won by SpaceShipOne in 2004) and NASA programmes such as COTS and Commercial Crew, which purchased services from private providers to supply the International Space Station.

Criticism

Critics raise concerns about safety oversight, space debris, the concentration of the industry among a few billionaire-backed firms, and the environmental impact of greatly increased launch rates.
