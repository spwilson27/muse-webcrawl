Kathmandu Valley

The Kathmandu Valley (Nepali: काठमाडौं उपत्यका), also known as the Nepal Valley, is a bowl-shaped valley in the Bagmati Province of central Nepal, containing the cities of Kathmandu, Lalitpur (Patan) and Bhaktapur. Lying at an altitude of about 1,400 metres, it is the cultural and political heart of Nepal and home to several million people.

History

According to legend the valley was once a lake, drained by the bodhisattva Manjushree, who cut the gorge at Chobar. It was ruled successively by the Gopala, Kirata, Licchavi and Malla dynasties. The Malla period (13th–18th centuries) produced the valley's famed Newar architecture — multi-tiered pagoda temples, palace squares and carved wooden windows. In 1768–69 Prithvi Narayan Shah conquered the valley's three kingdoms and made Kathmandu the capital of a unified Nepal.

World Heritage

UNESCO inscribed the Kathmandu Valley as a World Heritage Site in 1979, comprising seven monument zones: the Durbar Squares of Kathmandu, Patan and Bhaktapur, the Hindu temples of Pashupatinath and Changu Narayan, and the Buddhist stupas of Swayambhunath and Boudhanath. Many monuments were damaged by the April 2015 earthquake, and extensive restoration has followed. The valley is also a major centre of both Hinduism and Mahayana/Vajrayana Buddhism, whose festivals — such as Indra Jatra and the chariot procession of Rato Machhindranath — structure the calendar.
