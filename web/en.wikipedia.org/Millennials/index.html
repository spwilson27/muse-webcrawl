Millennials, also known as Generation Y, are the demographic cohort following Generation X and preceding Generation Z. Researchers and popular media generally use the early 1980s as starting birth years and the mid-1990s as ending birth years; the Pew Research Center defines Millennials as those born from 1981 to 1996.

Millennials grew up amid the rise of the internet, mobile devices, and social media, and are often described as the first generation of "digital natives." They came of age during events including the September 11 attacks, the Iraq and Afghanistan wars, and the Great Recession, which shaped their economic circumstances. In many countries they are on average more educated than earlier generations but have faced higher student debt, delayed home ownership, and later marriage and childbearing than their parents.

Characteristics and debates

Commentators attribute to Millennials such traits as civic-mindedness, preference for experiences over possessions, and comfort with diversity, while popular stereotypes — from entitlement to avocado toast — are frequently contested. Demographers caution that exact generational boundaries are imprecise and that within-cohort differences are large.
