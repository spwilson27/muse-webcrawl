Emperor Gaozu of Tang (7 April 566 – 25 June 635), personal name Li Yuan, was the founding emperor of the Tang dynasty of China, reigning from 618 to 626. A high aristocrat and military governor under the Sui dynasty, he seized the capital Chang'an in 617 and proclaimed the Tang in 618, reuniting China after the chaos of the Sui collapse. Much of the military reunification was directed by his second son, Li Shimin, the future Emperor Taizong.

Li Yuan, founder of the Tang dynasty

Background

Li Yuan came from the northwestern military aristocracy and was related by marriage to the Sui imperial house. As Duke of Tang and garrison commander at Taiyuan, he commanded a key frontier post against the Eastern Turks. As rebellions spread across the collapsing Sui empire, Li Yuan raised his banner in 617, allied with the Turks, and marched on the capital.

Founding the Tang

Li Yuan took Chang'an in late 617 and initially installed the boy Yang You as a puppet emperor; in 618, after the Sui emperor Yang was murdered, he took the throne himself. Over the following years, Tang armies — led above all by Li Shimin — defeated rival claimants including Xue Ju, Liu Wuzhou, Wang Shichong, and Dou Jiande, reuniting China by about 624. Gaozu's reign established the dynasty's administrative framework, its law codes, coinage, and land-equalisation policies.

Abdication and death

Rivalry between Crown Prince Li Jiancheng and Li Shimin culminated in the Xuanwu Gate Incident of 626, in which Li Shimin killed his brothers and forced Gaozu to abdicate. Gaozu lived on as retired emperor (Taishang Huang) until his death in 635.
