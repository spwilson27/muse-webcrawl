Food delivery

Food delivery is a courier service in which a restaurant, store, or independent food-delivery company delivers food to a customer. An order is typically made either through a restaurant's or grocer's website or mobile app, or through a third-party food delivery service. The delivered items can include entrées, sides, drinks, desserts, or grocery items, and delivery is usually performed by car, bicycle, or motorbike.

A delivery courier transporting food by bicycle.

History

Prepared-food delivery has ancient precedents, including the thermopolia of Roman cities. Pizza delivery became widespread in the United States after the Second World War, with chains such as Domino's building their business models around fast home delivery and guarantees of delivery within 30 minutes. Telephone ordering dominated until the 2000s, when online ordering emerged.

Online platforms

From the 2010s, third-party platforms—including DoorDash, Uber Eats, Deliveroo, Grubhub, and Meituan—transformed the industry by aggregating many restaurants in a single app, handling payment, and contracting couriers, often as gig workers. Platforms typically charge restaurants commissions of 15–30% per order plus delivery and service fees to customers. The COVID-19 pandemic greatly accelerated adoption as dine-in service closed. Debates around the model include courier employment status, the profitability of platforms, fees' effects on small restaurants, and the rise of "ghost kitchens" that cook solely for delivery.

Meal kits and grocery delivery

Related services include meal-kit delivery, which ships pre-portioned ingredients and recipes, and online grocery delivery, which grew rapidly with services such as Instacart and supermarket-operated fleets.
