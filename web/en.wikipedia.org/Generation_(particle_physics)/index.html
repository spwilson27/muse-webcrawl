Generation (particle physics)

In particle physics, a generation or family is a division of the elementary fermions. Particles of different generations differ only in mass: each generation consists of two quarks, two leptons, and their antiparticles, with the same charges and interactions.

Structure

The first generation comprises the up and down quarks, the electron, and the electron neutrino. The second generation comprises the charm and strange quarks, the muon, and the muon neutrino. The third generation comprises the top and bottom quarks, the tau, and the tau neutrino. Ordinary matter is made almost entirely of first-generation particles, since heavier generations rapidly decay into lighter ones.

Number of generations

Measurements of the Z boson width at LEP show there are exactly three generations with light neutrinos. Why nature has three generations remains an open question; the CP violation needed for baryogenesis requires at least three quark generations (the Kobayashi–Maskawa mechanism).
