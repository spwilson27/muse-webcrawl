Altitude (triangle)

In geometry, an altitude of a triangle is a line segment through a vertex and perpendicular to (forming a right angle with) the line containing the opposite side, called the base. The intersection of the extended base and the altitude is called the foot of the altitude. The length of the altitude is also called the height of the triangle, and the three altitudes of any triangle are concurrent at a single point called the orthocenter.

Properties

In acute triangles all three feet of the altitudes lie on the sides themselves; in obtuse triangles two altitudes fall outside the triangle. The altitude from a vertex in a right triangle to the hypotenuse creates two smaller triangles similar to the original, giving the geometric mean relationships used in the right triangle altitude theorem. Altitudes also figure in formulas for area: the area of a triangle equals one half the product of a base and its corresponding altitude.

Formulas

For a triangle with side lengths a, b, c and semiperimeter s, the altitude to side a is ha = (2/(a))·√(s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c)), by Heron's formula. The three altitudes, three medians, three angle bisectors and three perpendicular bisectors are among the classical "cevians" studied in triangle geometry; the altitudes are related to the circumradius R by ha = b·c/(2R).
