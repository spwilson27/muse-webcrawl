Vector calculus

Vector calculus, or vector analysis, is a branch of mathematics concerned with differentiation and integration of vector fields, primarily in three-dimensional Euclidean space. It provides the language for much of classical physics, including electromagnetism and fluid dynamics.

Basic operations

The fundamental differential operators are the gradient (grad), which turns a scalar field into a vector field pointing in the direction of greatest increase; the divergence (div), which measures a vector field's tendency to originate from or converge into a point; and the curl, which measures its local rotation. The Laplacian combines divergence and gradient and appears in equations for heat, waves, and potentials.

Integral theorems

Line integrals accumulate a field along a curve, and surface integrals over a surface. The great theorems of the subject connect these: the gradient theorem, Stokes' theorem (relating a surface integral of curl to a line integral around the boundary), and the divergence theorem of Gauss (relating the flux through a closed surface to the integral of divergence inside). Maxwell's equations are compactly expressed in this notation.

History

Vector calculus was developed from quaternion analysis by J. Willard Gibbs and Oliver Heaviside in the late 19th century; Gibbs's lecture notes, published as Vector Analysis (1901), established the modern notation.
