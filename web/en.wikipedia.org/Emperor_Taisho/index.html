Yoshihito (31 August 1879 – 25 December 1926), posthumously honoured as Emperor Taishō, was the 123rd emperor of Japan, according to the traditional order of succession, reigning from 1912 until his death in 1926. His reign, the Taishō era, saw the flowering of "Taishō democracy" and Japan's participation in the First World War on the Allied side.

Life

Yoshihito was the surviving son of Emperor Meiji. He suffered from meningitis as an infant, which left him with lifelong health problems and, in later life, apparent neurological impairment. He married Kujō Sadako (Empress Teimei) in 1900; their eldest son was Hirohito, the future Emperor Shōwa.

Reign

Taishō's reign began with a political crisis resolved in favour of party cabinets, and Japan expanded its influence in China and the Pacific during the First World War. The period also saw the Great Kantō earthquake of 1923 and the introduction of universal male suffrage in 1925. As the emperor's health deteriorated, his son Hirohito served as regent (sesshō) from 1921, an unusual arrangement for the modern Japanese monarchy.

Death

Emperor Taishō died of a heart attack in the early hours of 25 December 1926 at the imperial villa in Hayama. He was succeeded by Hirohito, and his era name passed to the reign that followed.
