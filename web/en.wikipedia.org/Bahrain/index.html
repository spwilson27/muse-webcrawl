Bahrain

The Kingdom of Bahrain is an island country in West Asia, situated in the Persian Gulf between Saudi Arabia and Qatar. It is an archipelago of about 50 natural islands and numerous artificial ones, centred on Bahrain Island. The capital and largest city is Manama. With a population of around 1.5 million, Bahrain is one of the smallest countries in Asia, but it has a long history as a trading centre and was the first Gulf state in which oil was discovered.

History

Bahrain is associated with the ancient Dilmun civilisation, a trading partner of Mesopotamia and the Indus Valley. Ruled in succession by Persian, Portuguese (1521–1602), and again Persian powers, the islands came under the Al Khalifa family in 1783. Bahrain became a British protectorate in the 19th century and gained independence on 15 August 1971. Oil was struck in 1932, earlier than in neighbouring states. In 2011 the country experienced mass pro-democracy protests that were suppressed with the assistance of Saudi-led Peninsula Shield forces.

Geography and economy

The mostly low-lying, arid islands are connected to Saudi Arabia by the 25-kilometre King Fahd Causeway. As oil reserves have declined, Bahrain has diversified into banking and finance — Manama is a regional financial hub — as well as aluminium production and tourism. The United States Navy's Fifth Fleet is headquartered in Bahrain.
