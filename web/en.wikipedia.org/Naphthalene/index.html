Naphthalene

Naphthalene
Formula: C10H8
Molar mass: 128.17 g/mol
Melting point: 80.3 °C
Boiling point: 218 °C

Naphthalene is an organic compound with formula C10H8. It is the simplest polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon, consisting of two fused benzene rings, and is a white crystalline solid with a characteristic odour familiar from mothballs. It sublimes readily at room temperature.

Production and uses

Naphthalene is obtained mainly from coal tar and, to a lesser extent, from petroleum reforming. Most of it is converted to phthalic anhydride, a precursor of plasticisers and resins. Other uses include surfactants (naphthalene sulfonates, used as concrete superplasticisers), dyes, carbaryl insecticide, and synthetic tanning agents. It was the traditional active ingredient in mothballs, though it has largely been replaced by paradichlorobenzene and camphor-based products.

Health effects

Exposure can destroy red blood cells (haemolysis), particularly dangerous for people with glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase deficiency and for infants. Naphthalene is classified by IARC as possibly carcinogenic to humans (Group 2B).
