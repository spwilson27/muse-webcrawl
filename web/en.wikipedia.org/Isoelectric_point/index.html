Isoelectric point

The isoelectric point (pI, pH(I), IEP) is the pH at which a molecule carries no net electrical charge. The concept applies chiefly to amino acids, peptides, and proteins, which bear both acidic and basic ionizable groups.

Calculation

For an amino acid with two ionizable groups, the pI is the average of the two relevant pKa values surrounding the zwitterionic form. For molecules with more ionizable groups, the pI corresponds to the average of the pKa values of the forms bounding the species of net zero charge.

Applications

At its isoelectric point a protein has minimum solubility, since the absence of net charge removes electrostatic repulsion between molecules, a property used in isoelectric precipitation and in methods such as isoelectric focusing, which separates proteins by pI in a pH gradient. The isoelectric point also matters in colloid science, where the IEP of a surface determines its zeta potential behaviour.
