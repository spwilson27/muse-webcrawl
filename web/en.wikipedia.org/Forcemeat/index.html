Forcemeat

Forcemeat (derived from the French word farcir, "to stuff") is a mixture of ground, lean meat mixed with fat (grinding the mixture—seasoned—while preserving some texture, producing 5–40% fat content). This mixture is produced by grinding, sieving, or puréeing the ingredients; the finished texture may be smooth or coarse. Forcemeats are used in the production of numerous items comprising charcuterie, such as quenelles, sausages, pâtés, terrines, roulades, and galantines.

Forcemeat is the basis of sausages, pâtés, and terrines.

Types

The four primary types are: straight forcemeat (pork, pork fat, and a dominant meat, ground and seasoned); country-style forcemeat (coarser, often including pork liver and garnishes); gratin forcemeat (in which part of the meat is seared and browned before grinding); and mousseline forcemeat (very light, based on lean white meat, egg white, and cream, used for delicate quenelles). A fifth style, "5/4/3" emulsion forcemeat, is used for fine frankfurter-style sausages.

Ingredients and technique

Salt is essential to extract the protein myosin, which binds the mixture; eggs, cream, and panada (a starch-and-liquid paste) may serve as binders and extenders. Keeping ingredients cold during grinding is crucial to a stable emulsion. Common seasonings include the French quatre épices blend.
