Nisqually people

RegionsSouth Puget Sound, Washington, United States
LanguagesNisqually (Lushootseed dialect), English
Related groupsOther Coast Salish peoples

The Nisqually people are a Native American tribe of the southern Puget Sound region of Washington state. They are a Southern Coast Salish people whose traditional homeland centers on the Nisqually River watershed, from its glacier-fed headwaters on Mount Rainier to its estuary on Puget Sound.

History

The Nisqually lived in villages along the river and practiced a seasonal economy based on salmon fishing, hunting, and gathering. In 1854, Chief Leschi and other leaders signed the Treaty of Medicine Creek with the United States, ceding vast lands in exchange for a small reservation and reserved fishing rights. Dissatisfaction with the reservation's location away from the river contributed to the Puget Sound War of 1855–1856, after which Leschi was tried and executed in 1858; he was exonerated by a historical court in 2004. Much of the reservation was later condemned for the expansion of Fort Lewis (now Joint Base Lewis–McChord).

Modern tribe

The federally recognized Nisqually Indian Tribe governs the Nisqually Reservation in Thurston and Pierce counties. The tribe was a central party in the Boldt decision of 1974, which affirmed treaty fishing rights, and it remains active in salmon habitat restoration on the Nisqually River.
