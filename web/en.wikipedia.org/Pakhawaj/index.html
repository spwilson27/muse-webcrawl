Pakhawaj

ClassificationMembranophone (barrel drum)
OriginIndia
Used inHindustani classical music, dhrupad, Odissi dance
RelatedMridangam, tabla

The pakhawaj is a barrel-shaped, two-headed drum from the Indian subcontinent, a variant of the mridangam. It is the standard percussion instrument of the dhrupad style of Hindustani classical music and is also used in Odissi dance accompaniment and devotional music. The pakhawaj is placed horizontally in front of the player, who strikes both heads with the hands and fingers.

Construction and technique

The drum's shell is traditionally carved from a single block of wood (such as sheesham or neem) and hollowed out. The right head is smaller, made of layers of skin with a central tuning spot (syahi) of iron filings and paste; the larger left head is played with a temporary application of flour-and-water dough to produce a deep bass tone. Tuning is adjusted with wooden blocks and straps.

Tradition

Pakhawaj playing is transmitted through hereditary gharanas, most famously the Nana Panse and Kudau Singh traditions. Its repertoire of rhythmic compositions (parans) is considered the source from which tabla technique partly developed.
