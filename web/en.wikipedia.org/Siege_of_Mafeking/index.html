Siege of Mafeking

The siege of Mafeking was a famous engagement of the Second Boer War, fought from 13 October 1899 to 17 May 1900 in the South African town of Mafeking (now Mahikeng). The British garrison of about 1,500 men, commanded by Colonel Robert Baden-Powell, held out for 217 days against a much larger Boer force.

Baden-Powell's defence relied on bluff, improvised defences, and deception, including dummy minefields and an armoured train. The siege attracted enormous attention in Britain, and its relief on 17 May 1900 sparked wild celebrations — the origin of the verb 'to maffick', meaning to celebrate extravagantly.

The experience led Baden-Powell to write about the usefulness of boys in the defence, who acted as messengers and lookouts, an idea that later contributed to his founding of the Scout Movement in 1907.
