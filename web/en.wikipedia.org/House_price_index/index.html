House price index

A house price index (HPI) measures the price changes of residential housing as a percentage change from some specific start date (which has an HPI value of 100). Methodologies commonly used to calculate an HPI are the hedonic regression, the simple moving average, and the repeat-sales regression.

House price indices track changes in residential property prices over time.

Major indices

In the United States, the best-known indices include the S&P CoreLogic Case–Shiller index, based on repeat sales of single-family homes in major metropolitan areas, and the Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA) HPI, calculated from mortgages purchased or securitised by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac.

In the United Kingdom, widely followed measures include the Nationwide House Price Index and the Halifax House Price Index, both derived from mortgage approvals data, and the official UK House Price Index published from Land Registry data. Eurostat publishes a House Price Index for European Union member states.

Methodology

Repeat-sales methods compare prices of the same property over time, controlling for constant quality but possibly missing changes from renovation. Hedonic methods regress price on property characteristics (size, location, rooms) to separate price change from composition change. Simple average or median measures are cheaper to compute but can be distorted by changes in the mix of properties sold.
