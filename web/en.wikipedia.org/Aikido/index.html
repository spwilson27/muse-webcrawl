Aikido (Japanese: 合気道) is a modern Japanese martial art founded by Morihei Ueshiba in the early 20th century. Drawing principally on Daitō-ryū Aiki-jūjutsu, aikido emphasizes blending with an attacker's motion and redirecting their energy into throws and joint locks, ideally resolving conflict without injuring the attacker.

Philosophy

Ueshiba, deeply influenced by the Ōmoto-kyō religion, developed aikido as a path of peace and reconciliation rather than a mere combat method; he called it "the art of peace". Training is cooperative: partners alternate as uke (attacker/receiver) and nage (thrower), practicing techniques such as iriminage, shihōnage, and kōtegaeshi.

Styles and organization

After Ueshiba's death in 1969, aikido spread worldwide, mainly through the Aikikai foundation headed by his descendants, while offshoots such as Ki Aikido, Yoshinkan, and Shodokan (Tomiki) aikido developed their own emphases. Practitioners typically wear a keikogi and, at advanced grades, the wide pleated trousers called hakama.
