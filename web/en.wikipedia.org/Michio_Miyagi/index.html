Michio Miyagi (Japanese: 宮城道雄, Miyagi Michio; 7 April 1894 – 25 June 1956) was a Japanese koto musician and composer, widely regarded as the father of modern koto music. Blind from childhood, he modernised the repertoire and technique of the koto, bringing it into concert halls in Japan and abroad.

Life

Born in Kobe, Miyagi lost his sight at the age of eight and was apprenticed to a koto master of the Ikuta school, making his debut at eleven. After a period teaching in Korea, he moved to Tokyo in 1917 and came to prominence with new compositions that blended traditional Japanese practice with elements of Western harmony and form, part of the "New Japanese Music" (Shin Nihon Ongaku) movement.

Works and innovations

His most famous composition is Haru no Umi ("The Sea in Spring", 1929) for koto and shakuhachi, inspired by the Inland Sea at Tomonoura; its arrangement for violin and piano, made after his celebrated 1932 performances in Paris with the French violinist Renée Chemet, spread his name internationally. Miyagi composed hundreds of works, wrote essays, and invented new instruments, including the jūshichigen (seventeen-string bass koto), the large kokyū, and a short koto (tanko). He became a professor at the Tokyo University of the Arts.

Miyagi died in 1956 after falling from a moving train while touring. A memorial museum in Tokyo preserves his instruments and manuscripts, and Haru no Umi remains a New Year staple of Japanese broadcasting.
