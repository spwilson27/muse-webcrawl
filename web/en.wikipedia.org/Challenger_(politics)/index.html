Challenger (politics)

In politics, a challenger is a candidate who seeks to unseat an incumbent office-holder in an election. The term is used in contrast to the incumbent, whose existing position, name recognition and fundraising networks usually confer significant advantages, collectively known as the incumbency advantage.

Challenger dynamics

Political scientists distinguish between high-quality challengers — candidates who have previously held elected office and can raise substantial funds — and inexperienced challengers, who rarely win. Challenger success rises when the incumbent is weakened by scandal, poor economic conditions or redistricting, and falls in "safe" districts where primary contests, rather than general elections, are decisive. Strategic politicians often wait for an open seat rather than challenge a strong incumbent, a pattern central to theories of congressional elections in the United States.

In primary elections

A challenger may also arise within the incumbent's own party in a primary election; notable primary challenges to sitting presidents or legislators are often read as signs of party discontent and can damage an incumbent even when they fail.
