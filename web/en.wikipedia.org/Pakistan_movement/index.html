The Pakistan Movement was a political movement in British India in the first half of the twentieth century that aimed to create an independent Muslim state in the north-western and eastern regions of the subcontinent. It achieved its goal with the partition of India and the establishment of the Dominion of Pakistan on 14 August 1947.

Origins

The movement grew out of the Aligarh Movement of Sir Syed Ahmad Khan, who promoted modern education for Indian Muslims and argued that Hindus and Muslims formed two separate nations. The All-India Muslim League was founded in Dhaka in 1906 to safeguard Muslim political interests. Separate electorates for Muslims were conceded in 1909, and the Lucknow Pact of 1916 briefly aligned the League with the Indian National Congress.

The road to partition

Under Muhammad Ali Jinnah, the League was transformed into a mass movement. The philosopher-poet Muhammad Iqbal proposed a separate Muslim state in his 1930 presidential address, and Choudhry Rahmat Ali coined the name "Pakistan" in his 1933 pamphlet Now or Never. The Lahore Resolution of March 1940 formally demanded independent states in the Muslim-majority regions. After sweeping the Muslim seats in the 1946 elections, the League pressed its claim against Congress opposition and British plans; the Mountbatten Plan of June 1947 accepted partition, and Pakistan came into being that August amid mass migration and communal violence that killed hundreds of thousands.
