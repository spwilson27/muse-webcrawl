Dnieper–Carpathian Offensive

Date24 December 1943 – 17 April 1944
LocationRight-bank Ukraine
ResultSoviet victory
BelligerentsSoviet Union vs Nazi Germany, Romania, Hungary

The Dnieper–Carpathian Offensive was a major Soviet offensive operation on the Eastern Front of the Second World War, fought from late December 1943 to mid-April 1944. Conducted by the 1st, 2nd, 3rd, and 4th Ukrainian Fronts, it liberated right-bank Ukraine, destroyed much of the German Army Group South, and carried Soviet forces to the borders of Romania and Poland.

Background

After the Battle of Kursk and the crossing of the Dnieper in autumn 1943, Soviet forces held bridgeheads on the river's western bank. The offensive aimed to exploit these positions, encircle German forces in the Korsun–Shevchenkovsky salient, and drive toward the Southern Bug and Dniester rivers.

Course of the offensive

The operation unfolded in two broad phases. In the first, the Zhitomir–Berdichev and Kirovograd operations expanded the bridgeheads, and in January–February 1944 the Korsun–Shevchenkovsky pocket was destroyed, though part of the encircled force broke out. In the spring phase, despite the rasputitsa mud, Soviet fronts under commanders including Ivan Konev and Rodion Malinovsky advanced to the Dniester, liberated Odessa, and reached the Carpathian foothills and the pre-war Romanian frontier.

Aftermath

The offensive inflicted heavy losses on Army Group South, split it into two parts, and set the stage for the Lvov–Sandomierz and Jassy–Kishinev offensives of summer 1944. It also led to the isolation of German forces in Crimea, retaken in May 1944.
