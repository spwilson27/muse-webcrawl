Copenhagen (Danish: København) is the capital and most populous city of Denmark. Located on the islands of Zealand and Amager at the southern end of the Øresund strait, it faces the Swedish city of Malmö across the water, to which it is connected by the Øresund Bridge. The city is Denmark's cultural, economic, and governmental centre and one of Northern Europe's major financial centres.

Nyhavn, the 17th-century waterfront, canal and entertainment district.

History

Copenhagen originated as a Viking fishing village in the 10th century. In 1167 Bishop Absalon built a castle on the small island of Slotsholmen, and the settlement grew as a trading port — its name derives from Køpmannæhafn, "merchants' harbour". It became Denmark's capital in the early 15th century. The city suffered plague, fires in 1728 and 1795, and British bombardment in 1807 during the Napoleonic Wars, when the Royal Navy seized the Danish fleet.

In the 19th century Copenhagen experienced a golden age of culture, home to Hans Christian Andersen, Søren Kierkegaard, and the sculptor Bertel Thorvaldsen. After German occupation during the Second World War, the post-war Finger Plan guided suburban development along five railway corridors. In recent decades the city has become known for cycling infrastructure, New Nordic cuisine, and ambitious climate goals.

Geography and climate

The city sits on low-lying coastal terrain in eastern Zealand and northern Amager. Copenhagen has an oceanic climate with mild summers and cool winters, influenced by the Gulf Stream and the surrounding sea. The harbour, once industrial, is now clean enough for public swimming baths.

Culture and landmarks

Major attractions include Tivoli Gardens (opened 1843), the Little Mermaid statue (1913) on the Langelinie promenade, the royal palaces of Amalienborg and Christiansborg, Rosenborg Castle, the Nyhavn canal district, and the National Museum. The University of Copenhagen, founded in 1479, is Denmark's oldest university. The city is renowned for Danish design, and restaurants such as Noma helped make it a global culinary destination.

Transport

Copenhagen is famous for bicycle culture: roughly half of all trips to work or school in the municipality are made by bike. Public transport includes the driverless Copenhagen Metro (opened 2002), S-trains, buses, and harbour ferries. Copenhagen Airport at Kastrup is the largest airport in Scandinavia.
