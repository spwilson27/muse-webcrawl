Přemyslid dynasty

CountryBohemia (later also Poland, Hungary)
FounderBořivoj I (legendary Přemysl the Ploughman)
Ruledc. 870 – 1306
Last rulerWenceslaus III

The Přemyslid dynasty (Czech: Přemyslovci) was a Bohemian royal dynasty that ruled in Bohemia and Moravia from the 9th century until 1306, and in Poland and Hungary in the early 14th century. According to legend, the dynasty descends from Přemysl the Ploughman, chosen as husband by the prophetess Libuše, the mythical founder of Prague.

Early dukes

The first historically documented Přemyslid duke was Bořivoj I, baptized in the 880s, who founded Prague Castle. His descendants Christianized Bohemia; Saint Wenceslaus (Duke Václav I, murdered 935 by his brother Boleslaus I) became Bohemia's patron saint. The dukes balanced between the Holy Roman Empire and Poland, and Břetislav I temporarily conquered Moravia and parts of Poland.

Kings of Bohemia

Vratislaus II received a royal crown in 1085, but hereditary kingship came only with Ottokar I in 1198, confirmed by the Golden Bull of Sicily (1212). Under Ottokar II (1253–1278) Bohemia reached its greatest medieval extent, stretching to the Adriatic, until his death at the Battle on the Marchfeld against Rudolf of Habsburg. Wenceslaus II extended Přemyslid rule to Poland (crowned 1300) and secured the Hungarian crown for his son.

Extinction and legacy

The male line ended when the young Wenceslaus III was assassinated at Olomouc on 4 August 1306. Through female lines, the succession passed eventually to the House of Luxembourg, whose John the Blind married the Přemyslid princess Elizabeth. The Přemyslids left a lasting legacy in Bohemian statehood, towns, and saints' cults, including Saint Agnes of Bohemia.
