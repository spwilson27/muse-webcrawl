Douglas Engelbart

Born: January 30, 1925, Portland, Oregon, U.S.
Died: July 2, 2013 (aged 88), Atherton, California, U.S.
Known for: Computer mouse, hypertext, graphical user interface, "The Mother of All Demos"

Douglas Carl Engelbart (January 30, 1925 – July 2, 2013) was an American engineer and inventor, and an early computer and Internet pioneer. He is best known for his work on founding the field of human–computer interaction, particularly while at his Augmentation Research Center Lab in SRI International, which resulted in the creation of the computer mouse, and the development of hypertext, networked computers, and precursors to the graphical user interface. These were demonstrated at "The Mother of All Demos" in 1968.

Early life and career

Engelbart grew up near Portland, Oregon, studied electrical engineering at Oregon State University, and served in the U.S. Navy as a radar technician in World War II. Inspired by Vannevar Bush's article "As We May Think", he resolved to devote his career to using computers to augment human intellect. He earned his doctorate from the University of California, Berkeley in 1955.

Augmentation Research Center

At SRI International, Engelbart founded the Augmentation Research Center (ARC) and developed the oN-Line System (NLS), an early hypertext system with collaborative editing, teleconferencing, and windowing. NLS was demonstrated on December 9, 1968, at the Fall Joint Computer Conference in San Francisco: the presentation showed the mouse, hypertext links, video conferencing, and multiple windows working together — technologies that would take decades to become common. ARC also became the second site on the ARPANET, and Engelbart's group ran its Network Information Center.

Later years and honours

Engelbart received the ACM Turing Award in 1997, the National Medal of Technology in 2000, and the Lemelson-MIT Prize. His vision of "bootstrapping" — using collective intelligence tools to improve the process of improving them — remained influential but, by his own assessment, only partially realised. He died in 2013 at the age of 88.
