Nakba Day

Nakba Day (Arabic: يوم النكبة, Yawm an-Nakba, "Day of the Catastrophe") is observed annually on 15 May by Palestinians to commemorate the displacement that accompanied the creation of the State of Israel in 1948. The day follows Israeli Independence Day (Yom Ha'atzmaut), which is celebrated on or near 14 May according to the Hebrew calendar.

Background

During the 1948 Palestine war, more than 700,000 Palestinian Arabs fled or were expelled from their homes in the territory that became Israel, and hundreds of Palestinian villages were depopulated or destroyed. Palestinians refer to these events as the Nakba ("catastrophe"). Descendants of the refugees, numbering in the millions, remain registered with UNRWA, and the Palestinian claim of a right of return remains a central issue in the Israeli–Palestinian conflict.

Observance

Nakba Day was formally inaugurated by Yasser Arafat in 1998. It is marked by speeches, marches and demonstrations in the West Bank, Gaza Strip, Israel and Palestinian communities abroad, often with marchers carrying large symbolic keys representing the keys of homes left in 1948. Commemorations have at times led to clashes; in 2011, protests at Israel's borders with Syria, Lebanon, Gaza and the West Bank resulted in multiple deaths.
