Irish Republican Brotherhood

Founded: 17 March 1858, Dublin

Dissolved: 1924

Founders: James Stephens, John O'Mahony

Ideology: Irish republicanism

The Irish Republican Brotherhood (IRB; Irish: Bráithreachas Phoblacht na hÉireann) was a secret oath-bound fraternal organisation dedicated to the establishment of an "independent democratic republic" in Ireland between 1858 and 1924. Its counterpart in the United States was initially the Fenian Brotherhood, later Clan na Gael. Members of both wings were commonly known as "Fenians".

History

The IRB was founded on St Patrick's Day 1858 by James Stephens, a veteran of the failed Young Ireland rising of 1848, with American support organised by John O'Mahony. It staged an unsuccessful rising in 1867, and Fenian raids were launched into Canada from the United States. In the late 19th century the organisation declined but was revived in the 1900s by a new generation including Tom Clarke and Seán Mac Diarmada.

Easter Rising and aftermath

The IRB's Supreme Council, acting as a government-in-waiting, planned the Easter Rising of 1916 in alliance with the Irish Volunteers and Irish Citizen Army; its president, Patrick Pearse, read the Proclamation of the Irish Republic. After the Rising the IRB, led by Michael Collins, infiltrated and directed much of the revolutionary movement during the War of Independence. Following the Anglo-Irish Treaty and Civil War, the organisation was wound up in 1924, its remaining members considering its purpose achieved with the establishment of the Irish Free State.
