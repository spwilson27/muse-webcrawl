Money order

A money order is a prepaid payment order for a specified amount of money. Because it is paid for in advance, it is a more trusted method of payment than a personal cheque, which may bounce. Money orders are issued by postal services, banks, and retail agents such as convenience stores.

History

The British Post Office introduced a money order system in the late 18th century, and the United States Postal Service began issuing money orders in 1864, partly to let soldiers send pay home safely during the Civil War.

Use

The purchaser pays the face value plus a fee, receives a paper instrument naming a payee, and the payee can cash or deposit it. International money orders allow remittances between countries, though electronic transfers have displaced much of this traffic.
