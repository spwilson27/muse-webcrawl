Overseas Chinese

Overseas Chinese refers to people of Chinese birth or ethnicity who reside outside the territories administered by the People's Republic of China and the Republic of China (Taiwan). Estimates of their number commonly exceed 40 million, concentrated in Southeast Asia, North America, and Oceania.

History

Chinese emigration has a long history, from maritime traders in Southeast Asia to nineteenth-century labour migration to the Americas and Australasia, often under exclusionary laws such as the United States Chinese Exclusion Act of 1882. Communities maintained ties through remittances, clan associations, and Chinese-language schools and media.

Terminology

Terms include huaqiao (Chinese nationals abroad) and huayi (foreign citizens of Chinese descent), and identities range from full assimilation to strong cultural retention, varying by country and generation.
