Saudi Aramco, officially the Saudi Arabian Oil Company, is the national petroleum and natural gas company of Saudi Arabia. Headquartered in Dhahran, it is one of the largest companies in the world by revenue and the world's largest oil producer, managing the kingdom's proven crude oil reserves of roughly 260 billion barrels and operating the Ghawar field, the largest conventional oil field on Earth.

History

The company began in 1933 with a concession agreement between Saudi Arabia and Standard Oil of California, which formed the California-Arabian Standard Oil Company (CASOC). Commercial oil was discovered at Dammam Well No. 7 in 1938. Renamed the Arabian-American Oil Company (Aramco) in 1944, it was gradually bought out by the Saudi government during the 1970s; full state ownership was achieved by 1980, and in 1988 the enterprise was re-established as the Saudi Arabian Oil Company.

Operations

Aramco produces around 9–10 million barrels of crude oil per day in recent years and operates refineries, petrochemical plants (including through its majority stake in SABIC), gas processing facilities and an international marketing network. In December 2019 the company listed about 1.7% of its shares on the Saudi Tadawul exchange; the offering raised about $25.6 billion — up to $29.4 billion with the greenshoe option — making it the largest initial public offering in history and briefly valuing the company near $2 trillion. The Saudi state and its sovereign wealth fund retain the overwhelming majority of shares.

Challenges and strategy

An attack with drones and cruise missiles on the Abqaiq and Khurais facilities in September 2019 temporarily knocked out about half of Saudi production, underlining the company's geopolitical exposure. Aramco has announced ambitions to expand gas and petrochemicals and to reach net-zero operational emissions by 2050, while maintaining its role as the central revenue source of the Saudi state and a linchpin of the OPEC+ oil-market alliance.
