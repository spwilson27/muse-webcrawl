The LGM-25C Titan II was an intercontinental ballistic missile developed by the Glenn L. Martin Company from the earlier Titan I missile. It carried the W-53 warhead, at 9 megatons the largest ever deployed on a United States ICBM, and served with the United States Air Force from 1963 to 1987.

Design and service

Unlike the cryogenic Titan I, the Titan II used storable hypergolic propellants, allowing it to be launched from inside its silo within about a minute. A total of 54 missiles were deployed at bases in Arizona, Arkansas and Kansas. A space-launch version, the Titan II GLV, launched NASA's Gemini crewed missions in 1965–66, and converted Titan II missiles later flew as satellite launchers until 2003.

Accidents and retirement

The type's toxic propellants caused serious accidents, including a 1978 fuel leak at Rock, Kansas, and the 1980 Damascus, Arkansas explosion, in which a dropped socket punctured a fuel tank and blew the W-53 warhead out of the silo (it did not detonate). The Titan II was retired in the 1980s as part of the modernisation of the ICBM force; one silo complex near Tucson, Arizona survives as the Titan Missile Museum.
