Spinning is a textile process in which fibres are drawn out and twisted together to form yarn or thread. It is one of the oldest technologies of civilisation, practised for at least ten thousand years, and remains the essential first step in producing most cloth.

Hand spinning

The earliest method twists fibres with a hand-held spindle, sometimes weighted by a whorl; a distaff holds the prepared fibre supply. The spinning wheel, developed in Asia and reaching Europe by the later Middle Ages, greatly increased output; the treadle-driven Saxony wheel and the charkha (associated with Gandhi's promotion of homespun khadi in India) are classic forms.

Industrial spinning

Mechanisation during the Industrial Revolution transformed the craft: Hargreaves's spinning jenny (1764) spun many threads at once, Arkwright's water frame produced stronger yarn, and Crompton's spinning mule combined their advantages. Modern mills use ring spinning and open-end (rotor) spinning to produce yarn at high speed.

Process

For cotton, the stages are opening and cleaning, carding, drawing, roving and spinning; wool may be worsted or woollen spun depending on fibre alignment. The amount of twist determines yarn strength and character, and singles may be plied together for balance.
