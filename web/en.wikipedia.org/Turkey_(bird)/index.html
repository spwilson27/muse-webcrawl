Turkey (bird)

A male wild turkey displaying

The turkey is a large bird in the genus Meleagris, native to North America. There are two extant turkey species: the wild turkey (Meleagris gallopavo) of eastern and central North America and the ocellated turkey (Meleagris ocellata) of the Yucatán Peninsula in Mexico. Males of both species have a distinctive fleshy wattle, called a snood, that hangs over the beak.

Characteristics

Turkeys are among the largest birds in their ranges. Males, or toms, have iridescent bronze-green plumage and fan-shaped tails which they spread in courtship displays, accompanied by characteristic "gobbling" calls. Females, or hens, are smaller and duller in colour. Turkeys are omnivorous, foraging on the ground for seeds, nuts, insects and small vertebrates.

Domestic turkey

The domestic turkey descends from the southern Mexican subspecies of wild turkey, domesticated by indigenous peoples of Mesoamerica. Turkeys were taken to Europe in the early 16th century and are now farmed worldwide for their meat. Roast turkey is traditionally eaten at Thanksgiving in the United States and at Christmas in many countries. Commercial broad-breasted white turkeys have been bred for rapid growth and large breast muscles, and typically cannot reproduce naturally.

Name

The English name "turkey" arose from early confusion with the guinea fowl, which reached Europe via Turkish merchants and was called the "turkey-cock"; the name transferred to the similar American bird.
