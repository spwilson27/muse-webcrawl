Sambadrome

A sambadrome (Portuguese: sambódromo) is an exhibition venue for the parades of samba schools during Brazilian Carnival. The most famous is the Sambódromo Marquês de Sapucaí in Rio de Janeiro, designed by architect Oscar Niemeyer and opened in 1984.

Rio de Janeiro

The Rio Sambadrome runs along the Marquês de Sapucaí Avenue in the Santo Cristo district. Its parade strip is 700 metres long, flanked by concrete grandstands seating some 70,000 spectators, and it terminates in Apotheosis Square beneath Niemeyer's signature parabolic arch. Before 1984, the samba schools paraded in temporary structures erected on avenues such as Presidente Vargas.

Each Carnival, the twelve schools of the Special Group parade on Sunday and Monday nights, judged on drumming, song, floats, costumes and choreography; lower divisions parade on other nights. The venue is used year-round as a school and event space, and during the 2016 Summer Olympics it hosted archery and the marathon finish.

Other sambadromes

São Paulo built its own Sambódromo do Anhembi, also designed in part by Niemeyer and opened in 1991. Many Brazilian cities, including Manaus, Florianópolis and Vitória, maintain sambadromes or similar "passarelas do samba" for their carnival parades.
