Battle of Assaye

Battle of Assaye
Date: 23 September 1803
Location: Assaye, Maharashtra, India
Result: British victory
Part of the Second Anglo-Maratha War

The Battle of Assaye was a major battle of the Second Anglo-Maratha War fought on 23 September 1803 near the village of Assaye in western India, between the Maratha Confederacy and the British East India Company. The British and Company force, under the command of Major-General Arthur Wellesley (later the Duke of Wellington), defeated a much larger Maratha army commanded by Colonel Anthony Pohlmann, a Hanoverian formerly in British service.

Background

Wellesley's army marched into Maratha territory in August 1803 and captured Ahmednagar. The Maratha forces of Daulat Rao Scindia and Raghoji II Bhonsle, numbering perhaps 40,000 to 50,000 men with over 100 guns, took up a strong position between the Kaitna and Juah rivers near Assaye. Wellesley had only about 13,500 men, of whom roughly 4,500 were infantry in the line of battle.

The battle

Although heavily outnumbered, Wellesley attacked. His infantry advanced under heavy artillery fire, re-formed under cover of the Kaitna river, and drove the Maratha infantry back in hard fighting. The Maratha gunners fought stubbornly around their cannon, and some feigned death before turning captured guns on the British rear; they were cleared by British cavalry. The Marathas lost around 6,000 killed and wounded and 98 guns, while Wellesley's force suffered roughly 1,600 casualties — about a third of those engaged.

Legacy

Wellesley later described Assaye as the finest of his victories, bloodier in proportion than even Waterloo. The battle broke Maratha power in the Deccan and is commemorated by the Assaye colour and elephant badge still borne by successor regiments.
