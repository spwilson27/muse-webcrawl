Waze (pronounced "ways") is a crowdsourced GPS navigation software application owned by Google. It provides turn-by-turn navigation and real-time traffic information based on reports submitted by its users — including accidents, hazards, road closures, congestion and police presence — supplemented by automatically collected speed and location data from users' phones. Waze also supports community map editing, allowing volunteers to update roads, landmarks and house numbers.

History

The project began in Israel in 2006 as FreeMap Israel, a free digital map built by community contributions, led by Ehud Shabtai; the company (as LinQmap, then Waze Mobile) launched the Waze app around 2008–2009. It grew rapidly by gamifying contributions with points and user ranks. In June 2013 Google acquired Waze for a reported price of about US$966 million to US$1.1 billion, keeping its development team in Israel. Waze continued as a separate app alongside Google Maps, the two sharing data — Waze incident reports appear in Google Maps, while Google provides search and satellite imagery to Waze.

Features and criticism

Beyond navigation, Waze offers planned drives, integration with music and podcast apps, celebrity and custom voices, toll and permit information, and the Waze Carpool service (discontinued in 2022). Its routing has been criticised by residents and officials when it diverts heavy through-traffic onto quiet residential streets, and by police associations for its officer-location alerts. Municipal partnerships under the "Connected Citizens"/Waze for Cities programme exchange closure and incident data with transport authorities.
