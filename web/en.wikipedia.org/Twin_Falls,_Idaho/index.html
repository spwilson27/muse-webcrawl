Twin Falls, Idaho

Twin Falls is a city in southern Idaho and the county seat of Twin Falls County. It is the largest city of Idaho's Magic Valley region, with a population of about 51,800 at the 2020 census. The city takes its name from twin waterfalls on the nearby Snake River.

History and economy

Twin Falls was founded in 1904 after irrigation projects of the Carey Act, promoted by I. B. Perrine, brought water to the arid Snake River Plain, becoming a major agricultural processing centre; the Perrine Bridge over the Snake River Canyon bears his name. Food processing remains central to the economy, with large plants for potato processing, cheese, and yogurt — Chobani opened its largest yogurt plant in the world there in 2012.

Landmarks

Nearby attractions include Shoshone Falls, a 212-foot waterfall on the Snake River higher than Niagara Falls, and the Perrine Bridge, a popular BASE jumping site and near the ramp where Evel Knievel attempted his 1974 Skycycle jump over the canyon.
