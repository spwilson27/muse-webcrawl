Osculating plane

The osculating plane of a curve in three-dimensional space is the plane determined, at a point of the curve, by the tangent vector and the (principal) normal vector at that point. It is the plane that "kisses" (osculari in Latin) the curve most closely at that point, containing the osculating circle, which best approximates the curve locally. Together with the tangent line and the normal and rectifying planes, it forms the moving Frenet–Serret frame.

Definition

For a smooth curve r(t), the osculating plane at a point where the curvature κ ≠ 0 is spanned by the unit tangent T and unit normal N; its normal direction is the binormal B = T × N. Points of zero curvature (inflection points of the spatial curve) have no well-defined osculating plane. As the point moves along the curve, the osculating plane twists; the rate of that twisting is measured by the curve's torsion τ.

Related concepts

The osculating plane should not be confused with the osculating orbit in astronomy—an instantaneous Keplerian ellipse tangent to a body's trajectory—though both uses share the idea of closest local approximation.
