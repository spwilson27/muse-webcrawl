Remus

In Roman mythology, Remus was the twin brother of Romulus, the founder and first king of Rome. The sons of the god Mars and the priestess Rhea Silvia, the twins were abandoned at birth, suckled by a she-wolf (the Lupa Capitolina), and raised by the shepherd Faustulus. In the best-known version of the myth, Remus was killed by Romulus during the founding of Rome — after mocking the low wall Romulus had built on the Palatine Hill by leaping over it — making his death the city's founding fratricide.

The myth

According to tradition recorded by Livy, Plutarch, and Dionysius of Halicarnassus, the twins' great-uncle Amulius usurped the throne of Alba Longa and ordered the infants drowned in the Tiber; the river god spared them, and a she-wolf and a woodpecker — both sacred to Mars — nourished them until Faustulus found them. Grown to manhood, the twins restored their grandfather Numitor to the throne and resolved to found a city of their own. They disagreed over the site — Romulus favouring the Palatine, Remus the Aventine — and sought the answer in augury: Remus saw six vultures first, but Romulus saw twelve.

Death and variants

Ancient sources differ: in the dominant account Romulus killed Remus in anger when he scornfully jumped over the new city's wall (murus), declaring, "So perish every one that shall hereafter leap over my walls." Other versions attribute the killing to Romulus's follower Celer, or place Remus's death in a later battle. Romans later associated Remus with the Aventine and the Remuria (Lemuria) festival of the dead.
