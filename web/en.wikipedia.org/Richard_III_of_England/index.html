Richard III of England

Richard III (2 October 1452 – 22 August 1485) was King of England from 26 June 1483 until his death in 1485. He was the last king of the House of York and the last of the Plantagenet dynasty. His defeat and death at the Battle of Bosworth Field marked the end of the Wars of the Roses and effectively the end of the English Middle Ages.

Rise to power

Richard, Duke of Gloucester, was the youngest surviving son of Richard, Duke of York, and a loyal supporter of his brother Edward IV during the Wars of the Roses. After Edward's death in April 1483, Richard became Lord Protector for his nephew Edward V. When the late king's marriage was declared invalid and his children illegitimate by the document known as Titulus Regius, Richard accepted the crown. Edward V and his brother, the "Princes in the Tower", disappeared and were widely presumed murdered — a mystery in which Richard remains the chief suspect in popular tradition.

Reign and death

Richard's short reign saw reforms of the legal system, the founding of the Council of the North, and a rebellion by the Duke of Buckingham in 1483. In August 1485 Henry Tudor landed in Wales and met Richard at Bosworth, where the king was killed in battle — the last English king to die in combat.

Rediscovery

In 2012, excavations in a Leicester car park on the site of the Greyfriars church uncovered a skeleton with severe scoliosis and battle wounds; DNA comparison with descendants of Richard's sister confirmed it was the king. He was reburied in Leicester Cathedral in March 2015.

Reputation

Tudor historians, above all through Shakespeare's play Richard III, portrayed him as a deformed, murderous usurper, while the Richard III Society and others have argued that much of this was propaganda.
