Kaw Nation

The Kaw Nation (Kaw: Káⁿza), historically known as the Kansa or Kanza, is a federally recognized Native American tribe in Oklahoma. A Siouan-speaking people of the Dhegiha branch, the Kaw gave their name to the Kansas River and to the state of Kansas ("People of the South Wind"). Their tribal headquarters is in Kaw City, Kay County, Oklahoma.

History

The Kaw once lived in the Ohio Valley before migrating west to the Missouri and Kansas river valleys, where by the 18th century they controlled a large territory in present-day Kansas. Treaties in 1825 and 1846 ceded most Kaw land, and the tribe was settled on a reservation near Council Grove, Kansas. In 1873 the remaining Kaw were removed to Indian Territory (Oklahoma), where their numbers fell to barely 200 by the early 20th century. Under the allotment policy their Oklahoma reservation was broken up, but the tribe maintained its government and federal recognition.

Government and culture

The Kaw Nation is governed under a constitution adopted in 1990, with a business committee and general council of enrolled citizens. The tribe operates enterprises including the SouthWind Casino near Newkirk. In Council Grove the tribe preserves the Allegawaho Memorial Heritage Park, centred on a monument to an unknown Kaw warrior, and supports Kansa language revitalisation from archival recordings and documents. The last fluent native speaker, Walter Kekahbah, died in 2009, but language classes continue.

Notable people

The most famous Kaw citizen is Charles Curtis, who rose to become U.S. Senate Majority Leader and Vice President of the United States under Herbert Hoover (1929–1933) — still the highest office held by a Native American and the only Vice President with acknowledged tribal enrollment. The writer and chief William A. Mehojah and the artist and educator are among other notable tribal members.
