Bezymianny (Russian: Безымянный, "the nameless one") is an active stratovolcano on the Kamchatka Peninsula in far eastern Russia, part of the Klyuchevskaya group of volcanoes. Long considered extinct, it returned to activity in 1955 and produced one of the largest eruptions of the 20th century in 1956.

The 1956 eruption

After roughly a thousand years of quiet, Bezymianny awoke in October 1955. On 30 March 1956 the summit collapsed in a massive sector failure, followed by a directed lateral blast that devastated some 500 km² of forest — a sequence closely paralleling the 1980 eruption of Mount St. Helens, which it preceded by 24 years. The eruption reduced the mountain's height by more than 200 m and created a wide horseshoe-shaped crater open to the east.

Subsequent activity

Since 1956 a viscous lava dome has grown within the crater, punctuated by periodic explosive eruptions and pyroclastic flows. Major explosions in the 21st century — including events in 2017, 2019, and 2022 — sent ash plumes more than 10 km high, periodically disrupting North Pacific air routes. Bezymianny is monitored by the Kamchatka Volcanic Eruption Response Team (KVERT).

Geology

The volcano is built of andesitic lavas and forms part of a cluster including Klyuchevskoy, the highest active volcano in Eurasia, and Tolbachik. Its name, "nameless", reflected its former status as an inconspicuous, separate peak beside its famous neighbors.
