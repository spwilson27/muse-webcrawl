AI alignment

AI alignment is the field of research concerned with steering artificial intelligence systems toward their designers' intended goals, preferences, and ethical principles. An AI system is considered aligned if it advances its designers' intended objectives; it is misaligned if it pursues unintended objectives instead.

Problems

Researchers distinguish problems such as specification gaming (or reward hacking), in which a system exploits flaws in its objective rather than achieving the intended outcome; goal misgeneralisation, where a system pursues an unintended goal in new situations; and concerns that sufficiently capable systems might develop instrumental goals such as self-preservation or resistance to shutdown. These difficulties relate to broader questions in AI safety.

Approaches

Key techniques include reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), used to train large language models to follow instructions and be helpful and harmless; constitutional methods that train models against explicit principles; interpretability research aimed at understanding models' internal computations; and evaluations and red-teaming to detect misaligned behaviour before deployment. Many researchers and policy-makers argue alignment becomes more urgent as AI capabilities grow.
