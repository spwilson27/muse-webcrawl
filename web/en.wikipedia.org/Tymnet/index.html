Tymnet

Tymnet's network of Tymsat switches and concentrators
TypePublic packet-switched data network
Launched1971
OperatorTymshare, later McDonnell Douglas / BT

Tymnet was an international data communications network headquartered in Cupertino, California that used virtual call packet switching technology and X.25, SNA, and other protocols. It was developed by Tymshare, Inc. as an internal network to connect remote terminals to its time-sharing host computers, and opened to public use in 1971. At its peak it was one of the largest public data networks in the world, with nodes in the United States and dozens of other countries.

Tymnet was an early commercial application of packet switching, using minicomputer-based nodes called Tymsats. Users dialled in to a local node, which built a virtual circuit across the network to the destination host. Major online services of the 1970s and 1980s — including CompuServe, The Source, and various database and airline reservation systems — were reachable through Tymnet.

History

Tymshare spun the network into a subsidiary, and in 1984 Tymshare was acquired by McDonnell Douglas. Tymnet was later sold to British Telecom in 1993. As the Internet and the World Wide Web displaced proprietary online networks in the 1990s, Tymnet declined, and the network was shut down in 2003.
