The Japanese yen (Japanese: 円, symbol ¥, code JPY) is the official currency of Japan. It is the third most traded currency in the foreign exchange market after the U.S. dollar and the euro, and a major reserve currency. The yen was established by the Meiji government under the New Currency Act of 1871, modelled on European decimal currency systems and originally defined by weight of silver and gold.

History

After the Second World War the yen was fixed at ¥360 per U.S. dollar under the Bretton Woods system. It floated from 1973 and appreciated sharply after the 1985 Plaza Accord, contributing to Japan's asset-price bubble. Despite decades of low growth, the yen is regarded as a safe-haven currency in times of financial stress.

Coins and banknotes

Coins are issued in denominations of 1, 5, 10, 50, 100 and 500 yen; the 5 and 50 yen coins have a central hole. Banknotes are issued in 1,000, 2,000 (rare), 5,000 and 10,000 yen denominations. A new series issued in 2024 features Eiichi Shibusawa (10,000), Umeko Tsuda (5,000) and Shibasaburo Kitasato (1,000), with advanced anti-counterfeiting holograms.

Subunit

The yen was historically divided into 100 sen, but sen coins ceased to be used after 1953; prices today are denominated in whole yen.
