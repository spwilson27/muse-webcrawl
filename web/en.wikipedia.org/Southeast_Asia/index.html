Southeast Asia

Southeast Asia is the geographical south-eastern region of Asia, consisting of the countries that are situated south of mainland China, east of the Indian subcontinent, and north-west of Oceania. It is composed of two primary regions: Mainland Southeast Asia (historically Indochina), comprising Cambodia, Laos, Myanmar, Peninsular Malaysia, Thailand, and Vietnam; and Maritime Southeast Asia, comprising Brunei, East Malaysia, East Timor, Indonesia, the Philippines, and Singapore.

The region covers about 4.5 million square kilometres and is home to roughly 690 million people. It lies at the crossroads of major sea routes between East Asia and South Asia, including the Strait of Malacca, one of the world's busiest shipping lanes. Southeast Asia is culturally and religiously diverse, with large populations of Buddhists, Muslims, Christians, and Hindus, and hundreds of languages.

History and politics

Historic states and empires of the region include Srivijaya, the Khmer Empire, Ayutthaya, and Majapahit. Most of the region was colonized by European powers between the 16th and 20th centuries, followed by independence movements after World War II. Ten of the region's states (with East Timor acceding later) form the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN), founded in 1967, which promotes regional economic and political cooperation.

Economy

Southeast Asia's economies include major manufacturing and trading hubs such as Singapore, Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia, and Indonesia; agriculture (notably rice, palm oil, and rubber) remains important, and the region is a leading global tourist destination.
