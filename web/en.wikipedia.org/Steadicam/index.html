Steadicam

Steadicam
Inventor: Garrett Brown
Introduced: 1975
Manufacturer: Tiffen

Steadicam is a brand of camera stabilizer mounts for motion picture cameras invented by Garrett Brown and introduced in 1975 by Cinema Products Corporation. The system isolates the camera from the operator's movement, allowing smooth, floating shots even when the camera moves over irregular surfaces, up stairs, or through crowds.

Design

The camera is mounted on a sled balanced around a gimbal; an iso-elastic articulated arm connects the sled to a support vest worn by the operator, carrying the weight while decoupling the camera from body motion. A monitor and battery provide counterweight. Operating a Steadicam is a specialized craft; operators train extensively to control framing, balance, and movement.

Use in cinema

The device debuted in Bound to Glory (1976) and achieved fame in Rocky (1976), Halloween (1978), and The Shining (1980), whose long tracking shots through the Overlook Hotel became iconic uses. Brown won an Academy Award for the invention, and Steadicam has become a genericized term for camera stabilizers; smaller descendants include handheld gimbal stabilizers for phones and mirrorless cameras.
