Minusinsk Basin

The Minusinsk Basin (also called the Minusinsk Hollow or the Khakass-Minusinsk Basin) is a fertile depression in southern Siberia, in present-day Krasnoyarsk Krai and the Republic of Khakassia in Russia. It lies between the Sayan Mountains to the south and the Kuznetsk Alatau and other ranges to the west and north, and is watered by the upper Yenisei River and its tributaries, the Abakan and the Tuba. The town of Minusinsk, on the Yenisei, gives the basin its name.

Geography and climate

Sheltered by the surrounding ranges, the basin enjoys a notably mild, dry continental climate by Siberian standards, with warm summers that have made it a regional center for grain, fruit, and melon growing—sometimes called the "Siberian Italy" or the breadbasket of eastern Siberia. Its steppe and forest-steppe landscapes support agriculture that would be difficult at the same latitude elsewhere in Siberia.

Archaeology

The basin is one of the most important archaeological regions of Siberia. Its sequence of Bronze and Iron Age cultures includes the Afanasievo culture (c. 3300–2500 BC), among the earliest Indo-European-associated cultures of the steppe; the Okunev culture; the Andronovo culture; the Karasuk culture; and the Tagar culture (c. 800–100 BC), a Scythian-related culture famous for its great burial mounds (kurgans) such as those in the Salbyk valley, deer stones, and bronze animal art. Turkic peoples, including the Yenisei Kyrgyz, later dominated the region, and Russian settlement began in the 17th and 18th centuries.
