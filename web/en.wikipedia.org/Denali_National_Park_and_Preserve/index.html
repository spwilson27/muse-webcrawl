Denali National Park and Preserve is a United States national park and preserve in Interior Alaska, centred on Denali, the highest mountain in North America. The park covers over six million acres of wild land, bisected by the Alaska Range, with glaciers, tundra, taiga forest and braided glacial rivers.

History

The naturalist Charles Alexander Sheldon campaigned for the area's protection after studying its Dall sheep, and the park was established in 1917 as Mount McKinley National Park. The Alaska National Interest Lands Conservation Act of 1980 enlarged it, created the adjacent preserve, and renamed it Denali. In 2015 the mountain itself was officially renamed from Mount McKinley to Denali, its Koyukon Athabascan name meaning "the tall one."

Wildlife and landscape

The park is known for viewing the "big five" mammals: grizzly bears, moose, caribou, Dall sheep and wolves. More than a million acres are covered by glaciers, including the Kahiltna Glacier, the longest in the Alaska Range. Most of the park is designated wilderness.

Access

A single 92-mile (148 km) road, mostly unpaved, penetrates the park; private vehicles are restricted beyond the first 15 miles, and most visitors travel by tour or transit bus. Mountaineers attempting Denali are managed through a ranger station at Talkeetna.
