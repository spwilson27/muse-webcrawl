José Efraín Ríos Montt (16 June 1926 – 1 April 2018) was a Guatemalan military officer and politician who seized power in a coup in March 1982 and ruled as de facto head of state until he was overthrown in August 1983. His 17 months in power coincided with the bloodiest phase of Guatemala's civil war.

Rule and atrocities

Ríos Montt, a born-again evangelical Christian, pursued a counterinsurgency strategy known as "beans and bullets" (frijoles y fusiles), combining food-for-work programmes with scorched-earth military operations against Maya communities accused of supporting guerrillas. The UN-sponsored Historical Clarification Commission later concluded that acts of genocide were committed against Maya groups, particularly the Ixil, under his regime; the army massacred entire villages, most notoriously at Dos Erres.

Trial

In 2013 Ríos Montt was tried in Guatemala and convicted of genocide and crimes against humanity, receiving an 80-year sentence — the first former head of state convicted of genocide in his own country's courts. The conviction was overturned days later on procedural grounds, and a retrial was ordered; he died in 2018 while proceedings were ongoing.
