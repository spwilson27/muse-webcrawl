Yuan (currency)

Yuan
Chinese元 / 圓
Meaning"Round object" (coin)
Used byChina (renminbi), historically others

The yuan is the basic unit of a number of currencies in the Chinese-speaking world. In contemporary usage it most often refers to the base unit of the Chinese renminbi, issued by the People's Bank of China. The word derives from a term meaning a round coin, and currencies based on the yuan were historically also used in Japan (yen) and Korea (won), which share the same root.

History

Silver yuan coins were first minted in the late Qing dynasty in imitation of foreign silver dollars circulating in China. The Republic of China continued the yuan, and after 1949 the People's Republic issued the renminbi, whose primary unit is the yuan, subdivided into 10 jiao or 100 fen.

Usage

In everyday Chinese, amounts are commonly spoken of in yuan or colloquially in kuài ("pieces"). The currency is symbolised by ¥, a symbol shared with the Japanese yen. The yuan's role in international trade and finance has grown, and it was added to the IMF's Special Drawing Rights basket in 2016.
