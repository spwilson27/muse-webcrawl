Centering prayer

Centering prayer is a method of silent, contemplative Christian prayer in which the practitioner seeks to rest in God's presence by letting go of thoughts and consenting to the action of the Holy Spirit within. It was developed in the 1970s by Trappist monks at St. Joseph's Abbey in Spencer, Massachusetts, notably Fathers William Meninger, Basil Pennington, and Thomas Keating.

Method

The method has four guidelines: choose a sacred word as a symbol of consent to God's presence; sit comfortably and introduce the word silently; whenever engaged with thoughts, return gently to the sacred word; and end with a period of silence after the prayer time (usually 20 minutes) concludes. The practice draws on the 14th-century spiritual classic The Cloud of Unknowing, the Jesus Prayer tradition, and the writings of John of the Cross and Teresa of Ávila.

Spread and organisation

Thomas Keating co-founded Contemplative Outreach in 1984 to teach and network the practice, which spread widely among lay Christians and retreat movements. Keating's book Open Mind, Open Heart (1986) is its classic presentation.

Discussion

Supporters see centering prayer as a recovery of the Church's contemplative tradition; some critics in evangelical and Catholic circles have questioned its similarities to Eastern meditation techniques, a comparison its teachers generally reject while affirming its distinctly Trinitarian intent.
