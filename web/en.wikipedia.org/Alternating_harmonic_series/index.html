Alternating harmonic series

The alternating harmonic series is the infinite series 1 − 1/2 + 1/3 − 1/4 + 1/5 − ⋯, formed by taking the terms of the harmonic series with alternating signs. Unlike the harmonic series, which diverges, the alternating harmonic series converges, and its sum is the natural logarithm of 2.

Convergence

Convergence follows from the alternating series test: the terms decrease monotonically to zero. The convergence is conditional rather than absolute, since the series of absolute values is the divergent harmonic series. By the Riemann series theorem, its terms can be rearranged to converge to any real value, or to diverge.

Sum

The sum ln 2 can be obtained from the Mercator series, the Taylor expansion of ln(1 + x) evaluated at x = 1, or by combining the harmonic numbers of even and odd order.
