Extended Riemann hypothesis

The extended Riemann hypothesis (ERH) is the conjecture that all nontrivial zeros of Dedekind zeta functions of number fields lie on the critical line Re(s) = 1/2. It extends the classical Riemann hypothesis, which concerns the Riemann zeta function, and is in turn a special case of the grand Riemann hypothesis.

Statement

For a number field K, the Dedekind zeta function ζK(s) generalises the Riemann zeta function by counting ideals of the ring of integers of K rather than integers. The ERH asserts that every zero of ζK in the critical strip 0 < Re(s) < 1 has real part exactly 1/2.

Consequences

Assuming the ERH, one obtains the best-known error terms for the distribution of prime ideals (a generalisation of the prime number theorem via the Chebotarev density theorem) and effective results in algebraic number theory. A celebrated application is in computational number theory: the correctness and efficiency of algorithms for primality testing and class-group computation are often proved under the ERH. For example, Ankeny's result, refined by Montgomery and Bach, implies that under the ERH the least quadratic non-residue modulo a prime p is O(log² p), which bounds the witnesses needed for the deterministic Miller–Rabin primality test.

Status

Like the classical hypothesis, the ERH remains unproved for any number field beyond the rational numbers, although numerical evidence supports it extensively. The generalised Riemann hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions occupies an intermediate position between the Riemann hypothesis and the ERH.
