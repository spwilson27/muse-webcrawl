Thrash metal (or simply thrash) is an extreme subgenre of heavy metal music characterised by its overall fast tempo and aggression. The songs usually use fast percussive beats and low-register guitar riffs, overlaid with shredding-style lead work. The genre emerged in the early 1980s when musicians began fusing the speed and attitude of hardcore punk with the riffing and twin-guitar approach of the new wave of British heavy metal.

Characteristics

Thrash metal guitarists play fast, muted, palm-muted riffs with heavy use of down-picking, while drummers employ rapid double-bass patterns and the "D-beat". Vocals are typically shouted or barked rather than sung. Lyrics frequently address war, nuclear annihilation, political corruption, social injustice and horror themes. tempos commonly exceed those of earlier metal, and abrupt tempo and time-signature changes are common.

History

Early landmarks include Venom's Welcome to Hell (1981) and Metallica's Kill 'Em All (1983). By the mid-1980s the "Big Four" of American thrash — Metallica, Slayer, Megadeth and Anthrax — had defined the genre with landmark albums such as Master of Puppets, Reign in Blood, Peace Sells... but Who's Buying? and Among the Living. Important scenes developed in the San Francisco Bay Area (Exodus, Testament, Death Angel), New York (Anthrax, Overkill, Nuclear Assault) and Germany, where Kreator, Sodom, Destruction and Tankard formed the "Teutonic Big Four".

Decline and revival

Thrash's commercial peak came around 1990–91; the rise of grunge and alternative rock in the 1990s pushed many bands toward slower, groove-oriented styles. A revival since the 2000s, led by veterans and new bands such as Municipal Waste, Evile and Warbringer, returned the genre to festival prominence, and the Big Four performed together on tour in 2010–11. Thrash strongly influenced death metal and black metal.
