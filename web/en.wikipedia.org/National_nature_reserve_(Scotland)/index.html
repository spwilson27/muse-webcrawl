National nature reserve (Scotland)

National nature reserves (NNRs) in Scotland are areas of land set aside for the conservation of important habitats and species and for public enjoyment of nature. They are designated and managed under the auspices of NatureScot, Scotland's nature agency, often in partnership with landowners and community bodies.

Purpose and designation

NNRs showcase Scotland's finest natural heritage, including Atlantic woodlands, blanket bogs, seabird islands, and mountain habitats. Sites are selected because they are nationally important for wildlife or earth science, well managed for conservation, and accessible for public engagement and research. Many NNRs overlap with other designations such as Sites of Special Scientific Interest and Natura 2000 sites.

Notable reserves

Well-known Scottish NNRs include St Kilda, a remote archipelago famous for seabirds; the Isle of May in the Firth of Forth; Beinn Eighe, Britain's first NNR (designated in 1951), protecting ancient Caledonian pinewood; Loch Leven; and the Forsinard Flows in the Flow Country of Caithness and Sutherland. There are more than 40 NNRs covering roughly 1.5% of Scotland's land area.

Management

Reserves are managed by NatureScot directly or by partners including the National Trust for Scotland, RSPB Scotland, Forestry and Land Scotland, and private estates, guided by reserve management plans and the Scottish Outdoor Access Code.
