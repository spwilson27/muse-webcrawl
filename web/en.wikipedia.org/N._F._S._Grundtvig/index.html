N. F. S. Grundtvig

Nikolaj Frederik Severin Grundtvig (8 September 1783 – 2 September 1872), usually referred to as N. F. S. Grundtvig, was a Danish pastor, author, poet, philosopher, historian, teacher and politician. He is among the most influential figures in Danish cultural history, a leader of the Danish national awakening and the founder of the folk high school movement.

He wrote an enormous body of hymns, many still sung in Danish churches, and his ideas shaped Danish education, church life and ideas of national identity.

Life and thought

Grundtvig studied theology at the University of Copenhagen and, after a religious crisis, became a controversial preacher whose attacks on rationalist clergy led to a period of censorship. His 'matchless discovery' of 1825 held that the Christian church rests on the 'living word' — the Apostles' Creed and the sacraments as confessed by the congregation through history — rather than on the Bible alone, a doctrine that underpinned the liberal 'Grundtvigian' movement within the Danish church.

He championed folkelighed ('people-ness'): the idea that nationality and culture spring from the common people's language, history and songs, and he campaigned for freedom of religion, education and assembly.

Folk high schools and legacy

Grundtvig's educational writings inspired the folk high schools — residential adult schools teaching history, literature and civic life 'for life' rather than for examinations — beginning at Rødding in 1844 and spreading across Scandinavia and beyond. He sat in the assembly that drafted Denmark's first constitution (1849) and in parliament for much of his later life. Denmark's largest hymn writer, he is commemorated by Grundtvig's Church in Copenhagen, completed in 1940.
