Ngorongoro Conservation Area

The Ngorongoro Conservation Area is a protected area and UNESCO World Heritage Site in northern Tanzania, covering about 8,292 km² of highland plains, savanna, and forest west of Arusha. Its centerpiece is the Ngorongoro Crater, the world's largest inactive, intact, and unfilled volcanic caldera, formed when a huge volcano collapsed on itself two to three million years ago.

Wildlife and archaeology

The crater floor, about 260 km², supports some 25,000 large animals, including one of Africa's densest lion populations, black rhinoceros, elephants, wildebeest, and hippos. The wider area includes Olduvai Gorge, one of the most important paleoanthropological sites in the world, where Mary and Louis Leakey found early hominin fossils.

People

Unlike a national park, the conservation area permits human habitation; Maasai pastoralists graze livestock there, though their numbers and rights have been a long-running source of controversy and government relocation programs.
