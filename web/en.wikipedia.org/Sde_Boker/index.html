Sde Boker

Sde Boker (Hebrew: שדה בוקר, "herdsman's field") is a kibbutz in the Negev desert of southern Israel. Founded in 1952, it is best known as the retirement home of Israel's first Prime Minister, David Ben-Gurion, who moved there in 1953 believing the desert should be made to bloom and that the Negev was Israel's future frontier. His modest wooden hut is preserved as a museum, and he is buried beside his wife Paula at the nearby cliff-edge graves overlooking the Zin valley.

Ben-Gurion's legacy

Adjacent to the kibbutz is Midreshet Ben-Gurion, an educational centre housing the Jacob Blaustein Institutes for Desert Research of Ben-Gurion University of the Negev, which study desert agriculture, water and solar energy. Ben-Gurion's vision of settling and developing the Negev remains a touchstone of Israeli national mythology.
