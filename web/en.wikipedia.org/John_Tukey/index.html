John Tukey

John Wilder Tukey (June 16, 1915 – July 26, 2000) was an American mathematician and statistician, best known for the development of the fast Fourier transform (FFT) algorithm and the Tukey box plot. The Tukey range test, the Tukey lambda distribution, the Tukey test of additivity, and the Teichmüller–Tukey lemma are named after him. He is also credited with coining many statistical terms, such as "bit" and "software".

Career

Tukey earned degrees in chemistry from Brown University and a doctorate in mathematics from Princeton University in 1939. During World War II he worked at the Fire Control Research Office. Afterwards he divided his time between Princeton and AT&T Bell Laboratories. He chaired the committee whose report criticized the Kinsey reports on statistical grounds.

Contributions

With James Cooley, Tukey published the Cooley–Tukey FFT algorithm in 1965, transforming signal processing. His 1977 book Exploratory Data Analysis championed visualization-based methods including the box plot and stem-and-leaf plot. He developed robust statistics methods, the jackknife concept (named by him), and the concept of exploratory versus confirmatory data analysis. Tukey received the National Medal of Science in 1973.

Terminology

Tukey coined the word "bit" as a contraction of "binary digit" in a 1947 Bell Labs memo, and in 1958 used "software" in print in its modern computing sense.
