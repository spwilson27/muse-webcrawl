Okunoin

Okunoin (奥の院, "the inner sanctuary") is a sacred site and the largest cemetery in Japan, located on Mount Kōya (Kōyasan) in Wakayama Prefecture. It is the site of the mausoleum of Kūkai (posthumously Kōbō Daishi), founder of Shingon Buddhism, whom devotees believe rests not in death but in eternal meditation, awaiting the coming of Maitreya, the Buddha of the Future.

Description

A roughly two-kilometre pilgrim path winds through ancient cedar forest past more than 200,000 tombstones and memorials — those of feudal lords, monks, and ordinary families, as well as monuments erected by modern corporations. The path crosses the Gobyo bridge to the Hall of Lanterns (Tōrō-dō), where thousands of donated lanterns burn, and then to the Gobyo, Kūkai's mausoleum. Monks still bring the saint two meals daily in a ritual more than a thousand years old.

Access and significance

Okunoin is the spiritual heart of the Mount Kōya World Heritage Site and the goal of pilgrims completing the Shikoku Pilgrimage. Temple lodgings (shukubō) in the town below allow visitors to stay overnight, attend morning prayers, and walk the cemetery at dawn.
