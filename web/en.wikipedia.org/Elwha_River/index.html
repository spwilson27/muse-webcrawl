Elwha River

The Elwha River is a 45-mile (72 km) river on the Olympic Peninsula in the U.S. state of Washington. From its source at Elwha snowfinger on Mount Olympus, it flows generally north to the Strait of Juan de Fuca. Most of the river lies within Olympic National Park.

Dam removal

Two hydroelectric dams, the Elwha Dam (built 1910–1913) and the Glines Canyon Dam (built 1927), blocked the river for a century and prevented salmon from reaching most of their historic habitat. Between 2011 and 2014, both dams were removed in the largest dam removal project in history at the time, restoring the river to a free-flowing state and reopening more than 70 miles of spawning habitat for salmon and steelhead. The project, carried out under the Elwha River Ecosystem and Fisheries Restoration Act of 1992, also involved extensive revegetation of the drained reservoirs.

Ecology

Following dam removal, sediment replenished the river's delta at the Strait of Juan de Fuca, and salmon populations began recolonizing the upper watershed.
