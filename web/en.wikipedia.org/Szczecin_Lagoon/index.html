Szczecin Lagoon

The Szczecin Lagoon (Polish: Zalew Szczeciński; German: Stettiner Haff) is a lagoon in the Oder estuary on the southern coast of the Baltic Sea, shared by Germany and Poland. It is separated from the Pomeranian Bay of the Baltic by the islands of Usedom and Wolin, and is connected to the sea by the straits of the Peene, Świna, and Dziwna.

Geography

The lagoon covers about 687 km² and is shallow, averaging under four metres deep. The Oder empties into its southern end near the Polish port city of Szczecin, from which it takes its English and Polish names. The German part of the lagoon is sometimes called the Kleines Haff (western basin), while the Polish part forms the Wielki Zalew. The navigable shipping channel from Szczecin and Świnoujście to the Baltic follows the Świna strait.

History

For centuries the lagoon lay within the Duchy and later Province of Pomerania. After the Second World War, the Oder–Neisse line placed its eastern portion in Poland while the western shore remained German; the border settlement was confirmed by treaty in 1990. Disputes over the exact border in the lagoon and the shipping channel persisted for decades.

Ecology and economy

The lagoon is an important fishery and bird habitat, though it suffers from eutrophication driven by nutrient loads from the Oder. Its shores support tourism, sailing, and the ports of Szczecin and Świnoujście, among Poland's largest seaports.
