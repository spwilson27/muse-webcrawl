Presbyterian Church in Ireland

The Presbyterian Church in Ireland (PCI; Irish: Eaglais Phreispitéireach in Éirinn) is the largest Presbyterian denomination in Ireland and the largest Protestant denomination in Northern Ireland. It has around 200,000 members in over 500 congregations across both Northern Ireland and the Republic of Ireland, with its headquarters at Church House in Belfast.

History

Irish Presbyterianism traces its origins to Scottish settlers in Ulster in the early 17th century; the first presbytery was formed at Carrickfergus in 1642 by chaplains of a Scottish army. The General Synod of Ulster and the Secession Synod united in 1840 to form the General Assembly of the Presbyterian Church in Ireland, and the church grew significantly during the Ulster Revival of 1859. It was largely unaffected organisationally by the partition of Ireland in 1921, remaining an all-island body.

Beliefs and organisation

The church subscribes to the Westminster Confession of Faith and is governed by kirk sessions, presbyteries and the General Assembly, which elects a Moderator annually. Its theological college is Union Theological College in Belfast. The PCI is a member of the World Communion of Reformed Churches and has historically been associated with the unionist community in Northern Ireland, though its membership spans both jurisdictions.
