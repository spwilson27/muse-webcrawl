Moses Isserles (c. 1525/1530–1572), known by the Hebrew acronym Rema (רמ״א), was an eminent Polish Ashkenazi rabbi, Talmudist and posek (decisor of Jewish law). He is best known for his glosses, called HaMapah ("The Tablecloth"), to Joseph Karo's Shulchan Aruch, which adapted the Sephardic-oriented code to Ashkenazi practice and made it authoritative for Ashkenazi Jewry.

Life

Isserles was born in Kraków into a wealthy family and studied under his father-in-law's circle and Rabbi Shalom Shachna of Lublin. He founded a yeshiva in Kraków and served as rabbi of the city, attracting students from across Europe. The Rema Synagogue in Kraków's Kazimierz district, founded by his father Israel in 1553, bears his name, and he is buried in the adjacent Old Jewish Cemetery.

Works

Besides the Mapah, Isserles wrote Darkhei Moshe, a commentary on the Beit Yosef and the Tur; Torat HaOlah, a philosophical work on the symbolism of the Temple; Torat HaChatat on dietary laws; and numerous responsa. His rulings characteristically integrated local custom (minhag) as a source of law.

Legacy

The combined Shulchan Aruch with Isserles's glosses remains the standard code of Jewish law. His gravestone in Kraków calls him "the Maimonides of Polish Jewry", and his tomb became a place of pilgrimage.
