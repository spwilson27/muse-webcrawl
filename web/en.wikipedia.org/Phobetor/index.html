Phobetor

In Ovid's Metamorphoses, Phobetor (Greek: Φοβήτωρ, "Frightener") or Icelos (Greek: Ἴκελος, "Like") is one of the thousand sons of Somnus (the Roman equivalent of the Greek god Hypnos, god of sleep). Phobetor is the personification of nightmares and appears in dreams in the form of animals or monsters, such as bears, wolves, snakes, and other terrifying creatures.

Role in mythology

According to Ovid, Phobetor is one of three especially skilled dream-shaping brothers, collectively known as the Oneiroi. His brother Morpheus takes human forms in dreams, while Phantasos takes the form of inanimate objects such as rocks, water, and trees. Phobetor's domain is the animal and monstrous form, and gods may send him to frighten sleepers with bestial visions.

The name "Phobetor" does not appear in Greek sources and appears to have been coined by Ovid; Greek authors called the figure Icelos. The word derives from the Greek phobos, meaning fear or panic — the same root as "phobia".

In popular culture

Figures modelled on Phobetor appear in fantasy literature and games, and the name has been given to an exoplanet: the planet PSR B1620-26 b, nicknamed "Methuselah", orbits a pulsar system whose host stars received mythological names; "Phobetor" is the official name of the exoplanet PSR B1257+12 C in the first discovered exoplanetary system, named through the IAU's NameExoWorlds campaign in 2015.
