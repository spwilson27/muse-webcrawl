Pilbara

The Pilbara is a large, arid region in the north of Western Australia, known for its ancient red landscapes, Aboriginal heritage, and enormous iron ore deposits. Its main towns include Karratha, Port Hedland, Newman and Tom Price.

Geology and history

The Pilbara Craton contains some of Earth's oldest rocks, over 3.5 billion years old, including stromatolite fossils evidencing early life. Aboriginal peoples have lived in the region for at least 40,000 years. European colonisation followed in the 1860s with pastoralism and pearling; the modern mining era began after the 1960 lifting of an iron-ore export embargo, following Lang Hancock's famous 1952 flight over the Hamersley Range gorges.

Economy

The Pilbara produces a large share of the world's seaborne iron ore, shipped through Port Hedland, the world's largest bulk port, plus liquefied natural gas, salt and other minerals. Its economy is dominated by mining and the fly-in fly-out workforce.
