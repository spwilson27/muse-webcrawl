Sufi music

Sufi music is the devotional music of the Sufis, inspired by the works of Sufi poets such as Rumi, Hafiz, Bulleh Shah, Amir Khusrow, and Khwaja Ghulam Farid. Music occupies a central place in the Sufi practice of sama — listening or audition — a ceremony of remembrance (dhikr) intended to induce states of spiritual ecstasy and closeness to God.

Forms

The best-known form is qawwali, the energetic devotional music of South Asia popularized worldwide by Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan, typically performed by a lead singer, chorus, harmonium, and tabla. In Turkey, the Mevlevi order founded by the followers of Rumi developed the ayin ceremony with ney (reed flute), tanbur, and the whirling dance of the dervishes. Other traditions include the gnawa music of Morocco, the ilahi hymns of the Balkans and Turkey, and the Hamd and Naat sung in praise of God and the Prophet.

Sama and debate

The permissibility of music in Islam has been debated for centuries; many Sufi orders embraced sama while more legalistic scholars criticized it. Figures such as al-Ghazali defended listening to music under proper conditions as a legitimate aid to devotion.
