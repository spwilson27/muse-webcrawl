Arrokoth

486958 Arrokoth (provisional designation 2014 MU69; formerly nicknamed Ultima Thule) is a trans-Neptunian object in the Kuiper belt. It is a contact binary — two lobes joined at a narrow neck — measuring about 36 km (22 mi) long. On 1 January 2019 it was visited by NASA's New Horizons spacecraft, becoming the most distant and most primitive object ever explored by a spacecraft.

Discovery and naming

Arrokoth was discovered in June 2014 during a Hubble Space Telescope search for a post-Pluto target for New Horizons. Its name, adopted in 2019, is a word meaning "sky" or "cloud" in the Powhatan language of the Chesapeake region. The larger lobe is nicknamed Wenu and the smaller Weeyo.

Flyby

New Horizons passed within about 3,500 km of Arrokoth at 05:33 UTC on 1 January 2019, at a distance of roughly 43.4 AU from the Sun. Images revealed a remarkably red, gently merged binary formed by the slow collision of two planetesimals — evidence that planets grew by gentle accretion of such bodies. The surface bears methanol ice and complex organic compounds (tholins), and shows few craters.
