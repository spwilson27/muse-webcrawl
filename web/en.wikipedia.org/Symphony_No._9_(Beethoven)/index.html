Symphony No. 9 (Beethoven)

The Ninth Symphony culminates in the choral "Ode to Joy."

The Symphony No. 9 in D minor, Op. 125, is a choral symphony, the final complete symphony by Ludwig van Beethoven, composed between 1822 and 1824. It was first performed in Vienna on 7 May 1824. The symphony is regarded by many critics and musicologists as a supreme masterpiece of Western classical music and one of the supreme achievements in the history of music.

The symphony was the first example of a major composer scoring vocal parts in a symphony. The final (fourth) movement includes a setting of Friedrich Schiller's 1785 poem "An die Freude" ("Ode to Joy"), sung by four vocal soloists and a chorus.

Structure

The four movements are an Allegro ma non troppo in sonata form; a scherzo in D minor; a slow Adagio in B-flat major; and the finale, which passes through reminiscences of the earlier movements before the orchestra and voices embrace the "Joy" theme. A typical performance lasts about 70 minutes.

Legacy

The "Ode to Joy" melody was adopted as the anthem of the Council of Europe and later the European Union. The symphony's manuscript was inscribed in UNESCO's Memory of the World register, and the work is traditionally performed in Japan at New Year and at major international celebrations.
