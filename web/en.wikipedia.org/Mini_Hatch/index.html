The Mini Hatch (styled as MINI Hatch, also known as the Mini Cooper or Mini One depending on trim) is a supermini hatchback built by BMW under the Mini marque since 2001. It revived the concept of the classic 1959 Mini in a larger, premium form, retaining the transverse engine, front-wheel drive layout, short overhangs and much of the original's styling language — wheel-at-each-corner stance, contrasting roof and round headlamps. Over four generations it has sold in the millions and spawned Cooper S and John Cooper Works performance versions, convertibles, estates and an electric model.

The Mini Hatch combines retro styling with modern engineering.

Generations

First generation (R50/R52/R53, 2001–2006)

Developed by Rover before BMW's takeover and finished by BMW after the Rover Group breakup of 2000, the new Mini launched in 2001 as the One and Cooper; the supercharged Cooper S followed, along with a convertible (R52). Engines were the Chrysler Tritec series built in Brazil.

Second generation (R56, 2006–2013)

A thorough redesign on a cheaper-to-build platform used Prince engines co-developed with PSA Peugeot Citroën — naturally aspirated and turbocharged 1.6-litre petrols and diesels. Variants multiplied: Clubman estate, Coupé, Roadster, Countryman crossover and Paceman. The John Cooper Works (JCW) became a full performance sub-brand.

Third generation (F56, 2013–2024)

Built on BMW's UKL front-drive architecture, the F56 grew again and introduced three-cylinder engines, a five-door version (F55) and, in 2019/2020, the battery-electric Cooper SE with a 32.6 kWh battery and about 110–145 miles of EPA range.

Fourth generation (F66/J01, 2024–present)

The fourth generation splits into a petrol model (F66), built in Oxford on an evolved platform, and a clean-sheet electric model (J01) built in China by the Spotlight Automotive joint venture with Great Wall Motor, offering substantially longer range.

Motorsport and culture

High-performance Coopers have competed in rallying (the Countryman WRC programme of 2011–12), touring cars and one-make series. The Hatch has appeared extensively in film and advertising and is marketed as a design-led premium product with extensive personalisation — roof graphics, mirror caps and paint combinations referencing the Swinging Sixties heritage of the original Mini.
