The Hockenheimring Baden-Württemberg is a motor racing circuit situated in the Rhine valley near the town of Hockenheim in Baden-Württemberg, Germany. It is best known as a former host of the German Grand Prix in Formula One and as a venue for the DTM touring car championship, drag racing and major music events.

History

Originally called the Kurpfalzring, the circuit opened in 1932 as a roughly triangular course through the forest, used for motorcycle racing. After the Second World War it was renamed the Hockenheimring, and in the mid-1960s the building of the Mannheim–Basel autobahn cut across the site, prompting the construction of the Motodrom stadium section, designed with input from John Hugenholtz.

The circuit hosted its first Formula One German Grand Prix in 1970. In 1980, French driver Patrick Depailler was killed in testing at the high-speed Ostkurve; chicanes were subsequently added to the long forest straights. Formula One driver Jim Clark had earlier been killed at Hockenheim in a Formula Two race in 1968.

Redesign and recent years

In 2002 the circuit was dramatically shortened to a design by Hermann Tilke: the long straights through the forest were removed, replaced by a tighter course with the new Parabolika and a hairpin that improved overtaking and spectator viewing; the old tarmac in the forest was torn up and trees planted. The German Grand Prix alternated or moved away in later years, with Hockenheim hosting the race for the last time (to date) in 2019. The circuit continues to host DTM, drag racing (the NitrOlympX), club racing and concerts, and the Hockenheimring museum displays racing cars and motorcycles, including a memorial to Jim Clark.
