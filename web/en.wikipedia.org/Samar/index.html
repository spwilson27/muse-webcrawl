Samar

Samar is the third-largest and seventh-most populous island in the Philippines, located in the eastern Visayas, central Philippines. It lies southeast of the Bicol Peninsula of Luzon (across the San Bernardino Strait) and northeast of Leyte, from which it is separated by the narrow San Juanico Strait. The island is divided into three provinces: Samar (formerly Western Samar), Northern Samar, and Eastern Samar.

Geography

Samar is rugged and heavily forested, with a mountainous, dissected interior and narrow coastal plains. It faces the Philippine Sea to the east and the Samar Sea to the west. The San Juanico Bridge, one of the longest bridges in the Philippines at about 2.16 km, connects Samar to Leyte across the San Juanico Strait. The climate is wet year-round with no pronounced dry season, and the island's Pacific coast is exposed to typhoons.

History

The island was among the first parts of the Philippines sighted by Ferdinand Magellan's expedition in 1521. Spanish colonization proceeded from the seventeenth century; the island's coasts suffered Moro raids, prompting the construction of fortifications. During the Philippine–American War, Samar was the scene of fierce fighting and the notorious Balangiga massacre of 1901, in which Filipino guerrillas attacked a U.S. garrison; the bells of Balangiga church, seized as war trophies, were returned to the Philippines in 2018. In October 1944, the Battle off Samar formed part of the Battle of Leyte Gulf, in which outgunned U.S. escort carriers and destroyers held off a Japanese battleship force.

Environment

The Samar Island Natural Park protects one of the largest remaining tracts of lowland rainforest in the Philippines, home to the Philippine eagle, Philippine tarsier, and many endemic species. The island also has notable cave systems and the Sohoton Natural Bridge National Park.
