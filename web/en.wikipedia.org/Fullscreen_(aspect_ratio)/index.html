Fullscreen (aspect ratio)

Fullscreen refers to the 4:3 (1.33:1) aspect ratio of early standard television screens and computer monitors, so named by contrast with the wider 16:9 widescreen format. Films shot in wider formats were often adapted for home video using pan and scan to fill the fullscreen frame, cropping the sides of the image.

History

The 4:3 ratio derived from the Academy ratio of early sound film. It remained the standard for broadcast television through the NTSC and PAL eras. As high-definition broadcasting adopted 16:9 in the 2000s, "fullscreen" editions of films on DVD declined, and the term fell out of common use.

Legacy

Some directors and archivists still prefer presentations preserving a work's original 4:3 framing, and certain modern releases (such as some restorations of classic television) are issued in their native fullscreen ratio.
