Three Secrets of Fátima

Date revealedFirst two: 1941–42; third written 1944, published 2000
SeerSister Lúcia dos Santos
ContextMarian apparitions at Fátima, Portugal, 1917

The Three Secrets of Fátima are a series of apocalyptic visions and prophecies said to have been given by an apparition of the Virgin Mary to three young Portuguese shepherds — Lúcia Santos and her cousins Francisco and Jacinta Marto — at Fátima, Portugal, beginning on 13 May 1917. The secrets were communicated, according to Lúcia, during the apparition of 13 July 1917.

The secrets

  • First secret: a vision of Hell, shown so the children would understand the fate of sinners.
  • Second secret: a prediction that the First World War would end but that a worse war would follow under Pius XI if people did not cease offending God; it called for the consecration of Russia to the Immaculate Heart of Mary and the communion of reparation on the First Saturdays.
  • Third secret: a symbolic vision including a "bishop dressed in white" being killed by soldiers with bullets and arrows — an image the Vatican, on publishing the text in 2000, interpreted as referring to the persecution of Christians in the twentieth century and the 1981 assassination attempt on Pope John Paul II.

Publication and controversy

Lúcia wrote the first two secrets in her memoirs of 1941–42. The third was written down in 1944, sealed, and held by the Vatican from 1957; Pope John Paul II released it in 2000 with a commentary by Cardinal Joseph Ratzinger. The text's release did not end debate: some believers hold that a portion of the secret remains unpublished, a claim the Holy See denies.
