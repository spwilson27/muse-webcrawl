R. D. Burman

Rahul Dev Burman
Born: 27 June 1939, Calcutta
Died: 4 January 1994, Bombay
Occupation: Composer, music director
Films scored: 331

Rahul Dev Burman (27 June 1939 – 4 January 1994), universally known as R. D. Burman and nicknamed Pancham, was an Indian music director and composer regarded as one of the most influential figures in Hindi film music. He composed musical scores for 331 films from the 1960s to the 1990s, most of them performed by singers such as Kishore Kumar, Asha Bhosle (his wife) and Lata Mangeshkar.

Career

The son of composer S. D. Burman, he received his first break with Chhote Nawab (1961) and achieved stardom with the hit soundtrack of Teesri Manzil (1966). Through the 1970s he dominated Bollywood music with a style fusing Indian melodies with jazz, rock, funk, Latin rhythms and electronic sounds. Landmark scores include Yaadon Ki Baaraat, Hare Rama Hare Krishna (with the iconic "Dum Maro Dum"), Amar Prem, Sholay, Aandhi and Deewaar.

Later years and legacy

His career declined in the 1980s as tastes changed, but his final film, 1942: A Love Story (1994), released after his death from a heart attack, was a major success. R. D. Burman's songs have been remixed and celebrated ever since, and he is credited with modernising the sound of Indian film music.
