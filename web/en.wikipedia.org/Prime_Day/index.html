Amazon Prime Day is an annual deal event held by the online retailer Amazon exclusively for members of its Amazon Prime subscription program. The event, typically held over two days in July, features time-limited discounts across product categories including electronics, home goods, and Amazon's own devices such as Echo smart speakers and Fire TV streaming devices.

History

The first Prime Day took place on July 15, 2015, marking the 20th anniversary of Amazon's founding. Although the inaugural event drew criticism for the quality of some deals, it exceeded the company's previous Black Friday sales records. The event expanded in subsequent years: in 2017 it ran for 30 hours, and from 2019 onward it has generally spanned a full 48 hours. In 2020, the event was postponed to October because of the COVID-19 pandemic. In 2022, Amazon added a second fall event, the Prime Early Access Sale, later renamed Prime Big Deal Days.

Sales

Prime Day has become one of the largest shopping events in the world, rivaling Black Friday and Cyber Monday. Amazon reported that customers purchased more than 375 million items worldwide during Prime Day 2023, with sales estimated by third-party analysts at over $12 billion. The event is also credited with driving new Prime subscriptions, since many deals are restricted to members.

Criticism

The event has attracted criticism from labor groups, which have organized strikes and protests at Amazon warehouses timed to coincide with Prime Day, citing concerns over working conditions. Consumer advocates have also noted that some listed discounts are measured against inflated list prices, and rival retailers including Walmart and Target have launched competing sales events on the same dates.
