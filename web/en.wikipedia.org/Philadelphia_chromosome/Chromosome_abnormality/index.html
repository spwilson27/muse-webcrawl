Chromosome abnormality

A chromosome abnormality (or chromosomal aberration) is any departure from the normal structure or number of chromosomes in a cell. Numerical abnormalities include aneuploidies such as trisomy 21 (Down syndrome) and monosomy X (Turner syndrome). Structural abnormalities include deletions, duplications, inversions, insertions, rings, and translocations, in which segments are exchanged between chromosomes.

The Philadelphia chromosome as a structural abnormality

The classic cancer-associated structural abnormality is the Philadelphia chromosome, a reciprocal translocation t(9;22)(q34;q11) first reported in 1960 by Peter Nowell and David Hungerford in patients with chronic myeloid leukaemia. The translocation fuses the ABL1 gene on chromosome 9 with the BCR gene on chromosome 22, creating the BCR::ABL1 fusion oncogene. Because it is acquired in somatic (blood stem) cells rather than inherited, it appears only in the malignant clone. The Philadelphia chromosome was the first consistent chromosomal abnormality linked to a specific cancer, and it established translocations — Burkitt lymphoma's t(8;14) among many others — as recurring drivers of malignancy.

Detection

Chromosomal abnormalities are detected by karyotyping, fluorescence in situ hybridisation (FISH), and molecular assays such as PCR for fusion transcripts — techniques routinely used to diagnose and monitor Philadelphia-chromosome-positive leukaemias.
