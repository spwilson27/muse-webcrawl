Tyrosine kinase

ClassEnzyme (protein kinase), EC 2.7.10
ReactionTransfers a phosphate group from ATP to tyrosine residues in proteins
Human genes~90 tyrosine kinases (58 receptor, 32 non-receptor)
Notable membersInsulin receptor, EGFR, Src, ABL1, JAK family

A tyrosine kinase is an enzyme that catalyses the transfer of a phosphate group from ATP to the amino acid tyrosine on a protein. Tyrosine kinases are central regulators of cell signalling: phosphorylation of tyrosine residues creates binding sites for downstream signalling proteins and switches pathways controlling growth, division, differentiation, metabolism, and survival on or off. The human genome encodes about 90 tyrosine kinases.

Classes

Receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs) are cell-surface receptors with an extracellular ligand-binding domain, a single transmembrane helix, and an intracellular kinase domain. They include the insulin receptor, epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR/HER family), VEGF receptors, and fibroblast growth factor receptors. Ligand binding usually dimerises the receptor, activating the kinase and triggering trans-autophosphorylation. Non-receptor tyrosine kinases are cytoplasmic or nuclear and include the Src, Abl, JAK, and Tec families; they are regulated by protein interactions and by other kinases and phosphatases.

Function

Phosphotyrosine motifs are read by proteins containing SH2 and PTB domains, assembling signalling complexes that activate pathways such as Ras–MAPK, PI3K–Akt, and STAT. Because tyrosine phosphorylation is rare (about 0.05% of protein phosphorylation) but highly consequential, its control by opposing protein tyrosine phosphatases is tight.

Disease and drugs

Dysregulated tyrosine kinases — by mutation, amplification, or chromosomal translocation — are major drivers of cancer: the BCR::ABL1 fusion of the Philadelphia chromosome causes chronic myeloid leukemia, and EGFR, HER2, ALK, and KIT alterations drive subsets of lung cancer, breast cancer, and gastrointestinal stromal tumours. Tyrosine kinase inhibitors such as imatinib, erlotinib, and trastuzumab-era anti-HER2 agents are mainstays of targeted cancer therapy; JAK inhibitors are used in myelofibrosis and inflammatory disease.
