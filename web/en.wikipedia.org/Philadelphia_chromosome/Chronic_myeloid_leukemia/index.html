Chronic myeloid leukemia (CML), also known as chronic myelogenous leukemia, is a cancer of the white blood cells. It is a myeloproliferative neoplasm in which a mutated haematopoietic stem cell produces excessive numbers of granulocytes. CML is defined by the presence of the Philadelphia chromosome, a reciprocal translocation t(9;22) that creates the BCR::ABL1 fusion gene, whose constitutively active tyrosine kinase drives the disease.

Signs and symptoms

Many patients are diagnosed incidentally in the chronic phase from an abnormal blood count, with a markedly elevated white cell count and a left shift. Symptoms can include fatigue, weight loss, night sweats, early satiety and left-upper-quadrant discomfort from an enlarged spleen. Untreated, the disease progresses through an accelerated phase to blast crisis, an acute-leukemia-like state that is difficult to treat.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis rests on the blood film and bone marrow findings together with demonstration of the Philadelphia chromosome by karyotyping or FISH, or of BCR::ABL1 transcripts by PCR, which is also used to monitor response to therapy.

Treatment

CML was transformed by the introduction of imatinib in 2001, the first targeted cancer therapy, which inhibits the BCR::ABL1 kinase. Second- and third-generation tyrosine kinase inhibitors (dasatinib, nilotinib, bosutinib, ponatinib, and the allosteric inhibitor asciminib) are used for resistance or intolerance; resistance often involves point mutations in the kinase domain such as T315I. With modern therapy, most chronic-phase patients achieve deep molecular responses and near-normal life expectancy, and some with sustained deep responses can discontinue treatment under monitoring. Allogeneic stem cell transplantation remains an option for selected patients, especially in advanced phases.

Epidemiology

CML accounts for about 15% of adult leukemias, with an incidence of roughly 1–2 cases per 100,000 people per year and a median age at diagnosis around 65 years. It is slightly more common in men. The only established environmental risk factor is high-dose ionising radiation.
