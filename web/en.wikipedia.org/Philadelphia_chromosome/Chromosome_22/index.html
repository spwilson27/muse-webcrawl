Chromosome 22

Length~51 million base pairs (assembly-dependent; sequenced euchromatic portion ~33–35 Mb)
GenesRoughly 450–500 protein-coding genes
TypeAutosome, acrocentric
Sequenced1999 — first human chromosome published
Notable genesBCR, NF2, COMT, TBX1, CHEK2

Chromosome 22 is one of the 23 pairs of chromosomes in humans. People normally have two copies of this chromosome. It is the second-smallest human chromosome, spanning about 51 million base pairs including its repetitive short arm, and was the first human chromosome to be fully sequenced, with the sequence of its long arm published in December 1999 as a landmark of the Human Genome Project.

Genes

The long arm (22q) contains roughly 450–500 protein-coding genes. Notable examples include BCR (breakpoint cluster region), NF2 (merlin, a tumour suppressor), COMT (catechol-O-methyltransferase), TBX1, and CHEK2. The short arm (22p) is acrocentric and carries ribosomal RNA gene clusters shared with the other acrocentric chromosomes; it was only resolved much later by telomere-to-telomere sequencing efforts.

Diseases and disorders

Chromosome 22 is best known for the Philadelphia chromosome, a translocation t(9;22) that fuses BCR on chromosome 22 with ABL1 on chromosome 9, causing chronic myeloid leukemia. Deletion of the 22q11.2 region causes DiGeorge syndrome (22q11.2 deletion syndrome), with heart defects, immune deficiency, and palatal abnormalities; the reciprocal duplication produces its own syndrome. Mutations in NF2 cause neurofibromatosis type 2, characterised by bilateral vestibular schwannomas. Trisomy 22 is usually lethal in utero; mosaic forms can survive. Emanuel syndrome results from a derivative chromosome 22 inherited from a t(11;22) carrier parent — the only known recurrent non-Robertsonian translocation mediated by palindromic repeats.
