Alexandra Feodorovna (born Princess Alix of Hesse and by Rhine; 6 June 1872 – 17 July 1918) was the last Empress of Russia as the wife of Emperor Nicholas II. A granddaughter of Queen Victoria, she was a carrier of haemophilia, which afflicted her son, Tsarevich Alexei — the source of her reliance on the mystic Grigori Rasputin and of much of the scandal that undermined the monarchy before the Russian Revolution.

Early life and marriage

Alix was the sixth child of Louis IV, Grand Duke of Hesse, and Princess Alice of the United Kingdom, who died when Alix was six. Known in the family as "Sunny", she married Nicholas, heir to the Russian throne, on 26 November 1894, weeks after the death of Alexander III, converting to Russian Orthodoxy and taking the name Alexandra Feodorovna. The marriage was a genuine love match, but Alexandra's shyness and intense religiosity made her unpopular at court and in society.

Empress

Alexandra bore four daughters before the birth in 1904 of Alexei, whose haemophilia was kept secret. Desperate for help, she placed her faith in Rasputin, whose apparent ability to ease the boy's suffering gave him influence at court and, during the First World War — when Nicholas was at the front and Alexandra effectively supervised the government — provoked rumours of treason and corruption that fatally damaged the dynasty's prestige.

Revolution and death

After Nicholas abdicated in March 1917, the family was held under house arrest at Tsarskoe Selo, then exiled to Tobolsk and Yekaterinburg. On the night of 16–17 July 1918 Alexandra, Nicholas, their five children and four retainers were murdered by the Bolsheviks in the basement of the Ipatiev House. The Russian Orthodox Church canonised the family as passion bearers in 2000; their remains were reburied in the Peter and Paul Cathedral, St Petersburg, in 1998.
