Lysippos

Lysippos (Greek: Λύσιππος) was a Greek sculptor of the 4th century BCE, regarded as one of the three greatest sculptors of the Classical age alongside Scopas and Praxiteles, and the court sculptor of Alexander the Great.

Style

Lysippos introduced a new canon of proportions — smaller heads (one-eighth of body height) and slimmer, more elongated bodies than Polykleitos — and favoured dynamic poses viewable from multiple angles. His works survive mainly in Roman marble copies, including the Apoxyomenos (the Scraper) and the Farnese Hercules.

Legacy

Pliny credits him with some 1,500 works in bronze. His innovations bridged Classical and Hellenistic sculpture.
