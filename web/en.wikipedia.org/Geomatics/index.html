Geomatics

Geomatics is the discipline of gathering, storing, processing, and delivering geographic information. It encompasses the fields formerly identified as surveying, cartography, remote sensing, photogrammetry, and geographic information systems (GIS), integrating them with computer science and spatial data management.

Scope

Geomatics engineering deals with the measurement of the Earth's surface and the representation of spatial information. Its tools include satellite positioning (GNSS), total stations, terrestrial and airborne laser scanning (lidar), aerial and satellite imagery, and hydrographic sounding. The outputs feed mapping, land administration, construction, precision agriculture, and environmental monitoring.

Terminology

The term "geomatics" (French: géomatique) was coined in the 1960s by the French surveyor Bernard Dubuisson and gained prominence in Canada, where Laval University established an early programme under that name. It is closely related to the terms geoinformatics and geoinformation science, which emphasise the computational side of the field.

Applications

Geomatics supports cadastral registration of land rights, deformation monitoring of dams and bridges, topographic mapping, disaster response, and the creation of digital twins of cities. Professional surveyors in many jurisdictions are now formally trained and licensed as geomatics engineers.
