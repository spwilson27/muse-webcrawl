Hops are the flowers (also called seed cones or strobiles) of the hop plant Humulus lupulus, a member of the Cannabaceae family of flowering plants. They are used primarily as a bittering, flavouring and stability agent in beer, to which they impart bitter, zesty or citric flavours; the hop is also used for various purposes in other beverages and in herbal medicine.

Cultivation

The hop is a vigorous climbing perennial that grows up strings or wires in a hop yard (hop garden). Commercial production is concentrated in temperate regions, notably the Hallertau region of Germany and the Yakima Valley of Washington State in the United States. Plants are dioecious, and only the unfertilized female cones are used in brewing.

Brewing use

Hops contain alpha acids, which isomerize during the boil to give beer its characteristic bitterness, and essential oils that contribute aroma. Brewers distinguish bittering hops, added early in the boil, from aroma hops, added late or as "dry hopping" after fermentation. Hops also exert a mild preservative effect that historically helped beer keep longer, a property codified in the German Reinheitsgebot of 1516, which listed hops among beer's permitted ingredients.

Varieties

Well-known varieties include Saaz, Hallertau Mittelfrüh, Cascade, Centennial and Citra, each with a distinct aroma profile ranging from floral and spicy to citrus and tropical fruit.
