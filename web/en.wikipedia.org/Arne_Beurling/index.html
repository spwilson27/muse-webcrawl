Arne Beurling

Born: 3 February 1905, Gothenburg, Sweden

Died: 20 November 1986, Princeton, New Jersey, USA

Nationality: Swedish

Fields: Mathematics

Institutions: Uppsala University; Institute for Advanced Study

Known for: Beurling algebra, Beurling–Lax theorem, cryptanalysis of the Geheimschreiber

Arne Carl-August Beurling (3 February 1905 – 20 November 1986) was a Swedish mathematician and professor of mathematics at Uppsala University and later at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton. He worked extensively in harmonic analysis, complex analysis, and potential theory.

Cryptanalysis work

Beurling is also famous for his successful cryptanalysis of the Siemens and Halske T52, also known as the Geheimschreiber ("secret teleprinter"), used by Nazi Germany during World War II for encrypted teleprinter traffic. In the summer of 1940, working essentially alone over a period of about two weeks, Beurling solved the cipher using only intercepted ciphertext and pencil-and-paper methods — a feat often described as one of the greatest accomplishments in the history of cryptanalysis. Sweden consequently was able to read German teleprinter traffic passing through Swedish cables, giving advance warning of events such as the German invasion of the Soviet Union.

Mathematical work

Beurling's research contributed to the theory of Dirichlet series, function algebras (Beurling algebras), invariant subspaces of the shift operator (the Beurling–Lax theorem), and potential theory. His collected works have influenced generations of analysts.

Career

Beurling received his doctorate from Uppsala University in 1933 and became professor there in 1937. From 1954 until his retirement he held a professorship at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton, New Jersey, where he remained for the rest of his life.
