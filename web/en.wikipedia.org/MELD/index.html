The Model for End-Stage Liver Disease (MELD) is a scoring system for assessing the severity of chronic liver disease. It was initially developed to predict mortality following elective transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS) placement, but was subsequently found to be useful in predicting prognosis generally in patients with end-stage liver disease and is now used by the United Network for Organ Sharing (UNOS) and Eurotransplant to prioritise allocation of liver transplants.

Three-month mortality increases steeply as the MELD score rises.

Calculation

The original MELD score is calculated from three laboratory values — serum bilirubin, serum creatinine, and the international normalized ratio (INR) for prothrombin time — using a logarithmic formula:

MELD = 3.78 × ln(bilirubin) + 11.2 × ln(INR) + 9.57 × ln(creatinine) + 6.43

Laboratory values below 1.0 are set to 1.0, the score is capped at 40, and patients on dialysis receive a creatinine value of 4.0. The later MELD-Na variant incorporates serum sodium, which improves prediction in patients with hyponatraemia, and MELD 3.0 further added albumin and a sex adjustment.

Interpretation

In hospitalised patients with end-stage liver disease, approximate three-month mortality ranges from under 2% at a MELD score of 9 or below to over 70% at scores of 40. On transplant waiting lists, candidates are ranked largely by MELD score so that organs go to the sickest patients first, replacing the older time-based system.
