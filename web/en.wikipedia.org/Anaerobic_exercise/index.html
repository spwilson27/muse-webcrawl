Anaerobic exercise

Anaerobic exercise is a type of exercise that breaks down glucose in the body without using oxygen; anaerobic means "without oxygen" or "without air". It consists of short, intense bursts of physical activity in which the energy demand exceeds the oxygen supply available. Examples include sprinting, weightlifting, jumping and high-intensity interval training (HIIT).

Metabolism

During intense exercise, muscles first use stored ATP and phosphocreatine, then rely on anaerobic glycolysis, which splits glucose into pyruvate and converts it to lactate, yielding energy much faster than aerobic respiration but in smaller amounts per molecule of glucose. The resulting metabolic by-products contribute to the burning sensation and fatigue that limit anaerobic efforts to seconds or a few minutes.

Training effects

Regular anaerobic training increases muscular strength, power, speed and muscle mass, improves bone density and enhances the body's ability to buffer and tolerate lactate. Anaerobic capacity is measured by tests such as the Wingate test. In contrast to aerobic exercise — long-duration, moderate-intensity activity such as distance running — anaerobic exercise cannot be sustained continuously for long periods.
