The Pacific Gas and Electric Company (PG&E) is an American investor-owned utility company that provides natural gas and electricity to most of Northern and Central California. It serves approximately 16 million people across a 70,000-square-mile service territory, making it one of the largest combined natural gas and electric utilities in the United States.

History

PG&E was formed in 1905 by the merger of the San Francisco Gas and Electric Company and the California Gas and Electric Corporation. The company expanded rapidly in the early twentieth century, building hydroelectric plants in the Sierra Nevada and acquiring smaller utilities across the state. Its dams and flumes in the Feather River canyon became landmark engineering projects.

Safety controversies and bankruptcy

The company has faced major legal and financial crises. In 2010, a PG&E natural gas pipeline exploded in San Bruno, California, killing eight people; the company was later convicted of federal charges related to the disaster. Equipment owned by PG&E was also linked to devastating wildfires, including the 2018 Camp Fire, the deadliest in California history. Facing billions of dollars in potential wildfire liabilities, PG&E Corporation filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in January 2019, emerging in 2020 after agreeing to a settlement that included a fund for fire victims.

Operations

PG&E operates an extensive generation portfolio including the Diablo Canyon Power Plant, California's last operating nuclear power station, as well as hydroelectric, natural gas, solar, and battery storage facilities. It maintains roughly 106,000 miles of electric distribution lines and 42,000 miles of natural gas distribution pipelines.
