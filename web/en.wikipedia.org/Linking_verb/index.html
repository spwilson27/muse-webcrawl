Linking verb

In traditional grammar, a linking verb is a verb that connects the subject of a sentence to a subject complement — a noun, pronoun, or adjective that describes or identifies the subject — rather than expressing an action. The term is closely related to the concept of the copula, of which the English verb be is the primary example: in "The sky is blue", is links the sky to its description blue.

English examples

Besides all forms of be, common linking verbs include the sensory verbs seem, appear, look, feel, smell, taste, and sound, and verbs of becoming such as become, grow, turn, and remain. Many of these can also be used as ordinary action verbs: compare "She looked happy" (linking) with "She looked at the painting" (action).

Analysis

Because the complement describes the subject rather than receiving an action, linking-verb constructions take adjectives rather than adverbs ("I feel bad", not "badly", in formal usage). In modern linguistic analysis, linking verbs are often treated as raising or copular verbs whose complement is a small clause, but the traditional term remains common in language teaching.
