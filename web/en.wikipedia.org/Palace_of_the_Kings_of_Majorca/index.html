Palace of the Kings of Majorca

The Palace of the Kings of Majorca (French: Palais des Rois de Majorque, Catalan: Palau dels Reis de Mallorca) is a medieval palace and fortified complex in Perpignan, in the Pyrénées-Orientales department of southern France. It was built from the late 13th century as the mainland residence of the Kings of Majorca, whose kingdom — created by James I of Aragon for his son James II in 1276 — included the Balearic Islands, Roussillon, and Cerdanya.

History

Construction began around 1276 under James II of Majorca. The palace combined royal apartments, a chapel, administrative buildings, gardens, and strong fortifications, reflecting Perpignan's role as a continental capital of the Majorcan crown. After the Crown of Aragon reabsorbed the kingdom in 1344, the palace remained an important fortress on the frontier between France and Spain. Following the Treaty of the Pyrenees (1659), Roussillon passed to France, and Vauban later incorporated the palace into Perpignan's citadel.

Architecture

The palace is arranged around a large arcaded courtyard and blends Romanesque and Gothic elements, built largely of brick and stone. Notable features include the two superimposed chapels (the lower Chapel of Saint Mary Magdalene and the upper Chapel of the Holy Cross), the great hall, and the royal apartments with their distinctive loggia. The complex is noted for its Hispano-Moorish decorative influences, typical of the medieval Crown of Aragon.

Present day

Owned by the departmental council of Pyrénées-Orientales, the palace is open to the public and hosts concerts and exhibitions, including the summer Guitares au Palais festival.
