Florida State University

Established: 1851
Location: Tallahassee, Florida
Students: ~45,000
Colours: Garnet and gold
Athletics: Seminoles

Florida State University (FSU) is a public research university in Tallahassee, Florida, United States. Founded in 1851, it is a senior member of the State University System of Florida and is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity." Florida State traces its origins to the West Florida Seminary, one of two seminaries authorised by the Florida legislature in 1851; the site of the present campus is the oldest continuous site of higher education in Florida.

History

The seminary became the Florida State College in 1901 and then the Florida State College for Women in 1905. After World War II, under pressure from returning veterans, it became coeducational and took its present name in 1947. The university grew into a major research institution, and in 1994 was chosen to host the National High Magnetic Field Laboratory.

Academics

FSU comprises 16 colleges offering more than 360 degree programs. It is noted for programs in criminology, creative writing, film, meteorology, and the sciences, and operates the FSU College of Medicine, founded in 2000. Its libraries include the Robert M. Strozier Library.

Athletics

The Florida State Seminoles compete in the Atlantic Coast Conference. The football team, long led by coach Bobby Bowden, has won three national championships (1993, 1999, and 2013) and plays at Doak Campbell Stadium. The university's use of the Seminole name is with the support of the Seminole Tribe of Florida.
