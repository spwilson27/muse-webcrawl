The River Liffey (Irish: An Life) is a river in eastern Ireland that flows through the centre of Dublin. It rises on the slopes of Kippure in the Wicklow Mountains and flows 132 kilometres through counties Wicklow, Kildare, and Dublin before entering the Irish Sea at Dublin Bay.

Course and use

The upper river is impounded at Poulaphouca, Golden Falls, and Leixlip for hydroelectric generation and for Dublin's water supply; the reservoir at Poulaphouca (Blessington Lakes) is the largest in the catchment. West of Dublin the river passes through Celbridge, Leixlip, and Lucan, then through the Strawberry Beds valley and the Phoenix Park before reaching the city quays.

The Liffey in Dublin

Within Dublin the walled quays built from the seventeenth century onward define the city's structure, dividing the northside from the southside — a social as well as geographic distinction. Bridges across the city reach include the Ha'penny Bridge (1816), O'Connell Bridge, the Samuel Beckett Bridge designed by Santiago Calatrava, and the Sean O'Casey Bridge. The river is immortalised in James Joyce's Finnegans Wake, whose personification Anna Livia Plurabelle gives the Liffey a voice, and in the city's popular nickname for it, the "Liffey water" once used in Guinness brewing.
