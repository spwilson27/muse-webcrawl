Daniel Kahneman

Daniel Kahneman (March 5, 1934 – March 27, 2024) was an Israeli-American psychologist and economist renowned for his work on the psychology of judgment and decision-making, as well as behavioral economics. He was awarded the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences in 2002, together with Vernon L. Smith, for integrating psychological research into economic science, especially concerning human judgment and decision-making under uncertainty — a rare honour for a psychologist.

Life

Kahneman was born in Tel Aviv while his mother was visiting relatives; his family lived in Paris, where he spent the years of the Nazi occupation as a small child — experiences he later credited with sparking his interest in human nature. After the war the family emigrated to Mandatory Palestine. He studied psychology and mathematics at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem, served as an officer in the Israel Defense Forces' psychology branch, and earned his doctorate at the University of California, Berkeley, in 1961. He later taught at the Hebrew University and at Princeton University, where he was Eugene Higgins Professor of Psychology.

Research

With Amos Tversky, Kahneman launched the heuristics-and-biases research programme, documenting systematic errors in intuitive judgment: representativeness, availability, and anchoring and adjustment. Their 1979 paper introduced prospect theory, which describes how people evaluate gains and losses asymmetrically — losses loom larger than gains — and it became one of the most-cited papers in economics. Kahneman later studied experienced well-being, distinguishing the "experiencing self" from the "remembering self", and phenomena such as the peak–end rule and duration neglect.

His bestselling book Thinking, Fast and Slow (2011) popularised his framework of two modes of thought: fast, intuitive "System 1" and slow, deliberative "System 2". With Olivier Sibony and Cass Sunstein he later wrote Noise: A Flaw in Human Judgment (2021), on unwanted variability in decisions. Kahneman died on 27 March 2024, aged 90.
