Alcoholic drink

Active ingredient: Ethanol
Main types: Beer, wine, spirits
Legal status: Regulated in most countries

An alcoholic drink (also called an alcoholic beverage, adult beverage, or simply a drink) is a drink that contains ethanol, a type of alcohol produced by fermentation of grains, fruits, or other sources of sugar. The consumption of alcoholic drinks, often referred to as "drinking", plays an important social role in many cultures.

Types

Alcoholic drinks are divided into three classes: beers, wines, and spirits. Beers, typically 4–6% alcohol by volume, are brewed from cereal grains; wines, usually 9–16%, are fermented from grapes or other fruits; and spirits such as whisky, vodka, and rum are produced by distillation, reaching 40% or more. Alcohol content is commonly measured as alcohol by volume (ABV).

History

Fermented beverages are among humanity's oldest drinks: chemical evidence from pottery jars in Jiahu, China, dates a fermented drink of rice, honey, and fruit to around 7000 BCE. Beer and wine were staples of ancient Egyptian and Mesopotamian societies, and distillation spread through the medieval Islamic world into Europe, where monastic brewing and regional wine cultures developed.

Health and society

Ethanol is a psychoactive drug that depresses the central nervous system. Excessive consumption causes intoxication and is associated with liver disease, cancer, addiction (alcohol use disorder), and accidents; health authorities note that risk rises with intake. Most countries regulate the sale and consumption of alcohol through minimum drinking ages, licensing, and taxation, and some religions, notably Islam, prohibit its consumption. Global consumption averages roughly 6 litres of pure alcohol per person per year.
