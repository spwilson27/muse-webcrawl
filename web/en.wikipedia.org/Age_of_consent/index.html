Age of consent


Ages of consent vary by jurisdiction worldwide

The age of consent is the age at which a person is considered to be legally competent to consent to sexual acts. Consequently, an adult who engages in sexual activity with a person younger than the age of consent is said to commit a criminal offence, commonly termed statutory rape. The age of consent varies widely by jurisdiction, ranging roughly from 11 to 21 years, with most countries setting it between 14 and 18.

Variation and related rules

Many jurisdictions have close-in-age exemptions (sometimes called "Romeo and Juliet laws") that decriminalise consensual activity between minors of similar age, and some apply different rules depending on positions of trust or authority, such as teacher–student relationships. Related but distinct legal thresholds include the age of majority, the marriageable age, and the ages for voting, drinking and criminal responsibility. Historically, ages of consent in many Western countries were as low as 10–13 in the 19th century and were raised to 16–18 by social reform campaigns around the turn of the 20th century.
