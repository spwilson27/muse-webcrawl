Kanishka the Great

Kanishka I
Emperor of the Kushan Empire
Reign: c. 127 – c. 150 CE
Predecessor: Vima Kadphises
Successor: Huvishka
Dynasty: Kushan

Kanishka I (c. 127 – c. 150 CE) was an emperor of the Kushan dynasty in the second century, famed for his military, political and spiritual achievements. Under his reign the Kushan Empire reached its greatest extent, stretching from Central Asia across the Hindu Kush into northern India, and became a major centre of trade on the Silk Road.

Reign and conquests

Kanishka succeeded Vima Kadphises and ruled from his capital at Peshawar (Purushapura), with a secondary capital at Mathura. His empire linked the markets of China, Persia and India, and his coinage — bearing images of Greek, Iranian, Hindu and Buddhist deities — reflects its cosmopolitan character.

Buddhism

Kanishka is remembered in Buddhist tradition as a great patron of Mahayana Buddhism, second only to Ashoka. He is credited with convening the Fourth Buddhist Council in Kashmir, at which the Sarvastivada canon was systematised. He also built the great Kanishka stupa at Peshawar, one of the tallest structures of the ancient world.

Legacy

The era beginning with his accession was long used as a calendar era in northern India. His patronage helped spread Buddhism along the Silk Road into Central Asia and China. See also Kushan Empire.
