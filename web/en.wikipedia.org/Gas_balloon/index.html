Gas balloon

A gas balloon is a balloon that rises because it is filled with a gas lighter than air, such as hydrogen, helium or coal gas. Unlike a hot-air balloon, it has no burner; pilots control altitude mainly by dropping ballast to rise and venting lifting gas to descend.

History

Jacques Charles and the Robert brothers flew a hydrogen balloon in 1783, shortly after the Montgolfier hot-air flights. Gas balloons became important for scientific ascents, military observation, sport racing and long-distance records during the nineteenth and twentieth centuries.

Operation

The envelope is filled at launch so that it becomes fully expanded at altitude. Temperature changes between day and night strongly affect lift, so long flights require careful ballast management. Safety concerns over flammable hydrogen led many users to prefer helium despite cost and scarcity.

Uses

Gas balloons are still flown in competitions such as the Gordon Bennett Cup, and uncrewed gas balloons are used for weather, research and communications platforms.
