Coolant

A coolant is a substance, typically liquid or gas, that is used to reduce or regulate the temperature of a system. An ideal coolant has high thermal capacity, low viscosity, is low-cost, non-toxic, chemically inert, and causes neither corrosion nor electrical insulation problems.

Applications

Water is the most common coolant in machinery and heat exchangers because of its high heat capacity and low cost. In internal combustion engines, a mixture of water and ethylene glycol (antifreeze) circulates through the engine block and radiator, simultaneously raising the boiling point and lowering the freezing point while inhibiting corrosion. Other coolants include air, oils, molten salts, liquid metals such as sodium, and specialised dielectric fluids used to cool electronics.

Nuclear reactors

Nuclear reactors depend on coolants — ordinary or heavy water, gases such as carbon dioxide and helium, or liquid metals — to remove fission heat and, in many designs, to drive turbines for power generation. Loss of coolant is a serious accident scenario, as in the Three Mile Island and Fukushima accidents, because residual decay heat can melt the core.

Cutting fluids

In machining, coolants (cutting fluids) are applied to the cutting zone to carry away heat, lubricate the tool interface, and flush away chips, improving tool life and surface finish. See also antifreeze and heat transfer.
