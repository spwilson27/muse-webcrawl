North Vancouver is a name commonly applied to the communities on the north shore of Burrard Inlet in British Columbia, Canada, opposite the city of Vancouver. Legally there are two adjacent municipalities: the City of North Vancouver, a compact urban municipality around the foot of Lonsdale Avenue, and the District of North Vancouver, a larger, more suburban municipality surrounding the city on three sides and extending westward and eastward along the mountains.

Geography

North Vancouver is built on the lower slopes of the North Shore Mountains, part of the Coast Mountains, beneath peaks such as Grouse Mountain, Mount Seymour and Mount Fromme. It is connected to Vancouver by the Lions Gate Bridge to the west and the Ironworkers Memorial Second Narrows Crossing to the east, and by the SeaBus passenger ferry across Burrard Inlet to Lonsdale Quay.

History

The area is the traditional territory of the Squamish Nation and Tsleil-Waututh Nation. Moodyville, established around a sawmill in the 1860s, was the first settlement on Burrard Inlet, predating Vancouver itself. The District was incorporated in 1891, and the City separated from it in 1907. Shipbuilding was a major industry through both world wars.

Recreation

The mountain setting makes North Vancouver one of Canada's leading centres for outdoor recreation, including skiing at Grouse Mountain and Mount Seymour, mountain biking on an extensive trail network, and hiking in Lynn Canyon Park, home to a suspension bridge, and Capilano River Regional Park, site of the Capilano Suspension Bridge.
