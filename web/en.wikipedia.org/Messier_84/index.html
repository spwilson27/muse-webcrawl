DesignationsM84, NGC 4374, UGC 7494

Messier 84 (also known as NGC 4374) is an elliptical or lenticular galaxy in the constellation Virgo. It lies in the heavily populated inner core of the Virgo Cluster of galaxies, at a distance of roughly 60 million light-years from Earth. It was discovered by Charles Messier on 18 March 1781, the same night he catalogued several neighbouring galaxies.

Characteristics

Messier 84 forms a close pair with the similar galaxy Messier 86, and the two dominate a chain of galaxies known as Markarian's Chain. Observations in radio wavelengths reveal two jets of material emanating from the galaxy's centre, as well as a disk of rapidly rotating gas and stars, indicating the presence of a supermassive black hole of about 1.5 billion solar masses at its core.

Supernovae

Three supernovae have been observed in Messier 84: SN 1957B, SN 1980I, and SN 1991bg. This is among the highest numbers of supernova events recorded in any single galaxy.
