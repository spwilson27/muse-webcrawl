Buddha's hand (Citrus medica var. sarcodactylis) is an unusually shaped citron variety whose fruit is segmented into finger-like sections, resembling a hand with the fingers in a gesture of prayer. The fruit has thick peel, little or no pulp, and few or no seeds, but it is highly fragrant.

Description

Like other citrons, the Buddha's hand is a small, thorny shrub or tree with irregular branches and large oblong leaves. The fruit's rind exudes an intense lemon-like perfume. Some fruits have closed fingers, while others, particularly those grown in China, have open, splayed fingers.

Uses

In China the fruit is a traditional symbol of happiness, longevity and good fortune, and it is frequently placed on altars as an offering. The peel is used in Chinese and Japanese cooking to flavor dishes and sweets, and the fruit is candied or used to perfume rooms and clothing. It is sometimes used as an ornamental plant in gardens in warm climates, and its zest can substitute for lemon zest.

Cultivation

The plant is sensitive to frost as well as intense heat and drought. It grows best in a temperate climate, and trees are usually propagated from cuttings taken from plants two to four years old.
