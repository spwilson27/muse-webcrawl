Standard of living

Standard of living varies widely between and within countries.

The standard of living is the level of income, comforts and services available to an individual, community or society. It is generally considered to include wealth, comfort, material goods, and necessities. A standard of living is a broader concept than quality of life, which also encompasses non-material factors such as social relationships, health, and environmental quality.

Measurement

Standards of living are often measured using indicators such as gross domestic product (GDP) per capita, life expectancy, literacy rates, infant mortality, and access to clean water, sanitation, housing, and healthcare. Composite indices such as the Human Development Index (HDI), published by the United Nations Development Programme, combine income, education, and life expectancy into a single measure.

Factors

Many factors influence the standard of living, including economic growth, employment, inflation, income distribution, technology, public policy, and political stability. Two countries with similar GDP per capita may have very different standards of living depending on how income is distributed and what public services are provided.
