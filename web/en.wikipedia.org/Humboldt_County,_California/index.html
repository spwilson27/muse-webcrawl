Humboldt County, California

Humboldt County is a county located in the U.S. state of California. As of the 2020 census, the population was 136,463. The county seat is Eureka. Humboldt County comprises the Eureka–Arcata micropolitan statistical area.

Located on California's North Coast, about 270 miles (435 km) north of San Francisco, the county is known for its dense redwood forests — it contains about 110 miles (177 km) of coastline, more than any other county in the state — and for Redwood National and State Parks. The region is also associated with the timber industry, commercial fishing, dairy farming, and, more controversially, cannabis cultivation as part of the so-called Emerald Triangle.

History

The area was originally inhabited by Native American peoples including the Wiyot, Yurok, Hupa, Karuk, Chilula, Mattole, and Nongatl. European-American settlement began with the arrival of ships in Humboldt Bay in 1850, during the California Gold Rush. The county was formed in 1853 from part of Trinity County and named for Humboldt Bay, which had itself been named for the German naturalist Alexander von Humboldt.

The nineteenth century saw violent conflict between settlers and Native peoples, including the 1860 Wiyot massacre on Indian Island in Humboldt Bay, an atrocity that drew national condemnation. The Wiyot Tribe has since regained land on the island, and the city of Eureka returned Indian Island to the tribe in 2019.

Geography

According to the U.S. Census Bureau, the county has a total area of 4,052 square miles (10,490 km²), of which 3,568 square miles (9,240 km²) is land and 484 square miles (1,250 km²) is water. The county is heavily forested and mountainous, crossed by the Coast Ranges and drained by rivers including the Eel, Klamath, Mad, Trinity, and Van Duzen. The Lost Coast, in the county's southwest, contains some of the most undeveloped shoreline in California.

Communities

Eureka is the largest city and county seat. Other communities include Arcata (home of California State Polytechnic University, Humboldt), Fortuna, Ferndale, Trinidad, Blue Lake, Rio Dell, McKinleyville, and Garberville in the south of the county.
