Parliament of Kazakhstan

The Parliament of the Republic of Kazakhstan is the bicameral legislature of Kazakhstan. Its upper house is the Senate and its lower house is the Mazhilis. Members of the Mazhilis are called deputies, and the body sits in the capital, Astana.

The Senate consists of 50 members: two are elected from each of the country's regions and largest cities, and additional members are appointed by the President. The Mazhilis has 98 deputies, elected following constitutional reforms approved in a 2022 referendum: 69 by party-list proportional representation and 29 in single-member districts, replacing the previous system in which some seats were allocated through the Assembly of People of Kazakhstan.

Functions

Parliament passes laws, approves the state budget, ratifies international treaties, and can express no confidence in the government under defined circumstances. Constitutional amendments require approval by both chambers at successive readings. Although Kazakhstan's system is strongly presidential — the President appoints key officials and can dissolve the Mazhilis — constitutional reforms in 2017 and 2022 transferred some powers to Parliament, including giving the Mazhilis a greater role in forming the government.

History

The Parliament was established under the 1995 Constitution, succeeding the unicameral Supreme Council of the Soviet era. Elections have been dominated for most of the post-independence period by the pro-presidential party — now called Amanat (formerly Nur Otan) — and international observers have repeatedly criticised Kazakh elections for falling short of democratic standards.
