Tsar Bomba (Russian: Tsar′-bomba; "Tsar-bomb"), codenamed AN602 and originally designed as RDS-220, was a thermonuclear aerial bomb developed in the Soviet Union and detonated on 30 October 1961 over Novaya Zemlya. At about 50 megatons of TNT, it remains the most powerful man-made explosion ever detonated — some 3,300 times as powerful as the bomb dropped on Hiroshima.

Design and test

Ordered by Nikita Khrushchev amid the Berlin Crisis of 1961 and designed by Andrei Sakharov, Yuli Khariton and the KB-11 team, the weapon weighed 27 tonnes and was dropped from a specially modified Tu-95V bomber over the Mityushikha Bay test range. The bomb was retarded by a huge parachute to give the plane time to escape. The design was deliberately limited: the planned third stage's uranium-238 tamper was replaced with lead, halving the yield from 100 to 50 megatons and greatly reducing the radioactive fallout.

Effects and legacy

The fireball was some 8 km wide and nearly reached the release altitude; the mushroom cloud rose to about 67 km. Windows broke up to 900 km away, the shockwave circled the Earth three times, and all buildings in the nearest settlement (Severny, 55 km away) were destroyed. The test helped prompt the Partial Test Ban Treaty of 1963, and the bomb itself, too heavy for practical delivery, remained a demonstration of power rather than a serviceable weapon.
