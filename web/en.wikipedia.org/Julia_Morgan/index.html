Julia Morgan (1872 – 1957) was an American architect who designed more than 700 buildings in California and elsewhere, most famously Hearst Castle at San Simeon for newspaper magnate William Randolph Hearst. She was the first woman admitted to the architecture program of the École des Beaux-Arts in Paris and the first woman licensed as an architect in California.

Education and early career

Morgan was born in San Francisco and raised in Oakland. She graduated from the University of California, Berkeley in 1894 with a degree in civil engineering — encouraged by her mentor Bernard Maybeck — and then persisted through repeated rejections to gain admission to the École des Beaux-Arts, earning her certificate in 1902. Licensed in California in 1904, she opened her own San Francisco practice and won attention for the Mills College bell tower, El Campanil (1904), which survived the 1906 earthquake undamaged, helping establish her reputation for reinforced-concrete construction.

Work

After the 1906 San Francisco earthquake she received major commissions, including the rebuilding of the Fairmont Hotel. She designed dozens of buildings for the YWCA and women's organizations, churches, residences and the Asilomar Conference Grounds in Pacific Grove. From 1919 until 1947 she collaborated with Hearst on his hilltop estate at San Simeon — its main house, guest cottages, twin-towered facade, Roman-style Neptune Pool and indoor Roman Pool — a vast, eclectic project that consumed much of her later career.

Legacy

Morgan closed her office in 1951 and died in 1957. In 2014 she became the first woman to receive the AIA Gold Medal, awarded posthumously. Hearst Castle is now a California State Park and National Historic Landmark.
