Michelin

Michelin (Manufacture française des pneumatiques Michelin) is a French multinational tyre manufacturing company based in Clermont-Ferrand, France. Founded in 1889 by brothers Édouard and André Michelin, it is one of the world's largest tyre manufacturers and is also famous for its Michelin Guide restaurant ratings and its mascot, the Michelin Man (Bibendum).

History

The Michelin brothers took over a rubber factory in Clermont-Ferrand in 1889. In 1891 Édouard patented a removable pneumatic bicycle tyre, which could be repaired in minutes rather than hours. The company went on to pioneer the detachable car tyre, the radial tyre (patented 1946 and marketed as the Michelin X), run-flat tyres and the Tweel airless tyre concept.

Products and operations

Michelin manufactures tyres for cars, trucks, aircraft, motorcycles, bicycles, agricultural and earthmover equipment, and the Space Shuttle. Its brands include Michelin, BFGoodrich, Uniroyal and Kleber. The company operates plants in many countries and employs well over one hundred thousand people.

The Michelin Guide

First published in 1900 to encourage motoring, the Guide Michelin became the most influential restaurant guide in the world. Its anonymous inspectors award one, two or three stars — three stars denoting "exceptional cuisine, worth a special journey." The guide also awards the Bib Gourmand for good value and, more recently, Green Stars for sustainability.

Bibendum

The Michelin Man, created in 1898 from a stack of tyres sketched by artist O'Galop, is named Bibendum after the Latin phrase Nunc est bibendum ("Now is the time to drink") — the tyre "drinks up" obstacles on the road.
