Cabibbo–Kobayashi–Maskawa matrix

The Cabibbo–Kobayashi–Maskawa matrix (CKM matrix, quark mixing matrix, sometimes called the KM matrix) is a unitary matrix containing information on the strength of the flavour-changing weak interaction. Technically, it specifies the mismatch of quantum states of quarks when they propagate freely and when they participate in the weak interactions.

The matrix was introduced in 1973 by Makoto Kobayashi and Toshihide Maskawa to explain CP violation within the Standard Model, extending earlier work by Nicola Cabibbo (1963) on the mixing of the first two generations of quarks. With three generations, the CKM matrix is a 3×3 unitary matrix described by three mixing angles and one CP-violating phase.

Structure

The matrix relates the down-type quark mass eigenstates (d, s, b) to their weak-interaction eigenstates (d′, s′, b′). Its elements Vud, Vus, Vub and so on give the coupling strengths for each quark transition. Most elements are measured experimentally; the near-diagonal entries are close to one, while mixings between the first and third generations are strongly suppressed.

CP violation and the unitarity triangle

The single complex phase in the CKM matrix accounts for CP violation observed in kaon and B-meson systems. Kobayashi and Maskawa shared part of the 2008 Nobel Prize in Physics for this work. The unitarity conditions can be represented geometrically as triangles in the complex plane; experiments at B-factories such as BaBar and Belle have precisely tested these relations.
