Padang is the capital and largest city of the Indonesian province of West Sumatra. Located on the western coast of the island of Sumatra facing the Indian Ocean, it serves as the principal gateway to the Minangkabau highlands and had a population of about 920,000 within the city limits at the 2020 census.

History

Padang developed as a trading post for pepper and gold from the Minangkabau interior. The Dutch East India Company established a post there in the seventeenth century, and the town was briefly occupied by the British in the late eighteenth century. During the Padri War of the early nineteenth century the region was incorporated into the Dutch East Indies.

On 30 September 2009, a magnitude 7.9 earthquake struck offshore near the city, killing over a thousand people in the region and severely damaging Padang's buildings; the city has since rebuilt with greater attention to seismic risk and tsunami evacuation.

Culture and cuisine

Padang is the homeland of the Minangkabau people, the world's largest matrilineal society. The city lends its name to Padang cuisine — most famously rendang and the masakan Padang style of serving many small dishes — which is served in "Padang restaurants" throughout Indonesia and beyond. Landmarks include the Adityawarman Museum, the Grand Mosque of West Sumatra, and the beaches and offshore islands popular with surfers traveling to the Mentawai Islands.
