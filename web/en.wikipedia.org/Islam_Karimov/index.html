Islam Karimov

Islam Abduganiyevich Karimov (30 January 1938 – 2 September 2016) was an Uzbek politician who served as the first President of Uzbekistan, from the country's independence in 1991 until his death in 2016. Previously he was the last First Secretary of the Communist Party of the Uzbek SSR (1989–1991). His rule was widely described as authoritarian, with pervasive human-rights abuses documented by international organizations.

Early life and Soviet career

Born in Samarkand and raised partly in an orphanage, Karimov trained as an engineer and economist, rising through the Uzbek planning bureaucracy to become minister of finance and a deputy chairman of the republic's council of ministers. In June 1989 he became First Secretary of the Uzbek Communist Party.

Presidency

Karimov declared Uzbekistan's independence on 31 August 1991 and won the December 1991 presidential election. He repeatedly extended his rule through referendums and elections criticized by observers as neither free nor fair. His government suppressed political Islam, most violently in the 2005 Andijan massacre, when security forces killed hundreds of protesters. Uzbekistan under Karimov balanced relations between Russia, China, and the United States, hosting a U.S. air base at Karshi-Khanabad from 2001 to 2005.

Death and legacy

Karimov suffered a brain hemorrhage in August 2016; his death was announced on 2 September 2016. He was buried in Samarkand. He was succeeded by his prime minister, Shavkat Mirziyoyev, who subsequently pursued a program of cautious liberalization and criticized aspects of Karimov's legacy.
