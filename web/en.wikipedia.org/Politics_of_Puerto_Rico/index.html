The politics of Puerto Rico take place within the framework of a republican form of government, with Puerto Rico organised as an unincorporated territory of the United States. The Governor of Puerto Rico heads the executive branch, the Legislative Assembly makes up the legislative branch, and the Supreme Court of Puerto Rico heads the judicial branch. Puerto Ricans are United States citizens but cannot vote in US presidential elections and are represented in Congress by a non-voting Resident Commissioner.

Status question

The defining axis of Puerto Rican party politics is the island's political status. The Popular Democratic Party (PPD) historically favoured the current commonwealth (Estado Libre Asociado) arrangement, the New Progressive Party (PNP) advocates US statehood, and the Puerto Rican Independence Party (PIP) supports independence. A series of status plebiscites (1967, 1993, 1998, 2012, 2017, 2020 and 2024) have produced contested results, with statehood winning recent votes amid boycotts and disputes over ballot options; any change requires action by the US Congress.

Government and oversight

Since 2016, Puerto Rico's finances have been overseen by a federally appointed Financial Oversight and Management Board created by the PROMESA law, following the island's debt crisis and the restructuring of roughly 0 billion in public debt. Local politics has also been shaken by scandal, including the 2019 Telegramgate protests that forced Governor Ricardo Rosselló's resignation.
