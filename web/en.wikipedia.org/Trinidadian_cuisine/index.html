Trinidadian cuisine


Doubles with chickpea curry and chutneys

The cuisine of Trinidad and Tobago is a fusion of African, Indian, European, Chinese, Amerindian, and Middle Eastern influences, reflecting the island's diverse population and colonial history.

Popular dishes

Well-known dishes include doubles (curried chickpeas in fried flatbread), roti served with curry goat, chicken, or shrimp, pelau (a one-pot rice dish with pigeon peas and meat), callaloo (a leafy green soup with coconut milk and okra), and bake and shark, a fried bread sandwich with shark sold at Maracas Beach. Street food such as aloo pie and pholourie is common.

Influences

Indian indentured workers arriving from 1845 brought curries, flatbreads, and sweets; African traditions contributed ground provisions and one-pot cooking; and Chinese, Syrian-Lebanese, and European communities added their own dishes and techniques. Hot pepper sauce, often made from Scotch bonnet or Moruga Scorpion peppers, accompanies most meals.

Drinks and sweets

Popular drinks include sorrel at Christmas, mauby, peanut punch, and sea moss drinks. Sweets range from Indian mithai such as barfi and kurma to tamarind balls and red mango.
