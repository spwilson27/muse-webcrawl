The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) is India's central bank and regulatory body responsible for the regulation of the Indian banking system and the control of the Indian currency, the rupee. It is owned by the Ministry of Finance, Government of India, and is responsible for the control, issue, and maintenance of the supply of the Indian rupee, as well as the management of the country's main payment systems.

History

The RBI was established on 1 April 1935 under the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934, following recommendations of the Hilton Young Commission. It began as a privately owned institution and was nationalised in 1949, after which it became fully owned by the Government of India. Its headquarters, originally in Calcutta (now Kolkata), were moved permanently to Bombay (now Mumbai) in 1937.

Functions

The Bank's functions include formulating and implementing monetary policy through its Monetary Policy Committee, issuing currency notes, regulating and supervising banks and non-banking financial companies, managing foreign exchange under the Foreign Exchange Management Act, and maintaining price stability while keeping in mind the objective of growth. The RBI also acts as banker to the government and to commercial banks, and serves as the lender of last resort.

Structure

The Bank is governed by a central board of directors headed by the Governor, with up to four deputy governors. It has regional offices in most state capitals and several specialised subsidiary institutions.
