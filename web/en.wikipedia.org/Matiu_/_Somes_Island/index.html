Matiu / Somes Island

Island in Wellington Harbour, New Zealand

Matiu / Somes Island is the largest of three small islands in Wellington Harbour, New Zealand, lying about 3 kilometres south of Petone and the Hutt River mouth. It covers some 25 hectares, rises to 38 metres, and has carried the dual Māori and European name — Matiu / Somes — officially since 2009.

History

According to Māori tradition the island was named for Matiu, a descendant (or relative) of the explorer Kupe. In the nineteenth century it was renamed for Joseph Somes, a director of the New Zealand Company. From 1866 the island served as a human quarantine station — for smallpox and other diseases among arriving immigrants — and later as an animal quarantine station. It was used as an internment camp for enemy aliens during both world wars, and as a quarantine facility by the Ministry of Agriculture until 1995.

Conservation

Since 1981 the island has been reforested and managed as a scientific reserve by the Department of Conservation. Rats and other predators were eradicated, and native species including tuatara, Wellington green geckos, little blue penguins and the island's endemic wētā have been introduced or protected. Day trips by ferry from Wellington make it a popular destination, with entry controlled by biosecurity checks.
