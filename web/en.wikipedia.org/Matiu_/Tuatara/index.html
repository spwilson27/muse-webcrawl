Matiu / Somes Island — Tuatara

Tuatara were reintroduced to Matiu / Somes Island in Wellington Harbour as part of the island's restoration as a predator-free sanctuary for New Zealand wildlife. Tuatara are reptiles endemic to New Zealand, the only surviving members of the order Rhynchocephalia, and are protected; they survive naturally on offshore islands free of rats and other mammalian predators.

Reintroduction

After rats were eradicated from Matiu / Somes Island, conservation managers began returning native species that had been lost from the Wellington region. Brothers Island tuatara were transferred to the island beginning in 1998, establishing a new population within sight of the capital city. The tuatara have bred on the island, and burrows are monitored by the Department of Conservation and volunteers.

Significance

The Matiu population is one of several created to spread tuatara beyond their few natural island refuges, reducing the risk from disease, fire or predator invasion. Tuatara on the island coexist with other reintroduced species such as Wellington green geckos, giant wētā and kākāriki, and visitors may see them near tracks in warm weather, where they bask at burrow entrances.
