Matiu / Somes Island — Quarantine station

Matiu / Somes Island, in Wellington Harbour, New Zealand, served as a human and animal quarantine station for over a century. The island's isolation in the middle of the harbour made it a natural place to hold arriving people and livestock suspected of carrying infectious disease, away from the city of Wellington.

Human quarantine

A human quarantine station was established on the island in 1872. Passengers from ships with diseases such as smallpox, typhoid, scarlet fever and influenza were landed and held in quarantine barracks; those who died were buried in the island's cemetery. The station was heavily used during the 1918 influenza pandemic, and during both world wars the island also served as an internment camp for enemy aliens. Human quarantine use declined after the Second World War as air travel and modern medicine changed disease risks.

Animal quarantine

From 1893 the island was also a quarantine station for imported farm animals, and from 1986 it hosted a maximum-security animal quarantine facility through which valuable breeding stock entered New Zealand. The animal station closed in 1995 as the island moved to conservation use.

Today

Surviving quarantine buildings, barracks and the cemetery are heritage features of the island, now a scientific and historic reserve managed by the Department of Conservation, reached by ferry from Wellington.
