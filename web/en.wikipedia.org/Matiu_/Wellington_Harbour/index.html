Matiu / Somes Island — Wellington Harbour

Matiu / Somes Island lies in the middle of Wellington Harbour (Te Whanganui-a-Tara), New Zealand, roughly midway between the city of Wellington and the Hutt Valley. At 24.9 hectares it is the largest of the harbour's islands, positioned on the main shipping route into the port, with a lighthouse at its southern end guiding vessels past its shores.

Setting in the harbour

The harbour is a drowned river valley, entered from Cook Strait between Pencarrow Head and the Miramar Peninsula. Matiu / Somes Island sits off Petone, with smaller Mokopuna Island just to its north. From the island there are views of Wellington city, Eastbourne, the Hutt River mouth and the Rimutaka ranges.

History in the harbour

Māori named the island Matiu; Pākehā settlers called it Somes Island after Joseph Somes of the New Zealand Company. Its harbour position led to its use as a quarantine and internment station, a lighthouse site (the 1866 lighthouse was among the harbour's early navigational aids), and a defence position during the Second World War, when anti-aircraft guns were installed. Since the 1990s it has been a scientific and historic reserve, planted in native forest by community volunteers and open to day visitors by ferry.
