Jordan Grand Prix was a Formula One racing team and constructor that competed from 1991 to 2005. Founded by Irish businessman Eddie Jordan, whose teams had already succeeded in Formula 3 and Formula 3000, the Silverstone-based outfit was known for its distinctive liveries, its eye for young talent and its underdog triumphs.

History

Jordan entered Formula One in 1991 with the widely admired 191 chassis, and gave a debut to Michael Schumacher at that year's Belgian Grand Prix before losing him to Benetton. The team's finest season was 1999, when Heinz-Harald Frentzen won two races and finished third in the drivers' championship, Jordan placing third among constructors. The four Jordan victories were scored by Damon Hill (Spa 1998, the team's first), Frentzen (Magny-Cours and Monza 1999) and Giancarlo Fisichella (Interlagos 2003).

Sale and legacy

Financial pressures led Jordan to sell the team to the Midland Group in early 2005; it raced as Jordan for the rest of that season before a chain of successor identities — Midland, Spyker, Force India, Racing Point — culminating in today's Aston Martin F1 team, still based at Silverstone. Drivers who passed through Jordan include Rubens Barrichello, Ralf Schumacher and Eddie Irvine.
