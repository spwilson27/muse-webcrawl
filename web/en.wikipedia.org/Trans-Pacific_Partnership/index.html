Trans-Pacific Partnership

The Trans-Pacific Partnership (TPP) was a proposed trade agreement between twelve Pacific Rim economies: Australia, Brunei, Canada, Chile, Japan, Malaysia, Mexico, New Zealand, Peru, Singapore, the United States and Vietnam. It was signed on 4 February 2016 in Auckland, after negotiations concluded in October 2015.

Contents

The agreement covered tariff reductions, services, intellectual property (including extended copyright and pharmaceutical data rules), labour and environmental standards, state-owned enterprises and investor–state dispute settlement. Supporters argued it would set rules for regional trade and counterbalance China's influence; critics objected to secrecy in negotiations, ISDS provisions and projected effects on jobs and medicine prices.

Withdrawal and successor

In January 2017 President Donald Trump signed a memorandum withdrawing the United States, and the TPP as signed could not enter into force. The remaining eleven members salvaged most of the text as the Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership (CPTPP), signed in March 2018 and in force from 30 December 2018.
