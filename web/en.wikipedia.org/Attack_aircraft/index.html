Attack aircraft

The Fairchild Republic A-10 Thunderbolt II is a dedicated attack aircraft.

An attack aircraft, or strike aircraft, is a tactical military aircraft that has a primary role of carrying out airstrikes with greater precision than bombers, and is prepared to encounter strong low-level air defences while pressing the attack. This class of aircraft is designed mostly for close air support and naval air-to-surface missions, overlapping the roles of light bombers and fighter-bombers.

History

Dedicated attack aircraft emerged during the First World War as armoured ground-attack types such as the German Junkers J.I. In the Second World War, aircraft like the Soviet Ilyushin Il-2 Shturmovik and the German Junkers Ju 87 Stuka dive bomber became famous in the role. During the Cold War, the United States developed the A-10 Thunderbolt II around a powerful cannon for anti-tank work, while the Soviet Union fielded the Sukhoi Su-25.

Characteristics

Attack aircraft are typically subsonic or transonic, heavily armed with cannon, rockets, missiles, and bombs, and often carry armour around the cockpit and engines to survive ground fire. Modern examples include the A-10, the Su-25, and armed variants of trainer and turboprop aircraft used in counter-insurgency roles.
