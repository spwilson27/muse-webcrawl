Recurrence relation

In mathematics, a recurrence relation is an equation that defines a sequence recursively: each term is expressed as a function of the preceding terms. Together with specified initial values, a recurrence relation determines the whole sequence. Recurrence relations arise throughout combinatorics, number theory, computer science (in the analysis of recursive algorithms) and mathematical modelling.

Definition and examples

A recurrence of order k relates each term an to the previous k terms. The most famous example is the Fibonacci sequence:

F(n) = F(n−1) + F(n−2), with F(0) = 0, F(1) = 1

Other examples include factorials, n! = n·(n−1)!, and the logistic map used in population dynamics and chaos theory.

Linear recurrences

A linear recurrence with constant coefficients has the form an = c1an−1 + … + ckan−k + f(n). Such recurrences can be solved in closed form using the characteristic equation: the roots of the associated polynomial determine exponential (or polynomial-times-exponential) solutions. For the Fibonacci numbers this yields Binet's formula, involving the golden ratio φ. Generating functions provide another powerful method for solving recurrences.

Applications

In computer science, divide-and-conquer algorithms are analysed with recurrences such as T(n) = 2T(n/2) + n, solved by the master theorem. Recurrences also model compound interest, population growth, branching processes and the number of ways to tile or partition discrete structures.
