The Amuktamalyada (Telugu: ఆముక్తమాల్యద) is a Telugu epic poem composed by Krishnadevaraya, the Vijayanagara Emperor who reigned from 1509 to 1529. It is regarded as one of the five great Telugu epics (the pancha-kavyas) and one of the masterpieces of Telugu literature.

The poem narrates the story of Andal (Goda Devi), one of the twelve Alwars and the only female Alwar saint, and her devotion to the god Vishnu in his form as Rangamannar of Srirangam. The title, "one who wears and gives away garlands", refers to Andal's practice of wearing the garlands intended for the deity before offering them. Interwoven with the devotional narrative are passages of political wisdom — instructions on kingship and statecraft that reflect Krishnadevaraya's own experience as a ruler.

Composition

According to the poem's own account, Krishnadevaraya resolved to compose it after a divine dream during a campaign, in which the god Andhra Vishnu appeared and directed him to write the story of Andal in Telugu. The work also incorporates episodes concerning Vishnuchitta (Periyalvar), Andal's father, including the famous debate at the Pandya court.

Significance

Beyond its devotional content, the Amuktamalyada is valued for its vivid descriptions of village and courtly life, seasons, and landscapes in the Telugu country, and for demonstrating the literary patronage and personal scholarship of Krishnadevaraya, whose court hosted the eight celebrated poets known as the Ashtadiggajas.
