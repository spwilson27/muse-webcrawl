Manichaeism was a major world religion founded in the 3rd century CE in the Sasanian Empire by the Persian prophet Mani. It taught an elaborate dualistic cosmology describing the struggle between a good, spiritual world of light and an evil, material world of darkness, and spread rapidly from Roman North Africa to Tang-dynasty China.

Doctrine

Mani presented himself as the final prophet in a line including Zoroaster, Buddha and Jesus. Believers were divided into an ascetic class of "Elect", who practised celibacy and abstained from harming light trapped in matter, and lay "Hearers" who supported them. Augustine of Hippo was a Hearer for about nine years before converting to Christianity.

Spread and decline

Manichaeism was persecuted by Zoroastrian, Roman, Islamic and Chinese authorities in turn. It survived longest along the Silk Road, and became the state religion of the Uyghur Khaganate in 763. It died out in China by the 17th century, though Manichaean temples and texts preserved in Fujian and Turfan document its history.

Texts

Mani wrote works including the Shabuhragan and the Living Gospel. Major manuscript finds include the Turfan fragments, the Cologne Mani-Codex, and the Chinese Manichaean manuscripts from Dunhuang.
