La Niña

La Niña is a coupled ocean–atmosphere phenomenon that is the colder counterpart of El Niño in the El Niño–Southern Oscillation (ENSO) cycle, marked by unusually cold sea surface temperatures in the central and eastern equatorial Pacific.

Impacts

La Niña typically strengthens trade winds and shifts rainfall patterns: wetter conditions in Australia, Indonesia and parts of South Asia, drier conditions in the southwestern United States and parts of South America, and often more Atlantic hurricanes. Events often follow El Niño and may persist for multiple years.

Monitoring

Agencies such as NOAA declare La Niña when Pacific anomalies and atmospheric responses meet thresholds over several months, using indices such as the Oceanic Niño Index.
