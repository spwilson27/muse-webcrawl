Chens theorem

Chen's theorem, proved by Chen Jingrun in 1966 and published in full in 1973, states that every sufficiently large even number can be written as the sum of a prime and a number that is either prime or the product of two primes (a semiprime).

Statement and significance

The result, written "1 + 2", is the closest classical approximation to the Goldbach conjecture, which asserts every even number greater than 2 is the sum of two primes ("1 + 1"). Chen's proof used a refined weighted sieve method, building on the work of Brun, Bombieri and others.

Chen primes

A related notion is a Chen prime: a prime p such that p + 2 is prime or semiprime. Whether infinitely many Chen primes exist remains open. Chen's theorem has been strengthened in various ways, including results on representations in arithmetic progressions.

History

Because of the Cultural Revolution, the full proof appeared in Scientia Sinica only in 1973, and the theorem brought Chen Jingrun wide fame in China.
