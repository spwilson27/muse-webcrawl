The Balkhash perch (Perca schrenkii) is a species of perch endemic to the Lake Balkhash watershed in Kazakhstan and western China, including the Ili River delta and nearby lakes such as Alakol and Sasykkol. It is one of only three living species in the genus Perca, alongside the European perch (P. fluviatilis) and the yellow perch (P. flavescens) of North America, and was described by Karl Kessler in 1874. It is named for the naturalist Alexander von Schrenk.

Description

The Balkhash perch resembles the European perch in form, with a laterally compressed, perch-shaped body, two dorsal fins (the first spiny), and dark vertical barring on the flanks, though its coloration tends to be paler and the barring less pronounced. It generally reaches about 40–50 cm in total length and weights around 1.5 kg, with large individuals reported historically. It is a predator feeding on smaller fish and invertebrates.

Habitat and ecology

The species inhabits fresh and slightly brackish waters of the Balkhash basin — the lake's fresher western reaches, river channels, and floodplain lakes — favouring vegetated shallows. It spawns in spring over submerged vegetation, laying adhesive egg ribbons in the manner of other Perca species.

Decline and conservation

Once a commercially important food fish and the dominant native predator of Lake Balkhash, the Balkhash perch declined severely in the late 20th century. The introduction of non-native species — especially the pikeperch (zander), which preyed on and outcompeted it, along with bream, carp, and other stocked fish — combined with falling lake levels following the damming of the Ili River at Kapchagay, devastated native fish communities. The Balkhash perch became scarce in the lake itself and persists mainly in riverine refuges; it is listed as Data Deficient by the IUCN and is protected in parts of its range.
