Photon antibunching

Photon antibunching generally refers to a light field with photons more equally spaced than a coherent (laser) field, so that fewer coincidences are recorded between simultaneously arriving photons. It is a signature of non-classical light and of single-photon sources.

Explanation

In a Hanbury Brown and Twiss interferometer, antibunched light gives a second-order correlation g(2)(0) below 1—ideally approaching 0 for a perfect single-photon emitter—whereas coherent light gives g(2)(0) = 1 and thermal light shows photon bunching with g(2)(0) = 2.

Sources

Antibunching was first demonstrated in resonance fluorescence from single atoms in 1977. Today single molecules, quantum dots, colour centres in diamond and single ions all serve as antibunched single-photon sources for quantum optics and quantum information.
