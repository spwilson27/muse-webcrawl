1906 San Francisco earthquake
Date: 18 April 1906, 5:12 a.m.
Magnitude: ~7.9 Mw
Epicentre: Off San Francisco, San Andreas Fault
Deaths: More than 3,000
Damage: ~$400 million (1906 USD)

The 1906 San Francisco earthquake struck the coast of Northern California at 5:12 a.m. on Wednesday, 18 April 1906. The quake ruptured some 296 miles of the San Andreas Fault and was felt from Oregon to Los Angeles. The fires that followed did most of the damage, devastating the city of San Francisco.

Damage and fires

More than 80 percent of San Francisco was destroyed by the earthquake and the conflagrations that burned for days, fed by broken gas mains while ruptured water mains hampered firefighting. Over 28,000 buildings were lost, more than 3,000 people died, and up to 300,000 of the city's 410,000 residents were left homeless.

Legacy

The disaster spurred major advances in seismology, including H. F. Reid's elastic rebound theory, and led to stricter building codes. San Francisco rebuilt rapidly, hosting the Panama–Pacific International Exposition in 1915, but its financial primacy on the West Coast passed to Los Angeles.
