Kokanee salmon

The kokanee salmon (Oncorhynchus nerka) is the non-anadromous, landlocked freshwater form of the sockeye salmon, native to lakes in western North America and also found in Japan. Kokanee spend their whole lives in fresh water rather than migrating to the sea.

Life cycle

Kokanee feed mainly on zooplankton and grow to typically 25–36 cm. After three to four years they spawn in autumn in lake inlets or gravel beds; adults develop bright red bodies and green heads, and die after spawning, like other Pacific salmon. Populations have been widely introduced for sport fishing and as forage, though some native stocks have declined through habitat change and competition with introduced species such as mysid shrimp.
