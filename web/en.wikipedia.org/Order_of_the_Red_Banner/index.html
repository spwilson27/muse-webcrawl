Order of the Red Banner

The badge of the Soviet Order of the Red Banner, featuring a red banner and the Cyrillic inscription "Workers of the world, unite!"

The Order of the Red Banner (Russian: Орден Красного Знамени, Orden Krasnogo Znameni) was the first Soviet military decoration. The order was established on 16 September 1918, during the Russian Civil War, by decree of the All-Russian Central Executive Committee, as the highest award of Soviet Russia. It was later adopted across the Soviet Union, with the all-Union version instituted in 1924.

Criteria

The order was awarded to individuals and military units for exceptional feats in combat and outstanding military merit. Recipients included soldiers, sailors, border guards, and internal troops, as well as formations and ships, which carried the order's name in their titles and displayed the badge on their banners.

History

The first recipient was Vasily Blyukher, who received the award in 1919. During the Second World War, the order was conferred hundreds of thousands of times. By the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991, more than 580,000 awards had been made. A civilian counterpart, the Order of the Red Banner of Labour, was established in 1920 for achievements in work.

Description

The badge is a silver and enamel medallion showing a red banner inscribed with "USSR" and the motto "Workers of the world, unite!" in Russian, backed by a wreath, crossed torch, rifle, and ploughshare, with a red star and hammer and sickle.
