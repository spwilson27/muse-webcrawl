Caliche

Caliche is a sedimentary rock, a hardened natural cement of calcium carbonate that binds other materials — such as gravel, sand, clay and silt. It occurs worldwide, in aridisol and mollisol soil orders, generally in arid or semiarid regions including the American Southwest, Australia, and the Mediterranean.

Formation

Caliche forms where downward-moving water carrying dissolved calcium carbonate evaporates, precipitating calcite that progressively cements soil horizons, sometimes into an impermeable hardpan metres thick over tens of thousands of years.

Uses and challenges

Crushed caliche serves as a cheap road base and building material in the southwestern United States and Mexico — 'caliche' also names the nitrate-rich ore mined in northern Chile for saltpetre and iodine. Hardpan complicates excavation, septic systems, and agriculture, while desert pavements and caliche layers preserve archaeological surfaces.

Terminology

Related terms include calcrete, hardpan, and duricrust; the Chilean ore usage derives from Spanish colonial mining.
