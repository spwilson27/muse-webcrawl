Local area network

A local area network (LAN) is a computer network that interconnects computers within a limited area such as a residence, school, laboratory, university campus or office building. In contrast, a wide area network (WAN) covers a much larger geographic area and generally uses leased telecommunication circuits.

Technology

The two most common LAN technologies are Ethernet, using twisted-pair or fibre-optic cabling and switches, and Wi-Fi, which uses radio waves under the IEEE 802.11 standards. Devices on a LAN share resources such as printers, file servers and internet connections, typically coordinated through routers, network switches and access points using the TCP/IP protocol suite and private address ranges such as 192.168.0.0/16.

History

Ethernet was developed at Xerox PARC in the 1970s by Robert Metcalfe and colleagues; competing early systems included Token Ring and ARCNET. By the 1990s, switched Ethernet over structured cabling had become the standard for offices, and wireless LANs spread rapidly after 1999.
