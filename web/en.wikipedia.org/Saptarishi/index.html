Saptarishi

The Saptarishi (Sanskrit: सप्तर्षि, "seven sages") are the seven greatest sages (rishis) of the Vedic and Hindu tradition, extolled in the Vedas and Puranas. They are regarded as the patriarchs of the Vedic religion, the mind-born sons of Brahma in some accounts, and the ancestors of the gotras — the patrilineal clans — of Brahmins. In Hindu astronomy, the seven sages are identified with the seven bright stars of the asterism of the Big Dipper in the constellation Ursa Major, which shares the name Saptarishi.

Lists of the seven sages

The membership of the group varies across texts and across the cosmic ages (manvantaras). A list frequently given for the present age includes Atri, Bharadvaja, Gautama Maharishi, Jamadagni, Kashyapa, Vasishtha, and Vishvamitra. Other lists name Bhrigu, Angiras, Marichi, and Pulastya among the seven. Vasishtha and Vishvamitra appear in most versions, and their legendary rivalry is a recurring theme of epic literature.

Role in tradition

The Saptarishi are credited with composing hymns of the Rigveda and with preserving and transmitting Vedic knowledge through the ages. According to Puranic cosmology, a new group of seven sages appears in each manvantara to re-establish dharma. In the night sky, Vasishtha is identified with the star Mizar and his wife Arundhati with its faint companion Alcor; newly married Hindu couples are traditionally shown the pair as an emblem of marital devotion.
