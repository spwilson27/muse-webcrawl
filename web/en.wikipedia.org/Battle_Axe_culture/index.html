Battle Axe culture

Periodc. 2800–2300 BC (Late Neolithic)
RegionScandinavia and the north European plain
Named forBoat-shaped stone battle axes in burials

The Battle Axe culture, also called the Boat Axe culture, is a Chalcolithic and early Bronze Age culture of Scandinavia and the north European plain, dating to roughly 2800–2300 BC. It is generally regarded as the Scandinavian regional variant of the Corded Ware culture, which spread across northern Europe in the third millennium BC.

The culture is named for its distinctive burials: single graves in which men were interred with a polished, boat-shaped stone battle axe, along with cord-decorated beakers, flint tools and amber ornaments. Over 3,000 Battle Axe graves are known from Sweden alone, with the culture extending into Norway, Denmark, Finland, the Baltic states, northern Germany and Poland. Economy was based on animal husbandry and some cultivation; settlement evidence is scarce, suggesting small farmsteads rather than villages.

Ancient DNA studies show that Battle Axe populations carried substantial ancestry from steppe pastoralists (related to the Yamnaya) admixed with earlier European farmers, indicating a real migration into Scandinavia. The culture is widely believed to have brought early Indo-European speech to Scandinavia, making it ancestral in part to the later Nordic Bronze Age cultures and, ultimately, to the Germanic peoples.
