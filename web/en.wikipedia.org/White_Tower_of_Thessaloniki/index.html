LocationThessaloniki, Greece
Built15th–16th century (Ottoman period)
Height33.9 m

The White Tower of Thessaloniki (Greek: Lefkós Pýrgos) is a monument and museum on the waterfront of Thessaloniki, Greece. The present tower replaced an older Byzantine fortification and is the city's most famous landmark and symbol.

History

The tower was built by the Ottomans, probably in the 15th or 16th century, to fortify the city's harbour as part of its sea walls. It later served as a garrison post and a prison. In 1826, Sultan Mahmud II ordered a massacre of the Janissaries held there, and the tower became known as the "Tower of Blood".

Whitewashing

After Greece gained control of Thessaloniki in 1912, the tower was whitewashed as a symbolic gesture of cleansing, giving it its present name. The whitewash has since worn away, but the name remained. In 1985 the tower was restored and converted to exhibition space.

Museum

The tower now hosts a permanent exhibition on the history of Thessaloniki from its founding in 316/315 BC to the present day, administered by the Museum of Byzantine Culture. A viewing platform at the top offers panoramas of the Thermaic Gulf.
