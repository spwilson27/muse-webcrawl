Transportation Safety Board of Canada

The Transportation Safety Board of Canada (TSB; French: Bureau de la sécurité des transports du Canada) is an independent Canadian federal agency responsible for investigating accidents and incidents in air, marine, rail, and pipeline transportation. It was established on 29 March 1990 under the Canadian Transportation Accident Investigation and Safety Board Act, replacing the Canadian Aviation Safety Board.

Mandate

The TSB's sole aim is to advance transportation safety; it does not assign fault or determine civil or criminal liability. It investigates occurrences, publishes reports identifying causes and contributing factors, issues safety recommendations, and maintains a public Watchlist of the safety issues it considers to pose the greatest risk. Its head office is in Gatineau, Quebec, with regional offices across Canada and an engineering laboratory in Ottawa.

Notable investigations

The TSB investigated the crash of Swissair Flight 111 off Nova Scotia in 1998, the Lac-Mégantic rail disaster of 2013, and numerous other major occurrences. Its recommendations are not binding, but the responsible ministers must respond publicly to them within 90 days.
