Trinity was the code name of the first detonation of a nuclear weapon, conducted by the United States Army on 16 July 1945, as part of the Manhattan Project. The test was of an implosion-design plutonium device, nicknamed "The Gadget", of the same design as the Fat Man bomb later used over Nagasaki. The test site, now the White Sands Missile Range, was in the Jornada del Muerto desert about 56 km (35 mi) southeast of Socorro, New Mexico.

The test

Scientists led by J. Robert Oppenheimer, with test director Kenneth Bainbridge, feared the risky plutonium implosion design would fizzle. The device was hoisted atop a 30-metre (100 ft) steel tower; at 05:29:21 local time it exploded with an energy equivalent to about 25 kilotons of TNT (modern estimates; wartime estimates were lower), leaving a crater lined with radioactive green glass (trinitite) and a mushroom cloud reaching 12 km. Observers including Oppenheimer, Vannevar Bush, James Conant, and General Leslie Groves watched from bunkers several miles away.

Reactions and aftermath

Oppenheimer later recalled the Hindu scripture: "Now I am become Death, the destroyer of worlds." Bainbridge called the test a "foul and awesome display" and told Oppenheimer, "Now we are all sons of bitches." The success cleared the way for the atomic bombings of Hiroshima (uranium gun-type device, untested) on 6 August and Nagasaki (plutonium implosion) on 9 August. The Trinity Site is now a National Historic Landmark, opened to the public twice a year; residual radiation is low but measurably above background.
