Newport News is an independent city in southeastern Virginia, United States. At the 2020 census its population was 186,247, making it the fifth-most populous city in Virginia. Located on the northern shore of Hampton Roads at the mouth of the James River, it forms the northern end of the Hampton Roads metropolitan area on the Virginia Peninsula.

History

The area was settled by English colonists in the early 17th century and remained largely rural until after the Civil War. Industrialist Collis P. Huntington extended the Chesapeake and Ohio Railway to the deep-water piers at Newport News in 1881 and founded the Newport News Shipbuilding and Dry Dock Company in 1886. The shipyard grew into one of the largest in the world and the city's dominant employer. Newport News became an independent city in 1896 and consolidated with Warwick County in 1958.

Economy

Newport News Shipbuilding, a division of Huntington Ingalls Industries, is the sole designer and builder of United States Navy aircraft carriers and one of two yards building nuclear submarines. The military presence includes nearby Joint Base Langley–Eustis, part of which, Fort Eustis, lies within the city. The city is also home to the Port of Virginia terminals at Newport News Marine Terminal.

Attractions

The Mariners' Museum and Park, one of the largest maritime museums in the world, holds the recovered turret and artifacts of USS Monitor. Other attractions include the Virginia Living Museum, the Peninsula Fine Arts Center, and Newport News Park, one of the largest municipal parks in the United States.
