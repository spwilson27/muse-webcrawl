The Virginia Peninsula is a peninsula in southeast Virginia, bordered by the York River, James River and Hampton Roads. It is the site of the first permanent English settlement in North America at Jamestown (1607) and forms the northern half of the Hampton Roads region.

Geography

The peninsula extends roughly 50 miles from the vicinity of Richmond to Fort Monroe at Old Point Comfort, where it faces Norfolk across Hampton Roads. It is crossed by Interstate 64 and U.S. Route 60, and connected to the Southside by the Hampton Roads Bridge–Tunnel, the Monitor–Merrimac Memorial Bridge–Tunnel and the James River Bridge.

History

Historic sites cluster in the "Historic Triangle" of Jamestown, Williamsburg and Yorktown, linked by the Colonial Parkway: Jamestown was the first capital of the Virginia Colony; Williamsburg was its capital from 1699 to 1780 and is now the centre of Colonial Williamsburg, a vast living-history museum; and Yorktown was the site of the decisive 1781 siege ending the American Revolutionary War.

During the Civil War, the Peninsula was the theatre of George B. McClellan's Peninsula Campaign of 1862, an attempt to capture Richmond by advancing from Fort Monroe. In the 20th century the peninsula became a centre of military and aerospace activity, with Fort Eustis, Langley Air Force Base and NASA's Langley Research Center, and of shipbuilding at Newport News.

Communities

Independent cities on the peninsula include Newport News, Hampton, Poquoson and Williamsburg, along with James City County and York County.
