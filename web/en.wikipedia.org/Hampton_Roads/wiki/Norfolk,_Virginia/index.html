Norfolk is an independent city in Virginia, United States. It had a population of 238,005 at the 2020 census, making it the third-most populous city in Virginia. Located at the core of the Hampton Roads metropolitan area on the Elizabeth River and Chesapeake Bay, Norfolk is a major port, military centre and cultural hub.

History

Norfolk was founded in 1682 and incorporated in 1736. It was one of colonial America's busiest ports, and was burned by retreating British forces in January 1776 during the Revolutionary War. Rebuilt, it grew as a shipping and shipbuilding centre; the Gosport Navy Yard (now Norfolk Naval Shipyard) in neighbouring Portsmouth dates to 1767. During the American Civil War, the Battle of Hampton Roads was fought in its harbour in 1862. The 1907 Jamestown Exposition spurred the Navy's acquisition of land that became Naval Station Norfolk in 1917, today the largest naval base in the world.

Economy and culture

The economy is dominated by the United States Navy, the Port of Virginia, shipping and NATO's Allied Command Transformation headquarters. Norfolk is home to Old Dominion University, Norfolk State University and Eastern Virginia Medical School. Cultural institutions include the Chrysler Museum of Art, the Virginia Opera, Nauticus maritime museum and the battleship USS Wisconsin, berthed downtown. The Tide light rail line, opened in 2011, was Virginia's first light rail system.

Geography

Norfolk is low-lying and surrounded by water on three sides; it is among the American cities most vulnerable to flooding from sea-level rise, and has undertaken major resilience and stormwater projects.
