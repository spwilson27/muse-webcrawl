The Battle of Hampton Roads, also known as the Battle of the Monitor and Merrimack, was a naval battle of the American Civil War fought on March 8–9, 1862, in Hampton Roads, Virginia. It is famous as the first meeting in combat of ironclad warships: the Union's USS Monitor and the Confederacy's CSS Virginia (built from the scuttled USS Merrimack).

March 8: Virginia attacks

On March 8, 1862, CSS Virginia steamed into Hampton Roads and attacked the Union blockading squadron. She rammed and sank the sloop USS Cumberland, forced the frigate USS Congress to surrender and burn, and drove the frigate USS Minnesota aground, while suffering only light damage herself. The day demonstrated the vulnerability of wooden warships to armored vessels and caused panic in Washington.

March 9: Monitor vs Virginia

That night, USS Monitor, designed by John Ericsson, arrived from New York. On the morning of March 9 the two ironclads fought a close-range duel of roughly four hours. Neither could seriously damage the other: Monitor's shots dented Virginia's armor, and Virginia's fire failed to penetrate Monitor's turret and low hull. After Monitor's captain was temporarily blinded by a shell burst at the pilothouse, she withdrew briefly, and Virginia, leaking and needing repairs, returned to Norfolk. The battle was tactically inconclusive, but the Union blockade remained intact.

Significance

The battle received worldwide attention and accelerated the shift to armored, steam-powered warships, effectively rendering wooden navies obsolete. Monitor sank in a storm off Cape Hatteras in December 1862; her wreck was located in 1973 and her turret recovered in 2002.
