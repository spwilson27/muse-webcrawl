Naval Station Norfolk is a United States Navy base in Norfolk, Virginia, that supports naval forces in the Atlantic Ocean, Mediterranean Sea and Indian Ocean. It is the world's largest naval station, occupying more than 6,000 acres on the Sewell's Point peninsula at the mouth of the Elizabeth River in Hampton Roads.

History

The site was the location of the Jamestown Exposition of 1907. The Navy acquired the land in 1917 as the United States entered the First World War, establishing a naval operating base, training station and air station. The base expanded greatly during the Second World War and the Cold War. In 1999 the shore establishment was consolidated as Naval Station Norfolk; the adjacent airfield, Chambers Field, operates as part of the station and was formerly Naval Air Station Norfolk.

Facilities and commands

The station has 14 piers and some 11 miles of pier and wharf space, capable of berthing dozens of ships, and is the home port of aircraft carriers of Carrier Strike Groups of the U.S. Atlantic Fleet. Major commands headquartered there include U.S. Fleet Forces Command, the United States Second Fleet, and numerous operational and support units. Around 82,000 active-duty personnel and 29,000 civilian employees and family activities make the station a cornerstone of the Hampton Roads economy.

Environmental and climate concerns

Because much of the installation lies only a few feet above sea level, the station faces significant challenges from sea-level rise and recurrent flooding, prompting long-term adaptation projects such as pier replacements and flood barriers.
