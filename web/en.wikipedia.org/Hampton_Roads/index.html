Hampton Roads

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Hampton Roads is the name of both a body of water in the United States and the surrounding metropolitan region in southeastern Virginia and northeastern North Carolina. The body of water, known historically as Hampton Road (singular), is a wide channel through which the waters of the James River, Nansemond River, and Elizabeth River pass — via the harbor of Hampton Roads — just before flowing into the Chesapeake Bay and the Atlantic Ocean.

The Hampton Roads metropolitan statistical area (officially the Virginia Beach–Norfolk–Newport News, VA–NC MSA) comprises nine counties and ten independent cities in southeastern Virginia and two counties in northeastern North Carolina, with a population of about 1.8 million, making it the 37th-largest metropolitan area in the United States. The region is home to the world's largest naval base, Naval Station Norfolk, and to major shipbuilding, port, and military installations that dominate its economy.

The body of water

Hampton Roads is one of the world's largest natural harbors. The channel is formed by the confluence of the James, Nansemond, and Elizabeth rivers near Old Point Comfort and Fort Wool, between the Virginia Peninsula to the north and South Hampton Roads to the south. In maritime usage, a "road" (or roadstead) is a sheltered anchorage; the waterway was recorded as "Hampton Roads" by the early 18th century. The port facilities of Norfolk, Newport News, and Portsmouth line its shores, and the Hampton Roads Bridge–Tunnel and Monitor–Merrimac Memorial Bridge–Tunnel carry Interstate highways beneath and across it.

The region

The region is divided by the harbor into two parts: the Virginia Peninsula (Newport News, Hampton, Poquoson, Williamsburg, and surrounding counties) to the north, and South Hampton Roads (Norfolk, Virginia Beach, Chesapeake, Portsmouth, and Suffolk) to the south. Virginia Beach, the most populous city in Virginia, is a major resort city on the Atlantic Ocean. Other localities include James City County and York County on the Peninsula; Currituck and Gates counties in North Carolina are included in the metropolitan area.

History

The area was the site of some of the earliest English settlement in North America: Jamestown was founded up the James River in 1607, and the landing at Cape Henry occurred the same year. In 1862 the harbor was the scene of the Battle of Hampton Roads, the first meeting in combat of ironclad warships — the USS Monitor and the CSS Virginia — a famous engagement of the American Civil War. Fort Monroe, at Old Point Comfort, remained an active Army post from 1823 until 2011 and sheltered escaping enslaved people as "contraband" during the Civil War. The 1907 Jamestown Exposition at Sewell's Point helped spur the creation of the great naval base at Norfolk.

Economy and military

The United States military has an enormous presence: Naval Station Norfolk is the largest naval station in the world; Naval Air Station Oceana, Joint Base Langley–Eustis, Naval Medical Center Portsmouth, and Coast Guard facilities are among the many installations. Huntington Ingalls Industries' Newport News Shipbuilding is the sole builder of U.S. Navy aircraft carriers and one of two builders of nuclear submarines. The Port of Virginia, with terminals in Norfolk, Portsmouth, and Newport News, is one of the busiest container ports on the U.S. East Coast. Tourism, centered on Virginia Beach and nearby Colonial Williamsburg, is also a major sector.

Transportation

Interstate 64 is the region's principal highway, crossing the harbor via the Hampton Roads Bridge–Tunnel (1957, expanded 1976) — the first bridge–tunnel complex in the world — and reaching the Peninsula and Richmond. The Monitor–Merrimac Memorial Bridge–Tunnel (Interstate 664, 1992) and the James River Bridge provide additional crossings. Norfolk International Airport and Newport News/Williamsburg International Airport serve air travellers, and Amtrak's Northeast Regional trains serve Newport News and Williamsburg.
