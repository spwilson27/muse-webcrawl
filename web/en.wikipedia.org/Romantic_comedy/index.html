Romantic comedy

A romantic comedy (often shortened to rom-com) is a genre of fiction — in film, television, theatre and literature — centred on a light-hearted love story, in which two people meet, are kept apart by misunderstandings or circumstances, and are finally united. Humour arises from the courtship itself and from supporting characters and situations.

History

The form descends from Shakespearean comedy such as Much Ado About Nothing and A Midsummer Night's Dream, through Restoration comedy and Jane Austen's novels. In cinema, screwball comedies of the 1930s — It Happened One Night (1934), Bringing Up Baby (1938) — defined the modern pattern. A revival from When Harry Met Sally... (1989) through Four Weddings and a Funeral (1994) and Notting Hill (1999) made the genre a box-office staple.

Conventions

Common devices include the "meet-cute", mistaken identity, the last-minute dash to declare love, and the contrasting best friend. Critics debate the genre's gender politics and formula, while streaming-era titles have broadened its settings and protagonists. See also comedy film and romance film.
