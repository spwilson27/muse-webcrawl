Adbusters

Type: Magazine (anti-consumerist)
Founded: 1989
Headquarters: Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada
Founder: Kalle Lasn and Bill Schmalz

Adbusters is a Canadian anti-consumerist and environmentalist magazine and media foundation, founded in 1989 in Vancouver by Kalle Lasn and Bill Schmalz. Published by the Adbusters Media Foundation, the reader-supported magazine is known as a leading voice of the culture jamming movement — the practice of subverting advertising and corporate imagery to challenge consumer culture.

Content and campaigns

The magazine is famous for its "subvertisements": full-page parodies of famous advertisements that invert their messages, such as the "Absolute End" spoof of Absolut Vodka ads and Joe Chemo, a Camel-cigarette parody whose mascot lies in a hospital bed. Adbusters has launched long-running campaigns including Buy Nothing Day (held on Black Friday), TV Turnoff Week, and the blackspot shoe, an "anti-logo" sneaker intended as an alternative to sweatshop-produced brands.

Occupy Wall Street

In July 2011, Adbusters issued the call "#occupywallstreet: Are you ready for a Tahrir moment?", accompanied by its iconic poster of a ballerina balancing on the Wall Street Charging Bull with protesters behind her. The call helped catalyse the Occupy movement, which began in New York's Zuccotti Park on 17 September 2011 and spread worldwide. Adbusters' role was primarily that of initiator; the movement itself developed its own decentralised leadership.

Reach and criticism

The magazine has circulated internationally with a peak readership in the tens of thousands and has received design awards for its provocative graphic style. Critics have accused it of hypocrisy for selling products of its own and of offering aesthetics rather than a constructive political programme — charges Lasn has generally embraced as culture jamming's deliberate ambiguity.
