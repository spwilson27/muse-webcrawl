Aloe

Aloe is a genus containing over 650 species of flowering succulent plants in the family Asphodelaceae. The most widely known species is Aloe vera, widely cultivated for its gel. Aloes are native to the Arabian Peninsula, sub-Saharan Africa, Madagascar, and nearby islands, and are naturalised worldwide in arid and semi-arid climates.

Aloe
Family: Asphodelaceae
Species: 650+
Best known: Aloe vera

Description

Most aloes have a rosette of large, thick, fleshy lance-shaped leaves with serrated margins, growing stemless or with short stems. Flowers are tubular, often yellow, orange, pink or red, borne densely on simple or branched inflorescences. Many species are stemless or very short-stemmed, growing in compact rosettes.

Uses

Aloe vera gel is used in cosmetics, skin lotions, and traditional medicine for burns and skin conditions, though clinical evidence for many claims is limited. Aloe latex contains aloin, a strong laxative whose oral use is restricted in many jurisdictions. Several species, such as Aloe arborescens, are popular ornamental houseplants and xeriscape plants.

Systematics

Molecular studies have split the former broad genus: Kumara, Haworthia relatives, and Aloiampelos were separated, while true Aloe remains one of the largest genera of succulents.
