School discipline


A classroom environment shaped by school rules

School discipline relates to the actions taken by teachers and school administrators toward students when their behaviour disrupts the educational activity or breaks rules governing conduct. Discipline aims to maintain a safe and orderly learning environment, teach self-control and protect the rights of other students.

Methods

Strategies range from positive approaches — such as clear expectations, praise, positive behaviour support and restorative practices — to sanctions including verbal warnings, loss of privileges, detention, in-school and out-of-school suspension, and expulsion. Historically many schools used corporal punishment, which is now prohibited in most countries and in many jurisdictions of the United States.

Criticism and debate

Exclusionary discipline has been criticised for disrupting students' education and for disparities affecting racial minorities, students with disabilities and other groups — a pattern sometimes linked to the "school-to-prison pipeline". Zero-tolerance policies adopted in the 1990s drew particular scrutiny for mandating harsh punishments for minor infractions. Alternatives such as restorative justice, social-emotional learning and school-wide positive behavioural interventions and supports seek to correct behaviour while keeping students in class.

Legal aspects

Student discipline is constrained by law in many countries, including due-process requirements for suspensions and expulsions, disability protections and anti-discrimination rules. Courts have balanced schools' authority to maintain order against students' rights to free expression and fair procedures.
