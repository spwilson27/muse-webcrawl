Ambroxan

Ambroxan, commonly known by trade names such as Ambrox and AmberX, is a synthetic aroma chemical that reproduces the prized ambergris note in perfumery. Chemically it is (−)-ambroxide, a tricyclic ether (C16H28O) derived from ambrein, the principal odorous component of natural ambergris from sperm whales.

Ambroxan
Formula: C16H28O
Odour: Woody-amber, musky, radiant
Synthesis: From sclareol (clary sage)

History and synthesis

The structure of ambrein was clarified in the 1940s (Ruzicka, Lardon) and ambroxide was first synthesised in 1950 by chemists at Firmenich. Industrial production starts from sclareol, extracted from clary sage, which is oxidatively degraded and cyclised to the ambroxide skeleton. Biotechnology routes using engineered microbes now supplement plant-based production.

Use in perfumery

Ambroxan is valued for its powerful, long-lasting woody-amber radiance at very low dosages, acting as a fixative and exalting other notes. It is a signature of modern minimalist fragrances — famously used at high concentration in Escentric Molecules Molecule 02 and in the ambroxan-rich base of many designer scents including Dior Sauvage. Perception varies: a fraction of people are partially anosmic to it.
