John Hadley

Born: 16 April 1682, Enfield Chase, Hertfordshire, England
Died: 14 February 1744 (aged 61), East Barnet, Hertfordshire
Occupation: Mathematician, inventor
Known for: Inventing the octant; improving the reflecting telescope

John Hadley (16 April 1682 – 14 February 1744) was an English mathematician and inventor who created the octant, a predecessor of the sextant, and made important improvements to the reflecting telescope.

Telescopes

Hadley built the first parabolic Newtonian reflecting telescope in 1721, with a mirror about six inches across and a focal length of over five feet. Presented to the Royal Society, it compared favourably with the long aerial telescopes of the day. He also developed improved methods of grinding and polishing speculum mirrors.

The octant

In 1730 Hadley invented the reflecting octant, a double-reflecting instrument for measuring the angle between a celestial body and the horizon at sea, allowing navigators to determine latitude accurately. He described it to the Royal Society in 1731. Thomas Godfrey in Philadelphia developed a similar instrument independently, leading to a priority dispute that the Royal Society resolved in Hadley's favour on publication grounds.

Career

Hadley was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1717 and served as a vice-president. His name is commemorated by Mount Hadley and the Hadley Rille on the Moon, near the Apollo 15 landing site.
