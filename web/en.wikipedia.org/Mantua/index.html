Mantua (Italian: Mantova) is a city in Lombardy, northern Italy, capital of the province of the same name. Surrounded on three sides by artificial lakes formed by the Mincio River, Mantua was one of the great courts of Renaissance Italy under the Gonzaga family, and its historic centre — together with Sabbioneta — is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. In 2016 it was the Italian Capital of Culture.

History

The site was an Etruscan settlement and then a Roman town; tradition names it for Manto, a prophetess, daughter of the Theban seer Tiresias. The Roman poet Virgil was born in 70 BC at Andes, a village near Mantua, and the city has honoured him ever since — Dante imagines meeting Virgil's spirit and describes his birthplace in the Divine Comedy.

After a period as a free commune and under the Bonacolsi family, Mantua passed in 1328 to the Gonzaga, who ruled it — as captains, marquesses (from 1433), and dukes (from 1530) — until 1708. Under Ludovico II, Francesco II, Federico II, and their successors, the Gonzaga court attracted artists and scholars of the first rank: Leon Battista Alberti built the churches of San Sebastiano and Sant'Andrea; Andrea Mantegna, the court painter from 1460, decorated the Camera degli Sposi in the Palazzo Ducale; Giulio Romano designed the Palazzo Te for Federico II; and Claudio Monteverdi, employed at court, premiered his opera L'Orfeo there in 1607.

The War of the Mantuan Succession (1628–1631) brought a catastrophic sack by Imperial troops in 1630 and plague, from which the duchy never recovered. Mantua passed to Austrian Habsburg rule in 1708, was contested in the Napoleonic wars — the young Napoleon besieged it in 1796–97, and the Tyrolean rebel Andreas Hofer was executed there in 1810 — and joined the Kingdom of Italy in 1866.

Main sights

  • Palazzo Ducale — the vast Gonzaga palace complex, with Mantegna's Camera degli Sposi frescoes.
  • Palazzo Te — Giulio Romano's Mannerist suburban villa, famous for the Sala dei Giganti (Hall of the Giants).
  • Basilica of Sant'Andrea — designed by Alberti; houses a relic of the Holy Blood and Mantegna's tomb.
  • Duomo (Cathedral of San Pietro) and the Rotonda di San Lorenzo, the city's oldest church.
  • Bibiena Theatre (Teatro Scientifico), where Mozart performed as a boy in 1770.

Mantua also hosts Festivaletteratura, one of Italy's leading literary festivals, held each September since 1997.
