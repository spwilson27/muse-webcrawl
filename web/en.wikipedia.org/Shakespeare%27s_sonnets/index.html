Shakespeare's sonnets

Shakespeare's sonnets are a collection of 154 poems written by William Shakespeare on themes such as love, beauty, mortality, procreation and the passage of time. They were first published together in a 1609 quarto, though many were probably written across the 1590s and circulated privately beforehand.

Structure

All but two of the sonnets follow the form later named after Shakespeare: three quatrains and a final couplet, rhyming ABAB CDCD EFEF GG, usually in iambic pentameter. Sonnets 99 and 126 depart from the pattern, and Sonnet 145 is in tetrameter.

Sequences and persons

The collection is traditionally divided into a long sequence (1–126) addressed to a young man, the "Fair Youth", and a shorter sequence (127–152) concerning a "Dark Lady", followed by two mythological sonnets (153–154). The identity of these figures and of the dedicatee "Mr. W.H." has been debated for centuries. Famous individual sonnets include Sonnet 18 ("Shall I compare thee to a summer's day?"), Sonnet 29, Sonnet 116 and Sonnet 130.

Reputation

The sonnets are among the most celebrated lyric poems in English and have been translated into many languages and adapted in music and theatre.
