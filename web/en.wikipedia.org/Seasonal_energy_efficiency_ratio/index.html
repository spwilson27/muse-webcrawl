Seasonal energy efficiency ratio

The seasonal energy efficiency ratio (SEER) is a measure of the cooling efficiency of air conditioners and heat pumps used in the United States. It is defined as the total cooling output of a unit over a typical cooling season, measured in British thermal units (BTU), divided by the total electrical energy consumed over the same period, measured in watt-hours. A higher SEER rating means greater energy efficiency.

Regulation

Minimum SEER standards are set by the US Department of Energy and have been raised over time; standards that took effect in 2023 use a revised test procedure and the new SEER2 rating, requiring roughly 14.3–15.2 SEER2 depending on region and equipment type. Modern high-efficiency systems may reach SEER values above 20.

Related measures

SEER is related to the energy efficiency ratio (EER), measured at a single set of conditions, and to the coefficient of performance (COP). For heating mode, heat pumps are rated by the heating seasonal performance factor (HSPF). In Europe and other regions, seasonal measures such as SEER equivalents are likewise used under ecodesign rules.
