Owens Valley

The Owens Valley is an arid valley in eastern California, running roughly 75 miles along the Owens River between the Sierra Nevada to the west and the White and Inyo Mountains to the east. It is one of the deepest valleys in the United States.

History

The valley was home to Owens Valley Paiute people who practised irrigation agriculture. In the early 20th century, Los Angeles secretly bought land and water rights and built the Los Angeles Aqueduct (completed 1913) to divert the Owens River, sparking the California water wars. Owens Lake dried up, becoming a major source of dust pollution, later partly mitigated.

Geography

Towns include Bishop, Lone Pine, Independence and Big Pine. Nearby are Mount Whitney, the highest peak in the contiguous United States, and Manzanar, the wartime Japanese-American incarceration camp, now a National Historic Site.
