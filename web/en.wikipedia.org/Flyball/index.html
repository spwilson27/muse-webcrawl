Flyball

Flyball is a dog sport in which teams of four dogs race relay-style over a course of hurdles. Each dog jumps four hurdles, triggers a spring-loaded flyball box that launches a tennis ball, catches the ball, and returns over the hurdles; the next dog begins when the previous dog crosses the start line. The team finishing fastest without errors wins.

History

The sport grew out of scent-hurdle racing combined with a ball-fetch device developed in California in the late 1960s; inventor Herbert Wagner demonstrated his tennis-ball launcher on television, and organized flyball spread during the 1980s. The North American Flyball Association (NAFA), founded in 1985, and the British Flyball Association are major governing bodies.

Racing

Two teams race side by side in separate lanes. Hurdle height is set from the shoulder height of the smallest dog on the team, encouraging mixed-size teams. Dogs are timed individually; elite dogs complete the course in under four seconds, and top teams finish all four dogs in around fifteen seconds. Faults include early starts, dropped balls, and missed hurdles.

Titles and popularity

Governing bodies award points and titles based on cumulative race performance, and the sport is played in North America, Europe, Australia, and elsewhere, welcoming virtually any dog including mixed breeds.
