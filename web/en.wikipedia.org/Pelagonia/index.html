Pelagonia

Pelagonia is a geographical region of Macedonia, named after the ancient kingdom of Pelagonia. It is a large fertile plain in the southwestern part of North Macedonia and northwestern Greece, traversed by the Crna River and containing the cities of Bitola and Prilep in North Macedonia and Florina in Greece.

Pelagonia
Countries: North Macedonia, Greece

History

In antiquity Pelagonia was a region of Upper Macedonia, inhabited by the Pelagones and ruled by its own kings before being annexed by Philip II in the 4th century BC. The Battle of Pelagonia in 1259, in which the Empire of Nicaea defeated the Principality of Achaea and its allies, was a decisive event in the Byzantine recovery of Constantinople.

Geography and economy

Ringed by mountain ranges, the Pelagonia plain is one of North Macedonia's principal agricultural areas, producing tobacco, wheat, maize and fruit, and it hosts much of the country's electricity generation at the Bitola power plant, fuelled by nearby lignite deposits.
