Paul Claudel

Paul Claudel (6 August 1868 – 23 February 1955) was a French poet, dramatist and diplomat, and the younger brother of the sculptor Camille Claudel. He was most famous for his verse dramas, which often convey his devout Catholicism. Claudel was nominated for the Nobel Prize in Literature in six different years.

Life

Claudel experienced a sudden conversion to Catholicism on Christmas Day 1886 while listening to vespers at Notre-Dame de Paris, an event that shaped his life's work. He entered the French diplomatic service, serving in the United States, China, Brazil, Japan, and elsewhere, and was ambassador to Tokyo, Washington, and Brussels. His long diplomatic residence in China informed his prose poems in Connaissance de l'Est.

Works

His major plays include L'Annonce faite à Marie (The Annunciation, 1912) and the monumental Le Soulier de satin (The Satin Slipper, written 1919–1924, first performed in full decades later). His poetry, written in free biblical cadence known as the verset claudélien, includes the Cinq grandes odes. He was elected to the Académie française in 1946.
