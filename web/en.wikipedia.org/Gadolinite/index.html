Gadolinite

CategoryNesosilicate mineral
Formula(Ce,La,Nd,Y)2FeBe2Si2O10
ColourBlack, greenish black, brown
Hardness6.5–7 (Mohs)
Type localityYtterby, Sweden

Gadolinite is a silicate mineral consisting principally of the silicates of cerium, lanthanum, neodymium, yttrium, beryllium, and iron. The name may refer to two related species, gadolinite-(Ce) and gadolinite-(Y), depending on which rare-earth element predominates. It is a historically crucial mineral: its analysis led to the discovery of several rare-earth elements.

History

The mineral was found in 1787 by Carl Axel Arrhenius at a feldspar quarry near Ytterby, Sweden, and was named in honour of the Finnish chemist Johan Gadolin, who in 1794 isolated from it a new "earth", yttria. That crude yttria was later resolved into the oxides of numerous rare-earth elements, making the Ytterby quarry the most prolific single locality for element discoveries — yttrium, terbium, erbium, and ytterbium all take their names from the village, and gadolinium, too, is named for Gadolin.

Properties

Gadolinite is black to greenish-brown, glassy to greasy in lustre, and often metamict — its crystal structure degraded by radiation from contained thorium and uranium. It occurs in granitic pegmatites. Although a source of rare earths and beryllium, it is now of mainly scientific and collector interest rather than a major ore.
