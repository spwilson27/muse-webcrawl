Northampton Saints (officially Northampton Rugby Football Club) is a professional rugby union club from Northampton, England. The club plays in Premiership Rugby, the top division of the English rugby union system, and is based at Franklin's Gardens, its home since 1880.

History

The club was founded in 1880 by the Reverend Samuel Wathen Wigg, curate of St James's church in Northampton, as a means of promoting order among the parish's young men — hence the nickname "Saints" (and "Jimmies," from St James). For most of its history an amateur club with a proud local tradition, Northampton emerged as a leading professional side in the late 1990s under coach Ian McGeechan and chairman Keith Barwell.

Honours

Saints won the Heineken Cup in 2000, defeating Munster 9–8 in the final at Twickenham, and the European Challenge Cup in 2009 and 2014. They won their first Premiership title in 2014, completing a double with the Challenge Cup that season, and won the Premiership again in 2024, beating Bath in the final. Notable players include Ben Cohen, Steve Thompson, Dylan Hartley, Courtney Lawes, and Lewis Ludlam.

Rivalries

The club's fiercest rivalry is the East Midlands derby with Leicester Tigers, one of the oldest and most intense fixtures in English rugby.
