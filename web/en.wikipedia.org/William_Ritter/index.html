William Ritter

William Emerson Ritter (November 21, 1856 – January 10, 1944) was an American biologist, a founder of the Scripps Institution of Oceanography and a leading figure in early American marine biology.

Life and career

Ritter was born in Wisconsin and studied at Harvard under Charles Otis Whitman. He taught at the University of California, Berkeley, and directed its marine station work, developing the idea of summer research stations on the Pacific coast.

Scripps Institution

With support from newspaper publisher E. W. Scripps and his sister Ellen Browning Scripps, Ritter established a marine biological station at Coronado in 1903, which moved to La Jolla and was incorporated into the University of California in 1912 as the Scripps Institution of Oceanography. He served as its first director until 1923.

Philosophy of biology

Ritter championed the "organismal" view — that living things must be studied as integrated wholes rather than reduced to isolated parts — set out in works such as The Unity of the Organism (1919). He also edited the Marine Biological Association reports and founded the Science Service news agency with E. W. Scripps in 1921.
