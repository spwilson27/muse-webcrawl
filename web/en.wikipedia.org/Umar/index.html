Umar

Umar ibn al-Khattab
Bornc. 582–583 CE, Mecca
DiedNovember 644 CE, Medina
Caliphate634–644 (second Rashidun caliph)
PredecessorAbu Bakr

Umar ibn al-Khattab (c. 583–644), known as al-Faruq ("the one who distinguishes right from wrong"), was the second Rashidun caliph, ruling from 634 until his assassination in 644. Under him the caliphate expanded dramatically and acquired the administrative structures of an empire. Sunnis rank him among the greatest Companions; Shia views are largely negative because of his role in the succession after Muhammad and events involving Fatimah and Ali.

Early life and conversion

A tall, formidable Qurayshi of the Banu Adi clan, Umar initially opposed Islam fiercely. His conversion, traditionally dated to 616 and linked to hearing verses of Surah Ta-Ha, greatly strengthened the young community. He emigrated to Medina and fought in Muhammad's campaigns.

Caliphate and conquests

Nominated by Abu Bakr, Umar oversaw victories at Yarmouk (636) against Byzantium and al-Qadisiyya (636) against Sasanian Persia, leading to the conquest of Syria, Egypt, Iraq and Persia. Jerusalem surrendered to him personally in 637/638, and tradition attributes to him the Pact of Umar governing dhimmi communities. He founded garrison cities such as Basra, Kufa and Fustat.

Administration and death

Umar is credited with the Hijri calendar, the diwan register of stipends, judicial appointments, market inspection, and canals and roads. Known for personal austerity and nightly patrols of Medina in popular lore, he was stabbed in 644 by Abu Lu'lu'a Firuz, a Persian captive, while leading prayer, and died days later after naming a council (shura) that chose Uthman. He is buried beside Muhammad and Abu Bakr in Medina. His reign laid foundations the Umayyad Caliphate inherited.
