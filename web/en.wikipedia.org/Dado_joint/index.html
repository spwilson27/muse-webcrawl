Dado (joinery)

A dado (in North American English), housing (in British English) or trench (in other contexts) is a slot or groove cut into a surface, usually of a board, into which the edge of another board fits. A dado joint is a strong and commonly used joint in cabinetry and shelving.

Description

A dado is cut across the grain of the receiving piece, distinguishing it from a groove, which runs with the grain, and from a rabbet, which is cut along an edge. A through dado passes entirely across the board; a stopped dado does not reach one or both edges and is hidden from view.

Making dadoes

Dadoes are traditionally cut with a saw and chisel. Modern woodworkers cut them with a table saw fitted with a stacked dado blade set, or with a router and straightedge guide. Dadoes are commonly used to house the ends of shelves in bookcases and cabinets, often glued for strength.
