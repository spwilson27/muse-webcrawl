Battle of Bloody Run

Battle of Bloody Run
Date: 31 July 1763
Location: Detroit, Michigan Country (modern Detroit, Michigan)
Result: Native American victory
Part of Pontiac's War

The Battle of Bloody Run was fought on 31 July 1763 during Pontiac's War, when a British force from Fort Detroit attempted a surprise night attack on the camp of the Ottawa chief Pontiac, but was itself ambushed and defeated beside a creek that thereafter bore the name Bloody Run.

Background

After the British victory in the French and Indian War, Native nations around the Great Lakes rose under leaders such as Pontiac against the new British garrisons. Pontiac besieged Fort Detroit from May 1763. Captain James Dalyell arrived with reinforcements and persuaded the post commander, Major Henry Gladwin, to allow a sortie against Pontiac's encampment at Parent Creek.

The battle

Dalyell led about 250 men out of the fort before dawn, but Pontiac had been warned — possibly by French settlers — and laid an ambush. As the British crossed the bridge over Parent Creek, warriors opened fire from cover on both sides. The British fought a retreat back to the fort, losing Dalyell himself and roughly twenty killed and forty wounded. The creek was said to have run red with blood, giving it its lasting name.

Aftermath

The defeat underlined British difficulties in the interior; the siege of Detroit continued until October 1763. Bloody Run is now a buried and built-over watercourse in Detroit, commemorated by a historical marker.
