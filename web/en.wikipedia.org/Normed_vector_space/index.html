Normed vector space

FieldFunctional analysis
DefinitionA vector space equipped with a norm (length function)
Complete caseBanach space
ExamplesEuclidean space, Lp spaces, C([a,b])

In mathematics, a normed vector space or normed space is a vector space over the real or complex numbers on which a norm is defined — a function assigning to each vector a non-negative length satisfying definiteness, homogeneity and the triangle inequality. A norm induces a metric and hence a topology, making it possible to discuss convergence and continuity of vectors and functions.

Definition

A norm on a vector space V is a function ‖·‖ : V → [0, ∞) such that for all vectors x, y and scalars a: ‖x‖ = 0 if and only if x = 0; ‖ax‖ = |a|‖x‖; and ‖x + y‖ ≤ ‖x‖ + ‖y‖ (the triangle inequality). If the first axiom is relaxed to allow non-zero vectors of norm zero, the result is a seminorm.

Examples

Finite-dimensional examples include Euclidean space with its usual length and, more generally, the ℓp norms. In function spaces, the Lp spaces of integrable functions and the space of continuous functions with the supremum norm are fundamental. A normed space that is complete in the induced metric is called a Banach space; a norm arising from an inner product yields, in the complete case, a Hilbert space.

History

The axiomatic theory of normed spaces was developed in the 1920s by Stefan Banach, whose 1920 doctoral dissertation and 1932 monograph Théorie des opérations linéaires founded modern functional analysis, with parallel work by Hans Hahn and Norbert Wiener.
