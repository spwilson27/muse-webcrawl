Komusō

The komusō (虚無僧, "priest of nothingness" or "monk of emptiness") were wandering Zen Buddhist monks of the Fuke school in feudal Japan. They were distinguished by the woven basket (tengai) worn completely over the head, symbolizing their detachment from the world, and by their playing of the shakuhachi flute.

Practice

Komusō played solo pieces called honkyoku as a form of meditation known as suizen ("blowing Zen"), and begged for alms in exchange for playing. Their ranks included many former samurai (rōnin) during the Edo period.

History

The Fuke sect was abolished in 1871 during the Meiji Restoration, partly because the basket-disguised monks had been used as spies by the Tokugawa shogunate, but the shakuhachi tradition they preserved survives in modern practice.
