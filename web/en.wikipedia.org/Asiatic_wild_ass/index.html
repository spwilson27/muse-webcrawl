Asiatic wild ass

The Asiatic wild ass (Equus hemionus), also called the onager, is a large mammal in the horse family native to the deserts and steppes of Asia. It has been listed as Near Threatened on the IUCN Red List since 2015. Five subspecies are generally recognised, including the Mongolian wild ass (khulan), the Turkmenian kulan, the Persian onager, and the Indian wild ass (khur).

Description

Asiatic wild asses are larger and more horse-like than African wild asses, with a sandy to reddish-brown coat, a pale belly, and a dark stripe along the back. They stand about 1.2 metres at the shoulder and can weigh over 200 kilograms. They are among the fastest land mammals in Asia, capable of speeds of 60–70 km/h.

Distribution and habitat

The species once ranged from the Middle East across Central Asia to Mongolia and northern China. It now survives in fragmented populations in Iran, Turkmenistan, Kazakhstan, Mongolia, India and Israel, where it was reintroduced to the Negev desert.

Behaviour

Wild asses live in herds led by a dominant stallion or in loose fission–fusion groups, feeding on grasses and shrubs and obtaining much of their water from food and seasonal waterholes. They are threatened by poaching, competition with livestock, and habitat fragmentation.
