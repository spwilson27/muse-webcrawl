Arcachon Bay (French: Bassin d'Arcachon) is a tidal bay on the Atlantic coast of southwestern France, in the Gironde department between the Côte d'Argent and the Landes forest. The bay covers about 150 km² at high tide but shrinks dramatically at low tide, exposing vast mudflats and oyster beds. It opens to the Atlantic between Cap Ferret and the Dune of Pilat, the tallest sand dune in Europe.

Geography and economy

The Leyre river flows into the bay's southeastern corner. In the centre lies the Île aux Oiseaux ("Bird Island"), with its distinctive stilted huts, the cabanes tchanquées. The bay is famous for oyster farming, an industry dating to the 19th century, and for seaside resorts including Arcachon itself, a popular 19th-century resort town with its Ville d'Hiver of ornate villas. Fishing, boating, and tourism are also important.

Ecology

Eelgrass beds, salt marshes, and mudflats support migratory birds and marine life; the Banc d'Arguin at the bay's entrance is a protected nature reserve and nesting site for sandwich terns.
