Spoon-billed sandpiper

The spoon-billed sandpiper (Calidris pygmaea) is a small wading bird that breeds in northeastern Russia and winters in Southeast Asia. This species is highly threatened, and it is said that since the 1970s the breeding population has decreased significantly. By 2000, the estimated breeding population of the species was 350–500 individuals, and it is listed as Critically Endangered by the IUCN.

Description

The most distinctive feature of this 14–16 cm long bird is its spatulate bill. In breeding plumage the adult has a rufous-chestnut head, neck, and breast with dark brown streaks; non-breeding birds are pale grey above and white below.

Distribution and threats

It breeds on the Chukchi Peninsula and southward along the Kamchatka isthmus, and migrates down the Pacific coast through Japan, Korea, and China to wintering grounds in Myanmar, Bangladesh, Thailand, and Vietnam. The main threats are habitat loss on its staging and wintering grounds, especially tidal flat reclamation such as at Saemangeum in South Korea, and hunting in Myanmar.

Conservation

An international conservation breeding programme was established at Slimbridge in the United Kingdom, and "headstarting" — rearing chicks from wild eggs and releasing them — has been used on the breeding grounds to boost productivity.
