Lateen

A lateen sail rigged on a traditional Mediterranean boat

A lateen (from French latine, meaning "Latin") or latin-rig is a triangular sail set on a long yard mounted at an angle on the mast, running in a fore-and-aft direction. The lateen was one of the most important sail plans of the ancient and medieval world.

Description

The sail is attached along its head to a long, low-slung yard that crosses the mast at an angle of roughly 45 degrees. The forward end of the yard is tacked down near the bow. Because the sail can be trimmed to sail relatively close to the wind, lateen-rigged vessels were more manoeuvrable than square-rigged ships, especially in the variable winds of the Mediterranean.

History

The lateen rig emerged in the eastern Mediterranean during late antiquity and became widespread in the Roman, Byzantine, and Arab worlds. It was used on dhows in the Indian Ocean and on Mediterranean craft such as the xebec and the felucca. Combined with the square sail on Portuguese caravels, it enabled the voyages of the Age of Discovery.

Variants

Related rigs include the settee sail, a quadrilateral variant with a short luff, and the modern recreational lateen used on boats such as the Sunfish.
