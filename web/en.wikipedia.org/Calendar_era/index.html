Calendar era

A calendar era is the period of time elapsed since one epoch (starting point) of a calendar, counted for the purpose of dating years. Eras provide a continuous count of years from a fixed origin, which may be a religious event, the accession of a ruler, the founding of a city or state, or a legendary date for the creation of the world.

Types of era

  • Religious eras: the Christian era (Anno Domini, AD/CE) counts from the traditional year of the birth of Jesus; the Islamic era (AH) counts from the Hijrah of 622 CE; the Buddhist era counts from the death of the Buddha; the Jewish era (AM, Anno Mundi) counts from a traditional date of Creation.
  • Regnal eras: years counted from a monarch's accession, used historically in Egypt, Mesopotamia and China, and still used in Japan, where each emperor's reign defines an era name (nengō).
  • Foundation eras: Rome's ab urbe condita (AUC) counted from the legendary founding of the city; the Seleucid era counted from 312/311 BC.
  • Political eras: the Republic of China (Minguo) era counts from 1912; the North Korean Juche era counts from the birth year of Kim Il-sung (1912).

Usage

Within an era, years may be counted forwards or, for periods before the epoch, backwards (e.g. BC, "before Christ"). Some systems, such as Anno Domini, have no year zero, passing directly from 1 BC to AD 1. Multiple eras may be in simultaneous use in one society for civil, religious and scholarly purposes.
