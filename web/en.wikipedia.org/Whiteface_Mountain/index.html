Whiteface Mountain

Whiteface Mountain
Elevation: 1,483 m (4,867 ft)
Location: Adirondack Mountains, New York
Known for: Olympic ski venue (1980)

Whiteface Mountain is the fifth-highest mountain in the U.S. state of New York, and one of the High Peaks of the Adirondack Mountains. It is known for a ski area with the greatest vertical drop east of the Rockies (3,430 feet) and for hosting the alpine skiing events of the 1980 Winter Olympics at nearby Lake Placid.

Features

The mountain takes its name from its bare, light-colored summit slide. The Whiteface Mountain Veterans Memorial Highway, opened in 1935, carries motorists most of the way to the summit, where a castle-like stone building, an elevator carved through the rock and a weather station serve visitors. The summit offers panoramic views reaching, on clear days, to Vermont and Montreal.

Ski area

Whiteface ski resort is operated by the New York State Olympic Regional Development Authority. Its trails include the expert "Slides", a series of steep off-piste chutes open when conditions permit. The mountain also hosts mountain biking in summer and the atmospheric sciences research station of the University at Albany on its summit.
