Galoshes

Galoshes, also known as rubbers, overshoes, or gumshoes, are a type of rubber boot worn over shoes to protect them from mud and water. They are typically made of waterproof material such as rubber or a synthetic polymer and are pulled on over ordinary footwear.

History

The word "galosh" derives from the French galoche and ultimately from terms for a wooden shoe or clog. Early galoshes were wooden soles with leather uppers strapped over shoes. Rubber galoshes became practical in the 19th century after Charles Goodyear's vulcanization process made rubber durable and weather-resistant. By the late 1800s, rubber overshoes were mass-produced and widely worn in wet climates.

Use

Galoshes are worn in rain and snow to keep leather shoes dry and clean. Modern versions include lightweight slip-on overshoes, zippered models, and heavy-duty boots for industrial work. They remain common in regions with slushy winters and muddy conditions.
