Child abduction

Child abduction or child theft is the unauthorized removal of a minor (a child under the age of legal adulthood) from the custody of the child's natural parents or legally appointed guardians. The majority of child abductions are parental child abductions, in which a parent removes a child in violation of a custody arrangement; a smaller proportion are committed by strangers or acquaintances.

Types

Child abduction includes parental abduction, abduction by strangers for purposes such as extortion, exploitation, or murder, and international child abduction, which is addressed by the Hague Convention on the Civil Aspects of International Child Abduction (1980), providing for the prompt return of wrongfully removed children to their country of habitual residence.

Prevention and response

Responses include alert systems such as AMBER Alert in North America, child identification programs, and coordinated law-enforcement and border measures. Research indicates most stranger abductions are resolved quickly, while parental abductions may involve concealment across jurisdictions.
