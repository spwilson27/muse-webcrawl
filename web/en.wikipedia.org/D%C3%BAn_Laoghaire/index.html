Dún Laoghaire (pronounced "dunleary") is a suburban coastal town in County Dublin, Ireland, about 12 km south of Dublin city centre. It is the administrative centre of the county of Dún Laoghaire–Rathdown. The name means "fort of Laoghaire", referring to Lóegaire mac Néill, a 5th-century High King of Ireland, who is said to have used the site as a base for raids on Britain and Gaul.

History

The town grew around its great artificial harbour, constructed between 1817 and 1842 with two granite piers — the East Pier and West Pier — enclosing one of the largest man-made harbours in the world at the time. In 1821 the town was renamed Kingstown in honour of a visit by George IV, a name it kept until 1920, when the town council restored the Irish name Dún Laoghaire. The coming of the Dublin and Kingstown Railway in 1834 — Ireland's first railway — tied the port closely to the capital, and for many years the mailboat ferry to Holyhead in Wales was a principal link between Ireland and Britain.

The town today

Dún Laoghaire remains a centre for sailing and watersports, with several yacht clubs and a marina, and the East Pier is a popular promenade. Landmarks include the National Maritime Museum of Ireland in the former Mariners' Church, the James Joyce Tower at nearby Sandycove, and the modern DLR Lexicon library. Scheduled ferry services to Holyhead ended in 2014, though the harbour continues to receive cruise ships and leisure craft.
