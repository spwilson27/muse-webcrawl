Visa policy

Entry visas and stamps in a passport

Related concepts: Visa (document), passport, border control

A visa policy is the set of rules a government applies to determine whether foreign nationals may enter, transit, or remain in its territory, and under what conditions. Visa policies specify which nationalities require a visa in advance, which may obtain a visa on arrival, which qualify for electronic visas (eVisas), and which are exempt from visa requirements altogether.

Purposes

Governments use visa policy to regulate migration, screen for security and health risks, enforce reciprocity with other states, and support tourism and business. Requirements typically depend on the purpose of travel — tourism, business, study, work, or residence — and each category has its own eligibility criteria and permitted duration of stay.

Common arrangements

Visa policies are shaped by bilateral agreements and regional blocs. Members of the European Union's Schengen Area share a common short-stay visa policy, and Gulf Cooperation Council and ASEAN states operate partial exemptions among their citizens. Programs such as the United States Visa Waiver Program allow nationals of trusted countries to enter without a visa for short visits, subject to electronic authorization.

Passport power

The number of destinations a passport allows its holder to enter without a prior visa is often used as a measure of travel freedom, tracked by indexes such as the Henley Passport Index.
