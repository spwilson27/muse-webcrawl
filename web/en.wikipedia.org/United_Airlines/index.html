United Airlines, Inc. is a major airline in the United States headquartered at the Willis Tower in Chicago, Illinois. Measured by fleet size and number of routes, it is one of the largest airlines in the world. United operates an extensive domestic and international route network across the United States and all six inhabited continents, with major hubs including Chicago–O'Hare, Denver, Houston–Intercontinental, Newark, San Francisco, Washington–Dulles, Los Angeles, and Guam.

History

United traces its origins to Varney Air Lines, founded by Walter Varney in 1926 in Boise, Idaho, which flew one of the first US airmail contracts and began passenger service between Pasco, Washington, and Elko, Nevada. William Boeing's Boeing Air Transport acquired Varney and other carriers, combining them in 1931 into United Air Lines under the United Aircraft and Transport Corporation; after the Air Mail scandal of 1934 forced the breakup of the holding company, United became an independent airline.

In the jet age, United grew into one of the "Big Four" US carriers, introducing aircraft types from the DC-8 to the Boeing 747. The airline passed through bankruptcy from 2002 to 2006. In 2010, United merged with Continental Airlines in a deal that created the then-largest airline in the world; the combined carrier kept the United name, Chicago headquarters, and Continental's globe livery, completing operational integration in 2012 and retiring the Continental brand.

Operations

United is a founding member of the Star Alliance and operates a fleet of roughly a thousand mainline aircraft from Airbus and Boeing, plus regional services branded United Express. Its loyalty programme is MileagePlus. The airline employs over 100,000 people and is a subsidiary of United Airlines Holdings, Inc., listed on the Nasdaq.
