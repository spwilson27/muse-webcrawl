The Dassault Mirage III is a family of single-seat, single-engine, supersonic fighter aircraft designed and manufactured by the French company Dassault Aviation. With its distinctive tailless delta wing, the Mirage III became the first Western European combat aircraft to exceed Mach 2 in level flight, and it was exported widely, serving with air forces in Europe, the Middle East, South America, Africa, Asia and Australia.

The design grew out of a 1950s French requirement for a lightweight interceptor. The delta configuration gave excellent high-speed performance at the cost of high landing speeds and poor low-speed manoeuvrability, traits later mitigated in derivative designs. Israeli Mirage IIICs achieved notable success in the Six-Day War of 1967, which boosted exports; the type also saw extensive combat in Indo-Pakistani wars, the Falklands War, and other conflicts.

Variants and legacy

The family spawned the Mirage 5 ground-attack derivative, the Mirage 50, and numerous licensed and unlicensed developments, including Israel's Nesher and Kfir and South Africa's Cheetah. Many air forces kept upgraded Mirage IIIs in service into the 21st century, and Pakistan's Project ROSE upgrades extended the type's life considerably.
