Ligne claire

Ligne claire (French for "clear line") is a style of drawing in Franco-Belgian comics characterised by strong, clean, uniform outlines, flat colours with minimal shading, and clear, highly legible compositions. The style is inseparably associated with Hergé, creator of The Adventures of Tintin, and with the magazine tradition that grew around his work.

Characteristics

In ligne claire work, every element is drawn with lines of roughly equal weight; backgrounds are rendered in meticulous, realistic detail while characters are simplified and iconic. Shadows and hatching are used sparingly so that the image reads instantly. Hergé refined the approach through the 1930s–50s, redrawing early albums to unify the style, and his studio trained a generation of artists at the Journal de Tintin.

Influence

The term was popularised in the late 1970s by Dutch artist Joost Swarte, who championed a revival. Artists such as Edgar P. Jacobs, Bob de Moor, Jacques Martin, Yves Chaland, and Ted Benoit worked in or against the style, and its influence extends beyond comics into illustration, animation, and graphic design.
