An Internet meme is a cultural item — an image, video, phrase, or concept — that spreads across the Internet, typically through imitation and remix by users. Memes mutate as they travel: each new version comments on, parodies, or repurposes the previous ones, so a meme is often less a single artefact than a family of related creations united by a recognisable template.

Image macros pair a stock reaction image with user-supplied captions.

Origins of the concept

Evolutionary biologist Richard Dawkins coined "meme" in The Selfish Gene (1976) for a unit of cultural transmission that replicates by imitation, analogous to a gene. Internet memes gave the idea a concrete, fast-moving form. Academic work, notably Limor Shifman's Memes in Digital Culture (2013), defines an Internet meme as a group of digital items sharing characteristics of content, form, or stance, created with awareness of one another and circulated and transformed by many users.

History and forms

Pre-social-media memes travelled by email, Usenet, and early forums: the Dancing Baby animation (1996), the Hamster Dance page (1998), and the Engrish catchphrase "All your base are belong to us" (2000–01). Image macros — pictures with bold overlaid captions — flourished with LOLcats and the "I Can Has Cheezburger?" site (2007), and advice-animal templates on Reddit and 4chan. Reaction GIFs, demotivational posters, rage comics, and later short-form video formats on Vine and TikTok each defined eras. Sites such as Know Your Meme document origins and spread.

Uses and impact

Memes function as jokes, in-group signals, commentary, and persuasion. They are used in marketing, political campaigning, and protest movements, and governments and extremist groups alike have exploited their deniable, humorous packaging. Brands' attempts to manufacture memes often fail because perceived authenticity matters. Memes can also carry legal and ethical issues, including copyright claims over template photographs and harassment of the people depicted.
