The Battle of Chantilly (also known as the Battle of Ox Hill) took place on 1 September 1862 in Fairfax County, Virginia, as the concluding battle of the Northern Virginia Campaign of the American Civil War. It was fought during a violent thunderstorm and is remembered chiefly for the deaths of two Union generals, Isaac Stevens and Philip Kearny.

Background

After his victory at the Second Battle of Bull Run (Manassas), Robert E. Lee sought to cut off the retreat of John Pope's Army of Virginia before it could reach the Washington defences. Lee sent Stonewall Jackson's wing on a wide flanking march via Sudley Springs to seize the Little River Turnpike near Fairfax Court House, astride Pope's line of retreat. Pope, alerted to the danger, ordered his army to fall back toward Germantown and Centreville, and sent divisions under Jesse Reno, Isaac Stevens and Philip Kearny to block Jackson.

Battle

On the afternoon of 1 September, Stevens's division attacked Jackson's men deployed along an unfinished railroad grade near the Chantilly plantation. In driving rain, Stevens personally led a charge carrying the regimental colours and was shot dead at the head of his troops, but his attack drove back A.P. Hill's Confederates and restored the Union position. Later, Kearny, riding forward in the gathering darkness and storm to reconnoitre, stumbled into Confederate lines and was killed when he tried to ride away. Fighting sputtered out in the downpour and darkness.

Aftermath

The battle was tactically inconclusive, but Jackson had been prevented from cutting the Little River Turnpike, and Pope's army completed its retreat into the Washington perimeter by 2 September. Combined casualties were roughly 2,100. Pope was soon relieved of command and his army merged into the Army of the Potomac under George B. McClellan, while Lee turned north into Maryland, beginning the Maryland Campaign. The battlefield at Ox Hill is partially preserved as a small park in Fairfax County, much of the ground having been lost to suburban development.
