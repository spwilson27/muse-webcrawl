Microsoft HoloLens

Microsoft HoloLens is a pair of mixed reality smartglasses developed and manufactured by Microsoft. HoloLens was the first head-mounted display running the Windows Mixed Reality platform on the Windows 10 operating system. It projects holographic images into the wearer's field of view, allowing digital content to be anchored to and interact with the physical environment.

Development and release

HoloLens, codenamed "Project Baraboo", was unveiled on 21 January 2015 at Microsoft's Windows 10 event. The device grew out of work by Alex Kipman's team, which had previously developed the Kinect sensor. A Development Edition shipped on 30 March 2016, priced at US$3,000, aimed at enterprise and developer customers rather than consumers.

Hardware

The headset is a self-contained computer containing a CPU, GPU, and a custom Holographic Processing Unit (HPU) that processes data from the device's sensors, including an inertial measurement unit, depth camera, and environment-understanding cameras. Waveguide displays project light into the eyes, compositing holograms over the real world. Users interact through gaze, voice commands, and hand gestures such as the "air tap". The original model weighed about 579 grams and offered a relatively narrow field of view of roughly 30 degrees.

HoloLens 2

HoloLens 2 was announced at Mobile World Congress on 24 February 2019 and released later that year at US$3,500. It more than doubled the field of view, added eye tracking and articulated hand tracking, improved comfort with a flip-up visor design, and adopted a Qualcomm Snapdragon 850 processor. Microsoft targeted industrial, medical, and military applications; a modified version was developed for the United States Army's Integrated Visual Augmentation System (IVAS) program.

Applications

HoloLens has been used for remote assistance, design visualization, surgical planning, astronaut training aboard the International Space Station, and guided manufacturing. Microsoft's Dynamics 365 Remote Assist and third-party software supported collaboration with holographic annotations.
