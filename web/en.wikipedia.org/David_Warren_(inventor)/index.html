David Warren (inventor)

David Ronald de Mey Warren AO (20 March 1925 – 19 July 2010) was an Australian scientist who invented and developed the flight data recorder and cockpit voice recorder — the device popularly known as the "black box" — which became standard safety equipment on commercial aircraft worldwide.

Early life

Warren was born in Groote Eylandt in the Northern Territory, where his father was a missionary; his father died in one of Australia's earliest air disasters, the crash of the DH86 Miss Hobart over Bass Strait in 1934. Warren was educated in Sydney and studied science at the University of Sydney, later working at the Aeronautical Research Laboratories in Melbourne on the chemistry of fuels.

Invention of the flight recorder

In 1953 Warren was part of the team investigating the baffling crashes of the de Havilland Comet, the world's first commercial jet airliner. Reasoning that a recording of the crew's voices and instrument readings in the moments before a crash would help investigators, he recalled seeing a miniature personal recorder at a trade fair and sketched a device that recorded onto steel wire. He built a prototype, the "ARL Flight Memory Unit", in 1957. Initially dismissed in Australia, the idea was taken up after interest from Britain, and flight recorders were progressively mandated, first in Australia and then internationally.

Later life and recognition

Warren continued his career at the Aeronautical Research Laboratories until retiring in 1983. Recognition came late: he was appointed an Officer of the Order of Australia in 2002, and an Airbus A380 was later named in his honour by Qantas. He died in Melbourne in 2010.
