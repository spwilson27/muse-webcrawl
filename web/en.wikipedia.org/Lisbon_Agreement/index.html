Lisbon Agreement

The Lisbon Agreement for the Protection of Appellations of Origin and their International Registration, signed in Lisbon on 31 October 1958, is an international treaty administered by the World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO). It establishes a system for the international registration and protection of appellations of origin — geographical names designating products whose quality and characteristics derive essentially from their place of origin, such as Champagne, Bordeaux, or Tequila.

How it works

A competent authority in a member state files an application with WIPO's International Bureau, which registers the appellation and notifies other members. Each member has one year to declare that it cannot protect the appellation in its territory; otherwise it must protect it against usurpation or imitation, even where the true origin is indicated or the name is accompanied by terms such as "kind" or "type". Protection under the Agreement is not subject to renewal and cannot lapse through genericization while the name remains protected at home.

Revision and the Geneva Act

The Agreement was revised at Stockholm in 1967 and amended in 1979. The Geneva Act of the Lisbon Agreement, adopted on 20 May 2015 and in force from 26 February 2020, modernized the system: it extends coverage to geographical indications as well as appellations of origin, allows accession by intergovernmental organizations (enabling the European Union to join), and introduces safeguards concerning prior trademark rights, generic terms, and fees. Membership long remained modest — around thirty parties — concentrated in Europe, Latin America, and parts of Africa and Asia.

Relation to other law

The Lisbon system complements the TRIPS Agreement's provisions on geographical indications and national or regional GI regimes. Its stronger, "absolute" protection standard has been debated in trade negotiations, particularly between the EU, which favours Lisbon-style protection, and countries where many European names are used generically.
