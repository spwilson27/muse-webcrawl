Ministry of Foreign Affairs of the People's Republic of China

Formed: 1949

Headquarters: Chaoyang District, Beijing

Agency executive: Minister of Foreign Affairs

Parent agency: State Council

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs of the People's Republic of China (MFA) is the constituent department of the State Council responsible for the foreign relations of China. It formulates and implements foreign policy, manages diplomatic missions abroad, and represents China at the United Nations and other international organisations.

Functions

The ministry oversees China's embassies, consulates, and permanent missions, negotiates treaties and agreements in coordination with other agencies, handles consular protection for Chinese citizens abroad, and conducts the daily press briefings for which its spokespersons are widely quoted. Although the ministry executes policy, major foreign policy decisions are made by the leadership of the Chinese Communist Party, including the Central Foreign Affairs Commission.

History

The ministry was established in 1949 with Zhou Enlai serving simultaneously as premier and first foreign minister. Its headquarters, a large curved building on Chaoyangmen Nandajie in Beijing, was completed in 1997. Notable foreign ministers have included Chen Yi, Qiao Guanhua, Qian Qichen, Yang Jiechi, and Wang Yi.
