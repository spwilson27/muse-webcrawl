Gregory of Tours (born Georgius Florentius; c. 538 – 17 November 594) was a Gallo-Roman historian and Bishop of Tours. He is the principal contemporary source for the history of the Merovingian Franks in the 6th century, through his ten-book Decem Libri Historiarum, better known as the History of the Franks. He is venerated as a saint in the Catholic Church.

Life

Gregory was born at Clermont into a leading Gallo-Roman senatorial family, several of whose members had been bishops of Tours and other sees. Educated at Clermont under his uncle's influence and cured, he believed, of an illness by Saint Martin of Tours, he was ordained deacon and in 573 succeeded his cousin Eufronius as Bishop of Tours. As bishop he navigated the violent civil wars of the Merovingian kings — Chilperic I, whom he criticised sharply, Guntram and Childebert II — and defended his church's rights and the cult of Saint Martin at Tours, then one of the great pilgrimage shrines of the West.

The History of the Franks

Gregory's main work begins with the Creation and reaches to 591 in its later books, which become a detailed, vivid contemporary chronicle. Written in the Latin of his day, which Gregory himself apologised for as unpolished, the History records wars, murders, miracles, portents, plague and church councils. It is indispensable — and often the only — narrative source for figures such as Clovis (whose baptism Gregory describes), Queen Fredegund and Brunhilda.

Other works

Gregory also wrote extensive hagiography, including the Glory of the Martyrs, Glory of the Confessors, and books on the miracles of Saint Martin and of Saint Julian, as well as a work on the offices of the church and an account of the martyrs of the persecutions.
