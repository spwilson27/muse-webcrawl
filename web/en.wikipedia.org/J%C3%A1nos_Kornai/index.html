János Kornai (21 January 1928 – 18 October 2021) was a Hungarian economist best known for his analysis of socialist economic systems. His book Economics of Shortage (1980) and his concept of the soft budget constraint became foundational in the study of centrally planned and transition economies.

Life and work

Kornai began his career as a journalist in communist Hungary, turning to economics research after the 1956 revolution. His early work Overcentralization (1957) was one of the first insider critiques of the command economy. In Economics of Shortage he argued that chronic shortages in socialist economies were systemic, arising from enterprises facing soft budget constraints — the expectation of being bailed out — which weakened price responsiveness and encouraged hoarding and quantity-driven production.

Later work

After 1989 Kornai advised on Hungary's transition to a market economy, warning in The Road to a Free Economy (1990) about sequencing and the social costs of reform. He held posts at the Institute of Economics in Budapest and at Harvard University. His memoir, By Force of Thought (2006), recounts his intellectual journey.
