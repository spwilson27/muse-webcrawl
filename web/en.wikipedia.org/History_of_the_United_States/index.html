History of the United States

The history of the United States begins with the Indigenous peoples of North America and the European colonisation that followed, and runs through independence in 1776, continental expansion, civil war, industrialisation, world wars, and the country's emergence as a global superpower.

The Declaration of Independence was adopted on 4 July 1776.

Colonial era

Indigenous societies had inhabited the continent for millennia before Europeans arrived. England founded Jamestown in 1607 and the Plymouth colony in 1620; by 1733 thirteen colonies lined the Atlantic coast, alongside French, Spanish and Dutch possessions. Slavery became central to the southern economy, and friction with Britain over taxation and governance escalated after the Seven Years' War.

Independence and the early republic

The American Revolutionary War (1775–1783) ended with British recognition of the United States. The Constitution of 1787 created a federal republic; George Washington was its first president. The Louisiana Purchase (1803) doubled the national territory, and expansion westward — driven by the ideology of Manifest Destiny — displaced Native nations and raised the question of slavery's extension.

Civil War and Reconstruction

The election of Abraham Lincoln in 1860 prompted southern states to secede; the Civil War (1861–1865) preserved the Union and ended slavery through the Thirteenth Amendment. Reconstruction's promises to freedpeople were curtailed after 1877, and segregation hardened in the South.

Industrialisation and world power

Gilded Age industrialisation, immigration and urbanisation transformed the country. The United States emerged from the Spanish–American War (1898) as an overseas power, intervened decisively in the First World War, and after the Great Depression enacted the New Deal. In the Second World War American industry and arms were decisive, and the postwar order made the US one of two superpowers, locked in the Cold War with the Soviet Union.

Modern era

The civil rights movement dismantled legal segregation; the US landed men on the Moon in 1969. After the Cold War ended in 1991, the United States remained the world's leading economic and military power, its politics increasingly polarised in the 21st century.
