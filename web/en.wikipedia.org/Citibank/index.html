Citibank, N.A. (National Association) is the primary U.S. banking subsidiary of the multinational financial services company Citigroup. It was founded on June 16, 1812, as the City Bank of New York, and later became First National City Bank of New York. Citibank is one of the largest banks in the United States and operates a global network serving consumers, corporations, governments and institutions in numerous countries.

History

The bank was chartered in 1812 with $2 million of capital, with Samuel Osgood as its first president. It served New York merchants in the aftermath of the War of 1812. In 1865 it joined the national banking system, becoming The National City Bank of New York. Under president James Stillman and later Frank A. Vanderlip, it grew into the largest bank in the United States by the early 20th century, opening its first foreign branch in Buenos Aires in 1914.

In 1955 the bank merged with the First National Bank of New York to form the First National City Bank of New York, shortened to "Citibank" in 1976. Under Walter Wriston, Citibank pioneered negotiable certificates of deposit, automated teller machines (ATMs) and the proliferation of credit cards. In 1998 its parent, Citicorp, merged with Travelers Group to form Citigroup, one of the largest financial mergers in history.

Operations

Citibank provides retail banking, credit cards, mortgages, wealth management and commercial banking services. Its U.S. branch network is concentrated in major metropolitan areas including New York, Chicago, Los Angeles, San Francisco, Washington, D.C., and Miami, while its consumer-franchise footprint abroad has been refocused toward global wealth hubs. The bank is a major issuer of credit cards and a member of the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC).

2008 financial crisis

During the 2008 financial crisis, Citigroup suffered heavy losses from subprime mortgage exposures. In November 2008 the U.S. government provided a rescue package including a $25 billion capital injection under the Troubled Asset Relief Program (in addition to an earlier $25 billion) and guarantees on a pool of troubled assets; Citibank remained the principal banking unit through the crisis and subsequent restructuring.
