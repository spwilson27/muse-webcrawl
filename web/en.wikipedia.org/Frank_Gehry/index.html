Frank Gehry

The Guggenheim Museum Bilbao (1997), Gehry's most celebrated building

Frank Owen Gehry (born Ephraim Owen Goldberg; February 28, 1929) is a Canadian-born American architect and designer known for his postmodern designs and use of unconventional materials. A number of his buildings, including his private residence in Santa Monica, California, have become world-renowned attractions, and he is widely regarded as among the most influential architects of his generation. He received the Pritzker Architecture Prize in 1989.

Career and works

Born in Toronto, Gehry moved to Los Angeles in 1947 and studied architecture at the University of Southern California. His remodelled Santa Monica house (1978), wrapped in corrugated metal and chain-link fencing, brought early fame. After designing furniture and commercial projects, he gained international acclaim with the Vitra Design Museum (1989), the Dancing House in Prague (1996, with Vlado Milunić), and above all the Guggenheim Museum Bilbao (1997), whose titanium curves were credited with revitalising the city — the "Bilbao effect". Later landmarks include the Walt Disney Concert Hall in Los Angeles (2003), the Stata Center at MIT (2004), the Louis Vuitton Foundation in Paris (2014), and 8 Spruce Street in New York.

Approach

Gehry's sculptural forms were enabled by CATIA software adapted from aerospace design, allowing complex curves to be engineered and fabricated. Critics have called some works wasteful or dysfunctional, but his influence on contemporary architecture — blurring building and sculpture — is undisputed.
