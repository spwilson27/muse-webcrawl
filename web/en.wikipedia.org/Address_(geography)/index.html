Address (geography)

An address is a collection of information, presented in a mostly fixed format, used to give the location of a building, apartment, or other structure or a plot of land, generally using political boundaries and street names as references, along with other identifiers such as house or apartment numbers and organisation names. It can also be seen as a standardised method of describing the whereabouts of a location so that it may be found by postal services, delivery services, emergency services, and visitors.

Format and systems

Address formats vary greatly between countries. In much of Europe and North America, addresses proceed from the specific to the general: recipient, house number and street, locality, region, and postcode. In Japan, addresses are structured by descending area units — prefecture, city, district, block (chōme and banchi) — without street names for most streets. South Korea adopted a road-name address system in 2011 alongside its older land-lot system. Postal codes (such as ZIP Codes in the United States, introduced 1963) support automated sorting, and international mail follows conventions promoted by the Universal Postal Union, which recommends writing the destination country's name in an internationally recognised form.

Uses and data

Beyond mail delivery, addresses underpin census-taking, taxation, land registries, utilities, emergency response, geographic information systems, and geocoding (the conversion of addresses to coordinates). Many countries maintain national address databases, and open data projects such as OpenAddresses aggregate address data globally. Addresses can be personal data and are handled under privacy law in various jurisdictions.
