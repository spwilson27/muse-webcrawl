Roman Libya

Roman Libya refers to the coastal region of North Africa corresponding to modern Libya during the period of Roman rule, from the 2nd century BC until the Vandal conquest of the 5th century AD. The area comprised two historic regions: Tripolitania in the west and Cyrenaica in the east.

History

Cyrenaica, long a Greek-speaking region centred on Cyrene, came under Rome in 96 BC and was united with Crete as a province. The west, former Carthaginian territory, was organised after Rome's defeat of Carthage. Under the Empire the coastal cities — Leptis Magna, Sabratha, Oea (Tripoli) and Cyrene — prospered from trade in grain, olive oil and wild beasts. Leptis Magna, birthplace of Emperor Septimius Severus, received lavish imperial building. The interior was guarded by frontier forts against desert tribes. Roman rule ended with the Vandal invasion of AD 455, followed by Byzantine reconquest in the 6th century and Arab conquest in the 7th.
