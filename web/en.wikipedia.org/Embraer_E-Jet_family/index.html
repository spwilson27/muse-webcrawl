Embraer E-Jet family

ManufacturerEmbraer
First flight19 February 2002
Introduction17 March 2004 with LOT Polish Airlines
VariantsE170, E175, E190, E195

The Embraer E-Jet family is a series of four-abreast, narrow-body, short- to medium-range, twin-engine jet airliners designed and produced by the Brazilian aircraft manufacturer Embraer. Announced at the 1999 Paris Air Show, the family comprises the E170, E175, E190, and E195, seating 66 to 124 passengers.

Development and design

The E-Jets were a clean-sheet design, larger and more capable than Embraer's ERJ regional jets, with a double-bubble fuselage, fly-by-wire controls on the larger variants' successors, and General Electric CF34 engines. They were designed to compete in the gap between regional jets and smaller mainline aircraft such as the Boeing 737 and Airbus A320 families.

Operations

More than 1,500 E-Jets have been delivered to airlines worldwide, including Republic Airways, JetBlue, Azul, and Air Canada. The family was succeeded by the re-engined E-Jet E2 family, while the E175 in particular remains in production for the U.S. regional market.
