Singularity (mathematics)

In mathematics, a singularity is a point at which a given mathematical object is not defined or not "well-behaved", for example not differentiable or not analytic. For example, the reciprocal function f(x) = 1/x has a singularity at x = 0, where the value of the function is not defined, as involving a division by zero.

Real analysis

In real analysis, singularities are either discontinuities or discontinuities of the derivative. Point discontinuities occur when a function has a jump or removable gap; essential discontinuities involve oscillation or unbounded behaviour near the point, as with sin(1/x) near zero.

Complex analysis

In complex analysis, isolated singularities of holomorphic functions are classified as removable singularities, poles, or essential singularities. The Casorati–Weierstrass theorem and Picard's great theorem describe the wild behaviour near an essential singularity, while Laurent series expansions characterize each type.

Algebraic geometry

In algebraic geometry, a singular point of an algebraic variety is a point where the tangent space is not well defined, detected by the vanishing of partial derivatives. Resolving singularities — replacing a singular variety by a smooth one — is a major theme, with Hironaka's theorem guaranteeing resolution in characteristic zero.
