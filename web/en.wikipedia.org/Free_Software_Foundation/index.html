Free Software Foundation

The Free Software Foundation (FSF) is a 501(c)(3) non-profit organization founded by Richard Stallman on October 4, 1985, to support the free software movement, with a preference for copyleft licensing (the GNU General Public License) for free software. The FSF was incorporated in Boston, Massachusetts, United States, where it is also based.

From its founding until the mid-1990s, FSF's funds were mostly used to employ software developers to write free software for the GNU Project. Since the mid-1990s, the FSF's employees and volunteers have mostly worked on legal and structural issues for the free software movement and the free software community.

History

The Free Software Foundation was founded in 1985 as a non-profit corporation supporting free software development. It continued existing GNU projects, such as the sale of manuals and tapes, and employed developers of the free software system. Since then, it has continued these activities, as well as advocating for the free software movement. In January 1986, the FSF began publishing the GNU's Bulletin; this later became the FSF's newsletter.

The FSF is the copyright holder for much of the GNU software and many other free software packages, an arrangement intended to protect the software from legal challenges. It maintains the licenses it publishes, including the GNU General Public License (GPL), the GNU Lesser General Public License (LGPL), and the GNU Free Documentation License (GFDL).

Projects

The FSF hosts and sponsors the GNU Project, the Free Software Directory, and the GNU software evaluation process. It runs campaigns against software patents, digital restrictions management, and proprietary JavaScript, and it maintains the definition of free software and a list of licenses it considers free or compatible with the GPL. Its companion organizations include FSF Europe, FSF Latin America, and FSF India.

Funding

The FSF is funded primarily through memberships, donations, and the sale of manuals and merchandise. Associate memberships form a large part of its income, and the foundation publishes audited financial statements and Form 990 filings in keeping with its tax-exempt status.
