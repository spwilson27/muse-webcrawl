Bona vacantia

Bona vacantia: ownerless goods passing to the state

Bona vacantia (Latin for "vacant goods") is a legal concept in common law jurisdictions referring to property that has no owner. It typically covers the estates of people who die intestate with no entitled relatives, the assets of dissolved companies that were not distributed before dissolution, and certain other ownerless property. Such property generally passes to the Crown or the state.

United Kingdom

In England and Wales, bona vacantia estates are administered by the Bona Vacantia Division of the Government Legal Department on behalf of the Treasury Solicitor. The Treasury Solicitor publishes lists of unadministered estates in the hope of finding entitled relatives, who have up to 12 years (and in discretionary cases up to 30 years) to claim. Within the Duchies of Lancaster and Cornwall, bona vacantia instead accrues to the respective duchy, which traditionally applies part of the funds to charitable purposes. In Scotland, ownerless property falls to the Crown as ultimus haeres and is administered by the Queen's and Lord Treasurer's Remembrancer.

Other jurisdictions

Analogous doctrines exist across the Commonwealth and the United States, where the equivalent process — unclaimed property passing to the state — is usually called escheat. Many jurisdictions maintain searchable registers so that heirs and former owners can recover property or its proceeds.
