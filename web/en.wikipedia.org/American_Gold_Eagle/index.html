American Gold Eagle

The American Gold Eagle is an official gold bullion coin of the United States. Authorised by the Gold Bullion Coin Act of 1985, it was first released by the United States Mint in 1986. The coins are struck in 22-karat gold (91.67% gold, with silver and copper added for durability), and by law the gold must come from sources within the United States.

Design

The obverse reproduces Augustus Saint-Gaudens' celebrated design for the 1907 double eagle, showing Liberty striding forward with a torch and olive branch. The reverse originally depicted a family of eagles designed by Miley Busiek, showing a male eagle returning to a nest; in 2021, the coin's 35th anniversary, the reverse was changed to a close-up profile of an eagle's head designed by Jennie Norris.

Denominations

Gold Eagles are issued in four weights: 1 oz (face value $50), 1/2 oz ($25), 1/4 oz ($10), and 1/10 oz ($5). Although they are legal tender, their real value is determined by their gold content and they trade at a premium over the spot price of gold. The Mint also produces proof and uncirculated versions for collectors, struck at West Point.
