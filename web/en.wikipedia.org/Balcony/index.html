Balcony

Balcony
Type: Architectural element
Materials: Stone, concrete, steel, timber

A balcony (from Italian balcone) is a platform projecting from the wall of a building, supported by columns, brackets or cantilevered beams, and enclosed with a balustrade or railing. Balconies are usually accessible from an upper-floor window or door.

Types

True balconies project far enough to stand on, while "Juliet balconies" are shallow guard rails at an opening window. Maltese balconies are enclosed wooden projections, and in theatres "balcony" refers to a raised tier of seating. French balconies and loggias are related forms.

History

Balconies appear in ancient and medieval architecture, including Islamic mashrabiya screens and medieval Italian palazzi. In the 19th century, cast-iron balconies became a hallmark of Georgian and Victorian terraces, the French Quarter of New Orleans, and apartment houses across Europe.

Uses

Balconies provide outdoor space in dense housing, serve ceremonial purposes — popes and monarchs address crowds from famous balconies — and feature in literature, most famously the balcony scene in Shakespeare's Romeo and Juliet. Modern building codes regulate their structural loading and railing heights.
