Bride

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A bride in Western wedding dress

A bride is a woman who is about to be married or who is newlywed. The male counterpart is the bridegroom (or groom). In Western culture, a bride traditionally wears a white dress, a custom popularised by Queen Victoria's wedding in 1840, often with a veil, bouquet and other ceremonial accessories. The members of the bride's accompanying party are called bridesmaids, led by a maid or matron of honour.

Etymology

The word derives from Old English brȳd, from a Germanic root associated with cooking and brewing in the household. In older usage, compounded forms such as "bride-ale" (the origin of the word bridal) referred to the wedding feast.

Bridal customs

Bridal customs vary greatly across cultures. In many South Asian weddings the bride wears a red sari or lehenga, symbolising prosperity and fertility, and her hands and feet may be decorated with henna (mehndi). In traditional Chinese weddings the bride wears red and may change outfits several times during the banquet. In Japan, a bride may wear a white shiromuku kimono and a traditional hood. In many traditions, gifts or wealth exchanges such as a dowry or bride price are associated with the marriage. The term "blushing bride" reflects older expectations of modesty, and a newly married woman may be called a bride for some time after the wedding day.
