Polyethylene terephthalate (PET or PETE) is the most common thermoplastic polymer resin of the polyester family. It is used in fibres for clothing (where it is known simply as polyester), in thermoformed packaging, and — in biaxially oriented film form (Mylar) — in electronics, insulation and food wrapping. PET is the standard material for disposable water and soft-drink bottles.

Production

PET is produced by the polycondensation of ethylene glycol with terephthalic acid (or dimethyl terephthalate), releasing water or methanol. The polymer can be spun into fibres, extruded into film, or injection stretch-blow moulded into bottles. Depending on processing, PET may be amorphous (transparent) or semi-crystalline (opaque and white).

History

PET was patented in 1941 by John Rex Whinfield and James Tennant Dickson at the Calico Printers' Association in Manchester, England, and commercialised as Terylene; DuPont developed it independently in the United States as Dacron. The PET bottle was patented by Nathaniel Wyeth in 1973.

Recycling

PET carries resin identification code 1 and is the most widely recycled plastic. Recycled PET (rPET) is made into fleece clothing, carpets, strapping and new food-grade containers. Because PET slowly hydrolyses and degrades with each cycle, chemical recycling routes that depolymerise it back to monomers are an active area of development.
