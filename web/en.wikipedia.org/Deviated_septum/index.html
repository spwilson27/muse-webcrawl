Deviated septum

A deviated septum is a condition in which the nasal septum — the cartilage and bone dividing the nasal cavity into two nostrils — is significantly displaced to one side, making one nasal passage smaller than the other.

Causes

In many people the septum is slightly off-centre from birth or as a result of normal growth; more pronounced deviations usually follow trauma to the nose, including injury during childbirth or later accidents and sports injuries.

Symptoms

Mild deviations are common and usually cause no symptoms. Severe deviation can obstruct airflow on one side, causing difficulty breathing through the nose, nasal congestion, recurrent sinus infections, nosebleeds, facial pain, and noisy breathing during sleep. It may contribute to snoring and sleep apnoea.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is by physical examination of the nose, sometimes with nasal endoscopy or imaging. Symptoms may be managed with decongestants, antihistamines, or nasal steroid sprays. The only permanent correction is surgery, usually septoplasty, in which the septum is straightened and repositioned.
