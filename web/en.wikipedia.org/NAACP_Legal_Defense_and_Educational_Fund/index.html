NAACP Legal Defense and Educational Fund

The NAACP Legal Defense and Educational Fund, Inc. (NAACP LDF or simply LDF) is an American civil rights law organisation and law firm based in New York City. It was founded in 1940 under the leadership of Thurgood Marshall, who subsequently became the first African-American United States Supreme Court justice.

Founded: 1940

Headquarters: New York City, New York, U.S.

Type: Nonprofit civil rights law firm

First director-counsel: Thurgood Marshall

History

LDF emerged from the legal department of the NAACP and became a fully separate organisation in 1957. While initially affiliated, it has been independent since then, with its own board and staff.

Notable cases

LDF litigated the landmark school-desegregation case Brown v. Board of Education (1954), in which the Supreme Court held that racial segregation in public schools was unconstitutional. The organisation has since argued cases concerning voting rights, the death penalty, employment and housing discrimination, and affirmative action, including Furman v. Georgia (1972).

Work today

LDF organises its work around political participation, criminal justice, economic justice, and education equity, combining litigation with policy advocacy, research, and public education, and it administers scholarship programmes for African-American law students.
