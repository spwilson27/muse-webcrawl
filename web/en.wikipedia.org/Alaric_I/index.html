Alaric I (Gothic: Alareiks; c. 370 – 410 AD) was the first king of the Visigoths, reigning from 395 to 410. He is best known for leading the Sack of Rome on 24 August 410, the first time in almost 800 years that the city of Rome had fallen to a foreign enemy — an event that shocked the Roman world and is often taken as a landmark in the decline and fall of the Western Roman Empire.

Early life

Alaric was born around 370 on Peuce Island at the mouth of the Danube, in what is now Romania. He belonged to the Balti dynasty, a noble Visigothic family. The Visigoths had crossed into Roman territory fleeing the Huns, and in 378 they defeated the Emperor Valens at the Battle of Adrianople. Alaric grew up within the Roman world and served in the Roman army; in 394 he commanded Gothic auxiliaries under the Emperor Theodosius I at the Battle of the Frigidus.

King of the Visigoths

On the death of Theodosius in 395, the Visigoths renounced their treaty with Rome and proclaimed Alaric king. He led them through Thrace and Greece, sacking Piraeus, Corinth, Argos and Sparta, before the Western Roman general Stilicho forced his withdrawal. In 401 and again in 408 Alaric invaded Italy, bargaining for land, gold and official Roman rank for his people.

Sack of Rome

After negotiations with the Emperor Honorius repeatedly failed, Alaric besieged Rome three times. On 24 August 410 his troops entered through the Porta Salaria and plundered the city for three days. Many great buildings were looted, though Alaric, a Christian, ordered that churches be respected and those sheltering in them spared. The psychological impact was enormous: St Jerome, writing from Bethlehem, lamented that "the city which had taken the whole world was itself taken."

Death and burial

Alaric marched south intending to invade Africa, but his fleet was wrecked by storms. He died at Cosenza in late 410. According to the historian Jordanes, he was buried with great treasure in the bed of the river Busento, which was temporarily diverted; the slaves who dug the grave were then killed to keep its location secret. He was succeeded by his brother-in-law Athaulf.
