Martial law in Poland (Polish: stan wojenny) refers to the period between 13 December 1981 and 22 July 1983 when the government of the Polish People's Republic, led by General Wojciech Jaruzelski, imposed martial law in an attempt to crush the Solidarity trade union and the democratic opposition.

Course of events

In the early hours of 13 December 1981 the Military Council of National Salvation took power. Tanks and army units deployed in the streets, telephones were cut, curfews imposed and borders closed. Around 10,000 people were interned, including Solidarity leader Lech Wałęsa, and the union was suspended and later outlawed. Violent suppression of protests included the pacification of the Wujek coal mine on 16 December, where nine miners were shot dead. Dozens of people died during the martial law period overall.

End and legacy

Martial law was suspended at the end of 1982 and formally lifted on 22 July 1983, though many repressive laws remained. Jaruzelski later argued the measure had forestalled a Soviet invasion; historians continue to debate that justification. The round table talks of 1989, held with a re-legalised Solidarity, eventually ended communist rule in Poland.
