Scale (ratio)

The scale of a map or model is the ratio of a distance on the representation to the corresponding distance in reality. A scale of 1:50,000 means that one unit on the map represents 50,000 of the same units on the ground. Scales may be expressed as a ratio, as a verbal statement ("one centimetre to one kilometre"), or graphically with a scale bar.

A graphical scale bar remains correct when a map is resized

Large and small scale

Terminology can be confusing: a large-scale map (such as 1:10,000) shows a small area in great detail because the fraction is large, while a small-scale map (such as 1:1,000,000) covers a large area with little detail. Cartographers generally class maps above about 1:150,000 as large scale.

Applications

Scale is central to architecture, engineering drawings and model making. Scale models use standard ratios — 1:72 and 1:48 for aircraft kits, 1:87 (HO scale) for model railways — so that proportions match the real object. Photographic enlargement and reduction, and the representative fraction, are governed by the same principle. A representation drawn at actual size is said to be at full scale, 1:1.
