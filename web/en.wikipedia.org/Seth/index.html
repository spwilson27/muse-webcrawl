Seth

Seth, Egyptian god of deserts, storms and disorder

Seth (also Set, Sutekh, or Setesh) is a god of deserts, storms, disorder, violence and foreigners in ancient Egyptian religion. He is depicted as a composite creature, the mysterious "Set animal", with a curved snout, long rectangular ears and a forked tail, or as a man with the head of that animal. In the Osiris myth, the most important myth in Egyptian mythology, Seth murders his brother Osiris and usurps the throne, before being defeated by Osiris's son Horus.

Role in Egyptian religion

Seth's role was ambiguous: he was a necessary force of chaos and strength as well as a dangerous one. He was said to stand at the prow of the sun god Ra's barque each night, spearing the serpent Apep and defending the sun through the underworld. He was associated with the red desert lands beyond the Nile valley, with foreign peoples, and with thunder and storms.

Mythology

In the Osiris myth, Seth, jealous of his brother, murdered Osiris, dismembered his body and scattered the pieces across Egypt; Osiris's wife Isis reassembled him and bore Horus, who contested the throne with Seth in a long series of battles and legal disputes eventually resolved in Horus's favour. The conflict between Horus and Seth symbolized the struggle between order and disorder, and the union of Upper and Lower Egypt.

Cult and later reputation

Seth was venerated especially in Upper Egypt and in the eastern Delta, and was a patron god of the Ramesside pharaohs of the Nineteenth Dynasty — Pharaoh Seti I's name means "Man of Set". In later periods, as Seth became increasingly associated with Egypt's foreign oppressors, he was progressively demonized, and many of his images were defaced or reassigned to other gods. The Greeks identified him with the monster Typhon.
