Edo (also known as Bini) is a Niger–Congo language spoken in Edo State in south-western Nigeria, principally by the Edo people, whose historic centre is Benin City, capital of the former Kingdom of Benin. It is the best documented of the Edoid languages and serves as a lingua franca across parts of the state, used in local media, education and religious broadcasting.

Classification and phonology

Edo belongs to the Edoid group of Benue–Congo languages, related to Esan, Urhobo and Etsako. Like most languages of the region it is tonal, with two basic level tones (high and low) and derived contours, and tone carries both lexical and grammatical distinctions. Its vowel system includes seven oral vowels with matching nasal vowels, and its consonant inventory includes the labial-velar stops /kp/ and /ɡb/ typical of West African languages.

Grammar and writing

Edo has subject–verb–object word order and marks tense and aspect with particles and auxiliaries. The language is written in a Latin-based alphabet developed during the colonial period; Bible translations and school materials date from the early twentieth century. Edo has a rich oral literature of proverbs, folktales and royal praise poetry associated with the court of the Oba of Benin. It should not be confused with the unrelated Edo period of Japanese history, named for the city of Edo (modern Tokyo).
