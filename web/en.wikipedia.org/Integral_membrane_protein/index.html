Integral membrane protein

An integral membrane protein (IMP) is a type of membrane protein that is permanently attached to the biological membrane. All transmembrane proteins are IMPs, but not all IMPs are transmembrane proteins. Integral membrane proteins make up a significant fraction of the proteins encoded in an organism's genome and carry out many essential functions, including transport, signalling and energy transduction.

Structure

Most integral membrane proteins span the membrane with alpha-helical segments of hydrophobic amino acids, typically 20–25 residues long, or form beta barrels, as in the outer membranes of Gram-negative bacteria. Their structures are harder to determine than those of soluble proteins because detergents are needed to keep them stable outside a lipid bilayer.

Functions

Examples include ion channels, transporters, pumps such as the sodium–potassium ATPase, photosynthetic reaction centres, and G protein-coupled receptors, which are major drug targets.
