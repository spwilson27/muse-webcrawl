Lena (river)

The Lena is a river in eastern Siberia, Russia, and the easternmost of the three great Siberian rivers that flow into the Arctic Ocean (the others being the Ob and the Yenisey). At about 4,294 km (2,668 mi) long, it is the longest river entirely within Russia and one of the longest in the world. It rises in the Baikal Mountains west of Lake Baikal and flows northeast and then north across the Sakha Republic (Yakutia) to a vast delta on the Laptev Sea.

Course and basin

The Lena's basin covers some 2.5 million square kilometres, almost entirely underlain by permafrost. Its major tributaries include the Kirenga, Vitim, Olyokma, and Aldan from the right, and the Vilyuy from the left. The only major city on its banks is Yakutsk, founded in 1632, a key port for supplying northeastern Siberia. Near the river's middle course stand the Lena Pillars, dramatic limestone rock formations protected as a national park and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The Lena Delta, one of the largest in the world at about 30,000 km², is a maze of channels and tundra islands and a major wildlife refuge.

History and transport

Russian explorers and Cossacks reached the Lena in the 1620s–30s, and the river became the main artery for the conquest and administration of Siberia and the Russian Far East; the name of Vladimir Lenin's pseudonym is popularly (though uncertainly) derived from the river, recalling the Lena goldfields strike of 1912, in which tsarist troops shot hundreds of striking miners. The river is navigable for roughly 3,500 km but is frozen for much of the year; summer navigation delivers supplies to remote Arctic settlements, and the spring ice breakup often causes severe flooding, notably at Lensk in 2001.
