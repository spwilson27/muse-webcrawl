Vyatka River

The Vyatka River (Russian: Вятка) is a river in the European part of Russia, the largest right tributary of the Kama River. It is 1,314 kilometres (816 miles) long, drains a basin of about 129,000 square kilometres, and flows through Udmurtia, Kirov Oblast, and Tatarstan before joining the Kama near the town of Mamadysh.

The Vyatka is the principal river of Kirov Oblast.

Course

The Vyatka rises in the northern part of Udmurtia and flows north, then turns southwest and finally south in a broad arc through the city of Kirov (formerly also called Vyatka), the largest settlement on its banks. Other towns on the river include Slobodskoy, Kirovo-Chepetsk, Vyatskiye Polyany, and Sosnovka. The river freezes from early November until late April and is navigable in its lower and middle reaches during the ice-free season.

History

The Vyatka basin was a contested frontier between the Volga Bulgars, the Rus' principalities, and later the Khanate of Kazan. Russian settlers founded the town of Khlynov (later Vyatka, now Kirov) in the 14th century, and the region maintained a reputation for semi-independent, merchant-led governance — the Vyatka veche republic — before being absorbed into the Grand Duchy of Moscow at the end of the 15th century. The river long served as a trade route for grain, timber, and furs.
