The Prespa Agreement (Greek: Συμφωνία των Πρεσπών; Macedonian: Преспански договор), also known as the Prespes Agreement or the Treaty of Prespa, is an agreement reached in 2018 between Greece and the Republic of Macedonia, under the auspices of the United Nations, resolving a long-standing dispute over the latter's name. Under the agreement, the country renamed itself the Republic of North Macedonia, a change that took effect on 12 February 2019.

Background

When the Socialist Republic of Macedonia declared independence from Yugoslavia in 1991 as the "Republic of Macedonia", Greece objected that the name implied territorial claims on its own region of Macedonia and appropriated Greek history and symbols. The dispute kept the country out of the UN under its chosen name — it was admitted in 1993 under the provisional reference "the former Yugoslav Republic of Macedonia" — and Greece blocked its accession to NATO and the European Union. A 1995 Interim Accord regulated relations without settling the name. Negotiations revived after Zoran Zaev became prime minister in Skopje in 2017 and Alexis Tsipras's government in Athens showed willingness to compromise; UN mediator Matthew Nimetz, who had worked on the issue since 1994, facilitated the final rounds.

Terms

  • The country's name becomes "Republic of North Macedonia", used erga omnes (in all contexts, domestic and international).
  • Its language is recognised as "Macedonian", within the group of South Slavic languages, and its citizens' nationality is "Macedonian/citizen of the Republic of North Macedonia".
  • The agreement acknowledges that the parties understand the terms "Macedonia" and "Macedonian" differently: for Greece they refer to its northern region and Hellenic civilisation; for North Macedonia, to its own territory, language and people, whose history is distinct from ancient Macedon.
  • North Macedonia agreed to review monuments and public works, such as the giant statue in Skopje promoted as Alexander the Great, and to rename infrastructure named after ancient Macedonian figures; Greece agreed to support North Macedonia's NATO and EU accession.

Ratification

A referendum in Macedonia on 30 September 2018 approved the deal by over 90% of votes cast, but turnout was only about 37%, below the 50% threshold, after a boycott campaign. The Macedonian parliament nevertheless passed the constitutional amendments in January 2019, and the Greek parliament ratified the agreement on 25 January 2019 by 153 votes to 146, amid street protests in Athens. The country formally became North Macedonia on 12 February 2019, and it joined NATO on 27 March 2020 as the alliance's 30th member.

Reactions and legacy

The agreement was widely praised internationally — its architects Zaev and Tsipras were nominated for the Nobel Peace Prize — but remained contentious in both countries, where opponents saw it as national capitulation. In 2024, relations soured again when North Macedonia's new president Gordana Siljanovska-Davkova used the name "Macedonia" at her inauguration, which Athens and the EU called a violation of the treaty.
