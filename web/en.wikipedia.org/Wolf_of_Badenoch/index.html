Wolf of Badenoch

NameAlexander Stewart, Earl of Buchan
Bornc. 1343
Died1394 (or 1405)
ParentsRobert II of Scotland; Elizabeth Mure
TitlesEarl of Buchan; Lord of Badenoch; Justiciar of Scotia

Alexander Stewart, Earl of Buchan (c. 1343 – 1394), known as the Wolf of Badenoch, was the third surviving son of King Robert II of Scotland. A powerful Highland magnate, he acquired a lasting reputation for violence and lawlessness, most famously for burning Elgin Cathedral in 1390.

Career

Stewart built his power base in the lordship of Badenoch in the central Highlands, ruling from Lochindorb Castle and Ruthven. He served as royal lieutenant and Justiciar of Scotia, but his rule relied on bands of Highland caterans — a practice condemned by the crown and church alike. He also acquired the earldom of Buchan through marriage to Euphemia I, Countess of Ross, whom he later abandoned for another woman, provoking a bitter ecclesiastical dispute.

Burning of Elgin

In 1390, the Bishop of Moray, Alexander Bur, excommunicated Stewart after a matrimonial judgment went against him. Stewart retaliated by descending on the burgh of Forres and then, on 17 June 1390, burning Elgin and its cathedral — the "Lantern of the North" — along with the manses of the canons. He was ordered to do penance, and is said to have submitted, barefoot, before the bishops at Perth. Tradition dates his death to 1394, though some sources place it in 1405; he was buried in Dunkeld Cathedral, where his effigy tomb survives.

Legacy

The Wolf's name became a byword for predatory lordship in medieval Scotland, and folklore — including the tale that he died after playing chess with the Devil at Ruthven — has kept his legend alive.
