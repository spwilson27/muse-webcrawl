The University of Parma (Italian: Università degli Studi di Parma) is a public university in Parma, Emilia-Romagna, Italy. Tracing its origins to a school founded in the tenth century and formally reorganised in the medieval period, it is one of the oldest universities in the world, organised into nine departments with particular strengths in food science, mathematics, medicine and law.

History

The university's origins are traditionally dated to 962, when the emperor Otto I granted Bishop Uberto of Parma authority over schools in the city. A studium generale flourished in the Middle Ages, teaching law and medicine. The university was suppressed and refounded several times, notably under the Farnese dukes in the seventeenth century, when it passed to the Jesuits, and under the Bourbon duke Ferdinand in the eighteenth century. It grew substantially in the second half of the twentieth century, developing the science campus on the southern edge of the city.

Organisation

The university is organised into departments including Chemistry, Life Sciences and Environmental Sustainability; Law, Politics and International Studies; Humanities, Social Sciences and Cultural Industries; Medicine and Surgery; Food and Drug; Mathematical, Physical and Computer Sciences; Engineering and Architecture; and Economics and Management. It hosts the European Food Safety Authority's host city connections informally, and Parma's designation as a UNESCO Creative City of Gastronomy in 2015 reinforced the university's food science profile.

Campus

The historic seat is the Palazzo dell'Università in the city centre, while the sciences are concentrated at the Parco Area delle Scienze campus of about 77 hectares, housing departments, the university library system, sports facilities and the technopole. Notable university collections include the Palatina Library's neighbourhood institutions and the natural history museum.

Notable alumni

Graduates include the mathematician and physicist Macedonio Melloni (who taught at Parma), writers and jurists, and the composer Giuseppe Verdi received an honorary association with the city's musical institutions; the university awarded honorary degrees to figures in food science and European integration.
