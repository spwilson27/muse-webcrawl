Società Sportiva Lazio (SS Lazio) is an Italian professional sports club based in Rome, best known for its football activity. Founded on 9 January 1900 in the Prati district of Rome, Lazio plays in sky blue and white — colours chosen in homage to Greece, the home of the Olympic Games — and shares the Stadio Olimpico with city rivals AS Roma, against whom it contests the Derby della Capitale. The club's crest features a golden eagle, symbol of the Roman legions.

History

Lazio is a multisport club (società polisportiva) with sections in dozens of sports. The football team was a founding member of Serie A in 1929. After decades of mostly mid-table finishes and one relegation, Lazio won its first major trophies in the 1970s: the legendary side led by Giorgio Chinaglia won the club's first Scudetto in 1973–74. Following financial turmoil and spells in Serie B in the 1980s, Lazio rose again under president Sergio Cragnotti in the 1990s, buying stars such as Alessandro Nesta, Pavel Nedvěd, Juan Sebastián Verón and Hernán Crespo. That team won the UEFA Cup Winners' Cup and UEFA Super Cup in 1999 and a second Serie A title in 1999–2000, along with several Coppa Italia triumphs.

Honours and support

Lazio's major honours comprise two Serie A championships (1973–74, 1999–2000), seven Coppa Italia titles, five Supercoppa Italiana titles, the 1998–99 UEFA Cup Winners' Cup and the 1999 UEFA Super Cup. The club's organised ultras, historically the Irriducibili (founded 1987), have been influential but also controversial, with sections of the support repeatedly sanctioned for racist and far-right displays. Since 2004 the club has been owned by entrepreneur Claudio Lotito, who rescued it from near-bankruptcy and has kept it consistently competitive in Serie A and European competition.
