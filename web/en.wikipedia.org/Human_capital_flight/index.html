Human capital flight

Human capital flight
Also calledBrain drain
DefinitionEmigration of highly trained or intelligent people from a place
OppositeBrain gain; reverse brain drain

Human capital flight, more commonly known as brain drain, is the emigration or immigration of individuals who have received advanced training at home. The net benefits of such emigration accrue mainly to the receiving country or region, while costs are borne by the place of origin. Historically the term referred to the mass exodus of scientists and technologists from post-war Europe to North America, but it is now used for skilled migration from developing to developed countries and between regions of any kind.

Causes

Push factors include low wages, limited research funding, political instability and lack of opportunity, while pull factors include higher salaries, better facilities and quality of life abroad. Medical brain drain — the emigration of doctors and nurses from poorer countries — is a prominent policy concern, addressed in instruments such as the WHO Global Code of Practice on the International Recruitment of Health Personnel (2010).

Effects and counterarguments

Origin countries lose their investment in education and face shortages in key sectors. Offsetting effects include remittances, diaspora knowledge networks, "brain circulation" as migrants return with new skills, and incentives for more people to gain education than would otherwise (the "beneficial brain drain" hypothesis). Related phenomena include brain gain, reverse brain drain and internal brain drain between sectors or regions.
