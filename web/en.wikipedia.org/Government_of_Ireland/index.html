Government of Ireland

Head of governmentTaoiseach

MembersUp to 15 ministers

The Government of Ireland (Irish: Rialtas na hÉireann) is the cabinet that exercises executive authority in Ireland. It is headed by the Taoiseach (prime minister) and consists of no more than fifteen ministers, each in charge of a department of state. The government is appointed by the President on the nomination of Dáil Éireann, the lower house of the Oireachtas (parliament), and is collectively responsible to the Dáil.

Constitutional basis

Executive power under the Constitution of Ireland (1937) is exercised by or on the authority of the government. The Taoiseach is nominated by the Dáil and appointed by the President; the other members are nominated by the Taoiseach with the approval of the Dáil and then appointed by the President. The Tánaiste (deputy prime minister) deputises for the Taoiseach. Ministers of State ("junior ministers") may be appointed to assist ministers but are not members of the government itself.

Composition and working

Members of the government must be members of the Oireachtas, with at most two drawn from Seanad Éireann (the Senate); the Taoiseach, Tánaiste and Minister for Finance must be members of the Dáil. The government meets in Government Buildings on Merrion Street, Dublin. Cabinet confidentiality is constitutionally recognised, and decisions are made collectively, usually on the basis of memoranda circulated by departments.

Coalitions

Because Ireland elects the Dáil by proportional representation (single transferable vote), majority single-party government has become rare; since 1989 Irish governments have usually been coalitions, notably between Fine Gael and Labour, Fianna Fáil and smaller parties, and from 2020 the historic coalition of Fianna Fáil, Fine Gael and the Green Party.

Functions

The government directs the civil service (organised into departments such as Finance, Foreign Affairs, Health, Education, Justice and the Department of the Taoiseach), prepares the budget, conducts foreign policy, and is responsible for national defence through the Defence Forces. It can fall if it loses a vote of confidence in the Dáil, after which the Taoiseach may resign or seek a dissolution and a general election.
