Iraq, officially the Republic of Iraq, is a country in West Asia. It is bordered by Saudi Arabia to the south, Turkey to the north, Iran to the east, the Persian Gulf and Kuwait to the southeast, Jordan to the southwest and Syria to the west. The capital and largest city is Baghdad. Iraq is home to diverse ethnic and religious groups, including Arabs, Kurds, Turkmen, Assyrians, Yazidis, Shabaks, Armenians and Mandaeans, and to both Shia and Sunni Muslims; roughly 95–99% of its citizens are Muslim.

History

The land between the Tigris and Euphrates — Mesopotamia — was a cradle of civilisation, home to Sumer, Akkad, Babylon and Assyria and to early writing, law and cities. Conquered by Alexander the Great and later ruled by Parthian and Sasanian Persia, it became a centre of the Islamic world after the Arab conquest of the 7th century; Baghdad, founded in 762 by the Abbasid caliph al-Mansur, was for centuries among the world's largest and most learned cities. Mongol invasion in 1258 devastated the region, which later became a frontier province of the Ottoman Empire.

After the First World War, Britain assembled the Ottoman provinces of Baghdad, Basra and Mosul into the Kingdom of Iraq under a League of Nations mandate, granting independence in 1932. The monarchy was overthrown in 1958; a series of coups culminated in the Ba'ath Party's seizure of power in 1968 and Saddam Hussein's presidency from 1979. His rule brought the Iran–Iraq War (1980–88), the invasion of Kuwait and the Gulf War (1990–91), and harsh sanctions and repression, including the Anfal campaign against the Kurds.

Modern Iraq

The U.S.-led invasion of 2003 toppled Saddam Hussein; the ensuing insurgency and sectarian civil war (2006–08) killed many tens of thousands. A federal parliamentary system was established under the 2005 constitution, with an autonomous Kurdistan Region in the north. After U.S. withdrawal in 2011, the militant group Islamic State (ISIL) seized much of northern and western Iraq in 2014, including Mosul; Iraqi forces, Kurdish Peshmerga and an international coalition retook the territory by 2017, amid mass displacement and the Yazidi genocide's recognition. Iraq remains a major oil producer — petroleum dominates exports and government revenue — while facing challenges of corruption, militia power, water scarcity and climate stress.

Geography and demographics

Iraq's terrain ranges from the Zagros mountains of the northeast to the alluvial plain of Mesopotamia and the Syrian and Arabian deserts of the west and south; the Shatt al-Arab gives it a short Gulf coastline. Its population, estimated around 45 million, is concentrated along the two rivers; Baghdad, Basra, Mosul and Erbil are the largest cities.
