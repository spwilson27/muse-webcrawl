Salesforce Tower

Salesforce Tower is a 61-story supertall skyscraper at 415 Mission Street in the South of Market district of San Francisco, California. Completed in 2018, it rises 1,070 feet (326 m), making it the tallest building in San Francisco and, at its completion, the tallest building west of Chicago — since surpassed by the Wilshire Grand Center in Los Angeles in overall height including spire comparisons. It is the headquarters of the cloud software company Salesforce.

History

The site, formerly a parking lot, formed part of the Transbay redevelopment programme around the new Transbay Transit Center (now the Salesforce Transit Center). The tower was originally proposed as the Transbay Tower. In 2014 Salesforce signed a lease for more than half the building — then the largest office lease in San Francisco history — and the building was renamed Salesforce Tower. Boston Properties took a majority stake and Hines developed the project. The tower topped out in April 2017 and opened in 2018.

Design

Designed by César Pelli of Pelli Clarke Pelli Architects, the tower has a gently tapered form with rounded corners, wrapped in a lattice of white aluminium fins and horizontal sunshades. Its structure uses a reinforced concrete core and is built to stringent seismic standards for the San Francisco Bay Area. The building achieved LEED Platinum certification and is noted for its blackwater recycling system and under-floor air distribution.

The crown and light installation

The top nine stories form a perforated crown wrapped in programmable LED panels. Artist Jim Campbell's installation Day for Night displays low-resolution moving images captured around the city each day, making the crown a kind of ambient public artwork visible across the Bay Area at night.

Salesforce Park and transit center

Adjacent to the tower is the Salesforce Transit Center, a multimodal station topped by the 5.4-acre Salesforce Park, a public rooftop green with gardens, an amphitheatre, and walking trails. The park is connected to the tower area by a gondola and is a distinctive feature of the district.
