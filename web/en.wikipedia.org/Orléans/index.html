Orléans is a city in north-central France, the prefecture of the Loiret department and capital of the Centre-Val de Loire region. It stands on the Loire River at the river's northernmost point, about 120 kilometres southwest of Paris.

History

The city began as Cenabum, a Gallic stronghold and trading port of the Carnutes, destroyed by Julius Caesar and rebuilt by the emperor Aurelian, from whom its present name derives. It was one of the great intellectual centres of medieval Europe, its university (chartered 1306) famed for law. Orléans is best known for the siege of 1428–29 during the Hundred Years' War, when Joan of Arc — thereafter "the Maid of Orléans" — helped lift the English siege, a turning point of the war commemorated every year in the Fêtes Johanniques.

The city today

Sainte-Croix Cathedral, rebuilt in Gothic style from the seventeenth century, dominates the skyline. The old town preserves half-timbered houses, including the Maison de Jeanne d'Arc. Orléans is a centre for the cosmetics industry (the "Cosmetic Valley"), logistics, and pharmaceuticals, and its UNESCO-listed Loire Valley setting draws many visitors. The city was largely spared industrial decline and has grown as a commuter and university hub within reach of Paris.
