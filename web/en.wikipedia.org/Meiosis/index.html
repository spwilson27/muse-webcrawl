Meiosis

Overview of meiosis: one diploid cell gives rise to four haploid cells.

Meiosis (from Ancient Greek μείωσις meíōsis, "lessening") is a special type of cell division of germ cells in sexually reproducing organisms that produces gametes such as sperm or egg cells. It involves two rounds of division that ultimately result in four cells with only one copy of each chromosome (haploid). Additionally, prior to the division, genetic material from the paternal and maternal copies of each chromosome is crossed over, creating new combinations of DNA on each chromosome.

Overview

Meiosis begins after DNA replication in interphase. In the first division (meiosis I), homologous chromosomes pair up, exchange segments by crossing over at chiasmata, and are separated into two daughter cells — a reductional division that halves the chromosome number. In the second division (meiosis II), sister chromatids separate in an equational division similar to mitosis. The result is four genetically distinct haploid cells.

Phases

  • Prophase I — subdivided into leptotene, zygotene, pachytene (crossing over occurs), diplotene, and diakinesis; homologous chromosomes form synaptonemal complexes.
  • Metaphase I — bivalents align at the equator; independent assortment shuffles maternal and paternal homologues.
  • Anaphase I / Telophase I — homologues move to opposite poles; two haploid cells form.
  • Meiosis II — prophase II, metaphase II, anaphase II, and telophase II separate sister chromatids.

Significance

Meiosis maintains a constant chromosome number across generations in sexual reproduction and generates genetic diversity through crossing over and independent assortment. Errors such as nondisjunction can lead to aneuploid gametes, underlying conditions such as Down syndrome. In females, meiosis is arrested in prophase I from before birth and completes only at ovulation and fertilisation, producing one ovum and polar bodies; in males it proceeds continuously from puberty, producing four spermatids per primary spermatocyte.
