The Yang di-Pertuan Agong (Jawi: يڠ دڤرتوان اݢوڠ), informally known as the King of Malaysia, is the constitutional monarch and head of state of Malaysia. The office was established in 1957 upon the independence of the Federation of Malaya. The Agong is elected to a five-year term by and from among the nine hereditary rulers of the Malay states, making Malaysia one of the world's few elective monarchies.

Election

Elections are conducted by the Conference of Rulers (Majlis Raja-Raja) by secret ballot, following an informal rotation system based on the seniority list established at the first election; a ruler is generally eligible only once per cycle. The youngest ruler or a ruler deemed unfit may be passed over. The deputy is the Timbalan Yang di-Pertuan Agong, who acts during vacancies or incapacity.

Role and powers

As a constitutional monarch, the Yang di-Pertuan Agong acts on the advice of the Prime Minister and Cabinet in most matters, but holds discretionary powers including appointing a prime minister who in his judgment commands the confidence of the Dewan Rakyat, withholding consent to dissolve Parliament, and requesting meetings of the Conference of Rulers. He is the Supreme Commander of the Malaysian Armed Forces, head of Islam in the Federal Territories and in states without rulers, and the fountain of honours, with the power to grant pardons for offences in the Federal Territories and military courts. Federal legislation requires his royal assent to become law.

Residence

The official residence is the Istana Negara in Kuala Lumpur; the former palace now serves as the Royal Museum. The Agong's official birthday is celebrated as a public holiday in June.
