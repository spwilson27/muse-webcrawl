Greater Santiago

Spanish: Gran Santiago

Country: Chile

Region: Santiago Metropolitan Region

Population: approx. 6.8 million (2017 census, urban area)

Greater Santiago (Spanish: Gran Santiago) is the conurbation and metropolitan area centred on Santiago, the capital of Chile. It comprises the continuous urban area formed by most of the communes of Santiago Province together with parts of the neighbouring provinces of Cordillera, Maipo, Talagante and Chacabuco in the Santiago Metropolitan Region.

Extent and administration

Greater Santiago is not a single administrative unit; it is made up of some 37–40 communes (municipalities), each with its own mayor and council, including Santiago (the historic centre), Providencia, Las Condes, Ñuñoa, Maipú, La Florida, Puente Alto, San Bernardo and Pudahuel. Chile's official statistics define the "Santiago metropolitan area" for census purposes, while planning agencies use the term Gran Santiago for the urban footprint. With around 6.8 million inhabitants in the 2017 census, it contains roughly 40% of Chile's population.

Geography and urban issues

The city lies in the Chilean Central Valley between the Andes and the Coastal Range, a setting that traps air pollution, especially in winter, making air quality a persistent public policy concern. Greater Santiago is Chile's political, economic, cultural and transport hub, served by the Santiago Metro — one of Latin America's largest rapid-transit systems — and by Arturo Merino Benítez International Airport. Socioeconomic segregation between the wealthier northeast and poorer southern and western communes is a widely studied feature of the metropolis. The October 2019 protests (estallido social) began in Greater Santiago over a transit fare increase before spreading nationwide.
