Valentina Vladimirovna Tereshkova (born 6 March 1937) is a Russian engineer, politician and former cosmonaut. On 16 June 1963 she became the first woman to fly in space, piloting Vostok 6 on a solo mission that orbited the Earth 48 times over nearly three days.

Early life and selection

Tereshkova worked in a textile factory and was an amateur parachutist when she volunteered for the Soviet space programme. She was selected from more than 400 applicants in 1962, partly because her parachuting experience was relevant to the ejection-seat landing used by Vostok capsules.

Vostok 6

Launched two days after Valery Bykovsky in Vostok 5, Tereshkova's flight made the final dual mission of the Vostok programme. She logged more flight time than all American astronauts combined up to that date. Despite nausea and discomfort, she completed her programme of observations and photography before landing near Altai.

Later career

Tereshkova became a prominent public figure, serving in the Supreme Soviet and on international women's organisations, and later as a deputy in the Russian State Duma. She remains the only woman to have flown a solo space mission and the youngest woman in space at the time of her flight, aged 26.
