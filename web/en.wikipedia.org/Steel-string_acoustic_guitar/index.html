Steel-string acoustic guitar

The steel-string acoustic guitar is a modern form of guitar that descends from the gut-strung Classical guitar, but is strung with steel strings for a brighter, louder sound. It is often referred to simply as an acoustic guitar, and is a prominent instrument in folk, country, blues, bluegrass, and popular music.

Construction

Steel strings exert far more tension than gut or nylon strings, so steel-string guitars are sturdier: the top is typically braced with an X-bracing pattern (developed by C. F. Martin & Co. in the 19th century), the neck is reinforced with an adjustable truss rod, and the bridge is pinned and glued. Common body shapes include the dreadnought, grand auditorium, jumbo, and parlor sizes. Tonewoods such as spruce for the soundboard and mahogany or rosewood for the back and sides are traditional.

Playing and history

Steel-string guitars are played with the fingers (fingerstyle) or a flatpick, and techniques include strumming, Travis picking, and slide. Martin, Gibson, and Taylor are historically significant builders. The dreadnought, introduced by Martin in 1916 and standardized in the 1930s, became the iconic bluegrass and folk rhythm guitar, while smaller-bodied models are favoured by fingerstyle players.
