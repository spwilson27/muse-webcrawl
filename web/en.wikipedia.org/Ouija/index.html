The Ouija (pronounced /ˈwiːdʒə/), also known as a talking board, is a flat board marked with the letters of the alphabet, the numbers 0–9, and the words "yes", "no", and "goodbye", used with a movable pointer called a planchette. Participants place their fingers lightly on the planchette, which is said to move — guided, in the belief of spiritualists, by spirits — to spell out messages. Commercialized in 1891 during the heyday of American spiritualism, the Ouija became a parlour game and remains a staple of popular occult culture.

History

Talking boards emerged from the 19th-century spiritualist movement's interest in automatic writing and table-turning. Elijah Bond patented a talking board in 1891 for the Kennard Novelty Company; its employee William Fuld later took over production and popularized the name "Ouija" — supposedly the board's own answer when asked its name, though Fuld claimed it combined the French and German words for "yes" (oui and ja). Parker Brothers acquired the rights in 1966 and, remarkably, the board outsold Monopoly in its first year of their ownership. Hasbro has sold it as a novelty/game since 1991.

Scientific explanation

Scientists explain the planchette's movement by the ideomotor effect: unconscious, involuntary muscle movements by participants, the same mechanism behind dowsing and automatic writing. Controlled experiments show that blindfolded users produce gibberish, while studies of the "Ouija phenomenon" demonstrate that the board can tap into participants' implicit knowledge and beliefs. Religious groups frequently condemn the board as dangerous or demonic, a reputation amplified by horror films such as The Exorcist (1973) and Ouija (2014).
