John van Bolland (Latin: Johannes Bollandus; 18 August 1596 – 12 September 1665) was a Flemish Jesuit priest and hagiographer. He organized the vast project of the Acta Sanctorum, the critical collection of saints' lives, and gave his name to the Bollandists, the scholars who continue the work.

Life

Bolland was born at Julémont in the Duchy of Limburg and entered the Society of Jesus in 1612. He taught humanities at colleges in Flanders before being assigned to Antwerp to take charge of the papers of Heribert Rosweyde, who had conceived a plan for publishing all extant lives of the saints from manuscripts.

The Acta Sanctorum

Bolland greatly expanded Rosweyde's plan, travelling and corresponding with libraries across Europe to collect manuscripts, and organizing the lives by the saints' feast days through the calendar year. With his collaborator Godfrey Henschen, he published the first two volumes (for January) in 1643; the February volumes followed in 1658. The work continued after his death and continues today under the Society of Bollandists in Brussels.

Legacy

The Acta Sanctorum remains the standard critical reference on the saints of the Roman calendar, and Bolland is regarded as a founder of modern critical hagiography.
