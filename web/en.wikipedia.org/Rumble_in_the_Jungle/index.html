The Rumble in the Jungle was a heavyweight boxing championship fight held on 30 October 1974 in Kinshasa, Zaire (now the Democratic Republic of the Congo), between undefeated champion George Foreman and challenger Muhammad Ali. It is widely regarded as one of the greatest sporting events of the 20th century.

The fight

Foreman, who had destroyed Joe Frazier and Ken Norton, was a heavy favourite against the 32-year-old Ali. Ali adopted his famous "rope-a-dope" strategy: leaning back on the ropes, covering up, and letting Foreman exhaust himself throwing punches at Ali's guard in the tropical heat. In the eighth round Ali came off the ropes with a rapid combination that knocked Foreman down for the count, regaining the heavyweight championship seven years after it had been stripped from him.

Context and legacy

The event was promoted by Don King and bankrolled by Zaire's president Mobutu Sese Seko, who staged it to showcase his country; it was accompanied by the Zaire 74 music festival. Ringside chants of "Ali, bomaye!" ("Ali, kill him!") became part of its lore. The fight was chronicled in Norman Mailer's The Fight (1975) and the Academy Award-winning documentary When We Were Kings (1996).
