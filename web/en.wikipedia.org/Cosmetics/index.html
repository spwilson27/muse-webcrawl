Cosmetics

Cosmetics are substances or products used to enhance or alter the appearance of the face or body, or the fragrance of the body. They include make-up (such as lipstick, foundation, mascara and eye shadow), skin care products (creams, lotions and sunscreens), hair care products, perfumes, and personal hygiene products such as toothpaste and deodorant. The global cosmetics industry is worth hundreds of billions of dollars annually, dominated by companies such as L'Oréal, Unilever, Procter & Gamble and Estée Lauder.

History

Cosmetic use is ancient: Egyptians used kohl eyeliner, malachite eye shadow and henna by 4000 BCE; Greeks and Romans used white lead face paint (with toxic consequences), and cosmetics were refined in the Islamic world and China. In the Victorian era cosmetics acquired disreputable associations in the West, but the twentieth century saw mass-market make-up popularised by figures such as Max Factor and Helena Rubinstein, cinema, and advertising.

Regulation

Cosmetics are regulated differently around the world. In the European Union, Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009 requires safety assessment and bans animal testing of cosmetic products and ingredients. In the United States, cosmetics (other than colour additives) do not require pre-market approval by the Food and Drug Administration, though the Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act of 2022 expanded FDA oversight. Products intended to treat disease — for example anti-dandruff shampoos or anti-caries toothpaste — may be classified as drugs.

Criticism and trends

Debates around cosmetics include the safety of ingredients such as parabens and PFAS, animal testing, unrealistic beauty standards, and environmental impact of microplastics. Counter-trends include "clean beauty," cruelty-free and vegan certification, and growing men's grooming markets.
