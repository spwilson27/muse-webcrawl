Cabernet Sauvignon

SpeciesVitis vinifera
OriginBordeaux, France
ParentageCabernet Franc × Sauvignon blanc
ColourBlack (noir)

Cabernet Sauvignon is one of the world's most widely recognized red wine grape varieties. It is grown in nearly every major wine-producing country among a diverse spectrum of climates. DNA profiling in 1997 revealed it to be the offspring of Cabernet Franc and Sauvignon blanc, most likely arising from a chance crossing in 17th-century southwestern France.

The grape is small, thick-skinned and rich in tannins and colour, producing deeply coloured, full-bodied wines with characteristic aromas of blackcurrant, and, depending on ripeness and oak ageing, notes of cedar, tobacco, green bell pepper and mint. Its thick skins make it relatively resistant to rot and pests, and it ripens late, favouring warm climates.

Regions

Cabernet Sauvignon is the signature grape of the left bank of Bordeaux, where it is blended with Merlot and Cabernet Franc in estates of the Médoc and Graves. It is also the dominant variety of Napa Valley in California and is widely planted in Chile's Maipo Valley, Australia's Coonawarra and Margaret River, South Africa's Stellenbosch, and Tuscany, where it features in "Super Tuscan" blends. For much of the late 20th century it was the most widely planted premium red wine grape in the world.
