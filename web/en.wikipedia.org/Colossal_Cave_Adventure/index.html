Colossal Cave Adventure

Colossal Cave Adventure (also known as Adventure or ADVENT) is a text adventure game first developed in 1976 by Will Crowther for the PDP-10 mainframe computer and expanded by Don Woods in 1977. It is considered the first well-known example of interactive fiction and the first adventure game, and it established many conventions of the genre.

Gameplay

The player explores a vast cave system modelled in part on Kentucky's Mammoth Cave, typing simple two-word commands such as "take lamp" or "go north" to navigate, collect treasures and return them to a small building above ground. The game introduced phrases that became famous in computing culture, including "You are in a maze of twisty little passages, all alike" and the magic word "xyzzy".

Legacy

Distributed widely over the ARPANET, Adventure inspired countless successors, including Zork and the commercial interactive fiction of Infocom, and influenced the design of later graphical adventure and role-playing games. Crowther, a caver and programmer at Bolt, Beranek and Newman, based the cave layout on his surveys of the Bedquilt region of Mammoth Cave.
