Giddha

Giddha is a popular folk dance of women in the Punjab region of India and Pakistan. Energetic and graceful, it is traditionally performed at festivals, weddings, and other celebrations, and is often described as the female counterpart of the men's dance bhangra, though the two differ greatly in character.

Performance

Dancers form a circle, clapping and singing traditional verses known as boliyan — short, witty, often teasing couplets that comment on village life, family relationships, love, and social customs. One woman, accompanied by a drum (often a dholki), leads the singing while others repeat the lines, and dancers step into the centre in pairs to act out or mime the themes of the verses through dance and gesture.

The dance is noted for its freedom of expression: within the circle, women can satirise in-laws, husbands, and social norms with a frankness not always permitted in everyday life. Movements are lively and spontaneous rather than formally choreographed.

Costume and occasions

Dancers wear brightly coloured traditional dress — salwar kameez or lehenga with embroidered dupattas — and jewellery, and often braid their hair with decorative tassels (parandi). Giddha is performed at seasonal festivals such as Teeyan, which celebrates the monsoon and is a special occasion for women's gatherings, as well as at weddings and cultural programmes in Punjab and in the Punjabi diaspora.
