The Ichneumonidae, also known as ichneumon wasps, ichneumonid wasps, ichneumonids, or Darwin wasps, are a family of parasitoid wasps of the insect order Hymenoptera. They are one of the most diverse groups within the Hymenoptera with roughly 25,000 described species, and estimates of total diversity ranging from 60,000 to over 100,000 species worldwide.

Biology

Ichneumonid wasps are parasitoids of other invertebrates, principally the larvae and pupae of beetles, butterflies, moths, and other wasps. The female uses her ovipositor to lay eggs in or on a host; the larva develops inside the living host, eventually killing it. Some species have extremely long ovipositors used to reach hosts deep inside wood.

Distribution

Ichneumonids occur on all continents except Antarctica and are especially diverse in temperate and tropical regions. Unlike many insect groups, their diversity does not peak solely in the tropics.

Relation to Darwin

Charles Darwin famously cited the parasitoid lifestyle of ichneumons in an 1860 letter to Asa Gray, writing that he could not persuade himself that a beneficent and omnipotent God would have designedly created the Ichneumonidae to feed within the living bodies of caterpillars. The group is therefore sometimes called Darwin wasps.

Subfamilies

The family contains dozens of subfamilies, including Ichneumoninae, Cryptinae, Pimplinae, Ophioninae, and Anomaloninae, and its taxonomy remains actively revised.
