Maqluba

Maqluba (Arabic: مقلوبة, literally "upside-down") is a traditional Levantine dish of rice, fried vegetables and meat cooked together in a pot and then flipped upside down onto a serving tray, so the vegetables form a decorative top layer. It is considered a national dish of Palestine and is eaten throughout Jordan, Syria, Lebanon and Iraq.

Ingredients and preparation

Typical ingredients include rice, eggplant, cauliflower, potatoes and carrots, with chicken, lamb or beef. The vegetables are fried and layered with the meat and rice in the pot; after cooking, the pot is inverted over a large platter and lifted away in a moment of some ceremony. Maqluba is commonly served with yogurt or a chopped salad.

History

The dish is documented in medieval Arabic cookbooks, including the 13th-century Kitab al-Tabikh compiled in Baghdad, and legends in Palestine associate its name with Salah ad-Din (Saladin).
