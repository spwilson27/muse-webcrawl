Jacobian matrix and determinant

In vector calculus, the Jacobian matrix of a vector-valued function of several variables is the matrix of all its first-order partial derivatives. When the function maps n variables to n outputs, the matrix is square and its determinant is the Jacobian determinant, often simply called "the Jacobian". Both are named after the mathematician Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi.

Definition

For a function f : Rⁿ → Rᵐ differentiable at a point, the Jacobian matrix is the m × n matrix whose (i, j) entry is ∂fᵢ/∂xⱼ. It is the matrix of the linear map that best approximates f near the point, generalising the derivative of a single-variable function: f(x) ≈ f(p) + J(p)(x − p).

Jacobian determinant

When m = n, the determinant of the Jacobian at a point measures the factor by which the function locally expands or contracts volume near that point. A non-zero Jacobian determinant implies, by the inverse function theorem, that the function is locally invertible. The absolute value of the Jacobian determinant appears in the change-of-variables formula for multiple integrals, where it accounts for the distortion of area or volume under a coordinate transformation.

Applications

Jacobians are used in solving nonlinear systems of equations (Newton's method), in robotics for relating joint velocities to end-effector velocities, in continuum mechanics, in optimisation, and in statistics for transforming probability densities.
