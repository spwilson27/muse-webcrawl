An Jung-geun

Born2 September 1879, Haeju, Joseon
Died26 March 1910 (aged 30), Lüshunkou, Kwantung Leased Territory
Known forAssassination of Itō Hirobumi

An Jung-geun (Korean: 안중근; 2 September 1879 – 26 March 1910) was a Korean independence activist. He is remembered to this day for his role in assassinating Itō Hirobumi, the former Resident-General of Korea and former Prime Minister of Japan, on 26 October 1909 at Harbin railway station in Manchuria.

Assassination of Itō Hirobumi

Itō had overseen the Eulsa Treaty of 1905, which made Korea a Japanese protectorate. An, a commander in a Korean righteous army, shot Itō three times as he arrived at Harbin to meet a Russian representative; he also wounded several members of Itō's party. After the shooting, An unfurled a Korean flag and shouted for Korean independence in Russian, before being arrested by Russian guards.

Trial and legacy

An was tried by a Japanese colonial court and sentenced to death. During his imprisonment he wrote an unfinished treatise, On Peace in East Asia, and famous calligraphy. He was executed by hanging on 26 March 1910. In South Korea, An is honoured as a national hero; a memorial hall stands in Seoul. Assessments remain divided internationally, and in 2014 a memorial to An opened in Harbin, a move welcomed in South Korea and China but protested by Japan.
