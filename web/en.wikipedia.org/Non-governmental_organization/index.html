Non-governmental organization

A non-governmental organization (NGO) is an organization that generally is formed independent from government. They are usually nonprofit entities, and many of them are active in humanitarianism or the social sciences, including health, the environment, human rights and development. The term originated with the United Nations Charter of 1945, whose Article 71 provided for consultative status for organizations that are neither governments nor member states.

Types and activities

NGOs may be classified by their orientation — charitable, service, participatory or empowering — and by their level of operation, from local community-based organizations to international NGOs (INGOs) such as Oxfam, Amnesty International, Médecins Sans Frontières (Doctors Without Borders) and the World Wide Fund for Nature. Activities include delivering humanitarian aid, monitoring elections and human rights, environmental advocacy, public-health campaigns and development projects.

Funding and structure

NGOs are funded by private donations, membership dues, foundations, corporations and, frequently, government grants — a fact that complicates the label "non-governmental". Many employ professional staff alongside volunteers and are governed by boards of directors under the nonprofit law of their home countries.

Criticism

Critics have raised concerns about accountability, since NGOs are not elected; about aid dependency and the crowding out of local institutions; and about foreign-funded NGOs acting as vehicles of outside influence, which has led some governments to restrict their registration and funding.
