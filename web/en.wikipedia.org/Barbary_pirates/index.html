Barbary pirates

The Barbary pirates, or Barbary corsairs, were pirates and privateers operating from the Barbary Coast of North Africa — the ports of Algiers, Tunis, Tripoli and Salé — from the 16th to the early 19th century. They raided shipping in the Mediterranean and Atlantic and conducted slave raids on coastal Europe.

History

Under loose Ottoman suzerainty, corsairs such as Hayreddin Barbarossa made Algiers a powerful base. Captives were ransomed or enslaved; over centuries the number of Europeans taken is estimated at up to a million or more.

Barbary Wars

The young United States fought the First (1801–1805) and Second (1815) Barbary Wars rather than continue paying tribute — the origin of the line "to the shores of Tripoli" in the US Marine Corps hymn. Stephen Decatur's 1815 expedition ended American tribute payments.

End

European bombardments (notably Exmouth's attack on Algiers in 1816) and the French conquest of Algiers in 1830 ended the corsair era.
