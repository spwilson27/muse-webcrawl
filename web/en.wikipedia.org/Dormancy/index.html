Dormancy


Dormancy helps organisms survive adverse conditions.

Dormancy is a period in an organism's life cycle when growth, development, and physical activity are temporarily suspended. It allows organisms to survive unfavourable conditions such as cold, drought, or food scarcity, resuming normal activity when conditions improve.

In plants

Many seeds exhibit dormancy, requiring specific cues — such as cold stratification, scarification, or light exposure — before they germinate. Perennial plants may enter seasonal dormancy, shedding leaves and halting growth over winter; buds and underground organs survive the cold and regrow in spring. Plant dormancy is regulated by hormones, notably abscisic acid and gibberellins.

In animals

Animal dormancy includes hibernation in mammals, brumation in reptiles, and aestivation in some amphibians, fish, and invertebrates during hot, dry periods. In insects, the corresponding state is called diapause.
