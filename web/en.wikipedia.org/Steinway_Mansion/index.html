The Steinway Mansion is a historic large Italianate villa in the Astoria neighbourhood of Queens, New York City. Built in 1857–1858 on a hill overlooking the East River, the granite mansion was constructed for Benjamin Pike Jr., a wealthy manufacturer of scientific instruments.

History

In 1870 the mansion and its estate were bought by William Steinway of the piano-making firm Steinway & Sons, whose factory stood nearby; the surrounding company town grew into the Steinway Village section of Astoria. The house, with some 27 rooms, extensive marble fireplaces, a central rotunda with a glass dome, and grounds that once covered a much larger estate, remained a Steinway family residence and later passed through private hands.

Designated a New York City Landmark in 1967, the mansion survived as the surrounding neighbourhood industrialized. For decades it was owned by the Halberian family, who maintained it as a private residence and museum-like repository of its furnishings. It was sold in 2014 to a consortium of investors, prompting preservation concerns, and has since been the subject of plans and disputes over its future and the development of its remaining grounds.
