Earthrise

Photographer: William Anders
Date: 24 December 1968
Mission: Apollo 8

Earthrise is a photograph of Earth and some of the Moon's surface that was taken from lunar orbit by astronaut William Anders on December 24, 1968, during the Apollo 8 mission. Nature photographer Galen Rowell described it as "the most influential environmental photograph ever taken."

The photograph

As Apollo 8 emerged from the far side of the Moon on its fourth orbit, the crew saw Earth appearing to rise above the lunar horizon. Frank Borman took an initial black-and-white shot, and Anders then took the famous colour image (catalogued by NASA as AS08-14-2383) with a modified Hasselblad camera. Audio recordings of the moment capture the crew's astonishment and scramble for colour film.

Impact

The image, showing a small, vivid, apparently fragile Earth against the blackness of space and above a barren lunar landscape, is credited with helping to inspire the modern environmental movement; the first Earth Day followed in 1970. It appeared on a U.S. postage stamp in 1969 and became one of the most reproduced photographs in history. In 2013, NASA released a recreation of the moment using Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter data.
