GO Transit

A GO Transit train in Toronto

GO Transit is a regional public transit system serving the Greater Toronto and Hamilton Area (GTHA) and the Greater Golden Horseshoe region of Ontario, Canada. With its hub at Union Station in Toronto, it operates commuter trains (GO Train) and buses (GO Bus) carrying tens of millions of passengers a year. Since 2009 it has operated as a division of Metrolinx, the provincial transportation agency. "GO" stands for "Government of Ontario".

History

GO Transit began on 23 May 1967 as a single commuter rail line along Lake Ontario between Oakville and Pickering, an experiment by the Government of Ontario in providing publicly funded commuter rail. It was the first service of its kind in Canada. Successive expansions added lines radiating from downtown Toronto — to Georgetown (now Kitchener), Richmond Hill, Milton, Bradford (now Barrie), and Stouffville — together with an extensive network of connecting buses and park-and-ride stations.

Services

GO Train operates seven lines, using diesel locomotives hauling Bombardier BiLevel coaches, with service historically focused on peak-period commuting but expanding toward all-day, two-way operation under the GO Expansion programme, which also includes electrification of core segments. GO Bus serves communities beyond the rail network, including express services along Highway 401 and to Pearson International Airport. Fares are distance-based and are paid using the Presto smart card system.

Network facts

  • Founded: 1967
  • Hub: Union Station, Toronto
  • Parent: Metrolinx (Government of Ontario)
  • Services: 7 rail lines, dozens of bus routes, over 60 stations
