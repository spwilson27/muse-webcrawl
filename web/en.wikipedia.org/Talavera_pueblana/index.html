Talavera pueblana

Talavera pueblana
RegionPuebla and Tlaxcala, Mexico
TypeMaiolica pottery
RecognitionUNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage (2019)

Talavera is a Mexican pottery tradition and a type of maiolica (tin-glazed earthenware) originating in the city of Puebla and nearby communities. It is named after Talavera de la Reina in Spain, whose ceramic traditions blended with local and other imported techniques in colonial Mexico.

Production

Artisans shape local clays, apply a white tin glaze as a base, and hand-paint designs—classically in cobalt blue, but also yellow, green, orange and black—before a second firing. Genuine Talavera is regulated by a denomination of origin and must follow traditional methods in the certified zone.

History

Spanish potters, including members of religious orders, introduced maiolica techniques to Puebla in the 16th and 17th centuries, where the craft absorbed Chinese influences arriving on the Manila galleons. Pots, tiles and tableware adorn Puebla's churches and kitchens; in 2019 UNESCO inscribed the artisanal Talavera process on its Intangible Cultural Heritage list jointly for Mexico and Spain.
