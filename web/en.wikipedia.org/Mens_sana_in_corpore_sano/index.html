Mens sana in corpore sano

Mens sana in corpore sano (Classical Latin: [mẽːs ˈsaːna ɪŋ ˈkɔrpɔrɛ ˈsaːnoː]) is a Latin phrase, usually translated as "a healthy mind in a healthy body". It comes from Satire X of the Roman poet Juvenal (1st–2nd century AD) and has become a widely used motto of schools, sports clubs and physical education movements.

Original context

The full line in Juvenal reads orandum est ut sit mens sana in corpore sano — "you should pray for a healthy mind in a healthy body". Juvenal's tenth satire surveys the vanity of human wishes — wealth, power, eloquence, military glory, long life — and concludes that what one should actually pray for is a sound mind in a sound body, a brave heart and freedom from fear of death. In context the phrase is about modest, virtuous desires rather than athletic training.

Later use

The philosopher John Locke quoted the phrase at the opening of Some Thoughts Concerning Education (1693), praising it as a full description of happiness in this world. From the 19th century it was adopted as a motto of physical education and the modern Olympic-era sports movement, reinterpreted as an ideal of balanced physical and intellectual cultivation — the meaning it retains in common use today.

Derivations

The phrase survives in altered forms such as the Japanese sportswear company ASICS, whose name is an acronym of the variant anima sana in corpore sano ("a healthy soul in a healthy body"). Numerous schools and gymnasia around the world bear the Latin motto in its original form.
