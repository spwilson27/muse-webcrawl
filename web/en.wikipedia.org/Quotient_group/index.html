Quotient group

A quotient group or factor group is a mathematical group obtained by aggregating similar elements of a larger group using an equivalence relation that preserves some of the group structure (the rest of the structure is "factored out"). For example, the cyclic group of addition modulo n can be obtained from the group of integers under addition by factoring out multiples of n.

Definition

Let N be a normal subgroup of a group G. The quotient group G/N is the set of cosets of N in G, with the group operation defined by

(aN)(bN) = (ab)N

Normality of N is exactly the condition needed for this operation to be well defined. The identity element is the coset N itself, and the inverse of aN is a−1N. The natural projection map G → G/N is a surjective group homomorphism with kernel N.

Examples

  • The quotient of the integers ℤ by the subgroup nℤ is the cyclic group ℤ/nℤ of integers modulo n.
  • The quotient of the real numbers under addition by the integers is the circle group of angles modulo 2π.
  • If G is abelian, every subgroup is normal, so every subgroup yields a quotient.

Properties

The first isomorphism theorem states that the image of a group homomorphism is isomorphic to the quotient of the domain by the kernel. The order of G/N equals the index [G : N] = |G| / |N| for finite groups (Lagrange's theorem). Quotients of cyclic groups are cyclic, and quotients of solvable groups are solvable; quotient groups are fundamental to the Jordan–Hölder theorem and the classification of finite groups.
