Branches of physics

Physics is a fundamental science that seeks to understand matter, energy, space and time. It is conventionally divided into branches according to the scale and phenomena studied, though the boundaries between them overlap heavily.

Classical physics

Classical mechanics describes the motion of macroscopic objects, from projectiles to planetary orbits, using Newton's laws, and its reformulations by Lagrange and Hamilton. Classical electromagnetism, unified by Maxwell's equations, covers electricity, magnetism and optics. Thermodynamics and statistical mechanics treat heat, energy and entropy, bridging microscopic behaviour and bulk properties; acoustics and fluid mechanics study waves in media and the flow of fluids.

Modern physics

Quantum mechanics governs atoms and subatomic particles, and with quantum field theory underlies atomic, molecular, nuclear and particle physics — the latter organised by the Standard Model. Relativity, special and general, describes high velocities and gravitation. Condensed matter physics studies solids and liquids — superconductivity, semiconductors and magnetism — and is the largest single subfield by number of researchers.

Interdisciplinary branches

Astrophysics and cosmology apply physics to stars, galaxies and the universe as a whole. Geophysics, biophysics, chemical physics, medical physics and plasma physics connect physics to the Earth sciences, life sciences and engineering, while applied fields such as optics, materials science and electronics grow directly out of the core branches.
