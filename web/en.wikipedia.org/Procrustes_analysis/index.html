Procrustes analysis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Superimposition of two shapes by Procrustes analysis

Procrustes analysis is a form of statistical shape analysis used to analyse the distribution of a set of shapes. The name Procrustes refers to a bandit from Greek mythology who made his victims fit his bed either by stretching their limbs or cutting them off.

In mathematics and statistics, Procrustes analysis is a technique for comparing shapes of objects. Given two sets of corresponding landmarks (points) describing two configurations, the method optimally translates, rotates (and, in the full Procrustes fit, uniformly scales) one configuration to superimpose it on the other, minimising the sum of squared distances between corresponding landmarks — the Procrustes distance. The optimal rotation is obtained from the singular value decomposition of a cross-covariance matrix, a solution related to the orthogonal Procrustes problem and the Kabsch algorithm used in molecular science.

Generalised Procrustes analysis

Generalised Procrustes analysis (GPA) extends the method to a sample of many configurations simultaneously, iteratively aligning each shape to the current mean shape and recomputing the mean until convergence. The aligned coordinates can then be subjected to multivariate statistical analysis, such as principal component analysis, to describe patterns of shape variation. GPA is a standard tool in geometric morphometrics, where it separates variation in size, position and orientation from true shape differences in biological specimens.

Applications

Applications include morphometrics and evolutionary biology, medical image registration, computer vision and face recognition, protein structure comparison, and psychometrics, where Procrustes rotation is used to align factor-analytic solutions.
