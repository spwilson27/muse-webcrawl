The Greco-Turkish War of 1919–1922 was fought between Greece and the Turkish National Movement of Mustafa Kemal Atatürk during the partitioning of the Ottoman Empire after World War I. It formed the western front of the Turkish War of Independence and is known in Greece as the Asia Minor Campaign and in Turkey as the Western Front (Batı Cephesi).

Course of the war

Greek forces landed at Smyrna (İzmir) on 15 May 1919 with Allied authorization, seeking to realize the Megali Idea by annexing Greek-populated parts of Anatolia. After initial advances and the Treaty of Sèvres (1920), Greek armies pushed deep inland toward Ankara, but were stopped at the Battle of the Sakarya (1921). In August 1922 the Turkish Great Offensive broke the Greek lines; Turkish forces recaptured Smyrna on 9 September 1922, and the Great Fire of Smyrna followed days later.

Consequences

The war ended with the Armistice of Mudanya and the Treaty of Lausanne (1923), which recognized the Republic of Turkey and mandated a population exchange between Greece and Turkey involving roughly 1.5 million people. The defeat destroyed the Megali Idea, led to the fall of the Greek monarchy government, and is remembered in Greece as the Asia Minor Catastrophe.
