Botafumeiro

Location: Santiago de Compostela Cathedral, Spain
Type: Thurible (incense burner)
Weight: ~53 kg
Height: 1.5 m

The Botafumeiro is a famous thurible used at the Santiago de Compostela Cathedral in Galicia, Spain. It is one of the largest censers in the world, weighing about 53 kilograms and measuring 1.5 metres in height, and is swung from the ceiling of the cathedral's transept during certain masses.

History

Great censers have been used at Santiago since the Middle Ages, traditionally said to fumigate the cathedral after the arrival of pilgrims on the Camino de Santiago. The present Botafumeiro was made by the silversmith José Losada in 1851. An earlier botafumeiro was carried off by Napoleon's troops in 1809 and replaced.

Operation

A team of eight men, the tiraboleiros, pulls on ropes to swing the vessel in a great arc across the transept, reaching speeds of up to about 68 km/h and nearly touching the ceiling. It is used on major feast days and for Friday evening masses in Holy Years and by arrangement.
