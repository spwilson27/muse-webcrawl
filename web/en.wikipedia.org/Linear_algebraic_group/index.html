Linear algebraic group

In mathematics, a linear algebraic group is a subgroup of the group of invertible n×n matrices that is defined by polynomial equations, equivalently an algebraic group that is an affine algebraic variety whose multiplication and inversion are regular maps.

Examples include the general linear group GL(n), the special linear group SL(n), orthogonal and symplectic groups, the additive group and the multiplicative group. Over an algebraically closed field, connected reductive groups are classified by root data, a theory developed by Claude Chevalley and others.

Structure

Every linear algebraic group has a unipotent radical; the quotient by it is reductive. Tori, Borel subgroups and parabolic subgroups play central roles in the theory and in representation theory.
