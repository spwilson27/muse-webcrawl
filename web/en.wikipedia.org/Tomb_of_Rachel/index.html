Tomb of Rachel

Tomb of Rachel
Hebrew: קבר רחל, Kever Rachel
Arabic: قبة راحيل, Qubbat Rahil
Location: Near Bethlehem, West Bank

The Tomb of Rachel (Biblical Hebrew: קְבֻרַת רָחֵל, romanized: Qǝbūrat Rāḥēl) is a site revered as the burial place of the biblical matriarch Rachel, wife of Jacob and mother of Joseph and Benjamin. It is located at the northern entrance of Bethlehem, just south of Jerusalem.

Biblical account

According to the Book of Genesis (35:19–20), Rachel died in childbirth on the road to Ephrath (Bethlehem), and Jacob set up a pillar over her grave. The tomb is also mentioned in Jeremiah 31:15, in which Rachel is described as weeping for her children — a passage later quoted in the Gospel of Matthew in connection with the Massacre of the Innocents.

The structure

The present structure, with its distinctive white dome, largely dates from the Ottoman period; the site was restored and expanded in the 19th century by Sir Moses Montefiore, who added an antechamber. The tomb is one of the holiest sites in Judaism, ranked after the Temple Mount and the Cave of the Patriarchs, and is also venerated by Christians and Muslims.

Modern history

Following the Six-Day War in 1967, the tomb came under Israeli control. Since the 1990s the site has been enclosed within a fortified compound due to security concerns, and access is controlled. The tomb continues to draw large numbers of Jewish worshippers, particularly women praying for fertility, echoing Rachel's biblical story.
