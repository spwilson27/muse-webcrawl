Cú Chulainn

Cú Chulainn, also spelled Cú Chulaind or Cúchulainn, is the great hero of the Ulster Cycle of Irish mythology. The son of the god Lugh and the mortal Deichtine, he is famed for defending Ulster single-handedly as a boy warrior during the events of the Táin Bó Cúailnge (The Cattle Raid of Cooley).

Youth and name

Born Sétanta, the boy earned his name — "Culann's Hound" — after killing the ferocious guard dog of the smith Culann and offering to take its place until a replacement could be reared. Trained in arms by the warrior woman Scáthach in Alba, he received the terrible barbed spear the Gáe Bulg, deliverable only by a throw with the foot.

The Táin

When Queen Medb of Connacht invaded Ulster to seize the brown bull Donn Cúailnge, the men of Ulster lay under a curse, and the seventeen-year-old Cú Chulainn held the province's border alone, fighting a series of single combats at fords. In battle he was seized by a ríastrad, or warp spasm, that contorted his body into a monstrous form. He was eventually forced to kill his beloved foster-brother Ferdiad in a three-day duel at the ford.

Death

Cú Chulainn died young, as foretold, in battle against the combined armies of Ireland incited by Medb and the sons of men he had slain. Mortally wounded, he tied himself to a standing stone so that he would die on his feet; his enemies dared not approach until a raven landed on his shoulder, the sign that the Morrígan's prophecy was fulfilled and the hero dead.

Legacy

Cú Chulainn remains a potent figure in Irish culture, depicted in sculpture — notably Oliver Sheppard's dying hero in Dublin's General Post Office, a memorial to the 1916 Easter Rising — and in countless retellings of the Ulster tales.
