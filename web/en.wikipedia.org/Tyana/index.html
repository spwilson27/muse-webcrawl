Tyana (Ancient Greek: Τύανα) was an ancient city in the Anatolian region of Cappadocia, in modern south-central Turkey. It was the capital of a Luwian-speaking Neo-Hittite kingdom in the 1st millennium BC and later became an important Roman city. Its ruins lie near the modern town of Kemerhisar, about 15 km south of Niğde.

Strategically placed on the road leading from the Cilician Gates through the Taurus Mountains into central Anatolia, Tyana was a wealthy trading and agricultural centre, watered by a Roman aqueduct whose remains are still visible. In the Hellenistic period it was sometimes known as Eusebeia by the Taurus.

History

Tyana is identified with the Hittite-era Tuwanuwa and was later ruled by kings such as Warpalawas (Urballa), known from Assyrian records. Under Roman rule it flourished; the emperor Caracalla granted it colonial status. The city is best known as the birthplace of the 1st-century AD Neo-Pythagorean philosopher and sage Apollonius of Tyana. It was also the birthplace of Saint George, according to some traditions. In late antiquity it was an important bishopric; it was later destroyed and rebuilt several times during the Arab-Byzantine wars.
