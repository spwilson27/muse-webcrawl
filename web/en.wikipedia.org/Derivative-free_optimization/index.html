Derivative-free optimization

Nelder–Mead simplex search, a classic derivative-free method

Derivative-free optimization (DFO) is a discipline of mathematical optimization dealing with problems where derivative information is unavailable, unreliable, or impractical to obtain — for example when the objective function is computed by an expensive simulation or is non-smooth or noisy.

Methods

Approaches include direct-search methods (such as the Nelder–Mead simplex method and pattern search), model-based methods that build local surrogate models (often quadratics fitted by interpolation or regression), and trust-region DFO methods such as those implemented in the BOBYQA and NEWUOA software of M. J. D. Powell.

Applications

DFO is used in engineering design, hyperparameter tuning, and simulation-based optimization where gradients do not exist.
