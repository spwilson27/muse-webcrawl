Cordillera Administrative Region

Regional centre: Baguio
Provinces: Abra, Apayao, Benguet, Ifugao, Kalinga, Mountain Province
Population: ~1.8 million (2020)

The Cordillera Administrative Region (CAR) is an administrative region of the Philippines located in the north-central part of the island of Luzon. It is the country's only landlocked region, encompassing most of the Cordillera Central mountain range.

Geography and provinces

The region comprises the provinces of Abra, Apayao, Benguet, Ifugao, Kalinga, and Mountain Province, plus the highly urbanised city of Baguio, which serves as the regional centre. Its rugged terrain is home to the Banaue Rice Terraces, often called the "Eighth Wonder of the World."

People and culture

The Cordillera is the ancestral homeland of a collective group of ethnic peoples known as the Igorots, including the Kankanaey, Ibaloi, Kalinga, Bontoc, and Ifugao. The region was established on 15 July 1987 by Executive Order No. 220, partly to prepare for a contemplated autonomous region for the Cordillera peoples.
