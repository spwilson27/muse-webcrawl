Cajamarca
City in northern Peru
Region: Cajamarca Region
Elevation: 2,750 m (9,020 ft)
Population: approx. 283,000 (2017)

Cajamarca (Qajamarka) is a city in northern Peru and the capital of the Cajamarca Region. It lies in the northern highlands at an elevation of about 2,750 metres above sea level, in a valley crossed by the Mashcon and Chonta rivers. With roughly 283,000 inhabitants it is among the largest cities of the Peruvian northern Andes.

History

The Cajamarca valley has been inhabited for more than three thousand years. The Cajamarca culture flourished in the region during the first millennium AD, and the area later came under the influence of the Wari empire and then the Inca Empire. The Inca emperor Pachacuti incorporated Cajamarca into Tahuantinsuyu in the mid-15th century.

Cajamarca is most famous as the site of the Battle of Cajamarca on 16 November 1532, when a small force of Spanish conquistadors under Francisco Pizarro ambushed and captured the Inca emperor Atahualpa. Atahualpa famously filled a large room with gold and twice with silver as a ransom — the room, known as El Cuarto del Rescate (the Ransom Room), still survives — but the Spanish executed him in July 1533 nonetheless. The event marked the beginning of the Spanish conquest of Peru.

Geography and climate

Cajamarca sits in an inter-Andean valley at about 2,750 m. Its climate is equatorial but moderated by altitude: mild days, cool nights, and a rainy season from roughly October to April. Average temperatures hover around 13–14 °C year-round.

Economy

The surrounding region is one of Peru's most important dairy-producing areas, known for cheese, butter and manjar blanco. Gold mining is also significant: the Yanacocha mine, one of the largest gold mines in South America, lies in the province. Tourism centres on the colonial old town, the Ransom Room, the Baños del Inca hot springs, and the pre-Inca aqueduct and carved rock channels of Cumbe Mayo.

Landmarks

  • El Cuarto del Rescate (Ransom Room)
  • Baños del Inca — thermal baths said to have been used by Atahualpa
  • Cumbe Mayo — ancient aqueduct and rock formations
  • Cajamarca Cathedral and the church of San Francisco
  • Ventanillas de Otuzco — pre-Inca necropolis of burial niches
