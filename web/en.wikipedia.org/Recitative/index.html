Recitative

Recitative (Italian: recitativo) is a style of delivery in opera, oratorio, and cantata in which a singer adopts the rhythms and delivery of ordinary speech. It is used for dialogue and narrative passages, contrasting with the more melodic, song-like aria.

Types

Recitativo secco ("dry recitative") is accompanied only by a basso continuo, typically harpsichord and cello, allowing great rhythmic freedom. Recitativo accompagnato (or stromentato) is accompanied by the full orchestra and is used for moments of high drama.

History

Recitative developed around 1600 with the Florentine Camerata and the first operas of Jacopo Peri and Claudio Monteverdi, who sought a musical speech imitating the declamation of ancient Greek drama. It remained the principal vehicle for advancing plot in Baroque and Classical opera, including the operas of Handel and Mozart, before gradually blending into more continuous musical textures in the 19th century.
