Gallurese

Gallurese (native name gadduresu) is a Romance language variety spoken in the Gallura region of northeastern Sardinia, Italy. It is closely related to Corsican, particularly the southern Corsican dialects, reflecting centuries of migration and contact across the Strait of Bonifacio that separates the two islands.

Classification and features

Scholars debate whether Gallurese should be classed as a dialect of Corsican, a transitional variety between Corsican and Sardinian, or a distinct language of the Italo-Romance group. It shares much vocabulary and phonology with Corsican but has absorbed substantial influence from Sardinian (especially Logudorese) and from Italian. The nearby variety Sassarese, spoken around Sassari, shows a similar mixed character.

Status

Gallurese is spoken by tens of thousands of people in towns such as Tempio Pausania, Olbia, and Arzachena, though transmission to younger generations has weakened under the dominance of Italian. It enjoys recognition under Italian and Sardinian regional laws protecting linguistic minorities, and local associations promote its literature and music.
