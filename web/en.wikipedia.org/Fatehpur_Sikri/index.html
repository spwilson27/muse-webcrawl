Fatehpur Sikri is a town in the Agra district of Uttar Pradesh, India, about 37 kilometres west of Agra. It was founded in 1571 by the Mughal emperor Akbar and served as the capital of the Mughal Empire from 1571 to 1585, after which it was largely abandoned, traditionally said to be because of a shortage of water.

History

Akbar chose the village of Sikri as the site of his new capital after the Sufi saint Salim Chishti, who lived there, prophesied the birth of his heir, the future emperor Jahangir. The city, renamed Fatehpur Sikri ("City of Victory") after Akbar's conquest of Gujarat in 1573, was built largely of red sandstone on a rocky ridge. Akbar moved his court to Lahore in 1585, and the city was never again a capital, though its buildings remained remarkably well preserved.

Architecture

The complex includes the Jama Masjid, one of the largest mosques in India; the Buland Darwaza, a 54-metre gateway built to commemorate the Gujarat victory; the marble tomb of Salim Chishti; the Panch Mahal, a five-storeyed open pavilion; and palace buildings including Jodha Bai's Palace, the Diwan-i-Khas with its central pillar, and the Anup Talao tank. Fatehpur Sikri was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1986.
