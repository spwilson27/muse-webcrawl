Stockfish (chess)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Stockfish analysing a chess position

Stockfish is a free and open-source chess engine, commonly used by chess players to analyse games and positions. Since 2014 it has been among the strongest chess engines in the world, and it has won numerous computer chess championships, including multiple Top Chess Engine Championship (TCEC) titles. It is available for various desktop and mobile platforms.

Stockfish originated as a fork of the Glaurung engine by Tord Romstad, with Marco Costalba and Joona Kiiski leading early development. Released under the GNU General Public License, it is developed by a community of volunteers, using the Fishtest distributed testing framework to validate improvements.

Playing strength and technique

Stockfish uses alpha–beta search enhanced by techniques such as transposition tables, null-move pruning and late move reductions. In 2020 it incorporated an efficiently updatable neural network (NNUE) evaluation, originally developed for shogi engines, which substantially raised its playing strength while retaining fast classical search. Its Elo rating on computer rating lists has exceeded 3600, far above the strongest human players.

In 2017 Stockfish's open challenge match against AlphaZero drew wide attention when DeepMind's self-taught program won the match, though the conditions and hardware were debated. Stockfish subsequently re-established itself as the leading conventional engine, and its analysis is the default in many chess databases, broadcasting tools and the analysis boards of major chess websites.

Versions

Major numbered releases, from Stockfish 1.0 (2008) onward, have appeared roughly annually in recent years, with development versions published continuously. The engine communicates via the Universal Chess Interface (UCI) protocol, making it compatible with most chess graphical interfaces.
