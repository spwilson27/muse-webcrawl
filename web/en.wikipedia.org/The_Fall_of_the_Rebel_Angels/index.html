The Fall of the Rebel Angels is an oil-on-panel painting of 1562 by the Flemish Renaissance artist Pieter Bruegel the Elder. It depicts the fall of Lucifer and the rebel angels, a subject drawn from the Book of Revelation.

Description

In the centre of the composition the Archangel Michael, in armour and a blue cloak, drives his sword downward amid a dense, tumbling mass of falling angels. As they plunge from heaven, the rebels transform into fantastical hybrid monsters — part insect, fish, reptile, and machine — rendered in meticulous detail influenced by the earlier work of Hieronymus Bosch. Bruegel painted the work in the same year as Dulle Griet and The Triumph of Death.

History

The painting was once attributed to Pieter Brueghel the Younger and even to Bosch before Bruegel's signature and the date 1562 were uncovered. It is now a highlight of the Royal Museums of Fine Arts of Belgium in Brussels.
