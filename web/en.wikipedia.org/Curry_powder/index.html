Curry powder

Curry powder is a spice mix originating from British colonial adaptations of Indian seasoning blends. It is a pre-mixed combination of ground spices, typically dominated by turmeric — which gives it its characteristic yellow colour — together with coriander seed, cumin, fenugreek, and red chilli, and often including black pepper, ginger, mustard seed, curry leaf, cinnamon, cardamom, and cloves.

History

British traders and officials returning from India in the 18th century sought to reproduce the flavours of Indian curries at home. Commercial curry powders appeared in Britain by the late 18th century — the first known advertisement ran in 1784 — and a recipe for "currey powder" appeared in Hannah Glasse's The Art of Cookery. Curry powder thus represents a standardized Western invention: Indian cooks traditionally blend spices for each dish (as in garam masala and regional masalas), roasting and grinding them fresh rather than using a universal premix.

Varieties and uses

Commercial blends are commonly sold in mild, medium, and hot strengths, and regional styles — such as Madras curry powder — have their own profiles. Curry powder underlies British dishes such as coronation chicken and curried soups, travelled with the British Empire to the Caribbean (where local blends flavour goat curry and rotis), and to Japan, where it was adapted into the sweet, thick Japanese curry roux blocks. It is also a key ingredient in South African Cape Malay curries and in currywurst sauce in Germany.
