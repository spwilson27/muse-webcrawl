Portal venous system

A portal venous system is a system of blood vessels in which blood from one capillary bed passes through a vein (a portal vein) to a second capillary bed, without first returning to the heart. The name derives from the Latin porta, "gate", in reference to the porta hepatis, the gateway of the liver.

Hepatic portal system

The most important example in humans is the hepatic portal system. The hepatic portal vein collects nutrient-rich but oxygen-poor blood from capillaries of the stomach, intestines, spleen, and pancreas, and delivers it to the sinusoids of the liver. This arrangement allows the liver to process absorbed nutrients, store glucose as glycogen, and detoxify harmful substances before the blood enters the general circulation via the hepatic veins and the inferior vena cava.

Other portal systems

The hypophyseal portal system connects the hypothalamus with the anterior pituitary gland, carrying releasing and inhibiting hormones from the hypothalamus directly to pituitary cells at high concentration. In many non-mammalian vertebrates, a renal portal system carries blood from the tail and hind limbs to the kidneys; birds retain a functional renal portal system, whereas in mammals it is absent.

Clinical significance

Obstruction of flow through the liver, as in cirrhosis, raises pressure in the hepatic portal system — portal hypertension — causing splenomegaly, ascites, and the development of portosystemic collateral vessels such as oesophageal varices, which may rupture and bleed dangerously.
