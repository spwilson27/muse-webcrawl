Chigi family


Arms of the House of Chigi

The House of Chigi is an Italian princely family of Sienese origin, descended from the counts of Ardenghesca, whose members attained great power in Renaissance and Baroque Rome. The family's most famous members were the banker Agostino Chigi (1466–1520), a great patron of the arts, and Fabio Chigi, who reigned as Pope Alexander VII from 1655 to 1667.

History

The Chigi had been prominent in Siena since the Middle Ages, serving as castellans of the counts of Ardenghesca, but their ascent to European significance began with Agostino "il Magnifico", who built an immense fortune through the alum mines of Tolfa and banking operations across the continent. Agostino commissioned Raphael to decorate his Villa Farnesina on the Tiber and was among the greatest art patrons of the Italian Renaissance.

In 1655 Cardinal Fabio Chigi was elected pope as Alexander VII. He initially resisted nepotism but eventually elevated his nephew Flavio Chigi to the cardinalate and settled the family in Rome, acquiring the Palazzo Chigi in Piazza Colonna — today the official residence of the Italian Prime Minister — and extensive estates. The family received the principality of Farnese and intermarried with other great Roman houses such as the Borghese and the Barberini.

Coat of arms and motto

The Chigi arms quarter the six mountains and star granted by Pope Julius II to Agostino with the oak tree of the Della Rovere. A legend recounts that the family's motto, and the origin of the six hills topped by a star, derives from an episode in the life of an early member. The family's emblematic oak and mountains appear throughout Rome, notably in the Chigi Chapel in Santa Maria del Popolo, designed by Raphael and completed by Bernini, which contains the tombs of Agostino and his brother Sigismondo.

Notable members

  • Agostino Chigi (1466–1520), banker and patron of Raphael
  • Pope Alexander VII (Fabio Chigi, 1599–1667)
  • Flavio Chigi (1631–1693), cardinal
  • Sigismondo Chigi Albani della Rovere (1894–1982), prince and editor
