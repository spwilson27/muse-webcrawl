Stall (fluid dynamics)

Stall
Field: Aerodynamics
Cause: Flow separation at high angle of attack
Effect: Sharp loss of lift, increased drag

In fluid dynamics, a stall is a reduction in the lift coefficient generated by a foil (such as an aircraft wing) as its angle of attack increases beyond a critical value, caused by separation of the airflow from the upper surface.

Critical angle

For most wings the critical angle of attack is roughly 15 degrees, though it varies with airfoil shape, speed and surface condition. Beyond it, lift falls, drag rises sharply, and the aircraft may drop a wing if flow separates asymmetrically.

Recovery and devices

Recovery is achieved by reducing the angle of attack, typically by lowering the nose. Design features such as slats, vortex generators and stall strips delay or tame stall behaviour; pilots are trained extensively in stall recognition and recovery.
