Lake Nicaragua

Location: Nicaragua
Type: tectonic freshwater lake
Surface area: 8,264 km²
Max. depth: 26 m
Surface elevation: 32 m

Lake Nicaragua or Lago de Nicaragua (Spanish: Lago de Nicaragua), also called Lake Cocibolca, is a freshwater lake in Nicaragua. Of tectonic origin and with an area of 8,264 km², it is the largest lake in Central America.

The lake, some 160 km long and up to 65 km wide, drains eastward to the Caribbean Sea via the San Juan River. Its primary port city is Granada, on the northwestern shore. It lies only about 18 km from the Pacific Ocean, separated by the narrow Isthmus of Rivas — a geography that made it a key trans-isthmian transport route in the nineteenth century and the planned route of the never-completed Nicaragua Canal.

Islands and wildlife

The lake contains more than 365 small islands, including the Solentiname Archipelago, the islets of Granada, and Ometepe, formed by two volcanoes, Concepción and Maderas, joined by an isthmus. Unusually for a freshwater lake, it historically supported sawfish, tarpon and bull sharks that ascended the San Juan River, though populations have sharply declined.
