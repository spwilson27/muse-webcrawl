Clay mineral

Clay minerals
ClassPhyllosilicates
Main groupsKaolinite, illite, smectite, chlorite, vermiculite
Grain size< 2 μm
Key propertiesPlasticity; cation exchange; swelling (smectites)

Clay minerals are hydrous aluminium phyllosilicates, sometimes containing variable amounts of iron, magnesium, alkali metals and other cations. They are the principal constituents of clay and form by the chemical weathering of silicate rocks. Their crystals are extremely small — typically less than two micrometres — and consist of stacked sheet-like layers, a structure that gives clays their plasticity, high surface area and ability to exchange ions and absorb water.

Structure

Clay mineral layers are built from two kinds of sheets: tetrahedral sheets of silicon-centred tetrahedra and octahedral sheets of aluminium- or magnesium-centred octahedra. In 1:1 minerals such as kaolinite, one tetrahedral sheet is bonded to one octahedral sheet. In 2:1 minerals such as illite, smectite and vermiculite, an octahedral sheet is sandwiched between two tetrahedral sheets. Isomorphous substitution within the sheets creates a net negative charge, balanced by interlayer cations; in smectites, water enters the interlayer and the mineral swells.

Main groups

Kaolinite is the principal mineral of china clay, valued for ceramics and paper coating. Illite is the most common clay mineral in shales and mudrocks. The smectite group, including montmorillonite, forms bentonite and is prized for its swelling and sealing properties, used in drilling muds, foundry sands and cat litter. Chlorite and vermiculite are common in soils and metamorphic terrains.

Formation and significance

Clay minerals form at the Earth's surface by hydrolysis of feldspars and other silicates, and are redistributed by rivers into soils, floodplains and ocean sediments, where they constitute most mudrocks. In soils they govern fertility through cation exchange. Industrially they are indispensable raw materials; geologically, their transformations during burial record temperature and time, and swelling clays are a major factor in landslides and foundation heave.
