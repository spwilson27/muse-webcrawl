Wadi Araba

Wadi Araba (Arabic: وادي عربة, Wādī ʿAraba; Hebrew: עֲרָבָה, Arava) is a long, arid valley forming part of the Great Rift Valley system between the Dead Sea in the north and the Gulf of Aqaba in the south. Since 1994 it has marked the border between Israel to the west and Jordan to the east.

Geography

The valley runs roughly 166 km from the southern shore of the Dead Sea — the lowest land on Earth — southward to Aqaba and Eilat at the head of the Red Sea's northeastern gulf. It is flanked by the Negev highlands on the west and the mountains of Edom on the east, and receives very little rainfall. Archaeologically it is notable for ancient copper mining at sites such as Timna and Faynan, associated with biblical traditions about King Solomon's mines.

Modern history

The Israel–Jordan peace treaty of 1994 is commonly called the Wadi Araba Treaty after the valley, where the signing ceremony was held on 26 October 1994. Today the valley hosts border crossings, agriculture in scattered settlements, and proposed joint projects in water, energy, and transport.
