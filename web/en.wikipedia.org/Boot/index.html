Boot

Type: Footwear
Materials: Leather, rubber, synthetics
Covers: Foot and ankle (often leg)
Fastenings: Laces, zips, buckles, pull-on

A boot is a type of footwear that typically covers the foot and the ankle, and sometimes part of the lower leg. Boots are worn both for their functionality — protecting the foot and leg from water, snow, mud or hazards in work and sport — and for reasons of style and fashion.

History

Early boots consisted of three separate parts: uppers, soles and leggings. Around 1000 BC these components were joined to form a single covering for the foot and lower leg. Boots have been worn by soldiers, riders and labourers throughout history, and evolved into specialised forms such as the riding boot, the hiking boot and the cowboy boot.

Types

Common types include work boots, safety boots with steel toe caps, Wellington boots, hiking boots, snow boots, ski boots, riding boots, Chelsea boots and fashion boots such as go-go boots. Military combat boots are standard issue for armed forces worldwide.

Construction

Traditional boots are made of leather with stitched or Goodyear-welted soles; modern boots frequently use rubber, neoprene and synthetic membranes such as waterproof-breathable linings.
