Moroni (Book of Mormon prophet)

[Image: The angel Moroni as depicted in Latter-day Saint art]

In the Book of Mormon, Moroni is the last Nephite prophet, historian and military commander, son of the prophet Mormon. According to the narrative, Moroni lived in the Americas in the late fourth and early fifth centuries AD. After the destruction of the Nephite nation at the Battle of Cumorah, in which his father died, Moroni wandered alone for years, completing his father's record on the golden plates, abridging the Jaredite record (the Book of Ether), adding his own book, and finally burying the plates in a hill.

Role in the Book of Mormon

Moroni's contributions include the Book of Moroni, which describes church ordinances, the sacrament prayers, and concludes with the exhortation known as "Moroni's promise" - a challenge to readers to ask God whether the Book of Mormon is true. Earlier in the record, as a military leader he is credited (in the Book of Alma, which covers an earlier period, by a different Moroni - see below) with defensive innovations; the Moroni of the title page, however, is Mormon's son.

The angel Moroni

In the theology of the Latter Day Saint movement, Moroni returned as a resurrected being, the angel Moroni, who appeared to Joseph Smith beginning in September 1823 and directed him to the golden plates, which Smith translated and published as the Book of Mormon in 1830. A gilded statue of Moroni, trumpet in hand, crowns many temples of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, although newer temple designs increasingly omit the figure.
