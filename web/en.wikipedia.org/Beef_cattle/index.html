Beef cattle

Beef cattle are cattle raised for meat production, as distinct from dairy cattle, which are raised for milk. The meat of adult or nearly adult cattle is called beef. Beef production follows a cycle in which breeding cows produce calves that are raised on pasture, then commonly "finished" on grain in feedlots before slaughter. The world's leading beef producers include the United States, Brazil, the European Union, China, and India, and beef is one of the most widely consumed meats globally, though its production is also one of the most land- and emissions-intensive forms of agriculture.

Production cycle

Cow–calf operations maintain breeding herds on pasture and rangeland; calves are weaned at roughly six to ten months. Stocker or backgrounding operations grow weaned calves on forage, after which many animals enter feedlots, where they are fed energy-dense grain rations for rapid weight gain and marbling. Grass-fed and grass-finished systems keep animals on forage throughout. Cattle are typically slaughtered at 18–30 months depending on the system, and carcasses are graded for yield and quality (for example, USDA Prime, Choice, and Select).

Breeds

British breeds such as Angus and Hereford are prized for marbling and maternal traits; continental European breeds such as Charolais, Limousin, and Simmental for growth and muscling. Zebu (Bos indicus) breeds such as Brahman and Nelore predominate in tropical regions for heat and parasite tolerance. Japanese Wagyu breeds produce intensely marbled beef commanding premium prices. Crossbreeding to combine traits — "hybrid vigour" — is standard practice.

Environmental impact

Beef production accounts for a disproportionate share of food-system greenhouse-gas emissions, largely through enteric methane, manure, and land-use change including deforestation for pasture and feed crops; it also uses far more land per unit of protein than pork or poultry. Mitigation strategies under study include feed additives that reduce methane (such as the red seaweed Asparagopsis and 3-NOP), improved pasture management, and breeding for feed efficiency.
