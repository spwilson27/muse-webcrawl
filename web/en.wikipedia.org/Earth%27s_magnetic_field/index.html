Earth's magnetic field, also known as the geomagnetic field, is the magnetic field that extends from Earth's interior out into space, where it interacts with the solar wind, a stream of charged particles emanating from the Sun. The field ranges in magnitude from about 25 to 65 microteslas at the surface.

Origin

The field is generated by the geodynamo: convection of molten iron and nickel in the liquid outer core, combined with Earth's rotation, produces electric currents that sustain the magnetic field.

Importance

The magnetosphere deflects most solar wind particles, shielding the surface from charged-particle radiation and preserving the atmosphere. Many animals use the field for navigation, and humans have used it with compasses for centuries.

Change over time

The field varies on all timescales; the magnetic poles wander, the dipole strength fluctuates, and the polarity has reversed hundreds of times in Earth's history, recorded in the magnetization of rocks.
