Dale Patrick Chihuly (born September 20, 1941) is an American glass artist and entrepreneur, widely credited with reviving and transforming studio glassblowing into large-scale sculpture and environmental installation art. His vividly coloured, organic forms — chandeliers, towers, "Persians," "Macchia," and garden installations — are exhibited worldwide.

Early life and education

Born in Tacoma, Washington, Chihuly studied interior design at the University of Washington and then glass at the University of Wisconsin–Madison under Harvey Littleton, founder of the American studio glass movement. On a Fulbright Fellowship he worked at the Venini factory in Murano, Italy, in 1968, absorbing Venetian team-based glassblowing. He later taught at the Rhode Island School of Design, where he established its glass programme.

Pilchuck and working method

In 1971 Chihuly co-founded the Pilchuck Glass School in Stanwood, Washington, now a leading international centre for glass art. After losing the sight in his left eye in a 1976 car accident and dislocating his shoulder bodysurfing in 1979, he ceased blowing glass himself, instead directing teams of gaffers — a shift he has compared to a film director or choreographer — which enabled the scale and complexity of his mature work. His practice has also drawn occasional controversy over authorship and workshop production.

Major works and exhibitions

Signature projects include the Chihuly Over Venice installations (1995–96), the ceiling of the Bellagio hotel in Las Vegas (Fiori di Como, 1998), exhibitions at the Tower of David in Jerusalem (2000), Kew Gardens (2005), and the long-running Chihuly Garden and Glass exhibition at Seattle Center, opened in 2012. His work is held in more than 200 museum collections.
