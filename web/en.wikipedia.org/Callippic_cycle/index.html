Callippic cycle

The Callippic cycle (or Calippic) is a particular approximate common multiple of the tropical year and the synodic month, proposed by the Greek astronomer Callippus of Cyzicus around 330 BC. It was designed to refine the earlier Metonic cycle used in the Athenian lunisolar calendar.

Definition

The Callippic cycle spans 76 years, comprising 940 lunar months and 27,759 days. Callippus took four Metonic cycles (4 × 19 = 76 years; 4 × 6,940 = 27,760 days) and dropped one day, giving a much better fit: 940 synodic months average about 29.530851 days each in this scheme, and the mean year becomes 365¼ days, matching the Julian year. After one Callippic cycle the phases of the Moon recur on the same dates and at nearly the same times of day.

Use

The cycle was used in Greek astronomy for dating observations; Ptolemy's Almagest refers many observations to years counted in Callippic periods, with the first period conventionally beginning at the summer solstice of 330 BC. Callippic cycles also appear in the workings of the Antikythera mechanism, the ancient Greek astronomical calculator, which has a dedicated dial for the 76-year cycle. Later, the astronomer Hipparchus proposed a still more accurate cycle of 304 years built from four Callippic cycles minus one day.
