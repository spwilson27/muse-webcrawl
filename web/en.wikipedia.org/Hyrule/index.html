Hyrule

Hyrule is a fictional kingdom that serves as the primary setting of Nintendo's The Legend of Zelda video game series. Created by Shigeru Miyamoto and introduced in the original The Legend of Zelda (1986), Hyrule is a medieval-inspired fantasy land explored by the protagonist Link as he opposes the villain Ganon and aids Princess Zelda.

Geography and lore

Although its geography changes between games, recurring features include Death Mountain, the Lost Woods, Lake Hylia, the Gerudo Desert, Hyrule Castle and Kakariko Village. In the series' mythology, the world was created by three goddesses — Din, Nayru and Farore — who left behind the sacred Triforce, a relic that grants the wishes of whoever possesses it, around which much of the series' conflict revolves.

Appearances

Hyrule appears in most mainline Zelda games, including A Link to the Past, Ocarina of Time, Twilight Princess, Skyward Sword, Breath of the Wild and Tears of the Kingdom. Breath of the Wild's open-world recreation of the kingdom was widely praised for its scale and interactivity, and the kingdom was also featured in spin-offs such as Hyrule Warriors and in The Super Mario Bros. Movie-era adaptations of Nintendo properties.
