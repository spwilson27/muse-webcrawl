Historical Jesus

The historical Jesus is studied as a first-century Jewish preacher from Galilee in Roman Judea.

The historical Jesus is the reconstruction of the life and teachings of Jesus of Nazareth by critical historical methods, in contrast to the Christ of faith presented in Christian theology. Scholars generally agree that Jesus existed, was baptized by John the Baptist, and was crucified by order of the Roman prefect Pontius Pilate.

The quest for the historical Jesus began in the Enlightenment and has passed through several phases, from the early liberal lives of Jesus, through the skepticism of the early twentieth century, to the renewed "third quest" that situates Jesus firmly within first-century Judaism.

Sources

The principal sources for the historical Jesus are the four canonical gospels, especially the synoptic gospels of Mark, Matthew, and Luke, supplemented by the letters of Paul, the earliest Christian writings. Non-Christian sources include the Jewish historian Josephus, whose Antiquities of the Jews mentions Jesus twice, and the Roman historian Tacitus, who records that Christus was executed under Pontius Pilate during the reign of Tiberius.

Criteria of authenticity

Historians have used criteria such as multiple attestation (material found in several independent sources), embarrassment (details early Christians would have been unlikely to invent, such as the baptism by John and the crucifixion), and coherence with the cultural context of Roman Judea to assess which traditions about Jesus are most likely historical.

Scholarly consensus

Nearly all scholars of antiquity hold that Jesus was a historical figure. Two events are considered virtually certain: his baptism by John the Baptist and his crucifixion. Jesus is generally portrayed as an apocalyptic preacher, a teacher of wisdom, a charismatic healer, or a social reformer — often some combination of these roles.
