Quantum decoherence

Quantum decoherence
FieldQuantum mechanics, quantum information
Key figuresH. Dieter Zeh, Wojciech Zurek
RelatedMeasurement problem, pointer states, einselection

Quantum decoherence is the loss of quantum coherence: the process by which a quantum system, through interaction with its environment, loses the phase relations between components of its state, so that its observable behaviour becomes effectively classical. In modern terms, coherence is "leaked" into correlations with environmental degrees of freedom that are not observed.

Overview

An isolated quantum system evolves unitarily and can exhibit interference between superposition components. When the system couples to an environment — gas molecules, photons, phonons — the joint state becomes entangled, and the reduced density matrix of the system alone rapidly loses its off-diagonal (coherence) terms in a particular basis, the pointer basis selected by the interaction. The resulting state is an improper mixture that behaves, for all practical purposes, like a classical probability distribution over pointer states.

History

The mechanism was first articulated by H. Dieter Zeh in 1970 and developed extensively by Wojciech Zurek in the 1980s, who introduced the notion of environment-induced superselection ("einselection"). Decoherence timescales were analysed in detail by Joos and Zeh (1985), showing that for macroscopic objects they are extraordinarily short — a dust grain in interstellar space decoheres in microseconds or less.

Decoherence and measurement

Decoherence explains why macroscopic superpositions are never observed and why measurement outcomes appear definite and classical. It does not, by itself, select a single outcome; whether it fully resolves the measurement problem depends on the interpretation of quantum mechanics adopted.

Applications and obstacles

Decoherence is the principal obstacle to building quantum computers, since it destroys the superpositions and entanglement on which quantum computation depends; quantum error correction and decoherence-free subspaces are strategies for mitigating it. Conversely, controlled decoherence is a tool in quantum optics and foundations experiments, which have observed the gradual disappearance of interference as environmental coupling is increased.
