The lesser capybara (Hydrochoerus isthmius) is a large semi-aquatic rodent of the family Caviidae, native to Central and South America. It is one of two living species of capybara, the other being the larger capybara (Hydrochoerus hydrochaeris). Long treated as a subspecies of its better-known relative, it was recognised as a full species on the basis of its smaller size, distinct skull anatomy, and separate distribution.

Description

The lesser capybara resembles a scaled-down capybara: a barrel-shaped body covered in coarse brownish fur, a blunt muzzle, partially webbed toes, and eyes, ears, and nostrils set high on the head for swimming. Adults average about 28 kilograms, roughly half the mass of a large common capybara, with a head-and-body length near 100 centimetres.

Distribution and habitat

The species is found from eastern Panama through northern Colombia to northwestern Venezuela (around Lake Maracaibo). It lives along rivers, marshes, swamps, and flooded savannas, always close to water, into which it retreats from predators such as caimans and big cats.

Behaviour and conservation

Lesser capybaras are herbivorous grazers, feeding on grasses and aquatic vegetation, and are most active at dawn, dusk, and night. They live in family parties smaller than the herds of the common capybara. The IUCN lists the species as Data Deficient: it is hunted for meat in parts of its range and threatened by wetland drainage, but population data are sparse.
