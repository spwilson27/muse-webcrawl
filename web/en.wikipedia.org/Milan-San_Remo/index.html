Milan–San Remo

Milan–San Remo (Italian: Milano–Sanremo) is an annual one-day road cycling race between Milan and Sanremo in northwestern Italy. At roughly 298 km it is the longest professional one-day race in modern cycling, and it is traditionally the first of the five "Monuments" of the season, usually held in mid-to-late March. Because of its early-season date and coastal finish it is nicknamed La Primavera ("The Spring Classic").

Course

From Milan the route crosses the Po plain, climbs the Passo del Turchino to reach the Ligurian coast, and follows it westward. The decisive features come in the final 50 km: the three small Capi hills, then the Cipressa (introduced 1982) and the Poggio di Sanremo (introduced 1960), whose descent leads into the finishing straight on the Via Roma in Sanremo.

Character of the race

Despite the late climbs, Milan–San Remo is often called "the sprinters' classic", because strong sprinters who survive the Poggio frequently win; the Poggio's slopes also offer the classic launching pad for a late attack. Eddy Merckx holds the record with seven victories; Costante Girardengo won six editions in the interwar years. Famous editions include Gerald Ciolek's snow-shortened 2013 win and Vincenzo Nibali's successful Poggio attack in 2018.
