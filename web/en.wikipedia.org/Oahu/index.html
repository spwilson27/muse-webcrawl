Oahu (Hawaiian: Oʻahu), known as "The Gathering Place," is the third-largest of the Hawaiian Islands and home to roughly two-thirds to 70 percent of the population of the U.S. state of Hawaiʻi. The island is coextensive with the City and County of Honolulu, and its principal city, Honolulu, is the state capital and the anchor of the metropolitan area.

Geography

Oʻahu was formed by two separate shield volcanoes, Waiʻanae and Koʻolau, whose eroded ranges are joined by a broad central valley and plain. The island is 44 miles (71 km) long and 30 miles (48 km) across, with 227 miles of shoreline. Diamond Head (Lēʻahi), the tuff cone at the eastern end of Waikīkī, is its most famous landmark. Mount Kaʻala in the Waiʻanae Range is the highest point at 4,025 feet.

History

Polynesian settlers reached Oʻahu centuries before European contact; the island was the seat of powerful aliʻi (chiefs), and the Battle of Nuʻuanu in 1795, fought in the Nuʻuanu Valley above Honolulu, completed Kamehameha I's unification of the Hawaiian Islands. Honolulu's natural harbour made it the kingdom's capital and a major Pacific port for whaling, sandalwood and later sugar and pineapple trades. On 7 December 1941, Japanese forces attacked the U.S. Pacific Fleet at Pearl Harbor on the island's south shore, bringing the United States into the Second World War; the USS Arizona Memorial commemorates the attack.

Economy and tourism

Tourism centres on Waikīkī's hotels and beach, while the North Shore — with breaks such as Banzai Pipeline and Waimea Bay — is world-famous for big-wave surfing. Defence is the second pillar of the economy: Pearl Harbor Naval Shipyard, Joint Base Pearl Harbor–Hickam, Schofield Barracks and other installations make Oʻahu a central hub of U.S. military presence in the Pacific.

Climate

Oʻahu has a tropical semi-arid to tropical climate moderated by the northeast trade winds; the windward (eastern) side is wetter and greener, the leeward (western and southern) side drier and sunnier. Temperatures vary little, generally between 24 and 32 °C (75–89 °F) year-round.
