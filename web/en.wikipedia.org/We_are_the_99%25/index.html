We are the 99%

"We are the 99%" is a political slogan widely used and coined during the Occupy movement of 2011. It refers to the concentration of income and wealth among the top-earning 1% of the population, in contrast with the remaining 99%. The slogan grew out of a Tumblr blog of the same name where people posted photographs of themselves holding handwritten accounts of economic hardship.

Origins

The phrase built on earlier statistical work on income inequality, including analyses by economists such as Joseph Stiglitz, whose 2011 article "Of the 1%, by the 1%, for the 1%" highlighted the rising share of income captured by the richest Americans. Occupy Wall Street protesters adopted the slogan as the movement spread from New York's Zuccotti Park in September 2011 to cities worldwide.

Impact

The slogan helped push income inequality into mainstream political discourse and was named among the most influential phrases of 2011. Critics questioned the precision of the 1%/99% framing, but its role in framing public debate over inequality is widely recognised.
