Ferdinand Magellan (Portuguese: Fernão de Magalhães; Spanish: Fernando de Magallanes; c. 1480 – 27 April 1521) was a Portuguese explorer in the service of Spain. He is best known for organising and leading the Spanish expedition to the East Indies from 1519 to 1522, which accomplished the first circumnavigation of the Earth, completed by survivors led by Juan Sebastián Elcano after Magellan's death.

Early life

Magellan was born around 1480 in Sabrosa, northern Portugal, into a family of minor nobility. Orphaned young, he served as a page at the court of Queen Eleanor of Viseu and later sailed for the Portuguese crown in the fleets of Francisco de Almeida to India, taking part in battles including Cannanore and Diu. He later served in Morocco, where he was wounded and accused of misconduct; disillusioned with Portuguese service, he renounced his nationality and offered his services to King Charles I of Spain.

The circumnavigation expedition

Magellan proposed reaching the Spice Islands (the Moluccas) by a westward route through a strait he believed existed through South America. He departed Sanlúcar de Barrameda on 20 September 1519 with five ships and about 270 men. After wintering in Patagonia and suppressing a mutiny, in October–November 1520 his fleet traversed the strait now named the Strait of Magellan and entered the ocean he named the Pacific.

The Pacific crossing took nearly four months, during which the crew suffered severe scurvy and starvation. The fleet reached Guam in March 1521 and then the Philippines. At Cebu, Magellan formed an alliance with Rajah Humabon and became involved in local conflicts.

Death and aftermath

On 27 April 1521, Magellan was killed at the Battle of Mactan, fighting the forces of the chieftain Lapulapu on the island of Mactan. The remaining ships reached the Moluccas; only the Victoria, under Juan Sebastián Elcano, completed the return to Spain in September 1522 with 18 survivors, completing the first circumnavigation of the globe.

Legacy

Magellan's expedition proved the Earth could be circumnavigated by sea and gave Europe its first measurement of the planet's true size. His name is borne by the strait he discovered, the Magellanic Clouds, and numerous spacecraft and places.
