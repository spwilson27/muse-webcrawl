Carl Lewis
Born: 1 July 1961, Birmingham, Alabama, U.S.
Sport: Track and field
Events: 100 m, 200 m, long jump, 4×100 m relay
Olympic medals: 9 gold, 1 silver

Frederick Carlton "Carl" Lewis (born 1 July 1961) is an American former track and field athlete who won nine Olympic gold medals, one Olympic silver medal, and ten World Championships medals, including eight gold. His career spanned from 1979 to 1996, when he last won an Olympic title; he is one of only six Olympic athletes to win a gold medal in the same individual event in four consecutive Games.

Career highlights

At the 1984 Los Angeles Olympics, Lewis matched Jesse Owens's feat of winning four gold medals (100 m, 200 m, long jump, and 4×100 m relay). At the 1988 Seoul Olympics he won the 100 m after Ben Johnson's disqualification and defended his long jump title. He won further long jump golds at Barcelona 1992 and Atlanta 1996, the latter at age 35.

Long jump rivalry

At the 1991 World Championships in Tokyo, Lewis and Mike Powell contested one of the greatest long jump duels in history: Lewis jumped a wind-assisted 8.91 m, but Powell set the world record of 8.95 m, which still stands — breaking Bob Beamon's 23-year-old mark.

Legacy

Lewis was voted "World Athlete of the Century" by the International Association of Athletics Federations and "Olympian of the Century" by Sports Illustrated. After retiring, he pursued acting, music, and politics, and later became a track and field coach at the University of Houston.
