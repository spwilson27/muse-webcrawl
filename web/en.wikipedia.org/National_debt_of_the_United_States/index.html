National debt of the United States

The national debt of the United States is the total national debt owed by the federal government to Treasury security holders. It consists of debt held by the public and intragovernmental debt (owed to trust funds such as Social Security). Gross federal debt exceeded $34 trillion in early 2024 and passed $36 trillion by late 2024.

History

The federal government has run debt since the Revolutionary War era; Alexander Hamilton's funding programme of 1790 established federal credit. Debt spiked during the Civil War, the Great Depression, and the Second World War (over 100% of GDP in 1946), fell as a share of GDP until the 1980s, and rose again with tax cuts, wars, the 2008 financial crisis, and the COVID-19 pandemic.

Debt ceiling and risks

The statutory debt limit has repeatedly produced standoffs — in 2011 (leading to a credit-rating downgrade of U.S. debt by S&P), 2013, and 2023. Economists debate sustainability in terms of interest costs, which approached $1 trillion annually by 2024, and the real interest rate relative to growth.
