Insignia

An insignia (plural insignia or insignias) is a symbol or token of personal power, status, or office, or of an official body of government or jurisdiction. Insignia are especially used as emblems of specific or general authority: military rank insignia worn on uniforms, the badges and collar ornaments of orders of chivalry, heraldic devices, and the great seals and regalia of states all fall within the term.

Uses

Armed forces use insignia of rank — stripes, pips, stars, and shoulder boards — to identify the wearer's grade at a glance, as well as unit patches and qualification badges. Civil insignia include the badges of police and fire services, the emblems of courts and legislatures, and the insignia of orders, decorations, and medals, typically worn as ribbons, stars, and crosses whose designs are prescribed by statute. Religious insignia include episcopal regalia such as the mitre, crosier, and ring.

Legal protection

Many insignia are protected by law: unauthorised use of military decorations, police badges, or the emblems of the Red Cross and other protected signs is an offence in numerous jurisdictions. National symbols such as flags and coats of arms are likewise regulated, and heraldic authorities in some countries grant and record armorial insignia.

Etymology

The word comes from Latin insignia, plural of insigne ("badge, mark"), from in- and signum ("sign").
