Union catalog

A union catalog is a combined library catalog describing the collections of a group of libraries, indicating which library holds which items. Union catalogs enable resource sharing, interlibrary loan, and cooperative cataloguing among participating institutions.

History and examples

Historically maintained as card or printed-book catalogs — the United States National Union Catalog, begun by the Library of Congress in the early 20th century, eventually filled hundreds of printed volumes — union catalogs are now almost entirely electronic. The largest is WorldCat, maintained by OCLC, which aggregates holdings from tens of thousands of libraries worldwide. National and regional examples include the German K10plus, the French Sudoc, and COPAC/Jisc Library Hub Discovery in the United Kingdom.

Functions

Beyond locating copies for interlibrary lending, union catalogs reduce duplicate cataloguing effort through shared records, support collection analysis, and help libraries coordinate preservation of rarely held items.
