Yamanote Line

Yamanote Line
Operator: JR East
Type: Commuter loop line
Length: 34.5 km
Stations: 30
Opened: 1885 (loop completed 1925)

The Yamanote Line (Japanese: 山手線, Yamanote-sen) is a loop service in Tokyo, Japan, operated by the East Japan Railway Company (JR East). It is one of Tokyo's busiest and most important lines, connecting most of the city's major stations and urban centres.

Route

The Yamanote is a loop, not a line with termini: trains run continuously clockwise (sotomawari, "outer loop") and counter-clockwise (uchimawari, "inner loop"). Its 30 stations include Tokyo, Ueno, Ikebukuro, Shinjuku, Shibuya, Shinagawa, and Yurakucho. Shinjuku, on the loop, is the busiest railway station in the world.

The name "Yamanote" refers to the hilly, uptown districts of old Edo that the original route skirted, as opposed to the low-lying "Shitamachi" areas. The complete loop was finished in 1925; the final new station, Takanawa Gateway, opened in 2020.

Service

Trains run roughly every two to four minutes at peak times, completing the loop in about an hour. The line is famous for its distinctive green (uguisu, "bush warbler") colour, its station departure melodies, and onboard advertisements. Rolling stock is the E235 series, introduced from 2015, replacing the E231-500 series.

Daily ridership is in the millions, making the Yamanote a symbol of Tokyo commuting and a fixture of popular culture.
