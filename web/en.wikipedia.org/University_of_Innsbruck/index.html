The University of Innsbruck (German: Universität Innsbruck) is a public university in Innsbruck, the capital of the Austrian federal state of Tyrol. Founded in 1669 by Emperor Leopold I, it is the largest university in western Austria and a major research institution for the Alpine region, with notable strengths in physics, earth sciences and economics.

The main building of the University of Innsbruck on Innrain.

History

In 1562 the Jesuits established a gymnasium in Innsbruck, and in 1669 Emperor Leopold I founded the university with faculties of philosophy, law, theology and medicine, funded by a tax on salt from nearby Hall in Tirol. The university was dissolved and refounded several times in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries before becoming a full university again under Emperor Franz Joseph I in the 1860s. Its main building on the Innrain was completed in 1924. After the Anschluss of 1938, many professors were dismissed, and the university was renamed; it regained autonomy within the Austrian university reforms of the 1970s and 2004.

Organisation

The university is organised into 16 faculties, including Catholic Theology, Law, Business, Educational Sciences, Humanities, Psychology and Sport Science, Mathematics, Computer Science and Physics, Chemistry and Pharmacy, Geo- and Atmospheric Sciences, Architecture and Civil Engineering, and Biology. The Medical University of Innsbruck became a separate institution in 2004. Research focuses include Alpine space, digital sciences, molecular biosciences, and physics — the university is internationally known for experimental quantum physics, associated with researchers such as Anton Zeilinger, who worked in Innsbruck, and the Nobel laureate Anton Zeilinger's long-term collaborator networks, as well as the experimental physics groups of Rainer Blatt and Peter Zoller.

Campus

Facilities are spread across several sites in Innsbruck, including the Innrain campus, the technology campus in the west of the city, and the University Centre Obergurgl in the Ötztal Alps, used for conferences and Alpine research. The university botanical garden and the observatory reflect its long tradition of Alpine and atmospheric research.
