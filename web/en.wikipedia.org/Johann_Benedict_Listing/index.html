Johann Benedict Listing (25 July 1808 – 24 December 1882) was a German mathematician and physicist, a student of Carl Friedrich Gauss, noted as one of the founders of topology and for the first published description of the Möbius strip.

Life

Born in Frankfurt to a poor family of Czech origin, Listing showed early talent and won a scholarship to the University of Göttingen, where he studied under Gauss and received his doctorate in 1834 with a dissertation on surfaces of constant curvature. On Gauss's recommendation he travelled in Italy, studying optics and seismology, before returning to become professor of physics at Göttingen in 1839, succeeding in the chair associated with Wilhelm Weber.

Topology

Gauss suggested to Listing the study of the properties of knots and surfaces independent of measurement. In his Vorstudien zur Topologie (1847) — in which he coined the word "Topologie" — Listing laid early foundations of the subject, classifying spatial configurations and introducing ideas later developed by Riemann, Möbius and others. In 1858, independently and slightly before August Ferdinand Möbius, Listing discovered the one-sided surface now called the Möbius strip, describing it in a paper of 1861.

Other work

Listing also worked in physiological optics, studying the human eye; "Listing's law" describes the orientation of the eye in its socket, and "Listing's reduced eye" is an optical model. His Beitrag zur physiologischen Optik (1845) was an important contribution. He contributed to geodesy and to the study of knots, including the figure-eight knot sometimes called Listing's knot.
