Terrine

Terrine may refer to several related things, most prominently the deep, rectangular earthenware or cast-iron cooking vessel with a tight-fitting lid from which a class of French dishes takes its name.

The vessel

The terrine dish is traditionally made of glazed earthenware, enamelled cast iron or porcelain. In classic French cuisine a pâté or forcemeat mixture is packed into the dish, weighted, and cooked in a bain-marie, then chilled under pressure so that it slices cleanly.

The food

By extension, terrine (food) denotes the dish itself: a loaf of layered or ground meat, fish, vegetables or fruit cooked or set in the mould, unmoulded, and served cold in slices, similar to a pâté but typically coarser in texture.

Other uses

A terrine is also a synonym for a tureen, a large serving dish for soups and stews, particularly in older English usage.
