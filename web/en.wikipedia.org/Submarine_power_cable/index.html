Submarine power cable

A submarine power cable is a major transmission cable for carrying electric power beneath the surface of the water. Such cables connect offshore wind farms to shore, link islands to mainland grids, and interconnect the power systems of different countries across seas.

Submarine power cable
Type: Electric power transmission
Applications: Interconnectors, offshore wind
Technologies: AC and HVDC

Construction

Submarine cables have a conductor of copper or aluminium, layers of insulation (such as cross-linked polyethylene or mass-impregnated paper), and steel-wire armouring for protection against anchors, fishing gear, and abrasion. High-voltage direct current (HVDC) cables are preferred for long distances because they avoid the reactive-power losses of AC.

Installation

Specialized cable-laying ships unreel the cable onto the seabed, where it may be buried by ploughs or water jets for protection. Repair of a damaged cable requires grappling it to the surface and splicing in a new section.

Notable links

Examples include the North Sea Link between Norway and the United Kingdom, among the world's longest subsea interconnectors, and numerous links across the Baltic Sea and the English Channel.
