Hugo Laurenz August Hofmann von Hofmannsthal (1 February 1874 – 15 July 1929) was an Austrian novelist, librettist, poet, dramatist, narrator, and essayist, and one of the leading figures of Viennese modernism.

Early genius

Hofmannsthal published exquisite lyric poems and verse dramas as a schoolboy under the pseudonym "Loris," astonishing the Viennese literary world. By his mid-twenties he had become central to the Jung Wien circle around Arthur Schnitzler and Stefan George. His fictional "Chandos Letter" (1902), describing a crisis of language in which words dissolve beneath experience, became a canonical statement of modernist skepticism about language.

Drama and libretti

He wrote the play Elektra (1903), which Richard Strauss set as an opera, beginning one of the great composer–librettist collaborations: Der Rosenkavalier (1911), Ariadne auf Naxos (1912/1916), Die Frau ohne Schatten (1919), Die ägyptische Helena (1928), and the posthumous Arabella. His morality play Jedermann (Everyman), staged annually at the Salzburg Festival he co-founded with Max Reinhardt and Strauss in 1920, remains a fixture of German-language theatre.

Later years

After the collapse of the Habsburg monarchy Hofmannsthal championed a "conservative revolution" of European cultural values. He died of a stroke in 1929, two days after the suicide of his son Franz.
