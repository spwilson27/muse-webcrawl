Biblical literalism

Biblical literalism or biblicism is a term used by some religious Christians to interpret the Bible. It holds that, except where a passage is clearly intended by the context as allegory, poetry, or metaphor, the Bible should be interpreted literally. Biblical literalism is often associated with fundamentalist and evangelical Christianity.

Background

Literal interpretation was a common approach in both Jewish and Christian tradition, though pre-modern interpreters frequently combined literal readings with allegorical and moral senses. The modern emphasis on literalism developed in reaction to Enlightenment skepticism and the rise of historical-critical methods in the 18th and 19th centuries.

Characteristics

Literalist readings typically affirm doctrines such as the verbal inspiration of Scripture, a historical Adam and Eve, and a literal interpretation of the creation accounts in Genesis. Many literalists reject the theory of evolution and support creationism or intelligent design. Others accept figurative readings of some passages while maintaining a generally literal approach.

Criticism

Critics argue that a strictly literal reading ignores the literary genres present in the Bible, such as poetry, parable, apocalyptic literature and wisdom sayings, and that it can produce internal inconsistencies. Supporters reply that literalism means reading each passage according to its intended sense rather than applying wooden literalism to every figure of speech.

Influence

Biblical literalism has shaped education debates, museum exhibits such as creation museums, and public policy discussions in the United States and elsewhere. Surveys regularly find that a substantial minority of Americans say the Bible should be taken literally, word for word.
