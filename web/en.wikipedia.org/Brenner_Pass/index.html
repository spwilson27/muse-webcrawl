Brenner Pass

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The Brenner motorway and Europabrücke

The Brenner Pass (German: Brennerpass; Italian: Passo del Brennero) is a mountain pass through the Alps which forms the border between Italy and Austria. At 1,370 metres (4,495 ft) above sea level, it is the lowest of the major Alpine passes and the only one usable year-round without a tunnel at the summit. It separates the Ötztal Alps from the Zillertal Alps and has been one of the most important trade and transit routes between northern and southern Europe since prehistoric times.

History

The Romans improved the route, and it became part of the Via Claudia Augusta network of roads; medieval merchants and armies used it to travel between the Holy Roman Empire and Italy. The pass was historically part of the County of Tyrol. After the First World War, the Treaty of Saint-Germain moved Italy's border north to the Brenner watershed, dividing Tyrol and placing German-speaking South Tyrol within Italy. In March 1938, Hitler and Mussolini's alliance focused attention on the pass, and it was at the Brenner that the two dictators met in 1940.

Transport

The Brenner railway, opened in 1867, crosses the pass and was for over a century the only rail link; the Brenner Base Tunnel is now under construction beneath it to relieve congestion. The A13 (Austria) and A22 (Italy) motorways cross via the Europabrücke, one of Europe's tallest bridges, and the corridor carries one of the heaviest volumes of trans-Alpine freight traffic, a persistent source of environmental and political debate in the region.
