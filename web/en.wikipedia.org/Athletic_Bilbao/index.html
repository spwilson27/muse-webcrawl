Athletic Club, commonly known internationally as Athletic Bilbao, is a professional football club based in Bilbao, in the Basque Country of Spain. Founded in 1898, it is one of only three clubs — along with Real Madrid and FC Barcelona — never to have been relegated from La Liga, the top division of Spanish football, since its creation in 1929.

The cantera policy

Athletic is famous for its cantera policy of signing only players who were born in, or trained in the youth academies of, the greater Basque Country (including Navarre and the French Basque Country). This policy, in place in various forms since the early 20th century, is a source of immense local pride and is summed up by the club motto: Con cantera y afición, no hace falta importación ("With local players and support, there's no need for imports"). Despite this restriction, the club has remained consistently competitive at the highest level.

History and honours

The club was founded by Basque students who had studied in England and by British workers in Bilbao's shipyards and mines, which accounts for the English spelling "Athletic" rather than the Spanish "Atlético". Athletic has won La Liga eight times, most recently in 1983–84, and the Copa del Rey 24 times, second only to Barcelona in that competition; the club ended a 40-year major-trophy drought by winning the 2024 Copa del Rey final against Mallorca on penalties. Its red-and-white striped shirts are said to derive from shirts brought from Southampton (some accounts say Sunderland) in the early 1900s.

Stadium and identity

Athletic plays at San Mamés, known as La Catedral ("The Cathedral") for the devotion of its supporters and its proximity to a church of Saint Mammes — the early Christian saint thrown to the lions, from whom the team's nickname, Los Leones (The Lions), derives. The current stadium opened in 2013 on the site of the original 1913 ground. The club's fiercest rivalry is the Basque derby against Real Sociedad of San Sebastián.
