Environmental management system

An environmental management system (EMS) is a set of processes and practices that enable an organization to reduce its environmental impacts and increase its operating efficiency. An EMS helps an organization address its regulatory demands in a systematic and cost-effective manner, and can assist in reducing the risk of non-compliance and improving health and safety practices for employees and the public.

The Plan-Do-Check-Act cycle at the heart of most environmental management systems

Framework

Most environmental management systems are built around the Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA) cycle of continual improvement. An organization first develops an environmental policy and identifies its significant environmental aspects (Plan), implements operational controls and training (Do), monitors performance and audits the system (Check), and then reviews results at management level and makes improvements (Act).

Standards

The most widely used framework is ISO 14001, part of the ISO 14000 family of standards published by the International Organization for Standardization. Organizations can seek third-party certification of their EMS against ISO 14001. In the European Union, the Eco-Management and Audit Scheme (EMAS) provides an alternative, more demanding framework that includes public reporting requirements.

Benefits and criticism

Proponents argue that an EMS reduces waste, energy use, and legal risk while improving reputation. Critics note that certification demonstrates the existence of a management system rather than a particular level of environmental performance, so a certified organization may still be a significant polluter.
