Women's Social and Political Union

Founded10 October 1903, Manchester
FounderEmmeline Pankhurst
Motto"Deeds, not words"
Dissolved1917

The Women's Social and Political Union (WSPU) was a women-only political movement and leading militant organisation campaigning for women's suffrage in the United Kingdom. Founded in Manchester in 1903 by Emmeline Pankhurst and her daughters Christabel and Sylvia, its members were known as suffragettes.

Militancy

Frustrated by the slow pace of parliamentary reform, the WSPU adopted militant tactics from 1905: heckling ministers, mass demonstrations, window-smashing, hunger strikes in prison, and an arson campaign against property. Imprisoned suffragettes who hunger-struck were force-fed, and the government's 1913 "Cat and Mouse Act" allowed their temporary release and re-arrest.

Outcome

The WSPU suspended militancy at the outbreak of the First World War and supported the war effort. In 1918 the Representation of the People Act enfranchised women over 30 who met a property qualification; equal voting rights followed in 1928.
