Atari, Inc.

Atari, Inc. was an American video game developer and home computer company founded in Sunnyvale, California, on June 27, 1972, by Nolan Bushnell and Ted Dabney. Atari was a pioneer of the arcade game industry and is widely credited with founding the video game industry. Its first major hit, the arcade game Pong (1972), launched the commercial video game era. The Atari Video Computer System (later renamed the Atari 2600), released in 1977, popularised the home game console with interchangeable cartridges.

History

Bushnell, inspired by the mainframe game Spacewar!, co-developed Computer Space (1971) before founding Atari with Dabney; the company name comes from a term in the game of Go. Pong, engineered by Allan Alcorn, became the first commercially successful video game. In 1976 Bushnell sold Atari to Warner Communications for an estimated $28–32 million to fund the development of the VCS/2600. The console, together with games such as Space Invaders (1980) and Pac-Man (1982), drove explosive growth; Atari became the fastest-growing company in American history to that point.

After the video game crash of 1983, in which Atari posted enormous losses (and famously buried unsold stock in New Mexico), Warner sold Atari's consumer electronics and home computer divisions in July 1984 to Jack Tramiel, who formed Atari Corporation. The arcade division continued as Atari Games. The Atari brand passed through several owners and is today used by Atari SA of France.

Legacy

Atari established design and business practices the industry still follows, and its alumni founded companies such as Activision and Apple (Steve Jobs and Steve Wozniak both worked at Atari). The three-pronged "Fuji" logo remains one of the most recognisable in gaming.
