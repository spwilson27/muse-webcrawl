Thomas Rowe Price Jr. (March 16, 1898 – October 20, 1983) was an American investment manager who founded the Baltimore firm T. Rowe Price in 1937. Known as "the father of growth stock investing," he popularized the strategy of buying shares in well-managed companies whose earnings were expected to grow faster than the economy and inflation, and holding them for the long term.

Career

After training as a chemist and working at firms including Mackubin, Goodrich & Co., Price launched his own firm just before the Second World War, managing accounts with a growth philosophy he had developed after studying market history. He later pioneered the multi-fund family structure and, unusually, advocated a fee based on assets managed rather than sales commissions.

Legacy

Price retired in 1971 and sold his stake in the firm, which grew after his death into one of the world's largest asset managers. His disciplined, research-driven growth approach remains a reference point for fundamental equity investing.
