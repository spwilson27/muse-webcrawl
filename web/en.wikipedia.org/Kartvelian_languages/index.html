The Kartvelian languages (Georgian: ქართველური ენები, romanized: kartveluri enebi) are a language family indigenous to the South Caucasus, spoken primarily in Georgia and by significant minorities in northeastern Turkey and elsewhere. The family consists of four closely related languages: Georgian, Svan, Mingrelian, and Laz.

Classification

Georgian, with about four million speakers, is the official language of Georgia and the family's literary language. Svan, spoken in the northwestern Georgian region of Svaneti, is the most divergent branch and is thought to have separated from Proto-Kartvelian in the 2nd millennium BC or earlier. Mingrelian and Laz form a Zan subgroup: Mingrelian is spoken in western Georgia (Samegrelo), while Laz is spoken mainly along the Black Sea coast of Turkey and adjacent Georgia.

Features

The Kartvelian languages are known for their complex verb morphology, polypersonal agreement, split ergativity, and large consonant clusters. Georgian is written in its own distinctive script (Mkhedruli), while the other languages are mostly unwritten or written in the Georgian or Latin scripts. The family has no established relationship to any other known language family, though proposed connections with other Caucasian families remain unproven.
