Multiple sclerosis

Multiple sclerosis (MS) is an autoimmune disease in which the insulating myelin covers of nerve cells in the brain and spinal cord are damaged (demyelination), along with axonal loss. This disrupts communication within the nervous system, producing a wide range of symptoms.

SymptomsVision problems, numbness, weakness, spasticity, fatigue, cognitive change
OnsetUsually age 20-40; women affected ~2-3x more often
DiagnosisMRI, McDonald criteria, lumbar puncture

Course

Most patients begin with relapsing-remitting MS, with attacks followed by partial recovery; many later develop secondary progressive disease. A minority have primary progressive MS from onset.

Causes

Cause is unknown; risk factors include genetic susceptibility (HLA-DRB1), low vitamin D / latitude, obesity in adolescence, smoking, and Epstein-Barr virus infection, which a large 2022 study identified as a strong preceding factor.

Treatment

No cure exists. Acute relapses are treated with high-dose corticosteroids. Disease-modifying therapies (interferons, glatiramer, fingolimod, ocrelizumab, natalizumab and others) reduce relapses; methotrexate was used historically but is not a standard modern therapy.
