Orthotics

Orthotics is a medical specialty concerned with the design, manufacture, and application of orthoses — externally applied devices used to support, align, prevent, or correct deformities, or to improve the function of movable parts of the body. The term derives from the Greek ortho, "to straighten."

Types of orthoses

Common orthoses include shoe inserts (foot orthoses), ankle–foot orthoses (AFOs), knee braces, spinal braces such as the Boston brace used for scoliosis, and wrist and hand splints. Devices may be prefabricated or custom-made from casts or digital scans of the patient's limb.

Practice

An orthotist is a clinician who assesses patients, prescribes and fits orthoses, and follows up on their function. Orthotics is closely related to prosthetics, though prostheses replace missing body parts rather than support existing ones.
