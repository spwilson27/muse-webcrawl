Johann Tetzel (c. 1465 – 11 August 1519) was a German Dominican friar and preacher. As a seller of indulgences he became the immediate catalyst of the Protestant Reformation: his preaching campaign in 1517 provoked Martin Luther to write the Ninety-five Theses.

Life and indulgence preaching

Tetzel was born in Pirna in Saxony and entered the Dominican order at Leipzig. He preached indulgences for various campaigns for more than a decade, including one for the Teutonic Knights in Livonia. In 1517 he was commissioned to preach the indulgence issued by Pope Leo X, whose proceeds were to fund the rebuilding of St. Peter's Basilica in Rome and, secretly, to repay Archbishop Albert of Mainz's debts to the Fugger banking house.

Barred from Electoral Saxony by Frederick the Wise, Tetzel preached just across the border at places such as Jüterbog, where he drew crowds from Wittenberg. He was reputed — though modern historians doubt the attribution — to have used the jingle "As soon as the coin in the coffer rings, the soul from purgatory springs." Luther's parishioners returned from Tetzel's sermons believing their indulgences made repentance unnecessary.

Conflict with Luther

On 31 October 1517 Luther addressed his Ninety-five Theses to Archbishop Albert, attacking the theology behind Tetzel's preaching. Tetzel responded in 1518 with counter-theses drafted with Konrad Wimpina in defence of indulgences and papal authority, but public opinion turned against him. Discredited and in poor health, he withdrew to the Dominican convent in Leipzig, where Luther reportedly sent him a letter of consolation. Tetzel died there on 11 August 1519.

Legacy

Although Tetzel was an agent rather than the author of the indulgence system, his name became a byword for the abuses that ignited the Reformation; historians note that some later Protestant portrayals caricatured him beyond the documentary record.
