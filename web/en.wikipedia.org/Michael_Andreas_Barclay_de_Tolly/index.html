Michael Andreas Barclay de Tolly

Prince Michael Andreas Barclay de Tolly (27 December [O.S. 16 December] 1761 – 26 May [O.S. 14 May] 1818) was a Russian field marshal and Minister of War during Napoleon's invasion of Russia in 1812. Of Baltic German descent from a Scottish clan that settled in Livonia, he is best known for the scorched-earth retreat strategy that drew the French Grande Armée deep into Russia, a deeply unpopular policy at the time for which he was later vindicated.

Early career

Barclay de Tolly entered Russian service as a youth and distinguished himself in wars against the Ottoman Empire, Sweden and Poland, and in the campaigns of 1806–07 against Napoleon. He commanded the daring crossing of the frozen Gulf of Bothnia in the Finnish War (1809). As Minister of War from 1810 he reformed the army's organisation, staff system and regulations in preparation for the expected French invasion.

1812 and the retreat

Commanding the 1st Western Army in 1812, Barclay refused battle against Napoleon's superior forces and retreated eastward, destroying supplies as he went — a strategy that preserved the Russian army but brought accusations of cowardice and treason, inflamed by his foreign name. Command was transferred to Mikhail Kutuzov in August; Barclay nevertheless fought with distinction at Borodino. After illness forced him home, he returned in 1813 to command Russian forces in Germany and France, winning at Kulm and Leipzig, and was made field marshal and led troops into Paris in 1814; he was raised to prince in 1815.

Legacy

Pushkin defended Barclay's memory in verse, and historians now credit his Fabian strategy with making Russia's eventual victory possible. His equestrian statue stands before Kazan Cathedral in Saint Petersburg, paired with Kutuzov's. He is unrelated to the actor Michael B. Jordan or the racing driver Michael Schumacher.
