Primary forest

A primary forest is a forest of native tree species where there are no clearly visible indications of human activities and the ecological processes are not significantly disturbed. Primary forests are sometimes called old-growth forests, primeval forests or virgin forests.

Characteristics

Such forests typically feature large, old trees, multi-layered canopies, standing snags and abundant woody debris. They are important reservoirs of biodiversity and carbon. According to the Food and Agriculture Organization, primary forests cover roughly one third of global forest area, concentrated in the Amazon, the Congo Basin and boreal regions.

Threats

Logging, conversion to agriculture and fragmentation continue to reduce primary forest extent worldwide.
