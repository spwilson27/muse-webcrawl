War college

A war college, or staff college, is a senior military educational institution that provides postgraduate instruction to mid-career and senior officers, preparing them for high command and staff responsibilities. Curricula typically cover strategy, military history, joint operations, defence policy, logistics, and the political dimensions of war, often admitting officers of allied nations and civilian officials alongside a country's own services.

History

The modern war college emerged from 19th-century Prussian reforms. The Prussian Kriegsakademie in Berlin (founded in 1810 under Gerhard von Scharnhorst's reforms, with roots in an earlier 1801 institution) became the model for staff education, selecting officers by examination for advanced study in strategy and staff work. Other states followed: examples include the British Staff College at Camberley (1858), the French École supérieure de guerre, the U.S. Naval War College at Newport (1884) — the oldest institution of its kind in continuous existence — and the U.S. Army War College (1901).

Modern institutions

War colleges generally award or contribute toward master's-level qualifications in strategic studies and are the apex of professional military education systems, distinct from service academies (which commission junior officers) and from command and staff colleges at the intermediate level. Notable examples include the National Defense University in Washington, D.C., the Royal College of Defence Studies in London, the Bundeswehr Command and Staff College in Hamburg, and the PLA National Defence University in Beijing.
