Ballistics

Ballistics is the field of mechanics concerned with the launching, flight behaviour and impact effects of projectiles, especially ranged weapon munitions such as bullets, unguided bombs, rockets, and the like. The science has applications in the design of weapons and ammunition, in military and spaceflight engineering, and in forensic science.

Branches

Internal ballistics studies the motion of a projectile within the barrel of a gun, driven by burning propellant gases. Transition ballistics covers the brief phase as the projectile exits the muzzle. External ballistics deals with the projectile in free flight, subject to gravity, drag, and wind; mathematically it involves the trajectory of a projectile, traditionally computed using ballistic tables and now with computational models. Terminal ballistics studies the behaviour of the projectile when it strikes its target, including penetration and wounding effects.

History

Early systematic study of projectile motion came with Galileo's parabolic trajectory, contradicting the Aristotelian view, and Newton's laws of motion provided the theoretical basis for classical ballistics. The ballistic pendulum, invented by Benjamin Robins in the 18th century, allowed the first accurate measurement of projectile velocity.

Forensic ballistics

In criminal investigations, forensic ballistics examines bullets, cartridge cases, and firearms to match fired ammunition to a specific weapon through markings imparted by the barrel's rifling and firing mechanism.
