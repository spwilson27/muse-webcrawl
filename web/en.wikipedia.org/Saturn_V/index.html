The Saturn V is a retired American super heavy-lift launch vehicle developed under the Apollo programme for human exploration of the Moon and used from 1967 to 1973. Designed under Wernher von Braun at NASA's Marshall Space Flight Center, it remains the tallest, heaviest, and most powerful rocket ever operated successfully, and the only vehicle to carry humans beyond low Earth orbit.

Design

The three-stage rocket stood 110.6 metres tall. The first stage (S-IC) burned five enormous F-1 engines; the second (S-II) and third (S-IVB) stages used hydrogen-fuelled J-2 engines. The single S-IVB engine restarted in orbit to perform translunar injection, and a modified S-IVB became the Skylab space station. Thrust at liftoff exceeded 34 million newtons.

Operational history

Saturn V flew 13 times without failure: two uncrewed tests (Apollo 4 and 6), nine crewed Moon missions (Apollo 8–17, six of which landed), and the Skylab launch in 1973. Its most famous flight carried Apollo 11, which landed the first humans on the Moon on 20 July 1969. The programme was cancelled with Apollo 18–20 for budgetary reasons; three complete vehicles survive as museum displays.

Legacy

The Saturn V remains the benchmark against which heavy launch vehicles are measured. Its F-1 engines and clustered-stage architecture influenced later designs, and NASA's Space Launch System is its institutional successor for crewed deep-space flight.
