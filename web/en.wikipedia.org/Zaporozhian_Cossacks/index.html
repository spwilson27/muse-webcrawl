The Zaporozhian Cossacks were the Cossack community that lived beyond (za) the rapids (porohy) of the Dnieper River in the wild steppe frontier of what is now Ukraine. Organised around the fortified camp of the Zaporizhian Sich, they formed a self-governing military society that played a central role in the history of Ukraine and in the wars between the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, the Ottoman Empire, the Crimean Khanate, and Russia.

The Sich

The first Sich is traditionally associated with Dmytro Vyshnevetsky, who built a fortress on the island of Mala Khortytsia in the 1550s. The Sich was governed by a general council (rada) of all Cossacks, which elected the Kosh Otaman and other officers annually. The Zaporozhians raided Ottoman and Tatar targets by land and in their light boats (chaiky), reaching the Black Sea coast and even the suburbs of Constantinople, while also serving as mercenaries in European wars.

The Hetmanate and decline

In 1648 the registered Cossack leader Bohdan Khmelnytsky raised a great uprising against the Commonwealth and founded the Cossack Hetmanate, whose allegiance to Moscow at the Pereyaslav Council of 1654 began decades of conflict known as the Ruin. Zaporozhian autonomy was progressively curtailed by the Russian Empire; in June 1775 imperial troops surrounded and destroyed the Sich, and its lands were annexed to New Russia. Many Cossacks fled to Ottoman territory to form the Danubian Sich, while others were later resettled as the Kuban Cossacks.

The famous reply

The Cossacks' legendary 1676 letter to Sultan Mehmed IV — a defiant, richly insulting rejection of his ultimatum — became a celebrated episode of Ukrainian folklore and the subject of Ilya Repin's monumental painting.
