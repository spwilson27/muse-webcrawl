Universal Music Group N.V. (often abbreviated as UMG) is a Dutch–American multinational music corporation and the largest music company in the world. Its corporate headquarters are in Hilversum, Netherlands, with operational headquarters in Santa Monica, California. UMG's labels include Interscope, Capitol, Republic, Def Jam, Island, Geffen and the classical labels Deutsche Grammophon and Decca.

History

The company's origins lie in the American Decca and MCA music businesses. In 1995, Seagram acquired MCA and renamed its music division Universal Music Group. The 1998 merger with PolyGram created the world's largest record company, combining labels such as Interscope, A&M, Island, Def Jam, Motown, Geffen and Deutsche Grammophon. Vivendi acquired the group in 2000. In 2012, UMG bought EMI's recorded music division (retaining Capitol and other labels, while selling Parlophone to Warner Music Group), consolidating its position as the biggest of the "big three" record companies alongside Sony Music and Warner Music Group.

Recent developments

UMG was spun off from Vivendi and listed on Euronext Amsterdam in September 2021, one of Europe's largest-ever listings. The Chinese conglomerate Tencent acquired a substantial minority stake beginning in 2019. Under chairman and CEO Lucian Grainge, the company has expanded in music publishing (Universal Music Publishing Group), merchandising and film and television production.

Artists and labels

UMG's roster has included many of the world's best-selling artists, among them Taylor Swift, Drake, The Weeknd, Billie Eilish, Kendrick Lamar, the Beatles (through Capitol), ABBA, Queen, U2 and Lady Gaga. Its catalogue operations manage recordings dating back more than a century.
