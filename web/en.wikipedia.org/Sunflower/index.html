Sunflower

The common sunflower (Helianthus annuus) is a species of large annual flowering plant in the daisy family Asteraceae, native to North America and cultivated worldwide as a source of edible oil and seeds. It is noted for its very large flower heads and tall stems, which can exceed 3 metres.

Description

What appears to be a single flower is a composite inflorescence (capitulum) made of many small florets: outer sterile ray florets form the yellow "petals", while the central disc florets mature into seeds (achenes) arranged in striking spiral patterns related to the Fibonacci sequence. Young plants exhibit heliotropism, tracking the sun across the sky, a behaviour that diminishes as the head matures.

Cultivation and uses

Indigenous peoples of North America domesticated the sunflower several thousand years ago. Spanish explorers carried it to Europe in the 16th century, and by the 19th century Russia had become the centre of oilseed breeding; sunflower oil is now one of the world's major vegetable oils, with Ukraine and Russia historically the largest producers. Seeds are also eaten as a snack, used in bird feed, and the plants are grown ornamentally. Sunflowers are used in phytoremediation to extract heavy metals and toxins from soil, notably after the Chernobyl and Fukushima disasters.

Cultural significance

The sunflower is the state flower of Kansas and the national flower of Ukraine. It is strongly associated with the Dutch painter Vincent van Gogh, whose Sunflowers series (1888–89) is among the most famous still-life subjects in Western art. In politics, the sunflower became an international symbol of solidarity with Ukraine following the 2022 Russian invasion.
