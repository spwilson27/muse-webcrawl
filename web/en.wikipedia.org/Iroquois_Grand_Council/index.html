Iroquois Grand Council

The Grand Council of the Haudenosaunee, or Iroquois Confederacy, is the central governing body of the confederacy of originally five — later six — Native American nations: the Mohawk, Oneida, Onondaga, Cayuga and Seneca, joined in 1722 by the Tuscarora. Under the Great Law of Peace (Kaianere'kó:wa), fifty sachems (hoyaneh) sit on the council.

Structure

The fifty council titles are distributed among the nations: nine Mohawk, nine Oneida, fourteen Onondaga, ten Cayuga and eight Seneca. The Onondaga, as keepers of the council fire and of the wampum records, host the council. Sachems are nominated by the clan mothers of the matrilineal clans and can be removed by them for misconduct. Decisions are reached by consensus, with matters passing between the "elder brothers" (Mohawk and Seneca), the "younger brothers" (Oneida and Cayuga, later Tuscarora) and the Onondaga firekeepers.

History

Tradition attributes the confederacy's founding to the Peacemaker Deganawida and his spokesman Hiawatha, who ended cycles of warfare among the five nations. The Grand Council managed diplomacy and war with European colonial powers for centuries. Today the Haudenosaunee Confederacy Chiefs Council continues to meet at Onondaga, alongside elected band councils in many communities.
