Mike Flanagan

Mike Flanagan (born May 20, 1978) is an American filmmaker known for his work in horror. He has directed films including Oculus (2013), Hush (2016), Gerald's Game (2017) and Doctor Sleep (2019), and created the Netflix horror series The Haunting of Hill House (2018), The Haunting of Bly Manor (2020), Midnight Mass (2021), The Midnight Club (2022) and The Fall of the House of Usher (2023).

Career

Flanagan was born in Salem, Massachusetts, and raised in Maryland. He began with the micro-budget film Absentia (2011), funded partly through crowdfunding, which brought him critical attention. Oculus, expanded from his earlier short film, was a commercial breakthrough. He frequently collaborates with his wife, actress Kate Siegel, and a recurring ensemble of actors across his series. His adaptation of Stephen King's Doctor Sleep — a sequel to both King's novel and Stanley Kubrick's The Shining — was praised by King himself. Flanagan's later work includes the King adaptation The Life of Chuck (2024), which won the People's Choice Award at the Toronto International Film Festival.

Style

Flanagan's horror emphasises character, grief and family trauma, often using long takes, extended monologues and quiet dread rather than jump scares alone. He frequently serves as his own editor.
