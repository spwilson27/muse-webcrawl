The Fifth Element (French: Le Cinquième Élément) is a 1997 English-language French science fiction action film conceived and directed by Luc Besson, and co-written by Besson and Robert Mark Kamen. It stars Bruce Willis, Gary Oldman, Ian Holm, Milla Jovovich, and Chris Tucker. Primarily set in the 23rd century, the film's central plot involves the survival of planet Earth, which becomes the responsibility of Korben Dallas (Willis), a taxicab driver and former special forces major, after Leeloo (Jovovich), a young woman who is the embodiment of a supreme being, falls into his cab.

Plot

Every 5,000 years, a "Great Evil" — a living planet of darkness — attempts to destroy life, guided this cycle by industrialist Jean-Baptiste Emmanuel Zorg, in the service of the entity Mr. Shadow. The Mondoshawan aliens safeguard four elemental stones and a fifth element, the Divine Light; when their ship is destroyed, scientists reconstruct the fifth element from recovered cells, producing Leeloo. With priest Vito Cornelius, Dallas races to Fhloston Paradise and then to the Mondoshawans' temple in Egypt to assemble the stones before the Evil reaches Earth; the weapon is activated when Dallas declares his love for Leeloo, teaching her that humanity is worth saving.

Production and reception

Besson began writing the story as a teenager. The film's look drew on French comics artists Jean Giraud (Mœbius) and Jean-Claude Mézières, with costumes by Jean-Paul Gaultier and the famous "Diva Dance" featuring impossible soprano vocals. At $90 million it was the most expensive European film ever made at the time. Reviews were divided between praise for its visual invention and criticism of its tone, but it grossed nearly $264 million and became a durable cult favourite; it won the BAFTA for Best Special Visual Effects and several César Awards.
