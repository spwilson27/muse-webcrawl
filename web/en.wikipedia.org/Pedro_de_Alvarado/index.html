Pedro de Alvarado


Portrait of Pedro de Alvarado

Pedro de Alvarado y Contreras (c. 1485 – 4 July 1541) was a Spanish conquistador and governor of Guatemala. He participated in the conquest of Cuba, the exploration of the Gulf of Mexico coast under Juan de Grijalva, and as Hernán Cortés's closest lieutenant in the conquest of the Aztec Empire, before leading the conquest of Guatemala and much of Central America. The Nahua called him Tonatiuh — "the Sun" — for his blond hair and fair skin.

Conquest of Mexico

Born in Badajoz, Extremadura, Alvarado sailed to the Indies and joined Cortés's 1519 expedition. He was brave and impulsive: in May 1520, left in command of Tenochtitlan during Cortés's absence, he ordered the massacre of Aztec nobles celebrating the festival of Toxcatl, igniting the uprising that drove the Spanish from the city in the Noche Triste. During the retreat he was said to have vaulted a canal with his lance — the "Salto de Alvarado".

Guatemala and later career

In 1523–24 Alvarado marched south and conquered the K'iche', Kaqchikel, and other Maya kingdoms of highland Guatemala, founding Santiago de los Caballeros. He governed Guatemala almost as a personal fief, campaigning in Honduras, El Salvador, and Nicaragua, and in 1534 sailed to Peru in an abortive attempt to seize part of the Inca Empire, selling his fleet and claims to Diego de Almagro. In 1541, campaigning against the Indigenous revolt in Nueva Galicia (the Mixtón War), he was crushed by a falling horse during a retreat near Nochistlán and died of his injuries at Guadalajara.

Alvarado is remembered as one of the most ruthless of the conquistadors, and his campaigns left a legacy of violence and forced labour in Guatemala.
