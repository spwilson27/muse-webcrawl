Young Europe (Italian: Giovine Europa) was an international association of nationalist movements founded in 1834 at Bern by the Italian revolutionary Giuseppe Mazzini. Modelled in part on his own Young Italy (founded 1831), it united Young Italy, Young Poland and Young Germany in a common commitment to republican government, national self-determination and the brotherhood of peoples.

Aims

In the "Act of Brotherhood" signed at Bern in April 1834, the member movements declared their shared faith in liberty, equality and the progress of humanity, and pledged mutual support in overthrowing the absolutist order imposed on Europe by the Congress of Vienna. Mazzini envisioned a future Europe of free, democratic nation-states cooperating in a loose federation — an early vision of a united democratic Europe — with national missions balanced by duties to humanity as a whole, expressed in his formula "God and the People".

Activity and influence

Young Europe functioned largely through exile networks, journals and conspiratorial contacts, and inspired similar organisations — Young Turks, Young Ireland and others later borrowed the naming pattern, though often with very different ideologies. It had limited practical success against the police states of the 1830s, and Mazzini's insurrectionary ventures repeatedly failed, but the association spread nationalist doctrine across Europe and trained a generation of activists who resurfaced in the Revolutions of 1848.

Legacy

Historians regard Young Europe as a founding expression of liberal nationalism and an ancestor of later projects of European integration, distinguished by its insistence that nationalism be internationalist and democratic rather than dynastic or chauvinist.
