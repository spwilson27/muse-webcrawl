John Frederic Daniell

Born12 March 1790, London, England
Died13 March 1845 (aged 55), London, England
Known forDaniell cell, dew-point hygrometer

John Frederic Daniell FRS (12 March 1790 – 13 March 1845) was an English chemist and physicist, best known for inventing the Daniell cell, an early and much improved form of electric battery, and the dew-point hygrometer.

Life and career

Daniell worked in sugar refining and resin manufacture before turning to science. In 1831 he became the first professor of chemistry at the newly founded King's College London. He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society and received its Copley Medal in 1837.

The Daniell cell

Announced in 1836, the Daniell cell used zinc and copper electrodes in separate compartments of zinc sulfate and copper sulfate solutions, separated by a porous barrier. Unlike the voltaic pile, it delivered a steady current without the polarisation caused by hydrogen bubbles, producing about 1.1 volts. It became the standard battery for telegraphy.

Other work

Daniell invented the dew-point hygrometer in 1820, wrote on meteorology, and introduced the concept of ions in his electrochemical writings. He died suddenly in 1845 while attending a meeting of the Royal Society council.
