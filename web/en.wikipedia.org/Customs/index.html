Customs

Customs is the government authority responsible for regulating the flow of goods, animals, and personal effects into and out of a country, primarily at its borders. Customs agencies collect import duties (customs tariffs), enforce prohibitions and restrictions, and combat smuggling. The word also denotes the duties themselves.

Functions

Modern customs administrations assess and collect tariffs and taxes on imports, inspect cargo, enforce trade and sanitary rules, intercept contraband such as narcotics and counterfeit goods, and protect endangered species under CITES. Travellers typically declare goods at border crossings, using "nothing to declare" and declaration channels.

History

Customs duties are among the oldest taxes: ancient Egypt, Greece, and Rome taxed goods crossing their borders, and tariff revenue was a mainstay of state income before the spread of income tax. The World Customs Organization (founded 1952) coordinates practices among more than 180 member administrations, and the Harmonized System standardizes commodity classification codes worldwide.

Customs unions and free trade

Members of a customs union abolish internal duties and adopt a common external tariff. Free trade agreements may reduce duties between partners while each keeps its own external policy; preferential rules of origin then determine whether goods qualify for reduced rates.
