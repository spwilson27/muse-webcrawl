Failure to appear

In law, a failure to appear (often abbreviated FTA) occurs when a defendant or respondent in a legal proceeding does not appear in court on the date and time specified in a summons, subpoena, citation or court order. Failure to appear is itself an offence in many jurisdictions, separate from the underlying matter, and can lead to additional penalties.

Consequences

Common consequences of failing to appear include:

  • Issuance of a bench warrant for the person's arrest;
  • Forfeiture of bail or bond posted to secure release;
  • Additional criminal charges or fines;
  • Suspension of a driver's licence, particularly in traffic cases;
  • In civil cases, a default judgment against the absent party.

Criminal proceedings

Where a defendant released before trial fails to appear, the court may issue a warrant and, on arrest, impose stricter release conditions such as higher bail or remand in custody. In the United States, federal law (18 U.S.C. § 3146) makes failure to appear a separate federal offence with penalties that depend on the seriousness of the underlying charge. Bail bondsmen and bounty hunters may be engaged to locate defendants who have absconded.

Traffic and minor matters

For minor offences such as traffic tickets, failure to appear or to pay by the due date commonly results in a default conviction, licence suspension and additional fees. Many courts allow a missed appearance to be excused or rescheduled on a showing of good cause, such as illness or lack of proper notice.
