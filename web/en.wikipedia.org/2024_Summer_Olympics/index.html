The 2024 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the XXXIII Olympiad and branded as Paris 2024, were an international multi-sport event held in Paris, France, from July 26 to August 11, 2024. Paris became the second city, after London, to host the Summer Olympics three times, having previously hosted in 1900 and 1924 — exactly a century earlier.

Highlights

The opening ceremony was held outdoors along the River Seine, the first in Olympic history staged outside a stadium, with athletes parading on boats. Breaking (breakdancing) made its Olympic debut, while surfing events were held in Teahupo'o, Tahiti. The United States and China tied with 40 gold medals each, with the U.S. topping the overall medal count with 126. French swimmer Léon Marchand won four gold medals, and American gymnast Simone Biles returned to win three golds.

Venues and legacy

Competitions used iconic Parisian settings, including beach volleyball beneath the Eiffel Tower, equestrian events at Versailles, and fencing at the Grand Palais. Organizers emphasized sustainability, with 95 percent of venues pre-existing or temporary and the Olympic Village later converted to housing. The Games were widely regarded as a popular success, and Paris passed the Olympic flag to Los Angeles, host of the 2028 Summer Games.
