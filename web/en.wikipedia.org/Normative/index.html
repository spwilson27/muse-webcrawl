Normative

Normative generally means relating to an evaluative standard. Normativity is the phenomenon in human societies of designating some actions or outcomes as good, desirable, or permissible, and others as bad, undesirable, or impermissible. A normative statement expresses an evaluation or value judgement, in contrast to a descriptive statement, which reports facts.

Scales symbolising judgement and standards

In philosophy

Philosophers distinguish normative ethics — the study of how one ought to act — from descriptive ethics. David Hume's is-ought problem questioned whether normative conclusions can be derived from purely factual premises. Normative claims pervade ethics, aesthetics, law, and epistemology ("normative epistemology" concerns how beliefs ought to be formed).

Normative economics

In economics, normative analysis concerns value judgements about what economic policy should be, as opposed to positive economics, which describes the world as it is. Statements such as "the government should raise the minimum wage" are normative and cannot be settled by evidence alone.

In other fields

Linguistics contrasts prescriptive (normative) grammar with descriptive grammar. In law, normative theory addresses how legal systems ought to operate. Social sciences use "normative" for the influence of social norms on behaviour.
