Baden-Württemberg

Baden-Württemberg is a German state (Land) in southwest Germany, east of the Rhine and bordering France, Switzerland, and Austria. Its capital and largest city is Stuttgart. With about 11.1 million inhabitants it is Germany's third-most populous state, and it is one of the country's most prosperous regions, known worldwide for its automotive and engineering industries.

History

The state was created in 1952 by merging the historical states of Baden, Württemberg-Baden, and Württemberg-Hohenzollern, entities that had been reorganised under French and American occupation after the Second World War. The merger, confirmed by referendum, united the formerly separate territories of Baden and Württemberg.

Geography and economy

The state includes the Black Forest, the Swabian Alb, Lake Constance (Bodensee) on its southern border, and the upper Rhine plain. Its economy centres on Stuttgart and the Neckar region: Daimler (Mercedes-Benz) and Porsche have their headquarters there, alongside Bosch, SAP (in Walldorf), and a dense network of Mittelstand manufacturers. Heidelberg, Freiburg, Tübingen, and Ulm are noted university cities.
