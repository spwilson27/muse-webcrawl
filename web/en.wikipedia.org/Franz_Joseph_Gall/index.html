Franz Joseph Gall (9 March 1758 – 22 August 1828) was a German neuroanatomist, physiologist, and pioneer in the study of the localization of mental functions in the brain. He is best known as the founder of phrenology, a doctrine that held that the shape of the skull reflected the development of underlying brain organs and hence a person's character and mental faculties.

Life

Gall was born in Tiefenbronn, Baden, into a wealthy Catholic family of Italian origin (the family name was originally Gallo). He studied medicine in Strasbourg and Vienna, where he settled as a physician and lecturer. His anatomical lectures drew large audiences, but in 1801 the Austrian emperor Francis II banned his public lectures on the grounds that they were subversive of religion and morality.

In 1807 Gall moved to Paris, where he spent the rest of his life. Together with his collaborator Johann Gaspar Spurzheim, he published the multi-volume Anatomie et physiologie du système nerveux en général (1810–1819). Spurzheim later broke with Gall and carried a popularized version of the doctrine, under the name "phrenology," to Britain and the United States.

Doctrine

Gall proposed that the brain is the organ of the mind, that it is composed of distinct "organs" each responsible for a particular faculty (such as amativeness, combativeness, or veneration), and that the relative size of these organs could be inferred from bumps and depressions on the skull. Although phrenology itself was eventually discredited as a pseudoscience, Gall's core idea of cerebral localization influenced later neurology, including the work of Paul Broca on speech areas, and his meticulous brain dissections advanced neuroanatomy.

Criticism and legacy

Contemporaries such as Pierre Flourens attacked Gall's methods experimentally, and by the mid-nineteenth century phrenology had lost scientific standing. Gall's collection of skulls and casts, assembled to support his theory, is preserved in Paris. Historians of neuroscience credit Gall with shifting attention to the cerebral cortex and with stimulating empirical study of brain function.
