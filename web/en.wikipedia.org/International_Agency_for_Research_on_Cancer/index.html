The International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC; French: Centre International de Recherche sur le Cancer, CIRC) is an intergovernmental agency forming part of the World Health Organization of the United Nations. Its role is to conduct and coordinate research into the causes of cancer. It also maintains a registration system for cancer incidence worldwide and publishes statistical compilations such as Cancer Incidence in Five Continents and the GLOBOCAN estimates.

IARC Monographs

The IARC is best known for its Monographs on the Identification of Carcinogenic Hazards to Humans, which classify agents into groups according to the strength of evidence that they can cause cancer:

  • Group 1: Carcinogenic to humans
  • Group 2A: Probably carcinogenic to humans
  • Group 2B: Possibly carcinogenic to humans
  • Group 3: Not classifiable as to its carcinogenicity to humans

Group 1 includes tobacco smoke, asbestos, plutonium, alcoholic beverages and processed meat; the classifications describe the strength of evidence, not the level of risk posed by an exposure.

History

The agency was created in 1965 following an initiative promoted by French journalists and public figures and supported by President Charles de Gaulle, who offered Lyon as its seat. Its participating states fund its programme of epidemiology, laboratory research, and training of cancer researchers, particularly in low- and middle-income countries.
