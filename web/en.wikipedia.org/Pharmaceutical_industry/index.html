The pharmaceutical industry discovers, develops, produces and markets pharmaceutical drugs for use as medications. It is one of the world's most research-intensive and heavily regulated industries, with global revenues exceeding one and a half trillion US dollars annually.

Drug development

New medicines pass from laboratory discovery through preclinical testing and three phases of clinical trials in humans before regulators such as the US Food and Drug Administration or the European Medicines Agency may approve them for sale. Development typically takes over a decade and, by industry estimates, may cost more than a billion dollars per approved drug when failures are included. Patents and market exclusivity reward innovation; once they expire, generic manufacturers supply copies at much lower prices.

Structure and criticism

The largest companies — often called "Big Pharma" — include Pfizer, Johnson & Johnson, Roche, Novartis, Merck and AstraZeneca, alongside a vast biotechnology sector and contract manufacturers. The industry is credited with transformative therapies from antibiotics to mRNA vaccines, yet faces sustained criticism over high drug prices (especially in the United States), marketing practices such as direct-to-consumer advertising, its role in the opioid epidemic, and lobbying. Debates continue over patent regimes, access to medicines in low-income countries, and public funding of underlying research.
