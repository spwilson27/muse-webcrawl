Scafell Pike

Elevation978 m (3,209 ft)
LocationLake District, Cumbria, England
RangeSouthern Fells

Scafell Pike is the highest mountain in England, at 978 metres (3,209 ft) above sea level. It lies in the Lake District National Park in Cumbria, among the Southern Fells above Wasdale and Borrowdale.

Geology and setting

The summit is a rocky plateau of Borrowdale Volcanic Group rocks, strewn with boulders and crowned by a large cairn and a war memorial. The land was donated to the National Trust in 1919 in memory of the men of the Lake District who fell in the First World War — the largest single donation in the Trust's history at the time. Nearby peaks include Broad Crag, Ill Crag, and Scafell, separated by the col of Mickledore.

Climbing

Popular ascent routes run from Wasdale Head (the shortest), Seathwaite in Borrowdale, Langdale, and Eskdale. Scafell Pike is the highest of the three peaks in the National Three Peaks Challenge, alongside Ben Nevis and Snowdon. On clear days the summit offers views to Scotland, Wales, Ireland, and the Isle of Man.
