Solicitor General for Scotland

The Solicitor General for Scotland (Scottish Gaelic: Àrd-neach-lagha a' Chrùin an Alba) is one of the Law Officers of the Crown in Scotland. The Solicitor General is the deputy of the Lord Advocate, to whom all prosecution decisions in Scotland ultimately belong, and assists the Lord Advocate in the discharge of their functions within the Crown Office and Procurator Fiscal Service.

The office is one of the oldest in Scottish public law, with holders recorded from the late 15th century. Together with the Lord Advocate, the Solicitor General heads the prosecution system of Scotland and provides legal advice to the Scottish Government on Scots law. Since devolution in 1999, the two law officers are members of the Scottish Government, although neither is required to be a member of the Scottish Parliament; by convention they do not attend cabinet except when required.

Functions

The Solicitor General deputises for the Lord Advocate in prosecuting serious crime in the High Court of Justiciary, appears for the Crown in appeals, and undertakes such other duties as the Lord Advocate assigns. In the event of a vacancy in the office of Lord Advocate, the Solicitor General may exercise the Lord Advocate's functions. The Solicitor General is appointed by the King on the recommendation of the First Minister, with the agreement of the Scottish Parliament.
