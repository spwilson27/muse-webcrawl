Vigna mungo, the black gram or urad bean, is a legume grown in South Asia. Like its cousin the mung bean (Vigna radiata), it was transferred from the genus Phaseolus to Vigna. The product sold as black gram is usually the whole urad bean, whereas the split bean with the black skin removed is the white lentil; despite the name, it is not botanically a true lentil.

Description

Black gram is an erect, hairy annual herb reaching 30–100 cm in height, with trifoliate leaves, yellow flowers and cylindrical pods containing 4–10 oblong black (occasionally olive-green) seeds about 4–6 mm long. It is a warm-season, short-day crop grown in the rainy (kharif) and summer seasons across India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Myanmar and other parts of South and Southeast Asia.

Cultivation and use

India is the world's largest producer and consumer of black gram. The beans are rich in protein (about 25%) and are a staple of Indian cuisine: split and dehusked urad dal is ground with rice into the fermented batter for idli and dosa, forms the white lentils in dal makhani when used whole, and is used in papad, vada, and numerous regional dishes. Skinned split urad is an essential tempering and batter ingredient throughout South Indian cooking.

Like other legumes, black gram fixes atmospheric nitrogen through root-nodule bacteria, improving soil fertility in rotations with cereals. Its fodder and green manure are also valued.
