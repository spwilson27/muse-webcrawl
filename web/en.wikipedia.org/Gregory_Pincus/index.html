Gregory Goodwin Pincus (9 April 1903 – 22 August 1967) was an American biologist and researcher who co-invented the combined oral contraceptive pill, one of the most socially consequential pharmaceuticals of the 20th century.

Work on the pill

Pincus, trained at Cornell and Harvard, conducted early research on mammalian reproduction and in vitro fertilization in rabbits. In 1944 he co-founded the Worcester Foundation for Experimental Biology in Massachusetts. In 1951, birth-control advocate Margaret Sanger and philanthropist Katharine McCormick engaged and funded Pincus to develop a hormonal contraceptive. Working with Min Chueh Chang and with clinician John Rock, he showed that progesterone and later synthetic progestins such as norethynodrel could suppress ovulation safely in humans. Large field trials in Puerto Rico led to FDA approval of Enovid for menstrual disorders in 1957 and as a contraceptive in 1960.

Legacy

Pincus also organized the influential Laurentian Hormone Conference. He died in 1967 of a blood disease, having seen the pill begin its worldwide impact on women's health and society.
