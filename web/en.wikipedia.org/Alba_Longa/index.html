Alba Longa (occasionally written Albalonga) was an ancient Latin city in Central Italy, traditionally founded around 1151 BC by Ascanius, son of Aeneas, and destroyed in the mid-seventh century BC by the Roman king Tullus Hostilius. Situated in the Alban Hills southeast of Rome, it was remembered as the mother city of Rome itself and the head of the Latin League of thirty peoples.

Legendary history

According to the Roman foundation tradition preserved by Livy and Dionysius of Halicarnassus, Aeneas, fleeing fallen Troy, settled in Latium and founded Lavinium; his son Ascanius (also called Iulus) later founded Alba Longa, named "long white" either for its elongated shape on a ridge or for a white sow seen in a prophecy. A line of Alban kings descending from Aeneas ruled for some three centuries, culminating in Numitor and Amulius; it was from Numitor's daughter Rhea Silvia that Romulus and Remus, the founders of Rome, were said to descend.

Destruction

Roman tradition held that during the war between Rome and Alba under Tullus Hostilius, the issue was settled by combat between the Horatii and Curiatii — triplets representing each city — after which Alba's treacherous dictator Mettius Fufetius was executed and the city destroyed, its population transferred to Rome and settled on the Caelian Hill. The sanctuary of Jupiter Latiaris on the Alban Mount nevertheless remained the cult centre of the Latin peoples, where the Feriae Latinae festival continued to be celebrated into the imperial age.
