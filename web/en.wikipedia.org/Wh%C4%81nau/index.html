Whānau

Whānau
LanguageMāori
Literal meaning"to give birth"; extended family
Related structuresHapū (subtribe), iwi (tribe)

Whānau is a Māori-language word for an extended family or community of related families. In contemporary New Zealand it is often translated as "family", but its meaning is broader than the Western nuclear family: a whānau may include several generations, multiple households, and relationships of care and obligation extending beyond blood ties.

Traditionally, the whānau was the basic social and economic unit of Māori society, intermediate between the individual and the hapū (subtribe) and iwi (tribe). Members cooperated in food production, child-rearing and the care of elders, and whānau identity is anchored in whakapapa (genealogy). The verb whānau also means "to be born" or "to give birth".

Modern usage

Today the term is used widely in New Zealand English and in public policy. Concepts such as "whānau-centred" services and the Whānau Ora commissioning approach shape health and social services for Māori. People also speak of kaupapa whānau — groups united by a shared purpose, such as a workplace, sports team or kura (school) community — alongside whānau by descent.
