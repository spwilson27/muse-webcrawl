Huilliche language

Huilliche (also Williche, and in the language itself chedungun or chesungun) is a language of southern Chile, spoken historically by the Huilliche people. It belongs to the Araucanian language family together with Mapudungun, though it is often treated as a southern dialect of Mapudungun or as a separate language; the two are only partially mutually intelligible.

Classification and distribution

Huilliche was spoken in the valleys and coastal ranges between Valdivia and the Chiloé Archipelago, in what are now the Los Ríos and Los Lagos regions of Chile. It is usually regarded as one of the southern varieties of the Araucanian family; some linguists distinguish two varieties, the Huilliche proper of the mainland and the variety of Chiloé.

Endangerment

The language is severely endangered. Fluency is now largely confined to a small number of elderly speakers, often estimated in the low thousands or fewer, most of them bilingual in Spanish. Revitalisation efforts by Huilliche communities and Chilean institutions include teaching materials, recordings of traditional narratives, and community language classes.

Features

Like Mapudungun, Huilliche is an agglutinative language with complex verbal morphology. It shows distinctive sound correspondences compared with Mapudungun, which is one basis for its classification as a separate language within the family.
