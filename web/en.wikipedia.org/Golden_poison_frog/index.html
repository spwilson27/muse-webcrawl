Golden poison frog


Phyllobates terribilis in its yellow form.
Scientific name: Phyllobates terribilis
Family: Dendrobatidae
Range: Pacific coast of Colombia
Conservation status: Endangered

The golden poison frog (Phyllobates terribilis), also known as the golden dart frog or golden poison arrow frog, is a poison dart frog endemic to the rainforests of the Pacific coast of Colombia. It is considered one of the most toxic animals on Earth.

Description

Adults are brightly coloured — yellow, orange or pale green (mint) depending on locality — an example of aposematic coloration advertising toxicity. They are among the largest poison dart frogs, reaching about 5.5 cm. Unlike many frogs, they are social and live in groups in the wild.

Toxicity

The skin secretes powerful alkaloid toxins, principally batrachotoxin, which prevents nerve cells from firing and causes paralysis and death. A single frog carries on the order of one milligram of toxin — reputedly enough to kill many adult humans. The indigenous Emberá people traditionally used the secretion to tip blowgun darts for hunting. The frogs obtain their toxins from their invertebrate diet; captive-bred specimens raised on fruit flies are non-toxic.

Conservation

The species is listed as Endangered, threatened by habitat loss in its small range in the Cauca and Valle del Cauca departments, though it is bred in captivity for the pet trade and research.
