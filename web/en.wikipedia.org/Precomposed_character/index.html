Precomposed character

A precomposed character (alternatively composite character or decomposable character) is a Unicode entity that can be defined as a sequence of one or more other characters. A precomposed character may typically represent a letter with a diacritical mark, such as é (Latin small letter e with acute accent, U+00E9).

Comparison with decomposed forms

The same visible character can often also be encoded as a base character followed by one or more combining characters — for example, é as U+0065 (e) plus U+0301 (combining acute accent). Unicode defines canonical equivalence between the precomposed and decomposed sequences, and its normalisation forms (NFC, NFD) convert text between them. Precomposed characters were largely included in Unicode for compatibility with legacy encodings such as ISO/IEC 8859.

Examples

Many scripts use precomposed characters: Latin letters with accents (à, ñ, ü), Greek letters with tonos, Hangul syllables (which are algorithmically decomposable into jamo), and numerous Indic and Southeast Asian characters. Some, like the ligature ﬁ, are compatibility characters rather than canonically equivalent composites.
