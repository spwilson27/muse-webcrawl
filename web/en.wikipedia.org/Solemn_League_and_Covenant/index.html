Solemn League and Covenant

The Solemn League and Covenant was an agreement made in 1643 between the Scottish Covenanters and the leaders of the English Parliament, during the First English Civil War. In return for Scottish military assistance against Charles I, the English Parliament undertook to reform the Church of England along Presbyterian lines and to preserve the reformed Church of Scotland.

Terms

The Covenant bound its subscribers to preserve the doctrine and government of the Church of Scotland, to bring the churches of England and Ireland "to the nearest conjunction and uniformity in religion" according to the Word of God and the example of the best reformed churches, to extirpate popery and prelacy, to preserve the rights of Parliament and the king's person and authority, and to maintain the union of the three kingdoms. It was sworn and subscribed across Scotland and by the English Parliament and the Westminster Assembly in September 1643.

Consequences

A Scottish army of some 21,000 men crossed into England in January 1644 and contributed to the Parliamentary victory at the Battle of Marston Moor. However, the English Independents, ascendant in the New Model Army, had no intention of imposing Presbyterianism, and relations between the allies deteriorated. After the Engagement of 1647 and the Scottish defeat at Preston, the Covenant's political programme collapsed in England, though it remained a defining document for Scottish Presbyterianism and for later Covenanter resistance under the Stuarts.
