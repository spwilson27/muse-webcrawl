Bayesian network

A Bayesian network (also called a Bayes network, belief network, or probabilistic directed acyclic graphical model) is a probabilistic graphical model that represents a set of random variables and their conditional dependencies by means of a directed acyclic graph (DAG). Each node in the graph is a variable, and each node is associated with a conditional probability table quantifying the effect its parent nodes have on it.

Definition

Formally, a Bayesian network encodes the joint probability distribution over its variables as the product of the local distributions of each variable given its parents. This factorisation rests on the conditional independence assumption that a variable is independent of its non-descendants given its parents.

Inference and learning

Probabilistic inference in a Bayesian network computes the posterior distribution of unobserved variables given evidence. Exact methods include variable elimination and the junction tree algorithm; approximate methods include sampling and loopy belief propagation. Networks can be constructed by experts or learned from data, both in structure and in parameters — a topic greatly developed through the work of Judea Pearl and others in the 1980s.

Applications

Bayesian networks are used in medical diagnosis, machine learning, bioinformatics, risk analysis, fault diagnosis, document classification, and decision support, and form the basis of dynamic Bayesian networks used for time-series modelling.
