Panchgani (Marathi: पांचगणी) is a hill station and municipal council in the Satara district of Maharashtra, India. It is renowned for its large volcanic plateau, known as "Table Land" — one of the largest mountain plateaus in Asia — and for its numerous boarding schools. The town's name derives from the five hills that surround it (panch, five; gani, hills).

History

Panchgani was developed as a summer retreat during the British Raj in the 1860s under the supervision of Superintendent John Chesson, who is credited with selecting the site and planting many of the species introduced there. Its temperate climate made it a sanatorium and holiday resort for British officials, and Chesson is buried in the cemetery of St Peter's Church in the town.

Geography and attractions

Situated at an elevation of about 1,293 metres in the Sahyadri ranges of the Western Ghats, Panchgani lies about 100 km from Pune and 18 km from the neighbouring hill station of Mahabaleshwar. Table Land, a flat laterite rock formation, offers views over the Wai valley and the Krishna river. Other points of interest include Sydney Point, Parsi Point, Devil's Kitchen caves, Mapro Garden, and the nearby Bhilar waterfall. The region is a major centre of strawberry cultivation, and the town hosts an annual strawberry festival.

Education

Panchgani is noted for its residential schools, established since the late nineteenth century for children of various communities and denominations; its schools attract students from across India and abroad, and education is a mainstay of the local economy along with tourism.
