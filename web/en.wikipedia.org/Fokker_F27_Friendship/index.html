Fokker F27 Friendship

The Fokker F27 Friendship is a twin-turboprop short-haul airliner designed and built by the Dutch aircraft manufacturer Fokker. First flown on 24 November 1955, it entered service in 1958 and became one of the most successful European airliners of its era, with 586 aircraft built, plus a further 207 of the licence-built Fairchild F-27 and FH-227 versions in the United States.

Design

The F27 is a high-wing monoplane powered by two Rolls-Royce Dart turboprop engines. It was among the first airliners to make extensive use of metal-to-metal bonding in its structure, adopted from wartime research. Depending on the variant, it seated between 40 and 56 passengers. The type was designed as a successor to the Douglas DC-3 on short regional routes.

Variants and service

Major production variants included the Mk 100/200 series and the stretched Mk 500 and Mk 600. The F27 served with airlines on every continent and in military roles including maritime patrol; the F27 Maritime and the stretched Fairchild FH-227 are notable derivatives. Fokker later developed the design into the Fokker 50. Many F27s remained in cargo and charter service into the 21st century.

Specifications (F27 Mk 500, typical)

Capacity: about 56 passengers; length: 25.06 m; wingspan: 29.0 m; maximum speed: about 518 km/h; range: roughly 1,900–2,600 km depending on variant and load.
