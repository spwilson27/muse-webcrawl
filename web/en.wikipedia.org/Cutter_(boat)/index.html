Cutter (boat)

A cutter is a type of small or medium-sized vessel with several historical and modern meanings. In sailing terms, a cutter is a single-masted boat with a fore-and-aft rig in which the mast is set well aft and the vessel carries two or more headsails. This distinguishes it from a sloop, which has a single headsail and a mast set further forward.

Historical uses

In the 18th and 19th centuries, cutters were fast naval and revenue vessels used by the Royal Navy and coastguard services to patrol for smugglers; the smugglers themselves also favoured the type for its speed. Pilot cutters ferried harbour pilots to incoming ships, and their seaworthiness in rough weather made them famous, notably the Bristol Channel pilot cutters.

The sailing rig

The cutter rig divides the fore-sail area between a staysail and a jib (and sometimes a flying jib), making each sail smaller and easier to handle than a sloop's single large headsail. Traditional cutters were gaff-rigged with a bowsprit. Modern cruising yachts often use a cutter rig with a removable inner forestay for heavy-weather sails.

Other meanings

The term is also used for a ship's boat carried aboard larger vessels, for the armed patrol vessels of the United States Coast Guard of any size, and for motor vessels such as the revenue cutters that preceded them.
