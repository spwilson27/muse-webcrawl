Trader Vic


Victor "Trader Vic" Bergeron

Victor Jules Bergeron Jr. (December 10, 1902 – October 11, 1984), better known as Trader Vic, was an American restaurateur and founder of the Trader Vic's chain of Polynesian-themed restaurants and tiki bars. Along with Donn Beach (Don the Beachcomber), he is credited with creating the tiki culture of the mid-20th century.

Bergeron opened a small bar in Oakland, California, in 1934 called Hinky Dinks. After a trip to the Caribbean and the South Pacific, he refashioned it in 1937 as Trader Vic's, decorated with bamboo, tikis and nautical artefacts, serving rum-based exotic drinks and a hybrid "island" cuisine cooked in Chinese wood-fired ovens. The chain expanded from the 1950s, often in partnership with Hilton hotels, to locations across the United States and abroad.

Bergeron is famed for creating the Mai Tai in 1944 — he maintained he mixed it in Oakland for friends from Tahiti, one of whom exclaimed "Mai Tai — roa ae!" ("out of this world — the best!"). His claim was long disputed by Donn Beach. Other drinks associated with Trader Vic's include the Fog Cutter and the Scorpion bowl. Bergeron wrote several books, including Trader Vic's Book of Food and Drink (1946) and the Bartender's Guide (1947).

Though most locations closed as tiki fashion faded, a small number of Trader Vic's restaurants still operate, and Bergeron is remembered as one of the most influential figures in American cocktail history.
