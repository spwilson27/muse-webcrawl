Robert Arthur Mould (born October 16, 1960) is an American musician, principally known for his work as guitarist, vocalist, and songwriter for the alternative rock bands Hüsker Dü in the 1980s and Sugar in the 1990s. His guitar style — dense, melodic walls of distortion — was a major influence on alternative rock and on bands such as the Pixies and Nirvana.

Hüsker Dü

Mould formed Hüsker Dü in St. Paul, Minnesota, in 1979 with drummer Grant Hart and bassist Greg Norton. The band evolved from hardcore punk to a melodic, intense sound on albums including Zen Arcade (1984), New Day Rising (1985), and Flip Your Wig (1985) for SST Records, before signing to Warner Bros. for Candy Apple Grey (1986) and Warehouse: Songs and Stories (1987). Internal conflict and Hart's drug problems led to the band's breakup in 1988.

Solo work and Sugar

Mould's solo debut Workbook (1989) was a largely acoustic, introspective departure; Black Sheets of Rain (1990) returned to a heavier sound. In 1992 he formed the power trio Sugar, whose debut Copper Blue (1992) — with songs such as "If I Can't Change Your Mind" and "Helpless" — became his biggest commercial success and was named album of the year by NME. Sugar also released the EP Beaster (1993) and File Under: Easy Listening (1994) before disbanding in 1996.

Later career

Mould has continued recording solo albums — among them Silver Age (2012), Beauty & Ruin (2014), Patch the Sky (2016), Sunshine Rock (2019), and Blue Hearts (2020) — touring with a power trio including Jason Narducy and Jon Wurster. He also worked as a script consultant for professional wrestling (WCW) around 1999–2000, has DJed electronic dance events ("Blowoff"), and published the autobiography See a Little Light: The Trail of Rage and Melody (2011), written with Michael Azerrad. Mould is openly gay and a prominent figure in LGBTQ rock music.
