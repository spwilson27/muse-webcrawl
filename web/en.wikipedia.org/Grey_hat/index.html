Grey hat

Grey hat
FieldComputer security
Related termsWhite hat, black hat, hacker

A grey hat (or gray hat) is a computer hacker or computer security expert who may sometimes violate laws or typical ethical standards, but usually does not have the malicious intent typical of a black hat hacker. The term attempts to describe hackers who fall between the ethical white hat hackers, who act to protect systems, and black hat hackers, who act maliciously.

Characteristics

Grey hat hackers may search for vulnerabilities in a system without the owner's permission, then disclose them — sometimes publicly, sometimes to the vendor, occasionally offering to fix the flaw for a fee. A common grey hat practice is searching for and publishing proof-of-concept exploits to pressure vendors into patching software. Critics argue that unauthorised probing is illegal in many jurisdictions regardless of intent, while supporters contend such research improves overall security.

Terminology

The hat terminology derives from old Western films, in which heroes wore white hats and villains black hats. "Grey hat" appeared in hacker culture in the late 1990s as the security industry professionalised and debates grew over full disclosure of vulnerabilities.

Examples and debate

Incidents frequently cited in grey hat debates include researchers scanning the internet for exposed systems, hackers disclosing flaws after vendors ignored reports, and hacktivist actions claimed to serve a public interest. The legality and ethics of such actions remain contested within law enforcement and the security community.
