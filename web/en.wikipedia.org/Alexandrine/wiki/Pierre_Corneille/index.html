Pierre Corneille

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Born: 6 June 1606, Rouen, France

Died: 1 October 1684 (aged 78), Paris, France

Occupation: Playwright

Movement: Classicism

Notable works: Le Cid, Horace, Cinna, Polyeucte

Pierre Corneille (6 June 1606 – 1 October 1684) was a French tragedian, generally considered one of the three great seventeenth-century French dramatists, along with Molière and Jean Racine. He has been called "the founder of French tragedy" and was famous for the exceptionally strong wills of his characters and the elevated, heroic tone of his verse.

Life

Corneille was born in Rouen, the eldest son of a lawyer, and was educated by the Jesuits before studying law himself. He began writing plays while working as a minor official in Rouen. His first comedy, Mélite (1629), was a success in Paris and launched his career. In 1635 he wrote his first tragedy, Médée. Cardinal Richelieu recruited him into a group of dramatists, but Corneille's independent spirit made the arrangement difficult.

Le Cid and the quarrel

His masterpiece Le Cid (1637), a tragicomedy based on a Spanish play about the hero Rodrigo Díaz de Vivar, was an enormous popular success but provoked the famous "Quarrel of the Cid": the newly founded Académie Française, at Richelieu's instigation, judged the play irregular for violating the classical unities. After a period of silence, Corneille returned with a series of Roman tragedies written in strict accordance with the rules: Horace (1640), Cinna (1641) and Polyeucte (1642).

Later work

Corneille's later plays, though admired for their rhetoric and moral grandeur, were gradually eclipsed by the rising star of Racine. His tragedy Pertharite (1651) failed, and he stopped writing for several years. He returned with Œdipe (1659) and continued writing until Suréna (1674). His dramas typically place strong-willed heroes and heroines in situations where duty, honour and love conflict, resolved through acts of magnanimity and self-mastery. He was elected to the Académie Française in 1647.

Corneille is also closely associated with the alexandrine, the twelve-syllable line that became the standard verse form of French classical tragedy.
