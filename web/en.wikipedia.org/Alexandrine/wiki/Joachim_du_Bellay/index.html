Joachim du Bellay

Joachim du Bellay (c. 1522 – 1 January 1560) was a French poet, critic and a founder, with Pierre de Ronsard, of the Pléiade group of Renaissance poets. His Défense et illustration de la langue française (1549) is the manifesto of French Renaissance poetry.

Bornc. 1522, Liré, Anjou, France
Died1 January 1560, Paris, France
MovementLa Pléiade
Notable worksLes Regrets, Les Antiquités de Rome, Défense et illustration de la langue française

Life and work

Born into a distinguished Angevin family, du Bellay studied at Poitiers and Paris, where he joined Ronsard and Jean-Antoine de Baïf. The Défense (1549) urged French writers to enrich their language by imitating classical and Italian models rather than writing in Latin, and appeared alongside his sonnet sequence L'Olive.

From 1553 to 1557 he served in Rome as secretary to his cousin, Cardinal Jean du Bellay. Disillusionment with the papal court produced his finest poetry: Les Antiquités de Rome and Les Regrets (both 1558), the latter containing the famous sonnet "Heureux qui, comme Ulysse, a fait un beau voyage."

Legacy

Du Bellay died in Paris in 1560, aged about 37. His sonnets on Roman ruins influenced Spenser and later poets, and his critical manifesto shaped the course of French literature.
