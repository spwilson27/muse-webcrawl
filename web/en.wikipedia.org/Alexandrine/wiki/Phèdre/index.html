Phèdre

Phèdre is a tragedy in five acts by the French playwright Jean Racine, first performed on 1 January 1677. Written in rhyming alexandrine couplets, it is widely considered Racine's masterpiece and one of the supreme achievements of French classical theatre.

AuthorJean Racine
Premiere1 January 1677, Paris
GenreTragedy
Verse formAlexandrines

Plot

Phèdre, wife of Theseus, is consumed by a forbidden passion for her stepson Hippolyte. Believing Theseus dead, she allows her love to be revealed; when Theseus unexpectedly returns, her nurse Oenone falsely accuses Hippolyte of attempting to seduce the queen. Theseus calls on Neptune to destroy his son, who is killed by a sea monster. Wracked with guilt, Phèdre poisons herself and confesses the truth as she dies.

Sources and themes

Racine drew on Euripides' Hippolytus and Seneca's Phaedra. The play explores passion as an inescapable, inherited curse — Phèdre is a descendant of the Sun god, and Venus's vengeance pursues her family — within the strict unities and decorum of French classicism.

Reception

A rival Phèdre et Hippolyte by Nicolas Pradon, backed by Racine's enemies, briefly overshadowed the play, but Racine's version prevailed. After Phèdre, Racine abandoned the secular stage for twelve years. The role of Phèdre became a summit for French tragediennes, notably Sarah Bernhardt.
