Decasyllable

Decasyllable (Italian: decasillabo; Serbian: десетерац, deseterac) is a poetic metre of ten syllables, used where syllabic verse is the norm. It is the metre of the Italian endecasillabo tradition's close cousin, of French classical epic verse before the alexandrine, and of South Slavic oral epic poetry.

Uses

In Serbian and Croatian epic folk poetry, the deseterac is a line of ten syllables with a caesura after the fourth syllable, sung to the accompaniment of the gusle; it carried the great cycles of heroic song, including the Kosovo cycle. In Polish literature, especially in Romantic poetry such as Mickiewicz's Pan Tadeusz (written in thirteen-syllable lines related to the tradition), syllabic metres of this family dominate.

In English, "decasyllabic" usually describes lines of ten syllables — notably the iambic pentameter of Chaucer, Shakespeare, and Milton, whose lines generally contain ten syllables arranged in five iambic feet. Geoffrey Chaucer is credited with introducing the decasyllabic line into English verse, adapting French and Italian models.
