Lighthouse of Alexandria

The Lighthouse of Alexandria, also known as the Pharos of Alexandria, was a lighthouse built by the Ptolemaic Kingdom of Egypt between 280 and 247 BC on the island of Pharos at the entrance to the harbour of Alexandria. Estimated at over 100 metres (330 feet) tall, it was among the tallest human-made structures of the ancient world and was counted among the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World.

Construction

Designed by the Greek architect Sostratus of Cnidus and begun under Ptolemy I, the tower was completed under Ptolemy II. It stood in three tapering stages — square, octagonal, and circular — topped by a mirror that reflected sunlight by day and a fire by night. The word "pharos" became the generic word for lighthouse in many languages, including French phare and Spanish faro.

Destruction

The lighthouse survived for more than a millennium, repeatedly damaged by earthquakes. It was badly shaken in 796 and 951, and finally ruined by major earthquakes in 1303 and 1323. In 1480 the Mamluk sultan Qaitbay built a citadel on the site, partly from its stones. Blocks of the fallen tower were found on the harbour floor by underwater archaeologists in the 1990s.
