Couplet

A couplet is a pair of successive lines of poetry, typically rhyming and of the same length. Couplets are found in many poetic traditions, including English, Chinese (duilian and regulated verse), Persian, Urdu (the sher of the ghazal) and Sanskrit poetry.

FormTwo successive lines
UsuallyRhymed, same meter
Famous formHeroic couplet (iambic pentameter)

Heroic couplet

The heroic couplet is a rhymed pair of iambic pentameter lines. Geoffrey Chaucer pioneered it in English; it became the dominant verse form of the 17th and 18th centuries in the hands of John Dryden and Alexander Pope, whose polished, closed couplets balance antithesis within the line and closure at the rhyme. A "closed" couplet completes its sense within the two lines, while an "open" couplet runs its meaning into the following lines.

Other traditions

In Chinese poetry, couplets (duìlián) are written in matching characters and displayed at doorways, especially at New Year. In the Persian and Urdu ghazal, each couplet (sher) is semantically independent within a unifying rhyme and refrain.

Use

The couplet's brevity and balance make it ideal for epigram, proverb and witty conclusion — Shakespeare often ends a sonnet or a scene with a rhyming couplet.
