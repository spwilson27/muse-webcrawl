Wars of Alexander the Great

The wars of Alexander the Great were a series of conquests carried out by Alexander III of Macedon between 336 and 323 BC. Beginning with the reassertion of Macedonian control over Greece and the Balkans, they culminated in the complete conquest of the Achaemenid Persian Empire and campaigns reaching northwestern India, creating one of the largest empires of the ancient world.

Persian campaign

Crossing into Asia in 334 BC, Alexander defeated Persian satraps at the Granicus River, then King Darius III at Issus in 333 BC. After the hard sieges of Tyre and Gaza he entered Egypt unopposed, founding Alexandria in 331 BC. At Gaugamela in 331 BC he destroyed Darius's main army; Babylon, Susa, Persepolis and Ecbatana fell in turn, and Darius was murdered by his own nobles in 330 BC.

Central Asia and India

From 330 to 327 BC Alexander campaigned through Bactria and Sogdiana, subduing guerrilla resistance and marrying the Bactrian princess Roxana. In 326 BC he crossed the Indus and defeated King Porus at the Hydaspes, but his exhausted army refused to march further at the Hyphasis River, forcing the return westward through Gedrosia.

Death and aftermath

Alexander died in Babylon in June 323 BC, aged 32, leaving no adult heir. His generals divided the empire, inaugurating the Hellenistic age.
