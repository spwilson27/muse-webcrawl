Phèdre is a tragedy in five acts by the French playwright Jean Racine, first performed in 1677 under the title Phèdre et Hippolyte. Written in rhyming alexandrine couplets, it is generally considered Racine's masterpiece and one of the supreme achievements of French classical tragedy. Its subject — Phaedra's forbidden passion for her stepson Hippolytus — derives from Euripides' Hippolytus and Seneca's Phaedra.

Plot

Phèdre, wife of Theseus, is consumed by a guilty love for her stepson Hippolyte, who himself loves Aricie. Believing Theseus dead, Phèdre declares her passion; when Theseus returns alive, her nurse Oenone accuses Hippolyte of attempted seduction to protect her mistress. Theseus calls on Neptune to destroy his son, who is killed by a sea monster. Consumed by remorse, Phèdre poisons herself and confesses the truth as she dies.

Reception

The premiere was sabotaged by a rival cabal backing a competing Phaedra play by Nicolas Pradon, and the discouraged Racine abandoned the secular stage for twelve years. Posterity reversed the verdict: Phèdre became the most performed of French classical tragedies, a touchstone for actresses from Rachel to Sarah Bernhardt, and the subject of famous translations, including Robert Lowell's English version.
