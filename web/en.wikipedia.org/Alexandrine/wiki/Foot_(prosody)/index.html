Foot (prosody)

In poetic meter, a foot is the basic repeated unit of rhythm, consisting of a fixed pattern of stressed and unstressed (or long and short) syllables. The number of feet in a line gives its measure name: monometer (one), dimeter (two), trimeter (three), tetrameter (four), pentameter (five), and so on.

Common feet

The iamb is the most common foot in English verse, underlying iambic pentameter. Trochees appear in song-like verse (as in Longfellow's Hiawatha, in trochaic tetrameter). Anapestic meter drives comic and narrative verse, while dactylic hexameter was the meter of classical Greek and Latin epic.

Scansion

Scansion is the analysis of a line into its feet. Poets routinely substitute one foot for another — a trochee for an iamb, for example — to vary the rhythm and emphasize meaning, so actual lines seldom consist of perfectly regular feet.
