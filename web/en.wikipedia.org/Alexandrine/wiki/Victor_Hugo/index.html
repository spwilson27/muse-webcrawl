Victor Hugo

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Born: 26 February 1802, Besançon, France

Died: 22 May 1885 (aged 83), Paris, France

Occupation: Poet, novelist, playwright

Movement: Romanticism

Notable works: Les Misérables, The Hunchback of Notre-Dame, Les Contemplations

Victor Marie Hugo (26 February 1802 – 22 May 1885) was a French Romantic writer and politician. During a literary career that spanned more than sixty years, he wrote in a variety of genres and forms, and he is considered to be one of the greatest writers of all time. His most famous works are the novels The Hunchback of Notre-Dame (1831) and Les Misérables (1862).

Early life and Romanticism

Hugo was born in Besançon, the son of a general in Napoleon's army. He won literary prizes as a teenager and published his first volume of poetry, Odes et poésies diverses, in 1822. His preface to the play Cromwell (1827) became a manifesto of French Romanticism, and the premiere of his verse drama Hernani (1830), written in alexandrines, provoked a famous battle between classicists and romantics in the audience. The Hunchback of Notre-Dame made him famous across Europe and helped spur the preservation of French Gothic architecture.

Politics and exile

Hugo entered politics, was elected to the Académie Française in 1841, and after the Revolution of 1848 sat in the National Assembly. When Louis-Napoléon Bonaparte seized power in the coup of 1851, Hugo denounced him and fled into exile, first to Brussels and then to the Channel Islands — Jersey and later Guernsey — where he lived for nearly two decades. In exile he wrote some of his greatest work, including the poetry collection Les Châtiments (1853), Les Contemplations (1856), and the vast social novel Les Misérables, a sweeping portrayal of injustice and redemption in nineteenth-century France.

Return and death

Hugo returned to Paris after the fall of the Second Empire in 1870 and was hailed as a national hero. He continued to write and to campaign against the death penalty and for social justice. When he died in 1885, more than two million people joined his funeral procession; he was interred in the Panthéon.
