Pierre de Ronsard

Pierre de Ronsard (11 September 1524 – 27 December 1585) was a French poet, leader of the Pléiade group and the dominant French poet of the Renaissance, known in his lifetime as the "prince of poets."

Born11 September 1524, Couture-sur-Loir, France
Died27 December 1585, Saint-Cosme, France
MovementLa Pléiade
Notable worksOdes (1550), Les Amours, Sonnets pour Hélène (1578)

Life

Ronsard served as a page at the courts of France and Scotland before deafness ended his diplomatic prospects and turned him to scholarship and poetry. With Joachim du Bellay and others he formed the circle first called the Brigade, then the Pléiade, which set out to raise French to the level of Latin and Greek as a literary language.

Poetry

His Odes (1550) imitated Pindar and Horace; the collections of Les Amours addressed sonnets to Cassandre, Marie and Hélène. The Sonnets pour Hélène (1578) include "Quand vous serez bien vieille," among the most celebrated lyric poems in French. He also attempted an epic, La Franciade (1572), left unfinished, and wrote political verse during the Wars of Religion.

Legacy

Though eclipsed by the classicism of the 17th century, Ronsard was restored to eminence by the Romantics and remains a central figure of French poetry. The rose cultivar 'Pierre de Ronsard' is named for him.
