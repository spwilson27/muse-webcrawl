Jah Wobble (born John Joseph Wardle, 11 August 1958) is an English bass guitarist and singer. He became known as the original bass player in the post-punk band Public Image Ltd (PiL) in the late 1970s and early 1980s, and since leaving the band has developed a long solo career drawing on dub, world music and spoken word.

Early life and PiL

Wardle grew up in Stepney, East London, and was part of the circle of friends around John Lydon (Johnny Rotten) at Kingsway College. His nickname "Jah Wobble" was reportedly coined by Sid Vicious. When Lydon formed Public Image Ltd in 1978 with guitarist Keith Levene, Wobble joined on bass despite limited experience. His deep, dub-influenced bass lines, inspired by reggae players such as Robbie Shakespeare and Aston "Family Man" Barrett, became central to the band's sound on the albums First Issue (1978) and Metal Box (1979). Wobble left PiL in 1980 after disagreements over the use of material from his solo recordings.

Solo career

Wobble's early solo work included collaborations with Holger Czukay and Jaki Liebezeit of Can, notably the single "How Much Are They?" (1981). In the 1990s he formed Jah Wobble's Invaders of the Heart, whose album Take Me to God (1994) was a commercial success and featured guest vocalists including Sinéad O'Connor. His 1995 album Heaven and Earth continued his fusion of dub bass with global instrumentation.

Later work has included collaborations with Brian Eno (Spinner, 1995), the album Chinese Dub (2008) with Chinese musicians, which won a Songlines world music award, and the 2016 reunion album The Usual Suspects with Keith Levene. Wobble has also recorded a series of dub and ambient albums and published a memoir, Memoirs of a Geezer (2009).

Style

Wobble is noted for low, melodic, drone-like bass lines played with space and restraint, a style that has influenced post-punk, dub and electronic music. He typically plays a Fender Precision Bass or Ovation Magnum.
