Capital gains tax

Capital gains tax
TypeTax on profits from asset sales
Tax baseSale price minus cost basis
RelatedIncome tax, corporate tax

A capital gains tax (CGT) is a tax levied on the profit realised on the sale of a non-inventory asset, such as stocks, bonds, precious metals, real estate, and other property. The gain is the difference between the sale price and the original purchase price (the cost basis), and the tax is generally due only when the asset is sold, not while it is held.

Features

Many jurisdictions distinguish short-term from long-term gains, taxing long-held assets at lower rates. Capital losses can usually be offset against gains. Some countries, including New Zealand for most assets historically, have no general capital gains tax, while others exempt a taxpayer's main home.

Debate

Supporters argue CGT improves fairness by taxing investment income similarly to wages. Critics argue it discourages saving and investment, locks investors into existing holdings (the "lock-in effect"), and can amount to double taxation of corporate profits.
