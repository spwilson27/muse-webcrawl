Oil platform

An oil platform, or offshore platform, is a large structure with facilities for drilling wells and extracting and processing oil and natural gas from beneath the seabed. Platforms house drilling rigs, production equipment, and crew accommodation, and may be fixed to the seabed, floating, or compliant towers depending on water depth. The largest platforms rank among the tallest human-made structures ever moved.

Types

Fixed platforms stand on steel or concrete legs anchored to the seabed in waters up to several hundred metres deep. Jack-up rigs rest on movable legs that can be raised for towing. In deep water, semisubmersible platforms, tension-leg platforms, spars, and floating production storage and offloading (FPSO) vessels are used. The Troll A platform in Norway, a concrete gravity-based structure, is one of the heaviest objects ever transported.

Operations and safety

Crews rotate offshore for shifts lasting weeks, reaching the platform by helicopter or boat. Disasters such as the Piper Alpha explosion in 1988, which killed 167 workers, and the Deepwater Horizon blowout in 2010 led to sweeping changes in safety regulation, well-control standards, and environmental liability for offshore operations.
