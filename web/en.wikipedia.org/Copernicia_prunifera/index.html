Copernicia prunifera

Copernicia prunifera, the carnauba palm (Portuguese: carnaúba), is a species of palm tree native to northeastern Brazil, principally the states of Piauí, Ceará, Maranhão, Bahia, and Rio Grande do Norte. It is the source of carnauba wax, a hard natural wax harvested from the coating of its leaves, and is sometimes called the "tree of life" locally because of its many uses.

Description

The palm grows to 7–15 metres (23–49 ft) tall, with a straight trunk and a crown of fan-shaped (palmate) leaves. It grows in seasonally flooded plains and along riverbanks in the semi-arid caatinga region, tolerating long dry seasons: the waxy coating on its leaves reduces water loss, an adaptation to the hot, dry climate. The tree produces small black fruits eaten by livestock and wildlife.

Uses

Carnauba wax, scraped from the dried leaves as a powder and then refined, is one of the hardest natural waxes, with a high melting point. It is used in car and floor waxes, polishes, cosmetics, food coatings (as glazing agent E903), and as a coating for tablets and dental floss. The wood is used in construction, the leaves for thatch, baskets and hats, and the roots have traditional medicinal uses. Wax production is an important seasonal source of income in the Brazilian northeast, particularly in Piauí and Ceará.
