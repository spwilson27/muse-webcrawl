Kama

Kama (Sanskrit: काम, "desire, longing, pleasure") is one of the four aims of human life (purusharthas) in Hindu philosophy, alongside dharma (duty), artha (prosperity), and moksha (liberation). Kama denotes aesthetic and emotional enjoyment, love, desire, and pleasure of the senses, pursued within the bounds set by dharma.

Meaning and scope

In classical texts, kama is broader than sexual desire: it embraces enjoyment of the arts, music, beauty, affection, and companionship. The Kama Sutra of Vatsyayana, the best-known treatise on the subject, treats kama as a legitimate and cultivated sphere of life for householders, covering courtship, marriage, social graces, and the arts as well as erotic love.

Kamadeva

Desire is personified as the god Kamadeva, who wields a bow of sugarcane strung with bees and arrows tipped with flowers. In a famous myth, Kamadeva is burned to ashes by Shiva's third eye after disturbing the god's meditation, and is later restored, in some tellings only as a disembodied presence — hence his epithet Ananga, "the bodiless".

Place in the purusharthas

Hindu thought generally holds that kama should neither be suppressed unnaturally nor indulged without restraint; its pursuit is proper when regulated by dharma and balanced against artha and the final goal of moksha.
