OO gauge

OO gauge or OO scale (also spelled 00) is the most popular standard gauge model railway standard in the United Kingdom, outside of which it is virtually unknown. The scale is 4 mm to the foot (1:76) with a track gauge of 16.5 mm, the same track as the internationally popular HO scale (1:87).

Scale4 mm to 1 ft (1:76)
Track gauge16.5 mm
Prototype gaugeStandard gauge (nominally)

History

OO gauge emerged in the 1920s, notably with Hornby Dublo in 1938. British loading-gauge constraints and the small motors of the era made a true 4 mm scale on scale-width track impractical, so manufacturers adopted the narrower HO track gauge while retaining the larger British body scale. The compromise persists because of the huge installed base of models and layouts.

Standards

Finer-scale alternatives using the same 4 mm scale include EM gauge (18.2 mm) and P4 (18.83 mm), which model the prototype gauge more accurately. Major British manufacturers of OO models include Hornby, Bachmann Branchline, Dapol, and Heljan.
