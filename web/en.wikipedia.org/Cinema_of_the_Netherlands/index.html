Cinema of the Netherlands

The cinema of the Netherlands dates from the first film screenings in 1896. Although the domestic market is small and dominated by imported—mainly American—films, Dutch cinema has produced internationally recognized directors and several Academy Award-winning films.

History

Early Dutch filmmaking included the work of the Hollandia studio in Haarlem. The Dutch documentary school flourished after the Second World War with directors such as Bert Haanstra, whose Glass (1958) won an Academy Award. In the 1970s Paul Verhoeven and actor Rutger Hauer achieved popular success with films like Turkish Delight (1973), the most successful Dutch film of all time.

International recognition

Dutch films have won the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film three times: The Assault (1986), Antonia's Line (1995), and Character (1997). The Netherlands Film Festival, held annually in Utrecht, awards the Golden Calf prizes. Amsterdam hosts the International Documentary Film Festival Amsterdam (IDFA), one of the world's largest documentary festivals.
