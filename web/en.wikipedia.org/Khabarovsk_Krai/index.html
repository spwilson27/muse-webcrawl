Khabarovsk Krai (Russian: Хабаровский край) is a federal subject of Russia (a krai) in the Russian Far East. Its administrative centre is the city of Khabarovsk, located at the confluence of the Amur and Ussuri rivers near the Chinese border. The krai is one of Russia's largest federal subjects by area but is sparsely populated.

Geography

The territory stretches along the western shore of the Sea of Okhotsk and includes the lower Amur valley, the Sikhote-Alin mountain range in the south-east, and extensive taiga forest. Its climate is continental, with cold winters influenced by Siberian air masses and warm, monsoon-influenced summers in the south. The Shantar Islands lie offshore in the Sea of Okhotsk.

History

The region was historically home to Tungusic peoples including the Nanai. Russia acquired the left bank of the Amur from Qing China under the Treaty of Aigun (1858) and the Convention of Peking (1860). Khabarovsk was founded as a military post in 1858 and named for the 17th-century explorer Yerofey Khabarov. The krai in its modern form was created in 1938.

Economy

Major industries include timber and fishing, shipbuilding at Komsomolsk-on-Amur, aircraft manufacture (Sukhoi jets are built at Komsomolsk), oil refining, and mining of gold and tin. The Trans-Siberian Railway and the Baikal–Amur Mainline cross the territory.
