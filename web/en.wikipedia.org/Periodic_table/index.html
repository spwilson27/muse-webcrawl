Periodic table

The periodic table, also known as the periodic table of the elements, is an arrangement of chemical elements into rows (periods) and columns (groups), ordered by atomic number, electron configuration and recurring chemical properties.

Periodic table

Elements: 118 confirmed
Devised: Dmitri Mendeleev, 1869

Structure

Seven periods and 18 groups; metals on the left, nonmetals on the right, with s, p, d and f blocks. Trends include atomic radius, ionisation energy and electronegativity. The table's ordering by atomic number was explained by Henry Moseley's work and Moseley's law.

History

Mendeleev (1869) predicted undiscovered elements; Lothar Meyer developed a similar table independently. Noble gases discovered by William Ramsay formed a new group. Synthetic elements completed period 7 in the 2010s.
