José Daniel Ortega Saavedra (born 11 November 1945) is a Nicaraguan politician who has served as President of Nicaragua since 2007, having previously held the office from 1985 to 1990. A leader of the Sandinista National Liberation Front (FSLN), he helped overthrow the Somoza dictatorship in 1979 and led the revolutionary government during the Contra War against US-backed rebels.

Early life and guerrilla activity

Ortega was born in La Libertad, Chontales, and joined the FSLN as a young man. He participated in bank robberies and urban guerrilla operations against the regime of Anastasio Somoza Debayle, was imprisoned for seven years, and after his release in a 1974 prisoner exchange trained in Cuba before returning to fight in the insurrection that triumphed in July 1979.

First presidency

Ortega became coordinator of the ruling Junta of National Reconstruction and won the 1984 presidential election. His government pursued literacy and health campaigns while fighting the Contras in a civil war marked by a US trade embargo and the Iran–Contra affair. He lost the 1990 election to Violeta Chamorro and accepted the result, a rare peaceful transfer of power.

Return to power

After losing elections in 1996 and 2001, Ortega won the 2006 election with about 38 percent of the vote and returned to the presidency in January 2007. He was re-elected in 2011, 2016, and 2021; constitutional changes and court rulings removed term limits, and his wife Rosario Murillo became vice president in 2017. His later governments have been widely criticised internationally for the violent suppression of the 2018 protests, the imprisonment of opposition candidates and critics, and the consolidation of authoritarian rule, while supporters credit his administrations with infrastructure investment and poverty reduction in his earlier terms.
