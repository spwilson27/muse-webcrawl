Darwin, Northern Territory

Country: Australia
Territory: Northern Territory
Population: ~140,000 (metropolitan, approx.)
Established: 1869 (as Palmerston)

Darwin is the capital city of the Northern Territory, Australia. Situated on the Timor Sea on the Larrakia people's traditional Country, it is the smallest and most northerly of Australia's capital cities, and acts as a regional centre for the Top End. Its population is the largest in the Northern Territory but small by national standards, giving it a distinctive frontier character.

History

The harbour was named Darwin Harbour by John Lort Stokes in 1839 after his former shipmate Charles Darwin. The town was founded as Palmerston in 1869 and renamed Darwin in 1911. It was bombed by Japanese aircraft on 19 February 1942, the largest single attack ever mounted on Australia. On 25 December 1974 Cyclone Tracy devastated the city, killing 71 people and destroying most buildings; Darwin was rebuilt to tougher cyclone codes.

Geography and climate

Darwin has a tropical savanna climate with distinct wet (November–April) and dry (May–October) seasons, spectacular wet-season lightning storms, and average temperatures around 30 °C year-round. The city faces Asia across the Timor Sea and maintains close ties with Southeast Asia; it hosts major Australian Defence Force bases and a growing liquefied natural gas industry.
