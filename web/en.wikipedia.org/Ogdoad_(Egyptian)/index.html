Ogdoad (Egyptian)

In Egyptian mythology, the Ogdoad (Ancient Greek: ὀγδοάς, "the Eightfold") were eight primordial deities worshipped in Hermopolis. They represented the primordial, chaotic state of the universe before creation and were arranged in four male-female pairs.

The eight deities

  • Nu and Naunet — the primordial waters and their counterpart, the hidden sky or "counter-sky".
  • Heh and Hauhet — infinity or eternity ("millions of years") and its feminine counterpart.
  • Kek and Kauket — darkness and obscurity.
  • Amun and Amaunet — hiddenness and the unseen.

The male deities were often depicted with frog heads, and the females with serpent heads. Collectively, they embodied the conditions of the primeval world: water, darkness, formlessness, and hiddenness.

Cosmogony

In the Hermopolitan creation myth, the interaction of the Ogdoad in the primordial waters produced the primeval mound (or, in some versions, an egg or a lotus) from which the sun god — variously Ra, Atum, or Thoth-associated forms — emerged, bringing light and order. Hermopolis, in Egyptian Khemenu ("Eight-Town"), took its name from the eight gods.

Relation to other theologies

The Ogdoad was one of several Egyptian creation systems, alongside the Ennead of Heliopolis and the Memphite theology centred on Ptah. In later periods, the Ogdoad were sometimes associated with the Ogdoad of Gnostic thought, though the two systems are distinct.
