The Battle of Tsushima was a major naval battle of the Russo-Japanese War, fought on 27–28 May 1905 in the Tsushima Strait between Korea and southern Japan. The Imperial Japanese Navy under Admiral Tōgō Heihachirō destroyed the Russian Baltic Fleet, commanded by Admiral Zinovy Rozhestvensky, which had sailed some 18,000 nautical miles from the Baltic to relieve the besieged Russian squadron at Port Arthur.

Background

After Japan's early victories in 1904, Russia dispatched its Second and Third Pacific Squadrons on an epic voyage around Africa. The fleet finally reached East Asian waters in May 1905, short of supplies and with exhausted crews, and attempted to slip through to Vladivostok. Japanese scouts located it in the Tsushima Strait.

The battle

Tōgō executed a daring turn across the Russian fleet's bow, "crossing the T", allowing his ships to concentrate their fire. Japanese gunnery, aided by high-explosive shells with sensitive fuses and superior speed and training, devastated the Russian battle line. By nightfall most of the Russian fleet had been sunk, captured, or scattered; night torpedo attacks and the following day's pursuit completed the destruction. Russia lost 21 ships sunk and more than 4,000 sailors killed, while Japan lost only three torpedo boats and 117 men.

Aftermath

Tsushima shocked the world: a non-European power had annihilated the fleet of a European great power. The defeat helped push Russia to negotiate the Treaty of Portsmouth, ending the war, and confirmed Japan's emergence as a major naval power.
