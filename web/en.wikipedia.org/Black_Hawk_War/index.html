The Black Hawk War was a brief conflict between the United States and Native Americans led by Black Hawk, a Sauk leader, in 1832. Black Hawk's band of Sauk, Meskwaki (Fox) and Kickapoo — about a thousand people including women, children and elders — crossed the Mississippi River into Illinois in April 1832 to reclaim their ancestral village of Saukenuk, whose cession under the disputed Treaty of St. Louis (1804) they rejected.

Course of the war

Illinois mobilised militia, and federal troops under General Henry Atkinson joined the pursuit. An early clash at Stillman's Run (14 May 1832), where undisciplined militia fled from a small Sauk party, panicked settlers across the frontier. There followed raids and skirmishes, including the Battle of Wisconsin Heights in July, as Black Hawk's starving band retreated northward. The war ended at the Battle of Bad Axe on 1–2 August 1832, where hundreds of Native people, many of them non-combatants, were killed as they tried to cross the Mississippi.

Aftermath

Black Hawk was captured and taken east, where he dictated his autobiography, the first Native American autobiography published in the United States. The war completed the removal of Native nations from the Old Northwest east of the Mississippi and accelerated white settlement of Illinois and Wisconsin. Among the militia officers who served were Abraham Lincoln, Zachary Taylor and Jefferson Davis, all future national leaders.
