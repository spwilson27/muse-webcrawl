Freight transport

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Freight transport, also known as shipping or cargo transport, is the physical process of transporting commodities and merchandise goods and cargo. Freight is moved by many modes of transport, including ship, rail, road, and air, often in combination through intermodal transport using standardized shipping containers.

Modes

Maritime shipping carries the great majority of world trade by volume, including bulk commodities such as oil, grain, and iron ore in bulk carriers and tankers, and manufactured goods in container ships. Rail freight moves heavy and bulk goods efficiently over land, often in unit trains dedicated to a single commodity. Road freight by truck provides flexible door-to-door service and dominates short-haul and regional distribution. Air freight carries a small share by weight but a large share by value, specializing in urgent, perishable, and high-value goods. Pipelines transport liquids and gases continuously.

Intermodal freight

The adoption of the standardized ISO shipping container in the 1950s and 1960s, pioneered commercially by Malcom McLean, revolutionized freight by allowing cargo to move between ships, trains, and trucks without being unloaded and repacked. Intermodal terminals, container ports, and double-stack rail services sharply reduced handling costs and transit times and enabled modern global supply chains.

Logistics and regulation

Freight transport is coordinated by freight forwarders, carriers, and logistics providers under contracts documented by instruments such as the bill of lading. International shipments are governed by conventions covering liability and customs, and freight activity is a major source of greenhouse gas emissions, prompting regulation and investment in more efficient vehicles and fuels.
