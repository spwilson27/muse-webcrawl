The Samoyed is a breed of medium-sized herding and sled dog originating with the Samoyedic peoples — particularly the Nenets — of Siberia. Famous for its thick white double coat and the upturned mouth corners that give it a perpetual "Samoyed smile", it is one of the ancient spitz breeds used for herding reindeer, hauling sledges and guarding camps.

History

Nenets herders bred these dogs to work and live closely with families, even sleeping in tents as a source of warmth — a history reflected in the breed's sociable temperament. Explorers brought Samoyeds to the West in the late 19th century; the dogs served on polar expeditions, including those of Fridtjof Nansen and Roald Amundsen, whose lead dog Etah reached the South Pole region in 1911. The breed was recognised in England and America in the early 1900s.

Characteristics

Samoyeds have a dense, weather-resistant double coat that sheds heavily, almond-shaped dark eyes, erect triangular ears and a tail curled over the back. They are intelligent, vocal and famously friendly — poor guard dogs but devoted companions. They need substantial exercise and mental stimulation, and can be stubborn in training.

Health

Generally hardy, the breed is prone to hip dysplasia, diabetes, progressive retinal atrophy and a hereditary kidney disease (Samoyed hereditary glomerulopathy). The undercoat wool can be spun into yarn, traditionally used for garments.
