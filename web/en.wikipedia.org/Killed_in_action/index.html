Killed in action (KIA) is a casualty classification generally used by militaries to describe the deaths of their own combatants at the hands of hostile forces. A service member is classed as killed in action when they are killed during an engagement with the enemy, whether they die immediately or of wounds received in the action before reaching a medical treatment facility.

Definition

The United States Department of Defense defines killed in action as a casualty who has incurred "fatal wounds in the line of duty" as the direct result of hostile action, distinguishing KIA from deaths due to accident, illness, or other non-hostile causes ("died of wounds" (DOW) is sometimes used separately for those who reach medical care but later die). NATO and many other armed forces use comparable casualty terminology.

Related classifications

  • Wounded in action (WIA) – injured by hostile action but survives.
  • Missing in action (MIA) – whereabouts unknown after an engagement.
  • Prisoner of war (POW) – captured alive by the enemy.
  • Died of wounds (DOW) – reaches treatment but subsequently dies of injuries received in action.

KIA rates are used in military history and analysis as a key measure of a battle's or campaign's cost, and the phrase appears on casualty lists, headstones, and memorials commemorating fallen soldiers.
