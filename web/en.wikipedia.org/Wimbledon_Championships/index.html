Wimbledon Championships

The Championships, Wimbledon
Founded: 1877
Venue: All England Club, Wimbledon, London
Surface: Grass (outdoor)
Timing: Late June – mid July

The Wimbledon Championships, commonly called Wimbledon, is the oldest tennis tournament in the world and is widely regarded as the most prestigious. It has been held at the All England Club in Wimbledon, London, since 1877 and is played on outdoor grass courts, traditionally over two weeks in late June and early July. It is one of the four Grand Slam tournaments, alongside the Australian Open, the French Open, and the US Open.

History

The first Championships, in 1877, featured a single event, the Gentlemen's Singles, won by Spencer Gore before a crowd of about 200. Ladies' Singles began in 1884, the year the tournament moved decisively to its present site in Church Road (1922). Wimbledon became the dominant global championship of the amateur era, embraced professionalism in 1968, and added a retractable roof over Centre Court in 2009 and over No. 1 Court in 2019. The tournament was cancelled in 2020 because of the COVID-19 pandemic — the first cancellation since the Second World War.

Traditions

Wimbledon is known for its traditions: a strict dress code requiring predominantly white clothing, strawberries and cream, Royal patronage, the absence of sponsor advertising around the courts, and the famous queue for tickets. Champions receive trophies rather than retaining them; the Gentlemen's Singles winner receives a gilt cup and the Ladies' Singles winner the Venus Rosewater Dish. Prize money for men and women was equalised in 2007.

Records

Roger Federer holds the record for Gentlemen's Singles titles with eight; Martina Navratilova won the Ladies' Singles a record nine times. The longest match in tennis history — John Isner versus Nicolas Mahut in 2010, lasting 11 hours 5 minutes over three days, with a final set of 70–68 — was played at Wimbledon and prompted the eventual introduction of final-set tiebreaks.
