Valery Fyodorovich Bykovsky (1934–2019) was a Soviet cosmonaut who flew on three space missions, including the longest solo spaceflight of its era.

Career

Selected in the first cosmonaut group in 1960, Bykovsky flew Vostok 5 in June 1963, spending nearly five days in orbit — still the longest solo spaceflight ever made — in a joint mission with Valentina Tereshkova aboard Vostok 6. He later commanded Soyuz 22 (1976), an Earth-observation mission using a backup Apollo–Soyuz spacecraft, and Soyuz 31 (1978) to the Salyut 6 station, flying with East German cosmonaut Sigmund Jähn.

Later life

Bykovsky totalled about 20 days in space, served in training and administrative roles, and died in 2019 at the age of 84.
