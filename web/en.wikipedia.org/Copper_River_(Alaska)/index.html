Copper River (Alaska)

The Copper River (Ahtna: 'Atna' tuu) is a 290-mile (470 km) river in south-central Alaska in the United States. It drains a large rural region of the Wrangell, Chugach, and Alaska mountain ranges, and flows into the Gulf of Alaska near Cordova. It is the tenth longest river in the United States and is known for its highly prized salmon runs and its vast, braided delta, one of the largest intact wetland complexes on North America's Pacific coast.

Course

The river rises from the Copper Glacier on Mount Wrangell in the Wrangell–St. Elias National Park, flows generally south through a broad valley, receiving tributaries including the Chitina, Tazlina and Klutina rivers, and empties into Copper Bay of the Gulf of Alaska east of Cordova. The Richardson Highway follows part of its upper course; the Million Dollar Bridge on the former Copper River and Northwestern Railway crosses near its mouth.

Ecology and fisheries

The Copper River supports major runs of sockeye, Chinook (king) and coho salmon. "Copper River salmon" command premium prices in seafood markets because of their high fat content, accumulated for the long migration upstream. The river also supports a traditional dipnet and fishwheel subsistence fishery at Chitina. Its delta, some 700,000 acres of wetlands, provides critical habitat for migratory shorebirds and waterfowl and is a major stopover on the Pacific Flyway, celebrated annually at the Copper River Delta Shorebird Festival in Cordova.

History

The river's name comes from the rich copper deposits worked by the Indigenous Ahtna people of its upper valley; the Kennecott copper mines, developed in the early 20th century, were served by the Copper River and Northwestern Railway, completed in 1911.
