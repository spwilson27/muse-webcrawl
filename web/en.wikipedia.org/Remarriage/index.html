Remarriage

Remarriage is a marriage that takes place after a previous marital union has ended, usually through divorce or widowhood. Rates of remarriage vary widely by country, age, gender, and culture.

Patterns

In many Western countries, a substantial share of all marriages involve at least one previously married partner. Remarriage tends to be more common among men than women and declines with age. People who remarry often do so within a few years of a divorce, and second marriages statistically face somewhat higher dissolution rates than first marriages, though outcomes vary greatly.

Family and legal aspects

Remarriage commonly creates blended or stepfamilies, raising questions about step-parenting, inheritance, custody, and ongoing obligations such as alimony and child support. Religious traditions differ in their treatment of remarriage after divorce, from prohibition to full acceptance.
