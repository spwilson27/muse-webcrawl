Statutory rape

Statutory rape is a legal term for sexual activity in which one of the individuals is below the age required to legally consent to the conduct. Although it usually refers to adults engaging in sexual contact with minors under the age of consent, the term is a generic legal concept. Because a person below the age of consent is considered legally incapable of consenting, the act may be an offence even if it was factually consensual.

Legal framework

The age of consent varies by jurisdiction, generally ranging between 14 and 18 years. Many jurisdictions structure offences by the age difference between the parties and provide close-in-age exemptions (sometimes called "Romeo and Juliet" laws) for sexual activity between adolescents of similar age. In jurisdictions such as the United States, statutory offences are often classified as strict liability crimes, meaning the prosecution need not prove the defendant knew the other person's age, though some jurisdictions permit a reasonable-mistake-of-age defence.

Terminology and variation

The term "statutory rape" is used mainly in common-law jurisdictions such as the United States; other countries criminalize the same conduct under names such as sexual activity with a child, unlawful sexual intercourse, or rape of a child. Modern statutes are typically gender-neutral, whereas historical versions often applied only to female victims.
