Professional Engineer

Professional Engineer (PE) is a designation used in the United States and other countries for an engineer who is licensed to practise engineering under a regulatory framework. In the U.S., licensure is administered by state boards and typically requires an accredited degree, passing two examinations, and several years of qualifying experience under the supervision of a licensed engineer.

Licensure process in the United States

The standard path to licensure involves four steps: (1) earning a bachelor's degree in engineering from an ABET-accredited program; (2) passing the Fundamentals of Engineering (FE) examination, after which the candidate is known as an Engineer in Training (EIT) or Engineer Intern (EI); (3) gaining typically four years of progressive engineering experience under a licensed PE; and (4) passing the Principles and Practice of Engineering (PE) examination in a specific discipline such as civil, mechanical, electrical, or structural engineering. Both examinations are administered by the National Council of Examiners for Engineering and Surveying (NCEES).

Scope of practice

Licensed professional engineers may seal and sign engineering plans and drawings, offer engineering services directly to the public, and take legal responsibility for work affecting public health, safety, and welfare. In many U.S. states, engineering work for public infrastructure, buildings, and certain industrial facilities must be performed or supervised by a PE. Engineers working in some industries, such as certain manufacturing sectors, may fall under an "industrial exemption" and not require licensure.

Other countries

In Canada, the equivalent designation is Professional Engineer (P.Eng), regulated by provincial and territorial associations. In the United Kingdom, the analogous titles are Chartered Engineer (CEng) and Incorporated Engineer (IEng), administered by the Engineering Council. Similar regulated titles exist in Australia (CPEng), South Africa (Pr Eng), and many other jurisdictions.
