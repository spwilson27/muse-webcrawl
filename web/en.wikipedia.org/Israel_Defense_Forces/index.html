Israel Defense Forces

The IDF is the combined military of the State of Israel.

The Israel Defense Forces (IDF; Hebrew: צְבָא הַהֲגָנָה לְיִשְׂרָאֵל, Tzva HaHagana LeYisra'el), alternatively referred to by the Hebrew-language acronym Tzahal (צה״ל), are the national military of the State of Israel. Established on 26 May 1948 by order of David Ben-Gurion during the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, the IDF succeeded the Haganah and absorbed other Jewish paramilitary groups.

Organisation

The IDF consists of the Ground Forces, the Air Force and the Navy, under a unified General Staff headed by the Chief of the General Staff (Ramatkal). Unlike most militaries, the service branches operate as subordinate arms of a single service. Conscription applies to most Jewish, Druze and Circassian citizens at age 18 — men serve about 32 months and women about 24 — followed by reserve duty. Active personnel number roughly 170,000, with a large trained reserve.

History

The IDF has fought in the 1948 war, the Suez Crisis (1956), the Six-Day War (1967), the War of Attrition, the Yom Kippur War (1973), the Lebanon wars of 1982 and 2006, and repeated conflicts in Gaza. It is known for advanced domestic weapons development, including the Merkava tank, Iron Dome air defence system, and a large high-technology and intelligence sector, notably Unit 8200.
