The TranzAlpine is a passenger train operated by KiwiRail on New Zealand's South Island, running between Christchurch and Greymouth via the Midland Line. The journey crosses the Canterbury Plains, climbs through the gorges and viaducts of the Waimakariri River, passes through Arthur's Pass and the Otira Tunnel beneath the Southern Alps, and descends through the rainforest of the West Coast. It is widely regarded as one of the world's great scenic rail journeys.

Route

Trains leave Christchurch and travel across the flat Canterbury Plains to Springfield, where the line begins climbing into the foothills. In the upper Waimakariri Gorge the line crosses a series of viaducts, including the Staircase Viaduct, and passes through short tunnels before reaching Arthur's Pass station, at 737 metres the highest station on the route. From there the train descends through the 8.5-kilometre Otira Tunnel (opened 1923, with a gradient of 1 in 33 that requires the train to be hauled by additional locomotives on the westbound journey) to Otira, then follows the Taramakau and Grey valleys through Moana, on the shore of Lake Brunner, to Greymouth on the Tasman Sea coast.

History and service

The TranzAlpine was introduced on 22 November 1987 by the New Zealand Railways Corporation as a tourist-oriented successor to earlier Christchurch–Greymouth express services, becoming an immediate success and helping to establish rail tourism in New Zealand. The service runs once daily in each direction, with a stop of about an hour in Greymouth for the returning service. Carriages feature large panoramic windows, an open-air viewing carriage, and a café car; in 2011 new purpose-built panoramic carriages built at Hillside Workshops entered service. GPS-triggered commentary is provided in several languages.

The service was suspended for periods after the 2010 and 2011 Canterbury earthquakes and during the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020–2021, when it was briefly restructured as a shorter return excursion. Patronage has historically been dominated by international tourists, and the train forms part of KiwiRail's Great Journeys New Zealand brand, alongside the Northern Explorer and Coastal Pacific services.
