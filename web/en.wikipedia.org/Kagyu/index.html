Kagyu

Founder: Gampopa (lineage from Marpa Lotsawa)
Region: Tibet, Himalayas, worldwide
Key practices: Mahamudra, Six Yogas of Naropa

The Kagyu school (Tibetan: བཀའ་བརྒྱུད, bka' brgyud, "oral lineage" or "whispered transmission") is one of the four major schools of Tibetan Buddhism, alongside Nyingma, Sakya, and Gelug. The Kagyu lineages trace themselves to the Indian mahasiddhas Tilopa and Naropa, transmitted through the Tibetan translator Marpa Lotsawa (1012–1097), his disciple Milarepa, and Milarepa's disciple Gampopa.

Teachings

The Kagyu schools are renowned for their emphasis on meditative practice, particularly Mahamudra — direct insight into the nature of mind — and the Six Yogas of Naropa, including tummo (inner heat), dream yoga, and phowa (transference of consciousness). The name Kagyu derives from the "four special transmissions" (bka' babs bzhi) and the school's reliance on an unbroken oral lineage from teacher to student.

Sub-schools

Gampopa's disciples founded the "four greater" Kagyu schools, among them the Karma Kagyu, headed by the Karmapa — the first tulku lineage in Tibetan Buddhism — and the Barom, Phagdru, and Tsalpa Kagyu. The Phagdru line further diversified into "eight lesser" schools, including Drikung Kagyu and Drukpa Kagyu, the latter dominant in Bhutan. The Shangpa Kagyu, founded by Khyungpo Naljor, forms a related but distinct transmission.

Modern era

Following the Tibetan diaspora of 1959, Kagyu teachers established centres worldwide. The Karma Kagyu school was affected by a long-standing controversy over the identity of the 17th Karmapa. Kagyu monasteries such as Rumtek in Sikkim and Tsurphu in Tibet remain important seats of the tradition.
