Cucurbita pepo

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cucurbita pepo is a cultivated plant of the genus Cucurbita, native to North America. It yields varieties of winter squash and pumpkin, as well as summer squash such as zucchini (courgette), yellow squash, and crookneck squash, along with many ornamental gourds and the acorn squash. It is one of the oldest domesticated species, with evidence of cultivation in Mexico dating back thousands of years.

Domestication and varieties

C. pepo was domesticated independently at least twice, in Mexico and in the eastern United States, giving rise to distinct lineages of cultivars. Because its varieties cross freely, the species displays extraordinary diversity in fruit shape, size, colour, and rind texture, from small decorative gourds to giant pumpkins grown for competition. Economically important forms include the common field pumpkin used for jack-o'-lanterns, spaghetti squash, delicata, and the zucchini, which became a staple summer squash worldwide after development in Italy.

Cultivation

Plants are frost-sensitive annual vines or bushes bearing large yellow flowers pollinated by bees, including specialist squash bees. The fruit is botanically a berry (a pepo) with a hardened rind when mature. Seeds of some varieties are eaten as pepitas and pressed for pumpkin seed oil.
