Cuboctahedron

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A cuboctahedron is a polyhedron with 8 triangular faces and 6 square faces. It has 12 identical vertices, with two triangles and two squares meeting at each, and 24 identical edges. The cuboctahedron is one of the Archimedean solids and is quasiregular, being the rectification of both the cube and the regular octahedron — it can be formed by cutting off the corners of either solid to the midpoints of the edges.

Properties

The cuboctahedron has octahedral symmetry and is unique among the uniform polyhedra in that its circumradius equals its edge length, so the 12 vertices can be placed at the centres of close-packed spheres around a central sphere of the same size. Its dual polyhedron is the rhombic dodecahedron. Buckminster Fuller called the solid the "vector equilibrium" because of the way vectors from its centre to its vertices balance, and studied its transformation into other polyhedra as the "jitterbug" motion.

Applications

The arrangement of vertices corresponds to the root system of the three-dimensional cubic lattice and appears in crystallography, in the geometry of close packing, and in games and puzzles derived from its spinning transformations.
