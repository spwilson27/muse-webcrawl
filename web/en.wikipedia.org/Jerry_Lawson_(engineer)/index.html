Gerald Anderson "Jerry" Lawson (December 1, 1940 – April 9, 2011) was an American electronic engineer. He is known for his work in designing the Fairchild Channel F video game console and for leading the team that pioneered the commercialised interchangeable video game cartridge, which transformed the video game industry by allowing players to buy and swap separate games for a single console.

Career

A self-taught electronics enthusiast from Queens, New York, Lawson joined Fairchild Semiconductor in the San Francisco Bay Area in 1970. As director of engineering and marketing for Fairchild's video game division, he headed development of the Channel F ("F" for fun), released in 1976 as the first home console to use interchangeable ROM cartridges and the first to use a microprocessor. Lawson also built the coin-operated game Demolition Derby in his garage, and in 1980 he founded Videosoft, a company that developed games for the Atari 2600.

Recognition

One of the few African-American engineers in the early video game industry, Lawson was honoured by the International Game Developers Association shortly before his death in 2011, and his story has since been widely celebrated, including through scholarships and a Google Doodle marking his 82nd birthday in 2022.
