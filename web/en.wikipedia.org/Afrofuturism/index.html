Afrofuturism

Afrofuturism
MediumLiterature, music, film, visual art
Key figuresSun Ra, Octavia E. Butler, George Clinton, Samuel R. Delany
Term coined1993, by Mark Dery

Afrofuturism is a cultural aesthetic that explores the African-American experience through science fiction, history, and fantasy, blending elements of Black history and culture with technology, space exploration, and speculative futures. The term was coined in 1993 by cultural critic Mark Dery in his essay "Black to the Future".

Themes

Afrofuturist works frequently address alienation, abduction, and otherness — using the alien or the spaceship as metaphors for the Middle Passage and slavery — while imagining liberated Black futures. Common themes include reclamation of ancestral identities, cosmic mythology, and the merging of African traditions with futuristic technology.

Notable examples

In music, Sun Ra's cosmic jazz philosophy and George Clinton's Parliament-Funkadelic "Mothership" mythology are founding texts. Writers such as Octavia E. Butler, Samuel R. Delany, and N. K. Jemisin have shaped the literary tradition. In popular culture, Janelle Monáe's android persona and Marvel's Black Panther (2018), with its technologically advanced African nation of Wakanda, brought Afrofuturism to a global mainstream audience.
