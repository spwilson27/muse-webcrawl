Abel

Abel is a biblical figure in the Book of Genesis, the second son of Adam and Eve and the younger brother of Cain. According to the narrative, Abel was a shepherd whose offering of the firstborn of his flock was accepted by God, while the offering of his brother Cain, a farmer, was not. In jealousy, Cain murdered Abel — making Abel, in the biblical account, the first human to die and the first murder victim.

Biblical narrative

Genesis 4 recounts that Abel "kept flocks" and brought "fat portions from some of the firstborn of his flock" as an offering. When God looked with favour on Abel's offering but not on Cain's, Cain became angry and, in the field, killed his brother. God confronted Cain ("The voice of your brother's blood cries out to me from the ground") and cursed him to wander. Eve later bore a third son, Seth, described as a replacement for Abel.

Interpretation

Commentators have long debated why Abel's offering was accepted: proposed explanations include the quality of the offering, Abel's faith and righteousness (as in Hebrews 11:4), and the contrast between pastoral and agricultural life. In the New Testament, Abel is called righteous, and Jesus refers to "the blood of righteous Abel". In the Quran, the story of Adam's two sons (identified in later tradition as Habil and Qabil) teaches that the God-fearing brother refused to retaliate, and the murderer bore the sin of both.

In tradition

Abel is venerated as a saint and proto-martyr in Christianity, commemorated in various calendars, and figures prominently in art, literature, and Gnostic and Mandaean traditions.
