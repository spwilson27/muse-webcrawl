Caleb

Caleb (Hebrew: כָּלֵב, Kālēḇ) is a figure in the Hebrew Bible. He is described as a representative of the Tribe of Judah among the twelve spies sent by Moses to scout the land of Canaan, and, together with Joshua, one of only two of the Israelites who left Egypt to survive the forty years of wandering and enter the Promised Land.

Biblical account

According to the Book of Numbers, Caleb son of Jephunneh the Kenizzite was chosen as the spy for Judah. When the spies returned, ten of them discouraged the people with reports of giants and fortified cities, but Caleb — and Joshua — urged the Israelites to trust God's promise and take the land. For this faithfulness, God decreed that Caleb and Joshua alone of their generation would enter Canaan.

After the conquest described in the Book of Joshua, Caleb, at the age of 85, asked Joshua for the hill country of Hebron, which had been promised to him, and drove out the Anakim who lived there. His daughter Achsah was given in marriage to Othniel, who captured the town of Debir; Othniel later became the first of the biblical judges.

Interpretation

Caleb's name is traditionally associated with the Hebrew word kelev, "dog", and is sometimes interpreted as "whole-hearted" or "faithful" — an interpretation reinforced by the biblical refrain that Caleb "wholly followed the LORD". He is remembered in Jewish, Christian and Islamic tradition as a model of courage and loyalty.
