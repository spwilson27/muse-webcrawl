Alexandra Feodorovna (born Princess Alix of Hesse and by Rhine, 6 June 1872 – 17 July 1918) was the last Empress of Russia, as the wife of Emperor Nicholas II. A granddaughter of Queen Victoria, she was a carrier of haemophilia, which she passed to her son, Tsarevich Alexei — a fact that drew her under the influence of the mystic Grigori Rasputin and badly damaged the reputation of the monarchy.

Early life and marriage

Alix was the sixth child of Louis IV, Grand Duke of Hesse, and Princess Alice of the United Kingdom. After her mother's early death she was largely raised in Britain under Queen Victoria's supervision. She met the future Nicholas II in 1884 and, despite initial reluctance — she had to convert from Lutheranism to Russian Orthodoxy — they married on 26 November 1894, weeks after the death of Alexander III.

Empress

Shy and intensely religious, Alexandra never won popularity at court or with the public, and withdrew into family life at Tsarskoe Selo. After four daughters, the birth of Alexei in 1904 was celebrated, but his haemophilia was kept secret. Alexandra's reliance on Rasputin, who seemed able to relieve the boy's bleeding, scandalised society; during the First World War, with Nicholas at the front and Alexandra effectively supervising ministers through Rasputin's counsel, rumours of treason (she was German-born) further undermined the throne. Rasputin's murder in December 1916 came too late to save the regime.

Revolution and death

After Nicholas abdicated in March 1917, the family was placed under house arrest and eventually exiled to Tobolsk and then Yekaterinburg. There, on the night of 16–17 July 1918, Alexandra was shot and bayoneted with her husband, their five children and retainers by Bolshevik guards. The remains, identified by DNA testing decades later, were reburied in the Peter and Paul Cathedral in 1998. She was canonised by the Russian Orthodox Church as a passion-bearer in 2000.
