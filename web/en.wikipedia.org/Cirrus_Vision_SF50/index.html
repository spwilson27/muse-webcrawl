Cirrus Vision SF50

The Cirrus Vision SF50, also known as the Vision Jet, is a single-engine very light jet designed and produced by Cirrus Aircraft of Duluth, Minnesota, United States. It is the lowest-cost and first single-engine personal jet to gain certification, and it won the Collier Trophy in 2017.

Development

The aircraft was first announced in 2006 and its prototype flew in July 2008. Development slowed during the financial crisis, and after Cirrus was acquired by CAIGA the programme accelerated again. The conforming prototype flew in 2014, FAA type certification was granted in October 2016, and first customer deliveries followed in December 2016. The improved second-generation G2 model, with an autothrottle and higher cruise altitude, was announced in 2019, followed by the G2+.

Design

The Vision Jet seats up to seven occupants, is powered by a single Williams FJ33 turbofan mounted atop the rear fuselage, and has a V-tail. It cruises at about 300 knots with a range of roughly 1,275 nautical miles and a service ceiling of 31,000 feet. Like other Cirrus aircraft, it is equipped with the Cirrus Airframe Parachute System (CAPS); it also features the Garmin Perspective Touch avionics suite and, in later models, the Safe Return emergency autoland system.

Specifications (G2)
