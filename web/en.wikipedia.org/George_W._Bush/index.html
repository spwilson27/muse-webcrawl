George Walker Bush (born July 6, 1946) is an American politician and businessman who served as the 43rd president of the United States from 2001 to 2009. A member of the Republican Party, he previously served as the 46th governor of Texas from 1995 to 2000. He is the eldest son of George H. W. Bush, the 41st president, making them one of only two father-son pairs to hold the office.

George W. Bush, 43rd president of the United States.

Presidency

Bush won the disputed 2000 election against Al Gore, losing the popular vote but prevailing in the Electoral College after the Supreme Court's decision in Bush v. Gore. Eight months into his presidency, the September 11 attacks of 2001 transformed his administration: he launched the "war on terror", invaded Afghanistan to destroy al-Qaeda and remove the Taliban, and in 2003 invaded Iraq to overthrow Saddam Hussein, citing weapons of mass destruction that were never found. Domestically, he signed major tax cuts, the No Child Left Behind education law, and the Medicare prescription drug benefit.

Second term and legacy

Re-elected in 2004, Bush's second term was marked by criticism over the Iraq War, the federal response to Hurricane Katrina in 2005, and the financial crisis of 2007–2008, during which he signed the $700 billion bank bailout (TARP). His presidency also created PEPFAR, a major global HIV/AIDS relief programme credited with saving millions of lives in Africa. After leaving office in 2009, he returned to Texas, published the memoir Decision Points (2010), and took up painting; the George W. Bush Presidential Center opened in Dallas in 2013.
