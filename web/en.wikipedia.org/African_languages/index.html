African languages

The languages of Africa number more than 2,000, belonging mainly to four language families: Afroasiatic, Nilo-Saharan, Niger–Congo, and Khoisan. Nigeria alone has over 500 living languages, making Africa the most linguistically diverse continent.

Families

The Niger–Congo family, including the vast Bantu group (Swahili, Zulu, Yoruba, Igbo), is the largest by speakers and area. Afroasiatic includes Arabic, Hausa, Amharic, and Somali, spoken mainly in the north and Horn. Swahili serves as a lingua franca across East Africa, while former colonial languages—English, French, Portuguese, and Spanish—remain official in many states.

Status

Many African languages are endangered, though others thrive in media and education. South Africa recognizes twelve official languages, including South African Sign Language.
