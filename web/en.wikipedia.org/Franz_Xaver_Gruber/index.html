Franz Xaver Gruber (25 November 1787 – 7 June 1863) was an Austrian primary school teacher, church organist and composer, best known for composing the melody of the Christmas carol "Silent Night" (Stille Nacht, heilige Nacht) in 1818 to words by Joseph Mohr.

Life

Gruber was born in the village of Unterweitzberg in Hochburg, Upper Austria, the son of linen weavers. Working at first at his father's trade, he trained as a teacher and in 1807 became schoolmaster, sacristan and organist at Arnsdorf, also serving as organist at the neighbouring village of Oberndorf bei Salzburg.

Silent Night

On Christmas Eve 1818, at the church of St Nicholas in Oberndorf, the assistant priest Joseph Mohr gave Gruber a short poem he had written two years earlier and asked for a melody for voice and guitar — tradition holds that the church organ was broken, possibly damaged by floodwater. Gruber composed the tune that day, and the two men sang the carol together at midnight Mass, Mohr on guitar. Travelling organ builders and Tyrolean singing families spread the song across Europe and America; it is now one of the most widely translated and recorded carols in the world, and was declared an intangible cultural heritage by UNESCO in 2011.

Gruber later moved to Berndorf and then to Hallein, where he directed the church choir and where his grave stands beside the Silent Night Museum. His son Franz Xaver Gruber Jr. founded the Hallein choral society. The original St Nicholas church in Oberndorf was replaced by the Silent Night Chapel after flooding.
