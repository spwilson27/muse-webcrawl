Oculus was a brand of virtual reality headsets developed by Oculus VR, a company founded in 2012 by Palmer Luckey and acquired by Facebook (now Meta Platforms) in 2014. Its products — the Rift, Go, Quest and Quest 2 — were central to the modern revival of consumer virtual reality. The Oculus brand was retired in 2022 in favour of Meta Quest.

History

Luckey launched a Kickstarter campaign in August 2012 for the Oculus Rift, a low-cost, wide-field-of-view headset for gaming, raising nearly US$2.5 million. Developer kits (DK1 and DK2) followed, and in March 2014 Facebook acquired the company for about US$2 billion in cash and stock. The first consumer Rift (CV1) shipped in March 2016, using external tracking sensors and later the Oculus Touch motion controllers.

Standalone headsets

In 2018 the company released Oculus Go, an inexpensive all-in-one headset, followed in 2019 by Oculus Quest, a standalone headset with inside-out tracking and full six-degrees-of-freedom controllers. The Quest 2 (2020) became one of the best-selling VR headsets, though a requirement to use a Facebook account proved controversial. After Facebook renamed itself Meta in 2021, the Quest line was rebranded as Meta Quest and the Oculus name phased out, with development continuing within Meta's Reality Labs division.

Software and platform

Oculus headsets run software distributed through the Oculus Store (now Meta Horizon Store), and the Rift platform supported PC gaming through the Oculus app and compatibility with SteamVR. John Carmack, the noted game programmer, served as the company's chief technology officer from 2013 to 2019.
