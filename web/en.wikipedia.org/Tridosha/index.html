Tridosha

In Ayurveda, the traditional system of medicine of India, tridosha (Sanskrit: त्रिदोष, "three doshas") is the theory that health and disease arise from the balance or imbalance of three fundamental bodily humours or energies: Vata, Pitta, and Kapha. The doctrine is set out in the classical texts, the Charaka Samhita and Sushruta Samhita.

The three doshas

  • Vata, composed of space and air, governs movement — breathing, circulation, nerve impulses, and elimination. It is characterised as dry, light, cold, and mobile.
  • Pitta, composed of fire and water, governs digestion, metabolism, and transformation, and is hot, sharp, and intense.
  • Kapha, composed of earth and water, governs structure, lubrication, and stability, and is heavy, slow, cool, and steady.

Prakriti and vikriti

Each person is held to be born with a unique constitution (prakriti) — a fixed proportion of the three doshas. Health is the maintenance of that original balance; illness (vikriti) arises when one or more doshas become aggravated by diet, season, behaviour, or age. Ayurvedic diagnosis, including pulse and tongue examination, aims to identify the imbalance, and treatment seeks to restore balance through diet, herbal medicines, oil massage, daily and seasonal routines, and purification procedures (panchakarma).

Reception

The tridosha theory is often compared with the humoral medicine of ancient Greece. While Ayurveda is practised by millions and officially recognised in India, its theoretical framework is regarded by the scientific mainstream as a traditional, pre-scientific system, and specific Ayurvedic treatments vary widely in their evidence base.
