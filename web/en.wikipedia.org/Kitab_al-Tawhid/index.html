Kitab al-Tawhid

A manuscript of Kitab al-Tawhid, the foundational text of the Wahhabi movement.

Kitab al-Tawhid (Arabic: كتاب التوحيد, "The Book of Monotheism") is the best-known work of Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab (1703–1792), the Arabian scholar whose reform movement, Wahhabism, became the doctrinal foundation of the Saudi state. Written in the 18th century, the book sets out its author's understanding of tawhid, the absolute oneness of God, and condemns practices he regarded as idolatry (shirk).

Contents

The book is organised into short chapters, each built around verses of the Quran and hadith reports, followed by brief summaries of the points to be drawn from them. Its themes include the meaning of the testimony of faith, the prohibition of seeking intercession from saints or at tombs, condemnation of amulets, astrology, magic and the veneration of trees, stones and graves, and the obligation to direct all worship — prayer, sacrifice, vows and supplication — to God alone.

Influence

Kitab al-Tawhid became a standard text of the educational curriculum in Saudi Arabia and in institutions influenced by the Wahhabi and broader Salafi movements worldwide. It is frequently printed together with classical commentaries, including those by descendants of the author. Critics of the movement argue that its narrow reading of monotheism was used to declare other Muslims unbelievers, while its supporters regard it as a straightforward compilation of scriptural texts.
