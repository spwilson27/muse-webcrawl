Hindi cinema

Hindi cinema, popularly known as Bollywood — a portmanteau of "Bombay" and "Hollywood" — is the Hindi-language film industry based in Mumbai (formerly Bombay). It is the largest segment of Indian cinema by revenue and one of the largest film industries in the world by output and audience reach. Hindi films are known for their musical numbers, melodrama, and blend of genres within a single film, often called the masala format, and they are widely watched across South Asia and the global diaspora as well as in markets from the Middle East to East Africa and Russia.

History

Indian cinema began with Dadasaheb Phalke's silent feature Raja Harishchandra (1913). Sound arrived with Alam Ara (1931), which established the song-and-dance format. The decades after independence in 1947 — the "Golden Age" — produced directors such as Raj Kapoor, Guru Dutt, Mehboob Khan, and Bimal Roy; Mother India (1957) was India's first submission to be nominated for the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film. The 1970s saw the rise of superstar Amitabh Bachchan as the "angry young man" and the blockbuster Sholay (1975), still among the most celebrated Indian films. The 1990s brought family romances and overseas markets driven by stars such as Shah Rukh Khan, with Dilwale Dulhania Le Jayenge (1995) running in a Mumbai theatre for over 25 years.

Music

Songs, usually pre-recorded by playback singers and lip-synced by actors, are integral to Hindi films and their marketing. Playback legends include Lata Mangeshkar, Mohammed Rafi, Kishore Kumar, and Asha Bhosle; composers from Naushad and S. D. Burman to A. R. Rahman shaped the industry's sound. Rahman won two Academy Awards for Slumdog Millionaire (2008), a British production deeply influenced by Bollywood conventions.

Economics and reach

Hindi cinema typically releases well over a hundred films a year. Dangal (2016), starring Aamir Khan, became one of the highest-grossing Indian films ever, earning especially large revenues in China. Since the 2010s, streaming platforms have expanded the audience and encouraged mid-budget, content-driven films alongside star vehicles, while the term "Bollywood" is sometimes criticized by filmmakers who prefer "Hindi film industry".
