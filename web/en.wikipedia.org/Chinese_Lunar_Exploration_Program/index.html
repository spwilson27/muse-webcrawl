Chinese Lunar Exploration Program

Chinese Lunar Exploration Program
Also known as: Chang'e Project
Agency: China National Space Administration
First mission: Chang'e 1 (2007)

The Chinese Lunar Exploration Program (CLEP), also known as the Chang'e Project after the Chinese Moon goddess Chang'e, is an ongoing series of robotic Moon missions operated by the China National Space Administration (CNSA).

Phases

The programme proceeds in phases: orbiting, landing, returning samples, and ultimately building a research station. Chang'e 1 (2007) and Chang'e 2 (2010) orbited the Moon; Chang'e 3 (2013) landed the Yutu rover; Chang'e 4 (2019) made the first soft landing on the far side of the Moon, supported by the Queqiao relay satellite.

Sample return

Chang'e 5 (2020) returned about 1.7 kg of lunar samples, the first since the 1970s. Chang'e 6 (2024) returned the first samples from the far side, collected in the South Pole–Aitken Basin.

Future

Chang'e 7 and 8 are planned to explore the lunar south pole and test in-situ resource utilisation, as precursors to the International Lunar Research Station planned with partners.
