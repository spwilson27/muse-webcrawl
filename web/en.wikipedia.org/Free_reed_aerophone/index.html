A free reed aerophone is a musical instrument that produces sound as air flows past a vibrating reed in a frame. Air pressure and vacuum cause the reed to vibrate, creating a musical tone. Unlike beating reeds such as those of the clarinet or oboe, the free reed vibrates freely through a closely fitting slot without striking against any surface.

History

Free reed instruments have a long history in Asia. The Chinese sheng, a mouth organ with bamboo pipes, dates back several thousand years and is one of the oldest free reed instruments. The free reed was introduced to Europe in the late 18th and early 19th centuries, where it inspired the development of the harmonica, the accordion, the concertina, and the reed organ.

Types

Free reed aerophones include the harmonica, button and piano accordions, concertinas, harmoniums, reed organs, melodicas, and the traditional Asian mouth organs such as the sheng, khaen, and shō. In these instruments a stream of air, supplied by the player's breath or by bellows, is directed at a thin metal or cane tongue mounted in a frame, and the tongue vibrates at a frequency determined mainly by its length, thickness, and stiffness.

Acoustics

The pitch of a free reed is determined primarily by the physical properties of the reed itself rather than by the length of an air column, which allows many differently tuned reeds to be compactly arranged in a single instrument. Reeds may be tuned by filing material from the tip (raising pitch) or near the base (lowering pitch).
