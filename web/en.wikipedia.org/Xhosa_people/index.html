Xhosa people

The Xhosa people (amaXhosa) are a Nguni ethnic group native to southern Africa, principally the Eastern Cape of South Africa, and the second-largest ethnic group in the country. Their language, isiXhosa, is a Bantu language notable for its click consonants and is one of South Africa's official languages.

History

Xhosa chiefdoms expanded southward over centuries, clashing with Khoisan groups and, from the late 18th century, with British and Boer settlers in the nine Cape Frontier Wars. The cattle-killing movement of 1856–57, following the prophetess Nongqawuse's visions, devastated Xhosa society. Under apartheid, the Ciskei and Transkei 'homelands' were imposed on Xhosa territories.

Culture

Traditional culture includes initiation (ulwaluko), beadwork, praise poetry (izibongo) and strong clan and lineage identities. Prominent Xhosa figures include Nelson Mandela, Desmond Tutu and Thabo Mbeki.
