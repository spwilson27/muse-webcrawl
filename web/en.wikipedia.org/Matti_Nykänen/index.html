Matti Nykänen

Matti Ensio Nykänen
Born: 17 July 1963, Jyväskylä, Finland
Died: 4 February 2019 (aged 55), Lappeenranta, Finland
Height: 1.74 m
World Cup wins: 46
Olympic medals: 4 gold, 1 silver

Matti Ensio Nykänen (17 July 1963 – 4 February 2019) was a Finnish ski jumper widely regarded as the greatest in the history of the sport. He dominated ski jumping in the 1980s, winning five Winter Olympic medals, nine World Championship medals, and the overall World Cup four times.

Career

Nykänen won his first World Championship title on the large hill in Oslo in 1982. At the 1984 Sarajevo Olympics he took gold on the large hill and silver on the normal hill. His peak came at Calgary 1988, where he won all three ski jumping events — normal hill, large hill, and the inaugural team event — a sweep unmatched in Olympic history. His 46 individual World Cup victories stood as the record for decades. He was also a pioneer of the V-style technique that transformed the sport.

Later life

After retirement Nykänen pursued a singing career and remained a Finnish celebrity, but his life was marked by alcohol problems, financial troubles, and criminal convictions, including prison sentences for violent offences. His aphorisms became so embedded in Finnish culture that "Nykänenisms" are quoted nationwide. He died in 2019 at the age of 55.
