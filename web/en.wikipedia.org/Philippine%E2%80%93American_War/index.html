Philippine–American War
DateFebruary 4, 1899 – July 2, 1902
LocationPhilippines
ResultAmerican victory; dissolution of the First Philippine Republic

The Philippine–American War, known in the Philippines as the Philippine War of Independence, was an armed conflict between the First Philippine Republic and the United States that lasted from February 4, 1899, until July 2, 1902. It arose after the United States, having defeated Spain in the Spanish–American War, purchased the Philippines under the Treaty of Paris rather than recognize the independence declared by Filipino revolutionaries under Emilio Aguinaldo.

Course of the war

After initial conventional battles around Manila, the Filipino army was defeated and resorted to guerrilla warfare from November 1899. Aguinaldo was captured in March 1901, and President Theodore Roosevelt proclaimed the war over on July 4, 1902, though resistance continued in some areas for years. The war resulted in at least 200,000 Filipino civilian deaths, largely from famine and disease, and some 20,000 Filipino and 4,200 American combatant deaths.

Aftermath

The Philippines became an unincorporated territory of the United States, governed by the Philippine Commission and later an elected assembly, until the Commonwealth era and full independence in 1946.
