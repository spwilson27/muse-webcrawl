F. N. Souza

Francis Newton Souza (12 April 1924 – 28 March 2002) was an Indian modernist painter, and a founding member of the Progressive Artists' Group of Bombay. He was the first post-independence Indian artist to achieve high recognition in the West, and his expressionistic, often provocative paintings of landscapes, portraits, nudes, and religious subjects have set repeated records at auction for Indian art.

Souza's work blended Indian themes with European modernism

Life and work

Born in Saligao, Goa, Souza studied at the Sir J. J. School of Art in Bombay, from which he was expelled in 1945 for participating in the Quit India movement. In 1947 he founded the Progressive Artists' Group with artists including M. F. Husain and S. H. Raza, aiming to break with the conventions of the Bengal School. He moved to London in 1949, where his 1955 essay "Nirvana of a Maggot" and an exhibition at Gallery One brought him critical attention. He later settled in New York in 1967.

Style and legacy

Souza's paintings are characterised by aggressive line, thick impasto, and distorted, robot-like heads. His 1955 painting Birth sold at Christie's in 2015 for over four million dollars, then a record for an Indian artist. He died in Mumbai in 2002.
