Potalaka

Potalaka

Sanskrit: Potalaka

Significance: Mythical abode of Avalokiteśvara

Tradition: Mahāyāna Buddhism

Namesake of: Potala Palace, Lhasa

Potalaka (Sanskrit: Potalaka) is the mythical mountain abode of Avalokiteśvara, the bodhisattva of compassion, described in Mahāyāna Buddhist scriptures such as the Gaṇḍavyūha Sūtra and the Avataṃsaka Sūtra. The pilgrim Sudhana is described as travelling to the mountain, located "in the southern regions", where he receives teaching from the bodhisattva.

Texts describe Potalaka as a fragrant, jewel-like mountain rising from the ocean, covered with streams, pools and flowering trees. Because of its association with Avalokiteśvara, real locations across Asia were later identified with it.

Identifications

Scholars have proposed that the original Potalaka was inspired by Mount Pothigai in the southern Western Ghats of India. In China, Mount Putuo in the Zhoushan archipelago became the great pilgrimage site of Guanyin (the Chinese form of Avalokiteśvara) and is known as the Chinese Potalaka. In Tibet, the Potala Palace in Lhasa — residence of the Dalai Lamas — took its name from the mountain.

Influence

The name Potalaka thus travelled with Buddhism across Asia, attaching itself to sacred geography in India, China, Tibet and Japan, and remains a potent symbol of the bodhisattva's compassionate presence in the world.
