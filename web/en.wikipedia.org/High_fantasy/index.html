High fantasy

High fantasy, or epic fantasy, is a subgenre of fantasy defined by the epic nature of its setting or by the epic stature of its characters, themes or plot. It is typically set in an alternative, fictional ("secondary") world with its own internal rules and history, in contrast to low fantasy, in which magical elements intrude on the real world.

Characteristics

High fantasy narratives often centre on a quest against a powerful antagonist, a coming-of-age hero, and large-scale struggles between good and evil. Settings commonly feature invented languages, mythologies, maps and non-human races such as elves and dwarves. The term was articulated by Lloyd Alexander and developed by critics including Brian Attebery.

Examples

J. R. R. Tolkien's The Lord of the Rings (1954–55) is the archetypal work of the subgenre, drawing on medieval literature and philology. Later prominent high fantasy includes Ursula K. Le Guin's Earthsea books, Robert Jordan's The Wheel of Time, George R. R. Martin's A Song of Ice and Fire, and the works of Brandon Sanderson, which emphasise rule-based "hard" magic systems.
