Oxbow lake

An oxbow lake is a U-shaped lake or pool that forms when a wide meander of a river is cut off, creating a free-standing body of water. The term "oxbow" refers to the U-shaped frame fitted around the neck of an ox for plowing.

Formation

As a river meanders across flat land, erosion on the outer bank and deposition on the inner bank cause the bend to migrate and tighten. During a flood, the river may cut through the narrow neck of the meander, taking a straighter course; sediment then seals off the old channel, leaving a crescent-shaped lake. Over time oxbow lakes silt up and become wetlands or meander scars.

Ecology and examples

Oxbow lakes provide calm, nutrient-rich habitat important for fish, amphibians, and water birds, and many are protected as nature reserves. Famous examples line the lower Mississippi River, where oxbow lakes form state-border features, and the Amazon Basin, where they harbor rich aquatic ecosystems. The largest oxbow lake in the United States is Lake Chicot in Arkansas.
