Pechora River


The Pechora in its lower reaches

The Pechora River (Russian: Печо́ра) is a river in north-west Russia, flowing through the Komi Republic and the Nenets Autonomous Okrug into the Barents Sea. At about 1,809 kilometres (1,124 miles) long, it is the sixth-longest river in Europe, and its basin — roughly 322,000 square kilometres — drains much of the northern Ural Mountains' western slope and the Pechora lowland. Its average discharge of some 4,100 cubic metres per second makes it one of the largest rivers of Europe by volume, exceeded only by the Volga and the Danube.

Course

The Pechora rises in the northern Urals near Mount Koyp and flows first west, then north past the towns of Troitsko-Pechorsk, Vuktyl, Pechora and Naryan-Mar. Below Ust-Tsilma the river turns in a broad arc across the tundra lowland; near its mouth it forms a delta opening into Pechora Bay of the Barents Sea. Principal tributaries include the Ilych, the Shchugor, the Usa, the Shapkina, and the Izhma.

History and economy

Historically, the Pechora basin was home to the Yugra and Komi peoples and was a route used by Novgorod's fur traders. In the Soviet era, the middle Pechora region became notorious for the Pechorlag Gulag camps and coal mining centred on Vorkuta (in the Usa basin) and Inta. Today the basin is important for coal, oil and natural gas production (including the Timan-Pechora petroleum province), timber, and fisheries. The lower Pechora and its delta are major wetlands for migratory waterfowl, and the upper Ilych basin is protected in the Pechoro-Ilychsky Nature Reserve, part of the Virgin Komi Forests UNESCO World Heritage site.
