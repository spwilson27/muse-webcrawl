Prosthodontics

Prosthodontics, also known as dental prosthetics or prosthetic dentistry, is the area of dentistry that focuses on dental prostheses. It is a recognized dental specialty pertaining to the diagnosis, treatment planning, rehabilitation, and maintenance of oral function, comfort, appearance, and health of patients with missing or deficient teeth and/or oral and maxillofacial tissues using biocompatible substitutes.

Scope

Prosthodontists provide fixed prosthodontics (crowns, bridges, veneers), removable prosthodontics (complete and partial dentures), implant prosthodontics, and maxillofacial prostheses for patients with defects from surgery, trauma, or congenital conditions. Training typically involves a multi-year postgraduate residency after dental school.

History

Artificial teeth date to antiquity, but modern prosthodontics developed with vulcanite denture bases in the 19th century and expanded rapidly with dental implants in the late 20th century, following the discovery of osseointegration.
