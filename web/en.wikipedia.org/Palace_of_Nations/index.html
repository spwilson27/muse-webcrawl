Palace of Nations

The Palace of Nations (French: Palais des Nations) is the home of the United Nations Office at Geneva, Switzerland, and was built between 1929 and 1938 to serve as the headquarters of the League of Nations. It is located in Ariana Park in Geneva, overlooking Lake Geneva, and is one of the largest diplomatic conference centres in the world.

History

An architectural competition in 1926 failed to select a single winner, and five architects — Carlo Broggi, Julien Flegenheimer, Camille Lefèvre, Henri-Paul Nénot, and Joseph Vago — collaborated on a neoclassical design. Construction began in 1929 and the League of Nations moved in during the later 1930s. After the League's dissolution, the building was transferred to the United Nations in 1946 and became the European office of the UN, expanded with new wings in the 1950s and with the E Building in the 1970s.

Description

The palace contains some 2,800 offices and 34 conference rooms, including the Assembly Hall and the Human Rights and Alliance of Civilizations Room, whose ceiling was redesigned by the artist Miquel Barceló. It hosts thousands of intergovernmental meetings each year, including sessions of the Human Rights Council and the Conference on Disarmament. Works of art, including gifts from member states, decorate its halls, and the surrounding park contains the Celestial Sphere monument commemorating the League of Nations.
