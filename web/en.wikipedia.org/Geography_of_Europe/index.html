Geography of Europe

Europe is a continent forming the westernmost part of Eurasia, covering about 10.2 million square kilometres, or roughly 2 percent of Earth's surface. It is bordered by the Arctic Ocean to the north, the Atlantic Ocean to the west, the Mediterranean Sea to the south, and Asia to the east, with the boundary conventionally running along the Ural Mountains, Ural River, Caspian Sea, and Caucasus.

Physical features

Europe's terrain ranges from the mountainous south — the Alps, Pyrenees, Carpathians, and Balkans — through the rolling Central European uplands to the vast North European Plain stretching from France to the Urals. Major peninsulas include Scandinavia, Iberia, Italy, and the Balkans; major islands include Great Britain, Ireland, Iceland, Sicily, and Sardinia. The Volga is the longest river entirely in Europe, followed by the Danube, which flows through ten countries.

Climate

Western Europe has a temperate maritime climate warmed by the North Atlantic Drift, giving it milder winters than comparable latitudes elsewhere. The Mediterranean south has hot, dry summers; the interior east has a continental climate with cold winters; and the far north reaches subarctic and tundra conditions.

Coastline and seas

Europe has an exceptionally long, indented coastline relative to its area, with major marginal seas including the North Sea, Baltic Sea, Mediterranean Sea, Black Sea, and Barents Sea. This maritime geography historically encouraged seafaring, trade, and fishing, and no point in Europe is far from the sea by continental standards.
