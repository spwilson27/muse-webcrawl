Pulp Fiction
Director: Quentin Tarantino
Starring: John Travolta, Samuel L. Jackson, Uma Thurman, Bruce Willis
Released: 1994
Awards: Palme d'Or; Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay

Pulp Fiction is a 1994 American crime film written and directed by Quentin Tarantino from a story by Tarantino and Roger Avary. Told in a non-linear structure, it interweaves the stories of two Los Angeles hitmen, a boxer, a gangster's wife and a pair of diner bandits.

Plot

The film's narrative is divided into several episodes presented out of chronological order. Hitmen Vincent Vega and Jules Winnfield retrieve a mysterious briefcase for their boss, Marsellus Wallace; Vincent later entertains Marsellus's wife Mia and saves her from an overdose. Boxer Butch Coolidge, paid to throw a fight, instead wins it and must flee. Jules, after surviving a shooting he interprets as a miracle, resolves to leave the criminal life, while a robbery at a diner brings the strands together.

Production and release

Made for a modest budget of about $8.5 million, the film premiered at the 1994 Cannes Film Festival, where it won the Palme d'Or. It went on to gross more than $200 million worldwide, revitalised John Travolta's career, and won the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay.

Influence

The film is widely regarded as Tarantino's masterpiece and one of the most influential films of the 1990s. Its eclectic dialogue, pop-culture references, soundtrack, ironic humour and stylised violence helped define postmodern cinema, and it was selected for preservation in the National Film Registry in 2013.
