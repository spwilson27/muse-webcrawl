Europoort ("Eurogate", sometimes spelled Europort) is a large industrial harbour area of the Port of Rotterdam in the Netherlands, situated on the island of Rozenburg where the Nieuwe Waterweg meets the North Sea. Together with the adjacent Maasvlakte, it forms the seaward, deep-water part of Europe's largest seaport.

Development

Europoort was constructed from the late 1950s to accommodate the rapidly growing trade in crude oil and bulk goods that the older Rotterdam docks could no longer handle. Deep-dredged harbours such as the Caland Canal allowed the largest tankers and bulk carriers of the day to berth. The area hosts oil refineries and petrochemical plants (including facilities of Shell, ExxonMobil and others), oil storage terminals, coal and ore transshipment, chemical plants, and the ECT Delta container terminal, one of the most automated container terminals in the world.

Geography

The area lies west of the older Botlek harbour district and east of the Maasvlakte reclamation projects. The village of Rozenburg sits amid the port industry. Europoort is connected to the hinterland by the A15 motorway, the Betuweroute freight railway, and pipelines carrying crude oil and products to the Ruhr and Antwerp industrial regions.
