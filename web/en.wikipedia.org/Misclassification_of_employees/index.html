Misclassification of employees

Misclassification of employees is the practice by employers of wrongly classifying workers as independent contractors rather than as employees. The distinction matters because employees are typically entitled to protections and benefits — such as minimum wage, overtime pay, unemployment insurance, workers' compensation, and employer payroll tax contributions — that independent contractors do not receive.

Legal tests

Different legal regimes apply different tests to determine worker status. The United States Internal Revenue Service considers behavioural control, financial control, and the relationship of the parties. Many US states apply an "ABC test", under which a worker is an employee unless the employer proves the worker is free from control, performs work outside the employer's usual business, and has an independently established trade. California's ABC test was codified by Assembly Bill 5 in 2019.

Consequences

Misclassification can deprive workers of benefits and shift business costs onto them, reduce government tax revenue, and give offending employers an unfair competitive advantage. Employers found to have misclassified workers may face back taxes, penalties, and liability for unpaid wages and benefits.

Gig economy

The issue gained prominence with the growth of the gig economy, as platform companies such as ride-hailing and food-delivery firms classify their workers as independent contractors. Ballot measures such as California's Proposition 22 (2020) created carve-outs for app-based drivers, while other jurisdictions have moved toward presuming employment status.
