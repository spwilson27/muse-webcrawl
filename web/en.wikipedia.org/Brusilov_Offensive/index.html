Brusilov Offensive

The Brusilov Offensive (4 June – 20 September 1916) was the Russian Empire's greatest feat of arms during the First World War and one of the most lethal offensives in history. It was named for its commander, General Aleksei Brusilov.

Brusilov broke with conventional practice by attacking along a broad front after short, precise artillery preparations, using shock troops to exploit weak points. The offensive shattered Austria-Hungary's armies, which lost around 1.5 million men including 400,000 prisoners, and forced Germany to divert troops from Verdun.

Despite its success, enormous Russian casualties (up to a million), logistical failure, and Romania's disastrous entry into the war on the Allied side limited the gains. The offensive fatally weakened both the Austro-Hungarian and Russian empires.
