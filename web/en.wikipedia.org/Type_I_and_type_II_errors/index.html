Type I and type II errors

Type I errorFalse positive (rate α)
Type II errorFalse negative (rate β)
FieldStatistical hypothesis testing

In statistical hypothesis testing, a type I error is the mistaken rejection of a null hypothesis that is actually true — a "false positive". A type II error is the failure to reject a null hypothesis that is actually false — a "false negative". Much of statistical theory revolves around managing the trade-off between the rates of these two kinds of error.

Definitions

Null hypothesis trueNull hypothesis false
Reject null hypothesisType I error (false positive)Correct (true positive)
Fail to reject null hypothesisCorrect (true negative)Type II error (false negative)

The probability of a type I error is denoted α (alpha) and is set by the significance level of the test, commonly 0.05. The probability of a type II error is denoted β (beta); its complement, 1 − β, is the statistical power of the test — the probability of correctly detecting a real effect.

Trade-offs

For a fixed sample size, decreasing the risk of one type of error generally increases the risk of the other. Increasing the sample size can reduce both error rates simultaneously. The relative costs of the two errors depend on the application: in medical screening, a false negative may allow a disease to go untreated, while a false positive leads to further testing; in criminal trials, legal traditions such as Blackstone's ratio treat convicting an innocent person (analogous to a type I error) as far worse than acquitting a guilty one.

Examples

A spam filter that flags legitimate email as spam commits a type I error; one that lets spam through commits a type II error. In medicine, a test that reports a healthy patient as ill is a false positive, while missing an actual illness is a false negative.
