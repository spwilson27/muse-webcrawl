Aortic valve

The aortic valve guards the outflow of the left ventricle

The aortic valve is a valve in the heart of humans and some other animals, located between the left ventricle and the aorta. It is one of the four heart valves and one of two semilunar valves, the other being the pulmonary valve. Normally it has three cusps (leaflets); about 1–2% of people are born with a congenitally bicuspid valve with two cusps.

Structure and function

The valve's three cusps—left coronary, right coronary, and non-coronary—are named for their relation to the coronary artery ostia that lie just above them in the sinuses of Valsalva. During ventricular systole the valve opens to allow oxygenated blood into the aorta; during diastole it closes to prevent regurgitation back into the ventricle, contributing to the second heart sound.

Diseases

Aortic stenosis is a progressive narrowing of the valve, most often from age-related calcific degeneration or a bicuspid valve, causing breathlessness, angina, and syncope. Aortic regurgitation (insufficiency) is leakage of blood back into the ventricle, arising from valve disease or dilation of the aortic root; it may follow infective endocarditis or rheumatic fever.

Treatment

Severe symptomatic disease is treated by surgical aortic valve replacement with a mechanical or biological prosthesis, or by transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI/TAVR), in which a replacement valve is delivered via catheter.
