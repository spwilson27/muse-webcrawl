Baron Roman Fyodorovich von Ungern-Sternberg (10 January 1886 – 15 September 1921), often called the "Mad Baron" or "Bloody Baron", was a Baltic German officer in the Imperial Russian Army and an anti-communist warlord of the Russian Civil War. In 1921 his Asiatic Cavalry Division drove Chinese forces out of Mongolia and briefly restored the Bogd Khan, making him the de facto ruler of Mongolia.

Career

Ungern served on the Eastern Front in the First World War and, after the October Revolution, seized control of territory in Transbaikalia with his patron Grigory Semyonov. A mystic influenced by Buddhism and by dreams of a restored Eurasian monarchy, he ruled Mongolia with notorious brutality for several months in 1921 before invading Soviet Siberia, where his forces disintegrated. Captured by the Red Army, he was tried in Novonikolaevsk and executed by firing squad on 15 September 1921.
