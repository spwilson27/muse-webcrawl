American studies

American studies or American civilization is an interdisciplinary field of scholarship that examines American literature, history, society, and culture. It traditionally incorporates the study of history, literature, and critical theory, and has expanded to include ethnic studies, gender studies, film, and transnational approaches.

History

The field emerged in the United States in the 1930s and 1940s, partly as a program of national self-definition during and after World War II. Early "myth and symbol" scholars such as Henry Nash Smith (Virgin Land, 1950) and Leo Marx (The Machine in the Garden, 1964) examined recurring themes in American culture. The American Studies Association was founded in 1951.

Approaches

From the 1970s onward, scholars influenced by feminism, critical race theory, and postcolonial studies challenged exceptionalist narratives, emphasizing race, gender, class, and empire. More recent work adopts transnational and hemispheric frameworks, situating the United States within the Americas and global flows of people and culture.

International scope

American studies programs exist worldwide, including in Europe, Asia, and Latin America, often under names such as North American studies.
