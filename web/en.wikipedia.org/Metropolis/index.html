Metropolis

For other uses, see Metropolis (disambiguation).

A metropolis is a large city or conurbation which is a significant economic, political and cultural area for a country or region, and an important hub for regional or international connections, commerce and communication. The word comes from the Greek mētropolis, "mother city", the city or state of origin of a colony. A big city belonging to a larger urban agglomeration but not the core of that agglomeration is generally not regarded as a metropolis but as part of it.

Definitions

There is no universal population threshold. The United Nations and various national statistical offices use figures from about one million inhabitants for a metropolitan area; global-city research classifies metropolises by their command-and-control functions in finance, media and corporate headquarters rather than size alone. A metropolis usually anchors a metropolitan area including suburbs and commuter towns.

History

Ancient metropolises such as Rome, Constantinople and Baghdad exceeded a million inhabitants at their peaks. Industrialisation produced explosive growth in cities such as London and New York, and the 20th and 21st centuries saw the rise of megacities and megalopolises — networks of adjoining metropolitan areas, such as the Northeast megalopolis of the United States and the Pearl River Delta in China.
