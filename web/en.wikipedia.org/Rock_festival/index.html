Rock festival

A rock festival is a large-scale rock music concert, usually outdoors and lasting several days, featuring multiple performers. Rock festivals became a defining institution of popular music culture from the late 1960s onward.

History

The Monterey International Pop Festival (1967) is often credited with launching the era of the big rock festival, followed by the Woodstock Festival of 1969, attended by some 400,000 people and emblematic of the counterculture. The Isle of Wight festivals and events such as Glastonbury in Britain developed the form further. From the 1990s, touring festivals like Lollapalooza and permanent annual events such as Coachella, Reading and Leeds, and Primavera Sound became major fixtures of the music industry.

Features

Festivals typically offer multiple stages, camping, food and art installations, and draw large crowds over several days; they are significant economically for host regions and important showcases for both established and emerging acts.
