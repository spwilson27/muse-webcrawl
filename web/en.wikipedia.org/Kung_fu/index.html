Kung fu

Kung fu is an umbrella term for the Chinese martial arts, also known collectively as wushu or kuoshu. In colloquial use, the term (from Cantonese) refers to any of hundreds of fighting styles developed over centuries in China; in its original Chinese sense, "kung fu" denotes any skill achieved through hard work and practice.

History

Chinese martial arts trace legendary origins to the Yellow Emperor and to the Shaolin Monastery, where tradition associates the monk Bodhidharma with exercises that became Shaolin kung fu. Styles spread through military training, folk practice, and opera troupes, diversifying into northern and southern systems, external (hard) and internal (soft) schools.

Styles

Well-known systems include Shaolin, Wing Chun, Tai chi, Baguazhang, Xingyiquan, Hung Gar, and Choy Li Fut. Forms (taolu) — choreographed sequences of techniques — are central to training and modern wushu competition. Kung fu entered global popular culture through Hong Kong cinema and stars such as Bruce Lee and Jackie Chan.
