Work–life balance

Work–life balance is the equilibrium an individual seeks between time allocated for work and other aspects of life, including family, leisure, community involvement and personal care. The term came into common use in the 1980s as longer hours, dual-earner households and mobile communications blurred the boundary between job and home.

Issues

Poor balance is associated with stress, burnout, and physical and mental ill health, while organisations report that balanced employees show higher engagement and lower turnover. Contributing factors include excessive workload, inflexible schedules, unpaid overtime, commuting, and the expectation of constant availability by email and messaging. Care responsibilities — for children and for elderly relatives — fall unevenly, and research consistently finds women performing a larger share of unpaid domestic work alongside paid employment.

Responses

Employers have introduced flexible working hours, compressed workweeks, job sharing, parental leave and remote or hybrid work, the last greatly expanded by the COVID-19 pandemic. Legislators have acted through working-time rules such as the European Union's Working Time Directive and "right to disconnect" laws adopted in France and elsewhere. Four-day week trials in several countries have reported maintained productivity with improved wellbeing. Critics note that balance rhetoric can shift responsibility onto individuals for structural overwork; related concepts include the workweek and weekend.
