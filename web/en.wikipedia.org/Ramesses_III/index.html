Ramesses III, last great pharaoh of the New Kingdom.

Ramesses III (reigned c. 1186–1155 BC) was the second pharaoh of the Twentieth Dynasty of Egypt and is often regarded as the last great king of the New Kingdom. Modelling himself on Ramesses II, he defended Egypt against invasions by the Libyans and the Sea Peoples and built extensively, most famously his mortuary temple at Medinet Habu.

Wars

In his fifth and eleventh years Ramesses repelled major Libyan invasions of the western Delta. In year eight he defeated the Sea Peoples in twin battles — a land battle in Djahi (southern Levant) and a celebrated naval battle at the mouths of the Nile, depicted in detail on the walls of Medinet Habu, among the earliest detailed depictions of a sea battle. These victories preserved Egypt while other Bronze Age powers collapsed.

Later reign and death

The later reign saw economic strain: grain shortages led to the first recorded labour strike in history, by the royal tomb builders of Deir el-Medina. Ramesses III was assassinated in the "harem conspiracy" led by his secondary wife Tiye, who sought the throne for her son Pentawere; CT scans of his mummy in 2012 showed his throat had been cut. The conspirators were tried and condemned, as recorded in the Judicial Papyrus of Turin.
