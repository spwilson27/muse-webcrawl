Lameness (equine)

Lameness (equine)
FieldEquine veterinary medicine
SignsAbnormal gait, head nod, hip hike
Common sitesFoot, hock, stifle, fetlock

Lameness in horses is an abnormal gait or stance caused by pain, mechanical restriction, or neurological dysfunction. It is one of the most common veterinary problems in horses and a leading cause of lost use in performance animals.

Evaluation

A lameness examination typically begins with observing the horse at walk and trot, in hand and on a lunge line, on hard and soft surfaces. Forelimb lameness often produces a characteristic head nod — the head rises as the lame leg strikes the ground — while hindlimb lameness shows as a pelvic "hip hike". Severity is commonly graded on the AAEP 0–5 scale. Flexion tests, hoof testers, and perineural or intra-articular anaesthesia ("nerve blocks") help localise the source of pain.

Causes

The majority of lameness originates in the foot, including hoof abscesses, navicular syndrome, and laminitis. Joint disease such as osteoarthritis, tendon and ligament injuries, fractures, and developmental orthopaedic diseases are also frequent causes. Diagnosis may require radiography, ultrasonography, or MRI.
