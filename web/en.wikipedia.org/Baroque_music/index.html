Baroque music


A Baroque ensemble performing with period instruments

Baroque music (UK: /bəˈrɒk/ or US: /bəˈroʊk/) is a period or style of Western classical music from approximately 1600 to 1750, originating in Western Europe. The Baroque period follows the Renaissance period and was followed in turn by the Classical period. It is associated with composers such as Claudio Monteverdi, Antonio Vivaldi, George Frideric Handel, Arcangelo Corelli, Henry Purcell and Johann Sebastian Bach.

The word "baroque" comes from the Portuguese word barroco, meaning a misshapen pearl, a term used pejoratively by later critics to describe the style's perceived excess and ornamentation. Only later did it come to be applied to the music of the period.

Characteristics

Baroque music is characterised by the use of the basso continuo (figured bass), played typically by a harpsichord and a bass instrument such as a cello or bassoon, providing a harmonic foundation over which melodies unfold. Other hallmarks include the doctrine of the affections, concertato contrasts between groups of instruments, elaborate ornamentation, and the emergence of tonality replacing the older modal system. Major and minor scales became the dominant means of organising melody and harmony.

Forms and genres

The Baroque saw the birth of opera in Florence around 1600, and the development of the oratorio, cantata, concerto (including the concerto grosso), sonata, suite, and fugue. Vocal music dominated the early Baroque, while instrumental music grew in independence toward the end of the period. Dance rhythms permeated much instrumental writing, and the keyboard works of Bach, including The Well-Tempered Clavier, demonstrated the possibilities of equal temperament and contrapuntal mastery.

Phases

Musicologists divide the period into early Baroque (1600–1650), marked by monody and the new operatic style; middle Baroque (1650–1700), when Italian opera spread and tonal patterns crystallised; and late Baroque (1700–1750), when the styles of Vivaldi, Handel and Bach brought the period to its culmination.
