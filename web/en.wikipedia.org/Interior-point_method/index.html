Interior-point method

Interior-point method
Field: Mathematical optimization
Key paper: Narendra Karmarkar (1984)
Applies to: Linear and convex optimization

Interior-point methods (also referred to as barrier methods) are algorithms for solving linear and non-linear convex optimization problems. Unlike the simplex method, which moves along the boundary of the feasible region, interior-point methods approach the optimum through the interior of the feasible set.

Idea

Inequality constraints are handled by adding a barrier function—typically a logarithmic barrier—to the objective, which grows to infinity as the boundary is approached, keeping iterates strictly feasible. As a parameter is reduced, the approximate solution converges to the true optimum along a smooth trajectory called the central path.

History

Barrier ideas date to early work by Anthony Fiacco and Garth McCormick in the 1960s, but interest exploded in 1984 when Narendra Karmarkar announced a polynomial-time algorithm for linear programming that proved extremely fast in practice. Primal-dual methods developed in the late 1980s and 1990s became the standard, extending the approach to quadratic, conic, and semidefinite programming.

Applications

Interior-point methods are implemented in commercial and open-source solvers and used for very large problems in logistics, finance, engineering design, and machine learning. See also Simplex algorithm and Linear programming.
