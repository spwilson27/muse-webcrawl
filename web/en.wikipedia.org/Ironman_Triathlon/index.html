An Ironman Triathlon is a long-distance triathlon race organised by the World Triathlon Corporation (WTC). It consists of a 2.4-mile (3.86 km) swim, a 112-mile (180.25 km) bicycle ride and a marathon 26.22-mile (42.2 km) run, raced in that order — a total of 140.6 miles (226.3 km). It is widely considered one of the most difficult one-day sporting events in the world.

History

The idea arose in 1977 during an awards banquet for the Waikiki Swim Club in Honolulu, when athletes debated whether swimmers, cyclists or runners were the fittest. US Navy Commander John Collins proposed combining three existing Oahu endurance races into a single event. The first Ironman was held on 18 February 1978 with 15 competitors; 12 finished, and Gordon Haller became the first "Ironman", finishing in 11 hours 46 minutes.

In 1981 the race moved from Oahu to Kailua-Kona on the Island of Hawaii, where the Ironman World Championship was held for decades. From 2023 the world championship has been split between venues, with the men's and women's races rotating between Kona and Nice, France.

The race today

Most Ironman events have a strict time limit of 16 or 17 hours; athletes must complete each leg within cutoff times. The series includes dozens of full-distance races worldwide each year plus the shorter Ironman 70.3 format (half the distances). Competitors qualify for the World Championship through age-group slots at qualifying races.

Records

Course records at Kona have fallen steadily; the men's record stands under 7 hours 40 minutes (Gustav Iden, 2022) and the women's under 8 hours 25 minutes. The fastest Ironman-branded race times overall are quicker still, set on faster courses.
