Amitabha

Amitabha (Sanskrit: अमिताभ, "Infinite Light"; Chinese: 阿彌陀佛, Amítuófó; Japanese: 阿弥陀仏, Amida Butsu; Tibetan: འོད་དཔག་མེད་, Öpakmé) is a celestial Buddha in Mahayana Buddhism, the principal Buddha of Pure Land Buddhism. According to the Larger Sutra of Immeasurable Life, Amitabha was, in a former life, the monk Dharmakara, who made 48 vows to save all beings; on fulfilling them he became a Buddha presiding over the western Pure Land of Sukhavati ("Land of Bliss"), where conditions are ideal for attaining enlightenment. Devotees aspire to be reborn there through faith and the recitation of his name — the nembutsu in Japanese (Namu Amida Butsu, "Hail to Amitabha Buddha") and nianfo in Chinese.

Scriptural basis

Three sutras form the textual core of Amitabha devotion: the Longer Sukhāvatīvyūha (Sutra of Immeasurable Life), recounting Dharmakara's vows and the wonders of Sukhavati; the shorter Sukhāvatīvyūha (Amitabha Sutra), describing the Pure Land's jeweled trees, lotus ponds, and birds that sing the Dharma; and the Contemplation Sutra, teaching visualization of Amitabha and rebirth in his land. Sculptures of Amitabha appear in Gandhara by the 2nd century CE, among the earliest Mahayana images.

Practice and schools

In China, teachers such as Huiyuan (who formed a White Lotus society on Mount Lu in 402 CE) and Shandao systematized Pure Land practice around single-minded recitation of the Buddha's name (nianfo). In Japan, Honen (1133–1212) founded the Jodo-shu school teaching exclusive nembutsu, and his disciple Shinran (1173–1263) founded Jodo Shinshu, emphasizing total reliance on Amitabha's "other-power" (tariki) and the 18th ("Primal") Vow, by which even a person who calls the name ten times — or, in Shinran's radical reading, even once with sincere entrusting — is assured rebirth. Jodo Shinshu became Japan's largest Buddhist tradition. In Tibetan Buddhism, Amitabha (Öpakmé) and his form Amitayus are central to longevity practices and the aspiration to rebirth in Dewachen (Sukhavati).

Iconography

Amitabha is typically shown in meditation posture (dhyana mudra), sometimes holding an alms bowl, red in colour in Tibetan iconography as the Buddha of the lotus family and lord of the west. He is frequently flanked by the bodhisattvas Avalokiteshvara (compassion) and Mahasthamaprapta (wisdom-power), and in East Asia massive outdoor statues — such as the 33-metre bronze at Kamakura (the Kamakura Daibutsu is Amitabha) and the 81-metre Donglin Great Buddha — attest his popularity.
