Shenyang

Shenyang (沈阳) is the capital and largest city of Liaoning Province in northeastern China.

Shenyang (Chinese: 沈阳; pinyin: Shěnyáng), formerly known by its Manchu name Mukden, is the capital and largest city of China's Liaoning Province, as well as the largest city in Northeast China by urban population. Shenyang is a sub-provincial city and a major industrial, commercial and transportation centre with a metropolitan population of around nine million.

History

Shenyang rose to prominence in the early seventeenth century when the Manchu leader Nurhaci made it his capital in 1625 and built the Mukden Palace; his son Hong Taiji proclaimed the Qing dynasty there before the Qing moved their capital to Beijing in 1644. Mukden remained the dynasty's secondary capital. In 1905 the city was the site of the Battle of Mukden, the largest land battle of the Russo-Japanese War. On September 18, 1931, the Mukden Incident — a staged explosion on the Japanese-owned railway — provided the pretext for Japan's invasion of Manchuria.

Economy and sights

Under the People's Republic, Shenyang became one of China's principal heavy-industry bases, producing machine tools, aircraft and equipment, and it remains an industrial hub of the northeast. Its chief historic sights are the early Qing imperial sites: the Mukden Palace and the Zhaoling (Northern) and Fuling (Eastern) tombs of the first Qing emperors, which together form a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the Imperial Palaces and Tombs of the Ming and Qing Dynasties.
