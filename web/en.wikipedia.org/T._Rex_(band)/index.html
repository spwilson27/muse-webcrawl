T. Rex (band)

Also known as: Tyrannosaurus Rex
Origin: London, England
Years active: 1967–1977
Genres: Glam rock, folk rock, psychedelic folk
Key member: Marc Bolan

T. Rex were an English rock band formed in London in 1967 by singer-songwriter and guitarist Marc Bolan. The band began as Tyrannosaurus Rex, an acoustic psychedelic folk duo of Bolan and percussionist Steve Peregrin Took, releasing albums of whimsical, Tolkien-influenced songs. After Took's departure and the arrival of Mickey Finn, Bolan shortened the name to T. Rex, plugged in an electric guitar, and pioneered glam rock.

History

The breakthrough came with the single "Ride a White Swan" (1970), followed by a string of UK hits including "Hot Love", "Get It On", "Telegram Sam", "Metal Guru" and "Children of the Revolution". The albums Electric Warrior (1971) and The Slider (1972) are considered landmarks of the glam era, and the frenzy around the band was dubbed "T. Rextasy".

Bolan's success waned in the mid-1970s amid changing tastes and his declining health, though the band continued recording, releasing Dandy in the Underworld in 1977. Bolan died in a car crash in Barnes, London, on 16 September 1977, two weeks before his 30th birthday, effectively ending the band.

Legacy

T. Rex influenced punk rock, Britpop and numerous alternative acts. "Get It On" (released as "Bang a Gong (Get It On)" in the US) remains their best-known song internationally. The band was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2020.
