The Screen Actors Guild – American Federation of Television and Radio Artists (SAG-AFTRA) is an American labour union representing approximately 160,000 film and television actors, journalists, radio personalities, recording artists, singers, voice actors and other media professionals worldwide. It was formed on March 30, 2012, by the merger of the Screen Actors Guild (SAG) and the American Federation of Television and Radio Artists (AFTRA).

History

The two predecessor unions had considered merging several times, and a 2003 referendum failed narrowly. In 2012 members of both unions voted overwhelmingly to combine, uniting performers who had been split as television work blurred the line between the two jurisdictions. The merged union negotiates collective bargaining agreements covering wages, working conditions, residuals and streaming compensation with the Alliance of Motion Picture and Television Producers.

2023 strike

On July 14, 2023, SAG-AFTRA went on strike over a labour dispute with the studios, joining the Writers Guild of America strike that had begun in May. Central issues included streaming residuals and limits on the use of artificial intelligence to replicate performers' likenesses. The strike, the union's first against film and television employers since 1980, lasted 118 days and ended with a tentative agreement on November 8, 2023.

Governance

SAG-AFTRA is governed by a national board and a president elected by the membership. Fran Drescher, star of The Nanny, was elected president in 2021 and re-elected in 2023, leading the union through the 2023 strike. The union also administers the SAG Awards (Screen Actors Guild Awards), presented annually since 1995.
