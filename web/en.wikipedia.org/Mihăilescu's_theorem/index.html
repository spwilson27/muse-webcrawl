Mihăilescu's theorem

Mihăilescu's theorem (formerly called Catalan's conjecture) is a theorem in number theory stating that the only solution in integers of the equation

ap − bq = 1

for a, b > 0 and exponents p, q > 1 is a = 3, p = 2, b = 2, q = 3: that is, 32 − 23 = 9 − 8 = 1. In other words, 8 and 9 are the only consecutive perfect powers greater than 1.

History

The Belgian mathematician Eugène Charles Catalan stated the conjecture in 1844. Partial results accumulated over 150 years: Lebesgue showed in 1850 that no solution exists with p = q implications for certain cases; Ko Chao proved in 1965 that 2 and 3 are the only consecutive powers with restricted parity; and Robert Tijdeman showed in 1976, using Baker's method, that only finitely many solutions exist. The full proof was finally given in 2002 by the Romanian mathematician Preda Mihăilescu, and published in 2004 in the Journal für die reine und angewandte Mathematik.

Proof sketch

Mihăilescu's proof uses cyclotomic fields and the theory of algebraic number theory developed by Ernst Kummer, combined with bounds arising from Tijdeman's work and results of Maurice Mignotte. A key step shows that the exponents p and q must divide each other in a strong sense ("double Wieferich prime pairs" arise in one case analysis), ultimately forcing the small solution.

Related results

Pillai's conjecture generalises the statement to differences between perfect powers, and the theorem is related to results on Catalan numbers and the abc conjecture, which implies Mihăilescu's theorem for all sufficiently large powers.
