A Gun for Sale is a 1936 thriller novel by British author Graham Greene, published in the United States as This Gun for Hire. One of the works Greene classified as an "entertainment", it follows a cold, hare-lipped assassin named Raven who, betrayed by his employers after murdering a politician, hunts them down while being pursued by police — and finds an unexpected ally in a chorus girl, Anne Crowder. The novel foreshadows Greene's mature themes of pity, betrayal, and moral ambiguity.

Plot

Raven, an orphaned, disfigured professional killer, assassinates a European minister of war in the hope (encouraged by agents of a British armaments firm) that the killing will help provoke a war from which arms manufacturers will profit. Paid in stolen banknotes whose numbers are circulated, he is betrayed by the intermediary Cholmondeley (pronounced "Chumley") on behalf of the steel magnate Sir Marcus. Fleeing the police led by Detective-Sergeant Mather, Raven kidnaps Mather's fiancée, Anne Crowder, a showgirl engaged at a Midland pantomime, and gradually — through her kindness — becomes emotionally entangled for the first time. Trapped by police in a railway siding office, Raven extracts a confession from Cholmondeley before being shot dead; Anne, though grieving, is free to marry Mather honestly.

Background and themes

Written against the backdrop of European rearmament and the approach of the Second World War, the novel attacks the profiteering of the armaments industry — its conspiracy plot echoes contemporary "merchants of death" controversies — while tracing Raven's damaged humanity. Greene later connected Raven's harelip and loveless upbringing to his method of making an unsympathetic protagonist credible.

Adaptations

The novel was famously filmed by Paramount in 1942 as This Gun for Hire, directed by Frank Tuttle and starring Alan Ladd (in his breakthrough role) as Raven and Veronica Lake as the heroine, reimagined as a nightclub singer-magician. It was also adapted for American television in 1957 and as a 1991 TV movie starring Robert Wagner.
