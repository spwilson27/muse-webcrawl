Selection Sunday

Teams gather to watch the Selection Sunday broadcast, when the NCAA tournament field and bracket are revealed.

Selection Sunday is the day when the selection committees of the National Collegiate Athletic Association (NCAA) announce the teams that will participate in the NCAA Division I men's and women's basketball tournaments, together with the seeding and bracket assignments. It takes place on the Sunday in mid-March immediately before the tournaments begin, marking the start of the "March Madness" period.

Process

For the men's tournament, 68 teams are selected: the champions of the 32 Division I conferences receive automatic bids by winning their conference tournaments, and the committee chooses the 36 best remaining teams as at-large entries. The committee seeds the field into four regions, with the four lowest-seeded automatic qualifiers and four lowest-seeded at-large teams contesting the "First Four" play-in games. Teams on the margin of selection are described as being "on the bubble"; those that narrowly miss out are often invited to the National Invitation Tournament instead.

Broadcast

The selection show, broadcast by CBS (and in some years simulcast with Turner networks), reveals the bracket region by region to an audience of millions, and is watched at team gatherings and in sports bars nationwide. Within hours, fans fill out bracket predictions, a ritual that has become one of the largest informal betting and office-pool phenomena in American sport. In 2018 the format was briefly changed to announce all qualifying teams alphabetically before the bracket, which drew criticism, and the traditional reveal was restored the following year.
