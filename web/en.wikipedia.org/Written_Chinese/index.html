Written Chinese

Written Chinese is the writing system used for the Chinese languages, based on Chinese characters (hanzi). It comprises Classical Chinese, the literary standard used for two millennia, and modern vernacular written Chinese based on Mandarin, standardised in the early 20th century.

Characters

Characters are largely logographic: most combine a semantic radical with a phonetic component. Literate readers know roughly 3,000–4,000 characters. Two character sets are in use: traditional characters in Taiwan, Hong Kong and Macau, and simplified characters, promoted in mainland China from the 1950s and also used in Singapore.

Features

Written Chinese is largely mutually intelligible across spoken varieties because it is not tied to one pronunciation, though written Cantonese uses some distinct characters and grammar. Text historically ran vertically; horizontal left-to-right writing is now standard. Punctuation was adopted in the 20th century under Western influence.
