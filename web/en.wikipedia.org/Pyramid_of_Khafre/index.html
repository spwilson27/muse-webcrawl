Pyramid of Khafre

The Pyramid of Khafre is the second-tallest and second-largest of the three pyramids of the Giza pyramid complex in Egypt, built as the tomb of the Fourth Dynasty pharaoh Khafre (reigned c. 2558–2532 BCE, also known as Chephren). It stands on the Giza Plateau southwest of the Great Pyramid of Khufu, and the Great Sphinx is widely believed to have been carved as part of Khafre's complex.

Size and construction

The pyramid originally rose about 143.5 metres (471 ft) and today stands about 136.4 metres tall, with a base of roughly 215 metres per side. Because it sits on higher bedrock and has a slightly steeper angle of inclination (about 53°), it can appear taller than Khufu's pyramid. It uniquely retains a substantial cap of its smooth Tura limestone casing near the summit, the casing having been stripped elsewhere in antiquity and the medieval period. Its core was built of local limestone blocks, with a lower course of red granite casing.

Interior

Two entrances on the north face lead to descending corridors; the lower passage opens into a subsidiary chamber, while the upper corridor reaches the main burial chamber, cut into the bedrock and roofed with gabled limestone slabs. The chamber contained a sunken granite sarcophagus, found empty when Giovanni Belzoni entered in 1818. Unlike Khufu's pyramid, the internal plan is comparatively simple.

The complex

Khafre's mortuary temple, valley temple, and causeway are among the best preserved of the Old Kingdom. The valley temple, built of massive limestone blocks faced with granite, yielded the celebrated diorite statue of Khafre enthroned with the Horus falcon, now in the Egyptian Museum in Cairo. The Great Sphinx stands adjacent to the causeway and valley temple; geological and stylistic evidence associates it with Khafre's reign, though its attribution remains debated.
