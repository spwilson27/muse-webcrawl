Red herring

A "red herring" is literally a strongly cured, reddish smoked fish, from which the figurative sense derives.

A red herring is something that misleads or distracts from a relevant or important question. It may be either a logical fallacy or a literary device that leads readers or audiences toward a false conclusion. A red herring may be used intentionally, as in mystery fiction or as part of rhetorical strategies (e.g., in politics), or may be used in argumentation inadvertently.

Origin of the idiom

A red herring is a type of strong-smelling smoked fish, reddened by the curing process. The figurative sense is rooted in the idea of dragging such a fish across a trail to divert hunting hounds from a scent. The journalist William Cobbett is credited with popularising the image in 1807, when he wrote of having used a red herring to throw hounds off the scent as a boy, and used the metaphor to criticise the English press for diverting attention from the defeat of Napoleon. Although later hunting writers debated whether red herrings were actually used in training hounds, the idiom was firmly established by the late 19th century.

As a logical fallacy

In informal logic, a red herring is an argument that introduces an irrelevant topic in order to divert attention from the original issue. It is a subtype of the relevance fallacies: the arguer shifts the discussion to a different subject — often one that provokes an emotional response — and then claims victory or implies the original point has been addressed. Unlike a straw man argument, which misrepresents the original position, a red herring abandons it for a new topic altogether.

As a narrative device

In fiction and drama, a red herring is a clue intended to mislead the audience toward an incorrect solution or inference. Mystery writers in particular plant false suspects and misleading evidence so that the true reveal is surprising yet retrospectively fair. Agatha Christie was noted for her masterful deployment of red herrings, and the device is standard in thrillers, horror and detective stories.
