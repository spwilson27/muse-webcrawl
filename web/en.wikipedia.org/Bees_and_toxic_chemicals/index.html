Bees and toxic chemicals concerns the effects of pesticides and other toxic substances on bees, including the Western honey bee (Apis mellifera) and thousands of wild bee species. Bees are vital pollinators of crops and wild plants, and their exposure to agrochemicals has been implicated in widespread declines in bee populations.

Pesticide exposure

Bees may be exposed to chemicals through direct contact with sprays, by collecting contaminated nectar and pollen, or through systemic insecticides that move through plant tissues. Neonicotinoids such as imidacloprid, clothianidin and thiamethoxam act on the insect nervous system and can cause disorientation, impaired learning and failure to return to the hive at sublethal doses.

Effects on colonies

Chronic exposure can reduce queen production, weaken immune defences and increase susceptibility to parasites such as the Varroa mite and to viruses. Interactions between multiple chemicals, poor nutrition and pathogens are thought to contribute to colony collapse disorder.

Regulation

The European Union restricted several neonicotinoids in 2013 and later banned their outdoor use, while other jurisdictions have adopted labelling rules, buffer zones and restrictions on spraying during bloom. Research continues into integrated pest management and bee-friendly farming practices.
