Ready-to-use therapeutic food (RUTF) is a type of therapeutic food used to treat severe acute malnutrition in children, typically as a lipid-based nutrient supplement paste. Because it needs no cooking, no added water and resists bacterial contamination, it can be given at home by caregivers, transforming treatment that once required long hospital stays with therapeutic milks.

Composition and development

The best-known RUTF is Plumpy'Nut, developed in 1996 by French paediatrician André Briend and manufactured by the company Nutriset. The standard recipe combines ground peanuts, powdered milk, sugar and vegetable oil fortified with vitamins and minerals, yielding a peanut-butter-like paste of around 520–550 kcal per 100 g, packaged in foil sachets with a shelf life of up to two years without refrigeration. Locally produced equivalents (such as eeZeePaste and various national brands) are now made in countries including Malawi, Niger, Ethiopia and India.

Use and impact

RUTF is the cornerstone of community-based management of acute malnutrition (CMAM), adopted by UNICEF, the World Health Organization and Médecins Sans Frontières: children without medical complications are treated as outpatients with weekly rations and check-ups, achieving recovery rates commonly above 85–90%. Field trials in Malawi and large-scale use during the 2005 Niger food crisis demonstrated outcomes comparable or superior to inpatient care at far lower cost and coverage. Debates continue over cost, patents and franchising, local production versus imports, and adaptation of formulations to local ingredients and tastes.
