Odin (Old Norse: Óðinn) is a widely revered god in Norse mythology, the chief of the Æsir and ruler of Asgard. He is associated with wisdom, healing, death, royalty, the gallows, knowledge, war, victory, sorcery, poetry, frenzy, and the runic alphabet, and is the husband of the goddess Frigg. In Norse texts, Odin is depicted as one-eyed and long-bearded, frequently wielding a spear named Gungnir and wearing a cloak and a broad hat. He is accompanied by the animal companions the wolves Geri and Freki and the ravens Huginn and Muninn, who bring him information from all of Midgard, and he rides the flying eight-legged steed Sleipnir across the sky and into the underworld.

Mythology

Odin is a son of Borr and the jötunn Bestla, and with his brothers Vili and Vé he slew the primordial giant Ymir and fashioned the world from his body. In pursuit of wisdom he sacrificed an eye at Mímir's well and hanged himself on the world tree Yggdrasil for nine nights, pierced by his own spear, to win knowledge of the runes — a self-sacrifice recounted in the Hávamál. He stole the mead of poetry from the giants and gave it to gods and humans. Odin presides over Valhalla, where half of those slain in battle (the einherjar) feast and train for Ragnarök, while Freyja receives the other half. Despite his foreknowledge, he cannot avert his doom: at Ragnarök he is swallowed by the monstrous wolf Fenrir and avenged by his son Víðarr.

Attestation and worship

Odin corresponds to the Old English Wōden and Old High German Wuotan, all from Proto-Germanic *Wōdanaz ("lord of frenzy"); the weekday Wednesday preserves his name. Tacitus, in Germania (1st century CE), identified the chief Germanic god with Mercury. Adam of Bremen described sacrifices to Odin ("Wodan") at the Temple at Uppsala. Place names compounded with Odin's name occur across Scandinavia and Anglo-Saxon England. The principal sources are the Poetic Edda and Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda (13th century), along with skaldic poetry, sagas, and runic inscriptions.

Modern influence

Odin has been a recurring figure in art, literature, and music — from early modern antiquarianism through Wagner's Ring cycle (as Wotan) to modern comics, novels, video games, and the Marvel films (portrayed by Anthony Hopkins). He is also venerated in modern Heathenry (Ásatrú), for some adherents as the All-Father.
