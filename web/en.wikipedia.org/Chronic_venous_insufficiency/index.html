Chronic venous insufficiency

Specialty: Vascular medicine

Symptoms: Leg swelling, aching, skin change, ulcers

Chronic venous insufficiency is a condition in which veins in the legs do not efficiently return blood to the heart, usually because valves are damaged or vein walls are weakened. Blood pools in the lower limbs, raising venous pressure.

Signs and causes

Symptoms include swelling, heaviness, cramps, varicose veins, itching, brown skin discoloration, eczema, lipodermatosclerosis, and in advanced cases venous ulcers near the ankle. Risk factors include age, pregnancy, obesity, prolonged standing, family history, and prior deep vein thrombosis.

Treatment

Management focuses on leg elevation, exercise, weight control, skin care, and compression stockings. Selected patients benefit from endovenous ablation, sclerotherapy, or surgery. Ulcers require wound care and treatment of infection when present.
