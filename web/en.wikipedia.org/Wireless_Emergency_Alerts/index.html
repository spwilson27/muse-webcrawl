Wireless Emergency Alerts

Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA), formerly known as the Commercial Mobile Alert System (CMAS), is an alerting network system in the United States that allows authorized government agencies to send emergency messages to mobile devices in a defined geographic area. The system is based on cell broadcast technology, so alerts are delivered to all capable phones connected to cell towers in the affected area rather than to individual phone numbers.

Operation

WEA was established under the Warning, Alert, and Response Network (WARN) Act of 2006 and is operated jointly by the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) and the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA). Alerts are sent through FEMA's Integrated Public Alert and Warning System (IPAWS).

There are several classes of alert: Presidential alerts (National Alerts), which cannot be opted out of; Imminent Threat alerts for extreme weather and other emergencies; AMBER alerts for abducted children; and Public Safety alerts. Users may opt out of all but National Alerts. WEA messages were originally limited to 90 characters; later enhancements (WEA 3.0) increased the limit to 360 characters on 4G LTE and later networks, added Spanish-language versions, and improved geographic targeting.

Notable uses

The system is widely used for tornado, flash flood, hurricane and tsunami warnings. It drew attention in January 2018 when a false ballistic missile alert was mistakenly transmitted in Hawaii, prompting reforms to alerting procedures.
