Adventure racing

Adventure racing is a multidisciplinary team sport in which competitors navigate an unmarked wilderness course using map and compass, typically combining trekking, mountain biking, paddling and rope work over events lasting from hours to more than a week.

History

The sport grew from events such as New Zealand's Coast to Coast (1983) and the Raid Gauloises (1989), and was popularised by the Eco-Challenge races of the 1990s.

Format

Teams of two to five, often mixed-gender, must stay together and pass checkpoints in order; sleep strategy and navigation skill are decisive. Expedition races form the Adventure Racing World Series, culminating in a world championship. See also Orienteering and Multisport race.
