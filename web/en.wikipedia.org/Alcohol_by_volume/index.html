Alcohol by volume

Alcohol by volume (abbreviated ABV, abv, or alc/vol) is a standard measure used worldwide to express how much alcohol (ethanol) is contained in a given volume of an alcoholic beverage. It is defined as the number of millilitres of pure ethanol present in 100 ml of solution at 20 °C (68 °F), expressed as a percentage.

Typical values

Beers usually range from about 3% to 7% ABV (with strong craft styles considerably higher), wines from about 9% to 16%, and distilled spirits are commonly bottled at 40% ABV (80 US proof), though they range widely. Low-alcohol and alcohol-free drinks are typically below 0.5% ABV.

Measurement and regulation

ABV can be determined from the change in specific gravity during fermentation using a hydrometer, or by laboratory distillation and densitometry; modern producers use instruments such as oscillating U-tube density meters and infrared analysers. Labelling ABV is legally required in most jurisdictions, with permitted tolerances varying by country and beverage class.

Proof

In the United States, "proof" is defined as twice the ABV percentage. Historically, British proof spirit was defined by a gunpowder test, with 100 degrees proof corresponding to about 57.15% ABV.
