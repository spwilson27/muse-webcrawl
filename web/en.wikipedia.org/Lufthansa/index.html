Deutsche Lufthansa AG, commonly shortened to Lufthansa, is the flag carrier of Germany. When combined with its subsidiaries, it ranks among the largest airline groups in Europe in terms of passengers carried and fleet size. Lufthansa is one of the five founding members of the Star Alliance, the world's largest airline alliance, formed in 1997.

History

The name Lufthansa derives from the earlier Deutsche Luft Hansa, founded in 1926 and Germany's flag carrier until 1945. The new company was founded in Cologne in 1953 and began scheduled operations in April 1955, after West Germany regained air sovereignty; East Germany's airline also used the Lufthansa name until it was renamed Interflug. Lufthansa was a state-owned enterprise until 1994 and was fully privatised by 1997.

Group and operations

The Lufthansa Group includes Austrian Airlines, Swiss International Air Lines, Brussels Airlines, Eurowings and stakes in other carriers, plus Lufthansa Cargo and Lufthansa Technik, one of the world's largest aircraft maintenance providers. The airline's main hubs are Frankfurt Airport and Munich Airport. Its crane logo, designed in 1918 by Otto Firle, is one of the oldest airline trademarks still in use.

Fleet

Lufthansa operates a mixed Airbus and Boeing fleet, including the Airbus A380 (returned to service after the COVID-19 pandemic), Boeing 747-8, Airbus A350 and A340, alongside narrow-body A320-family aircraft. The group carried over 120 million passengers annually in peak pre-pandemic years.
