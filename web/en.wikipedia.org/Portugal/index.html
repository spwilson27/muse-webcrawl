Portugal, officially the Portuguese Republic, is a country in southwestern Europe, occupying the western part of the Iberian Peninsula. It is bordered by Spain to the north and east and by the Atlantic Ocean to the west and south. Portugal also includes the autonomous Atlantic archipelagos of the Azores and Madeira. Its capital and largest city is Lisbon; other major cities include Porto, Braga, Coimbra and Funchal.

History

The territory of Portugal has been inhabited since prehistoric times and was successively settled by Celts, Romans, Germanic peoples and Moors. The County of Portugal emerged in the 9th century, and the Kingdom of Portugal was established in 1139, with Afonso Henriques as its first king; its independence was recognised by the Treaty of Zamora (1143) and by the papacy in 1179. Portugal's borders, finalised by the Treaty of Alcañices in 1297, are among the oldest in Europe.

In the 15th and 16th centuries Portugal pioneered the Age of Discovery: sailors such as Bartolomeu Dias, Vasco da Gama and Ferdinand Magellan opened sea routes to Africa, India, Brazil and East Asia, and Portugal built the first global maritime empire. After periods of dynastic union with Spain (1580–1640), the 1755 Lisbon earthquake, Napoleonic invasion and the loss of Brazil in 1822, the monarchy fell in 1910. The authoritarian Estado Novo regime ended with the Carnation Revolution of 1974, after which Portugal became a democracy and decolonised its African empire. Portugal joined the European Community in 1986 and adopted the euro in 1999.

Geography and climate

Mainland Portugal is divided roughly in two by the river Tagus: the north is mountainous and greener, while the south, including the Alentejo and the Algarve coast, is drier and flatter. The climate is Mediterranean, with hot dry summers and mild wet winters, moderated by the Atlantic. The highest point on the mainland is the Serra da Estrela (1,993 m); Mount Pico in the Azores reaches 2,351 m.

Economy and culture

Portugal has a developed, service-based economy in which tourism, textiles, cork (of which it is the world's largest producer), wine — including Port wine from the Douro valley — and increasingly technology play major roles. Portuguese is spoken by more than 250 million people worldwide. The country's culture is noted for fado music, azulejo tilework, and writers such as Luís de Camões, Fernando Pessoa and Nobel laureate José Saramago.
