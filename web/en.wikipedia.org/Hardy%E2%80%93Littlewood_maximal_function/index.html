Hardy–Littlewood maximal function

FieldHarmonic analysis, real analysis
IntroducedHardy and Littlewood (1930)
Key resultWeak (1,1) and strong (p,p) bounds

In mathematics, the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function is an operator used in real and harmonic analysis. For a locally integrable function f on Euclidean space, the maximal function Mf at a point x is the supremum, over all balls centred at x, of the average of |f| over the ball.

Maximal theorem

The Hardy–Littlewood maximal theorem states that the maximal operator is of weak type (1,1) and bounded on Lp for every p > 1, with a norm estimate depending only on p and the dimension. Norbert Wiener proved the one-dimensional result and the n-dimensional version followed.

Applications

The maximal theorem is a cornerstone of analysis. It is used to prove the Lebesgue differentiation theorem (averages over shrinking balls converge to the function value almost everywhere), to control singular integrals, and in the study of partial differential equations and ergodic theory, where a maximal ergodic theorem parallels it.

Variants

Variants include the uncentred maximal function (supremum over all balls containing x), dyadic maximal functions on grids, and fractional maximal operators. Covering lemmas such as the Vitali and Besicovitch lemmas underlie the proofs.
