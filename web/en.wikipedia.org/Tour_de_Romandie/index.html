The Tour de Romandie is a road cycling stage race held annually in Romandy, the French-speaking part of western Switzerland. Founded in 1947 to mark the 50th anniversary of Swiss cycling, it forms part of the UCI World Tour and, falling in late April, serves as a key preparation race for the Giro d'Italia.

Race character

The race typically consists of a short prologue, four or five road stages and an individual time trial, with mountain stages in the Jura and the Alps and frequent bad spring weather — snow has repeatedly forced stage shortenings. Time-trialling strength and climbing decide the general classification: past winners such as Bradley Wiggins (2012) and Chris Froome (2013) used the race as a stepping stone toward Tour de France victory the same summer.

History

Irishman Stephen Roche remains the only three-time winner (1983, 1984, 1987). Other champions include Ferdinand Kübler, Hugo Koblet, Eddy Merckx, Laurent Jalabert, Tyler Hamilton, Cadel Evans, Primož Roglič (twice) and Adam Yates. A women's Tour de Romandie Féminin was launched in 2022 as part of the Women's World Tour.
