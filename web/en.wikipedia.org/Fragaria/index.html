Fragaria

Scientific classification
Kingdom: Plantae
Order: Rosales
Family: Rosaceae
Subfamily: Rosoideae
Genus: Fragaria L.
Fragaria vesca, the woodland strawberry, widespread in the Northern Hemisphere.

Fragaria is a genus of flowering plants in the rose family, Rosaceae, commonly known as strawberries for their edible fruits. There are more than 20 described species and many hybrids and cultivars, found throughout the temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere and in South America. The plants are low-growing perennials that spread by stolons (runners) and bear trifoliate leaves, white (occasionally pink) five-petalled flowers, and accessory fruits studded with achenes.

Species and ploidy

Fragaria species occur at several ploidy levels. Diploid species include the woodland strawberry F. vesca and F. viridis. The musk strawberry F. moschata is hexaploid, and the beach strawberry F. chiloensis and Virginia strawberry F. virginiana are octoploid. The modern garden strawberry, Fragaria × ananassa, is an octoploid hybrid of F. chiloensis and F. virginiana that arose in Brittany, France, in the 18th century and has almost entirely replaced earlier kinds in commercial production.

Cultivation and uses

Strawberries are grown worldwide for their sweet, aromatic fruit, eaten fresh and used in preserves, desserts and flavourings. The genus is also a model system in plant genomics: the diploid F. vesca genome was sequenced in 2011 because of its small size and short generation time, aiding breeding of the cultivated strawberry.
