Magnesium

Symbol: Mg
Atomic number: 12
Group: Alkaline earth metal
Melting point: 650 °C
Density: 1.738 g/cm³

Magnesium is a chemical element with the symbol Mg and atomic number 12. It is a shiny gray solid and the eighth most abundant element in the Earth's crust. Magnesium is an alkaline earth metal whose ion, Mg²⁺, is essential to all cells and to hundreds of enzymes.

Properties

Magnesium is light — about two-thirds the density of aluminium — and tarnishes slightly in air, forming a thin oxide layer that slows further corrosion. It burns with a brilliant white flame, a property once used in photographic flash powder and still seen in flares and fireworks. The free metal is produced mainly by electrolysis of magnesium chloride from seawater and brines.

Uses

Magnesium alloys are used where low weight matters: aircraft and car parts, laptops, cameras, and power tools. Magnesium compounds serve as refractory lining in steel furnaces (magnesia), in cement, fertilisers, and medicine — magnesium hydroxide is "milk of magnesia," and magnesium sulfate is Epsom salt.

Biological role

Magnesium is required for the synthesis of DNA, RNA, and proteins, for energy metabolism through ATP (which binds magnesium), and sits at the centre of the chlorophyll molecule in plants. Adult humans contain about 25 grams, mostly in bone; deficiency can cause muscle cramps, arrhythmias, and metabolic disturbances.
