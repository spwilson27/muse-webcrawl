Scooter (motorcycle)

A classic Italian-style motor scooter.

A scooter (also called a motor scooter) is a motorcycle with an underbone or step-through frame, a seat, a transmission that shifts without the operator having to operate a clutch lever, a platform for the operator's feet, and a method of operation that emphasises comfort and fuel economy. Elements of the scooter design date to at least 1914, but the type became popular worldwide after the Second World War.

Design

Scooters typically have small wheels, an enclosed engine and bodywork that protects the rider from road spray, and a leg shield at the front. Engines range from 50 cc mopeds to "maxi-scooters" of 850 cc with automatic (CVT) transmissions. Storage is commonly provided under the seat.

History

The postwar Vespa, introduced by Piaggio in 1946, and the rival Lambretta of Innocenti defined the classic scooter and became cultural icons, associated in Britain with the Mod subculture of the 1960s. In Asia, scooters and underbone motorcycles became the dominant form of personal transport, with manufacturers such as Honda, Yamaha, and Bajaj producing them in the tens of millions.

Electric scooters

Since the 2010s, electric scooters with removable battery packs and swappable-battery networks have grown rapidly in cities, especially in Taiwan, China, India, and Europe.
