IMAX

Type: Motion picture film format / theatre system
Founded: 1967, Montreal, Canada
Company: IMAX Corporation
Film gauge: 70 mm, 15 perforations per frame

IMAX is a proprietary system of high-resolution cameras, film formats, projectors, and theatres known for very large screens and steep stadium seating. The name derives from "Image Maximum". The company behind it began as Multiscreen Corporation, founded in Montreal for Expo 67, where its multi-projector film Tiger Child premiered the concept in 1970 at Expo 70 in Osaka.

Technology

Classic IMAX film runs horizontally through the projector, 15 perforations per frame on 70 mm stock — an image area about ten times that of standard 35 mm — and is advanced by a rolling-loop mechanism against a vacuum-flattened field. Screens in purpose-built theatres can exceed 22 by 16 metres. Digital IMAX, introduced in 2008, uses dual projectors; IMAX with Laser (2014) added 4K laser projection and 12-channel sound. IMAX-certified cameras, including digital cameras approved from 2020, shoot sequences for feature films such as Christopher Nolan's productions and Dune.

History

Early venues were in museums and science centres showing nature documentaries. Hollywood features expanded from 2000 with digitally remastered (DMR) releases, beginning with Apollo 13 in 2002. By the 2020s more than 1,700 IMAX theatres operated in over 80 countries.
