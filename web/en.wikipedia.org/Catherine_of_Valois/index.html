Catherine of Valois (1401–1437) was Queen of England from 1420 until 1422 as the wife of King Henry V. The youngest daughter of Charles VI of France, her marriage formed part of the Treaty of Troyes, which made Henry heir to the French throne. As the mother of Henry VI and, through her second union with Owen Tudor, the grandmother of Henry VII, she is the ancestress of the Tudor dynasty.


Catherine of Valois, Queen of England

Marriage to Henry V

Catherine's hand had been sought in negotiations since her childhood. Following Henry V's victory at Agincourt (1415) and his conquest of Normandy, the Treaty of Troyes (1420) settled the marriage, a large dowry, and Henry's recognition as regent and heir of France. The couple married at Troyes in June 1420. Catherine was crowned queen at Westminster in February 1421 and gave birth to the future Henry VI that December. Henry V died of dysentery in August 1422, leaving her a widow at twenty with an infant king.

Second union and the Tudors

Parliament, anxious that the queen dowager not remarry without consent, passed a statute forbidding it. Nevertheless, by about 1428–1429 Catherine had formed a union — probably a clandestine marriage — with Owen Tudor, a Welsh courtier in her household. They had at least five children, including Edmund Tudor, Earl of Richmond, and Jasper Tudor, Earl of Pembroke. Catherine died in 1437 at Bermondsey Abbey, shortly after childbirth.

Legacy

Through Edmund Tudor's marriage to Margaret Beaufort, Catherine became the grandmother of Henry VII, founder of the Tudor dynasty, giving her an outsized place in English history for so short a life. Her effigy was carried in procession at later royal funerals, and her remains were eventually reinterred in Henry V's chantry chapel in Westminster Abbey.
