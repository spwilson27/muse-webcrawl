Ross Brawn

Ross James Brawn OBE (born 23 November 1954) is a British Formula One managing director and former team owner and technical director. He is best known as the mastermind behind the technical and strategic successes of the Benetton and Ferrari teams during Michael Schumacher's championship years, and as owner of Brawn GP, which won both World Championships in its only season, 2009.

Career

Brawn began his career as a machinist and millwright before joining Williams in 1978. As technical director at Benetton, he oversaw the cars that won the 1994 and 1995 Drivers' Championships with Michael Schumacher. Following Schumacher to Ferrari in 1997, Brawn helped engineer an era of dominance: six consecutive Constructors' Championships (1999–2004) and five consecutive Drivers' titles (2000–2004).

Brawn GP and Mercedes

In 2009 Brawn bought the departing Honda team for a nominal sum, renamed it Brawn GP, and exploited the "double diffuser" concept to dominate the season; Jenson Button won the Drivers' Championship and the team the Constructors'. The team was sold to Mercedes for 2010, becoming the Mercedes works team; Brawn served as team principal until the end of 2013. In 2017 he returned to the sport as Formula One's Managing Director of Motorsports, overseeing the 2022 technical regulations, before retiring at the end of 2022.
