Longitudinal stability

Longitudinal stability
DomainFlight dynamics
ConcernsPitching motion of an aircraft
Key factorsCentre of gravity, tailplane, neutral point

In flight dynamics, longitudinal stability is the stability of an aircraft in the longitudinal, or pitching, plane — its tendency to return to a trimmed angle of attack after a disturbance such as a gust or a movement of the elevator.

Static stability

An aircraft is statically longitudinally stable if a nose-up disturbance produces a nose-down (restoring) pitching moment. This requires the centre of gravity to lie ahead of the neutral point, the point at which the total lift effectively acts for stability purposes. The horizontal tailplane contributes a restoring moment because a change in angle of attack alters the lift on the tail. The distance between the centre of gravity and the neutral point, expressed as a fraction of the mean aerodynamic chord, is called the static margin.

Dynamic stability

Even a statically stable aircraft may oscillate. Longitudinal dynamic modes include the short-period oscillation (a rapid pitching motion) and the phugoid, a slow exchange between speed and height at nearly constant angle of attack. Adequate damping of these modes is required for acceptable handling qualities.
