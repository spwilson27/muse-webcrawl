Victim blaming

Victim blaming occurs when the victim of a crime or wrongful act is held entirely or partially at fault for the harm that befell them. The study of victimology seeks to mitigate the prejudice against victims that has led to the perception that victims are in any way responsible for the actions of offenders.

History

The phrase was coined by psychologist William Ryan in his 1971 book Blaming the Victim, written as a response to Daniel Patrick Moynihan's 1965 report on the Black family in the United States. Ryan described victim blaming as an ideology used to justify racism and social injustice against Black people in the US.

Secondary victimisation

Secondary victimisation is the re-traumatisation of a victim through the responses of individuals and institutions, including victim blaming, disbelief, inappropriate post-assault behaviour by medical or legal personnel, and other negative responses.
