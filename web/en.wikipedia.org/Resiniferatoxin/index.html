Resiniferatoxin

Resiniferatoxin (RTX) is a naturally occurring chemical found in the resin spurge (Euphorbia resinifera), a cactus-like plant native to Morocco. It is an ultrapotent analogue of capsaicin, the active compound in chilli peppers, and scores around 16 billion Scoville heat units — roughly 500 to 1,000 times hotter than pure capsaicin.

Pharmacology

Resiniferatoxin activates the TRPV1 ion channel in sensory neurons, causing intense burning pain followed by desensitisation and, at sufficient doses, defunctionalisation of the neuron. This property has made it a research tool and a candidate therapeutic for severe chronic pain, including cancer pain, administered intrathecally in clinical trials.

Safety

Because of its extreme potency, resiniferatoxin is dangerous in minute quantities and must be handled with great care; exposure can cause severe burns and neurotoxic effects.
