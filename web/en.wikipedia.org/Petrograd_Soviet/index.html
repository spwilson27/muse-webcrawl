The Petrograd Soviet of Workers' and Soldiers' Deputies was a council (soviet) established in Petrograd (now Saint Petersburg) in March 1917 during the February Revolution. Alongside the Russian Provisional Government it formed one pole of the "dual power" (dvoevlastie) that governed Russia between the fall of the Tsar and the Bolshevik seizure of power in the October Revolution.

Formation and dual power

The Soviet was formed on 27 February (Old Style) 1917, the day mutinous soldiers of the Petrograd garrison joined striking workers, taking as its model the St Petersburg Soviet of 1905. Delegates were elected from factories and military units, with soldiers quickly outnumbering workers — giving the Soviet effective control over the capital's armed force. Its first act, Order No. 1 (1 March 1917), instructed soldiers to obey the Provisional Government only where its orders did not contradict the Soviet's, and to elect committees in their units — a step that fatally undermined officers' authority and cemented dual power.

1917

Dominated initially by Mensheviks and Socialist Revolutionaries, the Soviet and its Executive Committee broadly supported the Provisional Government while pressing it on peace, land and the convening of a Constituent Assembly. Through the crises of 1917 — the April Crisis, the July Days, and the Kornilov affair — support shifted toward the Bolsheviks, who won majorities in the Petrograd and Moscow soviets in September 1917. Leon Trotsky, recently released from prison, became the Soviet's chairman, and its Military Revolutionary Committee organised the insurrection of 25 October (7 November) 1917, transferring power to the Second All-Russian Congress of Soviets. After the revolution the Petrograd Soviet continued as the capital's soviet until the government moved to Moscow in 1918 and the city's soviets were reorganised.
