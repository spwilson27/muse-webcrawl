Michelle LaVaughn Robinson Obama (born January 17, 1964) is an American attorney and author who served as the first lady of the United States from 2009 to 2017, being married to Barack Obama, the 44th president of the United States. Raised on the South Side of Chicago, she is a graduate of Princeton University and Harvard Law School.

Early life and career

Obama grew up in a working-class family on Chicago's South Side; her father Fraser Robinson III was a city water plant employee and her mother Marian a homemaker. After graduating from Whitney Young High School, she studied sociology at Princeton and earned a Juris Doctor from Harvard Law School in 1988. She worked at the law firm Sidley Austin — where she met Barack Obama, whom she mentored as a summer associate — and later in Chicago city government, the nonprofit sector (Public Allies), and the University of Chicago, including its Medical Center.

First Lady

As first lady, Obama championed several signature initiatives: Let's Move! (2010), aimed at reducing childhood obesity; the White House Kitchen Garden; Joining Forces (2011), supporting military families, with Jill Biden; Reach Higher, encouraging young people to continue education beyond high school; and Let Girls Learn (2015), promoting girls' education worldwide. She was praised as a fashion icon and an effective campaign speaker, delivering notable Democratic National Convention addresses in 2008, 2012, 2016 ("When they go low, we go high"), and 2020.

After the White House

Her memoir Becoming (2018) became one of the best-selling memoirs ever published, and its accompanying tour and Netflix documentary extended her public profile. With her husband she founded Higher Ground Productions, which won an Academy Award for the documentary American Factory, and she published The Light We Carry in 2022.
