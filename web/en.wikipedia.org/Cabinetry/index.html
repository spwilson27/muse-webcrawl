Cabinetry

Cabinetry is the craft and trade of making cabinets—box-like furniture with doors, drawers or open shelving used for storage—particularly built-in kitchen, bathroom and storage cabinetry for buildings. A cabinetmaker is a skilled woodworker who designs, builds and installs such work, a specialization distinct from general carpentry and from furniture making, though the skills overlap.

Construction

Traditional cabinetry is built with a face frame of solid wood and carcass panels of plywood or solid timber joined with dovetails, mortise and tenon, or dowels; doors are commonly frame-and-panel construction. Modern mass-produced and flat-pack cabinetry uses engineered panels such as medium-density fibreboard (MDF) and particleboard with melamine or veneer surfaces, knock-down fittings, and standardized box sizes—in North America, base cabinets are typically 34.5 inches high before the countertop and 24 inches deep. Frameless ("European") construction dispenses with the face frame to maximize interior space.

Types and hardware

Cabinetry ranges from stock, semi-custom and fully custom grades. Hardware includes hinges (increasingly soft-close), full-extension drawer slides, lazy Susans, pull-out racks and integrated lighting. Cabinet refacing—replacing doors and veneering existing boxes—is a common lower-cost alternative to replacement.
