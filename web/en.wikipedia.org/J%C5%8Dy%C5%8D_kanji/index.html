Jōyō kanji

The jōyō kanji (常用漢字, "regular-use kanji") is the official list of Chinese characters and their readings maintained by the Japanese Ministry of Education for use in everyday writing. The current list, issued in 2010, contains 2,136 characters.

History

The jōyō kanji replaced the earlier tōyō kanji list of 1946, which had 1,850 characters. The first jōyō list was promulgated in 1981 with 1,945 characters; the 2010 revision added 196 characters and removed five, bringing the total to 2,136, reflecting characters commonly used in modern life and place names.

Education and use

Japanese schoolchildren learn the list progressively: 1,026 characters (the kyōiku kanji) are taught in elementary school, and the remainder in junior high school. Government documents, newspapers, and general publications are expected to use the listed characters and their approved readings (on'yomi and kun'yomi); characters outside the list are often printed with furigana glosses. Mastery of the jōyō kanji is generally considered the standard for adult literacy in Japanese.
