Citizenship of the European Union

Citizenship of the European Union is afforded to all nationals of member states of the European Union (EU). It was formally created by the Treaty of Maastricht in 1992, in Articles 20–24 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. EU citizenship is additional to national citizenship and does not replace it: every person holding the nationality of a member state is a citizen of the Union.

Rights

EU citizenship confers a set of rights, including:

  • the right to move and reside freely within the territory of the member states;
  • the right to vote and stand as a candidate in elections to the European Parliament and in municipal elections in the member state of residence;
  • the right to protection by the diplomatic and consular authorities of any member state in a third country where one's own state is not represented;
  • the right to petition the European Parliament, to apply to the European Ombudsman, and to address the institutions in any Treaty language and receive a reply in the same language.

Free movement

The most consequential right is free movement: EU citizens may live, work, study, and retire in any member state, subject to the conditions of Directive 2004/38/EC. For stays over three months, economically inactive citizens must generally have sufficient resources and comprehensive sickness insurance; permanent residence is acquired after five years of continuous legal residence.

Development

The Court of Justice has developed EU citizenship in a series of landmark cases — Grzelczyk, Baumbast, Zambrano, and others — describing it as destined to be the fundamental status of nationals of the member states. Withdrawal of a member state from the EU, as with the United Kingdom in 2020 (Brexit), removes EU citizenship from its nationals.
