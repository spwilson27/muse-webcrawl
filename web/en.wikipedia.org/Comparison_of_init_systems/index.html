Comparison of init systems

An init system is the first process started by the kernel of a Unix-like operating system (PID 1). It initialises the system, starts and supervises services (daemons), and handles shutdown. Init systems differ in design philosophy — from simple sequential shell scripts to dependency-based service managers with logging, socket activation, and sandboxing.

Major systems

SysVinitHistorically most Unix/LinuxSequential runlevel scripts in /etc/init.d

runitVoid Linux, othersMinimal supervision suite based on daemontools ideas

UpstartUbuntu (2006–2015), formerlyEvent-based; superseded by systemd
launchdmacOSApple's unified service manager
Service Management FacilitySolaris/illumosXML manifests, dependency graph, fault management

Debate

The adoption of systemd by most large distributions prompted sustained controversy over adherence to the Unix philosophy, the scope of the project, and its logging and binary formats; distributions such as Devuan were founded to offer alternatives.
