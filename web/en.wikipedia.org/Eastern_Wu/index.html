Eastern Wu

Wu (222–280), commonly known as Eastern Wu or Sun Wu, was one of the three major states that competed for supremacy over China during the Three Kingdoms period (220–280). It was founded by Sun Quan and ruled the southeast of China, south of the Yangtze River, with its capital at Jianye (present-day Nanjing).

Founding

Sun Quan inherited the Jiangdong territories from his brother Sun Ce in 200. At the Battle of Red Cliffs (208), allied with Liu Bei, his forces defeated Cao Cao and secured the south. Sun Quan declared himself King of Wu in 222 and emperor in 229.

Rivalry and decline

Wu fought Wei and later Jin to the north and contested Jing Province with Shu Han to the west; the Battle of Xiaoting (222) checked Liu Bei's attempt to retake it. After Sun Quan's death in 252, court intrigues weakened the state. In 280 the Jin dynasty invaded with a great fleet down the Yangtze, and Sun Hao surrendered, ending the Three Kingdoms division.

Society and economy

Wu developed the lower Yangtze economy, promoted maritime trade reaching Southeast Asia, and dispatched envoys as far as the Roman Orient trade network; Buddhism and Daoism spread in its cities.
