Emblem book

An emblem book is a book collecting emblems (allegorical illustrations) with accompanying explanatory text, typically moral lessons. Emblem books were extremely popular in Europe during the 16th and 17th centuries.

Structure

A typical emblem combined three parts: a motto or inscription (inscriptio), a picture (pictura), and an explanatory epigram or text (subscriptio). The genre was inaugurated by Andrea Alciato's Emblemata, first published in Augsburg in 1531, which went through numerous editions and inspired thousands of successors across Europe. Emblem books influenced literature, visual art, and decorative arts, and their imagery fed into religious, political and scientific works.
