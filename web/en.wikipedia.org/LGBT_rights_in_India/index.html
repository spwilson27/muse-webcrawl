LGBT rights in India

Lesbian, gay, bisexual and transgender people in India face legal and social challenges, though major court rulings have expanded recognition. Sexual activity between consenting adults of the same sex is legal.

History

Section 377 of the Indian Penal Code, a colonial-era law, criminalised 'carnal intercourse against the order of nature'. The Delhi High Court read it down in 2009; the Supreme Court reversed that in 2013, then in Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India (2018) decriminalised consensual same-sex relations.

Transgender rights

In NALSA v. Union of India (2014) the Supreme Court recognised transgender people as a third gender. The Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act, 2019 followed, amid criticism from activists.

Relationships

Same-sex marriage is not recognised. In Supriyo v. Union of India (2023) the Supreme Court declined to recognise a fundamental right to marry for same-sex couples, leaving the matter to Parliament, while affirming protections against discrimination.
