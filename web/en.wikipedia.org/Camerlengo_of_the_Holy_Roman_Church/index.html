Camerlengo of the Holy Roman Church

The Camerlengo of the Holy Roman Church (Italian: Camerlengo di Santa Romana Chiesa) is an office of the papal household that administers the property and revenues of the Holy See. The Camerlengo is best known for the role he plays during the sede vacante, the period between the death or resignation of a pope and the election of a successor, when he formally verifies the pope's death and administers the temporal goods of the Church.

Duties

During a vacancy of the Apostolic See, the Camerlengo seals the pope's apartments, takes possession of the Apostolic Palace, and formally certifies the pope's death — traditionally by calling the pope's baptismal name three times. He notifies the Cardinal Vicar and the Dean of the College of Cardinals, oversees arrangements for the funeral, and directs preparations for the conclave together with the College. The Camerlengo's authority is strictly administrative: he may not innovate or exercise the pope's supreme jurisdiction.

History

The office developed in the 11th–12th centuries from the chamberlain (camerarius) who managed the papal treasury. Over time the Camerlengo became one of the most senior officials of the Roman Curia, overseeing the Apostolic Camera, the financial administration of the Papal States. After the loss of the Papal States in 1870 and later reforms, the office's ordinary financial functions shrank, leaving chiefly its ceremonial and sede vacante duties.

Appointment

The Camerlengo is appointed by the pope, normally from among the cardinals; if no cardinal is available, the duties may fall to the Vice-Camerlengo. Pope Francis appointed Cardinal Kevin Farrell as Camerlengo in February 2019.
