János Kádár

János Kádár (born János Czermanik; 26 May 1912 – 6 July 1989) was the communist leader of Hungary from 1956 until 1988, first as head of government installed after the Soviet suppression of the Hungarian Revolution, and as general secretary of the Hungarian Socialist Workers' Party for over three decades.

Path to power

Kádár joined the illegal communist movement as a young typewriter mechanic, was repeatedly imprisoned, and served as interior minister in the post-war party-state before himself being jailed on fabricated charges (1951–54). During the October 1956 revolution he initially joined Imre Nagy's government, then defected to the Soviets and returned with the invading forces, heading the new "Revolutionary Worker-Peasant Government."

Kádárism

After initial reprisals — including the 1958 execution of Nagy — Kádár's regime gradually traded political liberalisation for consumer comfort: "Whoever is not against us is with us," and the New Economic Mechanism (1968) introduced market elements. The resulting system, nicknamed "goulash communism," gave Hungary the highest living standards in the Eastern Bloc and relative cultural latitude, financed increasingly by Western loans.

Fall and death

Ageing and ill, Kádár was removed as general secretary in May 1988 in favour of Károly Grósz. He died on 6 July 1989 — the same day Hungary's Supreme Court formally rehabilitated Imre Nagy. His legacy remains divisive: condemned for collaboration and repression, yet remembered by some for stability and modest prosperity.
