Toompea Castle

LocationToompea Hill, Tallinn, Estonia
Built13th–18th centuries (on earlier fortifications)
Current useSeat of the Riigikogu (Estonian Parliament)

Toompea Castle (Estonian: Toompea loss) is a castle on Toompea Hill in the central part of Tallinn, the capital of Estonia. It has been the seat of power in Estonia for some 800 years and today houses the Riigikogu, the Estonian parliament.

History

An Estonian hill fort stood on the site by the 9th century; Danish crusaders took it in 1219, and the stone castle was developed by the Livonian Order, whose Tall Hermann tower (Pikk Hermann), at 45.6 metres, remains a national symbol — the Estonian flag is raised atop it each sunrise. The pink Baroque palace fronting the castle was built under Russian rule in 1767–1773 and now fronts the parliamentary buildings inserted into the castle courtyard in the 1920s.

Today

The complex dominates Tallinn's Old Town skyline opposite St Alexander Nevsky Cathedral and is a focal point of Estonian statehood.
