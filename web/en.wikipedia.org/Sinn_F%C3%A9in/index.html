Sinn Féin ("we ourselves") is an Irish republican and democratic socialist political party active throughout Ireland, in both the Republic of Ireland and Northern Ireland. It advocates the reunification of Ireland as a socialist republic.

History

The original Sinn Féin was founded in 1905 by Arthur Griffith. After the Easter Rising of 1916 the party won a landslide in Ireland in the 1918 general election, declared an Irish Republic, and formed the First Dáil. Following the Anglo-Irish Treaty and the Irish Civil War, the party split and fragmented. The modern party emerged from the 1970 split into Official and Provisional Sinn Féin, and grew electorally under Gerry Adams's leadership during and after the Northern Ireland peace process.

Recent politics

Under Mary Lou McDonald, president since 2018, Sinn Féin won the popular vote in Ireland's 2020 general election and became the largest party in the Northern Ireland Assembly in 2022; Michelle O'Neill became First Minister of Northern Ireland in 2024. Sinn Féin MPs elected to Westminster follow a long-standing policy of abstentionism and do not take their seats.
