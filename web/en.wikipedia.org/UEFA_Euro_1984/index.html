UEFA Euro 1984

The 1984 UEFA European Football Championship final tournament was held in France from 12 to 27 June 1984. It was the seventh UEFA European Championship. Hosts France won the tournament, their first major international title, defeating Spain 2–0 in the final at the Parc des Princes in Paris.

Tournament summary

Eight teams contested the finals: France, Denmark, Belgium and Yugoslavia in Group A; Spain, West Germany, Portugal and Romania in Group B. In the semi-finals, France defeated Portugal 3–2 after extra time in a celebrated match in Marseille, while Spain beat Denmark on penalties after a 1–1 draw.

Final

In the final on 27 June 1984, France beat Spain 2–0. Michel Platini opened the scoring with a free kick that slipped under goalkeeper Luis Arconada, and Bruno Bellone added a second in stoppage time. France captain Platini scored a record nine goals in five matches, including two group-stage hat-tricks — a record for a single European Championship tournament that still stands.

Venues

Matches were played in Paris (Parc des Princes), Marseille, Lyon, Saint-Étienne, Lens, Nantes and Strasbourg. The tournament is remembered as one of the finest of the early championships, notable for the attacking play of Henri Michel's France side, built around the midfield "carré magique" of Platini, Alain Giresse, Jean Tigana and Luis Fernández.
