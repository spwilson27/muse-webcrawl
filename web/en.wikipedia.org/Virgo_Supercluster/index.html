Virgo Supercluster

The Virgo Supercluster (Virgo SC) or the Local Supercluster (LSC or LS) is a mass concentration of galaxies containing the Virgo Cluster and the Local Group, which in turn contains the Milky Way and Andromeda galaxies, as well as others. At least 100 galaxy groups and clusters are located within its diameter of 33 megaparsecs (110 million light-years).

Structure

The Virgo Supercluster is arranged roughly in a flattened disk, with the massive Virgo Cluster near its centre and the Local Group near its edge. Its structure includes several clouds of galaxies, among them the Virgo II cloud, the Canes Venatici cloud, the Virgo III cloud, and the Crater cloud. Because of the presence of the Great Attractor, matter in the supercluster shows peculiar velocities toward the Norma Cluster region in addition to the general expansion of the Universe.

Laniakea

In 2014 it was proposed that the Virgo Supercluster is only a lobe of a greater supercluster, Laniakea, defined by the relative velocities of galaxies, which would encompass some 100,000 galaxies spread over 160 megaparsecs (520 million light-years). Under this definition, the Virgo Supercluster is one constituent appendage of the larger structure centred on the Great Attractor region.

History of observation

The existence of a local concentration of nebulae was suggested by William and John Herschel; Charles Messier and others catalogued many of its member galaxies as nebulae. The supercluster's structure was mapped in detail in the 1950s by Gérard de Vaucouleurs, who argued that the Local Group belonged to a flattened system of galaxies centred on the Virgo Cluster.
