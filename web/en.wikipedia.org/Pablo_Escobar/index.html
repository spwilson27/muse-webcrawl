Pablo Emilio Escobar Gaviria (1 December 1949 – 2 December 1993) was a Colombian drug lord, narcoterrorist and politician who founded and led the Medellín Cartel. Dubbed "the king of cocaine", Escobar is regarded as one of the richest and most powerful criminals in history; at the height of his power the Medellín Cartel supplied an estimated 80% of the cocaine smuggled into the United States, and Escobar's personal fortune was estimated in the tens of billions of dollars.

Rise of the cartel

Born in Rionegro and raised in Medellín, Escobar began his criminal career as a teenager stealing cars and gravestones before entering the cocaine trade in the mid-1970s. Working with the Ochoa brothers and others, he built an empire that industrialised smuggling, using aircraft, boats and even submarines to move tonnes of cocaine from Peru and Bolivia via Colombia to the United States. In 1982 he was elected as an alternate member of Colombia's Chamber of Representatives, projecting an image as a benefactor who built housing and football fields for Medellín's poor.

Violence and downfall

When Colombia moved toward extraditing traffickers to the United States, Escobar waged a campaign of terror — summarised by his policy of plata o plomo ("silver or lead", a bribe or a bullet) — assassinating presidential candidate Luis Carlos Galán, judges, police officers and journalists, and bombing an Avianca airliner in 1989, killing 107 people. In 1991 he surrendered under a deal banning extradition and was held in his self-built luxury prison, La Catedral, from which he escaped in 1992 when the government tried to move him.

After a 16-month manhunt by the Colombian Search Bloc, assisted by U.S. agencies and aided by the rival Los Pepes vigilantes, Escobar was located in a Medellín safe house and shot dead on a rooftop on 2 December 1993, the day after his 44th birthday. His death effectively ended the Medellín Cartel.

Legacy

Escobar remains a polarising figure: revered by some of Medellín's poor for his charity, and condemned for a campaign of violence that killed thousands. His life has been the subject of numerous books, films and the Netflix series Narcos. The hippos he imported to his Hacienda Nápoles estate have multiplied into an invasive population in Colombia's rivers.
