Sarah Records was a British independent record label based in Bristol, active between 1987 and 1995. Founded by Clare Wadd and Matt Haynes, it became the defining label of the twee pop and indie pop underground, issuing exactly 100 releases — numbered SARAH 1 to SARAH 100 — before deliberately closing, announcing its end with obituary notices in the music press.

History and ethos

Wadd and Haynes, who had run fanzines, started the label to release 7-inch singles by guitar bands such as The Field Mice, Heavenly, The Sea Urchins, Even As We Speak, The Orchids and St. Christopher. The label cultivated a strong identity: uniform minimalist sleeves, cheap prices, virulently anti-corporate and anti-macho manifestos inserted in records, and a refusal of standard industry practices such as licensing deals and promotional excess. Its releases helped define "indiepop" as a genre and ethos.

Legacy

The hundredth release, a compilation marking the label's end in 1995, closed the catalogue as planned. Sarah's influence on DIY and twee pop internationally — especially in the United States, Sweden and Japan — has been widely recognised; the label's story was documented in the 2014 film My Secret World: The Story of Sarah Records and a 2015 exhibition at the Arnolfini in Bristol.
