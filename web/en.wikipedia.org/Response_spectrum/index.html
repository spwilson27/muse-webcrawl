Response spectrum

Response spectrum
FieldEarthquake engineering, structural dynamics
Key quantitiesSpectral displacement (Sd), spectral velocity (Sv), spectral acceleration (Sa)
Related conceptsSeismic analysis, damping, natural frequency

A response spectrum is a plot of the peak or steady-state response (displacement, velocity or acceleration) of a series of oscillators of varying natural frequency, that are forced into motion by the same base vibration or shock. The resulting plot can then be used to pick off the response of any linear system, given its natural frequency of oscillation. One such use is in assessing the peak response of buildings to earthquakes. The science of strong ground motion may use some values from the response spectrum (computed from recordings of surface ground motion from seismographs) for correlation with seismic damage.

If the input used in calculating a response spectrum is steady-state periodic, then the steady-state result is recorded. Damping must be present, or else the response will be infinite. For transient input (such as seismic ground motion), the peak response is reported. Some level of damping is generally assumed, but a value will be obtained even with no damping.

A typical response spectrum: peak acceleration plotted against natural period for several damping ratios.

Construction

For a given ground motion record, a single-degree-of-freedom (SDOF) oscillator is defined by its natural period T (or frequency) and its damping ratio ζ. The oscillator is subjected to the ground motion and its maximum absolute response is recorded. Repeating the calculation for a range of periods yields the response spectrum. The three most common forms are:

  • Spectral displacement Sd(T, ζ) — the maximum relative displacement of the mass.
  • Spectral velocity Sv(T, ζ) — the maximum relative velocity, often approximated by the pseudo-velocity ωSd.
  • Spectral acceleration Sa(T, ζ) — the maximum absolute acceleration, often approximated by the pseudo-acceleration ω2Sd, where ω = 2π/T is the circular frequency.

Design spectra

Building codes such as Eurocode 8 and ASCE 7 specify design response spectra: smoothed, enveloped curves that represent the seismic demand for a site, accounting for soil class, importance factors and the acceptable level of risk. A design spectrum is typically anchored to a mapped peak ground acceleration and has characteristic plateau and descending branches in the acceleration, velocity and displacement regions.

Applications

Response spectra are central to the response spectrum method of seismic analysis, in which the peak modal responses of a multi-degree-of-freedom structure are estimated from the spectrum and combined using rules such as SRSS (square root of the sum of squares) or CQC (complete quadratic combination). The same concept is used in shock analysis of equipment and in the design of vibration-isolated systems.
