Migration

Migration most commonly refers to human migration, the movement of people from one place to another with the intention of settling, permanently or temporarily, at a new location. Migration may occur within a country (internal migration) or across international borders (international migration), and people who move are termed migrants, immigrants, or emigrants depending on perspective. The United Nations estimated the number of international migrants at about 281 million in 2020, roughly 3.6% of the world's population.

The word also has many specialised senses: in biology, animal migration is the seasonal movement of species, and bird migration in particular is a major field of study; in computing, migration can mean moving data or systems between platforms; in chemistry and physics it describes the movement of atoms, ions, or particles. Human migration is studied across demography, geography, economics, and sociology, with theories of push and pull factors explaining movement driven by employment, safety, family reunification, education, and environmental pressures such as climate change.

History

Human migration is as old as humanity itself, from the prehistoric dispersal of modern humans out of Africa to the great transoceanic movements of the 19th and early 20th centuries and contemporary flows shaped by globalisation, conflict, and labour markets.
