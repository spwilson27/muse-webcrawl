Digital object identifier

A digital object identifier (DOI) is a persistent identifier or handle used to uniquely identify various objects, standardised by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO 26324). DOIs are an implementation of the Handle System, and are widely used to identify academic, professional and government information, such as journal articles, research reports, data sets and official publications.

Structure

A DOI name consists of a prefix beginning with "10." that identifies the registrant, followed by a suffix assigned by the registrant, separated by a slash — for example, 10.1000/xyz123. DOIs are resolved through the doi.org proxy to the current location of the object, so links remain stable even when the object's URL changes.

Registration and use

DOIs are assigned by registration agencies such as Crossref (for scholarly publications) and DataCite (for research data), coordinated by the International DOI Foundation, founded in 1997. The system was launched in 2000 and hundreds of millions of DOI names have since been assigned. Publishers routinely print DOIs in reference lists, and citation styles increasingly request them.

Comparison with URLs

Unlike a URL, a DOI identifies the object itself rather than its location, and carries associated metadata. This persistence makes DOIs a cornerstone of scholarly citation linking and bibliometrics.
