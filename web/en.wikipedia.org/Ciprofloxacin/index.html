Ciprofloxacin

Trade nameCipro, others
ClassFluoroquinolone antibiotic
FormulaC17H18FN3O3

Ciprofloxacin is a fluoroquinolone antibiotic used to treat a number of bacterial infections, including urinary tract infections, respiratory tract infections, skin infections, bone and joint infections, and intra-abdominal infections. Marketed by Bayer under the name Cipro, it was patented in 1980 and approved for medical use in 1987. It appears on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Mechanism

Ciprofloxacin works by inhibiting bacterial DNA gyrase and topoisomerase IV, enzymes the bacteria need to replicate their DNA. It is bactericidal and active against a broad spectrum of Gram-negative and Gram-positive organisms, including Pseudomonas aeruginosa. It is one of the antibiotics recommended for the treatment of anthrax.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include nausea, diarrhoea and rash. Fluoroquinolones carry warnings for rare but serious effects, including tendon rupture, peripheral neuropathy and effects on the central nervous system, so ciprofloxacin is generally reserved for infections where its use is justified.
