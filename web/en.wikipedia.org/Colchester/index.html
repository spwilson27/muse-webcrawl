Colchester

Colchester is a city in Essex, England. It had a recorded population of about 130,000 in the city proper, with the wider borough exceeding 200,000. Colchester claims to be among Britain's oldest recorded towns: it was known to the Romans as Camulodunum, the first capital of Roman Britain, and it is mentioned by Pliny the Elder, making it one of the earliest named settlements in Britain.

History

Before the Roman conquest, Camulodunum was the stronghold of the Trinovantes and the seat of kings such as Cunobelinus. After the invasion of AD 43, the emperor Claudius received the surrender of British kings there. The town was destroyed in AD 60/61 during the revolt of Boudica, whose forces burned the Temple of Claudius with its defenders inside. Rebuilt with massive town walls — large stretches of which survive — Colchester later declined in the early medieval period but revived as a market town. Its Norman castle, built around 1076 on the podium of the Roman temple, has the largest Norman keep in England. Colchester was granted city status in 2022 as part of the Platinum Jubilee civic honours.

Modern Colchester

The city is home to the University of Essex, Colchester Zoo, the Mercury Theatre and the Firstsite gallery. The British Army's Colchester Garrison, home to 16 Air Assault Brigade, is based in the city. Colchester is connected to London Liverpool Street by rail, with journey times of under an hour from Colchester North station.
