Benin

Benin (French: Bénin), officially the Republic of Benin, is a country in West Africa bordered by Togo to the west, Nigeria to the east, Burkina Faso to the north-west, and Niger to the north-east, with a short coastline on the Bight of Benin in the south. Its capital is Porto-Novo, while Cotonou is the largest city, seat of government, and economic centre. The country is named after the Bight of Benin (and ultimately after the historical Kingdom of Benin in present-day Nigeria). Formerly the French colony of Dahomey, it gained independence in 1960, and was renamed Benin in 1975. Since the early 1990s it has been noted for a relatively stable multiparty democracy.

History

The precolonial south was dominated by the Fon kingdom of Dahomey, centred on Abomey, which grew wealthy through the Atlantic slave trade conducted via the port of Ouidah; the region was known to Europeans as the "Slave Coast." France established a protectorate in the 1890s and incorporated Dahomey into French West Africa. After independence in 1960, the country endured a series of coups, then a Marxist-Leninist regime under Mathieu Kérékou (1972–1991). A sovereign national conference in 1990 peacefully ushered in multiparty democracy, a transition widely praised as a model for Africa.

Geography and economy

Benin stretches from the Niger River in the north to the Atlantic in the south, encompassing coastal lagoons, savanna, and the Atakora mountains. Agriculture — cotton above all — dominates employment and exports; cotton is shipped through the autonomous port of Cotonou, which also serves landlocked Niger and trade with Nigeria. Vodun (Voodoo), originating among the Fon and Yoruba peoples of this region, is an officially recognised religion, celebrated on National Vodun Day (10 January).

Culture

Benin has more than 50 ethnic groups; the largest are the Fon, Yoruba, Adja, Bariba, and Fulani. It is the birthplace of singer Angélique Kidjo and actor Djimon Hounsou. The royal palaces of Abomey are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
