Paremiology

Paremiology is the study of proverbs, from the Greek paroimia (proverb) and -logy. It is a subdiscipline of folklore and linguistics concerned with collecting, classifying, and interpreting proverbs and other formulaic sayings across languages and cultures. The collection of proverbs itself is called paremiography.

Scope

Paremiologists analyse the structure, meaning, origins, and social functions of proverbs, distinguishing true proverbs from related forms such as proverbial phrases, idioms, maxims, and Wellerisms. Because proverbs are short, memorable, and traditional, they are a window into the values and everyday reasoning of the communities that use them.

History

Proverbs were compiled as early as ancient Mesopotamia and Egypt, and Erasmus's Adagia (1500) was a landmark of Renaissance collection. Modern paremiology is associated with scholars such as Archer Taylor, whose The Proverb (1931) set the field's definitions, and Wolfgang Mieder, a prolific contemporary proverb scholar. The concept of a "paremiological minimum" — the core set of proverbs every adult speaker of a language is expected to know — was developed by Grigorii Permiakov.
