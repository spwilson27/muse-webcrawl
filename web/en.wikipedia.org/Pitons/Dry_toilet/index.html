Dry toilet

A dry toilet is a toilet that operates without flush water, unlike a flush toilet. Waste falls through a drop hole, usually into a pit, vault or removable container below. Dry toilets include the simple pit latrine, the ventilated improved pit (VIP) latrine, composting toilets and urine-diverting dry toilets.

Types

The pit latrine is the most widespread form worldwide. More advanced designs manage odour and flies with ventilation pipes and fly screens, or treat waste by dehydration or composting. Urine diversion keeps the solid fraction dry, reducing smell and easing handling.

Public health

Where safely managed, dry toilets form part of the sanitation chain promoted by the WHO and UNICEF Joint Monitoring Programme. Poorly built pits, however, can contaminate groundwater, so siting distances from wells and water tables are an important design consideration.
