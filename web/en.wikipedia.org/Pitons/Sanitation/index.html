Sanitation

Sanitation refers to public health conditions related to clean drinking water and the adequate treatment and disposal of human excreta and sewage. Preventing human contact with faeces is part of sanitation, as is handwashing with soap. Sanitation systems aim to protect human health by providing a clean environment that breaks the cycle of disease, especially diarrhoeal diseases that historically killed large numbers of children.

Components

The "sanitation chain" covers the capture, storage, transport, treatment and reuse or disposal of excreta and wastewater, together with solid waste management, drainage and hygiene promotion. Technologies range from simple pit latrines to sewer networks with treatment plants, and ecological sanitation systems that recover nutrients.

Global status

The WHO estimates that billions of people still lack safely managed sanitation services. Sustainable Development Goal 6 targets universal access to adequate and equitable sanitation and hygiene by 2030. The economic case is strong: poor sanitation imposes large costs through illness, lost productivity and polluted water.
