Piraeus is a port city in the Attica region of Greece, on the Saronic Gulf about 12 kilometers southwest of central Athens. It has served as the port of Athens since classical antiquity and is today the largest seaport in Greece and one of the largest passenger ports in Europe, the main gateway for ferries to the Greek islands.

History

Piraeus was developed as Athens' naval and commercial port in the early fifth century BC under Themistocles, who fortified its three natural harbors — Kantharos, Zea and Munichia — and connected it to Athens by the Long Walls. The grid plan of the town is associated with the architect Hippodamus of Miletus. As home to the Athenian fleet, Piraeus underpinned the city's maritime empire; after Athens' defeat in the Peloponnesian War the Long Walls were demolished, though the port was later rebuilt.

Modern Piraeus

In the modern era Piraeus grew into a major industrial and shipping center, absorbing waves of refugees after 1922 and becoming a distinct municipality within the Athens urban area. Its container terminal, expanded under a concession to China's COSCO from 2009, became one of the Mediterranean's busiest. Landmarks include the Zea marina, the Municipal Theatre, the Archaeological Museum of Piraeus with its bronze statues, and the home stadium of Olympiacos football club.
