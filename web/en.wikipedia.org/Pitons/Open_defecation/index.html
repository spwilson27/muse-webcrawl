Open defecation

Open defecation is the human practice of defecating outside — in fields, forests, bushes, open bodies of water or other open spaces — rather than into a toilet. It occurs where people lack access to sanitation facilities, and also sometimes by custom or preference. The practice spreads disease through the faecal–oral route, contaminating water and food.

Scale

According to the WHO/UNICEF Joint Monitoring Programme, the number of people practising open defecation fell from about 1.3 billion in 1990 to roughly 420–490 million in the early 2020s. India accounted for the largest share historically, and its Swachh Bharat Mission (2014–2019) built over 100 million toilets and declared the country open-defecation free, though independent surveys found continued pockets of practice.

Eradication

Community-Led Total Sanitation (CLTS), developed in Bangladesh by Kamal Kar around 2000, triggers collective behaviour change rather than subsidising hardware. Ending open defecation is an explicit target of UN Sustainable Development Goal 6. World Toilet Day, 19 November, raises awareness of the issue.
