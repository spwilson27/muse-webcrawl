Pit latrine

A pit latrine, also known as a pit toilet or long drop, is a type of toilet that collects human feces in a hole in the ground. Urine and feces enter the pit through a drop hole in the floor, which might be connected to a toilet seat or squatting pan for user comfort. Pit latrines can be built to function without water, or they can have a water seal (pour-flush pit latrines). When properly built and maintained, pit latrines can decrease the spread of disease by reducing the amount of human feces in the environment from open defecation. This decreases the transfer of pathogens between feces and food by flies. These pathogens are major causes of infectious diarrhea and intestinal worm infections. Infectious diarrhea resulted in about 700,000 deaths in children under five years old in 2011 and 250 million lost school days. Pit latrines are a low-cost method of separating feces from people.

A pit latrine generally consists of three major parts: a hole in the ground, a concrete slab or floor with a small hole, and a shelter. The shelter is also called an outhouse. The pit is typically at least three meters deep and one meter across. The hole in the slab should not be larger than 25 centimeters (9.8 inches) to prevent children falling in. Light should be discouraged from entering the pit, to reduce access by flies. This may require the use of a lid to cover the hole in the floor when not in use.

Contents

Access to a pit latrine may be free and open to the public, restricted to certain people, or it may be for an individual at their home. They are frequently used in rural and peri-urban areas of developing countries.

Design considerations

The pit design affects how long the latrine can be used without emptying or moving it. The volume of the pit is calculated based on the accumulation rate of solids, the number of users, and the design life. The pit should be located at a safe distance from wells and surface water to avoid groundwater pollution. Lining the pit can prevent collapse but may also prevent liquids from infiltrating into the surrounding soil, which can cause the pit to fill up faster.

Types

The ventilated improved pit (VIP) latrine adds a ventilation pipe covered with a fly screen to reduce odors and fly breeding. The arborloo is a shallow pit latrine designed to be planted over with a tree once full. The twin-pit pour-flush toilet uses two pits alternately so that the contents of the full pit can safely decompose before emptying.

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages of pit latrines include low construction cost, no requirement for water supply, and the ability to be built by local people using locally available materials. Disadvantages include odor and fly problems if poorly maintained, the risk of groundwater contamination, and the need for periodic emptying or relocation.
