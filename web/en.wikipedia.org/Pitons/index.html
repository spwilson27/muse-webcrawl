Pitons

The Pitons are two mountainous volcanic plugs, volcanic spires, located in Saint Lucia. The larger, Gros Piton, is 798.25 m (2,618.9 ft) high, and the smaller, Petit Piton, is 743 m (2,438 ft) high; they are linked by the Piton Mitan ridge. The Pitons are a World Heritage Site, covering 2,909 hectares, designated by UNESCO in 2004.

Geography

The Pitons are located near the town of Soufrière, Saint Lucia, on the island's southwestern coast. They are remnants of an ancient volcanic complex, formed from hardened magma that remained after the surrounding cone eroded. The Pitons Management Area includes a marine portion protecting coral reefs and marine life in the bay between the peaks.

Climbing and tourism

Gros Piton can be climbed without ropes or specialist equipment, on a maintained trail with local guides, and offers views over the island and the Caribbean Sea. Petit Piton is steeper and more hazardous, and climbing it is discouraged. The Pitons are a national symbol of Saint Lucia: they appear on the local Piton beer label and in much of the island's imagery. They are also referenced in local legend and were known to the indigenous Kalinago people, for whom the peaks held spiritual significance.

Geology

The peaks belong to the Soufrière Volcanic Complex, part of the Lesser Antilles volcanic arc. Nearby geothermal features include the Sulphur Springs, marketed as a "drive-in volcano", and warm mineral baths founded in the 18th century.
