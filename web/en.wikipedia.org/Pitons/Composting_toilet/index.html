Composting toilet

A composting toilet is a type of dry toilet that treats human waste by a biological process called thermophilic composting. The system uses little or no water and converts excreta into a compost-like material through aerobic decomposition, usually with the addition of carbon-rich bulking material such as sawdust, coconut coir or peat moss.

Design

Composting toilets require a balance of moisture, oxygen, heat and carbon-to-nitrogen ratio. Ventilation removes odours and excess moisture, and some designs separate urine to keep the solids drier. Finished material is typically buried or removed after pathogens have been destroyed by prolonged storage and heat.

Applications

They are used in cabins, boats, roadside rest areas, national parks and eco-buildings where water or sewer connections are unavailable or undesirable. Notable users include trail systems and remote facilities in protected areas, where conventional sewage would pollute fragile ecosystems.
