Athens is the capital and largest city of Greece, and one of the world's oldest cities, with a recorded history spanning over 3,400 years. Classical Athens, a powerful city-state of ancient Greece, is celebrated as the birthplace of democracy and a cradle of Western civilisation, philosophy and theatre.

History

Athens rose to prominence in the 5th century BC, when, after the Persian Wars, it led the Delian League and produced thinkers such as Socrates, Plato and Aristotle, and monuments such as the Parthenon on the Acropolis. After centuries under Macedonian, Roman, Byzantine and Ottoman rule, Athens became the capital of the modern Greek state in 1834. It hosted the first modern Olympic Games in 1896 and again in 2004.

Landmarks

The Acropolis with the Parthenon dominates the city centre. Other major sites include the Ancient Agora, the Temple of Olympian Zeus, the Panathenaic Stadium and the Acropolis Museum, opened in 2009. Greater Athens is home to more than three million people, roughly a third of Greece's population.
