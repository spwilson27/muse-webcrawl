Hara Takashi

Hara Takashi (原 敬)
15 March 1856 – 4 November 1921
Prime Minister of Japan, 1918–1921

Hara Takashi (1856–1921), also known as Hara Kei, was a Japanese politician who served as Prime Minister of Japan from 1918 to 1921. He was the first commoner — a man without noble rank — to become prime minister and the first to lead a government based on a political party with a majority in the Diet, earning him the nickname "the commoner premier".

Career

Born into a samurai family of Morioka domain, Hara worked as a journalist and diplomat, including service as vice minister of foreign affairs, before entering politics with the Rikken Seiyūkai party of Itō Hirobumi. As the party's chief organiser and later president, he built its nationwide machine through patronage and public works.

Premiership

Appointed after the Rice Riots of 1918, Hara expanded party government, cautiously widened the franchise, pursued cooperative diplomacy with the United States and China, and joined the League of Nations. He resisted universal male suffrage and military reform pressures, and was criticised for corruption scandals. On 4 November 1921 he was stabbed to death at Tokyo Station by a young railway worker, becoming the first sitting Japanese prime minister to be assassinated. His extensive diary is a major source for the politics of the Taishō era.
