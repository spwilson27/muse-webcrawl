Avid Media Composer

Avid Media Composer is a non-linear film and video editing application developed by Avid Technology. Released in 1989 for the Macintosh, it was one of the pioneering digital editing systems and became the dominant tool in professional film and television production.

History and features

Media Composer popularised workflows such as offline editing at low resolution, media management through bins, and trim-based editing modelled on film practice. Films edited on Avid systems have won the Academy Award for Best Film Editing in most years since the mid-1990s, and the software remains a standard in scripted television and feature production, noted for collaborative workflows in networked facilities. It runs on macOS and Windows and is sold in subscription tiers, including the free Media Composer First (later replaced by a trial model).

Competition

Its main competitors include Adobe Premiere Pro, Apple Final Cut Pro and DaVinci Resolve, which have gained ground in online and independent production, while Media Composer retains strength in large-scale broadcast and Hollywood post-production.
