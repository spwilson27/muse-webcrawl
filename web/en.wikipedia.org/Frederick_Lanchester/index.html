Frederick William Lanchester (1868–1946) was an English polymath and engineer who made important contributions to automotive engineering, aerodynamics and operations research. He built one of Britain's first petrol cars and formulated Lanchester's laws of combat.

Automobiles

With his brother George, Lanchester designed a car from first principles, completed in 1895–96, featuring a balanced engine, epicyclic gearbox and later disc brakes (patented 1902) and fuel injection concepts. The Lanchester Motor Company produced luxury cars noted for their engineering refinement; the marque later passed to BSA/Daimler.

Aviation and Lanchester's laws

In Aerodynamics (1907) Lanchester laid out the vortex theory of lift, largely ignored in Britain at the time but influential in Germany through Prandtl. In Aircraft in Warfare (1916) he published his "laws": the linear law for ancient combat and the famous square law, stating that with aimed fire the fighting strength of a force is proportional to the square of its numbers — a foundation of operations research.
