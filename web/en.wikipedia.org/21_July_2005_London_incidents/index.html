On 21 July 2005, four attempted bomb attacks disrupted part of London's public transport system, and a fifth bomber dumped his device without attempting detonation. Coming exactly two weeks after the 7 July 2005 London bombings that killed 56 people, the attacks were carried out with poorly constructed devices whose detonators fired but whose main explosive charges failed to ignite, causing no deaths or serious injuries.

The incidents

Around midday, small explosions — later established to be detonators going off alone — were reported at Shepherd's Bush (Hammersmith & City line), Warren Street (Victoria line) and Oval (Northern line) Underground stations, and aboard a No. 26 bus in Hackney Road, Bethnal Green. Passengers described a popping sound, smoke and a smell like burning vinegar; the bom­bers fled, in one case chased by members of the public. A fifth device was later found abandoned on Wormwood Scrubs. The bombs used hydrogen peroxide mixed with chapati flour, but the peroxide had deteriorated or been incorrectly prepared, so the main charges did not detonate.

Investigation and trials

A major manhunt followed. All five suspects were arrested within nine days: Ramzi Mohammed and Yasin Omar in Birmingham, Muktar Said Ibrahim in London, and Hussain Osman in Rome, Italy, having fled via the Channel Tunnel. The following day, in an atmosphere of acute tension, Metropolitan Police officers shot dead Jean Charles de Menezes, an innocent Brazilian electrician, at Stockwell station after mistaking him for Osman.

In 2007 Ibrahim, Mohammed, Omar and Osman were convicted of conspiracy to murder and sentenced to life imprisonment with minimum terms of 40 years; the fifth man, Manfo Kwaku Asiedu, pleaded guilty to a lesser charge. The failed attacks led to lasting changes in British counter-terrorism policing and in the handling of suspect devices on the transport network.
