Kingdom of Sicily

The Kingdom of Sicily (Latin: Regnum Siciliae) was a state in southern Italy and Sicily founded by the Norman count Roger II in 1130. One of the wealthiest and most administratively advanced kingdoms of medieval Europe, it united Sicily with the Norman territories of southern Italy and later expanded briefly into Malta and parts of North Africa.

History

Roger II, crowned at Palermo on Christmas Day 1130, combined the Hauteville conquests into a single kingdom ruled through an efficient bureaucracy drawing on Norman, Byzantine, and Arab administrative traditions. The kingdom passed by marriage to the Hohenstaufen emperor Henry VI in 1194; his son Frederick II made it a centre of law (the Constitutions of Melfi) and culture. After the Sicilian Vespers revolt of 1282, the island kingdom split from the mainland — which became the Kingdom of Naples — beginning centuries of division.

Culture

Norman Sicily was famously plural: Latin, Greek, and Muslim scholars worked at court, Arabic remained a language of administration, and monuments such as the Palatine Chapel and Monreale Cathedral fuse Norman, Byzantine, and Islamic art. See also Normans and Tabula Rogeriana.
