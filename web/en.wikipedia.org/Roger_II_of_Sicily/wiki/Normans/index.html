Normans

The Normans (from "Northmen") were a people who emerged in the early 10th century in the region of northern France that became Normandy, descended from Norse Viking settlers and the local Frankish and Gallo-Roman populations. Adapting the French language and Christianity, they became renowned soldiers and adventurers who founded states from England to Sicily and the Levant.

History

In 911 the Viking leader Rollo received land around Rouen from the Frankish king Charles the Simple. His descendants built a powerful duchy. In 1066 Duke William II conquered England at the Battle of Hastings, transforming English society, language, and architecture. Meanwhile Norman freebooters — notably the Hauteville family — conquered Byzantine and Lombard southern Italy and Muslim Sicily, culminating in Roger II's Kingdom of Sicily in 1130. Normans also played major roles in the Crusades, founding the Principality of Antioch.

Legacy

Norman achievements include Romanesque architecture, motte-and-bailey castles, the Domesday Book, and lasting influences on English law and vocabulary. See also Vikings and Norman Conquest.
