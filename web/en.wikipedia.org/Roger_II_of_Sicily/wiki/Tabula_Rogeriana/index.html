Tabula Rogeriana

The Tabula Rogeriana — formally the Kitāb nuzhat al-mushtāq fī ikhtirāq al-āfāq ("The Excursion of One Who Is Eager to Traverse the Regions of the World"), often called the Book of Roger — is a description of the world and world map created by the Arab geographer Muhammad al-Idrisi in 1154 for King Roger II of Sicily. It remained among the most accurate world maps for centuries.

Creation

Al-Idrisi worked at Roger's multilingual court in Palermo for some fifteen years, synthesising classical geography (notably Ptolemy), earlier Arabic scholarship, merchants' and travellers' reports, and information gathered from emissaries sent out by the king. The work originally accompanied a great planisphere engraved on a disc of solid silver — about two metres across — which was destroyed a few years later; the book, with its 70 regional maps plus a world map, survives in a number of manuscript copies.

Content and legacy

The text describes the known world divided into seven climates, covering Europe, Asia, and Africa with details of cities, trade, peoples, and distances. It was translated into Latin in the early 17th century and remains a key source for medieval geography. See also Kingdom of Sicily and al-Idrisi.
