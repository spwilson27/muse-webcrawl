Nepenthes rajah

Nepenthes rajah is a carnivorous pitcher plant species of the family Nepenthaceae. It is endemic to Mount Kinabalu and neighbouring Mount Tambuyukon in Sabah, Malaysian Borneo, where it grows at elevations of roughly 1,500–2,650 m on serpentine (ultramafic) soils. The species was described by Joseph Dalton Hooker in 1859; its specific epithet honours Sir James Brooke, the first White Rajah of Sarawak.

N. rajah is famous for producing the largest pitchers of any Nepenthes species: its ground pitchers can exceed 40 cm in height and hold up to 3.5 litres of fluid, with traps occasionally exceeding two litres of digestive liquid. The plants are scrambling vines whose stems may reach several metres in length, though the inflorescence and pitchers are borne on sprawling rosettes.

Ecology

Like other pitcher plants, N. rajah traps insects — especially ants — in its fluid-filled pitchers, digesting them to supplement the nutrient-poor soils in which it grows. Unusually, the species also maintains a mutualistic relationship with two small mammals: the mountain treeshrew (Tupaia montana) and the summit rat (Rattus baluensis). These animals feed on nectar secreted by the pitcher lid and defecate into the trap, providing the plant with nitrogen. Studies have shown this faecal nitrogen can account for a large fraction of the plant's nitrogen intake.

Conservation

N. rajah is listed as Endangered on the IUCN Red List and is included in CITES Appendix I, prohibiting commercial international trade in wild-collected plants. Its entire natural range lies within Kinabalu Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, but illegal collection and habitat disturbance remain threats.
