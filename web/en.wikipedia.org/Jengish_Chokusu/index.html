Jengish Chokusu

Jengish Chokusu (Kyrgyz: Жеңиш чокусу, "Victory Peak") is the highest mountain in the Tian Shan range and the highest point of Kyrgyzstan, rising to 7,439 metres (24,406 ft) on the border between Kyrgyzstan and China's Xinjiang region. Known in the Soviet era and still widely in Russian as Pik Pobedy ("Victory Peak"), it is the northernmost seven-thousand-metre mountain in the world and is regarded as one of the most difficult and dangerous of the great Central Asian summits, owing to its severe weather, long approaches, and avalanche-prone slopes. It forms, with Khan Tengri (7,010 m), one of the two Kyrgyz summits of the Snow Leopard award.

Geography

The mountain lies in the Kakshaal Too, the southernmost and highest sub-range of the Kyrgyz Tian Shan, above the South Inylchek (Engilchek) Glacier, one of the largest mountain glaciers outside the polar regions. Its long summit ridge runs along the Kyrgyzstan–China frontier; the Chinese name is Tomur Feng. The massif is heavily glaciated, and its northern approaches cross the Zvyozdochka ("Little Star") Glacier to the base camps shared with expeditions to Khan Tengri.

Climbing history

The peak was surveyed and its height estimated during Soviet topographic work in the 1940s and was named Pobedy ("Victory") in 1946 in honour of the Soviet victory in the Second World War — for a time its elevation was mis-stated, and it was only later established as higher than Khan Tengri and thus the Tian Shan's highest summit. An attempt in 1955 by a Kazakh team ended in disaster when eleven climbers died in a storm near the summit. The first successful ascent was made on 30 August 1956 by a Soviet team under the celebrated mountaineer Vitaly Abalakov, climbing from the Zvyozdochka Glacier. The mountain's standard route ascends the western ridge, typically via a sub-summit known as Pik Vazha-Pshavela (6,918 m), with base camp on the South Inylchek Glacier reached through the Kyrgyz town of Karakol and the Inylchek valley.

Difficulty and danger

Despite being lower than the eight-thousanders, Jengish Chokusu has a formidable record: its latitude makes for violent winds and extreme cold, storms can persist for days, and the long summit ridge is exposed and avalanche-swept. Dozens of climbers have died on the mountain. Together with Khan Tengri, Lenin Peak, Ismoil Somoni Peak, and Korzhenevskaya, it is one of the five "Snow Leopard" peaks whose ascent earned the USSR's (and now a successor) mountaineering award. Since Kyrgyz independence, the name Jengish Chokusu — Kyrgyz for "Victory Peak" — has been official, although Pik Pobedy remains common in Russian and mountaineering usage.
