The Wounded Warrior Project (WWP) is an American charity and veterans service organization that offers programs and services for veterans and service members who incurred a physical or mental injury, illness, or wound while serving in the military on or after September 11, 2001. It is one of the largest veterans charities in the United States.

History

WWP began in 2003 as an effort by John Melia — a Marine veteran wounded in a helicopter crash — and friends and family in Roanoke, Virginia, to deliver backpacks filled with comfort items to wounded service members arriving at Walter Reed Army Medical Center and Bethesda Naval Hospital. The organization grew rapidly on the strength of direct-mail and television fundraising, relocating its headquarters to Jacksonville, Florida, and expanding into mental health, career counselling, benefits assistance, adaptive sports, and long-term rehabilitative care programs.

Programs

Programs include Warrior Care Network, which funds intensive outpatient treatment for post-traumatic stress disorder in partnership with academic medical centres; WWP Talk, a non-clinical emotional support line; career transition and job placement services; benefits claims assistance accredited by the Department of Veterans Affairs; and Independence Program services for the most severely injured veterans.

Controversy and reform

In 2016, media investigations alleged lavish spending on staff conferences and events, prompting the departure of chief executive Steven Nardizzi and chief operating officer Al Giordano and a sharp drop in donations. The organization subsequently cut expenses, restructured under new leadership (retired Lt. Gen. Michael Linnington became CEO in 2016), and regained improved ratings from charity watchdogs.
