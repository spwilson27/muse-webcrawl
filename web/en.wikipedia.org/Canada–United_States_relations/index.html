Canada–United States relations are the bilateral relations between Canada and the United States of America. The two countries share the world's longest border, extensive trade ties, and close military and cultural cooperation, and are frequently described as among the closest allies in the world.

Trade

The United States is Canada's largest trading partner and Canada is consistently one of the top trading partners of the United States, with integrated supply chains in energy, automobiles and agriculture. Free trade agreements — the Canada–United States Free Trade Agreement of 1989, NAFTA from 1994, and its successor the United States–Mexico–Canada Agreement (USMCA) from 2020 — structure the economic relationship, though disputes over softwood lumber, dairy and steel have periodically arisen.

Defence and diplomacy

Both countries are founding members of NATO and cooperate in continental air defence through NORAD, a joint command established in 1958. Canadians and Americans fought together in both world wars, Korea and Afghanistan, though Canada declined to join the 2003 invasion of Iraq. Management of shared waters is handled partly through the International Joint Commission, founded under the Boundary Waters Treaty of 1909.
