The M16 rifle is a family of assault rifles adapted from the ArmaLite AR-15 and used by the United States military. Chambered in 5.56×45mm NATO, it became the standard service rifle of the U.S. armed forces during the Vietnam War, replacing the M14 rifle.

History

Designed by Eugene Stoner at ArmaLite in the late 1950s, the AR-15 was sold to Colt's Manufacturing Company, which marketed it to the military as the M16. Early models in Vietnam suffered reliability problems, traced to changes in propellant, lack of cleaning kits, and inadequate training; the improved M16A1 of 1967 addressed these issues with a chrome-lined chamber and a forward assist.

Variants

Later variants include the M16A2 (1982) with a heavier barrel and three-round burst mode, and the M16A4 with a detachable carrying handle and Picatinny rail. The shorter M4 carbine, derived from the M16, has largely supplanted it in front-line service. The M16 remains one of the most widely produced 5.56 mm rifles in the world, with over 8 million units built.
