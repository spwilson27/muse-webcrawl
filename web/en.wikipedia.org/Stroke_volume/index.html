Stroke volume

In cardiovascular physiology, stroke volume (SV) is the volume of blood pumped out of a ventricle of the heart with each heartbeat. It is typically measured for the left ventricle and is calculated as the difference between end-diastolic volume and end-systolic volume: SV = EDV − ESV. In a healthy adult at rest it is roughly 70 mL.

Determinants

Stroke volume is governed by three factors: preload (the degree of ventricular stretch before contraction, related through the Frank–Starling law), contractility (the intrinsic strength of contraction, increased by sympathetic stimulation), and afterload (the resistance the ventricle must pump against, chiefly arterial pressure). Together with heart rate, stroke volume determines cardiac output: CO = SV × HR.

Measurement and significance

Stroke volume can be estimated non-invasively by echocardiography and cardiac MRI, or invasively with a pulmonary artery catheter. It falls in heart failure and hypovolemia and rises with exercise and training; the ejection fraction — stroke volume divided by end-diastolic volume — is a key clinical measure of ventricular function.
