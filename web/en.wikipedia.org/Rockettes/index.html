The Rockettes

The Rockettes are an American precision dance company founded in St. Louis in 1925. Based at Radio City Music Hall in New York City, they are famed for their synchronised high kicks, eye-level precision choreography, and starring role in the annual Christmas Spectacular. The line typically features 36 dancers on stage from a larger company roster.

History

Choreographer Russell Markert founded the troupe as the "Missouri Rockets" in 1925, inspired by the precision of John Tiller's dancers and the Ziegfeld Follies. Impresario Samuel "Roxy" Rothafel brought them to New York, first to the Roxy Theatre as the "Roxyettes," and then to Radio City Music Hall, which opened in December 1932, where they became the Rockettes. Their famous "Parade of the Wooden Soldiers," performed since 1933, remains a signature number.

Performances

Beyond the Christmas Spectacular — seen by more than a million people a year — the Rockettes have performed at the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade, Super Bowl halftime shows, presidential inaugurations, and, from 1979, the Radio City Easter show in earlier decades. Dancers must meet strict height requirements (between 5'6" and 5'10½" in stocking feet) so that the line appears uniform, with taller dancers at the centre.

Legacy

The Rockettes are an enduring symbol of New York entertainment. In 2014 a spring show, Heart and Lights (later New York Spectacular), extended their presence beyond the holiday season. Generations of dancers have passed through the line, which has gradually diversified since the first African American Rockette joined in 1987.
