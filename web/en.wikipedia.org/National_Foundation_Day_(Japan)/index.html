National Foundation Day (Japan)

National Foundation Day
Native name建国記念の日 (Kenkoku Kinen no Hi)

TypeNational holiday
DateFebruary 11

National Foundation Day (建国記念の日, Kenkoku Kinen no Hi) is an annual public holiday in Japan on February 11, celebrating the foundation of Japan and the accession of its legendary first Emperor Jimmu, traditionally dated to 660 BC.

History

The origin of the day lies in Kigensetsu (Empire Day), a holiday instituted by the Meiji government in 1872 and first celebrated in 1873, marking the founding of the Empire of Japan by Emperor Jimmu according to the Nihon Shoki. After World War II, Kigensetsu was abolished in 1948 as part of the occupation reforms because of its association with State Shinto and imperial mythology. Following years of debate, National Foundation Day was established in 1966 by amendment of the National Holidays Act and first observed in 1967, framed as a day to "reflect on the establishment of the nation and to nourish a love of country."

Celebrations

Celebrations are relatively restrained compared with other national days. Shrines hold festivals, and Kashihara Shrine in Nara Prefecture — built where Jimmu is said to have ascended the throne — hosts prominent ceremonies. Some demonstrations both supporting and opposing the holiday occur, reflecting continuing debate over its historical and ideological roots.
