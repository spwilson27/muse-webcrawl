Long Island is a densely populated island in southeastern New York State, extending 118 miles eastward from New York Harbor into the Atlantic Ocean. It comprises four counties: Kings and Queens (the New York City boroughs of Brooklyn and Queens) in the west, and Nassau and Suffolk in the east, the latter two being what is usually meant by "Long Island" in regional usage.

Geography

The island was shaped by glacial moraines from the last ice age, forming its two ridges of hills. Its north shore faces Long Island Sound, while the south shore borders barrier islands and the Atlantic. At its eastern end it splits into the North Fork and the South Fork, the latter containing the Hamptons.

History

Long Island was inhabited by Algonquian-speaking peoples before European settlement by the Dutch in the west and the English in the east in the seventeenth century. It was the scene of the Battle of Long Island in 1776, the largest battle of the American Revolutionary War. After the Second World War its suburbs expanded rapidly, epitomised by Levittown, a model of mass-produced suburban housing.

Transport

The Long Island Rail Road, the busiest commuter railroad in North America, links the island with Manhattan, and numerous bridges and tunnels connect it to New York City.
