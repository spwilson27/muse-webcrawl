Du Yuesheng

Du Yuesheng in the 1930s (placeholder image)

Du Yuesheng (22 August 1888 – 16 August 1951), commonly known as "Big-Eared Du", was a Chinese mob boss who spent much of his life in Shanghai. He was a leader of the Green Gang, the most powerful criminal organization in early-20th-century Shanghai, and became one of the most influential figures in the city's underworld and, through alliances with the Kuomintang, in its politics and business.

Early life

Born in Gaoqiao, Pudong, Du was orphaned young and raised in poverty. Orphaned and apprenticed to a fruit seller in the Shanghai French Concession, he joined the Green Gang and rose through its ranks under the patronage of Huang Jinrong, the gang's leader and a senior detective in the French Concession police.

Rise to power

Together with Huang Jinrong and Zhang Xiaolin, Du formed the "Three Tycoons" of the Shanghai underworld. The gang controlled opium trafficking with the tolerance of the French Concession authorities, alongside gambling, prostitution and protection rackets. Du cultivated respectability, founding the Zhonghui Bank and holding company directorships and charitable posts.

Alliance with the Kuomintang

In April 1927 Du's gang, acting with Chiang Kai-shek's forces, carried out the Shanghai massacre of communists and trade unionists, crushing the labour movement in the city. Du was rewarded with official honours and influence. During the Second Sino-Japanese War he initially supported resistance efforts before relocating to Hong Kong and then Chongqing.

Later years and death

After 1949, as the communists took power in China, Du settled in Hong Kong, where he died in 1951. His life has been the subject of numerous books and films and remains emblematic of Republican-era Shanghai's fusion of crime, commerce and politics.
