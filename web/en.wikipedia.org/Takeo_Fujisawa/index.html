Takeo Fujisawa

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Takeo Fujisawa (藤沢 武夫, Fujisawa Takeo, 10 November 1910 – 30 December 1988) was a Japanese businessman who co-founded the Honda Motor Co., Ltd. alongside Soichiro Honda. Fujisawa oversaw the financial and marketing side of the company while Honda concentrated on engineering, and the partnership is credited with turning a small motorcycle workshop into one of the world's largest manufacturers of motorcycles and automobiles.

Early life and career

Fujisawa was born in Tokyo in 1910. He worked in the steel industry and in advertising before the Second World War, and served as an officer in the Imperial Japanese Army. After the war he ran a small business trading in building materials.

Partnership with Honda

Fujisawa met Soichiro Honda in 1949 and invested capital in Honda's fledgling company, joining as managing director. He built Honda's sales network, pushed the company into overseas markets — most famously establishing American Honda in 1959 — and promoted the Super Cub, which became the best-selling motor vehicle in history. Fujisawa insisted the company enter automobile production and supported Honda's participation in Formula One racing.

Fujisawa and Honda retired together in 1973, remaining as supreme advisers. Fujisawa died in Tokyo in 1988. A famous exchange between the two men — Honda's request that Fujisawa "look after the money" and Fujisawa's reply that Honda should simply make good products — is a staple of Japanese business lore.
