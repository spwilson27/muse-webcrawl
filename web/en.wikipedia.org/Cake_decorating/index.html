Cake decorating

Cake decorating is the art of covering a cake with icing, frosting, fondant, or other edible decorations to improve its appearance. Decorated cakes are central to celebrations such as birthdays, weddings, anniversaries, graduations, and holidays, and range from simple piped designs to elaborate multi-tiered sculpted creations.

History

Cake decoration developed in Europe in the 17th century as refined sugar became more available; royal icing was named for its use on Queen Victoria's wedding cake in 1840. The French developed elaborate patisserie decoration, and in the late 19th century the Lambeth method of intricate piped royal-icing work became influential. In the 20th century, rolled fondant from Australia and new tools and airbrushing made sculpted celebration cakes widely popular, and television baking competitions in the 2000s further popularized the craft.

Techniques

Common techniques include piping with buttercream or royal icing through shaped nozzles; covering cakes with rolled fondant or marzipan; gum-paste and fondant modelling of flowers and figures; painting with edible colours; stencilling; and creating textures with combs and impression mats. Structural supports such as dowels and cake boards are used for tiered cakes.

Types of icing

Buttercream is soft, flavourful, and easy to pipe. Royal icing, made from egg whites and icing sugar, dries hard and suits detailed piping and construction. Fondant gives a smooth, matte finish suited to sculpted and wedding cakes. Ganache — chocolate and cream — is used as both filling and a firm coating.
