Bournville

Bournville is a model village in southwest Birmingham, England, founded by the Quaker Cadbury family for the workers of its chocolate factory. The name combines the Bourn, a stream running through the area, with the French word ville (town), chosen partly because French confectionery had a prestige Cadbury wished to invoke.

History

In 1879 brothers Richard and George Cadbury moved their cocoa and chocolate works from central Birmingham to the greenfield Bournbrook estate, renamed Bournville, served by the new railway station at Stirchley Street. From 1893 George Cadbury bought additional land and, in 1900, established the Bournville Village Trust to develop a garden suburb of well-built houses with gardens, schools, baths, and green spaces — deliberately open to non-employees as well as workers, and famously without public houses, in keeping with Quaker temperance. The estate influenced the wider garden city movement.

Bournville today

Chocolate production by Cadbury (now part of Mondelez International) continues on the site, and the visitor attraction Cadbury World opened in 1990. The name also survives in Cadbury's Bournville dark chocolate, launched in 1908. The Bournville Village Trust still manages the estate, whose Arts and Crafts architecture and low-density layout are protected by conservation rules.
