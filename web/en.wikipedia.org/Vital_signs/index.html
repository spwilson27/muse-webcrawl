Vital signs (also known as vitals) are a group of the four to six most crucial medical signs that indicate the status of the body's vital (life-sustaining) functions. These measurements are taken to help assess the general physical health of a person, give clues to possible diseases, and show progress toward recovery.

The four primary vital signs

The four primary vital signs routinely monitored by medical professionals are body temperature, blood pressure, pulse (heart rate), and respiratory rate. Normal ranges vary with age, sex, weight, and overall health. A normal resting heart rate for adults ranges from 60 to 100 beats per minute; normal blood pressure is commonly cited as around 120/80 mmHg; and normal body temperature is near 37 °C (98.6 °F).

Additional signs

Pain has been called the "fifth vital sign" in some clinical settings, and pulse oximetry (oxygen saturation) is also routinely measured with modern monitoring equipment. In emergency medicine, composite scores such as the Modified Early Warning Score (MEWS) and NEWS aggregate vital-sign deviations to flag deteriorating patients.

Monitoring

Vital signs may be measured manually with a thermometer, sphygmomanometer, and watch, or automatically with patient monitors. Wearable devices and home health equipment have made continuous measurement of heart rate, temperature, and oxygen saturation common outside hospitals.
