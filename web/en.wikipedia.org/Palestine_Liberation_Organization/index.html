The Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO; Arabic: منظمة التحرير الفلسطينية) is a Palestinian nationalist umbrella organisation founded in 1964 with the goal of establishing an independent Palestinian state. It has been widely recognised as the "sole legitimate representative of the Palestinian people" by over 100 states and by the Arab League in 1974, and it holds observer status at the United Nations.

History

The PLO was established at the first Arab League summit conference in Cairo in January 1964 and formally founded in Jerusalem that May, with Ahmad Shuqayri as its first chairman. Its founding charter called for the liberation of Palestine through armed struggle. In 1969, Yasser Arafat, leader of the Fatah movement, became chairman and Palestinian fedayeen factions took control of the organisation. After the 1970–71 Black September conflict, the PLO was expelled from Jordan and relocated to Lebanon, from which it operated until Israel's 1982 invasion drove it to Tunis.

Oslo and recognition of Israel

In 1988 the Palestinian National Council issued a declaration of independence, and in 1993 the PLO and Israel formally recognised each other in the letters exchanged before the Oslo I Accord. Chairman Arafat renounced terrorism and recognised Israel's right to exist, while Israel recognised the PLO as the representative of the Palestinian people. The Oslo process created the Palestinian National Authority to administer limited self-rule in parts of the West Bank and Gaza Strip. Arafat died in 2004 and was succeeded by Mahmoud Abbas.

Structure

The PLO's legislative body is the Palestinian National Council (PNC), effectively a parliament-in-exile, and its executive body is the Executive Committee, elected by the PNC. Its major constituent factions include Fatah, the Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine (PFLP) and the Democratic Front for the Liberation of Palestine (DFLP). Hamas and Islamic Jihad, which emerged later, are not members.
