CNN effect

The CNN effect is a theory in political science and media studies that holds that global, real-time television news coverage — exemplified by CNN's 24-hour reporting of the 1991 Gulf War — can exert significant influence on the foreign policy decisions of governments.

Concept

Proponents argue that vivid, continuous coverage of wars, famines and disasters creates public pressure that forces policymakers to act, shortens decision-making time, and can accelerate or impede military operations. The concept is associated with cases such as the US intervention in Somalia in 1992 and its subsequent withdrawal after images of dead American soldiers in Mogadishu in 1993.

Criticism

Scholars debate the strength of the effect. Research by Steven Livingston and others distinguishes agenda-setting, accelerant and impediment effects, and the "manufacturing consent" model argues the causation often runs the other way: officials use media strategically. Empirical studies find media influence strongest where policy is uncertain and coverage is critically framed.
