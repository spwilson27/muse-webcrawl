Krynica Morska (Polish pronunciation: [krɨˈɲit͡sa ˈmɔrska]; German: Kahlberg) is a town on the Vistula Spit in northern Poland, in Pomeranian Voivodeship. It is one of the smallest towns in Poland by population, yet a popular summer seaside resort on the Baltic Sea.

Geography and tourism

The town lies on the narrow Vistula Spit between the Baltic Sea and the Vistula Lagoon, not far from the border with Russia's Kaliningrad Oblast. Known before 1945 as the German resort of Kahlberg, it offers sandy beaches, a harbour serving excursion boats to Frombork and Elbląg, and a lighthouse originally built in 1851. Since September 2022, the Vistula Spit canal near Nowy Świat has allowed ships to reach the lagoon and the port of Elbląg without passing through Russian waters.

History

First mentioned in the 15th century, the settlement developed in the 19th century as a bathing resort for visitors from Königsberg and Elbing. After the Second World War it became part of Poland and was renamed Łysica before receiving its present name; it obtained town rights in 1991.
