Fernando António Nogueira Pessoa (13 June 1888 – 30 November 1935) was a Portuguese poet, writer, literary critic, translator, and philosopher, regarded as one of the most significant literary figures of the 20th century and one of the greatest poets in the Portuguese language. He wrote extensively under multiple heteronyms — fully realised alternative authorial personalities, each with a distinct biography, style, and worldview.

Life

Pessoa was born in Lisbon. After his father's death, his mother remarried the Portuguese consul in Durban, South Africa, where Pessoa was educated in English and developed a lasting affinity for English literature. He returned to Lisbon in 1905 and earned his living as a commercial translator and foreign correspondent for firms in the Baixa district, while writing for modernist journals such as Orpheu, which he helped launch in 1915.

Heteronyms

Unlike mere pseudonyms, Pessoa's heteronyms possessed their own biographies, physiognomies, and poetic programmes. The three principal ones are Alberto Caeiro, a naive pastoral "master" of the others; Álvaro de Campos, a naval engineer and flamboyant futurist; and Ricardo Reis, a classicist physician who wrote Horatian odes. Pessoa created dozens more, including Bernardo Soares, the assistant bookkeeper credited with The Book of Disquiet, a fragmentary "factless autobiography" published only decades after his death.

Legacy

Pessoa published little in his lifetime — only one book of Portuguese poems, Mensagem (1934) — but left a trunk containing some 25,000 pages of manuscripts. Posthumous editing and publication of this archive established his international reputation; his statue sits outside the Café A Brasileira in Lisbon's Chiado, where he was a regular.
