Dykstraflex

The Dykstraflex is a motion-control camera system developed in the 1970s by special effects pioneer John Dykstra and his colleagues. It revolutionized visual effects cinematography by allowing camera movements to be precisely programmed and repeated.

Development

Dykstra developed the system while working on Star Wars (1977) at Industrial Light & Magic. By computerizing the movement of a camera mounted on a crane and track, the system could film miniatures with dynamic, swooping moves while keeping focus, and repeat the identical move for multiple passes so that separately filmed elements could be composited together.

Impact

The Dykstraflex made possible the high-energy space battle sequences of Star Wars, for which Dykstra and his team shared the Academy Award for Best Visual Effects. It founded the field of motion-control photography, which became standard practice in effects work for decades.
