Ecdysis

Ecdysis is the moulting of the cuticle in many invertebrates of the clade Ecdysozoa. Because the cuticle forms a rigid exoskeleton that cannot grow, the animal must periodically shed it and form a larger replacement in order to increase in size. The term derives from the ancient Greek ékdysis, "shedding" or "taking off."

Process

Moulting begins with apolysis, the separation of the old cuticle from the underlying epidermis, followed by secretion of a new cuticle and the shedding of the old one. The steroid hormone ecdysone, after which the process and the clade are named, triggers and coordinates the sequence. During and just after ecdysis the animal is soft-bodied and vulnerable, so many species hide until the new cuticle hardens.

Groups

Ecdysozoans include arthropods (insects, spiders, crustaceans), nematodes, velvet worms, tardigrades, and several other phyla — together a majority of described animal species. In arthropods with metamorphosis, ecdysis separates the successive developmental stages, or instars.
