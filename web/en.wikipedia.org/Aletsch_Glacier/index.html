Aletsch Glacier

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The Aletsch Glacier from the Eggishorn area

The Aletsch Glacier (German: Aletschgletscher) is the largest glacier in the Alps. It is located on the southern slopes of the Bernese Alps in the canton of Valais, Switzerland. The glacier is about 23 kilometres (14 mi) long, covers more than 120 square kilometres (46 sq mi), and holds an estimated 15.4 cubic kilometres of ice.

The whole area, including other glaciers, is part of the Jungfrau-Aletsch Protected Area, which was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2001.

Geography

The Aletsch Glacier is composed of four smaller glaciers converging at Konkordiaplatz, where its thickness is estimated to reach nearly 900 metres (3,000 ft). From there the ice stream flows south-east in a sweeping curve toward the Rhône valley, giving rise to the Massa river.

The glacier is flanked by well-known peaks including the Jungfrau, Mönch and Eiger to the north, and the Aletschhorn to the west. Popular viewpoints over the glacier include the Jungfraujoch, Eggishorn, Bettmerhorn and Riederfurka.

Retreat

Like most glaciers worldwide, the Aletsch is retreating due to climate change. It has lost substantial length and thickness since the late 19th century, and projections suggest it will shrink dramatically over the 21st century if warming continues, with consequences for water supply in the Rhône basin.

Tourism

The Aletsch Arena resorts of Riederalp, Bettmeralp and Fiescheralp provide access for hiking and skiing, and guided glacier treks depart in summer. The Pro Natura Center Aletsch at Riederfurka offers exhibitions on the glacier and its environment.
