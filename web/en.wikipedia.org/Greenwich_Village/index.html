Greenwich Village

Greenwich Village, often simply called "the Village", is a neighbourhood on the west side of Lower Manhattan in New York City, roughly bounded by 14th Street to the north, the Hudson River to the west, Houston Street to the south and Broadway to the east. Unlike most of Manhattan, its streets largely follow an older, irregular pattern rather than the 1811 grid plan. The area is home to New York University and Washington Square Park.

Cultural history

From the early 20th century Greenwich Village was the centre of American bohemian culture, attracting artists, writers and political radicals. It was closely associated with the Beat Generation in the 1950s and with the folk music revival of the 1960s, when performers such as Bob Dylan played its clubs. The Stonewall riots of 1969 at the Stonewall Inn on Christopher Street made the Village a symbolic centre of the modern gay liberation movement. Since the late 20th century, gentrification has made it one of New York's most expensive residential areas, though it retains landmark-protected historic districts and an active off-Broadway theatre scene, including the historic Provincetown Playhouse and the Village Vanguard jazz club.
