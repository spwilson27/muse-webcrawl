Non-prosecution agreement

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A non-prosecution agreement (NPA) is a contract between a prosecutor and a defendant — frequently a corporation — under which the prosecutor agrees not to bring criminal charges in exchange for the defendant's compliance with specified conditions. Unlike a deferred prosecution agreement, an NPA is not filed with a court, so no judge formally approves or supervises it.

Terms

Typical conditions include payment of penalties and restitution, full cooperation with ongoing investigations, admissions of relevant facts, remedial measures, and reforms to compliance programmes. If the defendant breaches the agreement, prosecutors may bring charges, often using the admitted facts. If the term expires without breach, no charges are filed and there is no criminal conviction.

Use and criticism

The United States Department of Justice has used NPAs and deferred prosecution agreements extensively in corporate criminal enforcement since the 1990s, particularly after the collapse of Arthur Andersen demonstrated the collateral consequences of indicting a large company. Critics argue the practice lets corporations and executives avoid accountability, while supporters say it secures cooperation, restitution, and reform without destroying companies and innocent employees' jobs.
