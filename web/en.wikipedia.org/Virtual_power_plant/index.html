Virtual power plant

A virtual power plant (VPP) is a system that integrates several types of distributed energy resources — such as rooftop solar panels, battery storage systems, electric vehicles, and controllable loads — so that they can be monitored, coordinated, and dispatched collectively, effectively operating as if they were a single conventional power plant.

Operation

A central software platform (sometimes called an aggregator) communicates with connected devices over the internet, forecasting generation and demand and optimising dispatch. VPPs can sell energy into wholesale markets, provide grid services such as frequency response and peak shaving, and reduce curtailment of renewable generation.

Examples

Notable deployments include the South Australian VPP, rolled out from 2018 by Tesla and the Government of South Australia, linking household solar and Powerwall batteries in social housing; utility programs in the United States enrolling smart thermostats and home batteries; and aggregators in Europe pooling flexible industrial and residential loads. Regulatory reforms such as FERC Order 2222 in the U.S. opened wholesale markets to aggregated distributed resources.
