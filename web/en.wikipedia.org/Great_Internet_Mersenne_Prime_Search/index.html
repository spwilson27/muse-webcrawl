The Great Internet Mersenne Prime Search (GIMPS) is a collaborative project of volunteers who use freely available software to search for Mersenne prime numbers. Founded in January 1996 by computer scientist George Woltman, GIMPS is one of the earliest and most successful distributed computing projects on the Internet.

Background

A Mersenne prime is a prime number of the form 2p − 1 where p is itself prime. Only about fifty such numbers are known, and each very large prime discovered since the 1990s has been a Mersenne prime. GIMPS software — chiefly the Prime95 program — tests candidates using the Lucas–Lehmer primality test, optimized with fast Fourier transform multiplication, and coordinates the assignment and double-checking of exponents through the PrimeNet server.

Discoveries

Volunteers have found the last seventeen or so Mersenne primes. The largest known prime number for much of recent history has been a GIMPS discovery: in December 2018 Patrick Laroche found 282,589,933 − 1, a number with nearly 25 million digits. In October 2024 a former Nvidia engineer, Luke Durant, using GPUs in a large-scale cloud effort coordinated through GIMPS, discovered 2136,279,841 − 1, the 52nd known Mersenne prime, with over 41 million digits.

The Electronic Frontier Foundation offered a $100,000 cooperative computing award for the first prime of at least ten million digits, claimed for the 2008 discovery of 243,112,609 − 1.
