Volpone

Volpone
Written by: Ben Jonson
First performed: 1605–1606
Genre: Comedy / satire
Setting: Venice, Italy

Volpone (Italian for "sly fox") is a comedy play by the English playwright Ben Jonson, first produced in 1605–1606. It is a savage satire of greed and lust, and is regarded as one of the greatest comedies in English literature and Jonson's finest work alongside The Alchemist.

Plot

Volpone, a wealthy Venetian magnifico with no children, pretends to be on his deathbed in order to trick a succession of legacy-hunters — Voltore the lawyer, Corbaccio the miser and Corvino the merchant — each of whom showers him with gifts in the hope of being named his heir. Volpone is assisted by his cunning servant Mosca (the "fly"), who stokes each suitor's expectations. The schemes unravel when Volpone attempts to seduce Corvino's wife Celia, and in the final scene the deceivers are punished by the Venetian court.

Themes and style

The characters bear the names of animals — fox, fly, vulture, raven, crow — reflecting the play's beast-fable structure. Jonson drew on Roman satire and the commedia dell'arte, and the play's prologue promises to "mix profit with your pleasure."

Reception

Volpone has been regularly revived since the Restoration and adapted into operas, films and Stefan Zweig's celebrated free adaptation of 1926.
