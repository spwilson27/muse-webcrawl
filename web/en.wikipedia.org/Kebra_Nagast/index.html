Kebra Nagast

The Kebra Nagast, or The Glory of the Kings, is a 14th-century Ethiopian national epic written in Ge'ez. It recounts how the Queen of Sheba (Makeda) met King Solomon of Israel, the birth of their son Menelik I, and how Menelik brought the Ark of the Covenant to Ethiopia — thereby founding the Solomonic dynasty that ruled Ethiopia until 1974.

Content

The work has 117 chapters. It begins with theological discussion among 318 "orthodox fathers" about the glory of God and the kingship of Zion, and proceeds through biblical history retold from an Ethiopian perspective. The central narrative describes Makeda's journey to Jerusalem, Solomon's seduction of her, and the young Menelik's voyage to meet his father, after which he returns home accompanied by the firstborn sons of Israel's elders — and, unbeknownst to Solomon at first, carrying the true Ark of the Covenant, leaving a replica behind in Jerusalem.

Significance

Compiled around 1321, traditionally attributed to the Aksumite noble Nebura'ed Yeshaq, the text draws on older Ethiopian, Arabic, Coptic and biblical sources. It provided the ideological foundation for the Ethiopian monarchy's claim of Solomonic descent, was invoked in Ethiopia's 1955 constitution, and remains sacred in Ethiopian Orthodox tradition and influential in Rastafari belief. The standard English translation is that of E. A. Wallis Budge (1922).
