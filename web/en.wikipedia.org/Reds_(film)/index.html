Reds is a 1981 American epic historical drama film, co-written, produced, and directed by Warren Beatty, about the life and career of the American communist journalist and writer John Reed, who chronicled the October Revolution in his 1919 book Ten Days That Shook the World. Beatty stars as Reed, with Diane Keaton as the writer and activist Louise Bryant, and Jack Nicholson as the playwright Eugene O'Neill.

Plot

The film follows Reed and Bryant from their meeting in 1915 through the Greenwich Village bohemian scene, Reed's reporting on the Pancho Villa revolution in Mexico and the labour struggles of industrial America, and their journey to Russia in 1917, where they witness the Bolshevik seizure of power. Intercut with the drama are interviews with "The Witnesses" — real-life contemporaries of Reed and Bryant, filmed in old age — who recall the era and comment on the couple's lives.

Production

Beatty nursed the project for more than a decade, shooting the Witnesses interviews from the early 1970s. Principal photography took place in England, Finland, Spain and the United States. Vittorio Storaro's cinematography and Stephen Sondheim's score were widely praised, and the film was one of the last major Hollywood epics of its kind.

Reception and awards

Reds received twelve Academy Award nominations and won three: Best Director for Beatty, Best Supporting Actress for Maureen Stapleton as the anarchist Emma Goldman, and Best Cinematography for Storaro. In 2008 the American Film Institute ranked it among the ten greatest epic films ever made.
