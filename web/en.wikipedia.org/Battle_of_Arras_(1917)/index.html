Battle of Arras (1917)

Battle of Arras
Date9 April – 16 May 1917
LocationNear Arras, Pas-de-Calais, France
BelligerentsBritish Empire vs German Empire
CommandersEdmund Allenby, Hubert Gough, Henry Horne; Ludwig von Falkenhausen, Erich Ludendorff
ResultBritish tactical gains; no strategic breakthrough

The Battle of Arras was a British offensive on the Western Front of the First World War, fought from 9 April to 16 May 1917. British, Canadian, New Zealand, Newfoundland and Australian troops attacked German defences near the French city of Arras. It formed the British part of the Allied spring offensive, whose French component was the Nivelle Offensive on the Aisne.

The attack

The opening day, 9 April 1917 (Easter Monday), was among the most successful of the war for the British: in the north, the Canadian Corps captured Vimy Ridge in a carefully prepared assault using creeping barrages, tunnels, and rehearsed platoon tactics; in the centre the First Army advanced up to 5 km in places, the deepest single-day advance since trench warfare began. South of the Scarpe, however, III Corps and the cavalry exploitation at Monchy-le-Preux proved costly. A snowstorm on the first day hampered movement but also concealed the attackers.

Attrition

After the initial success the battle degenerated into attritional fighting for positions such as Bullecourt, where Australian troops suffered heavily in two attacks (11 April and 3 May). By its end, the British had gained ground and drawn German reserves away from the French sector, but at a cost of roughly 150,000 British and Commonwealth casualties against around 120,000–130,000 German. The daily casualty rate was the highest of any major British offensive of the war, yet the battle demonstrated new infantry-artillery methods that matured later in 1917 and 1918.
