Mike Powell (long jumper)

Michael Anthony Powell (born 10 November 1963) is an American former track and field athlete, and the holder of the long jump world record. At the 1991 World Championships in Athletics in Tokyo, Powell broke Bob Beamon's almost 23-year-old record by jumping 8.95 metres (29 ft 4¼ in) — a record that still stands. He is a two-time world champion and two-time Olympic silver medallist.

The 1991 world record

The Tokyo final on 30 August 1991 produced one of the great duels in athletics history. Carl Lewis, unbeaten in the long jump for a decade, produced the finest series of jumps ever seen, including a wind-assisted 8.91 m that surpassed Beamon's mark but could not count as a record. In the fifth round Powell responded with a legal jump of 8.95 m, with a tailwind of +0.3 m/s, breaking Beamon's 8.90 m record set at altitude in Mexico City in 1968. Lewis's final jumps of 8.87 m and 8.84 m were not enough, and Powell took gold.

Career

Powell had earlier won silver behind Lewis at the 1988 Seoul Olympics and repeated the result at Barcelona in 1992. He defended his world title in Stuttgart in 1993 with 8.59 m and took bronze at the 1995 World Championships. He retired after the 1996 Atlanta Olympics but made brief comeback attempts. Powell later worked as a coach and athletics ambassador and commentator.

Legacy

His 8.95 m remains, as of the mid-2020s, the longest-standing record in the men's long jump and one of the oldest records in men's athletics; only a handful of athletes have ever jumped beyond 8.80 m. Powell's rivalry with Lewis elevated the event in the early 1990s.
