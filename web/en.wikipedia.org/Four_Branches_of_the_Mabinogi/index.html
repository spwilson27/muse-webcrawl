The Four Branches of the Mabinogi or Pedair Cainc y Mabinogi are the earliest prose stories in the literature of Britain. Originally written in Wales in Middle Welsh, they are some of the earliest stories in the Welsh language. The stories survive in the White Book of Rhydderch (c. 1350) and the Red Book of Hergest (c. 1400), though the tales themselves are generally dated to the 11th or 12th century.

The four branches

The first branch, Pwyll Prince of Dyfed, tells of Pwyll's sojourn in the otherworld of Annwn, his marriage to Rhiannon, and the birth and disappearance of their son Pryderi. The second, Branwen Daughter of Llŷr, recounts the disastrous marriage of Branwen to the king of Ireland and the devastating war between Britain and Ireland. The third, Manawydan Son of Llŷr, follows Manawydan and Pryderi in an enchanted, emptied Dyfed. The fourth, Math Son of Mathonwy, deals with the wizard Math, Gwydion, Arianrhod, and the creation of Blodeuedd, a woman made of flowers.

Significance

Though drawing on older Celtic mythology and oral tradition, the tales are works of sophisticated medieval literature. Together with the other tales included in translations under the title The Mabinogion, they have deeply influenced Welsh culture and modern fantasy literature.
