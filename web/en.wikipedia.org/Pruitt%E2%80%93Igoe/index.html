The Pruitt–Igoe housing project was a large urban public housing complex in St. Louis, Missouri. Completed in 1954, it consisted of 33 eleven-storey apartment buildings designed by architect Minoru Yamasaki. It was named for Wendell O. Pruitt, an African-American fighter pilot from St. Louis, and William L. Igoe, a former US Congressman.

Decline

Although initially praised, the complex deteriorated rapidly owing to underfunding of maintenance, high vacancy rates, segregation, and broader urban decline in St. Louis. By the late 1960s it had become notorious for poverty, crime, and vandalism, and was largely vacant.

Demolition and legacy

Demolition began in 1972 — the televised implosion of the first towers became an iconic image — and was completed by 1976. Architectural historian Charles Jencks famously declared that modern architecture "died" with Pruitt–Igoe's demolition. Later scholarship has emphasised that the failure owed as much to funding structures, racial segregation, and population loss as to design, a debate revisited in the 2011 documentary The Pruitt-Igoe Myth.
