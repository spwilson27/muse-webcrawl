Rugby union, commonly known simply as rugby, is a contact team sport that originated at Rugby School in England in the first half of the 19th century. It is played with an oval ball on a rectangular field, with teams of fifteen players carrying, passing, kicking and grounding the ball to score points.

Gameplay

Points are scored by grounding the ball beyond the opponents' goal line for a try (5 points), converting it with a kick (2 points), or kicking penalty and drop goals (3 points each). The ball may be passed only backwards or laterally, so territory is gained mainly by running with the ball or kicking. Set pieces — the scrum and the line-out — restart play after infringements and knock-ons, while tackles, rucks and mauls contest possession in open play.

History and spread

The Rugby Football Union was founded in 1871; a schism in 1895 over payments to working-class players produced the separate code of rugby league. Rugby union remained officially amateur until 1995. The sport is especially strong in New Zealand, South Africa, Wales, Ireland, France, Australia, England, Scotland, Fiji and Argentina. New Zealand's All Blacks and South Africa's Springboks have each won multiple Rugby World Cups; South Africa won the 2023 tournament in France.
