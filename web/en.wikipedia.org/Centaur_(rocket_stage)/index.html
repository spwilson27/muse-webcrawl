Centaur (rocket stage)

The Centaur is a family of rocket propelled upper stages that has been in use since 1962. It is currently produced by U.S. launch service provider United Launch Alliance, with one main active version, the 5.4 m (18 ft) diameter Common Centaur/Centaur V, flying on the Atlas V and Vulcan rockets. Centaur was the first rocket stage to use liquid hydrogen (LH2) and liquid oxygen (LOX) propellants.

A single-stage version flies as the upper stage of the Atlas V, while the two-stage variant flew on the Atlas III. Work began in 1956 at General Dynamics under Krafft Ehricke, and the stage pioneered pressure-stabilised balloon tanks of stainless steel, common-bulkhead insulation and the RL10 engine.

History

The first Atlas-Centaur launch in May 1962 failed, but subsequent flights carried Surveyor landers to the Moon and Mariners to Mars and Venus. Centaur upper stages flew on Titan III and Titan IV for large military and NASA payloads, launching the Viking, Voyager, Cassini, New Horizons and other planetary missions. On Vulcan, the enlarged Centaur V continues the line.

Design

Centaur's balloon tanks require constant pressurisation to maintain structural integrity. The stage is powered by one or two Aerojet Rocketdyne RL10 engines, restartable in space, giving high specific impulse (~450 s) from the hydrogen/oxygen combination.
