Ratio test

The ratio test compares a series to a geometric series

In mathematics, the ratio test (or d'Alembert's criterion) is a criterion for the convergence of a series whose terms are real or complex numbers. The test was first published by Jean le Rond d'Alembert and is sometimes known as d'Alembert's ratio test.

Statement

For a series Σ an, consider the limit L = lim |an+1 / an| as n → ∞. If L < 1, the series converges absolutely. If L > 1, the series diverges. If L = 1, the test is inconclusive, and the series may converge or diverge.

Examples

Applied to the series Σ n / 2n, the ratio of successive terms approaches 1/2, so the series converges. Applied to the harmonic series Σ 1/n, the limit is 1, and the test gives no information—the series in fact diverges. The test is especially effective for series involving factorials and powers, such as the power series for the exponential function, which it shows to converge for all real and complex arguments.

Refinements

When the limit itself does not exist, the test can be stated using the limit superior and limit inferior of the ratio: if the lim sup is less than 1 the series converges absolutely, while if the lim inf exceeds 1 the series diverges. Stronger criteria, such as Raabe's test, can decide some cases where the ratio test is inconclusive.
