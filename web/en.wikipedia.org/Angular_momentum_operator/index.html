Angular momentum operator

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In quantum mechanics, the angular momentum operator is one of several related operators analogous to classical angular momentum. It plays a central role in the theory of atomic and molecular structure, because the total angular momentum of a system is conserved and its quantisation explains the discrete spectra of atoms.

Orbital and spin angular momentum

The orbital angular momentum operator L arises from the classical expression r × p, with the momentum replaced by the operator −iħ∇. Spin angular momentum, described by the operator S, is an intrinsic property of particles with no classical analogue. The total angular momentum is J = L + S.

Commutation relations and eigenvalues

The components of angular momentum do not commute: [Jx, Jy] = iħJz, with cyclic permutations. However, J2 commutes with any single component, conventionally Jz, so states can be labelled by the quantum numbers j and m. The eigenvalues of J2 are ħ2j(j+1), where j is a non-negative integer or half-integer, and those of Jz are ħm with m ranging from −j to +j in integer steps. Ladder operators J+ and J− raise and lower m.

Applications

Angular momentum operators underpin the classification of atomic orbitals (s, p, d, f…), the theory of angular momentum coupling and Clebsch–Gordan coefficients, selection rules for spectroscopic transitions, and the description of particle spin.
