Erdős–Turán conjecture

The Erdős–Turán conjecture is an old and influential problem in additive number theory formulated by Paul Erdős and Pál Turán in 1941. It concerns the size of the largest subset of the integers {1, …, n} that contains no three-term arithmetic progression, a quantity that later became known as a central question of extremal combinatorics.

Statement

The conjecture asserts that if A is a subset of the natural numbers such that the sum of the reciprocals of its elements diverges, then A contains arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions. This statement is much stronger than an ordinary density result, because a set whose reciprocals sum diverges can still have density zero.

History

Klaus Roth proved in 1953 that subsets of positive upper density contain three-term progressions, and Endre Szemerédi later proved the full Szemerédi theorem giving arbitrarily long progressions in any set of positive upper density. The Erdős–Turán conjecture, however, would go far beyond this, applying to sparse sets as well. In 1976 Szemerédi proved a special case related to the conjecture for four-term progressions in restricted settings.

Related work

Progress continued with the work of Ben Green and Terence Tao, who proved in 2008 that the prime numbers contain arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions — the Green–Tao theorem. The full Erdős–Turán conjecture remains open, and is regarded as one of the deepest unsolved problems in additive combinatorics.
