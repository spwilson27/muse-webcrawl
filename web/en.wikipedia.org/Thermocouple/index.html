Thermocouple

A thermocouple is an electrical device consisting of two dissimilar electrical conductors forming a junction that produces a temperature-dependent voltage — an application of the Seebeck effect discovered in 1821. Thermocouples are among the most widely used temperature sensors in industry and science because they are simple, rugged, self-powered and cover very wide temperature ranges.

Types

Standardised types are designated by letters: type K (chromel–alumel) is the general-purpose standard, type J uses iron–constantan, and types R, S and B use platinum–rhodium alloys for high temperatures. Accuracy is typically a few degrees, lower than resistance thermometers but adequate over ranges from cryogenic temperatures to above 2000 °C.
