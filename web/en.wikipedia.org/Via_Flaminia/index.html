Via Flaminia

LocationCentral Italy
Built220 BC
BuilderGaius Flaminius
RouteRome to Ariminum (Rimini)

The Via Flaminia was an ancient Roman road leading from Rome over the Apennine Mountains to Ariminum (Rimini) on the coast of the Adriatic Sea, and was the main road from Rome into northern Italy for centuries. It was constructed by Gaius Flaminius during his censorship in 220 BC. It leaves Rome through the Porta del Popolo (the ancient Porta Flaminia), crosses the Tiber at the Milvian Bridge, passes through Umbria — including Ocriculum, Narnia, Interamna and Spoletium — and crosses the Apennines at the Furlo Pass before reaching Fanum Fortunae (Fano) and Ariminum.

History

The road saw the Battle of the Metaurus nearby in 207 BC and was repeatedly restored, notably by Augustus and later emperors. It remained in use throughout the Middle Ages and the Renaissance; the modern Strada Statale 3 follows much of its course. At Rimini the road met the Via Aemilia, where the Arch of Augustus marked its terminus.
