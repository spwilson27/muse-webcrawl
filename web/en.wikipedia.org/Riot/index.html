Riot

A riot is a form of civil disorder characterised by a group lashing out with violence against authority, property, or people. Riots typically involve destruction of property, public or private, and may include arson, looting and attacks on police or bystanders.

Causes

Riots arise from varied grievances — perceived injustice, police violence, economic hardship, political repression, sports results, or food prices. Historical examples range from the anti-Catholic Gordon Riots in London (1780) and bread riots across Europe, to the race riots of 20th-century American cities and the 2011 England riots. Sociologists note that riots often occur where crowds believe peaceful channels of protest have failed, though many riots are spontaneous and driven by local triggers.

Police response and law

Riot control is conducted by police (and sometimes the military) using formations, shields, batons, tear gas, water cannon, rubber or plastic bullets, and mass arrests. In England and Wales, riot is a statutory offence under the Public Order Act 1986, requiring 12 or more people using or threatening unlawful violence for a common purpose; historically, the Riot Act 1714 allowed authorities to order crowds to disperse after a proclamation. Many jurisdictions criminalise incitement to riot as well.

Riot types

Analysts distinguish political and protest riots, race riots, prison riots, sports riots, and food riots, though real events often combine motives, and the boundary between a riot and a protest or uprising is frequently contested.
