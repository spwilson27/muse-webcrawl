The Humberstone and Santa Laura Saltpeter Works are two former saltpeter (sodium nitrate) refineries in the Atacama Desert of the Tarapacá Region in northern Chile. They preserve the remains of the nitrate industry that dominated Chile's economy from the 1880s to the 1930s, and were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2005.

History

The Santa Laura works were established in 1872 and Humberstone — originally called La Palma — in the same period; Humberstone was rebuilt and renamed in 1934 after James Thomas Humberstone, a British chemical engineer who introduced the Shanks extraction process to the region. At their peak the two company towns housed thousands of workers (pampinos) drawn from Chile, Bolivia, and Peru. The collapse of the natural nitrate market after the development of synthetic nitrogen fixation (the Haber–Bosch process) and the Great Depression devastated the industry; both works closed in 1960 and the towns were abandoned to the desert.

Preservation

The ghost towns retain industrial plant, offices, housing, a theatre, church, market, and swimming pool built from a recycled ship's hull. UNESCO listed the site in 2005 while simultaneously placing it on the List of World Heritage in Danger because of looting, earthquake damage, and the fragility of the wooden and adobe structures; it was removed from the danger list in 2019 following conservation work.
