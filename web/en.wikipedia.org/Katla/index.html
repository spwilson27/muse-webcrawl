Katla is a large volcano in southern Iceland, partially covered by the Mýrdalsjökull glacier. It is one of Iceland's most active volcanoes, having erupted at least twenty times since AD 930. Its caldera is about 10 kilometres in diameter, and the volcano has a long history of producing powerful subglacial eruptions accompanied by enormous glacial outburst floods, known in Icelandic as jökulhlaup.

Eruptions

The 1918 eruption melted vast quantities of ice and caused a flood whose peak discharge is estimated to have rivalled that of the Amazon River, extending Iceland's southern coastline by several kilometres. Katla's eruptions have often followed eruptions of the neighbouring Eyjafjallajökull volcano, as happened in 2010, when a subsequent Katla eruption was feared but did not occur.

Monitoring

Because a large eruption could flood the coastal plain and destroy farms, Katla is among the most closely monitored volcanoes in Iceland, with seismic stations, GPS measurements and river gauges watching for signs of unrest.
