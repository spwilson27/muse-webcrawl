The River Severn (Welsh: Afon Hafren) is the longest river in Great Britain, at about 354 kilometres (220 miles). It rises on Plynlimon in the Cambrian Mountains of mid Wales, flows through Shropshire, Worcestershire, and Gloucestershire, and discharges into the Bristol Channel through the Severn Estuary, which has the second-highest tidal range in the world.

Course

From Plynlimon the river descends through Llanidloes, Newtown, and Welshpool in Powys, then enters England and loops around Shrewsbury. It passes through the Ironbridge Gorge — cradle of the Industrial Revolution and site of the first iron bridge, opened in 1781 — then flows past Worcester, Tewkesbury (where it is joined by the Warwickshire Avon), and Gloucester. Its chief tributaries are the Vyrnwy, Teme, Stour, and Avon.

Estuary and bore

Below Gloucester the river becomes a broad tidal estuary crossed by the two Severn road bridges. The estuary's funnel shape and huge tidal range — up to about 15 metres — generate the Severn bore, a tidal wave that travels upstream on high spring tides. Proposals for a tidal barrage to harness this energy have been debated for over a century. The Severn Estuary is designated for its birdlife and intertidal habitats.
