Cell nucleus

The cell nucleus (from Latin nucleus, "kernel" or "seed") is a membrane-bound organelle found in eukaryotic cells. It contains most of the cell's genetic material, organised as DNA molecules complexed with histone proteins to form chromosomes, and serves as the control centre of the cell.

Structure

The nucleus is enclosed by the nuclear envelope, a double membrane perforated by nuclear pore complexes that regulate the passage of molecules. Inside lies the nucleoplasm, containing chromatin and the nucleolus, the site of ribosomal RNA synthesis and ribosome assembly. During division in the cell cycle, the envelope breaks down in most eukaryotes.

Function

The nucleus stores the genome, controls gene expression and thereby protein synthesis, replicates DNA before cell division, and produces ribosomal and messenger RNA. By separating transcription from translation, it allows elaborate regulation of gene activity.

History

The nucleus was among the first organelles observed: Antonie van Leeuwenhoek noted it in fish red blood cells, and Robert Brown described it as a constant feature of plant cells in 1831. Franz Bauer had drawn it as early as 1804.
