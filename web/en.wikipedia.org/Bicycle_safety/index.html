Bicycle safety

Bicycle safety is the use of road traffic safety practices to reduce risk associated with cycling. Risk can be defined as the number of incidents occurring for a given amount of cycling time. Safety measures include cyclist behaviour and equipment, infrastructure design, and vehicle design, and are debated in relation to phenomena such as "safety in numbers".

Risks

The main risks to cyclists arise from collisions with motor vehicles, falls, and collisions with other road users or street furniture. Head injuries account for a large share of serious cyclist injuries and fatalities, which has motivated debate over bicycle helmets and helmet legislation. Studies comparing countries generally find lower injury rates where cycling is common and infrastructure is well developed, such as the Netherlands and Denmark.

Infrastructure

Design measures include separated cycle tracks, protected junctions, traffic calming, and lower speed limits. Research generally finds that physically separated infrastructure on busy roads reduces injury risk, while the effect of painted on-road lanes is more modest. Roundabouts are a particular hazard unless designed with cyclists in mind.

Equipment and behaviour

Common recommendations include functioning brakes, lights and reflectors at night, visible clothing, and riding predictably in traffic. The effectiveness and possible risk-compensation effects of high-visibility clothing and helmets remain subjects of research and public debate.
