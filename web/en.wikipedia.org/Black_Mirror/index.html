Black Mirror

Black Mirror is a British anthology television series created by Charlie Brooker. The series explores various genres, with most episodes set in near-future dystopias containing sci-fi technology — a type of speculative fiction. The series uses the themes of technology and media to comment on contemporary social issues. Most episodes are written by Brooker with heavy involvement by executive producer Annabel Jones.

The "black mirror" of the title is the dark screen of phones and monitors.

Premise and themes

Brooker has explained that the title refers to the black screens of phones, tablets and televisions when switched off: "the black mirror" found on every wall and desk. Episodes are standalone, generally set in alternative presents or near futures, and often examine social media, virtual reality, artificial intelligence, surveillance and digital consciousness with bleak or satirical outcomes.

History

The first two series aired on Channel 4 in 2011 and 2013, plus the 2014 Christmas special "White Christmas". Netflix commissioned twelve further episodes in 2015, released as series three (2016) and four (2017), followed by the interactive film Black Mirror: Bandersnatch (2018), a fifth series in 2019, a sixth in 2023, and a seventh in 2025.

Reception

The series has received widespread critical acclaim and won multiple Primetime Emmy Awards for Outstanding Television Movie for the episodes "San Junipero", "USS Callister" and "Bandersnatch". Episodes such as "Nosedive", "Fifteen Million Merits" and "The Entire History of You" have entered popular discussion as shorthand for technology-related anxieties.
