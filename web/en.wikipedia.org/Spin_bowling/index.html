Spin bowling

Spin bowling is a bowling technique in cricket in which the ball is delivered slowly but with rapid rotation, so that it deviates in flight through the Magnus effect and bounces unpredictably off the pitch. A bowler who uses this technique is called a spinner. The main aim of spin bowling is to deceive the batter with the flight, turn and bounce of the ball.

Categories

Spin bowling is divided by the hand used and the direction of spin. Right-arm bowlers bowl either off spin, turning the ball from off side to leg side for a right-handed batter, or leg spin, turning it the other way using the wrist. Left-arm bowlers bowl orthodox spin (mirroring off spin) or wrist spin ("chinaman"). Finger spinners rely on their fingers to impart revolutions; wrist spinners use the wrist and can generate more turn but usually with less control.

Deliveries and variations

Spinners vary pace, flight and spin: the googly (a leg-break bowled with an off-break action), the doosra, the arm ball, the top spinner and the carrom ball are classic variations designed to beat the batter. Because spin bowling is slow — usually 70–95 km/h — its effectiveness increases on dry, worn pitches that grip the ball.

Famous spin bowlers

Shane Warne and Muttiah Muralitharan, the two highest wicket-takers in Test cricket history, were both spinners, as were Anil Kumble, Jim Laker, Clarrie Grimmett and Bishan Bedi.
