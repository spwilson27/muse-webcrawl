16:9 is the standard international aspect ratio for high-definition television, computer monitors, and most modern video content. It describes a display width of 16 units and height of 9 units, giving a ratio of approximately 1.78:1. Since the mid-2000s, 16:9 has displaced the older 4:3 format in television and computing.

History

The ratio was proposed in the 1980s by Kerns H. Powers, a member of the SMPTE working group on high-definition electronic production, as a compromise geometrically midway between the 4:3 television ratio and the wider 2.35:1 cinematic ratios. It was adopted for HDTV standards including 720p and 1080i/1080p.

Common resolutions

Standard 16:9 resolutions include 854×480, 1280×720 (HD), 1920×1080 (Full HD), 2560×1440 (QHD), and 3840×2160 (4K UHD). Derivative ratios such as 16:10 were once common in laptop displays.
