Gauss's law

In physics and electromagnetism, Gauss's law, also known as Gauss's flux theorem, is one of Maxwell's equations relating the distribution of electric charge to the resulting electric field.

FieldElectromagnetism
Formulated byCarl Friedrich Gauss (published 1867, devised 1835)

Statement

In integral form, the law states that the electric flux through any closed surface is equal to the total electric charge enclosed by the surface divided by the permittivity of free space: ΦE = Q/ε0. Its differential form states that the divergence of the electric field equals the charge density divided by ε0.

Applications

Gauss's law simplifies the calculation of electric fields for charge distributions with spherical, cylindrical, or planar symmetry. Analogous laws exist for magnetism (no magnetic monopoles) and gravity, where the gravitational flux is proportional to the enclosed mass.
