English football league system

The English football league system, also known as the football pyramid, is a series of interconnected leagues for men's association football clubs in England, with five teams from Wales also competing in it, plus one from Guernsey, one from Jersey and one from the Isle of Man. Hierarchical, with promotion and relegation between leagues at different levels, the system allows even the smallest club the theoretical possibility of ultimately rising to the very top.

Structure

At the summit sits the Premier League (level 1, 20 clubs), a single division whose members are shareholders. Below it, the English Football League (EFL) comprises the Championship (level 2), League One (level 3) and League Two (level 4), each of 24 clubs. Levels 1–4 together form "League" football, with 92 clubs in total.

Beneath the EFL lies non-League football: the National League (level 5) and its regional feeder divisions at level 6 (National League North and South), then the semi-professional leagues of levels 7–8 (Northern Premier League, Southern League, Isthmian League), and county and local leagues extending down beyond level 10.

Promotion and relegation

Three clubs move between the Premier League and Championship each season. Automatic promotion and play-offs govern movement within the EFL; two clubs are relegated from League Two to the National League, whose champions and play-off winners replace them. At lower levels, promotion depends also on ground grading and league constitutions.

History

The Football League was founded in 1888, the world's first national league competition. The pyramid's unified structure, with automatic promotion and relegation between the League and non-League, was completed only in the 1980s; the Premier League broke away in 1992 under a separate organisation while retaining promotion and relegation links.

Cup competitions

Alongside the league system run the FA Cup, open to clubs down to level 9, the EFL Cup for the 92 League clubs, the FA Trophy for non-League clubs, and the FA Vase for the lowest tiers.
