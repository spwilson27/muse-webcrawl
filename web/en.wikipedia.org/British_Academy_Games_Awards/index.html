British Academy Games Awards

The British Academy Games Awards is an annual British awards ceremony honouring creative achievement in the video game industry. First presented in 2004 by the British Academy of Film and Television Arts (BAFTA), they are among the most prestigious awards in the games industry, often described as the British equivalent of the Academy Awards for video games. The ceremony is usually held each spring in London.

History

BAFTA first recognised video games with dedicated awards in 1998 and 1999 as the BAFTA Interactive Entertainment Awards. The standalone Games Awards were established in 2004, covering games released in 2003, and have been presented annually since, with the second ceremony held in 2005 and the awards settling into a regular spring schedule thereafter. Over the years the event has been hosted by presenters including Dara Ó Briain, who hosted many ceremonies, and has been streamed live to a global audience.

Categories

The flagship award is Best Game. Other categories include British Game, Debut Game, Game Design, Artistic Achievement, Audio Achievement, Music, Narrative, Original Property, Multiplayer, Performance in a Leading Role and in a Supporting Role, Technical Achievement, and the publicly voted EE Game of the Year (formerly EE Mobile Game of the Year / Players' Choice). BAFTA also presents a Fellowship, its highest honour, to individuals who have made an outstanding contribution to games; recipients have included Shigeru Miyamoto, Peter Molyneux, Gabe Newell, John Carmack, Hideo Kojima, Tim Sweeney, and Shuhei Yoshida.

Notable winners

Best Game winners have included Half-Life 2, Fallout 3, Mass Effect 2, Portal 2, Dishonored, The Last of Us, Destiny, Fallout 4, Uncharted 4: A Thief's End, What Remains of Edith Finch, God of War (2018), Outer Wilds, The Last of Us Part II, Returnal, Vampire Survivors, and Baldur's Gate 3.
