Ho Chi Minh City

Ho Chi Minh City (Vietnamese: Thành phố Hồ Chí Minh), commonly known by its former name Saigon (Vietnamese: Sài Gòn), is the most populous city in Vietnam, with a population of around 10 million in the municipality and some 14 million in the metropolitan area. Located in southeastern Vietnam, the city surrounds the Saigon River and covers about 2,061 square kilometres. It is the country's largest economic and financial centre, though Hanoi is the capital.

History

The area was originally a Khmer fishing settlement called Prey Nokor before Vietnamese settlers arrived in the 17th century. Renamed Gia Định and then Saigon, the city was captured by the French in 1859 and became the capital of French Cochinchina, acquiring grand colonial boulevards and buildings such as the Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica, the Central Post Office and the Municipal Theatre.

After the Geneva Accords of 1954, Saigon became the capital of the Republic of Vietnam (South Vietnam). During the Vietnam War it hosted the US-backed government and a large American presence; the war ended with the Fall of Saigon on 30 April 1975, marked by North Vietnamese tanks entering the Independence Palace. In 1976 the reunified Socialist Republic of Vietnam renamed the city after Hồ Chí Minh, the revolutionary leader and first president of North Vietnam. The name "Saigon" remains in everyday use, especially for the city centre.

Geography and climate

The city lies in the transition between the Mekong Delta and southeastern Vietnam, at an average elevation of only about 19 metres, making it vulnerable to flooding and sea-level rise. It has a tropical savanna climate with a wet season from May to November and a dry season from December to April; average temperatures hover near 28 °C year-round.

Economy

Ho Chi Minh City is Vietnam's economic engine, contributing roughly a fifth to a quarter of national GDP. Its economy is driven by services, finance, real estate, electronics and garment manufacturing, and high-tech industry in parks such as the Saigon Hi-Tech Park. The first line of the Ho Chi Minh City Metro opened in December 2024, linking Bến Thành with Suối Tiên.

Landmarks

Popular sites include Independence (Reunification) Palace, the War Remnants Museum, Bến Thành Market, the Saigon Notre-Dame Cathedral, the Central Post Office designed in the era of Gustave Eiffel's workshop, the Bitexco Financial Tower and Landmark 81 — among the tallest buildings in Southeast Asia — and the backpacker district of Phạm Ngũ Lão. The Củ Chi tunnels lie on the city's northwestern outskirts.

Administration

The municipality is administered as a centrally governed city; since a 2025 reorganization, its district structure was streamlined, and the former Thủ Đức city and districts were reorganized into wards and communes. The city's People's Committee governs under the national government in Hanoi.
