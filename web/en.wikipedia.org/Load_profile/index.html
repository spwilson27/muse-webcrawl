Load profile

In electrical engineering, a load profile is a graph of the variation in the electrical load versus time. A load profile varies according to customer type (residential, commercial and industrial), temperature and holiday schedules. Power producers use this information to plan how much electricity they need to deliver at any given time, and grid operators use it to balance supply and demand.

Characteristics

Load profiles are typically expressed for a day, showing the pattern of demand across 24 hours. Residential profiles commonly show a morning peak as people wake and an larger evening peak as they return home, cook and use lighting and heating or cooling. Commercial loads rise with business hours; industrial loads may be flatter where processes run continuously. Seasonal profiles capture the effect of weather, with summer peaks in hot climates driven by air conditioning and winter peaks in cold climates driven by heating.

Utilities derive standard profiles for classes of customers where interval metering is unavailable — a practice known as load profiling or load research — and use them in electricity markets to settle consumption between suppliers. With the spread of smart meters, actual interval data increasingly replaces estimated profiles.

Use in planning

Generation planning, storage sizing and demand response programmes all depend on load profiles. Concepts derived from them include the load duration curve, which ranks demand from highest to lowest regardless of when it occurs; peak demand and load factor, the ratio of average to peak load. Flattening the profile — through storage, time-of-use pricing or demand-side management — reduces the need for rarely used peaking plant.
