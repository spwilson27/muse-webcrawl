United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea

The United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS) is an international treaty that establishes a legal framework for all marine and maritime activities. It was adopted on 10 December 1982 in Montego Bay, Jamaica, after more than a decade of negotiation at the Third United Nations Conference on the Law of the Sea, and it entered into force on 16 November 1994. It is often described as a "constitution for the oceans."

Maritime zones

UNCLOS defines baselines from which maritime zones are measured. Coastal states enjoy sovereignty over a territorial sea up to 12 nautical miles; a contiguous zone extends to 24 nautical miles for customs, fiscal, immigration, and sanitary enforcement; and an exclusive economic zone (EEZ) extends to 200 nautical miles, within which the coastal state has sovereign rights over natural resources while other states retain freedoms of navigation and overflight. States may also claim a continental shelf, in some cases beyond 200 nautical miles, with rights to seabed resources. Beyond national jurisdiction lie the high seas and "the Area" — the deep seabed whose mineral resources are declared the common heritage of mankind and administered by the International Seabed Authority.

Other provisions

The convention guarantees innocent passage through territorial seas and transit passage through international straits, sets obligations for protecting the marine environment, regulates marine scientific research, and provides compulsory dispute settlement, usually before the International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea in Hamburg or arbitral tribunals.

Non-parties

The United States signed the 1994 implementing agreement on Part XI (deep seabed mining) but has never ratified the convention, principally over seabed-mining provisions; successive U.S. administrations have nonetheless stated that the convention's navigation and EEZ provisions reflect customary international law, which the U.S. observes. In 2023, states adopted a further implementing agreement on biodiversity beyond national jurisdiction (the "High Seas Treaty").
