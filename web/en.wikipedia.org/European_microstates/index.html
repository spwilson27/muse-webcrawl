European microstates

European microstates or European ministates are a set of very small sovereign states and city-states in Europe. In the modern context the term is typically used to mean the six smallest states in Europe by area (and generally also by population): Andorra, Liechtenstein, Malta, Monaco, San Marino and Vatican City. Four of these states are monarchies (three principalities—Andorra, Liechtenstein, and Monaco—and one papal elective theocracy: Vatican City). These states trace their status back to at least the nineteenth century.

Definition

Definitions vary, but the common six are distinguished from dependencies such as the Faroe Islands, Gibraltar, Guernsey, the Isle of Man and Jersey, which are not sovereign states. Luxembourg, with over 670,000 inhabitants, is often too large to be counted among the microstates, although it was historically part of the group in the nineteenth century.

Political and economic characteristics

All six are parliamentary or constitutional systems of varying forms. Malta is a republic and a member of the European Union; San Marino, Andorra, Monaco and Vatican City use the euro under monetary agreements with the EU without being members. Liechtenstein is in the European Economic Area and uses the Swiss franc. Andorra is a parliamentary co-principality whose heads of state are the President of France and the Bishop of Urgell.

Sports

The microstates compete together at the Games of the Small States of Europe, a multi-sport event held biennially, in addition to participating in the Olympic Games and other international competitions.
