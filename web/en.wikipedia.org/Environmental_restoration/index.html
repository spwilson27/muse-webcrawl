Environmental restoration

Environmental restoration is the practice of assisting the recovery of ecosystems that have been degraded, damaged, or destroyed. It includes ecological restoration of habitats, remediation of contaminated sites, reforestation, wetland reconstruction, and removal of barriers such as obsolete dams.

Goals and methods

Projects may reintroduce native species, control invasive organisms, rebuild soils, restore natural water flow, and reconnect fragmented habitats. Practitioners often use reference ecosystems to set goals while recognizing that climate change may make historical conditions impossible to reproduce exactly.

Policy and scale

Restoration is required after mining and industrial activity in many jurisdictions and is promoted by international initiatives for forests, wetlands, and degraded lands. Large programs include river restoration, prairie reconstruction, mangrove planting, and cleanup of hazardous waste sites.

Challenges

Restoration can fail when pollution sources remain, funding ends early, or planted species are unsuited to future conditions. Monitoring and adaptive management are essential. See also ecological restoration, reforestation, and environmental remediation.
