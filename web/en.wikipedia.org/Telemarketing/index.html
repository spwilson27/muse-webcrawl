Telemarketing

Telemarketing (sometimes known as inside sales, or telesales in the UK and Ireland) is a method of direct marketing in which a salesperson solicits prospective customers to buy products, subscriptions or services, either over the phone or through a subsequent face to face or web conferencing appointment scheduled during the call. Telemarketing can also include recorded sales pitches programmed to be played over the phone via automatic dialing, commonly known as robocalls.

Categories

Telemarketing activity is commonly divided by direction and audience:

  • Outbound — agents proactively call potential or existing customers.
  • Inbound — agents respond to calls generated by advertising or sales activity.
  • Business-to-business (B2B) — sales directed at other businesses.
  • Business-to-consumer (B2C) — sales directed at individual consumers.
  • Lead generation — gathering information about prospective customers for later follow-up.

History

Telephone selling grew with the spread of household telephones in the mid-20th century. In the 1970s and 1980s, WATS lines and later computer-assisted dialing made large call centres economical, and the practice expanded into a major industry. The rise of predictive dialers allowed firms to place calls automatically and connect agents only when a person answered. In later decades, much consumer telemarketing was outsourced to lower-cost regions, and the growth of caller ID, mobile phones and call-blocking apps steadily reduced answer rates.

Regulation

Telemarketing is regulated in many jurisdictions. In the United States, the Telephone Consumer Protection Act of 1991 and the Federal Trade Commission's Telemarketing Sales Rule restrict calling hours, require disclosure of the seller's identity, and prohibit calls to numbers on the National Do Not Call Registry, established in 2003. The TRACED Act of 2019 strengthened measures against illegal robocalls, and the STIR/SHAKEN framework was deployed to authenticate caller ID. Other countries operate similar preference services, such as the Telephone Preference Service in the United Kingdom, and the European Union's privacy rules restrict unsolicited marketing calls and electronic messages.

Criticism

Telemarketing is frequently criticized as intrusive, and fraudulent telemarketing — including scams targeting the elderly — has been a persistent enforcement problem. Legitimate operators distinguish themselves through compliance with do-not-call lists, scripted disclosures, and opt-out mechanisms.
