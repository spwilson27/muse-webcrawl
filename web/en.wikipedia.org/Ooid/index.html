Ooid

Ooids are small, spheroidal, coated sedimentary grains, usually composed of calcium carbonate, but sometimes of iron- or phosphate-based minerals. They typically have a diameter of 0.25 to 2 millimetres; grains larger than 2 mm are called pisoids. A rock composed mainly of ooids is an oolite.

Ooids form on shallow marine shoals where strong tidal currents and agitation keep grains suspended and rolling, allowing concentric layers of calcite or aragonite to precipitate around a nucleus such as a shell fragment or sand grain. Famous modern examples occur in the Bahamas and Shark Bay, Australia; ancient oolitic limestones include the Cotswold stones of England.

Formation

Formation combines chemical precipitation from supersaturated warm seawater, microbial mediation, and physical abrasion that keeps grains rounded and polished. Growth stops when grains become too heavy or are buried.
