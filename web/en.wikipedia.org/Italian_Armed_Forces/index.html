Italian Armed Forces

The Italian Armed Forces comprise the Army, Navy, Air Force and Carabinieri.

The Italian Armed Forces (Italian: Forze armate italiane) are the military forces of the Italian Republic. They consist of four branches: the Italian Army (Esercito Italiano), the Italian Navy (Marina Militare), the Italian Air Force (Aeronautica Militare) and the Carabinieri (Arma dei Carabinieri), a gendarmerie with both military and civilian police functions.

Organisation

The President of the Republic is commander-in-chief in a largely ceremonial capacity; executive authority rests with the government through the Ministry of Defence and the Chief of the Defence Staff. Total active personnel number around 165,000. Compulsory military service was suspended in 2005, making Italy's forces an all-volunteer professional body.

Role and operations

Italy is a founding member of NATO and one of the largest contributors to United Nations and European Union peacekeeping missions, with deployments historically in Lebanon, Kosovo, Afghanistan and the Mediterranean. The Navy operates two light aircraft carriers, Cavour and Trieste, unusual for a regional power.
