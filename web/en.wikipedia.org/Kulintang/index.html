Kulintang

The kulintang, a row of bossed gongs played as the melodic core of southern Philippine ensembles.

Kulintang is a modern term for an ancient instrumental form of music composed on a row of small, horizontally laid gongs that function melodically, accompanied by larger, suspended gongs and drums. This music is part of the larger gong-chime culture of Southeast Asia and plays a major role in the culture of a number of peoples in the Malay Archipelago, most prominently among the Maguindanao, Maranao, and Tausug peoples of the southern Philippines, as well as related traditions in Malaysia, Indonesia (where it is known as kolintang in North Sulawesi), and Brunei.

Instrument

The kulintang instrument itself consists of a row of five to nine (commonly eight) graduated bossed gongs, kettle-shaped with a raised central knob, laid horizontally on a wooden rack or frame called an antangan, often elaborately carved and painted with okir designs. The gongs are graduated by pitch, and the largest gong, having the lowest pitch, is placed at the player's left. The player, traditionally a woman in Maguindanao and Maranao practice, strikes the knobs with two light wooden beaters, weaving rapid, interlocking melodic patterns.

Ensemble

In the Maguindanao kulintang ensemble, the kulintang proper provides the melody, while other instruments supply the rhythmic and colotomic framework: the gandingan, a set of four large hanging bossed gongs used for "talking" or answering phrases; the agong, a pair of large, wide-rimmed suspended gongs marking the main rhythmic cycle; the babandil, a small gong with a high, thin sound that keeps the beat; and the dabakan, a goblet-shaped wooden drum. Among the Maranao, a similar ensemble centers on the kulintang with the agong, gandingan, and the babandir.

History and cultural role

Gong-chime music arrived in the Philippine archipelago through centuries of trade and cultural exchange with the wider Austronesian and Malay world. Kulintang music is used at weddings, feasts, healing rituals, and other festive occasions, and historically served as a medium of communication and entertainment in royal courts. Because the tradition predates the Islamization and Christianization of the Philippines, it remains a valued symbol of indigenous identity. The American composer and scholar Danongan Kalanduyan was influential in bringing Maguindanao kulintang music to international audiences and universities in the United States.
