Dov Levine

Dov Levine.

Dov Levine is an American physicist known for his contributions to the theory of quasicrystals and soft condensed matter physics. Together with Paul Steinhardt in 1984, he introduced the concept of quasicrystals—structures that are ordered but not periodic—as a theoretical explanation for the diffraction patterns observed by Dan Shechtman, work that reshaped crystallography.

Career

Levine received his doctorate from the University of Pennsylvania, where he worked with Steinhardt. He later held positions at the Technion – Israel Institute of Technology in Haifa, where he became a professor of physics. His research interests include quasicrystals, granular materials, jamming, and the physics of disordered and soft matter.

Honours

Levine shared major recognition for the quasicrystal work, and he is a fellow of professional physics societies. His papers with Steinhardt coined the term "quasicrystal" and proposed quasiperiodic tilings as models of the new phase of matter.
