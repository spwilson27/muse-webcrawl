Tosafot

The Tosafot (Hebrew: תוספות, "additions" or "supplements") are medieval commentaries on the Talmud, composed by a network of rabbinic scholars — the Tosafists — in France and Germany during the 12th to 14th centuries. They appear in nearly every standard printed edition of the Talmud, traditionally on the outer margin of the page, opposite the commentary of Rashi.

Character and method

Whereas Rashi's commentary explains the text line by line, the Tosafot are analytical and dialectical: they pose contradictions between passages, reconcile Rashi with other sources, refine legal definitions and often reach conclusions that differ from the earlier commentator. Beginning with Rashi's own grandsons — notably Rabbeinu Tam — successive generations of Tosafists corresponded and debated across northern France and the Rhineland, and their discussions were collected into editions associated with schools such as those of Sens, Évreux and Touques.

Significance

Together with Rashi, the Tosafot became the foundational glosses through which the Babylonian Talmud has been studied ever since. Their method of critical comparison (pilpul in its early form) profoundly shaped Ashkenazi rabbinic scholarship and halakhic reasoning.
