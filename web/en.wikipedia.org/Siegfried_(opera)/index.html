Siegfried (WWV 86C) is the third of the four music dramas that constitute Richard Wagner's cycle Der Ring des Nibelungen (The Ring of the Nibelung). It premiered at the Bayreuth Festspielhaus on 16 August 1876, as part of the first complete performance of the Ring.

Composition

Wagner began work on Siegfried in 1856–57 but interrupted it for twelve years to compose Tristan und Isolde and Die Meistersinger von Nürnberg, resuming in 1869 and completing the score in February 1871. The libretto draws chiefly on the Norse Völsunga saga and the Middle High German Nibelungenlied. Wagner described it as the "scherzo" of the cycle, lighter in tone than its companions.

Synopsis

The hero Siegfried, son of Siegmund and Sieglinde, has been raised in the forest by the dwarf Mime, who hopes the fearless boy will kill the dragon Fafner and win him the Nibelung treasure. Siegfried reforges his father's shattered sword Nothung, slays Fafner, and tastes the dragon's blood, gaining understanding of the Woodbird's song. He kills the treacherous Mime, crosses the ring of fire around Brünnhilde's rock, and awakens her with a kiss, in a closing love duet.

Notable music

The orchestral interlude Waldweben ("Forest Murmurs") in Act 2, Siegfried's forging songs in Act 1, and his horn call are among the best-known passages of the Ring.
