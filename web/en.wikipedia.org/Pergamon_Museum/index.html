Pergamon Museum

The Pergamon Museum (German: Pergamonmuseum) is an archaeological museum on Museum Island in Berlin, Germany, and the most visited art museum in the country. Built between 1910 and 1930, it houses monumental reconstructions of ancient architecture within its halls.

Collections

The museum's three wings hold the Collection of Classical Antiquities, the Museum of the Ancient Near East (Vorderasiatisches Museum) and the Museum of Islamic Art. Its star exhibits — largely excavated by German teams in the Ottoman world — are the Pergamon Altar from Pergamon (built by the Attalid kings), the Market Gate of Miletus, the Ishtar Gate and Processional Way of Babylon, and the Mshatta Facade from a Jordanian desert palace. The Islamic collection also displays the Aleppo Room's painted panelling.

History and renovation

Designed by Alfred Messel and Ludwig Hoffmann, the building was purpose-built for these oversized finds after an earlier, smaller Pergamon museum (1901) proved inadequate. Damaged in the Second World War and divided in Berlin's partition, it has been undergoing phased renovation since 2013 as part of the Museum Island master plan, with major wings closed for extended periods; the Pergamon Altar hall was scheduled to remain closed until around 2027. The museum's holdings, like those of many Western institutions, face ongoing restitution debates.
