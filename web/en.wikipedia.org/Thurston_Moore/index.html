Thurston Moore

Thurston Joseph Moore
Born: 25 July 1958, Coral Gables, Florida, U.S.
Genres: Alternative rock, noise rock, experimental, no wave
Instruments: Guitar, vocals
Associated acts: Sonic Youth, Chelsea Light Moving, The Thurston Moore Group

Thurston Joseph Moore (born 25 July 1958) is an American guitarist, singer, and songwriter best known as a co-founder of the alternative rock band Sonic Youth. With Sonic Youth, Moore pioneered the use of alternate guitar tunings, prepared guitars, feedback, and dissonance in rock music, influencing generations of indie and noise-rock musicians. Rolling Stone ranked him among the greatest guitarists of all time, and his memoir Sonic Life was published in 2023.

Career

Moore was born in Coral Gables, Florida, and grew up in Bethel, Connecticut. He moved to New York City in the late 1970s, immersed himself in the downtown no wave and punk scenes, and briefly played in Glenn Branca's guitar ensembles, where he met Lee Ranaldo. In 1981 Moore formed Sonic Youth with Kim Gordon (whom he later married) and Ranaldo. The band developed a distinctive sound built on Moore's and Ranaldo's radically retuned guitars, releasing albums such as Daydream Nation (1988), Goo (1990), and Dirty (1992) that helped define alternative rock and brought underground noise aesthetics to a wider audience.

Outside Sonic Youth, Moore has released solo albums including Psychic Hearts (1995), Trees Outside the Academy (2007), Demolished Thoughts (2011, produced by Beck), The Best Day (2014), Rock n Roll Consciousness (2017), and By the Fire (2020). He founded the Ecstatic Peace! record label, collaborated widely in free-improvisation settings (including with Yoko Ono and John Zorn), and has written and edited books on music, including Mix Tape: The Art of Cassette Culture and a biography of no wave. Following his separation from Gordon, Sonic Youth ceased activity in 2011. Moore has since been based mainly in London.
