Commercial law

Commercial law, also known as business law or mercantile law, is the body of law that governs business and commercial transactions. It covers the rights and conduct of persons and companies engaged in trade, merchandising, and sales, and is considered a branch of civil law dealing with both private and public law issues.

Scope

Commercial law includes the law of contract, sale of goods, negotiable instruments, secured transactions, agency, carriage of goods by sea and land, insurance, and insolvency. In the United States, much of it is codified in the Uniform Commercial Code (UCC), first published in 1952 and adopted in some form by nearly every state.

History

Its roots lie in the lex mercatoria, the customary law developed by medieval merchants and enforced in their own courts at fairs and ports. These customs were gradually absorbed into national legal systems between the 17th and 19th centuries.

International trade law

Cross-border commerce is governed by treaties and model laws such as the UN Convention on Contracts for the International Sale of Goods (1980) and the Incoterms rules published by the International Chamber of Commerce.
