Japanese National Railways

Japanese National Railways (日本国有鉄道, Nippon Kokuyū Tetsudō), abbreviated JNR, was the public corporation that operated Japan's national railway network from 1949 until its privatisation and division on 1 April 1987 into the seven companies of the Japan Railways (JR) Group.

Founded: 1 June 1949

Defunct: 1 April 1987

Successor: Japan Railways Group (JR)

Headquarters: Tokyo, Japan

History

JNR was created in 1949 as a public corporation taking over the government railways previously run by the Ministry of Transport, a reform carried out under the Allied occupation. It oversaw the reconstruction of the war-damaged network and the opening of the Tōkaidō Shinkansen in 1964 — the world's first high-speed railway — in time for the Tokyo Olympics.

Decline and privatisation

From the mid-1960s JNR fell into chronic deficit, driven by motorisation, political pressure to build unprofitable rural lines, labour disputes and mounting interest payments; by the 1980s its debts exceeded ¥37 trillion. The government of Yasuhiro Nakasone resolved on reform: in 1987 JNR was split into six regional passenger companies and one freight company, with long-term debt transferred to a settlement corporation. The privatised JR companies went on to become among the world's most successful railways.
