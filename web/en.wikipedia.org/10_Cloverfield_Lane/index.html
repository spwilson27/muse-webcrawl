10 Cloverfield Lane is a 2016 American science fiction psychological thriller film directed by Dan Trachtenberg in his directorial debut, produced by J. J. Abrams, and written by Josh Campbell, Matthew Stuecken, and Damien Chazelle. The film stars Mary Elizabeth Winstead, John Goodman, and John Gallagher Jr. It is the second installment in the Cloverfield franchise.

The film follows Michelle (Winstead), a young woman who, after a car crash, wakes up in an underground bunker with two men who insist that an attack has left the surface of the Earth uninhabitable. The story was developed from a script titled The Cellar, which was acquired by Bad Robot Productions and reshaped into a loose, "spiritual" successor to the 2008 found-footage film Cloverfield.

Plot

After an argument with her fiancé Ben, Michelle leaves New Orleans and drives through rural Louisiana. Late at night, her car is struck and rolls off the road. She wakes up injured in a bare room, chained to a pipe. Her captor, Howard Stambler, explains that he rescued her and brought her to his underground fallout shelter because a massive attack — possibly chemical or nuclear — has rendered the outside air poisonous.

In the bunker Michelle meets Emmett DeWitt, a young man who helped Howard build the shelter and forced his way in as the attack began. Michelle remains skeptical, and after stealing Howard's keys she nearly escapes, but at the airlock door she encounters a deranged, lesion-covered woman begging to be let in — seeming confirmation of Howard's story.

Over time the three settle into an uneasy domestic routine, but Michelle and Emmett discover evidence suggesting Howard kidnapped and murdered a local teenage girl, Brittany. Conspiring to escape, they construct a makeshift hazmat suit and gas mask. When Howard discovers the plot, he shoots Emmett and dissolves his body in a vat of perchloric acid. Michelle fights Howard, knocking the acid onto him and sparking a fire. She dons the suit, climbs through the air ducts, and escapes the burning bunker.

Outside, Michelle removes her mask and breathes safely — but then sees a biomechanical alien craft in the distance. Attacked by the creature, she destroys it with a Molotov cocktail improvised from Howard's whiskey. Driving away in the dead woman's car, she hears a radio broadcast calling survivors to Baton Rouge and requesting help in Houston. As lightning illuminates more alien ships in the sky, Michelle turns the car toward Houston.

Cast

Mary Elizabeth Winstead as Michelle; John Goodman as Howard Stambler; John Gallagher Jr. as Emmett DeWitt; Suzanne Cryer as Leslie, the woman outside the bunker; Bradley Cooper as Ben (voice, uncredited).

Production

The screenplay, originally titled The Cellar (and earlier Valencia), was written by Josh Campbell and Matthew Stuecken and acquired by Bad Robot under Paramount's Insurge Pictures label. Damien Chazelle was initially attached to direct but left to make Whiplash, contributing rewrites instead; Dan Trachtenberg was hired to direct. Principal photography took place in New Orleans, Louisiana, in late 2014, in near-total secrecy. The alien-involving third act was developed as the Cloverfield connection was layered into the script.

Marketing

The film's existence was revealed publicly only on January 14, 2016, when a teaser trailer was attached to screenings of 13 Hours: The Secret Soldiers of Benghazi — less than two months before release. An alternate reality game involving the fictional Tagruato corporation and Howard's employer Bold Futura extended the franchise's viral marketing tradition.

Release

10 Cloverfield Lane premiered in New York City on March 8, 2016, and was released in the United States on March 11, 2016, in conventional and IMAX theaters. It grossed over $110 million worldwide against a production budget of about $15 million.

Reception

The film received positive reviews, with particular praise for the performances of Winstead and Goodman, Trachtenberg's direction, and the film's claustrophobic tension. Critics were divided over the abrupt genre shift of the final act. It won the Saturn Award for Best Thriller Film, and Goodman won Best Supporting Actor at the same ceremony.

Sequel

A third film, The Cloverfield Paradox, was released on Netflix in February 2018 after a surprise announcement during Super Bowl LII.
