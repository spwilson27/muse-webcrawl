Ahmed Niyazi Bey
Born: 1873, Resne, Ottoman Empire
Died: 17 April 1913, Avlonya (Vlorë)
Allegiance: Ottoman Empire
Known for: Initiating the Young Turk Revolution

Ahmed Niyazi Bey (1873 – 17 April 1913) was an Ottoman military officer of Albanian origin, notable as one of the initiators, guides and heroes of the Young Turk Revolution of 1908 and its political movement, the Committee of Union and Progress (CUP).

Role in the 1908 revolution

Stationed at Resne (modern Resen, North Macedonia), Niyazi Bey feared arrest after the Ottoman investigation into CUP cells in Macedonia. On 3 July 1908 he fled to the mountains with about 200 followers and issued a proclamation demanding the restoration of the 1876 constitution. His defiance, joined days later by Enver Bey and other officers, set off the mutiny that forced Sultan Abdul Hamid II to restore constitutional rule on 24 July 1908.

Later life and death

Celebrated as a "hero of liberty", Niyazi grew disillusioned with the CUP's direction. He retired toward Albania, where he was assassinated on 17 April 1913 in Avlonya while boarding a ship; the assassin was never conclusively identified.
