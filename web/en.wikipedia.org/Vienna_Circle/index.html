Vienna Circle

The Vienna Circle (German: Wiener Kreis) was a group of philosophers, scientists and mathematicians who met regularly in Vienna between 1924 and 1936, principally around Moritz Schlick, to develop and promote logical positivism.

[Image: A group portrait-style photograph of interwar philosophers gathered in a Viennese seminar room.]

Members and ideas

Core members included Rudolf Carnap, Otto Neurath, Hans Hahn, Kurt Gödel (as an occasional participant), Herbert Feigl and Friedrich Waismann. Influenced by Wittgenstein's Tractatus, the Circle championed the verification principle: a statement is meaningful only if it is analytic or empirically verifiable; metaphysics, ethics and theology, as ordinarily framed, were held to be cognitively meaningless.

Dissolution and legacy

The Circle dissolved after Schlick's murder in 1936 and the Nazi annexation of Austria in 1938, dispersing its members to Britain and the United States, where logical positivism strongly shaped analytic philosophy of science.
