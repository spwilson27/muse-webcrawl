Via Alpina

The Via Alpina is a network of five long-distance hiking trails across the Alps, connecting eight countries: Slovenia, Austria, Germany, Liechtenstein, Switzerland, Italy, France and Monaco. The complete network comprises more than 340 stages and over 5,000 km (3,100 mi) of trails. It was created as a project of the Alpine Convention, with the cooperation of national and local authorities, to promote sustainable tourism and cross-border awareness of the Alpine region; the trails were largely completed and inaugurated between 2000 and 2005.

Routes

The longest and best-known itinerary, the Red Trail, runs for 161 stages from Trieste on the Adriatic through Slovenia, Austria, Germany, Liechtenstein, Switzerland, France and Italy to Monaco on the Mediterranean, traversing the whole arc of the Alps. The other routes are the Purple Trail (through Slovenia, Austria and Germany), the Yellow Trail (Italy and Austria), the Green Trail (Liechtenstein and Switzerland), and the Blue Trail (Italy, Switzerland and France).

Organisation

The Via Alpina is coordinated by an international secretariat hosted by the Alpine Convention, together with national secretariats in each country. The routes mostly follow existing, waymarked footpaths and use mountain huts, refuges and village accommodation, so walkers can cross the range without camping equipment. The project emphasises cultural as well as natural heritage, passing through regions speaking German, French, Italian, Slovene and Romansh.
