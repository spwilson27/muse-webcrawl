Mass-Observation

Mass-Observation is a United Kingdom social research organization founded in 1937 by anthropologist Tom Harrisson, poet Charles Madge, and filmmaker Humphrey Jennings. It aimed to create an "anthropology of ourselves" by recruiting volunteer observers and diarists to record everyday life, opinions, dreams, and behavior across Britain.

Work

Through day-diaries, monthly directives (open-ended questionnaires), and reports from trained observers, Mass-Observation documented the abdication crisis, popular superstition, pub culture, and — most famously — the home front during the Second World War, when its reports on morale were supplied to the government. Its study of Bolton, the "Worktown" project, became a landmark of British social research.

Archive and revival

Activity wound down in the 1950s as the organization turned to commercial market research. The archive was deposited at the University of Sussex in 1970, and in 1981 the Mass Observation Project revived the panel of volunteer writers, whose responses to directives continue to be collected as a record of contemporary British life.
