Battle of Warsaw (1920)

The Battle of Warsaw (12–25 August 1920), known in Poland as the "Miracle on the Vistula", was the decisive battle of the Polish–Soviet War. The reborn Polish Republic under Józef Piłsudski defeated the invading Red Army of Mikhail Tukhachevsky at the gates of Warsaw, halting the Soviet westward advance into Europe.

Background

In 1920 Poland's spring offensive into Ukraine was thrown back by a Soviet counteroffensive that drove deep into Poland. By August, Tukhachevsky's Western Front was closing on Warsaw, and Soviet leaders expected the fall of the capital to ignite revolution in Germany and the West. Piłsudski prepared a risky counterstroke from the Wieprz River south of the city while Polish forces, including the Fifth Army of Władysław Sikorski, held and then attacked in the north.

The battle

Sikorski's counterattack from the Modlin area on 14–15 August struck the Soviet flank, while on 16 August Piłsudski's strike group advanced from the Wieprz into the exposed Soviet rear. The Red Army's front collapsed into a chaotic retreat; one Soviet army was driven into East Prussia and interned. Polish intelligence, which had broken Soviet radio codes, played a major role in the victory.

Aftermath

Further Polish victories at Komarów and the Niemen completed the rout. The Treaty of Riga (1921) fixed the Polish–Soviet border well east of the Curzon Line. The battle is counted among the most decisive in European history, preserving Polish independence for two decades and stopping the spread of communism westward by force.
