Role-playing video game

A role-playing video game, commonly abbreviated RPG or CRPG for computer games, is a video game genre in which the player controls the actions of a character (or party of characters) immersed in a defined world, undertaking quests and developing through experience points, levels, and equipment. The genre descends from tabletop role-playing games such as Dungeons & Dragons.

Characteristics

Typical features include a strong narrative, character creation and customisation, statistical attributes (strength, intelligence, and so on), skill trees, inventory management, dialogue with non-player characters, and tactical combat that may be turn-based, real-time, or a hybrid. Player choices often affect the storyline and the game world.

Subgenres and history

Early mainframe games such as dnd (1974) led to home-computer classics including Ultima and Wizardry (both 1981). Japanese console RPGs ("JRPGs") such as Dragon Quest (1986) and Final Fantasy (1987) emphasised linear storytelling and defined characters, while Western RPGs ("WRPGs") such as Fallout and Baldur's Gate stressed open-ended choice. Other branches include action RPGs (Diablo, The Elder Scrolls), massively multiplayer online RPGs (World of Warcraft), tactical RPGs, and roguelikes. The genre remains one of the industry's most commercially and critically successful.
