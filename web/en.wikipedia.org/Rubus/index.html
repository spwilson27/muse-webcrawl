Rubus is a large and diverse genus of flowering plants in the rose family, Rosaceae, that includes raspberries, blackberries and dewberries. Most of these plants have woody stems with prickles like roses; spines, bristles and gland-tipped hairs are also common in the genus.

Description

Rubus plants are usually perennial shrubs with biennial stems (canes) arising from a perennial rootstock. The fruit is an aggregate of drupelets, easily distinguished from a true berry. In raspberries the drupelets separate from the receptacle when picked, leaving a hollow fruit; in blackberries the receptacle is eaten along with the fruit.

Taxonomy

The taxonomy of Rubus is notoriously complex because of hybridisation, polyploidy and apomixis (asexual seed formation). Specialists known as batologists have described hundreds of microspecies, particularly within the blackberry aggregate Rubus fruticosus sensu lato.

Cultivation and uses

Several species are grown commercially for their fruit, including the red raspberry (Rubus idaeus), the blackberry (R. fruticosus aggregate) and hybrids such as the loganberry and boysenberry. The fruits are eaten fresh and used in jams, desserts and drinks, and the leaves are used in herbal teas.
