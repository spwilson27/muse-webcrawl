Bundesrat (German Federal Council)

TypeUpper house (federal council of state governments)
Seats69 votes
Meeting placeFormer Prussian House of Lords, Berlin
Established1949 (Basic Law)

The Bundesrat (Federal Council) is a legislative body that represents the sixteen federated states (Länder) of Germany at the federal level. Unlike a conventional senate, it is not elected: its members are delegates of the state governments, appointed and recallable by them, and each state's votes — between three and six depending on population — must be cast as a bloc. Together with the directly elected Bundestag, it forms part of Germany's federal legislature under the Basic Law of 1949.

Functions

The Bundesrat participates in federal legislation. Laws affecting state finances, administration or constitutional competences require its consent (Zustimmungsgesetze); for other laws it may lodge an objection that the Bundestag can override. Because federal laws are generally administered by the states, the states' consent rights are extensive, and the Bundesrat also approves many government regulations and participates in European Union affairs affecting state competences.

Composition and presidency

The 69 votes are distributed by population: the largest state, North Rhine–Westphalia, holds six, while Bremen holds three. The presidency of the Bundesrat rotates annually among the state minister-presidents in a fixed order based on population; the Bundesrat president is also the constitutional deputy who acts for the Federal President if the office falls vacant or its holder is incapacitated. Since 2000 the Bundesrat has sat in the former Prussian House of Lords building in Berlin.
