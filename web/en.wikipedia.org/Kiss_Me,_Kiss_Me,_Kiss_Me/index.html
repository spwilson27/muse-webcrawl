Kiss Me, Kiss Me, Kiss Me

The Cure's sprawling 1987 double album.

Kiss Me, Kiss Me, Kiss Me is the seventh studio album by the English rock band the Cure, released on 26 May 1987 by Fiction Records. A double album of eighteen tracks, it deliberately mixed the band's dark, atmospheric side with bright, accessible pop, and it became the Cure's international breakthrough, reaching the top ten in several countries and the top 40 of the US Billboard 200.

Recording and style

The album was recorded at Studio Miraval in southern France. Frontman Robert Smith assembled a wide range of material, from the aggressive opener "The Kiss" and the moody "If Only Tonight We Could Sleep" to exuberant pop songs. Smith described the record as the product of two complementary sides of the band: songs written largely by him and songs developed from the whole group's ideas.

Singles and reception

Four singles were released: "Why Can't I Be You?", "Catch", "Just Like Heaven" — which became the band's first significant American hit — and "Hot Hot Hot!!!". Critics praised the album's variety and abundance, and it is frequently ranked among the Cure's essential records, a bridge between the gloom of Pornography and the romantic expansiveness of Disintegration (1989).
