Vita Nuova

The Vita Nuova (Italian for "The New Life") or Vita Nova (Latin title) is a text by Dante Alighieri published in 1294. It is an expression of the medieval genre of courtly love in a prosimetrum style: a combination of prose commentary and 31 poems, chiefly sonnets, centred on Dante's love for Beatrice Portinari.

[Image: Dante Gabriel Rossetti's painting of Dante encountering Beatrice in Florence.]

Content

Dante recounts first seeing Beatrice at the age of nine and falling in love with her at first sight, their greeting nine years later, his schemes to conceal his feelings, her death in 1290, and his eventual resolve to write of her "what has never before been written of any woman" — a promise fulfilled in the Divine Comedy, where Beatrice becomes his guide through Paradise.

Significance

The Vita Nuova is a landmark of the dolce stil novo and one of the earliest examples of autobiographical literature in a European vernacular.
