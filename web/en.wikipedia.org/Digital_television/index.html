Digital television

A flat-panel digital television set

Digital television (DTV) is the transmission of television signals using digital encoding, in contrast to the earlier analog television technology which used analog signals. At the time of its development it was considered an innovative advancement and represented the first significant evolution in television technology since color television in the 1950s.

Advantages

Digital television supports many more channels in the same bandwidth, delivers higher picture quality including high-definition formats, and allows ancillary services such as electronic program guides and interactive features. Digital signals are also more robust: reception tends to be either perfect or absent entirely, rather than gradually degrading as with analog “snow”.

Broadcast standards

Several regional standards were adopted: ATSC in North America, DVB in Europe and much of the world, ISDB in Japan and parts of South America, and DTMB in China. Transitions from analog to digital broadcasting (“digital switchover”) took place country by country from the late 1990s through the 2010s; the United States ended full-power analog broadcasts in June 2009.

Reception

Viewers receive digital television via terrestrial antennas, cable, satellite, or broadband streaming. Older analog sets require a digital-to-analog converter box. Modern sets incorporate integrated digital tuners.
