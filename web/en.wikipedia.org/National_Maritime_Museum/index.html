The National Maritime Museum (NMM) in Greenwich, London, is the leading maritime museum in the United Kingdom and one of the largest of its kind in the world. Together with the Queen's House, the Royal Observatory and the tea clipper Cutty Sark, it forms Royal Museums Greenwich within the Maritime Greenwich World Heritage Site.

History

The museum was created by an Act of Parliament in 1934 and formally opened by King George VI on 27 April 1937. Its core collections came from the Royal Naval College and earlier naval exhibitions. The buildings had housed the Royal Hospital School before its move to Suffolk.

Collections and galleries

Holdings include ship models, navigational instruments, charts, paintings and artefacts spanning British seafaring from the Tudor period onward. Notable exhibits include the uniform coat worn by Admiral Lord Nelson at Trafalgar, the royal barge built for Frederick, Prince of Wales, and extensive polar exploration material. The Sammy Ofer Wing, opened in 2011, added galleries and the museum's main entrance.
