Archive.today

Type of site: Web archiving
Launched: 2012
Also known as: archive.is, archive.ph, archive.md and mirror domains
Registration: None required

Archive.today (stylized archive.today; originally archive.is) is a web archiving service founded in 2012. It takes "snapshots" of web pages on demand and preserves them, including text and images rendered at the time of capture, in a form that remains accessible even if the original page changes or disappears.

Operation

Users submit a URL and the service fetches and stores the page, producing a permanent short link to the archived copy. Unlike crawler-based archives, archive.today captures pages in response to user requests, and it also indexes archives by the original URL so visitors can search for earlier snapshots. The site operates through a rotating set of mirror domains, including archive.is, archive.ph, archive.md, archive.li, archive.vn, and archive.fo.

Differences from other archives

Archived pages are stored largely as static renderings, so elements driven by scripts may be simplified. The service does not observe the robots exclusion standard in the same way as the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine, and it provides a means for page owners to request removal of snapshots, typically a "report abuse" mechanism, though removal policies have been a recurring subject of debate.

Usage and reception

Journalists, researchers, and readers use archive.today links to preserve pages that are paywalled, frequently edited, or at risk of deletion; the service is also frequently used to share copies of social media posts. Its opaque ownership and finances — the operator is not publicly identified — have attracted comment, as has its extensive use during events such as Gamergate and major news stories.
