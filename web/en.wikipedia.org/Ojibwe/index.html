Ojibwe

The Ojibwe (also Ojibwa, Chippewa, or Anishinaabe) are an Indigenous people in North America, among the largest Native groups north of the Rio Grande, living around the Great Lakes in present-day Canada (Ontario, Manitoba) and the United States (Minnesota, Wisconsin, Michigan, North Dakota).

History

Ojibwe oral tradition recounts migration from the Atlantic coast to the Great Lakes. Central to the fur trade with French and British partners, Ojibwe nations signed many treaties ceding land while retaining hunting and fishing rights, and many were removed or confined to reservations and reserves in the 19th century.

Culture

Clans (doodem), the Midewiwin society, birchbark scrolls, wild rice (manoomin) harvesting, and jingle dress traditions are significant. Language revitalisation efforts support Anishinaabemowin immersion schools.
