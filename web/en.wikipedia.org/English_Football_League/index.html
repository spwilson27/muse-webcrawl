English Football League

Founded: 1888

Country: England (and some Welsh clubs)

Divisions: Championship, League One, League Two

Number of clubs: 72

The English Football League (EFL) is a league of professional football clubs from England and Wales. Founded in 1888 as the Football League, it is the oldest such competition in the world, and it was the top-level football league in England until 1992, when the top 22 clubs split from it to form the Premier League.

Structure

Since the 2004–05 season the EFL's three divisions have been branded the Championship, League One, and League Two, each with 24 clubs. Clubs are promoted and relegated between the divisions and, at the bottom, exchanged with the National League, while Championship clubs compete for promotion to the Premier League through the season standings and the play-offs.

History

The Football League was founded by Aston Villa director William McGregor, with twelve original member clubs from the Midlands and northern England. It absorbed its rival, the Football Alliance, in 1892 and expanded steadily, reaching four divisions by the 1920s. The league's clubs have won the EFL Cup (founded 1960) and contest the EFL Trophy as well as the FA Cup.

Competitions

Besides the league itself, the EFL organizes the EFL Cup and the EFL Trophy, and its play-off finals at Wembley Stadium are among the most valuable matches in world football because of the promotion at stake.
