Christian denomination

A Christian denomination is a distinct religious body within Christianity, identified by traits such as a name, history, and system of doctrine and practice, that operates under a common organisational structure. Denominations differ from one another in matters including the nature of the Church, authority, sacraments, and worship.

Major branches

Christianity's principal branches are usually given as Roman Catholicism, Eastern Orthodoxy, Oriental Orthodoxy, the Church of the East, and Protestantism, together with Restorationist movements. The deepest divisions arose from historical schisms: the split between the Church of the East (5th century), the Oriental Orthodox (after 451), the East–West Schism of 1054, and the Protestant Reformation of the 16th century.

Protestant denominations

Protestantism encompasses many denominational families — Lutheran, Reformed, Anglican, Baptist, Methodist, Presbyterian, Pentecostal, Adventist, and others — which themselves contain numerous independent denominations and non-denominational churches. Estimates of the total number of Christian denominations run into the tens of thousands worldwide.

Ecumenism

The modern ecumenical movement seeks greater cooperation and, in some cases, visible unity among denominations, through bodies such as the World Council of Churches and through bilateral doctrinal dialogues.
