San Francisco–Oakland Bay Bridge

The San Francisco–Oakland Bay Bridge, commonly known as the Bay Bridge, is a complex of bridges spanning San Francisco Bay in California, connecting San Francisco and Oakland via Yerba Buena Island. It carries Interstate 80 and about 260,000 vehicles per day across two decks. Opened in 1936, six months before the Golden Gate Bridge, it forms part of one of the longest bridge spans in the United States.

Structure

The western crossing consists of two suspension bridges joined at a central anchorage, meeting the eastern crossing through a tunnel on Yerba Buena Island — once the largest-bore tunnel in the world. The original eastern crossing was a cantilever and truss structure. During the 1989 Loma Prieta earthquake, a section of the eastern span's upper deck collapsed, killing one person and closing the bridge for a month. The eastern span was replaced by a new seismically designed structure, featuring a self-anchored suspension span with a single tower, which opened on 2 September 2013 at a cost of about $6.5 billion. The old eastern span was demolished by 2018.

History

Proposals for a bay bridge date to the Gold Rush era; the project was finally built by the California Toll Bridge Authority beginning in 1933, employing thousands during the Great Depression. Originally carrying cars on its upper deck and trucks, buses and interurban trains on the lower, it was reconfigured in the early 1960s to five westbound lanes above and five eastbound lanes below after rail service ended in 1958. A decorative LED light installation, The Bay Lights, illuminated the western span from 2013.
