Cathy Berberian

Cathy Berberian (born Catherine Anahid Berberian; July 4, 1925 – March 6, 1983) was an American mezzo-soprano and composer based in Italy. She is regarded as one of the most important interpreters of contemporary music of her generation, celebrated for her extraordinary vocal range, theatricality, and ability to move between avant-garde works, folk songs, and popular repertoire.

Early life

Berberian was born in Attleboro, Massachusetts, to Armenian immigrant parents. She studied dance, acting, and voice, and moved to Milan in the early 1950s to study singing. In Milan she met the composer Luciano Berio, whom she married in 1950; they divorced in 1964, but their artistic collaboration continued for many years.

Career

Berio wrote numerous works specifically for Berberian's voice, including Thema (Omaggio a Joyce) (1958), Circles (1960), Sequenza III (1965), and Visage. Her own best-known composition, Stripsody (1966), is a virtuosic solo work built entirely from the onomatopoeic sounds of comic strips. She also premiered or championed works by composers such as John Cage, Sylvano Bussotti, and Igor Stravinsky.

Berberian's repertoire ranged widely: she recorded an album of Beatles songs in Baroque style (Beatles arias), performed Monteverdi and Kurt Weill, and brought a comic, theatrical flair that made her a distinctive figure in postwar music. She died in Rome in 1983, on the day she was scheduled to perform a Marilyn Monroe tribute in a cabaret show.

Legacy

Berberian expanded the expressive possibilities of the singing voice, using extended vocal techniques — laughter, gasps, murmurs, and imitations — that influenced generations of singers and composers. Her recordings of Berio's Folk Songs (1964) remain landmarks of the genre.
