Patanjali

Patanjali (Sanskrit: पतञ्जलि, Patañjali) was a sage in ancient India, thought to be the author of a number of Sanskrit works. The greatest of these are the Yoga Sutras, a classical yoga text, and the Mahabhasya ("Great Commentary") on Sanskrit grammar attributed to him. Scholars debate whether these works were written by the same person, and proposed dates for Patanjali range from the 2nd century BCE to the 4th century CE.

Yoga Sutras

The Yoga Sutras of Patanjali comprise 196 sutras (aphorisms) on the theory and practice of yoga, including the eight limbs of yoga (ashtanga): yama, niyama, asana, pranayama, pratyahara, dharana, dhyana and samadhi. The text became the foundational scripture of the Yoga school of Hindu philosophy.

Mahabhasya

The Mahabhasya is a commentary on selected rules of Sanskrit grammar from Panini's Ashtadhyayi and Katyayana's Varttika. It is dated to the 2nd century BCE and is a major work of Indian linguistics.

Legacy

Patanjali is revered in yoga traditions, and the opening invocation of many yoga classes honours him. In later mythology he is depicted as half-serpent, an incarnation of the serpent Shesha.
