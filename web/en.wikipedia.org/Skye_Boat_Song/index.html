The Skye Boat Song

“The Skye Boat Song” is a Scottish folk song recalling the escape of Prince Charles Edward Stuart (Bonnie Prince Charlie) to the Isle of Skye after his defeat at the Battle of Culloden in 1746, aided by Flora MacDonald, who disguised him as her maid.

Origins

The words were written by Sir Harold Boulton to an air collected by Anne Campbelle MacLeod in the 1870s; the song was published in Songs of the North (1884). The tune is said to have been a Gaelic rowing song, and the swaying rhythm imitates the motion of oars. The famous chorus begins, “Speed, bonnie boat, like a bird on the wing; onwards, the sailors cry.” An alternative set of lyrics was written by Robert Louis Stevenson, beginning “Sing me a song of a lad that is gone.”

Later popularity

The song has been recorded by many performers, including Paul Robeson, and reached new audiences as the theme of the television series Outlander, whose version adapts Stevenson's lyrics. It remains one of the best-known Jacobite songs.
