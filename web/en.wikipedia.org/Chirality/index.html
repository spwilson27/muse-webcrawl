Chirality

Chirality is a property of asymmetry in which an object is not identical to its mirror image — it cannot be superimposed on it — like a human hand. The term derives from the Greek cheir, hand.

In chemistry

A chiral molecule has non-superimposable mirror images called enantiomers, often arising from a carbon atom bonded to four different groups. Enantiomers rotate polarised light oppositely and can behave very differently in biology; one enantiomer of thalidomide is a sedative, the other teratogenic. Life on Earth is homochiral, using L-amino acids and D-sugars.

In physics and mathematics

In particle physics chirality relates to helicity for massless particles and governs weak interactions, which couple only to left-handed particles. In mathematics, knots, helices and other objects can be chiral.
