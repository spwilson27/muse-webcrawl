Untouchability

Untouchability is a form of social ostracisation historically practised in India, in which Dalit communities were excluded, segregated and subjected to discrimination based on notions of ritual purity and pollution.

History

The practice is associated with the caste system, assigning certain communities hereditary occupations considered polluting and denying access to temples, wells and schools.

Abolition

Article 17 of the Constitution of India abolishes untouchability. Laws including the Protection of Civil Rights Act 1955 and the Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act 1989 penalise its practice.

Reformers

B. R. Ambedkar, Mahatma Gandhi and many Dalit movements campaigned against untouchability, though discrimination persists in places.
