Hindutva

IdeologyHindu nationalism
Key textHindutva: Who Is a Hindu? (1923)
AuthorVinayak Damodar Savarkar

Hindutva (lit. 'Hindu-ness') is a political ideology encompassing the cultural justification of Hindu nationalism and the belief in establishing Hindu hegemony within India. The term was formulated by Vinayak Damodar Savarkar in his 1923 pamphlet Hindutva: Who Is a Hindu?, which defined a Hindu in cultural, territorial, and ancestral terms rather than purely religious ones.

Definitions

Savarkar defined a Hindu as one who regards the land of Bharatvarsha, from the Indus to the seas, as both fatherland (pitribhumi) and holy land (punyabhumi). This definition could include practitioners of religions originating in India, such as Hindus, Jains, Buddhists, and Sikhs, while treating adherents of religions with holy lands elsewhere differently.

Organisations and politics

Hindutva is the predominant ideology of organisations including the Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS, founded 1925), the Vishwa Hindu Parishad, and the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP), collectively known as the Sangh Parivar. The BJP has formed governments in India, and Hindutva has been a central force in modern Indian politics.

Reception

Supporters describe Hindutva as a cultural and civilisational identity; critics associate it with majoritarianism and discrimination against religious minorities. The term remains one of the most debated concepts in Indian public life.
