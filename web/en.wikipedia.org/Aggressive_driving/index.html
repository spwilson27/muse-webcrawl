Aggressive driving

Aggressive driving is defined by the United States National Highway Traffic Safety Administration as the behaviour of an individual who "commits a combination of moving traffic offences so as to endanger other persons or property." It is distinguished from the more extreme escalation known as road rage, in which a driver uses a vehicle or other weapon with intent to harm.

Behaviours

Behaviours commonly associated with aggressive driving include:

  • Speeding and racing other vehicles
  • Tailgating (following too closely)
  • Frequent or unsafe lane changes and weaving through traffic
  • Running red lights and ignoring stop signs or other traffic controls
  • Failing to yield the right of way
  • Blocking other vehicles from passing or changing lanes
  • Excessive use of the horn, flashing headlights, or rude gestures

Causes and effects

Contributing factors include traffic congestion, time pressure, anonymity behind the wheel, stress, and a sense of competition. Studies by road-safety organisations such as the AAA Foundation for Traffic Safety have found that aggressive driving behaviours are a factor in a substantial share of fatal crashes; speeding alone is implicated in roughly a third of traffic fatalities in many years.

Countermeasures

Enforcement strategies include targeted patrols, automated speed and red-light cameras, and public education campaigns. Many jurisdictions impose enhanced penalties when several offences are committed together, and some treat aggressive driving as a separate statutory offence, distinct from individual infractions such as speeding.
