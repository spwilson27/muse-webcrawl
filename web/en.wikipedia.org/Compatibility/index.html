Compatibility

Compatible components are designed to work together

Compatibility is the capacity of two or more things — systems, components, substances, or people — to exist or work together without conflict, modification, or harmful interaction. The term is used across engineering, computing, biology, chemistry, medicine, and interpersonal relations.

In computing and engineering

In technology, compatibility describes the ability of hardware, software, or protocols to interoperate. Backward compatibility means a newer system can work with data, formats, or devices designed for an older one; forward compatibility is the ability of an older system to accommodate future designs. Interoperability standards, such as file formats, application programming interfaces, and communication protocols, exist largely to guarantee compatibility between products of different manufacturers.

In biology and medicine

Biological compatibility includes the ability of tissues and organs to be transplanted without rejection, determined by factors such as blood type and human leukocyte antigen (HLA) matching. In pharmacology, compatibility refers to whether drugs can be mixed or administered together without adverse reactions. In ecology, species compatibility concerns whether organisms can coexist in the same habitat.

In relationships

In social psychology, interpersonal compatibility describes the ease with which two people interact harmoniously, based on shared values, complementary temperaments, communication styles, and common interests. Research on relationship satisfaction frequently treats perceived compatibility as a predictor of long-term partnership success, though the construct is difficult to measure objectively.
