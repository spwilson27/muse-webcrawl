Charity Commission


The Charity Commission for England and Wales, regulator of registered charities.

The Charity Commission for England and Wales is the non-ministerial government department that regulates registered charities in England and Wales and maintains the Central Register of Charities. It is independent of ministerial control and of the charities it regulates. Its statutory objectives, set out in the Charities Act 2011, include increasing public trust and confidence in charities, promoting compliance by trustees and protecting charitable assets.

Functions

The Commission registers organisations that are charitable in law and have an income over £5,000 (with some exceptions), issues guidance to trustees, and has powers to investigate misconduct or mismanagement, suspend or remove trustees, freeze bank accounts and appoint interim managers. It publishes an online register covering more than 160,000 registered charities, whose combined income exceeds £80 billion.

History

The Commission's origins lie in the Charitable Trusts Act 1853, which created the Charity Commissioners to supervise charitable endowments following the Brougham inquiry into the abuse of charitable trusts. Successive Charities Acts — notably those of 1960, 1993, 2006 and 2011 — expanded its registration and enforcement role.

Other UK regulators

Scotland's charities are regulated by the Office of the Scottish Charity Regulator (OSCR), and Northern Ireland's by the Charity Commission for Northern Ireland, established in 2009.
