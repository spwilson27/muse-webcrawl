The Cabrini–Green Homes were a Chicago Housing Authority (CHA) public housing project on the Near North Side of Chicago, Illinois. The project was named for St. Frances Xavier Cabrini and William Green, a former president of the American Federation of Labor. At its peak, the complex housed roughly 15,000 people in mid- and high-rise apartment buildings.

History

Construction began with the Frances Cabrini Rowhouses in 1942, followed by the Cabrini Extension in the 1950s and the William Green Homes in 1962. The neighbourhood had earlier been a poor Italian immigrant enclave known as "Little Sicily". Over time the population became predominantly African American, and the project became nationally emblematic of the failures of high-rise public housing — concentrated poverty, gang violence, poor maintenance and social isolation.

Decline and notoriety

By the 1970s and 1980s Cabrini–Green had become infamous for violent crime. In 1981, after a series of murders, Mayor Jane Byrne temporarily moved into the project to publicise efforts to restore safety. The murder of seven-year-old Dantrell Davis in 1992 became a national symbol of urban violence. The development also entered popular culture, most famously as the setting of the 1992 horror film Candyman.

Demolition and redevelopment

Under the CHA's Plan for Transformation, demolition began in 1995 and the last high-rise closed in 2011. The site has been redeveloped with mixed-income housing, including the Parkside of Old Town development, alongside shops and a school, while former residents dispersed across the city — a process that remains debated by housing scholars and former tenants.
