River Stour, Suffolk

The River Stour is a river in East Anglia, England. It is 47 miles (76 km) long and forms most of the county boundary between Suffolk to the north and Essex to the south. It rises in eastern Cambridgeshire and flows through Haverhill, Clare, Sudbury, Bures and Dedham Vale before becoming tidal below Manningtree and joining the North Sea at Harwich, where its estuary meets that of the River Orwell.

Constable Country

The lower Stour valley, known as Dedham Vale National Landscape, is famous as "Constable Country" — the landscape painted by John Constable in works such as The Hay Wain (1821), depicting Flatford Mill on the river. The artist Thomas Gainsborough also lived in Sudbury on its banks. The river is crossed at Flatford by Willy Lott's Cottage and Bridge Cottage, preserved beside the mill.

Navigation and history

An Act of Parliament in 1705 made the Stour navigable from Sudbury to Manningtree, and barges carried grain, bricks and coal along the river; lighters from Sudbury were a distinctive local craft. Navigation declined with the railways but the river remains popular for rowing, canoeing and angling, and the Sudbury to the Sea trail follows its banks. The estuary is an important habitat for wildfowl and wading birds.
