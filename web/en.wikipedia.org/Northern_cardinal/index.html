The northern cardinal (Cardinalis cardinalis), also known as the redbird or common cardinal, is a mid-sized songbird found in North America. Males are brilliant crimson red with a black mask around the bill; females are a fawn colour with red accents on the wings, tail and crest.

Description

The species measures about 21–23.5 cm long with a distinctive crest on the head and a strong, cone-shaped orange-red bill adapted for seed eating. Both sexes sing, which is unusual among North American songbirds.

Distribution and habitat

Cardinals inhabit woodland edges, gardens, shrublands and wetlands from southern Canada through the eastern and central United States into Mexico. They are non-migratory and have expanded northward over the past two centuries, aided by bird feeders.

Cultural significance

The northern cardinal is the state bird of seven US states, more than any other species, and a familiar mascot for sports teams and winter holiday imagery.
