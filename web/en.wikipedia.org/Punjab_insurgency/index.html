Punjab insurgency

The Punjab insurgency was an armed campaign in the Indian state of Punjab from the early 1980s to the mid-1990s, waged by Sikh militant groups seeking an independent Sikh state called Khalistan, along with allied grievances over water sharing, the status of Chandigarh, and the 1978 Sikh–Nirankari clash.

Background and escalation

The movement gathered force around Jarnail Singh Bhindranwale, whose followers fortified the Golden Temple complex in Amritsar. In June 1984 Prime Minister Indira Gandhi ordered Operation Blue Star, an army assault on the complex that killed Bhindranwale and hundreds of others and badly damaged the Akal Takht. On 31 October 1984 Gandhi was assassinated by her Sikh bodyguards, triggering anti-Sikh riots in Delhi and elsewhere in which thousands of Sikhs were killed.

Counterinsurgency

Violence peaked around 1990–1992 with assassinations, bombings, and attacks on police and civilians, including the killing of Punjab Chief Minister Beant Singh in 1995 (after the main insurgency had waned). Under police chief K. P. S. Gill, Punjab Police campaigns broke the militant networks by the mid-1990s. The period produced serious human-rights controversies, including Jaswant Singh Khalra's documentation of mass secret cremations of disappeared Sikhs, for which officials were later prosecuted.
