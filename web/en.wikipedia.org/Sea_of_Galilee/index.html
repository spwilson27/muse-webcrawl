Sea of Galilee

The Sea of Galilee, also called Lake Tiberias or the Kinneret (Hebrew: ים כנרת), is the largest freshwater lake in Israel and the lowest freshwater lake on Earth, lying about 209 metres below sea level in the Jordan Rift Valley. The Jordan River flows into it from the north and out of it to the south. It is famous as the setting of much of the ministry of Jesus described in the Gospels.

Geography

The lake measures about 21 km north–south and 13 km across, with a maximum depth of roughly 43 m. It lies in the Syrian–African rift, with the hills of Galilee to the west and the Golan Heights to the east. Sudden violent storms can arise when cold air funnels down through the surrounding wadis — a phenomenon reflected in Gospel accounts of storms on the lake. The city of Tiberias, founded by Herod Antipas, stands on the western shore.

Biblical significance

Much of Jesus's Galilean ministry took place around the lake: Capernaum, Bethsaida and Magdala stood on its northern shore, and the Gospels describe Jesus calming a storm, walking on the water, and the miraculous catches of fish. The area draws large numbers of Christian pilgrims.

Water use

The lake long supplied a major share of Israel's drinking water via the National Water Carrier (opened 1964), but its level fluctuates with drought, and policy has shifted toward desalination and reduced pumping.
