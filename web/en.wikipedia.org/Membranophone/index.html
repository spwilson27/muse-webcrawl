Membranophone

A membranophone is any musical instrument which produces sound primarily by way of a vibrating stretched membrane. It is one of the four main divisions of instruments in the original Hornbostel–Sachs scheme of musical instrument classification.

Classification

Most membranophones are drums: struck drums, in which the membrane is hit directly by hand or stick or indirectly; friction drums, sounded by rubbing the membrane or a cord attached to it; and plucked drums. The scheme also includes "singing membranes" such as the kazoo and mirlitons, in which a membrane modifies the sound of the player's voice rather than producing sound itself.

Drums

Drums consist of at least one membrane, called a drumhead or drum skin, that is stretched over a shell and struck to produce sound. They include frame drums, kettle drums such as timpani, tubular drums (conical, cylindrical, barrel, and goblet shapes such as the djembe), and friction drums. Membranes were traditionally animal skin or hide; modern heads are often synthetic.

Use

Membranophones are found in virtually every musical culture, serving in ritual, signalling, dance, and military music as well as concert and popular music.
