Richard P. Gabriel

Richard P. Gabriel (born 1949) is an American computer scientist known for his work on the Lisp programming language, software design philosophy, and his influential essay Lisp: Good News, Bad News, How to Win Big, which introduced the phrase "worse is better".

Career

Gabriel received his PhD in computer science from Stanford University. He co-founded Lucid Inc., which produced a major commercial Common Lisp implementation, and later worked at Sun Microsystems and IBM Research. He has served as a Distinguished Engineer and worked on topics ranging from object-oriented programming to the nature of software design.

Worse is better

In his widely cited essay, Gabriel contrasted the "MIT approach" (the right thing: correctness, completeness, consistency) with the "New Jersey approach" (worse is better: simplicity of implementation and interface above all), arguing that the latter often spreads more successfully, as with C and Unix. The essay sparked decades of debate about design philosophy.

Other work

Gabriel wrote Performance and Evaluation of Lisp Systems (1985), a benchmark classic, and the poetry-inflected Patterns of Software (1996), drawing on Christopher Alexander's pattern language. He has also published poetry and received the ACM-AAAI Allen Newell Award.
