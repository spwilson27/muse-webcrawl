Juan Perón

Juan Domingo Perón (8 October 1895 – 1 July 1974) was an Argentine Army general and politician who served as President of Argentina from 1946 to 1955, when he was overthrown in a coup d'état, and again from October 1973 until his death in July 1974. He is the only Argentine president to have been elected three times, and his political movement, Peronism, remains a dominant force in Argentine politics. His second wife, Eva "Evita" Perón, was a hugely popular First Lady and political figure in her own right.

Early career

Perón entered the National Military College in 1911 and rose through the army, serving as a military attaché and training in Italy in the late 1930s. He participated in the 1943 military coup and, as head of the Secretariat of Labour, built a powerful base among trade unions by championing workers' rights, wage increases, and social welfare measures.

First presidencies (1946–1955)

After his arrest by rival officers in October 1945 provoked mass demonstrations by workers — the famous 17 October mobilisations — Perón was released and won the 1946 presidential election. His governments pursued a "Third Position" between capitalism and communism, expanding labour rights, women's suffrage (1947), state-led industrialisation, and public works, while restricting opposition press and parties. Eva Perón's charitable foundation and her advocacy for the poor made her a legendary figure; her death from cancer in 1952 was a national trauma. Economic difficulties and conflict with the Catholic Church eroded Perón's position, and in September 1955 the military overthrew him in the Revolución Libertadora.

Exile and return

Perón spent 18 years in exile, eventually settling in Spain, from where he directed the Peronist movement, which was banned from elections for much of the period. Amid escalating political violence, Peronist candidate Héctor Cámpora won the March 1973 elections and resigned to allow Perón's return. Perón won the September 1973 election with his third wife, Isabel, as vice president. He died in office on 1 July 1974; Isabel succeeded him and was herself overthrown in 1976.

Legacy

Peronism — organised today principally through the Justicialist Party — is characterised by labour-based populism, economic nationalism, and social justice rhetoric, and it has produced later presidents including Carlos Menem, Néstor Kirchner, and Cristina Fernández de Kirchner. Perón remains one of the most debated figures in Argentine history.
