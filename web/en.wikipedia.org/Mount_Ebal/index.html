Mount Ebal (Hebrew: Har ʿEival; Arabic: Jabal Ash-Shamali) is one of the two mountains in the immediate vicinity of the Palestinian city of Nablus (biblical Shechem), and forms the northern side of the valley in which Nablus lies; the southern side is formed by Mount Gerizim.

Biblical significance

In the Book of Deuteronomy, Mount Ebal is designated the mountain of the curse, opposite Gerizim, the mountain of blessing, in the ceremony of blessings and curses to be performed when Israel entered the Promised Land. Joshua is said to have built an altar on Ebal and set up inscribed stones there (Joshua 8:30–35).

Archaeology

In the 1980s, archaeologist Adam Zertal excavated a large rectangular stone structure on the mountain's northeastern slope, which he identified as an early Israelite altar dating to the Iron Age I; the identification remains debated among scholars. A Samaritan tradition also associates sites on the mountain with the patriarchs.
