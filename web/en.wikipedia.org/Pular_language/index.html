Pular language

Writing systems: Latin, Ajami (Arabic), Adlam

Pular is a variety of the Fula language spoken mainly in Guinea, particularly in the Fouta Djallon highlands, by the Fula (Fulɓe) people. It is one of the major regional varieties of Fula, a continuum of dialects stretching across West and Central Africa; the Guinean variety is sometimes called Pular Fuuta Jallon.

Distribution and status

Pular is the most widely spoken indigenous language in Guinea, used by a large share of the population as a first or second language and widely used in commerce and radio broadcasting, though French remains the country's official language. Speaker communities also live in neighbouring Guinea-Bissau, Mali, Senegal and Sierra Leone, and in a large diaspora.

Features

Like other Fula varieties, Pular has an elaborate noun-class system with more than twenty classes marked by suffixes, initial consonant alternation (mutation), and implosive consonants such as ɓ, ɗ and ƴ. Its phonology and vocabulary differ in notable respects from the Pulaar of Senegal and Mauritania and from the Fulfulde varieties further east.

Writing

Pular has historically been written in the Arabic-based Ajami script and in an official Latin-based alphabet adopted in Guinea in the twentieth century. Since 2016 the Adlam script, invented by the Barry brothers from Guinea, has spread rapidly for writing Fula varieties including Pular.
