Lead climbing

Lead climbing (or leading) is a technique in climbing in which the lead climber ascends a route while periodically attaching their climbing rope to protection equipment (such as quickdraws bolted to the rock, or removable traditional gear) as they go, while a second climber (the belayer) remains at the base feeding out rope and prepared to arrest a fall. Unlike top rope climbing, where the rope is anchored above the climber before the ascent, the lead climber starts with the rope at the base and carries it up, so a fall before clipping the next piece of protection can be substantially longer.

Technique

The leader clips the rope into protection points at intervals of a few metres. Between clips, the potential fall distance is roughly twice the distance above the last protection point, plus rope stretch and slack. Clipping position, rope management (avoiding the rope running behind the leg, which can invert a falling climber), and clear communication with the belayer are central skills.

Styles

In sport climbing, protection consists of pre-placed bolts; the climber clips quickdraws into the bolts. In traditional ("trad") climbing, the leader places removable protection such as nuts and camming devices into cracks. Lead climbing is also practised indoors on artificial walls, and forms one of the three disciplines of competition climbing alongside bouldering and speed climbing.
