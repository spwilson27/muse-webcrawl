Ivrea

Ivrea (Piedmontese: Ivréa) is a town and comune in the Metropolitan City of Turin, Piedmont, northwestern Italy, on the Dora Baltea river at the outlet of the Aosta Valley. It is famous as the home of the Olivetti typewriter and computer company, and for its historic carnival orange-throwing battle; its 20th-century industrial quarter is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

Founded as the Roman colony of Eporedia in 100 BC on the road into Gaul, Ivrea in the early Middle Ages gave its name to the March of Ivrea; its margrave Arduin became king of Italy in 1002. Later it passed between the bishops, the marquises of Montferrat, and the House of Savoy.

Olivetti

In 1908 Camillo Olivetti founded Italy's first typewriter factory in Ivrea. Under his son Adriano Olivetti the company became a byword for industrial design and enlightened paternalism, building modernist factories, housing, and social facilities between the 1930s and 1960s — the ensemble inscribed by UNESCO in 2018 — and producing landmarks such as the Lettera 22 typewriter and the Programma 101, an early desktop computer.

Battle of the Oranges

Each February the town's carnival re-enacts a medieval revolt against a tyrant, as teams of aranceri on foot pelt horse-drawn carriages with oranges, filling the streets with tonnes of fruit.
