The Glock 19 is a semi-automatic pistol designed and produced by the Austrian manufacturer Glock. Introduced in 1988, it is essentially a reduced-size version of the full-size Glock 17, chambered for the 9×19mm Parabellum cartridge.

Design

The pistol uses Glock's "Safe Action" striker-fired system with three passive safeties and a polymer frame with a steel slide and barrel. The compact "mid-size" format — a shorter barrel and grip than the Glock 17 with a standard 15-round magazine — was intended for use by military and police as both a service and concealed-carry weapon. Like other Glock models it has been produced in successive generations (Gen 3, Gen 4, Gen 5) with changes to grip texture, magazine release and rifling.

Use

The Glock 19 is among the most widely used pistols in the world. It is issued to numerous military and law-enforcement agencies, serves as a standard sidearm in several countries, and a variant (the Glock 19X, and its derivative the MHS-derived models) competed in the US Army's Modular Handgun System trials. It is also one of the most popular civilian handguns for concealed carry in the United States.
