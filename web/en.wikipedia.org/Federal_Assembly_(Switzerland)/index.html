Federal Assembly (Switzerland)

The Federal Assembly (German: Bundesversammlung; French: Assemblée fédérale; Italian: Assemblea federale) is the federal legislature of Switzerland. It meets in the Federal Palace in Bern. The Assembly is bicameral and comprises the National Council, with 200 seats representing the people, and the Council of States, with 46 seats representing the cantons.

Composition

Under the Swiss system of direct democracy, the Assembly is the supreme authority of the Confederation, subject to the rights of the people and the cantons. Its two chambers are of equal standing: legislation must be approved by both. Members of the National Council are elected by proportional representation for four-year terms, apportioned among the cantons by population. The Council of States consists of two representatives from each full canton and one from each former half-canton, elected according to cantonal rules.

United Federal Assembly

When the two chambers sit together as the United Federal Assembly, they exercise special powers, most importantly electing the seven members of the Federal Council (the federal executive), the President of the Confederation, the Federal Chancellor, and the judges of the Federal Supreme Court. The joint body also decides on conflicts of jurisdiction and grants pardons in certain cases.

Powers

The Federal Assembly enacts federal statutes, approves the budget and international treaties, supervises the Federal Council and the federal administration, and elects senior officials. Its decisions are subject to the optional referendum: citizens who gather 50,000 signatures within 100 days may challenge a law at the ballot box.

Unlike most legislatures, the Assembly's relationship with the executive is not governed by votes of confidence; Federal Councillors serve fixed four-year terms and are customarily re-elected.
