Coast Salish peoples

The Coast Salish peoples are the Indigenous nations of the central and northern coasts of the Salish Sea region in present-day British Columbia and Washington. They comprise dozens of distinct tribal and First Nations communities bound together by related languages of the Salishan family, intermarriage, shared ceremonies, and a common reliance on the salmon rivers and marine waters of the region.

List of peoples

Major Coast Salish peoples include, in Canada, the Squamish, Musqueam, Tsleil-Waututh, Tsawwassen, Stó:lō nations of the Fraser Valley, Cowichan Tribes, Snuneymuxw, Hul'qumi'num-speaking nations of southeastern Vancouver Island, and the Comox and Sechelt to the north; and in the United States, the Lummi, Swinomish, Tulalip Tribes, Snoqualmie, Duwamish, Suquamish, Puyallup, Nisqually, and Squaxin Island Tribe, among others.

Society and governance

Traditionally there was no single Coast Salish government; each winter village or group of villages was autonomous, led by respected heads of extended families. Kinship networks crossed present-day international boundaries, a fact that colonial borders disrupted but did not erase. Today each nation governs itself through tribal councils or band governments and asserts distinct rights and title, while also cooperating in bodies such as tribal fisheries commissions.

Contemporary issues

Coast Salish peoples continue to exercise fishing rights confirmed by treaty litigation in the United States (notably the Boldt decision of 1974) and by court rulings and negotiations in Canada. Environmental protection of the Salish Sea, language revitalization, and the recovery of ancestral remains and cultural property remain central concerns.
