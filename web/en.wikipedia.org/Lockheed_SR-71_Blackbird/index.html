Lockheed SR-71 Blackbird

The Lockheed SR-71 "Blackbird" is a retired long-range, high-altitude, Mach 3+ strategic reconnaissance aircraft developed and manufactured by the American aerospace company Lockheed Corporation. It was operated by the United States Air Force and by NASA. The SR-71 was built largely of titanium to withstand the frictional heating of sustained flight at more than three times the speed of sound.

Design and development

The SR-71 was developed by Lockheed's Skunk Works under Clarence "Kelly" Johnson, evolving from the A-12 Oxcart reconnaissance aircraft built for the CIA. It first flew on 22 December 1964 and entered service in 1966. The aircraft carried a crew of two — a pilot and a reconnaissance systems officer — and was powered by two Pratt & Whitney J58 engines, which operated partly as ramjets at high speed, using a special low-volatility fuel, JP-7.

Operational history

Operating from bases in the United States, England and Okinawa, SR-71s flew reconnaissance missions over areas where other aircraft could not safely operate, relying on speed and altitude rather than armament for protection. No SR-71 was ever shot down, although twelve of the 32 built were lost in accidents. The type set speed and altitude records that still stand for air-breathing manned aircraft, including a 1976 flight at 2,193 mph (3,530 km/h).

The USAF retired the SR-71 in 1989 (with a brief recall in the mid-1990s) and the last flights were made by NASA in 1999. Surviving aircraft are displayed in museums across the United States and one at Duxford in England.
