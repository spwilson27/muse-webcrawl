Atlas
Definition: A collection of maps, usually bound in book form
Named for: Atlas, the Titan of Greek mythology
First modern atlas: Mercator's Atlas, published posthumously 1595

An atlas is a collection of maps; it is typically a bundle of maps of Earth or of a region of Earth. Atlases have traditionally been bound into book form, but today many atlases are in multimedia formats. In addition to presenting geographical features and political boundaries, many atlases feature geopolitical, social, religious, and economic statistics, as well as information about the places they cover.

History

The word "atlas" derives from the Greek Titan Atlas, condemned to hold up the heavens; his image appeared on the title pages of early map collections. The first systematic collection often called an atlas was Abraham Ortelius's Theatrum Orbis Terrarum of 1570. Gerardus Mercator's collection, published in 1595 after his death, was the first to carry the title Atlas. In the 17th century, Dutch publishers such as Joan Blaeu produced lavish multi-volume world atlases, and national atlases became standard projects of governments in the 19th and 20th centuries.

Types

Atlases include world atlases, road atlases, historical atlases, and thematic atlases devoted to topics such as climate, astronomy (star atlases), or anatomy (anatomical atlases showing structures of the human body).
