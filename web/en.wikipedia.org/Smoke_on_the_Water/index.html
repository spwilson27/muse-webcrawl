Smoke on the Water

ArtistDeep Purple
AlbumMachine Head
Released1972 (album); 1973 (single)
GenreHard rock, heavy metal

"Smoke on the Water" is a song by the English rock band Deep Purple. It was first released on the band's 1972 album Machine Head and became their best-known song, recognised worldwide for Ritchie Blackmore's four-note central guitar riff, one of the most famous in rock history.

Background

The lyrics recount a true event: on 4 December 1971, the band were in Montreux, Switzerland, to record at the Montreux Casino using the Rolling Stones Mobile Studio, when the casino burned down during a Frank Zappa and the Mothers of Invention concert after a fan fired a flare gun. Watching the smoke drift over Lake Geneva inspired the title and the song's narrative.

Legacy

The song is a staple of classic rock radio and beginner guitar repertoire, and has been ranked among the greatest guitar songs of all time. The band eventually recorded at the Grand Hotel in Montreux after the casino fire forced them to relocate.
