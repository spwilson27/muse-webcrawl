Human Development Index

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Human Development Index (HDI) is a summary measure of average achievement in key dimensions of human development: a long and healthy life, being knowledgeable, and having a decent standard of living. It was created by the Pakistani economist Mahbub ul Haq, working with Amartya Sen and others, and is published annually by the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) in the Human Development Report.

Method

The HDI is the geometric mean of three normalised indices: a life expectancy index (based on life expectancy at birth), an education index (mean and expected years of schooling), and an income index (gross national income per capita, PPP, logged). Values range from 0 to 1; countries are grouped into low (below 0.550), medium (0.550–0.699), high (0.700–0.799), and very high (0.800 and above) human development.

Rankings

Switzerland, Norway, and Iceland have typically occupied the top positions in recent reports, while countries affected by conflict and poverty, such as South Sudan, Chad, and Niger, rank lowest. The index is widely used to compare development beyond purely economic measures such as GDP per capita.

Criticism and related indices

Critics note that the HDI omits inequality, poverty, sustainability, and political freedom. The UNDP also publishes complementary measures, including the inequality-adjusted HDI (IHDI), the Gender Development Index, and the Multidimensional Poverty Index, and in 2020 introduced an experimental planetary-pressures–adjusted HDI.
