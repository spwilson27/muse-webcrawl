Cultural Center Historic District (Detroit)


Detroit Institute of Arts on Woodward Avenue
LocationMidtown Detroit, Michigan, along Woodward Avenue
LandmarksDetroit Institute of Arts, Detroit Public Library, Wayne State University, Charles H. Wright Museum
NRHPListed on the National Register of Historic Places (1983)

The Cultural Center Historic District is a historic district in Midtown Detroit, Michigan, centred on Woodward Avenue around the city's great cultural institutions. It contains one of the finest concentrations of early-20th-century monumental architecture in the American Midwest and was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1983.

Buildings and institutions

The district's anchors face each other across Woodward Avenue: the Beaux-Arts Detroit Public Library (1921, Cass Gilbert) and the Detroit Institute of Arts (1927, Paul Philippe Cret), whose Italian Renaissance-style building houses Diego Rivera's celebrated Detroit Industry Murals. Around them stand the neo-Gothic Rackham Building, the Detroit Historical Museum, the Charles H. Wright Museum of African American History — the largest museum of its kind — the Michigan Science Center, the College for Creative Studies, and the older campus buildings of Wayne State University, including the Maccabees Building and the Hilberry Theatre.

History

Planned in the City Beautiful spirit of the 1910s–20s, the centre clustered Detroit's library, art museum and university along its main avenue at the height of the auto boom. The district remains the heart of Midtown's revival, hosting the annual Dally in the Alley and Noel Night festivals.
