Reform War

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Reform War (Spanish: Guerra de Reforma), also known as the Three Years' War (Guerra de los Tres Años), was a civil war in Mexico fought from 1857 to 1861 between the liberal government of Benito Juárez and conservative forces who rejected the liberal Constitution of 1857 and the Reform Laws.

Background

After the fall of Antonio López de Santa Anna in 1855, liberals led by Juan Álvarez and later Ignacio Comonfort began implementing the Reform Laws, which curtailed the privileges of the Catholic Church and the military, expropriated corporate property, and separated church and state. The Constitution of 1857 embodied these principles, provoking a fierce conservative reaction. In December 1857 General Félix Zuloaga launched the Plan of Tacubaya, dissolving Congress and beginning the conflict.

Course of the war

The conservative government held Mexico City under presidents Zuloaga and then Miguel Miramón, while Juárez established a rival constitutional government in Guanajuato and later in the port of Veracruz. Fighting spread across the country. The United States recognized the Juárez government in 1859, and the McLane–Ocampo Treaty was signed though never ratified. Liberal forces gradually gained the upper hand; Miramón's attempt to take Veracruz failed, and in December 1860 the liberal general Jesús González Ortega defeated the conservatives at the Battle of Calpulalpan.

Aftermath

Juárez entered Mexico City in January 1861 and was elected president. Although the liberals won, the war exhausted the treasury and deepened divisions; conservative exiles soon sought European intervention, setting the stage for the French intervention in Mexico and the Second Mexican Empire under Maximilian I.
