Benito Pablo Juárez García (21 March 1806 – 18 July 1872) was a Mexican lawyer and statesman of Zapotec origin who served as president of Mexico from 1858 until his death in 1872. He is regarded as Mexico's greatest national hero: he led the liberal Reform (La Reforma), defended Mexican sovereignty against the French Intervention and the empire of Maximilian, and his axiom "respect for the rights of others is peace" (El respeto al derecho ajeno es la paz) remains famous.

Early life and Reform

Born in poverty in the village of San Pablo Guelatao, Oaxaca, and orphaned young, Juárez learned Spanish as a boy and trained as a lawyer in Oaxaca City, entering liberal politics and becoming governor of Oaxaca. As minister of justice under President Juan Álvarez he authored the Ley Juárez (1855), abolishing special courts (fueros) for clergy and military — the first of the Reform Laws, followed by the Ley Lerdo and the liberal Constitution of 1857.

Reform War and French Intervention

Conservative opposition triggered the Reform War (1858–1861), during which Juárez, as president by constitutional succession, led the liberal government from Veracruz and issued the full Reform Laws separating church and state. After the liberal victory, debt disputes led France to invade in 1862; Napoleon III installed Maximilian of Habsburg as emperor in 1864. Juárez's itinerant republican government resisted from the north (notably at Paso del Norte, now Ciudad Juárez), refused to resign, and with United States pressure on France and republican victories, Maximilian was captured and executed at Querétaro in 1867.

Later years and legacy

Restored to the capital, Juárez was re-elected in 1867 and 1871, pursued secular education and infrastructure, and died in office of a heart attack in 1872. He is honoured as the Benemérito de las Américas; his birthday is a Mexican national holiday, and his name is borne by cities, airports and countless streets and schools across Mexico.
