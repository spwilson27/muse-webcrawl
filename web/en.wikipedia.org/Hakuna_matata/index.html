Hakuna matata

Hakuna matata is a Swahili language phrase. In English, it roughly translates to "no worries", meaning "no problems" or "no trouble". The word hakuna means "there is no/there are no" and matata means "concerns" or "troubles".

Popularization

The phrase was popularized internationally by the 1994 Disney animated film The Lion King, whose song "Hakuna Matata", composed by Elton John with lyrics by Tim Rice, was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Original Song. In the film the phrase is the carefree motto of the meerkat Timon and the warthog Pumbaa, who teach it to the exiled lion cub Simba. Earlier, the Kenyan band Them Mushrooms had a hit in 1980 with "Jambo Bwana", which repeats the phrase, and Boney M. released a cover in 1984.

Trademark controversy

In 2018, ahead of the release of the photorealistic remake of The Lion King, a petition started by Zimbabwean activist Shelton Mpala called on Disney to drop its trademark on the phrase — registered in the United States in 1994 — arguing it amounted to appropriation of East African culture. The petition gathered more than 200,000 signatures, though the trademark, which does not prevent ordinary use of the phrase in Swahili-speaking countries, remained in force.
