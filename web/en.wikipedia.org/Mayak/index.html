The Mayak Production Association is a nuclear facility near the city of Ozyorsk in Chelyabinsk Oblast, Russia. Built in the late 1940s as part of the Soviet atomic bomb project, it was one of the first Soviet plants for producing plutonium and remains a major centre for reprocessing spent nuclear fuel and manufacturing radioisotopes.

History

Construction began in 1946 in deep secrecy; the first reactor went critical in June 1948 and produced material for the Soviet Union's first atomic bomb, tested in 1949. High-level waste was initially discharged into the Techa River, severely contaminating downstream villages.

Kyshtym disaster

On 29 September 1957 a cooling failure in a high-level waste storage tank caused a chemical explosion that released roughly 70–80 tonnes of radioactive material, contaminating a large area known as the East Ural Radioactive Trace. More than 10,000 people were evacuated. Rated level 6 on the International Nuclear Event Scale, it was the third most serious nuclear accident in history after Chernobyl and Fukushima, and was kept secret by the Soviet authorities for decades.

Modern operations

Mayak continues to reprocess fuel from Russian reactors, fabricate isotopes for medicine and industry, and manage legacy wastes, while long-running programmes address the contaminated reservoirs, including Lake Karachay, once described as among the most polluted places on Earth.
