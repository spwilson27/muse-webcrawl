Gorgons

The Gorgons are three monstrous sisters in Greek mythology: Stheno, Euryale, and Medusa. They are usually described as having snakes for hair, bronze claws, boar-like tusks, and a gaze that turned anyone who met it to stone. Of the three, only Medusa was mortal, and she was slain by the hero Perseus.

Mythology

According to Hesiod, the Gorgons were daughters of the sea god Phorcys and the sea monster Ceto, and lived beyond the land of the Hesperides at the edge of the world. In later tradition, Medusa was said to have been a beautiful priestess of Athena who was transformed into a monster; in Ovid's version, she was punished after being violated by Poseidon in Athena's temple.

Perseus and Medusa

King Polydectes sent Perseus to fetch the head of Medusa, hoping the quest would kill him. Aided by gifts from the gods — a mirrored shield from Athena, winged sandals from Hermes, and a special sword — Perseus beheaded Medusa by looking only at her reflection. From her neck sprang the winged horse Pegasus and the giant Chrysaor. Perseus later used the severed head, which retained its petrifying power, as a weapon.

Cultural significance

The Gorgon's head, the gorgoneion, was a common apotropaic symbol in Greek art, placed on shields, temples, coins, and armour to ward off evil. Gorgon imagery has remained influential in Western art, from ancient pottery to Caravaggio's famous painted Medusa shield.
