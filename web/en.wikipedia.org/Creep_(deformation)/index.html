Creep (deformation)

Creep
Field: Materials science
Stages: Primary, secondary, tertiary
Key parameters: Temperature, stress, time

In materials science, creep is the tendency of a solid material to deform permanently under mechanical stress over long periods, especially at high temperature. It occurs below the yield strength and can lead to failure after extended service.

Stages

A creep curve shows three stages: primary creep, with a decreasing strain rate as the material strain-hardens; secondary (steady-state) creep, with a near-constant minimum rate used in design; and tertiary creep, where necking and internal voids accelerate strain until rupture.

Mechanisms

Mechanisms include dislocation glide and climb, vacancy diffusion (Nabarro–Herring and Coble creep), and grain-boundary sliding. Creep resistance is critical in jet engine turbine blades, power-plant piping, and nuclear reactors; nickel-based superalloys are engineered specifically to resist it.
