Montreal

Montreal (French: Montréal) is the largest city in the province of Quebec and the second-largest in Canada. Founded in 1642 as Ville-Marie, it sits on the Island of Montreal at the confluence of the Saint Lawrence and Ottawa rivers. The city takes its name from Mount Royal, the triple-peaked hill in its heart. Montreal is the largest French-speaking city in North America and is renowned for its festivals, universities, and its extensive "underground city" (RÉSO).

History

Jacques Cartier visited the Iroquoian village of Hochelaga at the foot of Mount Royal in 1535. French colonists under Paul Chomedey de Maisonneuve founded Ville-Marie in 1642 as a mission settlement. Montreal grew into a centre of the fur trade and, after the British conquest of New France in 1760, a hub of commerce. It was Canada's economic capital until overtaken by Toronto in the 1970s, a shift accelerated by Quebec's language politics.

Geography and climate

The city occupies most of the Island of Montreal, surrounded by the Saint Lawrence River and the Rivière des Prairies. It has a humid continental climate with warm, humid summers and very cold, snowy winters; January averages about −10 °C (14 °F) and July about 21 °C (70 °F).

Demographics

French is the first language of a majority of residents, with substantial English-speaking and immigrant communities; Italian, Arabic, Spanish, and Mandarin are widely spoken. The metropolitan area's population exceeds 4.2 million.

Economy

Montreal is a centre for aerospace (Bombardier, CAE), artificial intelligence, video games, film and television production, and pharmaceuticals. The Port of Montreal is a major inland port, and the city hosts the headquarters of the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO).

Culture

The Montreal International Jazz Festival is the world's largest jazz festival. The city hosted the 1976 Summer Olympics and Expo 67, and is home to the Montreal Canadiens of the NHL. Notable districts include Old Montreal, the Plateau-Mont-Royal, and the Quartier des Spectacles.
