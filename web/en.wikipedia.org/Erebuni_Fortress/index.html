Erebuni Fortress

Erebuni Fortress
Location: Yerevan, Armenia
Founded: 782 BC
Built by: Argishti I of Urartu
Erebuni Fortress on Arin Berd hill, Yerevan

Erebuni Fortress (Armenian: Էրեբունի ամրոց), also known as Arin Berd, is an Urartian fortified city located in Yerevan, Armenia. It was founded in 782 BC by King Argishti I of Urartu, as recorded in a cuneiform inscription found at the site. The modern name of the Armenian capital, Yerevan, derives from Erebuni, making the fortress's foundation the conventional birth date of the city — celebrated with great ceremony at its 2,750th anniversary in 1968.

History

Erebuni was built as a military and administrative stronghold guarding Urartu's northern frontier in the Ararat plain. Its foundation inscription boasts that Argishti settled 6,600 soldiers from conquered lands there. After Urartu's power shifted, the fortress at nearby Teishebaini took over some functions, but Erebuni remained occupied, passing to Achaemenid Persian control in the 6th century BC, with continued occupation into the Hellenistic period.

Excavations and remains

Although the site was identified in the 19th century, systematic excavations began in 1950 under Konstantine Hovhannisyan after a cuneiform tablet was found by a local farmer. Excavations uncovered palace and temple complexes, columned halls, storage rooms with large pithoi, wall paintings, cuneiform inscriptions, helmets, seals, and pottery. A temple of the god Khaldi and a later Achaemenid-era apadana-like hall are among the principal remains. Some walls were partially reconstructed for the 1968 jubilee.

Erebuni Museum

The Erebuni Historical-Archaeological Museum-Reserve, opened in 1968 at the foot of the hill, displays finds from Erebuni and Teishebaini, including the celebrated foundation inscriptions and Urartian metalwork.
