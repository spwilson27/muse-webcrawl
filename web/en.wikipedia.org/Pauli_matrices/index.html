Pauli matrices

In mathematical physics and mathematics, the Pauli matrices are a set of three 2 × 2 complex matrices that are Hermitian, involutory and unitary. Usually indicated by the Greek letter sigma (σ), they are occasionally denoted by tau (τ) when used in connection with isospin symmetries. They are named after the physicist Wolfgang Pauli.

The matrices are:

σ1 = [[0, 1], [1, 0]]   σ2 = [[0, −i], [i, 0]]   σ3 = [[1, 0], [0, −1]]

Properties

Together with the identity matrix I, the Pauli matrices form an orthogonal basis for the real Hilbert space of 2 × 2 Hermitian matrices. They satisfy the commutation relations [σa, σb] = 2i εabc σc and the anticommutation relation {σa, σb} = 2δab I. Each matrix squares to the identity and has eigenvalues ±1.

Applications

Pauli matrices appear in the Pauli equation, which takes into account the interaction of the spin of a particle with an external electromagnetic field. They generate the Lie algebra su(2), describe spin-½ particles, and serve as the single-qubit gates X, Y, and Z in quantum computing.
