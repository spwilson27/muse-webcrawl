Negative theology

Negative theology, also known as apophatic theology, is a type of theological thinking that attempts to approach God by negation — speaking of what God is not rather than what God is — on the grounds that the divine nature transcends all human concepts and language.

History

Apophatic approaches appear in the writings of Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite (c. 500), whose Mystical Theology describes an ascent into "darkness" beyond affirmation and denial. The Jewish philosopher Maimonides argued in the Guide for the Perplexed that God can be described only by negative attributes. In Christian scholasticism, Thomas Aquinas held that we know of God "what He is not" rather than what He is. Apophatic themes also run through Eastern Orthodox theology, Meister Eckhart's mysticism, and, in parallel forms, Islamic, Hindu, and Buddhist thought.

Practice

Beyond doctrine, apophaticism shapes contemplative prayer: the anonymous English work The Cloud of Unknowing advises the seeker to place all created things beneath a "cloud of forgetting."
