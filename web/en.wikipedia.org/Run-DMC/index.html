Run-DMC

OriginHollis, Queens, New York City, U.S.
GenresHip hop, rap rock
Years active1983–2002
MembersJoseph "Run" Simmons, Darryl "D.M.C." McDaniels, Jason "Jam Master Jay" Mizell

Run-DMC (also formatted Run-D.M.C.) was an American hip hop group from Hollis, Queens, New York City, formed in 1983 by Joseph Simmons, Darryl McDaniels, and Jason Mizell. Widely regarded as one of the most influential acts in the history of hip hop, Run-DMC was the first rap group to achieve mainstream success, to have a gold and then platinum album, and to appear on the cover of Rolling Stone.

Run-DMC helped bring hip hop into the mainstream in the 1980s.

History

The group's debut album Run-D.M.C. (1984) was the first gold record in hip hop. King of Rock followed in 1985. Their third album, Raising Hell (1986), produced by Rick Rubin and Russell Simmons, became the first platinum hip hop album; its cover of Aerosmith's "Walk This Way", recorded with Steven Tyler and Joe Perry, broke rap into the rock mainstream and revived Aerosmith's career. The group also popularised the laceless Adidas sneaker as a fashion statement, celebrated in the song "My Adidas", leading to the first major endorsement deal between a musical act and an athletic company.

Later years and legacy

Subsequent albums included Tougher Than Leather (1988), Back from Hell (1990), Down with the King (1993), and Crown Royal (2001). On 30 October 2002, Jam Master Jay was shot and killed in a recording studio in Queens; the surviving members announced the group's retirement shortly afterwards. Run-DMC was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2009, only the second hip hop act so honoured, and received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2016.
