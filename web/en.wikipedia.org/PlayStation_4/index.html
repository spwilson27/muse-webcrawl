The PlayStation 4 (PS4) is a home video game console developed by Sony Computer Entertainment. Announced as the successor to the PlayStation 3 in February 2013, it was launched on November 15, 2013, in North America, November 29, 2013, in Europe, South America and Australia, and on February 22, 2014, in Japan. A console of the eighth generation, it competed with Microsoft's Xbox One and Nintendo's Wii U and later the Nintendo Switch.

Moving away from the more complex Cell microarchitecture of its predecessor, the console features an AMD Accelerated Processing Unit built upon the x86-64 architecture, which can theoretically peak at 1.84 teraflops. The PlayStation 4 places an increased emphasis on social interaction and integration with other devices and services, including the ability to play games off-console on PlayStation Vita and other supported devices ("Remote Play"), the ability to stream gameplay online or to friends, and support for video game streaming services.

History

According to lead architect Mark Cerny, development of Sony's fourth video game console began as early as 2008. The console was publicly revealed at a press conference on February 20, 2013, and officially launched in North America on November 15 of that year. Critics and customers embraced the console, praising its controller design, lower price relative to the Xbox One, and its library of games. The PS4's success helped Sony regain a leading position in console sales after the PlayStation 3 era.

Hardware

The technology in the PlayStation 4 is similar to the hardware found in modern personal computers. This familiarity was designed to make it easier and less expensive for game studios to develop games for the PS4. The console's controller, the DualShock 4, introduced a touchpad, a "Share" button, and a light bar that can be tracked by the PlayStation Camera for motion detection.

Revisions

In September 2016, Sony unveiled two hardware revisions: a slimmer version of the original model, and a higher-end "Pro" version with an upgraded GPU and higher CPU clock rate to support enhanced performance and 4K resolution in supported games.

Software and games

The PlayStation 4 system software is frequently updated with features and bug fixes. The console's library includes critically acclaimed titles such as God of War, Marvel's Spider-Man, The Last of Us Part II, Horizon Zero Dawn, Bloodborne, and Uncharted 4: A Thief's End. By 2022, more than 117 million units had been sold worldwide, making it one of the best-selling video game consoles of all time.
