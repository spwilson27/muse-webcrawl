Mindanao is the second-largest island in the Philippines, at the southern end of the archipelago. Together with its surrounding islets — including the Sulu Archipelago — it forms one of the country's three principal island groups (with Luzon and the Visayas). Mindanao is known as the "Land of Promise" for its fertile soil; it produces much of the Philippines' agricultural exports, including bananas, pineapples and rubber, and holds significant mineral wealth. Its population of about 26 million is ethnically and religiously diverse, including the Moro (Muslim) peoples, Lumad indigenous groups and Christian settlers.

Mount Apo, the highest peak in the Philippines, stands on Mindanao.

Geography

Mindanao is irregularly shaped, with great peninsulas and deep bays; the Zamboanga Peninsula juts westward toward Borneo. Mountain ranges run along the coasts, and between them lie fertile plains and river valleys — the Agusan, Rio Grande de Mindanao (Pulangi) and Davao basins. Mount Apo (2,954 m), an active volcano near Davao, is the country's highest summit. Lake Lanao, one of the world's ancient lakes, lies in the Lanao plateau. The island lies south of the main typhoon belt, giving it a more even, arable climate than the rest of the Philippines.

History

Hindu-Buddhist influences reached Mindanao by the first millennium; by the 14th–15th centuries Islam spread from Brunei and Malacca, and powerful sultanates — Sulu (c. 1450) and Maguindanao (c. 1515) — dominated trade and resisted colonisation. Spanish expeditions from the 1560s established footholds at Zamboanga and the northern coast but never fully subdued the Moro sultanates, which maintained slave-raiding fleets and fought the Spanish for three centuries. The United States took control after 1898, defeating Moro resistance by 1913 and encouraging Christian migration from Luzon and the Visayas, especially after independence in 1946 — a policy that transformed the island's demography and land ownership and sowed modern conflicts.

Conflict and autonomy

From the late 1960s, Moro separatist movements — the Moro National Liberation Front (MNLF) and later the Moro Islamic Liberation Front (MILF) — fought for self-determination. Peace agreements created the Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao (1989) and, after the 2014 Comprehensive Agreement on the Bangsamoro, the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao (BARMM) in 2019. Extremist groups such as Abu Sayyaf and the Maute group — which seized Marawi City in 2017, prompting a five-month battle — have remained a security problem, as has a long-running communist insurgency.

Economy and culture

Mindanao accounts for a large share of Philippine agriculture: Dole and Del Monte pineapple plantations in Bukidnon, Cavendish bananas from Davao, tuna from General Santos ("the tuna capital"), and rubber, coffee and cacao. Mining (gold, copper, nickel) is extensive but controversial. Principal cities include Davao, Cagayan de Oro, Zamboanga, General Santos and Butuan. Cultural life blends Moro brassware and weaving (malong, okir designs), Lumad traditions, and settler festivals such as Davao's Kadayawan.
