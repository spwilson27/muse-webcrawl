Einstein@Home is a volunteer distributed computing project that uses idle computer time donated by the public to search for signals from rotating neutron stars — including continuous gravitational waves — in data from the LIGO and Virgo gravitational-wave detectors, the Arecibo radio telescope and the Fermi Gamma-ray Space Telescope.

History

The project was launched on 19 February 2005 as part of the World Year of Physics celebrations marking the centenary of Einstein's annus mirabilis papers. It runs on the BOINC software platform developed at the University of California, Berkeley, and is hosted by the Albert Einstein Institute in Germany with support from the University of Wisconsin–Milwaukee. Hundreds of thousands of volunteers have contributed computing power exceeding that of many supercomputers.

Discoveries

While the search for continuous gravitational waves from known and unknown neutron stars continues, the project's radio and gamma-ray pulsar searches have yielded concrete discoveries: in 2010 volunteers' computers found a previously unknown pulsar, PSR J2007+2722, in Arecibo data, and the project has since discovered dozens of radio pulsars and gamma-ray pulsars, including millisecond pulsars and rare binary systems, in Fermi and Arecibo/PALFA survey data.
