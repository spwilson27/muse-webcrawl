The Zariski topology is a topology defined on algebraic varieties and, more generally, on the spectrum of a commutative ring. Introduced by Oscar Zariski, it is the standard topology of algebraic geometry and is far coarser than the Euclidean topology: its closed sets are the solution sets of polynomial equations.

Definition

On affine space kn over a field k, the closed sets are the algebraic sets V(S) — the common zeros of a set S of polynomials. That these sets satisfy the axioms for closed sets follows from the properties of ideals in the polynomial ring, using the correspondence between algebraic sets and radical ideals given by Hilbert's Nullstellensatz. The open sets D(f), consisting of points where a polynomial f does not vanish, form a basis.

Properties

The Zariski topology is typically not Hausdorff: on the affine line over an infinite field, closed sets are just finite sets and the whole space, so any two nonempty open sets intersect. It is, however, quasi-compact and Noetherian, and irreducible closed sets correspond to prime ideals — the basis for the modern notion of the spectrum Spec(R) of a ring, whose points are prime ideals with closed sets V(I) of ideals containing I.

Significance

By making algebraic varieties into topological spaces, the Zariski topology allows geometric intuition and topological methods — sheaf cohomology, dimension theory — to be applied to polynomial equations, and it underlies Grothendieck's theory of schemes.
