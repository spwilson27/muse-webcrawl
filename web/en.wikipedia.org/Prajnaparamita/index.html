Prajnaparamita

Prajñāpāramitā (Sanskrit: प्रज्ञापारमिता) means the "Perfection of (Transcendent) Wisdom" in Mahāyāna Buddhism. Prajñāpāramitā also refers to a body of sutras (scriptures) and to the personification of the concept as a goddess — "mother of all Buddhas".

The literature

The Prajñāpāramitā sutras are among the earliest Mahāyāna scriptures, composed in India from roughly the 1st century BCE onward. Best known are the Aṣṭasāhasrikā Prajñāpāramitā ("Perfection of Wisdom in Eight Thousand Lines") and its expanded versions in 25,000 and 100,000 lines, as well as the much shorter and enormously influential Heart Sutra and Diamond Sutra, the latter dated to 868 CE in its famous Dunhuang printed edition, the world's earliest dated printed book.

Teaching

The central theme of the literature is śūnyatā (emptiness): all phenomena lack inherent, independent existence (svabhāva) and arise dependently. The Heart Sutra famously declares that "form is emptiness, emptiness is form". The bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, and the Buddha's disciple Subhūti in dialogue with the Buddha and Śāriputra, are the principal speakers. The wisdom of emptiness is presented as the culminating perfection among the six pāramitās cultivated by a bodhisattva.

Influence

Prajñāpāramitā thought underlies the Madhyamaka philosophy of Nāgārjuna and profoundly influenced East Asian Buddhism, including Chan/Zen, and Tibetan Buddhism, where its study remains central to monastic curricula. In Java and Cambodia the goddess Prajñāpāramitā was an important object of devotion; the Singhasari statue from East Java is considered a masterpiece of Indonesian classical art.
