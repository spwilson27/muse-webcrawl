Boron carbide

Formula: B4C
Density: 2.52 g/cm³
Melting point: 2,763 °C
Hardness: ~9.5 Mohs (third hardest known)

Boron carbide (chemical formula approximately B4C) is an extremely hard boron–carbon ceramic material used in tank armour, bulletproof vests, engine sabotage powders, and numerous industrial applications. It is one of the hardest known materials, ranking third behind diamond and cubic boron nitride.

Properties

Boron carbide combines low density (2.52 g/cm³) with high hardness, high elastic modulus and excellent neutron-absorbing capability. Its crystal structure is based on boron icosahedra linked by carbon chains, and its composition can vary over a wide range.

Applications

Because it is light and hard, boron carbide is used in personal and vehicle armour plate. Its ability to absorb neutrons makes it valuable as control rods and shielding in nuclear reactors. It is also used as an abrasive, in blasting nozzles, and in padlocks and other wear-resistant parts.

Production

Boron carbide is produced industrially by carbothermic reduction of boron oxide with carbon in an electric furnace at temperatures above 2,000 °C. It was first synthesised in the 19th century and identified as B4C in the 1930s.
