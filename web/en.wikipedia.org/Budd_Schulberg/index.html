Budd Wilson Schulberg (March 27, 1914 – August 5, 2009) was an American screenwriter, television producer, novelist and sports writer. He is best known for the novel What Makes Sammy Run? (1941), a classic study of ambition in Hollywood, and for the screenplay of On the Waterfront (1954), which won the Academy Award for Best Story and Screenplay.

Early life

Schulberg was born in New York City, the son of B. P. Schulberg, a pioneer film producer and former head of Paramount Pictures. He grew up in Hollywood and attended Dartmouth College, graduating in 1936.

Career

His first novel, What Makes Sammy Run?, follows the rise of Sammy Glick, a ruthless young man clawing his way to the top of the studio system, and caused a sensation in Hollywood. The Disenchanted (1950) fictionalized his collaboration with F. Scott Fitzgerald. His screenplay for Elia Kazan's On the Waterfront, starring Marlon Brando, produced the famous "I coulda been a contender" speech. Schulberg later founded the Watts Writers Workshop in Los Angeles after the 1965 Watts riots.

Death

Schulberg died of natural causes at his home in Quiogue, New York, on August 5, 2009, aged 95.
