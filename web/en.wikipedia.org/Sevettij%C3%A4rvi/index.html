Sevettijärvi

Sevettijärvi (Skolt Sami: Čeavetjávri, Northern Sami: Geabbajávri, Inari Sami: Čevetjävri) is a village in the municipality of Inari, Finland.

Sevettijärvi is a village in the municipality of Inari in Finnish Lapland, Finland. It is located near the eastern border with Norway, on the shore of the lake of the same name, and is one of the main centres of the Skolt Sami people in Finland.

History

Sevettijärvi was founded in 1949 when 51 Skolt Sami families were evacuated from the Petsamo region, which Finland ceded to the Soviet Union after the Second World War, and resettled in the Inari area. The Finnish state built houses for the families around Lake Sevettijärvi. A school was later established in the village, and services such as a health centre, a shop and a post office followed.

The village is a centre of Skolt Sami culture and language. Most residents are Orthodox Christians, as is traditional among the Skolt Sami, and the village has an Orthodox church dedicated to Saint Tryphon of Pechenga. The Skolt Sami Heritage House (Äigil) in the village presents Skolt Sami traditions and history.

Geography

Sevettijärvi lies along the road between Kaamanen and the Norwegian border near Näätämö (Neiden). The surrounding area is sparsely populated forest and fell country in the Kaldoaivi Wilderness Area vicinity. Reindeer herding, fishing and hunting remain important traditional livelihoods.
