Production2010–present
ClassSmall family car (C-segment)

The Nissan Leaf (Japanese: 日産・リーフ), stylised as LEAF, is a battery-electric powered compact car manufactured by Nissan. It was introduced in Japan and the United States in December 2010 and is one of the best-selling electric cars in history, with global sales passing 500,000 units by 2020. "LEAF" is a backronym for "leading, environmentally friendly, affordable family car."

Generations

The first-generation Leaf (ZE0/AZE0) launched with a 24 kWh battery and a range of about 117 km (EPA). A second generation (ZE1) debuted for 2018 with sleeker styling, a 40 kWh battery, and ProPILOT driver assistance; the Leaf Plus added a 62 kWh pack. The Leaf was among the first mass-market highway-capable battery electric vehicles, and helped demonstrate a viable market for affordable EVs.

Technology

The Leaf uses a lithium-ion battery pack mounted under the floor and an AC synchronous motor. Later models support vehicle-to-grid and vehicle-to-home power supply, notably used in Japan during outages. Battery degradation, especially in hot climates with the passively cooled pack, has been a frequently discussed ownership issue.

Reception

The Leaf won the 2011 European Car of the Year and World Car of the Year awards. As newer rivals offered longer range, the model gradually ceded its early sales lead, though it remains a significant entry-level EV in many markets.
