Meuse-Argonne offensive

The Meuse-Argonne offensive was a major part of the final Allied offensive of World War I that stretched along the entire Western Front. Fought from 26 September 1918 until the Armistice of 11 November 1918, it was the largest battle in the military history of the United States, involving about 1.2 million American soldiers, and one of the deadliest, with roughly 350,000 total casualties.

Background

The offensive formed part of Marshal Ferdinand Foch's plan for simultaneous Allied attacks (the Hundred Days Offensive). The American Expeditionary Forces under General John J. Pershing, alongside French forces, attacked between the Meuse River and the Argonne Forest toward Sedan, aiming to cut the vital German railway line supplying the front.

Battle

Progress was slow at first against deeply entrenched German positions and difficult, forested terrain. The battle saw notable episodes including the "Lost Battalion," the actions of Corporal Alvin York, who became one of the most decorated American soldiers of the war, and early American tank operations. By early November, Allied forces broke through and advanced toward Sedan.

Legacy

The Meuse-Argonne American Cemetery near Romagne-sous-Montfaucon, with more than 14,000 graves, is the largest American military cemetery in Europe.
