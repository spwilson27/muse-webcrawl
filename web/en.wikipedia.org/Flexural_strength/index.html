Flexural strength

Flexural strength, also known as modulus of rupture, bend strength, or transverse rupture strength, is a material property defined as the stress in a material just before it yields in a flexure test. The transverse bending test is most frequently employed, in which a specimen having either a circular or rectangular cross-section is bent until fracture or yielding using a three-point or four-point flexural test technique.

Measurement

For a rectangular sample under a load in a three-point bending setup, the flexural strength is calculated as σ = 3FL / (2bd²), where F is the load at fracture, L the support span, b the width and d the thickness of the specimen. For a four-point bend test the formula differs according to the position of the loading points.

Considerations

The flexural strength usually exceeds the direct tensile strength of a brittle material, because in bending only the surface layers experience the maximum stress, so the probability of a critical flaw being present at peak stress is lower. Flexural properties are standard quality measures for concrete, timber, plastics, composites and ceramics.
