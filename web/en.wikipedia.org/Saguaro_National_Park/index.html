Saguaro National Park is an American national park in Pima County, southeastern Arizona, surrounding parts of the city of Tucson. It preserves a landscape of the Sonoran Desert dominated by the giant saguaro cactus, the largest cactus in the United States and a symbol of the American Southwest.

Geography

The park consists of two separate districts separated by Tucson: the Tucson Mountain District to the west and the Rincon Mountain District to the east. The western district protects dense saguaro forest on lower, hotter desert terrain, while the eastern district rises from cactus desert through grassland to pine-oak forest in the Rincon Mountains, exceeding 8,600 feet (2,620 m) at Mica Mountain. Scenic loop drives and hundreds of miles of trails serve both districts.

History

President Herbert Hoover proclaimed Saguaro National Monument in 1933, largely through the advocacy of University of Arizona scientists concerned about threats to the cacti from theft, grazing and development. The Tucson Mountain District was added in 1961. Much of the park was designated wilderness in 1976, and Congress elevated it to national park status in 1994. Civilian Conservation Corps crews built many of the park's early roads, trails and picnic structures in the 1930s.

Ecology

The saguaro grows extremely slowly, may take 50–70 years to produce its first arm, and can live 150–200 years, reaching 12–18 metres tall. Its flowers — the state flower of Arizona — open at night in late spring and are pollinated by bats, bees and birds; its summer fruit is a traditional food of the Tohono O'odham people. The park is home to six biotic communities, from desert scrub to coniferous forest, and animals including javelinas, coyotes, Gila woodpeckers, cactus wrens, desert tortoises and mountain lions. Invasive buffelgrass and increasing urban light and heat pose management challenges.
