Brenda Milner

Born: 15 July 1918, Manchester, England
Field: Neuropsychology
Institution: Montreal Neurological Institute, McGill University
Known for: Studies of patient H.M. and memory systems
Brenda Milner, founder of neuropsychology

Brenda Milner (born 15 July 1918) is a British-Canadian neuropsychologist who has contributed extensively to the research literature on the functions of the temporal lobes and other brain areas in learning, memory, and speech. She is a professor at McGill University and a researcher at the Montreal Neurological Institute, and is often regarded as a founder of neuropsychology.

Work with patient H.M.

Milner's doctoral work under Donald Hebb brought her to study patients of neurosurgeon Wilder Penfield. Her studies of Henry Molaison, known as patient H.M., who had undergone bilateral medial temporal lobe surgery for epilepsy in 1953, showed that although H.M. could no longer form new long-term declarative memories, he could still learn new motor skills — demonstrating through tasks such as mirror tracing that memory is not a single system, and establishing the distinction between declarative and procedural memory.

Honours

Milner has received numerous awards, including the Kavli Prize in Neuroscience (2014), the Gairdner Award, and the Balzan Prize. She remained professionally active past the age of 100.
