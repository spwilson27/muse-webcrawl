Toru Iwatani (born 25 January 1955) is a Japanese video game designer who created the arcade game Pac-Man (1980), one of the most successful and influential video games of all time.

Career

Iwatani joined Namco in 1977 as a self-taught programmer. At a time when arcade games were dominated by space shooters such as Space Invaders, he set out to design a game that would appeal to a broader audience, including women. His famous inspiration was a pizza with a slice missing, which suggested the character's wedge-shaped mouth. The game's theme of eating — reflected in the Japanese title Puckman, from the onomatopoeia paku paku for munching — and its non-violent maze chase made it a worldwide phenomenon.

Pac-Man

Released in Japan in May 1980 and in North America later that year, Pac-Man became the best-selling arcade game of its era and an enduring cultural icon. Iwatani also designed the earlier pinball-video hybrid Gee Bee (1978) and later worked on other titles before leaving game development.

Later life

Iwatani left Namco in 2007 and became a professor, teaching game design at Tokyo Polytechnic University. In 2010 he was presented with a Guinness World Records certificate recognising Pac-Man as the most successful coin-operated arcade game.
