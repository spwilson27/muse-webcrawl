Metamora (play)

Metamora; or, The Last of the Wampanoags is an 1829 American play written by John Augustus Stone. It was created as a star vehicle for the actor Edwin Forrest, who had offered a prize for an original American play. The drama romanticised the life of Metacomet (King Philip), the Wampanoag leader of King Philip's War.

Reception and legacy

The play was enormously popular throughout the nineteenth century and became a touchstone of early American theatre, embodying the era's fascination with the "noble savage" and the mythology of the vanishing Native American. Forrest performed the title role for some forty years. The play's themes were later echoed, often ironically, in works such as Melville's The Confidence-Man.
