White Ensign

UseNaval ensign
DesignRed cross on a white field, Union Jack in the canton
Used byRoyal Navy (since 1864)

The White Ensign, at one time called the St George's Ensign due to the simultaneous existence of a crossless version, is an ensign flown on British Royal Navy ships and shore establishments. It consists of a red St George's Cross on a white field, with the Union Jack in the upper canton.

History

From the 16th century, English warships flew coloured ensigns according to their squadron — red, white, and blue. In 1864 an Admiralty order reorganised the system: the White Ensign was reserved to the Royal Navy, the Red Ensign to merchant ships, and the Blue Ensign to government vessels and naval auxiliaries.

Use

The White Ensign is flown at the stern of commissioned Royal Navy warships while in harbour and on special occasions at sea. It is also flown at naval shore establishments and by the Royal Yacht Squadron. A small number of Commonwealth navies, including those of Australia and New Zealand, fly their own national variants of the White Ensign with the Southern Cross or other devices in the fly.

Customs

By tradition, the White Ensign is never flown over a church except in the case of certain memorials; St George's Church customs aside, a White Ensign draped at funerals of naval personnel is a recognised honour.
