Variable star

A variable star is a star whose brightness as seen from Earth changes with time. This variation may be caused by a change in emitted light (intrinsic variables) or by an object eclipsing part of the light (extrinsic variables), such as an orbiting companion star or planet.

Classification

Intrinsic variables include pulsating variables — Cepheids, RR Lyrae stars, Mira variables, and irregular and semiregular variables — whose outer layers expand and contract; eruptive variables such as flare stars and T Tauri stars; and cataclysmic variables such as novae and dwarf novae, which undergo sudden outbursts. Extrinsic variables include eclipsing binaries such as Algol, and rotating stars with large starspots.

Importance

Cepheid variables obey a period–luminosity relation discovered by Henrietta Swan Leavitt, making them standard candles for measuring cosmic distances. Observations of variable stars, many contributed by amateur astronomers through organisations such as the AAVSO, are catalogued in the General Catalogue of Variable Stars, which lists tens of thousands of variables.
