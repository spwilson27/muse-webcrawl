Chain of custody

Chain of custody (CoC), in legal contexts, is documentation that chronicles the sequence of custody, control, transfer, analysis and disposition of physical or electronic evidence. Maintaining an unbroken chain of custody is essential for evidence to be admissible in court, because it demonstrates that the item has not been tampered with or substituted.


Evidence with custody documentation

Procedure

Each person who handles evidence records their identity, the date and time, and the purpose of the transfer. Evidence is typically sealed in tamper-evident packaging, labelled, and stored in a secure location; any opening of the seal must itself be documented. Forensic laboratories apply the same principles to samples and, increasingly, to digital evidence, where hash values verify file integrity.

Other applications

The term is also used in supply-chain certification — for example, the Forest Stewardship Council's chain-of-custody certification tracks timber from certified forests to finished products — and in clinical and doping-control sample handling.
