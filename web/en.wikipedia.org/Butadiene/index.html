Butadiene

1,3-Butadiene
Formula: C4H6
Appearance: Colourless gas
CAS: 106-99-0
Use: Synthetic rubber manufacture

Butadiene, almost always meaning 1,3-butadiene, is the organic compound with the formula CH2=CH–CH=CH2. It is a colourless gas that is easily condensed to a liquid, and is the simplest conjugated diene.

Production

Most butadiene is produced as a by-product of the steam cracking of hydrocarbons to produce ethylene, from which it is separated by extractive distillation. It can also be made by catalytic dehydrogenation of butane or butenes. Annual world production is on the order of ten million tonnes.

Uses

Butadiene is principally used to manufacture synthetic rubbers: styrene-butadiene rubber (SBR), polybutadiene rubber, and nitrile rubber, as well as ABS plastics and nylon intermediates. The demand for synthetic rubber grew sharply during the Second World War when natural rubber supplies were cut off.

Reactivity and hazards

As a conjugated diene, butadiene undergoes Diels–Alder reactions and polymerises readily, sometimes forming explosive "popcorn" polymer deposits. It is a flammable gas and a known human carcinogen; exposure is regulated in the workplace.
