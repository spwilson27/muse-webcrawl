ii–V–I progression

The ii–V–I progression (also written ii–V–I or 2-5-1) is a common chord progression in tonal music, especially jazz, built from the supertonic, dominant, and tonic chords of a major key. In C major the chords are Dm7 – G7 – Cmaj7. It is generally regarded as the most important cadential progression in jazz harmony.

Structure

The progression combines smooth voice leading with strong root motion by descending fifths: the ii chord functions as a predominant (subdominant function) preparing the dominant V chord, whose tritone and leading tone resolve to the tonic I. In minor keys the variant iiø7–V7–i is used. Variants and extensions include adding ninths and thirteenths, substituting the V chord with its tritone substitute (ii–♭II–I), and "backdoor" progressions.

Usage

The ii–V–I underlies countless jazz standards and popular songs; sequences of ii–V–I progressions in different keys ("turnarounds" and chained ii–Vs) form the harmonic backbone of bebop repertoire such as "All the Things You Are" and rhythm changes tunes based on "I Got Rhythm".
