Fribourg

Fribourg (French) or Freiburg (German) is the capital of the Swiss canton of Fribourg and the district of La Sarine. Located on both sides of the river Sarine/Saane, it is known for its well-preserved medieval old town.

History

Fribourg was founded in 1157 by Berthold IV, Duke of Zähringen, on a rocky promontory above the Sarine. It joined the Swiss Confederation in 1481 and remained a Catholic stronghold during the Reformation. The University of Fribourg, founded in 1889, is Switzerland's only bilingual university.

Landmarks

The old town features some 200 Gothic facades, the Cathedral of St. Nicholas with its 74-metre tower, and historic bridges across the Sarine, including the Bern Bridge and the modern Poya Bridge. The city sits on the language border between French- and German-speaking Switzerland, often called the Röstigraben.
