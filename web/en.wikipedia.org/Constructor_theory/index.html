Constructor theory

Constructor theory expresses physical laws as statements about possible and impossible tasks.

Constructor theory is a proposed framework for fundamental physics, developed by David Deutsch and elaborated with Chiara Marletto, that seeks to reformulate physical laws in terms of which transformations, or tasks, are possible and which are impossible. A constructor is a physical system that can bring about a given transformation and retain the capacity to do so again, as a catalyst or a machine does.

Core ideas

In the prevailing conception, laws of physics are expressed as equations of motion plus initial conditions, from which the future state of a system is predicted. Constructor theory inverts this: its fundamental statements are counterfactual, declaring that certain tasks can be performed to arbitrary accuracy, or that they cannot be performed at all, no matter how advanced the technology. Conservation laws, for instance, correspond to the impossibility of tasks that would create energy from nothing.

Deutsch has argued that existing laws such as thermodynamics, information theory, and even the theory of computation can be unified in this language, and that constructor theory of information can define measurable quantities such as information media without reference to quantum mechanics, potentially guiding the construction of a successor theory to quantum theory itself. Marletto has extended the approach to thermodynamics, life, and the theory of measurement.

Reception

The programme, introduced in Deutsch's 2013 paper "Constructor Theory", remains a minority research direction, praised for conceptual novelty and criticised for not yet yielding new empirical predictions. It is closely associated with the Oxford quantum information and foundations community.
