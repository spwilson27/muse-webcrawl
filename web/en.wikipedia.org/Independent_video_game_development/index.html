Independent video game development

Independent video game development, or indie game development, is the creation of video games by individuals or small teams without the financial and technical support of a large publisher. Indie developers typically work with modest budgets, distributed digitally through platforms such as Steam, itch.io, and console storefronts, and are known for experimental design, distinctive visual styles, and revival of older genres.

History

The earliest computer games were effectively independent creations, and bedroom programmers drove the British microcomputer scene of the 1980s. Rising budgets in the 1990s pushed the mainstream industry toward publisher-driven, large-team "AAA" production. The modern indie movement emerged in the mid-2000s with digital distribution (Xbox Live Arcade, Steam), accessible engines and tools (GameMaker, Unity, RPG Maker, later Unreal Engine), and breakout successes such as Cave Story (2004), Braid (2008), Super Meat Boy (2010), Minecraft (2011), Fez (2012), and later Undertale (2015), Stardew Valley (2016), Hollow Knight (2017), and Hades (2020). The 2012 documentary Indie Game: The Movie chronicled the movement.

Funding and distribution

Indie projects are financed through personal savings, crowdfunding (notably Kickstarter), early access sales, grants, and increasingly through independent publishers and subscription services. Crowded storefronts have made discoverability a central challenge, and the term "indie" has blurred as mid-sized studios and publisher-backed labels adopt similar aesthetics.
