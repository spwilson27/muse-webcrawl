Worldwide LHC Computing Grid

The Worldwide LHC Computing Grid (WLCG), formerly the LHC Computing Grid (LCG), is a global collaboration of computing centres designed to store, distribute and analyse the enormous volumes of data produced by the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) at CERN. It is one of the largest distributed computing infrastructures in the world, linking more than 170 sites in over 40 countries.

Design

The LHC's four major experiments (ATLAS, CMS, ALICE and LHCb) generate tens of petabytes of data per year — far more than a single data centre could store or analyse. The WLCG therefore adopts a tiered model. Tier 0, at CERN (with a remote extension at the Wigner Research Centre in Budapest), records the raw data and performs first-pass reconstruction. Tier 1 centres — large national facilities such as Fermilab, Brookhaven, GridPP (UK), IN2P3 (France) and KIT (Germany) — provide archival storage and reprocessing. Around 160 Tier 2 centres, mostly at universities, supply simulation and end-user analysis capacity.

Operation

Data flow from CERN to the Tier 1 sites over dedicated optical links (the LHC Optical Private Network, LHCOPN), and between tiers over research networks. Middleware stacks, historically based on grid technologies and now incorporating cloud and container tools, schedule jobs across sites, while data management systems (such as Rucio) place replicas where analysis demand arises. The grid processes on the order of a million computing jobs per day.

Significance

The WLCG was a pioneering demonstration of large-scale distributed "grid" computing and provided the computational backbone for the discovery of the Higgs boson in 2012. Preparations for the High-Luminosity LHC, expected to multiply data volumes several-fold in the 2030s, are driving its ongoing evolution.
