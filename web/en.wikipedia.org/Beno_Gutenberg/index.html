Beno Gutenberg
Born4 June 1889, Darmstadt, German Empire
Died25 January 1960, Pasadena, California, USA
FieldSeismology, geophysics

Beno Gutenberg (June 4, 1889 – January 25, 1960) was a German-American seismologist who made foundational contributions to the understanding of the Earth's interior. Working for much of his career at the California Institute of Technology, he collaborated with Charles Richter on the measurement of earthquake magnitude and on the frequency distribution of earthquakes.

Work

Gutenberg studied the propagation of seismic waves and refined the location of the boundary between the mantle and the core — the core–mantle boundary at a depth of about 2,900 km is known as the Gutenberg discontinuity. With Richter he published the classic monograph Seismicity of the Earth and derived the Gutenberg–Richter law describing the relationship between earthquake magnitude and frequency. He also identified a low-velocity zone in the upper mantle, interpreted as the asthenosphere.

Career

Born in Darmstadt, Gutenberg took his doctorate at Göttingen under Emil Wiechert. After limited prospects in Germany he emigrated to the United States in 1930, joining the Seismological Laboratory in Pasadena and becoming its director in 1947.
