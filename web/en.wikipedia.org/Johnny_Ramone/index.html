Johnny Ramone

John William Cummings (October 8, 1948 – September 15, 2004), known professionally as Johnny Ramone, was an American musician who was the guitarist and a founding member of the Ramones, one of the most influential bands of all time. He remained in the band throughout its entire career. He is recognized as an influential guitarist and is known for his fast down-picking guitar style.

Career

Cummings co-founded the Ramones in Forest Hills, Queens, in 1974 with friends from the neighborhood. The band's stripped-down, high-speed songs — barred chords played with relentless downstrokes on his Mosrite guitar — became foundational to punk rock. Johnny acted as the band's strict taskmaster and business manager, driving their relentless touring schedule over 2,263 concerts until their retirement in 1996.

Death and legacy

Johnny Ramone died at his Los Angeles home in 2004, five years after being diagnosed with prostate cancer. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame with the Ramones in 2002 and is widely ranked among the greatest guitarists of all time. His posthumous autobiography, Commando, was published in 2012.
