Bible Belt

The Bible Belt is an informal region of the United States, mainly in the South and parts of the Midwest, where evangelical Protestantism plays an especially strong role in society and politics, and church attendance is among the highest in the nation.

Extent and culture

The region is generally considered to encompass states such as Alabama, Mississippi, Tennessee, Kentucky, Georgia, the Carolinas, Arkansas, Oklahoma and Texas. Southern Baptist and other evangelical churches predominate, and the region's religious culture has historically influenced its conservative social and political outlook. The term was popularised in the 1920s by journalist H. L. Mencken.

Related terms

Similar terms have been coined for other regions, including the "Unchurched Belt" of the Pacific Northwest for the area of lowest church attendance, and analogues exist in other countries, such as the Dutch Bible Belt in the Netherlands.
