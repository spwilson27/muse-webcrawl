Wrasse

The wrasses are a family, Labridae, of marine fish, many of them brilliantly coloured. The family includes more than 600 species in about 81 genera, found in tropical and temperate Atlantic, Indian and Pacific waters, typically on coral reefs and rocky shores.

Biology

Wrasses range from a few centimetres to the humphead (Napoleon) wrasse, which can exceed 2 metres. Many species are protogynous hermaphrodites, beginning life female and transforming into males. Cleaner wrasses pick parasites from larger fish at 'cleaning stations'. Some species, such as the orange-dotted tuskfish, use rocks as tools to crack shellfish.

Human use

Some wrasses are food fish or aquarium species; ballan wrasse and others are farmed for use as cleaner fish in salmon aquaculture.
