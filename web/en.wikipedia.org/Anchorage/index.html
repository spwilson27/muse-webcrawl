Anchorage

The term anchorage has several meanings. In nautical usage, an anchorage is a location where ships can anchor safely, typically an area of a harbour or roadstead with suitable depth and holding ground, sometimes designated on charts and subject to port rules. In engineering and construction, anchorage refers to devices or methods that fix structural elements in place, such as anchor bolts set in concrete or the anchoring blocks of a suspension bridge.

Nautical anchorage

Anchorages may be assigned by vessel traffic services and are often located near ports where ships wait for berths, pilots, or favourable tides. Good holding ground, shelter from prevailing winds, and adequate depth at low water are the principal criteria. Ships at anchor display anchor lights and shapes and maintain an anchor watch.

Structural anchorage

In structural engineering, anchorage ensures that forces are transferred safely between connected components. Examples include the anchorages of cables in suspension and cable-stayed bridges, ground anchors and rock bolts in geotechnical works, and fasteners used in building facades.
