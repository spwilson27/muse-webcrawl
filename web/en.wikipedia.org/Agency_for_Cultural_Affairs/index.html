Agency for Cultural Affairs

Agency overview
Formed: 15 June 1968
Headquarters: Kyoto, Japan
Parent agency: Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology (MEXT)
Website: bunka.go.jp

The Agency for Cultural Affairs (Japanese: 文化庁, Bunka-chō) is a special body of the Japanese Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology (MEXT). It was set up in 1968 to promote Japanese arts and culture.

History

The agency was formed on 15 June 1968 through the merger of the Cultural Bureau of the Ministry of Education and the Cultural Properties Protection Commission. Its remit covers the promotion of culture and the arts, the protection of cultural properties, copyright administration, and policies relating to the Japanese language and religious affairs.

In April 2023 the agency relocated the bulk of its operations from Tokyo to Kyoto, the first central government agency to move its headquarters out of the capital, as part of a regional revitalisation policy. A smaller office remains in Tokyo.

Responsibilities

The agency administers the system for designating National Treasures and Important Cultural Properties of Japan, oversees the registration of tangible and intangible cultural heritage, and supports traditional performing arts such as kabuki, noh and bunraku. It also runs the Japan Media Arts Festival, administers the Japanese Agency for Cultural Affairs copyright framework, and promotes the Japanese language through surveys and the annual Kanji of the Year.

Notable programmes include the designation of Living National Treasures (holders of Important Intangible Cultural Properties), support for the preservation of historic districts, and the promotion of Japanese culture overseas.
