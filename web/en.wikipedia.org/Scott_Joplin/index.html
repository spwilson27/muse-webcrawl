Scott Joplin

Born: c. 1868, Texas, United States

Died: 1 April 1917, New York City

Occupation: Composer, pianist

Known for: Ragtime compositions

Scott Joplin (c. 1868 – April 1, 1917) was an American composer and pianist. Dubbed the "King of Ragtime", he composed more than 40 ragtime pieces, including the "Maple Leaf Rag" (1899) and "The Entertainer" (1902), which became two of the most famous works in the genre.

Life

Joplin was born in Texas, the son of a former slave and a free-born woman, and grew up in Texarkana, where he received classical music training. He worked as an itinerant pianist across the Midwest and performed at the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago. He later settled in Sedalia, Missouri, where the "Maple Leaf Rag" was published and brought him fame and steady royalties.

Later work

Ambitious to expand ragtime into larger forms, Joplin composed the opera Treemonisha, which was never fully staged in his lifetime. He died in New York in 1917, impoverished and largely forgotten as ragtime fell from fashion.

Legacy

Joplin's music was revived in the 1970s, when "The Entertainer" was featured in the film The Sting (1973) and became a popular hit. Treemonisha received its full premiere in 1972, and Joplin was awarded a posthumous Pulitzer Prize in 1976.
