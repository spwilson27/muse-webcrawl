The monarchy of Malaysia is the constitutional monarchy system of the Federation of Malaysia. The national monarch, titled the Yang di-Pertuan Agong (often rendered "King"), is one of the world's few elected monarchs: every five years the Conference of Rulers chooses one of the nine hereditary Malay rulers to serve as head of state.

Structure

Nine of Malaysia's thirteen states are themselves monarchies, headed by sultans (and in Perlis a raja, in Negeri Sembilan a Yang di-Pertuan Besar). These nine rulers form the electoral college; by convention the office rotates among them broadly in order of seniority. The four remaining states have governors (Yang di-Pertua Negeri) who sit in the Conference of Rulers without voting in royal elections.

Role

The Agong is head of state and commander-in-chief, appoints the prime minister and the cabinet on the usual constitutional conventions, assents to legislation, and is the head of Islam in the federal territories and his own state. The first Yang di-Pertuan Agong, Tuanku Abdul Rahman of Negeri Sembilan, took office at independence in 1957. The official residence is the Istana Negara in Kuala Lumpur.
