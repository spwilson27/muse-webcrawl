Game Developers Conference

The Game Developers Conference (GDC) is an annual conference for video game developers, and the largest professional event of its kind in the world. Founded in 1988 by Chris Crawford as the Computer Game Developers Conference — first held in his living room in San Jose, California — it has grown into a week-long event at the Moscone Center in San Francisco attracting tens of thousands of attendees.

Program

GDC features hundreds of lectures, panels, tutorials, and roundtables on game design, programming, art, audio, production, and business, many of them postmortems of notable games. The main conference hosts the Game Developers Choice Awards and the Independent Games Festival, showcases for independent games. An expo floor presents tools, middleware, and platform holders, and the event serves as a major recruiting and networking venue. Recordings of talks are archived in the GDC Vault.

History and related events

The conference moved to San Francisco in 2005. Sister events have included GDC Europe and GDC China. The 2020 conference was postponed and replaced by a virtual event, GDC Summer, during the COVID-19 pandemic, before returning in person. GDC is organized by Informa (through the former UBM/Gamasutra group) and is closely associated with the website Game Developer (formerly Gamasutra).
