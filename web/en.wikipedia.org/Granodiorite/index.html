Granodiorite is a coarse-grained intrusive igneous rock similar to granite, but containing more plagioclase feldspar than orthoclase feldspar. It is one of the most abundant intrusive rocks of the continental crust and commonly forms large batholiths in mountain belts above subduction zones.

Composition

On the QAPF classification diagram, granodiorite contains between 20% and 60% quartz, and plagioclase makes up 65% to 90% of its total feldspar content. Typical accessory mafic minerals are biotite and hornblende, which give the rock its speckled grey appearance. Its extrusive (volcanic) equivalent is dacite.

Occurrence and use

Granodiorite underlies much of ranges such as the Sierra Nevada batholith in California. The famous Rosetta Stone is a stela of granodiorite, and the rock has been used since antiquity for statuary, paving and building stone quarried notably at Mons Claudianus in Egypt.
