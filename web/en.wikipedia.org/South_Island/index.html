South Island
Māori name: Te Waipounamu
Area: ~150,437 km²
Largest city: Christchurch

The South Island (Māori: Te Waipounamu, "the waters of greenstone") is the larger of the two main islands of New Zealand, separated from the North Island by Cook Strait. It is the world's twelfth-largest island but holds only about a quarter of New Zealand's population.

Geography

The Southern Alps run the length of the island, culminating in Aoraki / Mount Cook (3,724 m), New Zealand's highest peak. West of the Alps lies the wet, forested West Coast with the Franz Josef and Fox Glaciers and the fiords of Fiordland, including Milford Sound; to the east lie the dry Canterbury Plains, the Mackenzie Country, and the vineyards and sounds of Marlborough in the northeast.

Cities and economy

The main centres are Christchurch (the largest), Dunedin, Nelson, Invercargill, and Queenstown. The economy rests on agriculture and horticulture (dairy, sheep, wine — Marlborough sauvignon blanc), forestry, hydroelectric power, and tourism, from whale watching at Kaikōura to adventure sports in Queenstown-Lakes. The island was severely affected by the Canterbury earthquakes of 2010–2011, which devastated central Christchurch.
