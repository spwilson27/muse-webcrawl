The Council for New England, formally the Council for Plantation of New England, was a joint-stock company chartered by King James I on November 3, 1620, to colonise and govern the territory of New England in North America, between the 40th and 48th parallels.

Origins

The council succeeded the Plymouth Company, part of the Virginia Company scheme of 1606. Its leading figure was Sir Ferdinando Gorges, an energetic promoter of English colonisation. The council's sweeping charter granted it control over land, trade, and government across the whole of coastal northern America assigned to it.

Land grants

The council itself planted few settlements directly but issued patents and grants to others, including grants underlying the Dorchester Company, the Massachusetts Bay Colony, and settlements in Maine and New Hampshire. In 1622 it granted territory to Gorges and John Mason that later became Maine and New Hampshire.

Dissolution

Beset by disputes among its members and by the independent charters granted directly by the Crown, the council divided its territory among its members in 1635 and surrendered its charter, hoping for a royal governorship-general that never materialised.
