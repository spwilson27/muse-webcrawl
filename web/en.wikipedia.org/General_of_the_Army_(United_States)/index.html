General of the Army (United States)

General of the Army is a five-star general officer rank in the United States Army. It is the second-highest possible rank, senior to a general and junior only to the special grade of General of the Armies. The rank is equivalent to fleet admiral in the Navy and General of the Air Force.

History

The five-star rank was established by Public Law 78-482 on 14 December 1944. Four officers received it during World War II: George C. Marshall, Douglas MacArthur, Dwight D. Eisenhower, and Henry H. "Hap" Arnold (later also made General of the Air Force). Omar Bradley received the rank in 1950, the last officer so promoted. Ulysses S. Grant was posthumously promoted to the rank in 2024. Earlier, the title "General of the Army of the United States" had been held by Grant, William Tecumseh Sherman, and Philip Sheridan in the 19th century.
