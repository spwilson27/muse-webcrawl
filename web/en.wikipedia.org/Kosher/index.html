Kosher

Common kosher certification marks include the OU, OK, Star-K and Kof-K symbols.

Kosher (from Hebrew כָּשֵׁר, kāshēr, "fit") describes food that conforms to the dietary laws of Judaism, known as kashrut. Foods that may be consumed in accordance with Jewish law (halakha) are termed kosher, while foods that are not permissible are called treif.

Dietary laws

The laws of kashrut, derived from the Torah and elaborated in the Talmud, include: only land animals that chew the cud and have cloven hooves (such as cattle and sheep) may be eaten, excluding pigs and camels; only fish with fins and scales are permitted, excluding shellfish; permitted birds exclude birds of prey; meat and dairy products must not be cooked or eaten together; animals must be slaughtered by a trained shochet in the ritual manner (shechita), and blood must be drained; and grape products and wine require special supervision.

Certification

Commercial kosher certification is granted by agencies such as the Orthodox Union (OU), whose symbols appear on packaged foods worldwide. Certification is also sought by many non-Jewish consumers who regard kosher marks as an indicator of quality or purity.
