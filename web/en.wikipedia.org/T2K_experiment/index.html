T2K experiment

Type: Long-baseline neutrino oscillation experiment
Locations: J-PARC, Tokai; Super-Kamiokande, Gifu, Japan
Baseline: 295 km
Beam: Muon neutrinos, peak ~0.6 GeV
Running since: 2010

The T2K experiment (Tokai to Kamioka) is a long-baseline neutrino oscillation experiment in Japan. A beam of muon neutrinos is produced at the J-PARC accelerator complex in Tokai, on Japan's east coast, and directed 295 km through the Earth to the Super-Kamiokande detector beneath Mount Ikeno in Gifu Prefecture.

Design

Protons from J-PARC strike a graphite target, producing pions that decay into muon neutrinos. The beam is aimed slightly off-axis (2.5 degrees) so that the neutrinos arriving at Super-Kamiokande have a narrow energy spectrum peaking near 0.6 GeV, maximising the oscillation effect over the baseline. A near detector complex, ND280, 280 metres from the target, characterises the beam before oscillation.

Results

In 2011 T2K reported the first indication that muon neutrinos transform into electron neutrinos (the θ13 mixing angle), confirmed in 2013. The experiment now searches for CP violation in the lepton sector by comparing oscillation of neutrinos and antineutrinos, and has reported hints favouring CP violation. Its successor will be the Hyper-Kamiokande experiment.
