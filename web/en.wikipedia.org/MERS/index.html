Middle East respiratory syndrome (MERS) is a viral respiratory infection caused by the Middle East respiratory syndrome–related coronavirus (MERS-CoV), a species of coronavirus closely related to the viruses that cause SARS and COVID-19.

Signs and symptoms

MERS symptoms range from none at all to severe pneumonia, fever, cough, shortness of breath, and acute respiratory distress. Complications include respiratory failure, kidney failure, and septic shock. The case fatality rate is around 35% among reported cases, with most deaths occurring in people with underlying medical conditions.

Epidemiology

The virus was first identified in Saudi Arabia in 2012. Dromedary camels are a natural host and the principal zoonotic source of human infection; person-to-person transmission occurs mainly in healthcare settings with close contact. The largest outbreak outside the Arabian Peninsula occurred in South Korea in 2015, with 186 cases and 36 deaths. There is no specific vaccine or antiviral treatment; care is supportive.
