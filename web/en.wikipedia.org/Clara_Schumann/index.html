Clara Schumann

Born: 13 September 1819, Leipzig
Died: 20 May 1896, Frankfurt
Occupations: Pianist, composer, piano teacher
Spouse: Robert Schumann

Clara Schumann, one of the foremost pianists of the Romantic era.

Clara Josephine Schumann (née Wieck; 13 September 1819 – 20 May 1896) was a German pianist, composer and piano teacher. Regarded as one of the most distinguished pianists of the Romantic era, she exerted her influence over a concert career spanning 61 years, championing the works of her husband Robert Schumann and of Johannes Brahms, and changing the format and repertoire of the piano recital. She was also an influential composer, though her compositions were long undervalued.

Early life and career

Clara Wieck was a child prodigy trained by her father, Friedrich Wieck, one of the leading piano teachers of the day. She made her debut at the Leipzig Gewandhaus at age nine and toured Paris and Vienna to acclaim while still a teenager; in Vienna she was named Royal and Imperial Chamber Virtuoso. Against her father's bitter opposition and after a celebrated legal battle, she married Robert Schumann in 1840, the day before her 21st birthday.

Marriage and musical life

The Schumanns' household in Leipzig, then Dresden and Düsseldorf, stood at the centre of German musical life. Clara balanced international concert tours with raising eight children and promoting Robert's music; her own compositions of the period include the Piano Concerto in A minor, Op. 7, written in her teens, the Piano Trio, Op. 17, songs and character pieces. After Robert's death in 1856 she supported the family by performing and teaching, formed a lifelong close friendship with Brahms, and became the authoritative interpreter and editor of Schumann's works. She was a noted performer of Chopin, Mendelssohn and Beethoven, and from 1878 taught at the Hoch Conservatory in Frankfurt.

Legacy

Clara Schumann's playing was praised for its depth, technique and emotional restraint, and she helped establish the modern recital convention of playing from memory and programming serious repertoire. Renewed scholarly and recording interest since the late 20th century has restored her standing as a significant composer in her own right. See also Robert Schumann and Johannes Brahms.
