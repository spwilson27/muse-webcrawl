Harley-Davidson

Founded: 1903, Milwaukee, Wisconsin, U.S.

Founders: William S. Harley, Arthur Davidson, Walter Davidson, William A. Davidson

Industry: Motorcycles

Headquarters: Milwaukee, Wisconsin

Harley-Davidson, Inc. is an American motorcycle manufacturer founded in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, in 1903. One of two major American motorcycle manufacturers to survive the Great Depression (alongside Indian), it is iconic for its heavyweight cruiser and touring motorcycles powered by air-cooled V-twin engines, and for the strong brand community built around its riders.

History

William S. Harley and the Davidson brothers built their first motor-bicycle in a Milwaukee shed in 1903. The company supplied motorcycles to the U.S. military in both world wars and became the largest motorcycle manufacturer in the world by the 1920s. After ownership by AMF (1969–1981), a management buyout restored independence; the 1980s tariff on imported heavyweight motorcycles and the Evolution engine helped revive the brand. Model families include the Sportster, Softail, Dyna, Touring, and the modern Revolution Max-powered Pan America and Sportster S; the electric LiveWire line was later spun off as a separate brand.

Culture

Harley-Davidson owners are served by the Harley Owners Group (H.O.G.), founded in 1983, and the brand is celebrated at rallies such as Sturgis. The Harley-Davidson Museum opened in Milwaukee in 2008.
