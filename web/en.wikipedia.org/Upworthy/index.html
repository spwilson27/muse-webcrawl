Upworthy is a storytelling media company founded in March 2012 by Eli Pariser, co-founder of MoveOn and author of The Filter Bubble, and Peter Koechley, former managing editor of The Onion. The site curates and repackages videos, infographics and articles on social and political topics, optimising headlines and presentation for sharing on social media, with the stated mission of promoting serious, important content — in its words, "things that matter".

History and growth

Backed by early investment from figures including Alexis Ohanian and Chris Hughes, Upworthy grew extremely fast on the strength of Facebook distribution and its two-headline A/B testing of "curiosity gap" headlines. By late 2013 it was among the fastest-growing media sites ever measured, drawing tens of millions of monthly visitors. The site raised venture funding rounds including a $4 million round in 2012 and an $8 million round in 2013.

Decline and changes

Changes to Facebook's News Feed algorithm in December 2013, aimed at higher-quality content, cut sharply into Upworthy's traffic, which fell by roughly half within two months. The company went through rounds of layoffs, a shift toward original video production for platforms, and a 2017 merger with GOOD Worldwide, forming Good Media Group. Upworthy's style of emotionally framed, curiosity-driven headlines became highly influential — and widely parodied — across digital media, and it is frequently cited in discussions of clickbait and viral publishing.

Model

Upworthy described its work as drawing "massive amounts of attention to the topics that matter most" using data-driven packaging. Its editors selected content for civic value, then tested dozens of headlines per item, measuring success by total attention minutes rather than page views alone.
