Cosine

In mathematics, the cosine is a trigonometric function of an angle. The cosine of an acute angle is defined in the context of a right triangle: for the specified angle, it is the ratio of the length of the side adjacent to the angle to the length of the longest side of the triangle (the hypotenuse). More generally, cosine is defined for all real and complex arguments using the unit circle or a power series.

Definitions

For an angle θ in a right triangle, cos θ = adjacent / hypotenuse. On the unit circle, cos θ is the x-coordinate of the point where the terminal side of the angle meets the circle. Cosine is an even, periodic function with period 2π, and satisfies the identity cos²θ + sin²θ = 1. Its Taylor series is cos x = 1 − x²/2! + x⁴/4! − ⋯.

Values and properties

Notable values include cos 0 = 1, cos(π/3) = 1/2, cos(π/2) = 0 and cos π = −1. The derivative of cos x is −sin x. By Euler's formula, cos x = (eix + e−ix)/2. The inverse function is arccosine (arccos), with range [0, π].

Applications

Cosine appears throughout physics and engineering: in describing oscillations and waves, in the law of cosines for solving triangles, in Fourier analysis, and in the dot product formula a·b = |a||b| cos θ. In signal processing, the discrete cosine transform is fundamental to image and audio compression such as JPEG.
