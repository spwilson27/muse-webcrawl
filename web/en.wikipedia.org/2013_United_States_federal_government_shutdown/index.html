2013 United States federal government shutdown

The 2013 United States federal government shutdown ran from 1 to 16 October 2013, when Congress failed to enact appropriations for fiscal year 2014. The impasse centred on Republican efforts in the House of Representatives to delay or defund the Affordable Care Act ("Obamacare") as a condition of funding the government. About 800,000 federal employees were furloughed at the outset, national parks and museums closed, and many services were suspended; the Congressional Budget Office and private analysts estimated the shutdown cost the economy billions of dollars.

Background

With the Senate controlled by Democrats and the House by Republicans, the chambers passed incompatible continuing resolutions in late September 2013. House versions funded the government while defunding or delaying the Affordable Care Act, whose insurance exchanges were due to open on 1 October; the Senate stripped those provisions, and the House declined to accept the clean bill before the midnight deadline. Senator Ted Cruz's 21-hour floor speech in support of defunding became a symbol of the strategy.

Effects

Agencies implemented contingency plans: roughly 800,000 employees were initially furloughed (the number declined as some were recalled, notably most Defense Department civilians under the Pay Our Military Act). National parks, Smithsonian museums, and monuments closed — leading to confrontations over barricades at open-air memorials in Washington. Processing of some benefits, permits, food-safety inspections, and research grants slowed or stopped. Standard & Poor's estimated the shutdown took $24 billion out of the economy; the Office of Management and Budget estimated 6.6 million lost workdays among furloughed staff, who received back pay after the shutdown ended.

Resolution

On 16 October, with a debt-ceiling deadline also approaching, Senate leaders Mitch McConnell and Harry Reid negotiated a compromise: the Continuing Appropriations Act, 2014 funded the government through 15 January 2014, suspended the debt limit through 7 February, and included only a minor change to the health law (income verification for subsidies). The Senate passed it 81–18 and the House 285–144; President Barack Obama signed it shortly after midnight, and employees returned to work on 17 October. Polls showed the public blamed Republicans more than Democrats, and Republican approval ratings fell sharply, though the party gained Senate control in the 2014 midterms regardless.
