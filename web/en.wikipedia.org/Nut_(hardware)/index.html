Nut (hardware)

TypeFastener
Used withBolt, screw
MaterialsSteel, brass, stainless steel, nylon
StandardsISO 4032, DIN 934, ASME B18.2.2

A nut is a type of fastener with a threaded hole. Nuts are almost always used in conjunction with a mating bolt to fasten multiple parts together: the bolt passes through the parts, and the nut is screwed onto it so that the assembly is clamped by the tension in the bolt and the friction between the threads. The most common shape is the hexagonal hex nut, whose six flats give a wrench good purchase at a wide range of angles.

Types

Common types include hex nuts; lock nuts and nyloc nuts, which resist loosening under vibration; wing nuts, tightened by hand; cap (acorn) nuts, which cover the exposed bolt end; flange nuts with an integral washer face; square nuts; T-nuts and cage nuts for captive mounting; castle nuts used with a cotter pin; and lug nuts, which secure vehicle wheels. Nuts are classified by thread size and pitch (metric or imperial), strength grade, and material.

History and manufacture

Threaded fasteners date to antiquity, but nuts and bolts became cheap and truly interchangeable only with 19th-century standardisation — notably Joseph Whitworth's British Standard Whitworth thread (1841) — and with mass-production machinery. Modern nuts are typically cold-forged from steel wire, then tapped to cut or form the internal thread, and may be plated or galvanised for corrosion resistance.
