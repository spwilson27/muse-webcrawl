Beekeeping (or apiculture) is the maintenance of bee colonies, commonly in man-made beehives, by humans. A beekeeper (or apiarist) keeps bees in order to collect their honey and other products of the hive — beeswax, propolis, flower pollen, bee pollen and royal jelly — to pollinate crops, or to produce bees for sale to other beekeepers. A location where bees are kept is called an apiary or "bee yard".

History

Humans have harvested honey from wild bees for at least 8,000 years, as shown by Mesolithic rock art in Spain. Organised beekeeping began in ancient Egypt and spread through the Mediterranean world, using clay pots, logs and woven skeps. Destroying the colony to harvest honey was standard until the 19th century, when the American clergyman Lorenzo Langstroth patented (1852) a hive built around the principle of "bee space", with movable frames that could be removed without harming the bees — the basis of nearly all modern hives. The centrifugal honey extractor (1865) and wax foundation followed, industrialising the craft.

The modern hive and colony

A colony consists of a single queen, tens of thousands of sterile female workers, and, in summer, a few hundred male drones. In a Langstroth hive, stacked boxes ("supers") hold frames in which the bees build comb: the lower boxes form the brood chamber, while upper supers store surplus honey. The beekeeper inspects frames regularly, manages swarming, treats disease and adds or removes supers according to the nectar flow.

Threats to bees

Modern beekeeping is challenged by the parasitic mite Varroa destructor, viruses it transmits, bacterial diseases such as American foulbrood, pesticide exposure, loss of forage, and the poorly understood syndrome called colony collapse disorder, which caused heavy losses in North America and Europe from 2006 onward.

Scale and pollination

There are an estimated 80–100 million managed hives worldwide. Beyond honey production (about 1.8 million tonnes a year), managed bees provide pollination services of enormous agricultural value; in the United States, migratory beekeepers truck millions of colonies to pollinate almonds, apples, berries and other crops.
