Oatmeal

Oatmeal is a preparation of oats that have been dehusked, steamed, and flattened, or a coarse flour of hulled oat grains (groats) that have been milled or steel-cut. Ground oats are also called white oats. Steel-cut oats are known as coarse oatmeal, Irish oatmeal, or pinhead oats. Rolled oats were traditionally thick oat groats, but can be made thinner to cook faster. In Scotland, oatmeal cooked in water is porridge.

Preparation

Oatmeal is cooked in water or milk to make a porridge, often eaten at breakfast with sweeteners, fruit, or nuts. Overnight oats are soaked without cooking. Oats are also used in baking, granola, muesli, oatcakes, and oat milk.

Nutrition and health

Oats are rich in the soluble fibre beta-glucan, which has been shown to help lower LDL cholesterol; regulators in the United States and Europe permit health claims linking oat beta-glucan to reduced risk of coronary heart disease. Oats also provide manganese, phosphorus, magnesium, and the antioxidant avenanthramides.

History

Oats have been cultivated since the Bronze Age and were a staple in Scotland, where the cool climate suited them better than wheat. Oatmeal became a mass-market breakfast food in the 19th and 20th centuries with the spread of rolled oats and instant products.
