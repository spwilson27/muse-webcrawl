Ether Dome
LocationMassachusetts General Hospital, Boston
Built1821 (Bulfinch Building)
Famous eventFirst public demonstration of ether anaesthesia, 16 October 1846

The Ether Dome is a surgical operating amphitheatre in the Bulfinch Building at Massachusetts General Hospital in Boston. It is famous as the site, on 16 October 1846, of the first successful public demonstration of the use of inhaled ether as a surgical anaesthetic.

Ether Day

Dentist William T. G. Morton administered ether to patient Edward Gilbert Abbott while surgeon John Collins Warren removed a tumour from Abbott's neck. The patient reported feeling no pain, prompting Warren's celebrated remark, "Gentlemen, this is no humbug." News of the demonstration spread rapidly and transformed surgery worldwide.

The room today

The amphitheatre served as an operating room until 1867. It later held stores and even a dormitory before being restored as a museum and teaching space. It contains a famous painting of the 1846 operation, medical artefacts, and an Egyptian mummy that has been part of the hospital's collection since 1823. The Ether Dome was designated a National Historic Landmark.
