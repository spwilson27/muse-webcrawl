Overhead line

An overhead line or overhead wires is an electrical cable used to transmit electrical energy to electric locomotives, trams, trolleybuses or trains. On railways the system is often called a catenary, after the curve of the supporting wire.

Construction

A contact wire is suspended above the track from a messenger wire by droppers, keeping it level as the vehicle's pantograph slides along it. Registration assemblies hold the wire in a slight zigzag over the pantograph to even out wear. Systems are sectioned and fed from substations at intervals.

Voltages

Common systems include 1,500 V and 3,000 V DC, and 15 kV and 25 kV AC; 25 kV AC is standard for new high-speed and main lines. Alternatives to overhead supply include third rail and ground-level power supply.
