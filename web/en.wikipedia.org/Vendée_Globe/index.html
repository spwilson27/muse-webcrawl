Vendée Globe

First held: 1989–90
Frequency: Every 4 years
Start/finish: Les Sables-d'Olonne, France
Class: IMOCA 60
Format: Single-handed, non-stop, unassisted circumnavigation

The Vendée Globe is a single-handed (solo), non-stop, unassisted round-the-world yacht race that takes place every four years, starting and finishing at Les Sables-d'Olonne in the Vendée department of France. Founded by Philippe Jeantot, the first race was held in 1989–90 and won by Titouan Lamazou. It is widely regarded as the most demanding event in ocean racing and is nicknamed the "Everest of the seas".

Course and rules

Competitors sail IMOCA 60 monohulls eastward around the world, rounding the three great capes — Good Hope, Leeuwin and Horn — and crossing the Southern Ocean, without stopping or receiving outside assistance. The theoretical course is about 24,300 nautical miles; winning times have fallen from over 109 days in the first edition to under 75 days in 2016–17 (Armel Le Cléac'h) and 64 days in 2024–25 (Charlie Dalin).

History

The race grew out of the 1968 Sunday Times Golden Globe Race. Notable editions include Ellen MacArthur's second place in 2000–01 and the 1996–97 race, in which Pete Goss rescued fellow competitor Raphaël Dinelli. Attrition is high: typically only about half the starters finish.
