Boat launch

A boat launch, also known as a boat ramp or launch ramp, is a ramp on the shore by which boats can be moved to and from the water. They are used for launching and retrieving boats on trailers towed by automobiles, and are typically made of concrete, asphalt, or gravel, sloping down into the water at a shallow angle.

Use

To launch a boat, the driver backs the trailer down the ramp until the boat floats free, then moves the boat to a dock or shore while the vehicle and trailer are parked nearby. Retrieval reverses the process: the trailer is submerged to the proper depth and the boat is driven or winched onto it. Ramps may include courtesy docks, staging areas, and parking lots for tow vehicles and trailers.

Design and access

Public boat launches are common at lakes, rivers, reservoirs, and coastal marinas, and are often maintained by parks agencies or municipalities. Typical ramp slopes range around 12 to 15 percent. In tidal waters, ramp length must accommodate low-tide launching. Facilities may charge launch fees and impose rules on parking, fish cleaning, and invasive species inspection of boats and trailers.
