Hexapla

EditorOrigen of Alexandria
Datec. 240s CE
ContentsSix parallel versions of the Old Testament
SurvivalFragments only; copies at Caesarea

Hexapla (Greek: Ἑξαπλᾶ, "sixfold") is the great comparative edition of the Old Testament compiled by the Christian scholar Origen of Alexandria around the 240s CE. It arranged six versions of the text side by side in parallel columns, making it the most ambitious work of biblical textual criticism in antiquity.

The six columns

The columns presented, in order: (1) the Hebrew text; (2) the Hebrew transliterated into Greek letters; (3) the Greek translation of Aquila; (4) the Greek translation of Symmachus; (5) the Septuagint, revised by Origen; and (6) the Greek translation of Theodotion. For some books, additional Greek versions raised the total to eight or nine columns (in which case the work is sometimes called the Octapla or Enneapla).

Method and signs

In the Septuagint column Origen used critical signs borrowed from Alexandrian Homeric scholarship: the obelus marked words present in Greek but not Hebrew, and the asterisk marked material supplied from other Greek versions to match the Hebrew. His goal was to give the church a reliable text for debate and study without discarding the traditional Septuagint.

Fate and influence

The complete work, amounting to thousands of pages, was never copied in full; the original was kept at Caesarea, where Pamphilus and Eusebius consulted it, and it was reportedly destroyed when Caesarea fell to Arab armies in the seventh century. The revised Septuagint column circulated widely and influenced later biblical manuscripts; fragments of the Hexapla survive in palimpsests and in marginal notes of later copies.
