MDMA

IUPAC name1-(1,3-benzodioxol-5-yl)-N-methylpropan-2-amine
FormulaC11H15NO2
Molar mass193.25 g/mol
Legal statusSchedule I (US); Class A (UK); prohibited internationally
RoutesOral, insufflation

3,4-Methylenedioxymethamphetamine (MDMA), commonly known as ecstasy (tablet form) or molly (crystal form), is an entactogen with stimulant and minor psychedelic properties. It increases the release and inhibits the reuptake of the neurotransmitters serotonin, dopamine, and norepinephrine in the brain.

Effects

MDMA produces feelings of euphoria, empathy, emotional openness, and increased energy, along with altered sensory perception. Adverse effects can include hyperthermia, dehydration, bruxism, and, with heavy or repeated use, neurotoxicity and serotonin depletion.

History

MDMA was first synthesized in 1912 by the German pharmaceutical company Merck. It was used experimentally as an adjunct to psychotherapy in the 1970s and early 1980s before spreading to recreational use in dance clubs and raves. It was placed in Schedule I of the U.S. Controlled Substances Act in 1985. In recent years, clinical trials have investigated MDMA-assisted therapy for post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD).
