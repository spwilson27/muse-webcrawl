Exothermic reaction

An exothermic reaction is a chemical reaction that releases energy by light or heat. It is the opposite of an endothermic reaction. Expressed in a chemical equation: reactants → products + energy. In an exothermic reaction, the change in enthalpy (ΔH) is negative, because energy required to break bonds in the reactants is less than the energy released when new bonds form in the products.

Examples

Examples include combustion (burning of fuels), the thermite reaction, neutralisation of acids by alkalis, cellular respiration, the setting of cement, condensation and freezing of liquids, and many oxidation reactions such as the rusting of iron. Nuclear fission and nuclear fusion are also exothermic.

Measurement

Energy changes in exothermic reactions are measured by calorimetry; the heat released at constant pressure equals the enthalpy change. Values are commonly reported in kilojoules per mole of reactant.
