Startup accelerator

Startup accelerators, also known as seed accelerators, are fixed-term, cohort-based programs that provide early-stage companies with mentorship, education, workspace, and seed funding, usually in exchange for equity.

History and model

The model was pioneered by Y Combinator, founded in 2005 by Paul Graham, which funded companies such as Airbnb and Dropbox. Techstars and many corporate and university accelerators followed. Programs typically run about three months and culminate in a "demo day" at which startups pitch to investors.

Distinction from incubators

Accelerators differ from business incubators in being time-limited, cohort-based, and investment-oriented, whereas incubators often provide longer-term space and services without a fixed end date.
