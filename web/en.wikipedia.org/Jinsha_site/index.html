Jinsha site

Jinsha (Chinese: 金沙; pinyin: Jīnshā) is an archaeological site in Chengdu, Sichuan, China. Discovered by chance in February 2001 during road construction, the site dates to about 1200–650 BCE and is associated with the ancient Shu kingdom, closely related to and probably succeeding the culture represented at Sanxingdui.

Finds

Excavations uncovered a sacrificial area with more than a thousand ritual objects of gold, bronze, jade, stone, and ivory, including elephant tusks, a gold mask, jade blades, and the celebrated gold foil "Sun and Immortal Birds" ornament, whose design was adopted as the symbol of Chinese cultural heritage and of the city of Chengdu. Unlike Sanxingdui, Jinsha has yielded no massive bronze statues or city walls, but house foundations, kilns, tombs, and pottery show it was a major political and religious centre of Bronze Age Sichuan.

Museum

The Jinsha Site Museum, opened in 2007, preserves the excavation area in situ and displays the principal finds.
