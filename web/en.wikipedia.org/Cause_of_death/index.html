Cause of death

In law, medicine, and statistics, cause of death is a determination of the specific disease or injury that results in a person's death, recorded on a death certificate. It is distinguished from the manner of death (natural, accident, suicide, homicide, or undetermined) and from the physiological mechanism of death (such as cardiac arrest).

Certification

A medical practitioner, coroner, or medical examiner certifies the cause of death after examination and investigation. The international standard format, promoted by the World Health Organization, lists the immediate cause of death followed by the chain of antecedent conditions, ending with the underlying cause that initiated the fatal sequence.

Statistics

Coded cause-of-death data, classified under the International Classification of Diseases (ICD), underpin national mortality statistics and global health research. Globally, the leading causes of death are cardiovascular diseases, cancers, and respiratory diseases, though rankings vary widely by income level and region. Studies comparing death certificates with autopsy findings show significant rates of inaccurate certification, which complicates statistical use.

Legal significance

The stated cause and manner of death can determine criminal liability, insurance outcomes, and inheritance matters, making accurate certification a significant medico-legal function.
