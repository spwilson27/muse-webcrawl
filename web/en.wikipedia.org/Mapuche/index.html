Mapuche

Regions: South-central Chile and southwestern Argentina
Language: Mapudungun
Population: Over 1.5 million in Chile (with communities in Argentina)
Historical territory: Wallmapu

The Mapuche are an Indigenous people of south-central Chile and southwestern Argentina, including parts of Patagonia. Their traditional territory, Wallmapu, stretches between the Pacific and the Andes roughly from the Biobío River southward. They speak Mapudungun and form the largest Indigenous group in Chile.

History

The Mapuche resisted first Inca expansion and then, from the 16th century, Spanish colonisation in the long Arauco War, adopting horses and new tactics and maintaining independence south of the Biobío for some three centuries — a resistance celebrated in the Spanish epic La Araucana. Only in the 1860s–1880s did the Chilean state's "Pacification of Araucanía" and Argentine campaigns incorporate Mapuche territory, confining communities to reductions (reservations) and opening their land to settlers.

Culture

Traditional Mapuche society was organised in local communities (lof) led by lonkos, with machi shamans central to ritual life, including the machitún healing ceremony and the nguillatún communal prayer gathering. Weaving, silverwork and the game of palín are notable crafts and traditions, and the wenufoye flag has become a symbol of Mapuche identity.

Contemporary issues

Land restitution, forestry plantations on claimed ancestral land, language revitalisation and constitutional recognition remain live political questions in Chile and Argentina, with movements ranging from cultural activism to, in some areas, direct land conflict.
