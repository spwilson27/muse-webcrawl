The ringed seal (Pusa hispida) is an earless seal inhabiting the Arctic and sub-Arctic regions of the Northern Hemisphere. It is the smallest and most abundant seal of the Arctic, named for the light-coloured rings on its dark grey coat. Adults average about 1.5 m (5 ft) in length and 50–70 kg (110–150 lb) in weight.

Habitat and behaviour

Ringed seals are associated with sea ice throughout their range. Females excavate lairs in snow drifts over land-fast ice, where pups are born in March or April and sheltered from polar bears, their chief predator. Seals maintain breathing holes in the ice by scratching with the claws of their foreflippers.

Subspecies and freshwater populations

Recognised subspecies include the Arctic, Baltic and Okhotsk populations, together with two freshwater subspecies: the Ladoga seal of Lake Ladoga and the Saimaa ringed seal of Lake Saimaa in Finland, the latter endangered with only a few hundred individuals.

Threats

Climate-driven loss of sea ice and adequate spring snow cover is the principal long-term threat, since pups depend on stable ice and snow lairs for survival.
