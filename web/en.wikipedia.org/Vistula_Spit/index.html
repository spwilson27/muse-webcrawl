The Vistula Spit (Polish: Mierzeja Wiślana; Russian: Балтийская коса, Baltiyskaya kosa; German: Frische Nehrung; Kashubian: Szkarpawa) is an aeolian-sand peninsula, or spit, that separates Vistula Lagoon from Gdańsk Bay in the Baltic Sea, with its tip separated from the mainland by the Strait of Baltiysk. The border between Poland (Pomeranian Voivodeship) and Kaliningrad Oblast, a semi-exclave of Russia, runs across the spit, splitting it politically.

Geography

The spit stretches roughly 65 kilometres northeast from the Gdańsk area, narrowing in places to only a few hundred metres, with sand dunes rising to about 25–30 metres at their highest points (such as the Camel's Hump near Krynica Morska). It is covered largely by pine forest and is a protected landscape area on the Polish side. Rivers of the Vistula delta, including the Nogat and Szkarpawa, discharge into the lagoon behind the spit.

History

Historically part of East Prussia, the spit became divided in 1945 under the Potsdam settlement, with the northeastern portion passing to the Soviet Union. The Polish side developed small fishing and resort villages, of which Krynica Morska is the largest. During the Second World War the spit's northern end saw heavy fighting during the East Prussian offensive of 1945.

Vistula Spit canal

To give Poland shipping access from the port of Elbląg to the Baltic independent of the Russian-controlled Strait of Baltiysk, Poland constructed a canal across the Polish section of the spit near Skowronki. The canal, about 1.3 km long with a lock and swing bridges, was officially opened on 17 September 2022.
