Georges Dumézil (4 March 1898 – 11 October 1986) was a French philologist and historian of religion, best known for his comparative studies of Indo-European mythology and his trifunctional hypothesis.

Career

Educated at the École Normale Supérieure, Dumézil taught in Istanbul and Uppsala before returning to Paris. From 1949 to 1968 he held the chair of Indo-European civilisation at the Collège de France. He mastered an extraordinary range of languages, including the Ubykh and other Caucasian languages whose oral traditions he recorded.

Trifunctional hypothesis

Dumézil argued that Proto-Indo-European society and religion were organised around three functions: sovereignty (priests and rulers), military force (warriors), and fertility and production (farmers and herders). He traced this pattern through Indian, Iranian, Roman, Germanic and Celtic myth and epic, in works such as the three-volume Mythe et épopée (1968–1973).

Legacy

Dumézil was elected to the Académie française in 1978. His methods influenced structuralism and scholars such as Claude Lévi-Strauss, though aspects of his early political associations and the universality of his scheme have been debated.
