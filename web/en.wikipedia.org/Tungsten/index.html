Tungsten

Tungsten is a chemical element with the symbol W and atomic number 74. It is a rare, hard, steel-grey transition metal found naturally in minerals such as wolframite and scheelite. Its name comes from the Swedish tung sten, "heavy stone", while the symbol W derives from wolfram.

Properties

Tungsten has the highest melting point of all elements, 3,422 °C (6,192 °F), the lowest vapour pressure among metals, and very high density (19.25 g/cm³), comparable to gold. It retains strength at high temperatures and has the highest tensile strength of any element at elevated temperatures.

Applications

About half of tungsten production goes into cemented carbide (tungsten carbide), an extremely hard material used in cutting tools, drills, and wear parts. Other uses include light-bulb filaments, electrical contacts, X-ray tube targets, superalloys, radiation shielding, and kinetic-energy penetrators. China accounts for the large majority of world production.
