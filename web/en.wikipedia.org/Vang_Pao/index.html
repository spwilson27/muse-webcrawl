Vang Pao (1929–2011) was a Laotian Hmong military leader, a major general in the Royal Lao Army and leader of the CIA-supported Hmong forces during the Laotian civil war (the "Secret War"). He remains the most prominent figure in Hmong history and a revered, sometimes controversial, leader of the Hmong diaspora.

Early career

Born in Nong Het near the Vietnamese border, Vang Pao served as a teenage interpreter and soldier against the Japanese and then with the French during the First Indochina War. He rose rapidly in the Royal Lao Army, becoming one of its few Hmong officers, and worked with Prime Minister Souvanna Phouma's neutralist government era politics in the kingdom's complex civil conflict.

The Secret War

From 1961, the CIA recruited Vang Pao to build a clandestine army that grew to tens of thousands, headquartered at Long Tieng. Hmong forces rescued downed American airmen, interdicted the Ho Chi Minh Trail and held the Plain of Jars front at enormous cost — a large share of Hmong military-age men died. American support ended with the Paris Peace Accords and the 1973 Vientiane agreement; when the Pathet Lao took power in 1975, Vang Pao was evacuated by air to Thailand and then to the United States.

Later life

Settling in California and Minnesota, Vang Pao led exile and veterans' organisations and lobbied for Hmong refugees and recognition of Hmong veterans. In 2007 he was arrested in a federal sting over an alleged plot to overthrow the Lao government; charges against him were dropped in 2009. He died in 2011, and his multi-day funeral in Fresno drew tens of thousands. A campaign for his burial at Arlington National Cemetery was unsuccessful, but memorials honour him in several US cities.
