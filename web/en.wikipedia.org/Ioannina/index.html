Ioannina (Greek: Ιωάννινα), often called Jannena or Janina in older sources, is the capital and largest city of the Ioannina regional unit and of Epirus, an administrative region in north-western Greece. It lies on the western shore of Lake Pamvotida, at an elevation of about 480 metres, beneath the Mitsikeli mountain.

History

The city is traditionally said to have been founded by the Byzantine emperor Justinian I in the 6th century AD and named after Saint John the Baptist. It flourished in the late Byzantine period, when many aristocratic families settled there after the Fourth Crusade, and became a major centre of Greek learning during Ottoman rule. From 1788 until his fall in 1822, Ioannina was the seat of Ali Pasha of Yanina, the powerful Ottoman Albanian ruler whose court drew visitors including Lord Byron. The city was incorporated into Greece in 1913 during the Balkan Wars.

Landmarks

The old town lies within the Ioannina Castle, whose walls enclose the Its Kale citadel, the Fethiye Mosque and the tomb of Ali Pasha. The island in Lake Pamvotida — one of the few inhabited lake islands in Greece — holds monasteries and the museum marking the spot where Ali Pasha was killed in 1822. Nearby is the ancient theatre and oracle of Dodona, and the Perama Cave.

Modern city

Ioannina is a regional commercial and educational centre, home to the University of Ioannina (founded 1964), noted for its medical school, and to a traditional silversmithing craft. The city's rainy climate — among the wettest in Greece — contrasts with the popular image of the country.
