Papal election, 1268–1271

The papal election of 1268–1271, held in Viterbo following the death of Pope Clement IV, was the longest papal election in the history of the Roman Catholic Church, lasting two years and nine months.

Deadlock

The cardinals, split between French and Italian factions, could not reach agreement. In frustration, the people of Viterbo locked the electors in the episcopal palace, removed its roof, and eventually reduced their rations to bread and water.

Outcome and legacy

The election finally chose a non-cardinal, Teobaldo Visconti, as Pope Gregory X. In response he promulgated the decree Ubi periculum (1274), formalising the conclave system that still governs papal elections.
