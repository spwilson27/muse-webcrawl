Scotland national rugby union team

Scotland playing at Murrayfield Stadium, Edinburgh.

The Scotland national rugby union team represents Scotland in men's international rugby union. The team is administered by Scottish Rugby, the governing body for the sport in Scotland, and plays its home matches at Murrayfield Stadium in Edinburgh. Scotland competes annually in the Six Nations Championship and every four years in the Rugby World Cup.

History

Scotland played in the first-ever international rugby match, defeating England in Edinburgh on 27 March 1871. The team contests the Calcutta Cup with England, first played in 1879. Scotland won Grand Slams in the Five Nations in 1925, 1984, and 1990, the last sealed by a famous victory over England at Murrayfield. In the Six Nations era Scotland has not won the championship but has recorded notable victories, including retaining the Calcutta Cup for several consecutive years in the early 2020s.

Rugby World Cup

Scotland has competed in every Rugby World Cup. Its best result came in 1991, when the team reached the semi-finals, losing to England, and then lost the third-place play-off to New Zealand.

Identity

The team's traditional strip is navy blue jerseys with white shorts, and its emblem is the thistle, the national flower of Scotland. The anthem "Flower of Scotland" is sung before home matches.
