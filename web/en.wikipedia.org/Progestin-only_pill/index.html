Progestin-only pill

Progestogen-only pills or progestin-only pills (POPs) are oral contraceptive pills that contain only synthetic progestogens (progestins) and do not contain oestrogen. They are colloquially known as mini pills. Common formulations contain norethisterone, levonorgestrel, desogestrel or drospirenone.

Mechanism and use

POPs work primarily by thickening cervical mucus to block sperm, and by thinning the endometrium; newer desogestrel- and drospirenone-containing pills also reliably suppress ovulation. Traditional mini pills must be taken within a strict three-hour window each day, whereas desogestrel and drospirenone pills allow a 12- or 24-hour window. They are taken continuously without a pill-free break.

Advantages and side effects

Because they lack oestrogen, POPs are suitable for people who cannot use combined pills, including those who are breastfeeding or have a history of venous thromboembolism or migraine with aura. The most common side effect is irregular bleeding; other effects can include acne and breast tenderness. With perfect use they are more than 99% effective.
