Acadia National Park

Location: Maine, United States
Area: 49,075 acres (198.6 km²)
Established: February 26, 1919
Governing body: National Park Service

Acadia National Park is a national park of the United States located along the mid-section of the Maine coast, southwest of Bar Harbor. The park includes about half of Mount Desert Island, part of the Isle au Haut, the tip of the Schoodic Peninsula, and portions of 16 smaller outlying islands. It preserves a landscape of granite mountains, woodlands, lakes, ponds, and rugged Atlantic shoreline.

Acadia is the oldest national park east of the Mississippi River. It was established as Sieur de Monts National Monument in 1916, became Lafayette National Park in 1919, and was renamed Acadia in 1929. Much of its land was donated by wealthy summer residents, most prominently John D. Rockefeller Jr., who also financed the park's famous system of carriage roads.

Geography

The park's highest point is Cadillac Mountain, at 1,530 feet (466 m) the tallest mountain on the U.S. Atlantic coast. From October to March, its summit is among the first places in the country to see the sunrise. The Somes Sound, a deep fjord-like inlet, nearly bisects Mount Desert Island.

Features

Acadia is noted for its 45 miles (72 km) of historic carriage roads, closed to motor vehicles and popular with walkers, cyclists, and horse-drawn carriages. The 27-mile (43 km) Park Loop Road gives access to landmarks such as Otter Cliff, Thunder Hole, Jordan Pond, and Sand Beach. Bass Harbor Head Light, a picturesque lighthouse at the island's southwest tip, is one of the most photographed sites in the park.

Flora and fauna

The park lies in a transition zone between northern coniferous and southern deciduous forests, supporting an unusual diversity of plant life. Wildlife includes white-tailed deer, moose, black bears, beavers, peregrine falcons, and harbour seals offshore.
