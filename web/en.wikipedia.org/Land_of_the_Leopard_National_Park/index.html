Land of the Leopard National Park

Land of the Leopard National Park (Russian: Национальный парк «Земля леопарда») is a national park in Primorsky Krai in the Russian Far East, established on 5 April 2012. It protects habitat for the critically endangered Amur leopard and also supports Amur tigers.

Land of the Leopard National Park
Location: Primorsky Krai, Russia
Nearest city: Vladivostok
Area: about 2,799 km² (with additions)
Established: 5 April 2012

Geography

The park stretches along Russia's borders with China and North Korea, south-west of Vladivostok, encompassing the Kedrovaya Pad Nature Reserve and other protected areas. Its forests are Manchurian mixed forests of Korean pine and broadleaf species, unusually rich in wildlife for a temperate region.

Wildlife

Camera-trap surveys credited the park's creation and anti-poaching enforcement with growth in the wild Amur leopard population, which rose from around 30 animals in the region in the early 2000s to roughly 100 or more in Russia and China combined by the late 2010s. The park also protects Siberian (Amur) tigers, Asiatic black bears, brown bears and ungulates such as sika and roe deer.

Management

The park is administered together with Kedrovaya Pad under a joint directorate. An eco-tourism programme includes camera-trap "adoptions" and naming of individual leopards by their guardians.
