Wireless power transfer

Wireless power transfer (WPT), wireless power transmission, wireless energy transmission (WET), or electromagnetic power transfer is the transmission of electrical energy without wires as a physical link. In a wireless power transmission system, an electrically powered transmitter device generates a time-varying electromagnetic field that transmits power across space to one or more receivers.

Near-field techniques

Inductive coupling, used in electric toothbrush chargers and smartphone charging pads (such as the Qi standard), transfers power over short distances through magnetic fields between two coils. Resonant inductive coupling adds capacitors to tune the coils to a common resonant frequency, increasing range and efficiency.

Far-field techniques

Far-field or radiative methods, including microwave and laser beaming, can transfer power over longer distances but must be aimed at the receiver. They have been proposed for solar power satellites and for powering drones and remote sensors.

History

Nikola Tesla experimented with wireless power transmission in the 1890s, culminating in the Wardenclyffe Tower project. Practical commercial adoption began in the 2000s with inductive charging for consumer electronics.
