Aden is a port city in Yemen, located by the eastern approach to the Red Sea (the Gulf of Aden), some 170 km east of the Bab el-Mandeb strait. Its natural harbour lies in the crater of a dormant volcano. Aden has been an important trading port since antiquity, serving as a key entrepôt between the Red Sea, the Persian Gulf, India, and East Africa.

History

The British East India Company seized Aden in 1839, and it became a coaling station and garrison guarding the sea route to India, especially after the opening of the Suez Canal in 1869. Aden was a British colony from 1937 until 1967. It then became the capital of South Yemen (the People's Democratic Republic of Yemen) until unification with North Yemen in 1990. Since 2015 it has served as the interim seat of Yemen's internationally recognised government.

Geography and economy

The city is built on a peninsula connected to the mainland by a narrow isthmus. Its districts include Crater, Ma'alla, Tawahi (the old port quarter, once called Steamer Point), and Sheikh Othman. The port and a major oil refinery at Little Aden underpin the economy, though the civil war has severely disrupted activity.
