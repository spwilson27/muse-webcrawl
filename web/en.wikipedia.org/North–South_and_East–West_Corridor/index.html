The North–South and East–West Corridor is a major highway network in India, comprising the North–South Corridor from Srinagar in the north to Kanyakumari in the south, and the East–West Corridor from Silchar in Assam to Porbandar in Gujarat. Together they form one of the largest highway projects undertaken in the country, developed under the National Highways Development Project by the National Highways Authority of India.

Routes

The North–South Corridor runs roughly 4,076 km, largely along National Highway 44, passing through Jammu, Delhi, Agra, Jhansi, Hyderabad, Bengaluru, Madurai, and on to Kanyakumari at the tip of the Indian peninsula, with a spur to Kochi. The East–West Corridor runs about 3,640 km, largely along NH 27, passing through Guwahati, Siliguri, Muzaffarpur, Kanpur, Jhansi, Udaipur, and Rajkot to Porbandar. The two corridors intersect near Jhansi in Uttar Pradesh.

Development

The corridors were implemented mainly under Phases I and II of the NHDP, with most stretches upgraded to four-lane divided highways. Construction proceeded section by section through the 2000s and 2010s, and the project substantially reduced travel times for long-distance road freight across India, complementing the Golden Quadrilateral connecting Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, and Kolkata.
