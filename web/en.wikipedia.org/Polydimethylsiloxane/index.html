Polydimethylsiloxane

Polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) is a polymeric organosilicon compound that is commonly referred to as dimethylpolysiloxane or dimethicone. It is the most widely used silicon-based organic polymer, particularly known for its unusual rheological (or flow) properties. PDMS is optically clear, generally considered inert, non-toxic and non-flammable.

Structure

The chemical formula for PDMS is CH3[Si(CH3)2O]nSi(CH3)3, where n is the number of repeating monomer units. The backbone consists of alternating silicon and oxygen atoms, with two methyl groups attached to each silicon. The Si–O bond is flexible, giving the polymer a low glass transition temperature (about −125 °C) and high gas permeability.

Properties

PDMS is viscoelastic: at low temperatures or high frequencies it behaves like an elastic solid, while at high temperatures or long flow times it acts like a viscous liquid or honey. It is hydrophobic, has good thermal stability, and is resistant to oxidation. Its surface can be oxidized, for example by oxygen plasma treatment, to create silanol groups that render the surface temporarily hydrophilic and allow bonding to glass and other PDMS layers.

Applications

PDMS is used in a very wide range of products. As silicone oil it is found in lubricants, hydraulic fluids, cosmetics, shampoos (as dimethicone), and food additives (as an antifoaming agent, E900). Elastomeric PDMS is used in silicone rubber, sealants, and medical devices.

In research, PDMS is the standard material for soft lithography and microfluidics: it can be cast against a master mould to replicate features down to nanometre scale, and replica-moulded PDMS devices are widely used in labs-on-a-chip, cell culture, and micro-total-analysis systems. It is also the polymer from which many contact lenses and medical implants derive their elasticity.
