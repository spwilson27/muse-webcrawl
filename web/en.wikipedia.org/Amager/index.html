Amager

Amager (Danish pronunciation: [ˈɑˌmɑˀ]) is a Danish island in the Øresund, located just south of Copenhagen, to which it is connected by several bridges and tunnels. With an area of about 96 km² it is Denmark's most densely populated island, home to more than 200,000 people. The island hosts Copenhagen Airport, Denmark's largest, at Kastrup, and the Danish end of the Øresund Bridge to Sweden.

History

In the 16th century King Christian II invited Dutch farmers to settle on Amager to supply the royal court with vegetables; their descendants long preserved Dutch customs around Store Magleby, and the island became known as Copenhagen's vegetable garden. The historic fishing town of Dragør, with its preserved yellow-painted houses, lies on the island's east coast.

Modern Amager

The northern part of the island forms part of the city of Copenhagen, including the districts of Amager Øst and Amager Vest, the new Ørestad development along the Copenhagen Metro, and the University of Copenhagen's South Campus. West Amager includes Kalvebod Fælled, a large reclaimed nature area, and Amager Strandpark, an artificial beach lagoon popular with residents. The municipalities on the island are Tårnby and Dragør, together with the Copenhagen portion.
