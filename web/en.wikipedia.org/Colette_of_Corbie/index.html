Colette of Corbie

Saint Colette of Corbie (13 January 1381 – 6 March 1447), born Nicole Boellet, was a French abbess and the foundress of the Colettine Poor Clares, a reform branch of the Order of Saint Clare. She is honoured as a saint in the Roman Catholic Church, with her feast day on 6 March.

Early life

Colette was born in Corbie, Picardy, to a carpenter at the local Benedictine abbey. Orphaned at seventeen, she gave away her possessions and successively tried life as a beguine, a Benedictine oblate, and an anchoress enclosed in a cell attached to a church, where she lived for several years receiving visitors and counsel-seekers.

Reform of the Poor Clares

Believing herself called to restore the strict observance of the Rule of Saint Clare, Colette obtained authorization from Benedict XIII of the Avignon obedience in 1406. After an initial failure at Besançon, she founded a convent at Besançon successfully in 1410, and went on to found or reform some seventeen monasteries in France and Flanders, emphasizing poverty, prayer, and enclosure. Her constitutions were approved in 1434. She also influenced the Franciscan friars' reform and corresponded with figures such as Saint Vincent Ferrer, who supported her work.

Death and legacy

Colette died at Ghent in 1447. She was beatified in 1623 and canonized in 1807. The Colettine Poor Clares continue today in monasteries in Europe and elsewhere, and she is invoked as a patroness of women seeking to conceive and of unborn children.
