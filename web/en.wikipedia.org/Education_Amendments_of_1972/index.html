Education Amendments of 1972

The Education Amendments of 1972 (Public Law 92-318) are a United States federal statute, enacted on 23 June 1972, that amended the Higher Education Act of 1965 and related laws. The act is best known for its Title IX, which prohibits sex discrimination in federally funded education programmes, but it also reshaped federal student aid and created new institutions.

President Richard Nixon signs the amendments into law

Title IX

Title IX provides: "No person in the United States shall, on the basis of sex, be excluded from participation in, be denied the benefits of, or be subjected to discrimination under any education program or activity receiving Federal financial assistance." Championed by Senator Birch Bayh and Representative Patsy Mink, its most visible effect was a dramatic expansion of women's athletics in schools and universities, but it also governs admissions, employment, and institutional responses to sexual harassment and assault. See Title IX.

Student aid provisions

The amendments created the Basic Educational Opportunity Grant — renamed the Pell Grant in 1980 — which became the cornerstone of need-based federal student aid, and established the Student Loan Marketing Association (Sallie Mae) to support the federal student loan market. They also authorised aid to developing institutions, including historically black colleges and universities.

Other titles

Title X of the act authorised the creation of the National Institute of Education and provided for community colleges and occupational education programmes. The statute formed part of a broader 1970s expansion of federal involvement in American higher education.
