Cysteine (symbol Cys or C) is a semiessential proteinogenic amino acid with the formula HS−CH2−CH(NH2)−COOH. It is distinguished by its thiol (sulfhydryl) side chain, which makes it chemically reactive and structurally important. It is encoded by the codons UGU and UGC.

Biological roles

The thiol group of cysteine is nucleophilic and readily oxidised; two cysteine residues can form a disulfide bond, creating the dimer cystine — a key stabiliser of protein tertiary structure, especially in secreted and extracellular proteins. Cysteine is a precursor of the antioxidant glutathione and of the amino acid taurine. In enzymes, cysteine residues frequently serve as catalytic nucleophiles (as in cysteine proteases) and as ligands for metal cofactors such as iron–sulfur clusters.

Sources and uses

Cysteine is synthesised in humans from methionine via the transsulfuration pathway, and is found in protein-rich foods. Industrially, L-cysteine is used as a food additive (E920, formerly a dough conditioner) and in cosmetics; its derivative N-acetylcysteine is used medically as a mucolytic and as an antidote to paracetamol (acetaminophen) poisoning.
