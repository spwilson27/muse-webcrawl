Jacob Bjerknes

Jacob Aall Bonnevie Bjerknes (1897 – 1975) was a Norwegian-American meteorologist, son of Vilhelm Bjerknes, who co-developed the polar front theory of cyclones and later made foundational contributions to understanding El Niño and the Southern Oscillation.

Born2 November 1897, Stockholm, Sweden
Died7 July 1975, Los Angeles, California
FieldsMeteorology, oceanography
InstitutionsBergen School; UCLA

Early work

Working with his father and Halvor Solberg at the Bergen School of Meteorology in Norway, the young Bjerknes helped formulate the Norwegian cyclone model, describing the life cycle of mid-latitude storms along the polar front — work that transformed weather forecasting.

Later career

Emigrating to the United States in 1940, Bjerknes founded the meteorology department at UCLA and trained wartime forecasters. In the 1960s he connected sea-surface temperature anomalies in the equatorial Pacific (El Niño) with Gilbert Walker's Southern Oscillation, proposing the coupled ocean–atmosphere feedback now known as the Bjerknes feedback.
