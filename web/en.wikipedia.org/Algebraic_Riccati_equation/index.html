Algebraic Riccati equation

An algebraic Riccati equation is a type of nonlinear equation that arises in the context of infinite-horizon optimal control problems in continuous time or discrete time. It is named after Jacopo Riccati, who studied related differential equations in the 18th century.

Continuous-time equation

The continuous-time algebraic Riccati equation (CARE) is ATX + XA − XBR−1BTX + Q = 0, where X is the unknown symmetric matrix, A is the system matrix, B the input matrix, Q a positive semi-definite state-cost weighting matrix, and R a positive definite input-cost weighting matrix.

Discrete-time equation

The discrete-time algebraic Riccati equation (DARE) arises in discrete-time linear-quadratic regulator problems and can be written X = ATXA − ATXB(R + BTXB)−1BTXA + Q.

Solution methods

Solutions are typically computed numerically using Hamiltonian matrix methods, the Schur vector approach, or iterative refinement, as implemented in control-systems software libraries. Under stabilizability and detectability conditions, a unique stabilizing positive semi-definite solution exists.

Applications

Algebraic Riccati equations are central to linear-quadratic regulator (LQR) and linear-quadratic Gaussian (LQG) control, Kalman filtering, and H-infinity control theory.
