Hove

Hove is a seaside resort town and a component part of the city of Brighton and Hove, in East Sussex, England, immediately west of Brighton. It was a small fishing village until the 19th century, when it developed alongside its more famous neighbour as a fashionable residential resort, noted for its wide Regency avenues and squares such as Brunswick Square and Adelaide Crescent.

History

Evidence of Bronze Age settlement was found in 1852 with the discovery of the Hove amber cup, one of only a handful of such Bronze Age amber vessels found in Britain, now in Hove Museum. Hove is recorded in the Domesday Book as a small agricultural settlement. Its transformation began in the 1820s with the Brunswick estate development just over the border from Brighton, and continued through the Victorian era with grand terraces, St John's and other parish churches, and Hove's own town hall.

Hove became a borough in 1898 and merged with Brighton in 1997 to form the unitary authority of Brighton and Hove, which received city status in 2000. A local joke, used when residents are asked if they live in Brighton, is to reply "Hove, actually" — a phrase that has become the town's unofficial motto.

Landmarks and sport

Landmarks include Hove Lagoon, the seafront lawns and beach huts, and Hove Park. The County Cricket Ground, Hove, is the home of Sussex County Cricket Club, and the former Goldstone Ground hosted Brighton & Hove Albion Football Club until 1997.
