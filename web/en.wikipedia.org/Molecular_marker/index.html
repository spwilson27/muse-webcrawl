Molecular marker

A molecular marker is a molecule contained within a sample taken from an organism (biological markers) or other matter. In genetics, a molecular marker is a specific DNA sequence with a known location on a chromosome that can be used to identify individuals, populations, or species, or to track the inheritance of nearby genes, because its variation (polymorphism) can be reliably detected.

Genetic markers

Common types of DNA markers include:

  • RFLP — restriction fragment length polymorphisms, among the earliest DNA markers.
  • RAPD and AFLP — PCR-based fingerprinting methods producing dominant markers.
  • Microsatellites (SSR/STR) — short tandem repeats that are highly polymorphic and codominant, widely used in forensics, paternity testing, and population genetics.
  • SNPs — single-nucleotide polymorphisms, the most abundant form of genetic variation, assayed en masse by microarrays and sequencing.
  • Allozymes — protein variants detected by electrophoresis, an earlier class of molecular markers.

Applications

Molecular markers are used to construct genetic linkage maps, in marker-assisted selection in plant and animal breeding (selecting for traits linked to a marker rather than waiting for the trait itself), in QTL mapping, in studies of genetic diversity and phylogenetics, in conservation genetics, and in human genetics for disease-gene mapping and identity testing.

Other meanings

In chemistry and biomarker research, a molecular marker (biomarker) can be a measurable molecule indicating a biological state or condition, such as a protein or metabolite used in medical diagnosis. In geology, molecular fossils (biomarkers such as hopanes and steranes) are preserved organic molecules used to infer past life and environments.
