Character (arts)


A character is a person or other being in a narrative work of art.

A character is a person or other being in a narrative (such as a novel, play, radio or television series, music, film, or video game). The character may be entirely fictional or based on a real-life person, in which case the distinction of a "fictional" versus "real" character may be made. Derived from the ancient Greek word χαρακτήρ, the English word dates from the Restoration, although it became widely used after its appearance in Tom Jones by Henry Fielding in 1749. From this, the sense of "a part played by an actor" developed.

Types of character

Characters are commonly classified by their role and development. A protagonist is the central figure whose fortunes the story follows, opposed by an antagonist. Critics following E. M. Forster distinguish flat characters, built around a single trait, from round characters, who are complex and capable of surprising the reader convincingly. Characters may further be described as static or dynamic depending on whether they change over the course of the work, and stock characters draw on instantly recognisable types such as the braggart soldier or the wise fool.

Characterisation

Characterisation is the process by which an author reveals a character's personality, through direct description, action, dialogue, thought and the reactions of others. Aristotle, in the Poetics, subordinated character (ethos) to action (mythos), but later criticism — especially of the novel — often places character at the centre of literary value.

In performance

In theatre, film and role-playing games, characters are embodied by performers; methods from Stanislavski's system onward have taught actors to construct a character's inner life. In video games and interactive fiction, characters may be controlled by the player or act as non-player characters driven by the game's systems.
