Palazzo Venezia

The Palazzo Venezia (Italian: [paˈlattso veˈnɛttsja]; English: "Venice Palace") is a palace in central Rome, Italy, just north of the Capitoline Hill, facing the Piazza Venezia. Built in the mid-15th century, it is one of the first great Renaissance palaces in Rome and is notable as the residence from whose balcony Benito Mussolini addressed crowds during the Fascist era. Today the building houses the National Museum of Palazzo Venezia.

History

The palace was begun around 1455 for the Venetian cardinal Pietro Barbo, who later became Pope Paul II, and was enlarged after his election. Incorporating the medieval tower known as the Torre della Biscia and the church of San Marco, it served as a papal residence and later as the embassy of the Republic of Venice to the Papal States, from which it takes its name. After 1797 it passed to Austria as its embassy to the Holy See, and in 1916 Italy seized the building in retaliation during the First World War.

Mussolini era

From 1929 to 1943 Palazzo Venezia was the seat of the head of government, and the Sala del Mappamondo served as Mussolini's office. He delivered many of his most famous speeches — including the declaration of the Italian Empire in 1936 and Italy's declaration of war in 1940 — from the balcony overlooking Piazza Venezia.

Museum

The Museo Nazionale del Palazzo di Venezia holds collections of Renaissance applied arts: paintings, sculptures, ceramics, weapons, tapestries, and small bronzes, as well as the library of the Italian national institute of archaeology and art history.
