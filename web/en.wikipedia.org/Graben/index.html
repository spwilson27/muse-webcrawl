Graben

A graben (from German Graben, "ditch" or "trench") is a depressed block of the crust of a planet or moon, bordered by parallel normal faults. It forms where extensional tectonics pull the crust apart: the central block drops down relative to the flanking blocks along the faults. The raised counterpart of a graben is a horst; a graben bordered on only one side by a fault is a half-graben.

Formation and examples

Grabens are characteristic of rift valleys, where continental crust is being stretched and thinned. The East African Rift contains a series of grabens occupied by long, deep lakes such as Lake Tanganyika. In Europe, the Upper Rhine Graben between Basel and Frankfurt is a major Cenozoic rift filled with sediments and bordered by the Vosges and Black Forest horsts. The Basin and Range Province of the western United States consists of alternating grabens and horsts produced by crustal extension. The Dead Sea basin and the rift occupied by the Jordan Valley are transform-related grabens (pull-apart basins).

Significance

Because grabens subside, they accumulate thick sedimentary sequences and are important sites for groundwater, geothermal resources, and hydrocarbon reservoirs; many oil and gas fields are trapped in tilted fault blocks within rift grabens. On other planets, graben systems record past extension — the canyons of Mars such as parts of Valles Marineris include graben, and lunar rilles include straight graben formed over buried dikes.
