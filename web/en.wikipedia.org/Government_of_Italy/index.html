The Government of Italy (Italian: Governo della Repubblica Italiana) is the executive branch of the Italian Republic. It is headed by the President of the Council of Ministers — generally called the prime minister — and is composed of ministers, each responsible for a ministry. Under Article 92 of the Constitution of 1948, the government consists of the president of the council and the ministers, who together form the Council of Ministers.

Formation

After elections or the fall of a government, the President of the Republic holds consultations with party leaders and the presidents of the two chambers, then appoints a prime minister-designate, who must assemble a cabinet able to win confidence votes in both the Chamber of Deputies and the Senate. Italy's "perfect bicameralism" requires confidence in both houses, making governments historically short-lived: the republic has had dozens of governments since 1946, averaging little more than a year each.

Structure and functions

The prime minister directs the general policy of the government and coordinates the ministers, but is formally primus inter pares rather than a strong executive on the Westminster model. Key ministries include the Interior, Foreign Affairs, Economy and Finance, Justice, Defence, and Economic Development. The Council of Ministers issues decree-laws in urgent cases (which Parliament must convert within 60 days) and legislative decrees under parliamentary delegation.

Instability and reform

Post-war Italian politics was dominated by Christian Democracy–led coalitions until the corruption scandals of the early 1990s collapsed the old party system. Subsequent decades saw alternating centre-left and centre-right coalitions, technocratic governments (such as those of Mario Monti and Mario Draghi), and populist-led cabinets. Proposals to strengthen the premiership — including direct election of the prime minister ("premierato") — have repeatedly been debated; a constitutional reform along those lines was advanced by the government of Giorgia Meloni, who became prime minister in October 2022.
