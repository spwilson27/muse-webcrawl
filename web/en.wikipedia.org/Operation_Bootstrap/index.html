Operation Bootstrap

Operation Bootstrap (Spanish: Operación Manos a la Obra, "Operation Hands to Work") was the name given to a series of projects which transformed the economy of Puerto Rico from an agricultural economy, based principally on sugar, into a modern industrial economy from the 1940s onward. The programme was championed by Luis Muñoz Marín, Puerto Rico's first elected governor, and implemented with the help of the economist Harvey Perloff and the development administrator Teodoro Moscoso.

Industrialisation under Operation Bootstrap reshaped Puerto Rican society.

Programmes

The Puerto Rico Industrial Development Company (PRIDCO, Spanish: Fomento) built factories and leased them to manufacturers. The Industrial Incentives Act of 1947 granted long exemptions from insular taxes, and United States federal law exempted Puerto Rico-sourced corporate income from federal tax, attracting labour-intensive industries — first apparel and textiles, later petrochemicals, electronics and pharmaceuticals. A government development bank, an investment in infrastructure, roads, electrification and mass education supported the shift.

Effects

Between the late 1940s and the 1970s, Puerto Rico's per-capita income rose sharply and the island was widely cited as a model of development. However, the programme also displaced agricultural workers, and large numbers of Puerto Ricans migrated to the mainland United States, especially New York. Critics pointed to dependence on foreign capital and tax policy; after federal tax exemptions (notably Section 936) were phased out between 1996 and 2006, much manufacturing departed and the economy entered a long recession.
