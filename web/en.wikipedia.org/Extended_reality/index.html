Extended reality

Extended reality (XR) is an umbrella term for immersive technologies that merge the physical and virtual worlds, encompassing virtual reality (VR), augmented reality (AR), and mixed reality (MR). The term draws on Paul Milgram's reality–virtuality continuum (1994), which arranges environments from the wholly real to the wholly virtual, with mixed reality covering everything in between.

Forms

Virtual reality replaces the user's surroundings with a fully simulated environment, typically through a head-mounted display. Augmented reality overlays digital content on a view of the real world, as in smartphone applications and smart glasses. Mixed reality anchors virtual objects to real spaces so that the two can interact, using spatial mapping and tracking of the user's environment. Consumer and enterprise XR hardware includes Meta's Quest headsets, Apple's Vision Pro (marketed as "spatial computing"), Microsoft's HoloLens, and a wide range of AR glasses.

Applications

XR is used in gaming and entertainment, industrial design and prototyping, remote assistance and training, medicine (surgical planning and therapy), education, architecture, and retail visualization. Challenges include display resolution and field of view, motion sickness, battery life, privacy concerns raised by always-on cameras and eye tracking, and the high cost of content production.
