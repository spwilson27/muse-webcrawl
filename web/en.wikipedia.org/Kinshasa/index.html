Kinshasa

Kinshasa, formerly Léopoldville, is the capital and largest city of the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Located on the southern bank of the Congo River opposite Brazzaville — the only place in the world where two national capitals face each other across a river — it is one of the world's fastest-growing megacities, with a metropolitan population estimated between 15 and 17 million.

History

The site was a fishing and trading settlement of the Humbu and Teke peoples when Henry Morton Stanley founded a trading station there in 1881 and named it Léopoldville after King Leopold II of Belgium. It became capital of the Belgian Congo in 1923, replacing Boma. After independence in 1960 it was renamed Kinshasa in 1966 under Mobutu Sese Seko's authenticité campaign, after a nearby village called Kinchassa.

Geography and life

Kinshasa spreads across a plain between the Congo River's Pool Malebo and surrounding hills, administratively divided into 24 communes including Gombe (the governmental and business centre), Ngaliema, and the densely populated Masina and Kimbanseke. French is the official language; Lingala is the lingua franca of the streets and of Congolese rumba music — Kinshasa is UNESCO-designated a Creative City of Music, home to artists such as Franco Luambo and Papa Wemba, and to the flamboyant sapeur fashion culture.

Transport and economy

The port of Kinshasa is the DRC's principal river port, with traffic up the Congo and Kasai rivers to Kisangani and Ilebo. Ndjili International Airport connects the city internationally; a ferry links it to Brazzaville. The city's economy is dominated by informal trade, government, services, and construction.
