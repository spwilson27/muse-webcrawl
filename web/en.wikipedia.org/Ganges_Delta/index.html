Ganges Delta

The Ganges Delta (also known as the Ganges–Brahmaputra Delta or Bengal Delta) is the world's largest river delta, formed where the Ganges, Brahmaputra and Meghna rivers empty into the Bay of Bengal. It spans roughly 110,000 km² across Bangladesh and the Indian state of West Bengal, and is home to well over one hundred million people.

Geography

The delta is a vast, low-lying plain of fertile alluvial silt criss-crossed by hundreds of distributary channels. Its seaward fringe includes the Sundarbans, the world's largest mangrove forest, habitat of the Bengal tiger. The rivers carry enormous sediment loads from the Himalayas — on the order of a billion tonnes per year — continually reshaping the coastline.

Climate and hazards

The region has a tropical monsoon climate. Because much of the delta lies within a few metres of sea level, it is exceptionally vulnerable to cyclones, storm surges and flooding: the 1970 Bhola cyclone killed hundreds of thousands. Sea-level rise, salinity intrusion and riverbank erosion are major ongoing concerns, partly offset by new land accretion from sediment.

Economy and population

Intensive rice cultivation, jute farming and fishing support one of the densest rural populations on Earth. Major cities include Kolkata near the western edge and Khulna and Barisal within the delta.
