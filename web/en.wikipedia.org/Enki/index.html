Enki (Sumerian: EN-KI, "Lord of the Earth") is the Sumerian god of water, knowledge (gestú), crafts (gašam), and creation (nudimmud), and one of the Anunnaki. He later became known as Ea in Akkadian and Babylonian mythology. His principal sanctuary was the E-abzu temple at Eridu, regarded by the Sumerians as the first city in the world, near the Persian Gulf in southern Mesopotamia.

Domains and character

Enki dwelt in the Abzu, the subterranean ocean of fresh water beneath the earth, from which springs and rivers flowed. He was the keeper of the me, the divine decrees that governed civilisation, and was portrayed as clever and benevolent — a trickster who used his wits to help gods and humans alike. His symbols were the goat-fish (suhurmašu) and streams of water flowing from his shoulders, often shown with fish swimming in them.

Myths

In the myth Enki and the World Order, Enki organises the cosmos and assigns destinies to lands and gods. In Enki and Ninhursag, set in Dilmun, he figures in a cycle of divine births. Famously, in the flood story told in the Atrahasis epic and echoed in the Epic of Gilgamesh, Enki defied the other gods' decision to destroy humanity by warning the hero (Atrahasis / Utnapishtim) to build a boat — the Mesopotamian source of the later biblical flood narrative. In Inanna and Enki, the goddess Inanna gets him drunk and carries off the me to her city of Uruk.

Creation of humanity

According to Enki and Ninmah and the Atrahasis, humans were fashioned from clay mixed with the flesh and blood of a slain god, to relieve the lesser gods of their labour. Enki devised the plan and oversaw the birth goddesses who shaped mankind.

Legacy

As Ea, Enki remained a major god through Babylonian and Assyrian religion, patron of wisdom, incantations and the arts of the exorcist. His son Marduk rose to head the Babylonian pantheon. Scholars note parallels between Ea's role as humanity's clever protector and figures in later Near Eastern traditions.
