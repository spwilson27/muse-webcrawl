Medicine

Medicine is the science and practice of caring for patients, managing the diagnosis, prognosis, prevention, treatment and palliation of their injury or disease, and promoting their health. Medicine encompasses a variety of health care practices evolved to maintain and restore health by the prevention and treatment of illness. Contemporary medicine applies biomedical sciences, biomedical research, genetics and medical technology — including medications, imaging and surgery — within systems of professional practice regulated by law and ethics.

Branches

Medicine divides broadly into basic sciences (anatomy, physiology, biochemistry, pharmacology, pathology, microbiology), clinical specialties (internal medicine and its subspecialties, surgery, paediatrics, obstetrics and gynaecology, psychiatry), and interdisciplinary fields such as radiology, emergency medicine, public health and family medicine.

Clinical practice

Clinical encounters typically proceed from history-taking and physical examination to a differential diagnosis, investigations, a working diagnosis, and a management plan made with the patient. Evidence-based medicine seeks to ground these decisions in the best available research evidence, combined with clinical expertise and patient values.

History

Healing practices predate written history. Systematic traditions developed in ancient Egypt, Mesopotamia, India (Ayurveda) and China. Hippocratic medicine in ancient Greece emphasised observation and prognosis; Galen's synthesis dominated European practice for over a millennium. The scientific revolution, germ theory in the 19th century, anaesthesia, antisepsis and vaccination transformed outcomes; the 20th century added antibiotics, medical imaging, transplantation and molecular medicine.

Regulation and education

Physicians are licensed after completing medical school and postgraduate residency training. Medical practice is governed by professional standards, licensing bodies and medical ethics — classically beneficence, non-maleficence, autonomy and justice.
