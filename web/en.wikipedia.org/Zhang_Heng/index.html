Zhang Heng

Born78 AD, Nanyang, Han dynasty (present-day Henan, China)
Died139 AD (aged 60–61)
OccupationAstronomer, mathematician, inventor, geographer, poet, statesman
Known forSeismoscope (132 AD), armillary sphere, odometer, poetry

Zhang Heng (Chinese: 張衡; AD 78–139), courtesy name Pingzi, was a Chinese polymathic scientist and statesman of the Han dynasty. An astronomer, mathematician, inventor, geographer, cartographer, artist and literary scholar, he served as chief astronomer at the imperial court in Luoyang.

Inventions

Zhang is best known for inventing the world's first known seismoscope in AD 132 — an ornate bronze vessel, the houfeng didong yi, which reportedly detected a distant earthquake in Longxi hundreds of kilometres away that had not been felt in the capital. He also improved the water-powered armillary sphere, adding an extra ring and a water clock mechanism, and is credited with an early odometer.

Astronomy and mathematics

In his work Ling Xian he catalogued some 2,500 visible stars and estimated the universe as "like an egg" with the Earth as its yolk. He calculated pi as between 3.1466 and 3.1622 (commonly cited as 730/232 ≈ 3.1466, and also as √10).

Literature

Zhang was also a celebrated poet, authoring the famous rhapsodies (fu) on the Western and Eastern Capitals, which satirised court extravagance; his writings survive in later compilations.
