TransUnion is an American consumer credit reporting agency. The company gathers and aggregates information on over one billion individual consumers in more than thirty countries, and is one of the three largest credit agencies in the United States, along with Equifax and Experian.

TransUnion collects credit, employment, and public-record information from lenders and other furnishers, and sells credit reports, credit scores, and identity-protection and fraud-prevention services to businesses and directly to consumers.

History

TransUnion was formed in 1968 as a holding company for the Union Tank Car Company, a railcar leasing business. In 1969 it acquired the Credit Bureau of Cook County, which manually maintained 3.6 million card files, and began automating credit reporting. The business was sold to the Pritzker family's Marmon Group in 1981, and later passed through private-equity ownership, including Madison Dearborn Partners and, from 2012, Advent International and GS Capital Partners. TransUnion went public on the New York Stock Exchange in June 2015.

Products and services

TransUnion's business lines include credit reporting and scoring (including the VantageScore model it co-developed with Equifax and Experian), fraud and identity management (including the iovation device-fingerprinting business, acquired in 2018), marketing analytics through its TruAudience products, and healthcare revenue-cycle services. In 2021 it acquired Neustar, a major provider of identity resolution and marketing data, for about $3.1 billion.

Regulation and controversies

Like other US credit bureaus, TransUnion is regulated under the Fair Credit Reporting Act. The company has faced enforcement actions and litigation over inaccurate reporting and marketing practices, including a 2017 Consumer Financial Protection Bureau order—also involving Equifax—requiring payment over the marketing of credit scores and subscription products. It has also been the subject of consumer lawsuits concerning the reporting of OFAC terrorist/watchlist alerts on credit reports, culminating in the Supreme Court case TransUnion LLC v. Ramirez (2021), which addressed standing to sue in federal court.
