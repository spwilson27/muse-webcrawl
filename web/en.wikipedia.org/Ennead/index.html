Ennead

The Ennead or Great Ennead was a group of nine deities in Egyptian mythology worshipped at Heliopolis: the sun god Atum; his children Shu and Tefnut; their children Geb and Nut; and their children Osiris, Isis, Seth, and Nephthys. The word derives from the Greek enneas, meaning "group of nine"; the Egyptian name was pesedjet. Although nine was the canonical number, some Egyptian "enneads" in fact contained more or fewer gods.

Mythology

According to the Heliopolitan creation myth, the self-created god Atum emerged from the primordial waters of Nun and produced the first divine pair, Shu (air) and Tefnut (moisture). They in turn produced Geb (earth) and Nut (sky), who were separated by Shu. Geb and Nut's children were Osiris, Isis, Seth, and Nephthys, completing the nine. This genealogy framed major myths, including the murder of Osiris by Seth and his restoration through Isis, and provided a theological center for the cult at Heliopolis, one of ancient Egypt's oldest religious centers.

Significance

The Ennead appears prominently in the Pyramid Texts of the Old Kingdom, among the oldest religious writings in the world. Competing priesthoods elsewhere promoted their own groupings, such as the Ogdoad of Hermopolis and the Theban Triad, but the Heliopolitan scheme remained influential throughout Egyptian history.
