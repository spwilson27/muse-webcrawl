British America

British America was the British Empire's set of colonial territories in North America, Bermuda, the Caribbean, and northern South America during the 17th and 18th centuries. At its greatest extent after the Seven Years' War in 1763, it comprised the Thirteen Colonies on the Atlantic seaboard, Canada and the former French territories east of the Mississippi, British Florida, numerous Caribbean islands, and colonies such as British Honduras and British Guiana.

Colonisation

Permanent English settlement began at Jamestown, Virginia, in 1607, followed by Plymouth (1620), Maryland, the Carolinas, and New Netherland, seized from the Dutch in 1664 and renamed New York. The colonies were founded under a mixture of royal charters, proprietary grants, and corporate ventures, and administered under the mercantilist framework of the Navigation Acts, which tied their trade to the mother country. Colonisation displaced Indigenous peoples through disease, warfare, and treaties, while enslaved Africans were imported on a large scale to work Caribbean sugar plantations and mainland tobacco, rice, and indigo economies.

American Revolution and aftermath

Grievances over taxation without parliamentary representation and imperial reorganisation after 1763 escalated into the American Revolutionary War. The Thirteen Colonies declared independence in 1776, and by the Treaty of Paris (1783) Britain recognised the United States, losing roughly half its American empire. What remained — Quebec, Nova Scotia, Newfoundland, Rupert's Land, and the Caribbean and Atlantic colonies — was thereafter administered as British North America, much of which federated as Canada in 1867.
