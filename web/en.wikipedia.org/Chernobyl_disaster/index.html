Chernobyl disaster

Date: 26 April 1986

Location: Chernobyl Nuclear Power Plant, Ukrainian SSR, USSR

INES level: 7 (major accident)

Deaths: 31 direct (operators and firefighters); long-term estimates vary

The Chernobyl disaster was a catastrophic nuclear accident on 26 April 1986 at Reactor No. 4 of the Chernobyl Nuclear Power Plant near Pripyat, in what is now Ukraine.

Accident

During a late-night safety test, design flaws of the RBMK reactor — including a positive void coefficient — combined with operator errors to cause an uncontrolled power surge, steam explosions and a graphite fire that released large quantities of radioactive material across Europe. The graphite moderator was also subject to the Wigner effect family of radiation damage.

Response and toll

Firefighters suffered acute radiation sickness; 134 cases were confirmed and 28 died in 1986. Some 116,000 people were evacuated from the 30 km Exclusion Zone, and hundreds of thousands of 'liquidators' performed cleanup. A concrete sarcophagus covered the reactor, replaced by the New Safe Confinement in 2016.

Legacy

Chernobyl remains, with Fukushima, one of only two Level 7 nuclear accidents. It reshaped nuclear safety culture worldwide through the IAEA and conventions on early notification and assistance.
