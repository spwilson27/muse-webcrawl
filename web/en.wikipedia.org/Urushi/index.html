Urushi

Urushi is the traditional Japanese lacquer made from the sap of the lacquer tree, Toxicodendron vernicifluum. It has been used in Japan for thousands of years—archaeological finds date lacquered objects to the Jōmon period—to coat and protect wood, and as a medium for decorative art.

Properties and process

Raw urushi contains urushiol, the same irritant found in poison ivy, and the sap is toxic until cured. Unlike most finishes, urushi hardens not by evaporation but by polymerization, which requires warm, humid conditions; artisans cure pieces in a humid cabinet called a furo. Cured urushi is remarkably hard, glossy, and resistant to water, heat, and chemicals.

Uses

Urushi is used for tableware, furniture, musical instruments, and the gold-sprinkled decoration of maki-e. It is also the adhesive medium in kintsugi, the art of repairing broken pottery with lacquer dusted with gold.
