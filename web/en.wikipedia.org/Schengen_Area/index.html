The Schengen Area is a zone of European countries that have abolished internal border controls, allowing free movement of people, with a common visa policy for international travellers. It is named after the 1985 Schengen Agreement signed near Schengen, Luxembourg.

History

The Benelux countries, France, and West Germany signed the Schengen Agreement in 1985 aboard the riverboat Princesse Marie-Astrid on the Moselle. The 1990 Convention implemented it from 1995. The rules were incorporated into EU law by the Treaty of Amsterdam (1999), though Ireland kept an opt-out and non-EU states Iceland, Norway, Switzerland, and Liechtenstein participate by association.

Membership

Members include most EU states; Bulgaria and Romania became full members on 1 January 2025 after air/sea controls lifted in 2024. Cyprus is not yet a member and Ireland maintains its Common Travel Area with the UK.

Rules

External borders are controlled to common standards, supported by the Schengen Information System (SIS) and visa code. Third-country nationals may generally stay 90 days in any 180-day period. Temporary internal controls may be reintroduced for security or public-health emergencies, as occurred during the 2015 migration crisis and COVID-19.
