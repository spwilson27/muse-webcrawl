Haemophilia

Haemophilia (hemophilia) is a mostly inherited genetic disorder that impairs blood clotting, causing prolonged bleeding, spontaneous joint bleeds and easy bruising. It is X-linked recessive, carried on the X chromosome.

Haemophilia
Types: A (factor VIII), B (factor IX), C (factor XI)
Inheritance: X-linked recessive (A, B)
Treatment: Factor replacement, emicizumab

Types and symptoms

Haemophilia A (factor VIII deficiency) is about four times more common than B (factor IX, 'Christmas disease'). Severity depends on factor level: severe (<1%) causes spontaneous haemarthroses leading to joint damage.

History

Known as the 'royal disease' after Queen Victoria's descendants spread it through European royal houses, including Tsarevich Alexei of Russia.

Treatment

Prophylactic clotting factor concentrates, recombinant and extended half-life products, the bispecific antibody emicizumab, and gene therapies approved in the 2020s. See sex linkage.
