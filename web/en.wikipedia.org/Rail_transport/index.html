Rail transport
Means of transport using wheeled vehicles on rails
First public railway: Stockton and Darlington, 1825

Rail transport (also known as train transport) is a means of transport using wheeled vehicles running in tracks, which usually consist of two parallel steel rails. Rail transport is one of the two primary means of land transport, next to road transport. It is used for about 8% of passenger and freight transport globally, thanks to its energy efficiency and potentially high speed.

History

Wagonways with wooden and then iron rails date to the 16th century in mines. Steam locomotion began with Richard Trevithick's 1804 locomotive and the Stockton and Darlington Railway (1825), followed by the Liverpool and Manchester Railway (1830). Railways spread rapidly through the 19th century, reshaping industry, standard time and settlement. Diesel and electric traction displaced steam in the mid-20th century, and Japan's Shinkansen (1964) inaugurated modern high-speed rail.

Technology

Trains run on track of standard (1,435 mm) or other gauges, guided by flanged wheels and directed by switches. Propulsion is by diesel-electric or electric locomotives, or distributed multiple units; signalling systems control safe separation. Variants include metros, trams, monorails and funiculars.

Role

Rail excels at moving bulk freight over land and passengers on dense corridors and into city centres, with lower emissions per tonne-kilometre than road or air. Networks are most extensive in Europe, China, India, Russia and North America.
