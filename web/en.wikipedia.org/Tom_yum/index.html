Tom yum

Tom yum goong, the shrimp version of the soup, is one of Thailand's best-known dishes.

TypeSoup
Place of originThailand
Main ingredientsLemongrass, galangal, kaffir lime leaves, lime juice, fish sauce, chillies, straw mushrooms
VariationsTom yum goong (shrimp), gai (chicken), pla (fish), nam khon (creamy)

Tom yum (Thai: ส้มยำา) is a family of Thai hot and sour soups, where the iconic flavour is a balance of fragrant herbs and spices with heat and acidity. The name derives from two Thai words: tom refers to the boiling process, while yum means "mixed" and refers to the spicy salad-like dressing of flavours.

Preparation

The broth is seasoned with crushed lemongrass, galangal, kaffir lime leaves, shallots, lime juice, fish sauce, dried or fresh chillies and (often) chilli jam (nam prik phao). Straw mushrooms and the principal ingredient—most famously river prawns in tom yum goong—are added near the end. Two broad styles exist: the clear broth (nam sai) and the creamy version (nam khon), enriched with evaporated milk or coconut milk and chilli jam. The soup is typically garnished with coriander leaves.

Variations and related dishes

Versions are made with chicken (tom yum gai), fish, mixed seafood, or pork ribs. Tom yum paste is used to flavour other dishes, from fried rice to instant noodles. The soup is related to, but distinct from, tom kha, a galangal soup with coconut milk, and to the Lao soup tom yam. Tom yum goong is frequently cited among the world's most popular soups and is a standard-bearer of Thai cuisine internationally.
