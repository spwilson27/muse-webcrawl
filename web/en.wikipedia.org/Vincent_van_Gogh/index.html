Vincent van Gogh

Vincent Willem van Gogh (30 March 1853 – 29 July 1890) was a Dutch Post-Impressionist painter who is among the most famous and influential figures in Western art history. In just over a decade, he created approximately 2,100 artworks, including around 860 oil paintings, most of them in the last two years of his life. His oeuvre includes landscapes, still lifes, portraits, and self-portraits, and is characterised by bold colour, dramatic brushwork, and expressive emotion.

Life and work

Born into an upper-middle-class family, Van Gogh worked as an art dealer and briefly as a lay preacher in the Borinage mining region of Belgium before deciding to become an artist in 1880. His early Dutch works, culminating in The Potato Eaters (1885), are sombre in tone. In Paris (1886–88), living with his devoted brother and dealer Theo, he encountered Impressionism and Japanese prints and developed his vivid palette and broken brushwork.

In Arles in Provence (1888) he painted Sunflowers, The Night Café, and many landscapes, hoping to found an artists' colony with Paul Gauguin. Their collaboration ended after Van Gogh's mental crisis in December 1888, during which he mutilated part of his own ear. In the asylum at Saint-Rémy (1889–90) he painted The Starry Night and Irises. He died in Auvers-sur-Oise two days after shooting himself. Largely unrecognised in his lifetime, he achieved fame rapidly after his death, promoted by Theo's widow, Johanna van Gogh-Bonger.
