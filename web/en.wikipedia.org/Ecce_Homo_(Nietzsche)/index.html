Ecce Homo (Nietzsche)

Ecce Homo
Author: Friedrich Nietzsche
Written: 1888
Published: 1908
Subtitle: How One Becomes What One Is

Ecce Homo: How One Becomes What One Is (German: Ecce homo. Wie man wird, was man ist) is an autobiographical book by the philosopher Friedrich Nietzsche, written in late 1888, shortly before his mental collapse, and published posthumously in 1908. Its title quotes the words of Pontius Pilate presenting Christ ("behold the man").

Contents

In chapters with ironic titles such as "Why I Am So Wise", "Why I Am So Clever", "Why I Write Such Good Books" and "Why I Am a Destiny", Nietzsche reviews his life, his works and his philosophy, presenting himself as the prophet of a coming revaluation of all values and reflecting on his health, diet, ancestry and climate.

Reception

The book's grandiosity has often been read as a symptom of Nietzsche's impending madness, but scholars also treat it as a deliberately provocative, self-parodying literary performance. It remains one of the most distinctive philosophical autobiographies ever written.
