Merrimack River

Merrimack River
Length: 117 miles (188 km)
Source: confluence of Pemigewasset and Winnipesaukee rivers, Franklin, New Hampshire
Mouth: Gulf of Maine at Newburyport, Massachusetts

The Merrimack River is a river in the northeastern United States, flowing 117 miles through New Hampshire and northeastern Massachusetts before emptying into the Gulf of Maine. It begins in Franklin, New Hampshire, where the Pemigewasset and Winnipesaukee rivers join, runs south into Massachusetts past Manchester, Nashua, Lowell, Lawrence and Haverhill, then turns northeast to the sea at Newburyport.

Industrial history

The Merrimack was one of the cradles of the American Industrial Revolution. Its falls — notably Pawtucket Falls at Lowell and the drop at Lawrence — powered the great textile mill cities of the nineteenth century, where corporations built canals, dams and boarding houses for mill workers, many of them young women ("mill girls") and later immigrant families. Henry David Thoreau chronicled an 1839 journey on the river in A Week on the Concord and Merrimack Rivers.

Environment

Industrial and sewage pollution once made the Merrimack one of America's dirtiest rivers; the Clean Water Act of 1972 spurred treatment plants and a long recovery. The river is now a drinking-water source for hundreds of thousands of people and supports recreation and returning fish runs, though combined-sewer overflows during storms and development pressures remain concerns. Its watershed covers about 5,000 square miles.
