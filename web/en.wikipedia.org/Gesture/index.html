Gesture

Hand gestures are among the most studied forms of nonverbal communication.

A gesture is a form of nonverbal communication in which visible bodily actions — movements of the hands, arms, face, head, or body — communicate particular messages, either in place of, or in conjunction with, speech. Gestures include movement of the hands, face, or other parts of the body, and they differ from physical nonverbal communication that does not communicate specific messages, such as purely expressive displays or displays of joint attention.

Categories

Researchers commonly distinguish several kinds of gesture. Emblems are conventionalised signs with agreed verbal equivalents, such as a wave goodbye or a thumbs-up. Illustrators accompany speech and depict what is being said. Regulators manage conversation, such as nodding to encourage a speaker. Adaptors are self-directed movements, such as fidgeting, that often betray emotional states. Co-speech gestures, studied by researchers such as David McNeill, are spontaneous movements tightly integrated with spoken language and thought.

Gestures and language

Gesture plays a central role in child language development and in signed languages, where structured manual gestures constitute full languages in their own right. The meanings of emblematic gestures vary considerably between cultures: a gesture considered friendly in one country may be obscene in another.

Study

The academic study of gesture, sometimes called gesture studies, spans linguistics, psychology, anthropology, and human–computer interaction, where gesture recognition allows people to control devices without touching them.
