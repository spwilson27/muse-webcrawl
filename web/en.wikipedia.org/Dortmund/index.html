Dortmund

Dortmund (German pronunciation: [ˈdɔʁtmʊnt]) is a city in the state of North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany, in the Ruhr region. With a population of about 590,000, it is one of the largest cities in the Ruhr and the third-largest in the state. Founded around 882, Dortmund was for centuries a free imperial city and Hanseatic trading town; in the 19th and 20th centuries it became a centre of coal mining, steel production, and brewing. Since the decline of heavy industry it has reinvented itself as a centre of technology, insurance, logistics, and education, and it is internationally known for its football club, Borussia Dortmund.

History

The settlement appears in records around 882 as Throtmanni. It grew at the crossing of trade routes, received market and minting rights, and became a free imperial city and member of the Hanseatic League, trading as far as England and Russia. The city declined after the Thirty Years' War and was absorbed by Prussia in the early 19th century. Industrialisation transformed Dortmund into one of Germany's great industrial cities: coal mines, the Hoesch and Dortmund-Hörder steelworks, and breweries such as Dortmunder Actien-Brauerei made it the "beer capital" of Germany. Heavy bombing in the Second World War destroyed much of the old town; it was rebuilt in a modern style. The last coal mine closed in 1987 and steel production ended by 2001, prompting a structural transformation toward services and technology.

Economy and transport

Today Dortmund hosts the Technical University of Dortmund (founded 1968), research institutes including Fraunhofer facilities, insurance companies, and the TechnologieZentrum. The Port of Dortmund, connected to the Dortmund–Ems Canal, is among Europe's largest inland canal ports. The main station and motorways link the city across the Rhine-Ruhr metropolitan region.

Sport and culture

Borussia Dortmund, founded in 1909, plays at Signal Iduna Park (Westfalenstadion), Germany's largest stadium, with capacity over 81,000 and the famous standing "Yellow Wall" south stand. The club won the European Cup/UEFA Champions League in 1997. Cultural landmarks include the Reinoldikirche and other medieval churches, the Dortmunder U arts centre in a former brewery tower, the German Football Museum (opened 2015), and the Westfalenpark with the Florianturm television tower.
