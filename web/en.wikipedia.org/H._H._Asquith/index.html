Herbert Henry Asquith, 1st Earl of Oxford and Asquith (12 September 1852 – 15 February 1928), generally known as H. H. Asquith, was a British statesman and Liberal politician who was Prime Minister of the United Kingdom from 1908 to 1916. He was the last Liberal prime minister to command a parliamentary majority, and his government enacted foundational welfare reforms and curbed the power of the House of Lords.

Reforms

Succeeding Henry Campbell-Bannerman in 1908, Asquith's government introduced old-age pensions (1908), labour exchanges, and — through David Lloyd George's "People's Budget" of 1909 — progressive taxation to fund social programmes and naval rearmament. The House of Lords' rejection of the budget provoked a constitutional crisis resolved by the Parliament Act 1911, which removed the Lords' veto over money bills and limited their power to delay other legislation.

First World War

Asquith led Britain into the First World War in August 1914. His seemingly leisurely conduct of the war, the Shell Crisis of 1915, the Gallipoli failure and the Easter Rising eroded confidence; he formed a coalition in 1915 but was forced out in December 1916 and replaced by Lloyd George. The split between Asquith's and Lloyd George's Liberals fatally weakened the party, which never again held power on its own.

Later life

Asquith lost his seat in 1918, returned for Paisley in 1920, led the reunited Liberals, and in 1925 accepted a peerage as Earl of Oxford and Asquith. His daughter Violet Bonham Carter became a noted politician and writer; his descendants include the actress Helena Bonham Carter, his great-granddaughter.
