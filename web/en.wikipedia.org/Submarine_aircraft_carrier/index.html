Submarine aircraft carrier

A submarine aircraft carrier is a submarine equipped with aircraft for observation or attack missions. Such vessels combine the stealth of a submarine with the reach of aircraft, but their complexity and the need to surface to launch and recover planes limited their military value.

Submarine aircraft carrier
Type: Submarine with aircraft
Notable class: Japanese I-400 class
Aircraft: Floatplanes, typically 1–3

Early developments

Germany experimented with submarine-carried aircraft in the First World War, and between the wars Britain, France, the United States, and Japan tested small seaplanes launched from submarines, usually for reconnaissance. France's Surcouf carried a floatplane in a hangar.

I-400 class

Imperial Japan built the largest submarine aircraft carriers, the I-400 class, each carrying three Aichi M6A Seiran attack floatplanes in a watertight hangar. They were intended for surprise attacks on targets such as the Panama Canal but were surrendered at the end of the Second World War without completing their missions.

Later concepts

Post-war proposals, including submarine-launched cruise missiles and modern drone-carrying concepts, displaced the idea of manned aircraft operating from submarines.
