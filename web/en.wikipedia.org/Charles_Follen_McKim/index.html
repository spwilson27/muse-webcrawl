Charles Follen McKim

Charles Follen McKim (August 24, 1847 – September 14, 1909) was an American architect who, with William Rutherford Mead and Stanford White, founded the firm McKim, Mead & White, the leading practice of the American Renaissance and Beaux-Arts movements.

Education and firm

McKim was born in Chester County, Pennsylvania, the son of the abolitionist James Miller McKim. He briefly studied engineering at Harvard before training at the École des Beaux-Arts in Paris. After working in the office of H. H. Richardson, he formed a partnership in 1879 that became McKim, Mead & White, which grew into one of the largest architectural firms in the world.

Major works

His notable buildings include the Boston Public Library (1895), a landmark of classical public architecture inscribed with the motto "Free to All"; the Columbia University campus plan and Low Memorial Library in New York; the Morgan Library; the University Club of New York; and the original Pennsylvania Station (1910), modelled on the Baths of Caracalla. He also directed the restoration of parts of the White House in 1902 and served as an architectural adviser for the Senate Park Commission's plan for Washington, D.C.

Legacy

McKim was a founder of the American Academy in Rome and a leading figure of the City Beautiful movement. He received the Royal Institute of British Architects' Royal Gold Medal in 1903. He died at St. James, Long Island, in 1909.
