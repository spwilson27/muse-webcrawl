Shakti Peethas


The Shakti Peethas of the subcontinent

The Shakti Peethas (Sanskrit: शक्तिपीठ, "seats of Shakti") are significant shrines and pilgrimage destinations in Shaktism, the goddess-focused denomination of Hinduism. The shrines are dedicated to various forms of the goddess Adi Shakti. There are 51, 52, 64, or 108 Shakti Peethas by various accounts, of which the most prominent are the 18 Maha Shakti Peethas.

Legend

According to the mythology, after the goddess Sati self-immolated at the sacrifice of her father Daksha, the grief-stricken Shiva carried her body through the universe; Vishnu dismembered the corpse with his discus, and the body parts fell at sites across the subcontinent, each becoming a Shakti Peetha where the goddess is worshipped in a particular form alongside a corresponding form of Bhairava.

Major Peethas

Important Peethas include Kamakhya in Assam (the yoni, a major center of Tantric worship), Kalighat in Kolkata, Vishalakshi in Varanasi, Hinglaj Mata in Balochistan, Jwalamukhi in Himachal Pradesh, Sharada Peeth in Kashmir, and the Tripura Sundari temple in Tripura. Texts such as the Devi Bhagavata Purana and the Kalika Purana enumerate the lists of Peethas and the body parts associated with them, though the lists differ among sources.
