Real Madrid CF

Founded: 6 March 1902
Stadium: Santiago Bernabéu, Madrid
Nickname: Los Blancos
Honours: Record 15 European Cups/Champions League titles; record La Liga titles

Real Madrid Club de Fútbol, commonly referred to as Real Madrid, is a Spanish professional football club based in Madrid. Founded in 1902 as Madrid Football Club, it received the title Real ("royal") from King Alfonso XIII in 1920. The club plays in La Liga, wears an all-white kit, and since 1947 has played home matches at the Santiago Bernabéu Stadium. It is one of three founding members of La Liga never to have been relegated, alongside Athletic Bilbao and FC Barcelona.

History

Under president Santiago Bernabéu, Madrid built its stadium and assembled the team — led by Alfredo Di Stéfano and Ferenc Puskás — that won the first five European Cups consecutively from 1956. Later eras of dominance include the Quinta del Buitre of the 1980s, the Galácticos policy of Florentino Pérez in the 2000s (Zidane, Ronaldo, Beckham, Figo), and the side coached by Zinedine Zidane that won three straight Champions League titles in 2016–2018 with Cristiano Ronaldo, the club's record scorer.

Rivalries and identity

Madrid's fiercest rivalry is El Clásico against FC Barcelona, a fixture laden with political and cultural symbolism, and the Madrid derby against Atlético Madrid. The club is owned by its members (socios) and is regularly ranked among the world's wealthiest and most valuable sports teams, with one of the largest global fan bases.

Honours

Real Madrid holds the record for European Cup/UEFA Champions League titles (15, most recently in 2024), a record number of La Liga championships (36), numerous Copa del Rey trophies, and multiple FIFA Club World Cup and Intercontinental Cup titles. FIFA named it the best club of the 20th century.
