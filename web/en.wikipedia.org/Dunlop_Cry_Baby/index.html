Cry Baby (wah pedal)

A Dunlop Cry Baby wah pedal (placeholder image)

The Cry Baby is a wah-wah pedal for electric guitar, originally introduced in the 1960s and produced since the 1980s by Dunlop Manufacturing. It is among the best-selling guitar effects pedals of all time and is widely used in rock, funk and blues.

History

The wah-wah effect was developed in 1966 at the Thomas Organ Company by engineer Brad Plunkett, who modified a tone-control circuit, and the pedal was marketed by Vox as the Clyde McCoy model, named for a trumpeter whose muted sound the effect evoked. Because Thomas Organ failed to protect the "Cry Baby" trademark, the Jen company in Italy produced the pedal under that name, and numerous other manufacturers sold identical or similar units. Dunlop acquired the Cry Baby name and design in the early 1980s and has produced it since, with the standard GCB95 model becoming the industry benchmark.

Operation

The pedal is a foot-operated rocking treadle connected to a potentiometer that sweeps a band-pass filter's resonant peak. Rocking the foot forward raises the peak frequency, producing the characteristic vocal "wah" sound. Pressing the toe fully down engages a switch that turns the effect on or off.

Users and variants

The Cry Baby was popularized by players such as Jimi Hendrix and Eric Clapton in the late 1960s. Dunlop produces many variants, including signature models for artists such as Slash, Kirk Hammett, Zakk Wylde and John Petrucci, as well as bass and mini versions.
