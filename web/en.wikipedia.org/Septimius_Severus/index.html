Lucius Septimius Severus (145–211) was Roman emperor from 193 to 211. Born at Leptis Magna in Roman Africa to a family of Punic and Italian descent, he rose through a senatorial career and, as governor of Pannonia, was proclaimed emperor by his legions during the chaos of the Year of the Five Emperors (193). Defeating his rivals by 197, he founded the Severan dynasty, which ruled until 235.

Reign

Severus increased the power and pay of the army, disbanded the old Praetorian Guard and replaced it with loyal troops, and relied increasingly on equestrian officials — a militarizing trend summed up in his reported deathbed advice to his sons: "Be harmonious, enrich the soldiers, and scorn all others." He campaigned successfully against the Parthian Empire, sacking Ctesiphon in 198 and annexing northern Mesopotamia, and strengthened the African frontier with extensive fortifications. His major building works included the Arch of Septimius Severus in the Roman Forum and the monumental enlargement of his home city Leptis Magna.

Britain and death

In 208 Severus travelled to Britain with his sons Caracalla and Geta, campaigning beyond Hadrian's Wall into Caledonia and refortifying the wall. Ill health forced him back to Eboracum (modern York), where he died on 4 February 211. He was succeeded jointly by Caracalla and Geta, a partnership that ended with Geta's murder within the year.

Legacy

Severus stabilized the empire after civil war and extended its eastern frontier, but his favouritism toward the military and debasement of the coinage foreshadowed the third-century crisis. The famous painted Severan Tondo, showing his family, survives in Berlin.
