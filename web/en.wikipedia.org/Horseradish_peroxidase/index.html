Horseradish peroxidase

EC number1.11.1.7
SourceHorseradish root (Armoracia rusticana)
TypeMetalloenzyme (haem-containing)

Horseradish peroxidase (HRP) is an enzyme found in the roots of horseradish, where it catalyses the oxidation of a wide variety of substrates using hydrogen peroxide. It is a single-chain polypeptide of about 44,000 daltons containing a haem prosthetic group, two calcium ions, and four disulfide bridges.

Applications

HRP is one of the most widely used enzyme labels in biochemistry and immunology. Conjugated to antibodies, it is used in enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISA), Western blotting, and immunohistochemistry, where it converts chromogenic substrates such as DAB, TMB, or ABTS into coloured or insoluble products that reveal the location of the target antigen. Chemiluminescent substrates such as luminol and enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) reagents make HRP detection extremely sensitive.

Properties

The enzyme is valued because it is small, stable, relatively inexpensive, and has a high turnover number. Many isoenzymes exist in the plant; the most studied is the C isoenzyme (HRP C). HRP has also been used in neuroscience as a neuronal tracer, because it is taken up by nerve terminals and transported retrogradely.
