Butler swing

In psephology, Butler swing is a measure of the shift in support between two political parties between elections, named after British psephologist David Butler. It is calculated as the average of one party's gain in vote share and the other party's loss: if party A gains 4 percentage points and party B loses 6, the Butler swing from B to A is 5 points.

Comparison with other measures

Butler swing differs from the related Steed swing, which discards votes for third parties before computing the shift. The two-party swing is a standard device of election-night broadcasting in the United Kingdom and Australia, used to summarise the movement of voters and to project seat outcomes from a uniform national swing. Critics note that swings vary widely between constituencies, limiting the reliability of uniform-swing projections.
