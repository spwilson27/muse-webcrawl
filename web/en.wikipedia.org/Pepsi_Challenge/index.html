Pepsi Challenge

The Pepsi Challenge was a marketing campaign launched by Pepsi in 1975, in which representatives set up tasting booths in shopping malls and public places and invited passers-by to take a blind taste test between Pepsi and Coca-Cola. Participants who preferred Pepsi — a majority in Pepsi's reporting — were filmed for television advertisements.

Impact

The campaign was a major salvo in the "cola wars" and helped Pepsi gain market share in the United States. Coca-Cola's response included reformulating its drink as New Coke in 1985, a famous failure widely attributed in part to the pressure of the Challenge.

Criticism

Critics noted that small "sip tests" favour sweeter drinks, and that results might differ in home-consumption conditions. Malcolm Gladwell discussed this sip-test effect in his 2005 book Blink. Pepsi revived the campaign in later years, including online versions.
