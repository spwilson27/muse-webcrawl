Lebanon is a small country in West Asia, located on the eastern shore of the Mediterranean Sea. It is bordered by Syria to the north and east and by Israel to the south, with Cyprus lying to the west across the Mediterranean. Despite its small area of about 10,452 square kilometres, Lebanon has a varied landscape of coastal plains, two parallel mountain ranges, and an inland fertile valley.

Terrain

The country can be divided into four main physiographic regions running roughly parallel from west to east: the narrow coastal plain along the Mediterranean; the Lebanon Mountains (Mount Lebanon), which rise to Qurnat as Sawda' at 3,088 metres, the highest point in the Levant outside Turkey; the Beqaa Valley, a fertile depression that forms part of the Great Rift Valley system; and the Anti-Lebanon Mountains along the Syrian border.

Climate

Lebanon has a Mediterranean climate with hot, dry summers and cool, wet winters. Snow falls on the mountains in winter, and Lebanon is one of the few countries in the Middle East with ski resorts, a feature celebrated in the claim that one can ski in the morning and swim in the Mediterranean in the afternoon.

Rivers and lakes

The Litani River, about 170 kilometres long, is the longest river that flows entirely within Lebanon. Other notable rivers include the Orontes, which flows north into Syria, and the Hasbani. Lake Qaraoun in the Beqaa is the country's largest artificial lake, created by a dam on the Litani.
