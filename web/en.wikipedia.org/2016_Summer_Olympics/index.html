2016 Summer Olympics

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Maracanã Stadium hosted the opening and closing ceremonies of Rio 2016.

The 2016 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the XXXI Olympiad and officially branded as Rio 2016, were an international multi-sport event held from 5 to 21 August 2016 in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil, with preliminary events in some sports beginning on 3 August. Rio de Janeiro was announced as the host city at the 121st IOC Session in Copenhagen, Denmark, on 2 October 2009.

Rio became the first South American city to host the Summer Olympics. These were the first games to be held in a Portuguese-speaking country, the first held entirely during the host country's winter, the first since 1968 to be held in Latin America, and the first since 2000 to be held in the Southern Hemisphere. More than 11,000 athletes from 207 nations took part, including first-time entrants Kosovo, South Sudan, and the Refugee Olympic Team.

Host city selection

Rio de Janeiro defeated Madrid, Tokyo, and Chicago in the bidding process. The final vote saw Rio defeat Madrid 66 to 32 in the third round. The choice was celebrated in Brazil, but preparations were overshadowed by the Brazilian political crisis and concerns over the Zika virus.

The Games

With 306 sets of medals awarded, the games featured 28 Olympic sports, including rugby sevens and golf, which were added to the Olympic programme in 2009. Events took place at venues clustered in four zones: Barra, Copacabana, Deodoro, and Maracanã.

Highlights

  • American swimmer Michael Phelps won five gold medals and one silver, bringing his career total to 28 medals, the most of any Olympian.
  • Jamaican sprinter Usain Bolt completed a "triple-triple", winning the 100 m, 200 m, and 4 × 100 m relay for the third consecutive Games.
  • American gymnast Simone Biles won four gold medals in her Olympic debut.
  • Brazil won its first Olympic football gold medal, defeating Germany on penalties in the final at the Maracanã, with Neymar scoring the winning penalty.
  • Judoka Rafaela Silva won Brazil's first gold medal of the games, in the women's 57 kg division.

Concerns and controversies

The games were preceded by concerns about the Zika virus epidemic, pollution in Guanabara Bay, and a state-sponsored doping scandal in Russia that led to the exclusion of many Russian athletes. The Russian track and field team was banned outright, and the entire Russian team was barred from the subsequent Paralympics.

Legacy

Rio's Olympic Park in Barra da Tijuca was converted to public and sporting use after the games, although several venues fell into disrepair. The games were credited with major transport improvements in Rio, including a new metro line connecting Barra to the rest of the city.
