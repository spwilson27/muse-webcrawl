KingdomAnimalia

SpeciesAbout 37

The Gonorynchiformes are an order of ray-finned fishes within the Ostariophysi, the group that also includes carps, catfishes and characins. The order is small and diverse in form, comprising around 37 species in four families, most of them freshwater fishes of Africa, with one commercially important marine species.

Families

The Chanidae (milkfish) are represented today by a single living species, Chanos chanos, though the fossil record is much richer. The Gonorynchidae (beaked sandfishes) are slender marine bottom-dwellers. The Kneriidae (shellears) and Phractolaemidae (hingemouths) are small African freshwater families adapted to oxygen-poor waters.

Characteristics

Gonorynchiforms share distinctive anatomical features, including a specialised epibranchial organ behind the gills and unusual rib and skull structures; as ostariophysans, the freshwater members possess a Weberian apparatus linking the swim bladder to the inner ear, enhancing hearing. Most are small, toothless or small-mouthed fishes feeding on invertebrates and algae.

Importance

The milkfish is one of the world's most important aquaculture species, farmed for centuries in ponds in Indonesia and the Philippines, where it is the national fish (bangus). Fossil gonorynchiforms are common in Cretaceous and Eocene deposits and inform the early evolution of the Ostariophysi.
