Goleta is a city in southern Santa Barbara County, California, United States, located just west of the city of Santa Barbara. Known as "The Good Land", it was incorporated as a city in 2002, although its community history long predates incorporation. The area includes the Goleta Valley, the Santa Barbara Airport, and the main campus of the University of California, Santa Barbara, which sits on a mesa adjacent to the city in the unincorporated community of Isla Vista.

History

The Goleta area was home to Chumash villages for thousands of years; the large village of Helo' stood on Mescalitan Island in the Goleta Slough. Spanish explorers named the area for a schooner — goleta in Spanish — anchored in the slough. In the 19th century the valley became ranch and then lemon-growing country; at one point the Ellwood Canyon area was a center of lemon production, and the Stow House (1873) survives as the valley's oldest frame house.

During World War II the area gained a Marine Corps air station at what is now Santa Barbara Airport. On February 23, 1942, the Japanese submarine I-17 shelled the Ellwood oil field, the first naval bombardment of the continental United States during the war, causing minor damage but major alarm on the West Coast. After the war Goleta grew rapidly as a suburb of Santa Barbara. Voters approved cityhood in 2001, and the City of Goleta incorporated on February 1, 2002.

Geography and economy

Goleta occupies a narrow coastal plain between the Pacific Ocean and the Santa Ynez Mountains. Its economy is anchored by technology and defense firms, the university, and Goleta Valley Cottage Hospital. The Goleta Slough and the monarch butterfly groves at Ellwood Mesa are notable natural features.
