The Unknown Warrior

Buried: 11 November 1920

Location: Westminster Abbey, London

Commemorates: British and Empire dead of the First World War

The Unknown Warrior is an unidentified British serviceman killed in the First World War whose body was buried in Westminster Abbey, London, on 11 November 1920, simultaneously with the interment of the French Unknown Soldier at the Arc de Triomphe. The grave represents all British and Empire soldiers who died with no known resting place.

Selection and burial

The idea was proposed by the army chaplain the Reverend David Railton. In November 1920 one body was selected at random from four exhumed from battlefields in France and brought home aboard HMS Verdun. The coffin, of English oak with a crusader's sword, was buried among kings in the abbey in the presence of George V, the grave filled with soil from the battlefields and covered by a slab of black Belgian marble.

Significance

Within a week over a million people had visited the grave. It inspired similar tombs of unknown soldiers worldwide, and the slab remains the only part of the abbey floor on which visitors may not walk. Elizabeth Bowes-Lyon began the tradition of royal brides laying their bouquets on the grave.
