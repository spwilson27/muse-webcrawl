Brass quintet

Instrumentation2 trumpets, horn, trombone, tuba
TypeChamber music ensemble
Notable ensemblesCanadian Brass; Philip Jones Brass Ensemble; Empire Brass

A brass quintet is a chamber ensemble of five brass instruments, most commonly two trumpets, one French horn, one trombone, and one tuba (the trombone is sometimes replaced by a euphonium or baritone horn, and the tuba by a bass trombone). It is the most established chamber formation for brass instruments, analogous to the string quartet for strings.

History and repertoire

Although brass chamber music dates to the 19th century — including quintets by Victor Ewald — the modern brass quintet flourished after the Second World War with ensembles such as the New York Brass Quintet and the American Brass Quintet. Repertoire ranges from Renaissance and Baroque transcriptions (Bach, Gabrieli) to original works by composers such as Malcolm Arnold, Leonard Bernstein, and Jan Bach, plus popular and jazz arrangements.

Prominent groups

The Canadian Brass, founded in 1970, brought the format to wide popular audiences with virtuosic and theatrical performances. Other influential ensembles include the Philip Jones Brass Ensemble (which often expanded the quintet format), Empire Brass, and Stockholm Chamber Brass. Brass quintets are now standard in conservatoires, orchestras' outreach programmes, and ceremonial music.
