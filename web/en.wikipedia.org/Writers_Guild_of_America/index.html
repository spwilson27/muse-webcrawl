The Writers Guild of America (WGA) is the joint name of two American labor unions representing writers in film, television, radio and new media: the Writers Guild of America East, based in New York, and the Writers Guild of America West, based in Los Angeles. The guilds negotiate and administer contracts, collect and distribute residuals, arbitrate writing credits and operate health and pension plans.

History

The guilds descend from the Screen Writers Guild, founded in 1933, and the earlier Dramatists Guild tradition. In 1954 writers in screen, television and radio reorganised into the East and West guilds. The WGA has struck several times to win or defend compensation structures, notably in 1960 (winning residuals and pension funding), 1988 (22 weeks) and 2007–08 (100 days, over new-media residuals).

2023 strike

From 2 May to 27 September 2023 the WGA struck for 148 days alongside a concurrent actors' strike, halting most American scripted production. The resulting agreement included improved streaming residuals, minimum staffing provisions for television writers' rooms and guardrails on the use of artificial intelligence in writing.

Awards

The guilds jointly present the Writers Guild Awards each year, honouring outstanding writing for film, television, radio and new media.
