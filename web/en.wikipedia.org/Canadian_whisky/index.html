Canadian whisky is a type of whisky produced in Canada. Most Canadian whiskies are blended multi-grain spirits containing a large proportion of corn-sprit whisky, typically distilled to a high proof and aged in used or new oak barrels; they are generally lighter and smoother than other whisky styles and are often called "rye whisky" or simply "rye" in Canada, even when rye is a minority grain.

Regulations

Canadian law requires that Canadian whisky be mashed, distilled and aged in Canada, be aged in small wood for at least three years, and contain at least 40 percent alcohol by volume. Regulations permit the addition of up to 9.09 percent of other spirits or flavouring, a provision that distinguishes Canadian whisky from American standards.

History and brands

Distilling began with British and American settlers in the early nineteenth century; firms such as Hiram Walker (Canadian Club), Joseph Seagram, and Gooderham and Worts made Ontario a distilling centre, and prohibition-era demand from the United States greatly expanded the trade. Well-known brands include Crown Royal, Canadian Club, Forty Creek, Alberta Premium and Lot 40, while a growing craft sector has revived single-cask and high-rye styles.
