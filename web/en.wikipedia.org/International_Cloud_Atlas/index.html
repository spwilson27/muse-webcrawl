International Cloud Atlas

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The International Cloud Atlas (also called the Cloud Atlas) is a cloud atlas that was first published in 1896 and has remained in print since. Its initial purposes included aiding the training of meteorologists and promoting a consistent use of names for clouds, which is still important today for weather forecasting. The first edition contained colour plates of colour photographs, taken by members of the Commission for the Study of Clouds.

History

A nineteenth-century classification of clouds was popularized by Luke Howard in 1802. The first International Cloud Atlas was published in 1896 under the International Meteorological Organization, drawing in part on photographs and classifications developed by Hugo Hildebrand Hildebrandsson, Ralph Abercromby and others. Subsequent editions appeared in 1932 and 1939, and a major revision was published by the World Meteorological Organization (WMO) in 1956 (Volume I, a descriptive manual) and 1975 (Volume II, a volume of photographic plates).

2017 digital edition

On World Meteorological Day, 23 March 2017, the WMO released a fully digital, web-based edition of the Atlas. The update recognised several newly described cloud forms, including the species volutus and five supplementary features such as asperitas, as well as clouds formed by human activity and by phenomena such as wildfires and volcanic eruptions. The cloud genera classified in the Atlas are cirrus, cirrocumulus, cirrostratus, altocumulus, altostratus, nimbostratus, stratocumulus, stratus, cumulus and cumulonimbus.
