James Hudson Taylor

Born: 21 May 1832, Barnsley, England

Died: 3 June 1905, Changsha, China

James Hudson Taylor was a British Protestant Christian missionary to China and founder of the China Inland Mission (now OMF International). He spent 51 years in China and pioneered reaching the country's interior provinces.

Life and work

Taylor sailed for China in 1853, arriving during the Taiping Rebellion. He adopted Chinese dress and the queue hairstyle to reduce foreignness — controversial among contemporaries — and emphasised faith principles: no guaranteed salaries and no direct appeals for funds. Founded in 1865, his mission grew to over 800 missionaries by his death, establishing schools and stations across inland China, surviving crises including the Boxer Rebellion of 1900, in which many of its members were killed.

Legacy

Taylor influenced generations of "faith missions" and figures like Robert Morrison had preceded him as translator; Taylor's contribution was organisational and geographic — the inland movement — and his biographies remain classics of missionary literature.
