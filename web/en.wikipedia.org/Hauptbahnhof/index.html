Hauptbahnhof

Hauptbahnhof (German for "main station"; abbreviated Hbf, and in Austria also Hbf) is the designation for the principal passenger railway station of a city in German-speaking countries. In cities with more than one station, the Hauptbahnhof is generally the largest and most important, serving long-distance (Intercity and Intercity-Express), regional, and often local S-Bahn services. Notable examples include Berlin Hauptbahnhof, Hamburg Hauptbahnhof, München Hauptbahnhof, Frankfurt (Main) Hauptbahnhof, and Wien Hauptbahnhof in Austria.

Usage

The term is a generic designation rather than a proper name, and is usually combined with the city name. Historically, German cities often had several terminus stations belonging to different railway companies; these were later consolidated, or one station emerged as the main station and received the name in the early twentieth century. Hamburg's station, opened in 1906, was among the earliest to bear the name officially.

In some cities the principal station carries a different name for historical reasons — for example Dresden Hauptbahnhof does bear the term, while others, such as Kassel-Wilhelmshöhe, function as main stations without it. Since the 1990s many Hauptbahnhöfe have been redeveloped as combined transport hubs and shopping centres ("Bahnhof 2000" and similar programmes), and new main stations have been built in Berlin (2006) and Vienna (2014).

Characteristics

Hauptbahnhöfe are categorised by Deutsche Bahn in its station category system, with the busiest — including those in Berlin, Hamburg, Munich, Cologne, Frankfurt, Stuttgart, Düsseldorf, Leipzig, Hanover, and Nuremberg — placed in category 1, handling tens of millions of passengers annually.
