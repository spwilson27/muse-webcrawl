History of anime

The history of anime, Japanese animated film and television, begins in the early twentieth century with short experiments influenced by Western animation, develops a distinctive identity before and during the Second World War, and expands after the war — above all through the work of Osamu Tezuka — into a major cultural industry and a global phenomenon.

Origins (1917–1945)

The earliest Japanese animated films date to about 1917, by pioneers such as Ōten Shimokawa, Seitarō Kitayama and Jun'ichi Kōuchi, using chalkboards, cut-outs and later cels. Sound and cel animation arrived in the 1930s. Animation was used for education and propaganda; Mitsuyo Seo's Momotaro: Sacred Sailors (1945), sponsored by the Imperial Navy, was Japan's first feature-length animated film.

Postwar and the Tezuka era

Toei Animation, founded in 1948, produced the first colour anime feature, Hakujaden (The Tale of the White Serpent, 1958). Osamu Tezuka founded Mushi Production and created Tetsuwan Atomu (Astro Boy, 1963), the first popular animated TV series, establishing the economical limited-animation techniques, cinematic framing and big-eyed character style drawn from manga that defined TV anime. Series such as Tetsujin 28-gō and Speed Racer followed, and anime began to be exported.

Expansion (1970s–1980s)

The 1970s brought mecha series such as Mazinger Z and the landmark Mobile Suit Gundam (1979), which launched the "real robot" genre and a devoted adult fan culture. Leiji Matsumoto's space operas and the films of Hayao Miyazaki and Isao Takahata — who co-founded Studio Ghibli in 1985 — raised anime's artistic standing; Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind (1984) and Akira (1988) won international attention. The home-video (OVA) market expanded creative freedom and niche genres.

Global boom (1990s–present)

In the 1990s anime became a worldwide phenomenon through series such as Dragon Ball Z, Sailor Moon, Pokémon and Neon Genesis Evangelion, and films such as Miyazaki's Spirited Away (2001), which won the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature. Digital production replaced cels around 2000; streaming in the 2010s made anime instantly global, with works such as Your Name (2016) and Demon Slayer (2019) achieving record revenues, and anime becoming one of Japan's largest cultural exports.
