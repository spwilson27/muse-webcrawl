Self-hypnosis

Self-hypnosis or auto-hypnosis is a form of hypnosis in which a person induces a hypnotic state in themselves, without the guidance of a hypnotist. It is commonly taught as a self-help and therapeutic technique for relaxation, habit change and pain management.

Methods

Typical methods involve a structured induction — relaxation of the body, focused breathing, counting down, or fixation on an object or mantra — followed by self-suggestion, visualising desired outcomes in the trance state, and a counting-up procedure to end the session. Techniques were popularised in the 20th century by figures such as Émile Coué, whose method of conscious autosuggestion used the repeated phrase "Every day, in every way, I'm getting better and better", and by later writers on self-hypnosis and autogenic training.

Uses

Self-hypnosis is used by some people for stress reduction, sleep, chronic pain, anxiety and performance preparation. Evidence for its effectiveness varies by application, and it is generally presented as a complement to, not a substitute for, medical treatment.
