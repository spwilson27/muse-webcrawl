Edvard Benes (28 May 1884 – 3 September 1948) was a Czech statesman and one of the founders of Czechoslovakia. He served as the country's foreign minister for most of the interwar period and twice as its president, leading the Czechoslovak government-in-exile during the Second World War.

Early life and independence movement

Born in Kozlany, Bohemia, then part of Austria-Hungary, Benes studied law and sociology in Prague and Paris. During the First World War he worked abroad with Tomas Garrigue Masaryk to promote the creation of an independent Czechoslovak state, organising the Czechoslovak National Council and helping to secure Allied recognition in 1918.

Foreign minister and president

As foreign minister from 1918 to 1935, Benes was a leading figure at the League of Nations and architect of the Little Entente alliance with Yugoslavia and Romania. He succeeded Masaryk as president in 1935. In 1938, the Munich Agreement, concluded by Britain, France, Germany and Italy without Czechoslovak participation, forced the cession of the Sudetenland; Benes resigned and went into exile.

Second World War and aftermath

From London, Benes headed the Czechoslovak government-in-exile, which obtained Allied repudiation of the Munich Agreement. Returning in 1945, he again became president. His government issued the Benes decrees, which authorised the expulsion of ethnic Germans and Hungarians from Czechoslovakia and confiscated their property — measures that remain controversial. Benes resigned in June 1948, months after the Communist takeover of February 1948, and died that September.
