Educational technology

Educational technology (commonly abbreviated as edutech, or edtech) is the combined use of computer hardware, software, and educational theory and practice to facilitate learning. When referred to by its abbreviation, edtech, it usually comprises the industry of companies that create educational technology. In addition to the practical educational experience, educational technology is based on theoretical knowledge from various disciplines such as communication, education, psychology, sociology, artificial intelligence, and computer science.

History

Helping people and children learn in ways that are easier, faster, more accurate, or less expensive can be traced to the emergence of very early tools, such as paintings on cave walls. Writing slates and blackboards, the printed textbook, and correspondence courses preceded twentieth-century audiovisual instruction — film, radio, and television — and B. F. Skinner's teaching machines and programmed instruction of the 1950s. Mainframe-based systems such as PLATO (1960) pioneered online communities, forums, and screen-based learning long before the personal computer. Microcomputers in the 1980s, the World Wide Web in the 1990s, and mobile devices in the 2000s successively widened access.

Forms and practices

Major forms include e-learning and learning management systems (such as Moodle and Canvas), computer-based training, virtual classrooms, educational games and simulations, adaptive learning driven by learner data, and massive open online courses (MOOCs), which rose to prominence around 2012 with platforms such as Coursera, edX, and Udacity. The COVID-19 pandemic forced a worldwide shift to emergency remote teaching in 2020, accelerating adoption and debate about the digital divide. More recently, generative artificial intelligence tools such as large language models have been adopted as tutors, writing aids, and content generators, prompting new questions about assessment integrity and pedagogy.

Theory

The field draws on behaviorism, cognitivism, and constructivism, and on instructional design models such as ADDIE. Frameworks like TPACK describe the knowledge teachers need to integrate technology, pedagogy, and content. Research debates include media comparison studies — Richard Clark's argument that media are "mere vehicles" versus Robert Kozma's reply — and concerns about screen time, privacy, equity, and the commercialization of education data.

Industry

The global edtech market encompasses K–12, higher education, corporate training, and consumer learning products, funded heavily by venture capital in the 2010s and early 2020s, with major centers in the United States, China, and India.
