Barry Schwartz (psychologist)

Barry Schwartz (born 15 August 1946) is an American psychologist. He is the Dorwin P. Cartwright Professor of Social Theory and Social Action (now emeritus) at Swarthmore College, where he taught for many years. Schwartz studies the links between economics, psychology, and morality, and is best known for his work on choice and practical wisdom.

Schwartz is known for his research on the paradox of choice.

Work

In his book The Paradox of Choice: Why More Is Less (2004), Schwartz argues that although modern Western societies offer an unprecedented abundance of choices, having too many options produces anxiety, paralysis, and dissatisfaction rather than freedom and happiness. He distinguishes between "maximizers", who seek the best possible option, and "satisficers", who settle for good enough, noting that maximizers tend to be less happy with their decisions.

With Kenneth Sharpe, he wrote Practical Wisdom: The Right Way to Do the Right Thing (2010), arguing that rules and incentives have displaced the practical wisdom (the Aristotelian virtue of phronesis) that professionals such as doctors, teachers, and judges need to do their work well. His other books include The Costs of Living (1994), Learning and Memory, and Why We Work (2015). His TED talks on choice and practical wisdom have been widely viewed.
