Kähler manifold

In mathematics, and especially differential geometry, a Kähler manifold is a manifold endowed with three mutually compatible structures: a complex structure, a Riemannian metric, and a symplectic form. Equivalently, it is a complex manifold with a Hermitian metric whose associated (1,1)-form, the Kähler form, is closed.

Definition

A Kähler manifold is a complex manifold X with a Hermitian metric h such that the imaginary part of h, a real two-form called the Kähler form, satisfies dω = 0. Locally, any Kähler metric can be written in terms of a real-valued Kähler potential. The name honors the German mathematician Erich Kähler.

Examples

Complex Euclidean space with its flat metric, complex projective space with the Fubini–Study metric, complex tori, and smooth complex projective varieties are all Kähler. Calabi–Yau manifolds are Ricci-flat Kähler manifolds of central importance in string theory. Not every complex manifold is Kähler: the Hopf manifold is a classical non-Kähler example.

Properties

Kähler manifolds satisfy strong restrictions from Hodge theory: their odd Betti numbers are even, they are formal in the sense of rational homotopy theory, and their cohomology carries a Hodge decomposition. The Calabi–Yau theorem (conjectured by Calabi, proved by Yau) guarantees a unique Ricci-flat Kähler metric in each Kähler class on a compact Kähler manifold with vanishing first Chern class.
