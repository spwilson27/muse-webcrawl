24 Hours Nürburgring

The 24 Hours Nürburgring (German: 24-Stunden-Rennen auf dem Nürburgring) is a 24-hour annual touring car and GT endurance racing event held since 1970 on the combined Nordschleife and Grand Prix circuits of the Nürburgring in Nürburg, Germany.

Circuit

The race uses a configuration of roughly 25.4 km combining the modern Grand Prix track with the historic Nordschleife, featuring more than 70 corners and significant elevation change through the Eifel mountains. With over 150 cars in many classes starting simultaneously, it is one of the largest motor races in the world by participation.

History

The event was first held in 1970 as a test of endurance for production-based cars. It has grown into a major fixture of German motorsport, attracting factory teams from Porsche, BMW, Mercedes-AMG, Audi and others, alongside hundreds of amateur entries.

Notable features

Weather in the Eifel is famously changeable, and fog or heavy rain has repeatedly forced interruptions, most notably the shortened 2024 race. Manthey Racing and Phoenix Racing are among the most successful teams, and Porsche the most successful manufacturer overall.
