Roger Federer

Roger Federer (born 8 August 1981) is a Swiss former professional tennis player. He was ranked as the world No. 1 in men's singles by the Association of Tennis Professionals (ATP) for 310 weeks, including a record 237 consecutive weeks, and finished as the year-end No. 1 five times.

Career

Federer won 103 ATP Tour-level singles titles, including 20 major singles titles — a record eight Wimbledon titles, six Australian Open titles, five US Open titles, and one French Open title. He is one of eight men to have completed a career Grand Slam. He also won a record six ATP Finals titles, 28 ATP Masters titles, an Olympic gold medal in doubles (Beijing 2008) with Stan Wawrinka and a silver medal in singles (London 2012), and led Switzerland to the Davis Cup title in 2014.

Federer's rivalry with Rafael Nadal and Novak Djokovic — the "Big Three" — defined men's tennis for two decades. Widely regarded as one of the greatest tennis players of all time, he was known for his graceful, seemingly effortless style of play, versatile shot-making, and longevity at the top of the sport.

Retirement

After a series of knee injuries, Federer announced his retirement in September 2022 and played his final professional match in doubles alongside Nadal at the Laver Cup in London.

Personal life and philanthropy

Federer is married to former WTA player Mirka Federer, and they have two sets of twins. Through the Roger Federer Foundation, he has supported education projects for children in southern Africa and Switzerland.
