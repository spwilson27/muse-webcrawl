Voyages Extraordinaires

Voyages Extraordinaires
Author: Jules Verne
Publisher: Pierre-Jules Hetzel
Published: 1863–1905 (with posthumous volumes)
Number of novels: 54 in the original sequence

The Voyages Extraordinaires ("Extraordinary Voyages" or "Extraordinary Journeys") is a sequence of fifty-four novels by the French writer Jules Verne, published by Pierre-Jules Hetzel between 1863 and 1905. The series aimed to summarise all the geographical, geological, physical and astronomical knowledge of the age in entertaining adventure stories.

Overview

Beginning with Five Weeks in a Balloon (1863), the series includes Verne's most famous works: Journey to the Centre of the Earth (1864), From the Earth to the Moon (1865), Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Seas (1870), Around the World in Eighty Days (1873) and Michael Strogoff (1876). The volumes were issued in distinctive red-and-gold illustrated editions that became icons of French publishing.

Themes

Verne and Hetzel shaped the series as both entertainment and popular education. Recurring themes include exploration of unknown regions, ingenious machines, the power of science and the clash between optimism about progress and its dangers.

Legacy

The Voyages Extraordinaires founded much of modern science fiction and adventure literature, and Verne remains one of the most translated authors in the world.
