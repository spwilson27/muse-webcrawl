Icatibant, sold under the brand name Firazyr, is a medication used for the treatment of acute attacks of hereditary angioedema (HAE) in adults with C1-esterase inhibitor deficiency. It is a selective, competitive antagonist of the bradykinin B2 receptor, administered by subcutaneous injection, and patients may self-administer it after training.

Medical uses

Icatibant is indicated for the symptomatic treatment of acute attacks of hereditary angioedema. It was approved in the European Union in 2008 and by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration in 2011. Because HAE attacks are mediated by excess bradykinin, blocking the B2 receptor reduces vascular permeability and swelling.

Pharmacology

Icatibant is a synthetic decapeptide with a structure similar to bradykinin but containing non-proteinogenic amino acids, which make it resistant to breakdown by bradykinin-cleaving enzymes. By occupying B2 receptors without activating them, it competitively inhibits bradykinin's effects.

Adverse effects

The most common adverse reactions are transient injection-site reactions, including redness, swelling, pain, and itching. Fever and dizziness have also been reported.

History

Icatibant was originally developed by Hoechst (as HOE 140) as a research tool for studying bradykinin receptors before being developed clinically for hereditary angioedema by Jerini AG, later acquired by Shire (now part of Takeda).
