Adopted21 December 1965
LocationNew York
Effective4 January 1969
Parties182

The International Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Racial Discrimination (ICERD) is a United Nations convention adopted by the General Assembly in 1965. It commits its parties to eliminate racial discrimination and promote understanding among all races.

Provisions

The convention defines racial discrimination broadly and requires states to prohibit it in law and practice, to criminalise incitement to racial hatred, and to guarantee equality before the law in civil, political, economic and cultural rights. It permits special measures (affirmative action) to secure advancement of disadvantaged groups.

Monitoring

Compliance is overseen by the Committee on the Elimination of Racial Discrimination (CERD), which reviews state reports and, where states have made the relevant declaration, may hear individual complaints.
