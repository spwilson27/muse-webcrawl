Laplace–Runge–Lenz vector

The Laplace–Runge–Lenz (LRL) vector is a vector used in classical mechanics, principally to describe the shape and orientation of the orbit of one astronomical body around another, such as a planet around a star. For the Kepler problem it is conserved, pointing from the centre of force to the periapsis with magnitude proportional to the orbital eccentricity.

Definition

The LRL vector is defined from the momentum, angular momentum and position of the orbiting body, together with the strength of the inverse-square force.

Significance

Its conservation explains why Keplerian orbits are closed ellipses. In quantum mechanics the corresponding operator accounts for the accidental degeneracy of the hydrogen atom's energy levels, as shown by Wolfgang Pauli.
