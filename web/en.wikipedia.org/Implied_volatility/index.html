Implied volatility

Implied volatility (IV) is the market's forecast of the likely movement in the price of a security, derived from the market price of an option on that security. It is a forward-looking measure: rather than being calculated from past prices, it is backed out of an option-pricing model such as Black–Scholes.

Calculation

Given an option's market price, strike, time to expiry, the risk-free rate and the underlying price, implied volatility is the volatility value that makes the model price equal to the observed market price. Because the model cannot be inverted in closed form, traders solve for it numerically.

Volatility smile and skew

In practice, options with different strikes on the same underlying show different implied volatilities, producing the patterns known as the volatility smile and skew, which contradict the constant-volatility assumption of simpler models.

Use

Traders use implied volatility to judge whether options are relatively cheap or expensive and to gauge expected market moves around events such as earnings announcements.
