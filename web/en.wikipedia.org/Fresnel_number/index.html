Fresnel Number

The Fresnel number (F), named after the physicist Augustin-Jean Fresnel, is a dimensionless number occurring in optics, in particular in scalar diffraction theory. It is defined as F = a² / (Lλ), where a is a characteristic size of an aperture (for example its radius), L is the distance from the aperture to the observation screen, and λ is the wavelength of the light.

Significance

The Fresnel number determines which diffraction regime applies. For Fresnel numbers much greater than 1, the observation point lies in the near field, and Fresnel diffraction theory must be used; geometric shadowing dominates and the diffraction pattern resembles the aperture shape. For Fresnel numbers much less than 1, the system is in the far field, and Fraunhofer diffraction theory applies, giving a pattern described by the Fourier transform of the aperture. Intermediate values around 1 mark the transition between the two regimes.

Applications

The concept is used in designing optical systems, analysing laser beam propagation, and in radio engineering, where related ideas of Fresnel zones determine clearance requirements for line-of-sight links. The Gaussian beam concept of confocal parameter can also be expressed in terms of a Fresnel number.
