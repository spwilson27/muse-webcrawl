Rapping

Rapping (also called emceeing or MCing) is a rhythmic, rhyming vocal delivery performed over a beat, and one of the core elements of hip hop music. Rappers structure their verses around rhythm, rhyme schemes, wordplay, and "flow" — the interaction of cadence, timing, and phrasing with the instrumental track.

History

Rapping's roots lie in West African griot traditions, Jamaican sound-system toasting, and African American spoken-word and blues. It coalesced in the Bronx, New York, in the 1970s at block parties hosted by figures such as DJ Kool Herc and Afrika Bambaataa, with Coke La Rock often credited among the first MCs. Commercial breakthroughs came with the Sugarhill Gang's "Rapper's Delight" (1979), and by the late 1980s artists such as Rakim, Big Daddy Kane, and KRS-One had elevated lyricism and complex internal rhyme.

Technique

Rappers compose in bars (measures), typically in 16-bar verses with hooks or choruses between them. Techniques include multisyllabic and internal rhymes, enjambment, punchlines, and varying rhythmic placement against the beat. Battle rap and freestyle improvisation remain important parts of the culture.
