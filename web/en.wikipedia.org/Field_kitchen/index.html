Field kitchen

A field kitchen is a mobile kitchen, food truck or catering vehicle used to provide food services to soldiers in the field, or to civilians in disaster relief operations. Field kitchens may be towed trailers, truck-mounted units, or containerised systems, and are generally designed to prepare hot meals for anywhere from a few dozen to several hundred people at a time.

History

Organised mobile cooking became a standard part of military logistics in the nineteenth century, as armies recognised that feeding troops hot meals sustained both health and morale. In the First World War, horse-drawn field kitchens — such as the German Gulaschkanone ("goulash cannon"), named for its chimney pipe resembling artillery — followed units close behind the lines and often cooked while on the move. The Second World War saw widespread motorisation, and rolling kitchens became fixtures of all major armies.

Modern use

Modern military field kitchens, such as containerised systems and trailer-mounted units used by NATO forces, run on diesel burners or electricity and include refrigeration, water boilers and hygiene facilities, allowing full meals to be prepared under field conditions. Humanitarian organisations and civil protection agencies use similar kitchens to feed displaced people after earthquakes, floods and other disasters; large relief kitchens can serve thousands of meals per day.
