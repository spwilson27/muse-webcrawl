CALPHAD

CALPHAD (CALculation of PHAse Diagrams) is a phenomenological method for predicting the phase equilibria and thermodynamic properties of multicomponent materials. Developed from the 1970s, notably through the work of Larry Kaufman and the CALPHAD community, it has become a core tool of computational materials science and alloy design.

Method

In the CALPHAD approach, the Gibbs energy of each phase is modelled with mathematical functions whose parameters are assessed from experimental data and first-principles calculations. Binary and ternary system descriptions are combined and extrapolated to predict the behaviour of multicomponent alloys, allowing phase diagrams and properties to be computed for compositions and temperatures that have not been measured.

Applications

Thermodynamic databases and software such as Thermo-Calc, Pandat and FactSage are used to design steels, superalloys, solders and semiconductors, and to optimise heat treatment and solidification processing. The journal Calphad, founded in 1977, is the field's principal periodical.
