Accreditation mill

An accreditation mill is an organisation that purports to grant educational accreditation to higher education institutions but lacks any recognised authority or legitimate standards for doing so. Accreditation mills are closely associated with diploma mills: an unaccredited institution can claim to be "accredited" by an accreditation mill in order to appear legitimate to prospective students and employers.

Like diploma mills, accreditation mills typically charge a fee for their services and provide accreditation with little or no meaningful evaluation of the institution's academic quality. Some operate with impressive-sounding names deliberately similar to those of recognised accrediting agencies, and may list "members" that do not exist or that have never sought accreditation.

Characteristics

Indicators that an accrediting body may be a mill include: accreditation offered for a flat fee without a site visit or peer review; absence from the lists of accreditors recognised by the United States Department of Education or the Council for Higher Education Accreditation (CHEA); accreditation granted very quickly; and the agency accrediting institutions that are themselves widely regarded as diploma mills.

Regulation and detection

In the United States, accreditation by a recognised agency is a condition for an institution's students to receive federal financial aid, so the Department of Education and CHEA maintain databases of legitimate accreditors. Some US states, such as Oregon and Texas, publish lists of unrecognised accrediting bodies and restrict the use of degrees accredited by them for public employment. Internationally, UNESCO and CHEA maintain an international directory of recognised quality-assurance bodies to help employers and institutions verify claims.
