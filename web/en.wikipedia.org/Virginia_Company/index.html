Virginia Company

The Virginia Company was an English trading company chartered by King James I on 10 April 1606 to establish colonial settlements in North America. The charter in fact created two competing companies: the Virginia Company of London and the Virginia Company of Plymouth. The London company founded Jamestown in 1607, the first permanent English settlement in the Americas, and governed the Colony of Virginia until the company's charter was revoked in 1624, when Virginia became a royal colony.

The two companies

The 1606 charter authorised the London company to settle between latitudes 34° and 41° N and the Plymouth company between 38° and 45° N, with an overlap zone neither could settle without the other's consent. The Plymouth company planted the short-lived Popham Colony in Maine in 1607, which was abandoned within a year. The London company's expedition of three ships — the Susan Constant, Godspeed and Discovery — reached Chesapeake Bay in May 1607 and founded Jamestown.

Jamestown and the tobacco economy

The early colony nearly failed through disease, famine (including the "Starving Time" of 1609–10), and conflict with the Powhatan confederacy. Its survival was secured by resupply fleets, the leadership of figures such as Captain John Smith, and after 1612 by the profitable cultivation of tobacco introduced by John Rolfe, which gave the colony an export crop and drove an insatiable demand for land and labour — met first by indentured servants and, from 1619, by the first enslaved Africans brought to English America. In 1619 the company authorised the House of Burgesses, the first elected legislative assembly in the New World, and promoted emigration through the headright system.

Dissolution

Internal factionalism, poor returns for investors, the 1622 Powhatan attack that killed about a quarter of the colonists, and critical royal investigations undermined the company. In 1624 the Court of King's Bench annulled its charter, and Virginia became a crown colony. The company's experiment nonetheless established the joint-stock model and representative institutions that shaped later English colonisation.
