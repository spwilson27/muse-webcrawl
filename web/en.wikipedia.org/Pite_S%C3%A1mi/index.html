Pite Sámi

Native toNorway, Sweden
RegionPite River valley, Arjeplog and Arvidsjaur area
FamilyUralic, Sámi, Western Sámi
StatusSeverely endangered

Pite Sámi (also called Pite Saami; in the language itself Bidumsámegiella) is a Sámi language traditionally spoken along the Pite River in Swedish Lapland, in the municipalities of Arjeplog and Arvidsjaur, and in adjacent parts of Nordland in Norway. It is one of the Western Sámi languages, closely related to Lule Sámi.

Status

Pite Sámi is severely endangered, with only a few dozen fluent speakers remaining, most of them elderly. On the Norwegian side of the border the language has no native speakers left. Documentation projects in the 21st century, including the Pite Sámi dictionary and corpus work at Umeå University, have recorded the language extensively, and a standard orthography was approved in 2019.

Features

Like other Sámi languages, Pite Sámi distinguishes three numbers (singular, dual, plural) in pronouns and verbs and has a rich case system. Its phonology includes consonant gradation and a system of vowel alternations shared with its Western Sámi neighbours.
