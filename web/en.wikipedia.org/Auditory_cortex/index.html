Auditory cortex

Auditory cortex

Location: Temporal lobe, superior temporal gyrus

Brodmann areas: 41 and 42 (primary), surrounding belt and parabelt regions

Function: Processing of sound

The auditory cortex is the part of the temporal lobe that processes auditory information in humans and many other vertebrates. It is part of the auditory system, performing basic and higher functions in hearing, such as possible relations to language switching. It is located bilaterally, roughly at the upper sides of the temporal lobes.

Structure

The primary auditory cortex (A1) lies on Heschl's gyrus, on the upper bank of the superior temporal gyrus, and receives point-to-point input from the ventral division of the medial geniculate nucleus of the thalamus. It is organised tonotopically: neurons are arranged in an orderly map by the sound frequency to which they respond best. Surrounding belt and parabelt areas carry out more complex processing of sound patterns.

Function

The auditory cortex identifies fundamental elements of sound such as pitch and loudness, and contributes to sound localisation and the recognition of voices, speech, and music. Damage to the auditory cortex can cause hearing deficits ranging from difficulty localising sounds to cortical deafness or the inability to understand spoken words (pure word deafness).
