Mount Ngauruhoe

Elevation2,291 m (7,516 ft)
LocationTongariro National Park, New Zealand
TypeStratovolcano
Last eruption1977

Mount Ngauruhoe is an active stratovolcano in New Zealand, forming the youngest vent in the Tongariro volcanic complex on the Central Plateau of the North Island. Rising 2,291 metres, its nearly perfect cone — built in the last roughly 2,500 years — is one of the most recognisable mountains in the country, and it lies within Tongariro National Park.

Eruptive history

Ngauruhoe erupted at least 45 times in the twentieth century, with notably frequent activity between 1948 and 1975; its last eruption was in 1977. Eruptions are typically Strombolian to sub-Plinian, producing ash columns, bombs and small lava flows in the summit crater. Because the mountain is geologically a secondary cone of Tongariro rather than a separate volcano, it is monitored as part of that system.

Climbing and culture

The mountain stands beside the Tongariro Alpine Crossing, one of New Zealand's most popular day walks; climbers traditionally ascended the cone from the Mangatepopo saddle, though visitors are now asked to respect the wishes of Ngāti Hikairo ki Tongariro, for whom the summit is tapu (sacred), and not to climb to the top. Ngauruhoe achieved worldwide fame as the stand-in for Mount Doom in Peter Jackson's The Lord of the Rings films, with digital alteration applied to its slopes.
