Vanwall

Vanwall was a motor racing team and racing car constructor that was active in Formula One during the 1950s. Founded by Tony Vandervell, the Vanwall name was derived by combining the name of the team owner with that of his Thinwall bearings produced at the Vandervell Products factory at Acton, London. The team won the inaugural Formula One World Constructors' Championship in 1958.

History

Vandervell initially supported the BRM project, then set up his own team, entering modified Ferraris before commissioning his own cars. The first true Vanwalls appeared in 1954, with a 2.0-litre engine later enlarged. Aerodynamic bodywork designed with input from Frank Costin and a chassis developed with the young Colin Chapman made the cars increasingly competitive.

1957–1958 success

In 1957 Stirling Moss and Tony Brooks shared victory at the British Grand Prix at Aintree — the first World Championship win for a British-designed car. In 1958 Vanwall won six of the eleven races and took the first Constructors' Championship, though Moss narrowly lost the Drivers' title to Mike Hawthorn. The season ended in tragedy when Stuart Lewis-Evans died from burns suffered in a crash at the Moroccan Grand Prix; a grief-stricken Vandervell, also suffering ill health, withdrew the team from full-time racing.

Legacy

Vanwall's triumph broke the dominance of Italian and German teams and ushered in the era of British Formula One success, directly influencing the rear-engined revolution at Cooper and Lotus. Sporadic entries continued until 1961.
