Copenhagen Airport

Copenhagen Airport, Kastrup (Danish: Københavns Lufthavn, Kastrup; IATA: CPH, ICAO: EKCH) is an international airport serving Copenhagen, Denmark, Zealand, the Øresund Region and southern Sweden. It is the largest airport in the Nordic countries and one of the oldest international airports in Europe, having opened in 1925.

History

The airport opened in 1925 as one of the world's first civil airports, with a grass runway and a harbour for seaplanes. It expanded through the jet age; the landmark Terminal 2 opened in 1960 and Terminal 3 in 1998, the latter integrating a railway and metro station. The airport was central to Scandinavian Airlines' hub strategy and grew to handle around 30 million passengers in 2019, its busiest year before the COVID-19 pandemic.

Facilities

CPH has two main runways (04L/22R and 04R/22L) plus a crosswind runway, and piers A–F serving Schengen and non-Schengen traffic. The low-cost pier CPH Go opened in 2010. Extensive shopping and dining occupy the transit area, and the airport has frequently won awards for efficiency and passenger experience.

Ground transport

The airport is directly connected across the Øresund to Sweden: trains run to Copenhagen Central Station in about 13 minutes and across the Øresund Bridge to Malmö and beyond. The Copenhagen Metro's M2 line serves the airport station, and motorway links connect it to the E20.
