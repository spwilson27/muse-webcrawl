Regular polytope

In mathematics, a regular polytope is a polytope whose symmetry group acts transitively on its flags — sequences of incident faces of every dimension. Regular polytopes are the highest-symmetry generalizations of the regular polygons and the Platonic solids to arbitrary dimensions.

Classification

In two dimensions there are infinitely many regular polygons; in three dimensions there are five Platonic solids. Ludwig Schläfli showed that in four dimensions there are six convex regular polychora, while in five and higher dimensions only three families remain: the simplex, the hypercube, and the cross-polytope. Non-convex (star) regular polytopes — the Kepler–Poinsot polyhedra in 3D and their 4D analogues — extend the classification.

Notation

Regular polytopes are described by Schläfli symbols such as {3,3} for the tetrahedron and {4,3,3} for the tesseract, encoding the numbers of edges per face and faces meeting at each lower-dimensional face.
