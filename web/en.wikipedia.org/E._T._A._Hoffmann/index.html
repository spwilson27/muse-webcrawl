E. T. A. Hoffmann

Ernst Theodor Amadeus Hoffmann (born Ernst Theodor Wilhelm Hoffmann, 24 January 1776 – 25 June 1822) was a German Romantic author of fantasy and Gothic horror, a jurist, composer, music critic and artist. He is the author of the novella The Nutcracker and the Mouse King, on which Tchaikovsky's ballet The Nutcracker is based. He is also the author of the novel The Devil's Elixirs and the story "The Sandman", which influenced Freud's essay on the uncanny.

Life

Born in Königsberg in East Prussia, Hoffmann studied law and pursued a career in the Prussian judiciary, while composing music and writing in his spare time. He changed his third name from Wilhelm to Amadeus in homage to Mozart. After periods in Posen, Warsaw, Berlin and Bamberg — where he worked as a music director and critic — he returned to Berlin in 1814 as a judge of the Kammergericht. His stories, many written in his final decade, made him one of the most influential writers of the German Romantic movement.

Legacy

Hoffmann's tales of the fantastic, doubles and automata profoundly influenced Poe, Gogol, Dostoevsky, Baudelaire and Kafka. Jacques Offenbach's opera The Tales of Hoffmann (1881) is based on several of his stories, with Hoffmann himself as the protagonist. His Kreisleriana essays helped shape Romantic music criticism, and his own compositions include the opera Undine (1816).
