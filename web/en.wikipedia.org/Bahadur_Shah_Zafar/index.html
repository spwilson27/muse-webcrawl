Bahadur Shah Zafar

Bahadur Shah II (1775–1862), better known by his pen name Zafar, was the twentieth and last Mughal emperor of India. He reigned from 1837 to 1857, but his authority was confined largely to Delhi and the Red Fort, real power lying with the British East India Company. After the Indian Rebellion of 1857, in which rebel sepoys proclaimed him their leader, he was deposed, tried, and exiled to Rangoon in British Burma, where he died.

Reign and the rebellion of 1857

Zafar ascended the throne at the age of 62, inheriting a pensioned, ceremonial emperorship. When mutinous sepoys reached Delhi in May 1857, they declared him Emperor of Hindustan, giving the uprising a unifying symbol. After the British recaptured Delhi in September 1857, Zafar surrendered at Humayun's Tomb; his sons were executed by William Hodson. A military court convicted the emperor of aiding the rebellion, and in October 1858 he was exiled with his wife Zinat Mahal to Rangoon.

Poet and legacy

Zafar was an accomplished Urdu poet, calligrapher, and patron of the arts; his court included poets such as Ghalib and Zauq. His ghazals — several written in exile lamenting his lost kingdom — remain beloved, including the couplet traditionally attributed to him, "Kitna hai badnaseeb Zafar..." ("How unfortunate is Zafar, for his burial he could not get even two yards of land in the street of his beloved"). He was buried in Rangoon (Yangon), where his tomb is now a shrine.
