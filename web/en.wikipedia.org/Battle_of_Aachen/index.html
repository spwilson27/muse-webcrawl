Battle of Aachen

Date2–21 October 1944
LocationAachen, Germany
ResultAmerican victory
BelligerentsUnited States vs. Nazi Germany
Strengthc. 100,000 US troops; c. 13,000 German troops plus reinforcements

The Battle of Aachen was a major combat action of the Second World War, fought by American and German forces in and around Aachen, Germany, between 2 and 21 October 1944. The city had been incorporated into the Siegfried Line (Westwall), the main defensive network on Germany's western border, and the United States Army intended to capture it to open a route into the German heartland. Aachen was the first major German city attacked — and captured — by Allied ground forces.

Background

Following the breakout from Normandy and the rapid advance across France, American forces reached the German border in September 1944. Aachen, a historic city of some 165,000 peacetime residents associated with Charlemagne, lay in a gap in the Westwall but was ringed by pillboxes and fortifications. Hitler ordered the city held at all costs, and most of the civilian population was evacuated.

The battle

The US 1st and 30th Infantry Divisions encircled the city while fighting through the surrounding fortifications in heavy combat, including battles in the Hürtgen Forest region to the south. Beginning on 13 October, the 26th Infantry Regiment fought block by block through Aachen's streets, using tanks, artillery firing over open sights, and an 8-inch howitzer firing directly into buildings. The German garrison commander, Colonel Gerhard Wilck, surrendered on 21 October after the last strongpoints were overrun.

Aftermath

American casualties were roughly 5,000 and German losses around 5,000 killed with 5,600 taken prisoner. The city was largely destroyed. The battle demonstrated the difficulty of breaching the Westwall and foreshadowed the costly fighting of the following winter, while the capture of Aachen provided the Allies a base for the advance to the Roer and Rhine rivers.
