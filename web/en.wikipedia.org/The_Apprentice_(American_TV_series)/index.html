The Apprentice (American TV series)

The Apprentice is an American reality television program that judges the business skills of a group of contestants. Created by Mark Burnett and hosted for its first fourteen seasons by businessman (and later US President) Donald Trump, it premiered on NBC on January 8, 2004.

Format

Contestants, divided into teams ("corporations"), compete in business-related tasks in New York City, such as selling products or creating advertising campaigns. Each week the losing team attends a boardroom meeting where Trump and his advisers — typically including George H. Ross and Carolyn Kepcher in early seasons — question the project manager, ending with Trump's catchphrase dismissal, "You're fired". The season winner received a one-year contract and a $250,000 starting salary to run one of Trump's companies.

Versions

The show's success spawned The Celebrity Apprentice (from 2008), in which celebrity contestants competed for charity donations; winners included Piers Morgan, Joan Rivers, John Rich, Arsenio Hall and Matt Iseman. After Trump left to pursue politics, Arnold Schwarzenegger hosted one season as The New Celebrity Apprentice (2017). The format was licensed internationally, including the long-running British version hosted by Alan Sugar.

Impact

The original series was a major ratings hit in its first season and significantly raised Trump's public profile as a business figure, an image central to his later political career.
