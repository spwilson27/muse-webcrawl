Chinese room

The Chinese room argument is a thought experiment by philosopher John Searle, first published in 1980, intended to refute the claim that a computer running a program can literally have a mind, understanding, or consciousness — the position Searle called "strong AI."

The thought experiment

Searle imagines himself alone in a room, following an English instruction book for manipulating Chinese symbols. Slips of paper with Chinese characters are passed in, and by following the rules he produces appropriate Chinese responses, so that to outside observers he appears to understand Chinese — although he understands none of it. By analogy, Searle argues, a computer that passes the Turing test merely manipulates symbols syntactically and has no semantic understanding of what they mean.

Replies and debate

The argument generated decades of debate. The "systems reply" holds that while the man does not understand Chinese, the whole system of room, rules, and operator does; Searle responded that the man could internalize the entire system without gaining understanding. Other responses include the robot reply, the brain simulator reply, and connectionist approaches. The Chinese room remains a central reference point in philosophy of mind and artificial intelligence.
