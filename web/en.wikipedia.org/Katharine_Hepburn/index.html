Katharine Hepburn

Katharine Hepburn
Born: 12 May 1907, Hartford, Connecticut
Died: 29 June 2003, Fenwick, Connecticut
Occupation: Actress
Academy Awards: 4 Best Actress wins (record)

Katharine Houghton Hepburn (1907–2003) was an American actress whose career as a Hollywood leading lady spanned more than sixty years. Known for her fierce independence and spirited personality, she won a record four Academy Awards for Best Actress.

Career

Hepburn rose to fame in the early 1930s with films such as Morning Glory (1933) and Little Women. After being labelled "box office poison" in the late 1930s, she engineered a comeback with The Philadelphia Story (1940). Her nine films with Spencer Tracy, including Adam's Rib and Guess Who's Coming to Dinner, became classics, and she won late-career Oscars for The Lion in Winter and On Golden Pond.

Legacy

In 1999 the American Film Institute named Hepburn the greatest female star of classic Hollywood cinema. See also Spencer Tracy.
