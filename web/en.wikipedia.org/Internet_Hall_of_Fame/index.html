Internet Hall of Fame

The Internet Hall of Fame is an honorary lifetime achievement award administered by the Internet Society (ISOC), recognising individuals who made significant contributions to the development and advancement of the global Internet. It was established in 2012, on the twentieth anniversary of ISOC.

Induction

The inaugural inductees, announced in April 2012 in Geneva, Switzerland, included pioneers of packet switching and the ARPANET, creators of core protocols such as TCP/IP, and the inventor of the World Wide Web, Tim Berners-Lee. Inductees are chosen by an advisory board of previous honourees and Internet Society appointees.

Categories

Honourees are inducted in three categories. Pioneers are recognised for foundational technical and organisational work in the Internet's early development. Global Connectors are honoured for helping to bring Internet access and use to regions and communities worldwide. Innovators are recognised for significant technical, commercial or policy advances.

Well-known inductees include Vint Cerf, Robert Kahn, Jon Postel, Radia Perlman, Linus Torvalds, and community builders from Africa, Asia and Latin America who extended networking to their regions. Induction ceremonies have been held in cities including Berlin, Hong Kong, Honolulu and Washington, D.C., with some rounds conducted virtually.
