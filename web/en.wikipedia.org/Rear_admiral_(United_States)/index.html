Rear admiral is a flag officer rank in the United States Navy, the United States Coast Guard, the NOAA Commissioned Officer Corps and the United States Public Health Service Commissioned Corps. Uniquely among United States ranks, it exists in two grades: rear admiral (lower half), a one-star rank with pay grade O-7, and rear admiral (upper half), a two-star rank with pay grade O-8.

History

The United States Navy had no admirals at all until 1862, partly from a republican reluctance to adopt titles associated with European navies. During the American Civil War, Congress created the grades of rear admiral, vice admiral and admiral, with David Farragut the first officer promoted through them. The title derives from the historical practice of dividing a fleet into squadrons: the admiral commanded the centre, the vice admiral the van, and the rear admiral the rear squadron.

Between 1899 and 1947 the single grade of rear admiral was informally split by seniority, and officers on the upper half of the list drew higher pay. The Officer Personnel Act of 1947 formalised the two grades, and since then officers have been promoted from lower half to upper half as separate appointments. For a period the lower-half grade was styled "commodore admiral" and then simply commodore, before the title rear admiral (lower half) was restored in 1983.

Appointment and limits

Rear admirals are nominated by the President and confirmed by the Senate. Tour lengths are typically two to three years, and statute caps the number of flag officers and the proportion who may hold two-star rank. Rear admirals (upper half) command carrier strike groups, numbered fleets' task forces, major shore installations and large staff directorates; those of the lower half command carrier strike groups in some cases, expeditionary strike groups, and major programme offices.

Insignia

A rear admiral (lower half) wears one broad stripe on the sleeve with one narrower stripe above it in the Navy's system of sleeve lace, or a single star on shoulder boards; an upper-half rear admiral adds a second star. On the service dress blue uniform both grades wear the broad gold stripe associated with flag rank, distinguished by the number of stars on the shoulder board and the flag flown at their command — one star or two.
