Weight loss

[Image: Weight loss]

Weight loss, in the context of medicine, health, or physical fitness, refers to a reduction of the total body mass, due to a mean loss of fluid, body fat (adipose tissue), or lean mass (namely bone mineral deposits, muscle, tendon, and other connective tissue). Weight loss can either occur unintentionally because of malnourishment or an underlying disease, or from a conscious effort to improve an actual or perceived overweight or obese state.

Intentional weight loss

Intentional weight loss is commonly pursued through dietary changes that reduce total energy intake, increased physical activity, or both. Dietary approaches include low-fat, low-carbohydrate, Mediterranean, and intermittent fasting regimens; evidence suggests that adherence and total energy balance are more important than macronutrient composition for most people. Pharmacological treatments, including GLP-1 receptor agonists such as semaglutide, and bariatric surgery are used for people with obesity or weight-related health conditions.

Unintentional weight loss

Unexplained weight loss that is not caused by reduction in calorific intake or exercise is called cachexia and may be a symptom of a serious medical condition, including cancer, hyperthyroidism, diabetes, chronic infections, gastrointestinal disorders, or depression. A loss of 5 percent or more of body weight within six to twelve months, without trying, is generally considered clinically significant and warrants medical evaluation.

Health effects

For people with overweight or obesity, modest weight loss of 5 to 10 percent of body weight can improve blood pressure, blood glucose, and lipid levels and reduce the risk of type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease. Maintaining weight loss over the long term is a common challenge, as metabolic adaptation and behavioral factors promote regain.
