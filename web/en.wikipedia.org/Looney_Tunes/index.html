Looney Tunes

Looney Tunes is an American animated comedy franchise produced by Warner Bros. It began as a series of theatrical short films released from 1930 to 1969, alongside its sister series Merrie Melodies. The shorts introduced some of the most famous cartoon characters in history, including Bugs Bunny, Daffy Duck, Porky Pig, Tweety, Sylvester, Elmer Fudd, Yosemite Sam, Wile E. Coyote and the Road Runner, Marvin the Martian, Speedy Gonzales, Foghorn Leghorn, and Pepé Le Pew.

History

The series was created to promote Warner Bros.' music catalogue in the early sound era — hence "Tunes" in the title. Directors and animators such as Tex Avery, Bob Clampett, Chuck Jones, Friz Freleng, and Robert McKimson developed the characters' personalities during the golden age of American animation. Voice actor Mel Blanc provided nearly all the major voices. Composer Carl Stalling's rapid-fire scores became a signature of the cartoons.

Legacy

Several Looney Tunes shorts have been inducted into the National Film Registry, and "What's Opera, Doc?" (1957) is frequently ranked among the greatest cartoons ever made. The franchise has continued through television series, compilation films, and features such as Space Jam (1996). The characters' sign-off, "That's all Folks!", delivered by Porky Pig, became iconic.
