Alopecia areata

SpecialtyDermatology

CausesAutoimmune disorder
Frequency~2% of people at some point

Alopecia areata, also known as spot baldness, is an autoimmune disorder in which the immune system attacks the hair follicles, causing hair to fall out, often in small round patches on the scalp or other parts of the body. In most people the hair grows back within a year without treatment, although episodes may recur.

Signs and symptoms

The condition usually begins with one or more smooth, coin-sized bald patches. Short broken "exclamation mark" hairs may be seen at the edges of patches, and the nails may show pitting. Extensive forms include alopecia totalis (loss of all scalp hair) and alopecia universalis (loss of all body hair).

Causes

Alopecia areata is believed to arise when the normal immune privilege of the hair follicle breaks down in genetically susceptible individuals, allowing T lymphocytes to attack the follicle. It is associated with other autoimmune conditions such as vitiligo, thyroid disease and atopy, but it is not contagious and is not caused by poor hygiene.

Treatment

There is no cure, but treatments can promote regrowth. Options include topical or injected corticosteroids, minoxidil, contact immunotherapy, and, for severe disease, JAK inhibitors such as baricitinib, which was approved for severe alopecia areata in 2022. Many mild cases resolve spontaneously.
