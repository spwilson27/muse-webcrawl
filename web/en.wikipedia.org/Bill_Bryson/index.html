Bill Bryson

BornWilliam McGuire Bryson, 8 December 1951, Des Moines, Iowa, U.S.
OccupationAuthor, journalist
GenreTravel, popular science, language

William McGuire Bryson (born 8 December 1951) is an American-British author known for his humorous books on travel, the English language, and popular science. His best-known works include Notes from a Small Island (1995), about Great Britain, and A Short History of Nearly Everything (2003), a widely acclaimed survey of science.

Early life and career

Bryson was born in Des Moines, Iowa, and first visited Britain in 1973, eventually settling there and working as a journalist. His travel writing, beginning with The Lost Continent (1989), combined observation with self-deprecating humour.

Major works

A Walk in the Woods (1998) recounts his attempt to hike the Appalachian Trail and was adapted into a 2015 film. A Short History of Nearly Everything won the Aventis Prize for best science book. Later works include At Home: A Short History of Private Life (2010), One Summer: America, 1927 (2013), and The Body: A Guide for Occupants (2019).

Recognition

Bryson served as chancellor of Durham University from 2005 to 2011 and has received numerous honorary degrees and awards for his contributions to literature.
