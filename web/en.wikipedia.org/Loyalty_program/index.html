Loyalty program

A loyalty program is a marketing strategy in which a company rewards customers who make frequent purchases, typically with points, miles, discounts or exclusive benefits that encourage repeat business.

History and forms

Precursors included trading stamps such as S&H Green Stamps. Modern programmes were transformed by airline frequent-flyer schemes, beginning with American Airlines' AAdvantage in 1981, and spread to hotels, supermarkets, coffee chains and credit cards. Coalition programmes unite multiple retailers under one currency, while tiered programmes grant status levels. Programmes also give firms valuable data on customer behaviour, and unredeemed points represent a significant financial liability (and profit source when sold to partners such as card issuers).
