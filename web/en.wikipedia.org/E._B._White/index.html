Elwyn Brooks White (July 11, 1899 – October 1, 1985) was an American writer. He was a long-time contributor to The New Yorker magazine and a co-author of the English language style guide The Elements of Style. He also wrote books for children, including Stuart Little (1945), Charlotte's Web (1952), and The Trumpet of the Swan (1970), which are considered classics of children's literature.

Life and career

Born in Mount Vernon, New York, White graduated from Cornell University in 1921. After stints as a reporter and advertising copywriter, he joined the fledgling New Yorker in the 1920s, where his essays, poems, and "Notes and Comment" pieces helped define the magazine's voice for more than five decades. In the late 1930s he and his wife Katharine Angell White, a New Yorker editor, moved to a saltwater farm in North Brooklin, Maine, from which he wrote the monthly "One Man's Meat" column for Harper's.

Works

Charlotte's Web, about a pig named Wilbur saved by the words a spider weaves into her web, has been ranked among the greatest children's novels ever written; it grew out of White's life on the farm and his fascination with animals. In 1959 he revised and expanded William Strunk Jr.'s The Elements of Style — "Strunk and White" — whose maxims ("Omit needless words") shaped generations of American prose writers.

Honors

White received a special Pulitzer Prize citation in 1978 for his letters, essays, and the full body of his work, the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 1963, and the Laura Ingalls Wilder Medal in 1970 for his children's books. He died of Alzheimer's disease in 1985.
