Robert Devereux, 2nd Earl of Essex

Robert Devereux, 2nd Earl of Essex, KG (10 November 1565 – 25 February 1601) was an English nobleman, soldier, and favourite of Queen Elizabeth I. A charismatic courtier and military commander, he fell from favour after a failed campaign in Ireland and was executed for treason after leading an abortive rebellion.

Career

Essex rose rapidly at court in the late 1580s, becoming one of the Queen's principal favourites. He won fame in expeditions against Spain, including the capture of Cádiz in 1596. In 1599 he was sent to Ireland as Lord Lieutenant with a large army to suppress Tyrone's Rebellion, but instead of defeating the rebels he agreed a truce with the Earl of Tyrone and returned to England without permission.

Downfall and death

Placed under house arrest and stripped of his offices and monopolies, Essex attempted in February 1601 to raise the City of London against the Queen's ministers. The rising collapsed within a day; he was tried for high treason and beheaded at the Tower of London on 25 February 1601.
