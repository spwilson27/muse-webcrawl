Semi-automatic pistol

A semi-automatic pistol (also called a self-loading pistol) is a handgun that fires one round with each pull of the trigger and automatically loads the next cartridge, using part of the energy of the fired round to cycle the action. Most are magazine-fed and use a reciprocating slide.

Mechanisms

Common operating systems include blowback, used mainly for lower-powered cartridges; short recoil operation, in which the barrel and slide recoil together briefly before unlocking; and gas operation, rare in pistols. Triggers may be single action, double action, double action only, or striker-fired, and pistols incorporate safeties of various kinds, including manual levers, grip safeties and trigger safeties.

History and examples

The first commercially successful designs appeared in the 1890s, including the Borchardt C-93 and the Mauser C96. Landmark service pistols include the Colt M1911, the Walther PP, the Browning Hi-Power, the Beretta 92 and the polymer-framed Glock 17, introduced in 1982. Semi-automatic pistols have largely replaced revolvers in military and police service because of their greater magazine capacity and faster reloading.
