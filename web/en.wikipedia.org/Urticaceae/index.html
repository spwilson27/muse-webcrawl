Urticaceae

Common nettle (Urtica dioica), a typical member of the family.

The Urticaceae are a family of flowering plants, the nettle family, in the order Rosales. The family includes about 54 to 79 genera and some 2,625 species, the largest genus being Pilea (500 to 715 species), followed by Elatostema (about 300 species) and Urtica (about 80 species). Urticaceae species occur worldwide, though predominantly in tropical regions.

Description

Members are herbs, shrubs, small trees, or lianas. Many, most famously the stinging nettles of the genus Urtica, bear stinging hairs (trichomes) that inject irritating compounds such as histamine and formic acid when touched. The flowers are typically small, greenish, and unisexual, and the leaves are often opposite with stipules. Several species are economically useful: ramie (Boehmeria nivea) is a fibre crop, and Pilea and Soleirolia are grown as ornamentals.

Classification

The APG IV system places Urticaceae in Rosales, as sister to the Moraceae (mulberry family). The family formerly recognised as Cecropiaceae is now included within Urticaceae.
