Wernher Magnus Maximilian Freiherr von Braun (23 March 1912 – 16 June 1977) was a German and later American aerospace engineer who was the leading figure in the development of rocket technology, first in Nazi Germany and then in the United States.

Career in Germany

Inspired by Hermann Oberth, von Braun joined the amateur Society for Space Travel (VfR) and in 1932 began working for the German Army's rocket programme. At Peenemünde he became technical director of the team that developed the V-2, the world's first long-range guided ballistic missile, used against London and Antwerp in 1944–45. Its production at Mittelwerk used concentration-camp labour, a fact that has permanently shadowed his reputation.

Career in the United States

In 1945 von Braun and much of his team surrendered to American forces and were brought to the United States under Operation Paperclip. Working for the U.S. Army in Huntsville, Alabama, his team developed the Jupiter-C rocket that launched the first American satellite, Explorer 1, in 1958. In 1960 the team transferred to the newly created NASA, where von Braun became the first director of the Marshall Space Flight Center and chief architect of the Saturn V launch vehicle that carried Apollo astronauts to the Moon.

Later life

Von Braun became a prominent public advocate of human spaceflight, collaborating with Walt Disney on popular television programmes in the 1950s. He left NASA in 1972 for private industry and died of cancer in 1977.
