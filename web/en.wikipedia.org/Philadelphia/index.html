Philadelphia, commonly called Philly, is the largest city in the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania and the sixth-most populous city in the United States. Founded by William Penn in 1682 as the capital of the Pennsylvania Colony, it played a central role in the American Revolution and early republic.

History

Penn designed Philadelphia — from the Greek for "brotherly love" — as a "greene country towne" of gridded streets and squares. It grew into the largest city in British North America. In 1774–1775 the Continental Congresses met there; the Declaration of Independence was signed in 1776 at the Pennsylvania State House (Independence Hall), and the city served as the revolutionary capital for most of the war despite British occupation in 1777–78. The Constitutional Convention of 1787 drafted the United States Constitution in the same building, and Philadelphia was the national capital from 1790 to 1800 while Washington, D.C. was built.

Economy and culture

Philadelphia became a great industrial and rail centre in the nineteenth century and remains a hub for education and medicine, with institutions including the University of Pennsylvania. Landmarks include Independence Hall and the Liberty Bell, the Philadelphia Museum of Art (with its "Rocky Steps"), Reading Terminal Market, and extensive collections of murals — the most of any American city. Famous foods include the cheesesteak, soft pretzels and water ice; major sports teams include the Eagles (NFL), Phillies (MLB), 76ers (NBA) and Flyers (NHL).
