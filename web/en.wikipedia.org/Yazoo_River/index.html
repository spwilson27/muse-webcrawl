Yazoo River

The Yazoo River is a river in western Mississippi, United States, formed by the confluence of the Tallahatchie and Yalobusha rivers at Greenwood. It runs generally south-southwest about 188 miles (303 km) — paralleling the Mississippi River — and joins it at Vicksburg. The river gives its name to the Yazoo lands and to the Mississippi Delta region between the two rivers.

History

The river is named for the Yazoo people, a Tunica-speaking group once living near its mouth, encountered by La Salle in 1682. The French built Fort St. Pierre on the river in 1719; it was destroyed in a Yazoo and Natchez attack in 1729. During the American Civil War, the Yazoo was strategically vital: Confederate forces sank the Union gunboat USS Cairo in the river in December 1862 — the first vessel sunk by an electrically detonated naval mine — and Union expeditions such as the Yazoo Pass Expedition (1863) tried to reach Vicksburg through its bayous. The Cairo was raised in 1964 and is displayed at Vicksburg National Military Park.

Geography and flooding

The Yazoo Basin (the "Delta") is a flat alluvial plain of rich cotton soil protected by levees. The river system is managed by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers' Yazoo Backwater Project and the Mississippi River and Tributaries Project; the long-contested Yazoo Pumps proposal to relieve backwater flooding — notably the 2019 flood that inundated half a million acres — has been a major environmental controversy.
