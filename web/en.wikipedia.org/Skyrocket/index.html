Skyrocket

A skyrocket is a type of firework that uses a self-propelled rocket motor to rise rapidly into the air before bursting. Typically mounted on a guiding stick, it carries a payload of stars, effects, and an explosive charge that produces an aerial display.

Construction

A classic skyrocket consists of a paper or plastic tube packed with black powder or another propellant composition, closed by a clay plug with a nozzle; a payload section containing coloured stars and a bursting charge; a cap; and a wooden or bamboo stick several times the length of the tube, which stabilizes the rocket in flight like the fletching of an arrow. A fuse ignites the motor. On burnout the bursting charge ignites, scattering the stars in a sphere.

History and use

Rockets originated in medieval China, where gunpowder warfare rockets and festive versions both developed, and spread through Asia and Europe. Rockets became staples of European fireworks displays by the early modern period and remain common at celebrations such as Bonfire Night in Britain, Diwali in India, and Independence Day in the United States. Because errant rockets can start fires and cause injuries, many jurisdictions regulate or restrict their sale, requiring launches from bottles or tubes and wide safety margins.
