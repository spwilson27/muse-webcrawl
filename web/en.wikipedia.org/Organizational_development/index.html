Organizational development

Organization development (OD) is the study and implementation of practices, systems, and techniques that affect organizational change, aiming to improve an organization's effectiveness and its members' well-being.

History

OD emerged in the 1940s and 1950s from the work of Kurt Lewin on group dynamics and action research, and from sensitivity training (T-groups) developed at the National Training Laboratories. Key contributors include Richard Beckhard, who coined the term in its modern management sense, and Warren Bennis.

Practice

Interventions range from diagnostic surveys and team building to process consultation, large-scale change, and culture change. OD is distinguished by its humanistic values, reliance on behavioural-science knowledge, and the use of change agents who facilitate rather than direct change. Critics question its measurable impact, but its methods influenced modern human-resource and change-management practice.
