Car classification is the grouping of automobiles by size, body style, purpose, and market segment. Governments and regulatory agencies define classes for taxation, safety, and emissions purposes, while manufacturers and the media use market segments such as subcompact, compact, mid-size, and full-size cars.

Classification systems

The European Commission uses lettered segments from A (mini cars) to J (sport utility vehicles). The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency classifies cars by interior volume. Euro NCAP groups cars for safety comparisons. Insurance, racing, and rental-car industries maintain their own schemes.

Body styles

Common body styles include the sedan (saloon), hatchback, estate/wagon, coupé, convertible, roadster, minivan/MPV, SUV and crossover, and pickup truck. Styling trends have blurred traditional boundaries, especially between sedans, hatchbacks, and crossovers.
