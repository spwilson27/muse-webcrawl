Flowering plant

Flowering plants are plants that bear flowers and fruits, and form the clade Angiospermae (from Greek words meaning "vessel" and "seed"). The term "angiosperm" is derived from the Greek words angeion ('container, vessel') and sperma ('seed'), meaning that the seeds are enclosed within a fruit. They are by far the most diverse group of land plants with 64 orders, 416 families, approximately 13,000 known genera and 300,000 known species. Angiosperms were formerly called Magnoliophyta, a division name still used by some systems.

Characteristics

The ancestors of flowering plants diverged from the common ancestor of all living gymnosperms before the end of the Carboniferous, about 300 million years ago. The closest fossil relatives of flowering plants are uncertain and contentious, but abundant angiosperm pollen and leaves appear in the fossil record of the Early Cretaceous, and the group diversified explosively during the Cretaceous — a rapid radiation Darwin called an "abominable mystery". Distinctive features include flowers, endosperm formed by double fertilisation, fruits enclosing the seeds, and reduced male and female gametophytes.

Diversity and classification

Traditional classifications divided angiosperms into monocotyledons and dicotyledons; phylogenetic work, summarised by the Angiosperm Phylogeny Group (APG IV system, 2016), recognises early-diverging lineages (the ANA grade: Amborellales, Nymphaeales, Austrobaileyales) followed by magnoliids, monocots, and the great eudicot radiation. Familiar families include the grass family Poaceae, the orchid family Orchidaceae (among the largest families by species), the legume family Fabaceae, and the daisy family Asteraceae.

Importance

Flowering plants dominate most terrestrial ecosystems and provide nearly all plant-based food for humans, directly or as livestock feed: cereals, legumes, fruits, vegetables, oils, sugar and many beverages derive from angiosperms, as do timber from hardwood trees, fibres such as cotton, and numerous medicines and ornamentals. Their flowers support pollinator communities, and their coevolution with insects is a major theme of evolutionary biology.
