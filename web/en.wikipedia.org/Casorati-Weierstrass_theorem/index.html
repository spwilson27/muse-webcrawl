Casorati-Weierstrass theorem

The Casorati-Weierstrass theorem is a result in complex analysis describing holomorphic functions near essential singularities: the image of any punctured neighbourhood of such a singularity is dense in the complex plane.

Statement

If f is holomorphic on a punctured neighbourhood of z0 and has an essential singularity there, then f maps that punctured neighbourhood onto a dense subset of C. Equivalently, for any complex value w and any neighbourhood of the singularity, there are points where f comes arbitrarily close to w.

Background

Isolated singularities of holomorphic functions are classified as removable, poles, or essential. Near a pole the function tends to infinity, but near an essential singularity its behaviour is far wilder, as this theorem and the stronger Picard theorems demonstrate. The result is associated with Felice Casorati, Karl Weierstrass, and Yulian Sokhotski.
