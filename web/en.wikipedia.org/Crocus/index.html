Crocus

Crocus is a genus of perennial flowering plants in the iris family, Iridaceae, comprising about 200 species native to woodland, scrub, and meadows from sea level to alpine tundra across Europe, North Africa, and Asia as far east as western China. Crocuses grow from corms and are prized as ornamental garden plants, flowering in autumn, winter, or early spring. The spice saffron is obtained from the stigmas of Crocus sativus.

Description

Crocus flowers are cup- or goblet-shaped, in shades of purple, lilac, yellow, and white, often with contrasting throats and striped or veined outer petals. The grass-like leaves, usually with a pale central stripe, appear with or after the flowers depending on the species. The plant dies back to the corm during its dormant season.

Cultivation and uses

Garden crocuses are among the most popular spring bulbs, planted in lawns, borders, and rock gardens. The large-flowered Dutch hybrids derive mainly from Crocus vernus. Saffron, harvested from the three crimson stigmas of each C. sativus flower, is among the most expensive spices in the world by weight. Crocuses should not be confused with the toxic autumn-flowering genus Colchicum, sometimes called "autumn crocus."
