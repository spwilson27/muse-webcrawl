Seattle is a seaport city on the West Coast of the United States. It is the seat of King County, Washington, and the largest city in both the state of Washington and the Pacific Northwest region of North America. With a 2020 population of 737,015, it is the 18th-largest city in the United States. The Seattle metropolitan area's population is 4.02 million, making it the 15th-largest in the United States. The city is situated on an isthmus between Puget Sound and Lake Washington. It is the northernmost major city in the United States, located about 100 miles (160 km) south of the Canadian border.

History

Archaeological excavations suggest that Native Americans have inhabited the Seattle area for at least 4,000 years. The first European to visit the area was George Vancouver in 1792. In 1851 a settlement was established at Alki Point, and after relocation to the eastern shore of Elliott Bay it took the name of Chief Sealth of the local Duwamish and Suquamish tribes. Logging was Seattle's first major industry, and the Klondike Gold Rush of the late 1890s made it a major trade and shipping center. Growth returned after World War II, partly because of the local Boeing company, which established Seattle as a center for aircraft manufacturing. The Seattle area has been a center for technology since the 1980s, home to Microsoft and, since 1994, Amazon. The stream of new software, biotechnology, and Internet companies led to an economic revival.

Geography and climate

Seattle lies between Puget Sound to the west and Lake Washington to the east, with the Cascade Range beyond. The terrain is hilly, carved by glaciers, and includes several of the city's named hills, including Capitol Hill, Queen Anne, and Beacon Hill. The climate is temperate oceanic, with cool, wet winters and mild, relatively dry summers; the city is famous for frequent cloud cover and drizzle, though its total annual rainfall is less than that of many East Coast cities.

Economy and culture

Major companies headquartered in the Seattle metropolitan area include Amazon, Microsoft (in nearby Redmond), Starbucks, Boeing's commercial airplane division, Costco, and Nordstrom. The city's music scene produced the grunge movement of the late 1980s and early 1990s, associated with bands such as Nirvana, Pearl Jam, Soundgarden, and Alice in Chains. Landmarks include the Space Needle, built for the 1962 World's Fair, Pike Place Market, and the Museum of Pop Culture.
