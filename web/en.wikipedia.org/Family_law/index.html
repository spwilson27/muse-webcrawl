Family law

ScopeMarriage, divorce, child custody and support, adoption, domestic relations
AdjudicationFamily courts or general civil courts, varying by jurisdiction
Governing principle (children)Best interests of the child
Family law governs legal relations within families and households.

Family law (also called domestic relations law) is an area of law that deals with family matters and domestic relations. It governs the formation and dissolution of marriages and civil partnerships, the legal rights and duties of spouses and parents, and the care, custody, and financial support of children.

Subject matter

Principal topics include marriage requirements and validity; divorce and annulment and their financial consequences, including division of marital property and spousal support (alimony); child custody and visitation, generally decided under a "best interests of the child" standard; child support, often set by statutory guidelines; adoption and surrogacy; paternity; and protective orders in cases of domestic violence. Related areas include juvenile justice and emancipation of minors.

Variation and reform

Family law varies substantially between jurisdictions: in the United States it is primarily state law, while many other countries codify it nationally, sometimes alongside religious personal-law systems governing marriage and inheritance for particular communities. Twentieth- and twenty-first-century reforms have included no-fault divorce, recognition of same-sex marriage in many jurisdictions, and a shift from maternal or paternal presumptions toward shared-parenting arrangements. International aspects, such as cross-border child abduction, are addressed by the Hague Convention of 1980. See also divorce, child custody, and marriage law.
