Sternocleidomastoid muscle

OriginManubrium of the sternum and medial clavicle
InsertionMastoid process of the temporal bone; superior nuchal line
NerveAccessory nerve (CN XI); ventral rami of C2–C3
ActionsFlexion and rotation of the head; accessory muscle of inspiration

The sternocleidomastoid muscle is a long, thick strap muscle of the neck, running obliquely from the sternum and clavicle up to the mastoid process behind the ear. It is one of the most prominent superficial muscles of the body and divides each side of the neck into anterior and posterior triangles, a division of great importance in surgery and clinical examination.

Structure

The muscle arises by two heads: a rounded sternal head from the front of the manubrium sterni, and a broader clavicular head from the medial third of the clavicle. The two heads blend and ascend to insert on the lateral surface of the mastoid process and the lateral half of the superior nuchal line of the occipital bone. It is supplied by the spinal accessory nerve, with proprioceptive fibres from the second and third cervical nerves.

Function

Acting alone, the muscle rotates the head to the opposite side and tilts it toward the same shoulder. Acting together, the two muscles flex the neck forward. With the head fixed, they assist in elevating the sternum during forced inspiration, making the muscle an accessory respiratory muscle recruited in respiratory distress.

Clinical significance

Spasmodic contraction or congenital shortening of the muscle produces torticollis ("wry neck"). The muscle is a landmark for central venous catheterisation of the internal jugular vein, which lies deep to it, and for locating the external jugular vein, which crosses it superficially. Its border also guides dissection in neck surgery, where injury to the accessory nerve must be avoided.
