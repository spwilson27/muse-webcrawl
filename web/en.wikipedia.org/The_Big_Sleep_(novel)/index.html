The Big Sleep (1939) is a hardboiled crime novel by American-British writer Raymond Chandler, the first to feature the detective Philip Marlowe. The plot is notoriously convoluted, woven from Chandler's earlier pulp short stories — principally "Killer in the Rain" (1935) and "The Curtain" (1936) — a technique he called "cannibalising". It is regarded as a landmark of detective fiction; in 1999 it was named among Le Monde's 100 Books of the Century and the Modern Library's 100 best English-language novels of the twentieth century.

Plot summary

Private detective Philip Marlowe is hired by the dying, wealthy General Sternwood to resolve a blackmail attempt against his wild younger daughter, Carmen. The blackmailer, bookseller Arthur Geiger, is soon found murdered — with Carmen drugged at the scene and the compromising photographs missing. Marlowe's investigation threads through a pornography racket, a missing son-in-law (Rusty Regan), a casino run by Eddie Mars, and a series of further killings, culminating in the revelation that Carmen killed Regan and that her elder sister Vivian has been protecting her. Marlowe arranges for Carmen to be committed to an institution rather than exposed. The title refers to death: "the big sleep".

The unsolved chauffeur

Famously, the novel never makes clear who killed the Sternwoods' chauffeur, Owen Taylor, found dead in a car driven off the Lido pier. When the screenwriters of the 1946 film adaptation cabled Chandler to ask, he replied that he did not know either.

Adaptations

The novel was filmed in 1946, directed by Howard Hawks and starring Humphrey Bogart and Lauren Bacall, and again in 1978 with Robert Mitchum, in a version transposed to England.
