Vice admiral

Vice admiral is a senior naval flag officer rank, equivalent to lieutenant general in armies and air marshals in air forces. A vice admiral is typically senior to a rear admiral and junior to an admiral. In the United States Navy and Coast Guard, vice admiral is a three-star rank (pay grade O-9), and appointments to the grade require confirmation by the Senate; by statute, the number of officers who may hold the rank at any one time is limited. In the Royal Navy, the substantive rank of vice admiral has existed since the sixteenth century, when the vice admiral commanded the van squadron of a fleet.

History

The rank derives from the role of deputy to the admiral commanding a fleet. In the Tudor navy, squadrons were divided into van, centre and rear, commanded respectively by the vice admiral, the admiral and the rear admiral — an arrangement that gave all three flag ranks their names. Vice admirals of the United Kingdom also once held ceremonial maritime roles in coastal counties.
