Tina Fey

Elizabeth Stamatina "Tina" Fey (born May 18, 1970) is an American comedian, writer, producer and actress. She was the first female head writer of Saturday Night Live, created and starred in the NBC sitcom 30 Rock, and co-created the Netflix series Unbreakable Kimmy Schmidt.

Career

Fey studied drama at the University of Virginia and trained at Chicago's Second City, where she met Amy Poehler. She joined Saturday Night Live as a writer in 1997, became head writer in 1999, and co-anchored Weekend Update from 2000. Her 2008 impersonations of vice-presidential candidate Sarah Palin won wide acclaim and an Emmy. She wrote the film Mean Girls (2004), later adapted into a Broadway musical. 30 Rock (2006–2013), loosely based on her SNL experience, won multiple Emmy Awards, including Outstanding Comedy Series three times. Her memoir Bossypants (2011) was a bestseller.

Awards

Fey has won nine Primetime Emmy Awards, three Golden Globe Awards, five Screen Actors Guild Awards and seven Writers Guild of America Awards, and received the Mark Twain Prize for American Humor in 2010, at the time its youngest recipient.
