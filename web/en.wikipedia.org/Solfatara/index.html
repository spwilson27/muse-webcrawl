Solfatara

A solfatara is a volcanic vent or fumarole that emits sulfurous gases, named after the Solfatara crater in the Phlegraean Fields near Pozzuoli, Italy. Solfatara (Italian for "sulfur mine") is a shallow volcanic crater whose floor is covered with mud pools, jets of steam, and sulfur-encrusted fumaroles.

The Italian crater

The Solfatara crater formed about 3,800 years ago and was known in antiquity; the Romans associated it with Vulcan and the entrance to the underworld. Its largest fumarole, the Bocca Grande, emits superheated steam mixed with carbon dioxide and hydrogen sulfide at around 160 °C. The crater has been used for sulfur and alum extraction and, in the nineteenth century, for medicinal mud baths.

Term in volcanology

By extension, volcanologists call any fumarolic field dominated by sulfur emissions a solfatara, and the stage of declining volcanic activity characterised by such emissions the "solfataric stage".
