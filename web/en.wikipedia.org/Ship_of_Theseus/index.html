Ship of Theseus

Ship of Theseus
TypeThought experiment / paradox of identity

The Ship of Theseus is a thought experiment about identity over time, first recorded by the Greek historian Plutarch. The Athenians preserved the ship in which the hero Theseus was said to have sailed to Crete by continually replacing its rotting planks with new timber — until, philosophers debated, no original plank remained. The question: is the fully repaired ship still Theseus's ship? Thomas Hobbes later sharpened the puzzle by imagining the discarded planks reassembled into a second ship, asking which of the two, if either, is the original.

The paradox

The puzzle exposes competing criteria for identity: sameness of material, sameness of form and design, continuity of function, and spatiotemporal continuity. The gradually repaired ship wins on continuity of form and custody; the reassembled ship wins on sameness of parts. Since both cannot uniquely be the original — identity being one-to-one — something in our assumptions must give. The paradox is a special case of the broader problem of "identity through change" that applies equally to organisms (whose cells are replaced), people, institutions, and artefacts.

Proposed resolutions

Philosophers have answered in many ways. Aristotelians appeal to form and function: the ship persists while its structure and purpose do. Four-dimensionalists hold that objects are extended through time as sequences of temporal parts, both ships sharing early stages. Others accept relative identity, deny that artefacts have strict identity conditions, or treat "the same ship" as a matter of convention and purpose. John Locke applied a related analysis to personal identity, grounding it in continuity of consciousness rather than of substance.

In culture

The paradox recurs across popular culture — in discussions of restored classic cars, grandfather's axe ("Trigger's broom" in the British sitcom Only Fools and Horses), band line-ups, and software systems — as the standard illustration of how identity survives the replacement of parts.
