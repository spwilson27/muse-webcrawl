Single-reed instrument

A single-reed instrument is a woodwind instrument that produces sound using a single reed — a thin strip of cane (or synthetic material) mounted on a mouthpiece. When the player blows, air pressure and the player's embouchure set the reed vibrating against the mouthpiece, periodically interrupting the airflow and exciting resonances in the instrument's bore.

Principal instruments

The main single-reed families are the clarinets and the saxophones. Clarinets have a largely cylindrical bore, overblow at the twelfth, and are mostly made of wood; saxophones have a conical bore, overblow at the octave, and are made of brass, yet are classed as woodwinds because of their reed. Historically, folk single-reed instruments include the chalumeau (ancestor of the clarinet), the Welsh pibgorn, various bagpipe chanters, and the ancient Greek aulos's rivals — though the aulos itself used double reeds.

Reeds are graded by stiffness ("strength"), and players adjust reed choice to mouthpiece and style. In the Hornbostel–Sachs classification, single-reed instruments fall among the reed aerophones with the clarinet-type (422.2) group.
