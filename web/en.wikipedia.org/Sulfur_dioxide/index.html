Sulfur dioxide

Sulfur dioxide (IUPAC-recommended spelling: sulphur dioxide, systematic name sulfur(IV) oxide) is the chemical compound with the formula SO2. It is a colorless gas with a pungent, irritating, and rotten smell. It is produced by volcanoes and in various industrial processes. Coal and petroleum often contain sulfur compounds whose combustion generates sulfur dioxide.

Structure and bonding

The molecule is bent, with an O–S–O angle of about 119°. Sulfur is in the oxidation state +4. Sulfur dioxide is a Lewis acid and forms sulfites and bisulfites in aqueous solution; dissolved in water it gives sulfurous acid.

Production and uses

Sulfur dioxide is produced by burning sulfur or sulfur-containing ores such as pyrite, usually as an intermediate in the manufacture of sulfuric acid by the contact process. It is used as a preservative and antioxidant in winemaking and dried fruit (where it may be labelled E220), as a refrigerant historically, and as a bleaching agent.

Environmental and health effects

Sulfur dioxide is a major air pollutant. In the atmosphere it oxidises to sulfur trioxide and then sulfuric acid, a principal component of acid rain. It irritates the eyes and respiratory tract and can aggravate asthma. Emissions have been sharply reduced in many countries by flue-gas desulfurisation and low-sulfur fuels. Large volcanic eruptions can inject sulfur dioxide into the stratosphere, where the resulting sulfate aerosols cool the global climate.
