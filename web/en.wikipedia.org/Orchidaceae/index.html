Orchidaceae

The Orchidaceae — the orchid family — are a diverse and widespread family of flowering plants, with blooms that are often colourful and fragrant. With roughly 28,000 accepted species in about 700–900 genera, it is one of the two largest families of flowering plants, rivalling the Asteraceae.

Characteristics

Orchids are perennial herbs, terrestrial or epiphytic, with dust-like seeds that require mycorrhizal fungi to germinate. Their flowers are bilaterally symmetric, with three petals and three sepals, a specialised labellum, and pollen packaged into pollinia. Many species are highly adapted to particular pollinators, a relationship Darwin studied in his 1862 book on orchid fertilisation.

Ecology and distribution

The family occurs on every continent except Antarctica, with the greatest diversity in the tropics of the Americas and Asia. Epiphytic orchids anchor to trees in rainforest canopies, absorbing moisture through aerial roots covered in velamen.

Human uses

Orchids have been cultivated for centuries as ornamentals; the horticultural trade is dominated by hybrids of Phalaenopsis, Cattleya and Dendrobium. The family also yields vanilla, from the pods of Vanilla planifolia, one of the world's most valuable flavourings. All orchids are listed under CITES to regulate international trade.
