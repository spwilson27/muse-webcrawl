Narasimhavarman I (reigned c. 630–668 CE), also known by his title Mamalla ("great wrestler"), was a king of the Pallava dynasty of southern India. He avenged his father Mahendravarman I's defeats by the Chalukya king Pulakeshin II and raised the Pallava kingdom to the height of its power.

Wars with the Chalukyas

Narasimhavarman defeated Pulakeshin II in a series of battles — including at Pariyala, Manimangala and Suramara — and in 642 CE captured and sacked the Chalukya capital Vatapi (Badami), earning the title Vatapikondan ("conqueror of Vatapi"). Pallava forces, aided by the exiled Sri Lankan prince Manavamma, also intervened in Sri Lanka; Manavamma later became king of Anuradhapura with Pallava support.

Art and architecture

His reign is celebrated for the rock-cut temples and monolithic rathas at Mamallapuram (Mahabalipuram), the port city named after his title Mamalla. Masterpieces of his era include the Panamalai temple paintings and the continuing development of early Dravidian temple architecture carved from living rock.
