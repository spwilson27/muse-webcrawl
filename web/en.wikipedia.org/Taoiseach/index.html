Taoiseach

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Taoiseach (Irish: [ˈt̪ˠiːʃəx]) is the head of government of Ireland. The office was created in 1937 under the Constitution of Ireland, replacing the office of President of the Executive Council of the Irish Free State. The Taoiseach is appointed by the President of Ireland upon nomination by Dáil Éireann, the lower house of the Oireachtas (parliament), and must retain the support of a majority in the Dáil to remain in office.

Role and powers

The Taoiseach is the de facto chief executive of the Irish state: they nominate the other members of the Government for appointment by the President and approval by the Dáil, can advise the President to dismiss ministers, and nominate eleven members of Seanad Éireann. The Taoiseach is also responsible for the Department of the Taoiseach, which coordinates the work of government. By convention the Taoiseach is the leader of the largest party in the governing coalition.

History

The word taoiseach derives from Old Irish toísech, meaning "leader" or "chief", a term for a clan chief in early Irish law. Éamon de Valera was the first holder of the office under the 1937 Constitution, serving three separate terms totalling over fifteen years. Notable later Taoisigh include Seán Lemass, Jack Lynch, Garret FitzGerald, Charles Haughey, Bertie Ahern, Enda Kenny and Leo Varadkar.

The official residence of the Taoiseach is Steward's Lodge at Farmleigh, though it is used mainly for official entertaining rather than as a home.
