Huntington Library

The Huntington Library, Art Museum and Botanical Gardens, known generally as The Huntington, is a collections-based educational and research institution established by the railroad magnate Henry E. Huntington (1850–1927) and his wife Arabella in San Marino, California. The institution houses an extensive research library, art collections, and 120 acres (49 ha) of botanical gardens.

Library

The research library holds some 11 million items dating from the 11th to the 21st century, with strengths in British and American history and literature. Treasures include one of the eleven known copies of the Gutenberg Bible printed on vellum, the Ellesmere manuscript of Chaucer's Canterbury Tales, Benjamin Franklin's handwritten autobiography draft, a first folio of Shakespeare, and the papers of figures such as George Washington and Abraham Lincoln. The library is a major center for the study of the history of science and of the American West.

Art collections

The art museum is noted for its British portraits, including Thomas Gainsborough's The Blue Boy (c. 1770) and Thomas Lawrence's Pinkie (1794), long displayed as pendants, as well as American art and European decorative arts. Gainsborough's Blue Boy is among the most famous paintings in the United States.

Botanical gardens

The botanical gardens cover about 120 acres and include themed gardens — the Desert Garden, Japanese Garden, Chinese Garden (Liu Fang Yuan, the Garden of Flowing Fragrance), rose gardens, and jungle and subtropical gardens. The Desert Garden is one of the oldest and largest outdoor collections of cacti and succulents in the world.
