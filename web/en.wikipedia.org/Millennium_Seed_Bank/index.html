The Millennium Seed Bank (MSB) is an international conservation project coordinated by the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew. Housed in the Wellcome Trust Millennium Building at Wakehurst Place in West Sussex, it is the world's largest ex situ plant conservation programme: a seed bank storing seeds from tens of thousands of wild plant species in bomb-proof, flood-proof underground vaults, as insurance against extinction in the wild.

The Millennium Building at Wakehurst contains laboratories, a public visitor area and the underground seed vaults.

History and goals

Kew's seed banking began in 1974 at Wakehurst; the Millennium Seed Bank project was launched with Millennium Commission funding in the 1990s and the building opened in 2000. Its first phase, the Millennium Seed Bank Project, aimed by 2010 to bank seeds of 10 per cent of the world's wild plant species — a target reached in October 2009 with the banking of a Chinese pink banana (Musa itinerans) as the 24,200th species. The follow-on goal was to reach 25 per cent of the world's bankable flora by 2020, emphasising plants that are threatened, endemic or useful to communities facing climate change. Partners in more than 100 countries collect seeds in the field, keep duplicates in their home countries, and send duplicate collections to Wakehurst.

How seeds are stored

Collections arrive with herbarium vouchers and field data. Seeds are cleaned, counted or weighed, dried to about 5 per cent moisture in controlled dry rooms, and sealed in glass jars or foil packets inside vaults held at −20 °C, conditions under which most "orthodox" seeds remain viable for decades to centuries. Samples are periodically germination-tested. The bank works alongside facilities such as Norway's Svalbard Global Seed Vault, which concentrates on crop varieties, while Kew focuses on wild species. Research at the bank covers seed longevity, dormancy and germination, and banked material supports habitat restoration and reintroduction around the world.
