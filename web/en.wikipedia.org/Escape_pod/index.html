Escape pod

Escape pods are a staple of science fiction: small survival craft for abandoning a stricken vessel.

An escape pod, also called a life pod or lifeboat, is a small capsule or craft used to escape from a spaceship or aircraft in an emergency, typically when the parent vessel is about to be destroyed or rendered uninhabitable. The concept is most prominent in science fiction, though real-world analogues exist in ejection systems and crew rescue vehicles.

In fiction

Escape pods appear prominently in Star Wars (the pod carrying C-3PO and R2-D2 in the 1977 film), Star Trek, Alien (the Narcissus lifeboat), 2001: A Space Odyssey (the EVA pods), and many other franchises. They are usually depicted with limited propulsion and life support, sufficient to reach a nearby planet or await rescue, and in many stories serve as the device that delivers castaway characters to the setting of the plot.

Real-world analogues

Aircraft escape capsules were fitted to some military aircraft: early B-58 Hustler and XB-70 Valkyrie crews sat in encapsulated ejection seats or crew escape capsules usable at high supersonic speeds, and the F-111 used a detachable crew module rather than individual ejection seats. For spacecraft, proposals have included the MOSES and other station lifeboats; the Soyuz spacecraft docked to the International Space Station serves as its lifeboat, and the station's crew return has always relied on a docked vehicle rather than standalone pods. Launch escape systems, such as tractor towers and pusher abort motors on crewed capsules, perform a related function during ascent.
