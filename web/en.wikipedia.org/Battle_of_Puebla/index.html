The Battle of Puebla (Spanish: Batalla de Puebla) took place on May 5, 1862, near Puebla City during the second French intervention in Mexico. A smaller, poorly equipped Mexican army under General Ignacio Zaragoza defeated a larger and better-equipped French force. The date is commemorated annually as Cinco de Mayo.

Background

France, under Napoleon III, intervened in Mexico after the Mexican government suspended foreign debt payments. French troops landed at Veracruz and advanced inland toward Mexico City, intending ultimately to install a French-friendly monarchy.

The battle

The French army under Count Charles de Lorencez attacked the Mexican positions on the hills of Loreto and Guadalupe outside Puebla. Repeated French assaults failed against the fortified heights and were hampered by a heavy hailstorm that turned the battlefield to mud. A Mexican cavalry counterattack drove the French from the field, and they retreated toward the coast.

Aftermath

The victory delayed the French advance by a year and became a powerful symbol of Mexican resistance and national pride. The French returned with a much larger army, captured Puebla in 1863, and installed Maximilian I as emperor, but the 1862 victory remains the best-remembered engagement of the intervention. Cinco de Mayo is celebrated in Puebla and widely in the United States, though it is not Mexico's independence day.
