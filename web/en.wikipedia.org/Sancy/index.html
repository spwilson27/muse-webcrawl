Sancy

The Sancy is a famous pale yellow diamond of 55.23 carats (11.05 grams), cut in a distinctive shield-shaped double rose cut with no pavilion — a modification of the pear cut. It is one of the great historic diamonds of Europe and is now displayed in the Galerie d'Apollon of the Louvre Museum in Paris, alongside the French Crown Jewels.

History

The stone is believed to have been mined in India and cut in the 15th or 16th century. It takes its name from Nicolas de Harlay, seigneur de Sancy, a French diplomat and treasurer who acquired it in the later 16th century; legend holds that a messenger carrying the gem was murdered but the diamond was recovered from his stomach, he having swallowed it for safekeeping. Sancy sold the stone to James I of England in 1605; it appears in the 1605 Tower of London inventory.

The diamond later passed back into French hands and belonged to Cardinal Mazarin, becoming one of the "Mazarin diamonds" bequeathed to the French Crown. It was stolen during the robbery of the Garde-Meuble in 1792, during the French Revolution, and its whereabouts were unknown for decades before it resurfaced in the 19th century, passing through the hands of the Demidov family and the Astor family before being sold to the Louvre in 1978.

Characteristics

Weighing 55.23 carats, the Sancy is notable for its unusual two-sided crown of triangular facets and slight yellow tint. A smaller stone, the Beau Sancy (34.98 carats), was cut from the same rough and was sold at auction in 2012.
