Fire-and-forget

Fire-and-forget is a type of missile guidance that requires no further external input after launch, allowing the operator to take cover, reposition, or engage another target while the weapon guides itself. The term is also used more broadly in computing and business for actions initiated without waiting for confirmation of the result.

Missile guidance

Fire-and-forget weapons use onboard seekers — such as infrared homing, active radar homing, or imaging sensors — or pre-programmed inertial and satellite guidance to reach their target autonomously. Examples include the AIM-120 AMRAAM air-to-air missile, which is guided by mid-course updates only until its own radar activates, and the FGM-148 Javelin anti-tank missile, whose infrared seeker locks on before launch and lets the gunner move immediately after firing. The contrast is with command-guided or beam-riding weapons, and with "fire-and-update" systems that retain a data link to the launcher.

Advantages and limitations

The chief advantage is survivability: the launching platform is not exposed while guiding the weapon. Limitations include higher unit cost, the possibility of the seeker losing or misidentifying the target without human correction, and vulnerability to countermeasures such as flares and jamming.

Other uses

In computing, fire-and-forget describes one-way communication patterns, such as sending a UDP datagram or issuing an asynchronous request whose response the sender does not await.
