Organisation Todt

Organisation Todt built much of Germany's wartime infrastructure, including the Atlantic Wall.

Organisation Todt (OT) was a civil and military engineering organisation in Nazi Germany, named after its founder Fritz Todt, responsible for a vast range of engineering projects in Germany and in occupied Europe during the Second World War.

History

Todt, appointed Inspector General for German Roadways, had directed the construction of the Reichsautobahn network. In 1938 he founded Organisation Todt to build the Westwall (Siegfried Line) defences on Germany's western border. During the war the organisation's remit expanded enormously: it built the Atlantic Wall fortifications along the coasts of occupied Europe, submarine pens, airfields, roads, railways, and the launching sites for V-weapons, and it repaired bomb damage inside the Reich.

Leadership and labour

After Fritz Todt died in a plane crash in February 1942, Albert Speer took over the organisation along with Todt's ministerial posts. OT's workforce grew to roughly 1.4 million people by 1944. The great majority were not volunteers: the organisation depended on conscripted foreign labourers, prisoners of war, and concentration-camp inmates, many of whom worked and died in brutal conditions — a fact central to its record and to post-war trials.

Legacy

Organisation Todt epitomises the close integration of engineering, the Nazi state, and forced labour. Its surviving constructions, from Atlantic Wall bunkers to U-boat pens, remain prominent features of the European landscape.
