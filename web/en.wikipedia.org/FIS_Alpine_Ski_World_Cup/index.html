The FIS Alpine Ski World Cup is the top international circuit of alpine skiing competitions, held annually since 1967. Racers compete in a season-long series of races — principally in Europe and North America from late October to March — accumulating points toward overall and discipline titles, which are awarded with crystal globes.

Format

Events are held in five disciplines: downhill and super-G (the speed events), giant slalom and slalom (the technical events), and alpine combined. Race winners receive 100 points, with points descending to 1 point for 30th place. The skier with the most points at season's end wins the overall title; separate globes are awarded per discipline. Famous venues include Kitzbühel's Hahnenkamm downhill, the Lauberhorn at Wengen, Sölden, Val d'Isère, and Beaver Creek.

History and records

The circuit was conceived by Serge Lang, Honoré Bonnet, and Bob Beattie and launched in 1967, with Jean-Claude Killy and Nancy Greene as the first overall champions. Ingemar Stenmark holds the men's record of 86 race wins; Mikaela Shiffrin surpassed that mark to become the most successful World Cup skier overall, while Lindsey Vonn and Annemarie Moser-Pröll are among the most successful women. Marcel Hirscher won eight consecutive men's overall titles. See also alpine skiing and FIS.
