National Trust

The National Trust for Places of Historic Interest or Natural Beauty, commonly known as the National Trust, is a charity and membership organisation for heritage conservation in England, Wales, and Northern Ireland. (Scotland has its own National Trust for Scotland.)

Founded1895
FoundersOctavia Hill, Robert Hunter, Hardwicke Rawnsley
MembersOver 5 million
Holdings500+ historic places; ~780 miles of coastline; ~250,000 hectares of land

History

Founded in 1895, the Trust acquired its first building, the Clergy House at Alfriston, in 1896. The National Trust Act 1907 gave it the unique power to declare land inalienable. The country house schemes of the 1930s–50s brought hundreds of great houses into its care.

Work

The Trust cares for stately homes, castles, gardens, industrial heritage, nature reserves, and large areas of coast and countryside, funded by membership, donations, legacies, and commercial income. It is one of Britain's largest landowners and a major conservation charity.
