Paul Klee

Paul Klee (18 December 1879 – 29 June 1940) was a Swiss-born German artist whose highly individual style was influenced by expressionism, cubism, and surrealism. His lectures at the Bauhaus, published as the Pedagogical Sketchbook, are foundational texts of modern art theory.

Life and career

Born in Münchenbuchsee near Bern, Klee studied art in Munich and joined Wassily Kandinsky's Der Blaue Reiter circle. A 1914 trip to Tunisia transformed his sense of colour: "Colour possesses me," he wrote. After army service in the First World War, he taught at the Bauhaus in Weimar and Dessau (1921–1931) alongside Kandinsky. Dismissed by the Nazi regime in 1933 as a "degenerate" artist, he returned to Bern, where he died in 1940.

Work

Klee produced some 9,000 works, often small in scale, combining drawing, colour theory, music, and wit — paintings such as Senecio (1922), Twittering Machine (1922), and Ad Parnassum (1932). The Zentrum Paul Klee in Bern holds the largest collection of his work.
