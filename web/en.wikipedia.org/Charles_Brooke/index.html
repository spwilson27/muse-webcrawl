Charles Anthony Johnson Brooke (3 June 1829 – 17 May 1917) was the second of the White Rajahs of Sarawak, ruling the Raj of Sarawak in Borneo from 1868 until his death. The nephew and heir of the founder, James Brooke, he consolidated the state, extended its territory, and governed autocratically for nearly half a century.

Early life and service

Born Charles Johnson, he was the son of Emma Johnson, sister of James Brooke. After service as a midshipman in the Royal Navy, he joined his uncle in Sarawak in 1852, took the name Brooke, and worked as a Resident in the Lundu district. He became James Brooke's right hand, playing a leading role in suppressing the Chinese miners' uprising of 1857 and in campaigns against Iban resistance, including against the leader Rentap.

Reign

Succeeding his uncle in 1868, Charles Brooke ruled in a famously austere, hands-on manner. He extended Sarawak's territory northward in stages — the Baram in 1882, the Trusan in 1884–85, the Limbang in 1890, and the Lawas in 1905 — much of it at the expense of the declining Sultanate of Brunei. He kept administration tiny and inexpensive, entrusted governance partly to Malay and Iban chiefs, forbade Christian missionaries from proselytising among Muslims, and restricted foreign capital, keeping Sarawak free of large plantation interests during his lifetime. He was also known for suppressing headhunting and slavery within the state.

Personal life and death

In 1869 he married Margaret de Windt, thereafter Margaret, Ranee of Sarawak, who wrote the memoir My Life in Sarawak. Their surviving son, Charles Vyner Brooke, succeeded him as third Rajah in 1917. Charles Brooke died at his home at Fairford Park, Gloucestershire, in May 1917 and is buried at Berrow, Somerset, near his uncle.
