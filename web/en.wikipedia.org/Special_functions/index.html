Special functions

Special functions are particular mathematical functions that have more or less established names and notations because of their importance in mathematical analysis, functional analysis, geometry, physics or other applications. The term is conventional rather than rigorous: there is no single definition, but the recognised special functions share the property that they arise naturally as solutions of important differential equations or integrals and cannot be expressed in terms of elementary functions alone.

Examples

The classical special functions include the gamma function, which extends the factorial; the Bessel functions, arising in wave propagation and heat conduction in cylindrical geometry; the Legendre and Hermite polynomials and other orthogonal polynomials; the error function of probability theory; the elliptic integrals and elliptic functions; the hypergeometric function, which unifies many of the others as special cases; and the Riemann zeta function of number theory. In the twentieth century the list expanded to include distributions such as the Dirac delta function, and families such as the Mathieu and Painlevé transcendents.

Tables and computation

Because engineers and physicists constantly needed numerical values, special functions were compiled in great tables, most famously Abramowitz and Stegun's Handbook of Mathematical Functions (1964), now succeeded by the online NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions. Today the functions are built into computer algebra systems and numerical libraries, which evaluate them through series, continued fractions, asymptotic expansions and recurrence relations chosen according to the argument's size.
