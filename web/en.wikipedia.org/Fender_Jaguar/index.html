Fender Jaguar

The Fender Jaguar, introduced in 1962, became a favourite of surf, punk, and alternative rock guitarists.

The Fender Jaguar is an electric guitar that has been manufactured by Fender since 1962. It is characterised by its offset-waist body, short 24-inch scale length, floating tremolo system, and an unusually complex control system with multiple switches and rollers, derived from the earlier Jazzmaster.

Marketed initially at surf music guitarists as Fender's top-of-the-line model, the Jaguar sold poorly compared to the Stratocaster and Telecaster and was discontinued in 1975. Cheap second-hand Jaguars were adopted by punk and alternative rock musicians in the late 1970s and 1980s, and its popularity surged in the 1990s through players such as Kurt Cobain of Nirvana and John Frusciante of the Red Hot Chili Peppers. Fender resumed production with reissues beginning in 1999.

Design

The Jaguar features two single-coil pickups with metal "claw" shielding plates, a lead/rhythm circuit switch, individual pickup on/off switches, and a bass-cut ("strangle") switch. Its short scale and flexible tremolo give it a distinctive feel and sound. Signature models have been produced for Johnny Marr and Kurt Cobain, among others.
