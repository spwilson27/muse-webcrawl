Planescape is a campaign setting for the Dungeons & Dragons fantasy role-playing game, developed by TSR and released in 1994. The setting spans the entire multiverse of the D&D cosmology, centring on the city of Sigil, the "City of Doors", which sits atop the infinitely tall Spire at the centre of the neutral plane known as the Outlands.

Planescape is noted for its unusual tone, its philosophical themes, and the distinctive visual style created by artist Tony DiTerlizzi. Player characters explore the Great Wheel of planes, encountering angels, demons, and creatures of every alignment, while belief itself has tangible power to reshape reality.

Setting

At the heart of the setting is Sigil, a torus-shaped city ruled by the enigmatic Lady of Pain, who keeps even the gods out. Sigil contains portals to every plane and world, making it a crossroads for travellers, merchants, and exiles from across the multiverse. The city's factions — organisations such as the Athar, the Believers of the Source, the Dustmen, the Sensates, and the Xaositects — compete over philosophies that literally shape the planes around them.

Publications and video games

The line began with the Planescape Campaign Setting boxed set in 1994 and was supported by numerous supplements and adventures, including The Eternal Boundary, Harbinger House, and Dead Gods. The setting also inspired the 1999 computer role-playing game Planescape: Torment, developed by Black Isle Studios, which is widely regarded as one of the greatest video games of all time for its focus on story and dialogue.

Legacy

Although the Planescape line ended in 1998, its cosmology influenced later editions of Dungeons & Dragons. Wizards of the Coast revisited the setting with the 2023 release Planescape: Adventures in the Multiverse for the game's fifth edition.
