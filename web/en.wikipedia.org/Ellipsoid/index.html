Ellipsoid

An ellipsoid is a surface that can be obtained from a sphere by deforming it using directional scalings, or more generally, by means of an affine transformation. An ellipsoid is a quadric surface; it is defined as the zero set of a polynomial of degree two in three variables. The standard equation of an ellipsoid centred at the origin is x²/a² + y²/b² + z²/c² = 1, where a, b, and c are the lengths of the semi-principal axes.

Special cases

When two of the semi-axes are equal, the ellipsoid is a spheroid: an oblate spheroid if the equal axes are the longer ones (like the shape of the Earth), and a prolate spheroid if they are shorter. A sphere is the special case in which all three semi-axes are equal.

Applications

Reference ellipsoids are used in geodesy as mathematical models of the Earth's shape for mapmaking and satellite navigation. Ellipsoids also model the shapes of many small Solar System bodies in hydrostatic equilibrium and appear in the study of moments of inertia and gravitational fields.
