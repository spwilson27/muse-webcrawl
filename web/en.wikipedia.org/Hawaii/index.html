Hawaii

Capital: Honolulu
Admitted to the Union: August 21, 1959 (50th)
Population: ~1.45 million
Largest city: Honolulu

Hawaii (Hawaiian: Hawaiʻi) is an island state of the United States, in the Pacific Ocean about 2,000 miles (3,200 km) southwest of the U.S. mainland. It is the only U.S. state that is not on the North American mainland, and is composed entirely of islands. Hawaii was admitted to the union in 1959 as the 50th state.

The state encompasses the Hawaiian archipelago, formed by volcanic activity over a hotspot in the Earth's mantle. The eight main islands, from northwest to southeast, are Niʻihau, Kauaʻi, Oʻahu, Molokaʻi, Lānaʻi, Kahoʻolawe, Maui, and Hawaiʻi, after which the state is named.

History

Polynesian voyagers settled the islands roughly a thousand years ago. In 1778 Captain James Cook became the first recorded European to visit. King Kamehameha I united most of the islands into the Kingdom of Hawaii by 1795. The monarchy was overthrown in 1893 by American and European businessmen, and the Republic of Hawaii was annexed by the United States in 1898. The 1941 attack on Pearl Harbor brought the U.S. into World War II. Statehood followed in 1959 after a popular referendum.

Geography and climate

Hawaii's land area is still growing: Kīlauea volcano on the Big Island has added land through eruptions, including the major 2018 lower Puna eruption. Mauna Kea, a dormant volcano, is the state's highest point at 13,803 feet (4,207 m) above sea level—and the tallest mountain on Earth measured from its base on the ocean floor. The climate is tropical, moderated by trade winds, with abundant rainfall on windward slopes.

Economy and culture

Tourism is the largest industry, followed by military spending and agriculture (notably coffee, macadamia nuts, and pineapple historically). Native Hawaiian culture, language, and the hula have experienced a major renaissance since the 1970s. Hawaii has the highest proportion of multiracial and Asian American residents of any U.S. state.
