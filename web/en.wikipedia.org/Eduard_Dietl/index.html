Eduard Dietl

Eduard Wohlrath Christian Dietl (21 July 1890 – 23 June 1944) was a German general during the Second World War. He commanded the 3rd Mountain Division during the 1940 invasion of Norway, where his troops held Narvik against Allied forces, earning him wide publicity in Germany and the Knight's Cross with Oak Leaves. He later commanded German forces in northern Finland and the Arctic during Operation Barbarossa and the Continuation War.

Early life and First World War

Born in Bad Aibling, Bavaria, Dietl joined the Bavarian Army in 1909 and served as an infantry officer during the First World War, being wounded several times and decorated with the Iron Cross. He remained in the Reichswehr after the war and became an early member of the Nazi movement; he was among the small group involved in the failed Beer Hall Putsch of 1923.

Norway and Finland

In April 1940 Dietl led the naval infantry and mountain troops landed at Narvik by destroyers of Group 1. After the destroyers were sunk he fought ashore with sailors and mountaineers until relieved following the Allied evacuation. From 1941 he commanded the Mountain Corps Norway and then the 20th Mountain Army in Lapland, where his front remained largely static. He died in an air crash in Austria in June 1944 while returning from a meeting at the Berghof.
