Nantua sauce

TypeSauce
Place of originFrance (Nantua, Ain)
Main ingredientsBéchamel or velouté, crayfish butter, cream

Nantua sauce (French: sauce Nantua) is a classic French sauce named after the town of Nantua in the Ain department, famous for the crayfish of its lake. It is a creamy, coral-pink sauce traditionally served with fish quenelles (notably pike quenelles, quenelles de brochet), eggs, and other seafood dishes.

Preparation

In Auguste Escoffier's codification, Nantua sauce is based on a béchamel (or fish velouté) enriched with crayfish butter — butter coloured and flavoured with crushed crayfish shells — and finished with cream; whole crayfish tails are often added as a garnish. Modern versions may substitute prawns or lobster butter where freshwater crayfish are unavailable.

Use

The sauce is the canonical accompaniment to the pike quenelles of Lyonnaise and Bressane cooking, and is also served with poached fish, vol-au-vent fillings and gratinéed seafood.
