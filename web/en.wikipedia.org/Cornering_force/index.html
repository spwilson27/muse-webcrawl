Cornering force

Cornering force or side force is the lateral (i.e., parallel to the road surface) force produced by a vehicle tyre during cornering. Cornering force is generated by tyre slip, occurring whenever a rolling pneumatic tyre does not point in the direction it is travelling, a condition described by the slip angle.

Mechanism

As a tyre rolls along a path at an angle to its own heading, the contact patch is progressively deflected sideways; the resulting elastic deformation produces a lateral force roughly proportional to the slip angle at small angles. The ratio of cornering force to slip angle is the cornering stiffness, a fundamental parameter in vehicle dynamics modelling. At larger slip angles the relationship becomes nonlinear: the force saturates as the contact patch begins to slide, reaches a peak, and then declines, which underlies the loss of grip in drifting or understeer/oversteer behaviour.

Factors and related effects

Cornering force increases with vertical load on the tyre, but less than proportionally, so transferring load onto an outside wheel does not double its grip. Camber angle generates an additional lateral component called camber thrust. Because the resultant force acts behind the centre of the contact patch, at a distance called the pneumatic trail, it produces a self-aligning torque that the driver feels through the steering. Road surface, inflation pressure, tyre construction and tread compound all influence cornering force, which is central to the design of motorcycle and bicycle tyres as well as car tyres.
