The New York Yankees are an American professional baseball team based in the New York City borough of the Bronx. The Yankees compete in Major League Baseball (MLB) as a member club of the American League (AL) East Division. They are one of two major league clubs based in New York City, alongside the National League's New York Mets.

History

The franchise began in 1901 as the Baltimore Orioles and moved to New York in 1903 as the Highlanders, adopting the Yankees name in 1913. The acquisition of Babe Ruth from the Boston Red Sox in 1920 launched the team's golden age, and with stars such as Lou Gehrig, Joe DiMaggio, Mickey Mantle, and Yogi Berra the Yankees became the most successful franchise in North American professional sports.

Success and culture

The Yankees have won 27 World Series championships, more than any other MLB team, and 41 American League pennants. Their interlocking "NY" logo and pinstriped uniforms are among the most recognisable in sport. Under owner George Steinbrenner (1973–2010) the club became known for aggressive free-agent spending, and in the late 1990s a team led by Derek Jeter won four World Series in five years. The current Yankee Stadium opened in 2009.
