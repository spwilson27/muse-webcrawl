Starlink is a satellite internet constellation operated by Starlink Services, LLC, a wholly owned subsidiary of the American space company SpaceX. First launched in 2019, it provides coverage to over 100 countries and territories, and is also used for mobile backhaul. Starlink has been a major factor in maintaining Ukrainian communications during the Russo-Ukrainian War.

History

The constellation concept was unveiled in January 2015, with SpaceX filing plans with the U.S. Federal Communications Commission (FCC) for a large fleet of broadband satellites in low Earth orbit. The first operational satellites were launched on 11 November 2019 aboard a Falcon 9 rocket. By the mid-2020s, more than 7,000 Starlink satellites had been placed in orbit, making it by far the largest satellite constellation ever deployed.

Technology

Starlink satellites operate in low Earth orbit at altitudes of roughly 340 to 570 km, which reduces latency compared with traditional geostationary satellite internet. Each satellite carries phased-array antennas for communication with user terminals and, on later versions, optical inter-satellite links ("space lasers") that allow data to be routed through space rather than via ground stations. Version 2 Mini satellites, launched beginning in 2023, offer roughly four times the capacity of the first generation. The user terminal is a flat phased-array dish, nicknamed "Dishy McFlatface," which aligns itself automatically.

Services

Starlink offers residential, roaming ("Roam"), business, maritime, and aviation service tiers. Speeds typically range from 25 to 220 Mbps download depending on location and congestion. Direct-to-cell service, developed with mobile carriers, began rolling out to provide texting and eventually voice and data coverage to ordinary mobile phones in areas without terrestrial coverage.

Astronomical concerns

The constellation has drawn criticism from astronomers because the satellites are bright in optical and radio wavelengths and can interfere with observations. SpaceX has worked with observatories on mitigation measures, including dark coatings and sun visors, and later satellites are substantially dimmer than the first generation.
