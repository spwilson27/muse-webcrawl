Date20 October 1944 – 15 August 1945
LocationPhilippine Islands
ResultAllied victory; liberation of the Philippines

The Philippines campaign of 1944–1945 was the American and Filipino campaign to defeat and expel the Imperial Japanese forces occupying the Philippines during the Second World War. The Japanese Army overran the archipelago in 1942, forcing General Douglas MacArthur to withdraw to Australia; he returned with a large invasion force, fulfilling his promise of "I shall return".

Course of the campaign

The campaign began with landings on Leyte on 20 October 1944, accompanied by the Battle of Leyte Gulf, one of the largest naval battles in history, in which the Imperial Japanese Navy was effectively destroyed. Landings on Luzon at Lingayen Gulf followed in January 1945, and the month-long Battle of Manila in February–March devastated the capital and killed an estimated 100,000 civilians. Fighting continued against General Yamashita's forces in the mountains of northern Luzon and in Mindanao until Japan's surrender in August 1945.

Outcome

The campaign liberated the Philippines at heavy cost to Filipino civilians and to both armies, and set the stage for the independence of the Republic of the Philippines on 4 July 1946.
