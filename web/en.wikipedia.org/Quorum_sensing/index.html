Quorum sensing

Autoinducer accumulation triggers population-wide gene expression

Quorum sensing is a system of communication between cells that enables bacteria to regulate gene expression in response to fluctuations in cell-population density. Quorum-sensing bacteria produce and release chemical signal molecules called autoinducers, whose concentration increases as a function of cell density; when a threshold is reached, gene expression changes across the population.

Discovery

The phenomenon was first described in the marine bacterium Aliivibrio fischeri, where quorum sensing controls bioluminescence; the bacteria glow only when present at high density, as in the light organs of symbiotic squid and fish.

Mechanisms

Gram-negative bacteria commonly use acyl-homoserine lactones as autoinducers, while Gram-positive bacteria use processed oligopeptides. Many species also share the AI-2 signal, which has been proposed to permit interspecies communication.

Biological roles

Quorum sensing coordinates behaviours that are only effective when performed by many cells at once, including biofilm formation, virulence factor production, sporulation, competence for DNA uptake, and antibiotic production. Because it controls virulence without killing bacteria, interfering with quorum sensing — "quorum quenching" — is investigated as an anti-infective strategy that may impose weaker selection for resistance.
