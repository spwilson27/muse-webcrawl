Viracocha

Viracocha (also Huiracocha, Wiraqucha, or in full Apu Qun Tiqsi Wiraqucha) is the creator deity of the Inca and pre-Inca peoples of the Andes. In Inca mythology he created the universe, sun, moon, stars and humanity, then wandered the earth teaching civilisation before departing across the Pacific.

Mythology

According to chroniclers, Viracocha rose from Lake Titicaca, created the sun and moon at Tiwanaku, and fashioned humans from stone, breathing life into them and dispatching them to populate the world. He was often depicted as a bearded figure holding thunderbolts or staffs, associated with the Staff God of earlier Andean cultures.

The name was also borne by Viracocha Inca, the eighth Sapa Inca (reigned c. 1410–1438), father of Pachacuti. After the Spanish conquest, the term was applied by Andeans to Spaniards themselves.
