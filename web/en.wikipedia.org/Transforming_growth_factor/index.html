Transforming growth factor

Transforming growth factor (TGF) is a term for two classes of polypeptide growth factors, TGF-α and TGF-β, originally identified by their ability to transform cultured cells. Despite the similar names, they are structurally and functionally distinct families.

TGF-β superfamily

TGF-β1, β2 and β3 are multifunctional cytokines that regulate cell proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis, embryonic development and immune responses. They signal through serine/threonine kinase receptors that phosphorylate SMAD proteins. The wider TGF-β superfamily includes bone morphogenetic proteins (BMPs), activins and growth differentiation factors.

Role in disease

Dysregulated TGF-β signalling contributes to fibrosis, immune suppression and cancer progression, where it can act first as a tumour suppressor and later promote invasion and metastasis. TGF-α, an epidermal growth factor receptor ligand, is implicated in wound healing and some tumours.
