The Crystal Palace


The Crystal Palace in Hyde Park during the Great Exhibition of 1851.

The Crystal Palace was a cast iron and plate glass structure, originally built in Hyde Park, London, to house the Great Exhibition of 1851, the first in the series of World's Fairs. The structure, designed by Joseph Paxton, was 1,851 feet (564 m) long — a deliberate reference to the year — with an interior height of 128 feet (39 m), and it enclosed some 92,000 square metres of exhibition space. More than 14,000 exhibitors from around the world displayed examples of technology developed in the Industrial Revolution.

Design and construction

Paxton, a gardener and designer of greenhouses at Chatsworth House, produced his design in a matter of days, drawing on his experience with glasshouse construction, notably the lily house built for the giant water lily Victoria amazonica. The building was prefabricated from standardized cast-iron components and the largest sheets of glass then available, allowing it to be erected in under nine months. A transept with a barrel-vaulted roof was added to accommodate mature elm trees on the site that public opinion refused to see felled.

Later history

After the exhibition, the palace was dismantled and rebuilt in an enlarged form at Sydenham, in south London, reopening in 1854 as a centre for concerts, exhibitions, and popular education, surrounded by gardens designed with Paxton and containing the famous life-size dinosaur models of Benjamin Waterhouse Hawkins. The surrounding district took the name Crystal Palace. The building was destroyed by fire on the night of 30 November 1936; the blaze was visible across London. Only the terraced gardens, sphinxes, and the dinosaurs survive on the site, which is now Crystal Palace Park.

Legacy

The Crystal Palace became a symbol of Victorian progress and of modern prefabricated architecture, influencing exhibition buildings and glass-and-iron construction internationally. The name survives in the London district, a football club, and the Crystal Palace National Sports Centre on the park's grounds.
