Pierre Bonnard

Pierre Bonnard (3 October 1867 – 23 January 1947) was a French painter, illustrator, and printmaker, known especially for the intimate domestic scenes and landscapes in his mature work, rendered in luminous, high-key colour. He was a founding member of the Post-Impressionist group Les Nabis.

Early life and the Nabis

Bonnard studied law briefly before turning to art at the Académie Julian and the École des Beaux-Arts, where he met Édouard Vuillard and Maurice Denis. Around 1889 they formed Les Nabis ("the prophets"), influenced by Gauguin's Synthetism. Bonnard also designed celebrated posters, including France-Champagne (1891), and illustrations for La Revue Blanche.

Mature work

From about 1900 Bonnard developed the style for which he is best known: sunlit interiors, gardens, and nudes — above all his wife Marthe, his lifelong model, often shown bathing — painted from memory and notes rather than directly from the motif. Works such as The Dining Room in the Country (1913) and The Open Window exploit rich colour harmonies and unusual, snapshot-like viewpoints. He spent his last decades at Le Cannet on the French Riviera.
