Clemens Augustinus Emmanuel Joseph Pius Anthonius Hubertus Marie Graf von Galen (16 March 1878 – 22 March 1946), commonly known as Clemens August Graf von Galen, was a German count, Bishop of Münster, and cardinal of the Roman Catholic Church. During the Nazi era he became famous for his public opposition to the regime's euthanasia programme and its persecution of the Church, earning the nickname the "Lion of Münster".

Early life and priesthood

Galen was born at Haus Assen in Dinklage into one of the oldest aristocratic families of Westphalia. He studied theology at Innsbruck and Münster, and was ordained a priest in 1904. He served as a parish priest in Berlin and Münster, and became known for his conservative, patriotic views and his stern personal piety.

Bishop of Münster

Appointed Bishop of Münster in 1933, shortly after the Nazi seizure of power, Galen soon clashed with the regime over its interference in Catholic schools and youth organisations, and over the racial ideology promoted by Alfred Rosenberg, which he denounced in sermons from 1934 onwards.

The 1941 sermons

In the summer of 1941 Galen delivered three celebrated sermons condemning Gestapo terror against religious orders and, most famously, the secret killing of mentally ill and disabled people under the Aktion T4 euthanasia programme. The sermon of 3 August 1941 asked how Germans could remain silent while "unproductive" fellow citizens were murdered, and copies were spread throughout Germany and dropped by the Royal Air Force as propaganda leaflets. Although the Nazis considered arresting him, they feared making a martyr of him in Catholic Westphalia and postponed action until after the war.

Cardinal and death

In recognition of his stance, Pope Pius XII created Galen a cardinal in February 1946. On his return to Münster he was greeted by huge crowds, but he died only days later, on 22 March 1946, of an appendiceal infection. He was beatified by Pope Benedict XVI on 9 October 2005.
