Nothing

Nothing, used as a pronoun, is the absence of something or particular thing that one might expect or desire to be present ("We found nothing") or the inactivity of a thing or things that are usually or could be active ("Nothing moved"). As a noun, nothing is a concept denoting the absence of something. In nontechnical uses, nothing denotes things lacking importance, interest, value, relevance, or significance. Nothingness is the state of being nothing, the state of nonexistence of anything, or the property of having nothing.

Philosophy

The concept of nothing has puzzled philosophers since ancient times. The pre-Socratic philosopher Parmenides argued that non-being cannot be thought or spoken of, while Leucippus and Democritus treated the void as a real feature of the universe necessary for atomic motion. In the 20th century, existentialist writers such as Jean-Paul Sartre explored nothingness (le néant) as central to human consciousness and freedom. Martin Heidegger famously asked, "Why is there something rather than nothing?", a question first posed by Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz.

Science

In physics, "nothing" is often approximated by a vacuum, but quantum field theory describes the vacuum as seething with virtual particles and zero-point energy, so a true state of nothingness is not physically attainable. In mathematics, zero and the empty set formalize aspects of nothing, while in computing a null value indicates the absence of a value.

Religion

Some religious and philosophical traditions discuss creation ex nihilo (out of nothing), while others, such as certain schools of Buddhism, treat emptiness (śūnyatā) as a fundamental characteristic of phenomena.
