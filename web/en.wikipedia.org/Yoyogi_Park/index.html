Yoyogi Park (Yoyogi Kōen) is a large public park in Shibuya, Tokyo, Japan, adjacent to Harajuku Station and the Meiji Shrine grounds. At about 54 hectares it is one of Tokyo's largest city parks, known for its broad lawns, ponds, forested areas, and constant stream of festivals, performers, and cosplayers.

History

The site has a layered history: in 1910 it hosted Japan's first powered aircraft flight, by Captain Yoshitoshi Tokugawa. It later became an Imperial Japanese Army parade ground. After the Second World War it was occupied by United States military housing known as Washington Heights. For the 1964 Tokyo Olympics the land held the Olympic athletes' village and the Yoyogi National Gymnasium, designed by Kenzo Tange, which remains an architectural landmark beside the park. The area opened as a public park in 1967.

Features

The park is divided by a road into a main section with lawns, a central pond with fountains, a bird sanctuary, and an observation deck, and a second section with an event plaza and outdoor stage used for festivals and flea markets. Yoyogi Park is famous as a gathering place for Tokyo street culture — rockabilly dancers near the Harajuku entrance, musicians, martial artists, and cosplayers — and it is a popular spot for cherry-blossom and ginkgo viewing.
