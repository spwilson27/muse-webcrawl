Color line (race issue)

The color line, also known as the color barrier, is a term describing the racial segregation and exclusion of people on the basis of skin colour, particularly as practised in the United States. The phrase was popularised by the scholar and activist W. E. B. Du Bois, who wrote in The Souls of Black Folk (1903) that "the problem of the twentieth century is the problem of the color line".

Usage and history

The expression had earlier been used by Frederick Douglass in an 1881 essay, "The Color Line". In the United States the color line manifested in slavery, Jim Crow laws, segregation in public facilities, housing discrimination through redlining, and the exclusion of Black athletes from professional sports leagues. The baseball colour line was famously broken by Jackie Robinson in 1947.

In sport

In professional baseball, an unofficial but strictly enforced colour barrier excluded African American players from Major League Baseball from the late 19th century until 1947, forcing them into the Negro leagues. Similar barriers existed in American football, basketball and boxing, and were dismantled progressively during the mid-20th century as the civil rights movement advanced.
