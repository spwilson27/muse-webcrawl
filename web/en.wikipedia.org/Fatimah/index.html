Fatimah

Fatimah bint Muhammad
Bornc. 605 or 615 CE, Mecca

Fatimah bint Muhammad (died 632) was the youngest daughter of the Prophet Muhammad and his first wife Khadija. She is one of the most revered women in Islam, known by titles such as al-Zahra (the Radiant) and al-Batul, and in Shia Islam she is one of the Fourteen Infallibles and a member of the Ahl al-Bayt and the Ahl al-Kisa.

Life

Fatimah grew up in Mecca during the persecution of the early Muslims and emigrated to Medina in 622. Around 624 she married Ali ibn Abi Talib; their household was famous for its austerity and charity, including the episode of feeding a poor person, an orphan and a captive over three nights commemorated in Qur'an 76. Through her sons Hasan and Husayn, the Prophet's lineage (sayyids) continued.

After Muhammad's death

Fatimah died only a few months after her father in 632. Her final months were marked by dispute with the first caliph, Abu Bakr, over her inheritance claim to the lands of Fadak and over the succession, in which she supported Ali. Shia tradition also recounts an attack on her house and a miscarriage; Sunni tradition rejects that account and emphasises later reconciliation reports differ. Her burial place in Medina is uncertain — a fact itself mourned in Shia devotion.

Veneration

Fatimah is a model of piety and maternal devotion; her prayer beads (tasbih of Fatimah), the sermon of Fadak attributed to her, and titles such as Sayyidat Nisa al-Alamin (mistress of the women of the worlds) figure widely in Muslim devotion. In Twelver Shi'ism she links the Prophet and the Imams, and Fatimah's mourning (Fatimiyya) is observed annually.
