Coast redwood

Scientific name: Sequoia sempervirens
Family: Cupressaceae
Height: up to 115 m (380 ft)

The coast redwood (Sequoia sempervirens) is the sole living species of the genus Sequoia in the cypress family. It is an evergreen, long-lived conifer native to a narrow coastal strip of southwestern Oregon and northern California.

Description

Coast redwoods are the tallest trees on Earth; the tallest known living specimen, Hyperion, stands about 115.9 metres (380 feet). Trees can live over 2,000 years, with thick, fibrous, fire-resistant reddish bark. They reproduce both by seed and by sprouting from the root crown and burl.

Range and conservation

The species depends on coastal fog for moisture. Extensive logging from the 19th century onward removed most old-growth forest; remaining stands are protected in Redwood National and State Parks and other reserves, and the species is listed as endangered due to its restricted range and decline.
