Nellie Bly

Nellie Bly
Born: Elizabeth Cochran, 5 May 1864, Cochran's Mills, Pennsylvania
Died: 27 January 1922, New York City
Occupation: Journalist, writer, inventor

Elizabeth Cochran Seaman (born Elizabeth Jane Cochran; May 5, 1864 – January 27, 1922), better known by her pen name Nellie Bly, was an American journalist widely known for her record-breaking trip around the world in 72 days and her exposé of conditions in mental asylums.

Asylum exposé

In 1887, working for Joseph Pulitzer's New York World, Bly feigned insanity to investigate reports of brutality at the Women's Lunatic Asylum on Blackwell's Island. Her series, published as Ten Days in a Mad-House, brought reforms and made her famous.

Around the world

In 1889–1890 she travelled around the world in 72 days, eight hours, and eleven minutes, emulating the fictional journey in Jules Verne's Around the World in Eighty Days and setting a world record.
