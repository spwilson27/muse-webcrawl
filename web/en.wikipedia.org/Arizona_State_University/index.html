Arizona State University

Arizona State University (ASU) is a public research university in the Phoenix metropolitan area of Arizona, United States. Founded in 1885 as the Territorial Normal School, it became Arizona State University in 1958. ASU is one of the largest public universities in the United States by enrollment, with students across campuses in Tempe, Phoenix (Downtown), Mesa (Polytechnic), Glendale (West), and other locations, plus a large online programme.

Academics and research

Chartered in 1885 by the 13th Arizona Territorial Legislature, ASU is a member of the Association of American Universities and is classified among research universities with very high research activity. It comprises numerous schools, including the Ira A. Fulton Schools of Engineering, the W. P. Carey School of Business, the Walter Cronkite School of Journalism and Mass Communication, and the Sandra Day O'Connor College of Law. Since 2002, under president Michael M. Crow, the university has pursued the "New American University" model emphasising access, inclusion, and research addressing social challenges.

Athletics

ASU's teams, the Sun Devils, compete in NCAA Division I. The university is known for its athletics facilities, including Mountain America Stadium (formerly Sun Devil Stadium), and notable alumni such as golfer Phil Mickelson and baseball players Barry Bonds and Reggie Jackson. In 2024, ASU moved to the Big 12 Conference.
