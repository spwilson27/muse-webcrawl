Vasily Dokuchaev

Vasily Vasilyevich Dokuchaev (1846–1903) was a Russian geologist and geographer who is generally regarded as the father of soil science (pedology). He transformed the study of soil from a branch of geology into an independent natural science.

Work

Commissioned to study the soils of the Russian steppe after droughts and famine, Dokuchaev published Russian Chernozem (1883), in which he argued that soil is an independent natural body formed by five factors: climate, organisms, relief, parent material, and time — the state-factor model later formalized by Hans Jenny. He recognized the zonal distribution of soils, established the concept of the soil profile with distinct horizons, and founded the first soil classification system. He taught at Saint Petersburg University, where his students included Vladimir Vernadsky.

Legacy

The Dokuchaev Soil Science Institute in Moscow bears his name, as does a crater on Mars. His genetic approach to soil formation underlies modern pedology and soil survey worldwide.
