Prime Minister of Norway

The Prime Minister of Norway (Norwegian: statsminister) is the head of government of Norway and the most powerful figure in Norwegian politics. The prime minister and cabinet (collectively called the Council of State) are formally appointed by the monarch, but by convention the appointee must enjoy the confidence of the Storting, Norway's parliament. Unlike many countries, Norway's parliament cannot be dissolved early, and governments often rule as minorities.

History

The office in its modern form dates to the introduction of parliamentarism in 1884, when Johan Sverdrup became the first prime minister to govern with the Storting's support. Before that, the senior Norwegian government official bore titles such as Government Counsellor and, under the union with Sweden, operated alongside a viceroy or governor.

The dominant figure of post-war Norway was Einar Gerhardsen of the Labour Party, prime minister for some 17 years and regarded as the architect of the Norwegian welfare state. Gro Harlem Brundtland served three terms and became known internationally for the concept of sustainable development. Kjell Magne Bondevik and Jens Stoltenberg led governments in the 1990s and 2000s; Stoltenberg later became Secretary General of NATO. Conservative leader Erna Solberg served from 2013 to 2021, when Jonas Gahr Støre of Labour became prime minister.

Residence

The prime minister's office is located in the government quarter in central Oslo. The official residence, Inkognitogata 18 in the district behind the Royal Palace, was first used by Kjell Magne Bondevik.
