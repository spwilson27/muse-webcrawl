Pathfinding

Pathfinding, or pathing, is the plotting, by a computer application, of the shortest route between two points. It is a more practical variant of solving mazes and a core problem in artificial intelligence, robotics, video games, and geographic information systems. Pathfinding is typically based on graph search algorithms that explore a graph representing the traversable space.

The A* algorithm finding a route through a grid with obstacles.

Algorithms

The classic algorithms include Dijkstra's algorithm, which explores outward uniformly from the start and guarantees the shortest path on graphs with non-negative edge weights, and the A* search algorithm, which guides the search with an admissible heuristic estimate of the remaining distance and is the standard choice in games. Other methods include breadth-first search for unweighted grids, greedy best-first search, jump point search for uniform-cost grids, and hierarchical approaches that precompute routes over abstractions of the map. In continuous spaces, sampling-based planners such as rapidly exploring random trees are used for robot motion planning.

Applications

Video games use pathfinding to move characters around maps in real time, often over navigation meshes. Route-planning software applies large-scale shortest-path algorithms, accelerated by techniques such as contraction hierarchies, to road networks. Robotics, logistics, network routing, and circuit design all depend on variants of pathfinding.
