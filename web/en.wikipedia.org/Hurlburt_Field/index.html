Hurlburt Field

Summary

Location: Mary Esther, Florida, United States

Operator: United States Air Force

Command: Air Force Special Operations Command (AFSOC)

Host unit: 1st Special Operations Wing

ICAO: KHRT

Hurlburt Field is a United States Air Force installation in Okaloosa County, Florida, near the city of Mary Esther. It is the headquarters of Air Force Special Operations Command (AFSOC) and home to the 1st Special Operations Wing. The base was originally an auxiliary field of nearby Eglin Air Force Base.

History

The field was established during the Second World War as Eglin Auxiliary Field No. 9 and was used for training, including practice for the Doolittle Raid on Tokyo in 1942, whose crews trained at Eglin fields for short takeoffs. It was named in honour of First Lieutenant Donald Wilson Hurlburt, a pilot killed in a takeoff accident at Eglin in 1943. After periods of closure, the base was reactivated in the 1950s and grew during the 1960s as a centre for air commando and special operations training, including for operations in Southeast Asia.

Present role

Today Hurlburt Field hosts AFSOC headquarters and units flying aircraft such as the AC-130 gunship, MC-130, and CV-22 Osprey. It also supports the Air Force Special Operations Air Warfare Center and training for combat controllers, pararescuemen, and other special tactics airmen. The base is one of the largest employers in the Florida Panhandle.
