Nehru–Gandhi family

The Nehru–Gandhi family is a prominent Indian political family that has occupied a central place in the politics of the Republic of India since independence. Members have served as three of India's prime ministers and as successive leaders of the Indian National Congress. The family is not related to Mahatma Gandhi; the Gandhi surname comes from Feroze Gandhi, a Parsi politician who married Indira Nehru.

Generations

Motilal Nehru, a wealthy lawyer, was an early Congress president. His son Jawaharlal Nehru became India's first prime minister (1947–1964). Nehru's daughter Indira Gandhi served as prime minister in 1966–1977 and 1980–1984, when she was assassinated. Her elder son Rajiv Gandhi succeeded her and served until 1989; he too was assassinated, in 1991. Rajiv's widow Sonia Gandhi led Congress for two decades, and their children Rahul Gandhi and Priyanka Gandhi Vadra remain leading party figures. Indira's younger son Sanjay Gandhi was a powerful, controversial figure until his death in a 1980 plane crash; his widow Maneka Gandhi and son Varun Gandhi joined the rival BJP.

Criticism

Critics charge the family's dominance with encouraging dynastic politics in India; supporters credit it with holding Congress together and providing continuity.
