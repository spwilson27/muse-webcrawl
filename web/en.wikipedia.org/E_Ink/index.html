E Ink

CompanyE Ink Holdings Inc. (Taiwan)
Founded1997, spun out of the MIT Media Lab
TechnologyElectrophoretic (electronic paper) displays

E Ink is a brand of electronic paper (e-paper) display technology and the name of the company that commercialised it, E Ink Holdings Inc. of Taiwan. E Ink displays are electrophoretic: millions of tiny microcapsules containing charged black and white pigment particles are moved by electric fields to form images.

Technology

In an E Ink film, each microcapsule (about the width of a human hair) contains positively charged white particles and negatively charged black particles suspended in a clear fluid. Applying a negative or positive charge to electrodes above and below the capsule draws one set of particles to the visible surface and repels the other, making the pixel appear white or black; intermediate voltages produce greyscale. Because the image persists without power (bistability), E Ink screens consume energy only when the page changes, and because they reflect ambient light like paper they are readable in bright sunlight and easy on the eyes — at the cost, historically, of slow refresh rates that preclude smooth video.

History and applications

The technology was developed in the 1990s by Joseph Jacobson and colleagues at the MIT Media Lab, and E Ink Corporation was founded in 1997. Early products included large-format signage; the breakthrough consumer application was the e-reader, beginning with the Sony Librie (2004) and popularised by the Amazon Kindle (2007). Later generations (Pearl, Carta, Kaleido colour, Gallery) improved contrast, resolution and colour. Beyond e-readers, E Ink is used in electronic shelf labels in supermarkets, bus-stop signage, luggage tags, watches and architectural surfaces.
