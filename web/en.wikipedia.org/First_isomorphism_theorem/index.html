First isomorphism theorem

In mathematics, especially in abstract algebra, the isomorphism theorems (also known as Noether's isomorphism theorems) are theorems that describe the relationship between quotients, homomorphisms, and subobjects. Versions of the theorems exist for groups, rings, vector spaces, modules, Lie algebras, and various other algebraic structures. The first isomorphism theorem is the most fundamental of these: it states that the image of a homomorphism is isomorphic to the quotient of the domain by the kernel of the homomorphism.

Statement for groups

Let G and H be groups, and let f : G → H be a group homomorphism. Then:

  1. The kernel of f is a normal subgroup of G.
  2. The image of f is a subgroup of H.
  3. The image of f is isomorphic to the quotient group G / ker(f).

In particular, if f is surjective, then H is isomorphic to G / ker(f). The isomorphism is canonical: it is given by sending the coset g ker(f) to f(g), and one checks that this map is well defined, a homomorphism, injective, and surjective onto the image.

Intuition

The theorem expresses the idea that a homomorphism identifies exactly those elements that differ by an element of the kernel. Collapsing the domain by the kernel destroys precisely the information that the homomorphism discards, and what remains is a faithful copy of the image. A standard example is the determinant map from the general linear group GL(n, R) to the nonzero real numbers: its kernel is the special linear group SL(n, R), and the theorem yields GL(n, R) / SL(n, R) ≅ R*.

Versions for other structures

Analogous statements hold for rings and ideals, for modules over a ring, and for vector spaces, where the kernel of a linear map gives rise to an isomorphism between the quotient space and the image. In universal algebra and category theory, the theorem is related to the factorisation of morphisms through epimorphisms and monomorphisms, and to the notion of a regular category. The first isomorphism theorem is a special case of a more general result sometimes called the fundamental theorem on homomorphisms.

Related theorems

The second isomorphism theorem describes the relationship between a subgroup, a normal subgroup, their product, and their intersection. The third isomorphism theorem states that quotients of quotients behave as expected: if N and K are normal subgroups with N contained in K, then (G/N) / (K/N) ≅ G/K. Together with the correspondence theorem, these results, introduced by Emmy Noether in her 1927 paper Abstrakter Aufbau der Idealtheorie in algebraischen Zahl- und Funktionenkörpern, form a standard part of any course in abstract algebra.
