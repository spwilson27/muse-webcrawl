Beshbarmak

Beshbarmak is a dish from Central Asian cuisine, regarded as the national dish of Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan and also eaten in parts of Uzbekistan, Russia's Volga region and Xinjiang. The name means "five fingers" in the Turkic languages, referring to the traditional way of eating it by hand.

Preparation

The dish consists of finely chopped boiled meat — traditionally horse meat or mutton, though beef and chicken are also used — served over large flat noodles and covered with an onion sauce (chyk) made from sliced onions simmered in the meat broth. The meat is boiled in a large pot, and the rich broth is often served separately in bowls (shorpo) before or alongside the main platter.

Cultural significance

Beshbarmak is served on large platters at celebrations and to honoured guests. Custom dictates an order of serving, with particular cuts of meat offered to elders and guests of honour; in Kyrgyzstan the sheep's head (koi bash) may be presented to the most respected guest. The communal, hands-on style of eating reflects the nomadic heritage of the Kazakh and Kyrgyz peoples.
