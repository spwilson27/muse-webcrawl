Architectural pattern

An architectural pattern is a general, reusable resolution to a commonly occurring problem in software architecture within a given context. Architectural patterns are similar to software design patterns but have a broader scope: they address the overall structure and organisation of a software system—its components, their relationships, and the constraints on how they interact—rather than the design of individual classes or objects.

Examples

  • Layered architecture organises a system into layers (such as presentation, business logic and data) with restricted dependencies between them.
  • Client–server separates providers of resources or services from requesters.
  • Model–view–controller (MVC) divides an interactive application into a model, views and controllers.
  • Event-driven architecture and microservices structure systems around events and small independently deployable services, respectively.
  • Pipes and filters, blackboard, broker and peer-to-peer are further classical patterns.

History

The idea of reusable patterns in design derives from the architect Christopher Alexander's A Pattern Language (1977), about patterns in buildings and towns. Software researchers adapted the idea, and the book Pattern-Oriented Software Architecture (1996) catalogued architectural patterns for software systems, distinguishing them from smaller-scale design patterns and idioms.
