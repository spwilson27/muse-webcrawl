Operation Vijay ("Victory" in Hindi), also known in India as the Liberation of Goa and in Portugal as the Invasion of Goa, was the 1961 armed action by India that annexed the Portuguese colonies of Goa, Daman, and Diu—collectively Portuguese India—and ended more than four centuries of Portuguese colonial rule on the subcontinent.

Background

After Indian independence in 1947, Portugal refused to negotiate the transfer of its enclaves, arguing they were integral parts of metropolitan Portugal. Diplomatic pressure, a satyagraha movement by Goan and Indian activists—in which Portuguese forces killed unarmed protesters in 1955—and the enclaves' economic blockade failed to dislodge Lisbon. Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru, facing domestic pressure and inspired by decolonisation elsewhere, finally authorised military action under Defence Minister V. K. Krishna Menon.

The operation

On the night of 17–18 December 1961, some 45,000 Indian troops, supported by air strikes and a naval task force, advanced into Goa from the north, east, and south, while smaller forces moved against Daman and Diu. Portuguese forces numbered about 3,300 and had orders from Governor-General Manuel Vassalo e Silva to fight a delaying defence; most resistance was brief, though there was fighting at several points, the Portuguese frigate Afonso de Albuquerque was disabled by Indian warships at Mormugao, and an Indian Canberra bomber strike hit Dabolim airport. On the evening of 19 December, Vassalo e Silva signed the surrender, judging further resistance futile.

Aftermath

About 4,700 Portuguese personnel were taken prisoner and repatriated in 1962. Western governments, including the United States and United Kingdom, condemned the use of force, while the Soviet Union vetoed a critical UN Security Council resolution. Portugal did not recognise Indian sovereignty over Goa until after its 1974 Carnation Revolution. Goa, Daman and Diu became a union territory; Goa attained statehood in 1987. The Indian armed forces have reused the code name "Vijay"—notably for the 1999 Kargil War operation.
