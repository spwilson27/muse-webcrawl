Sugar

Sugar (sucrose)
Chemical formulaC12H22O11
Main sourcesSugarcane, sugar beet
Energy~387 kcal per 100 g

Sugar is the generic name for sweet-tasting, soluble carbohydrates, many of which are used in food. Table sugar, granulated sugar, or regular sugar refers to sucrose, a disaccharide composed of glucose and fructose. Sugars are found in the tissues of most plants; the two main commercial sources are sugarcane and sugar beet, from which sugar is extracted and refined for use in cooking and food manufacture.

History

Sugarcane was domesticated in New Guinea and spread through Southeast Asia and India, where techniques for producing crystalline sugar were developed. Sugar reached Europe through Arab trade and cultivation, remaining a luxury spice and medicine for centuries. Plantation production in the Americas, based on enslaved labour, transformed sugar into a mass commodity during the early modern period.

Production

Modern production involves harvesting cane or beet, extracting the juice, clarifying and evaporating it, and crystallising and refining the sucrose. By-products include molasses and bagasse. Brazil and India are among the world's largest producers.

Health

High consumption of added sugars is associated with tooth decay, obesity, and metabolic disease; health authorities recommend limiting free sugars to a small fraction of daily energy intake.
