Marken is a village in the municipality of Waterland in the province of North Holland, Netherlands. It had a population of around 1,800. Marken forms a peninsula in the Markermeer, and was an island in the Zuiderzee until it was connected to the mainland by a causeway in 1957.

History

Marken was separated from the mainland by a storm flood in the 13th century and remained an island community for centuries, developing its own distinctive dialect, dress and wooden house architecture. Its inhabitants traditionally earned their living from fishing in the Zuiderzee. After the Afsluitdijk closed off the Zuiderzee in 1932, the fishing industry declined.

Culture

The village is known for its characteristic green-painted wooden houses, many built on stilts or mounds (werven) to protect against flooding, and for the traditional costume still worn by some residents on festive occasions. Marken is now a popular tourist destination, reachable via the causeway from Monnickendam.

Landmarks

Notable sights include the wooden shoe factory, the Marker Museum and the Paard van Marken, a lighthouse built in 1839 at the eastern tip of the peninsula.
