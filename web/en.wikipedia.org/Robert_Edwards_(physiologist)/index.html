Robert Edwards (physiologist)

Sir Robert Edwards, pioneer of in vitro fertilisation

Sir Robert Geoffrey Edwards CBE FRS MAE (27 September 1925 – 10 April 2013) was a British physiologist and pioneer in reproductive medicine, particularly in vitro fertilisation (IVF). His work, with surgeon Patrick Steptoe, led to the birth of Louise Brown in 1978, the first baby conceived by IVF. He won the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 2010.

Early life and research

Born in Batley, Yorkshire, Edwards served in the British Army during the Second World War before studying agriculture and then genetics at the University of Edinburgh, receiving his PhD in 1955. He spent much of his research career at the University of Cambridge, working on the maturation and fertilisation of human eggs.

IVF breakthrough

From 1968 Edwards collaborated with gynaecologist Patrick Steptoe, who pioneered laparoscopy. After years of setbacks and ethical controversy, their patient Lesley Brown gave birth to Louise Brown on 25 July 1978 at Oldham General Hospital. In 1980 Edwards and Steptoe founded Bourn Hall Clinic near Cambridge, the world's first IVF clinic.

Honours

Edwards received the Lasker Award in 2001, was knighted in 2011, and won the 2010 Nobel Prize; Steptoe, who died in 1988, was ineligible. By the time of the award, more than four million babies had been born through IVF.
