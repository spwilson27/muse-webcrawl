The RPR was the principal Gaullist party of the French Fifth Republic.

The Rally for the Republic (French: Rassemblement pour la République, RPR) was a Gaullist and conservative political party in France. Founded by Jacques Chirac in 1976 as the successor to the Union of Democrats for the Republic (UDR), it was for a quarter-century the main party of the French right.

History

Chirac created the RPR after resigning as prime minister, rebuilding the Gaullist movement around his leadership. The party won control of the National Assembly in coalition with the UDF in 1986 and 1993, giving France its periods of "cohabitation" with Socialist president François Mitterrand. Chirac was elected President of the Republic in 1995 and re-elected in 2002. That year the RPR merged with other centre-right parties to form the Union for a Popular Movement (UMP), ending its independent existence.

Ideology

The RPR upheld Gaullist themes — national independence, a strong executive and state-led modernisation — combined increasingly with economic liberalism and European integration on negotiated terms. Notable leaders and figures included Alain Juppé, Édouard Balladur, Philippe Séguin and Nicolas Sarkozy, who later led the UMP and the presidency.
