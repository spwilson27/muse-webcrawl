Great Commandment

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Great Commandment (or Greatest Commandment) is a term used in the New Testament to describe Jesus's summary of the moral law when asked which commandment is the greatest. As recorded in the Gospels of Matthew (22:35–40), Mark (12:28–34), and in related form in Luke (10:25–28), Jesus answers with a double command: to love God with all one's heart, soul, and mind, and to love one's neighbour as oneself.

Biblical sources

The first part quotes the Shema from Deuteronomy 6:5 — "You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your might" — the central affirmation of Judaism, recited daily. The second quotes Leviticus 19:18: "You shall love your neighbour as yourself." Jesus declares that "on these two commandments hang all the Law and the Prophets" (Matthew 22:40).

Interpretation

The double command has been treated throughout Christian history as the summary of the entire ethical teaching of Scripture, uniting devotion to God with practical love of neighbour — a pairing Jesus illustrates in Luke with the Parable of the Good Samaritan, which expands the definition of "neighbour" to include the outsider and enemy. The scribe in Mark's account responds that such love is "much more than all whole burnt offerings and sacrifices," echoing prophetic priorities. In Judaism, parallel summaries exist: Hillel the Elder famously taught that "what is hateful to you, do not do to your fellow" is the whole Torah, "the rest is commentary."
