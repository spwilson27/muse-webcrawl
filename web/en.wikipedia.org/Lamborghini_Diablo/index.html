Lamborghini Diablo

The Lamborghini Diablo is a high-performance mid-engine sports car built by Italian manufacturer Lamborghini between 1990 and 2001. It succeeded the Countach as the brand's flagship V12 model and was succeeded by the Murciélago. The Diablo was the first Lamborghini capable of exceeding 320 km/h (200 mph).

Overview

Development began in 1985 under the code name Project 132. Styled by Marcello Gandini, whose design was softened considerably under Chrysler ownership, the Diablo debuted on 21 January 1990. The launch car had a 5.7-litre V12 producing 492 PS (362 kW), rear-wheel drive, a five-speed manual gearbox, and scissor doors inherited from the Countach; power steering and ABS were initially absent.

Variants

Subsequent versions included the all-wheel-drive Diablo VT (1993, "Viscous Traction"), the lightweight SE30 (1994), the open-top VT Roadster (1995), the high-performance SV (1995), and the racing-derived GT (1999). A facelift in 1999 brought fixed headlamps in place of pop-ups and standard ABS. After Audi acquired Lamborghini in 1998, the final Diablo 6.0 of 2000 used an enlarged 6.0-litre engine before production ended in 2001 after 2,884 cars.

Performance

The original Diablo accelerated from 0–100 km/h in about 4.5 seconds and reached a claimed top speed of 325 km/h (202 mph), with independent tests confirming over 200 mph — performance that made it one of the fastest production cars of its era.
