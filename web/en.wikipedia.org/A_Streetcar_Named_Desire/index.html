GenreDrama (Southern Gothic)

A Streetcar Named Desire is a play written by American playwright Tennessee Williams, first performed on Broadway in 1947. It won the Pulitzer Prize for Drama in 1948 and is widely regarded as one of the greatest American plays of the twentieth century.

Plot

Blanche DuBois, a fading Southern belle who has lost the family plantation, Belle Reve, arrives in New Orleans to live with her younger sister Stella and Stella's husband, Stanley Kowalski, a working-class Polish-American. Blanche's pretensions and fragile mental state clash with Stanley's raw, domineering manner. Stanley uncovers the truth about Blanche's past — scandal, dismissal from her teaching job, and the suicide of her young husband — and destroys her hopes of rescue through the gentle suitor Mitch. After Stanley assaults Blanche during Stella's confinement in hospital, Blanche's mind breaks entirely, and she is taken to a mental institution, delivering the famous closing line: "I have always depended on the kindness of strangers."

Productions and adaptations

The original Broadway production, directed by Elia Kazan, starred Jessica Tandy as Blanche and introduced Marlon Brando as Stanley. Kazan's 1951 film adaptation, with Vivien Leigh as Blanche and Brando reprising Stanley, won four Academy Awards. The play is regularly revived worldwide and has been adapted as an opera and ballet.
