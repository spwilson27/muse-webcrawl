Nancy Pelosi

Nancy Patricia Pelosi (born March 26, 1940) is an American politician who served as the 52nd Speaker of the United States House of Representatives from 2007 to 2011 and again from 2019 to 2023 — the only woman to hold the office and the highest-ranking elected woman in American history at the time. A member of the Democratic Party, she has represented San Francisco in Congress since 1987.

Career

Born into a political family in Baltimore — her father, Thomas D'Alesandro Jr., was a congressman and mayor of Baltimore — Pelosi rose through California Democratic politics before winning a special election to the House in 1987. She became House minority whip in 2001 and minority leader in 2002, the first woman to lead a major party in Congress. After Democrats won the 2006 midterms she became Speaker.

Speakerships

As Speaker she was instrumental in passing the Affordable Care Act (2010), the Dodd–Frank financial reform, and economic stimulus legislation under President Barack Obama. In her second speakership she presided over the House during two impeachments of President Donald Trump (2019 and 2021) and the passage of major legislation under President Joe Biden, including the American Rescue Plan and the Inflation Reduction Act. She stepped down from party leadership in 2023, remaining in the House as speaker emerita.
