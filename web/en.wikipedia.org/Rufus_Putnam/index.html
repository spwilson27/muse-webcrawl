Rufus Putnam (April 9, 1738 – May 4, 1824) was an American military officer who fought in the French and Indian War and the American Revolutionary War, and a pioneer in the settlement of the Ohio Country. As an organizer of the Ohio Company of Associates, he was instrumental in the initial settling of the Northwest Territory in present-day Ohio.

Revolutionary War

Putnam served as a military engineer under George Washington, designing fortifications at Dorchester Heights that forced the British evacuation of Boston in 1776, and later works at West Point. He ended the war as a brigadier general and was a founding member of the Society of the Cincinnati.

Ohio settlement

In 1786 Putnam helped found the Ohio Company of Associates, which purchased land in the Northwest Territory. In April 1788 he led a party of settlers to the confluence of the Ohio and Muskingum rivers, where they founded Marietta, the first permanent American settlement in the Northwest Territory. He later served as a judge and as Surveyor General of the United States (1796–1803). His home in Marietta, the Rufus Putnam House, is preserved within the Campus Martius Museum.

Legacy

Putnam's memoirs, published after his death, remain an important primary source for the Revolutionary War and the settlement of Ohio.
