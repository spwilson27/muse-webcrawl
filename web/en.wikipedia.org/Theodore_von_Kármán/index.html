Theodore von Kármán

Theodore von Kármán (born Tódor Mihály Kármán; 11 May 1881 – 6 May 1963) was a Hungarian-American mathematician, aerospace engineer and physicist. He is regarded as one of the founding figures of modern aerodynamics and a key architect of American air and space power.

Career

Educated in Budapest and Göttingen, where he worked under Ludwig Prandtl, von Kármán held a chair at Aachen before emigrating to the United States in 1930 to direct the Guggenheim Aeronautical Laboratory at Caltech. His research included the vortex street that bears his name, boundary-layer theory, turbulence, supersonic and hypersonic flight, and rocket propulsion. He co-founded the Jet Propulsion Laboratory and helped establish the company Aerojet.

Legacy

Von Kármán served as a scientific adviser to the US Air Force and was the first recipient of the National Medal of Science, awarded by President John F. Kennedy in 1963. The Kármán line — the altitude of 100 km often taken as the boundary of space — is named for his calculations of where the atmosphere becomes too thin for aeronautical flight.
