Reactive attachment disorder

Reactive attachment disorder (RAD) is a psychiatric disorder of infancy and early childhood, defined in the DSM-5, in which a child shows a consistent pattern of inhibited, emotionally withdrawn behaviour toward caregivers — rarely seeking or responding to comfort — as a result of severely insufficient early care, such as social neglect, repeated changes of primary caregiver, or rearing in institutions with high child-to-caregiver ratios.

Diagnosis and related conditions

Diagnosis requires that the disturbance began before age five, that the child has reached a developmental age of at least nine months, and that pathogenic care is presumed responsible. The DSM-5 separated the former "disinhibited type" of RAD into a distinct diagnosis, disinhibited social engagement disorder, in which children are excessively familiar with strangers. RAD must be distinguished from autism spectrum disorder and intellectual disability, though institutional deprivation studies — such as those of Romanian adoptees by Michael Rutter and the Bucharest Early Intervention Project — show outcomes improve markedly with early placement into stable, nurturing families.

Treatment

Evidence-based treatment centres on providing a stable, sensitive caregiving environment and caregiver-focused therapies; coercive "attachment therapies" involving holding or rebirthing are unsupported and have been condemned as dangerous.
