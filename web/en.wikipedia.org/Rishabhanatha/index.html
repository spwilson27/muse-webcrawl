Rishabhanatha

Rishabhanatha (Sanskrit: ऋषभनाथ), also known as Ādinātha ("first Lord"), is the first tirthankara (ford-maker, or supreme preacher) of Jainism in the present half-cycle of time. According to Jain tradition he lived millions of years ago and is credited with founding the institutions of society, teaching agriculture, writing, arithmetic and crafts, and showing the path to liberation.

Jain tradition

Jain texts recount that Rishabha was born to king Nabhi and queen Marudevi in Ayodhya. As king he is said to have organised society into occupations, introduced agriculture, animal husbandry, cooking, pottery, painting and writing (attributed to his daughter Brahmi, after whom the Brahmi script is traditionally named). After ruling for an immensely long period he renounced the world, attained omniscience (kevala jnana) and eventually liberation (moksha) from Mount Ashtapada. His eldest son Bharata became the first chakravartin (universal emperor), and India is said to take the name Bhārata from him; another son, Bahubali, is venerated for his renunciation.

Veneration elsewhere

Rishabha is also mentioned in Hindu Puranas as an avatar of Vishnu, and he appears in some Buddhist texts. The bull is his emblem, and his statues are typically shown seated in lotus posture or standing in kayotsarga, with hair falling in locks over the shoulders.
