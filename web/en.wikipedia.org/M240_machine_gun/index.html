The M240 machine gun, officially the Machine Gun, 7.62 mm, M240, is the U.S. military designation for the FN MAG, a family of belt-fed, gas-operated medium machine guns chambered for the 7.62×51mm NATO cartridge. It has been the standard general-purpose machine gun of the United States Armed Forces since the late 1970s.

History

The Belgian FN MAG (Mitrailleuse d'Appui Général), designed by Fabrique Nationale in the 1950s, was adopted by dozens of countries. The United States first adopted it as a coaxial weapon on armoured vehicles in 1977, and in the 1980s the Marine Corps and Army adopted infantry variants to replace the M60 machine gun. Guns for U.S. service are manufactured by FN America in South Carolina.

Design

The M240 fires from an open bolt, is fed by disintegrating-link belts, and has a quick-change barrel for sustained fire. Its gas regulator allows the cyclic rate to be adjusted. The M240B infantry version weighs about 12.5 kg and is fitted with a bipod, buttstock, and heat shield; the lighter M240L uses titanium components to cut weight, and the M240H and coaxial M240C/E1 variants serve in aircraft and vehicles.
