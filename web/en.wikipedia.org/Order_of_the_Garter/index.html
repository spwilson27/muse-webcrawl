Order of the Garter

Most Noble Order of the Garter
Founded: 1348, by Edward III
Motto: Honi soit qui mal y pense
Sovereign: The British monarch
Membership: 24 Knights and Ladies Companion, plus royal and foreign members

The Most Noble Order of the Garter is an order of chivalry founded by King Edward III of England in 1348. It is the most senior order of knighthood in the British honours system, outranked only by the Victoria Cross and the George Cross. The Order is dedicated to the image and arms of Saint George, England's patron saint.

Membership

Membership is limited to the sovereign, the Prince of Wales, and no more than 24 Knights or Ladies Companion, together with extra "royal" and "stranger" (foreign) members. Appointments are in the personal gift of the sovereign. New appointments are usually announced on St George's Day, 23 April. Members are granted stalls in St George's Chapel, Windsor Castle, where their heraldic banners and stall plates are displayed.

Insignia and motto

The Order's insignia include the Garter itself, worn by men below the left knee and by women on the left arm, bearing the motto Honi soit qui mal y pense (Anglo-Norman for "Shame on him who thinks evil of it"); the star; and the Lesser George pendant. According to legend, the motto refers to an incident in which Edward III retrieved a dropped garter of the Countess of Salisbury at a court ball, though this story's authenticity is doubtful.
