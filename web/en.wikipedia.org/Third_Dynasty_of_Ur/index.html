The Third Dynasty of Ur, also called the Neo-Sumerian Empire, was a Sumerian ruling dynasty based in the city of Ur in southern Mesopotamia, lasting from about 2112 to 2004 BC. It presided over a "Sumerian Renaissance" of administration, law, literature, and monumental building, and left behind one of the largest bodies of cuneiform documents from the ancient world.

History

The dynasty was founded by Ur-Nammu, who unified southern Mesopotamia after the Gutian period and issued one of the earliest known law codes. His son Shulgi, who reigned 48 years, vastly expanded the empire and built a highly centralized bureaucracy. Later kings — Amar-Sin, Shu-Sin, and Ibbi-Sin — faced Amorite incursions and internal strain.

Downfall

Famine, Amorite pressure, and the secession of provinces weakened the state until the Elamites sacked Ur around 2004 BC and carried Ibbi-Sin into exile. The dynasty's fall ended Sumerian political dominance, though its language and scholarship endured in scribal schools for centuries.
