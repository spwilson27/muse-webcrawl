Zimmermann Telegram

Sent16 January 1917
SenderArthur Zimmermann, German Foreign Secretary
RecipientHeinrich von Eckardt, German minister to Mexico
Intercepted byBritish Naval Intelligence, Room 40
Published1 March 1917

The Zimmermann Telegram (or Zimmermann Note) was a secret diplomatic communication issued from the German Foreign Office in January 1917 that proposed a military alliance between the German Empire and Mexico if the United States entered the First World War against Germany. In return, Germany promised to finance Mexico's war and help it recover the territories of Texas, New Mexico and Arizona, lost to the United States in the 19th century.

Interception and publication

The telegram was intercepted and decrypted by British Room 40 naval intelligence. To protect their source, the British arranged for a copy to be obtained in Mexico so the United States could be shown the document without revealing that Britain was reading transatlantic cables. The telegram was published in American newspapers on 1 March 1917.

Effect

Revelation of the telegram outraged American public opinion, which was already turning against Germany over its resumption of unrestricted submarine warfare. It is regarded as one of the key factors that drew the United States into the war: on 6 April 1917, Congress declared war on Germany. Significantly, Foreign Secretary Arthur Zimmermann publicly admitted the telegram was genuine, removing any doubt about its authenticity.
