The Dutch West India Company (Dutch: Geoctrooieerde Westindische Compagnie, WIC) was a Dutch chartered company founded in 1621 and granted a trade monopoly by the States General of the Dutch Republic in the Americas and West Africa. Patterned on the Dutch East India Company (VOC) but governed by the "Heren XIX" (Nineteen Gentlemen), it was as much an instrument of war against Spain and Portugal as a commercial enterprise.

Activities

The company's greatest single prize came in 1628, when Admiral Piet Hein captured the Spanish silver fleet off Cuba — the only time the feat was accomplished. The WIC established New Netherland in North America (including New Amsterdam, later New York), conquered sugar-producing Dutch Brazil (1630–1654), seized Curaçao and other Caribbean islands, and held forts on the West African Gold and Slave Coasts, becoming a central carrier in the Atlantic slave trade.

Decline and second company

Overextended and chronically indebted, the first WIC was dissolved in 1674 and replaced by a second, more modest company focused on its remaining colonies and the slave trade to Suriname and the Caribbean. It was finally wound up in 1792 amid the upheavals of the Dutch Patriot era.
