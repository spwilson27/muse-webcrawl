The Windward Islands are the southern, generally larger islands of the Lesser Antilles in the West Indies. They lie roughly from Martinique in the north to Grenada in the south, forming the eastern boundary of the Caribbean Sea where they face the prevailing trade winds — hence the name "windward," the side facing the wind. The northern islands of the Lesser Antilles are correspondingly known as the Leeward Islands.

Geography

The Windward Islands include the island nations and territories of Martinique (an overseas department of France), Saint Lucia, Barbados (geographically just east of the chain), Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, and Grenada. Many are volcanic in origin, with lush mountainous interiors rising steeply from the sea; the Pitons of Saint Lucia and the crater lake of Grenada's Grand Etang are notable features.

History

The islands were inhabited by the Kalinago (Island Caribs) when European colonization began. France and Britain contested control through the 17th and 18th centuries, with several islands changing hands repeatedly. Sugar plantations worked by enslaved Africans shaped the islands' economies and cultures. Most of the British Windwards formed the former British colony of the Windward Islands (1833–1960), administered from Grenada, before joining the West Indies Federation. Saint Lucia, Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, and Grenada are independent states today, while Martinique remains French.

Name in other languages

In French the islands are the Îles du Vent; in Spanish, Islas de Barlovento. In the French and Dutch usage, the grouping is sometimes arranged differently, with the French Îles du Vent also encompassing Guadeloupe.
