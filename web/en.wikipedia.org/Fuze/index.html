Fuze

In military munitions, a fuze (also spelled "fuse" in some uses) is the device that initiates the function of an explosive projectile or device — detonating it at the desired time, place, or under specific conditions such as impact, altitude, or proximity to a target. Modern military usage reserves "fuze" for sophisticated initiating devices, distinguishing them from simple burning fuses.

Types

Fuzes are classified by their mode of action: impact (percussion) fuzes detonate on striking a target; time fuzes function after a set interval, set mechanically or electronically; proximity (VT) fuzes, introduced in World War II, use radio or other sensors to detonate near a target; and command or remote fuzes are triggered by an operator. Safety-and-arming mechanisms prevent detonation until the projectile has safely left the weapon.

History

Early shells used burning time fuses lit by the propellant flash. The 20th century brought reliable mechanical time fuzes, the proximity fuze — ranked among the most important wartime technological developments — and today's programmable electronic multi-mode fuzes.
