International Ski and Snowboard Federation

The International Ski and Snowboard Federation (French: Fédération internationale de ski et de snowboard), commonly abbreviated FIS, is the highest international governing body for skiing and snowboarding. Founded in Chamonix, France, in 1924 during the first Winter Olympic Games, the federation sets the rules of competition for its disciplines and coordinates World Cup circuits and World Championships.

History

The FIS was founded on 2 February 1924 in Chamonix by delegates from 14 nations. A predecessor body, the International Ski Commission, had been formed in 1910 in Christiania (now Oslo), Norway, by ten national associations. The federation absorbed the commission and took over responsibility for standardising competition rules and organising international events.

In 2022 the federation formally adopted the name International Ski and Snowboard Federation, replacing International Ski Federation, to reflect the equal standing of snowboarding within the organisation.

Disciplines

The FIS governs alpine skiing, cross-country skiing, ski jumping, Nordic combined, freestyle skiing, snowboarding, speed skiing, grass skiing and Telemark skiing. For each discipline it organises a World Cup series and biennial World Championships, and its competitions form the core of the skiing programme at the Winter Olympic Games.

Organisation

The FIS Congress, held every two years, is the federation's highest authority. A council of 18 members, headed by a president, manages the organisation between congresses. More than 130 national ski associations are affiliated members.
