Chronic radiation syndrome

Chronic radiation syndrome (CRS), also known as chronic radiation enteritis in some contexts, is a constellation of health effects of radiation that occur after months or years of chronic exposure to high amounts of ionising radiation. It is distinct from acute radiation syndrome, which follows a single large exposure over a short period.

History

CRS was described in detail by Soviet physicians in the 1950s among residents of villages along the Techa River in the southern Urals, who were chronically exposed to radioactive waste discharged from the Mayak plutonium production complex. Several thousand people were diagnosed, typically after cumulative exposures exceeding about 1 sievert spread over years.

Signs and symptoms

The syndrome develops gradually and is characterised by suppression of blood cell production (leukopenia, thrombocytopenia and anaemia), impaired immune function, and changes in the nervous and cardiovascular systems. Later effects can include increased rates of leukaemia, other cancers and cataracts. Severity depends on the total dose and dose rate, and recovery is possible if exposure stops and cumulative doses are moderate.
