Sunak ministry

The Sunak ministry was formed by Rishi Sunak on 25 October 2022, after he was invited by King Charles III to form a government in his name, following the resignation of his predecessor Liz Truss amid a financial and political crisis. Sunak, leader of the Conservative Party, served as prime minister until 5 July 2024, when the Conservatives lost the 2024 general election and Keir Starmer formed a Labour government.

Formation

Sunak became Conservative leader unopposed after his rivals Boris Johnson and Penny Mordaunt failed to secure sufficient nominations or withdrew. His first cabinet retained Jeremy Hunt as Chancellor of the Exchequer and James Cleverly as Foreign Secretary, restored Suella Braverman as Home Secretary (she was dismissed in November 2023 and replaced by Cleverly, with former prime minister David Cameron making a surprise return as Foreign Secretary), and promoted Grant Shapps and others.

Policies and events

Sunak set out five pledges in January 2023: halve inflation, grow the economy, reduce national debt, cut NHS waiting lists, and "stop the boats" carrying migrants across the English Channel — the last pursued through the controversial Rwanda asylum plan, ruled unlawful by the Supreme Court in November 2023 and then enacted in modified statutory form in 2024. His government grappled with high inflation, industrial action in the public sector, and by-election defeats. In September 2023 he scaled back net-zero policies and cancelled the northern leg of the HS2 rail project.

Fall

Sunak called a general election for 4 July 2024. The campaign was marred by gaffes, including his early departure from the D-Day 80th anniversary commemorations in Normandy, and the Conservatives suffered their worst defeat in history, winning only 121 seats. Sunak resigned as prime minister on 5 July 2024 and as Conservative leader later that year.
