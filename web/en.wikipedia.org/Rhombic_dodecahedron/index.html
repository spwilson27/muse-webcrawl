The rhombic dodecahedron is a convex polyhedron with 12 congruent rhombic faces, 24 edges and 14 vertices. It is a Catalan solid, the dual of the cuboctahedron, and a zonohedron. Each face is a rhombus whose diagonals are in the ratio 1:√2.

Properties

The rhombic dodecahedron is face-transitive and can fill space without gaps, forming the rhombic dodecahedral honeycomb — the Voronoi tessellation of the face-centred cubic lattice. For a solid with edge length a, the volume is 16√3/9 a³ and the surface area 8√2 a².

Occurrences

The shape occurs naturally in garnet crystals and in the packing of spheres; it corresponds to the Brillouin zone of the body-centred cubic lattice. Johannes Kepler studied the solid and noted its occurrence in pomegranate seeds and bee honeycomb geometry.
