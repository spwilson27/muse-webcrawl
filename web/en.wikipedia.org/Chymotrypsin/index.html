Chymotrypsin

Type: Serine protease enzyme

Source: Pancreas

Precursor: Chymotrypsinogen

Chymotrypsin is a digestive enzyme that breaks down proteins in the small intestine. The pancreas secretes it as the inactive precursor chymotrypsinogen, which is activated by trypsin and further processing in the duodenum.

Action

As a serine protease, chymotrypsin uses a catalytic triad of serine, histidine, and aspartate to cleave peptide bonds, preferentially after large hydrophobic amino acids such as tryptophan, tyrosine, phenylalanine, and methionine.

Importance

Chymotrypsin is a classic model enzyme in biochemistry: studies of its structure and mechanism helped establish how enzymes achieve catalysis and specificity. Related proteases perform roles in blood clotting, immunity, and digestion.
