Gummy bear

Gummy bears are small, chewy fruit gum candies shaped like bears. They were invented in Germany in 1922 by Hans Riegel Sr., founder of the Haribo company, who called them Gummibärchen ("little rubber bears").

Composition and manufacture

Gummy bears are traditionally made from sugar, glucose syrup, starch, flavouring, citric acid, colouring and a gelling agent — originally gum arabic, now usually gelatin, which gives them their characteristic bounce and chew. The hot mixture is deposited into starch moulds, allowed to set, then steamed, dried and polished with beeswax or carnauba wax. Pectin- or starch-based versions are sold for vegetarian consumers. A typical bear is about 2 cm tall and weighs roughly 2 grams; classic flavours include raspberry, strawberry, orange, lemon and pineapple.

Popularity

Haribo's Goldbears became a worldwide success, and countless imitators followed. Gummy bears inspired an entire family of gummy candies in other shapes — worms, cola bottles, rings — and became a cultural staple, from the 1980s animated series Adventures of the Gummi Bears to novelty giant bears weighing several kilograms.
