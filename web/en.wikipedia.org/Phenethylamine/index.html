FormulaC8H11N

AppearanceColourless liquid

Phenethylamine (also phenylethylamine, PEA) is an organic compound and a natural monoamine alkaloid. It is a trace amine that acts as a central nervous system stimulant in humans. Phenethylamine is biosynthesized from the amino acid L-phenylalanine by enzymatic decarboxylation, and it occurs naturally in many organisms, including in small amounts in chocolate.

Chemistry

Phenethylamine consists of a benzene ring attached to an amino group via a two-carbon side chain. It is the parent compound of a large class of substituted phenethylamines, which includes many biologically active compounds such as the catecholamine neurotransmitters dopamine, norepinephrine and epinephrine (which are hydroxylated phenethylamines), as well as amphetamine and numerous psychedelic drugs.

Biological role

In the human brain, phenethylamine functions as a neuromodulator or neurotransmitter and is produced during exercise and in response to certain foods. It is rapidly metabolized by monoamine oxidase, mainly to phenylacetic acid, which keeps its concentrations low.
