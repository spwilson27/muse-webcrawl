Ludwig Wilhelm Erhard (4 February 1897 – 5 May 1977) was a German politician affiliated with the Christian Democratic Union (CDU) and the second Chancellor of the Federal Republic of Germany (West Germany) from 1963 until 1966. As Minister for Economic Affairs under Konrad Adenauer from 1949 to 1963, he was the architect of the social market economy and the father of the post-war economic miracle (Wirtschaftswunder).

Early life and currency reform

Erhard was born in Fürth, Bavaria, trained in commerce, and studied economics, earning a doctorate under Franz Oppenheimer. During the Nazi era he worked in market research institutes. After 1945 the American occupation authorities appointed him Bavarian economics minister, and in 1948 he became director of economics for the combined Bizone. In June 1948 he coupled the currency reform that introduced the Deutsche Mark with the abolition of most price controls — a gamble taken largely on his own authority that relaunched the West German economy.

Economics Minister and Chancellor

As federal economics minister (1949–1963), Erhard championed competition, free prices and an independent central bank, putting ordoliberal ideas into practice in the "social market economy". West Germany experienced rapid growth, full employment and rising living standards. Erhard succeeded Adenauer as Chancellor in October 1963 and won the 1965 federal election, but economic slowdown, budget disputes and friction with his own coalition partners forced his resignation on 1 December 1966.

Legacy

Erhard's cigar, rotund figure and slogan "prosperity for all" (Wohlstand für alle, the title of his 1957 book) made him one of West Germany's most popular politicians, and he is still invoked as the symbol of its economic model.
