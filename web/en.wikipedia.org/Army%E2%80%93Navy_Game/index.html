Army–Navy Game
Teams: Army Black Knights vs Navy Midshipmen
First played: 1890
Usually held: second Saturday of December
Part of the Commander-in-Chief's Trophy series

The Army–Navy Game is an American college football rivalry game between the Army Black Knights of the United States Military Academy (West Point) and the Navy Midshipmen of the United States Naval Academy (Annapolis). It is one of the most traditional and enduring rivalries in college football.

History

The teams first met in 1890, when Navy won 24–0. The game has been played annually almost without interruption since 1899 and is traditionally the final regular-season game of the college football season. Presidents have often attended; in 1901 Theodore Roosevelt began the tradition of the President sitting on the Army side for one half and the Navy side for the other.

Pageantry

The game is famous for the march-on of the Brigade of Midshipmen and the Corps of Cadets, flyovers, and the postgame singing of the schools' alma maters — the losing team's first, then the winner's, a ritual expressing mutual respect. The Commander-in-Chief's Trophy, contested among Army, Navy, and Air Force, is often decided by the outcome.

Venues

Though historically played most often in Philadelphia, the game now rotates among stadiums in the northeastern United States.
