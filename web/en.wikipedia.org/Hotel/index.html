Hotel

A hotel is an establishment that provides paid lodging on a short-term basis, usually with additional services such as restaurants, bars, room service, and meeting facilities. Hotels range from small family-run establishments to enormous resort complexes, and are commonly classified by star ratings or comparable systems reflecting their facilities and service levels. The word derives from the French hôtel, referring to a large house or mansion.

History

Inns and guest houses have existed since antiquity — Roman mansiones served travellers on official business, and coaching inns lined the roads of early modern Europe. The modern hotel industry developed in the 19th century with the growth of railway and steamship travel: establishments such as Boston's Tremont House (1829) pioneered private rooms, indoor plumbing, and free soap, while grand hotels like London's Savoy (1889) set new standards of luxury. In the 20th century, motels served motorists, and hotel chains standardised the experience across locations.

Types

Hotels include full-service and luxury hotels, boutique hotels distinguished by design and small size, extended-stay hotels for longer visits, resort hotels at leisure destinations, airport hotels, and budget brands. Many are operated under franchise or management agreements by international groups such as Marriott, Hilton, and InterContinental Hotels Group. Pricing and booking are now dominated by online travel agencies and revenue-management systems that vary room rates with demand.
