Suffolk

Suffolk is a ceremonial county in the East of England and East Anglia. It is bordered by Norfolk to the north, the North Sea to the east, Essex to the south, and Cambridgeshire to the west. The largest town is the county town of Ipswich; other notable towns include Lowestoft, Bury St Edmunds, Newmarket, and Felixstowe, one of the largest container ports in Europe.

The county's name means "southern folk," distinguishing its inhabitants from the "northern folk" of Norfolk; together they formed part of the Anglo-Saxon kingdom of East Anglia. The Sutton Hoo ship burial near Woodbridge, excavated in 1939, produced one of the richest early medieval finds in Britain and is associated with the East Anglian king Rædwald.

Geography and economy

Suffolk is a predominantly low-lying, agricultural county, with the Suffolk Coast and Heaths Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty along its North Sea shore. Agriculture, food processing, and tourism are major activities, alongside the Port of Felixstowe and the horse-racing industry centred on Newmarket. The county gave its name to the Suffolk Punch draught horse and the Suffolk sheep. Artists John Constable and Thomas Gainsborough were born in Suffolk, and the coastal town of Aldeburgh is associated with the composer Benjamin Britten.

Administration

Between 1889 and 1974 the county was divided into East Suffolk and West Suffolk for administrative purposes; the two were reunited under Suffolk County Council in 1974. Ipswich is the main centre of local government.
