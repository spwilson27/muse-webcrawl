Human body weight

Human body weight is a person's mass, conventionally measured in kilograms (or pounds in some countries) without clothing or carried items. Body weight varies with age, sex, height, genetics, diet, physical activity and health status, and average weights have risen in most countries over the past century.

Measurement and indices

Weight is assessed clinically with scales and interpreted relative to height using indices such as the body mass index (BMI), which classifies adults as underweight, normal weight, overweight or obese. Such measures are crude proxies for body composition — the proportions of fat, muscle, bone and water — which can be estimated by methods such as bioelectrical impedance and DXA scanning.

Health

Both underweight and obesity are associated with increased morbidity and mortality. Weight is influenced by energy balance, endocrine conditions, medications and socioeconomic factors, and its management is a major concern of public health worldwide.

Variation

Average body weight differs substantially between countries, reflecting diet, lifestyle and ancestry; the Netherlands has among the tallest and heaviest averages, while parts of South and Southeast Asia record the lowest mean weights.
