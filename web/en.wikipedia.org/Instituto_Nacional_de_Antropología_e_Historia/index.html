The Instituto Nacional de Antropología e Historia (INAH, National Institute of Anthropology and History) is a Mexican federal government bureau established in 1939 by President Lázaro Cárdenas. It is dedicated to the preservation, protection, study and dissemination of Mexico's archaeological, anthropological, historical and paleontological heritage, and to the recovery of that heritage from abroad.

History

INAH was created by law published on 3 February 1939, during the presidency of Lázaro Cárdenas del Río. It grew out of earlier institutions such as the Inspectorate General of Archaeological Monuments of the Republic, founded in 1885 under Porfirio Díaz. In 1946 the institute's historical-monuments responsibilities for the colonial and republican periods were partly separated with the creation of the Instituto Nacional de Bellas Artes (INBA), which took charge of artistic heritage from the colonial era onward, although INAH retained responsibility for historical monuments.

Functions

By law, INAH is responsible for more than 110,000 historical monuments built between the 16th and 19th centuries, some 29,000 registered archaeological zones (of which roughly 180 are open to the public), and Mexico's network of over 160 museums. Its tasks include:

  • Scientific research in anthropology and history
  • Conservation and restoration of cultural heritage
  • Protection and custody of archaeological and historical monuments
  • Publication and dissemination of research
  • Training of professionals through the Escuela Nacional de Antropología e Historia (ENAH) and the Escuela Nacional de Conservación, Restauración y Museografía (ENCRM)

Museums and sites

INAH administers major institutions including the Museo Nacional de Antropología (National Museum of Anthropology) in Mexico City, opened in 1964, and the Museo Nacional de Historia in Chapultepec Castle. It manages archaeological sites such as Teotihuacan, Palenque, Chichén Itzá and Monte Albán, many of them inscribed on UNESCO's World Heritage List.

Organization

The institute operates through regional INAH centers in every Mexican state, coordinating research and conservation locally. Since 2015 it has been attached to the Secretaría de Cultura (Ministry of Culture).
