Banach space

Banach space
Field: Functional analysis
Named after: Stefan Banach
Introduced: 1920–1922

In mathematics, particularly functional analysis, a Banach space is a complete normed vector space: a vector space equipped with a norm such that every Cauchy sequence converges to an element of the space. Banach spaces are among the central objects of modern analysis.

History

The concept was introduced by Stefan Banach in his 1920 doctoral dissertation and developed in his influential 1932 monograph Théorie des opérations linéaires. Related ideas appeared in the work of Hans Hahn and Norbert Wiener, but Banach gave the theory its axiomatic form.

Examples

Every finite-dimensional normed space is complete, so Euclidean spaces are Banach spaces. Key infinite-dimensional examples include the sequence spaces ℓp, the function spaces Lp, the spaces C(K) of continuous functions on compact sets with the supremum norm, and Sobolev spaces. Hilbert spaces are Banach spaces whose norms arise from an inner product.

Theory

Foundational results include the Hahn–Banach theorem, the uniform boundedness principle, the open mapping theorem and the closed graph theorem. Banach spaces support the theory of linear operators, duality and weak topologies, and are the natural setting for much of partial differential equations, probability and quantum mechanics.
