Resource security

Resource security is the reliable and affordable availability of the natural resources a society depends on — including energy, water, food, minerals, and land — and the resilience of their supply against disruption. It is closely related to energy security, water security, and food security.

Dimensions

Resource security analysis generally considers availability (physical stocks and flows), accessibility (infrastructure and markets), affordability, and acceptability, including environmental impact. Threats include resource depletion, price volatility, geopolitical competition for supplies, concentration of production of critical minerals in a few countries, and stresses from climate change and population growth.

The resource nexus

Energy, water, and food systems are interdependent — water is needed for energy production and agriculture; energy is needed for water treatment and food production — a relationship called the nexus. Disruption in one sector can cascade into others, so resource security policy increasingly treats the three together.

Policy approaches

Strategies to improve resource security include diversifying supply sources, building strategic reserves, recycling and the circular economy, improving resource efficiency, substituting abundant materials for scarce ones, and international cooperation on shared resources such as transboundary rivers. Growing demand for lithium, cobalt, rare earth elements, and other critical minerals for the energy transition has made resource security a central concern of industrial policy for many governments.
