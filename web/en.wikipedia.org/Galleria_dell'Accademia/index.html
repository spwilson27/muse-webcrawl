The Galleria dell'Accademia di Firenze, or "Gallery of the Academy of Florence", is an art museum in Florence, Italy. It is best known as the home of Michelangelo's statue of David. It also has other sculptures by Michelangelo and a large collection of paintings by Florentine artists, mostly from the period 1300–1600, the Trecento to the Late Renaissance.

History

The gallery was founded in 1784 by Grand Duke Pietro Leopoldo of Tuscany alongside the Accademia di Belle Arti, Florence's art academy, to display examples of historic and modern art for students. It absorbed works from suppressed churches and convents. In 1873 Michelangelo's David was moved here from Piazza della Signoria to protect it from the weather, and a purpose-built tribune designed by Emilio De Fabris was completed in 1882; a replica now stands in the piazza.

Collection

Beyond David, the museum displays Michelangelo's four unfinished Prisoners (or Slaves), his Saint Matthew and the Palestrina Pietà (attribution debated). Painting galleries hold works by Paolo Uccello, Domenico Ghirlandaio, Sandro Botticelli, Filippino Lippi and Perugino, as well as a notable collection of Florentine Gothic painting and Russian icons. The museum also houses the Department of Musical Instruments, including Stradivari and Amati instruments from the Medici collections.

Visitor information

The Accademia is Florence's second most visited museum after the Uffizi, drawing over a million visitors in peak years, most of them to see David. It is a state museum under the Italian Ministry of Culture.
