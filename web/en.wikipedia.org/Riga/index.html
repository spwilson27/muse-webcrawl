Riga

Riga is the capital and largest city of Latvia, lying on the Gulf of Riga at the mouth of the Daugava River. With a population of about 600,000, it is home to roughly a third of Latvia's people and is the largest city of the Baltic states. Its historical centre is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, noted for its Art Nouveau architecture and 19th-century wooden buildings.

History

Riga was founded in 1201 by Bishop Albert of Buxhoeveden as a base for German crusaders and merchants, and it joined the Hanseatic League in 1282. Over the centuries the city passed among Polish-Lithuanian, Swedish and Russian rule, becoming one of the Russian Empire's major western ports and industrial centres. Riga was the capital of independent Latvia between 1918 and 1940, endured Soviet and Nazi occupation, and became capital again when Latvia restored its independence in 1991.

Architecture

The medieval Old Town contains Riga Cathedral (founded 1211) and St Peter's Church, while the boulevard districts built around 1900 hold one of the world's finest concentrations of Art Nouveau (Jugendstil) apartment buildings, many designed by Mikhail Eisenstein. The Freedom Monument, erected in 1935, is a central national symbol.

Economy

Riga is the Baltic region's principal transport and logistics hub, with a major ice-free port, an international airport that is the largest in the Baltic states, and growing finance, IT and tourism sectors.
