Margin of safety (financial)

Diagram of intrinsic value versus market price (image placeholder).

The margin of safety is a principle of investing, popularised by Benjamin Graham and David Dodd in Security Analysis (1934) and The Intelligent Investor (1949), under which an investor buys a security only when its market price is significantly below its estimated intrinsic value. The discount provides a cushion against errors in judgement, bad luck, or greater-than-usual volatility.

Concept

Graham held that "the margin of safety is always dependent upon the price paid" and made the concept the cornerstone of value investing. Warren Buffett, Graham's student, described the margin of safety and "Mr. Market" as the two most important ideas an investor can learn. In accounting usage, the term also denotes the difference between actual (or budgeted) sales and the break-even level of sales.

Application

Value investors commonly require a discount of a third or more to a conservative estimate of intrinsic value before buying, so that even if their analysis is moderately wrong the investment is unlikely to result in permanent loss of capital.
