Croatian War of Independence

Date: 31 March 1991 – 12 November 1995
Location: Croatia
Result: Croatian victory
Belligerents: Croatia vs. Republic of Serbian Krajina, Yugoslav People's Army, Republika Srpska

The Croatian War of Independence was fought from 1991 to 1995 between Croat forces loyal to the Government of Croatia—which had declared independence from the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia (SFRY)—and the Serb-controlled Yugoslav People's Army (JNA) and local Serb forces, with the JNA ending its combat operations in Croatia by January 1992. In Croatia, the war is primarily referred to as the "Homeland War" (Domovinski rat).

Theatres of the Croatian War of Independence.

Background

Following the death of Yugoslav leader Josip Broz Tito in 1980, ethnic tensions within Yugoslavia rose. In 1990, Croatia held multi-party elections won by the nationalist Croatian Democratic Union (HDZ) led by Franjo Tuđman, who advocateded sovereignty for Croatia. Many Croatian Serbs feared the new government and, encouraged by Belgrade, began an armed rebellion known as the Log Revolution in August 1990.

Course of the war

Open war began in 1991. The JNA intervened on the side of the Croatian Serbs, and fighting escalated through the autumn, including the Battle of the Barracks, the Siege of Dubrovnik, and the Battle of Vukovar, which fell in November 1991 after an 87-day siege. By the end of 1991, Serb forces controlled nearly a third of Croatia's territory, proclaimed as the Republic of Serbian Krajina. The Sarajevo Agreement of January 1992 brought a ceasefire and the deployment of the United Nations Protection Force (UNPROFOR).

In 1995, the Croatian Army launched Operation Flash in May, retaking western Slavonia, and Operation Storm in August, which recaptured most of the Krajina in four days and precipitated an exodus of Serb civilians. The war effectively ended with the Erdut Agreement of 12 November 1995, which provided for the peaceful reintegration of eastern Slavonia, completed in 1998 under UNTAES administration.

Consequences

Estimates of the death toll range around 20,000 killed or missing on all sides, and hundreds of thousands were displaced. Croatia's independence was internationally recognised in January 1992. The International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia prosecuted a number of individuals for war crimes committed during the conflict.
