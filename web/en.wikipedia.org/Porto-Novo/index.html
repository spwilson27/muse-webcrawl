Porto-Novo

Porto-Novo (Portuguese for "New Port"; also known as Hogbonu or Adjacé) is the official capital of Benin, although the seat of government is in Cotonou, the country's largest city. Porto-Novo is the second-largest city in Benin, with a population of roughly 260,000, and lies on a lagoon near the Gulf of Guinea in the southeast of the country.

History

The city was founded probably in the late sixteenth century by the legendary King Te-Agbanlin and developed as a port for the Atlantic slave trade, flourishing under Portuguese influence — hence its Portuguese name. In the nineteenth century the kingdom of Porto-Novo accepted French protection, and the city became capital of the French colony of Dahomey and later of independent Benin (1960).

Porto-Novo also received a community of Afro-Brazilian returnees (Aguda), whose influence shows in the city's distinctive Brazilian-style architecture, such as the Great Mosque, built in the style of a Bahian church.

Landmarks

Notable sites include the Ethnographic Museum, the Honmé royal palace museum, the cathedral, and the da Silva Museum of Beninese history. The National Assembly of Benin sits in Porto-Novo, though most ministries are in Cotonou.
