Cyclic model

A cyclic model (or oscillating model) is any of several cosmological models in which the universe follows infinite, self-sustaining cycles. The universe is hypothesised to expand from a Big Bang and collapse in a Big Crunch, repeatedly, or — in newer versions — to evolve through cycles of expansion in which the Big Bang is a recurring transition rather than an absolute beginning.

History

Oscillating models were considered in the 1930s following the work of Alexander Friedmann and Richard Tolman, but Tolman showed that entropy would increase from cycle to cycle, making past cycles progressively shorter and casting doubt on an eternal oscillation. Interest declined after the discovery of the accelerating expansion of the universe in 1998.

Modern versions

The ekpyrotic and cyclic models developed by Paul Steinhardt, Neil Turok, and collaborators invoke branes in string theory: collisions between branes produce Big-Bang-like events, and dark energy dilutes entropy between cycles. The conformal cyclic cosmology of Roger Penrose proposes an infinite sequence of "aeons" linked conformally.
