Flinders Chase National Park

Flinders Chase National Park is a protected area in the Australian state of South Australia located at the west end of Kangaroo Island, about 177 kilometres (110 mi) south-west of the state capital of Adelaide and 21 kilometres (13 mi) south of the municipal seat of Kingscote. It is a sanctuary for endangered species and home to a few geological phenomena.

History and features

Proclaimed in 1919, Flinders Chase was the second national park declared in South Australia and the third in Australia. It covers roughly 32,000 hectares at the island's western end. Its best-known attractions are the Remarkable Rocks, a cluster of enormous wind-sculpted granite boulders perched on a coastal dome, and Admirals Arch at Cape du Couedic, a natural rock arch beneath which a colony of New Zealand fur seals can be observed. The park also preserves the Cape du Couedic lighthouse (built 1909) and the historic weirs and cottages associated with early settlement.

Wildlife

The park protects koalas, kangaroos (the Kangaroo Island subspecies), tammar wallabies, echidnas, Rosenberg's goannas and a colony of fur seals, as well as important bird habitat. Koalas, introduced to the island in the 1920s, thrive in the manna gum woodland around the Rocky River visitor area.

Bushfires

In December 2019 and January 2020, lightning-started bushfires burned nearly the whole of the park — part of the catastrophic 2019–20 Australian bushfire season that burned about half of Kangaroo Island. The visitor centre was destroyed and much wildlife was killed, but vegetation and animal populations have since been regenerating, and visitor facilities have been progressively rebuilt.
