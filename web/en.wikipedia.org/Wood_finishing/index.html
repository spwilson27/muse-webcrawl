Wood finishing

Wood finishing refers to the process of refining or protecting a wooden surface, especially in the production of furniture, flooring, and musical instruments. A finish may be evaporative (such as shellac or lacquer), reactive (such as drying oils and varnishes), or coalescing (such as water-based products).

Preparation

Surfaces are planed, scraped, or sanded through progressively finer grits. Defects are filled, and staining may be used to alter colour before the protective coat is applied.

Types of finish

Traditional finishes include shellac, applied by French polishing; boiled linseed oil and tung oil, which cure by oxidation; and oil-based varnishes. Modern finishes include nitrocellulose and conversion lacquers, polyurethane, and ultraviolet-cured coatings used in factory production.

Application

Finishes are brushed, wiped, padded, or sprayed. Multiple thin coats are built up, levelled between coats, and finally rubbed out or polished to the desired sheen, from matte to high gloss.
