Gerhard Schröder

Born: 7 April 1944, Mossenberg, Germany
Office: Chancellor of Germany (1998–2005)
Party: Social Democratic Party (SPD)

Gerhard Fritz Kurt Schröder (born 7 April 1944) is a German politician who served as chancellor of Germany from 1998 to 2005. A member of the Social Democratic Party (SPD), he previously served as minister-president of Lower Saxony from 1990 to 1998. His tenure as chancellor was marked by the reform programme known as Agenda 2010, which restructured Germany's labour market and welfare system, and by his government's opposition to the Iraq War of 2003. Within the history of Alliance 90/The Greens, Schröder is notable for forming Germany's first federal red-green coalition, governing together with the Greens under Joschka Fischer from 1998 until the snap election of 2005.

Early life and career

Schröder was born in Mossenberg in the Lippe district; his father was killed in the Second World War in 1944, and he grew up in modest circumstances. After training as a retail salesman, he studied law at the University of Göttingen and worked as a lawyer. He joined the SPD in 1963 and rose through its youth organisation, the Jusos, before entering the Bundestag in 1980.

Chancellorship

Schröder led the SPD to victory in the 1998 federal election, ending sixteen years of government under Helmut Kohl. Together with Alliance 90/The Greens he enacted a phase-out of nuclear power, reformed citizenship law, and introduced registered partnerships for same-sex couples. His second term, won narrowly in 2002, was dominated by the Hartz labour-market reforms. After a narrow defeat in 2005 he left front-line politics. His later work for Russian energy companies, including Nord Stream, drew sustained criticism, especially after Russia's invasion of Ukraine in 2022.
