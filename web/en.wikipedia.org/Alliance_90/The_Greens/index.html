Alliance 90/The Greens (German: Bündnis 90/Die Grünen), often simply The Greens (German: Grüne), is a green political party in Germany. It was formed in 1993 as the merger of The Greens, formed in West Germany in 1980, and Alliance 90, formed in East Germany in 1990. The party is one of the most successful green parties in the world, having been part of the federal government several times.

The party holds a dual leadership structure with two co-chairs, a federal structure reflecting Germany's states, and traditionally promotes gender equality through quotas and zipper-style candidate lists. Its symbol is the sunflower.

History

The Greens were founded in Karlsruhe in January 1980 from a coalition of environmental, anti-nuclear, peace, feminist, and alternative movements. They entered the Bundestag in 1983 with 5.6% of the vote. The party was divided between the pragmatic "Realo" wing, associated with Joschka Fischer, and the fundamentalist "Fundi" wing, which opposed coalitions with established parties. Following German reunification, the western Greens merged in 1993 with Alliance 90, a coalition of East German civil rights movements from the Peaceful Revolution of 1989, adopting the combined name.

In government

From 1998 to 2005, the Greens governed in a "red–green" coalition with the Social Democratic Party under Chancellor Gerhard Schröder, holding ministries including foreign affairs (Joschka Fischer), environment, and agriculture. The government decided the phase-out of nuclear power, reformed citizenship law, and introduced the Renewable Energy Sources Act (EEG), which drove the expansion of wind and solar energy.

The party achieved its best federal result to that point in the 2021 federal election, with 14.7% of the vote, and entered the "traffic light" coalition with the SPD and the FDP under Chancellor Olaf Scholz, taking ministries including foreign affairs (Annalena Baerbock), economy and climate (Robert Habeck), and environment. The coalition collapsed in November 2024.

Positions

The party advocates ambitious climate policy, a rapid transition to renewable energy, social justice, gender equality, LGBTQ rights, a humane migration policy, and European integration. Its membership, around 130,000–150,000 in the mid-2020s, is concentrated in urban and university regions, and the party governs or co-governs in numerous state governments, including Baden-Württemberg, where Winfried Kretschmann has served as minister-president since 2011.
