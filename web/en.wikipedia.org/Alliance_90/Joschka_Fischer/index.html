Joschka Fischer

Joseph Martin "Joschka" Fischer (born 12 April 1948) is a German politician of the Alliance 90/The Greens party. He served as foreign minister and vice chancellor of Germany in the government of Chancellor Gerhard Schröder from 1998 to 2005, and was long one of the most popular politicians in the country.

Born in Gerabronn, Württemberg, to a family of ethnic Germans expelled from Hungary, Fischer left school without a diploma, worked various jobs, and became active in the radical left and the Frankfurt squatter and Sponti scene of the 1970s, during which he took part in street clashes with police. He later renounced violence and joined the newly founded Green Party in 1982.

Political career

Fischer was elected to the Bundestag in 1983. In Hesse in 1985 he became the first Green state minister in Germany, serving as minister for the environment and energy — famously sworn in wearing sneakers. In 1993 the party became Alliance 90/The Greens, and after the 1998 federal election Fischer entered the red-green federal government as foreign minister.

As foreign minister he supported German participation in the NATO intervention in Kosovo in 1999 — the Bundeswehr's first combat deployment since 1945 — securing his party's backing at a special conference where a protester threw a paint bag that burst his eardrum. He opposed the 2003 invasion of Iraq alongside Schröder. After leaving office in 2005 he withdrew from front-line politics, taught at Princeton University, and worked as a political consultant and author.

Legacy

Fischer is widely regarded as the figure who transformed the German Greens from a protest movement into a party of government, and surveys during his tenure regularly ranked him among Germany's most trusted politicians.
