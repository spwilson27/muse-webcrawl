Alliance 90/The Greens


The sunflower logo of Bündnis 90/Die Grünen.

Alliance 90/The Greens (German: Bündnis 90/Die Grünen, often shortened to Grüne, B90/Grüne or Greens) is a green political party in Germany. It was formed in 1993 through the merger of The Greens, founded in West Germany in 1980, and Alliance 90, an alliance of civil-rights movements formed during the peaceful revolution in East Germany in 1989–1990. The party advocates climate protection, ecological modernisation, social justice, feminism, grassroots democracy and a pro-European course in foreign policy. It has participated in federal government in coalition with the Social Democratic Party (SPD) from 1998 to 2005 and again, in a three-way "traffic light" coalition with the SPD and the Free Democratic Party, from 2021.

History

The Greens were founded in Karlsruhe in January 1980, bringing together environmental activists, anti-nuclear campaigners, peace-movement groups and feminists under the slogan of "four pillars": ecology, social justice, grassroots democracy and nonviolence. The party entered the Bundestag in 1983 and became a model for green parties across Europe. Its early years were marked by a struggle between the pragmatic Realo wing, which favoured coalition politics, and the fundamentalist Fundi wing, which prioritised movement politics over government participation.

In East Germany, the citizens' movements that helped bring down the communist regime — including New Forum, Democracy Now and the Initiative for Peace and Human Rights — joined together as Alliance 90 (Bündnis 90) for the first free East German elections of 1990. After lengthy negotiations, Alliance 90 merged with the (western) Greens in 1993, three years after German reunification, creating the present party.

In government

After the 1998 federal election, the Greens formed a "red–green" coalition with Gerhard Schröder's SPD. Green foreign minister Joschka Fischer became one of Germany's most popular politicians, while environment minister Jürgen Trittin oversaw the nuclear phase-out agreement and the Renewable Energy Sources Act (EEG) of 2000, which spurred the rapid growth of wind and solar power. The coalition was re-elected in 2002 but fell in 2005. At state level the Greens have led governments in Baden-Württemberg under Winfried Kretschmann since 2011, the first Green minister-president of a German state.

In the 2021 federal election the party, led by chancellor candidate Annalena Baerbock, won 14.8% of the vote, its best federal result to that date, and entered government with the SPD and FDP. Baerbock became foreign minister and Robert Habeck vice-chancellor and minister for economic affairs and climate action. The coalition collapsed in November 2024, and in the February 2025 election the Greens moved into opposition.

Ideology and policies

The party's programme commits it to phasing out fossil fuels and reaching climate neutrality, expanding renewable energy, and combining ecological policy with social safeguards. It supports rent controls and affordable housing measures, a minimum wage, gender equality and liberal migration and citizenship policies, and it is strongly pro-European, favouring deeper EU integration, a common European defence capacity and firm support for Ukraine following Russia's invasion in 2022. Internally, the party maintains a dual leadership of one woman and one man, gender quotas for candidate lists, and a membership that votes on coalition agreements.

Organisation

Alliance 90/The Greens is affiliated with the European Green Party and the Global Greens, and its members of the European Parliament sit in the Greens/EFA group. Its political foundation is the Heinrich Böll Foundation. Membership grew rapidly in the late 2010s, passing 100,000 around 2020, making it one of Germany's larger parties by membership.
