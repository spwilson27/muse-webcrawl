Energiewende

The Energiewende (German for "energy turnaround" or "energy transition") is Germany's planned and ongoing transition from fossil fuels and nuclear power to a low-carbon, environmentally sound, reliable and affordable energy supply based predominantly on renewable energy sources such as wind, solar, biomass, hydro and geothermal power, together with greatly improved energy efficiency.

Background

The term originated in a 1980 publication by the Öko-Institut calling for complete abandonment of nuclear and petroleum energy. Policy milestones include the Renewable Energy Sources Act (EEG) of 2000, which introduced feed-in tariffs that drove rapid growth of wind and solar power, and the decision, accelerated after the 2011 Fukushima disaster under Chancellor Angela Merkel's government, to phase out nuclear power entirely — a goal completed in April 2023 when Germany's last three reactors shut down.

Targets and progress

Germany aims for climate neutrality by 2045, with renewable sources supplying the large majority of electricity well before that date. Renewables passed 50 percent of gross electricity consumption in the early 2020s. The Energiewende has been widely studied and imitated internationally, though it has also faced criticism over electricity prices, grid expansion delays, continued use of lignite coal in the interim, and Germany's increased reliance on imported natural gas before 2022.
