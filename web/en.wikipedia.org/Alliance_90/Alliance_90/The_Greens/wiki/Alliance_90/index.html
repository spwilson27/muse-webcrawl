Alliance 90

Alliance 90 (German: Bündnis 90) was a political movement in the German Democratic Republic (East Germany) formed in the final years of communist rule. It brought together opposition and citizens' movements that had campaigned against the Socialist Unity Party (SED) regime during the Peaceful Revolution of 1989, including New Forum, Democracy Now, and the Initiative for Peace and Human Rights.

History

The alliance was founded on 4 February 1990 ahead of East Germany's only free parliamentary election, the Volkskammer election of 18 March 1990. Running under the name Alliance 90, the three movements won about 2.9 percent of the vote and 12 seats in the Volkskammer. Activists from the alliance, such as Joachim Gauck and Vera Lengsfeld, played prominent roles in documenting the abuses of the Stasi secret police.

After German reunification in October 1990, Alliance 90 combined with the East German Green Party. On 14 May 1993 it merged with the western German Green Party, The Greens, to form the unified party Alliance 90/The Greens (Bündnis 90/Die Grünen), which retained the Alliance 90 name in recognition of the eastern movement's legacy.
