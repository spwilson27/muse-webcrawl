Green politics

Green politics, or ecopolitics, is a political ideology that aims to foster an ecologically sustainable society rooted in environmentalism, nonviolence, social justice and grassroots democracy. It began taking shape in the Western world in the 1970s, when green parties emerged first in New Zealand, Tasmania and Germany, and has since developed in many countries around the world.

Core tenets

The Global Greens Charter identifies six guiding principles: ecological wisdom, social justice, participatory democracy, nonviolence, sustainability and respect for diversity. The earlier "Four Pillars" of the German Greens — ecology, social justice, grassroots democracy and nonviolence — remain a common formulation. Green politics typically places environmental issues such as climate change, pollution and biodiversity loss at the centre of its programme, and often combines them with policies on decentralisation, economic reform and civil liberties.

History

Influences on green politics include conservation and environmental movements, the peace movements of the Cold War era, indigenous peoples' struggles for land rights, and thinkers such as Murray Bookchin and Petra Kelly. Green parties entered national governments as coalition partners in countries including Germany, Finland and Belgium from the 1990s onward, and green movements now participate in the Global Greens federation, founded in Canberra in 2001.
