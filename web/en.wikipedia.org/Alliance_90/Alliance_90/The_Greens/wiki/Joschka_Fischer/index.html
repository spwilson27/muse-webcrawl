Joschka Fischer

Joseph Martin "Joschka" Fischer (born 12 April 1948 in Gerabronn) is a German former politician of Alliance 90/The Greens who served as foreign minister and vice chancellor of Germany from 1998 to 2005 in the government of Chancellor Gerhard Schröder. For much of that period he was considered among the most popular politicians in Germany.

Early life and radical years

Fischer left school early, worked in factories and as a taxi driver, and became involved in the far-left and student movements of the late 1960s and 1970s in Frankfurt am Main, associating with the Revolutionary Struggle (Revolutionärer Kampf) group. Photographs of him fighting police at a 1973 demonstration later became controversial. He gradually moved toward parliamentary politics, publishing and working in bookselling before joining the new Green Party in 1982.

Political career

Fischer entered the Bundestag in 1983 and became one of the Greens' first leading figures, becoming Hesse's environment minister in 1985 — famously sworn in wearing sneakers. As federal foreign minister he supported NATO intervention in Kosovo in 1999, backed the war in Afghanistan in 2001, but opposed the 2003 invasion of Iraq, memorably telling US Defense Secretary Donald Rumsfeld "Excuse me, I am not convinced." After leaving office in 2005 he lectured at Princeton University and worked as a consultant and author on European and international affairs.
