Green party

Ideology: Green politics
Symbol: Sunflower
International: Global Greens
First parties: 1970s (New Zealand, Tasmania, Germany)

A Green party is a formally organised political party based on the principles of green politics, such as social justice, environmentalism, and nonviolence. Green parties exist in nearly 90 countries, typically emphasise environmental protection, grassroots democracy, and sustainability, and are usually placed on the left of the political spectrum. Many are members of the international Global Greens network.

History

The earliest Green parties emerged in the 1970s, including the Values Party in New Zealand (1972) and the United Tasmania Group (1972). The West German Greens, founded in 1980, became the most influential early example, entering the Bundestag in 1983. After German reunification the party merged with the East German civil-rights alliance Bündnis 90 to form Alliance 90/The Greens, which entered federal government in 1998 in coalition with the SPD under Gerhard Schröder.

Policy and organisation

Green parties generally advocate rapid decarbonisation, protection of biodiversity, renewable energy, and proportional representation. They often use gender quotas and dual leadership structures. Since the 1990s, Green parties have participated in coalition governments across Europe, in New Zealand, and elsewhere, and the European Green Party coordinates their work in the European Parliament.
