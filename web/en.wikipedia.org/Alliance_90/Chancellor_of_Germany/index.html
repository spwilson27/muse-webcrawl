The Chancellor of Germany, officially the Federal Chancellor of the Federal Republic of Germany (German: Bundeskanzler), is the head of the federal government of Germany and the commander-in-chief of the German Armed Forces during wartime.

Selection and powers

The Chancellor is elected by the Bundestag without debate on the proposal of the Federal President, and appoints and dismisses the federal ministers. Under the Basic Law the Chancellor sets the general guidelines of government policy (Richtlinienkompetenz) and bears responsibility for them. The Chancellor can only be removed by a constructive vote of no confidence, in which the Bundestag simultaneously elects a successor.

Office holders

Post-war chancellors have included Konrad Adenauer, the first Chancellor of the Federal Republic (1949–1963); Willy Brandt; Helmut Schmidt; Helmut Kohl, who presided over reunification; Gerhard Schröder; and Angela Merkel, who served from 2005 to 2021. The office is often described as the most powerful position in German politics.
