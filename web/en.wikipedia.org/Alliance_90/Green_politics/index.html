Green politics

Green politics, or ecopolitics, is a political ideology that aims to foster an ecologically sustainable society, often — but not always — rooted in environmentalism, nonviolence, social justice, and grassroots democracy. It began taking shape in the western world in the 1970s, and since then Green parties have arisen in many countries and formed governments in coalition in several, notably in Germany, where Alliance 90/The Greens first entered the federal government in 1998.

Core tenets

The Global Greens Charter articulates six guiding principles: ecological wisdom, social justice, participatory democracy, nonviolence, sustainability, and respect for diversity. Green politics typically advocates environmental protection, action on climate change, opposition to nuclear power, renewable energy, protection of civil liberties, and a more equitable distribution of wealth. It is often placed on the left of the political spectrum, though some green movements combine environmentalism with other positions ("neither left nor right, but ahead", as early German Greens put it).

History

The first political party to be called Green was the United Tasmania Group, formed in Australia in 1972 to oppose the damming of Lake Pedder. In Europe, the German Green Party (Die Grünen), founded in 1980 out of environmental, anti-nuclear and peace movements, became the most electorally successful green party of the era; in 1993 it merged with the East German Alliance 90 to form Alliance 90/The Greens. Green parties have since participated in national governments in countries including Belgium, Finland, France, Ireland, and New Zealand (in support arrangements).
