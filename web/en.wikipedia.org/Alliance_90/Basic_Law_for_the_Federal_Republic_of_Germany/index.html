The Basic Law for the Federal Republic of Germany (German: Grundgesetz) is the constitution of Germany. It was approved on 8 May 1949 in Bonn and came into force on 23 May 1949 as the constitution of West Germany; after reunification in 1990 it became the constitution of the whole country.

Contents

The Basic Law opens with inviolable human dignity (Article 1) and a catalogue of basic rights, followed by provisions on the federation and the Länder, the Bundestag and Bundesrat, the federal government, legislation, the judiciary, and finance. Notable features include the constructive vote of no confidence, the party-state democracy clauses, and the "eternity clause" (Article 79(3)) protecting human dignity, democracy, and federalism from amendment.

Development

Originally intended as a provisional document pending reunification, the Basic Law acquired permanence after 1990, when accession of the eastern states made a new constitution unnecessary. It has been amended more than sixty times, and is guarded by the Federal Constitutional Court in Karlsruhe, one of the most influential courts of its kind.
