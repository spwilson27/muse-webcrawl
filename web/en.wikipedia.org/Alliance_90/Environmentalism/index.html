Environmentalism

Environmentalism, or environmental rights, is a broad philosophy, ideology, and social movement advocating the protection and restoration of the natural environment and the improvement of environmental health, including through conservation, pollution control, and the mitigation of climate change.

History

Early roots lie in 19th-century conservation movements and the preservationism of figures such as John Muir. The modern movement is often dated to the publication of Rachel Carson's Silent Spring in 1962, which exposed the dangers of pesticides; the first Earth Day in 1970 mobilised millions and preceded landmark environmental legislation and the creation of environmental agencies in many countries.

Approaches and themes

Environmentalism ranges from local conservation and wilderness protection to deep ecology, ecofeminism, environmental justice, and the global climate movement. Green political parties — such as Germany's Alliance 90/The Greens, founded in 1993 from the merger of the West German Greens and the East German Alliance 90 — carry environmentalist programmes into government. Core contemporary concerns include biodiversity loss, deforestation, ocean pollution, and decarbonisation of energy systems.
