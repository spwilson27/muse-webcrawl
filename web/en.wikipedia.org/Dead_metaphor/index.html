Dead metaphor

A dead metaphor is a figure of speech that has lost its original figurative force through repeated use, so that speakers no longer perceive the image behind it. Examples include "the leg of a table," "the foot of a mountain," and "grasping an idea," where the physical image has become a literal conventional meaning.

Explanations

Linguists describe dead metaphors as cases of semantic change: a word's metaphorical sense becomes so common it is recorded as a primary dictionary definition. Philosophers such as Friedrich Nietzsche argued that all language is sedimented metaphor, while later thinkers debated whether dead metaphors still influence thought unconsciously, as cognitive linguistics suggests for conceptual metaphors.

Revival

Writers can resurrect a dead metaphor by extending or literalizing it—for instance, following "grasp an argument" with "but it slipped through his fingers"—restoring the buried image for comic or poetic effect.
