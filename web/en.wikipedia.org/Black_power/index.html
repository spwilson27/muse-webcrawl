Black power

Slogan popularized1966
Key advocatesStokely Carmichael, Huey Newton, Bobby Seale
OrganizationsBlack Panther Party, SNCC (later phase)

Black power is a political slogan and ideology that emphasizes racial pride, economic empowerment, and the creation of political and social institutions by black people. It emerged in the United States in the mid-1960s as activists grew disillusioned with the pace of integrationist civil rights reform.

Origins

The phrase was popularized by Stokely Carmichael during the Meredith March Against Fear in Mississippi in June 1966. In the same year Huey Newton and Bobby Seale founded the Black Panther Party in Oakland, California, advocating self-defence, community programs such as free breakfasts for children, and alliances with other oppressed groups.

Impact

Black Power reshaped African American culture and politics, inspiring black studies programs, assertive artistic movements, and the raised-fist salutes of Tommie Smith and John Carlos at the 1968 Olympics. Variants appeared internationally, including Black Consciousness in South Africa under Steve Biko and movements in Britain and the Caribbean.

Criticism

Leaders such as Martin Luther King Jr. worried the slogan suggested separatism and domination, while others debated its effectiveness; its legacy in affirming black identity and political organization remains widely acknowledged.
