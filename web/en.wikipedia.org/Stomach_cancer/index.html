Stomach cancer
SpecialtyOncology, gastroenterology
SymptomsOften none early; indigestion, nausea, weight loss, abdominal pain, vomiting blood
Risk factorsHelicobacter pylori infection, smoking, diet high in salted/smoked foods, family history
PrognosisFive-year survival roughly 30% overall (much higher if caught early)

Stomach cancer, also known as gastric cancer, is a cancer that develops from the lining of the stomach. Most cases (about 90 per cent) are adenocarcinomas. It is the fifth most commonly diagnosed cancer and a leading cause of cancer death worldwide, with the highest rates in East Asia. Because early disease usually causes few or no symptoms, it is often diagnosed at an advanced stage.

Causes and risk factors

The strongest risk factor is chronic infection with the bacterium Helicobacter pylori, which causes long-standing inflammation (gastritis), atrophy and intestinal metaplasia of the stomach lining — the precancerous cascade described by Pelayo Correa. Other risks include smoking, diets rich in salt-preserved and smoked foods and poor in fresh fruit and vegetables, obesity (for cancers of the cardia), pernicious anaemia, and inherited syndromes such as hereditary diffuse gastric cancer (CDH1 mutations).

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is by gastroscopy with biopsy; staging uses CT and endoscopic ultrasound. Treatment depends on stage: early cancers may be removed endoscopically or by partial or total gastrectomy, while advanced disease is treated with chemotherapy, and in selected cases targeted agents such as trastuzumab (for HER2-positive tumours) or immunotherapy. Japan and South Korea, which run national endoscopic screening programmes, detect many cancers early and report markedly better survival.

Epidemiology

Rates have fallen steadily in most Western countries over the past century, a decline attributed to refrigeration reducing reliance on salted food and to falling H. pylori prevalence. More than one million new cases and roughly 770,000 deaths were estimated globally in 2020.
