The Free University of Berlin (German: Freie Universität Berlin, often abbreviated as FU Berlin or simply FU) is a public research university in Berlin, Germany. Founded in West Berlin in 1948 with American support during the early Cold War period as a Western continuation of the Friedrich Wilhelm University (now Humboldt University), which was in East Berlin under Soviet administration, it has its campus in the residential garden district of Dahlem in southwestern Berlin. Its name refers to West Berlin's status as part of the "free world", in contrast to the communist East.

History

The university was founded by students and scholars on 4 December 1948, prompted by the persecution of protesting students at the traditional Berlin University, which stood in the Soviet sector. With support from the American military government and funds including the Ford Foundation, teaching began in buildings in Dahlem, several of them designed by prominent modernist architects.

The Free University became a centre of the German student movement of the 1960s; the shooting of the student Benno Ohnesorg by a policeman during a 1967 demonstration in West Berlin was a major catalyst of the protests. In the decades after reunification in 1990 the university expanded its research profile, and it has repeatedly won funding in Germany's Excellence Initiative/Strategy, maintaining an institutional strategy centred on international networks. By enrolment it is among the largest universities in Germany, strong in the humanities, social sciences, natural sciences, and medicine (through the Charité medical school, jointly operated with Humboldt University); five Nobel laureates have been associated with it, and its alumni include several German federal ministers.

Campus and organisation

The Dahlem campus contains the Philological Library designed by Norman Foster (nicknamed "The Brain"), the Henry Ford Building, and the botanical garden of Berlin (Botanischer Garten) in nearby Lichterfelde. The university is organised into departments covering law, economics, social and political sciences, mathematics and computer science, physics, biology and chemistry, earth sciences, history and cultural studies, philosophy and humanities, education and psychology, and veterinary medicine.
