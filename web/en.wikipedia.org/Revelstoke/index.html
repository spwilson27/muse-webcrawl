Revelstoke is a city in southeastern British Columbia, Canada, situated on the Columbia River at the western entrance to Rogers Pass in the Selkirk Mountains. It lies on the Trans-Canada Highway between Sicamous and Golden.

History

The town was founded in the 1880s as a station on the Canadian Pacific Railway and was originally called Farwell. It was renamed in honour of Edward Baring, 1st Baron Revelstoke, whose bank helped finance completion of the railway. The Last Spike of the CPR was driven at nearby Craigellachie in November 1885. Revelstoke became a major railway division point and later a centre for hydroelectric development, including the Revelstoke Dam completed in 1984.

Tourism and recreation

Revelstoke is known for deep powder snow and is home to Revelstoke Mountain Resort, opened in 2007, which boasts one of the longest vertical drops in North America. Mount Revelstoke and Glacier national parks lie nearby, and the area is a hub for skiing, heli-skiing, snowmobiling and mountain biking.
