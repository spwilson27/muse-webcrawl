A national day is a day on which celebrations mark the statehood or nationhood of a state or its people. It may be the date of independence, of becoming a republic, of the signing of a constitution, or of another significant national event, and it is often a public holiday. Many countries have more than one date of national significance, but designate one as the official national day.

Origins of particular days

Common categories include:

Independence days — for example the United States (4 July, adoption of the Declaration of Independence, 1776), India (15 August, independence in 1947), and Mexico (16 September, the Grito de Dolores, 1810). Republic and constitution days — Italy's Festa della Repubblica (2 June), Turkey's Republic Day (29 October), and Norway's Constitution Day (17 May). Foundation and unification days — Japan's National Foundation Day (11 February) and Germany's Day of German Unity (3 October). Monarchs' birthdays — the Netherlands' King's Day and Thailand's King's Birthday. Saints' days — Ireland's St Patrick's Day (17 March) and Scotland's St Andrew's Day (30 November).

Observance

National days are typically marked by flag flying, military and civic parades, addresses by heads of state, concerts, fireworks, and cultural festivals; embassies hold receptions abroad. France's Bastille Day (14 July) features one of Europe's largest military parades on the Champs-Élysées, while Spain's Fiesta Nacional (12 October) is held in Madrid.

Countries without a single national day

A few countries, notably the United Kingdom, have no officially designated single national day, though the sovereign's official birthday and the patron saints' days of its constituent countries are celebrated. Denmark likewise has no official national day, with Constitution Day (5 June) informally serving the role.
