Quadrilateral


Examples of quadrilaterals

A quadrilateral is a four-sided polygon in Euclidean plane geometry, with four edges (sides) and four vertices (corners). The word derives from the Latin quadri- (four) and latus (side). The sum of the interior angles of a simple quadrilateral is 360°.

Classification

Simple (non-self-intersecting) quadrilaterals may be convex or concave. Familiar special cases, arranged by increasing symmetry, include:

  • Trapezoid (trapezium in some conventions) — at least one pair of parallel sides
  • Parallelogram — both pairs of opposite sides parallel
  • Kite — two pairs of adjacent equal sides
  • Rhombus — all four sides equal
  • Rectangle — four right angles
  • Square — all sides equal and four right angles; the regular quadrilateral

Self-intersecting quadrilaterals are called complex quadrilaterals or bow-tie quadrilaterals.

Properties

Every convex quadrilateral can be divided by a diagonal into two triangles. Bretschneider's formula generalises Brahmagupta's formula for the area of a cyclic quadrilateral (one whose vertices lie on a common circle) to arbitrary quadrilaterals, using the side lengths and a pair of opposite angles. A quadrilateral's diagonals bisect each other if and only if it is a parallelogram.
