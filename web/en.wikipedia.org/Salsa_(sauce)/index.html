Salsa is a range of sauces in Mexican cuisine, used as condiments, dips and cooking bases. The word is Spanish for "sauce". Salsas are typically based on tomatoes or tomatillos blended with chilli peppers, onion, coriander (cilantro) and lime juice, ranging from mild to extremely hot.

Types

Salsa roja ("red sauce") is made from cooked or raw red tomatoes with chillies such as jalapeño or serrano. Salsa verde uses green tomatillos, often roasted. Pico de gallo is an uncooked, chunky mix of diced tomato, onion, chilli, coriander and lime. Other traditional varieties include salsa de mole, salsa macha (chillies in oil), chipotle-based salsas, and fruit salsas such as mango or pineapple. Ingredients are traditionally ground in a molcajete, a volcanic-stone mortar.

History

Combinations of chillies, tomatoes and ground seeds date to Aztec and Maya cooking long before the Spanish conquest; the Spanish gave them the generic name salsa. Commercial bottled salsa spread through the United States in the 20th century, and by the early 1990s salsa famously rivalled ketchup in American sales — a milestone of Tex-Mex popularity.

Use

Salsa is served with tortilla chips as a dip, spooned over tacos, huevos rancheros and grilled meats, and simmered as a cooking sauce (as in chilaquiles). Heat is measured informally or by the Scoville scale of the chillies used.
