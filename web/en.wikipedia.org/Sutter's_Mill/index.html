Sutter's Mill

Sutter's Mill on the American River, site of the 1848 gold discovery.

Sutter's Mill was a water-powered sawmill on the South Fork of the American River in the foothills of the Sierra Nevada in California. It was named after its owner John Sutter. A worker constructing the mill, James W. Marshall, found gold there in 1848. This discovery set off the California Gold Rush (1848–1855), transforming the region's population and economy.

The discovery

On January 24, 1848, Marshall noticed flakes of metal in the tailrace of the mill and confirmed they were gold. Although Sutter and Marshall tried to keep the discovery secret, word spread quickly, and by the following year tens of thousands of prospectors — the "forty-niners" — arrived in California from around the world.

Legacy

The original mill fell into ruin, but the site is now part of Marshall Gold Discovery State Historic Park in Coloma, California, where a replica mill stands near the discovery site and a monument marks Marshall's grave overlooking the valley.
