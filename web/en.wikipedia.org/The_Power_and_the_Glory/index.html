The Power and the Glory is a 1940 novel by British author Graham Greene. It follows an unnamed fugitive Roman Catholic priest — the "whisky priest" — in the Mexican state of Tabasco during the 1930s, when the government of Plutarco Elías Calles and his successors sought to suppress the Catholic Church. Hunted by a police lieutenant who embodies the regime's secular ideals, the alcoholic, morally compromised priest nonetheless finds a kind of sanctity in his persistence and final martyrdom. The novel is considered among Greene's finest works.

Background

Greene traveled through Mexico in 1938 to report on the religious persecution under the Calles laws and the Tabasco governor Tomás Garrido Canabal, whose Red Shirts destroyed churches and forced priests to marry or flee. His travel book The Lawless Roads (1939) recorded the journey; the novel followed a year later. The persecution depicted is related to the wider conflict known as the Cristero War.

Plot

The whisky priest, the last priest in the state, has fathered a child and survives on brandy and fear, yet continues to say Mass and hear confessions for villagers who risk death to shelter him. Pursued across the state by the lieutenant — an idealistic, ascetic officer who genuinely believes he is liberating the poor from superstition — the priest escapes to a neighboring state but returns when a dying American gunman asks for confession, knowing it is a trap. Captured, he goes before the firing squad, and the novel closes with a boy who had heard his story secretly welcoming a new fugitive priest to the door.

Themes and reception

The novel contrasts two kinds of faith — in God and in revolutionary progress — and explores grace working through a flawed vessel; its title echoes the doxology of the Lord's Prayer. Though condemned by some Catholic authorities for its portrayal of a sinful priest (the Holy Office pressed Greene about the book in the 1950s), it was praised by writers including John Updike and Evelyn Waugh, and appeared on Time magazine's list of the 100 best English-language novels since 1923.
