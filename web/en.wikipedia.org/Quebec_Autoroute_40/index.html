Quebec Autoroute 40

Autoroute 40 is a major autoroute in the Canadian province of Quebec, running along the north shore of the St. Lawrence River. Also known as the Autoroute Félix-Leclerc between Montreal and Quebec City, it is one of the two principal highways linking Quebec's two largest cities, the other being Autoroute 20 on the south shore.

Route description

The autoroute begins at the Ontario border west of Montreal, where it continues as Highway 417 toward Ottawa, and crosses the Island of Montreal as the Metropolitan Expressway (Autoroute Métropolitaine), one of Canada's busiest freeways. East of Montreal it passes through Repentigny, Trois-Rivières and Quebec City, ending in the suburb of Boischatel east of the provincial capital. The total length is about 347 kilometres.

History

The Montreal section opened in stages beginning in 1960 as part of Quebec's new autoroute network, and the through route to Quebec City was completed in the 1970s. In 1997 the segment between Montreal and Quebec City was named in honour of the poet and singer Félix Leclerc. The highway forms part of the main corridor of the Trans-Canada Highway route through Montreal via Autoroute 25 and Autoroute 20.
