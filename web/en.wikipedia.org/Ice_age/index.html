Ice age

An ice age is a long period of reduction in the temperature of Earth's surface and atmosphere, resulting in the presence or expansion of continental and polar ice sheets and alpine glaciers. Earth has experienced at least five major ice ages, including the Huronian, Cryogenian, Andean-Saharan, Karoo and the current Quaternary Ice Age, which began about 2.58 million years ago and continues today — the present Holocene is an interglacial within it.

Glacial cycles

Within an ice age, colder glacial periods alternate with warmer interglacials, paced largely by Milankovitch cycles — variations in Earth's orbit and axial tilt. At the Last Glacial Maximum, about 20,000 years ago, ice sheets kilometres thick covered much of North America and northern Europe, and global sea level was roughly 120 metres lower than today.

Evidence and causes

Evidence comes from glacial landforms, erratics, and chemical proxies in sediments and ice cores. Proposed causes include plate-tectonic changes in ocean circulation, declining atmospheric carbon dioxide, and feedbacks involving ice albedo and greenhouse gases. The possibility of a "Snowball Earth" during the Cryogenian remains a subject of research.
