Hearst Communications, Inc., commonly known as Hearst, is an American multinational mass media and business information conglomerate based in Hearst Tower in Midtown Manhattan, New York City. Founded by William Randolph Hearst in 1887, it is one of the largest diversified media companies in the world and remains privately held by the Hearst family.

History

Hearst began in 1887, when George Hearst gave his son William Randolph control of the San Francisco Examiner. The younger Hearst moved into New York publishing in 1895 by purchasing the New York Journal, sparking the famous circulation war with Joseph Pulitzer's New York World that gave rise to so-called "yellow journalism".

Over the following decades the company expanded into magazines, radio, television and syndication. King Features Syndicate, founded in 1914, became one of the largest distributors of comics and columns. In the twenty-first century Hearst diversified heavily into business information, acquiring stakes in Fitch Ratings and building a substantial portfolio of data and software businesses.

Holdings

Hearst publishes newspapers including the San Francisco Chronicle and the Houston Chronicle, and magazines such as Cosmopolitan, Elle, Esquire, Harper's Bazaar, Good Housekeeping and Popular Mechanics. Hearst Television operates dozens of local television stations, and the company holds major stakes in A&E Networks and ESPN. Through Hearst Ventures it has also invested in numerous digital start-ups.
