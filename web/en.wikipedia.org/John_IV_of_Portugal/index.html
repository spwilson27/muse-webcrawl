John IV (Portuguese: João IV; 18 March 1604 – 6 November 1656), nicknamed "the Restorer," was King of Portugal whose reign began the Portuguese Restoration War and ended 60 years of Iberian Union rule by the Spanish Habsburgs. As Duke of Braganza before his accession, he headed the wealthiest and most powerful noble house in Portugal, and his proclamation as king in 1640 restored Portuguese independence.

Background and accession

Portugal had been ruled from Madrid since 1580, and Spanish taxation, wars and neglect of the Portuguese empire bred deep resentment. On 1 December 1640 conspirators seized the palace in Lisbon, killed the secretary of state Miguel de Vasconcelos, and acclaimed the Duke of Braganza as King John IV. He was crowned on 15 December.

Reign

John spent his reign defending the restoration against Spain. He reorganised the army, fortified the frontier, founded the Council of War and sought allies, signing treaties with France, the Dutch Republic and, in 1642 and 1654, with England. The Restoration War would continue beyond his death until Spain recognised Portuguese independence in 1668. Abroad, his reign saw the recovery of Brazil and Angola from the Dutch. A keen musician and composer, John assembled one of the largest music libraries of his age, destroyed in the Lisbon earthquake of 1755.

Death and succession

John IV died in Lisbon in 1656 and was succeeded by his son Afonso VI under the regency of his widow, Luisa de Guzmán, who is credited with the remark that she would rather be queen for a day than duchess for a lifetime.
