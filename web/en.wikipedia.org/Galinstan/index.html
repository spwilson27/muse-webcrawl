Galinstan

Galinstan is a brand name for a eutectic alloy composed chiefly of gallium, indium, and tin, which melts at −19 °C (−2 °F) and is liquid at room temperature. The name is a portmanteau of gallium, indium, and stannum (Latin for tin). The typical composition is about 68.5% gallium, 21.5% indium, and 10% tin by weight.

Properties

Galinstan is silvery, has a low viscosity and high thermal and electrical conductivity, and unlike mercury — the liquid metal it most often replaces — it is considered non-toxic and environmentally benign. It boils above 1,300 °C. A thin oxide skin forms on its surface in air, which makes it stick to surfaces such as glass; the oxide also lets the liquid hold non-spherical shapes until mechanically disturbed.

Applications

The alloy's best-known use is in thermometers, replacing toxic mercury after the European Union restricted mercury instruments. It is also used as a thermal interface material in cooling, in soft and stretchable electronics, microfluidics, and laboratory demonstrations of liquid metals. Its chief practical limitation is its reaction with some metals, notably aluminium, which it embrittles.
