Piano Concerto (Schumann)

The Piano Concerto in A minor, Op. 54, by Robert Schumann was completed in 1845 and is the composer's only piano concerto. It is one of the most widely performed and recorded piano concertos of the Romantic era.

History

Schumann had sketched a one-movement Phantasie for piano and orchestra in 1841. In 1845 he expanded it, adding an Intermezzo and a finale to create a full three-movement concerto. His wife Clara Schumann, the work's dedicatee and champion, gave the premiere in Dresden on 4 December 1845, with Ferdinand Hiller conducting.

Music

The first movement (Allegro affettuoso) opens with a famous descending piano figure and an oboe theme that pervades the movement. The gentle Intermezzo (Andantino grazioso) flows without pause into the brilliant Allegro vivace finale. A performance lasts about 30–31 minutes. The concerto is notable for the intimate dialogue between soloist and orchestra rather than mere virtuoso display.
