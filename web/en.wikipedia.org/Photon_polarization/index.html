Photon polarization

Photon polarization is the quantum-mechanical description of the classical polarization of electromagnetic radiation: the orientation of the electric-field oscillation of a light wave. A photon carries spin angular momentum of one unit of ħ, and its polarization state is a two-state quantum system (a qubit), fundamental to quantum optics and quantum information science.

Description

Linear polarization states (horizontal and vertical) and circular states (left- and right-handed, the helicity eigenstates with spin projections +ħ and −ħ along the direction of motion) form common bases. Any polarization state can be written as a superposition of basis states; passage through birefringent materials such as wave plates transforms between them.

Applications

Polarization encoding is used in quantum key distribution protocols such as BB84, and polarizers that select photon polarization underlie tests of Bell's theorem and demonstrations of quantum entanglement.
