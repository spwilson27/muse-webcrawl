United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification

The United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD) is an international treaty addressing desertification and land degradation, especially in arid, semi-arid and dry sub-humid areas. Established in 1994 as one of the three "Rio Conventions" arising from the 1992 Earth Summit — alongside the climate and biodiversity conventions — it entered into force on 26 December 1996 and has 197 parties, making it nearly universal. Its secretariat is based in Bonn, Germany.

Objectives and approach

The convention is distinctive among environmental treaties for its emphasis on development and poverty: it promotes a bottom-up approach in which affected countries prepare National Action Programmes with the participation of local communities, farmers and pastoralists. Obligations are elaborated in regional annexes for Africa (given priority), Asia, Latin America and the Caribbean, the Northern Mediterranean, and Central and Eastern Europe. The Global Mechanism, hosted by IFAD, helps mobilize finance for implementation.

Land degradation neutrality

Since the 2010s the convention's central strategic goal has been Land Degradation Neutrality (LDN) — a state in which the quantity and quality of land resources remain stable or improve — which was incorporated into Sustainable Development Goal target 15.3 in 2015. More than 120 countries have set voluntary LDN targets. The UNCCD reports that up to 40 percent of the world's land is degraded, and it organizes the World Day to Combat Desertification and Drought each 17 June. Flagship initiatives associated with the convention include support for Africa's Great Green Wall.

Governance

The Conference of the Parties meets every two years; a Committee for the Review of the Implementation of the Convention and a Committee on Science and Technology advise it.
