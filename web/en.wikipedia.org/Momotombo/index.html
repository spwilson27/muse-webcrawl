Momotombo

Momotombo is a stratovolcano in Nicaragua, located on the northern shore of Lake Managua (Lake Xolotlán), about 40 kilometres (25 miles) northwest of the capital, Managua. It rises 1,297 metres (4,255 feet) above sea level and is notable for its nearly perfect conical shape. The volcano is part of the Reserva Natural Volcán Momotombo and is closely associated with the ruins of León Viejo, one of the oldest Spanish colonial settlements in the Americas, which was abandoned after eruptions in the early 17th century.

Eruptions

Momotombo is a young, active volcano whose edifice began forming roughly 4,500 years ago. A major eruption in 1610 forced the evacuation of the nearby city of León Viejo, whose inhabitants refounded León at its present location. The volcano erupted repeatedly in the 19th century, notably in 1852 and 1905, when it produced lava flows. After 110 years of quiet, Momotombo erupted again on 1–2 December 2015 with ash explosions, followed by further activity and a lava flow in early 2016.

Geothermal energy

The flanks of Momotombo host an important geothermal field. The Momotombo geothermal power plant, at the southern base of the volcano, generates electricity from volcanic steam and has an installed capacity of several tens of megawatts, making geothermal energy one of Nicaragua's most significant renewable power sources. A younger, smaller cone, Momotombito, rises from Lake Managua offshore.

In literature

The French writer Victor Hugo celebrated Momotombo in his poem about the proposed Nicaragua canal, and the volcano became a symbol in debates over the Nicaragua route for an interoceanic canal in the early 1900s; a Nicaraguan postage stamp depicting an erupting Momotombo is said to have influenced the United States Senate's decision to favour the Panama route instead.
