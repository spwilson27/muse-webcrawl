Garage house

Garage house (originally known as garage or the New York sound) is a style of electronic dance music that developed in New York City in the early 1980s. It is named after the Paradise Garage nightclub, where resident DJ Larry Levan developed a distinctive style blending disco, soul, funk, and R&B with emerging electronic production. Garage house is regarded as a direct precursor of house music.

Characteristics

Compared with the Chicago house sound that developed in parallel, garage house retained a stronger emphasis on soulful vocals, gospel-influenced piano chords, and lush orchestration inherited from disco. Tracks were often songs with full vocal performances rather than repetitive instrumentals, and tempos typically sat around 120 beats per minute. DJ Larry Levan's marathon sets and remixes at the Paradise Garage (1977–1987) defined the genre.

Influence

The garage sound strongly influenced the development of house music in the United Kingdom, where the term "garage" was later borrowed for the distinct genre of UK garage. Producers and labels associated with garage house include Tony Humphries, the Paradise Garage's successor clubs such as the Zanzibar in Newark, and labels like West End Records. Artists such as Blaze, Kerri Chandler, and Masters at Work carried the soulful, vocal-led style into the 1990s as "New Jersey sound" and soulful house.
