Launch vehicle

A launch vehicle carrying a payload into orbit

A launch vehicle is typically a rocket-powered vehicle designed to carry a payload (a crewed spacecraft or satellites) from Earth's surface or lower atmosphere to outer space. The most common form is the ballistic missile-type spacecraft launch vehicle, which is the focus of most orbital spaceflight.

Types

Launch vehicles are classified by payload capacity: small-lift (under 2,000 kg to low Earth orbit), medium-lift, heavy-lift, and super heavy-lift vehicles. Examples include the Saturn V and Space Shuttle of the United States, the Soviet and Russian Soyuz family, the European Ariane series, and modern commercial vehicles such as Falcon 9 and Falcon Heavy.

Staging and reusability

Most launch vehicles use multiple stages, discarding spent structures during ascent to improve efficiency as described by the rocket equation. Historically most vehicles were expendable; partially reusable systems, pioneered by the Space Shuttle and later by SpaceX's recoverable boosters, aim to lower cost per launch.

Launch operations

Vehicles are assembled at spaceports such as Cape Canaveral, Baikonur Cosmodrome, and Kourou, and launched into orbits chosen for their mission. Launch failures, while increasingly rare, remain a significant risk for uncrewed payloads.
