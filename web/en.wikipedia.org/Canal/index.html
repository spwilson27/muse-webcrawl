Canal
Artificial waterway for transport, irrigation, or drainage.

A canal is an artificial channel for water conveyance or for the transport of boats or ships. Canals carry free water flow under atmospheric pressure and can be thought of as artificial rivers.

History

The oldest known canals are those built in Mesopotamia around 4000 BCE for irrigation. Canals also played a central role in the Industrial Revolution, when Britain and other countries built extensive networks to move coal, raw materials, and finished goods before railways took over.

Types

Canals serve several purposes: navigation canals carry vessels, irrigation canals distribute water to farmland, drainage canals remove excess water, and power canals carry water to mills and hydroelectric stations.

Notable canals

Famous canals include the Suez Canal (1869), the Panama Canal (1914), and China's Grand Canal, the longest artificial waterway in the world.
