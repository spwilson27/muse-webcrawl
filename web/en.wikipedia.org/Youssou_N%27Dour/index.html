Youssou N'Dour (born 1 October 1959) is a Senegalese singer, songwriter, musician, composer, and occasional actor and politician. He is widely regarded as one of the most celebrated African musicians in history and helped develop mbalax, the popular music of Senegal that blends traditional Wolof rhythms with Latin and pop influences.

Career

N'Dour rose to prominence with the band Étoile de Dakar and later his own group, the Super Étoile. International recognition followed collaborations with artists such as Peter Gabriel and Paul Simon, and his 1994 duet with Neneh Cherry, "7 Seconds", became a worldwide hit. His album Egypt (2004) won a Grammy Award.

Politics and activism

N'Dour has long been involved in social and humanitarian causes, working with UNICEF and campaigning against malaria. He ran for president of Senegal in 2012 but was disqualified on signature technicalities; he subsequently served as Minister of Tourism under President Macky Sall.
