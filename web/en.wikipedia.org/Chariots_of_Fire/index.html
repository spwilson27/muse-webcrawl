Chariots of Fire is a 1981 British historical sports drama film directed by Hugh Hudson and written by Colin Welland. It tells the fact-based story of two British athletes at the 1924 Paris Olympics: Eric Liddell, a devout Scottish Christian sprinter who runs for the glory of God, and Harold Abrahams, an English Jewish student at Cambridge who runs to overcome prejudice.

Plot and themes

The film contrasts the motivations of its protagonists against the backdrop of class and religious tension in post–First World War Britain. Liddell famously refuses to run a heat held on a Sunday, and switches to the 400 metres, which he wins; Abrahams wins the 100 metres. Both men are celebrated on their return home.

Reception and legacy

The film won four Academy Awards, including Best Picture and Best Original Screenplay, and its electronic score by Vangelis — especially the main theme accompanying the slow-motion beach run filmed at St Andrews — became iconic. The title derives from a line in William Blake's poem "Jerusalem": "Bring me my Chariot of fire!"
