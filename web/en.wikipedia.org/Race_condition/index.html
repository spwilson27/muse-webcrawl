Race condition


Placeholder image: Race condition

A race condition is a condition in a system, typically concurrent software, where the output depends on the sequence or timing of other uncontrollable events. It becomes a bug when one or more possible behaviours are undesirable.

Software

In multithreaded programs, a race condition occurs when two threads access shared data simultaneously and at least one access is a write, without proper synchronization (a data race). Consequences range from corrupted data to security vulnerabilities such as time-of-check to time-of-use (TOCTOU) flaws. Locks, atomic operations and lock-free algorithms are used to prevent races; tools such as ThreadSanitizer help detect them.

Electronics

In digital logic, races arise when signals propagate through gates with different delays, producing glitches or metastability. Critical races in asynchronous circuits can cause the circuit to settle in a wrong state.
