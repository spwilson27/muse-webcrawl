Arkansas Razorbacks

The Arkansas Razorbacks, also known as the Hogs, are the intercollegiate athletics teams representing the University of Arkansas, located in Fayetteville. The university fields 19 varsity teams (8 men's and 11 women's) in National Collegiate Athletic Association (NCAA) Division I as a member of the Southeastern Conference (SEC), which Arkansas joined in 1991 after leaving the Southwest Conference.

History and identity

The teams were originally known as the Cardinals until 1909, when coach Hugo Bezdek remarked that his undefeated football team had played "like a wild band of razorback hogs", and the name was adopted. Fans are famous for the "Hog Call" — "Wooo, Pig Sooie!" — one of the best-known cheers in American college sports. The programme's mascot is the razorback hog, represented by the costumed Big Red and the live mascot Tusk.

Major sports

The football team, playing at Donald W. Reynolds Razorback Stadium, claims the 1964 national championship. The men's basketball team won the NCAA championship in 1994 under coach Nolan Richardson and his "Forty Minutes of Hell" style. Arkansas is a perennial powerhouse in track and field and cross country, having won dozens of NCAA team titles under coach John McDonnell, and the baseball team is a regular participant in the College World Series.
