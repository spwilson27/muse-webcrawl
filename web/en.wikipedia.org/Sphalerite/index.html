Sphalerite

Sphalerite is a sulfide mineral with the chemical formula (Zn,Fe)S. It is the most important ore of zinc. Sphalerite is found in a variety of deposit types, but it is primarily in sedimentary exhalative, Mississippi-Valley type, and volcanogenic massive sulfide deposits. It is found in association with galena, chalcopyrite, pyrite (and other sulfides), calcite, dolomite and fluorite. Miners have also been known to refer to sphalerite as zinc blende, black-jack and ruby blende. The mineral's name comes from the Greek sphaleros, meaning "deceiving", because its appearance can be mistaken for galena but it yields no lead.

Properties

Pure sphalerite (ZnS) is nearly colourless to pale yellow; with increasing iron content it becomes dark brown to black (the iron-rich variety is called marmatite). It has resinous to adamantine lustre, perfect dodecahedral cleavage in six directions, a hardness of 3.5–4 on the Mohs scale, and a specific gravity of about 3.9–4.1. Its crystal structure is the zinc blende structure, in which zinc and sulfur atoms occupy interpenetrating face-centred cubic lattices; the same structure is adopted by important semiconductors such as gallium arsenide grown in the zincblende phase.

Uses

Sphalerite is mined principally for zinc, used in galvanising steel, brass, and die-casting alloys. It is also a significant source of byproduct elements including cadmium, gallium, germanium and indium, which substitute into its lattice. Gem-quality transparent crystals, mainly from Spain and Mexico, are faceted for collectors, though the mineral's softness and perfect cleavage make it unsuitable for most jewellery.
