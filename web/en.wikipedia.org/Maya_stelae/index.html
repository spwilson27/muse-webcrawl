Maya stelae

Maya stelae (singular stela) are monuments composed of tall, sculpted stone shafts, generally associated with lowland Maya cities of the Classic period (600–900 AD) and with an important aspect of Maya kingship.

Description

Stelae typically bear carved portraits of rulers in elaborate costume, accompanied by hieroglyphic texts recording their genealogy, accession, warfare, and ritual acts, often paired with a circular stone altar. They were erected to mark period endings in the Long Count calendar.

Notable examples

The tallest known stela, Stela E at Quirigua in Guatemala, stands over 10 metres tall. Famous groups of stelae stand at Copán, Tikal, Piedras Negras, Yaxha, and Calakmul, and they are a primary source for reconstructing Maya dynastic history.
