British Guiana

British Guiana
Period: 1814/1831 – 1966
Capital: Georgetown
Succeeded by: Guyana (independent 26 May 1966)

British Guiana was a British colony on the northern coast of South America, now the independent nation of Guyana. The British gained control of the Dutch colonies of Essequibo, Demerara and Berbice in 1814 (formally ceded by the Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1814), and united them into a single colony in 1831. Georgetown, at the mouth of the Demerara River, was the capital.

History

The colony's economy was built on sugar plantations. After the abolition of slavery in the 1830s, planters imported indentured labourers in large numbers, principally from India, as well as from Portugal (Madeira), China and Africa, giving Guyana its characteristically diverse population. The discovery of gold in the interior in the 1880s, and the long-running boundary dispute with Venezuela over the Essequibo territory — referred to arbitration in 1899 — marked the late 19th century.

British Guiana is famous among philatelists for the British Guiana 1c magenta stamp of 1856, of which only one example is known; it has repeatedly set world records as the most valuable stamp.

Constitutional reform in the 20th century led to universal adult suffrage in 1953. Elections that year were won by Cheddi Jagan's People's Progressive Party; fearing a communist government, Britain suspended the constitution after 133 days and sent troops. Political conflict between the PPP and Forbes Burnham's People's National Congress, amid ethnic tensions between Indo-Guyanese and Afro-Guyanese, preceded independence. British Guiana became independent as Guyana on 26 May 1966, and a republic in 1970.
