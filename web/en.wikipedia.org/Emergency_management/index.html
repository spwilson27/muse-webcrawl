Emergency management

Emergency management, also called disaster management, is the organisation and management of resources and responsibilities for dealing with all humanitarian aspects of emergencies — in particular preparedness, response, mitigation and recovery — in order to reduce the harmful effects of hazards such as floods, earthquakes, hurricanes, epidemics and industrial accidents.

Phases

The discipline is commonly described in four phases. Mitigation seeks to reduce risk before disaster, through building codes, land-use planning and infrastructure. Preparedness covers planning, training, warning systems and stockpiling. Response includes search and rescue, evacuation, emergency shelter and medical care, often coordinated through an Incident Command System. Recovery restores services and livelihoods and aims to "build back better". In practice the phases overlap and repeat.

Organisation

Responsibility is shared among local, regional and national governments, humanitarian organisations such as the Red Cross and Red Crescent, and international bodies including the UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs. In the United States the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) supports state and local responders under the Stafford Act. Modern frameworks emphasise a whole-community approach and resilience, and international cooperation is guided by instruments such as the Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction (2015–2030).

Professional field

Emergency management is now a profession and academic discipline, with degrees, certification, and standards for exercises and after-action review.
