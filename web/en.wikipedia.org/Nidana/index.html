Nidana

Nidāna (Sanskrit and Pali for "cause, foundation, source") is a key concept in Buddhism referring to the causes or links in the chain of dependent origination (pratītyasamutpāda). The twelve nidānas describe how ignorance conditions formations, consciousness, mind-and-body, the senses, contact, feeling, craving, clinging, becoming, birth, and ageing-and-death — the cycle of saṃsāra.

The twelve nidānas

The standard list runs from avijjā (ignorance) through saṅkhāra (formations), viññāṇa (consciousness), nāmarūpa (name-and-form), saḷāyatana (six sense bases), phassa (contact), vedanā (feeling), taṇhā (craving), upādāna (clinging), bhava (becoming), jāti (birth) to jarāmaraṇa (ageing and death). Understanding and breaking the chain is central to the path to liberation.

The term also appears in Hindu contexts and in Ayurveda, where nidana means diagnosis or the causes of disease.
