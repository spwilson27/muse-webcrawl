Electronic paper

Electronic paper
Also known as: e-paper, electronic ink
Common technology: Electrophoretic display
Applications: E-readers, electronic shelf labels, signage

Electronic paper, also known as e-paper or by the trade name electronic ink (E Ink), is a display technology designed to mimic the appearance of ordinary ink on paper. Unlike conventional flat-panel displays that emit light, electronic paper reflects ambient light like ordinary paper, making it readable in sunlight and comfortable to read for long periods.

An e-reader using an electronic paper display.

Technology

The most widely used e-paper technology is the electrophoretic display, in which tiny microcapsules containing electrically charged black and white pigment particles are suspended in a fluid. Applying an electric field moves the particles to the viewing surface or away from it, forming an image. Because the display is bistable, power is needed only when the image changes, giving e-paper devices battery lives measured in weeks.

History

Early work on electronic paper was done at Xerox PARC in the 1970s with the Gyricon display. The modern electrophoretic form was developed at the MIT Media Lab in the 1990s, leading to the founding of the E Ink Corporation in 1997. The technology reached consumers in e-readers such as the Sony Librie (2004) and the Amazon Kindle (2007).

Applications

Beyond e-readers, electronic paper is used in electronic shelf labels in retail stores, public signage and timetables, smartwatches, luggage tags, and flexible displays. Color e-paper has been developed using color filters and multi-pigment systems, though refresh rates remain slow compared with LCD and OLED screens, limiting video use.
