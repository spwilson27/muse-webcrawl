Bertha Benz

Born3 May 1849, Pforzheim, Grand Duchy of Baden
Died5 May 1944 (aged 95), Ladenburg, Germany
Known forFirst long-distance automobile journey (1888)
SpouseKarl Benz

Bertha Benz (née Cäcilie Bertha Ringer; 3 May 1849 – 5 May 1944) was a German automotive pioneer and the wife and business partner of Karl Benz, inventor of the Benz Patent-Motorwagen. In August 1888 she became the first person to drive an automobile over a long distance, a journey that demonstrated the practicality of the horseless carriage and helped launch the automobile industry.

Early life and investment

Born in Pforzheim into a wealthy family, Bertha Ringer took an early interest in technology unusual for her time. In 1871, before her marriage, she used part of her dowry to invest in Karl Benz's iron construction company, providing crucial financial support for his experiments with a self-propelled vehicle. The couple married in 1872.

The 1888 journey

On 5 August 1888, without telling her husband, Bertha Benz set out with her sons Eugen (15) and Richard (13) in the Patent-Motorwagen No. 3, driving about 106 km (66 miles) from Mannheim to her mother's home in Pforzheim. Along the way she refueled with ligroin bought at a pharmacy in Wiesloch — often called the world's first filling station — cleaned a blocked fuel line with a hatpin, insulated a wire with a garter, and had a cobbler line the worn brake blocks with leather, effectively inventing the brake lining. She arrived after dark and telegraphed her husband her success.

Impact and legacy

The widely reported journey convinced skeptics that the automobile was a practical means of transport and led directly to orders for the Motorwagen. The Bertha Benz Memorial Route, opened in 2008, follows her path through Baden-Württemberg. She is commemorated as a co-creator of the automobile and a pioneer of women's role in engineering.
