20 July plot

The 20 July plot was a failed attempt to assassinate Adolf Hitler, leader of Nazi Germany, and to use the Operation Valkyrie emergency national mobilisation plan to seize control of the country on 20 July 1944. The conspiracy involved officers of the Wehrmacht and members of the German resistance who hoped to end the war and negotiate peace with the Western Allies.

The attempt

Colonel Claus von Stauffenberg, chief of staff of the Reserve Army, carried a bomb in his briefcase into a conference at the Wolf's Lair headquarters in East Prussia. After arming the device he left it under the heavy oak conference table near Hitler and excused himself. The bomb exploded at 12:42 pm, killing four men, but the blast was deflected by the table leg and the room's construction; Hitler survived with minor injuries.

Failure of the coup

In Berlin, delays and uncertainty allowed loyalist forces, including communications officer Major Otto Ernst Remer, to learn that Hitler was alive. The coup collapsed within hours. Stauffenberg and several co-conspirators were shot by firing squad in the courtyard of the Bendlerblock that night. In the ensuing crackdown, roughly 7,000 people were arrested and nearly 5,000 executed, among them Field Marshal Erwin Rommel, who was forced to commit suicide. The plot remains the most famous act of the German resistance, and 20 July is commemorated in Germany as a day of remembrance.
