Helmut Kohl

Helmut Josef Michael Kohl (3 April 1930 – 16 June 2017) was a German statesman who served as Chancellor of West Germany from 1982 to 1990 and of the reunified Germany from 1990 to 1998. His 16-year tenure — the longest of any postwar German chancellor until Angela Merkel — was dominated by the end of the Cold War and German reunification, which he drove through in 1990, and by his championship of European integration, including the Maastricht Treaty and the euro. He is widely regarded, alongside Konrad Adenauer, as one of the most consequential chancellors of the Federal Republic, and was named an Honorary Citizen of Europe.

Early career

Kohl was born into a Catholic family in Ludwigshafen, earned a doctorate in history from Heidelberg University, and rose rapidly within the Christian Democratic Union (CDU) in Rhineland-Palatinate, becoming Germany's youngest state premier in 1969 and modernising the state's administration. He became federal CDU chairman in 1973 and was the Union's unsuccessful chancellor candidate in 1976, when he nonetheless won a strong 48.6% of the vote.

Chancellorship

Kohl came to power in October 1982 through a constructive vote of no confidence against Helmut Schmidt, then won the 1983 election. He pursued market-oriented reforms, close ties with the United States (including deployment of Pershing II missiles) and friendship with France's François Mitterrand, symbolised by their hand-in-hand commemoration at Verdun in 1984. When the Berlin Wall fell on 9 November 1989, Kohl seized the moment: his Ten-Point Programme, swift diplomacy with Washington, Moscow, London and Paris, the monetary union of July 1990 and the Two Plus Four Treaty led to reunification on 3 October 1990. He won the first all-German election that December and further terms in 1994. With Mitterrand he pushed through the Maastricht Treaty (1992), creating the European Union and paving the way for the single currency, accepting the loss of the Deutschmark as the price of anchoring Germany in Europe.

Later years and legacy

Kohl lost the 1998 election to Gerhard Schröder. In 1999 a CDU donations scandal broke: Kohl admitted accepting undeclared donations and refused to name donors, citing his "word of honour"; the affair ended his role as honorary chairman and damaged his standing, though he was later partly rehabilitated in public memory. He died at his home in Ludwigshafen in 2017 and was honoured with a European act of state in Strasbourg — the first such ceremony — before burial in Speyer. Historians credit Kohl as the "Chancellor of Unity" and a principal architect of the modern European Union, while debating the economic costs of reunification and his deferral of structural reforms.
