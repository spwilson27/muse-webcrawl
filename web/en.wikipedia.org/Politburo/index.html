Politburo

OriginCommunist Party of the Soviet Union, 1917
FunctionHighest policymaking body of a communist party
Notable examplesCPSU Politburo; CCP Politburo and its Standing Committee

A politburo (from "political bureau") is the highest decision-making body of a communist party, elected by the party's central committee to direct policy between its meetings. In states ruled by a single communist party, the politburo is effectively the supreme organ of government.

Soviet Union

The first Politburo was created in Russia in October 1917, with members including Lenin, Trotsky, and Stalin; it became a permanent body in 1919. Under Stalin it was subordinated to his personal dictatorship, and from 1952 to 1966 it was renamed the Presidium. Thereafter, under leaders such as Brezhnev and Gorbachev, it again functioned as the collective apex of Soviet power, selecting the General Secretary who led it.

China

The Politburo of the Chinese Communist Party has 24–25 members; real authority is concentrated in its Standing Committee, typically of seven members, headed by the General Secretary. It governs alongside the State Council, with the party formally leading the state.

Other countries

Politburos existed in all Soviet-bloc parties and persist in China, Vietnam, Laos, Cuba, and North Korea, where the corresponding body of the Workers' Party of Korea is the Presidium of the Politburo.
