Emmanuel Jean-Michel Frédéric Macron (born 21 December 1977) is a French politician who has served as President of France since 2017. He was previously Minister of Economics, Industry and Digital Affairs under President François Hollande from 2014 to 2016, and deputy secretary-general of the Élysée from 2012 to 2014.

Early life and career

Born in Amiens to physician parents, Macron studied philosophy at Paris Nanterre University, earned a master's degree in public affairs at Sciences Po, and graduated from the École nationale d'administration (ÉNA) in 2004. He joined the Inspectorate General of Finance, then moved into investment banking at Rothschild & Cie, where he became a partner before entering government service.

Rise to the presidency

As economy minister, Macron sponsored a package of pro-business reforms ("loi Macron"). In April 2016 he founded the centrist movement En Marche!, resigned from government, and ran for the presidency on a pro-European, "neither left nor right" platform. He defeated Marine Le Pen in the runoff of the 2017 election with 66.1% of the vote, becoming the youngest president in French history, and won re-election against Le Pen in 2022 with 58.5%.

Presidency

Macron's first term included labour-law reforms, a contested fuel-tax increase that triggered the yellow vests protests (2018–19), pension reform proposals, and France's COVID-19 response. In his second term he passed a reform raising the retirement age from 62 to 64 amid mass protests in 2023. In June 2024 he dissolved the National Assembly after the European Parliament elections; the resulting snap election produced a hung parliament. In foreign policy he has championed European strategic autonomy and supported Ukraine following Russia's 2022 invasion.
