Refrigerator

A refrigerator, commonly shortened to fridge, is a commercial and home appliance consisting of a thermally insulated compartment and a heat pump that transfers heat from its inside to its external environment so that the inside is cooled to a temperature below the room temperature. Refrigeration is an essential food storage technique around the world, lowering the rate of bacterial reproduction.

How it works

Most refrigerators use the vapour-compression cycle: a refrigerant is compressed, condensed into a liquid releasing heat, then expanded and evaporated inside the cabinet, absorbing heat. Typical fresh-food compartments run at 3–5 °C, and freezers at about −18 °C.

History

Artificial refrigeration began with William Cullen's 1755 demonstration and Oliver Evans's 1805 vapour-compression design. Home electric refrigerators spread from the 1920s, replacing the icebox. Early refrigerants such as ammonia and later Freon (a CFC) were phased out over toxicity and ozone concerns, in favour of HFCs and hydrocarbons such as isobutane.
