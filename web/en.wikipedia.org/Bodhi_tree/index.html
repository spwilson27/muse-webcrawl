The Bodhi tree is a sacred fig tree (Ficus religiosa) under which Siddhartha Gautama, the spiritual teacher who became known as the Buddha, is said to have attained enlightenment (bodhi) while meditating. The specific tree at the Mahabodhi Temple in Bodh Gaya, Bihar, India, is known as the Sri Maha Bodhi and is a direct descendant of the original tree under which the Buddha meditated.

History

According to Buddhist tradition, the Buddha meditated under a pipal tree at Bodh Gaya, on the banks of the Phalgu River, resolving not to rise until he had found the truth. After forty-nine days of meditation, at the age of 35, he is said to have attained enlightenment. The site subsequently became one of the four main pilgrimage sites associated with the life of Gautama Buddha.

The emperor Ashoka is said to have visited the tree in the 3rd century BCE and to have constructed a diamond throne (vajrasana) at the spot. The present Mahabodhi Temple complex was built largely in the Gupta period, around the 5th–6th century CE.

The Sri Maha Bodhi

A sapling of the original tree was taken to Anuradhapura in Sri Lanka by the nun Sanghamitta, daughter of Ashoka, in the 3rd century BCE. The tree that grew from it, the Jaya Sri Maha Bodhi, still stands today and is one of the oldest trees in the world with a recorded planting date. The current tree at Bodh Gaya is believed to descend from this lineage; when the Bodh Gaya tree was destroyed at various points in history, replacements were grown from saplings of the Sri Lankan tree.

Religious significance

The Bodhi tree is a central symbol in Buddhism, representing the Buddha's enlightenment and the potential for awakening in all beings. Bodhi Day, commemorating the enlightenment, is celebrated on the eighth day of the twelfth lunar month in many East Asian traditions. Heart-shaped leaves of the tree are a common emblem, and Bodhi seeds are used for prayer beads.
