James A. Ford

James Alfred Ford (February 12, 1911 – February 25, 1968) was an American archaeologist known for his work on the prehistory of the southeastern United States and the development of cultural chronologies in the Lower Mississippi Valley.

Career

Ford studied at the University of Michigan and worked with the Louisiana State Museum and later the American Museum of Natural History. He is famous for the "Ford–Phillips debate" over seriation methods and for proposing a chronological sequence for the Lower Mississippi Valley that organized formative cultures such as Poverty Point, Tchefuncte, Marksville, Troyville, Coles Creek, and Plaquemine.

Legacy

His methodological writings, including Method and Theory in American Archaeology (1954, with Gordon Willey), influenced a generation of archaeologists. Ford also conducted fieldwork in Alaska, Colombia, and Peru.
