Child mortality

Child mortality is the mortality of children under the age of five. The under-five mortality rate (U5MR) is the probability that a child born in a specific year will die before reaching the age of five, expressed per 1,000 live births. It is a key indicator of child health and overall development, used by UNICEF, the World Health Organization, and the United Nations Inter-agency Group for Child Mortality Estimation (UN IGME).

Global trends

Global under-five deaths fell from about 12.8 million in 1990 to roughly 4.9–5.0 million in the early 2020s, and the global U5MR declined from 93 deaths per 1,000 live births in 1990 to about 37–38 per 1,000 in 2022. Despite this progress, mortality remains heavily concentrated in sub-Saharan Africa and Southern Asia, and a child born in sub-Saharan Africa faces many times the risk of dying before age five as a child born in Europe.

Causes

Leading causes of under-five deaths include complications of preterm birth, pneumonia, diarrhoea, malaria, congenital anomalies, and intrapartum-related events such as birth asphyxia. Undernutrition is an underlying contributor in nearly half of deaths. Neonatal deaths — those in the first 28 days of life — account for close to half of all under-five deaths.

Prevention

Most child deaths are preventable with low-cost interventions: immunisation, oral rehydration therapy, insecticide-treated bed nets, improved nutrition and breastfeeding, clean water and sanitation, antibiotics for pneumonia, and skilled care at birth. Reducing child mortality was Millennium Development Goal 4 and remains a target of Sustainable Development Goal 3.
