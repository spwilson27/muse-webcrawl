Gjende is a lake in Vågå Municipality in Innlandet county, Norway, within Jotunheimen National Park. The proglacial lake is famous for its distinctive turquoise-green colour, caused by rock flour (fine glacial silt) carried into the lake by meltwater, chiefly from the river Muru flowing from the Memurubu valley.

Geography

Gjende is about 18 kilometres long and narrow — in places only about one kilometre wide — lying at 984 metres above sea level with a maximum depth of 149 metres. It drains at its eastern end, at Gjendesheim, into the river Sjoa. The lake's western end is at Gjendebu, one of the oldest cabins of the Norwegian Trekking Association (DNT). Ferry boats cross the lake in summer between Gjendesheim, Memurubu and Gjendebu.

Tourism

The Besseggen ridge, separating Gjende from the higher, blue lake Bessvatnet, is one of Norway's most popular mountain hikes, walked by tens of thousands of people each summer; the dramatic colour contrast between the two lakes is its signature view. The area features in Henrik Ibsen's play Peer Gynt, whose hero boasts of a wild reindeer ride along the "Gjendin ridge" — inspired by the Besseggen.
