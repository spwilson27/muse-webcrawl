Image stitching

Image stitching or photo stitching is the process of combining multiple photographic images with overlapping fields of view to produce a segmented panorama or a high-resolution image. It is widely used in consumer panoramic photography, aerial and satellite imaging, medical imaging, and microscopy.

Process

A typical stitching pipeline involves camera calibration and correction of lens distortion; detection of matching features between images, commonly with algorithms such as SIFT or SURF; estimation of geometric transformations (homographies) aligning the images, often robustly using RANSAC; warping the images onto a common projection surface such as a plane, cylinder, or sphere; and blending along seams to hide exposure differences and minor misalignments, for example by multi-band blending or graph-cut seam selection.

Difficulties

Parallax caused by camera translation, moving objects, exposure differences between frames, and radial lens distortion can all degrade results. Dedicated software such as Hugin, Microsoft's Image Composite Editor, and the panorama modes built into smartphones automate most of the process.
