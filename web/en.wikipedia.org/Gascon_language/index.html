Gascon (Gascon: gascon) is a variety of the Occitan language spoken in Gascony, the region of southwestern France bounded roughly by the Garonne river, the Pyrenees and the Atlantic Ocean, and in the Val d'Aran in Catalonia, Spain, where its local form, Aranese, is co-official. Whether Gascon is a dialect of Occitan or a separate language has long been debated; it differs strikingly from other Occitan varieties in phonology and vocabulary, partly because of an ancient Basque or Aquitanian substrate.

Characteristics

Distinctive features of Gascon include the change of Latin initial f- to h- (as in Spanish), the loss of intervocalic -n-, distinctive plural formations, and the use of enunciative particles such as que, be and ja before verbs. These traits set Gascon apart from Languedocien, the central Occitan dialect on which the standard language is largely based.

History and status

Gascon was the administrative and literary language of the medieval Duchy of Gascony. From the sixteenth century onward French progressively displaced it in official life, and compulsory French schooling in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries sharply reduced intergenerational transmission. Today the number of speakers is estimated in the low hundreds of thousands, most of them older; UNESCO classifies Gascon as seriously endangered. Revival efforts include bilingual schools (calandretas), regional media and the work of the Institut d'Estudis Occitans.
