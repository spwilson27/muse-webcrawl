Incoloy

Incoloy is a registered trademark of Special Metals Corporation for a family of nickel–iron–chromium superalloys, with some grades also containing molybdenum, copper, titanium, or aluminium. Incoloy alloys are generally less expensive than the high-nickel Inconel alloys because of their higher iron content, while still offering good strength and resistance to corrosion and oxidation at elevated temperatures.

Common grades

Incoloy 800 (UNS N08800) and its variants 800H and 800HT are widely used in petrochemical furnaces, heat exchangers, and nuclear steam generators for their resistance to carburization and high-temperature oxidation. Incoloy 825 (UNS N08825), alloyed with molybdenum, copper, and titanium, resists reducing and oxidizing acids and is used in chemical processing, oil and gas production, and pollution-control equipment. Incoloy 925 is a precipitation-hardenable grade used for high-strength oilfield components. Incoloy 27-7SMO is a super-austenitic stainless grade for severe seawater and flue-gas desulfurization service.

Properties and applications

The alloys retain mechanical strength at temperatures up to roughly 800–900 °C depending on grade, and resist chloride stress-corrosion cracking better than conventional austenitic stainless steels. Typical applications include heat-treating equipment, power generation, marine engineering, and acid production and handling.
