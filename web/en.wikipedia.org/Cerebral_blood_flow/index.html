Cerebral blood flow

Cerebral blood flow
Typical value: ~50 mL/100 g/min
Share of cardiac output: About 15%
Regulation: Autoregulation, CO2

Cerebral blood flow (CBF) is the blood supply to the brain in a given period, typically about 50 millilitres per 100 grams of brain tissue per minute in adults. The brain consumes about 20% of resting oxygen despite being about 2% of body weight, and has little energy reserve, so flow must be tightly maintained.

Regulation

Autoregulation keeps CBF nearly constant across a range of arterial pressures by adjusting vessel diameter. Carbon dioxide is a potent vasodilator; increased CO2 raises flow. Local neural activity also increases regional flow, the basis of functional MRI.

Clinical significance

Falls below about 20 mL/100 g/min cause neuronal dysfunction, and lower flows cause infarction in stroke. CBF is measured with perfusion imaging, transcranial Doppler and PET. See Stroke and Brain.
