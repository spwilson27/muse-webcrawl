SDS-PAGE (sodium dodecyl sulfate–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis) is a discontinuous electrophoretic system developed by Ulrich K. Laemmli which is commonly used as a method to separate proteins with molecular masses between 5 and 250 kDa. The combined use of sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS, also known as sodium lauryl sulfate) and polyacrylamide gel eliminates the influence of protein structure and native charge: in the presence of SDS, the migration of a denatured protein through the gel is determined essentially only by its size.

Principle

Samples are boiled in a buffer containing SDS and a reducing agent such as β-mercaptoethanol or dithiothreitol (DTT). SDS binds along the polypeptide backbone at a roughly constant ratio (about 1.4 g per gram of protein), unfolding the protein and coating it with a uniform negative charge proportional to its length; the reducing agent breaks disulfide bonds, separating subunits. Because charge per unit mass is nearly constant, proteins separate by sieving through the polyacrylamide gel purely according to molecular mass: small proteins run farthest.

The Laemmli system

The standard discontinuous ("disc") system uses two gels: a low-percentage stacking gel (pH 6.8), in which proteins are concentrated into thin starting bands between leading chloride and trailing glycinate ions, and a higher-percentage resolving gel (pH 8.8), in which separation by size occurs. The pore size of the resolving gel is set by the acrylamide concentration — higher percentages resolve smaller proteins. A tracking dye such as bromophenol blue marks the running front, and a lane of known standards (a "ladder") allows the molecular weight of unknown bands to be estimated from a plot of log(mass) against mobility.

Visualisation and applications

Separated proteins are visualised by staining with Coomassie Brilliant Blue (sensitivity about 0.1 μg per band) or the roughly hundred-fold more sensitive silver stain, or transferred to a membrane for western blotting. SDS-PAGE is a routine tool for checking protein purity and size, monitoring purification and expression, estimating subunit composition, and as the first step of many proteomics workflows, including 2D gel electrophoresis.

Variants

These include gradient gels (a range of pore sizes for wide mass ranges), Tris-tricine gels for very small proteins and peptides, native and blue-native PAGE (which preserve structure and complexes by omitting SDS), and urea-PAGE for nucleic acids and small proteins.
