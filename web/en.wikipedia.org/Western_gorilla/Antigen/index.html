Antigen

Antibodies bind specific regions (epitopes) of an antigen — here, Y-shaped antibodies attaching to a pathogen protein.

DefinitionAny substance that can bind specifically to an antibody or T-cell receptor
ImmunogenAn antigen able to provoke an immune response by itself
EpitopeThe specific part of an antigen recognised by an antibody or receptor
TypesExogenous, endogenous, autoantigens, tumour antigens
Related conceptsAntibody, hapten, superantigen, allergen

In immunology, an antigen (from "antibody generator") is a molecule, molecular structure, foreign particulate matter, or pollen grain that can bind to a specific antibody or T-cell receptor. Antigens are present on the surface of pathogens such as bacteria and viruses, and also include toxins, allergens and the markers on cells that distinguish self from non-self. The presence of antigens in the body may trigger an immune response.

Recognition

The immune system recognises antigens through specific receptors: antibodies (immunoglobulins) secreted by B cells or displayed on their surfaces, and T-cell receptors, which recognise antigen fragments (peptides) presented by major histocompatibility complex (MHC) molecules. The small portion of an antigen that actually binds an antibody or receptor is called an epitope (or antigenic determinant); a single antigen typically bears many different epitopes.

Types

  • Exogenous antigens originate outside the body and are taken up by antigen-presenting cells.
  • Endogenous antigens are generated within cells, including viral proteins and, in cancer, abnormal tumour antigens.
  • Autoantigens are normal self components that the immune system mistakenly attacks in autoimmune disease.
  • Haptens are small molecules that become antigenic only when attached to a larger carrier protein.

Applications

Antigens are central to medicine: vaccines contain antigens (or instructions to produce them) that train the immune system against a pathogen; diagnostic antigen tests detect pathogen proteins directly, as in rapid tests for influenza and COVID-19; and blood group antigens (ABO and Rh) determine transfusion compatibility.
