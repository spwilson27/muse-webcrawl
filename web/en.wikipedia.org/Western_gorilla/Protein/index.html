Protein

The folded three-dimensional structure of myoglobin, the first protein solved by X-ray crystallography

Proteins are large biomolecules and macromolecules comprising one or more long chains of amino acid residues linked by peptide bonds. They perform a vast array of functions within organisms, including catalysing metabolic reactions (as enzymes), replicating DNA, responding to stimuli, providing structure to cells and organisms, and transporting molecules. The sequence of amino acids in a protein is defined by the nucleotide sequence of its gene, and usually results in the protein folding into a specific three-dimensional structure that determines its activity.

Structure

Protein structure is conventionally described at four levels. The primary structure is the linear amino acid sequence. The secondary structure consists of local folded patterns, principally alpha helices and beta sheets, stabilised by hydrogen bonds. The tertiary structure is the overall three-dimensional shape of a single molecule, and the quaternary structure is the assembly of multiple polypeptide subunits, as in haemoglobin. Proteins may be globular, fibrous or membrane-embedded, ranging in size from a few dozen to tens of thousands of amino acids.

Functions

Proteins carry out most of the work of the cell: enzymes such as amylase accelerate chemical reactions; antibodies of the immune system bind foreign antigens; structural proteins such as keratin and collagen give tissues strength; motor proteins (myosin, kinesin) generate movement; signalling proteins such as insulin regulate physiology; and transport proteins such as haemoglobin carry oxygen in the blood.

Study and synthesis

Proteins are studied by purification techniques such as chromatography and electrophoresis, structural methods such as X-ray crystallography, NMR spectroscopy and cryo-electron microscopy, and mass spectrometry-based proteomics. Abnormal folding is implicated in diseases including Alzheimer's and Parkinson's diseases. Computational structure prediction achieved a breakthrough with AlphaFold in 2020, which predicted structures for nearly all known proteins. Dietary protein supplies essential amino acids that the human body cannot synthesise.
