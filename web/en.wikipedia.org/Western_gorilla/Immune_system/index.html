Immune system

A neutrophil, a phagocyte of the innate immune system, engulfing anthrax bacteria

The immune system is a network of biological systems that protects an organism from disease. It detects and responds to a wide variety of pathogens, from viruses and bacteria to parasitic worms, as well as to cancer cells, and distinguishes them from the organism's own healthy tissue. In vertebrates, the immune system comprises two major subsystems: the innate immune system, which provides an immediate, non-specific response, and the adaptive immune system, which mounts a slower but highly specific response and retains an immunological memory of past infections.

Innate immunity

The innate system is the phylogenetically older and dominant defence of most organisms, present in plants, fungi, insects and primitive multicellular life as well as vertebrates. It includes physical barriers such as skin and mucous membranes, chemical defences, the complement system, and phagocytic cells — macrophages, neutrophils and dendritic cells — that recognise conserved molecular patterns of microbes via pattern-recognition receptors such as Toll-like receptors. Inflammation is one of its earliest responses to infection.

Adaptive immunity

The adaptive (acquired) immune system, found in jawed vertebrates, is built around lymphocytes. B cells produce antibodies (immunoglobulins) that neutralise pathogens and mark them for destruction — the humoral response — while T cells mediate cellular immunity, with helper T cells coordinating the response and cytotoxic T cells killing infected cells. Antigen presentation by major histocompatibility complex (MHC) molecules allows T cells to survey the body's cells. Following an infection, memory B and T cells persist, enabling a faster, stronger response on re-exposure — the principle behind vaccination.

Disorders

Dysfunction of the immune system causes immunodeficiency (such as AIDS or severe combined immunodeficiency), autoimmune diseases (such as type 1 diabetes, rheumatoid arthritis and lupus), and hypersensitivities including allergies and anaphylaxis. Immunology, the study of the immune system, underpins vaccination, transplantation medicine, monoclonal antibody therapy and cancer immunotherapy.
