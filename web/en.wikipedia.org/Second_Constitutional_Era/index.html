The Second Constitutional Era (Ottoman Turkish: ايكنجى مشروطيت; Turkish: İkinci Meşrutiyet Devri) is the period of restored parliamentary constitutional government in the Ottoman Empire that began with the Young Turk Revolution in July 1908 and ended with the effective dissolution of the empire's parliament after the First World War (formally 1920, when the last Ottoman parliament was dissolved under Allied occupation). It followed the suspension of the constitution by Sultan Abdul Hamid II after the short-lived First Constitutional Era of 1876–1878.

The Young Turk Revolution

In July 1908, officers of the Committee of Union and Progress (CUP) in Macedonia, among them Enver Bey and Resneli Niyazi, mutinied and demanded restoration of the 1876 constitution (Kanûn-u Esâsî). Fearing civil war, Abdul Hamid II conceded on 23 July 1908. Parliamentary elections — the first in a generation — produced a Chamber of Deputies of remarkable ethnic and religious diversity, and the opening of parliament in December 1908 was celebrated across the empire as a festival of liberty and fraternity.

Counter-coup and the CUP ascendancy

The honeymoon ended with the 31 March Incident (13 April 1909), a counter-revolutionary mutiny in Constantinople suppressed by the "Action Army" from Salonica, after which Abdul Hamid II was deposed and replaced by Mehmed V. Constitutional amendments in 1909 curtailed the sultan's powers and strengthened parliament. However, wars — the Italo-Turkish War (1911–12) and the Balkan Wars (1912–13), which stripped the empire of most of its European territory — radicalised politics. In January 1913 the CUP seized power in the Raid on the Sublime Porte, and the empire was effectively ruled by the CUP triumvirate of Enver, Talaat and Djemal Pasha, who brought it into the First World War on the side of the Central Powers. The era is also marked by the wartime destruction of the empire's Armenian, Greek and Assyrian communities. After defeat in 1918, the CUP leaders fled; the last Ottoman parliament adopted the Misak-ı Millî (National Pact) in 1920 before being dissolved by the occupying Allies, with many deputies joining the new Grand National Assembly in Ankara.
