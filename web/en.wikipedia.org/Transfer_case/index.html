Transfer case

[Image: Cutaway of a four-wheel-drive transfer case]
images/transfercase.png

A transfer case is a component of the drivetrain of four-wheel-drive and all-wheel-drive vehicles. It receives power from the transmission and distributes it to the front and rear axles via driveshafts. Many transfer cases include a set of reduction gears providing a "low range" for off-road crawling, and some include a centre differential or locking mechanism to manage torque split between the axles.

Transfer case
FunctionDistribute drive to front and rear axles
Drive typesChain-driven or gear-driven
Typical featuresLow-range gearing, diff lock, part-time or full-time 4WD

Types

  • Part-time systems — the driver manually engages four-wheel drive for slippery surfaces; front and rear outputs are locked together, so use on dry pavement causes drivetrain wind-up.
  • Full-time systems — include a centre differential allowing permanent all-wheel drive on any surface; the differential may be lockable.
  • Automatic/on-demand systems — electronically controlled clutches send torque to the secondary axle when slip is detected, common in modern crossovers.

Drive mechanisms and control

Transfer cases transmit power to the front output by either a silent chain or by gears; chain drives dominate modern designs for quietness and compactness. Low range typically reduces gearing by a factor between about 2:1 and 4:1, multiplying torque for steep climbs and rock crawling. Control has shifted from floor levers and manual locking hubs to dashboard switches and rotary dials operating electric shift motors and clutch packs. Power take-off (PTO) outputs on some units drive winches and auxiliary equipment.

History

Early four-wheel-drive vehicles such as the World War II Willys Jeep used gear-driven transfer cases with two-speed low range — a layout still recognisable in modern off-roaders. The rise of full-time systems in the 1970s (Range Rover, Jeep Quadra-Trac) and of car-based AWD in the 1990s broadened the transfer case's role and sophistication.
