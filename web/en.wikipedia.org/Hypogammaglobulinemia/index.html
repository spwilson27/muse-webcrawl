Hypogammaglobulinemia
FieldImmunology, haematology
DefinitionReduced serum immunoglobulin (gamma globulin) levels
CausesPrimary immunodeficiency (e.g. CVID, X-linked agammaglobulinemia); secondary causes (drugs, malignancy, protein loss)

Hypogammaglobulinemia is a medical condition in which the level of immunoglobulins (antibodies) in the blood is abnormally low. The word derives from hypo- ("under") and gamma globulin, once the standard term for the antibody fraction of blood proteins. Because antibodies are central to humoral immunity, people with hypogammaglobulinemia are prone to recurrent infections, especially with encapsulated bacteria affecting the sinuses, ears, and lungs.

Causes

Primary forms are inherited immunodeficiencies, including common variable immunodeficiency (CVID), X-linked agammaglobulinemia (Bruton's disease), selective IgA deficiency, and transient hypogammaglobulinemia of infancy. Secondary hypogammaglobulinemia is more common and can result from B-cell malignancies such as chronic lymphocytic leukaemia and multiple myeloma, protein-losing states (nephrotic syndrome, protein-losing enteropathy), malnutrition, and immunosuppressive drugs including rituximab and some anticonvulsants.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis rests on measurement of serum IgG, IgA, and IgM levels and assessment of specific antibody responses to vaccines. Management includes prompt antibiotic treatment of infections, prophylactic antibiotics in some cases, and immunoglobulin replacement therapy — intravenous (IVIG) or subcutaneous (SCIG) — for patients with significant deficiency and recurrent infections. Live vaccines are generally avoided in severe forms.
