Zephyr (protocol)

The Zephyr Notification Service is an instant-messaging protocol developed at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology as part of Project Athena in the 1980s. It was one of the first campus-wide instant messaging systems and remains in use at MIT and elsewhere.

Design

Zephyr operates on a publish–subscribe model: clients subscribe to "classes" and instances within them, and messages ("zgrams") are delivered by a network of servers via a host manager on each workstation. Unlike later chat systems, Zephyr was designed for notifications ranging from personal messages to system alerts.

Culture and legacy

Zephyr developed a distinctive culture at MIT, with conventions such as the punning, allusive style of "zephyr" exchanges and the use of personals classes. Tools such as the zwgc client and later integrations into clients like BarnOwl carried the system forward; it influenced subsequent messaging systems and remains a nostalgic fixture of MIT computing culture.
