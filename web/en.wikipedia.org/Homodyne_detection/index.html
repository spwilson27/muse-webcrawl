Homodyne detection

Homodyne detectionField: quantum optics, optical metrology
Key components: local oscillator, beam splitter, photodiodes

Homodyne detection is a technique in optics for measuring the amplitude and phase quadratures of a light field. The weak signal field is mixed on a beam splitter with a strong reference beam, the local oscillator, which has the same optical frequency as the signal — hence "homodyne" (same frequency), in contrast to heterodyne detection, where the frequencies differ.

Balanced homodyne detection

In the balanced configuration, the two outputs of the beam splitter are sent to a matched pair of photodiodes and their photocurrents are subtracted. The difference current is proportional to a quadrature of the signal field, with the measured quadrature selected by the phase of the local oscillator. Common-mode noise from the local oscillator cancels in the subtraction.

Applications

Homodyne detection is a cornerstone of quantum optics: it enables tomography of quantum states of light, measurement of squeezed states, and continuous-variable quantum information protocols such as quantum key distribution and teleportation. It is also used in coherent optical communications, laser Doppler measurements and optical coherence techniques.
