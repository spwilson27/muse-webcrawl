SALT II

Signed18 June 1979, Vienna, Austria
SignatoriesUnited States (Jimmy Carter); Soviet Union (Leonid Brezhnev)
StatusSigned; never ratified by the US Senate
Preceded bySALT I

SALT II was the second round of the Strategic Arms Limitation Talks between the United States and the Soviet Union, and the treaty signed by US President Jimmy Carter and Soviet leader Leonid Brezhnev in Vienna on 18 June 1979. It was intended to limit the manufacture and deployment of strategic nuclear weapons.

Background

SALT I, concluded in 1972, froze the number of intercontinental ballistic missile (ICBM) launchers and produced the Anti-Ballistic Missile Treaty, but it did not limit multiple independently targetable reentry vehicles (MIRVs) or total warheads. SALT II negotiations ran from 1972 to 1979, building on the Vladivostok framework of 1974 agreed by Gerald Ford and Brezhnev.

Terms

The treaty set an aggregate ceiling of 2,400 strategic delivery vehicles for each side, to fall to 2,250, with sub-limits: 1,320 launchers of MIRVed ballistic missiles, 820 MIRVed ICBM launchers, and a limit of one new type of ICBM. It also counted heavy bombers equipped with air-launched cruise missiles and restricted the number of warheads per missile. Unlike SALT I, it sought equal aggregates for both sides.

Ratification failure

The treaty met opposition in the US Senate from critics who considered it too favourable to the Soviet Union, especially after the 1979 "discovery" of a Soviet combat brigade in Cuba. Following the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan in December 1979, Carter asked the Senate to delay consideration, and the treaty was never ratified. Nevertheless, both sides largely observed its terms informally for several years; the United States did not formally exceed the limits until 1986.

Legacy

SALT II was the last of the SALT agreements. Arms control continued with the START negotiations of the 1980s, which shifted from limiting launchers to reducing warheads, culminating in START I in 1991.
