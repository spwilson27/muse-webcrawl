Louisiana hot sauce

Louisiana hot sauce is a style of hot sauce traditionally made from chili peppers (typically cayenne or tabasco peppers), vinegar, and salt, and usually aged in oak barrels. It is a staple condiment of Cajun and Creole cuisine in the U.S. state of Louisiana and a defining ingredient in dishes such as gumbo, jambalaya, and red beans and rice.

Production

In the traditional method, ripe peppers are mashed with salt and fermented — in the case of Tabasco sauce, for up to three years in oak barrels — before the mash is blended with vinegar, strained, and bottled. The resulting sauces are relatively thin, bright red, and moderate in heat, emphasising acidity and salt as much as spiciness, typically measuring in the range of a few hundred to a few thousand Scoville heat units.

Brands

The style is associated with several long-established Louisiana brands. Tabasco, produced by the McIlhenny Company on Avery Island since 1868, is the most famous internationally; others include Crystal Hot Sauce (Baumer Foods, founded 1923), the Louisiana brand hot sauce (Bruce Foods), Frank's RedHot (originally from Louisiana, now made elsewhere), and Trappey's. These sauces are standard on tables throughout the Gulf South and in oyster bars and seafood restaurants across the United States.

Use

Louisiana hot sauce is used as a table condiment and cooking ingredient across Cajun and Creole cooking, dashed over fried seafood, eggs, greens, and rice dishes, and it forms the base, with butter, of the classic Buffalo wing sauce (using Frank's RedHot).
