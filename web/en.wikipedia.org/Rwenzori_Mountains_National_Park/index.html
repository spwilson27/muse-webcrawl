Rwenzori Mountains National Park is a national park in western Uganda, protecting much of the Rwenzori mountain range on the border with the Democratic Republic of the Congo. The range — often identified with the ancient geographer Ptolemy's "Mountains of the Moon" — contains Africa's third-highest peak and some of the continent's last equatorial glaciers.

Geography and ecology

Unlike Africa's other great ranges, the Rwenzori is a non-volcanic fault-block range uplifted along the western arm of the East African Rift. Its slopes pass through distinct vegetation zones, from tropical rainforest to bamboo and giant heather forests, and finally to afro-alpine moorlands with giant lobelias and groundsels found nowhere else. The park supports around 70 mammal species, including forest elephants, chimpanzees, and the endemic Rwenzori turaco among more than 200 bird species.

Conservation

The park was established in 1991 and inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1994; it was listed as endangered from 1999 to 2004 owing to insecurity and poaching. Its glaciers have retreated dramatically over the past century, and scientists expect them to disappear within decades. Trekking the central circuit to Margherita Peak is a noted draw for mountaineers.
