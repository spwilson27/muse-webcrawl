Empress Dowager Cixi (29 November 1835 – 15 November 1908), of the Manchu Yehe Nara clan, was a Chinese empress dowager and regent who effectively controlled the Qing dynasty government for 47 years, from 1861 until her death in 1908. Entering the Forbidden City as a low-ranking concubine of the Xianfeng Emperor, she bore his only surviving son, the future Tongzhi Emperor.

Rule

After Xianfeng's death in 1861, Cixi seized power in a coup with Empress Dowager Ci'an and Prince Gong, ruling as regent during the minorities of the Tongzhi and Guangxu emperors. Her court oversaw the suppression of the Taiping Rebellion and cautious modernisation under the Self-Strengthening Movement. In 1898 she ended the Guangxu Emperor's Hundred Days' Reform in a coup and placed him under house arrest; during the Boxer Rebellion of 1900 she backed the Boxers against the foreign powers, and fled Beijing when the Eight-Nation Alliance occupied the capital. Returning in 1902, she implemented the late Qing reforms, including abolishing the imperial examination system in 1905.

Death and legacy

Cixi died in 1908, a day after the Guangxu Emperor, having installed the infant Puyi as emperor; the dynasty fell three years later. Long condemned in Chinese historiography as a reactionary who hastened the Qing collapse, she has been partly reassessed by modern historians, who credit her political skill while noting the constraints of her position.
