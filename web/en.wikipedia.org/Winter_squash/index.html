Winter squash

SpeciesCucurbita maxima, C. moschata, C. pepo, C. argyrosperma
HarvestAutumn, at full maturity
StorageWeeks to months in cool conditions

Winter squash is a category of squash harvested in the autumn, when the fruit are fully mature, the seeds are hard, and the rind has toughened — in contrast to summer squash, which is picked immature and eaten whole. The thick rind allows winter squash to be stored for weeks or months, historically providing food through the winter.

Varieties

Common types include butternut, acorn, Hubbard, delicata, kabocha, spaghetti squash, turban squash, and pumpkins grown for cooking. They belong chiefly to the species Cucurbita maxima, C. moschata, and C. pepo, all domesticated in the Americas.

Cultivation and use

Plants are warm-season annuals grown on vines or bushes, pollinated by bees. In the kitchen, winter squash is typically roasted, puréed into soups, baked into pies, or stuffed; the flesh is rich in beta-carotene, vitamin A, and fibre, and the seeds can be roasted as a snack. Curing in warm conditions after harvest hardens the rind and improves storage life and flavour.
