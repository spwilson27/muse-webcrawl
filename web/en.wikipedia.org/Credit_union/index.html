Credit union

A credit union is a member-owned, not-for-profit financial cooperative that provides savings, credit and other financial services to its members. Credit unions generally offer many of the same services as commercial banks — deposit accounts, loans, mortgages, and payment services — but any surplus is returned to members in the form of reduced fees, higher savings rates, and lower loan rates, rather than paid out to external shareholders.

History

The modern credit union movement traces its origins to 19th-century Germany, where Hermann Schulze-Delitzsch founded urban credit cooperatives and Friedrich Wilhelm Raiffeisen founded rural credit unions to free farmers from moneylenders. The first credit union in North America, the Caisse populaire de Lévis, was founded in Quebec in 1901 by Alphonse Desjardins. In the United States, the Federal Credit Union Act of 1934 established a federal chartering system, and the movement spread worldwide through organisations such as the World Council of Credit Unions.

Structure and governance

Credit unions are democratically governed by their members: each member has one vote regardless of the size of their deposits, and members elect a volunteer board of directors from among themselves. Membership is usually restricted by a "common bond" — for example, living or working in a particular community, belonging to an employer, union, religious group or other association — although many jurisdictions have broadened membership criteria over time.

Services

Credit unions accept deposits, make personal and business loans, issue mortgages and credit cards, and provide current/checking accounts and electronic banking. In the United States, deposits in federally insured credit unions are protected by the National Credit Union Share Insurance Fund, administered by the National Credit Union Administration, up to the same limits as FDIC insurance for banks.

Differences from banks

Unlike banks, credit unions are cooperative and not-for-profit, and in many countries they enjoy partial exemptions from taxes on their earnings, a status that is periodically contested by the banking industry. Credit unions are typically smaller and more locally focused than banks, though the largest have millions of members and assets comparable to mid-sized banks.
