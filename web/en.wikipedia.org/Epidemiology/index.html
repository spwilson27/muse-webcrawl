Epidemiology

Epidemiology is the study and analysis of the distribution (who, when, and where), patterns and determinants of health and disease conditions in a defined population. It is a cornerstone of public health, shaping policy decisions and evidence-based practice by identifying risk factors for disease and targets for preventive healthcare.

History

The field is often traced to John Snow, who identified a public water pump as the source of a London cholera outbreak in 1854 by mapping the pattern of cases — a pioneering use of a spot map and statistical reasoning. Later milestones include the Framingham Heart Study, Richard Doll and Austin Bradford Hill's case-control and cohort studies linking smoking to lung cancer, and the development of the Bradford Hill criteria for inferring causation.

Study designs

Epidemiologists use descriptive studies to characterize the distribution of disease, and analytical designs — ecological, cross-sectional, case-control and cohort studies, as well as randomized controlled trials — to test hypotheses about causes. Key measures include incidence, prevalence, relative risk, odds ratios and measures of association, all interpreted with attention to bias, confounding and chance.

Applications

Beyond infectious disease surveillance and outbreak investigation, epidemiology underpins occupational and environmental health, injury prevention, health services research and the evaluation of screening programmes.
