Naraka (Hinduism)

In Hinduism, Naraka is the underworld realm where sinners are tormented after death, roughly equivalent to hell or purgatory. It is described as situated beneath the earth and ruled by Yama, the god of death, whose capital is Yamapuri. The duration of suffering in Naraka is finite: once the specific karma is exhausted, the soul proceeds to rebirth.

Administration

Yama is assisted by Chitragupta, who reads out the record of each soul's deeds from the Akashic ledger, and by the Yamadutas, messengers who bring the dead to judgement and execute punishments. The righteous may instead ascend to Svarga, the heavenly realm of Indra.

The twenty-eight hells

The Bhagavata Purana and Vishnu Purana describe 28 hells. Among them: Tamisra (darkness, for those who seize others' wealth), Raurava (for those who harm living beings, tortured by serpent-like ruru creatures), Kumbhipaka (sinners boiled in oil), Kalasutra, Asipatravana (a forest of sword-leaves), Sukaramukha, Andhakupa, Taptasurmi (embracing a red-hot iron image) and Vajrakantaka-salmali (a silk-cotton tree with iron thorns). Each punishment corresponds, by the logic of karma, to the sin committed.

Philosophical interpretation

Many later commentators read the hells allegorically, as states of mind or as the natural fruition of harmful action rather than literal places, though in popular devotion they remain vividly real.
