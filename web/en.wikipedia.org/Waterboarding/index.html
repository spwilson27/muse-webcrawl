Waterboarding

Waterboarding is a form of torture in which water is poured over a cloth covering the face and breathing passages of an immobilised captive, causing the person to experience the sensation of drowning.

History

Variants of water torture were used by the Spanish Inquisition, during the Second World War, in the Algerian and Vietnam conflicts, and by the Khmer Rouge. After the September 11 attacks, the CIA used waterboarding on a small number of detainees under the "enhanced interrogation techniques" programme, prompting widespread controversy.

Legality and effects

The technique has been prosecuted as a war crime in some cases and is widely regarded by legal and medical experts as torture. It can cause extreme pain, lung damage, brain damage from oxygen deprivation, and lasting psychological trauma; US policy banned its use by executive order in 2009.
