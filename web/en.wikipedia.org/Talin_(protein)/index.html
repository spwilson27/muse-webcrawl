Talin (protein)

Talin-1
GeneTLN1
ParalogueTalin-2 (TLN2)
Length2,541 amino acids
Molecular mass~270 kDa
FunctionIntegrin activation; links integrins to actin cytoskeleton

Talin is a large cytoskeletal protein concentrated at regions of cell–substrate contact known as focal adhesions and at cell–cell junctions. It physically connects integrin receptors in the plasma membrane to the actin cytoskeleton and is a central activator of integrin-mediated cell adhesion.

Structure

Talin is a ~270 kDa protein of about 2,540 amino acids. It consists of an N-terminal head domain containing a FERM domain (with F1, F2, and F3 subdomains) and a long C-terminal rod made of a series of helical bundles. The rod contains multiple binding sites for the cytoskeletal protein vinculin that are cryptic in the folded, autoinhibited protein and become exposed when talin is stretched by mechanical force.

Function

Binding of the talin head's F3 subdomain to the cytoplasmic tail of integrin β subunits shifts the integrin to its high-affinity conformation — the key step of "inside-out" integrin activation. At the same time, talin's rod binds filamentous actin, forming a molecular clutch that transmits actomyosin-generated force to the extracellular matrix. This mechanosensitive linkage makes talin a core component of how cells sense the stiffness of their surroundings.

Isoforms

Vertebrates have two talin genes: TLN1, expressed widely, and TLN2, enriched in skeletal and cardiac muscle and the brain. Loss of talin-1 in mice is embryonic lethal owing to failures of cell adhesion and gastrulation.
