Triglav National Park (Slovene: Triglavski narodni park, TNP) is the only national park in Slovenia. It was established in its modern form in 1981 and is located in the northwestern part of the country and the southeastern part of the Alpine chain. Mount Triglav, the highest peak of Slovenia, stands at the centre of the national park and gives it its name. From there the valleys spread out radially, supplying water to two large river systems with catchments in the Adriatic and Black Seas: the Soča and the Sava.

Geography and nature

The park covers about 880 square kilometres, some 4% of Slovenia's territory, encompassing most of the Slovenian Julian Alps. It includes glacial lakes such as Lake Bohinj and the Triglav Lakes, the Savica waterfall, and the Tolmin gorges. Its wildlife includes chamois, ibex (reintroduced), red deer, brown bear, and lynx, and its flora includes several endemic Alpine species.

History

The first protected area, the Alpine Conservation Park, was established in 1924 in the Triglav Lakes valley; it was renamed Triglav National Park in 1961 and greatly enlarged in 1981. The park is also part of the Julian Alps biosphere reserve under UNESCO's Man and the Biosphere programme.
