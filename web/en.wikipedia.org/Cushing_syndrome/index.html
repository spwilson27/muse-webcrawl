Cushing syndrome

Cushing syndrome (also written Cushing's syndrome) is the disorder that results from chronic hypercortisolism — long-standing exposure of the body's tissues to excessive levels of the hormone cortisol. It may arise from medical treatment with glucocorticoid drugs or from tumours that overproduce cortisol or the pituitary hormone ACTH.

Clinical features

Characteristic features are weight gain distributed over the trunk and face, a dorsocervical fat pad, wide purple striae, proximal muscle weakness, hypertension, glucose intolerance, osteoporosis, susceptibility to infection, and psychological symptoms including depression and irritability.

Classification

Causes are divided into ACTH-dependent forms — pituitary Cushing's disease and ectopic ACTH syndrome — and ACTH-independent forms, chiefly cortisol-secreting adrenal adenomas and carcinomas. Prolonged therapeutic glucocorticoid use remains the commonest cause in clinical practice.

Management

Biochemical confirmation uses dexamethasone suppression testing, urinary free cortisol and salivary cortisol; source localisation then guides therapy. Definitive treatment usually involves transsphenoidal or adrenal surgery, with radiotherapy and steroidogenesis inhibitors such as ketoconazole, metyrapone or osilodrostat used in selected cases.
