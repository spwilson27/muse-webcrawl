Vertical stabilizer

Also calledFin, vertical tail
FunctionYaw (directional) stability
Moving partRudder
CounterpartHorizontal stabilizer

The vertical stabilizer, or fin, is the fixed vertical surface at the tail of an aircraft that provides stability about the yaw axis, keeping the nose pointed into the relative wind. The hinged rudder at its trailing edge lets the pilot control yaw. Together with the horizontal stabilizer, it forms part of the empennage, or tail assembly.

Function

Like the feathers of an arrow, the fin acts as a weathervane: if the aircraft yaws, airflow produces a sideways force on the fin that pushes the tail back into alignment. The fin is also sized to maintain control after an engine failure on multi-engine aircraft, when asymmetric thrust creates a strong yawing moment.

Configurations

Most aircraft have a single fin, but designs vary: twin tails (as on the F-14 Tomcat), triple tails (Lockheed Constellation), V-tails combining vertical and horizontal surfaces (Beechcraft Bonanza), and ventral or cruciform arrangements. Airlines commonly paint their livery and logos on the fin, making it a prominent branding surface.

Structural considerations

The fin must withstand large aerodynamic and rudder loads. The 2001 crash of American Airlines Flight 587, in which the vertical stabilizer of an Airbus A300 separated after extreme rudder inputs, led to revised training and structural standards.
