Four-velocity

The four-velocity is tangent to an object's worldline in spacetime.

In physics, in particular in special relativity and general relativity, the four-velocity is a four-vector in four-dimensional spacetime that generalises the ordinary three-dimensional velocity. It is defined as the rate of change of an object's four-position with respect to its proper time—the time measured by a clock travelling with the object:

U = dx / dτ = γ(c, v)

where γ is the Lorentz factor, c is the speed of light, and v is the ordinary velocity. Because proper time is invariant, the four-velocity transforms as a true four-vector under Lorentz transformations, unlike ordinary velocity.

Properties

The magnitude of the four-velocity is constant: U · U = c² (in the (+,−,−,−) metric signature), reflecting the fact that every object "moves through spacetime" at the speed of light. Its time component is γc and its spatial components are γv. Multiplying the four-velocity by an object's rest mass gives its four-momentum, and its derivative with respect to proper time is the four-acceleration, which is always perpendicular to the four-velocity.

Applications

Four-velocity is fundamental to relativistic mechanics and relativistic fluid dynamics. For a fluid, the four-velocity field U(x) describes the flow of matter through spacetime and enters the stress–energy tensor. For light, which has no proper time, four-velocity is not defined; instead the photon's motion is described by a null wave vector.
