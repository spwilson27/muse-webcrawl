William Moon (18 December 1818 – 21 October 1894) was an English teacher of the blind and the inventor of Moon type, an embossed reading system for blind people based on simplified raised forms of the ordinary printed alphabet.

Life and work

Moon lost the sight of one eye in childhood and became totally blind by the age of 21 after illness. Settling in Brighton, he taught blind children to read and became frustrated with the complex embossed systems then in use, which were hard for people who lost their sight in adulthood and already knew the shapes of letters. In 1845 he published his own system, in which the letters of the standard alphabet are simplified and raised in relief so they can be felt with the fingertips.

Moon type

Moon's system was used to print the Bible, hymns, stories and instructional works, and was exported widely. For many years it rivalled Braille, and it remains in use today, particularly among people who lose their sight later in life and those with additional disabilities that make Braille difficult to learn. Moon was elected a fellow of the Royal Geographical Society and the Society of Arts, and received an honorary degree from the University of Philadelphia.
