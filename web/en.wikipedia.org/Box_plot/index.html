Box plot

In descriptive statistics, a box plot or boxplot (also known as a box-and-whisker plot) is a method for graphically depicting groups of numerical data through their quartiles. Box plots may also have lines extending from the boxes (whiskers) indicating variability outside the upper and lower quartiles, hence the terms box-and-whisker plot and box-and-whisker diagram.

Elements

A box plot summarizes a distribution with five numbers: the minimum, first quartile (Q1), median (Q2), third quartile (Q3) and maximum. The box itself spans the interquartile range (IQR = Q3 − Q1) with a line at the median. In the convention introduced by John Tukey in 1970, the whiskers extend to the most extreme data points within 1.5 × IQR of the quartiles, and points beyond them are plotted individually as outliers.

Interpretation and variations

Box plots give a quick visual impression of a distribution's centre, spread and skewness, and they are especially effective for comparing distributions side by side across groups. Variants include notched box plots, which approximate a confidence interval for the median; variable-width boxes whose widths encode sample size; and violin plots, which combine a box plot with a kernel density estimate.
