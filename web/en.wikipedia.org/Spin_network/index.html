Spin network

In physics, a spin network is a type of diagram which can be used to represent states and interactions between particles and fields in quantum mechanics. From a mathematical perspective, the diagrams are a concise way to represent multilinear functions and functions between representations of matrix groups. The diagrammatic notation often simplifies calculation because simple diagrams may be used to represent complicated functions.

History

Spin networks were invented by Roger Penrose in 1971, as part of his "spin geometry" programme to derive the structure of spacetime from combinatorial rules applied to abstract networks labelled by angular momentum quantum numbers. In his formulation, the edges of a graph are labelled by spins (irreducible representations of SU(2)) and the vertices by intertwiners, and the value of a closed network gives a probability amplitude.

Role in loop quantum gravity

Spin networks were later rediscovered and became central to loop quantum gravity, where they form a basis of gauge-invariant states of the gravitational field (the Ashtekar–Lewandowski Hilbert space). In that theory, the edges of a spin network carry quanta of area and the vertices carry quanta of volume, so geometry itself emerges from the network's quantum numbers: area and volume operators have discrete spectra. The dynamics of evolving spin networks are described by spin foams, which are the two-dimensional histories (worldsheets) of spin networks in spacetime.
