Cargo

In logistics, cargo refers to goods carried by a large vehicle, ship or aircraft for commercial gain. The word comes from the Spanish cargo, "load, burden", from cargar, "to load". Cargo differs from baggage or personal effects, which are not carried for trade, and from freight in some usages — strictly, freight is the charge for carrying cargo, though the terms are often used interchangeably.

Types

Cargo is commonly divided into bulk cargo (unpacked commodities such as grain, coal and oil), break-bulk cargo (individual packaged items), and containerised cargo, which since the 1950s has dominated general merchandise trade through standardised intermodal containers. Air cargo emphasises high-value and perishable goods, and specialised categories include refrigerated ("reefer") cargo, dangerous goods regulated by codes such as the IMDG Code, and project cargo of oversized industrial components.

Carriage and law

Carriage of goods is governed by contract — bills of lading for sea transport, air waybills for air transport — and by international conventions such as the Hague-Visby Rules and the Montreal Convention, which allocate liability for loss or damage. Global supply chains depend on freight forwarders, ports and terminals that transfer cargo between modes. The scale of modern shipping, with container ships carrying over 20,000 TEU, makes cargo movement one of the foundations of the world economy.
