Nasal septum

Part ofNasal cavity
ComponentsSeptal cartilage, perpendicular plate of ethmoid, vomer

The nasal septum is the partition that separates the left and right sides of the nasal cavity. It supports the external nose and directs airflow through the nostrils.

Structure

The septum has three main parts: the quadrangular septal cartilage at the front, the thin perpendicular plate of the ethmoid bone above, and the vomer bone below and behind. The whole structure is covered by mucoperichondrium and mucoperiosteum. Near the front of the septum lies Kiesselbach's plexus (Little's area), a rich network of small blood vessels that is the most common source of nosebleeds.

Clinical significance

A deviated nasal septum — displacement of the partition to one side — is very common and usually harmless, but when severe it can obstruct breathing, contribute to sinusitis and nosebleeds, and may be corrected surgically by septoplasty. Trauma, cocaine use or disease can cause a septal perforation. The septal cartilage is also a common source of graft material in reconstructive rhinoplasty.
