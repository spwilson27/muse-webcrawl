Scottish country dance

Scottish country dance is a form of social dance involving groups of couples dancing in longwise or square sets, a descendant of the country dances of the 17th and 18th centuries. It is the social dance form most closely associated with Scotland, typically danced at ceilidhs, balls and social evenings, and should not be confused with Scottish Highland dance, a solo competitive form.

Dancers in a longwise set at a Scottish country dance ball

History

Country dances were danced throughout Britain and at the Scottish court from the 17th century; printed collections of dances appeared from the early 18th century. Interest declined in the 19th century, and in 1923 the Scottish Country Dance Society was founded in Glasgow by Jean Milligan and Ysobel Stewart to preserve the dances. Granted royal patronage in 1951, it became the Royal Scottish Country Dance Society (RSCDS), which has published hundreds of dances in standardised form and maintains branches worldwide.

Form

Dances are performed by sets of (usually) four couples arranged in two lines, men on one side and women on the other, or in square sets. Each dance is a sequence of figures — such as reels of three or four, rights and lefts, poussettes, allemandes and turns — danced to reels, jigs or the slower, elegant strathspey, a uniquely Scottish dance type. Dancers use soft shoes ("ghillies") and precise footwork including the skip-change of step and pas de basque. Music is usually provided by a band featuring fiddle and accordion, or piano.

Modern practice

Scottish country dancing is practised all over the world, with RSCDS branches and affiliated groups in Europe, North America, Australia, New Zealand, Japan and elsewhere. Annual events include summer schools at St Andrews and the society's winter school.
