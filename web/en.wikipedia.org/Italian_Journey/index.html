Italian Journey (German: Italienische Reise) is an account by Johann Wolfgang von Goethe of his travels through Italy between September 1786 and May 1788, compiled from his diary and letters and published decades later. It is a classic of German literature and a defining document of Weimar Classicism.

The journey

Goethe, then a minister and privy councillor at the Weimar court, fled incognito — travelling under the name Johann Philipp Möller — down through the Brenner Pass to Verona, Vicenza, Venice, Bologna and Rome, where he stayed from October 1786 to February 1787 among the German artists' colony (including Angelica Kauffmann and Johann Tischbein, who painted the famous portrait of Goethe in the Roman Campagna). He continued to Naples, climbed Vesuvius, sailed to Sicily — whose beauty, he wrote, made Italy intelligible to him — and returned for a second, longer stay in Rome before coming home in June 1788.

Themes

The Journey records Goethe's rebirth as an artist ("in Rome I first found myself"), his study of classical antiquity, architecture and painting, his botanical idea of the Urpflanze conceived in the public gardens of Palermo, and his eager observations of Italian everyday life, carnival and theatre. It appeared as part of his autobiographical writings in 1816–17, with the second Roman sojourn added in 1829.
