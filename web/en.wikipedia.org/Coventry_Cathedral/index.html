Coventry Cathedral, formally the Cathedral Church of Saint Michael, is the seat of the Bishop of Coventry in Coventry, England. The present cathedral was built beside the ruins of the medieval St Michael's, which was destroyed by German bombing in the Coventry Blitz of 14 November 1940, leaving only the tower, spire, outer walls, and the bronze effigies of its first bishop and his wife.

The new cathedral

Architect Basil Spence won the competition to design the new cathedral in 1951; it was consecrated on 25 May 1962 in the presence of Queen Elizabeth II. Benjamin Britten's War Requiem was premiered there days later. The building is Grade I listed and contains notable modernist artworks: Jacob Epstein's sculpture St Michael's Victory over the Devil, Graham Sutherland's tapestry Christ in Glory, John Hutton's engraved glass Saints and Angels, and John Piper's baptistery window.

Ministry of reconciliation

After the Blitz, Provost Richard Howard had the words "Father Forgive" inscribed behind the altar of the ruined cathedral, and two charred roof beams were bound into the Charred Cross. Coventry became a centre for reconciliation ministry, founding the Community of the Cross of Nails, with partners including Dresden and Kiel.
