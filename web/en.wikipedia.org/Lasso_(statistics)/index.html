Lasso (statistics)

In statistics and machine learning, lasso (least absolute shrinkage and selection operator) is a regression analysis method that performs both variable selection and regularization in order to enhance the prediction accuracy and interpretability of the statistical model it produces. It was introduced by Robert Tibshirani in 1996.

Definition

Lasso estimates coefficients by minimizing the residual sum of squares subject to the constraint that the sum of the absolute values of the coefficients (their L1 norm) is less than a constant; equivalently, it adds a penalty term λΣ|βj| to the least-squares objective. Because the constraint region is a diamond with corners on the axes, the solution frequently shrinks some coefficients exactly to zero, producing a sparse model—unlike ridge regression, whose L2 penalty shrinks coefficients but rarely eliminates them.

Computation and extensions

The lasso can be computed efficiently along the whole regularization path by the LARS (least-angle regression) algorithm and by coordinate descent. Extensions include the elastic net (combining L1 and L2 penalties, useful with correlated predictors), the group lasso, and the adaptive lasso, which uses data-dependent weights to achieve oracle variable-selection properties. Lasso's connection to compressed sensing—recovering sparse signals from few measurements—has made it influential well beyond statistics.
