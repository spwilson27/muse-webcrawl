The 2011 Canadian federal election was held on 2 May 2011 to elect members of the 41st Canadian Parliament. The election was triggered after the minority Conservative government of Stephen Harper was defeated on a contempt of Parliament motion, the first such finding against a government in Commonwealth history.

Results

The Conservatives won a majority government with 166 seats. The campaign's dramatic story was the surge of the New Democratic Party under Jack Layton, which won 103 seats — including 59 in Quebec — and became the Official Opposition for the first time. The Liberal Party under Michael Ignatieff fell to 34 seats, its worst result ever, and the Bloc Québécois collapsed to four seats. The Green Party won its first elected seat, with leader Elizabeth May victorious in Saanich—Gulf Islands.

Aftermath

The result reshaped Canadian politics: the NDP displaced the Liberals as the main party of the centre-left, and both Ignatieff and Bloc leader Gilles Duceppe resigned. Layton died of cancer in August 2011, only months after his historic breakthrough.
