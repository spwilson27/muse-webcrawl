Motorcycle speedway

Motorcycle speedway
Governing body: FIM
Bikes: 500cc, single gear, no brakes
Track: oval, loose dirt/shale, 260–425 m
Top series: Speedway Grand Prix; national leagues

Motorcycle speedway, usually referred to simply as speedway, is a motorcycle sport involving four, and sometimes up to six, riders competing over four anti-clockwise laps of an oval circuit. The motorcycles are specialist machines with no brakes and a single fixed gear, powered by 500cc single-cylinder engines running on methanol, and riders slide ("broadside") the rear wheel to slow down and corner on the loose dirt surface.

Format

League matches consist of a series of heats, typically fifteen, with riders scoring 3 points for a win, 2 for second, 1 for third and 0 for last, and team captains nominating riders for tactical and final heats. The world championship is decided by the Speedway Grand Prix series, held since 1995, and team racing is dominated by leagues in Poland (the Ekstraliga, the best-attended speedway competition in the world), Great Britain and Sweden.

History

The sport originated in Australia and the United States in the 1910s–1920s as dirt-track racing, and was popularised in Britain from 1928. Legendary riders include Ivan Mauger of New Zealand, six-time world champion, Sweden's Tony Rickardsson, and Poland's Bartosz Zmarzlik. Related disciplines include ice speedway, long track (grasstrack) and flat track.
