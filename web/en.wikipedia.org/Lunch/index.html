Lunch

Lunch is a meal eaten around the middle of the day, typically between about 11:00 and 14:00. It is generally the second meal of the day, after breakfast, and varies in size and formality by culture: in some countries it is the main meal of the day, while in others it is a light, quick meal taken during a work break.

Etymology and history

The word is a shortening of luncheon, attested from the late sixteenth century, possibly from "lump" (a hunk of bread or cheese) combined with "nuncheon", an earlier word for a midday snack or drink. Before the nineteenth century, the main meal (dinner) was eaten in the middle of the day; as artificial lighting and industrial working hours pushed dinner to the evening, lunch filled the midday gap, evolving from a snack into a distinct meal.

Around the world

In Spain and much of Latin America, lunch (almuerzo/comida) is traditionally the day's largest meal, often multi-course and followed by a siesta in some regions. In Germany the hot main meal (Mittagessen) is at midday, while in the United Kingdom and North America lunch is commonly sandwiches, salads or soup taken during a lunch break of 30–60 minutes. School lunches are a major institution and policy topic in many countries, and set midday meals — the Japanese teishoku, the Indian thali, the French formule déjeuner — all exemplify the midday meal's many forms.
