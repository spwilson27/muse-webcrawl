Vizing's theorem

Vizing's theorem
FieldGraph theory
Proved byVadim G. Vizing (1964)
StatementEvery simple graph has edge chromatic number Δ or Δ+1

In graph theory, Vizing's theorem states that every simple undirected graph may be edge colored using a number of colors that is at most one larger than the maximum vertex degree Δ. Since Δ colors are always necessary (the edges incident to a maximum-degree vertex must all differ), every simple graph has chromatic index either Δ or Δ+1. Graphs of the first kind are called class 1, and of the second, class 2.

History

The theorem was proved by the Ukrainian mathematician Vadim G. Vizing in 1964 and published in Russian; a similar result was obtained independently by Ram Prakash Gupta in 1966. Vizing's proof uses an inductive argument built around "Vizing fans" — sequences of neighbors of a vertex used to recolor edges and free a color for an uncolored edge.

Extensions and related results

For multigraphs, Shannon's theorem gives a bound of floor(3Δ/2), and Goldberg's conjecture (now a theorem in asymptotic form) refines this. Deciding whether a given graph is class 1 or class 2 is NP-complete, even for cubic graphs, although Holyer's theorem and results on snarks characterize special cases. The theorem is a cornerstone of edge coloring and has applications in scheduling, where edge colorings correspond to timetables.
