The Western Satraps, or Western Kshatrapas, were Indo-Scythian (Saka) rulers of western and central India, governing parts of modern Gujarat, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, and Madhya Pradesh for about 350 years, from around 35 CE to 405 CE. They were contemporaries and rivals of the Satavahana dynasty and, later, the Gupta Empire.

History

The dynasty comprised two principal houses. The earlier Kshaharata house included Bhumaka and Nahapana, whose power extended over Malwa and Gujarat before being broken by the Satavahana king Gautamiputra Satakarni in the early 2nd century CE. The succeeding Kardamaka house was founded by Chastana, who became mahakshatrapa around 120 CE.

The most celebrated Western Satrap was Rudradaman I (r. c. 130–150 CE), a great conqueror and patron of Sanskrit learning. His Junagadh rock inscription — the first major inscription in classical Sanskrit — records his victories and his repair of the Sudarshana lake dam originally built under Chandragupta Maurya.

Coinage and administration

The Western Satraps issued an extensive silver coinage modelled on Indo-Greek and Roman types, bearing the king's portrait with legends in Greek and Brahmi script, and dated in the Saka era — a practical source of chronology for early historic India. Trade through ports such as Bharukachchha (Bharuch) linked their realm to the Roman world, as described in the Periplus of the Erythraean Sea.

Decline

The last Western Satrap, Rudrasimha III, was defeated and killed by the Gupta emperor Chandragupta II around 395–405 CE, ending Saka rule in western India and annexing their territories to the Gupta Empire.
