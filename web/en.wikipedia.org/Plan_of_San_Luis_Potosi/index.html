Plan of San Luis Potosí

Issued5 October 1910 (dated)
AuthorFrancisco I. Madero
ResultStart of the Mexican Revolution

The Plan of San Luis Potosí (Spanish: Plan de San Luis Potosí) was a political manifesto issued by Francisco I. Madero, the wealthy landowner who had challenged the long dictatorship of Porfirio Díaz in the stolen election of 1910. Written after Madero escaped house arrest in San Luis Potosí and fled to San Antonio, Texas, it is conventionally regarded as the opening act of the Mexican Revolution.

Contents

Dated 5 October 1910 — Madero's last day in San Luis Potosí — the plan declared the 1910 elections null and void, disavowed the Díaz government, proclaimed Madero provisional president, and called the Mexican people to arms at 6 p.m. on Sunday, 20 November 1910. It promised the restitution of lands illegally taken from village communities under Díaz's land laws, universal suffrage, and a strict principle of no re-election for presidents and governors, summarized in Madero's slogan Sufragio efectivo, no reelección ("Effective suffrage, no re-election").

Outcome

Uprisings on 20 November were scattered, but armed movements led by Pascual Orozco and Pancho Villa in Chihuahua, Emiliano Zapata in Morelos, and others spread through 1911. Díaz resigned in May 1911 under the Treaty of Ciudad Juárez, and Madero was elected president later that year. His government, unable to satisfy the revolution's competing demands, fell to Victoriano Huerta's coup in February 1913, and the revolution entered a bloodier decade-long phase.
