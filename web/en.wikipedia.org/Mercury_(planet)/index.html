Mercury is the first planet from the Sun and the smallest in the Solar System. It is a rocky planet with a heavily cratered surface resembling the Moon, a very thin exosphere instead of a substantial atmosphere, and the largest metallic core, relative to its size, of any planet — iron makes up about 70% of its mass.

Physical characteristics

Mercury's surface is covered with impact craters, including the vast Caloris Basin, and with wrinkle ridges formed as the planet's huge core cooled and shrank. Despite daytime temperatures of 430 °C, permanently shadowed polar craters contain water ice. Mercury has a weak magnetic field, about 1% the strength of Earth's.

Orbit and rotation

Mercury races around the Sun in 88 days on the most eccentric orbit of any planet. It rotates exactly three times for every two orbits (a 3:2 resonance), so a single solar day on Mercury lasts two Mercurian years — 176 Earth days. Its perihelion advances by an extra 43 arcseconds per century, an anomaly explained only by Einstein's general relativity.

Observation and exploration

Known since antiquity and named for the swift Roman messenger god, Mercury is visible only near dawn or dusk. Only two spacecraft have visited it: NASA's Mariner 10 (flybys, 1974–75) and MESSENGER (orbiter, 2011–2015). The European–Japanese BepiColombo mission is en route and is expected to enter orbit in the mid-2020s.
