Bavaria

Bavaria (German: Bayern), officially the Free State of Bavaria, is a state (Land) in the southeast of Germany. With an area of about 70,550 square kilometres, it is the largest German state by land area, comprising roughly a fifth of the country's total. Its population of around 13 million makes it second only to North Rhine-Westphalia. The state capital and largest city is Munich, followed by Nuremberg and Augsburg.

Geography

Bavaria borders Austria, Czechia and Switzerland (across Lake Constance) and the German states of Baden-Württemberg, Hesse, Thuringia and Saxony. The Danube crosses the state from west to east, dividing northern Franconia and the Bavarian Forest from the Alpine foreland and the Bavarian Alps in the south, where the Zugspitze (2,962 m) is Germany's highest peak.

History

The Duchy of Bavaria was one of the stem duchies of the early medieval Holy Roman Empire. The House of Wittelsbach ruled Bavaria from 1180 until 1918, as dukes, electors and, from 1806, kings. Bavaria joined the German Empire in 1871 while retaining some privileges. After the First World War it became a free state of the Weimar Republic; the short-lived Bavarian Soviet Republic of 1919 was suppressed. Munich was a centre of the early Nazi movement, including the 1923 Beer Hall Putsch. After 1945 Bavaria formed part of the American occupation zone and then of West Germany, becoming one of the Federal Republic's most prosperous states, long dominated politically by the Christian Social Union (CSU).

Culture and economy

Bavaria is known for beer culture, including Munich's Oktoberfest; traditional dress such as Lederhosen and Dirndl; and landmarks including Neuschwanstein Castle. Its economy combines agriculture and tourism with major industrial and technology firms, including BMW, Siemens and Adidas, and it consistently records among the lowest unemployment rates in Germany.
