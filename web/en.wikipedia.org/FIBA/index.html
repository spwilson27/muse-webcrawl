FIBA (French: Fédération Internationale de Basketball), the International Basketball Federation, is the world governing body for the sport of basketball. It was founded in Geneva on June 18, 1932, as the Fédération Internationale de Basketball Amateur — the source of the acronym FIBA — by eight founding nations: Argentina, Czechoslovakia, Greece, Italy, Latvia, Portugal, Romania, and Switzerland. The word "Amateur" was dropped from the name in 1989, but the acronym was retained.

Role and structure

FIBA defines the official rules of basketball, specifies equipment and facilities, regulates the international transfer of players, and organizes major international competitions through its five continental zones: FIBA Africa, FIBA Americas, FIBA Asia, FIBA Europe, and FIBA Oceania. It has recognized basketball's version of 3x3 as an official discipline and oversees the FIBA 3x3 World Tour.

Tournaments

FIBA's flagship events are the FIBA Basketball World Cup (first held in 1950) and the FIBA Women's Basketball World Cup (first held in 1953), as well as the basketball tournaments of the Olympic Games, which have been governed by FIBA since basketball's Olympic debut in 1936. It also organizes continental championships (such as EuroBasket and the FIBA AmeriCup), youth world championships at under-17 and under-19 level, and the FIBA Intercontinental Cup for clubs. FIBA maintains world rankings for national teams and runs the Basketball Hall of Fame located in Alcobendas, Spain.
