Naltrexone

Naltrexone, sold under the brand names Revia and Vivitrol among others, is a medication used to manage alcohol dependence and opioid dependence. It is an opioid receptor antagonist, chemically related to naloxone, and works by blocking the euphoric and sedative effects of opioids and reducing cravings for alcohol. It is taken by mouth or as a monthly intramuscular injection.

Medical uses

Naltrexone is approved for the treatment of opioid use disorder (after detoxification, since it can precipitate withdrawal) and alcohol use disorder. The extended-release injection (Vivitrol) improves adherence compared with daily oral dosing. Combined with bupropion (as Contrave), it is also approved for chronic weight management. Low-dose naltrexone is used off-label for some pain and autoimmune conditions, though evidence remains limited.

Pharmacology and side effects

Naltrexone competitively antagonises the μ-opioid receptor, with weaker activity at κ- and δ-opioid receptors; its active metabolite, 6β-naltrexol, contributes to its long duration of action. Common side effects include nausea, headache and dizziness; high doses have been associated with liver enzyme elevations. Because it blocks opioid analgesics, patients must be opioid-free before starting treatment.
