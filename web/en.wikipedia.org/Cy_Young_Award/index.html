Cy Young Award

The Cy Young Award is given annually to the best pitchers in Major League Baseball (MLB), one each for the American League (AL) and National League (NL). The award was introduced in 1956 by Commissioner Ford Frick in honour of Hall of Fame pitcher Cy Young, who died in 1955.

History and voting

From 1956 to 1966 a single award covered all of MLB; since 1967 separate AL and NL awards have been presented. Winners are chosen by members of the Baseball Writers' Association of America (BBWAA), who rank pitchers on a ballot, voting before the postseason. Pitchers are judged primarily on wins, strikeouts, earned run average and innings pitched, though criteria have broadened with modern analytics.

Records

Roger Clemens won the award a record seven times, followed by Randy Johnson with five. Greg Maddux and Steve Carlton each won four. Don Newcombe was the first winner, in 1956. Notable multiple winners include Sandy Koufax, Pedro Martínez, Clayton Kershaw and Max Scherzer. In 2023 the AL winner was Gerrit Cole and the NL winner Blake Snell.
