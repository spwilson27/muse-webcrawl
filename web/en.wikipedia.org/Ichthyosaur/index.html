KingdomAnimalia
ClassReptilia
OrderIchthyosauria

Ichthyosaurs (from Greek ichthys, fish, and sauros, lizard) are an order of marine reptiles that thrived during much of the Mesozoic Era. They first appeared in the Early Triassic, about 250 million years ago, reached their greatest diversity in the Late Triassic and Early Jurassic, and died out in the Late Cretaceous, about 90 million years ago.

Anatomy

Ichthyosaurs evolved a fish-like, streamlined body convergent with dolphins and sharks, with a dorsal fin, a crescent-shaped tail fluke, and limbs modified into paddles. Many had very large eyes — in Ophthalmosaurus exceeding 20 cm across — suggesting deep diving or nocturnal hunting. They were air-breathing and gave birth to live young at sea.

Diet and behaviour

Most ichthyosaurs fed on fish, squid-like cephalopods, and ammonites. Giant forms such as Shastasaurus and Shonisaurus exceeded 15 metres in length, among the largest marine reptiles known.

Discovery

The first complete ichthyosaur fossils were found in the early 19th century at Lyme Regis, England, notably by Mary Anning. Genera include Ichthyosaurus, Temnodontosaurus, Stenopterygius, and Eurypterygia-type forms described across Europe, North America, and Asia.

Extinction

Ichthyosaurs declined during the Jurassic and Cretaceous and disappeared well before the end-Cretaceous mass extinction, possibly through competition and environmental change.
