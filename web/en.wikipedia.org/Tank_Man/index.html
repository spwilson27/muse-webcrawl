Tank Man

The Tank Man (also known as the Unknown Protester or Unknown Rebel) is the nickname of an unidentified Chinese man who stood in front of a column of Type 59 tanks leaving Tiananmen Square in Beijing on June 5, 1989, the day after the Chinese military had forcibly cleared the square of pro-democracy demonstrators. His act became one of the most iconic images of the 20th century.

The event

Around noon on June 5, the man, dressed in a white shirt and dark trousers and carrying a shopping bag in each hand, walked into the middle of Chang'an Avenue and stood before a column of People's Liberation Army tanks. As the lead tank tried to manoeuvre around him, he repeatedly shifted to block it. The column halted. He then climbed onto the lead tank, apparently speaking with its crew, before two figures pulled him away into the crowd. Whether they were concerned bystanders or plainclothes officers remains unknown.

Photographs

The scene was photographed and filmed from the balconies of the Beijing Hotel by several Western journalists, including Jeff Widener of the Associated Press and Charlie Cole of Newsweek; Stuart Franklin's wider shot for Magnum is also famous. The images were smuggled out of China and published worldwide.

Identity and legacy

The man's identity and fate have never been confirmed. He has been hailed internationally as a symbol of peaceful defiance; Time included "The Unknown Rebel" among its 100 most influential people of the 20th century. Within China, the image is heavily censored and largely unknown to younger generations, and public discussion of the event is suppressed.
