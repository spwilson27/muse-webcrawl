Ernst Käsemann

Born: 12 July 1906, Dahlhausen, Germany
Died: 17 February 1998, Tübingen, Germany
Occupation: Lutheran theologian, New Testament scholar

Ernst Käsemann (12 July 1906 – 17 February 1998) was a German Lutheran theologian and professor of New Testament at the universities of Mainz, Göttingen, and Tübingen. A pupil of Rudolf Bultmann, he is credited with reopening the "quest of the historical Jesus" among Bultmann's students.

The new quest

In a famous 1953 lecture, "The Problem of the Historical Jesus", Käsemann argued against his teacher's scepticism that historical knowledge of Jesus matters for faith, launching what became known as the "New Quest". His work emphasised the continuity between the historical Jesus and the proclamation of the early church.

Other work

Käsemann wrote a major commentary on Paul's Epistle to the Romans (1973) and works on apocalyptic, which he famously called "the mother of all Christian theology". During the Nazi era he was associated with the Confessing Church and was briefly imprisoned for his outspoken preaching.

Legacy

His daughter Elisabeth Käsemann was murdered by the Argentine military junta in 1977, an event that deeply marked his later years and his engagement with political theology.
