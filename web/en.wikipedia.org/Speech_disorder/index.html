Speech disorder

A speech disorder is a condition in which a person has problems creating or forming the speech sounds needed to communicate with others. It is distinct from a language disorder, which involves difficulty understanding or using language itself. Speech disorders can affect articulation, fluency, and voice quality.

Classification

Speech disorders are commonly divided into articulation disorders (difficulty producing specific sounds), fluency disorders (such as stuttering, in which the flow of speech is disrupted), and voice disorders (problems with pitch, volume, or quality of the voice).

Causes

Causes include hearing loss, neurological disorders, brain injury, intellectual disability, physical impairments such as cleft lip and palate, and vocal abuse. In many children the cause is unknown and the disorder resolves with development or therapy.

Treatment

Treatment is usually provided by speech-language pathologists through speech therapy, using exercises targeted at the specific sounds or fluency patterns involved. Severe cases may require augmentative communication aids.
