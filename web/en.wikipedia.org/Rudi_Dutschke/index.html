Alfred Willi Rudolf "Rudi" Dutschke (7 March 1940 – 24 December 1979) was a German sociologist and political activist, the most prominent spokesperson of the West German student movement of the 1960s. As a leading figure of the Socialist German Student Union (SDS) and the extra-parliamentary opposition (APO), he campaigned against the Vietnam War, the emergency laws (Notstandsgesetze) and what he saw as the authoritarian structures of West German society.

Student movement

Dutschke grew up in East Germany and moved to West Berlin in 1961, shortly before the Berlin Wall was built. Studying sociology at the Free University of Berlin, he advocated a strategy he called the "long march through the institutions" — transforming society by working within its institutions over the long term, a phrase that became a lasting political slogan.

Assassination attempt and later life

On 11 April 1968, Dutschke was shot and seriously wounded by Josef Bachmann, a young labourer influenced by anti-communist press coverage. The shooting triggered mass protests and riots against the Axel Springer publishing house. Dutschke suffered brain injuries and epilepsy as a result; he lived subsequently in Italy, Britain and Denmark, and helped found the German Green Party. He died in Aarhus in 1979 from health problems stemming from the assassination attempt.

Legacy

Dutschke remains the emblematic figure of the West German 1968 movement. A street in Berlin-Kreuzberg was renamed Rudi-Dutschke-Straße in 2008, amid considerable public controversy.
