Eliza Acton

Eliza Acton (17 April 1799 – 13 February 1859) was an English food writer and poet who produced one of Britain's first cookery books aimed at the domestic reader, Modern Cookery for Private Families (1845). The book introduced the now-standard practice of listing ingredients separately from the method, and giving cooking times — a format followed by virtually all later recipe writers.

Life

Acton was born in Battle, Sussex, the eldest of five daughters of John Acton, a brewer, and Elizabeth Mercer. She was raised in Ipswich, Suffolk, and ran a boarding school for girls in Suffolk as a young woman. After developing health problems, she spent time in France, and on her return published a volume of poetry in 1826.

Modern Cookery for Private Families

Acton's publisher, Longman, reportedly suggested she write a cookery book after her poetry collection sold poorly. The result, published in 1845, was enormously successful, running through numerous editions and remaining in print until 1918. Acton wrote explicitly for "private families" of moderate means, testing her recipes herself — unusual for the period.

The book contained one of the first recipes for Christmas pudding and is credited with giving the dish its name; it also included an early recipe for spaghetti (which Acton spelled "sparghetti") and for "bride cake". Acton followed it with The English Bread-Book (1857), a more scholarly work campaigning for better-quality bread.

Legacy

Acton's work heavily influenced later Victorian cookery writers, including Isabella Beeton, whose Book of Household Management drew extensively on Acton's recipes and format. Modern food historians rank Acton among the most important English cookery writers, and her book has been reissued and championed by writers such as Elizabeth David and Delia Smith.
