Chuck (engineering)

Type: Clamping device

Used on: Lathes, drills, mills

A chuck is a specialized clamp used to hold a rotating tool or workpiece, most familiarly the drill bit in a drill or a cylindrical part in a lathe. Jaws move radially, usually together, to grip the object securely and concentrically.

Types

Three-jaw self-centring chucks dominate general lathe and drill work. Four-jaw independent chucks can hold irregular shapes and be adjusted precisely. Collet chucks grip closely sized round stock with high accuracy, while magnetic, vacuum, and electrostatic chucks hold workpieces without conventional jaws.

Operation

Drill chucks may be tightened by a key or by hand. Correct gripping, clean jaws, and appropriate speed are essential to safety and machining accuracy.
