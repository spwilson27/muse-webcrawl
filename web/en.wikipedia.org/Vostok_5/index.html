Vostok 5

Vostok 5 was a Soviet crewed spaceflight launched on 14 June 1963, piloted by Valery Bykovsky. It flew jointly with Vostok 6, which carried Valentina Tereshkova, the first woman in space. Bykovsky's flight lasted just under five days — setting a solo endurance record that still stands for a single occupant — and completed 82 orbits of the Earth.

Vostok 5 flew jointly with Vostok 6 in June 1963.

Mission

The joint flight was intended to study the effects of prolonged weightlessness and to compare male and female physiology in orbit. Vostok 5 was launched two days before Vostok 6, and the two craft passed within a few kilometres of each other shortly after Tereshkova's launch, exchanging radio greetings. Bykovsky conducted scientific observations, took television images broadcast to Earth, and reported on his condition throughout the flight.

An originally planned longer mission was cut to just under five days, partly because of elevated solar activity raising radiation concerns. Vostok 5 landed on 19 June 1963 near Karaganda in Kazakhstan, roughly two hours before Vostok 6. Bykovsky's solo duration record for a single-seat flight has never been exceeded.
