Echo (mythology)

In Greek mythology, Echo (Greek: Ἠχώ, Ēkhṓ) was an Oread (mountain nymph) whose excessive talkativeness led the goddess Hera to curse her so that she could only repeat the last words spoken to her. Her unrequited love for Narcissus, and her wasting away until only her voice remained, gave the languages of Europe the word "echo".

The curse of Hera

Echo entertained Hera with long stories while Zeus dallied with the nymphs. Enraged, Hera deprived Echo of the ability to speak first or hold her tongue when another spoke — she could only repeat the final syllables she heard.

Echo and Narcissus

In Ovid's Metamorphoses, Echo fell in love with the beautiful youth Narcissus, but her curse made conversation impossible and he cruelly rejected her. Shamed, she hid in the woods and wasted away until nothing but her voice remained, while Narcissus was punished by Nemesis to fall in love with his own reflection and die by the pool.

Other tales

In Longus's novel Daphnis and Chloe, Echo is a mortal musician loved by Pan; in that account her scattered remains still sing. The myth is a classic aetiology for the acoustic phenomenon of the echo.
