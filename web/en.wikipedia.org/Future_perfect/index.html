Future perfect

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The future perfect is a verb form or construction used to describe an event that is expected or planned to happen before a point of time in the future. In English it is formed with the future auxiliary will (or shall), the perfect auxiliary have, and the past participle of the main verb.

English

By this time tomorrow, we will have arrived.

The construction locates the completion of an action prior to a future reference point, looking back on it, as it were, from that future moment. It contrasts with the simple future ("we will arrive"), which does not emphasise completion in advance of the reference point. The continuous variant, the future perfect continuous ("will have been travelling"), emphasises duration up to that point.

In other languages

Latin possessed a distinct future perfect tense (futurum perfectum), e.g. amāverō, "I will have loved". Romance languages form equivalents with the future of "to have" plus a past participle (French futur antérieur, Spanish futuro perfecto), used similarly for actions completed before a future time and, in some languages, for conjectures about the past. German uses Futur II ("werden" + participle + "haben/sein") in a similar way, often to express supposition.
