Christina Hambley Brown, Lady Evans (born 21 November 1953), known as Tina Brown, is an English journalist, magazine editor, columnist, and author. She is best known for her editorships of Tatler, Vanity Fair, and The New Yorker, which she revitalised during the 1980s and 1990s.

Career

Brown became editor of Tatler in 1979 at the age of 25, transforming the society magazine into a witty, commercially successful title. In 1984 she moved to New York to edit Vanity Fair, raising its circulation dramatically with a mixture of celebrity coverage, politics, and literary journalism. From 1992 to 1998 she edited The New Yorker, modernising its design and broadening its coverage. She later founded the news website The Daily Beast in 2008 and merged it with Newsweek, which she also edited.

Books

Brown is the author of The Diana Chronicles (2007), a biography of Diana, Princess of Wales; The Vanity Fair Diaries (2017); and The Palace Papers (2022), about the British royal family. She founded the Women in the World summit in 2010. She is married to the journalist Sir Harold Evans.
