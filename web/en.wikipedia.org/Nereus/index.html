In Greek mythology, Nereus (Greek: Νηρεύς, translit. Nērēus, "wet one") was the eldest son of Pontus (the Sea) and Gaia (the Earth), and the father of the Nereids — sea nymphs — by the Oceanid Doris. Along with Proteus and other figures he bears the title "Old Man of the Sea" (halios geron), a wise and truthful shape-shifting prophet of the deep.

Mythology

Hesiod's Theogony praises Nereus as truthful, gentle and righteous: "that Old One who is truthful and does not lie... thoughts are benign and righteous". A famous episode, depicted on vase paintings, tells how Heracles wrestled the shape-shifting Nereus to learn the way to the garden of the Hesperides — Nereus transformed into water, fire and wild beasts before submitting, a motif shared with Proteus in the Odyssey.

Nereus lived in the Aegean Sea with Doris and their fifty daughters, among them Thetis (mother of Achilles), Amphitrite (wife of Poseidon) and Galatea. In the Iliad, the Nereids mourn with Thetis for Achilles' fate.

Legacy

Nereus's name has been given to the asteroid 4660 Nereus, to a deep-diving robotic vehicle (which was lost in the Kermadec Trench in 2014), and to various ships. The element of truthfulness and prophecy distinguishes him from the more violent sea powers of later Greek myth such as Poseidon, to whom much of his domain was ceded in later tradition.
