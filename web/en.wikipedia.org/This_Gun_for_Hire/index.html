This Gun for Hire is a 1942 American film noir directed by Frank Tuttle and based on Graham Greene's 1936 novel A Gun for Sale. It stars Veronica Lake, Robert Preston and Laird Cregar, with Alan Ladd in the role that made him a star, as the hired killer Philip Raven.

Plot

Hitman Philip Raven murders a blackmailer and his companion in San Francisco and is paid off by his employer, Willard Gates, in marked bills that set the police on his trail. Raven discovers that Gates works for a chemical company selling poison-gas formulas to the Japanese. Travelling to Los Angeles to take revenge, Raven crosses paths with nightclub singer (and secret police operative) Ellen Graham, whose fiancé is the detective pursuing him. After a series of encounters in which Raven repeatedly spares or protects Ellen, she persuades him to extract a confession from the company's aged magnate before he is killed by the police — allowing him a measure of redemption.

Reception and legacy

The film was a major box-office success and established the screen pairing of Ladd and Lake, who co-starred in several further noirs including The Glass Key and The Blue Dahlia. Ladd's portrayal of the emotionally damaged, cat-loving, yet sympathetic killer helped define the noir antihero. The film has been remade several times, including a 1957 version (Short Cut to Hell) and a 1991 television film.
