India is among the most linguistically diverse countries in the world. The 2011 census recorded 121 languages each spoken by more than 10,000 people, and hundreds more in total. Most belong to four families: Indo-Aryan (spoken by about 78% of the population), Dravidian (about 20%), Austroasiatic and Sino-Tibetan.

Official status

The Constitution of India designates Hindi in the Devanagari script as the official language of the Union, with English continuing as an associate official language. The Eighth Schedule lists 22 scheduled languages, including Bengali, Tamil, Telugu, Marathi, Urdu, Gujarati, Malayalam, Kannada, Odia, Punjabi, Assamese and Sanskrit. States are free to choose their own official languages, and state boundaries were substantially redrawn along linguistic lines in 1956.

Major languages

Hindi is the most widely spoken first language (over 520 million speakers including related varieties), followed by Bengali, Marathi, Telugu and Tamil. The southern Dravidian languages — Tamil, Telugu, Kannada and Malayalam — have long independent literary traditions, and Tamil, Sanskrit, Kannada, Telugu, Malayalam and Odia have been designated classical languages.

Scripts and policy

Indian languages are written in many scripts, most derived from Brahmi — Devanagari, Bengali, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Gujarati, Gurmukhi and Odia among them — while Urdu uses a Perso-Arabic script. The three-language formula in education policy aims to promote multilingualism, though implementation varies. Many smaller and tribal languages are endangered, and documentation efforts continue.
