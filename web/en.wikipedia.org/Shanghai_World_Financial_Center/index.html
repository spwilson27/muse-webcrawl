The Shanghai World Financial Center (SWFC; Chinese: 上海环球金融中心) is a supertall skyscraper in the Lujiazui financial district of Pudong, Shanghai, China. Designed by the American architectural firm Kohn Pedersen Fox and developed by Japan's Mori Building Company, it was completed in 2008 and opened on 28 August 2008. Rising 492 metres (1,614 ft) with 101 floors, it was the tallest building in China at its completion and remains one of the tallest in the world; it was surpassed in Shanghai itself by the Shanghai Tower (632 m), completed in 2015.

The tower's distinctive trapezoidal aperture near its top — originally designed as a circle, then changed after objections that it resembled the Japanese rising-sun flag — gives the building its popular nickname, the "bottle opener". It houses offices, the Park Hyatt Shanghai hotel, conference facilities, shops, and observation decks on the 94th, 97th, and 100th floors, the last featuring a glass-bottomed skywalk that was for a time the highest observation deck in the world.

Design and construction

Ground was broken on 27 August 1997, but work stalled during the Asian financial crisis; the design was subsequently revised upward from 460 m to 492 m, and construction resumed in 2003. The structure combines a reinforced-concrete core and diagonal-braced megaframe with outrigger trusses, designed to withstand strong typhoon winds and earthquakes. A fire in August 2007 during construction caused no fatalities and did not delay completion by much.

Aperture redesign

The original design featured a circular opening 46 m in diameter at the summit to reduce wind stress. Because a circle atop a Japanese-developed building in China evoked the rising sun of the Japanese flag, the design drew criticism in Shanghai, and in 2005 it was changed to the present trapezoidal slot. The redesign, credited to KPF, also proved cheaper and simpler to build.

Tenants and facilities

The building contains about 381,600 m² of floor space. The Park Hyatt Shanghai occupies floors 79–93. Office tenants have included major international banks and financial firms. The observation decks — the Sky Arena 94, Sky Walk 97, and Sky Walk 100 at 474 m — offer views over the Huangpu River and the adjacent Jin Mao Tower and Oriental Pearl Tower.

Awards

The SWFC was named the Best Tall Building Worldwide for 2008 by the Council on Tall Buildings and Urban Habitat, praised for the clarity of its prismatic form and its structural innovation.
