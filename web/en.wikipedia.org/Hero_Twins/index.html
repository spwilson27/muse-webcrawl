Hero Twins

The Maya Hero Twins, Hunahpu and Xbalanque, are central figures of Maya mythology best known from the K'iche' Maya sacred narrative the Popol Vuh. Sons of the maize god Hun Hunahpu, who was killed by the lords of the underworld Xibalba, the twins avenge their father by outwitting the death gods.

The ballgame and descent to Xibalba

Summoned to Xibalba because their ball playing disturbed the underworld lords, the twins survive the deadly trial houses — of darkness, knives, cold, jaguars, fire, and bats — through trickery. In the House of Bats, Hunahpu loses his head to the bat god Camazotz, but the twins improvise a replacement and go on to win the ballgame against the lords of death.

Apotheosis

Allowing themselves to be killed and miraculously resurrected as performers, the twins trick the Xibalban lords into requesting the same feat and then refuse to revive them, defeating death's power. They then ascend into the sky, becoming the sun and the moon in many tellings. Twin heroes appear throughout Mesoamerican and North American indigenous traditions. See also Maya mythology.
