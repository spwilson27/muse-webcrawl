Astrology

Astrology is a range of divinatory practices, recognised as pseudoscientific since the 18th century, that propose that information about human affairs and terrestrial events may be discerned by studying the apparent positions of celestial objects.

The zodiac, divided into twelve signs, is central to Western astrology.

History

Astrology originated in Mesopotamia, where Babylonian omen astrology was systematised by the first millennium BCE. Hellenistic astrology, developed in Egypt and Greece, produced the horoscopic tradition — including natal charts and the twelve-sign zodiac — codified in Ptolemy's Tetrabiblos. Independent traditions arose in India (Jyotisha) and China. Astrology and astronomy were intertwined until the Scientific Revolution separated them.

Practice

Western astrologers cast charts based on the positions of the Sun, Moon, and planets at a person's birth, divided among twelve signs and twelve houses, and interpret aspects between bodies. Popular astrology centres on sun-sign horoscopes in newspapers and apps. Vedic astrology uses the sidereal zodiac and lunar mansions.

Scientific status

Astrology has not demonstrated any predictive validity in controlled studies, and no proposed mechanism is consistent with physics or biology; its apparent successes are attributed to psychological effects such as the Barnum effect. It is widely classified as a pseudoscience, though it retains substantial cultural popularity.
