Absorption (chemistry)

Type: Physical or chemical process

Contrast: Adsorption (surface phenomenon)

In chemistry, absorption is a physical or chemical phenomenon in which atoms, molecules or ions enter a bulk phase — a liquid or solid — and are taken up by its volume rather than merely accumulating on its surface. The process is the basis of many industrial separations and of biological uptake.

Mechanism

Absorption may be purely physical, as when a gas dissolves in a liquid according to Henry's law, or chemical, as when carbon dioxide is absorbed by an amine solution that reacts with it. Unlike adsorption, which is a surface phenomenon, absorption distributes the absorbed substance (absorbate) throughout the bulk of the absorbing material (absorbent). The general term "sorption" covers both processes when they occur together.

Applications

Gas absorption columns scrub pollutants from flue gases and purify natural gas; refrigeration cycles exploit the absorption of ammonia in water; and in physiology, nutrients and drugs are absorbed across the walls of the digestive tract into the bloodstream. Absorption rates depend on concentration gradients, surface area, temperature and the diffusion properties described by Fick's laws.
