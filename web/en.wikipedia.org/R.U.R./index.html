R.U.R.

A scene from a production of R.U.R.

R.U.R. is a 1920 science fiction play by the Czech writer Karel Čapek. Its title is short for Rossumovi Univerzální Roboti ("Rossum's Universal Robots"). Premiered in Prague in 1921, the play introduced the word "robot" to English and to science fiction — coined from the Czech robota, meaning forced labour, at the suggestion of the author's brother Josef.

Plot

The play is set in a factory on an island manufacturing artificial people from synthetic protoplasm. Cheap and emotionless, the robots are sold worldwide as labourers and soldiers, until they rebel and exterminate humanity, sparing only the factory clerk Alquist. In the final act, two robots, Primus and Helena, display love, and Alquist blesses them as a new Adam and Eve.

Themes and influence

Although Čapek's robots are biological rather than mechanical, the play established the enduring narrative of artificial beings revolting against their creators, a theme echoed through later works such as Metropolis, Blade Runner, and Westworld. The play is also a satire on industrial capitalism and utopian ideologies of its day.
