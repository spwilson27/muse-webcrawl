Rhea is the second-largest moon of Saturn and the ninth-largest moon in the Solar System, with a diameter of 1,527 kilometres. It was discovered in 1672 by Giovanni Domenico Cassini and named after the Titan Rhea of Greek mythology.

Physical characteristics

Rhea is an icy body composed of roughly three-quarters water ice and one-quarter rock, with a density of about 1.24 g/cm³ suggesting it is not fully differentiated. Its surface is heavily cratered and marked by bright "wispy terrain" — fractures and cliffs formed by tectonic activity — most prominently on its trailing hemisphere.

Exploration

The Voyager probes imaged Rhea in 1980–81, and the Cassini orbiter made several close flybys between 2005 and 2013. Cassini data briefly suggested Rhea might have a tenuous ring system — which would have been the first known around a moon — but later observations ruled this out.
