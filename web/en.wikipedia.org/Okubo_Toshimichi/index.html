Ōkubo Toshimichi

Ōkubo Toshimichi (大久保 利通, 26 September 1830 – 14 May 1878) was a Japanese statesman and one of the chief architects of the Meiji Restoration. A samurai of Satsuma Domain, he became one of the "Three Great Nobles of the Restoration" alongside Saigō Takamori and Kido Takayoshi, and served as the de facto head of the early Meiji government as Lord of Home Affairs.

Career

Ōkubo rose through the Satsuma administration and, with Saigō, helped forge the alliance that overthrew the Tokugawa shogunate in 1868. He pushed for the abolition of the domains, accompanied the Iwakura Mission abroad in 1871–1873, and on his return blocked the proposed invasion of Korea (Seikanron), breaking politically with his lifelong friend Saigō. As Home Minister he built a centralized police and promoted state-led industrialization, and he directed the government campaign that crushed the Satsuma Rebellion of 1877.

Assassination

Ōkubo was assassinated on 14 May 1878 in Tokyo by former samurai who blamed him for the government's authoritarianism and the death of Saigō. His firm, pragmatic leadership is generally credited with laying the institutional foundations of modern Japan.
