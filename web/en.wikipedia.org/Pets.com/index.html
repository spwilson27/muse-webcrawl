Pets.com

Founded1998 (as a website; company roots 1994)
DefunctNovember 2000
HeadquartersSan Francisco, California
IndustryOnline pet supplies retail
MascotSock puppet dog

Pets.com was a dot-com enterprise headquartered in San Francisco that sold pet supplies online. It became one of the most famous symbols of the dot-com bubble: launched with heavy venture funding and a celebrated advertising campaign, it went from initial public offering to liquidation in about nine months in 2000.

The Pets.com sock puppet, voiced in advertisements by Michael Ian Black

The company grew out of an earlier pet-supply business and relaunched online in 1998 under chief executive Greg McLemore, later succeeded by Julie Wainwright. Its mascot — a wisecracking sock puppet dog with a microphone — appeared in a Super Bowl advertisement, on magazine covers and even as a balloon in the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade, making Pets.com a household name well before it had a sustainable business. The company raised about $82.5 million in its February 2000 IPO on Nasdaq under the ticker IPET.

Failure

Pets.com sold bulky, low-margin goods such as pet food with free or subsidised shipping, losing money on most orders, and spent heavily on marketing. When capital markets turned in 2000 it could not raise further funds, and it announced liquidation in November 2000, selling its assets and the sock puppet's licensing rights. Its collapse is routinely cited alongside Webvan and eToys as an emblem of dot-com excess.
