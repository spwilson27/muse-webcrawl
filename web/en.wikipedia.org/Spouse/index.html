Spouse

A spouse is a significant other in a marriage. The word derives from the Latin sponsus, meaning a betrothed person. A male spouse is referred to as a husband, and a female spouse as a wife. The term is gender-neutral and is widely used in legal, administrative and immigration contexts where the sex of the partner is not specified or not relevant.

Legal status

Marriage confers a range of legal rights and obligations on spouses, which may include rights concerning property, inheritance, taxation, medical decision-making, immigration sponsorship and parental responsibility. The precise rights of a spouse differ by jurisdiction. In many legal systems, certain rights are also extended to partners in civil partnerships, civil unions or common-law marriages.

Terminology

Before marriage, an engaged partner may be called a fiancé or fiancée. Following the death of a spouse, the surviving partner is a widow (female) or widower (male). After divorce, a former spouse may be called an ex-husband or ex-wife. In historical documents, a spouse might be described as a consort, particularly in the case of the partner of a reigning monarch.
