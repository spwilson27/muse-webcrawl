Margiana

Margiana (Greek: Μαργιανή, Margianē; Old Persian: Marguš; Middle Persian: Marw) was a historical region in Central Asia centred on the oasis of Merv, in the delta of the Murghab River in what is now southeastern Turkmenistan. It was one of the eastern lands of the Achaemenid Empire and a crossroads of the Silk Road, famous in antiquity for its fertility and its wine.

Geography

Margiana consisted of the irrigated alluvial fan where the Murghab River, flowing north from the Hindu Kush, dissipates into the Karakum Desert. The oasis supported dense agriculture — grain, vines, fruit, and cotton in later periods — surrounded by steppe and desert. Its principal city, Merv, was successively known as Alexandria Margiana, Antiochia Margiana, and Marw al-Shahijan.

History

The region has one of the deepest archaeological records in Central Asia. In the late third and early second millennia BC the oasis hosted the flourishing Bronze Age culture known as the Bactria–Margiana Archaeological Complex (BMAC), with monumental sites such as Gonur Tepe, Togolok, and Kelleli, sometimes associated with early Indo-Iranian-speaking peoples.

Margiana appears as Marguš among the lands listed by Darius I at Behistun and became a satrapy of the Achaemenid Empire. Alexander the Great founded (or refounded) Alexandria Margiana after his campaigns of 329–328 BC; the Seleucid king Antiochus I rebuilt the city as Antiochia in Margiana and ringed the oasis with a great wall. Passing to Parthian control, Margiana became part of the Arsacid Empire's eastern frontier. Under Sasanian rule, Merv was a major military and administrative centre of the northeast and a hub of Zoroastrian, Christian (Nestorian), Buddhist, and later Islamic scholarship.

In the medieval Islamic period Merv was one of the great cities of Khurasan — a capital of the Seljuk sultan Sanjar, whose mausoleum still stands — until its destruction by the Mongols under Tolui in 1221, one of the most devastating massacres of the Mongol conquests. The oasis revived only partially in later centuries, and the Merv oasis shifted site over time, with Bayram Ali and modern Mary (founded 1884) succeeding the earlier cities.
