Erbium

Erbium
Symbol: Er
Atomic number: 68
Group: Lanthanides
Appearance: silvery-white metal

Erbium is a chemical element with the symbol Er and atomic number 68. It is a silvery-white solid metal when artificially isolated, and is a lanthanide, a rare-earth element, originally found in the gadolinite mine in Ytterby, Sweden, from which it takes its name.

Properties

Erbium is a trivalent element that is malleable and relatively stable in air. Its salts are rose-coloured, and the element has sharp absorption bands in visible, ultraviolet and near-infrared spectra. Natural erbium is a mixture of six stable isotopes, of which erbium-166 is the most abundant.

Applications

Erbium's principal use is in erbium-doped fibre amplifiers (EDFAs), which amplify optical signals at the 1.55 micrometre wavelength used in telecommunications without converting them to electrical signals. Erbium-doped lasers, including Er:YAG lasers, are used in medicine and dentistry, and erbium oxide gives a pink colour to glass and porcelain glazes.

History

Erbium was discovered in 1843 by Carl Gustaf Mosander, who separated the mineral yttria into three components, naming two of them erbia and terbia after the village of Ytterby. Pure erbium oxide was isolated in 1905, and the pure metal was produced in 1934.
