Crime and Punishment

Crime and Punishment is a novel by the Russian author Fyodor Dostoevsky, first published in the literary journal The Russian Messenger in twelve monthly installments during 1866. It is widely regarded as one of the greatest novels in world literature.

Plot

The novel follows Rodion Raskolnikov, an impoverished former student in Saint Petersburg who murders a pawnbroker, Alyona Ivanovna, and her sister Lizaveta, believing himself an extraordinary man entitled to transgress moral law. Tormented by guilt and paranoia, he is pursued by the detective Porfiry Petrovich and eventually confesses, influenced by the self-sacrificing Sonya Marmeladova, and is sentenced to exile in Siberia.

Themes

The novel explores guilt, redemption, morality, utilitarian ethics, and the psychology of crime. It engages with the radical ideas circulating in 1860s Russia, including nihilism and rational egoism, and is a cornerstone of psychological fiction and existentialist literature.
