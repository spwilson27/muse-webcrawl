Proserpina

Proserpina (Latin: Prōserpina) is an ancient Roman goddess whose cult, myths and mysteries were derived from the Greek Persephone. She was the daughter of Ceres (Greek Demeter) and Jupiter (Zeus), queen of the underworld and goddess of spring growth and the fertility of the earth.

Myth

In the central myth, Pluto (Greek Hades), god of the dead, abducted Proserpina while she gathered flowers near Lake Pergusa in Sicily and carried her to the underworld to be his wife. Ceres searched the earth in grief, neglecting the crops, until Jupiter brokered a compromise: because Proserpina had eaten pomegranate seeds in the underworld, she must spend part of each year there — in some versions a third, in others half — and the rest with her mother. Her annual return explained the seasons: when she is below, it is winter; when she returns, spring comes.

Cult and legacy

Proserpina was worshipped with Ceres and Liber in the Aventine triad at Rome, and her myth was retold by Ovid in the Metamorphoses and Fasti. The story has inspired extensive art and literature, from the sculpture of Bernini (The Rape of Proserpina, 1621–22) to Rossetti's painting Proserpine and Swinburne's poem "The Garden of Proserpine". The asteroid 26 Proserpina is named for her.
