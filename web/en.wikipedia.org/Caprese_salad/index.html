Caprese salad (Italian: insalata caprese) is a simple Italian salad, made of sliced fresh mozzarella, tomatoes, and sweet basil, seasoned with salt and olive oil. It originated on the island of Capri in the Campania region, and its red, white, and green components deliberately evoke the colours of the Italian flag.

History

The salad is first documented in the 1920s at Capri's Hotel Quisisana, where it was reportedly served to the futurist poet Filippo Tommaso Marinetti as a light, patriotic dish. It gained a royal association through King Farouk of Egypt, who popularised a sandwich version in the 1950s. Its fame spread with post-war tourism to the Amalfi Coast and Capri.

Preparation

Traditionally, ripe tomatoes and mozzarella — ideally buffalo mozzarella from Campania, mozzarella di bufala Campana — are sliced to similar thickness and arranged alternately on a plate, with fresh basil leaves between or scattered over, then dressed with extra-virgin olive oil and salt. Some versions add black pepper or a few drops of balsamic vinegar, additions purists generally reject. Because the dish has only a handful of ingredients, quality matters: it is best made in summer with fully ripe tomatoes and served at room temperature, never refrigerated.

Variations

Variations include Caprese skewers for parties, chopped Caprese over pasta, and versions substituting burrata or adding peaches. A related Neapolitan practice layers the same ingredients in a sandwich (caprese panino).
