Nicolas Paul Stéphane Sarkozy de Nagy-Bocsa (born 28 January 1955) is a French politician who served as President of France from 2007 to 2012. A dominant figure of the French right for two decades, he was nicknamed "Sarko" and "the hyperpresident" for his energetic, interventionist style.

Early career

The son of a Hungarian immigrant father and a French mother of Greek-Jewish descent, Sarkozy trained as a lawyer and became mayor of the wealthy Paris suburb of Neuilly-sur-Seine at 28. He served as budget minister and government spokesman under Édouard Balladur, then as Jacques Chirac's Minister of the Interior (2002–2004, 2005–2007), where he built a law-and-order reputation, and briefly as finance minister. Elected leader of the UMP in 2004, he won the presidency in May 2007, defeating Ségolène Royal.

Presidency

Sarkozy's term was dominated by the 2008 financial crisis and eurozone crisis, during which France held the EU's rotating presidency; he pushed through pension reform raising the retirement age, university autonomy, and a ban on full-face veils. Abroad he returned France to NATO's integrated military command (2009) and led, with Britain, the 2011 military intervention in Libya that toppled Muammar Gaddafi. He lost the 2012 election to François Hollande — the first one-term French president of the Fifth Republic since Valéry Giscard d'Estaing.

Later career and legal cases

After a failed comeback for the 2017 nomination, Sarkozy retired from frontline politics. He was subsequently convicted in two corruption cases: in 2021, in the "wiretapping affair", of corruption and influence peddling (a sentence to be served under electronic tagging), and in the "Bygmalion" campaign-finance case; in 2025 he was convicted in the Libyan financing case and sentenced to five years' imprisonment, beginning his sentence before being released under judicial supervision pending appeal. He has consistently maintained his innocence and appealed the verdicts.
