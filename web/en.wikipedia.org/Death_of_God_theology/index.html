Death of God theology

Death of God theology is a predominantly Christian theological movement of the 1960s, associated with writers such as Thomas J. J. Altizer, William Hamilton, Gabriel Vahanian, and Paul van Buren. Taking its slogan from Friedrich Nietzsche's proclamation that "God is dead" in The Gay Science (1882), the movement held that the traditional, transcendent God of classical theism had ceased to be a credible presence in modern Western culture, and that theology must be reconstructed accordingly.

Background

The phrase "God is dead" has earlier theological antecedents: in Lutheran hymnody and in Hegel's philosophy, the death of God referred to the crucifixion as God's self-emptying (kenosis). Nietzsche's madman passage gave the idea its modern cultural force: not a metaphysical claim about a being, but the observation that belief in God had become untenable for European civilisation, with enormous consequences. Dietrich Bonhoeffer's prison writings on a "religionless Christianity" and Paul Tillich's "God above God" also fed the movement.

The 1960s movement

The radical theologians differed among themselves. Gabriel Vahanian's The Death of God (1961) was largely a cultural diagnosis. William Hamilton and Thomas Altizer, in Radical Theology and the Death of God (1966), went further: Altizer, drawing on Blake and Hegel, taught a dialectical theology in which God genuinely dies into the world in Christ, while Hamilton urged a Christology centred on Jesus as a model for worldly life rather than on a transcendent deity. Paul van Buren's The Secular Meaning of the Gospel (1963) applied linguistic analysis to restate the gospel in purely secular terms. Jewish theologian Richard Rubenstein raised parallel questions after the Holocaust in After Auschwitz (1966).

The movement attracted wide publicity, including a famous Time magazine cover of 8 April 1966 asking "Is God Dead?" It had largely faded by the early 1970s, though its themes recur in later post-theistic and "weak theology" movements and in the work of philosophers such as John D. Caputo and Gianni Vattimo.
