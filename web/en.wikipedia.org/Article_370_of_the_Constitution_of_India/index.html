Article 370 of the Constitution of India

Article 370 of the Constitution of India was a provision that accorded special autonomous status to the state of Jammu and Kashmir. Drafted in 1949 as a "temporary provision", it limited the Indian Parliament's power to make laws for the state and allowed Jammu and Kashmir to have its own constitution and flag. In August 2019 the Government of India rendered the article largely inoperative and reorganised the state into two union territories.

Background

When the princely state of Jammu and Kashmir acceded to India in October 1947 amid war with Pakistan, its ruler signed an Instrument of Accession limited to defence, foreign affairs and communications. Article 370, drafted by N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar and shepherded by Jawaharlal Nehru and Sheikh Abdullah, embodied this special relationship, while Article 35A, added by a 1954 presidential order, allowed the state to define "permanent residents" with special rights.

Revocation in 2019

On 5 August 2019, President Ram Nath Kovind issued Constitutional Order 272, applying the entire Indian Constitution to Jammu and Kashmir and effectively superseding the 1954 order, following resolutions of Parliament. The Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act then split the state into the union territories of Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh, effective 31 October 2019. The move followed long-standing demands by the Bharatiya Janata Party and was accompanied by detentions, communications restrictions and a security lockdown in the Kashmir Valley.

Aftermath and litigation

Petitions challenging the changes were heard by the Supreme Court of India, which in December 2023 upheld the abrogation of Article 370 as constitutionally valid while directing that statehood be restored and elections held. The decision and the abrogation itself remain politically contested, welcomed by supporters as full integration and condemned by Kashmiri parties and Pakistan as a unilateral alteration of the region's status.
