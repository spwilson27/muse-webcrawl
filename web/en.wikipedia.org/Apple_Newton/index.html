The Newton is a series of personal digital assistants (PDAs) developed and marketed by Apple Computer from 1993 to 1998. The first device, the MessagePad, ran the Newton OS and featured handwriting recognition with a stylus — Apple CEO John Sculley coined the term "personal digital assistant" for the category the Newton defined.

Development and reception

The Newton project began in 1987 as an ambitious handheld computer and evolved into a smaller notepad-like device. Launched in August 1993 at a price of about $699, the MessagePad was praised for its vision but criticised for its initially unreliable handwriting recognition, which became a target of parody, including in the comic strip Doonesbury and on The Simpsons. Recognition improved substantially in Newton OS 2.0 (1995), and the MessagePad 2000 and 2100 (1997) were well reviewed.

Discontinuation and legacy

After Steve Jobs returned to Apple, the Newton line was cancelled in February 1998 as the company refocused. Despite commercial failure, Newton pioneered the ARM processor's use in mobile devices — its ARM chips came from a venture Apple co-founded — and anticipated features and concepts that later flourished in smartphones and tablets.
