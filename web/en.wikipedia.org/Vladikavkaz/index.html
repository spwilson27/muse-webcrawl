Vladikavkaz

Vladikavkaz (Russian: Владикавказ, "ruler of the Caucasus"; Ossetian: Дзæуджыхъæу, Dzæudzhyqæu) is the capital city of the Republic of North Ossetia–Alania, Russia, situated in the foothills of the Caucasus Mountains on the Terek River. Founded in 1784 as a Russian fortress guarding the northern entrance of the Georgian Military Road, it has a population of roughly 300,000 and serves as an industrial and educational centre of the North Caucasus. From 1931 to 1944 and again from 1954 to 1990 it was known as Ordzhonikidze, and from 1944 to 1954 as Dzaudzhikau.

History

The fortress of Vladikavkaz was established in 1784 near the Ossetian village of Dzaudzhikau to secure the route through the Darial Gorge into Georgia. It grew into a town, receiving city status in 1860, and became the northern terminus of the Georgian Military Road over the Caucasus. The city developed as a trade and administrative centre and, in the Soviet period, as an industrial city. In 1931 it was renamed Ordzhonikidze after the Bolshevik leader Sergo Ordzhonikidze; the historic name Vladikavkaz was restored in 1990. The Ossetian name Dzaudzhikau ("settlement of Dzauag") preserves the memory of the original village.

Geography and economy

Vladikavkaz lies at about 700 metres elevation on both banks of the Terek, where the river emerges from the mountains onto the plain. Its economy includes non-ferrous metallurgy, machinery, food processing and electrical engineering. The city is home to North Ossetian State University and several theatres and museums; its nineteenth-century centre retains notable architecture, including the Sunni Mukhtarov Mosque on the river bank.
