The Montreal Neurological Institute (MNI), known as The Neuro, is a research and medical centre in Montreal, Quebec, Canada, dedicated to neuroscience. Founded in 1934 by neurosurgeon Wilder Penfield with funding from the Rockefeller Foundation and the government of Quebec, it is part of McGill University and, together with the Montreal Neurological Hospital, forms one of the largest neuroscience centres in the world.

History

Penfield came to Montreal in 1928 and envisioned an institute where neurology, neurosurgery, neuropsychology, and basic brain research would be integrated under one roof. The institute opened in 1934 at 3801 University Street, on the slope of Mount Royal. A famous inscription at the institute quotes Penfield's aim to alleviate suffering caused by diseases of the nervous system.

The Neuro became known for the "Montreal procedure" for the surgical treatment of epilepsy, in which Penfield mapped the functions of the cortex by stimulating the exposed brain of awake patients. These studies produced the famous cortical homunculus diagrams and landmark findings by collaborators including Brenda Milner, whose studies of patient H.M. and other cases transformed the science of memory.

Research

The Neuro conducts basic and clinical research in epilepsy, brain tumours, neurodegenerative diseases such as Parkinson's and Alzheimer's, multiple sclerosis, stroke, pain, neuromuscular disorders, and brain imaging. Its facilities include the McConnell Brain Imaging Centre, operating from the late 1980s and among the pioneers of PET and MRI brain research.

In 2016, the Neuro became the first academic institution in the world to fully adopt open science principles, pledging to share data, resources, and findings without restriction and to forgo patenting its discoveries.
