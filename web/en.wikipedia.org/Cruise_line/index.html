Cruise line

A cruise line is a company that operates cruise ships, carrying passengers on voyages for pleasure in which the journey itself and the ship's amenities are part of the experience, typically visiting a series of ports on a round-trip itinerary.

Industry

The modern industry is dominated by a few large groups — Carnival Corporation, Royal Caribbean Group and Norwegian Cruise Line Holdings — which together control most of the global fleet. The largest ships carry more than 6,000 passengers and feature multiple restaurants, theatres, pools and other resort-style facilities.

History

Pleasure cruising developed from transatlantic liner services in the 19th and early 20th centuries. As jet aircraft took over long-distance travel in the 1960s, companies reinvented ocean liners as cruise ships sailing the Caribbean, Mediterranean and other leisure routes, an industry that grew steadily until the COVID-19 shutdown of 2020 and subsequent recovery.

Criticism

Cruise lines face criticism over emissions and waste, port impacts, labour conditions and flags of convenience registration.
