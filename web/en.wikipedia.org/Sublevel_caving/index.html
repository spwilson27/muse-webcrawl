Sublevel caving

Sublevel caving is an underground mining method used for large, steeply dipping orebodies of moderate to low strength. Ore is drilled and blasted from sublevels at regular vertical intervals, and the overlying waste rock is allowed to cave in and fill the void as ore is drawn from below.

Sublevel caving
Type: Underground mining
Suitable for: Steep, large orebodies
Examples: Kiruna mine, Sweden

Method

Horizontal drifts are driven into the orebody on each sublevel, typically 10–30 metres apart vertically. Fan-shaped rings of blastholes are drilled upward, blasted in retreat, and the broken ore is mucked from drawpoints while caved waste follows it down. Because ore and waste mix at the drawpoints, dilution is relatively high and some ore is lost.

Advantages and disadvantages

The method is highly mechanized, safe, and productive, and it does not require strong ore. Its drawbacks include surface subsidence above the mine, high development costs, and significant dilution.

Notable mines

Sweden's Kiruna iron ore mine, one of the largest underground mines in the world, is the classic example of large-scale sublevel caving.
