Collegiate wrestling

Collegiate wrestling, also known as folkstyle wrestling, is the form of amateur wrestling practised by men and women at colleges and universities in the United States, and in modified form at the high school and middle school levels. It differs from the international styles of freestyle and Greco-Roman wrestling in its emphasis on control of the opponent.

Rules

A collegiate match consists of three periods totalling seven minutes in college competition. Points are awarded for takedowns, escapes, reversals, near falls (exposing the opponent's back), and riding time — a distinctive folkstyle feature awarding a point to the wrestler who accumulates at least one minute more control time than the opponent. A fall (pin) ends the match immediately. Team scoring rewards bonus points for falls, technical falls, and major decisions.

Competition

The NCAA Wrestling Championships, first held in 1928, are the premier collegiate tournament. Programmes such as Iowa, Oklahoma State, Penn State, and Iowa State have dominated historically; under coach Cael Sanderson, Penn State won a long series of team titles in the 2010s and 2020s. Women's collegiate wrestling has grown rapidly and became an NCAA championship sport in the mid-2020s.

Relation to other styles

Many collegiate wrestlers go on to compete internationally in freestyle wrestling, and folkstyle skills such as mat wrestling and control translate well to mixed martial arts, where wrestling is one of the foundational disciplines.
