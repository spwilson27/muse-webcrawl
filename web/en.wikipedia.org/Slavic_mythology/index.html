Slavic mythology

Slavic mythology or Slavic religion is the body of myths, rituals and beliefs of the pre-Christian Slavic peoples of Eastern and Central Europe. Because the Slavs had no writing system for recording their religion before Christianisation (roughly the 9th–12th centuries), the mythology must be reconstructed from chronicles, sermons, archaeology, folklore and comparative linguistics.

Slavic mythology

Region: Eastern and Central Europe
Principal deities: Perun, Veles, Mokosh, Svarog
Suppressed: Christianisation, 9th–12th c.

Deities

The thunder god Perun stood at the head of the Kievan pantheon installed by Prince Vladimir in 980, alongside the earth-and-water god Veles, the goddess Mokosh, Dazhbog the sun god, Stribog of the winds and Simargl. Western Slavs worshipped gods such as Svetovit at Arkona and Triglav. Scholars debate how far a unified Slavic pantheon existed versus local cults.

Ritual and survival

Cult practice involved hilltop sanctuaries, sacred groves, idols, sacrifices and seasonal festivals tied to the agricultural calendar. After Christianisation, pagan customs survived for centuries in folk practice — a phenomenon Russian scholarship calls dvoeverie, "dual faith" — and figures such as the domovoi, leshy and rusalka persist in Slavic folklore.
