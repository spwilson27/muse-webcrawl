Pseudoinverse

In mathematics, and in particular linear algebra, a pseudoinverse A+ of a matrix A is a generalization of the inverse matrix. The most widely known type is the Moore–Penrose inverse, described independently by E. H. Moore in 1920, Arne Bjerhammar in 1951, and Roger Penrose in 1955. Pseudoinverses exist for any matrix, including singular and non-square matrices for which no ordinary inverse exists.

Definition

The Moore–Penrose inverse of an m × n matrix A is the unique n × m matrix A+ satisfying the four Penrose conditions: AA+A = A; A+AA+ = A+; (AA+)* = AA+; and (A+A)* = A+A, where * denotes the conjugate transpose.

Computation

A practical method of computing the pseudoinverse uses the singular value decomposition (SVD). If A = UΣV*, then A+ = VΣ+U*, where Σ+ is obtained by taking the reciprocal of each nonzero diagonal entry and transposing the matrix. For a full-column-rank matrix, the simpler formula A+ = (A*A)−1A* applies.

Applications

The best-known application is the solution of systems of linear equations by least squares: for an overdetermined system Ax = b, the vector x = A+b minimizes ‖Ax − b‖, and among all minimizers it has the smallest norm. Pseudoinverses are therefore central to linear regression, signal processing, control theory, and statistical estimation, and appear in the theory of the Kalman filter and in solving ill-posed inverse problems.
