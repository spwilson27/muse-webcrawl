Triton

[Image: The Greek sea god Triton blowing his conch shell]

Triton (Greek: Trītŏn) is a Greek god of the sea, the son of Poseidon and Amphitrite. He is usually represented as a merman, with the upper body of a human and the tailed lower body of a fish, and he carries a twisted conch shell, on which he blows like a trumpet to calm or raise the waves. According to Hesiod's Theogony, Triton dwelt with his parents in a golden palace in the depths of the sea.

Mythology

Triton appears in the story of Jason and the Argonauts, where he (or a Libyan local form of him) guided the Argo out of the shallows of Lake Tritonis. Virgil's Aeneid has Triton kill the Trojan trumpeter Misenus, who had challenged the gods to play as well. Ovid's Metamorphoses depicts Triton, at Poseidon's command, blowing his shell to signal the receding of the waters after the great flood of Deucalion. The plural "Tritons" came to denote a whole class of merman attendants of the sea gods.

Namesakes

The figure's name is shared by several other things, most prominently Triton, the largest moon of Neptune, named for the god as a son of Poseidon (the Roman Neptune), and the triton, a large predatory sea snail whose shell resembles Triton's trumpet. Tritons became common figures in Renaissance and Baroque fountain sculpture, such as Bernini's Triton Fountain in Rome.
