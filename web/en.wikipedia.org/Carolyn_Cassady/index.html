Carolyn Cassady (1923–2013)

Carolyn Cassady (28 April 1923 – 20 September 2013) was an American writer and artist associated with the Beat Generation. She is best known as the wife of Neal Cassady, the muse and companion of Jack Kerouac, and for her memoir Off the Road: My Years with Cassady, Kerouac, and Ginsberg (1990), a firsthand account of the early Beat circle.

Early life

Carolyn Elizabeth Robinson was born in Lansing, Michigan, and grew up in Nashville, Tennessee, in a well-to-do family. She studied art at Bennington College in Vermont — receiving her degree in 1944 — and moved to Denver to pursue graduate study in fine arts and art education at the University of Denver. In Denver in 1947 she met Neal Cassady, a charismatic young drifter who introduced her to Kerouac and Allen Ginsberg.

Life with Cassady and Kerouac

Carolyn married Neal Cassady in 1948 — shortly after his first marriage was annulled — and the couple settled in San Francisco and later Los Gatos, California, raising three children. Their household was a center of the early Beat scene: Kerouac lived with them for a period in 1951–52 and had a brief affair with Carolyn, an episode later dramatized in his fiction. She appears in Kerouac's novels under various pseudonyms, most fully as Camille in On the Road sequels and as Evelyn in Desolation Angels.

While Neal was repeatedly absent — traveling, working on the railroad, and later imprisoned at San Quentin for a marijuana offense — Carolyn supported the family and pursued portrait painting. The marriage ended in divorce in 1963; Neal died in Mexico in 1968.

Later life and writing

In 1983 Carolyn moved to England, settling in Bracknell, Berkshire. She published first a small memoir, Heart Beat: My Life with Jack and Neal (1976, with Jack Kerouac's letters), and then her full autobiography Off the Road (1990), widely regarded as one of the most valuable corrective accounts of the Beat myth, written from the perspective of the woman who maintained the household around which the movement revolved. She appeared in documentaries about the Beats and lectured widely. Cassady died in Bracknell in September 2013, aged 90.
