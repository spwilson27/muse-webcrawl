Crater Lake

Crater Lake
Location: Oregon, United States
Max. depth: 1,949 ft (594 m)
Surface area: 21 sq mi (54 km²)
Origin: Caldera of Mount Mazama

Crater Lake is a caldera lake in south-central Oregon, United States, and the centerpiece of Crater Lake National Park. It is the deepest lake in the United States, at 1,949 feet (594 m), and is famed for its strikingly blue, exceptionally clear water.

Formation

The lake occupies the caldera formed about 7,700 years ago when Mount Mazama, a stratovolcano in the Cascade Range, erupted violently and collapsed. With no inlets or outlets, the lake is filled entirely by snowmelt and precipitation, which accounts for its clarity. Wizard Island, a cinder cone, rises from the western shore.

Features

The "Old Man of the Lake" is a full-sized tree trunk that has bobbed vertically in the lake for more than a century. Crater Lake National Park was established in 1902, the fifth national park in the United States. Rim Drive circles the caldera, offering viewpoints around the 33-mile rim.
