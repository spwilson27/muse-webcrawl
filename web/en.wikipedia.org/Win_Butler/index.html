Edwin Farnham "Win" Butler III (born 14 April 1980) is an American-Canadian singer, songwriter, and multi-instrumentalist, best known as the lead vocalist and co-founder of the indie rock band Arcade Fire.

Life and career

Born in Truckee, California, and raised in The Woodlands, Texas, Butler studied at Phillips Exeter Academy and began university at Sarah Lawrence College before moving to Montreal, where he studied religious studies at McGill University. There he formed Arcade Fire with Régine Chassagne, whom he married in 2003, and Josh Deu. The band's debut album, Funeral (2004), won wide acclaim, and The Suburbs (2010) won the Grammy Award for Album of the Year. Subsequent albums include Reflektor (2013), Everything Now (2017), and WE (2022).

Butler is known for his baritone voice, anthemic songwriting, and energetic live performances. He is the grandson of swing guitarist Alvino Rey. In 2022, media reported allegations of sexual misconduct against Butler, which he acknowledged involved consensual relationships while denying misconduct.
