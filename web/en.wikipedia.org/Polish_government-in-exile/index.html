Polish government-in-exile

The Polish government-in-exile, formally known as the Government of the Republic of Poland in exile (Rząd Rzeczypospolitej Polskiej na uchodźstwie), was the government of Poland in exile formed in the aftermath of the Invasion of Poland of September 1939, and the subsequent occupation of Poland by Germany, the Soviet Union, and the Slovak Republic, which brought to an end the Second Polish Republic.

Despite the occupation of Poland by hostile powers, the government-in-exile exerted considerable influence in Poland through the structures of the Polish Underground State and its military arm, the Armia Krajowa (Home Army). Foreign-recognised until 5 July 1945, when the Western Allies withdrew diplomatic recognition in favour of the Provisional Government of National Unity, the government-in-exile continued in existence until 1990, when the last exile president, Ryszard Kaczorowski, handed the presidential insignia to Lech Wałęsa.

History

On 17 September 1939, the President of the Polish Republic, Ignacy Mościcki, interned in Romania, issued a decree appointing Władysław Raczkiewicz as his successor. Raczkiewicz formed a government in Paris under General Władysław Sikorski as Prime Minister. After the fall of France in 1940 the government moved to London, where it remained for the rest of the war.

The government concluded the Sikorski–Mayski Agreement with the Soviet Union in July 1941 following the German invasion of the USSR, but relations broke down in April 1943 after the discovery of the Katyn massacre. Sikorski died in the Gibraltar air crash in July 1943 and was succeeded by Stanisław Mikołajczyk. After the war the government's recognition was withdrawn by the Western powers, though it continued to function with diminishing influence from London, guarded by the support of the Polish diaspora.

Presidents in exile

Presidents of the Republic in exile included Władysław Raczkiewicz (1939–1947), August Zaleski (1947–1972), Stanisław Ostrowski (1972–1979), Edward Raczyński (1979–1986), Kazimierz Sabbat (1986–1989) and Ryszard Kaczorowski (1989–1990).
