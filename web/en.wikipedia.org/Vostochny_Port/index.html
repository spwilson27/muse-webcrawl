Vostochny Port


Location: Wrangel Bay, near Nakhodka, Primorsky Krai, Russia
Type: Seaport
Opened: 1970s
Main cargo: Coal, containers

Vostochny Port (Russian: Восточный порт, literally "Eastern Port") is a major ice-free deep-water seaport in the Russian Far East, located in Wrangel Bay near the city of Nakhodka in Primorsky Krai, on the coast of the Sea of Japan. Together with Nakhodka's own port it forms one of Russia's largest port complexes on the Pacific.

History

Construction began in the early 1970s as a Soviet project to expand trade with Japan and the wider Asia-Pacific region, taking advantage of the bay's depth and relative shelter. The port is connected to the interior by rail, including links to the Trans-Siberian Railway, making it a natural outlet for Siberian resources.

Operations

The port is best known as Russia's largest coal-export terminal, operated by JSC Vostochny Port, with highly mechanised coal-handling facilities including car dumpers, stacker-reclaimers and enclosed conveyor systems shipped out to capesize and panamax bulk carriers. A major expansion phase, including a third stage of the coal complex, substantially raised annual throughput capacity in the late 2010s. The port area also hosts container facilities, including the Vostochnaya Stevedoring Company terminal, and handles fertilisers, grain and general cargo.

Geography

Wrangel Bay lies east of Nakhodka Bay. Waters around the port are seasonally affected by ice but remain navigable year-round with icebreaker assistance in severe winters, and the deep approaches allow the port to receive some of the largest bulk carriers afloat.
