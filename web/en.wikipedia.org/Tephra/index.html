Tephra

Tephra is fragmental material produced by a volcanic eruption regardless of composition, fragment size or emplacement mechanism. Geologists group the loose deposits as pyroclastic deposits; once compacted and cemented, the rock is called tuff.

Uses

Distinctive tephra layers act as marker horizons in stratigraphy and archaeology: because an eruption deposits its ash over a wide area almost instantaneously, a chemically fingerprinted tephra layer can date and correlate sediments across regions, a method called tephrochronology.
