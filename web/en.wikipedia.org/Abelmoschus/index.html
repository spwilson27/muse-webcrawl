Abelmoschus

Kingdom: Plantae

Family: Malvaceae

Genus: Abelmoschus Medik.

Notable species: Okra (A. esculentus)

Abelmoschus is a genus of about fifteen species of flowering plants in the mallow family, Malvaceae, native to tropical Africa, Asia and northern Australia. The genus was formerly included in Hibiscus, and its name derives from the Arabic abū al-misk, "father of musk," referring to the musk-scented seeds of some species.

Description

Species are annual or perennial herbs growing to about two metres tall, with lobed leaves, large solitary five-petalled flowers in shades of white to yellow (often with a dark centre), and a capsular fruit containing many seeds. Several species produce mucilaginous tissues valued in cooking and traditional medicine.

Species and uses

The best-known species is okra (Abelmoschus esculentus), grown worldwide as a vegetable for its edible green pods. Abelmoschus moschatus (musk mallow) yields ambrette seeds used in perfumery, while Abelmoschus manihot (aibika) is eaten as a leaf vegetable in the Pacific and is also used in papermaking in East Asia. Other recognised species include A. crinitus, A. ficulneus, A. hostilis and A. manipurensis.
