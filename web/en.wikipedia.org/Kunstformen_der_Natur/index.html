Kunstformen der Natur

Kunstformen der Natur (German for "Art Forms in Nature") is a book of lithographic and autotype prints by the German naturalist Ernst Haeckel, published in instalments between 1899 and 1904. It contains 100 plates depicting organisms, many of them marine, rendered with extraordinary detail and symmetry.

Contents

The plates feature radiolarians, jellyfish, cephalopods, bats, orchids, and many other groups, arranged in geometrically ordered compositions. Haeckel coined or popularised terms such as "ecology" and drew the plates to support his evolutionary views and his aesthetic philosophy of nature.

Influence

The book strongly influenced Art Nouveau design and architecture — including René Binet's designs and the Amsterdam Stock Exchange — and remains a landmark in scientific illustration.
