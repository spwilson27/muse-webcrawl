Circumcenter

The circumcenter of a triangle is the point where the perpendicular bisectors of the triangle's sides meet. It is the center of the circumcircle, the unique circle passing through all three vertices of the triangle, and it is equidistant from the three vertices.

Position

For an acute triangle the circumcenter lies inside the triangle; for a right triangle it lies at the midpoint of the hypotenuse; for an obtuse triangle it lies outside the triangle. The circumcenter is one of the classical triangle centers and lies on the Euler line together with the centroid and orthocenter.

Properties

The distance from the circumcenter to each vertex is the circumradius R, related to the side lengths a, b, c and area Δ by R = abc / (4Δ). In trilinear coordinates the circumcenter is cos A : cos B : cos C.
