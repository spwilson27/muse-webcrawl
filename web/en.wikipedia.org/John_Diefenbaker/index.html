John Diefenbaker

[Image placeholder: portrait of John Diefenbaker]

John George Diefenbaker PC CH QC FRSC FRSA (September 18, 1895 – August 16, 1979) was a Canadian politician who served as the 13th prime minister of Canada, from 1957 to 1963. He was the only Progressive Conservative leader between 1930 and 1979 to guide the party to victory in a federal election, and he did so twice, winning a minority in 1957 and a landslide majority in 1958.

Born in Neustadt, Ontario, and raised in Saskatchewan, Diefenbaker became a celebrated criminal defence lawyer in Prince Albert before entering Parliament in 1940. A spellbinding orator, he won the Progressive Conservative leadership in 1956 at the age of 61.

Prime minister

Diefenbaker's government introduced the Canadian Bill of Rights (1960), extended the vote to First Nations peoples without requiring them to give up treaty status, appointed the first female cabinet minister (Ellen Fairclough) and first Indigenous senator (James Gladstone), and promoted his "Northern Vision" of Arctic and resource development. His tenure was also marked by the 1959 cancellation of the Avro Arrow interceptor — a decision that remains controversial — and by friction with the Kennedy administration over nuclear warheads for Canada's Bomarc missiles, which helped bring down his government in 1963.

Later life

Defeated by Lester B. Pearson's Liberals, Diefenbaker remained an MP and an elder statesman until his death in 1979, fiercely defending his record and feuding with successors. His grave lies beside the Diefenbaker Canada Centre at the University of Saskatchewan in Saskatoon.
