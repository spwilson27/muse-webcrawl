Jean Alesi (born Giovanni Roberto Alesi; 11 June 1964) is a French professional racing driver. Of Italian (Sicilian) heritage, he competed in Formula One between 1989 and 2001, racing for Tyrrell, Ferrari, Benetton, Sauber, Prost and Jordan. He won a single Formula One race, the 1995 Canadian Grand Prix, and was renowned for his passionate, emotive driving style and his loyalty among Ferrari's tifosi.

Early life and junior career

Alesi was born in Avignon, Vaucluse, to Sicilian immigrant parents. He began his career in karting before moving into French Formula Renault and French Formula Three, winning the French F3 championship in 1987. In 1989 he won the International Formula 3000 championship with the Eddie Jordan Racing team, a campaign that included a famous victory at Pau.

Formula One career

Tyrrell (1989–1990)

Alesi made his Formula One debut with Tyrrell at the 1989 French Grand Prix, finishing fourth. He announced himself to the world at the 1990 United States Grand Prix in Phoenix, where he led and duelled memorably with Ayrton Senna's McLaren, finishing second. A further second place at Monaco cemented his reputation as a star of the future.

Ferrari (1991–1995)

Despite interest from Williams, Alesi signed for Ferrari, the team his Italian roots made an emotional choice. The Ferraris of the early 1990s were uncompetitive and unreliable, yet Alesi became a beloved figure for the tifosi. His long-awaited first victory came at the 1995 Canadian Grand Prix on his 31st birthday, driving the number 27 car made famous by Gilles Villeneuve. The win was greeted by a track invasion.

Benetton, Sauber, Prost and Jordan (1996–2001)

Alesi moved to Benetton for 1996, scoring regular podiums but no further wins. He later raced for Sauber (1998–1999), Prost (2000–2001) and Jordan, ending his Formula One career at the 2001 Japanese Grand Prix with 201 starts, 1 win, 2 pole positions and 32 podiums.

Later career

After Formula One, Alesi raced in the DTM for Mercedes, winning several races, and contested the Indianapolis 500 in 2012. He has worked as a television pundit and as an ambassador for the sport. His son Giuliano Alesi also became a racing driver.
