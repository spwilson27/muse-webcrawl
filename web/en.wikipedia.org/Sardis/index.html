Sardis

Sardis was an ancient city in western Anatolia, in what is now the province of Manisa in Turkey. It was the capital of the kingdom of Lydia and one of the great cities of the ancient world.

History

Sardis reached its height under the Lydian kings of the Mermnad dynasty. Its king Croesus (reigned c. 560–546 BC) was famed for his wealth, and Lydia is credited with inventing coinage, minted from electrum found in the nearby Pactolus River. Cyrus the Great of Persia captured Sardis in 546 BC. The city later passed to Alexander the Great, the Seleucids, Pergamon, and Rome. Under the Romans it remained prosperous and was one of the Seven Churches of Asia addressed in the Book of Revelation.

Archaeology

Excavations, led since 1958 by Harvard and Cornell universities, have uncovered the great Temple of Artemis, a large Roman gymnasium and bath complex, a synagogue among the largest of the ancient world, Lydian fortifications, and the royal necropolis at Bin Tepe, including the great tumulus associated with King Alyattes.
