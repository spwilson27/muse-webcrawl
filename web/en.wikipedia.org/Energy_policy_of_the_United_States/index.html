Energy policy of the United States

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The energy policy of the United States is determined by federal, state, and local entities, addressing issues of energy production, distribution, and consumption, such as building codes and gas mileage standards. The Department of Energy, created in 1977 in the wake of the 1970s energy crisis, is the principal federal agency; other actors include the Environmental Protection Agency, the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission, and state public utility commissions.

History

Federal involvement grew from early regulation of hydroelectric sites and interstate pipelines to the Manhattan Project and the Atomic Energy Act of 1946. The oil shocks of 1973 and 1979 prompted the creation of the Strategic Petroleum Reserve, fuel-economy (CAFE) standards, and the Energy Policy and Conservation Act of 1975. Later landmarks include the Energy Policy Acts of 1992 and 2005, the Energy Independence and Security Act of 2007, and the Inflation Reduction Act of 2022, whose tax credits for clean electricity, electric vehicles, and domestic manufacturing constitute the largest US climate investment to date.

Production and consumption

The United States is the world's largest producer of oil and natural gas, following the shale revolution of the 2010s, and since 2019 has been a net exporter of petroleum products. Its electricity mix combines natural gas, coal (in long decline), nuclear power, and rapidly growing wind and solar generation. The US is the second-largest energy consumer and second-largest emitter of carbon dioxide, after China, though emissions have fallen from their mid-2000s peak.

Debates

Policy debate centres on energy security and prices versus climate change, the pace of the transition to renewables, federal leasing of public lands, nuclear power, grid modernisation, and the distributional effects of energy costs. Policy has oscillated markedly between administrations, particularly over climate regulation and international agreements such as the Paris Agreement.
