Jewish history

Jewish history is the history of the Jews, of their religion and culture, as it developed and interacted with other peoples and religions over more than three millennia. It begins with the ancient Israelites of the Levant and continues through exile and diaspora, the rabbinic tradition, medieval communities in Europe and the Islamic world, emancipation, the Holocaust, and the establishment of the State of Israel in 1948.

Ancient period

According to the Hebrew Bible, the patriarch Abraham led his family to Canaan, and his descendants, enslaved in Egypt, were freed under Moses and received the Torah at Sinai. Archaeology places the emergence of Israel in the highlands of Canaan in the late second millennium BCE. The kingdoms of Israel and Judah were destroyed by Assyria (722 BCE) and Babylon (586 BCE) respectively; the Babylonian exile was followed, under Persian rule, by a return and the rebuilding of the Temple in Jerusalem.

Second Temple and diaspora

Under Hellenistic and then Roman rule, Jewish life centred on the Second Temple until its destruction by the Romans in 70 CE after the First Jewish–Roman War; the failed Bar Kokhba revolt (132–136 CE) deepened the dispersion. Rabbinic Judaism, codified in the Mishnah and Talmud, became the sustaining framework of Jewish life in the diaspora.

Middle Ages to modernity

Medieval Jewish communities flourished in Islamic Spain and across Europe, producing figures such as Maimonides, but also suffered expulsions, blood libels and massacres. The Haskalah (Jewish Enlightenment) and political emancipation in the 18th and 19th centuries opened paths to integration, while Zionism arose in response to persistent antisemitism. In the Holocaust (1941–1945), Nazi Germany and its collaborators murdered approximately six million Jews. The State of Israel was proclaimed in 1948; today the largest Jewish communities are in Israel and the United States.
