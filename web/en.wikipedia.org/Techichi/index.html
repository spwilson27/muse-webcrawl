Techichi

The Techichi is an extinct breed of small dog kept by the Toltec peoples of Mesoamerica, in what is now Mexico. It is widely regarded as the principal ancestor of the modern Chihuahua, the smallest recognised dog breed.

History

Small dogs resembling the Techichi appear in Toltec carvings and pottery dating from around the 9th century CE, and earlier Colima dog effigies from western Mexico depict similar animals. The Techichi was larger and heavier than the modern Chihuahua, with a longer coat. The dogs were kept as companions and also had religious significance: in Mesoamerican belief, a dog could guide its owner's soul through the afterlife, and Techichis were sometimes sacrificed and buried with the dead.

Descendants

After the Aztec conquest of the Toltecs, the breed persisted, and after the Spanish conquest surviving dogs in the region of present-day Chihuahua state — possibly crossed with small hairless dogs brought from Asia — gave rise to the modern Chihuahua, recognised internationally in the early 20th century.
