State dinner

A state dinner is a formal dinner party hosted by a head of state in their official residence to honor a visiting head of government or head of state, typically as the centerpiece of a state visit. Such dinners are among the most prestigious events in diplomatic protocol and are used to demonstrate goodwill, celebrate alliances, and showcase national culture.

United States

In the United States, state dinners are held in the State Dining Room of the White House and are hosted by the President. The first was held by President Ulysses S. Grant in 1874 for King Kalākaua of Hawaii. Planning is led by the White House Social Secretary together with the Chief Usher, the Executive Chef, and the State Department's Office of Protocol. Menus typically feature American cuisine and wines; entertainment by prominent performers follows the meal.

United Kingdom

In the United Kingdom, the state banquet is usually held in the ballroom of Buckingham Palace, where some 170 guests sit at a horseshoe-shaped table. The Royal Household spends days polishing silver-gilt tableware from the Grand Service, and the monarch proposes a toast to the visiting guest of honour, who replies.

Protocol

Seating plans, dress codes (usually white tie or black tie), national anthems, and the exchange of toasts follow strict conventions. Gifts are exchanged between delegations, and menus avoid ingredients that offend religious observances or the guest's known preferences.
