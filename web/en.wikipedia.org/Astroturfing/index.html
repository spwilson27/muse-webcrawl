Astroturfing


Astroturfing masks sponsored campaigns as spontaneous grassroots movements.

Astroturfing is the practice of disguising a sponsored, organised campaign — by a corporation, political group or government — as a spontaneous grassroots movement. The term derives from AstroTurf, a brand of artificial grass: an astroturfed movement is a fake "grassroots" one.

Techniques

Astroturfing methods include forming front groups with civic-sounding names, paying people to write letters, reviews or comments, creating fake blogs ("flogs"), and deploying sockpuppet accounts and bot networks on social media to simulate broad public support or opposition. The aim is to influence public opinion, journalists or legislators by manufacturing the impression of a popular consensus.

Regulation and criticism

Consumer-protection law in several jurisdictions prohibits undisclosed paid endorsements, and electoral law may restrict covert campaign activity. Critics argue astroturfing corrodes democratic debate and trust in genuine civic movements; its detection has become a focus of investigative journalism and of research into coordinated inauthentic behaviour online.
