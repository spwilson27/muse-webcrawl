Heart block

Other namesAtrioventricular block (AV block)
SpecialtyCardiology
SymptomsLightheadedness, fainting, shortness of breath, or none
CausesNormal ageing, heart attack, medications, congenital conditions
TreatmentObservation, medication changes, pacemaker

Heart block, also known as atrioventricular block (AV block), is a disorder in the conduction of electrical impulses through the atrioventricular (AV) node of the heart. In this condition the electrical signals generated by the sinoatrial node are delayed or fail to reach the ventricles, disrupting the normal coordinated heartbeat.

Types

Heart block is classified by degree. In first-degree heart block, every impulse is conducted but more slowly than normal, producing a prolonged PR interval on the electrocardiogram. In second-degree heart block, some impulses fail to reach the ventricles; it is subdivided into Mobitz type I (Wenckebach), in which the PR interval lengthens until a beat is dropped, and Mobitz type II, in which beats are dropped without prior lengthening. In third-degree (complete) heart block, no impulses pass from the atria to the ventricles, and the atria and ventricles beat independently under separate pacemakers.

Causes

Causes include fibrosis and sclerosis of the conduction system associated with ageing, ischaemic heart disease and myocardial infarction, myocarditis, congenital heart defects, and increased vagal tone. Certain medications, including beta blockers, calcium channel blockers and digoxin, can also slow AV conduction.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is made by electrocardiography, which reveals the relationship between atrial depolarisations (P waves) and ventricular depolarisations (QRS complexes). Asymptomatic first-degree block usually requires no treatment. Higher degrees of block, especially complete heart block causing syncope or haemodynamic instability, are generally treated with an artificial pacemaker.
