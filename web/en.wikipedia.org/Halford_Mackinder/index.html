Halford Mackinder

Sir Halford John Mackinder (15 February 1861 – 6 March 1947) was an English geographer, academic and politician, regarded as one of the founding fathers of both geopolitics and geostrategy. He is best known for the Heartland theory, presented in his 1904 paper "The Geographical Pivot of History" and developed in Democratic Ideals and Reality (1919).

Heartland theory

Mackinder argued that the age of sea power was giving way to land power made mobile by railways. The interior of Eurasia — the "pivot area", later renamed the Heartland — was inaccessible to sea power and rich in resources; a state controlling it could dominate the "World-Island" (Eurasia and Africa) and ultimately the world. His 1919 formulation became famous: "Who rules East Europe commands the Heartland; who rules the Heartland commands the World-Island; who rules the World-Island commands the world."

Career

Mackinder helped establish geography as an academic discipline in Britain, teaching at the University of Oxford, where he became Reader in Geography, and serving as director of the London School of Economics from 1903 to 1908. His 1887 manifesto "On the Scope and Methods of Geography" is a foundational text of the modern subject. He was the first person recorded to have climbed Mount Kenya (1899). Mackinder sat as a Unionist Member of Parliament for Glasgow Camlachie from 1910 to 1922 and served briefly as British High Commissioner to South Russia in 1919–1920.

Legacy

Mackinder's ideas influenced interwar German Geopolitik and Cold War containment strategy, and continue to be debated in international relations, though critics regard the theory as environmentally deterministic and overtaken by air power, nuclear weapons and economic interdependence.
