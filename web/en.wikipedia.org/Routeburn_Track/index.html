The Routeburn Track is a 32-kilometre (20 mi) tramping track in the South Island of New Zealand, linking Mount Aspiring National Park with Fiordland National Park. It is one of New Zealand's ten Great Walks, administered by the Department of Conservation, and is renowned for its alpine scenery, beech forest, waterfalls and panoramic views.

Route

The track is usually walked in two to three days. From the Routeburn Shelter near Glenorchy at the head of Lake Wakatipu, the track follows the Routeburn River through beech forest to Routeburn Flats Hut, then climbs to Routeburn Falls Hut. Beyond the falls it enters alpine country, traversing beneath the Humboldt Mountains to the Harris Saddle (1,255 m), the highest point on the track, with a side trip to Conical Hill for views over the Hollyford Valley to Lake McKerrow and Martins Bay.

From the saddle the track descends past Lake Harris and along the Hollyford face to Lake Mackenzie Hut, set beside its namesake lake. The final section passes the 174-metre Earland Falls, crosses swing bridges and reaches The Divide on the Milford Road (State Highway 94), the western trailhead.

History and management

The route follows an old Māori greenstone (pounamu) trail between the Dart Valley and the coast. Guided walking began in the late nineteenth century as tourism to Milford Sound developed. During the Great Walk season (late October to late April) bookings are required for the huts, and independent walkers must carry a hut pass; outside the season facilities are reduced and alpine hazards increase. A separate concession operates guided walks using private lodges.

The track is notable for the diversity of landscapes compressed into a short distance, and for birdlife including the kea, kākā, mohua (yellowhead) and the tiny rifleman.
