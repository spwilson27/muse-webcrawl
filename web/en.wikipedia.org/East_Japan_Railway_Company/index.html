East Japan Railway Company

Native name: 東日本旅客鉄道株式会社
Founded: 1 April 1987
Headquarters: Shibuya, Tokyo, Japan
Services: Passenger railways, real estate, retail
Known as: JR East

The East Japan Railway Company, commonly known as JR East (Japanese: JR東日本), is a major passenger railway company in Japan and the largest of the seven Japan Railways Group companies. It operates intercity, regional, and commuter rail services in the Kantō and Tōhoku regions, including the Tokyo metropolitan network and several Shinkansen lines.

History

JR East was created on 1 April 1987 when the government-owned Japanese National Railways was broken up and privatised. The company was fully privatised in 2002 when the government's remaining shares were sold. It is listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange.

Operations

The company operates the Tōhoku, Jōetsu, Hokuriku (in part), Yamagata, and Akita Shinkansen lines, as well as dense commuter lines around Tokyo such as the Yamanote, Chūō, and Saikyō lines. Tokyo Station and other major terminals anchor large "station city" developments of offices, hotels, and retail operated by the group. JR East issues the Suica smart card, introduced in 2001, which became a widely used electronic money platform. Average daily ridership is among the highest of any railway company in the world.
