Segedunum

Segedunum was a Roman fort at what is now Wallsend, North Tyneside, in north-east England. It marked the eastern terminus of Hadrian's Wall and guarded the eastern end of the frontier where it met the River Tyne.

History

The fort was built around AD 127, shortly after work began on the Wall. It covered about 1.7 hectares (4.2 acres) and housed a mixed unit of infantry and cavalry, at various times including the Second Cohort of Nervians and the Fourth Cohort of Lingones. The fort was occupied for roughly 300 years, until the end of Roman Britain.

Museum

After centuries buried beneath terraced housing, the site was excavated and opened as the Segedunum Roman Fort, Baths and Museum. A reconstructed bath house and a viewing tower overlook the excavated remains, and a section of Hadrian's Wall survives nearby at Wallsend.
