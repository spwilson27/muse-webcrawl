Valentino Rossi (born 16 February 1979) is an Italian former professional motorcycle road racer and nine-time Grand Prix World Champion. Widely considered one of the greatest motorcycle racers of all time, he competed in MotoGP from 2000 until his retirement at the end of 2021.

Early career

The son of 250cc rider Graziano Rossi, he began in karting before switching to motorcycles, winning the 125cc world title with Aprilia in 1997 and the 250cc title in 1999. He moved to the premier 500cc class with Honda in 2000, winning the last 500cc championship in 2001 and the first two MotoGP titles in 2002 and 2003.

Yamaha years and rivalries

In a shock move, Rossi joined the unfancied Yamaha team for 2004 and won the title at the first attempt, repeating in 2005, 2008 and 2009. His career featured celebrated rivalries with Max Biaggi, Sete Gibernau, Casey Stoner, Jorge Lorenzo and Marc Márquez. A difficult two seasons at Ducati (2011–2012) brought no championships, and he returned to Yamaha in 2013, remaining competitive into his forties.

Legacy

Rossi's flamboyant celebrations, yellow number 46 and VR46 academy and team made him the sport's most popular figure; attendances and television audiences were credited with growing globally during his career. After retiring from MotoGP he moved into GT car racing and team ownership.
