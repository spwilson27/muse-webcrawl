Longeron

In aircraft and rocket construction, a longeron is a principal longitudinal structural member of the airframe. In a typical fuselage, longerons run the length of the structure and, together with transverse frames and lighter longitudinal stringers, form the skeleton over which the skin is attached.

Function

Longerons carry the major bending loads of the fuselage and transfer loads between frames. In older truss-type fuselages, four longerons braced by struts and wires formed the primary structure. In modern semi-monocoque construction, longerons are fewer and heavier while numerous stringers share the load with the stressed skin.

Related structures

The corresponding member in a wing is the spar. The term is also used in rocketry for the longitudinal beams joining stages or supporting tanks, and in boatbuilding for longitudinal hull stiffeners.
