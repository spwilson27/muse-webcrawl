Misrata (Arabic: مصراتة‎) is a city in the Misrata District in northwestern Libya, situated 187 km (116 mi) east of Tripoli on the Mediterranean coast near Cape Misrata. With a population of roughly 280,000, it is the third-largest city in Libya after Tripoli and Benghazi.

Misrata's economy is based on its deep-water port, the nearby steel complex at Qasr Ahmad, light manufacturing, and commerce. The city played a prominent role in the Libyan Civil War of 2011, when it endured a prolonged siege by forces loyal to Muammar Gaddafi and became a symbol of resistance.

History

The area around Misrata was settled in antiquity; the Roman-era settlement of Cephalae stood nearby. The modern city grew around a caravan and agricultural centre and expanded considerably in the twentieth century, especially after the construction of the commercial port and the Libyan Iron and Steel Company complex, one of the largest industrial projects in the country.

During the 2011 civil war, Misrata was besieged from March to May 2011. Rebel forces held the port, which became the city's lifeline for humanitarian aid and supplies. The battle left large parts of the city damaged and caused heavy civilian casualties. Misratan brigades subsequently became among the most powerful armed groups in post-Gaddafi Libya.

Economy

The Port of Misrata and its free zone handle a large share of Libya's commercial imports. The steel plant at Qasr Ahmad, operated by the Libyan Iron and Steel Company (LISCO), produces hot-briquetted iron, steel billets and rolled products. The city is also a centre for food processing, textiles, construction materials and trade.

Transport

Misrata is served by Misrata International Airport and by the coastal highway linking Tripoli with Benghazi. Its port provides ferry and cargo connections across the Mediterranean.
