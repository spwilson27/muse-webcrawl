Sheet music

Sheet music is a handwritten or printed form of musical notation that uses musical symbols to indicate the pitches, rhythms, and other elements of a composition. A single piece is often called a score when it shows all instrumental parts together, while parts are the extracted notation for individual performers.

History

Modern staff notation developed in medieval Europe from neumatic notation for chant, with Guido d'Arezzo credited with the four-line staff in the 11th century. Music printing began with movable type in the late 15th century (Petrucci's Harmonice Musices Odhecaton, 1501) and engraving later. In the 19th and early 20th centuries, sheet music sales were the main commercial form of popular music publishing, centred on New York's Tin Pan Alley, before recordings displaced it.

Modern forms

Today sheet music is produced with notation software, distributed digitally as PDF or in formats such as MusicXML, and includes specialised forms such as lead sheets, chord charts, and guitar tablature.
