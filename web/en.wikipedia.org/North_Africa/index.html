North Africa

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

North Africa is a region encompassing the northern portion of the African continent. There is no universally accepted definition of its extent, but it is generally taken to include Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Libya, and Egypt — the countries along the southern shore of the Mediterranean Sea — with the Sahara desert to the south separating it from sub-Saharan Africa. The United Nations geoscheme additionally includes Sudan and Western Sahara. The region had a population of roughly 270 million people in the mid-2020s.

Geography

The Atlas Mountains of the northwest, the Nile Valley and Delta in the east, and a narrow Mediterranean coastal plain contrast with the vast Sahara, which covers most of the region's area. The Nile, flowing north through Egypt and Sudan, supports one of the world's oldest and densest agricultural civilisations. Oil and natural gas reserves in Libya and Algeria are major economic assets.

History

North Africa was home to ancient Egypt and Nubia, Phoenician and Carthaginian colonies, and the Berber (Amazigh) kingdoms; it was incorporated successively into the Roman and Byzantine empires before the Arab conquests of the seventh century CE spread Islam and the Arabic language, which today predominate alongside Amazigh languages. Ottoman rule, then European colonisation by France, Spain, Italy, and Britain, shaped the modern states, which gained independence between 1922 (Egypt) and 1962 (Algeria). The region was a major theatre of the Second World War (the North African campaign) and a starting point of the Arab Spring uprisings of 2011 in Tunisia, Egypt, and Libya.

Culture and demographics

The population is majority Arab and Amazigh, predominantly Sunni Muslim, with ancient Christian minorities (notably Egypt's Copts) and historically significant Jewish communities. Arabic dialects (Maghrebi and Egyptian) coexist with French, widely used in the Maghreb, and reviving Amazigh (Tamazight) languages now officially recognised in Morocco and Algeria. Cities such as Cairo — North Africa's largest — Alexandria, Algiers, Casablanca, Tunis, and Tripoli anchor the region's urban life.
