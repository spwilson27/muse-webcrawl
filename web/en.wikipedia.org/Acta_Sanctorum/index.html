Acta Sanctorum

The Acta Sanctorum (Acts of the Saints) is an encyclopaedic text in 68 folio volumes of documents examining the lives of Christian saints, in essence a critical hagiography, drawn up and published by the Society of Jesus (the Jesuits) between 1643 and 1940. The volumes are arranged according to the saints' feast days in the calendar.

History

The project was conceived by the Jesuit Heribert Rosweyde (1569–1629), who published a plan and a preliminary volume of martyrologies. After his death, John van Bolland took up the work; the first volumes, covering January, appeared in Antwerp in 1643. The scholars who continued the work became known as the Bollandists. Notable among them was Daniel Papebroch, whose critical treatment of documents — including a controversial examination of charters — helped lay foundations for the science of diplomatics.

Significance

The Acta Sanctorum applied systematic source criticism to hagiographical texts, distinguishing genuine documents from later legends. The Bollandist society continues its work in Brussels, publishing the journal Analecta Bollandiana and reference works such as the Bibliotheca Hagiographica Latina.
