Cajamarca (Quechua: Kashamarka) is a region in northern Peru. It borders the regions of Piura, Lambayeque, and La Libertad to the west, Amazonas to the east, and Ecuador to the north. Its capital is the city of Cajamarca, and the region is divided into 13 provinces and 127 districts.

History

The area was home to the Cajamarca culture and was later incorporated into the Inca Empire. Cajamarca is famous as the site of the Battle of Cajamarca in 1532, where Francisco Pizarro captured the Inca emperor Atahualpa, who filled a room with gold and silver as ransom — the famous Ransom Room (Cuarto del Rescate) can still be visited in the city.

Economy

Agriculture and livestock, particularly dairy production, are central to the regional economy. Cajamarca is also one of Peru's most important mining regions, home to the Yanacocha gold mine, one of the largest gold mines in Latin America, as well as the Michiquillay copper project. Mining has brought both wealth and long-running social and environmental conflict over water use.

Geography

The region spans Andean highlands in the south and west, descending to low jungle in the northeast along the Marañón River. Elevations range from around 400 m to over 4,400 m above sea level, producing a wide range of climates.
