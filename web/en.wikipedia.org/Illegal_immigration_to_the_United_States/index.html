Illegal immigration to the United States

Illegal immigration to the United States is the process of migrating to the United States in violation of federal immigration laws. It may involve crossing the U.S. border without inspection, overstaying a temporary visa, or entering with false documents. Foreign nationals who arrive in this way are often referred to as unauthorized immigrants or undocumented immigrants.

Scale and origins

Research organisations such as the Pew Research Center have estimated the unauthorized immigrant population at roughly 10–11 million in recent years, down from a peak of about 12 million in 2007. Historically the largest single country of origin was Mexico, though in recent years arrivals from Central America, Venezuela, and other regions have grown. A large share of new unauthorized immigrants arrive legally on visas and overstay, rather than crossing a land border.

Law and politics

Immigration law is primarily federal; the Immigration and Nationality Act of 1965 and the Immigration Reform and Control Act of 1986, which legalised about three million people while introducing employer sanctions, are key statutes. Enforcement is carried out by agencies including Customs and Border Protection and Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE). The issue is a persistent subject of political debate, encompassing border security, asylum policy, economic effects, and proposals for pathways to legal status.
