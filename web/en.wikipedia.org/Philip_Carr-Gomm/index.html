Philip Carr-Gomm

Philip Carr-Gomm (born 31 January 1952) is an English writer and psychologist, best known as the leader ("Chosen Chief") of the Order of Bards, Ovates and Druids (OBOD) from 1988 to 2020, one of the largest international orders of modern Druidry. He studied psychology at University College London and trained in psychotherapy, later practising as a therapist alongside his work in the Druid tradition.

Life and work

Carr-Gomm encountered Druidry as a teenager through Ross Nichols, the founder of OBOD, who was a family friend. After Nichols's death in 1975 the Order went dormant; Carr-Gomm revived it in 1988, developing a correspondence-course training programme in Druidry that has enrolled tens of thousands of students in over a hundred countries. As Chief he sought to present Druidry as an open, creative spirituality emphasizing reverence for nature, healing, and the arts.

Writing

He is the author of numerous books, including The Elements of the Druid Tradition (1991), Druid Mysteries, The Druid Way, The Book of English Magic (2009, with Richard Heygate), Sacred Places, and The Prophecies, as well as the novel The Book of Falling. He has also written on nudism and naturism, on the history of magic, and on interfaith and environmental themes, and worked as a broadcaster. In 2020 he handed the leadership of OBOD to Eimear Burke.
