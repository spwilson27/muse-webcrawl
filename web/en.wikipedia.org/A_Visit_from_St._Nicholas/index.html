A Visit from St. Nicholas

A Visit from St. Nicholas, more commonly known by its first line 'Twas the Night Before Christmas, is a poem first published anonymously in the Troy Sentinel on 23 December 1823. The poem has been credited with shaping the modern American conception of Santa Claus, including his appearance, his sleigh pulled by eight reindeer, and his habit of delivering toys on Christmas Eve.

Text and imagery

The poem is written in anapaestic tetrameter. Its narrator, awakened on Christmas Eve, watches St. Nicholas arrive in a miniature sleigh drawn by eight "tiny reindeer" — Dasher, Dancer, Prancer, Vixen, Comet, Cupid, Donder and Blitzen. The saint is described as a jolly, plump "right jolly old elf" with a beard as white as snow, who fills the stockings with toys and departs up the chimney.

'Twas the night before Christmas, when all through the house
Not a creature was stirring, not even a mouse…

Authorship

The poem was published anonymously; Clement Clarke Moore later claimed authorship and included it in his collected poems of 1844. In the twentieth and twenty-first centuries the claim of Henry Livingston Jr., a New York poet of Dutch descent, was advanced by his descendants and supported by stylometric analyses by scholars such as Donald Foster, though the question remains disputed.

Influence

The poem established much of the popular iconography of Santa Claus in the United States and has been reprinted, parodied and set to music countless times. It remains one of the best-known pieces of American verse.
