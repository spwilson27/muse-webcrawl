Lake Superior

Lake Superior
Location: North America
Surface area: 82,100 km²
Max. depth: 406 m
Outflow: St. Marys River

Lake Superior is the largest freshwater lake in the world by surface area and the third-largest by volume. It is the largest, deepest, and coldest of North America's Great Lakes. The lake is shared by Ontario in Canada to the north, and by Minnesota, Wisconsin, and Michigan in the United States to the south.

Geography

Lake Superior has a surface area of about 82,100 square kilometres and holds roughly 12,000 cubic kilometres of water — about ten percent of the world's surface fresh water. Its maximum depth is 406 metres. Major islands include Isle Royale, the Apostle Islands, and Grand Island. The lake drains into Lake Huron via the St. Marys River and the Soo Locks.

History

Indigenous peoples, including the Ojibwe (Chippewa), have lived around the lake for millennia; the Ojibwe name is Gichigami, meaning "great sea". French explorers reached the lake in the 17th century, and it became a centre of the fur trade. The lake later became a major shipping route for iron ore and grain, and it is famous for shipwrecks such as the SS Edmund Fitzgerald, which sank in a storm in 1975.

Ecology

The lake's cold, oligotrophic waters support lake trout, whitefish, and introduced salmon species. Because of its size and low nutrient content, its ecosystem is sensitive to invasive species and climate change.
