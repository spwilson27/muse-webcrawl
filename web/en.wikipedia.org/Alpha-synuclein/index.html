Alpha-synuclein

GeneSNCA (chromosome 4)
Length140 amino acids
LocationPresynaptic terminals of neurons
Associated diseasesParkinson's disease, dementia with Lewy bodies, multiple system atrophy

Alpha-synuclein adopts an alpha-helical structure when bound to membranes.

Alpha-synuclein is a protein that, in humans, is encoded by the SNCA gene. It is abundant in the brain and is found mainly at the tips of neurons in presynaptic terminals, where it is thought to help regulate synaptic vesicle trafficking and neurotransmitter release. The protein is intrinsically disordered in solution but folds into alpha-helices on binding to lipid membranes.

Structure

The 140-amino-acid protein has three regions: an amphipathic N-terminal region that binds membranes, a central hydrophobic region (the NAC domain) that promotes aggregation, and an acidic C-terminal tail. Under pathological conditions alpha-synuclein misfolds into beta-sheet-rich amyloid fibrils.

Role in disease

Aggregated alpha-synuclein is the main component of Lewy bodies, the pathological hallmark of Parkinson's disease and dementia with Lewy bodies, and of the glial inclusions of multiple system atrophy — disorders collectively called synucleinopathies. Mutations and multiplications of the SNCA gene cause rare inherited forms of Parkinson's disease, and evidence suggests that misfolded protein can spread from cell to cell in a prion-like manner.

Research

Alpha-synuclein is a major therapeutic target: strategies under investigation include immunotherapies to clear extracellular aggregates, small molecules that stabilise the native form, and reduction of gene expression. Seed amplification assays of cerebrospinal fluid and skin are being developed as diagnostic biomarkers.
