Chicago Housing Authority

The Chicago Housing Authority (CHA) is a municipal corporation that oversees public housing within the city of Chicago, Illinois. Established in 1937, it is the third-largest housing authority in the United States, providing homes for tens of thousands of residents through public housing developments and Housing Choice Vouchers.

History

CHA was created in the wake of the federal Housing Act of 1937. Its first executive director, Elizabeth Wood, oversaw early low-rise developments such as the Ida B. Wells Homes. After World War II the authority built large high-rise projects, including the Robert Taylor Homes (completed 1962, then the largest public housing development in the world) and Cabrini–Green. Segregated siting practices led to the landmark Gautreaux v. Chicago Housing Authority litigation, decided in 1969, which found CHA had discriminated in tenant selection and site placement.

Plan for Transformation

Following federal receivership in 1995, CHA launched its Plan for Transformation in 2000, demolishing most of its high-rise stock — including Cabrini–Green and the Robert Taylor Homes — and replacing them with mixed-income communities. The plan became a national model in debates about public housing, displacement, and poverty deconcentration.

Today

CHA now administers more than 60,000 vouchers and a portfolio of family, senior, and mixed-income properties, serving over 100,000 Chicagoans.
