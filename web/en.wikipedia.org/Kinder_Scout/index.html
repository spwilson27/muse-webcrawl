Kinder Scout

Kinder Scout is a moorland plateau and the highest point in the Peak District and in Derbyshire, England, at 636 metres (2,087 ft) above sea level. Its summit is a broad, peaty plateau fringed by gritstone edges, noted for features such as the Kinder Downfall waterfall and the Woolpacks rock formations.

Mass trespass of 1932

Kinder Scout is famous as the site of the Mass Trespass of Kinder Scout on 24 April 1932, when some 400 ramblers, mostly from Manchester and Sheffield, deliberately trespassed on privately owned grouse moor in protest at the lack of public access to open country. Scuffles with gamekeepers led to arrests and prison sentences for several trespassers, including organiser Benny Rothman. The event is credited with galvanising the campaign that led to the National Parks and Access to the Countryside Act 1949 and ultimately the right-to-roam provisions of the Countryside and Rights of Way Act 2000.

Geography and walking

The plateau lies between the villages of Hayfield and Edale; the Pennine Way long-distance path crosses its northern edge. The peat landscape is severely eroded in places and is the subject of large-scale moorland restoration by the National Trust and Moors for the Future partnership, using sphagnum planting and gully blocking to store carbon and water.

Ownership

Much of Kinder Scout was acquired by the National Trust in 1982, funded partly by public appeal. In poor weather the featureless summit plateau is a serious navigation challenge, and the local mountain rescue teams are frequently called out to lost walkers.
