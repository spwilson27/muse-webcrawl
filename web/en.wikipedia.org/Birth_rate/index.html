Birth rate

The birth rate for a population is the number of live births per 1,000 people per year (the crude birth rate). Demographers also use age-specific rates and the total fertility rate — the average number of children a woman would have over her lifetime at current rates.

Measurement

Crude birth rate = (live births in a year / mid-year population) × 1000. Because it ignores age structure, comparisons between populations often prefer fertility rates. Replacement-level fertility is about 2.1 children per woman in low-mortality settings.

Trends and determinants

Global birth rates have fallen from around 37 per 1,000 in 1950 to under 17 in the 2020s, as part of the demographic transition: urbanization, education of women, contraception access, child survival and economic change lower desired and actual fertility. Highest rates (over 30 per 1,000) are concentrated in sub-Saharan Africa; many European and East Asian countries record rates under 7 and fertility well below replacement, prompting pro-natalist policies.

Effects

Birth rates shape population growth alongside death rates and migration, influencing age structure, labour supply, dependency ratios and public finances.
