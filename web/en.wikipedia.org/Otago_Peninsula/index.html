Otago Peninsula


Location: Dunedin, Otago, New Zealand
Length: about 20 km
Notable wildlife: Royal albatross, yellow-eyed penguins, fur seals

The Otago Peninsula is a long, hilly, indented finger of land that forms the easternmost part of Dunedin, New Zealand. Running about 20 kilometres along the southern shore of Otago Harbour from the city to Taiari Head (Taiaroa Head), the peninsula is of volcanic origin, formed as part of the eroded rim of a large extinct volcano. Its harbour side is dotted with small settlements such as Portobello, while its ocean side has high cliffs and sandy beaches.

Wildlife

The peninsula is internationally important for wildlife. Taiari Head hosts the world's only mainland breeding colony of royal albatross, established in the 1930s and protected at the Royal Albatross Centre. Beaches such as Sandfly Bay and Kaikorai support breeding yellow-eyed penguins (hoiho) — among the world's rarest penguins — little penguins, and colonies of New Zealand fur seals and sea lions.

Attractions

Historic sites include Larnach Castle, New Zealand's only castle, built in the 1870s, and Fletcher House. Ecotourism is a major activity, with guided tours to penguin reserves and albatross viewing, and the peninsula is often described as the wildlife capital of New Zealand.
