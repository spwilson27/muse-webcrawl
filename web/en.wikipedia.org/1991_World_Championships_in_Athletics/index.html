The 3rd World Championships in Athletics, under the auspices of the International Association of Athletics Federations (IAAF), were held from 23 August to 1 September 1991 at the National Stadium in Tokyo, Japan. A total of 1,517 athletes from 167 nations competed in 43 events.

Highlights

The championships are famous for the men's long jump duel between Carl Lewis and Mike Powell. Lewis, unbeaten in the event for a decade, produced the finest series of jumps in history, including a wind-assisted 8.91 m that broke Bob Beamon's 23-year-old championship-era benchmark. In the final round Powell leapt 8.95 m (29 ft 4½ in), breaking Beamon's legendary 1968 world record of 8.90 m. Powell's record still stands as the world record more than three decades later.

Lewis also won the 100 metres in a then-world record of 9.86 seconds, leading a race in which six men broke 10 seconds. Other standouts included Katrin Krabbe of Germany, who won the women's sprint double; Sergei Bubka, who won his third consecutive pole vault world title; and Heike Henkel, winner of the women's high jump. The United States topped the medal table with 10 golds, ahead of the Soviet Union — competing at these championships for the last time as a single team — and a reunified Germany.
