South Bank, Queensland

LocationBrisbane, Queensland, Australia
AreaSouth Bank Parklands approx. 17.5 hectares

FeaturesStreets Beach, Wheel of Brisbane, QPAC, Queensland Museum

South Bank is a cultural, social, educational, and recreational precinct in Brisbane, Queensland, Australia, on the southern bank of the Brisbane River opposite the city centre. It occupies parts of the suburb of South Brisbane.

History

The area was the site of World Expo 88, whose success persuaded the Queensland government to preserve the riverside as public parkland. The South Bank Parklands opened in 1992, incorporating the retained Nepal Peace Pagoda and the former Expo's rainforest walks.

Attractions

The precinct includes Streets Beach, a man-made swimming beach overlooking the river; the arbour of bougainvillea running through the parklands; the Wheel of Brisbane observation wheel; and the Queensland Cultural Centre, housing the Queensland Performing Arts Centre (QPAC), the Queensland Museum, the State Library of Queensland, and the Gallery of Modern Art (QAGOMA). The South Bank Corporation manages the precinct, which draws millions of visitors annually.

Transport

South Bank is served by its own railway station and busway station, and is linked to the CBD by the Victoria Bridge and the pedestrian Goodwill and Kurilpa bridges.
