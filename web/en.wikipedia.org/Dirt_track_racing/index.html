Dirt track racing

Dirt track racing is a form of motorsport in which cars or motorcycles race on oval tracks with surfaces of dirt or clay. It is especially popular in the United States, Australia and New Zealand, and is regarded as one of the most common forms of American auto racing.

Tracks and vehicles

Tracks are typically short ovals from a quarter-mile to a mile in length, with banked corners. Racing classes include sprint cars — lightweight, high-powered open-wheel cars with large wings — midget cars, late models, modifieds and street stocks. Drivers deliberately slide their cars sideways through turns in a technique known as opposite lock or "backing it in".

History

Dirt track racing developed in the United States in the early twentieth century, before paved speedways became common. It remains a grassroots proving ground; many NASCAR and IndyCar drivers, including Jeff Gordon and Tony Stewart, began their careers on dirt ovals. Major sanctioning bodies include the World of Outlaws, famous for its sprint car series.
