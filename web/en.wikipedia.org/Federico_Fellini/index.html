Federico Fellini

Federico Fellini (20 January 1920 – 31 October 1993) was an Italian filmmaker, one of the most influential directors of the twentieth century. Beginning in Italian neorealism, he developed a distinctive style blending memory, dreams, fantasy and baroque spectacle; the adjective "Felliniesque" entered common usage for work in his manner.

Born20 January 1920, Rimini, Italy
Died31 October 1993, Rome, Italy
OccupationFilm director, screenwriter
Notable worksLa Strada, La Dolce Vita, 8½, Amarcord

Career

Fellini began as a gag writer and co-wrote scripts for Roberto Rossellini, including Rome, Open City. His international breakthrough came with La Strada (1954) and Nights of Cabiria (1957), both starring his wife Giulietta Masina and both winning the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film. La Dolce Vita (1960) won the Palme d'Or at Cannes and coined the term "paparazzi".

Later work and honours

8½ (1963), a semi-autobiographical film about a director in creative crisis, is regularly ranked among the greatest films ever made. Fellini won four competitive Academy Awards for Best Foreign Language Film — a record — and received an honorary Oscar in 1993, the year of his death.
