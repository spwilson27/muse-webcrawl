Alpha-1 antitrypsin

Gene: SERPINA1 (chromosome 14)
Type: Serine protease inhibitor (serpin)
Produced in: Liver
Target: Neutrophil elastase

Alpha-1 antitrypsin (A1AT or α1-antitrypsin) is a protein belonging to the serpin superfamily of protease inhibitors. It is encoded by the SERPINA1 gene, produced mainly by the liver, and circulates in the blood, where its principal role is to protect tissues — especially the lungs — from the destructive enzyme neutrophil elastase released by white blood cells during inflammation.

Deficiency

Inherited variants of the SERPINA1 gene can cause alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency. The severe PiZZ variant produces a protein that polymerises inside liver cells: too little reaches the circulation, predisposing to early-onset emphysema, while the accumulated polymer damages the liver, causing hepatitis and cirrhosis. Smoking greatly accelerates lung disease in affected individuals. Augmentation therapy with purified human A1AT is available for selected patients with lung disease.

Clinical significance

A1AT is an acute-phase reactant, so blood levels rise during inflammation, pregnancy, and with oestrogen use. Measuring A1AT levels and phenotype is part of the workup for unexplained early emphysema and some liver diseases.
