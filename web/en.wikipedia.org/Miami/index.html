Miami

State: Florida, USA
County: Miami-Dade
Population: ~442,000 (city, 2020); ~6.1 million (metro)
Founded: 1896
Nickname: The Magic City; Gateway to the Americas

Miami is a coastal city in southeastern Florida and the seat of Miami-Dade County. Founded in 1896 after railroad magnate Henry Flagler extended his line south — encouraged by Julia Tuttle, the "Mother of Miami" — it grew from a frontier settlement into a global center of finance, commerce, culture, and tourism, and the cruise ship capital of the world.

Geography and climate

The city sits on a plain between the Everglades to the west and Biscayne Bay and the Atlantic Ocean to the east, at an average elevation of only about 2 m, making it highly vulnerable to sea-level rise and hurricanes (notably the 1926 Miami hurricane and Hurricane Andrew in 1992, which struck just south of the city). Miami has a tropical monsoon climate with hot, humid, stormy summers and warm, dry winters.

Demographics and culture

Miami is a majority Spanish-speaking city shaped by waves of immigration, especially Cuban exiles after 1959 (centered in Little Havana), along with Haitian, Nicaraguan, Colombian, and other Latin American and Caribbean communities. It is known for its Art Deco district in Miami Beach, the Art Basel Miami Beach fair, vibrant nightlife, and media industries including Telemundo and Univision.

Economy

PortMiami is among the world's busiest cruise ports; Miami International Airport is a leading gateway for Latin American cargo and passengers. The Brickell financial district hosts many international banks, earning Miami the label "Capital of Latin America."
