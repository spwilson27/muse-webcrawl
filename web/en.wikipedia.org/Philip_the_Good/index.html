Philip III, Duke of Burgundy (31 July 1396 – 15 June 1467), called Philip the Good, was Duke of Burgundy from 1419 until his death. A member of the Valois-Burgundy branch of the French royal house, he vastly expanded the Burgundian territories and made his court the most splendid in Europe.

Reign

Philip succeeded his father John the Fearless after the latter's assassination in 1419, and in revenge allied with England in the Hundred Years' War by the Treaty of Troyes (1420). His forces captured Joan of Arc in 1430 and handed her over to the English. In 1435, by the Treaty of Arras, he reconciled with Charles VII of France in exchange for territorial concessions and exemption from homage.

Territories and court

Through purchase, inheritance and treaty Philip acquired Namur, Brabant, Limburg, Holland, Zeeland, Hainault and Luxembourg, uniting much of the Low Countries. In 1430 he founded the Order of the Golden Fleece. His court at Bruges and Brussels patronized artists such as Jan van Eyck and Rogier van der Weyden, and composers of the Burgundian School.
