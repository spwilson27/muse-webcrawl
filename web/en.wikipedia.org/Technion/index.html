The Technion – Israel Institute of Technology is a public research university in Haifa, Israel, and the country's oldest university. Founded under the Ottoman Empire, it is a leading centre for science, engineering and medicine, and a major driver of Israel's technology industry.

History

The cornerstone of the Technion was laid in 1912, but its opening was delayed by the First World War and by the "War of the Languages" — a dispute over whether instruction would be in German or Hebrew, which ended in a landmark victory for Hebrew. The institute opened its doors in 1924 with programmes in civil engineering and architecture. Albert Einstein served as president of the first Technion Society in 1923 and planted a palm tree on the site.

Academics

The Technion has 18 faculties and dozens of research institutes, and operates the Ruth and Bruce Rappaport Faculty of Medicine. Three of its faculty — Avram Hershko, Aaron Ciechanover and Dan Shechtman — have won Nobel Prizes in Chemistry. The university also runs joint institutes abroad, including the Jacobs Technion-Cornell Institute in New York and a campus in Guangdong, China.
