John McLaren (horticulturist)

Born20 December 1846, Bannockburn, Scotland
Died12 January 1943 (aged 96), San Francisco, California, U.S.
OccupationHorticulturist, landscape designer
Known forSuperintendent of Golden Gate Park

John McLaren (20 December 1846 – 12 January 1943) was a Scottish-American horticulturist who served as superintendent of San Francisco's Golden Gate Park for 53 years, transforming its sand dunes into one of America's great urban parks.

Early life

McLaren trained as a gardener in Scotland, including at Balmoral Castle, before emigrating to California in 1870. He worked on estates around San Mateo and became known for landscape design on large private gardens.

Golden Gate Park

Appointed park superintendent in 1890, McLaren planted more than two million trees and introduced plants from around the world, stabilising the shifting dunes with grasses, ice plant and woodland. He famously resisted "keep off the grass" signs and buildings in the park, and pushed statues and structures to its edges so the landscape felt natural.

Later career and legacy

McLaren also helped lay out other San Francisco parks and the grounds of the 1915 Panama-Pacific International Exposition. He remained superintendent until his death in 1943. The John McLaren Park and a rhododendron dell in Golden Gate Park are named for him.
