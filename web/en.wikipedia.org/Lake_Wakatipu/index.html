Lake Wakatipu is an inland lake in the South Island of New Zealand, in the southwest corner of the Otago region near its boundary with Southland. With a length of about 80 kilometres it is the country's longest lake and, at 289 square kilometres, its third largest.

Geography

The lake has a distinctive zigzag or lightning-bolt shape, carved by glaciers into three arms. It is fed by the Dart and Rees rivers at its head near Glenorchy and drains into the Kawarau River at Queenstown, the resort town on its northeastern shore beneath the Remarkables mountain range.

Seiche

Lake Wakatipu is known for a pronounced seiche — a standing wave that makes the water level rise and fall by roughly 20 centimetres every half hour or so. Māori tradition explains the rhythm as the heartbeat of a giant, Matau, said to lie asleep beneath the lake.

Tourism

The lake is a centre of adventure tourism. The vintage steamship TSS Earnslaw, launched in 1912, still carries passengers from Queenstown to Walter Peak High Country Farm, and the lakeshore supports walking, cycling, and scenery tours year-round.
