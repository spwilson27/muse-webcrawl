Edom

Edom was an ancient kingdom in the southern Levant, located in the highlands east and south of the Dead Sea, in what is now southern Jordan and the Negev. Its people, the Edomites, were closely related to the Israelites, and the Hebrew Bible traces their descent from Esau, the brother of Jacob.

The rugged highlands of Edom in southern Jordan

Geography and economy

Edom's territory stretched from the Wadi Zered in the north to the Gulf of Aqaba in the south. Though rainfall was sparse, the Edomites practised terrace agriculture and controlled lucrative trade routes, including the King's Highway, carrying copper from mines at Timna and Punon, and incense and spices from Arabia. Its strongholds included Bozrah, its capital, and the rock-cut site later famous as Sela.

History

Archaeological evidence shows a developed kingdom by the 8th–6th centuries BCE, when Edom prospered under Assyrian and then Babylonian overlordship, producing distinctive painted pottery. The Bible depicts recurring hostility between Edom and Israel and Judah, and Edomites settled in southern Judah after the Babylonian destruction of Jerusalem in 586 BCE; their descendants, the Idumaeans, included the family of Herod the Great. The Nabataeans eventually displaced the Edomites from their homeland in the Persian and Hellenistic periods.

Religion

The Edomites worshipped a national god, Qos, alongside other deities; temples and shrines such as Horvat Qitmit have yielded figurines and inscribed material in the Edomite language, a close relative of Hebrew written in a similar script.
