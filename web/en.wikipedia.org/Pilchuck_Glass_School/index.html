Pilchuck Glass School

FounderDale Chihuly, with Ruth Tamura and others

TypeArt school (studio glass)

The Pilchuck Glass School is an international centre for glass art education located on a wooded campus near Stanwood, Washington, in the foothills of the Cascade Range. Founded in 1971 by glass artist Dale Chihuly together with Ruth Tamura and supported by patrons Anne and John Hauberg, the school grew out of a single summer workshop and became a central institution of the Studio Glass movement.

History

The first session in 1971 drew a small group of students who built their own furnaces and lived on the land, which the Haubergs made available from their tree farm. Pilchuck is named for the nearby Pilchuck River; the word derives from a Native American term for "red water". Over the following decades the campus grew to include hot shops, coldworking and casting studios, print and flat shops, and residences. Artists from around the world come to teach and study in intensive summer sessions, and the school also runs artist residencies and an annual auction in Seattle.

Programmes

Pilchuck offers courses in glassblowing, flameworking, kiln casting, coldworking, engraving, neon and mixed-media approaches to glass, taught by leading international artists. Its alumni and faculty include many of the most prominent figures in contemporary glass, and the school is widely credited with helping to establish the Pacific Northwest as a centre of glass art.
