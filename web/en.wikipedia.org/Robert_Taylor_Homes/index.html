The Robert Taylor Homes was a public housing project in the Bronzeville neighbourhood on the South Side of Chicago, Illinois, operated by the Chicago Housing Authority (CHA). At its completion in 1962 it was the largest public housing development in the United States: 28 identical sixteen-storey concrete high-rises containing 4,415 apartments, stretched along State Street for two miles within the "State Street corridor" of segregated Black housing. It was named for Robert Rochon Taylor, a housing activist and the first African American chairman of the CHA board.

History

Intended to provide modern housing for low-income families, the development housed some 27,000 people at its peak, overwhelmingly African American. Concentrated poverty, building design with open exterior galleries, maintenance failures, and the withdrawal of working families following income limits produced worsening conditions; by the 1970s and 1980s the Robert Taylor Homes were notorious for gang activity, drug markets, and violence, becoming a national symbol of the failures of high-rise public housing policy.

Demolition and redevelopment

Under the CHA's Plan for Transformation, all 28 buildings were demolished between 1998 and 2007. The site has been partly redeveloped as Legends South, a mixed-income community of low-rise housing, though redevelopment proceeded more slowly than planned and many former residents did not return.
