Soviet Union at the Olympics


The Soviet team at an Olympic opening ceremony

The Soviet Union participated in the Olympic Games for the first time at the 1952 Summer Olympics in Helsinki and competed at eighteen Olympic Games in total. Despite only competing in nine Winter Games, the USSR was the most successful nation in Winter Olympic history for much of that period, and it was a dominant force in the Summer Games as well, vying with the United States for supremacy in the medal tables throughout the Cold War.

History

The Soviet Olympic Committee was formed on 21 April 1951 and was recognized by the International Olympic Committee later that year. At the Helsinki Games of 1952, the Soviet team finished second in the medal standings behind the United States. The USSR topped the medal table at the Summer Games of 1956, 1960, 1972, 1976, 1980, and 1988, and at every Winter Games it entered from 1956 to 1988 except 1984.

Moscow hosted the 1980 Summer Olympics, which were boycotted by the United States and dozens of other nations in protest over the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan. In retaliation, the Soviet Union and most of its allies boycotted the 1984 Summer Olympics in Los Angeles.

Medal record

Across nine Summer Games, Soviet athletes won 1,010 medals, including 395 gold. In nine Winter Games they won 217 medals, including 78 gold. Standout athletes included gymnast Larisa Latynina, who held the record for the most Olympic medals by an individual (18) for 48 years, and weightlifter Vasily Alekseyev.

Following the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991, former Soviet republics competed together as the Unified Team at the 1992 Games before competing independently thereafter.
