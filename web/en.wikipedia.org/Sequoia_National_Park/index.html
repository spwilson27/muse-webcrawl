Sequoia National Park

Sequoia National Park is an American national park in the southern Sierra Nevada east of Visalia, California. The park was established on September 25, 1890, to protect 404,064 acres (about 1,635 km²) of mountainous terrain, and is famous for its giant sequoia trees, including the General Sherman tree, the largest tree on Earth by volume. The park is administered jointly with the adjacent Kings Canyon National Park.

Geography and features

The park spans the western slope of the Sierra Nevada, rising from about 400 m in the foothills to the summit of Mount Whitney (4,421 m / 14,505 ft), the highest point in the contiguous United States, which lies on the park's eastern boundary. The Giant Forest grove contains five of the ten largest trees in the world; the General Sherman Tree stands 83.8 m tall with a volume of roughly 1,487 m³. Other attractions include Moro Rock, a granite dome with a staircase to its summit; the drive-through Tunnel Log, cut through a fallen sequoia in 1938; Crystal Cave, a marble karst cave; and hundreds of kilometres of trails into the Sierra backcountry.

History

The region was inhabited for millennia by Native American groups including the Monache (Western Mono), Yokuts, and Tübatulabal. In the later 19th century, logging threatened the great groves — the nearby Converse Basin was almost completely cut — and conservationists led by Visalia residents, together with the advocacy of John Muir, secured federal protection. Sequoia was the second United States national park created (after Yellowstone), established only days before Yosemite and the short-lived General Grant park, which later became part of Kings Canyon.

Ecology

Elevation gradients create diverse habitats, from chaparral and oak woodland through montane conifer forest to alpine tundra. Giant sequoias depend on periodic low-intensity fire to clear undergrowth and open their cones; decades of fire suppression, followed by the reintroduction of prescribed burning, is a central management theme. The park supports black bears, mule deer, mountain lions, and the endangered Sierra Nevada bighorn sheep. Severe drought- and beetle-driven tree mortality and high-intensity fires in recent years — including the 2020 Castle Fire and 2021 KNP Complex, which killed thousands of large sequoias — have raised concern for the groves' future.
