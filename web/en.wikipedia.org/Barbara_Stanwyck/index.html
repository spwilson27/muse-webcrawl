Barbara Stanwyck

Barbara Stanwyck
Born: Ruby Catherine Stevens, 16 July 1907, Brooklyn, New York
Died: 20 January 1990, Santa Monica, California
Occupation: Actress

Barbara Stanwyck (born Ruby Catherine Stevens; 1907–1990) was an American actress, model and dancer. Orphaned at four and partly raised in foster homes, she became one of the most versatile and admired stars of Hollywood's Golden Age, known for playing strong, independent women across melodrama, screwball comedy and film noir.

Career

Stanwyck rose from the chorus line and Broadway to film stardom in the 1930s with Ladies of Leisure and Frank Capra's films. In the 1940s she starred in classics including Preston Sturges's The Lady Eve (1941), Billy Wilder's Double Indemnity (1944) — her performance as femme fatale Phyllis Dietrichson is iconic — and Sorry, Wrong Number (1948). She received four Academy Award nominations and was given an Honorary Academy Award in 1982.

Television

In later years she headlined the Western series The Big Valley (1965–1969), winning an Emmy, and appeared in The Colbys in the 1980s. She remains ranked among the greatest female screen legends by the American Film Institute.

Personal life

Stanwyck was married to actor Frank Fay and later to Robert Taylor. Renowned for professionalism and generosity on set, she was widely beloved by film crews. She died of congestive heart failure in 1990.
