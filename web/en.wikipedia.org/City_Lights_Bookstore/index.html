City Lights Bookstore

TypeIndependent bookstore and publisher
Founded1953
Location261 Columbus Avenue, San Francisco

City Lights is an independent bookstore-publisher in San Francisco, founded in 1953 by poet Lawrence Ferlinghetti and Peter D. Martin. It is located at 261 Columbus Avenue, on the border of North Beach and Chinatown, and is celebrated as a landmark of the Beat Generation.

History

City Lights began as one of the first all-paperback bookstores in the United States. Peter Martin, who named it after the Charlie Chaplin film City Lights, left the business in 1955, leaving Ferlinghetti as sole owner. Ferlinghetti launched the Pocket Poets Series, whose fourth volume was Allen Ginsberg's Howl and Other Poems (1956).

The Howl obscenity trial

In 1957 Ferlinghetti and store clerk Shigeyoshi Murao were arrested for publishing and selling the allegedly obscene Howl. Their acquittal by Judge Clayton Horn, who ruled the poem had redeeming social importance, was a landmark victory for free speech in American publishing.

Legacy

The store became a gathering place for Beat writers and later generations of authors. It was designated a San Francisco landmark in 2001. Nancy Peters joined as editor in the 1970s and later co-owned the business. Ferlinghetti remained involved until his death in 2021, aged 101.
