Equalization payments

Canadian federal equalization: provinces with below-average fiscal capacity receive unconditional payments from the federal government.

Equalization payments are cash payments made in some federal jurisdictions — most prominently Canada — by the federal government to subnational governments whose tax bases are below average, so that those governments can provide roughly comparable levels of public services at roughly comparable levels of taxation. In Canada the principle is entrenched in section 36(2) of the Constitution Act, 1982.

Canada

The Canadian program was introduced in 1957. It measures each province's fiscal capacity across five broad revenue categories (personal income tax, business income tax, consumption taxes, property taxes, and natural resource revenues) and compares it to a standard based on the average of all ten provinces. Provinces below the standard receive payments that bring them up to it; the federal government does not tax "have" provinces directly to fund the program — payments come out of general federal revenues raised across the country. No conditions are attached to how recipient provinces spend the money.

The formula has been revised repeatedly, including changes in 2007 following the O'Brien report, a cap tied to GDP growth introduced in 2009, and periodic five-year renewals. Quebec has historically been the largest recipient in absolute dollars, while Alberta, Saskatchewan and Newfoundland and Labrador have generally been non-recipients in recent decades, though entitlements shift with resource prices. Equalization is a recurring subject of political controversy in Canada, including referendums and court challenges threatened by some provincial governments.

Other federations

Australia operates a more comprehensive system of horizontal fiscal equalization through the Commonwealth Grants Commission, distributing GST revenue so that each state can provide services of equal standard. Germany's Länderfinanzausgleich redistributes revenue among the states, and Switzerland and several other federations have analogous fiscal capacity equalization schemes, differing in whether transfers flow through the central government (vertical) or directly between states (horizontal).
