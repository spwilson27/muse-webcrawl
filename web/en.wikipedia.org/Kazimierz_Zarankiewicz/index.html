Kazimierz Zarankiewicz (2 May 1902 – 5 September 1959) was a Polish mathematician whose interests lay mainly in topology, graph theory, complex analysis and number theory. He studied at the University of Warsaw, where he later became a professor.

Work

Zarankiewicz is best known for the Zarankiewicz problem, which asks for the maximum number of edges in a bipartite graph that contains no complete bipartite subgraph Ks,t; the Kővári–Sós–Turán theorem gives an upper bound. He also conjectured the value now known as the Zarankiewicz crossing number for complete bipartite graphs.

Life

During the Second World War he taught in the underground university in Warsaw and participated in the resistance; after the war he resumed his chair and was elected to the Polish Academy of Sciences.
