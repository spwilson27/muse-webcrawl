Bullet

A bullet is a kinetic projectile, a component of firearm ammunition that is shot from a gun barrel. They are made of a variety of materials, such as copper, lead, steel, polymer, rubber, and even wax; in modern cartridges the bullet is typically a lead core in a copper-alloy jacket. A complete round of ammunition consists of the bullet, the cartridge case, the propellant (gunpowder), and the primer.

History

Early firearms fired round lead or stone balls; the word "bullet" derives from the French boulette, "small ball". The invention of rifling, the elongated conical bullet (such as the Minié ball of the mid-19th century), and smokeless powder progressively increased accuracy and velocity. Modern bullets are aerodynamic, often boat-tailed, and spin-stabilized by the rifling of the barrel.

Design

Bullets are classified by their construction and intended effect: full metal jacket, hollow-point, soft-point, armor-piercing, tracer, and frangible types are common. Caliber expresses the bullet's diameter, in inches or millimetres. On impact the bullet transfers kinetic energy to the target; expanding designs are used in hunting and law enforcement to increase energy transfer and reduce over-penetration.

Physics

The study of bullet behavior divides into internal ballistics (within the barrel), external ballistics (in flight), and terminal ballistics (on impact). Muzzle velocities range from roughly 200 m/s for some handgun rounds to over 900 m/s for high-powered rifle rounds.
