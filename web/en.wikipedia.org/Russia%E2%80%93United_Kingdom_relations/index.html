Russia–United Kingdom relations

Russia–United Kingdom relations are the bilateral relations between Russia and the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland. Formal ties date to 1553, when the English mariner Richard Chancellor reached the White Sea and the Muscovy Company was chartered to trade with Ivan IV's Russia. Relations since have swung between alliance and rivalry.

History

Britain and Russia were allies against Napoleon, but became rivals in the 19th century "Great Game" for influence in Central Asia, and fought one another in the Crimean War (1853–1856). The Anglo-Russian Convention of 1907 settled colonial disputes and aligned the two powers in the Triple Entente against Germany; Russia left the First World War after the 1917 revolutions, and Britain intervened briefly in the Russian Civil War. The two were allies again against Nazi Germany from 1941 to 1945, then adversaries throughout the Cold War.

Post-Cold War

After the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991, relations initially warmed, with British investment in Russia and Russian wealth flowing into London. They deteriorated sharply after the 2006 poisoning of Alexander Litvinenko in London with polonium-210, over Russia's 2008 war with Georgia, the annexation of Crimea in 2014, and the 2018 Salisbury poisoning of Sergei and Yulia Skripal with a novichok nerve agent, which led to mass expulsions of diplomats. Following Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, the United Kingdom imposed extensive sanctions, expelled diplomats, and became one of Ukraine's largest military backers, while Russia designated the UK an "unfriendly country".

Diplomatic representation

Russia maintains an embassy in London and the United Kingdom an embassy in Moscow, though staffing on both sides has been heavily reduced by successive expulsions since 2018.
