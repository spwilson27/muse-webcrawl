George Gamow (March 4, 1904 – August 19, 1968), born Georgiy Antonovich Gamov, was a Soviet and American physicist and cosmologist. He was an early advocate and developer of Lemaître's Big Bang theory and discovered a theoretical explanation of alpha decay via quantum tunnelling.

Early life and career

Gamow was born in Odessa, then part of the Russian Empire. He studied in Leningrad and worked in Göttingen and Copenhagen, where in 1928 he explained alpha decay as a quantum tunnelling phenomenon. In 1933 he defected from the Soviet Union while attending a conference in Brussels, and in 1934 moved to the United States.

Cosmology

With Ralph Alpher and Robert Herman, Gamow developed the theory of Big Bang nucleosynthesis and predicted the existence of the cosmic microwave background radiation. The famous 1948 Alpher–Bethe–Gamow ("αβγ") paper described how the elements formed in the early universe.

Popular science and other work

Gamow was a celebrated popularizer of science, author of One Two Three... Infinity and the Mr Tompkins books. He also proposed an early model of the genetic code. He spent his final years at the University of Colorado Boulder, where the Gamow Tower is named in his honour.
