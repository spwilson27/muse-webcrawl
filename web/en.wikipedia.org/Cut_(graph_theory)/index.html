Cut (graph theory)

Illustration: Cut (graph theory)

In graph theory, a cut is a partition of the vertices of a graph into two disjoint subsets. The cut-set is the set of edges with one endpoint in each subset. Cuts are fundamental to the study of network flows and graph connectivity.

Minimum and maximum cuts

A minimum cut (min cut) separates the graph with the fewest edges or lowest total weight; by the max-flow min-cut theorem, its value equals the maximum flow between a source and sink. A maximum cut (max cut), which maximises the weight of crossing edges, is NP-hard in general.

Applications

Cut problems arise in network reliability, image segmentation, circuit layout, and clustering. Algorithms such as Karger's randomised algorithm and Stoer–Wagner compute minimum cuts efficiently.
