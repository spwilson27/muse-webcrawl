Public Domain Day

Public Domain Day is an observance held on January 1 of each year, the date on which copyrights traditionally expire in many jurisdictions and works enter the public domain. The day is marked by libraries, archives, and digital-rights organizations that publish lists of the newly free works and host events celebrating their reuse.

History

The first mention of Public Domain Day is attributed to Canadian activist Wallace McLean in 2004, and the idea was taken up by organizations including the Internet Archive, Creative Commons, and Duke University's Center for the Study of the Public Domain, which publishes an annual survey of notable works entering the public domain in the United States. Because of the Copyright Term Extension Act of 1998, no published works entered the U.S. public domain through expiration from 1998 until January 1, 2019, when works from 1923 were released; each year since has freed the works of one more year — including, on January 1, 2024, the original Steamboat Willie version of Mickey Mouse.

Celebrations

Events have been held in cities around the world under the umbrella of the Communia association and local Wikimedia chapters. Typical activities include public-domain film screenings, remixing contests, digitization drives, and "copyright cafés" discussing the cultural value of a rich public domain.
