Tourism

Tourists at a major heritage site

Tourism is travel for pleasure or business, and also the theory and practice of touring, the business of attracting, accommodating, and entertaining tourists, and the business of operating tours. The World Tourism Organization (UN Tourism) defines tourism more generally as the social, cultural and economic phenomenon which entails the movement of people to countries or places outside their usual environment for personal or business/professional purposes, for not more than one consecutive year.

Scale

Tourism is one of the world's largest industries. International tourist arrivals surpassed 1.4 billion in 2018 and, after a collapse during the COVID-19 pandemic, recovered to roughly that level by 2024. France, Spain, and the United States are perennially among the most visited countries. Tourism generates foreign exchange, employment, and infrastructure investment, but also congestion, price inflation, and environmental pressure in heavily visited destinations.

History

Leisure travel has ancient precedents — from Roman holiday villas to pilgrimage networks — but modern tourism grew from the aristocratic Grand Tour of the 17th–19th centuries, Thomas Cook's organized excursions beginning in 1841, the rise of seaside resorts, and, after 1945, paid holidays, package tours, and cheap aviation, which turned travel into a mass phenomenon.

Forms of tourism

  • Cultural and heritage tourism
  • Ecotourism and adventure tourism
  • Beach and cruise tourism
  • Business and conference tourism
  • Medical and wellness tourism
  • Religious tourism and pilgrimage
  • Space tourism

Impacts

Tourism's effects are debated under headings such as overtourism, sustainability, and authenticity. Critics point to carbon emissions from aviation, strain on housing and water, and the "tourist gaze" commodifying local culture; defenders stress income for conservation, cross-cultural contact, and livelihoods in regions with few alternatives. Responses include tourist taxes, visitor caps, and promotion of off-season and lesser-known destinations.
