Giorgione

Giorgione (Italian: [dʒorˈdʒoːne]; born Giorgio Barbarelli da Castelfranco; c. 1477/78 – 1510) was an Italian painter of the Venetian school during the High Renaissance. Giorgione is widely regarded as one of the most enigmatic figures in the history of Western art: very little is known of his life, only a handful of paintings can be attributed to him with confidence, and yet his work had a profound influence on the development of Renaissance painting.

Life

Giorgione was born in Castelfranco Veneto, a small town in the Veneto. He was probably apprenticed in the workshop of Giovanni Bellini in Venice, where he would have encountered the younger Titian, who later became his rival and, according to some accounts, his pupil. The name "Giorgione" means roughly "Big George", and Vasari suggests it referred to his physical stature or magnanimity of spirit. He died young, in 1510, reportedly of the plague.

Works

Among the few paintings securely attributed to Giorgione are The Tempest (c. 1506–08), one of the most discussed pictures in art history, whose meaning remains debated; the Pastoral Concert (c. 1509, sometimes attributed to Titian); the Sleeping Venus (c. 1510), completed after his death by Titian; the Castelfranco Madonna (c. 1504); The Three Philosophers; and the portrait known as Laura (1506), his only signed and dated work.

Style and influence

Giorgione's art is characterised by its poetic, atmospheric quality, soft tonal transitions (sfumato), and a new emphasis on landscape as a bearer of mood. His small-scale pictures for private collectors helped establish the cabinet picture as a genre. His influence on Titian, Sebastiano del Piombo, and the whole Venetian school was immense, and his reputation grew steadily from the Romantic era onward.
