Tony Hadley

Tony Hadley
Born: 2 June 1960, Islington, London, England
Genres: Pop, new wave, blue-eyed soul
Occupation: Singer, songwriter
Years active: 1979–present

Anthony Patrick Hadley (born 2 June 1960) is an English singer best known as the lead vocalist of the new wave band Spandau Ballet. His powerful, soulful baritone powered hits including "True", "Gold", "Through the Barricades" and "To Cut a Long Story Short", making him one of the defining voices of British pop in the 1980s.

Career

Hadley formed the band that became Spandau Ballet with school friends in Islington in the late 1970s. Emerging from the New Romantic club scene, the band achieved international success, selling more than 25 million albums worldwide. After a bitter split in 1990, Hadley pursued a solo career, releasing albums such as The State of Play (1992) and the big-band recording Passing Strangers (2006).

Spandau Ballet reformed in 2009 for a world tour and the album Once More. Hadley left the band permanently in 2017. He has since toured extensively as a solo artist performing orchestral versions of his hits, released the swing album Talking to the Moon (2018), and continued to appear on British television and radio.
