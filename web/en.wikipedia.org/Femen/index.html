Femen (stylised FEMEN) is a radical feminist activist group founded in Kyiv, Ukraine, in 2008. It became internationally known for organising controversial topless protests — in which activists appear bare-breasted with slogans painted on their bodies — against sex tourism, religious institutions, sexism and other social and political issues. The group describes itself as fighting patriarchy in its three manifestations: the sexual exploitation of women, dictatorship and religion.

History

Femen was founded by Anna Hutsol together with Oksana Shachko and Alexandra Shevchenko, initially to protest the sex industry and the treatment of women in Ukraine. Activist Inna Shevchenko became the group's most visible figure after sawing down a large wooden cross in Kyiv in 2012 in support of the jailed Russian punk band Pussy Riot. Fearing prosecution, several leaders moved to Paris in 2013, where the group opened a "training centre" and established branches in several countries.

Criticism

Femen has been criticised from several directions: religious and conservative groups condemn its protests at sacred sites; some feminists object to its tactics as reinforcing the objectification it opposes; and its anti-Islam protests drew accusations of Islamophobia, prompting a counter-campaign by Muslim women. The 2013 documentary Ukraine Is Not a Brothel also raised questions about the influence of a male adviser, Victor Svyatski, over the group's early activities.
