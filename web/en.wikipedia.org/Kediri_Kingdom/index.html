Kediri Kingdom

The Kediri Kingdom (also spelled Kadiri) was an East Javanese Hindu-Buddhist kingdom that existed from 1042 to 1222. Its capital was Daha, corresponding to present-day Kediri in the fertile Brantas River valley of East Java, Indonesia. The kingdom is remembered as the golden age of classical Javanese literature.

Origins

When the Javanese king Airlangga of Kahuripan divided his realm in 1042 between his two sons, the western portion became the kingdom of Kediri (Panjalu) and the eastern the kingdom of Janggala. Kediri eventually absorbed Janggala after a civil war and ruled most of East Java, with influence extending to parts of Kalimantan and the spice islands to the east.

Economy

Kediri's prosperity rested on wet-rice agriculture in the Brantas valley and on maritime trade. Chinese sources describe Java as a wealthy entrepôt exporting rice, salt, textiles and spices. The kingdom maintained trade relations with China, India and mainland Southeast Asia, and Kediri merchants dealt in cloves and nutmeg brought from the Maluku Islands.

Culture and literature

The Kediri court was a celebrated centre of kakawin poetry. The Bharatayuddha (1157) of Mpu Sedah and Mpu Panuluh retold the Mahabharata's fratricidal war as an allegory of the Janggala–Kediri conflict. King Jayabaya is credited in later tradition with the Jangka Jayabaya, prophetic verses that remained politically potent into the 20th century. Temple building in this era used sophisticated brick techniques, seen at sites such as Panataran's predecessors and the remains around Kediri.

Fall

King Kertajaya (also called Dandang Gendis) demanded to be worshipped as a god, alienating the Hindu and Buddhist clergy. The priests sought the protection of Ken Arok, the ambitious ruler of Tumapel. In 1222 Ken Arok crushed Kediri's army at the Battle of Ganter (Gonter), Kertajaya fled and was later killed, and Kediri became a province of the new kingdom of Singhasari. Kediri rose once more under Jayakatwang in 1292–93 before Raden Wijaya extinguished it and founded Majapahit on its periphery.
