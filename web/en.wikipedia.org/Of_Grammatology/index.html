Of Grammatology

Of Grammatology (French: De la grammatologie) is a 1967 book by the French philosopher Jacques Derrida, in which he introduces the concept of deconstruction and proposes a "science of writing" — grammatology. Together with Writing and Difference and Speech and Phenomena, published the same year, it established Derrida's reputation.

Content

Derrida argues that Western philosophy has been dominated by logocentrism — the privileging of speech (logos) over writing, on the assumption that speech is closer to thought and truth. Against this hierarchy he develops the notion of différance and claims that features traditionally attributed only to writing — the play of differences, the absence of the author, iterability — in fact condition all language. Part II applies this reading to Jean-Jacques Rousseau, tracing the logic of the "supplement" in Rousseau's account of the origin of language and society.

Reception

The English translation by Gayatri Chakravorty Spivak (1976), with its substantial translator's preface, became a foundational text of literary theory in the Anglophone world and was central to the Yale School of deconstruction. Critics have charged the book with obscurity and with misreading its source texts; defenders regard it as a landmark of 20th-century philosophy.
