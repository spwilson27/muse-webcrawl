Gun law in the United States

Gun law in the United States regulates the sale, possession and use of firearms and ammunition. Regulation exists at the federal, state and local levels, and is framed by the Second Amendment to the US Constitution.

Federal law

Key federal statutes include the National Firearms Act of 1934, which taxes and registers machine guns and short-barrelled weapons; the Gun Control Act of 1968, which prohibits sales to felons and other listed persons and created the federal firearms licence system for dealers; and the Brady Act of 1993, which requires background checks through the FBI's NICS system for sales by licensed dealers. The Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives (ATF) enforces federal firearms law. A federal ban on certain semi-automatic "assault weapons" was in force from 1994 to 2004.

State law and court rulings

States differ greatly: some impose permits to purchase, registration and bans on certain weapons, while others allow permitless carry of handguns. In District of Columbia v. Heller (2008) the Supreme Court held that the Second Amendment protects an individual's right to possess a handgun in the home; McDonald v. Chicago (2010) applied the right against the states; and New York State Rifle & Pistol Association v. Bruen (2022) recognised a right to carry handguns publicly for self-defence and set a history-based test for evaluating gun regulations.
