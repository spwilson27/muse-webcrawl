Cholesky decomposition

In linear algebra, the Cholesky decomposition or Cholesky factorization is a decomposition of a Hermitian, positive-definite matrix into the product of a lower triangular matrix and its conjugate transpose: A = LL*. It is named after the French mathematician and military officer André-Louis Cholesky, who developed it for use in geodesy.

Properties and uses

The Cholesky decomposition is roughly twice as efficient as the general LU decomposition for solving systems of linear equations, and it is numerically stable without pivoting when the matrix is positive-definite. It is widely used in Monte Carlo simulation to generate correlated random variables, in Kalman filtering, in least-squares optimisation, and as a test for positive-definiteness.

Algorithm

The standard Cholesky algorithm computes the entries of L column by column: each diagonal entry is the square root of the corresponding diagonal of A minus the sum of squares of the entries already computed in that row, and each off-diagonal entry follows by subtracting the dot product of the two partial rows and dividing by the diagonal element. Variants include the Cholesky–Banachiewicz and Cholesky–Crout algorithms, which differ in the order of computation.
