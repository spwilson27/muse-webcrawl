Metropolitan Museum of Art

The Metropolitan Museum of Art, colloquially "the Met", is an art museum in New York City, the largest art museum in the Americas and among the most-visited in the world. Founded in 1870, it opened at its present site on Fifth Avenue on the eastern edge of Central Park in 1880, in a Beaux-Arts building that has been greatly expanded since. Its permanent collection contains over 1.5 million works, divided among 17 curatorial departments, spanning 5,000 years of art from every part of the globe.

Collections

Holdings include American and European painting and sculpture, Egyptian art (including the Temple of Dendur), Greek and Roman art, Asian and Islamic art, African and Oceanic art, arms and armour, musical instruments, photographs, and the Costume Institute, famous for the annual Met Gala fundraiser. Highlights include works by Vermeer, Rembrandt, Monet and Van Gogh.

Sites and governance

Besides the main Fifth Avenue building ("The Met Fifth Avenue"), the museum runs The Met Cloisters in Fort Tryon Park, devoted to medieval European art and architecture; its former contemporary-art annex, the Met Breuer, closed in 2020. The Met is governed by a board of trustees, operates in a city-owned building, and is funded by endowment income, admissions, membership, retail and philanthropy.
