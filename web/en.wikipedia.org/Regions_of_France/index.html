Regions of France

The 13 metropolitan regions of France since 2016.

France is divided into regions (French: régions), the largest of its administrative divisions. Since a major territorial reform that took effect on 1 January 2016, metropolitan France has 13 regions, reduced from 22; together with five overseas regions there are 18 in total. Regions have responsibility for economic development, regional planning, transport, secondary education (lycées) and vocational training, and are governed by elected regional councils.

History

Regions were first created as administrative districts in 1960, became public establishments in 1972, and gained directly elected councils under the decentralisation laws of 1982–83. The 2014 law redrawing the map aimed to create regions of European scale; several new names — Grand Est, Hauts-de-France, Nouvelle-Aquitaine, Occitanie — were chosen by public consultation in 2016.

Overseas regions

The five overseas regions — Guadeloupe, Martinique, French Guiana, Réunion and Mayotte — are simultaneously departments and are integral parts of the French Republic and the European Union.
