P-hacking

P-hacking, also known as data dredging, data snooping, or data fishing, is the misuse of data analysis to find patterns in data that can be presented as statistically significant, thus dramatically increasing and understating the risk of false positives. This is done by performing many statistical tests on the data and only reporting those that come back with significant results.

The process of data dredging involves testing large numbers of hypotheses about a single data set by exhaustively searching — perhaps for combinations of variables that might show a "correlation", and groups of cases or observations that show differences in their mean or in their breakdown by some other variable.

Background

Conventional tests of statistical significance are based on the probability that a result could have arisen by chance alone, and necessarily accept some risk of mistaken conclusions of a certain type (mistaken rejections of the null hypothesis). This level of risk is called the significance. When large numbers of tests are performed, some produce false results of this type; hence 5% of randomly chosen hypotheses turn out to be significant at the 5% level by chance alone, 1% turn out to be significant at the 1% level by chance alone, and so on. When enough hypotheses are tested, it is virtually certain that some will be statistically significant even though nothing is going on, as many random data sets are expected to follow statistical patterns one might wish to interpret as evidence.

Relation to the replication crisis

P-hacking is widely cited as one cause of the replication crisis in psychology and other sciences. Practices that constitute p-hacking include stopping data collection once a significant result appears, selectively reporting dependent variables, excluding data points after checking their impact, and hypothesizing after the results are known (HARKing). Remedies proposed include pre-registration of studies, correction for multiple comparisons such as the Bonferroni correction, and reporting all analyses performed.
