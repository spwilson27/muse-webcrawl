Police corruption

Police corruption is a form of police misconduct in which law enforcement officers end up breaking their political contract and abusing their power for personal gain. A corrupt officer may act against the interests of the public or the police department, for instance by accepting bribes in exchange for not pursuing, or selectively pursuing, an investigation or arrest. Police corruption can also involve extortion, theft of seized property or money, planting or fabricating evidence, and the protection of organised crime or narcotics trafficking.

Types

Analysts commonly distinguish between petty corruption (small bribes and favours) and grand corruption (systematic involvement in crime), and between individual "rotten apples" and institutional or "noble cause" corruption, in which officers break rules believing they serve a just outcome. The distinction between corruption and other forms of misconduct, such as excessive force or perjury ("testilying"), is often debated.

Causes and consequences

Commonly cited contributing factors include low pay, discretionary power, weak supervision and accountability, the subculture of police solidarity often called the "blue wall of silence", and close contact with criminal markets such as drugs and illegal gambling. Police corruption erodes public trust, undermines the rule of law, and can allow organised crime to flourish.

Combating corruption

Responses include internal affairs units, civilian review boards, integrity testing, body-worn cameras, whistleblower protections, and external commissions of inquiry, such as the Knapp Commission in New York City in the 1970s and the Mollen Commission two decades later, which investigated corruption in the New York City Police Department.
