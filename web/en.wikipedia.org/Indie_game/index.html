Indie game

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An indie game, short for independent video game, is a video game created by individuals or small development teams without the financial or technical support of a large game publisher. Indie games often focus on innovation, experimental gameplay, and artistic expression, relying on digital distribution rather than retail release.

Although independent development has existed since the earliest days of personal computers and the shareware scene, the modern indie movement took off in the 2000s with the growth of online distribution through platforms such as Steam, Xbox Live Arcade, and later itch.io, Nintendo eShop, and mobile app stores. Accessible game engines — including Unity, Unreal Engine, GameMaker, and Godot — dramatically lowered the cost of development.

Characteristics

Indie games are typically smaller in scope than AAA titles, with shorter playtimes and often stylised 2D or low-fidelity 3D graphics. Freedom from publisher oversight allows unusual themes, genre hybrids, and personal storytelling. Many are funded through crowdfunding (notably Kickstarter), early access sales, or patronage.

Notable examples

Landmark indie successes include Braid (2008), Super Meat Boy (2010), Fez (2012), Undertale (2015), Stardew Valley (2016), Hollow Knight (2017), Celeste (2018), Hades (2020), and Vampire Survivors (2022). Minecraft (2009) began as an indie project before becoming one of the best-selling games of all time. Festivals such as the Independent Games Festival and IndieCade celebrate the field, and the 2012 documentary Indie Game: The Movie brought its culture to wider attention.

Industry impact

The indie sector has revived genres abandoned by major publishers, pioneered mechanics later adopted by mainstream titles, and provided an entry point for diverse developers. Successful indie studios may self-publish, sign with boutique publishers, or be acquired by larger companies.
