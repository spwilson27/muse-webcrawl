The Canadian Alliance (French: Alliance canadienne), formally the Canadian Reform Conservative Alliance (Alliance réformiste-conservatrice canadienne), was a Canadian federal political party that existed from 2000 to 2003. It was the successor to the Reform Party of Canada and the principal right-of-centre opposition party during its existence, serving as the Official Opposition in the House of Commons.

History

The Alliance grew out of the "United Alternative" initiative launched by Reform Party leader Preston Manning, which sought to unite Canada's fragmented conservative movement. On March 27, 2000, the Reform Party dissolved and reconstituted itself as the Canadian Alliance. Stockwell Day, a former Alberta cabinet minister, defeated Manning in the party's first leadership election in July 2000.

In the 2000 federal election, the Alliance won 66 seats, all but two of them west of Ontario, and remained the Official Opposition to Jean Chrétien's Liberals. Internal divisions over Day's leadership led to a caucus revolt in 2001 and his replacement by Stephen Harper in March 2002.

Merger

Following extended negotiations, Harper and Progressive Conservative leader Peter MacKay announced an agreement in October 2003 to merge their parties. The memberships of both parties ratified the merger, and on December 7, 2003, the Canadian Alliance and the Progressive Conservative Party of Canada formally united as the Conservative Party of Canada, with Harper elected its first leader in March 2004.
