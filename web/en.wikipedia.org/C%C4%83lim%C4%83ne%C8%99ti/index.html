Călimănești is a town in Vâlcea County, Romania, situated on the right bank of the Olt River in the foothills of the Southern Carpathians. Together with the neighbouring resort of Căciulata, it forms one of Romania's best-known spa towns, famed for its mineral springs.

History

The settlement grew up around the Cozia Monastery, founded in 1388 by Mircea the Elder, one of the most important medieval monuments in Romania. The therapeutic qualities of the local mineral waters were recognised in the 19th century, and the waters of Căciulata won prizes at international exhibitions, including Vienna in 1873.

Spa resort

The sulphurous, chlorinated and thermal waters of Călimănești-Căciulata are used in the treatment of digestive, renal, rheumatic and respiratory conditions. The resort lies at the entrance to the Olt Defile, near the Cozia National Park.
