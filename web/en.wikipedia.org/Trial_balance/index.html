Trial balance

A trial balance is an accounting report that lists the balances of all general ledger accounts of a business at a specific point in time. The accounts are arranged in the order they appear in the ledger, with debit balances in one column and credit balances in another. The totals of the two columns should agree, because in double-entry bookkeeping every transaction is recorded with equal debits and credits.

Purpose

The trial balance serves as a preliminary check on the arithmetical accuracy of the bookkeeping and as a worksheet for preparing the financial statements — the income statement and the balance sheet. If the debit and credit totals differ, an error has been made somewhere in journalising, posting, or extracting balances, and it must be located before the statements can be prepared.

Limitations

Agreement of the trial balance does not prove the accounts are error-free. It will not reveal:

  • a transaction omitted entirely, or one recorded twice;
  • a posting to the wrong account (error of commission) or wrong amount booked to both sides;
  • an error of principle, such as treating capital expenditure as revenue expenditure;
  • compensating errors that cancel each other out;
  • a complete reversal of entries.

Adjusted trial balance

An adjusted trial balance is prepared after adjusting entries — accruals, prepayments, depreciation, and similar period-end corrections — have been made. It is the final listing used to produce the financial statements, and after closing entries a post-closing trial balance contains only balance sheet accounts.
