Max Kleiber

Max Kleiber (4 January 1893 – 5 January 1976) was a Swiss agricultural biologist and professor at the University of California, Davis, known for his work on animal energetics and metabolic scaling.

Kleiber's law

In the 1930s Kleiber found that the basal metabolic rate of mammals scales to the 3/4 power of body mass — B ∝ M3/4 — rather than the 2/3 power predicted by Rubner's surface law. This relationship, known as Kleiber's law, became one of the most famous allometric scaling laws in biology, illustrated by his mouse-to-elephant curve.

Career

Educated at ETH Zurich, Kleiber emigrated to California in 1929 and spent his career at Davis, where he also popularized the use of the kilocalorie in bioenergetics.
