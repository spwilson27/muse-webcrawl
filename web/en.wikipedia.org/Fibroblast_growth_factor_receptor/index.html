Fibroblast growth factor receptor

The fibroblast growth factor receptors (FGFRs) are a family of receptor tyrosine kinases that bind fibroblast growth factors (FGFs). In humans there are four members, FGFR1–FGFR4, whose signalling regulates embryonic development, wound healing, angiogenesis and metabolism.

Structure and signalling

Each receptor has an extracellular region of immunoglobulin-like domains, a single transmembrane helix and an intracellular tyrosine kinase domain. Alternative splicing of the third immunoglobulin domain in FGFR1–3 produces IIIb and IIIc isoforms with different ligand specificities. Binding of FGF, assisted by heparan sulfate, dimerises the receptor and triggers pathways including MAPK/ERK, PI3K/AKT and PLCγ.

Clinical significance

Activating mutations cause skeletal disorders such as achondroplasia (FGFR3) and craniosynostosis syndromes (FGFR1 and FGFR2); altered FGFR signalling is also implicated in cancers, and FGFR inhibitors are used therapeutically, for example in cholangiocarcinoma and urothelial carcinoma.
