Sunset Boulevard (film)

Sunset Boulevard is a 1950 American film noir directed by Billy Wilder and starring William Holden and Gloria Swanson. Named after the famous Los Angeles street, it is widely regarded as one of the greatest films ever made — a dark portrait of Hollywood's treatment of its discarded stars.

Plot

Struggling screenwriter Joe Gillis (Holden), fleeing creditors, hides his car at a decaying mansion on Sunset Boulevard and is drawn into the delusional world of Norma Desmond (Swanson), a forgotten silent-film star who dreams of a comeback with a script she has written for Salome. Living off her money, Gillis edits her script while secretly collaborating on another with young studio reader Betty Schaefer. When Norma discovers the betrayal, she shoots Gillis, whose body is found floating in her pool — the film opens with his posthumous narration. Her final descent of the staircase, declaring "All right, Mr. DeMille, I'm ready for my close-up," is among cinema's most famous endings.

Reception

The film was nominated for eleven Academy Awards and won three, including Best Screenplay. In 1989 it was among the first films selected for the National Film Registry. Andrew Lloyd Webber adapted it into a stage musical in 1993. Swanson, herself a silent-era star, gave the defining performance of her career; Erich von Stroheim plays her ex-husband turned butler, Max.
