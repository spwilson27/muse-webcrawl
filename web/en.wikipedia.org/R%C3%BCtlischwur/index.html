Rütlischwur

The Rütlischwur (German for "Oath of the Rütli") is the legendary oath of the Old Swiss Confederacy, taken by the representatives of the three founding cantons, Uri, Schwyz, and Unterwalden, traditionally dated to 1307. According to the legend, the three men — traditionally named Werner Stauffacher, Walter Fürst, and Arnold von Melchtal — met on the Rütli, a meadow above Lake Lucerne, and swore mutual aid against the Habsburg bailiffs, founding the Swiss Confederation.

Legend and sources

The story is first told in detail in the chronicle of Aegidius Tschudi (16th century) and was popularised throughout Europe by Friedrich Schiller's play Wilhelm Tell (1804), whose oath scene has the confederates swear: "We shall be a single people of brothers." Historically, the Federal Charter of 1291 is the documentary basis of the Confederation, and modern historians treat the oath as legend rather than fact, though it remains foundational to Swiss national identity.

Significance

The Rütli meadow became a national shrine. In 1940, General Henri Guisan assembled the Swiss officer corps there for the Rütlirapport, affirming Switzerland's will to resist invasion during the Second World War. The oath, with its raised three fingers, is still used in Swiss official swearing ceremonies.
