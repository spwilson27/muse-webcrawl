The Earth Summit was the United Nations Conference on Environment and Development (UNCED), held in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil, from 3 to 14 June 1992. It was the first major international conference to place environmental concerns on the global political agenda, producing the foundational documents of modern international environmental law and coining the international commitment to "sustainable development."

Background

Convened on the 20th anniversary of the 1972 Stockholm Conference on the Human Environment, the Earth Summit followed the 1987 Brundtland Report (Our Common Future), which defined sustainable development. Delegations from 172 governments, 2,400 NGO representatives, and some 10,000 journalists attended, alongside a parallel Global Forum of civil-society organisations.

Agreements

  • Agenda 21 — a comprehensive non-binding action plan for sustainable development into the 21st century.
  • Rio Declaration on Environment and Development — 27 principles, including the precautionary principle (Principle 15) and the "polluter pays" principle (Principle 16).
  • Statement of Forest Principles — non-binding principles on the management and conservation of forests.
  • UN Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) — the treaty that later produced the Kyoto Protocol (1997) and the Paris Agreement (2015).
  • Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD) — covering conservation, sustainable use, and benefit-sharing of genetic resources.
  • Convention to Combat Desertification was negotiated subsequently (1994).

Outcomes and legacy

The summit created the UN Commission on Sustainable Development to monitor Agenda 21 and established that developed countries bear "common but differentiated responsibilities" for environmental damage. Critics noted that the agreements were largely non-binding and underfunded, and that deforestation, greenhouse-gas emissions, and biodiversity loss continued to accelerate. Nonetheless, Rio 1992 remains the cornerstone of international climate and environmental governance.

Subsequent summits

Follow-up conferences included the World Summit on Sustainable Development (Rio+10, Johannesburg, 2002), the UN Conference on Sustainable Development (Rio+20, 2012), and subsequent annual UNFCCC Conferences of the Parties (COPs).
