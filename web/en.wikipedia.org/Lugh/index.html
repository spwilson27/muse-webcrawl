Lugh (Old Irish: Lug) is one of the most prominent gods in Irish mythology, a member of the Tuatha Dé Danann famed as a master of all arts — hence his epithet Samildánach, "many-skilled".

Mythology

Lugh was the son of Cian of the Tuatha Dé Danann and Ethniu, daughter of the Fomorian tyrant Balor. Admitted to the court of King Nuada by demonstrating mastery of every craft, he led the Tuatha Dé Danann to victory at the Second Battle of Mag Tuired, where he slew Balor with a sling stone, fulfilling a prophecy. He was the father of the hero Cú Chulainn. The harvest festival of Lughnasadh (1 August) was said to have been instituted by him, and he is often compared with the Gaulish god Lugus and the Welsh Lleu Llaw Gyffes.
