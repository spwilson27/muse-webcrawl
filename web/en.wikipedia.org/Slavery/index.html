Slavery

Slavery has existed in many cultures since ancient times

Slavery is a system in which principles of property law are applied to people, allowing individuals to own, buy and sell other individuals. An enslaved person is deprived of personal liberty and compelled to perform labour or services, typically under coercion and without the freedom to withdraw. Slavery has been practised in most societies throughout recorded history, reaching particular scale in the transatlantic slave trade of the 16th to 19th centuries.

History

Slavery existed in ancient Egypt, Greece and Rome, in the Islamic world, in Africa, Asia and pre-Columbian and post-contact Americas. The Atlantic slave trade forcibly transported an estimated 12.5 million Africans to the Americas. Abolition movements of the 18th and 19th centuries led to legal emancipation across Europe and the Americas, but slavery persisted elsewhere well into the 20th century, and Mauritania became the last country to formally criminalise it, in 2007.

Modern slavery

Although legal slavery is now abolished worldwide, contemporary forms — often grouped under "modern slavery" — include forced labour, human trafficking, debt bondage, forced marriage and child labour. International bodies estimate tens of millions of people remain in such conditions. The 1926 Slavery Convention and Article 4 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights prohibit slavery in all its forms.
