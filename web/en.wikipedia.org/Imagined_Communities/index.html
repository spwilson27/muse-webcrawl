Imagined Communities

Imagined Communities: Reflections on the Origin and Spread of Nationalism (1983).

Imagined Communities: Reflections on the Origin and Spread of Nationalism is a book by the historian and political scientist Benedict Anderson, first published in 1983 and revised in 1991 and 2006. It is one of the most influential works in the study of nationalism. Anderson defines the nation as "an imagined political community — and imagined as both inherently limited and sovereign."

Argument

Anderson argues that a nation is imagined because its members will never know most of their fellow members, yet in the minds of each lives the image of their communion; it is limited because even the largest nation has finite boundaries beyond which lie other nations; and it is a community because it is conceived as a deep, horizontal comradeship. He traces nationalism's emergence to the decline of religious and dynastic worldviews and, centrally, to print capitalism: the mass production of books and newspapers in vernacular languages created unified fields of communication and a new sense of simultaneity in "homogeneous, empty time."

Influence

The book shifted nationalism studies toward cultural and historical explanations, emphasizing language, print media, census-taking, maps, and museums in the construction of national identity. It has been widely cited in history, anthropology, and political science, and its title phrase has entered general scholarly vocabulary.
