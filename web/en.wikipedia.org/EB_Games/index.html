EB Games

EB Games (formerly Electronics Boutique) is a computer and video game retailer that originated in the United States. Founded in 1977 by James Kim as an electronics kiosk business in Pennsylvania, the company grew into a major mall-based chain selling video games, consoles and accessories. In 2005 EB Games merged with GameStop in a deal valued at about US$1.44 billion, after which most EB Games stores in the United States were rebranded as GameStop, while the EB Games brand continued in Canada, Australia, New Zealand and parts of Europe for some years.

History

Electronics Boutique began in 1977 selling electronic items such as calculators and digital watches from kiosks in shopping malls. As the home video game market expanded in the 1980s and 1990s, the company shifted its focus to video game software and hardware, becoming one of the largest specialist game retailers in North America. It expanded internationally, including into Australia and New Zealand, where the EB Games name remained prominent.

In April 2005 GameStop and EB Games announced a merger, completed in October 2005. The combined company operated both brands for a transition period. In Canada, EB Games remained GameStop's trading name until 2021, when the Canadian stores were rebranded as GameStop; in Australia and New Zealand the EB Games brand continued under GameStop ownership, alongside the Zing Pop Culture collectibles stores.

Operations

EB Games stores sold new and pre-owned video games, consoles, accessories and pop-culture merchandise, and ran trade-in programmes for used games. Its loyalty programmes included the EB World programme in Australia.
