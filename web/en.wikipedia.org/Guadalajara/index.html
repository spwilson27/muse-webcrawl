Guadalajara
Country: Mexico
State: Jalisco
Founded: 14 February 1542
Population (metro): about 5.3 million
Elevation: 1,566 m

Guadalajara is the capital and largest city of the Mexican state of Jalisco and the seat of the municipality of Guadalajara. Located in Mexico's central Pacific region, it is the core of the country's third-largest metropolitan area and is widely regarded as the cultural heartland of mariachi music, tequila and the Mexican hat dance.

History

The city was founded by Spanish conquistador Cristóbal de Oñate in 1542, after three earlier settlements failed owing to conflicts with indigenous peoples. It became the capital of the Kingdom of Nueva Galicia and an important colonial city. In 1810, Miguel Hidalgo issued his decree abolishing slavery in Mexico from Guadalajara during the War of Independence.

Culture

Guadalajara hosts the Guadalajara International Book Fair, the largest Spanish-language book fair in the world, and an international film festival. Landmarks include the Guadalajara Cathedral, the Hospicio Cabañas (a UNESCO World Heritage Site with murals by José Clemente Orozco), and the Teatro Degollado. The city hosted matches of the 1970 and 1986 FIFA World Cups.

Economy

The metropolitan area is a major centre for software and electronics manufacturing — sometimes nicknamed Mexico's Silicon Valley — as well as for footwear, textiles and food processing.
