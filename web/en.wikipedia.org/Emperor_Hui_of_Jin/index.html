Emperor Hui of Jin (259 – 8 January 307), personal name Sima Zhong, courtesy name Zhengdu, was the second emperor of the Western Jin dynasty in China. Emperor Hui was a developmentally disabled ruler, and throughout his reign, there was constant internecine fighting between regents, imperial princes (his uncles and cousins), and his empress Jia Nanfeng for power.

Reign

Sima Zhong succeeded his father, Emperor Wu, in 290 despite widespread court awareness of his incapacity. Real power passed first to Yang Jun, then to Empress Jia Nanfeng, whose manipulation and purges destabilised the court. After her downfall in 300, imperial princes fought for control of the emperor in the conflict known as the War of the Eight Princes (291–306), which devastated northern China and fatally weakened the dynasty.

The "meat congee" anecdote

Emperor Hui is famous in Chinese historical tradition for his reported response when told his people had no rice to eat and were starving: "Why don't they eat meat congee (porridge)?" The anecdote has become a byword for an out-of-touch ruler, similar to the Western "let them eat cake".

Death

In 306 the prince Sima Yue emerged victorious in the war and brought the emperor back to Luoyang. Emperor Hui died in early 307, traditionally believed to have been poisoned on Sima Yue's orders, and was succeeded by his brother, Emperor Huai.
