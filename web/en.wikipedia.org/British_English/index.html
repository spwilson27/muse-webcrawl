British English

British English (BrE) is the set of varieties of the English language native to the United Kingdom, especially the standardised forms used in England. It differs from other varieties, such as American English, in spelling, vocabulary and pronunciation.

Features

Characteristic spellings include colour, centre, organise and travelled. Distinctive vocabulary includes words such as lorry, holiday (for vacation) and queue. Received Pronunciation was historically the prestige accent, though modern Standard British English is not tied to a single accent.

History

The variety developed from Early Modern English in Britain and was spread worldwide through the British Empire, influencing Commonwealth English varieties.
