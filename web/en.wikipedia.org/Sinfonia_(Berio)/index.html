Sinfonia is a composition by the Italian composer Luciano Berio for orchestra and eight amplified voices, written in 1968–69 for the 125th anniversary of the New York Philharmonic and dedicated to Leonard Bernstein. It is one of the most celebrated works of the post-war avant-garde, famous especially for its third movement, a vast musical collage built around the scherzo of Mahler's Second Symphony and layered with quotations from across the orchestral repertoire and spoken and sung texts.

Movements and texts

The first movement sets fragments from Claude Lévi-Strauss's The Raw and the Cooked, meditating on myth and its structures. The second, "O King", is a quiet memorial to Martin Luther King Jr., in which the voices gradually assemble his name from individual phonemes. The third movement, the centrepiece, superimposes on Mahler's "Resurrection" scherzo a stream of quotations — from Beethoven, Brahms, Debussy, Ravel, Stravinsky, Schoenberg, Webern, Boulez, Stockhausen and Berio himself — while the voices speak and sing texts from Samuel Beckett's The Unnamable, slogans of the May 1968 Paris protests, and other sources. The brief fourth movement is a subdued coda, and the fifth (added in 1969) draws together material and references from the preceding movements.

Premiere and reception

The first four movements were premiered by the New York Philharmonic under Berio on 10 October 1968 with the Swingle Singers as the vocal ensemble; the complete five-movement version followed in 1969. Critics hailed the work as a landmark of musical postmodernism, and its third movement is frequently cited as the quintessential musical collage. Sinfonia has been recorded many times and remains a fixture of the modern orchestral repertoire.
