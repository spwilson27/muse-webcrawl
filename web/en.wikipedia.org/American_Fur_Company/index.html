American Fur Company

American Fur Company
Industry: Fur trade
Founded: 1808
Founder: John Jacob Astor
Defunct: 1847
Headquarters: New York City

The American Fur Company (AFC) was founded by John Jacob Astor in 1808 and became one of the largest and most profitable businesses in the early United States, dominating the fur trade in the central and western United States during its first decades and monopolizing trade in the Great Lakes region and the Columbia River region.

History

Astor's company established posts across the Midwest and West, launched the Pacific Fur Company venture that founded Astoria, Oregon, and employed famous mountain men. After the War of 1812, the AFC expanded aggressively, aided by a 1816 law excluding foreign traders.

Decline

Astor sold his interest in 1834 as beaver populations collapsed and fashion shifted from beaver hats to silk. The company lingered under other owners before dissolving in 1847.
