Dabbala Rajagopal "Raj" Reddy (born 13 June 1937) is an Indian-American computer scientist and a pioneer of artificial intelligence, particularly speech recognition. He is a professor at Carnegie Mellon University, where he served as founding director of the Robotics Institute (1979) and dean of the School of Computer Science.

Career

Reddy earned his bachelor's degree from Guindy Engineering College, a master's from the University of New South Wales, and his PhD from Stanford University in 1966 under John McCarthy. After teaching at Stanford, he moved to Carnegie Mellon in 1969. His research groups built some of the first large-vocabulary continuous speech recognition systems, including Hearsay, Harpy and Sphinx, and he contributed to human–computer interaction, robotics and AI for social good, including the Million Book Project for universal digital libraries.

Honours

Reddy shared the 1994 ACM Turing Award with Edward Feigenbaum "for pioneering the design and construction of large scale artificial intelligence systems". He has also received the Padma Bhushan from India (2001), the Legion of Honour from France, and the Vannevar Bush Award, and is a member of the US National Academies of Sciences and Engineering.
