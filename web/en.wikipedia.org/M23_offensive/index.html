M23 offensive

Date2022–present
LocationNorth Kivu, Democratic Republic of the Congo
ResultOngoing
BelligerentsM23 (allegedly supported by Rwanda) vs. FARDC, MONUSCO and allied forces

The M23 offensive is an ongoing military offensive in the North Kivu province of the Democratic Republic of the Congo, launched by the March 23 Movement (M23) rebel group beginning in March 2022. The offensive is part of the wider Kivu conflict in the eastern Congo.

Background

M23 takes its name from the 23 March 2009 peace agreement between the Congolese government and the National Congress for the Defence of the People (CNDP). The group first mutinied in 2012 and seized the provincial capital Goma before being defeated in 2013 by the Congolese army and the UN Force Intervention Brigade. Fighters fled to Uganda and Rwanda, where they remained until resurfacing in late 2021.

Course of the offensive

After early attacks in November 2021, M23 launched a major offensive in March 2022, capturing the strategic border town of Bunagana in June of that year and large areas of Rutshuru territory. Despite regional ceasefire efforts, including the Luanda and Nairobi processes, fighting continued and displaced hundreds of thousands of civilians. In January 2025 M23 forces captured Goma, marking a major escalation.

International response

United Nations expert reports have accused Rwanda of supporting M23, including with troops on Congolese territory — charges Kigali denies. The offensive has drawn condemnation from the African Union, the European Union, and the United States, and has deepened the humanitarian crisis in eastern Congo.
