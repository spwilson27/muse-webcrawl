Toluca, officially Toluca de Lerdo, is the capital city of the State of Mexico in central Mexico and the centre of the country's fifth-largest metropolitan area. It lies in the Toluca Valley, about 63 km west-southwest of Mexico City, overlooked by the snow-capped volcano Nevado de Toluca (Xinantécatl). At 2,667 metres above sea level it is the highest state capital in Mexico, with a correspondingly cool climate. Toluca is a major industrial centre — one of the most important in the country — as well as the home of Deportivo Toluca F.C., one of Mexico's most successful football clubs.

History

The Toluca Valley was settled in pre-Hispanic times by Matlatzinca, Otomí and other peoples; the Matlatzinca city of Calixtlahuaca, with its circular temple to the wind god Ehecatl, stood nearby and was conquered by the Aztec emperor Axayacatl in the 1470s. After the Spanish conquest the modern city was founded in 1522 and grew as an agricultural and textile centre; lands in the valley were granted to Hernán Cortés as part of the Marquesado del Valle de Oaxaca. During the War of Independence, Miguel Hidalgo's army passed through shortly after the Battle of Monte de las Cruces (1810), and Toluca became the state capital in 1830. The suffix "de Lerdo" honours Sebastián Lerdo de Tejada.

Geography and climate

The city occupies the floor of the high Toluca Valley near the headwaters of the Lerma River. Its elevation gives it a subtropical highland climate with cool nights year-round, mild days and a rainy season from June to September; frosts are common in winter. Nevado de Toluca, an extinct volcano and national park with twin crater lakes (Lakes of the Sun and Moon), rises to the southwest and is a popular excursion.

Economy and culture

Toluca and its suburbs such as Metepec form a major manufacturing hub for automobiles, pharmaceuticals, food processing and beverages, served by Toluca International Airport, which for a time functioned as an alternate airport for Mexico City. Landmarks include the Cosmovitral, a former market hall converted into a botanical garden wrapped in one of the world's largest stained-glass murals, by Leopoldo Flores; the cathedral and the arched Los Portales arcade in the centre; and museums devoted to the state's popular arts. The city is famous for chorizo, produced in red and green varieties. Its football team, the "Diablos Rojos" of Deportivo Toluca, play at the Estadio Nemesio Díez.
