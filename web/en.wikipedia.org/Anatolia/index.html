Anatolia, also called Asia Minor, is a large peninsula in Western Asia that forms the greater part of the Republic of Turkey. It is bounded by the Black Sea to the north, the Mediterranean to the south, and the Aegean to the west, and separated from Europe by the Bosphorus, the Sea of Marmara, and the Dardanelles.

Geography

The interior is a high plateau (averaging about 1,000 m) framed by mountain ranges — the Pontic Mountains along the Black Sea and the Taurus Mountains along the Mediterranean. The region is seismically active, lying along the North and East Anatolian faults. Its climate ranges from Mediterranean on the coasts to continental steppe in the interior.

History

Anatolia is one of the world's oldest continuously inhabited regions and a cradle of early agriculture and settlement — Neolithic Çatalhöyük is among the earliest known farming towns. It was home to the Hittite Empire in the Bronze Age, and later to Phrygians, Lydians, Greeks, Persians, Romans, and Byzantines. After the Seljuk victory at Manzikert in 1071 it was progressively Turkified, becoming the heartland of the Sultanate of Rum and then of the Ottoman Empire. The Republic of Turkey was proclaimed from its new capital, Ankara, in 1923.
