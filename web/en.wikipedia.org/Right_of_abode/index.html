Right of abode

The right of abode is an immigration status in the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland that gives a person the unconditional right to live and work in the United Kingdom, free from immigration control. A person with the right of abode is not subject to immigration control when entering the United Kingdom and cannot ordinarily be deported.

The concept was introduced by the Immigration Act 1971. British citizens automatically have the right of abode. Certain Commonwealth citizens who had the right of abode before 1983 retained it, provided they had not ceased to be Commonwealth citizens.

Proof of the right of abode

The right of abode can be evidenced by a valid British citizen passport describing the holder as a British citizen, or by a certificate of entitlement to the right of abode placed in a foreign passport. Holders of the right of abode are exempt from the requirement to obtain leave to enter or remain.

Hong Kong

In Hong Kong, the term "right of abode" (Chinese: 居留權) refers to the status of a permanent resident of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region under the Basic Law and the Immigration Ordinance. Hong Kong permanent residents enjoy the right of abode in Hong Kong, including the right to land, to remain without any condition of stay, and not to be deported.
