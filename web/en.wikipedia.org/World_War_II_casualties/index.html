World War II casualties are estimated at between 70 and 85 million deaths, making the conflict the deadliest in human history. Fatalities included both military personnel and civilians, the latter dying from military action, war crimes, genocide (including the Holocaust), strategic bombing, disease, and war-related famine.

Totals by country

The Soviet Union suffered the highest absolute toll, with estimates of roughly 24 to 27 million military and civilian deaths. China lost an estimated 15 to 20 million people. Germany lost between 6.9 and 7.4 million, Poland about 5.9 to 6 million (including 3 million Polish Jews murdered in the Holocaust), and Japan roughly 2.5 to 3.1 million. The United States lost about 419,000, mostly military personnel.

Civilian deaths

Civilians accounted for the majority of deaths. Around 6 million Jews were murdered in the Holocaust, alongside millions of other victims of Nazi persecution, including Roma, Soviet prisoners of war, and people with disabilities. In Asia, Japanese war crimes caused millions of Chinese civilian deaths. War-related famines, including the Bengal famine of 1943 and the Soviet famine, added millions more.

Estimation difficulties

Exact figures remain contested because of incomplete records, destroyed documentation, differing counting methods, and politically influenced postwar estimates. Figures given by historians represent ranges based on demographic studies and archival research.
