National Federation of Coffee Growers of Colombia

Founded: 1927

Headquarters: Bogotá, Colombia

Spanish name: Federación Nacional de Cafeteros de Colombia (FNC)

Known for: Juan Valdez advertising character; Café de Colombia

The National Federation of Coffee Growers of Colombia (Spanish: Federación Nacional de Cafeteros de Colombia, FNC) is a non-profit business organisation representing Colombia's coffee growers. Founded in 1927, it represents more than 500,000 coffee-producing families, most of them smallholders, and is one of the largest rural non-governmental organisations in the world. It administers the National Coffee Fund, provides research, extension services and infrastructure to growers, and markets Colombian coffee internationally.

History and functions

The FNC was created in 1927 at a congress of coffee growers in Medellín. It established the National Coffee Fund (FoNC) in 1940, financed by a coffee export contribution, which funds services such as a guaranteed purchase system, rural roads, schools and health programmes in coffee regions. Its research institute, Cenicafé (founded 1938), developed rust-resistant coffee varieties such as the Colombia and Castillo cultivars. Through its extension service, the federation provides technical assistance to growers across the country's coffee departments.

Juan Valdez and branding

In 1959 the federation created the fictional character Juan Valdez — a coffee farmer accompanied by his mule Conchita — to promote 100% Colombian coffee, one of the most successful commodity advertising campaigns in history. The federation's company Procafecol launched the Juan Valdez Café chain of coffee shops in 2002. "Café de Colombia" is protected as a geographical indication in the European Union.
