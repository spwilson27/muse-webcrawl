Elizabeth Garrett Anderson

Elizabeth Garrett Anderson (9 June 1836 – 17 December 1917) was an English physician and suffragist. She was the first woman to qualify in Britain as a physician and surgeon, and co-founded the first hospital staffed by women in Britain. She was also the first female dean of a British medical school, the first woman in Britain to be elected to a school board, and the first female mayor and magistrate in Britain.

Born:9 June 1836, Whitechapel, London, England
Died:17 December 1917 (aged 81), Aldeburgh, Suffolk
Known for:First woman to qualify as a physician in Britain

Education and qualification

Born in Whitechapel, London, Garrett was inspired to pursue medicine after meeting Elizabeth Blackwell, the first woman on the British medical register. Barred from medical schools, she studied privately and in 1865 obtained the Licence of the Society of Apothecaries — exploiting a rule that did not explicitly exclude women — becoming the first woman qualified in England. The Society then changed its rules to prevent other women from following the same route. She earned an MD from the University of Paris in 1870.

Career

In 1866 she founded the St Mary's Dispensary for Women in London, which grew into the New Hospital for Women (later the Elizabeth Garrett Anderson Hospital), staffed entirely by women. In 1874 she co-founded the London School of Medicine for Women, the first medical school in Britain to train women, and served as its dean from 1883.

Suffrage and later life

Garrett Anderson was a leading figure in the women's suffrage movement alongside her sister Millicent Fawcett. In 1908 she became mayor of Aldeburgh, the first woman mayor in England. She retired to Aldeburgh, where she died in 1917.
