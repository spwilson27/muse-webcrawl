Pedagogy of the Oppressed


Paulo Freire (1921–1997)

Pedagogy of the Oppressed (Portuguese: Pedagogia do Oprimido) is a book by Brazilian educator Paulo Freire, written in Portuguese in 1967–68 during his exile in Chile and first published in English in 1970. It is one of the founding texts of critical pedagogy and one of the most cited works in education, proposing that teaching should treat learners as co-creators of knowledge rather than passive recipients, and that education is inseparable from the struggle against oppression.

Main ideas

Freire attacks the "banking model" of education, in which the teacher deposits information into students, as an instrument that dehumanises and domesticates. Against it he sets problem-posing education: a dialogical practice in which teacher and student learn together by investigating "generative themes" drawn from learners' own lives. Through dialogue, the oppressed develop conscientização — "conscientization", or critical consciousness of the social and political contradictions of their situation — and move toward praxis, reflection and action upon the world in order to transform it. The book draws on Marxism, phenomenology, Hegel's master–slave dialectic, and liberationist currents in Latin American Christianity.

Influence

The book emerged from Freire's literacy campaigns among Brazilian peasants, notably the 1963 Angicos experiment, which taught hundreds of sugarcane workers to read in weeks. After the 1964 military coup Freire was jailed and exiled; the book was banned in Brazil until 1974. It has since become a canonical text in teacher education worldwide and a reference point for liberation theology and progressive social movements, while attracting criticism from conservatives who view it as political indoctrination.
