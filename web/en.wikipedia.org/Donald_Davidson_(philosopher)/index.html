Donald Davidson (philosopher)

Donald Herbert Davidson (1917–2003) was an American philosopher. His work had a significant influence in the fields of philosophy of mind, philosophy of language, action theory, philosophy of science, and epistemology. He served as the Willis S. and Marion Slusser Professor of Philosophy at the University of California, Berkeley.

Philosophy

Davidson is best known for his theory of meaning and truth, which argued that a theory of meaning for a language should take the form of a Tarskian truth theory; and for anomalous monism, the view that mental events are physical events but cannot be reduced to laws governing the physical. His theory of radical interpretation explored how one could understand the utterances of a speaker of an unknown language.

Action and events

In his classic paper "Actions, Reasons, and Causes" (1963), Davidson defended the view that reasons can be causes of actions, and he developed an ontology of events as particulars. His essay collection Essays on Actions and Events (1980) and Inquiries into Truth and Interpretation (1984) are landmarks of analytic philosophy.
