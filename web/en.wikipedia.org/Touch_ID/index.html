Touch ID is an electronic fingerprint recognition feature designed and released by Apple Inc., which allows users to unlock devices, make purchases in the various Apple digital media stores, and authenticate Apple Pay online or in apps. It was introduced with the iPhone 5s in 2013.

Technology

The Touch ID sensor is a capacitive reader built into the Home button (or, on later devices, the top or side button), ringed by a stainless-steel detection ring. It captures a high-resolution image of the fingerprint's subepidermal layers at 500 ppi. Fingerprint data is converted into a mathematical representation stored in the Secure Enclave of the device's processor; it never leaves the device and is not stored as an image.

Generations

The second-generation sensor, introduced with the iPhone 6s in 2015, was roughly twice as fast. Touch ID later appeared in the iPad line, MacBook Pro and MacBook Air laptops, and the Magic Keyboard. On most flagship iPhones after 2017 it was replaced by the Face ID facial-recognition system, though it remained in the iPhone SE line and in power-button form on the iPad Air and iPad mini.

Security

Apple states that the probability of a random finger matching an enrolled fingerprint is about 1 in 50,000. The device requires the passcode after a restart, after 48 hours of inactivity, or after five unsuccessful fingerprint attempts.
