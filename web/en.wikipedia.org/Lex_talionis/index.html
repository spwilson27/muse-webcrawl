Lex talionis

Lex talionis, the law of retaliation, is the principle that a person who has injured another should receive a punishment of a similar degree — expressed in the famous formula "an eye for an eye, a tooth for a tooth". The principle appears in the Code of Hammurabi (c. 1754 BC), in the Hebrew Bible (Exodus 21:23–25, Leviticus 24, Deuteronomy 19), and in the Roman Twelve Tables.

Interpretation

Scholars debate whether the ancient provisions were meant literally or as a basis for monetary compensation. In rabbinic Judaism, the formula was interpreted as requiring financial restitution equivalent to the value of the injury. The principle's historical function was to limit vengeance: punishment was capped at equivalence, preventing the escalation of feuds.

Legacy

The lex talionis influenced early Roman law and remains a reference point in discussions of retributive justice and proportionality in punishment. Jesus's teaching in the Sermon on the Mount — "turn the other cheek" — is commonly read as a response to this principle. See also retributive justice.
