Karush–Kuhn–Tucker conditions

In mathematical optimisation, the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) conditions, also known as the Kuhn–Tucker conditions, are first derivative tests (necessary conditions) for a solution in nonlinear programming to be optimal, provided that some regularity conditions are satisfied. Allowing inequality constraints, the KKT approach generalises the method of Lagrange multipliers, which allows only equality constraints.

Formulation

Consider the problem of minimising f(x) subject to inequality constraints gi(x) ≤ 0 and equality constraints hj(x) = 0. With Lagrange multipliers μi ≥ 0 for the inequalities and λj for the equalities, a local optimum x* must satisfy:

  • Stationarity: ∇f(x*) + Σ μi∇gi(x*) + Σ λj∇hj(x*) = 0
  • Primal feasibility: gi(x*) ≤ 0 and hj(x*) = 0
  • Dual feasibility: μi ≥ 0
  • Complementary slackness: μigi(x*) = 0 for all i

History

The conditions are named after William Karush, who stated them in his 1939 master's thesis, and Harold W. Kuhn and Albert W. Tucker, who published them in 1951. For convex problems satisfying a constraint qualification such as Slater's condition, the KKT conditions are also sufficient for global optimality.

Applications

The KKT conditions underlie many algorithms in nonlinear programming, economics (where the multipliers are interpreted as shadow prices), control theory, and machine learning — for example in the derivation of support vector machines.
