Functional food

A functional food is a food claimed to have an additional health function — usually one related to promoting health or reducing disease risk — beyond its basic nutritional value, by adding new ingredients or more of existing ingredients. Examples include foods fortified with vitamins, minerals, probiotics, plant sterols or fibre, such as iodised salt, vitamin D milk, probiotic yoghurts and cholesterol-lowering spreads.

History and regulation

The concept was developed in Japan in the 1980s, where the government created a regulatory category for "Foods for Specified Health Use" (FOSHU) in 1991. In the European Union, health claims on foods are assessed by the European Food Safety Authority, while in the United States the Food and Drug Administration permits certain authorised and qualified health claims. There is no single statutory definition of "functional food" in the US or EU.

Criticism

Nutrition scientists caution that health claims for individual products may exaggerate benefits, that effects of isolated added ingredients may differ from those in whole foods, and that functional foods are no substitute for an overall balanced diet.
