Malay language

Malay (Bahasa Melayu) is an Austronesian language spoken across maritime Southeast Asia. It is the national language of Malaysia, Brunei and Singapore, and — in a standardised form called Indonesian — of Indonesia, giving the Malay–Indonesian continuum well over 200 million speakers, most of them second-language users of this historic lingua franca of trade and Islam.

History

Old Malay, influenced by Sanskrit, appears in 7th-century Srivijaya inscriptions. Classical Malay flourished at the Malacca Sultanate and spread with Islam, written in the Arabic-based Jawi script and absorbing Arabic, Persian, Tamil, Portuguese, Dutch and English vocabulary. Colonial-era standardisation in the Dutch East Indies and British Malaya produced the two modern standards.

Grammar and features

Malay is agglutinative, using prefixes and suffixes such as meN-, ber-, -kan and -lah to derive words; it has no grammatical gender, no verb conjugation for tense (time is shown by adverbs and aspect markers), simple plurals by reduplication, and a classifier system for counting. Word order is basically subject–verb–object. The modern standard is written mainly in Latin script (Rumi), with Jawi retained officially in Brunei and religious contexts in Malaysia.
