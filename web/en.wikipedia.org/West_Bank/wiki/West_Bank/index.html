West Bank

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The West Bank is a landlocked territory in West Asia, located west of the Jordan River. It is bordered by Jordan and the Dead Sea to the east and by Israel to the south, west and north. Together with the Gaza Strip, it constitutes the two main territories of the State of Palestine. The West Bank has an area of about 5,655 square kilometres and a population of roughly three million Palestinians, alongside several hundred thousand Israeli settlers.

Name and geography

The name "West Bank" derives from the territory's position on the western bank of the Jordan River, in contrast to Jordan's East Bank. The landscape is dominated by a north–south mountain ridge, with the fertile Jordan Valley to the east descending to the Dead Sea shore, the lowest land on Earth. Major cities include Hebron, Nablus, Ramallah, Bethlehem, Jericho and Jenin; East Jerusalem is claimed by Palestinians as their capital.

History

Under the 1947 United Nations Partition Plan the area was allotted to an Arab state, but in the 1948 Arab–Israeli War it was occupied and later annexed by Jordan. Israel captured the West Bank in the Six-Day War of 1967 and has occupied it since. The Oslo Accords of the 1990s divided the territory into Areas A and B, administered by the Palestinian Authority, and Area C, about sixty percent of the land, under full Israeli control. Israeli settlements, considered illegal under international law by most of the international community, have expanded steadily, while the Israeli West Bank barrier was built largely during the Second Intifada.

Status and administration

The West Bank's final status remains one of the core issues of the Israeli–Palestinian conflict. The Palestinian Authority exercises limited self-government in the urban areas, while Israel retains overall security control. The territory's economy depends heavily on Israel and on international aid, and movement is restricted by checkpoints and the separation barrier.
