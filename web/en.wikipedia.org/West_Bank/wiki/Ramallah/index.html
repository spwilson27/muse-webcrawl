Ramallah (Arabic: رام الله, romanized: Rām Allāh, lit. 'God's Height') is a Palestinian city in the central West Bank, about 10 kilometres north of Jerusalem. Together with its twin city al-Bireh, it serves as the de facto administrative capital of the Palestinian Authority and the seat of the presidency (the Muqata'a), the Palestinian Legislative Council and most ministries, although Palestinians claim East Jerusalem as their capital.

History

Modern Ramallah was founded in the mid-16th century by Haddadeen, Arab Christians who migrated from east of the Jordan River. Long a predominantly Christian town known as a summer resort, it grew under Ottoman and British Mandate rule, came under Jordanian administration in 1948 and Israeli occupation in 1967. The Palestinian Authority took control in December 1995 under the Oslo II arrangements. During the Second Intifada the Muqata'a compound was besieged; Yasser Arafat died in 2004 and is buried there, in a mausoleum and museum.

Modern Ramallah

Ramallah is the Palestinian territories' economic and cultural hub, hosting banks, technology firms, NGOs, diplomatic missions, the Khalil Sakakini Cultural Center and a relatively vibrant café and nightlife scene. The planned city of Rawabi was built to its north.
