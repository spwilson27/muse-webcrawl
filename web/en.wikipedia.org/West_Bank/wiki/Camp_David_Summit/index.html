Camp David Summit

The 2000 Camp David Summit was a meeting between United States President Bill Clinton, Israeli Prime Minister Ehud Barak, and Palestinian Authority President Yasser Arafat, held from 11 to 25 July 2000 at Camp David, Maryland. It was an ultimately unsuccessful attempt to negotiate a final settlement of the Israeli–Palestinian conflict.

Negotiations

Clinton convened the summit to resolve the core final-status issues: borders of a Palestinian state, the status of Jerusalem and the Temple Mount/Haram al-Sharif, Palestinian refugees' right of return, and security arrangements. Barak's proposals — the precise terms of which were never formally published and remain disputed — reportedly offered a Palestinian state in the Gaza Strip and most of the West Bank, with a capital in East Jerusalem suburbs, while Israel would annex settlement blocs. Arafat rejected the proposals, insisting on full sovereignty over East Jerusalem including the Haram al-Sharif and recognition of the right of return.

Aftermath

The summit ended without agreement. Clinton publicly blamed Arafat for the failure, while Palestinian accounts argued the offers fell short of international law and that no formal written proposal was presented. Negotiations continued at Taba in January 2001 but were overtaken by the outbreak of the Second Intifada in September 2000 and Barak's electoral defeat. The summit's collapse is widely regarded as a turning point in the collapse of the Oslo peace process.
