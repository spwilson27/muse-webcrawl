The United Nations Relief and Works Agency for Palestine Refugees in the Near East (UNRWA) is a UN agency established by General Assembly Resolution 302 (IV) of December 1949 to provide relief, education, health care and social services to Palestinians displaced by the 1948 war. It began operations in May 1950 and is unique among UN agencies in serving one population across five fields.

Services and mandate

UNRWA is by far the largest UN operation in the Middle East, employing some 30,000 staff, most of them Palestinian refugees themselves. It runs roughly 700 schools educating about half a million children, primary health clinics, microfinance programmes and food assistance, and operates in 58 recognized refugee camps. Its mandate is renewed periodically by the General Assembly pending a political settlement; unlike UNHCR, it does not resettle refugees, and refugee status under UNRWA's definition passes to descendants.

Controversies

Israel and some critics accuse UNRWA of perpetuating the refugee problem and allege staff links to armed groups; in 2024 several donor states temporarily suspended funding after Israeli allegations against a small number of employees, and Israel's Knesset passed laws restricting the agency's operations. UNRWA and its supporters argue it provides indispensable services and stability, and that only a political solution can end its raison d'être.
