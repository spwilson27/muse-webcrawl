Israeli–Palestinian peace process

The Israeli–Palestinian peace process refers to the intermittent negotiations and agreements between Israel and Palestinian representatives aimed at resolving the Israeli–Palestinian conflict, most prominently the Oslo process of the 1990s and the bilateral talks that followed it.

Major milestones

The Madrid Conference of 1991 launched public bilateral talks. Secret negotiations in Norway produced the Oslo I Accord (1993): Israel recognized the PLO, which in turn recognized Israel's right to exist and renounced terrorism, and the Palestinian Authority was established. Oslo II (1995) divided the West Bank into Areas A, B and C and set out interim self-government arrangements. Prime Minister Yitzhak Rabin was assassinated by a Jewish extremist weeks later.

The Camp David Summit of 2000 failed to reach a final-status agreement, and the Second Intifada followed. Later initiatives included the Quartet's Road Map (2003), the Annapolis Conference (2007), the Kerry negotiations of 2013–14, and the Arab Peace Initiative (2002, re-endorsed 2007), which offered Israel normalization with Arab states in return for withdrawal and a Palestinian state. The Abraham Accords of 2020 normalized Israeli relations with several Arab states outside the Palestinian track.

Status

No final-status agreement has been reached. Settlement expansion, the Hamas–Fatah split, repeated Gaza wars and mutual distrust have stalled substantive negotiations for over a decade; the war begun in October 2023 further reshaped the diplomatic landscape.
