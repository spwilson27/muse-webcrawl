Shimon Peres

Shimon Peres (born Szymon Perski; 2 August 1923 – 28 September 2016) was an Israeli politician and statesman who served twice as prime minister of Israel and from 2007 to 2014 as its ninth president. Over a political career spanning more than six decades, he held nearly every major cabinet post and became one of the last surviving founders of the state.

Career

Peres immigrated to Mandatory Palestine in 1934; most of his family who remained in Europe were murdered in the Holocaust. As director-general of the Defence Ministry in the 1950s he built Israel's defence industries, secured French arms supplies, and oversaw the creation of the Dimona nuclear reactor. He became Labour Party leader in 1977, prime minister in a national-unity rotation government in 1984, and foreign minister under Yitzhak Rabin in 1992.

Peacemaker and later life

As foreign minister, Peres was the chief Israeli architect of the Oslo Accords, for which he shared the 1994 Nobel Peace Prize with Rabin and Yasser Arafat. He succeeded Rabin as prime minister after Rabin's assassination in November 1995 but lost the 1996 election to Benjamin Netanyahu. After later ministerial posts he was elected president in 2007, serving as an elder statesman and promoting his Peres Center for Peace until his death in 2016, aged 93.
