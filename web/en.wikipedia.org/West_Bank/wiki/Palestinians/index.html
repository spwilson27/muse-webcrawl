Palestinians

Total population~14.3 million worldwide (2022 estimate)
LanguagesPalestinian Arabic
RegionsWest Bank, Gaza Strip, Israel, Jordan, Lebanon, Syria, diaspora

Palestinians (Arabic: الفلسطينيون, al-Filasṭīniyyūn) are an Arab ethnonational group native to the Levantine region of Palestine. They descend from the historic inhabitants of the region, with cultural and linguistic Arabization following the seventh-century Muslim conquests. Palestinians today number about 14 million, roughly half living in historic Palestine and half in a worldwide diaspora.

Distribution

About 5.4 million Palestinians live in the West Bank (including East Jerusalem) and Gaza Strip, and around 2 million Palestinian citizens form about a fifth of Israel's population. Jordan hosts over 2 million registered Palestinian refugees, and large communities live in Lebanon, Syria, Chile, the United States and Europe. Some 5.9 million people are registered as refugees with UNRWA, descendants of the more than 700,000 who fled or were expelled in the 1948 Nakba.

Culture and national movement

Palestinian identity crystallized in the early twentieth century around Arabic newspapers, resistance to Zionist settlement and the British Mandate, and fully coalesced as a national movement after 1948 and 1967 under the umbrella of the Palestine Liberation Organization, founded in 1964. Palestinian culture is expressed in embroidery (tatreez), cuisine, dabke dance, olive cultivation and a rich literature — from Ghassan Kanafani to Mahmoud Darwish. National aspirations centre on statehood in the West Bank and Gaza with East Jerusalem as capital, alongside the refugees' claimed right of return.
