Palestinian Authority

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Palestinian Authority (PA), officially the Palestinian National Authority (Arabic: السلطة الوطنية الفلسطينية), is the interim self-government body created in 1994 under the Oslo Accords between Israel and the Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO). It exercises varying degrees of civil administration and internal security control in parts of the West Bank (Areas A and B), and nominally also in the Gaza Strip, although since 2007 Gaza has been governed separately by Hamas. Its administrative capital is Ramallah.

History

Yasser Arafat became the PA's first president in 1996, elected alongside the Palestinian Legislative Council. During the Second Intifada (2000–2005), Israeli forces reoccupied West Bank cities and destroyed much PA infrastructure; Arafat died in 2004 and was succeeded by Mahmoud Abbas. Hamas's victory in the 2006 legislative election produced a short-lived unity government, then open conflict, ending in 2007 with Hamas in control of Gaza and the Fatah-led PA ruling the West Bank — a split that persists despite repeated reconciliation agreements.

Governance and status

The PA runs ministries, schools, health services and police, and coordinates security with Israel, while depending heavily on foreign aid and tax revenues collected by Israel on its behalf under the Paris Protocol. In 2012 the United Nations granted Palestine non-member observer state status, and in 2013 President Abbas decreed that the name "State of Palestine" replace "Palestinian Authority" in official use, though the PA's institutions and Oslo framework remain in place. Critics within Palestinian society frequently accuse the PA of corruption, authoritarianism and failure to advance statehood.
