Washington, D.C., formally the District of Columbia and commonly called Washington or D.C., is the capital city and federal district of the United States. It lies on the east bank of the Potomac River, between Maryland and Virginia, and was founded as the seat of government under the Residence Act of 1790.

History

The site was chosen by George Washington in a compromise between northern and southern states; the city was designed by Pierre Charles L'Enfant with a grid overlaid by diagonal avenues, monumental vistas and public squares. The federal government moved there in 1800. British forces burned the Capitol and White House in 1814. The district originally included land ceded by both Maryland and Virginia; the Virginia portion was retroceded in 1847. Residents gained the right to vote in presidential elections in 1961 (23rd Amendment) and an elected mayor and council under home rule in 1973, but lack voting representation in Congress — a grievance captured on licence plates reading "taxation without representation".

The city today

Washington is home to the White House, the Capitol, the Supreme Court, the Pentagon (in Arlington), and the free Smithsonian museums along the National Mall. Federal government, law, lobbying, defence contracting, education and tourism dominate its economy.

Washington has frequently served as the venue for Middle East diplomacy: the 1993 Oslo Accords signing on the White House lawn, the 1979 Egypt–Israel treaty and the 1994 Israel–Jordan treaty were all hosted or signed there.
