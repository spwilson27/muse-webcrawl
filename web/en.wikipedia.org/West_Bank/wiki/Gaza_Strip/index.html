Gaza Strip

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Gaza Strip (Arabic: قطاع غزة, Qiṭāʿ Ġazzah) is a narrow territory on the eastern coast of the Mediterranean Sea, bordered by Israel to the north and east and Egypt to the southwest. Together with the West Bank, it forms the two territories of the State of Palestine. About 41 kilometres (25 miles) long and 6 to 12 kilometres wide, with an area of some 365 square kilometres, it is one of the most densely populated places on Earth, home to more than two million Palestinians, a majority of them refugees or descendants of refugees from the 1948 war.

History

Gaza is an ancient city on the route between Egypt and the Levant. The Strip took shape after the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, when the 1949 armistice lines left it under Egyptian administration and it filled with refugees. Israel captured it in the Six-Day War of 1967 and occupied it, building settlements. Under the Oslo Accords of the 1990s, the Palestinian Authority assumed administration of most of the territory, and in 2005 Israel unilaterally withdrew its settlers and ground troops. In 2007, after winning the 2006 Palestinian legislative election and a brief civil conflict with Fatah, Hamas took control of the Strip; Israel and Egypt then imposed a blockade, and repeated wars followed between Israel and Gaza-based armed groups.

Geography and economy

The territory consists of five governorates centred on Gaza City, Jabalia, Deir al-Balah, Khan Yunis and Rafah. Its economy, constrained by the blockade, conflict damage and rapid population growth, has suffered chronic high unemployment; residents depend heavily on international aid, much of it channelled through UNRWA.
