The Palestinian Legislative Council (PLC; Arabic: المجلس التشريعي الفلسطيني) is the unicameral legislature of the Palestinian Authority, established under the Oslo Accords. Its 132 members are elected from districts in the West Bank and Gaza Strip, with reserved seats for Christians and one for Samaritans.

History

The first PLC was elected in January 1996, with Fatah winning a majority and Rawhi Fattouh later among its speakers. Its term, intended to last until the end of the interim period in 1999, was repeatedly extended. The second PLC was elected on 25 January 2006 in elections widely judged free and fair: Hamas's Change and Reform list won 74 of 132 seats, defeating Fatah. The result triggered international sanctions on the Hamas-led government, the 2007 Hamas takeover of Gaza, and the effective paralysis of the Council, which ceased to function as a unified body. President Mahmoud Abbas formally dissolved the PLC in December 2018 following a Constitutional Court ruling, and it has not been reconstituted.

Role

The PLC passes legislation, approves the budget and the cabinet, and can withdraw confidence from the government. Its headquarters in Ramallah were bombed by Israel during the Second Intifada; a new parliament building in Abu Dis remains unfinished.
