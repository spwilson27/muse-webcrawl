Palestine Liberation Organization

Flag of the PLO

The Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO; Arabic: منظمة التحرير الفلسطينية) is a Palestinian nationalist coalition founded in 1964 with the goal of establishing an independent Palestinian state. It is internationally recognised as the sole legitimate representative of the Palestinian people and holds observer status at the United Nations. Its largest faction is Fatah.

History

The PLO was founded at an Arab League summit in Cairo in 1964, initially chaired by Ahmad Shuqayri. After the 1967 Six-Day War, guerrilla factions gained control, and Yasser Arafat of Fatah became chairman in 1969. Based successively in Jordan, Lebanon, and Tunisia, the PLO waged armed struggle against Israel and was expelled from Lebanon in 1982 during the Lebanon War.

Oslo Accords and the Palestinian Authority

In 1993, the PLO recognised Israel's right to exist and renounced terrorism in the Oslo Accords, and Israel recognised the PLO as the representative of the Palestinian people. The accords created the Palestinian Authority to administer parts of the West Bank and Gaza Strip. Arafat led both the PLO and the Authority until his death in 2004, when Mahmoud Abbas succeeded him.

Structure

The PLO's institutions include the Palestinian National Council (its legislative body) and the Executive Committee. Member factions include Fatah, the Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine, and the Democratic Front; Hamas and Palestinian Islamic Jihad are not members.
