1948 Arab–Israeli War

The 1948 Arab–Israeli War, known in Israel as the War of Independence and in Arabic as al-Nakba ("the Catastrophe"), was fought between the newly proclaimed State of Israel and a military coalition of Arab states and Palestinian Arab forces. It was the first war of the Arab–Israeli conflict, following the civil war phase in Mandatory Palestine that began after the UN Partition Plan of November 1947.

Course of the war

On 14 May 1948, David Ben-Gurion proclaimed the establishment of the State of Israel. The following day, armies of Egypt, Transjordan, Syria, Lebanon and Iraq invaded. After initial Arab advances, Israeli offensives in the summer and autumn — including Operations Dani, Yoav and Hiram — turned the tide. Czechoslovak arms shipments and the consolidation of the Israel Defense Forces were decisive factors. Fighting ended with the 1949 Armistice Agreements signed on Rhodes between Israel and Egypt (February 1949), Lebanon, Jordan and Syria.

Consequences

Israel emerged controlling about 78 percent of former Mandatory Palestine — more than allotted by the Partition Plan. Jordan annexed the West Bank, and Egypt administered the Gaza Strip. More than 700,000 Palestinian Arabs fled or were expelled from their homes, an exodus Palestinians call the Nakba; Israel refused their return, and the refugee question remains unresolved. Some 6,000 Israelis, about one percent of the Jewish population, were killed.
