Madrid Conference of 1991

The Madrid Conference was an international peace conference held in Madrid, Spain, from 30 October to 1 November 1991, co-sponsored by the United States and the Soviet Union. It was the first time Israel sat at the same negotiating table with delegations from Syria, Lebanon, Jordan and the Palestinians, and it launched the bilateral and multilateral talks that structured Arab–Israeli diplomacy in the 1990s.

Background

Following the 1991 Gulf War, US Secretary of State James Baker spent months shuttling around the Middle East to convene a conference based on UN Security Council Resolutions 242 and 338 and the "land for peace" formula. Prime Minister Yitzhak Shamir's Israel accepted reluctantly; the Palestinians attended within a joint Jordanian–Palestinian delegation, with figures linked to the PLO but no formal PLO representation.

Outcome

The conference produced two tracks: bilateral negotiations between Israel and each Arab party, held mainly in Washington, D.C., and multilateral working groups on water, refugees, arms control, the environment and regional economic development. While the public Madrid tracks made slow progress, secret Israeli–PLO talks in Norway overtook them, producing the Oslo Accords in 1993. The Madrid framework nonetheless established the basic architecture — comprehensive peace based on land for peace — for the decade's diplomacy, and Israel's bilateral track with Jordan contributed to the 1994 Israel–Jordan peace treaty.
