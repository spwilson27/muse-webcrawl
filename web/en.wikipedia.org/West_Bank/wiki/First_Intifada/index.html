First Intifada

The First Intifada (Arabic: الانتفاضة الأولى, "the first uprising"), also known as the First Palestinian Intifada, was a sustained series of protests, civil disobedience, boycotts, strikes and stone-throwing demonstrations by Palestinians in the Israeli-occupied West Bank and Gaza Strip against Israeli occupation. It began on 9 December 1987 in the Jabalia refugee camp in Gaza, after an Israeli army truck collided with a civilian car, killing four Palestinians, and spread rapidly.

Course

The uprising was coordinated locally by popular committees and the clandestine Unified National Leadership of the Uprising, while the PLO leadership in Tunis supplied guidance. Mass demonstrations, commercial strikes, boycotts of Israeli goods and tax resistance were met with Israeli curfews, mass arrests, deportations and a policy of "force, might and beatings" under Defence Minister Yitzhak Rabin. More than 1,000 Palestinians were killed by Israeli forces during the uprising, along with over 160 Israelis; hundreds of Palestinians were also killed by other Palestinians accused of collaboration.

Outcome

The Intifada internationalized the Palestinian cause and demonstrated that the occupation could not be sustained without cost. It helped bring about the Madrid Conference of 1991 and, ultimately, the Oslo Accords of 1993, under which Israel and the PLO recognized each other and the Palestinian Authority was created. The uprising is generally dated 1987–1993.
