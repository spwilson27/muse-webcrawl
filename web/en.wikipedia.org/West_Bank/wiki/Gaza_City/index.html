Gaza City (Arabic: غزة, romanized: Ghazza) is the largest city in the Gaza Strip and in the State of Palestine. Located on the Mediterranean coast about 76 kilometres southwest of Jerusalem, it has been continuously inhabited for millennia and served as a major trading post between Egypt and the Levant.

History

Gaza was one of the five Philistine city-states and appears in ancient Egyptian, Assyrian and biblical records. It was conquered by Alexander the Great in 332 BC, and later passed through Roman, Byzantine, Arab, Crusader, Mamluk and Ottoman hands — the Great Omari Mosque and the old city's markets date largely to the Mamluk and Ottoman periods. Britain took Gaza in 1917 after three battles against Ottoman forces. Egypt administered the Gaza Strip after 1948, and Israel occupied it in 1967. The Palestinian Authority assumed control in 1994; Hamas took over the Strip in 2007.

Modern city

Before the war that began in October 2023, Gaza City was the Strip's economic and administrative centre, home to universities, hospitals, the al-Shati refugee camp and a small seaport. The subsequent conflict destroyed or damaged large parts of the city and displaced nearly its entire population.
