Israeli–Palestinian conflict

The Israeli–Palestinian conflict is an ongoing political and armed conflict between Israelis and Palestinians over land, national self-determination, security and the status of territory in the former British Mandate of Palestine. It is the core regional component of the wider Arab–Israeli conflict and is widely regarded as one of the world's most intractable disputes.

Background

The conflict's roots lie in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, when Zionist immigration to Ottoman and then British-ruled Palestine grew amid rising Jewish and Arab national movements. The UN Partition Plan of 1947 proposed two states; its rejection by Arab leaders led to the 1947–1949 war, Israel's establishment, and the displacement of over 700,000 Palestinians. Israel occupied the West Bank and Gaza Strip in the 1967 Six-Day War.

Peace process and uprisings

The First Intifada (1987–1993) preceded the Oslo Accords, which created the Palestinian Authority and envisaged a negotiated two-state solution. The Second Intifada (2000–2005) followed the failure of the Camp David Summit. Since 2007 the Palestinian polity has been split between the Fatah-led Palestinian Authority in the West Bank and Hamas in Gaza, which has seen repeated wars with Israel. Israeli settlement expansion in the West Bank, the status of Jerusalem, Palestinian refugees' claimed right of return, borders and security arrangements remain the core disputed issues.

Recent developments

On 7 October 2023 Hamas-led militants attacked southern Israel, killing about 1,200 people and taking roughly 250 hostages. Israel responded with a large-scale military campaign in Gaza that caused very heavy Palestinian casualties and widespread destruction, drawing international legal scrutiny and protests, alongside escalations involving Hezbollah and in the West Bank.
