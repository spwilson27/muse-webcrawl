Mediterranean Sea

Surface area2,500,000 km²
Average depth1,500 m
Max. depth5,267 m (Calypso Deep)
ConnectionsAtlantic Ocean (Gibraltar); Red Sea (Suez Canal)

The Mediterranean Sea is an intercontinental sea stretching from the Atlantic Ocean on the west to Asia on the east, separating Europe from Africa. Its name derives from the Latin mediterraneus, "in the middle of the earth". It covers about 2.5 million km² and is almost entirely enclosed by land: by Southern Europe and Anatolia to the north, North Africa to the south, and the Levant to the east.

Geography

The Mediterranean connects to the Atlantic through the narrow Strait of Gibraltar, to the Black Sea via the Dardanelles, the Sea of Marmara and the Bosporus, and since 1869 to the Red Sea through the Suez Canal — a link that has allowed Red Sea species to colonize the eastern basin ("Lessepsian migration"). Major subdivisions include the Aegean, Adriatic, Ionian, Tyrrhenian, Ligurian, Balearic and Levantine seas. Evaporation exceeds freshwater input, so Atlantic surface water flows continuously inward at Gibraltar. The sea is a remnant of the ancient Tethys Ocean.

History and environment

The Mediterranean was the cradle or crossroads of Egyptian, Phoenician, Greek, Roman, Arab and Ottoman civilizations, and remains one of the world's busiest shipping corridors. Its climate — hot, dry summers and mild, wet winters — defines the surrounding region. The sea faces severe pressures from overfishing, pollution, plastic waste, invasive species and rapid warming, and the migration routes across it from Africa and the Middle East to Europe have been among the world's deadliest.
