Six-Day War

The Six-Day War (5–10 June 1967), also known as the June War or the 1967 Arab–Israeli War (in Arabic, an-Naksah, "the setback"), was fought between Israel and a coalition of Egypt, Syria and Jordan. In six days Israel captured the Sinai Peninsula and Gaza Strip from Egypt, the West Bank and East Jerusalem from Jordan, and the Golan Heights from Syria, more than tripling the territory under its control.

Course of the war

Amid escalating tensions — Egypt's expulsion of UN peacekeepers from Sinai, massing of troops, and closure of the Straits of Tiran to Israeli shipping — Israel launched a surprise pre-dawn air strike on 5 June (Operation Focus) that destroyed most of the Egyptian air force on the ground. Israeli armour then overran Sinai; on the Jordanian front Israel captured East Jerusalem and the West Bank, and on 9–10 June it took the Golan Heights from Syria before a UN-brokered ceasefire took effect.

Consequences

Some 300,000 more Palestinians fled or were expelled from the newly occupied territories. In November 1967 the UN Security Council adopted Resolution 242, calling for Israeli withdrawal from occupied territories in return for peace ("land for peace"). Israel annexed East Jerusalem (a move not internationally recognized) and began building settlements in the occupied territories. The occupation that began in 1967 — and the political questions it raised — remain central to the Israeli–Palestinian conflict.
