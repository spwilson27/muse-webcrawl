State of Palestine

Flag of Palestine

The State of Palestine is a country in the southern Levant region of West Asia. It encompasses two disconnected territories — the West Bank in the east and the Gaza Strip in the southwest — with Jerusalem as its proclaimed capital, though administrative functions are based in Ramallah. It was proclaimed on 15 November 1988 in Algiers by the Palestinian National Council.

Recognition and status

Palestine has been recognised by a large majority of United Nations member states. In 2012 the UN General Assembly upgraded Palestine to non-member observer state status. In 2024 several additional countries, including Spain, Ireland, and Norway, extended recognition. The Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO) represents Palestine internationally, while the Palestinian Authority, created by the Oslo Accords, administers parts of the West Bank; Hamas has controlled Gaza since 2007.

Geography and demographics

The combined population of the West Bank and Gaza is roughly five million. The West Bank is landlocked and bordered by Israel and Jordan; Gaza has a Mediterranean coastline and borders Israel and Egypt. Much of the territory is regarded internationally as under Israeli occupation, a status affirmed by the International Court of Justice's 2024 advisory opinion.

History

Key events include the 1948 Nakba, the 1967 Six-Day War in which Israel captured the West Bank and Gaza, the First and Second Intifada uprisings, the Oslo peace process, and repeated Gaza wars culminating in the war that began in October 2023.
