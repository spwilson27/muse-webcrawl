1948 Arab–Israeli War

Date15 May 1948 – 10 March 1949 (main phase)
LocationFormer British Mandate of Palestine; Sinai and southern Lebanon
BelligerentsIsrael vs. Egypt, Transjordan, Syria, Lebanon, Iraq and allied forces
ResultIsraeli victory; 1949 Armistice Agreements; Palestinian exodus (Nakba)

The 1948 Arab–Israeli War, also known in Israel as the War of Independence and among Palestinians as part of the Nakba ("catastrophe"), was fought between the newly proclaimed State of Israel and a coalition of Arab states following the end of the British Mandate for Palestine. It was the second and final stage of the 1947–1949 Palestine war, preceded by civil war between Jewish and Arab communities in Palestine.

Course of the war

Israel declared independence on 14 May 1948. Armies of Egypt, Transjordan, Syria, Lebanon and Iraq invaded the following day. Fighting proceeded in phases separated by UN truces: an initial Arab advance was halted; Israeli offensives in July and October–December 1948 (including Operations Dani, Yoav and Horev) expanded Israeli-held territory beyond the UN Partition Plan boundaries, while Transjordan's Arab Legion held East Jerusalem and the West Bank and Egypt held the Gaza Strip.

Outcome

Armistice agreements signed on Rhodes in 1949 established the Green Line demarcation. Israel ended the war controlling about 78% of the former Mandate territory. More than 700,000 Palestinian Arabs fled or were expelled from their homes and were not permitted to return, becoming refugees; in the following years, large numbers of Jews from Arab and Muslim countries immigrated to Israel, many amid persecution or expulsion. The war set the territorial and political framework of the Arab–Israeli conflict for decades.
