Protocol on Economic Relations

The Protocol on Economic Relations, commonly called the Paris Protocol, is an agreement signed in Paris on 29 April 1994 between Israel and the Palestine Liberation Organization. Incorporated into the Gaza–Jericho Agreement and later Oslo II, it has governed economic relations between Israel and the Palestinian Authority ever since, shaping the Palestinian economy for three decades.

Provisions

The Protocol established a quasi-customs union: the Palestinian territories share Israel's external tariff envelope (with limited Palestinian import lists), use the Israeli shekel as their circulating currency, and leave indirect-tax policy largely aligned with Israel's. Israel collects customs duties, VAT and purchase taxes on goods destined for the Palestinian market and transfers the revenues monthly to the Palestinian Authority — "clearance revenues" that long constituted the largest share of PA income. The Protocol also regulated Palestinian labour flows into Israel, monetary arrangements (no Palestinian currency; the Palestine Monetary Authority acts as a quasi-central bank) and sectoral cooperation.

Criticism and disputes

Palestinian officials and economists criticize the Protocol as entrenching dependence on Israel, while Israel has periodically withheld clearance revenues as political leverage. Intended to last for an interim period of five years, the Protocol remains in force decades later, and scenarios of Palestinian "economic disengagement" are periodically debated.
