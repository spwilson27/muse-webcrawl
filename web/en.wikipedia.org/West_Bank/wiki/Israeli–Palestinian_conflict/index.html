Israeli–Palestinian conflict

The Israeli–Palestinian conflict is an ongoing political and military conflict between Israelis and Palestinians, rooted in competing national movements — Zionism and Palestinian nationalism — over the same land between the Jordan River and the Mediterranean Sea. It is the core of the wider Arab–Israeli conflict.

Origins

Large-scale Jewish immigration to Ottoman and then British Mandate Palestine from the 1880s onward, accelerated by European antisemitism and the Holocaust, collided with the Arab population's opposition, producing escalating intercommunal violence. In 1947 the United Nations proposed partitioning the Mandate into Jewish and Arab states. Jewish leaders accepted; Arab leaders rejected the plan. The ensuing 1948 war established Israel, left Jordan in control of the West Bank and Egypt in control of Gaza, and displaced over 700,000 Palestinians.

Occupation and uprisings

In the Six-Day War of 1967 Israel occupied the West Bank, East Jerusalem and the Gaza Strip, and began building settlements there, widely considered illegal under international law. Palestinian resistance took the form of armed struggle by the PLO, then mass uprisings: the First Intifada (1987–93) and the Second Intifada (2000–05). The Oslo Accords created the Palestinian Authority but no final settlement. Hamas's 2007 takeover of Gaza entrenched a split in Palestinian politics and led to repeated Gaza wars, culminating in the devastating war that began with Hamas's attack on Israel on 7 October 2023.

Core issues

The parties and international mediators generally identify the core issues as: the borders of a Palestinian state, the status of Jerusalem, Israeli settlements, security arrangements, and the Palestinian refugees' claimed right of return. The two-state solution long served as the dominant framework, though one-state and confederal proposals are also debated.
