Yasser Arafat

Yasser Arafat, chairman of the PLO

Yasser Arafat (born Mohammed Abdel Rahman Abdel Raouf Arafat al-Qudwa; 24 August 1929 – 11 November 2004), also popularly known by his kunya Abu Ammar, was a Palestinian political leader. He was chairman of the Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO) from 1969 to 2004, President of Palestine from 1989 to 2004, and President of the Palestinian Authority from 1994 to 2004. Ideologically an Arab nationalist and socialist, Arafat was a founding member of the Fatah political party, which he led from 1959 until 2004.

Early life and guerrilla leadership

Arafat was born in Cairo to Palestinian parents and studied civil engineering at Cairo University. He co-founded Fatah in Kuwait in the late 1950s. After the 1967 Six-Day War, the PLO under Arafat became the umbrella for Palestinian guerrilla groups, based first in Jordan — from which they were expelled in 1970–71 — then in Lebanon, and after 1982 in Tunisia. His 1974 "gun and olive branch" speech to the UN General Assembly brought the Palestinian cause to the world stage.

Peace process

In 1988 Arafat renounced terrorism and accepted UN resolutions recognising Israel. The Oslo Accords of 1993–95 followed, for which Arafat shared the 1994 Nobel Peace Prize with Yitzhak Rabin and Shimon Peres. He returned to Gaza in 1994 and was elected President of the Palestinian Authority in 1996.

Final years

After the failure of the 2000 Camp David Summit and the outbreak of the Second Intifada, Israel confined Arafat to his Ramallah compound, the Muqata, from 2001. He fell ill in October 2004, was flown to a Paris hospital, and died on 11 November 2004. He was succeeded by Mahmoud Abbas. A state funeral was held in Cairo, and he was buried in Ramallah.
