Mahmoud Abbas (Arabic: محمود عباس; born 15 November 1935), also known by the kunya Abu Mazen, is a Palestinian politician who has served as president of the Palestinian Authority and the State of Palestine since 2005 and as chairman of the Palestine Liberation Organization since 2004. A founding member of Fatah, he was a principal architect of the Oslo peace process.

Early life and PLO career

Abbas was born in Safed; his family fled to Syria during the 1948 war. He studied law in Damascus and earned a doctorate in Moscow with a dissertation on early Zionism. In exile he helped found Fatah in Kuwait in 1959, built the PLO's international connections, and in 1993 signed the Declaration of Principles on behalf of the PLO in Washington. He returned to Gaza in 1994 and briefly served as the Palestinian Authority's first prime minister in 2003 under Yasser Arafat.

Presidency

Elected president in January 2005 after Arafat's death, Abbas pursued negotiations with Israel, including the Annapolis talks of 2007–08 and the Kerry talks of 2013–14, without a final agreement. After Hamas won the 2006 legislative election and seized Gaza in 2007, Abbas's rule contracted to the West Bank, where the Palestinian Authority maintains security cooperation with Israel. His four-year term was extended indefinitely amid postponed elections, and his popularity in Palestinian polling has long been low, though he remains the internationally recognized Palestinian leader and secured UN observer-state status for Palestine in 2012.
