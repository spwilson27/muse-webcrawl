Second Intifada

Clashes during the Second Intifada

The Second Intifada, also known as the Al-Aqsa Intifada, was a major Palestinian uprising against Israel and its occupation, beginning in late September 2000 and continuing until roughly 2005. It was characterised by an initial wave of protests and riots, a large increase in violence including suicide bombings against Israeli civilians, and Israeli military operations throughout the West Bank and Gaza Strip.

Origins

The uprising followed the failure of the Camp David Summit in July 2000. Its immediate trigger is widely considered to be the visit by Israeli opposition leader Ariel Sharon to the Temple Mount / Haram al-Sharif compound in Jerusalem on 28 September 2000, followed by riots in which Palestinian demonstrators were killed. Underlying causes included the expansion of Israeli settlements, Palestinian frustration with the Oslo process, and economic deterioration.

Course of the uprising

Groups including Hamas, Palestinian Islamic Jihad, and the al-Aqsa Martyrs' Brigades carried out shootings and suicide bombings. Israel responded with targeted killings, incursions, and — after the Passover massacre of March 2002 — Operation Defensive Shield, reoccupying major West Bank cities and besieging Yasser Arafat's compound in Ramallah. Israel also began constructing the West Bank barrier and, in 2005, unilaterally withdrew from Gaza.

Casualties and aftermath

Estimates commonly cite around 3,000 Palestinians and about 1,000 Israelis killed. The intifada is generally considered to have faded by 2005, following Arafat's death and a truce agreed at the Sharm el-Sheikh Summit. It badly damaged the Israeli–Palestinian peace process and reshaped politics on both sides.
