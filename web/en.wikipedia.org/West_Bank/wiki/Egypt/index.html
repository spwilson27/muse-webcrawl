Egypt, officially the Arab Republic of Egypt, is a transcontinental country spanning the northeast corner of Africa and the Sinai Peninsula in Western Asia. It is bordered by the Mediterranean Sea to the north, Libya to the west, Sudan to the south, and the Red Sea, the Gaza Strip and Israel to the east. The Nile River, flowing north through the country to a fertile delta, has sustained one of the world's oldest civilizations.

History

Pharaonic civilization emerged around 3100 BC with the unification of Upper and Lower Egypt, and endured for three millennia, leaving monuments such as the pyramids of Giza and the temples of Luxor. Egypt later passed under Persian, Greek (Ptolemaic), Roman, Byzantine and Arab rule; the Arab conquest of 641 AD gradually transformed its language and religion. The Fatimid, Ayyubid and Mamluk periods made Cairo a major centre of Islamic learning. Egypt came under Ottoman rule in 1517, was occupied by Britain from 1882, and became a republic in 1953 following the Egyptian revolution of 1952.

Modern Egypt

Under Gamal Abdel Nasser, Egypt nationalized the Suez Canal in 1956, provoking the Suez Crisis. Anwar Sadat led Egypt in the 1973 October War and signed the 1979 peace treaty with Israel — the first between Israel and an Arab state. Hosni Mubarak ruled from 1981 until mass protests in the 2011 Egyptian revolution forced his resignation. Mohamed Morsi of the Muslim Brotherhood was elected president in 2012 but was removed by the military in 2013; Abdel Fattah el-Sisi has been president since 2014.

Geography and economy

Most Egyptians live in the narrow Nile Valley and Delta, while the rest of the country is desert. The economy depends on agriculture, petroleum and natural gas, Suez Canal revenues, tourism, and remittances from Egyptians working abroad.
