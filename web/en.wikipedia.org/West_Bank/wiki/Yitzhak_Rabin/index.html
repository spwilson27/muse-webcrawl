Yitzhak Rabin (1 March 1922 – 4 November 1995) was an Israeli politician and general who served twice as prime minister of Israel. As chief of staff of the Israel Defense Forces he led Israel to victory in the Six-Day War of 1967; two decades later, as prime minister, he signed the Oslo Accords with the Palestine Liberation Organization, for which he received the Nobel Peace Prize. He was assassinated in 1995 by an Israeli extremist opposed to the peace process.

Military and political career

Born in Jerusalem and raised in Tel Aviv, Rabin joined the Palmach in 1941 and fought in the 1948 war, becoming IDF chief of staff in 1964. After a term as ambassador to the United States, he became Labour Party leader and prime minister in 1974, resigning in 1977 over a financial scandal. As defence minister in the 1980s he confronted the First Intifada with a policy of "breaking bones".

Prime minister and peacemaker

Returning as prime minister in 1992 on a platform of peace and security, Rabin authorized the secret Oslo channel. On 13 September 1993 he shook Yasser Arafat's hand on the White House lawn after signing the Declaration of Principles; Oslo II followed in 1995, as did the 1994 peace treaty with Jordan. On 4 November 1995, after addressing a peace rally in Tel Aviv, he was shot by Yigal Amir, a Jewish law student opposed to ceding territory. His assassination is widely regarded as a turning point that derailed the Oslo process; hundreds of thousands attended his funeral, including King Hussein of Jordan and President Mubarak of Egypt.
