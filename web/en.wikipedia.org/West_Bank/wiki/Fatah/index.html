Fatah

Fatah (Arabic: فتح, romanized: Fatḥ, lit. 'conquest'), formally the Palestinian National Liberation Movement, is a Palestinian nationalist and social democratic political party. Founded in Kuwait in 1959 by Yasser Arafat, Khalil al-Wazir, Salah Khalaf and others, it is the largest faction of the Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO) and the dominant party of the Palestinian Authority in the West Bank. Its name is a reverse acronym of Ḥarakat al-Taḥrīr al-Waṭanī al-Filasṭīnī.

History

Fatah launched its first armed operations against Israel in 1965 and gained control of the PLO in 1969, with Arafat as chairman. After Black September in Jordan (1970) the movement relocated to Lebanon, and after the 1982 Lebanon War to Tunis. Fatah's leaders returned to the West Bank and Gaza following the Oslo Accords of 1993–95, and Arafat became president of the new Palestinian Authority. Fatah won the 1996 Palestinian elections but lost the 2006 legislative election to Hamas; a violent split in 2007 left Fatah governing the West Bank while Hamas took control of Gaza.

Ideology and structure

Fatah advocates Palestinian self-determination and a two-state solution negotiated with Israel, and is a member of the Socialist International. Since Arafat's death in 2004 it has been led by Mahmoud Abbas. The movement includes the al-Aqsa Martyrs' Brigades, an armed network that emerged during the Second Intifada.
