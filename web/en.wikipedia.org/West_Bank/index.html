The West Bank (Arabic: الضفة الغربية, romanised aḍ-Ḍiffah al-Ġarbiyyah; Hebrew: יהודה ושומרון) is a landlocked territory near the coast of the Mediterranean Sea in West Asia that forms the main bulk of the Palestinian territories along with the Gaza Strip. It is bordered by Jordan and the Dead Sea to the east and by Israel to the south, west, and north.

The territory's name derives from its position on the western bank of the Jordan River. Major cities include Hebron, Nablus, Ramallah (the administrative centre of the Palestinian Authority), Bethlehem, Jericho, and Jenin, as well as East Jerusalem, whose status is disputed.

History

The West Bank was part of the British Mandate for Palestine until 1948, when it was captured and annexed by Jordan in the 1948 Arab–Israeli War. Israel captured it in the Six-Day War of 1967 and has occupied it since. Under the Oslo Accords of the 1990s, the territory was divided into Areas A, B, and C: the Palestinian Authority exercises civil and security control in Area A, civil control in Area B, while Area C — about 60 percent of the land — remains under full Israeli control and contains most Israeli settlements, whose legality under international law is widely contested.

Geography and economy

The land consists largely of rugged highlands running north–south, descending eastward to the Jordan Valley, which lies below sea level along the Dead Sea shore. The economy depends on services, agriculture (notably olives), stone quarrying, remittances, and foreign aid, and is closely tied to Israel for trade, water, and employment.
