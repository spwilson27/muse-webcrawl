Metabarcoding

Metabarcoding is a method of identifying many species simultaneously from a mixed environmental sample using DNA barcodes and high-throughput sequencing. It extends DNA barcoding from single specimens to whole communities.

Method

A short, standardized gene region — such as the COI gene for animals, 16S rRNA for bacteria, or ITS for fungi — is amplified by PCR from an environmental sample (water, soil, gut contents, or bulk insect traps) and sequenced. The resulting reads are compared with reference databases to produce a list of taxa present, often as operational taxonomic units or amplicon sequence variants.

Applications

Metabarcoding is used for biodiversity monitoring, detecting invasive or endangered species from environmental DNA (eDNA), reconstructing diets from feces, and assessing pollination networks. Its strengths are speed and sensitivity; its limitations include primer bias, incomplete reference libraries, and difficulty converting read counts into true abundances.
