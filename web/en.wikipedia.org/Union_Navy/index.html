Union Navy

The Union Navy was the naval force of the United States during the American Civil War (1861–1865), fighting against the Confederate States Navy. Starting the war with a small and largely obsolete fleet, it expanded into one of the largest navies in the world, with over 600 vessels and some 51,000 personnel by 1865.

Role in the war

Under Secretary of the Navy Gideon Welles and Assistant Secretary Gustavus Fox, the Union Navy enforced the blockade of Southern ports that formed a central element of the "Anaconda Plan", strangling Confederate trade and cotton exports. Riverine squadrons cooperated with the army in the western theater, notably at Vicksburg. The navy pioneered the use of ironclads, most famously USS Monitor, whose duel with CSS Virginia at Hampton Roads in March 1862 transformed naval warfare.

Key engagements

Major Union naval successes included the capture of New Orleans under David Farragut in 1862, Farragut's victory at Mobile Bay in 1864 ("Damn the torpedoes, full speed ahead"), and the capture of Fort Fisher in 1865, which closed Wilmington, the Confederacy's last major port.
