Battle of Beersheba (1917)

Battle of Beersheba
Date: 31 October 1917
Location: Beersheba, Ottoman Palestine
Result: Allied victory
Part of the Sinai and Palestine campaign of the First World War

The Battle of Beersheba was fought on 31 October 1917, when the Egyptian Expeditionary Force attacked and captured Beersheba (now Be'er Sheva) from the Ottoman Empire during the Sinai and Palestine campaign of the First World War. The battle opened General Edmund Allenby's offensive to break the Gaza–Beersheba line.

The battle

After an artillery bombardment and infantry attacks by the XX Corps from the south-west, the defences were still holding as dusk approached. The commander of the Desert Mounted Corps, Lieutenant-General Harry Chauvel, ordered the 4th and 12th Light Horse Regiments of the Australian 4th Light Horse Brigade to mount a charge across open ground. The light horsemen, using bayonets as swords, galloped through machine-gun and artillery fire and overran the Turkish trenches, capturing the town and its vital wells largely intact.

Aftermath

The capture of Beersheba unhinged the Ottoman defensive line; Gaza fell a week later, and the way was open for the advance that took Jerusalem in December 1917. The charge is often described as one of the last successful large-scale cavalry charges in history, and it is commemorated annually in Australia and Israel.
