Lake Van (Turkish: Van Gölü) is the largest lake in Turkey, located in the Eastern Anatolia region in the provinces of Van and Bitlis. It is a saline soda lake with no outlet, formed when a volcanic eruption of Mount Nemrut blocked its original drainage in prehistoric times.

Geography

The lake lies at an elevation of about 1,640 metres amid high mountains, including the dormant Süphan Dağı on its northern shore. Its highly alkaline water is rich in sodium carbonate, and local people traditionally wash clothes in it without soap. Despite the harsh chemistry, the lake does not freeze in winter except in shallow northern bays.

Life

The only fish known to live in the lake is the pearl mullet (inci kefali), which migrates into the inflowing rivers to spawn. The lake is also associated with the legendary Van cat, a breed noted for swimming and for odd-coloured eyes, and with folklore about a monster said to inhabit its depths.

History

The lake was the heartland of the ancient kingdom of Urartu, whose capital Tushpa stood on its shore. Islands such as Akdamar hold notable Armenian churches, including the tenth-century Church of the Holy Cross.
