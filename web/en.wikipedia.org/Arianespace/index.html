Arianespace

Arianespace SA is a French company founded in 1980 as the world's first commercial space transportation company. It operates and markets the Ariane launch vehicle family on behalf of the European Space Agency and the European space industry, launching from the Guiana Space Centre at Kourou, French Guiana.

History

Arianespace was created after the tenth flight of the European Ariane 1 rocket demonstrated the viability of the programme. It took over commercial exploitation of the Ariane family while ESA and the French space agency CNES retained oversight of development and of the Kourou launch base. The company's shareholders historically included CNES, Airbus and Safran interests, and space companies from the ESA member states participating in the Ariane programme; in 2015–2016 majority control passed to ArianeGroup (then Airbus Safran Launchers), the joint venture that builds the launchers.

Operations

Arianespace has marketed and flown the Ariane 5, the medium-lift Soyuz-2 (as Soyuz at CSG, suspended in 2022), and the small Vega and Vega C launchers, offering a complete family for payloads of all sizes to all orbits. Its successor flagship is Ariane 6, which made its inaugural flight in July 2024. Arianespace competes in the global commercial launch market with providers such as SpaceX and United Launch Alliance.
