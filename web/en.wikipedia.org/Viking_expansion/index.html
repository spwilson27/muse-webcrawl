Viking expansion

Viking expansion
Period8th–11th centuries
HomelandScandinavia (Denmark, Norway, Sweden)
ReachedNorth America, Iceland, Greenland, Britain, Ireland, Francia, Rus', Byzantium

Viking expansion was the movement of Scandinavian seafarers during the Viking Age who raided, traded, explored, and settled across Europe, Asia, and the North Atlantic islands between roughly 750 and 1050 CE. It was one of the widest diasporas of the early medieval world, driven by maritime technology, trade, and a warrior-farmer culture.

Directions of expansion

West: Norwegians settled the Faroe Islands, Iceland (from c. 870), and Greenland (c. 985), and reached North America around 1000 (Vinland, identified with L'Anse aux Meadows in Newfoundland). South-west: Danes and Norwegians raided and settled in England (the Danelaw), Ireland (founding Dublin and other coastal towns), Scotland's islands, and Francia, where Rollo's followers became the Normans in 911. East: Swedish Vikings — the Rus' and Varangians — travelled the Russian river systems, trading furs, slaves, and amber for silver dirhams, founded the polities that became Kievan Rus', and served the Byzantine emperors as the Varangian Guard.

Methods and causes

The key instrument was the clinker-built longship, shallow-drafted and flexible, propelled by sail and oars. Historians debate causes: demographic pressure, inheritance customs, the lure of weak and wealthy monasteries and towns, growing trade networks, political consolidation in Scandinavia, and advances in sail technology all likely played roles.
