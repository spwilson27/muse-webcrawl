Morall Fabillis

The Morall Fabillis of Esope the Frygian is a cycle of verse fables in Scots by the 15th-century poet Robert Henryson, generally considered his masterpiece. Written probably in the 1480s, the work consists of thirteen fables adapted from Aesop and the medieval Reynard tradition, each followed by a moralitas, or moral interpretation.

AuthorRobert Henryson
LanguageMiddle Scots
Datec. 1480s
FormThirteen verse fables

Contents

The collection opens with a prologue in which Henryson defends the value of fable and introduces his source, "Esope". The tales include "The Cock and the Jasp", "The Twa Mice", "The Cock and the Fox", "The Wolf and the Lamb", "The Trial of the Fox", and "The Preaching of the Swallow", interspersed with versions of Reynard the Fox episodes. The poems are written in a variety of stanza forms, including rhyme royal and ballade stanzas.

Themes

Henryson's fables combine comedy with sharp social and moral commentary on corruption, justice, and the abuse of power in fifteenth-century Scotland. The relationship between tale and moral is often ironic or deliberately unresolved, which has attracted extensive modern critical attention.
