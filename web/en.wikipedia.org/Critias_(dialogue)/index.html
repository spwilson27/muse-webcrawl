Critias (dialogue)

Critias
Author: Plato
Form: Dialogue, unfinished
Subject: Atlantis

The Critias is an unfinished dialogue by Plato, written in the 4th century BC, which contains the story of the island empire of Atlantis and its war against ancient Athens. It follows the Timaeus and shares its cast: Socrates, Timaeus, Hermocrates, and Critias, who narrates.

Content

Critias recounts how the tale came from Egyptian priests via Solon. He describes Atlantis in detail — its concentric harbors, temples, laws, and wealth, and the god Poseidon as its founder — and contrasts it with a virtuous, austere primeval Athens of 9,000 years earlier. The dialogue breaks off mid-sentence just as Zeus, seeing Atlantean corruption, summons the gods to pronounce judgment.

Legacy

Together with the Timaeus, the Critias is the primary ancient source for the Atlantis legend, and its account has inspired centuries of speculation, literature, and pseudohistory.
