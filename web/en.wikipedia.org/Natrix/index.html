Natrix

Scientific classification

Family: Colubridae

Subfamily: Natricinae

Genus: Natrix Laurenti, 1768

Natrix is a genus of Old World snakes in the subfamily Natricinae of the family Colubridae. The genus includes the well-known grass snakes and water snakes of Europe, North Africa, and western and central Asia. The name derives from the Latin for "water snake."

Species

The best-known species is the grass snake, Natrix natrix, widespread across Europe; the barred grass snake N. helvetica of western Europe was formerly considered a subspecies. Other species include the viperine water snake (N. maura), the Iberian grass snake (N. astreptophora), and the dice snake (N. tessellata), which is strongly associated with aquatic habitats.

Ecology

Natrix snakes are non-venomous and generally harmless to humans. They feed mainly on amphibians, especially frogs and toads, and on fish. When threatened, grass snakes may feign death or release a foul-smelling musk rather than bite. They are commonly found near wetlands, ponds, and slow-moving water.
