Marc-Antoine Charpentier

Born1643, Paris, France
Died24 February 1704, Paris
OccupationComposer

Marc-Antoine Charpentier (1643 – 24 February 1704) was a French composer of the Baroque era. Exceptionally prolific and versatile, he produced music in nearly every genre of his time, yet was overshadowed during his life by Jean-Baptiste Lully's dominance of the French court.

Career

After study in Rome, possibly with Giacomo Carissimi, Charpentier returned to Paris and composed for Molière's theatrical troupe after its split with Lully, for the Dauphin, for the Jesuits, and later as maître de musique of the Sainte-Chapelle. His output includes operas such as Médée (1693), masses, motets, oratorios, pastorales and incidental music.

Legacy

His works were largely forgotten until a 20th-century revival. The prelude to his Te Deum (H. 146) became famous as the signature tune of the European Broadcasting Union, heard at the Eurovision Song Contest.
