"American Pie" is a song by American singer and songwriter Don McLean. Recorded and released on the American Pie album in 1971, the single was a number-one US hit for four weeks in 1972, starting on January 15. At over eight minutes long, it is famed for its cryptic, allusive lyrics, which are commonly understood as a lament for the lost innocence of early rock and roll and a chronicle of social change in the 1960s.

Lyrics and interpretation

The refrain "the day the music died" refers to the 1959 plane crash that killed Buddy Holly, Ritchie Valens, and the Big Bopper (J. P. Richardson). Across its six verses, the song is widely read as referencing figures and events including Elvis Presley, Bob Dylan, Janis Joplin, the Beatles, the Rolling Stones, the Altamont Free Concert, and the assassinations and unrest of the decade. McLean long declined to explain the lyrics in detail, though he offered partial annotations when his original manuscript was sold at auction in 2015.

Legacy

The song was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame and selected for the National Recording Registry. Madonna recorded a shortened dance-pop cover in 2000 that topped charts internationally. The phrase "American Pie" later named the 1999 film comedy, unrelated in content to the song.
