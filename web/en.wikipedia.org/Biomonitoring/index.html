Biomonitoring

Biomonitoring is the measurement of the body burden of toxic chemical compounds, elements, or their metabolites in biological specimens such as blood, urine, hair, or breast milk. In environmental and occupational health it provides a direct measure of the total internal dose of a substance from all sources and routes of exposure.

Uses

National programmes such as the U.S. National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) and the German Environmental Survey track population exposure to lead, mercury, pesticides, phthalates, PFAS, and many other chemicals. Occupational biomonitoring monitors workers exposed to solvents, metals, and other hazards against biological exposure limits.

Ecological biomonitoring

The term is also used in ecology for assessing the health of ecosystems by observing organisms — for example, using lichens to gauge air pollution, aquatic macroinvertebrates to assess water quality, or moss to map heavy-metal deposition.

Interpretation

Results must be interpreted against reference values and, where available, health-based guidance values; detecting a chemical does not by itself demonstrate harm, because modern analytical methods measure extremely low concentrations.
