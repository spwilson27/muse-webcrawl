Thrust fault

A thrust fault is a break in the Earth's crust, across which older rocks are pushed above younger rocks. Thrust faults are a type of reverse fault with a shallow dip, typically 30 degrees or less from horizontal, and they form under horizontal compression, shortening the crust.

Geometry and settings

The block above the fault, the hanging wall, moves up relative to the footwall. Large low-angle thrusts can transport sheets of rock — nappes — tens or hundreds of kilometres from their origin. Thrust systems dominate fold-and-thrust belts at convergent plate margins, such as the Himalayas, the Alps, and the Appalachian Mountains. A blind thrust does not reach the surface; the 1994 Northridge earthquake in California occurred on such a fault.

Economic significance

Thrust faults can create traps for petroleum and natural gas, and thrust-related structures are important targets in hydrocarbon exploration in mountain belts.
