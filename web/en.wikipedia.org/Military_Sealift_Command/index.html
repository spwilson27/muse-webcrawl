Military Sealift Command

Military Sealift Command (MSC)
Founded: 9 July 1949
Headquarters: Naval Station Norfolk, Virginia
Part of: United States Navy; component of U.S. Transportation Command
Ships: about 125 (plus surge reserve fleets)

The Military Sealift Command (MSC) is an organisation of the United States Navy that controls the replenishment and military transport ships of the Navy. It supplies fuel, ammunition, food and equipment to US forces worldwide and prepositions military hardware at sea. Most MSC ships are crewed by civilian mariners and are designated USNS (United States Naval Ship).

History

MSC traces its origins to 1949, when the Military Sea Transportation Service was created to consolidate ocean transport for the Department of Defense. Renamed Military Sealift Command in 1970, it carried the bulk of supplies for the Korean and Vietnam wars, the 1990–91 Gulf War and the wars in Iraq and Afghanistan. Since 2012 its headquarters has been at Naval Station Norfolk, Virginia.

Organisation and fleet

MSC operates several programme fleets: the Naval Fleet Auxiliary Force of underway-replenishment oilers, dry cargo/ammunition ships and fast combat support ships; the Special Mission programme of oceanographic, surveillance and missile-tracking ships; the Prepositioning programme, which stations equipment aboard ships near potential trouble spots; the Sealift programme of large roll-on/roll-off vehicle carriers; and high-speed expeditionary fast transports. It also maintains relationships with the Ready Reserve Force of surge sealift ships.
