The Möbius strip, or Möbius band, is a surface that can be formed by twisting a strip of paper once and joining its ends. It was discovered independently in 1858 by the German mathematicians August Ferdinand Möbius and Johann Benedict Listing.

Properties

The Möbius strip has only one side and one edge: an ant walking along its centreline would return to its starting point having traversed both "sides" of the original strip, and the surface is non-orientable. Cutting a Möbius strip along its centreline yields a single longer strip with two full twists rather than two separate bands; cutting it a third of the way in from the edge produces two interlocked strips, one of them itself a Möbius strip.

Applications

Half-twist conveyor belts wear evenly on their single surface, and the Möbius geometry appears in chemistry in Möbius aromaticity and in the design of electronic resonators. It is also the archetypal image of the recycling symbol's ribbon form and a popular emblem of infinity.
