Biometric passport

A biometric passport (also called an e-passport or digital passport) is a traditional passport that has embedded electronic microprocessor chips which contain biometric information that can be used to authenticate the identity of travellers. It uses contactless smart card technology, including a microprocessor chip and antenna embedded in the front or back cover, or centre page, of the passport.

Data and standards

The passport's critical information is both printed on the data page and stored in the chip according to standards set by the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO), principally Document 9303. The chip stores a digital image of the holder and may also store fingerprint and iris data. Biometric passports are identified by a distinctive camera-like symbol printed on the cover.

Security

Security mechanisms include Basic Access Control, which requires scanning the printed machine-readable zone before the chip can be read, Passive Authentication of the stored data signatures, and in some countries Active Authentication and Extended Access Control protecting fingerprint data. Critics have raised privacy and skimming concerns, partly mitigated by shielded covers.
