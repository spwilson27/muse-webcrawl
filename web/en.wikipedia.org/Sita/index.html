Sita (Sanskrit: सीता, Sītā), also known as Janaki and Maithili, is a Hindu goddess and the central female character of the ancient Sanskrit epic Ramayana. She is the consort of Rama, the hero of the epic, and is regarded as an avatar of the goddess Lakshmi. Sita is revered as an embodiment of devotion, purity and endurance, and is one of the most widely venerated figures in Hinduism.

In the Ramayana

According to the epic, Sita was discovered by King Janaka of Mithila as an infant in a furrow while ploughing a field — her name means "furrow" in Sanskrit. Raised as a princess, she married Rama after he strung and broke the great bow of Shiva at her swayamvara.

Sita accompanied Rama into his fourteen-year exile in the forest. There she was abducted by the demon king Ravana and held captive in Lanka. After her rescue and Ravana's defeat, she underwent the agnipariksha, a trial by fire, to prove her purity. In later books of the epic she was exiled again by Rama and took refuge in the hermitage of Valmiki, where she raised her twin sons, Lava and Kusha, before finally returning to the earth from which she had sprung.

Worship and cultural significance

Sita is worshipped together with Rama, Lakshmana and Hanuman in temples throughout India and Nepal; Janakpur in present-day Nepal is identified with ancient Mithila and her birthplace. Her birthday is celebrated as Sita Navami. She has been reinterpreted in many regional retellings of the Ramayana, including women's folk traditions that emphasise her perspective, and has become a focus of modern literary and feminist readings of the epic.
