The Shenzhou program (Chinese: 神舟, "Divine Vessel") is a crewed spaceflight initiative of the People's Republic of China, developed under Project 921 (authorised in 1992) and operated by the China Manned Space Agency. Its Shenzhou spacecraft — a three-module design of orbital module, re-entry capsule, and service module, influenced by the Russian Soyuz — has carried Chinese astronauts (taikonauts) since 2003, making China the third country to independently launch humans into space.

Milestones

Four uncrewed test flights (Shenzhou 1–4, 1999–2002) preceded Shenzhou 5 (15 October 2003), which carried Yang Liwei on a 21-hour, 14-orbit flight. Shenzhou 6 (2005) flew two crew for five days. Zhai Zhigang performed China's first spacewalk on Shenzhou 7 (2008). Shenzhou 8 (2011) docked uncrewed with the Tiangong-1 space laboratory; Shenzhou 9 (2012) brought the first Chinese woman in space, Liu Yang, and the first crewed docking; Shenzhou 10 (2013) completed the Tiangong-1 programme. Shenzhou 11 (2016) spent a month aboard Tiangong-2. From Shenzhou 12 (2021) onward, Shenzhou ferries crews to the modular Tiangong space station, with six-month rotations, and successive missions — Shenzhou 13 through 19 and beyond — have maintained a continuous Chinese crewed presence in orbit.

Spacecraft

Shenzhou is launched from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center in the Gobi Desert on the human-rated Long March 2F rocket, and its re-entry capsule lands by parachute on the steppes of Inner Mongolia. The design adds a powered, solar-array-equipped orbital module that can remain in orbit autonomously after the capsule returns — a capability beyond its Soyuz inspiration.
