List of prime ministers of France

The prime minister of France (French: Premier ministre français) is the head of government of the French Republic and the leader of the Council of Ministers. The officeholder resides at the Hôtel Matignon in Paris. The modern office was created by the Constitution of the Fifth Republic in 1958, although heads of government under earlier regimes — including the presidents of the Council of Ministers of the Third and Fourth Republics — are conventionally included in the list.

Since 1958 there have been 26 prime ministers, beginning with Michel Debré. The longest-serving Fifth Republic prime minister was Georges Pompidou (1962–1968), who later became president of the Republic. Lionel Jospin served the longest continuous term under a president of the opposing party during the "cohabitation" of 1997–2002.

Earlier heads of government

Under the Third Republic (1870–1940) and Fourth Republic (1946–1958), the head of government held the title President of the Council of Ministers (Président du Conseil des ministres). Notable holders include Jules Ferry, Georges Clemenceau — who led France through the end of the First World War — Léon Blum of the Popular Front, and Pierre Mendès France. Earlier still, under the July Monarchy and the Second Empire, figures such as François Guizot and Émile Ollivier directed the government, while under the Bourbon Restoration the office was often held by the president of the council, beginning with the duc de Richelieu in 1815.

Role and powers

Under Article 21 of the Constitution of the Fifth Republic, the prime minister directs the action of the Government, is responsible for national defence, and ensures the execution of the laws. The prime minister is appointed by the president of the Republic and is responsible to the National Assembly, which can force the government's resignation through a motion of censure. When the president and the parliamentary majority belong to opposing camps, the resulting "cohabitation" greatly expands the prime minister's effective power over domestic policy.
