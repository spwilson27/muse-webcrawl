Mohair

Mohair is a fabric or yarn made from the hair of the Angora goat. Both durable and resilient, mohair is notable for its high lustre and sheen, which has earned it the nickname the "Diamond Fibre", and is often blended with other fibres to add value and strength.

Properties

Mohair fibre is roughly 25–45 micrometres in diameter. It is warm, lightweight, flame-resistant, and takes dye exceptionally well. Unlike sheep's wool, it lacks prominent scales, giving it a smooth, silky feel and less tendency to felt. Kid mohair, from a goat's first shearing, is the finest and softest grade.

Production

Angora goats originated in the Ankara region of Turkey. Major producing countries today include South Africa (the largest producer), the United States (especially Texas), Turkey, Argentina, Australia, and Lesotho. Goats are typically shorn twice a year.
