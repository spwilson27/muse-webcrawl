Heuristic (computer science)

A heuristic guides search toward promising states

In mathematical optimisation and computer science, a heuristic is a technique designed for problem solving more quickly when classic methods are too slow for finding an exact or approximate solution, or when classic methods fail to find any exact solution in a search space. This is achieved by trading optimality, completeness, accuracy, or precision for speed. In a way, it can be considered a shortcut.

Definition and use

A heuristic function, also simply called a heuristic, ranks alternatives in search algorithms at each branching step based on available information to decide which branch to follow. In the A* search algorithm, for instance, an admissible heuristic estimates the cost to reach the goal and never overestimates it; the straight-line distance is the classic example for route finding.

Examples

Well-known applications include greedy algorithms, the nearest-neighbour heuristic for the travelling salesman problem, simulated annealing, tabu search, genetic algorithms and other metaheuristics, and the evaluation functions used in game-playing programs. In virus scanning and other forms of heuristic analysis, software flags suspicious behaviour patterns rather than exact signatures of known malware.

Trade-offs

Heuristics are judged by criteria such as whether they produce good solutions within reasonable time, how close those solutions are to optimal, and how often they fail. The "no free lunch" theorems show that no heuristic outperforms all others over every possible problem, so heuristics are typically tuned to the structure of a particular problem class.
