Reaction shot

In film and video, a reaction shot is a shot that cuts away from the main scene to show a character's reaction to something that has occurred — typically another character's words or actions, or an event the audience has just witnessed. Reaction shots are a fundamental tool of continuity editing and film grammar.

Function

A reaction shot conveys emotion and point of view, guides the audience's response to an event, and can create comedy or suspense through timing — holding on a face a beat longer than expected, or cutting to a listener rather than a speaker. The Kuleshov effect, demonstrated by Soviet filmmaker Lev Kuleshov, showed that viewers derive more meaning from an actor's neutral expression when it is juxtaposed with different images, the principle underlying the reaction shot.

Use

Reaction shots are ubiquitous in dialogue scenes (cutting to the listener), comedy (the deadpan take), horror (a character's frightened face before the threat is shown), and television coverage of speeches and sport, where crowd and bench reactions punctuate the action. Excessive or mistimed reaction shots are a common criticism of manipulative editing in reality television.
