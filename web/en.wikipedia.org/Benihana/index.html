Benihana

Benihana Inc. is an American restaurant company based in the United States, best known for its Japanese teppanyaki restaurants at which chefs cook and perform at a hot steel grill (hibachi) in front of the guests. The first Benihana opened in 1964 on West 56th Street in New York City, founded by Hiroaki "Rocky" Aoki, a Japanese-born wrestler and restaurateur. The chain operates more than 70 restaurants in the United States, the Caribbean, and Central and South America.

History and format

Aoki named the restaurant after the red safflower (benihana in Japanese) found outside his father's Tokyo coffee shop. The dining format seats guests around a shared teppan grill where the chef slices, flips and seasons steak, chicken and seafood with theatrical knife and spatula work — including the famous flaming "onion volcano". The concept proved highly profitable because of its communal seating and limited menu, and Benihana expanded rapidly by franchising and company restaurants from the 1970s onward. Its success inspired many imitator teppanyaki chains in the United States.
