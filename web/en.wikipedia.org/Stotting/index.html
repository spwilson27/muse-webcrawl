Stotting

Stotting (also called pronking) is a gait shown by several species of antelope and gazelle, and some other mammals, in which the animal bounces repeatedly into the air with all four legs held stiff and straight, the back arched and the head lowered. It is most famously performed by Thomson's gazelles and springbok on the African savanna, and by mule deer in North America.

Behaviour

Stotting is performed especially when a predator has been detected nearby, and often by whole groups at once. Springbok may pronk in exuberant mass displays, sometimes interleaved with ordinary running. Young animals also stot in play.

Explanations

The function of stotting has been debated as a classic problem in behavioural ecology. The leading hypothesis, supported by observations of cheetah hunts, is that it is an honest signal to predators: a stotting gazelle advertises its fitness and the fact that it has seen the predator, discouraging pursuit — cheetahs are less likely to chase stotting individuals, and hunts against them succeed less often. Other proposed functions include alarming and confusing the herd's predators, signalling to conspecifics, and improving the animal's view of the surroundings. Stotting is an example used in discussions of handicap signalling and pursuit-deterrent signals.
