Zero-day (computing)

A zero-day (also spelled 0-day) is a computer-software vulnerability previously unknown to those who should be interested in its mitigation, such as the vendor of the target software. A zero-day exploit is code that takes advantage of such a vulnerability before a patch is available, meaning developers have had "zero days" to fix it.

Markets and use

Zero-day vulnerabilities are traded by researchers, brokers, criminals and governments. Prices vary with the target's popularity and the difficulty of exploitation. Notable uses include the Stuxnet worm, which employed multiple zero-days against Iranian nuclear facilities in 2010, and commercial spyware such as Pegasus.

Disclosure

Practices for handling zero-days range from coordinated (responsible) disclosure to vendors, to full public disclosure, to sale through vulnerability markets. Defenders counter zero-days with defence-in-depth, behavioural detection and rapid patching once a fix is released.
