The Carter Family is a traditional American folk music group that recorded between 1927 and 1956. Their recordings were among the earliest commercial country music recordings and had a profound influence on bluegrass, country, folk, and rock music. The original group consisted of Alvin Pleasant "A.P." Carter, his wife Sara Carter, and his sister-in-law Maybelle Carter.

Bristol Sessions

In August 1927 the family travelled to Bristol, Tennessee, to audition for Victor Records producer Ralph Peer, at the sessions now known as the Bristol Sessions — often called the "big bang" of country music. Their 1928 recording of "Wildwood Flower", built on Maybelle's distinctive guitar style, became a standard.

Style and legacy

A.P. collected and arranged folk songs from southwestern Virginia; Sara sang lead with a deep, distinctive voice; Maybelle's "Carter scratch" guitar technique, playing melody on the bass strings with chordal strumming, became foundational for country guitar. Signature songs include "Can the Circle Be Unbroken", "Keep on the Sunny Side", and "Wabash Cannonball". Maybelle and her daughters later performed as Mother Maybelle and the Carter Sisters; June Carter, one of the daughters, married Johnny Cash.
