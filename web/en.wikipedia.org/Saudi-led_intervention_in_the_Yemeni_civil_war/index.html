Saudi-led intervention in the Yemeni civil war

The Saudi-led intervention in the Yemeni civil war began on 26 March 2015, when a coalition led by Saudi Arabia launched a military intervention in Yemen, codenamed Operation Decisive Storm, at the request of the internationally recognised government of President Abdrabbuh Mansur Hadi. The intervention followed the Houthi movement's seizure of Sanaa and its advance on Aden, and aimed to restore Hadi's government and counter what Riyadh saw as Iranian influence.

Course of the intervention

The opening air campaign was followed by a naval blockade and, from mid-2015, a ground offensive with Emirati and other coalition troops that retook Aden and much of the south. Fighting then settled into a grinding stalemate: a major front at Marib, the battle for the Red Sea port of Hodeidah (checked by the 2018 Stockholm Agreement), and Houthi missile and drone attacks into Saudi Arabia and, later, against shipping in the Red Sea. In 2019 the UAE drew down its forces as southern separatists allied with it fought Hadi's government.

Humanitarian impact and criticism

The war has produced one of the world's worst humanitarian crises: the United Nations estimated some 377,000 deaths by the end of 2021 from fighting and indirect causes such as disease and hunger, with millions displaced and dependent on aid. Coalition airstrikes hit markets, weddings, funerals, and a school bus in Dahyan in 2018, drawing condemnation from human-rights groups and UN experts; Houthi forces were likewise accused of war crimes. Western arms sales to the coalition became politically contentious in the US, UK, and Europe.

Truce and aftermath

A UN-brokered truce from April 2022 sharply reduced fighting and, though formally lapsed, was largely maintained. In April 2022 Hadi transferred power to a Presidential Leadership Council. Direct Saudi–Houthi talks in 2023 raised hopes of a settlement, though Yemen's final political status remains unresolved.
