Silica gel

Chemical formulaSiO2
AppearanceTranslucent beads
UseDesiccant

Silica gel is an amorphous and porous form of silicon dioxide (silica), consisting of an irregular three-dimensional framework of alternating silicon and oxygen atoms with nanometer-scale voids and pores. The voids may contain water or some other liquids, or may be filled by gas or vacuum. In the last case, the material is properly called silica xerogel.

Silica gel is most commonly encountered in everyday life as beads in a small paper packet or sachet. In this form, it is used as a desiccant to control local humidity to avoid spoilage or degradation of some goods; the packets are often labelled "Do not eat".

Properties

Silica gel's high specific surface area — around 750–800 m2/g — allows it to adsorb water readily, making it useful as a desiccant; it can hold up to roughly 40% of its own mass in moisture. Once saturated, it can be regenerated by heating to about 120 °C (250 °F) for several hours.

History and applications

Silica gel was patented by chemistry professor Walter A. Patrick at Johns Hopkins University in 1919. It was used extensively in World War I for adsorption of vapours and gases in gas mask canisters, and in World War II to keep penicillin dry. Beyond desiccant packets, silica gel serves as a stationary phase in chromatography and as the granules in many cat litters.
