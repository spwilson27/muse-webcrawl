Rhine–Meuse–Scheldt delta

The Rhine–Meuse–Scheldt delta is a river delta in the Netherlands and Belgium formed by the confluence of the Rhine, the Meuse and the Scheldt rivers. In some cases an H-shaped figure, it stretches through the South-Western Netherlands and Northern Belgium.

The result is a multitude of islands, branches and branch names that may at first sight look bewildering, especially as a waterway that appears to be one continuous stream may change names as many as seven times, e.g. Rhine → Bijlands Kanaal → Pannerdens Kanaal → IJssel → Nederrijn → Lek → Nieuwe Maas → Het Scheur → Nieuwe Waterweg → North Sea.

Geography

The Rhine–Meuse Delta is the most important natural region of the Netherlands. Roughly speaking, it is situated in the west and north-west of the Netherlands, roughly below the line Dordrecht – Gorinchem – Nijmegen – Arnhem. The whole region is characterised by the presence of numerous branched rivers, which often changed their course in the past and whose present course has mostly been determined by human intervention such as the construction of dykes, dams and canals, and the straightening of bends.

After the disastrous North Sea flood of 1953, the Delta Works were constructed, a series of dams, sluices, locks, dykes and storm surge barriers designed to shorten the Dutch coastline and protect the delta region from flooding.

Economy

The delta contains two of Europe's largest ports: Rotterdam, for many years the busiest port in the world, and Antwerp in Belgium. The navigable waterways of the delta connect the North Sea with the industrial heartland of Germany, including the Ruhr area.
