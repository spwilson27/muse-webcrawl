Fluorine-18

Symbol: 18F
Half-life: 109.77 minutes
Decay mode: β+ (96.7%), electron capture (3.3%)
Decay product: 18O

Fluorine-18 (18F) is a fluorine radioisotope which is an important source of positrons. It has a mass of 18.0009380 u and a half-life of 109.8 minutes — the longest of the commonly used positron emitters — which makes it feasible to manufacture 18F-labelled radiopharmaceuticals at a central cyclotron facility and distribute them to regional PET imaging centres.

Decay and production

Fluorine-18 decays by positron emission (96.7%) and electron capture (3.3%) to stable oxygen-18. The emitted positron annihilates with an electron, producing two 511 keV gamma photons detected by the PET scanner. 18F is produced most commonly by bombarding 18O-enriched water with protons in a cyclotron (the 18O(p,n)18F reaction).

Applications

The most important 18F radiopharmaceutical is fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG), an analogue of glucose that accumulates in tissues with high glucose metabolism. FDG-PET is widely used in oncology to detect and stage tumours, in cardiology to assess myocardial viability, and in neurology. Other tracers include sodium fluoride (18F-NaF) for bone imaging and amyloid and tau tracers for Alzheimer's disease research.
