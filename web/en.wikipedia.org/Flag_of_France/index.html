Flag of France

Flag of France
Name: Tricolore, le drapeau tricolore
Adopted: 15 February 1794 (re-adopted 1848)
Design: Vertical tricolour of blue, white, red
Proportions: 2:3 (civil); 30:33:37 (naval jack)

The national flag of France (French: drapeau tricolore, "tricolour flag") is a vertical tricolour of blue, white and red. It is one of the most influential flags in history, inspiring numerous other tricolour designs across Europe and the Americas.

Design

The flag consists of three equal vertical bands: blue at the hoist, white in the middle, and red at the fly. On land the bands are equal in width (1:1:1); the naval ensign uses narrower proportions of 30:33:37 for optical balance. In 2020 President Emmanuel Macron restored the darker navy blue (bleu marine) on presidential usage after it had been lightened in 1976.

History

The tricolour originated during the French Revolution, combining the blue and red of the cockade of Paris with the white of the Bourbon monarchy. It was adopted as the national flag on 15 February 1794. Napoleon retained it, the Bourbons restored the white flag in 1814, and the tricolour was re-adopted after the 1848 revolution.

Symbolism

The colours are traditionally associated with the revolutionary motto Liberté, égalité, fraternité and with the three orders of the old regime. Article 2 of the Constitution of the Fifth Republic declares the tricolour the national emblem of France.
