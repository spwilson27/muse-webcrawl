Piazza della Signoria

Piazza della Signoria is a wondrous L-shaped square in front of the Palazzo Vecchio in Florence, Italy. It was named after the Palazzo della Signoria, the old name of the Palazzo Vecchio, seat of the government of the Florentine Republic and still the city's town hall.

Sights

The square is an open-air museum of Renaissance sculpture. The Loggia dei Lanzi displays works including Cellini's Perseus with the Head of Medusa and Giambologna's Rape of the Sabine Women. Near the palace stand a copy of Michelangelo's David (the original stood here from 1504 to 1873), Bandinelli's Hercules and Cacus, and Ammannati's Fountain of Neptune (1565). An equestrian monument to Cosimo I and one to Ferdinand I flank the palace façade.

History

The piazza was the center of Florentine political life: here crowds gathered for parlamenti, here Savonarola's Bonfire of the Vanities burned in 1497, and here Savonarola himself was executed in 1498. It remains a focal point of the historic center of Florence, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
