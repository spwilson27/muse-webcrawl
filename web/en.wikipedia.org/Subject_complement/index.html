Subject complement

A subject complement is a word or phrase that follows a linking verb and describes or renames the subject of the clause. Subject complements are either predicate nominatives (nouns or pronouns) or predicate adjectives.

Subject complement
Field: Grammar
Follows: Linking verbs (be, seem, become)
Types: Predicate nominative, predicate adjective

Examples

In "She is a doctor," the noun phrase "a doctor" is a predicate nominative renaming the subject. In "The soup tastes good," the adjective "good" is a predicate adjective describing the subject. Unlike objects, subject complements refer to the same entity as the subject.

Linking verbs

The most common linking verb is be; verbs of perception (look, feel, smell, sound) and verbs such as seem, become, remain, and grow can also take subject complements. Because the complement describes the subject, adjectives—not adverbs—are used: "he feels bad," not "badly," in formal usage.

In other languages

Many languages require agreement between subject and complement, and some mark predicate nouns with special case forms or omit the copula entirely.
