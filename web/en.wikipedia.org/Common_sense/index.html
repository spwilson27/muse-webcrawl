Common sense

Common sense is sound, practical judgment concerning everyday matters, or a basic ability to perceive, understand, and judge in a manner that is shared by (hence "common to") nearly all people. In everyday usage it denotes the elementary knowledge and experience most people are expected to have without needing specialist training.

Philosophy

The concept descends in part from Aristotle's sensus communis, the faculty integrating the five senses, and from the Latin sensus communis as a shared sensibility. René Descartes treated good sense (bon sens) as the most evenly distributed faculty of reason. In the 18th century Thomas Reid founded the Scottish school of common sense philosophy, arguing that certain first principles are self-evident foundations of knowledge, against the scepticism of Hume. G. E. Moore later defended common-sense certainties against philosophical paradox.

Thomas Paine's pamphlet Common Sense (1776) famously appealed to ordinary judgment in arguing for American independence. In artificial intelligence research, "common sense" denotes the vast background knowledge humans use effortlessly, long a major challenge for machine reasoning.
