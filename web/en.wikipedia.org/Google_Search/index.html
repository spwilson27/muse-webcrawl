Google Search

Google Search is a search engine operated by Google. Handling over 3.5 billion searches per day, it has a dominant market share of global search queries, making it the most visited website in the world. It was developed by Larry Page and Sergey Brin while they were PhD students at Stanford University, and it launched in 1997.

History

Google Search began as a research project called BackRub, based on analyzing the web's link structure with the PageRank algorithm. Google was incorporated in 1998, and the search engine quickly grew beyond the university. Over the following decades Google added many features, including image search, news search, maps integration, personalized results, featured snippets, and the Knowledge Graph, which launched in 2012 to provide direct answers to factual queries. In the 2020s, AI-generated overviews became part of the results page.

How it works

Google Search works in three broad stages: crawling, in which automated programs called crawlers (notably Googlebot) discover pages across the web; indexing, in which the content of pages is analyzed and stored in a massive index; and serving, in which algorithms rank the indexed pages for a user's query using hundreds of signals, of which PageRank — based on the links pointing to a page — was historically one of the most important.

Features and products

Search supports special operators and vertical searches for images, videos, news, shopping, books, flights, and finance. Features include autocomplete suggestions, spelling correction ("Did you mean"), calculator and unit conversion tools, weather and sports results, and Rich Results such as recipes and event listings. Voice search and image-based search (Google Lens) extend the service beyond typed queries.

Criticism

Google Search has been criticized over privacy (data collection and tracking), the influence of search engine optimization and spam, alleged bias in results, and its market dominance, which has led to antitrust cases in the United States and the European Union, including major fines from the European Commission over shopping search and Android bundling.

Market share

Google holds roughly 90% of the global search engine market, far ahead of competitors such as Microsoft Bing, Yahoo, Baidu (in China), and Yandex (in Russia). Its dominance is reinforced by default placement deals, including paying Apple to be the default search engine in Safari.
