Director of Public Prosecutions

The DPP heads the public prosecution service in many common-law jurisdictions.

The Director of Public Prosecutions (DPP) is the office or official charged with responsibility for conducting criminal prosecutions on behalf of the state in many common-law jurisdictions, including England and Wales, Ireland, Australia, Canada, Hong Kong, Kenya, and a number of Caribbean states. The office exists to exercise prosecutorial discretion independently of the police and, ordinarily, of day-to-day political control.

England and Wales

In England and Wales the office was created by the Prosecution of Offences Act 1879. The DPP heads the Crown Prosecution Service (CPS), established by the Prosecution of Offences Act 1985, which took over prosecutions previously conducted by the police. The DPP decides whether to charge in serious and sensitive cases and issues the Code for Crown Prosecutors, which applies two tests: whether there is sufficient evidence for a realistic prospect of conviction, and whether a prosecution is in the public interest. The DPP is appointed by the Attorney General, to whom the office is answerable while remaining operationally independent.

Other jurisdictions

In Ireland, the DPP was established by the Prosecution of Offences Act 1974 and is constitutionally independent in the performance of the office's functions. Australia has a Commonwealth DPP (created in 1983) and separate state and territory directors. In Canada, the Director of Public Prosecutions heads the Public Prosecution Service of Canada, created in 2006. In several jurisdictions the DPP's consent is required before certain offences — such as official secrets, terrorism, or assisted suicide cases — may be prosecuted.

Functions

Typical functions include instituting and conducting criminal proceedings, taking over private prosecutions, advising police on charging, discontinuing proceedings where tests are not met, and representing the prosecution in appeals. The separation of investigation (police) from prosecution (DPP) is regarded as an important safeguard of fairness and consistency.
