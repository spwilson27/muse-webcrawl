Finitism

Finitism is a philosophy of mathematics holding that mathematical objects and proofs must be finite: it rejects completed infinities such as infinite sets taken as finished totalities. Associated with figures including Leopold Kronecker and, in a methodological form, David Hilbert's finitary standpoint, it insists that existence claims be backed by explicit constructions.

Hilbert's programme

Hilbert proposed securing all of mathematics by proving its consistency using only finitistic methods. Gödel's incompleteness theorems (1931) showed this could not be carried out for sufficiently strong systems, though Gentzen later proved the consistency of arithmetic using transfinite induction up to ε₀.

Variants

Ultrafinitism goes further, questioning even very large finite numbers. Finitist ideas survive in constructive mathematics and in the study of weak arithmetical theories such as primitive recursive arithmetic.
