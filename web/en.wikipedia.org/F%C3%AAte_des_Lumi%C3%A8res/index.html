The Fête des Lumières (Festival of Lights) is a popular festival held annually in Lyon, France, around December 8, lasting four nights. It is one of the largest events of its kind in the world, drawing millions of visitors to see large-scale light installations by international artists projected onto the city's buildings, streets, rivers, and hills.

Origins

The festival originates in the tradition of lighting candles in honour of the Virgin Mary. In 1643, Lyon's councillors vowed to honour Mary if the city were spared from plague, and processions to the hill of Fourvière followed. On September 8, 1852, the inauguration of a statue of the Virgin on the chapel of Fourvière was postponed by flooding to December 8; that evening, residents spontaneously placed candles — lumignons — on their windowsills, creating the tradition Lyonnais still observe by lighting their windows on December 8.

Modern festival

The modern artistic festival developed from the city's lighting plan adopted in 1989 and took its present form from 1999, when large-scale artistic installations were added. Landmarks such as Saint-Jean Cathedral, Place des Terreaux, and the Basilica of Notre-Dame de Fourvière serve as canvases for projection mapping. The 2015 edition was scaled back and dedicated to the victims of the November 2015 Paris attacks, with a candle tribute. The festival has inspired similar light festivals worldwide, and Lyon's lighting expertise is exported internationally.
