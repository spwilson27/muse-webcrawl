Photorespiration

Photorespiration (also known as the oxidative photosynthetic carbon cycle, or C2 photosynthesis) is a process in plant metabolism in which the enzyme RuBisCO oxygenates RuBP instead of carboxylating it, wasting some of the energy produced by photosynthesis and releasing previously fixed carbon dioxide.

Mechanism

When RuBisCO adds O2 to ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate, it produces one molecule of 3-phosphoglycerate and one of 2-phosphoglycolate, a toxic compound that must be recycled. The salvage pathway spans the chloroplast, peroxisome and mitochondrion, and for every two molecules of phosphoglycolate processed it recovers three-quarters of the carbon while releasing CO2 and ammonia and consuming ATP and reducing power.

Significance

Photorespiration can reduce the efficiency of photosynthesis in C3 plants by 20–50%, especially at high temperatures and low CO2 concentrations, when stomata close to conserve water. It arose because RuBisCO evolved in an ancient atmosphere poor in oxygen and retains an imperfect specificity for CO2 over O2. C4 and CAM plants suppress photorespiration by concentrating CO2 around RuBisCO. Researchers are engineering photorespiratory bypasses and improved RuBisCO to raise crop yields, and the pathway may also serve protective roles, dissipating excess energy and limiting photoinhibition.
