Formula Drift

Tandem drifting at a Formula Drift round.

Formula Drift, officially the Formula DRIFT Championship and often abbreviated as FD or Formula D, is a United States-based drifting series founded in 2003 by Jim Liaw and Ryan Sage under Slipstream Global Marketing, the same partnership that brought the D1 Grand Prix exhibition to North America. Its first season ran in 2004. Formula Drift is generally considered the premier professional drifting championship in North America and one of the top series in the world alongside Japan's D1 Grand Prix.

Format

Events are held at race tracks and temporary street courses across the United States, with rounds at venues such as the streets of Long Beach, Road Atlanta, Evergreen Speedway in Washington and Irwindale Speedway in California — the "House of Drift". Drivers qualify with solo judged runs, then compete in tandem elimination battles: a lead driver follows the ideal drifting line while the chase driver tries to mirror the lead's transitions while staying as close as possible. Three judges score line, angle and style, with speed factored in.

Cars and technology

Formula Drift cars are among the most powerful in drifting, with top Pro-category machines producing well over 1,000 horsepower, often using turbocharged or nitrous-assisted V8 engine swaps, sequential gearboxes and extensive suspension modification for extreme steering angle. Popular platforms include the Nissan Silvia and Z, Toyota Supra and 86, Ford Mustang, Chevrolet Corvette and BMW models.

History and champions

Chris Forsberg won the inaugural 2004 championship and became the series' first three-time champion. Other multiple champions include Vaughn Gittin Jr., Daigo Saito (2012), Fredric Aasbø, James Deane — who won three consecutive titles from 2017 to 2019 — and Chelsea DeNofa. The series also runs the ProSpec (formerly Pro2/ProAm feeder) category and licenses international affiliate series such as Formula Drift Japan.
