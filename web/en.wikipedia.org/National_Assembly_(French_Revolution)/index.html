National Assembly (French Revolution)

The National Assembly (French: Assemblée nationale) was the revolutionary assembly formed during the French Revolution by representatives of the Third Estate of the Estates-General. It existed from 17 June 1789 to 9 July 1789, when it became the National Constituent Assembly, which lasted until 30 September 1791.

Formation

When the Estates-General convened at Versailles in May 1789, deadlock over whether voting should be by order or by head led the Third Estate, joined by some clergy and nobles, to declare itself the National Assembly on 17 June. Locked out of their hall three days later, the deputies swore the Tennis Court Oath (20 June 1789) not to disperse until France had a constitution. After the storming of the Bastille, the Assembly abolished feudal privileges in the August Decrees (4 August 1789) and adopted the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen on 26 August 1789.

Work

As the Constituent Assembly it restructured France: it confiscated church lands, issued assignats, reorganised the country into departments, promulgated the Civil Constitution of the Clergy (1790), and completed the Constitution of 1791, turning France into a constitutional monarchy before dissolving itself in favour of the Legislative Assembly.
