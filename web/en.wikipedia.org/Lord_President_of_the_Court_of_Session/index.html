Lord President of the Court of Session

The Lord President of the Court of Session is the most senior judge in Scotland, the head of the Scottish judiciary and the presiding judge of the Court of Session, Scotland's supreme civil court. The holder also bears the title Lord Justice General, presiding over the High Court of Justiciary in its capacity as Scotland's supreme criminal court.

Role

The Lord President presides over the First Division of the Inner House of the Court of Session, leads the judiciary in representing its views to the Scottish Parliament and government, and oversees the administration of the Scottish courts through the Judicial Office for Scotland. The office ranks immediately after the royal family in the Scottish order of precedence among office-holders.

History

The Court of Session was established in 1532 by King James V, and its president has been its head since its foundation. Holders are appointed by the monarch on the recommendation of the First Minister of Scotland. Notable Lord Presidents include Lord Cooper, Lord Rodger of Earlsferry and Lord Carloway.
