Education in Canada

Education in Canada is for the most part provided publicly, and is funded and overseen by provincial, territorial, and local governments. Under the Canadian constitution, education is the exclusive responsibility of the provinces; there is no federal department of education and no integrated national system. Canada consistently ranks among the highest-performing countries in international assessments such as the OECD's PISA tests.

Structure

Schooling is generally divided into primary education, followed by secondary education (high school) and post-secondary education. Education is compulsory up to the age of 16 in most provinces and 18 in Ontario and New Brunswick. The school year normally runs from September to June, and children usually begin with kindergarten at age four or five and finish with Grade 12.

Both English and French are languages of instruction; most students outside Quebec are taught in English, while Quebec's schools are predominantly French-language. Publicly funded separate (Roman Catholic) school systems exist in several provinces under constitutional guarantees, and a small proportion of students attend private or independent schools or are home-schooled.

Post-secondary education

Canada has a large network of universities and colleges, including institutions such as the University of Toronto, McGill University, and the University of British Columbia. Public funding keeps tuition below American private levels, though fees have risen steadily. Canada is also a leading destination for international students, who form a significant share of enrolments.

Indigenous education

Education of First Nations, Métis, and Inuit people is a distinct and historically fraught part of the system. From the nineteenth century until the late twentieth, the federal government funded church-run residential schools intended to assimilate Indigenous children; the Truth and Reconciliation Commission (2015) characterised the system as cultural genocide, and reform of Indigenous education remains an ongoing policy priority.
