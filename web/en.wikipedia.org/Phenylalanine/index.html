Phenylalanine

FormulaC9H11NO2
AbbreviationsPhe, F
TypeEssential, aromatic α-amino acid
Precursor ofTyrosine; catecholamine neurotransmitters

Phenylalanine (symbol Phe or F) is an essential α-amino acid with the formula C9H11NO2. It is a benzyl-substituted alanine, classified as aromatic and nonpolar. Because the human body cannot synthesise it, phenylalanine must be obtained from the diet; good sources include meat, fish, eggs, dairy products, soy and legumes.

Biological role

Phenylalanine is a building block of proteins and the starting point of an important metabolic pathway. The enzyme phenylalanine hydroxylase converts it to tyrosine, which in turn is the precursor of the catecholamine neurotransmitters dopamine, norepinephrine and epinephrine, and of the pigment melanin. The L-enantiomer is the biologically common form; D-phenylalanine occurs in some bacterial products.

Phenylketonuria

Deficiency of phenylalanine hydroxylase causes the inherited metabolic disorder phenylketonuria (PKU), in which phenylalanine accumulates to neurotoxic levels. Untreated PKU causes intellectual disability, but newborn screening and a lifelong low-phenylalanine diet largely prevent harm. Because the artificial sweetener aspartame is a source of phenylalanine, foods containing it carry warnings for people with PKU.

Production and uses

Phenylalanine was first isolated in 1879 from lupin seedlings and its structure established shortly afterwards. Industrially it is produced by fermentation with engineered bacteria and is used in foods, nutritional supplements and in the manufacture of aspartame.
