Central Valley (California)

Length: ~450 mi (720 km)
Width: 40–60 mi (64–97 km)
Major cities: Sacramento, Fresno, Bakersfield, Stockton, Modesto

The Central Valley is a broad, flat valley that dominates inland California. Running roughly 450 miles (720 km) from north to south between the Sierra Nevada to the east and the Coast Ranges to the west, it comprises the Sacramento Valley in the north and the San Joaquin Valley in the south, which meet at the Sacramento–San Joaquin River Delta.

Agriculture

The valley is one of the world's most productive agricultural regions, producing a large share of the United States' fruits, vegetables, and nuts — including almonds, grapes, tomatoes, lettuce, and citrus — despite receiving little rainfall. Irrigation from rivers, reservoirs, and groundwater makes farming possible, and water allocation between farms, cities, and the environment is a persistent political issue. Heavy groundwater pumping has caused land subsidence in parts of the San Joaquin Valley.

Climate and environment

The valley has a Mediterranean to semi-arid climate with hot, dry summers; in winter, dense radiation fog known as tule fog forms in low areas. Air quality is among the poorest in the United States due to trapped pollution and dust. The valley floor was historically a mosaic of wetlands, grasslands, and riparian forest, much of it now converted to agriculture.
