Intelligence agency

Intelligence agency
PurposeCollection, analysis, and exploitation of information for national security and foreign policy
DisciplinesHUMINT, SIGINT, IMINT, OSINT
ExamplesCIA, MI6, BND, MSS, FSB

An intelligence agency is a government agency responsible for the collection, analysis, and exploitation of information in support of law enforcement, national security, military, public safety, and foreign policy objectives. Depending on the state, agencies may focus on foreign intelligence, domestic security, military intelligence, or signals intelligence, and are typically overseen — at least formally — by legislatures and courts.

Functions

Agencies collect information through human intelligence (HUMINT, espionage and agent handling), signals intelligence (SIGINT, interception of communications and electronic signals), imagery intelligence (IMINT, from satellites and aircraft), measurement and signature intelligence (MASINT), and open-source intelligence (OSINT). Analysts evaluate the material and produce assessments for policymakers; some agencies also conduct covert action.

History

Organised intelligence services emerged in their modern form in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries — Britain's Secret Service Bureau (1909), later MI5 and MI6, is an early example. The Second World War spurred large-scale organisations such as the American OSS, the predecessor of the CIA (founded 1947). The Cold War institutionalised intelligence bureaucracies worldwide.

Oversight and controversy

Democracies struggle to balance secrecy with accountability. Revelations about surveillance programmes, covert operations, and intelligence failures — such as those preceding the 2003 Iraq War — have repeatedly prompted reforms and inquiries. Intelligence agencies also conduct counter-intelligence to detect and neutralise foreign espionage.
