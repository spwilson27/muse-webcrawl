Dan Sperber

Dan Sperber
Born: 20 June 1942, Cagnes-sur-Mer, France
Fields: Anthropology, linguistics, cognitive science, philosophy
Known for: Relevance theory, epidemiology of representations

Dan Sperber (born 20 June 1942) is a French social and cognitive scientist, known for his work in anthropology, linguistics, philosophy, and the cognitive sciences. He has been a researcher at the CNRS in Paris, associated with the Institut Jean Nicod, and has held visiting positions at universities including Cambridge, Princeton, and the London School of Economics.

Work

With Deirdre Wilson, Sperber developed relevance theory, presented in their book Relevance: Communication and Cognition (1986), an influential cognitive approach to pragmatics that treats human communication as governed by a search for optimal relevance. In anthropology, his early book Rethinking Symbolism (1975) criticized purely semiotic interpretations of symbolism and proposed a cognitive account. He later developed the "epidemiology of representations", an approach to culture in which beliefs and practices spread through populations via chains of communication and transformation — a precursor to contemporary work on cultural evolution and cultural attraction theory. His later work includes the argumentative theory of reasoning, developed with Hugo Mercier.
