Cremation

Cremation is a method of final disposition of a dead body through burning. It is an alternative to burial and is practised by many cultures and religions, and is central to Hindu, Buddhist, Jain, and Sikh funeral traditions. Cremated remains ("ashes") are typically returned to the family in an urn, and may be kept, scattered, or interred.

Process

Modern cremation takes place in a crematorium furnace (retort) heated to roughly 760–980 °C (1400–1800 °F). The process takes one to several hours depending on body size; remaining bone fragments are then processed into a coarse, ash-like powder.

History and prevalence

Cremation dates back at least to the Bronze Age in Europe and is far older in some regions; the Mungo Lady remains in Australia, some 26,000–40,000 years old, are among the earliest known cremations. Cremation rates have risen steadily in Western countries since the 1960s and now exceed burial in the United States, Canada, and much of Europe; Japan has one of the highest rates in the world.
