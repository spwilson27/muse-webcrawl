Broadband

In telecommunications, broadband is the wide-bandwidth data transmission that carries multiple signals at a wide range of frequencies, enabling fast access to the Internet. The term is commonly used for high-speed, always-on Internet access provided over technologies such as digital subscriber line (DSL), cable modem, optical fibre, wireless and satellite connections.

Overview

Originally, "broadband" described a transmission medium with a wide frequency band, in contrast to baseband signalling. In Internet access the term came to mean any connection faster than dial-up access over the public switched telephone network. Regulatory definitions vary: the United States Federal Communications Commission has for many years used a benchmark of 25 Mbit/s download and 3 Mbit/s upload, raised in 2024 to 100/20 Mbit/s, while other bodies set different thresholds.

Technologies

DSL transmits data over copper telephone lines; variants such as ADSL and VDSL offer progressively higher speeds over shorter distances. Cable broadband uses coaxial cable networks built for television, standardised by DOCSIS. Fibre to the home (FTTH) carries data over optical fibre and supports gigabit speeds. Fixed wireless and mobile broadband (4G LTE and 5G) deliver service by radio, while satellite broadband serves remote areas, increasingly through low-Earth-orbit constellations.

Adoption and policy

Broadband has become essential infrastructure for commerce, education, healthcare and government services. Governments run subsidy and universal-service programmes to extend coverage, and the gap in access between urban and rural areas is commonly called the digital divide. By the mid-2020s, several billion people used broadband Internet worldwide.
