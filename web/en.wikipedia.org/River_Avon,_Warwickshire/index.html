The River Avon in Warwickshire is a river in central England, a left-bank tributary of the River Severn. It is 137 kilometres (85 miles) long and is often called the Warwickshire Avon or Shakespeare's Avon to distinguish it from the several other English rivers of the same name; "Avon" derives from the Brittonic word for river.

Course

The river rises near Naseby in Northamptonshire and flows west through Rugby, Warwick, Stratford-upon-Avon, Evesham, and Pershore, before joining the Severn at Tewkesbury in Gloucestershire. Its main tributaries include the Leam, the Stour, the Sowe, and the Arrow.

Navigation and culture

The Avon was made navigable from Tewkesbury to Evesham in the seventeenth century under the engineer William Sandys, and navigation was extended to Stratford by 1717. After falling derelict, the river was restored by volunteers in two campaigns: the Lower Avon reopened in 1962 and the Upper Avon Navigation in 1974, creating a cruising route connected to the canal network via the Stratford-upon-Avon Canal.

The river's association with William Shakespeare, born in Stratford in 1564, has made it a focus of literary tourism; the riverside at Stratford is overlooked by the Royal Shakespeare Theatre.
