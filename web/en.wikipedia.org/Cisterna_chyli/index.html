Cisterna chyli

Cisterna chyli
Part of: Lymphatic system
Drains to: Thoracic duct
Source: Intestinal trunk, lumbar lymph trunks

The cisterna chyli (also called the receptaculum chyli) is a dilated sac at the lower end of the thoracic duct in most mammals. It receives fatty chyle from the intestines via the intestinal lymph trunk and lymph from the lower body via the right and left lumbar trunks, and conveys this lymph into the thoracic duct, which ultimately empties into the venous circulation near the junction of the left internal jugular and left subclavian veins.

Anatomy

In humans, the cisterna chyli is located posterior to the abdominal aorta, at the level of the first and second lumbar vertebrae (L1–L2), just to the right of the aorta and behind the right crus of the diaphragm. It is an elongated, sac-like structure typically about 5 cm long. It is present as a distinct sac in roughly half of individuals; in others, the thoracic duct begins as a plexus of vessels.

Function

The cisterna chyli acts as a reservoir and conduit for lymph rich in absorbed dietary fats (chyle) from the lacteals of the small intestine, together with lymph drained from the pelvis, lower limbs, and abdomen. From the cisterna chyli, the thoracic duct ascends through the thorax to return the lymph to the bloodstream.

Clinical significance

Injury to the cisterna chyli or thoracic duct during surgery can cause chyle leakage (chylothorax or chylous ascites). The structure can be visualized with lymphangiography and is relevant in retroperitoneal and thoracic surgery.
