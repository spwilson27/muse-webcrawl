Junkers Ju 52

The Junkers Ju 52 is a German transport aircraft manufactured by the Junkers company, first flown in 1930 and produced from 1931 to 1945. Nicknamed Tante Ju ("Aunt Ju") by German troops and "Iron Annie" by Allied personnel, it became one of the most recognisable aircraft of the Second World War because of its distinctive corrugated metal skin and three-engine layout.

Development

The Ju 52 began as a single-engine freighter designed by Ernst Zindel at Junkers in Dessau. Its corrugated duralumin skin, a hallmark of Hugo Junkers' designs, gave the airframe great strength. The single-engine version proved underpowered, so the definitive three-engine variant, the Ju 52/3m, was produced, powered by BMW 132 radial engines.

Operational history

Lufthansa operated the Ju 52 as its main airliner during the 1930s. During the Spanish Civil War it served as a bomber and transport with the Condor Legion. In the Second World War it was the Luftwaffe's standard transport, used for paratroop drops in the Netherlands, Norway and Crete, and for supply missions at Stalingrad. After the war, examples were built in France (as the AAC.1 Toucan) and Spain (CASA 352), and the Swiss Air Force flew the type until 1982.

Specifications

The Ju 52/3m g7e had a crew of three, capacity for 18 troops, a maximum speed of about 265 km/h, a range of roughly 870 km, and a service ceiling near 5,500 m. Its fixed tailwheel undercarriage and low wing loading gave it excellent short-field performance.
