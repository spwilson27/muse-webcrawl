Treaty of Falaise

The Treaty of Falaise was an agreement concluded in December 1174 between William the Lion, King of Scots, and Henry II of England. William had been captured at the Battle of Alnwick in July 1174 while invading northern England in support of the revolt of Henry's sons. In exchange for his release, William was forced to accept humiliating terms.

Terms

Under the treaty, William acknowledged Henry as his feudal overlord, did homage for Scotland and his other lands, and surrendered the key castles of Roxburgh, Berwick, Jedburgh, Edinburgh, and Stirling to be garrisoned by English troops at Scottish expense. The Scottish church was also subordinated to the English church, and Henry could take hostages, including William's brother David, to guarantee compliance.

Aftermath

The treaty remained in force for fifteen years until Richard I, needing money for the Third Crusade, sold back Scottish independence by the Quitclaim of Canterbury in 1189 for 10,000 marks.
