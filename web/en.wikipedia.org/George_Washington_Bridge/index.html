The George Washington Bridge is a double-decked suspension bridge spanning the Hudson River between Fort Lee, New Jersey, and the Washington Heights neighbourhood of Manhattan in New York City. It is one of the busiest motor-vehicle bridges in the world, carrying over 100 million vehicles a year.

History

Designed by chief engineer Othmar Ammann, the bridge opened in 1931 as the longest main-span suspension bridge in the world, nearly doubling the previous record. Originally called the Hudson River Bridge, it was renamed for George Washington. A second, lower deck was added in 1962 to meet growing traffic demand.

Design

The bridge's two steel towers rise 604 feet above the river. The steel towers were originally intended to be clad in concrete and granite, but the exposed steel lattice was retained for economic reasons and came to be admired; Le Corbusier praised the bridge as a masterpiece of modern engineering.

Traffic

The bridge carries Interstate 95 and U.S. Routes 1, 9 and 46. Its toll plaza in Fort Lee was at the centre of the 2013 "Bridgegate" scandal, in which lanes were deliberately closed for political retaliation.
