Marabout

A marabout is a Muslim religious leader and teacher in West Africa and the Maghreb, historically often a member of a Sufi brotherhood, credited with spiritual power (baraka) and serving as a mediator, healer and adviser. The word derives from the Arabic murabit, originally one who garrisoned a frontier fort (ribat).

Roles

Marabouts historically led Quranic schools, composed amulets bearing verses of the Quran, arbitrated disputes and sometimes organised resistance to colonial rule. In Senegal, marabouts of the Mouride and Tijani brotherhoods remain influential religious, economic and political figures, and their tomb-shrines draw pilgrims.

Other uses

The term also names the marabou stork, a large African wading bird, and in Haiti a person of mixed African and indigenous or European ancestry in the colonial classification system.
