Mongol invasion of Hungary

The Mongol invasion devastated the Kingdom of Hungary in 1241–42

The Mongol invasion of Hungary (Hungarian: tatárjárás) was the invasion of the Kingdom of Hungary by the Mongol Empire in 1241–42, part of the wider Mongol invasion of Europe. Forces under Batu Khan and the general Subutai — perhaps 70,000 men in the invasion of Hungary proper — crushed the Hungarian royal army at the Battle of Mohi on 11 April 1241, occupied the Great Hungarian Plain, and killed a very large share of the population, with estimates of deaths ranging from 15% to as much as half of the kingdom's roughly two million people.

The campaign

After subjugating Kievan Rus', Mongol columns crossed the Carpathians in March 1241, one army having already destroyed a Polish-German force at Legnica. King Béla IV met the main Mongol army at the Sajó river near Mohi; a night assault on the bridge and an encirclement annihilated the Hungarian host, though Béla escaped, eventually fleeing to the Dalmatian coast. Mongol detachments pursued him, sacking cities including Pest and Esztergom, and crossed the frozen Danube in the winter of 1241–42 to raid into Austria and Croatia.

Withdrawal and aftermath

In the spring of 1242 the Mongols withdrew eastward. The traditional explanation is the death of Great Khan Ögedei in December 1241, requiring the princes to return for the election; modern historians also weigh Hungarian resistance from stone castles (which largely held), logistics, and weather. Béla IV — remembered as the "second founder of the state" — rebuilt with stone fortresses, resettled depopulated lands, and granted land to military orders. The invasion left a lasting mark on Hungarian memory and settlement patterns, and a second invasion in 1285 was successfully repelled.
