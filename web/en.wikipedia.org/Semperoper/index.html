Semperoper

Semperoper
Location: Theaterplatz, Dresden, Germany
Architect: Gottfried Semper; Manfred Semper
Home companies: Sächsische Staatsoper Dresden; Staatskapelle Dresden
Capacity: approx. 1,300

The Semperoper is the opera house of the Sächsische Staatsoper Dresden (Saxon State Opera) and the concert hall of the Staatskapelle Dresden, in Dresden, Germany. Standing on the Theaterplatz beside the Elbe, it is regarded as one of the most beautiful opera houses in Europe.

History

The first opera house on the site, designed by Gottfried Semper, opened in 1841 and premiered Wagner's Rienzi and The Flying Dutchman. It burned down in 1869. Semper, in exile for his part in the May Uprising, supplied the plans for a grander replacement, built under his son Manfred and opened in 1878; Strauss's Salome, Elektra and Der Rosenkavalier were premiered there. The house was destroyed in the bombing of Dresden in February 1945 and stood as a ruin for decades before being rebuilt to the historic plans, reopening on 13 February 1985 with Weber's Der Freischütz.

Features

Noted features include its richly decorated Renaissance Revival auditorium, the five-minute clock above the stage, and the famous painted curtain. The resident orchestra, the Staatskapelle Dresden, founded in 1548, is among the world's oldest.
