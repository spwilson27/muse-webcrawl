Nevado Ojos del Salado is an active stratovolcano on the border between Argentina and Chile. At 6,893 metres (22,615 ft) it is the highest volcano on Earth and the second-highest mountain in both the Western and Southern Hemispheres, after Aconcagua. It is also the highest peak in Chile.

Geography and geology

The mountain rises from the high desert plateau of the Puna de Atacama, one of the driest places on Earth, which accounts for its sparse snow cover despite its altitude. Ojos del Salado is a massive volcanic complex of lava domes and flows; it has shown fumarolic activity, and a possible small eruption was reported in 1993, though no major historical eruption is confirmed. On its eastern flank, at about 6,390 metres, lies what is often described as the world's highest permanent lake.

Climbing

The first recorded ascent was made in 1937 by Polish climbers Jan Alfred Szczepański and Justyn Wojsznis, members of a Polish expedition to the Andes that found traces of Inca presence in the region. The ascent from the Chilean side is relatively non-technical apart from a short rock scramble below the summit, but the extreme altitude, cold and wind make it a serious undertaking; vehicles have been driven to extraordinary altitudes on its flanks in record attempts. The mountain is one of the Volcanic Seven Summits.

Name

"Ojos del Salado" means "Eyes of the Salty (River)" in Spanish, referring to the Río Salado and to springs or lagoons at the mountain's base, though the precise origin of the name is debated.
