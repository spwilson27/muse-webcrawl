CauseCollision of an asteroid, comet, or meteoroid with a planet

An impact event is a collision between astronomical objects, usually an asteroid or comet, and a planet or moon. On Earth the consequences range from localised destruction to mass extinction, depending on the size, speed, and composition of the impactor and the place it strikes.

Effects

A large impact releases energy far exceeding any geological or human source. The immediate effects include a crater, shock waves, earthquakes, fires ignited by ejected material re-entering the atmosphere, and — for ocean impacts — tsunamis. Fine dust and sulphate aerosols thrown into the stratosphere can block sunlight for months or years (an "impact winter"), collapsing photosynthesis and food chains. The Chicxulub impact 66 million years ago, by an object about 10 km across that struck Mexico's Yucatán Peninsula, is widely credited with causing the Cretaceous–Paleogene mass extinction that ended the non-avian dinosaurs; its evidence includes a global iridium-rich clay layer and the buried Chicxulub crater itself, about 180 km wide.

Historical and modern events

The Tunguska event of 1908 flattened some 2,000 km² of Siberian forest and is attributed to the airburst of a stony body tens of metres across. In 2013 a roughly 20 m meteor exploded over Chelyabinsk, Russia, injuring about 1,500 people, mostly from shattered glass. Around 200 impact craters have been confirmed on Earth. Because even modest impacts are a real hazard, astronomers survey near-Earth objects, and in 2022 NASA's DART mission demonstrated that a spacecraft collision can measurably alter an asteroid's orbit — the first test of planetary defence.
