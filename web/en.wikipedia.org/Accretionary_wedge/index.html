Accretionary wedge

An accretionary wedge or accretionary prism forms from sediments accreted onto the non-subducting tectonic plate at a convergent plate boundary. Most of the material in the wedge is marine sediment scraped off the downgoing plate as it subducts beneath the overriding plate, mixed with oceanic crust fragments and trench fill.

The wedge deforms internally by thrust faulting and folding, behaving like a pile of sand pushed by a bulldozer, in accordance with critical taper theory. Well-known examples include the wedges offshore Japan (Nankai Trough), Barbados, and the Makran coast of Pakistan and Iran. Accretionary wedges are important in petroleum geology and in studies of megathrust earthquakes.
