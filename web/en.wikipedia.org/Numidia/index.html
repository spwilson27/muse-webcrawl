Numidia

Numidia
Kingdom: c. 202 BC – 40 BC
Later: Roman province
Capitals: Cirta, Hippo Regius
Notable rulers: Masinissa, Jugurtha, Juba I

Numidia was the ancient kingdom of the Numidians, a Berber people, in northwest Africa, roughly corresponding to parts of present-day Algeria and Tunisia. It emerged as a unified kingdom under Masinissa, who allied with Rome in the Second Punic War and was rewarded with territory taken from Carthage.

History

Masinissa (c. 238–148 BC) united the Massylii and Masaesyli peoples and made Numidia a prosperous agricultural kingdom; its cavalry was famous throughout the Mediterranean. Under his grandson Jugurtha, Numidia fought Rome in the Jugurthine War (112–105 BC) before Jugurtha was captured through the diplomacy of Sulla. The kingdom later became entangled in Rome's civil wars; after Juba I sided with Pompey and was defeated at Thapsus in 46 BC, Numidia was annexed and reorganized as a Roman province. Its cities, such as Cirta and Hippo Regius, became important Roman and early Christian centres — Hippo was the bishopric of Augustine.

Legacy

Numidia supplied Rome with cavalry, grain, and wild animals for the arena. Remarkable remains include the royal mausoleum of Medracen and the tomb at Tipaza traditionally associated with Juba II and Cleopatra Selene.
