pH

Image placeholder: Visual indicator chart of pH values

In chemistry, pH is a logarithmic scale used to specify the acidity or basicity of an aqueous solution. Solutions with a pH less than 7 are acidic, solutions with a pH of 7 are neutral, and solutions with a pH greater than 7 are basic.

Definition

pH is defined as the negative base-10 logarithm of the hydrogen ion activity: pH = −log10 aH+. The scale is usually considered to range from 0 to 14, although values outside this range are possible in concentrated acids and bases. The concept was introduced by Danish chemist Søren Peder Lauritz Sørensen in 1909 at the Carlsberg Laboratory.

Measurement

pH is measured using pH indicator papers or dyes that change color with acidity, or more precisely with a glass-electrode pH meter. Buffer solutions resist changes in pH and are central to chemistry and biology; pure water at 25 °C has a pH of 7.0.
