John Michael Stipe (born January 4, 1960) is an American singer, songwriter, and artist, best known as the lead singer and lyricist of the alternative rock band R.E.M. Stipe co-founded R.E.M. in Athens, Georgia, in 1980 with guitarist Peter Buck, bassist Mike Mills, and drummer Bill Berry. His distinctive vocal style, oblique and image-laden lyrics, and stage presence made him one of the defining figures of alternative rock; he is widely regarded as one of the greatest frontmen in rock music.

R.E.M.

With R.E.M., Stipe recorded fifteen studio albums, from the underground breakthrough of Murmur (1983) through the worldwide successes of Out of Time (1991, including "Losing My Religion" and "Shiny Happy People") and Automatic for the People (1992, with "Everybody Hurts" and "Man on the Moon"). Early in the band's career his vocals were famously mumbled and buried in the mix; his enunciation and lyrical ambition grew through the 1980s. R.E.M. was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2007 and disbanded amicably in 2011.

Other work and activism

Stipe has produced films through his company Single Cell Productions (including Being John Malkovich and Man on the Moon), worked as a visual artist and photographer, and pursued solo music, releasing his first solo single, "Your Capricious Soul", in 2019. He is a prominent political activist — a supporter of Democratic candidates, gun control, and environmental causes — and famously wore slogan T-shirts and face paint during the 1990s to draw attention to issues. Stipe, who is gay and has described his sexuality publicly since the 1990s, divides his time between New York City and Athens, Georgia.
