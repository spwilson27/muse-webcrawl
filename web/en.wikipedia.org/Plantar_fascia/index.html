Plantar fascia

The plantar fascia runs along the sole of the foot from heel to toes

OriginCalcaneus (heel bone), medial tubercle
InsertionProximal phalanges of the toes
FunctionSupports the arch of the foot

The plantar fascia (also plantar aponeurosis) is the thick connective tissue (fascia) which supports the arch on the bottom (plantar side) of the foot. It runs from the tuberosity of the calcaneus (heel bone) forward to the heads of the metatarsal bones and the bases of the toes. The plantar fascia is a broad band of dense fibrous tissue, composed mostly of collagen fibres, that acts as a tie-rod for the longitudinal arch of the foot.

Structure and function

The fascia consists of a thick central band and thinner medial and lateral portions. When the toes are dorsiflexed during walking, the fascia winds around the metatarsal heads — the "windlass mechanism" — tightening the band, raising the arch and stiffening the foot for push-off. Together with the intrinsic muscles and ligaments such as those of the ankle complex, it bears substantial loads: tension in the fascia may approach a large fraction of body weight during running.

Clinical significance

Plantar fasciitis, inflammation or degeneration near the fascial origin on the heel, is one of the most common causes of heel pain, especially in runners and people who stand for long periods; it typically causes sharp pain with the first steps in the morning. Most cases resolve with conservative measures such as stretching, footwear modification and orthoses. Other conditions affecting the fascia include plantar fibromatosis (Ledderhose disease), in which nodules form within the band, and traumatic rupture, occasionally seen in athletes.
