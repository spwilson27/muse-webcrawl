Ashtanga vinyasa yoga

Ashtanga vinyasa yoga is a modern style of yoga as exercise, developed by K. Pattabhi Jois in Mysore, India, during the twentieth century. It consists of a fixed sequence of postures (asanas) linked by flowing movements (vinyasa) synchronised with the breath.

Practice

The practice begins with sun salutations and a standing sequence, followed by one of six series of seated and finishing postures; most students work on the Primary Series. Movement is coordinated with ujjayi breathing, directed gazing points (drishti), and internal energy locks (bandhas). Traditionally it is taught "Mysore style", in which students practise the memorised sequence at their own pace under a teacher's individual guidance, six days a week.

Origins

Jois said he learned the system from his teacher, Tirumalai Krishnamacharya, and associated it with a purported ancient manuscript, the Yoga Korunta. The style was popularised in the West from the 1970s onward by Jois's students and became a foundation for derivative styles such as power yoga.

Note on terminology

The name references the "eight limbs" (ashtanga) of yoga described in Patanjali's Yoga Sutras, though the practice itself is distinct from the classical system described there.
