Clear Script

The Clear Script (Oirat: ᡐᡆᡑᡆ ᡋᡅᡔᡅᡎ, todo bichig, "clear writing"; Mongolian: Тод бичиг) is an alphabet created in 1648 by the Oirat Buddhist monk Zaya Pandita for writing the Oirat Mongolian language. It was developed on the basis of the traditional Mongolian vertical script with the goal of eliminating ambiguities and faithfully representing all the sounds of the spoken Oirat language.

Design

Whereas the classical Mongolian script used the same glyphs for several different vowels and consonants, the Clear Script assigned distinct letters to each phoneme, introduced new characters for sounds borrowed from Tibetan and Sanskrit, and marked vowel length systematically. Like its parent script it is written vertically, from top to bottom and left to right.

Use

The script became the literary standard of the Oirat Mongols, including the Kalmyks of the Volga steppe and the peoples of Dzungaria. It was used for a large body of Buddhist translations, epic literature, chronicles and legal documents. In Kalmykia it remained in official use until 1924, when it was replaced by a Cyrillic-based alphabet under Soviet language policy. In Xinjiang, China, Oirat communities continued to use the Clear Script into the modern era, and it is encoded in the Mongolian block of Unicode.

Revival

Since the 1990s there have been efforts in Kalmykia and Mongolia to teach the Clear Script as part of cultural revival, though Cyrillic and the classical vertical script remain dominant.
