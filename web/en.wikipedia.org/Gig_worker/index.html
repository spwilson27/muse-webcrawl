Gig worker

A gig worker is an independent contractor, freelancer, or temporary worker who performs short-term, on-demand jobs ("gigs") rather than holding a permanent position with a single employer. Since the 2010s the term has been strongly associated with the gig economy of digital platforms — ride-hailing and food-delivery apps such as Uber, Lyft, DoorDash, and Deliveroo, and online marketplaces for freelance services — which match workers to customers and manage the work through algorithms.

Characteristics

Gig work typically offers flexible scheduling and low barriers to entry, but no guarantee of hours or income, and workers are usually classified as independent contractors rather than employees. This classification generally excludes them from employer-provided benefits and protections such as minimum wage guarantees, overtime pay, unemployment insurance, paid leave, and collective bargaining rights, while making them responsible for their own taxes and equipment, such as vehicles and fuel.

Debates and regulation

Worker classification has become a major legal and political controversy. California's Assembly Bill 5 (2019) sought to reclassify many gig workers as employees under an "ABC test", while Proposition 22 (2020), backed by platform companies, exempted app-based drivers while granting some benefits. In 2021 the UK Supreme Court ruled that Uber drivers are "workers", an intermediate status with rights to minimum wage and holiday pay. The European Union adopted a Platform Work Directive in 2024 establishing a presumption of employment. Critics describe gig work as precarious and a form of algorithmic management and surveillance; supporters emphasise flexibility and entrepreneurship. Surveys suggest a substantial minority of adults in developed economies have earned money through gig work, often as a supplement to other income.
