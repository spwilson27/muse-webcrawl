Transrapid

The Transrapid is a German high-speed monorail train system using magnetic levitation (maglev). Planning of the system started in 1969, and a test facility was established in Emsland, Germany, in 1987. The trains levitate about 10 mm above the guideway using attractive electromagnets that wrap around the guideway beam, and are propelled by a linear synchronous motor built into the track. Because there is no wheel-rail contact, the Transrapid can cruise at speeds of up to 500 km/h with low wear and modest noise.

Shanghai Maglev

The only commercial Transrapid line is the Shanghai Maglev Train, which opened in 2004 and connects Shanghai Pudong International Airport with Longyang Road station on the city's metro network, a distance of about 30 km covered in roughly seven minutes. It reached 430 km/h in regular service, making it the fastest commercial electric train in the world; services were later operated at reduced maximum speed much of the time.

Abandoned projects

Several German projects were planned but never built, most prominently a Berlin–Hamburg line cancelled in 2000 on cost grounds, and a Munich airport link (the "Metrorapid" concept also failed in North Rhine-Westphalia). On 22 September 2006 a Transrapid on the Emsland test track collided with a maintenance vehicle at about 170 km/h, killing 23 people — the first fatal accident of a maglev train. The test facility closed in 2011.

Technology

Transrapid vehicles use electromagnetic suspension (EMS): electromagnets on the vehicle are attracted upward toward the ferromagnetic stator under the guideway, with electronic control maintaining the air gap. Propulsion comes from a long-stator linear motor; braking is normally regenerative through the same motor, with eddy-current brakes and skids for emergencies.
