Continental Divide

Continental divide
TypeDrainage divide
DefinitionLine separating watersheds draining to different oceans or seas
ExamplesContinental Divide of the Americas; Australian Great Dividing Range

A continental divide is a drainage divide on a continent such that the drainage basin on one side of the divide feeds into one ocean or sea, and the basin on the other side either feeds into a different ocean or sea, or else is endorheic — not connected to the open sea. Where a continent has multiple drainage basins flowing into different seas or oceans, and their shared boundary winds across the continent, that boundary is its continental divide.

Examples

The best known is the Continental Divide of the Americas — often simply called "the Continental Divide" in North America — which separates waters flowing to the Pacific Ocean from those flowing to the Atlantic and Arctic oceans and the Gulf of Mexico, running from the Bering Strait through the Rocky Mountains and the Andes to the Strait of Magellan. Other examples include the divide in Australia along the Great Dividing Range, separating rivers flowing to the Pacific from those flowing westward into the Murray–Darling system and internal drainage; and various divides in Africa, Asia, and Europe between the Atlantic, Mediterranean, Arctic, Pacific, and Indian Ocean basins.

Significance

Continental divides shape biogeography, climate, and human geography: they often follow mountain ranges, form political borders, and determine the routing of canals, roads, and pipelines. At certain points, such as Two Ocean Pass in Wyoming, a single stream splits to flow to two oceans — a bifurcation across the divide.
