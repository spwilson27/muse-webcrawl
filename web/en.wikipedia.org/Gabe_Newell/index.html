Gabe Logan Newell (born November 3, 1962), nicknamed Gaben, is an American businessman who is the president and co-founder of the video game company Valve Corporation. Educated at Harvard University, Newell dropped out to join Microsoft, where he worked for thirteen years on the first versions of the Windows operating system. In 1996 he left Microsoft with colleague Mike Harrington to found Valve.

Early life and Microsoft

Newell grew up in Colorado, where his mother worked as a museum artist, and attended Harvard from 1980 to 1983 before dropping out. At Microsoft he worked on Windows 1.0 through Windows 3.0 and Windows NT; he has called the first three Windows releases his main achievements there. Work on the port of Doom to Windows 95, championed by Newell within Microsoft, convinced him the PC could be a leading gaming platform.

Valve and Steam

Valve's first game, Half-Life (1998), was a critical and commercial success and won over fifty Game of the Year awards. Newell led the creation of Steam, launched in 2003, which became the dominant digital distribution platform for PC games. He later oversaw franchises including Portal, Team Fortress, Left 4 Dead, Dota 2 and Counter-Strike, the Source engine, Valve Index virtual reality hardware, and the Steam Deck handheld (2022).

Views and personal life

Newell is known for advocating user-generated content and mods, for his criticism of closed platforms, and for Valve's flat management structure. He has an extensive collection of knives and is an enthusiast of motorsport, sponsoring racing teams. During the COVID-19 pandemic he spent an extended period in New Zealand, where he also invested in local technology ventures. Forbes has estimated his net worth in the billions of dollars, largely from his stake in Valve.
