Bessel function

Bessel functions, first defined by the mathematician Daniel Bernoulli and then generalised by Friedrich Bessel, are the canonical solutions y(x) of Bessel's differential equation

x²y″ + xy′ + (x² − α²)y = 0

for an arbitrary complex number α, the order of the function. Although α and −α generate the same functions for integer order, the functions are defined so as to be smooth in α in general.

Kinds of Bessel functions

The most important cases are for integer and half-integer orders. Bessel functions of the first kind, Jα, are solutions finite at the origin and are defined by a power series (or equivalently by Bessel's integral). Bessel functions of the second kind, Yα (also called Weber or Neumann functions), are solutions singular at the origin. Linear combinations forming Hankel functions Hα(1) and Hα(2) represent incoming and outgoing cylindrical waves. Modified Bessel functions Iα and Kα solve the modified equation and grow or decay exponentially rather than oscillating.

Applications

Bessel functions arise when finding separable solutions of Laplace's equation and the Helmholtz equation in cylindrical or spherical coordinates: wave propagation and diffraction, vibrations of a circular drum membrane, heat conduction in cylindrical objects, electromagnetic waves in cylindrical waveguides, and frequency modulation in signal analysis all involve them. Spherical Bessel functions appear in three-dimensional problems with spherical symmetry, such as the quantum mechanics of a particle in a spherical well.
