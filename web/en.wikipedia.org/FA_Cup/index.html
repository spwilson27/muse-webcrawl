The Football Association Challenge Cup, commonly known as the FA Cup, is an annual knockout football competition in domestic English football. First played during the 1871–72 season, it is the oldest national football competition in the world. It is organised by and named after The Football Association (The FA). Since 2015 it has been sponsored by Emirates and styled the Emirates FA Cup.

History

The competition was proposed by Charles W. Alcock, secretary of the FA, in 1871; fifteen teams entered the first edition, and Wanderers beat Royal Engineers 1–0 in the final at Kennington Oval in March 1872. The cup quickly became the centrepiece of the English game, with finals held at Wembley Stadium from 1923 — the famous "White Horse Final" — to the present day, apart from a period at Cardiff's Millennium Stadium while Wembley was rebuilt (2001–2006).

Format

Clubs from the top ten levels of the English football league system may enter. The competition runs from August preliminary rounds for non-League clubs through to the final in May. League clubs enter in later rounds: League One and League Two clubs in the first round proper, and Premier League and Championship clubs in the third round. Ties are single matches, decided on the day; replays, once a hallmark of the competition, were abolished from the fifth round in 2018–19 and entirely from 2024–25.

Giant-killings and records

The FA Cup is famed for "giant-killings", in which lower-division clubs defeat top-flight sides — celebrated examples include Sutton United's defeat of Coventry City (1989), Wrexham's win over Arsenal (1992), and Lincoln City's run to the quarter-finals as a non-League club (2017). Arsenal hold the record with 14 wins; Ashley Cole is the most decorated player with seven winners' medals. The record attendance for a final is the official 126,047 at the 1923 White Horse Final.
