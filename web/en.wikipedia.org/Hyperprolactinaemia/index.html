Hyperprolactinaemia

Hyperprolactinaemia (also spelled hyperprolactinemia) is the presence of abnormally high levels of prolactin in the blood. Prolactin is a hormone secreted by the anterior pituitary gland whose secretion is normally inhibited by dopamine from the hypothalamus. Normal values differ by sex and rise physiologically in pregnancy and lactation.

Causes

The most common pathological cause is a prolactinoma, a benign prolactin-secreting pituitary adenoma. Other causes include drugs that block dopamine (many antipsychotics, metoclopramide), primary hypothyroidism, chronic kidney disease, pituitary stalk compression by other masses ("stalk effect"), and chest wall or nipple stimulation. Macroprolactin, a biologically inactive prolactin–antibody complex, can cause a spuriously high laboratory result.

Symptoms

In women: galactorrhea (milky discharge), amenorrhea or irregular periods, infertility and reduced libido. In men: erectile dysfunction, reduced libido, infertility and sometimes gynecomastia and galactorrhea. Long-standing hypogonadism reduces bone density. Large pituitary tumours can cause headaches and visual field defects (classically bitemporal hemianopia) by compressing the optic chiasm.

Treatment

Dopamine agonists such as cabergoline and bromocriptine are first-line therapy and usually normalise prolactin and shrink prolactinomas. Surgery or radiotherapy is reserved for resistant or intolerant cases.
