François Joseph Paul, comte de Grasse (1722–1788) was a French admiral best known for his decisive role in the American Revolutionary War, where his fleet's victory at the Battle of the Chesapeake in 1781 enabled the Franco-American victory at Yorktown.

Early career

De Grasse entered the naval service of the Order of Malta as a boy and joined the French Navy in 1740. He saw extensive service in the Seven Years' War, including against the British in the Indian Ocean and the Mediterranean.

American Revolutionary War

In 1781 de Grasse sailed from the West Indies to the Chesapeake Bay with 28 ships of the line. On 5 September 1781 his fleet fought the British fleet under Admiral Graves to a strategic victory, sealing off Lord Cornwallis's army at Yorktown and forcing its surrender — the decisive event of the war. The following April, however, de Grasse was defeated and captured by Admiral Rodney at the Battle of the Saintes in the Caribbean.

Later life

Released and tried by a court martial on his return to France, de Grasse was largely exonerated but never held another major command. He published memoirs defending his conduct and died in Paris in 1788. Several United States Navy ships have been named USS De Grasse in his honour.
