The Political Constitution of 1899, commonly known as the Malolos Constitution, was the basic law of the First Philippine Republic, adopted by the Malolos Congress at Barasoain Church in Malolos, Bulacan, and promulgated on 21 January 1899. It is regarded as the first republican constitution in Asia.

Drafting

The revolutionary congress, convened in September 1898, debated a draft principally written by Felipe Calderón, influenced by the constitutions of Spain, France, Belgium and Latin American republics. Apolinario Mabini, adviser to President Emilio Aguinaldo, had proposed a different framework granting the president stronger powers; the Calderón draft prevailed, establishing a parliamentary system in which the Assembly of Representatives held supremacy.

Provisions

The constitution guaranteed civil rights including due process and freedom of religion—a provision on separation of church and state passed by a single vote—established a unicameral legislature, and provided for a president elected by the assembly to a four-year term. Three days after promulgation, the First Philippine Republic was inaugurated at Malolos.

Fate

The constitution was never fully implemented because of the outbreak of the Philippine–American War in February 1899, which ended the republic's effective control of the archipelago.
