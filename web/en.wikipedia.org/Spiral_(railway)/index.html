Spiral (railway)

A spiral (sometimes called a spiral loop, helix, or simply loop) is a technique employed by railways to ascend steep hills. A spiral railway starts a way up a hill and circles the hill or a side valley to gain height over a longer distance, keeping the gradient within limits the trains can climb.

Design

The track typically crosses over itself by bridge or tunnel to form a loop. The ruling gradient is spread over the longer route; loops may be combined with switchbacks or tunnels. Examples include the Tehachapi Loop in California, the Brusio spiral viaduct on the Bernina Railway in Switzerland, and spirals on the Darjeeling Himalayan Railway in India.

Notable examples

Other famous spirals are found on the Gotthard Railway in Switzerland (spiral tunnels), the Flåm Railway in Norway, and the spiral tunnels of the Canadian Pacific Railway at Kicking Horse Pass, British Columbia.
