Rubber Soul is the sixth studio album by the English rock band the Beatles. It was released on 3 December 1965 in the United Kingdom on EMI's Parlophone label, accompanied by the non-album double A-side single "Day Tripper" / "We Can Work It Out".

Background

Recorded under pressure to produce a new LP for the Christmas market, the album marked a major artistic step for the band. Influenced by the folk rock of Bob Dylan and the Byrds, and by contemporary soul music, the Beatles wrote more reflective, introspective songs and experimented with new sounds, including the sitar on John Lennon's "Norwegian Wood (This Bird Has Flown)".

Songs

The UK edition contains fourteen tracks, among them "Drive My Car", "Nowhere Man", "Michelle", "Girl", "In My Life", "Wait" and "If I Needed Someone". The North American version issued by Capitol Records omitted four songs and included two from Help!, giving it a more distinctly folk flavour.

Reception and legacy

Rubber Soul was a commercial success and is now regarded as one of the greatest albums of all time. Brian Wilson of the Beach Boys cited it as a key inspiration for Pet Sounds. It frequently appears near the top of critics' lists of the best albums ever made.
