The Goldman Sachs Group, Inc. is an American multinational investment bank and financial services company headquartered in New York City. Founded in 1869, it is one of the largest investment banking enterprises in the world and a primary dealer in the United States Treasury security market.

History

Marcus Goldman founded the firm in 1869 dealing in commercial paper; his son-in-law Samuel Sachs joined in 1882, giving the firm its name. Goldman Sachs underwrote major early public offerings, joined the New York Stock Exchange in 1896, and remained a private partnership until its initial public offering in 1999, one of the largest of its era. During the 2007–2008 financial crisis the firm converted to a bank holding company and received investment and liquidity support, later becoming a focus of debate over its role in the crisis.

Business

The firm's activities are organised into Global Banking & Markets — investment banking advisory, underwriting, trading, and market making — and Asset & Wealth Management. Goldman Sachs advises on many of the world's largest mergers and acquisitions and is consistently ranked at or near the top of global league tables.

Criticism

The firm has faced controversies including its marketing of mortgage securities before 2008, the Abacus CDO settlement with the SEC in 2010, and its central role in the 1MDB scandal, for which it paid billions of dollars in penalties in 2020.
