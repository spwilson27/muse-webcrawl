Federal subjects of Russia

The federal subjects of Russia, also referred to as the subjects of the Russian Federation (Russian: субъе́кты Росси́йской Федера́ции), are the constituent entities of Russia, its top-level political divisions according to the Constitution of Russia. Since 2014 the federation has officially comprised 89 subjects, including territories annexed from Ukraine that most countries do not recognise as Russian.

Types

Subjects are classified into six types: 24 republics (nominally autonomous homelands of ethnic minorities, with their own constitutions and official languages), 48 oblasts (provinces), 9 krais (territories), 3 federal cities (Moscow, Saint Petersburg and Sevastopol, the last disputed), 1 autonomous oblast (the Jewish Autonomous Oblast) and 4 autonomous okrugs.

Governance

Each subject has a head of government and a regional legislature, and sends two representatives to the Federation Council, the upper house of the Federal Assembly. Since 2000 the subjects have also been grouped into federal districts supervised by presidential envoys.
