Technical communication

Technical communication is a means to convey scientific, engineering, or other technical information. Individuals in a variety of contexts and with varied professional credentials engage in technical communication. Some individuals are designated as technical communicators or technical writers as their primary role; for others, communication is an ancillary part of their work.

Technical communication involves the creation of documents and media such as user manuals, help systems, white papers, specifications, reports, proposals, and instructions. Its defining characteristics are clarity, accuracy, accessibility for a defined audience, and an emphasis on tasks and usability rather than on style for its own sake.

Process

The technical communication process typically includes analyzing the audience and purpose, researching the subject, designing the structure and format, drafting, reviewing and testing with users, revising, and publishing. Modern practice frequently uses single-source publishing, in which content is written once in a structured format and output to multiple media such as print, web help, and in-product assistance.

Deliverables

Common deliverables of technical communication include user guides, installation manuals, online help, API documentation, release notes, training materials, scientific and engineering reports, and environmental impact statements. Increasingly, technical communicators also produce videos, interactive tutorials, and chatbot content.

Profession

Technical communication is both a professional field and an academic discipline, with degree programs and professional societies such as the Society for Technical Communication. Practitioners often work closely with engineers, developers, designers, and subject-matter experts.
