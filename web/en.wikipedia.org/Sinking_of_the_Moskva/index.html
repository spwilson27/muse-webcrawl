Sinking of the Moskva

Date14 April 2022
LocationBlack Sea, near Snake Island
VesselSlava-class guided missile cruiser Moskva
OutcomeShip sank; flagship of the Russian Black Sea Fleet lost

The Russian warship Moskva, the flagship of the Russian Navy's Black Sea Fleet, sank on 14 April 2022 during the Russian invasion of Ukraine. Ukrainian officials said the vessel was struck by two Neptune anti-ship missiles. Russia's defence ministry stated that a fire had caused munitions to detonate and that the ship sank in stormy seas while under tow.

The Moskva was the largest combat vessel sunk in action since the ARA General Belgrano in 1982, and the loss was widely regarded as a significant symbolic and military blow to Russia.

Background

Laid down in 1976 and commissioned in 1983 as the Slava, the cruiser was renamed Moskva in 1995. Displacing about 12,500 tonnes, she carried sixteen P-1000 Vulkan anti-ship missiles and served as the air-defence and command centre of the Black Sea Fleet. In February 2022 the ship took part in the capture of Snake Island, where Ukrainian border guards famously refused a demand to surrender.

The sinking

On 13 April 2022, Ukraine reported that Neptune missiles launched from the coast near Odesa had struck the cruiser, causing a major fire. US officials later confirmed the Ukrainian account. Russia acknowledged a fire and the evacuation of the crew but said the ship remained afloat; on 14 April it announced the ship had sunk while being towed to Sevastopol. The number of casualties has never been officially confirmed; Russian reporting acknowledged at least one death and 27 missing, while Ukrainian and other estimates were higher.

Aftermath and significance

Ukraine declared the wreck a site of underwater cultural heritage. The loss deprived the Black Sea Fleet of its principal air-defence umbrella, and Russian naval operations in the northwestern Black Sea became more cautious. Ukraine issued a postage stamp depicting a soldier gesturing toward the cruiser, which sold out rapidly.
