Shakti

Shakti (Sanskrit: शक्ति, "power, energy, ability") is a central concept in Hinduism denoting the primordial cosmic energy and dynamic force that moves through the universe. Shakti is personified as the divine feminine — the Great Goddess, Devi or Adi Parashakti — and is regarded as the creative, sustaining, and destructive power of reality. In the tradition of Shaktism, one of the major denominations of Hinduism, Shakti is worshipped as the supreme being herself, of whom all gods and goddesses are manifestations.

Concept

Every Hindu deity has a shakti — a female counterpart or energy without which the god is held to be inert; a common saying has it that Shiva without Shakti is shava, a corpse. In the Samkhya-influenced picture, Shakti corresponds to prakriti, the active material principle of nature, paired with the passive consciousness of purusha. Goddesses embody shakti in beneficent forms such as Lakshmi (fortune) and Saraswati (learning) and in fierce forms such as Durga and Kali, who battle demons in myths like the Devi Mahatmyam (c. 5th–6th century CE), the principal scripture of goddess worship.

Shaktism

Shaktism focuses worship on the Goddess as supreme. It is especially strong in eastern India — West Bengal, Assam, Odisha — and in Nepal, and is expressed in festivals such as Durga Puja and Navaratri. Shakti pithas, shrines marking the places where parts of the body of the goddess Sati were said to have fallen, form a sacred geography of goddess pilgrimage across South Asia.

Forms and associations

The Goddess is worshipped in innumerable local and pan-Indian forms: as the consorts of the great gods (Parvati with Shiva, Lakshmi with Vishnu, Saraswati with Brahma), as independent village goddesses (gramadevatas), and in groups such as the Sapta Matrikas (Seven Mothers) and the ten Mahavidyas of Tantric tradition, including Tara, Tripura Sundari, and Bhuvaneshvari.

In Tantra and yoga

In Tantric practice, Shakti is the power awakened within the body as kundalini, the coiled energy at the base of the spine that rises through the chakras to unite with Shiva in the crown — a union symbolising liberation. Mantras, yantras (notably the Sri Yantra), and ritual worship are means of honouring and harnessing this energy.
