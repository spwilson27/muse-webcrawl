Auriga

Auriga
Genitive: Aurigae
Symbolism: The Charioteer
Brightest star: Capella (α Aur)
Area: 657 sq. deg.

Auriga is a constellation in the northern celestial hemisphere. It is one of the 88 modern constellations; it was among the 48 constellations listed by the 2nd-century astronomer Ptolemy. Its name is Latin for "(the) charioteer", associating it with various mythological beings, including Erichthonius and Myrtilus.

Notable features

Auriga's brightest star is Capella, an unusual multiple star system and one of the brightest stars in the night sky. The constellation contains the open clusters Messier 36, Messier 37, and Messier 38, popular targets for amateur astronomers, as well as the variable star Epsilon Aurigae, known for its long and deep eclipses.

Mythology

In Greek mythology Auriga is often identified with Erichthonius of Athens, the legendary king credited with inventing the four-horse chariot, or with Myrtilus, the charioteer of King Oenomaus. The Milky Way passes through Auriga, giving it many rich star fields.
