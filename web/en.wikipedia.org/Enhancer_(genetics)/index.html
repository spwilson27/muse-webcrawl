Enhancer (genetics)

In genetics, an enhancer is a short region of DNA that can be bound by transcription factors to increase the likelihood that a particular gene will be transcribed. Enhancers may lie near the gene they regulate, far away within the same chromosome, inside introns, or downstream of the gene.

Mechanism

Enhancer sequences work in cis, meaning they affect genes on the same DNA molecule. Proteins bound to an enhancer interact with the transcription machinery at a promoter, often through DNA looping and mediator proteins. Their activity is commonly cell-type specific because it depends on which transcription factors are present.

Discovery and examples

The first well-known enhancer was identified in the SV40 virus in the early 1980s. Developmental genes often have multiple enhancers that drive expression in different tissues or at different times; shadow enhancers can provide redundancy under stress or mutation.

Medical relevance

Changes in enhancers can alter gene expression without changing protein-coding sequence and have been linked to congenital disorders and cancer. Genome-wide association studies frequently find risk variants in noncoding regions that overlap enhancers. See also promoter, silencer, and transcription factor.
