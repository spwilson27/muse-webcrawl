Gulf of St. Lawrence

The Gulf of St. Lawrence is a large gulf in eastern Canada and the outflow of the St. Lawrence River into the Atlantic Ocean. It is the world's largest estuary and was the historic gateway for the European exploration and settlement of Canada.

Geography

The gulf is bounded by Quebec to the north and west, New Brunswick, Nova Scotia and Prince Edward Island to the south, and Newfoundland to the east. It connects to the Atlantic through the Strait of Belle Isle, Cabot Strait and the Strait of Canso. Islands within it include Anticosti Island, the Magdalen Islands and Prince Edward Island itself. Jacques Cartier entered the gulf in 1534, and it took its name from his landing near the feast day of Saint Lawrence.

Ecology and economy

The gulf supports major fisheries, historically for Atlantic cod (whose northern stocks collapsed in the early 1990s), snow crab, shrimp and lobster, and is an important feeding area for whales including the endangered North Atlantic right whale, for which shipping lanes and speed limits have been imposed. Ferries, shipping to the Great Lakes via the St. Lawrence Seaway, and tourism around its island provinces are economically significant.
