Kozhikode, historically known as Calicut, is a city on the Malabar Coast of Kerala in southwestern India. Once the capital of the Zamorin's kingdom and one of the great trading ports of the Indian Ocean, it was famed across Asia and Europe as a centre of the spice trade.

History

Kozhikode rose to prominence under the Zamorins (Samoothiri) from the 13th century, attracting Arab, Chinese and later European merchants trading in pepper, ginger, cardamom and textiles (the word "calico" derives from Calicut). In May 1498 the Portuguese navigator Vasco da Gama landed at nearby Kappad, opening the sea route from Europe to India. The city later came under Mysore rule and then British control, becoming part of Kerala state in 1956.

Economy and culture

Modern Kozhikode is a commercial and educational hub, home to the Indian Institute of Management Kozhikode and the National Institute of Technology Calicut. It is celebrated for its cuisine — including Kozhikodan halwa and biryani — and its literary culture; in 2023 UNESCO designated it India's first City of Literature.

Geography

The city lies on the Arabian Sea coast between the Kallai and Chaliyar rivers, with a humid tropical monsoon climate. Kozhikode International Airport is at Karipur, about 28 km away.
