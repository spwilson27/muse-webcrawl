Montérégie

Montérégie is an administrative region of Quebec, Canada, lying between the St. Lawrence River and the United States border, immediately south and east of Montreal. It had a population of about 1.46 million at the 2021 census, making it Quebec's second most populous region. Its name derives from the Monteregian Hills, the chain of isolated igneous mountains that dots its plain.

Geography

The region occupies fertile lowland between the St. Lawrence and Richelieu rivers, one of Quebec's principal agricultural zones, known for market gardens, dairy farming, and apple orchards on the slopes of Mont Saint-Hilaire and Mont Rougemont. The Monteregian Hills — including Saint-Hilaire, Saint-Bruno, Saint-Grégoire, Rougemont, Yamaska, and Shefford — rise abruptly from the plain.

Subdivisions and cities

Montérégie's largest urban centre is Longueuil, directly across the St. Lawrence from Montreal, followed by Saint-Jean-sur-Richelieu, Brossard, Granby, Saint-Hyacinthe, Sorel-Tracy, and Vaudreuil-Dorion. The region comprises numerous regional county municipalities and the urban agglomeration of Longueuil; a reorganisation in 2021 split off the new Les Jardins-de-Napierville and other areas administratively while retaining the region's name. Much of its population lives in Montreal's suburban belt.

History

The Richelieu valley was a strategic corridor in the French and British colonial wars and the American invasions of 1775 and the War of 1812. The region figured prominently in the Lower Canada Rebellion of 1837–38, including the Patriote victory at Saint-Denis-sur-Richelieu.
