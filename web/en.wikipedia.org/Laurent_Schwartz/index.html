Laurent Schwartz

Laurent-Moïse Schwartz (5 March 1915 – 4 July 2002) was a French mathematician who pioneered the theory of distributions (generalised functions), for which he received the Fields Medal in 1950 — the first French recipient. A member of the Bourbaki group, he taught at Nancy, the University of Paris and the École Polytechnique.

Distributions

Schwartz's Théorie des distributions (1950–51) gave rigorous meaning to objects such as the Dirac delta function used heuristically by physicists, founding modern analysis of partial differential equations and Schwartz spaces and kernels in functional analysis.

Activism

A committed leftist and anti-colonial activist, he opposed the Algerian War (chairing the Audin committee after the torture and murder of mathematician Maurice Audin), protested the Vietnam War and the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan, and was briefly stripped of his Polytechnique post for signing the Manifesto of the 121. He was also a noted lepidopterist with a major butterfly collection, and uncle of claims — his family included several prominent French intellectuals.
