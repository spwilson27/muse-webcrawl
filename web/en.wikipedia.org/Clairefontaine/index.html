Clairefontaine

INF Clairefontaine, officially the Centre Technique National Fernand Sastre, is the national football centre of France and the academy of the French Football Federation (FFF). Located in Clairefontaine-en-Yvelines, about 50 km southwest of Paris in the forest of Rambouillet, it is one of the most renowned football academies in the world.

History

The centre was conceived by FFF president Fernand Sastre and technical director Gérard Houllier as a national institute of football (Institut National du Football, INF). It was inaugurated on 4 June 1988 by French president François Mitterrand on the site of the former Château de Montjoye estate. The INF selects the best 12- to 15-year-old players from the Paris region (with sister regional academies around France) and gives them elite coaching combined with normal schooling.

Notable graduates

Clairefontaine's alumni include Thierry Henry, Nicolas Anelka, William Gallas, Louis Saha, Blaise Matuidi, and Kylian Mbappé, among many other French internationals. The women's section opened in 1998. The French national teams also use Clairefontaine as their training base before internationals and tournaments; the senior men's team famously prepared there before winning the 1998 FIFA World Cup on home soil.

Facilities

The grounds include numerous natural-grass and artificial pitches, a stadium, medical and fitness facilities, classrooms, and accommodation. A giant replica of the World Cup trophy, installed after 1998, greets visitors at the entrance.
