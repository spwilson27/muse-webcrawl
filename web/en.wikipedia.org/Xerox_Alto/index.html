Xerox Alto

The Xerox Alto is a computer system developed at Xerox PARC in 1973, one of the first computers designed for a graphical user interface (GUI) and widely regarded as a prototype of the modern personal computer.

Design

The Alto featured a bitmapped portrait display, a mouse, Ethernet networking, removable storage, and software including Bravo (an early WYSIWYG word processor), Gypsy, and environments in the Smalltalk programming language. Its design grew from Alan Kay's Dynabook vision and the work of Butler Lampson, Charles Thacker and others.

Influence

About 2,000 Altos were built and used within Xerox and at universities, but it was never sold commercially. Its ideas, seen by Steve Jobs in 1979 demonstrations, influenced the Apple Lisa and Macintosh and, through them, personal computing generally.
