Tutelary deity

Athena, patron and tutelary goddess of Athens

A tutelary (also tutelar) is a deity or spirit who is a guardian, patron, or protector of a particular place, geographic feature, person, lineage, nation, culture, or occupation. The etymology of the word is from the Latin tutela, "protection" or "guardianship".

Examples in world cultures

  • Greece and Rome — Athena was the tutelary goddess of Athens and Minerva of her temples; Apollo protected Delphi and Delos. In Rome, Juno guarded the city, and each person had a personal guardian spirit: the genius for men and the juno for women. Household gods, the Lares and Penates, protected the home and family.
  • Mesopotamia and Egypt — each city had its own patron god, such as Marduk of Babylon or Amun of Thebes.
  • China — City Gods (Chenghuang) protect individual settlements; Tudigong is a local earth god; Mazu protects seafarers in coastal regions.
  • Japan — kami of local shrines (ujigami) act as tutelaries of clans and communities.
  • Hinduism — village deities (gramadevata), family deities (kuladevata) and personal deities (ishtadevata) serve protective roles.
  • Indigenous traditions — guardian spirits and totemic beings protect places, peoples and individuals in many Native American, African and Oceanic religions.

Related concepts

The concept survives in altered form in the patron saints of Catholic and Orthodox traditions, who protect cities, professions and individuals, and in national personifications. In astrology and occultism, tutelary spirits or guardian angels are associated with individuals.
