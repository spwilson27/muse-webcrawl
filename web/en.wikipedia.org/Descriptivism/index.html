Descriptivism

Descriptivism is the approach in linguistics of describing how language is actually used by its speakers, without judging usages as correct or incorrect — as opposed to prescriptivism, which prescribes rules for how language ought to be used.

In philosophy of language

The term also denotes descriptive theories of reference, associated with Gottlob Frege and Bertrand Russell, holding that a proper name refers via an associated description — contrasted with Saul Kripke's causal-historical theory.

Modern dictionaries and grammars are generally compiled descriptively, recording usage and labelling registers rather than forbidding forms.
