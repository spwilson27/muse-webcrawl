Verderer

A verderer is a forestry official in England, historically an officer of the royal forest courts. Verderers were originally appointed under medieval forest law to protect the vert (the vegetation and habitats of the forest) and the venison (the game animals) of the king's hunting forests. The office survives today in the New Forest and the Forest of Dean.

History

Verderers formed part of the hierarchy of the royal forests established after the Norman Conquest, alongside wardens, foresters and agisters. They sat in the forest courts — the court of attachment and the swainmote — hearing offences against the vert and venison, though serious cases were reserved to the justices in eyre.

Modern role

In the New Forest, the Verderers' Court (the Court of Verderers) still meets at Lyndhurst. Under the New Forest Acts, ten verderers — five elected by the commoners, and five appointed by bodies including Forestry England, the national park authority and Natural England — administer the commoners' rights of pasture, regulate the grazing of ponies, cattle and other animals depastured on the Forest, employ the agisters who supervise the livestock, and protect the Forest's landscape and habitats. An Official Verderer chairs the court.
