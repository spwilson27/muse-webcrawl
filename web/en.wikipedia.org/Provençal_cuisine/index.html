Provençal cuisine

Provençal cuisine is the traditional cooking of Provence, a region in southeastern France. It is characterized by its use of olive oil, garlic, tomatoes, and aromatic herbs — notably the mixture known as herbes de Provence — together with the vegetables, lamb, goat cheese, and seafood of the Mediterranean littoral. The cooking is generally lighter and more vegetable-centred than that of northern France, reflecting the region's sunny climate and Italian and Catalan influences.

Signature dishes

  • Ratatouille — a stew of aubergine, courgette, peppers, tomato, onion, and garlic.
  • Bouillabaisse — the saffron-scented fish stew of Marseille, served with rouille and croutons.
  • Daube provençale — beef braised slowly in red wine with herbs and orange zest.
  • Socca — a chickpea-flour pancake from Nice, related to Italian farinata.
  • Salade niçoise — tuna, olives, eggs, green beans, and anchovies.
  • Pissaladière — an onion tart with anchovies and black olives.
  • Tapenade and anchoïade — olive- and anchovy-based spreads.
  • Aïoli — a garlic mayonnaise served with vegetables, fish, and eggs.
  • Soupe au pistou — a summer vegetable soup enriched with a basil-garlic paste.

Ingredients and influences

The cuisine draws on olive oil rather than butter, goat cheeses such as Banon, salt cod, chickpeas, rice from the Camargue, and the wines of the Rhône valley and Provence's rosé-producing vineyards. Historical influences include Roman, Catalan, Ligurian, and — through the port of Marseille — North African cooking.
