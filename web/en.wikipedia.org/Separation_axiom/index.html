Separation axiom

In topology and related branches of mathematics, a separation axiom is a condition imposed on a topological space describing how distinctly its points (or closed sets) can be "separated" from one another by open sets or continuous functions.

The hierarchy

The standard axioms form a hierarchy, denoted T0, T1, T2 and beyond. A space is T0 (Kolmogorov) if any two distinct points are topologically distinguishable; T1 (Fréchet) if points are closed; and T2 (Hausdorff) if any two distinct points have disjoint neighbourhoods. Stronger conditions include regular and completely regular spaces, and normal spaces (T4), in which disjoint closed sets can be separated by neighbourhoods and by continuous functions via Urysohn's lemma.

History

The axioms grew out of Felix Hausdorff's 1914 foundational work on set-theoretic topology. The T notation derives from the German Trennungsaxiom, "separation axiom".
