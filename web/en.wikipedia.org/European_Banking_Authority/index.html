The European Banking Authority (EBA) is an agency of the European Union tasked with maintaining financial stability in the EU's banking sector. It was established on 1 January 2011 by Regulation (EU) No 1093/2010, succeeding the Committee of European Banking Supervisors, in response to the financial crisis of 2007–2008. The EBA is headquartered in Paris, having moved from London in 2019 following the United Kingdom's withdrawal from the EU.

Tasks

The EBA's central mission is to build a single rulebook for banking regulation and supervision across the EU, developing binding technical standards that implement legislation such as the Capital Requirements Regulation and Directive (implementing the Basel III framework). It conducts EU-wide stress tests of major banks, publishes risk assessments, works to protect consumers of financial products, and can investigate and issue recommendations to national supervisors, including in cases of breaches of EU law.

Organisation

The EBA is governed by a Board of Supervisors composed of the heads of national competent authorities, with a full-time Chairperson and an Executive Director managing daily operations. It works alongside the other European Supervisory Authorities — ESMA (securities) and EIOPA (insurance and pensions) — and with the European Central Bank, which directly supervises significant banks in the euro area under the Single Supervisory Mechanism.
