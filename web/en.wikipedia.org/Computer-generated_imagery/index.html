Computer-generated imagery

Computer-generated imagery (CGI) is a specific technology or application of computer graphics for creating or improving images in art, printed media, simulators, videos and video games. These images are either static (still images) or dynamic (moving images). CGI both refers to 2D computer graphics and (more frequently) 3D computer graphics. The term "CGI animation" refers to dynamic CGI rendered as a film.

A CGI landscape created in Blender

History

Early CGI appeared in films such as Westworld (1973) and Futureworld (1976). The Genesis sequence in Star Trek II: The Wrath of Khan (1982) and the stained-glass knight in Young Sherlock Holmes (1985) marked milestones from Lucasfilm's computer graphics division, which became Pixar. Terminator 2: Judgment Day (1991) popularised photorealistic effects, Jurassic Park (1993) revolutionised creature effects, and Toy Story (1995) was the first fully CGI feature film.

Techniques

CGI production involves modelling geometry (often polygon meshes or subdivision surfaces), texturing and shading, rigging and animating characters, simulating physics such as cloth, fluids and destruction, lighting, and rendering — by rasterisation for real-time applications or ray tracing and path tracing for film-quality images. Motion capture transfers the performances of actors onto digital characters.

Applications

Beyond film and games, CGI is used in architecture visualisation, product design, medical imaging, flight simulators, forensic reconstruction and virtual production. The rise of real-time rendering engines has blurred the line between games and film production.
