QED (text editor)

QED ("Quick Editor") is a line-oriented computer text editor developed in the mid-1960s for time-sharing systems. It was written initially by Butler Lampson and L. Peter Deutsch for the Berkeley Timesharing System on the SDS 940, and was later implemented at Bell Labs by Ken Thompson, who added regular expressions — an innovation that strongly influenced the Unix editors ed, ex, and sed.

QED
Type: Line editor
Written: 1965–66
Authors: Lampson, Deutsch; Thompson (Bell Labs version)
Notable feature: Regular expressions
Successors: ed, sed

History

QED appeared around 1965–66 and circulated among several time-sharing systems, including CTSS at MIT. Ken Thompson's reimplementation for CTSS and later for Multics and Unix introduced a formalised system of regular expressions for pattern matching and substitution, which he also described in a 1968 paper. Dennis Ritchie read QED's manual and, by his own account, wrote within a day what became the Unix editor ed, borrowing its command style.

Features

As a line editor, QED operated on one or more addressed lines at a time with commands to print, delete, insert, and substitute text, and it allowed editing of multiple buffers. Its addressing by regular-expression patterns, rather than by line numbers alone, was revolutionary and became a standard feature of Unix text processing, feeding into tools such as grep.
