Cauchy stress tensor

In continuum mechanics, the Cauchy stress tensor σ, also called the true stress tensor, is a second-order tensor consisting of nine components σij that completely define the state of stress at a point inside a material in its deformed state. It is named after Augustin-Louis Cauchy, who introduced it in the 1820s.

Definition

The tensor relates a unit-length direction vector n to the traction vector T(n) acting across an imaginary surface perpendicular to n:

T(n) = σ · n, or Ti(n) = σji nj.

Diagonal components σxx, σyy, σzz are normal stresses; the off-diagonal components are shear stresses. In the absence of body couples, the Cauchy stress tensor is symmetric: σij = σji.

Uses

The Cauchy stress tensor appears in Cauchy's equation of motion, the fundamental balance law of continuum mechanics, and in constitutive equations relating stress to strain or strain rate. Its eigenvalues are the principal stresses, and its invariants are used in yield criteria such as the von Mises yield criterion. Related measures include the first and second Piola–Kirchhoff stress tensors, which refer stress to the undeformed configuration.
