Hanna Krall

Hanna Krall (born 20 May 1935 in Warsaw) is a Polish writer and journalist known for her work on the Holocaust and post-war Polish Jewish life, and for developing a distinctive form of literary reportage.

Biography

Krall was born into a Jewish family in Warsaw. During the Second World War she survived the Holocaust in hiding. After the war she studied journalism at the University of Warsaw and worked for the magazine Polityka and other Polish publications, reporting from Poland and the Soviet Union.

Work

Her best-known book is Zdążyć przed Panem Bogiem (1977), translated as Shielding the Flame or To Outwit God, based on interviews with Marek Edelman, the last surviving leader of the Warsaw Ghetto Uprising. The book is considered a classic of Polish reportage. Other works include Hipnoza, Taniec na cudzym weselu, Dowody na istnienie, and Król kier znów na wylocie, translated as Chasing the King of Hearts.

Style

Krall's writing is spare and precise, built from short scenes, dialogue, and factual detail, allowing the subjects' voices to carry the narrative with minimal authorial commentary. See also Holocaust and Warsaw Ghetto Uprising.
