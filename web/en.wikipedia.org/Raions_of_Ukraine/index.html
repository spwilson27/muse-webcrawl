Raions of Ukraine

Raions of Ukraine (Ukrainian: райони України) are the second-level administrative divisions of Ukraine, below the oblasts (provinces) and above the hromadas (municipal communities). Following a major reform adopted in July 2020, Ukraine has 136 raions, replacing the previous 490 smaller districts. Each raion is administered by an elected raion council and a raion state administration appointed by the president.

The raions of Ukraine as reorganized by the 2020 administrative reform.

History

The raion (district) system was introduced in the Ukrainian SSR in the 1920s on the Soviet model. After independence, Ukraine retained 490 raions along with cities of regional significance that stood outside them. The decentralization programme launched in 2014 created amalgamated territorial communities (hromadas) and transferred many functions downward, making the old raion tier increasingly redundant in its original form.

2020 reform

On 17 July 2020 the Verkhovna Rada adopted a resolution enlarging the raions to 136 (including 17 in the temporarily occupied territories of Crimea and the Donbas). The new raions, each typically centred on a substantial city and containing several hundred thousand inhabitants, took effect for the October 2020 local elections. The reform also abolished the separate status of cities of regional significance, incorporating them into raions.

Role

Because most service delivery was transferred to hromadas and oblasts, the enlarged raions chiefly coordinate state administration locally and manage institutions serving several communities. Their future role remains a subject of debate in the ongoing reform of Ukraine's administrative-territorial structure.
