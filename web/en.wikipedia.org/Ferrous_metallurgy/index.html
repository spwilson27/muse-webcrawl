Ferrous metallurgy

Ferrous metallurgy is the metallurgy of iron and its alloys. It began far back in prehistory; the earliest surviving iron artifacts date to around 3200 BCE in Egypt, made from meteoric iron.

History

Iron smelting in bloomeries spread across the ancient Near East and Europe, giving its name to the Iron Age. The Han dynasty in China developed cast iron, while crucible steel was made in India.

Smelting processes

The modern industry begins with the blast furnace, in which iron ore is reduced by coke to pig iron. Direct reduced iron processes offer lower-carbon alternatives.

Steel production

Pig iron is refined into steel in the basic oxygen furnace or the electric arc furnace; the earlier Bessemer process (1856) was the first cheap bulk steelmaking method.
