Effectiveness

Effectiveness is the capability of producing a desired result or the ability to produce desired output. When something is deemed effective, it means it has an intended or expected outcome. Effectiveness is distinct from efficiency: efficiency concerns doing things with minimal waste of resources, whereas effectiveness concerns doing the right things — achieving goals, regardless of the resources used. The distinction is commonly summarised as "efficiency is doing things right; effectiveness is doing the right things," a formulation popularised by management writer Peter Drucker.

In medicine

In clinical epidemiology, effectiveness refers to how well a treatment works in routine real-world practice, as opposed to efficacy, which is measured under ideal, controlled conditions such as randomised trials. Vaccine effectiveness studies, for example, estimate the reduction in disease risk among vaccinated people in ordinary settings.

In organisations

Organisational effectiveness is a broad concept in management theory concerning how well an organisation meets its goals, variously modelled through goal attainment, systems, strategic-constituency and competing-values approaches. Related terms include cost-effectiveness analysis in health economics, which compares interventions by cost per unit of health gain (e.g. per QALY).
