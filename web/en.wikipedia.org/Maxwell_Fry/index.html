Maxwell Fry

Edwin Maxwell Fry
Born2 August 1899, Liscard, Cheshire, England
Died3 September 1987, Cotherstone, County Durham
OccupationArchitect, town planner, writer
Notable worksSassoon House, Kensal House, Chandigarh planning team, University of Ibadan

Edwin Maxwell Fry (1899–1987) was an English modernist architect, writer and pioneer of the Modern Movement in Britain. With his wife and partner Jane Drew, he worked extensively in West Africa and India, becoming a leading figure in tropical modernism — designing for climate in the colonial and post-colonial world.

Early career

Trained at the Liverpool School of Architecture under Charles Reilly, Fry moved to London, worked for Adams, Thompson and Fry, and designed early modernist buildings including Sassoon House, Peckham (1934), Britain's first modernist social housing using access balconies. He was a founder member of the MARS Group, worked briefly with Walter Gropius after Gropius fled Nazi Germany (1934–1936, including the Impington Village College commission later completed by others), and designed Kensal House (1937) with Elizabeth Denby — a model low-cost housing scheme with nursery and social facilities.

Africa, Drew and Chandigarh

Serving in West Africa during the Second World War, Fry stayed on with Jane Drew as Fry, Drew and Partners, planning towns and designing schools and universities in Ghana and Nigeria — including the University of Ibadan and the celebrated village schools described in their book Village Housing in the Tropics (1947). In 1951 they joined Le Corbusier and Pierre Jeanneret on the planning team for Chandigarh, the new capital of Indian Punjab, designing sectors, housing and public buildings for three years.

Later life and legacy

Fry wrote Fine Building (1944) and an autobiography, and promoted humane modernism adapted to climate and social need. His and Drew's archive is held by the RIBA. Their work, once criticised as colonial, is now reassessed as an influential attempt to fuse modernist planning with local materials, ventilation and shade — tropical architecture studied worldwide.
