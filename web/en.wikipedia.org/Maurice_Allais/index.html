Maurice Allais

Maurice Félix Charles Allais (31 May 1911 – 9 October 2010) was a French physicist and economist, the winner of the 1988 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences "for his pioneering contributions to the theory of markets and efficient utilization of resources". He is also known for the Allais paradox, a classic challenge to expected utility theory.

Work in economics

Working largely in isolation in France, Allais developed — in works such as À la recherche d'une discipline économique (1943) and Économie et intérêt (1947) — results on general equilibrium and capital theory that paralleled later English-language contributions. His 1953 paradox showed that people's choices systematically violate the independence axiom of expected utility theory, foreshadowing behavioural economics.

Physics experiments

Allais also conducted pendulum experiments during solar eclipses in the 1950s, reporting anomalous movements (the "Allais effect"); the effect has not been reliably reproduced and remains controversial.
