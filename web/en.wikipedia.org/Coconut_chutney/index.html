Coconut chutney

Coconut chutney is a South Indian condiment and side dish made by grinding fresh coconut meat with green chillies, ginger, and salt, often finished with a tempering of mustard seeds, dried red chillies, curry leaves, and sometimes urad dal fried in oil. It is a staple accompaniment to breakfast dishes such as idli, dosa, vada, pongal, and upma throughout Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, and Telangana.

Preparation

Freshly grated coconut is blended with green chillies, a small piece of ginger, roasted chana dal (which gives body), and salt, with enough water to reach a smooth, pourable consistency. A tempering (tadka) of mustard seeds, dried red chillies, curry leaves, asafoetida, and urad dal is then poured over the chutney. Some versions add a squeeze of lemon juice or a small amount of tamarind for tang, and others grind in coriander or mint leaves, producing a green-tinted chutney.

Variations

Regional variations are numerous. Kerala-style thenga chammanthi is thicker, often made without water and with shallots and sometimes dried red chillies instead of green. Versions made with roasted coconut have a deeper, nuttier flavour and a longer shelf life. Coconut chutney is also served with snacks such as pakoras and sandwiches, and a thinner version accompanies dishes like appam and idiyappam.

Serving

The chutney is best eaten fresh, since the raw coconut and tempering aromatics lose their flavour on storage, though it can be refrigerated for a day or two. In a traditional South Indian breakfast, it is served alongside sambar and one or more additional chutneys, such as tomato or peanut chutney.
