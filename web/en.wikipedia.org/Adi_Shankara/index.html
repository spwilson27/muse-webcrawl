Adi Shankara

Adi Shankara (c. 788–820 CE), also called Adi Shankaracharya, was an Indian philosopher and theologian who consolidated the doctrine of Advaita Vedanta, a school of Hindu philosophy teaching the unity of the individual self (Atman) and ultimate reality (Brahman).

Life

Tradition places his birth in Kaladi, in present-day Kerala. He is said to have renounced worldly life at a young age, travelled across India debating philosophers of other schools, and founded four monastic centres (mathas) at Sringeri, Dwarka, Puri and Joshimath, associated with the Dashanami Sampradaya.

Works

Shankara wrote commentaries (bhashya) on the Upanishads, the Bhagavad Gita and the Brahma Sutras, as well as independent treatises such as the Upadesasahasri and devotional hymns attributed to him.

Philosophy

Advaita ("non-dual") holds that Brahman alone is ultimately real, the world of multiplicity is maya, and liberation (moksha) comes from knowledge dispelling ignorance of one's true nature.
