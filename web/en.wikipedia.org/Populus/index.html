Populus is a genus of 25–30 species of deciduous flowering plants in the family Salicaceae, native to most of the Northern Hemisphere. English names for species in the genus include poplar, aspen, and cottonwood.

Description

Poplars are fast-growing trees, often with tall straight trunks and leaves that tremble in the wind because of their flattened petioles. The genus is dioecious, with male and female flowers on separate trees. In spring, female trees release seeds attached to cotton-like fibers, the origin of the name "cottonwood".

Uses

Poplar wood is soft, light, and widely used for pallets, plywood, matches, and paper pulp. Hybrid poplars are planted extensively in short-rotation plantations for biomass and timber. The black poplar (Populus nigra) cultivar known as the Lombardy poplar is a familiar ornamental tree. The genome of Populus trichocarpa was sequenced in 2006, making it a model organism for tree genetics.
