Île de la Cité

LocationSeine, Paris, France
Arrondissements1st and 4th
Lengthapprox. 1.19 km
Areaapprox. 22.5 ha

The Île de la Cité is a natural island in the Seine in the centre of Paris. It is the historic heart of the city: the Celtic Parisii settled here, Roman Lutetia was founded on the island, and it remained the seat of royal and judicial power throughout the Middle Ages.

Landmarks

The island is dominated by Notre-Dame Cathedral at its eastern end, begun in 1163. Nearby stand the Sainte-Chapelle, Louis IX's 13th-century palace chapel famed for its stained glass, and the Conciergerie, part of the medieval Palais de la Cité where Marie Antoinette was imprisoned during the Revolution. The Palais de Justice still occupies much of the island's centre, and the flower market, the Mémorial des Martyrs de la Déportation, and the Square du Vert-Galant at the downstream point complete the site.

History

Under Baron Haussmann's renovation of Paris in the 19th century, most of the island's medieval residential quarters were demolished and replaced by administrative buildings, sharply reducing its resident population, which today numbers only in the hundreds. The island is connected to both banks and to the Île Saint-Louis by nine bridges, including the Pont Neuf (completed 1607), the oldest standing bridge in Paris, which in fact crosses the island's western tip.
