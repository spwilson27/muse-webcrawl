Gettysburg Cyclorama

The Gettysburg Cyclorama is a monumental circular painting depicting Pickett's Charge, the climactic Confederate infantry assault on 3 July 1863 during the Battle of Gettysburg of the American Civil War. It was painted by the French artist Paul Philippoteaux and a team of assistants and is among the largest and best-preserved cycloramas in existence.

Creation

Philippoteaux, an experienced panorama painter, was commissioned in 1882 by a group of Chicago businessmen. He spent months studying the battlefield, interviewing veterans, and working from photographs before completing the canvas with the help of some twenty assistants. The finished painting is about 42 feet (13 metres) high and 377 feet (115 metres) in circumference and weighs roughly three tons.

Four versions of the painting were produced. The version now at Gettysburg was first exhibited in Boston in 1884, where it was seen by many veterans of the battle, who praised its accuracy.

Exhibition history

After touring several cities, the painting was eventually purchased and displayed at Gettysburg. It later came into the care of the National Park Service, which exhibited it for decades in a dedicated building near the battlefield. Over time the canvas suffered damage from water, tearing, and earlier restorations that had cut away part of the sky.

Restoration

Beginning in 2003, the painting underwent a major conservation project costing several million dollars. Conservators cleaned and repaired the canvas, restored missing sections, and re-created the three-dimensional foreground diorama of fences, trees, and artefacts that blends into the painted scene. In 2008 the restored cyclorama opened to the public in the new Museum and Visitor Center at Gettysburg National Military Park, viewed from a raised platform with a light-and-sound presentation.
