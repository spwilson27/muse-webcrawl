Negative amortization

Negative amortization is an increase in the principal balance of a loan caused by a failure to make payments that cover the interest due. It typically occurs in adjustable-rate mortgages that allow the borrower to make minimum payments below the interest cost, with the unpaid interest added to the balance.

Mechanics

When the scheduled payment is less than the interest accrued, the shortfall is capitalised: the borrower owes more after the payment than before. Such loans often include a recast trigger—a maximum balance (such as 110–125 percent of the original amount) or a fixed date—after which payments are recalculated to fully amortise the enlarged balance, causing a sharp payment increase.

Risks

Negative amortization loans, including "option ARMs," were associated with payment shock and defaults during the United States housing crisis of the late 2000s, and they are now heavily restricted in many markets.
