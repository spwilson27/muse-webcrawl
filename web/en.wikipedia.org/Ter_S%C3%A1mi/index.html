Ter Sámi

Ter Sámi is the easternmost of the Sámi languages, once spoken on the northeastern Kola Peninsula in Russia. It is a critically endangered language: by the early 21st century only a handful of elderly speakers remained, and it is often described as nearly extinct.

History

Ter Sámi was spoken in communities around the Varzuga and Ponoy rivers. A primer was published in the 19th century, and Soviet-era collectivization, boarding schools, and resettlement scattered its speakers and accelerated language shift to Russian. Documentation efforts in recent decades have recorded the last fluent speakers.

Classification

Ter Sámi belongs to the Eastern Sámi group of the Uralic language family, closely related to Akkala Sámi (now extinct) and Skolt Sámi.
