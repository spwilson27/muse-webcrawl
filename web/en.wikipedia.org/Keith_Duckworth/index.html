David Keith Duckworth (10 August 1933 – 18 December 2005) was an English mechanical engineer who co-founded Cosworth Engineering and designed the Cosworth DFV (Double Four Valve) engine, the most successful engine in the history of Formula One motor racing.

Early life and career

Duckworth was born in Blackburn and educated at Giggleswick School before studying mechanical engineering at Imperial College London. After national service as a navigator in the Royal Air Force, he joined Lotus Cars in 1957 as a gearbox engineer under Colin Chapman. In 1958 he left with fellow Lotus engineer Mike Costin to found Cosworth in London, the company name combining their surnames. Cosworth moved to Northampton in 1964.

The DFV

Commissioned by Chapman and funded by Ford, Duckworth designed the DFV, a 3.0-litre V8 with four valves per cylinder and twin overhead camshafts, which was also designed to serve as a stressed member of the chassis. The engine debuted in the Lotus 49 at the 1967 Dutch Grand Prix, winning on its first outing with Jim Clark. The DFV went on to win 155 Grands Prix between 1967 and 1983, twelve Drivers' Championships and ten Constructors' Championships, and also won the 24 Hours of Le Mans and the Indianapolis 500 in turbocharged DFX form.

Later work and honours

Duckworth remained central to Cosworth's road-car and racing programmes, including engines for the Ford Sierra RS500 and Escort RS Cosworth. He received honorary recognition from the engineering profession and was inducted into the Motorsports Hall of Fame. He died in Northampton in December 2005.
