Sapporo (札幌) is the capital and largest city of Hokkaido, Japan's northernmost main island, and the fifth-largest city in Japan. It is one of the country's newest cities, having been founded in 1868 as part of the Meiji government's development of Hokkaido.

History

Before its founding, the area was home to Ainu settlements; the name Sapporo derives from the Ainu phrase sat poro pet, meaning "dry, great river". The city was laid out on a grid pattern modelled partly on North American cities. Sapporo hosted the 1972 Winter Olympics, the first held in Asia.

Culture and economy

Sapporo is famous for its annual Snow Festival (Yuki Matsuri), held each February, which draws millions of visitors to see enormous snow and ice sculptures in Odori Park. The city is also known for Sapporo beer, one of Japan's oldest beer brands (founded 1876), miso ramen, and jingisukan (grilled mutton). Major landmarks include the Sapporo Clock Tower, the former Hokkaido Government Office, and the ski jumps at Okurayama.

Climate

Sapporo has a humid continental climate with warm summers and very snowy winters; it is among the snowiest major cities in the world, receiving around 5–6 metres of snow annually.
