Work permit

A work permit or work visa is a legal authorisation for a person to take up employment in a country where they do not hold citizenship or, in some systems, for minors to take employment that would otherwise be restricted. Work permits are typically issued by immigration authorities, sometimes following a labour-market test.

Types

Permits may be employer-specific or open, temporary or convertible to residence, and tied to particular occupations or salary thresholds. Examples include the United States H-1B visa for specialty occupations, the EU Blue Card for highly qualified workers, and seasonal agricultural worker schemes.

Permits for minors

In many jurisdictions, including most US states, school-age workers require employment certificates ("working papers") limiting hours and hazardous duties, as part of child labour law.

Issues

Tied permits are criticised for creating dependency on a single employer and vulnerability to exploitation; points-based reforms aim to select migrants by skills while reducing employer discretion.
