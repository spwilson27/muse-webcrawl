Field: Volcanology, geology
Features: Fissures, dikes, pit craters, cinder cones

Rift zone

A rift zone is a feature of some volcanoes, especially shield volcanoes, in which a set of linear cracks (fissures) in the volcanic edifice allows lava to erupt from the flank of the volcano rather than only from its summit. Rift zones are zones of weakness along which magma intrudes as dikes and the volcano can split apart or subside.

Formation

Rift zones commonly form where the volcano's flanks spread under gravity or where regional tectonic stresses open pathways for magma. On Hawaiian volcanoes such as Kīlauea and Mauna Loa, two or three prominent rift zones radiate from the summit caldera, marked by fissures, pit craters, spatter ramparts, and cinder cones along their length.

Hazards and monitoring

Because eruptions along rift zones can reach populated areas far downslope — as in Kīlauea's 2018 lower East Rift Zone eruption, which destroyed hundreds of homes — rift zones are closely monitored for ground deformation and earthquakes that signal magma movement.
