Ocean heat content

Ocean heat content (OHC) or ocean heat uptake (OHU) is the energy absorbed and stored by oceans. To calculate the ocean heat content, it is necessary to measure ocean temperature at many different locations and depths. Integrating the areal density of ocean heat over an ocean basin or the entire ocean gives the total ocean heat content.

Ocean heat content has risen steadily since the mid-twentieth century.

Between 1971 and 2018, the rise in ocean heat content accounted for more than 90 percent of the excess energy accumulated in Earth's climate system due to global warming. The main driver of this increase is rising greenhouse gas concentrations caused by human activities.

Measurement

Historical measurements come from bathythermographs and oceanographic cruises; since the early 2000s the Argo array of autonomous profiling floats has provided near-global coverage of the upper 2,000 metres. Changes in OHC are expressed in zettajoules (1021 joules).

Consequences

Warmer oceans contribute to sea level rise through thermal expansion, intensify marine heatwaves, bleach coral reefs, and alter ocean circulation and weather patterns. Monitoring of the seas is supported by agencies and regulators around the world's coasts, including bodies such as Ofcom's counterparts in maritime communications and national meteorological services.
