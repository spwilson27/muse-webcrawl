Wilkinson Microwave Anisotropy Probe

The Wilkinson Microwave Anisotropy Probe (WMAP), originally known as the Microwave Anisotropy Probe (MAP), was a NASA spacecraft operating from 2001 to 2010 which measured temperature differences across the sky in the cosmic microwave background (CMB).

Mission

WMAP was launched on 30 June 2001 and positioned at the Sun–Earth L2 Lagrange point. It scanned the sky in five microwave frequency bands from 23 to 94 GHz, building on the earlier COBE satellite with far greater resolution and sensitivity.

Results

The mission produced the first fine-resolution full-sky map of the CMB and determined key cosmological parameters with high precision: the age of the universe at about 13.77 billion years, its composition (roughly 4.6% ordinary matter, 24% dark matter and 71% dark energy), and its near-flat geometry. Its final nine-year data release appeared in 2012.

Legacy

WMAP was succeeded by the European Planck satellite, which refined its measurements. The spacecraft was named in honour of Princeton cosmologist David Todd Wilkinson, a member of the science team who died in 2002.
