Jingdezhen

Jingdezhen (景德镇) is a prefecture-level city in northeastern Jiangxi Province, China, world-renowned as the "Porcelain Capital" for its more than a thousand years of ceramic production. Pottery has been made in the area since the Han dynasty, and during the Song dynasty the emperor Zhenzong gave the town the name of his reign era, Jingde (1004–1007), in recognition of its wares.


Jingdezhen blue-and-white porcelain

Imperial kilns

In the early Ming dynasty the Hongwu emperor established imperial kilns at Jingdezhen to supply the court. The city perfected blue-and-white porcelain using imported cobalt, as well as doucai, famille rose and other polychrome techniques. Jingdezhen porcelain reached Europe in vast quantities aboard East India Company ships, shaping Western taste and the development of European porcelain at Meissen and elsewhere.

Kaolin

The city's porcelain depends on a mixture of porcelain stone and kaolin clay, named after Gaoling ("high ridge") village near Jingdezhen — the source of the English word "kaolin".

Modern city

Today Jingdezhen remains a centre of ceramic art and industry, home to the Jingdezhen Ceramic University and the Sanbao international ceramic art village, attracting artists from around the world. Its population is roughly 1.6 million.
