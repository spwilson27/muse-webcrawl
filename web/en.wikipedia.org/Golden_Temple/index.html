The Golden Temple, also known as Sri Harmandir Sahib ("the abode of God") or Darbar Sahib, is a gurdwara located in the city of Amritsar, Punjab, India. It is the pre-eminent spiritual site of Sikhism and one of the most visited religious sites in the world.

[ Image placeholder: The Golden Temple reflected in the Amrit Sarovar ]

The temple stands in the middle of a man-made pool (sarovar) whose waters are considered sacred, and the city of Amritsar — "pool of nectar" — takes its name from it. The gurdwara is an open house of worship for all people, symbolised by its four entrances opening to all directions. Its sanctum is covered with 750 kilograms of gold leaf donated over time, giving the temple its popular name; the gilding was largely sponsored by Maharaja Ranjit Singh in the 19th century.

History

The fourth Sikh Guru, Guru Ram Das, founded Amritsar in 1577. The fifth Guru, Guru Arjan, designed the temple and installed the Adi Granth, the Sikh scripture, in it in 1604. The temple was repeatedly destroyed by Afghan invaders and rebuilt; the present structure dates mainly from a reconstruction begun in 1764. In June 1984 the Indian Army's Operation Blue Star, aimed at armed militants occupying the complex, severely damaged the Akal Takht and caused heavy loss of life, an event that led to the assassination of Prime Minister Indira Gandhi later that year.

The langar

The Golden Temple operates the world's largest free community kitchen (langar), serving hot vegetarian meals to tens of thousands of visitors daily — often around 50,000, and up to 100,000 on holidays — regardless of faith, wealth, or background, prepared and served by volunteers in keeping with the Sikh principle of seva (selfless service).
