Michael Lewis Webster (18 March 1952 – 24 September 2002) was an American football center who played 17 seasons in the NFL, principally with the Pittsburgh Steelers, winning four Super Bowls. Regarded as one of the greatest centers in league history, he was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 1997. After his death, Webster became the first former NFL player diagnosed with chronic traumatic encephalopathy (CTE), a finding that ignited the public controversy over football and brain injury.

Playing career

Drafted from Wisconsin in the fifth round of the 1974 NFL draft, Webster anchored the Steelers' offensive line through their dynasty years, starting 150 consecutive games and playing in nine Pro Bowls. He finished his career with the Kansas City Chiefs in 1989–90.

Later life and CTE

Webster's retirement was marked by cognitive decline, depression, homelessness, and financial ruin; he died of a heart attack at 50. Pathologist Bennet Omalu's examination of Webster's brain revealed CTE, published in 2005. Webster's case — his estate's disability litigation and Omalu's findings — became central to the NFL concussion crisis portrayed in the book and film Concussion.
