The Camino Real de Tierra Adentro (Spanish for "Royal Road of the Interior Land") was a 2,560-kilometre (1,600 mi) trade route between Mexico City and San Juan Pueblo (now Ohkay Owingeh, New Mexico) used from 1598 to 1882. It carried silver from the mines of Zacatecas, Guanajuato and San Luis Potosí south to the capital, and mercury, supplies and settlers north to the frontier province of New Mexico.

History

The northern portion of the route was pioneered by Juan de Oñate's colonising expedition of 1598. For nearly three centuries the road linked the silver-mining north with central Mexico, passing through Querétaro, Zacatecas, Durango, Chihuahua and across the harsh Jornada del Muerto to El Paso del Norte and up the Rio Grande to Santa Fe and San Juan Pueblo. Wagons travelled in caravans departing roughly every three years to resupply New Mexico.

Heritage

In 2010 the Mexican portion of the route, with 55 associated sites, was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List. In the United States, the El Camino Real de Tierra Adentro National Historic Trail, designated in 2000, is administered jointly by the National Park Service and the Bureau of Land Management.
