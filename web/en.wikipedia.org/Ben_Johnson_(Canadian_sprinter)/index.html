Benjamin Sinclair Johnson (born 30 December 1961) is a Canadian former track and field sprinter. Johnson won the 100 metres at the 1988 Seoul Olympics in a world-record 9.79 seconds, but was disqualified three days later after testing positive for the anabolic steroid stanozolol — the most notorious doping scandal in Olympic history. His world records and titles from 1987–88 were annulled, and the gold medal was awarded to Carl Lewis.

Early life and rise

Johnson was born in Falmouth, Jamaica, and emigrated to Canada at age 14, settling in Scarborough, Ontario. He joined the Scarborough Optimists track club under coach Charlie Francis. He won bronze medals in the 100 m and 4×100 m relay at the 1984 Los Angeles Olympics, and by the mid-1980s had developed his trademark explosive start. In 1986 he beat Lewis at the Goodwill Games and won Commonwealth gold in Edinburgh.

1987–1988: rivalry with Carl Lewis

At the 1987 World Championships in Rome, Johnson defeated Carl Lewis in 9.83 seconds, a new world record. In Seoul on 24 September 1988, Johnson beat Lewis by a wide margin in 9.79, telling reporters the record would take "50 years" to break. Within days, his urine sample tested positive for stanozolol. Johnson was stripped of the medal and record and suspended for two years.

Dubin Inquiry and comeback

The scandal prompted Canada's Dubin Inquiry (1989), at which Johnson admitted long-term steroid use and coach Charlie Francis testified to administering a doping programme; Johnson's 1987 world record was retroactively annulled. After serving his ban, Johnson attempted a comeback in 1991, but in January 1993 he tested positive again, for elevated testosterone, and received a lifetime ban from the IAAF. A 1999 appeal briefly reinstated him, but the ban was upheld.

Later life

Johnson has since worked as a coach and personal trainer, published an autobiography, Seoul to Soul (2010), and campaigned for clean sport, arguing that elite sprinting of his era was pervaded by doping. The Seoul 100 m final — in which six of the eight finalists were later implicated in doping — remains a touchstone in debates about performance-enhancing drugs in sport.
