Twelve Olympians

In ancient Greek religion and mythology, the Twelve Olympians are the major deities of the Greek pantheon, commonly considered to be Zeus, Poseidon, Hera, Demeter, Athena, Apollo, Artemis, Ares, Hephaestus, Aphrodite, Hermes, and either Hestia or Dionysus. They are said to reside on Mount Olympus, the highest mountain in Greece, and gained their supremacy in the Titanomachy, the ten-year war of gods led by Zeus against the Titans.

Variations and cult

The composition of the twelve was never fully fixed: Hades, king of the underworld, is a major god but dwells apart and is usually excluded, while Heracles and Hebe sometimes appear in lists. The concept of twelve gods appears early — an altar of the twelve gods stood in the Athenian Agora from 522/521 BC, serving as the central milestone of Athens. The Olympians are the principal subjects of the Homeric epics and Hesiod's Theogony, and their statues, temples, and festivals (above all the Olympic Games, held for Zeus) structured Greek civic religion.
