Mayahuel

Mayahuel is the female deity associated with the maguey plant in central Mexican (Aztec) mythology. She is a goddess of fertility and nourishment, and the discoverer or personification of pulque, the fermented drink made from maguey sap.

Myth

According to myth, the god Quetzalcoatl descended to the underworld, recovered the bones of Mayahuel — who had been killed by her grandmother Tzitzimitl and other star demons — and buried them; from the burial spot sprang the first maguey plant. In depictions she often has 400 breasts to feed her children, the Centzon Totochtin ("400 rabbits"), gods of drunkenness.

Symbolism

The maguey provided food, drink, fibre, and needles, so Mayahuel became a symbol of sustenance and maternal abundance in Nahua culture.
