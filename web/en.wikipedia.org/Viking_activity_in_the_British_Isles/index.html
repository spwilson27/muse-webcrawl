Viking activity in the British Isles

Viking activity in the British Isles began in the late 8th century, traditionally dated to the raid on Lindisfarne monastery in 793, and continued for nearly three centuries. Scandinavian raiders, traders and settlers profoundly shaped England, Scotland, Ireland and Wales.

England

The Great Heathen Army landed in East Anglia in 865 and conquered Northumbria, East Anglia and much of Mercia, establishing the Danelaw. Alfred the Great of Wessex halted their advance at Edington in 878. In the 11th century Cnut the Great ruled England as part of a North Sea Empire (1016–1035).

Ireland and Scotland

Vikings founded Dublin in 841 and other coastal towns including Waterford, Wexford, Cork and Limerick. Norse settlers colonised Orkney, Shetland, the Hebrides and Caithness. Viking influence waned after the Norman Conquest of 1066, though Magnus Barelegs campaigned in the Irish Sea as late as 1098–1103.
