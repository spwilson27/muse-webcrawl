Industrial and organizational psychology

Industrial and organizational psychology (I-O psychology) is the scientific study of human behaviour in the workplace. It is an applied discipline within psychology. I-O psychologists are trained in the scientist–practitioner model. They contribute to an organization's success by improving the job performance, wellbeing, motivation, communication, and job satisfaction of its employees.

History

The field emerged in the early 20th century from the work of Hugo Münsterberg, Walter Dill Scott, and others applying psychology to industry, and from Frederick Winslow Taylor's scientific management. The Hawthorne studies of the 1920s and 1930s shifted attention toward social factors and worker attitudes. During the Second World War, I-O psychologists contributed to personnel selection and training on a large scale.

Topics

The "industrial" side traditionally covers personnel matters: job analysis, recruitment, selection and assessment, training, and performance appraisal. The "organizational" side addresses motivation, leadership, job satisfaction, organizational culture and change, work–life balance, and occupational health. I-O psychologists conduct research and consult for businesses, government agencies, and non-profits; the professional body in the United States is the Society for Industrial and Organizational Psychology (SIOP).
