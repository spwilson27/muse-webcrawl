Shulamith Firestone

Born: 7 January 1945, Ottawa, Canada
Died: 28 August 2012, New York City
Known for: The Dialectic of Sex

Shulamith Firestone (7 January 1945 – 28 August 2012) was a Canadian-American radical feminist writer and activist, best known as the author of The Dialectic of Sex: The Case for Feminist Revolution (1970), a landmark of second-wave feminism.

Life

Born Shulamith Bath Shmuel Ben Ari Feuerstein in Ottawa, Canada, to Orthodox Jewish parents, she was raised in Kansas City and St. Louis. She studied painting at the Art Institute of Chicago before moving to New York in 1967.

Activism

Firestone co-founded New York Radical Women in 1967, and later the Redstockings and New York Radical Feminists. Her groups helped organize the 1968 Miss America protest and other foundational actions.

The Dialectic of Sex

Written when she was 25, the book argued that the biological division of reproduction underlies women's oppression, and envisioned technology freeing women from the burdens of pregnancy and child-rearing, alongside the abolition of the nuclear family and childhood as a separate segregated stage. It drew on Marx, Engels, Freud and Simone de Beauvoir. Later in life Firestone struggled with schizophrenia and lived reclusively; she published the memoir-like Airless Spaces in 1998.
