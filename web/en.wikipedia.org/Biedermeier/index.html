Biedermeier

The Biedermeier period was an era in Central European art, literature, and culture between 1815 and 1848, corresponding to the years between the Congress of Vienna and the revolutions of 1848. It describes the sensibility of the growing middle class, which turned inward toward domestic life, family, and private pleasures under the conservative political order of the Restoration.

Origins of the term

The name derives from the fictional schoolmaster Gottlieb Biedermaier, a satirical character created by Ludwig Eichrodt and Adolf Kussmaul in poems published from 1855 in the Munich magazine Fliegende Blätter. The character embodied the comfortable, apolitical bourgeois, and the name was later applied retrospectively to the whole period.

Furniture and design

Biedermeier furniture is characterised by clean lines, restrained ornament, and light-coloured native woods such as cherry, birch, and ash, often with ebonised details. It rejected the heavy grandeur of the Empire style in favour of pieces suited to intimate middle-class interiors, and it influenced later Scandinavian and modernist design.

Literature and music

Writers associated with the period include Adalbert Stifter, Annette von Droste-Hülshoff, and Jeremias Gotthelf. In music, the era corresponds to the intimate salon culture of early Romanticism: Schubert's Lieder and piano music for domestic performance are emblematic of the Biedermeier spirit.
