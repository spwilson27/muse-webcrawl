Saltillo

Saltillo is the capital and largest city of the Mexican state of Coahuila and the seat of the municipality of the same name. Located in northeastern Mexico, about 400 kilometres south of the border with the United States at an elevation of roughly 1,600 metres in the foothills of the Sierra Madre Oriental, it forms a metropolitan area together with neighbouring Ramos Arizpe and Arteaga. Its population is around 880,000, making it one of the largest cities in northern Mexico.

History

Saltillo was founded in 1577 by Spanish and Portuguese settlers under Alberto del Canto, making it the oldest city in northern Mexico. It became an important trading post on the road between Mexico City and Texas; its annual fair drew merchants from across New Spain. In 1824 it briefly served as capital of the combined state of Coahuila y Tejas. United States forces occupied the city during the Mexican–American War, and the Battle of Buena Vista was fought nearby in 1847.

Economy and culture

Historically renowned for its colourful woven sarapes, Saltillo is today a major manufacturing centre with large automotive and truck assembly plants operated by General Motors, Stellantis and Daimler, earning it the nickname "the Detroit of Mexico". Cultural landmarks include the 18th-century Cathedral of Santiago, the Museo del Desierto (Desert Museum), devoted to the paleontology and ecology of the Chihuahuan Desert, and the Museo de las Aves, one of Latin America's largest bird museums.
