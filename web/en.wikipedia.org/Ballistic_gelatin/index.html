Ballistic gelatin

Ballistic gelatin
Purpose: Terminal ballistics testing
Standard: 10% gelatine at 4 °C (FBI protocol)

Ballistic gelatin is a testing medium designed to simulate the density and viscosity of human and animal muscle tissue. It is used in terminal ballistics research to measure how bullets and other projectiles expand, fragment and penetrate soft tissue.

Preparation

The standard "FBI-type" block consists of a 10% gelatine solution by mass, refrigerated to 4 °C. Blocks are calibrated by firing a steel BB at a specified velocity and checking that penetration falls within a set range, ensuring consistency between tests.

Use

When a projectile strikes the gel, it creates a permanent cavity (the crushed tissue track) and a temporary cavity (the radial stretch), both of which can be photographed with high-speed cameras and measured. The FBI and other agencies use gelatin results to evaluate ammunition for penetration depth and expansion reliability.

Limitations

Gelatin does not replicate skin, bone, organs or clothing, and results are an approximation used for comparing projectiles under controlled conditions rather than predicting wounds exactly. Variants with embedded synthetic bones or fabrics partly address these limits.
