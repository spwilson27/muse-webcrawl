Lenaia

The Lenaia (Ancient Greek: Λήναια) was an annual Athenian festival with a dramatic competition, held in honour of Dionysus Lenaeus. It took place in the month of Gamelion (roughly January) and was probably the oldest of Athens' Dionysian festivals. Unlike the great City Dionysia in spring, when the seas were open and visitors flocked to Athens, the winter Lenaia was a mainly local celebration attended by Athenians and resident metics.

The festival

The name may derive from lēnos, a wine-press, or from the lēnai, the maenads who worshipped Dionysus. Vase paintings associated with the festival show women drawing wine from a mixing bowl and offering it before a pillar-like idol of the god draped with ivy and masks. The festival included a procession and sacrifices supervised by the archon basileus together with overseers chosen from the people.

Dramatic contests

The Lenaia is best known for its competitions in drama, formally organised around 440 BC. Comedy was pre-eminent: five comic poets competed with one play each, and Aristophanes staged many of his surviving comedies there, including The Acharnians, The Knights, The Wasps, and The Frogs. Tragedy was added later, with two tragedians presenting two plays each. Because the audience was Athenian, comedies at the Lenaia could indulge in sharper domestic political satire than at the international City Dionysia. Victories at the Lenaia were recorded in the official victor lists, and the festival's competitions were held in the Theatre of Dionysus by the 4th century BC.
