Theodoric the Great (454 – 30 August 526), also called Theodoric the Amal, was king of the Ostrogoths, ruler of the independent Ostrogothic Kingdom of Italy between 493 and 526, regent of the Visigoths, and a patrician of the Eastern Roman Empire. He is remembered for a long and largely peaceful reign that preserved Roman administration and culture in Italy, and he plays a prominent role in Germanic heroic legend as Dietrich von Bern.

Early life

Born in Pannonia to the Amal king Thiudimer, Theodoric was sent as a child hostage to Constantinople, where he spent about ten years at the court of Emperor Leo I, receiving a Roman education. Returning to lead Ostrogothic forces in the Balkans, he alternately served and threatened the empire until Emperor Zeno directed him against Odoacer, the Germanic ruler of Italy.

Conquest and rule of Italy

Theodoric invaded Italy in 489, defeating Odoacer at the Isonzo and Verona and besieging him in Ravenna. After a negotiated settlement, Theodoric murdered Odoacer at a banquet in 493. From Ravenna he ruled Italy with a policy of cooperation between Goths and Romans: Roman civil administration, the Senate, and Catholic institutions continued under figures such as Cassiodorus, while the Gothic army kept its separate role. An Arian, Theodoric nevertheless maintained religious toleration for most of his reign; its breakdown in his final years saw the execution of the philosopher Boethius (524) and of Symmachus.

Legacy

His building programme in Ravenna — including the palace, the Arian baptistery, Sant'Apollinare Nuovo, and his great domed mausoleum — survives in part. Within a few years of his death the kingdom was embroiled in the Gothic War between his successors and Justinian's generals. In Germanic legend he became Dietrich von Bern, hero of the Nibelungenlied tradition and Middle High German epics.
