Initial value problem

In multivariable calculus, an initial value problem (IVP) is an ordinary differential equation together with an initial condition specifying the value of the unknown function at a given point in the domain. Modelling a system in physics or other sciences is often reduced to solving an initial value problem; in these cases the differential equation is an evolution equation specifying how the system evolves over time given the initial conditions.

An initial condition picks out a single solution curve from the family satisfying a differential equation.

Definition

An initial value problem has the form: dy/dt = f(t, y), with y(t0) = y0, where f is a given function and y0 is the initial value. A solution is a function y that satisfies both the equation and the initial condition.

Existence and uniqueness

The Picard–Lindelöf theorem guarantees a unique local solution when f is Lipschitz continuous in y; Peano's theorem gives existence under continuity alone. Solutions may fail to exist for all time — they can blow up in finite time — and without suitable conditions an IVP may have infinitely many solutions or none.

Numerical methods

Most initial value problems cannot be solved in closed form and are integrated numerically with methods such as Euler's method, Runge–Kutta methods, and multistep methods. Stiff problems, where widely separated time scales occur, require implicit methods with special stability properties.
