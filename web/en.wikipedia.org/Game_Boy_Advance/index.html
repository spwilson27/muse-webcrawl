The Game Boy Advance (GBA) is a 32-bit handheld game console developed, manufactured, and marketed by Nintendo as the successor to the Game Boy Color. It was released in Japan on March 21, 2001, and in North America that June. With a 2.9-inch reflective color screen, a 32-bit ARM processor, and backward compatibility with Game Boy and Game Boy Color cartridges, the GBA brought near-Super NES-quality gaming to a handheld.

Revisions

The Game Boy Advance SP (2003) introduced a clamshell design with a front-lit (later backlit) screen and rechargeable battery, while the Game Boy Micro (2005) shrank the system to a compact form and dropped backward compatibility. The original model's unlit screen was a frequent criticism, frequently compared unfavorably with its successors.

Library and legacy

The system's library exceeded 1,500 titles, including Pokémon Ruby and Sapphire, Mario Kart: Super Circuit, The Legend of Zelda: The Minish Cap, Metroid Fusion, Advance Wars, and the breakout hit Golden Sun. Total sales of all GBA models reached 81.51 million units before production ended around 2010.
