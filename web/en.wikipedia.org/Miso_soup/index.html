Miso soup

Miso soup (味噌汁, miso-shiru) is a traditional Japanese soup consisting of a dashi stock flavoured with miso paste, served with small solid ingredients such as tofu, wakame seaweed and spring onions. It is a cornerstone of Japanese cuisine, eaten daily in most households — commonly at breakfast alongside rice and pickles — and served with nearly every traditional meal.

Preparation

Dashi, typically made from kombu (kelp) and katsuobushi (bonito flakes), forms the base. Miso paste — fermented soybean paste, in white (sweet), red (longer-fermented, robust) or mixed styles — is whisked into the hot stock at the end of cooking; care is taken not to boil the soup hard after adding the miso, which would dull its aroma. Solid ingredients vary by season and region: clams, daikon, potatoes, mushrooms, egg, fried tofu and leafy greens are all common, and heartier rural versions with many vegetables are known as tonjiru when made with pork.

Customs

Miso soup is drunk directly from the bowl, with the solid ingredients eaten with chopsticks. Regional variations are strong — in some areas it is a substantial meal in itself — and instant miso soup is ubiquitous in Japan and exported worldwide.
