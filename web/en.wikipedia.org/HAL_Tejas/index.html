The HAL Tejas is an Indian single-engine, delta wing, multirole light fighter designed by the Aeronautical Development Agency (ADA) in collaboration with Hindustan Aeronautics Limited (HAL) for the Indian Air Force and Indian Navy. The aircraft was officially named Tejas in 2003; the word means "radiance" in Sanskrit. It came from the Light Combat Aircraft (LCA) programme, which began in the 1980s to replace India's ageing MiG-21 fighters.

Design

The Tejas is the smallest and lightest in its class of contemporary supersonic combat aircraft. It features a tailless compound delta wing, relaxed static stability with quadruplex fly-by-wire controls, and extensive use of composite materials in the airframe. It is powered by the General Electric F404 turbofan (with the F414 planned for the Mk 2), giving a maximum speed of about Mach 1.8.

Variants and service

The Tejas Mk 1 entered Indian Air Force service with No. 45 Squadron ("Flying Daggers") in 2016. The improved Mk 1A adds an AESA radar, electronic warfare suite and beyond-visual-range missiles; a large order for 83 Mk 1A aircraft was placed in 2021, with further orders following. Trainer and naval prototypes have also been developed, and the twin-engine Tejas Mk 2 is under development as a larger successor.
