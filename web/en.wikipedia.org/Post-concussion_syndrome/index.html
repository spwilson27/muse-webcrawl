Post-concussion syndrome

Post-concussion syndrome
SpecialtyNeurology, psychiatry
SymptomsHeadache, dizziness, fatigue, irritability, difficulty concentrating
CausesConcussion / mild traumatic brain injury

Post-concussion syndrome (PCS), also known as persistent post-concussive symptoms, is a set of symptoms that may continue for weeks, months, or years after a concussion. Symptoms include headaches, dizziness, fatigue, irritability, anxiety, insomnia, loss of concentration and memory, and sensitivity to noise and light.

Causes and risk factors

PCS follows mild traumatic brain injury. The relative contributions of neurological damage and psychological factors are debated; risk factors for persistent symptoms include previous concussion, migraine history, anxiety or depression, and dizziness at the time of injury. Most people recover within days to weeks, but a minority experience symptoms for months or longer.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is clinical, based on the history of head injury and symptom pattern; neuroimaging is usually normal. Treatment focuses on managing individual symptoms, gradual return to activity, vestibular and cervical rehabilitation, cognitive behavioural therapy, and treatment of comorbid mood and sleep disorders. Early education and reassurance about expected recovery are important.
