Ministry of National Defense of the People's Republic of China

The Ministry of National Defense of the People's Republic of China (MND) is the second-ranked cabinet-level ministry in the State Council of the People's Republic of China. It is headed by the Minister of National Defense, who is usually a serving general of the People's Liberation Army (PLA).

Unlike defense ministries in most other countries, the MND does not exercise operational command over the PLA. Command authority over China's armed forces rests with the Central Military Commission (CMC) of the Chinese Communist Party and the state. The ministry's primary functions are external: it serves as the official channel for military diplomacy, communicates with foreign defense establishments, coordinates international peacekeeping and arms-control contacts, and represents China's military in official exchanges.

History

The ministry was established by a decision adopted at the First Session of the 1st National People's Congress on 28 September 1954, following the adoption of the 1954 Constitution of China. Peng Dehuai served as the first minister. Subsequent ministers have included Lin Biao, Ye Jianying, Xu Xiangqian, Geng Biao, Zhang Aiping, Qin Jiwei, Chi Haotian, Cao Gangchuan, Liang Guanglie, Chang Wanquan, Wei Fenghe, Li Shangfu, and Dong Jun.

Structure and role

The minister is customarily a member of the Central Military Commission and a state councillor, giving the post political weight above most other ministers. Beneath the minister, the ministry hosts offices dealing with foreign military exchanges, peacekeeping affairs, and spokesperson functions. The ministry's spokespersons hold regular press conferences in Beijing and are a principal public voice of the Chinese military establishment.

Because day-to-day command of the PLA belongs to the CMC and its Joint Staff Department, analysts generally describe the MND as a liaison and external-affairs organ rather than a headquarters in the Western sense.
