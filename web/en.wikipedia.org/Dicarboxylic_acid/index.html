Dicarboxylic acid

In chemistry, a dicarboxylic acid is an organic compound with the molecular formula HO2C–R–CO2H, containing two carboxyl (–COOH) functional groups. The general formula can be written HO2C–(CH2)n–CO2H for the straight-chain series.

Examples

The simplest member is oxalic acid (n = 0), followed by malonic, succinic, glutaric, and adipic acids. Adipic acid is produced on a large industrial scale as a precursor to nylon-6,6. Phthalic acid and terephthalic acid are aromatic dicarboxylic acids used in plastics and polyesters such as PET.

Properties

Dicarboxylic acids are white crystalline solids with melting points that alternate with chain length (the odd–even effect). They are more soluble in water than comparable monocarboxylic acids and can form salts and esters at both ends, making them useful monomers for polyamides and polyesters.

Occurrence

Several dicarboxylic acids occur naturally: oxalic acid in many plants, succinic acid as an intermediate of the citric acid cycle, and azelaic acid in grains.
