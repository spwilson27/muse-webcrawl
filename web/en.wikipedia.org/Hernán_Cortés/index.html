Hernán Cortés

Born: 1485, Medellín, Spain
Died: 2 December 1547, Castilleja de la Cuesta, Spain
Known for: Conquest of the Aztec Empire
Title: Marquis of the Valley of Oaxaca

Hernán Cortés de Monroy y Pizarro Altamirano, 1st Marquis of the Valley of Oaxaca (1485 – 2 December 1547), was a Spanish conquistador who led the expedition that caused the fall of the Aztec Empire and brought large portions of Mexico under the rule of the Spanish king in the early 16th century.

Conquest of Mexico

Cortés arrived in the New World in 1504 and settled in Cuba, becoming alcalde of Santiago. In 1519, defying Governor Velázquez's revocation of his orders, he sailed for the Mexican mainland with about 500 men, founded Veracruz, and famously destroyed his ships to prevent retreat. Aided by the interpreter La Malinche (Malintzin) and by alliances with peoples subjugated by the Aztecs — above all Tlaxcala — he marched on Tenochtitlan and was received by the emperor Moctezuma II, whom the Spanish took captive. Driven out in the disastrous retreat of La Noche Triste (1520), Cortés regrouped, and with tens of thousands of indigenous allies and a devastating smallpox epidemic weakening the city, he besieged and captured Tenochtitlan in August 1521.

Later career

Cortés became the first governor of New Spain, sponsored expeditions to Honduras and Baja California, and was made Marquis of the Valley of Oaxaca in 1529. Litigation, investigations, and diminishing favour at court overshadowed his later years; he returned to Spain in 1540 and died near Seville in 1547. His letters to Charles V (Cartas de relación) remain key sources for the conquest.

Legacy

Cortés is a deeply contested figure: long celebrated in Spain as a hero, he is widely viewed in Mexico as a symbol of colonial violence, while historians stress both his tactical skill and his dependence on indigenous allies and disease. The conquest he led reshaped the demographics, languages, and cultures of the Americas.
