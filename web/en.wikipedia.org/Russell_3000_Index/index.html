The Russell 3000 Index is a stock market index maintained by FTSE Russell that measures the performance of the 3,000 largest US-traded stocks by market capitalisation, representing approximately 98% of the investable US equity market. It is intended to be a comprehensive benchmark of the entire US stock market.

Structure

The index is divided into two sub-indices: the Russell 1000 Index, covering the 1,000 largest companies, and the Russell 2000 Index, covering the next 2,000 and serving as the best-known small-cap benchmark. Constituents are weighted by free-float-adjusted market capitalisation and screened for eligibility — including a minimum share price and US incorporation or headquarters rules.

Reconstitution

The Russell indices are rebuilt annually each June ("reconstitution") based on market capitalisations at a spring rank day. The event is one of the largest trading days of the year in US markets, as index funds buy newly added stocks and sell deleted ones, and arbitrageurs position ahead of the changes.

Investing

Exchange-traded funds tracking the index include the iShares Russell 3000 ETF (ticker IWV). Style sub-indices split the universe into growth and value components, and a defensive/dynamic classification is used in the Russell Stability Indexes.
