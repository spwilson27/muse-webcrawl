Medellín is the second-largest city in Colombia and the capital of the department of Antioquia. It lies in the Aburrá Valley of the central Andes at about 1,500 metres elevation, whose mild climate gives the city its nickname, the "City of the Eternal Spring". Medellín is an important centre for Colombia's textile, finance and technology industries.

History

The settlement was founded in 1616 as San Lorenzo de Aburrá and renamed Medellín, after the town in Extremadura, Spain; it became the capital of Antioquia in 1826. Coffee wealth and railways drove industrial growth in the late nineteenth and twentieth centuries. In the 1980s and early 1990s the city became notorious as the base of Pablo Escobar's Medellín Cartel and suffered extreme levels of violence; after Escobar's death in 1993 homicide rates fell dramatically, though armed groups remain a challenge in peripheral districts.

Urban innovation

Since the 2000s Medellín has won international attention for social urbanism: the Metrocable gondola lines (the first opened in 2004) link hillside neighbourhoods to the metro system, and library parks, escalators in Comuna 13 and new public spaces have targeted historically marginalized areas. The city was named the world's most innovative city by the Urban Land Institute in 2013.

Culture

Residents, known as paisas, celebrate the annual Feria de las Flores (Flower Festival) with the silleteros parade. The Museo de Antioquia and Plaza Botero display works by Medellín-born artist Fernando Botero, and the city is a noted centre for tango (Carlos Gardel died in a plane crash at its airport in 1935) and, more recently, reggaeton.
