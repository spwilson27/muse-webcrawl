Eugene Wesley Roddenberry Sr. (19 August 1921 – 24 October 1991) was an American television screenwriter and producer who created the Star Trek franchise. Inducted into the Science Fiction Hall of Fame, he was nicknamed "the Great Bird of the Galaxy" and is regarded as one of the most influential figures in science-fiction television.

Early life and career

Roddenberry was born in El Paso, Texas, and grew up in Los Angeles. He flew B-17 Flying Fortress bombers with the US Army Air Forces in the Pacific during the Second World War, surviving two plane crashes, and afterwards worked as a commercial pilot for Pan Am and as a Los Angeles police officer while learning to write for television. He wrote for series such as Highway Patrol, Have Gun – Will Travel and his own short-lived The Lieutenant.

Star Trek

In 1966 Roddenberry created Star Trek, a series depicting the voyages of the starship Enterprise and its multi-ethnic crew in a hopeful future for a united humanity. Cancelled after three seasons, it became a cultural phenomenon in syndication, spawning an animated series, a major film series beginning with Star Trek: The Motion Picture (1979), and the sequel series Star Trek: The Next Generation (1987), on which Roddenberry served as executive producer and creative consultant until his death.

Other work and legacy

Roddenberry developed several other series and pilots, and after his death the projects Earth: Final Conflict and Andromeda were produced from his notes. He was a humanist, and his optimistic vision of the future influenced generations of viewers, scientists and engineers. A portion of his ashes was flown into space aboard the space shuttle Columbia in 1992, among the first space burials.
