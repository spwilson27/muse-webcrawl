Constitution of France

The Constitution of France is the supreme law of the French Republic. The current constitution, that of the Fifth Republic, was adopted on 4 October 1958, largely at the instigation of Charles de Gaulle, and has since been amended numerous times. It replaced the Constitution of the Fourth Republic (1946) and established a semi-presidential system with a strong executive.

Constitution of France

Created: 28 September 1958 (referendum)
Presented: 1958
Ratified: 4 October 1958
System: Unitary semi-presidential constitutional republic
Government structure: Executive, legislative and judicial branches
Chambers: Bicameral (National Assembly and Senate)

History

France has had many constitutions since the Revolution of 1789, beginning with the Constitution of 1791. The instability of the Third and Fourth Republics led de Gaulle to insist on a text that strengthened the presidency. Michel Debré, the principal drafter, modelled the system partly on the Weimar and British examples but with a distinctly French rationalised parliamentarism.

Main provisions

The Constitution declares France an indivisible, secular, democratic and social republic (Article 1). National sovereignty belongs to the people (Article 3). The President of the Republic is elected for five years by direct universal suffrage, appoints the Prime Minister, may dissolve the National Assembly, and may call referendums. The Government, headed by the Prime Minister, directs the policy of the nation, while Parliament votes laws and controls the Government.

Constitutional Council

The Constitutional Council reviews the constitutionality of laws. Since the 2008 reform, the question prioritaire de constitutionnalité allows citizens to challenge the constitutionality of a law during litigation.

Amendments

The Constitution has been revised more than twenty times, notably to introduce direct election of the President (1962), reduce the presidential term to five years (2000), and add environmental protection through the Charter for the Environment (2005).
