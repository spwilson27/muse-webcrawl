APG system

SubjectClassification of flowering plants (angiosperms)
Published byAngiosperm Phylogeny Group
VersionsAPG (1998), APG II (2003), APG III (2009), APG IV (2016)

The APG system (Angiosperm Phylogeny Group system) is a modern system of plant taxonomy for classifying flowering plants. It is based largely on cladistic analyses of DNA sequences rather than on morphology alone, and it aims to define orders and families that are monophyletic — that is, consisting of an ancestor and all its descendants.

Development

An informal international group of botanists, the Angiosperm Phylogeny Group, published the first version in 1998 as a replacement for older morphology-based systems such as those of Cronquist and Takhtajan. Successive revisions appeared as APG II (2003), APG III (2009) and APG IV (2016), each incorporating new molecular evidence and adjusting the circumscription of families and orders.

Structure

APG IV recognises 64 orders and 416 families of flowering plants. Above the order level it uses informal clade names rather than formal ranks: the early-diverging ANA grade (Amborellales, Nymphaeales, Austrobaileyales), magnoliids, monocots, and eudicots, with the eudicots divided into further clades such as the superrosids and superasterids. Families whose placement is uncertain are bracketed, and alternative circumscriptions offered in APG II were largely resolved in later versions.

Adoption

The APG system has been adopted by most major herbaria, floras and botanical databases, including the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew, and is the reference classification behind Plants of the World Online.
