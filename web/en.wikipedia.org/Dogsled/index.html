Dogsled

A dogsled is a sled drawn by a team of sled dogs. Dogsleds have been used for transportation, hunting, and freight in the Arctic and sub-Arctic regions of North America, Greenland, and Siberia for centuries, and remain an important cultural symbol of the North.

Design

Traditional Inuit sleds, known as qamutiik, are built from wood lashed together with rawhide or other flexible bindings so that the structure can flex over rough ice rather than break. Racing sleds are lighter, made of ash, aluminum, or composite materials, and are designed for speed over groomed trails.

The team

Dogs are hitched in a fan formation or in pairs along a gangline. The driver, or musher, stands on the runners at the back and steers with voice commands, including "gee" (right), "haw" (left), and "whoa" (stop).
