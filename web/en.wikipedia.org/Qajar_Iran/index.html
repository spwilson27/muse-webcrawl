Qajar Iran

Qajar Iran, officially the Sublime State of Iran, was the Iranian state ruled by the Qajar dynasty from 1789 to 1925. The dynasty was founded by Agha Mohammad Khan Qajar, who reunified Iran after the civil wars that followed the fall of the Zand dynasty and made Tehran his capital. The Qajar era saw Iran caught between the expanding Russian and British empires, losing territory in the Caucasus, yet it also brought the first modernisation efforts and, in 1906, a constitution.

Period: 1789–1925

Ended by: Rise of Reza Shah Pahlavi

History

Agha Mohammad Khan crowned himself in 1796 and brutally subdued rivals, including the sack of Tbilisi. His nephew Fath-Ali Shah (r. 1797–1834) fought two disastrous wars with Russia, ceding the Caucasian territories by the treaties of Gulistan (1813) and Turkmenchay (1828). Under Naser al-Din Shah (r. 1848–1896), the longest-reigning Qajar monarch, Iran saw telegraph lines, a modern army in embryo, the reforming premier Amir Kabir, and growing foreign concessions, such as the Reuter concession and the Tobacco Régie, whose cancellation in 1892 after mass protest foreshadowed constitutional politics.

Constitutional revolution and fall

Protests in 1905–06 forced Mozaffar ad-Din Shah to grant a constitution and an elected Majlis (parliament), making Iran a constitutional monarchy. Civil war, Anglo-Russian rivalry, and the 1907 convention dividing Iran into spheres of influence weakened the state; during the First World War Iran was occupied despite its neutrality. In 1921 Reza Khan seized power in a coup, and in 1925 the Majlis deposed the last Qajar shah, Ahmad Shah, ending the dynasty and founding the Pahlavi era.

Culture

Qajar art is known for its distinctive portrait painting, tilework, and the palaces of Tehran such as the Golestan Palace. The era also saw the introduction of photography, printing, and new educational institutions.
