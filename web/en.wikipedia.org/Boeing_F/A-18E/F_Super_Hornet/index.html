Boeing F/A-18E/F Super Hornet

The Boeing F/A-18E and F/A-18F Super Hornet are a series of American supersonic twin-engine, carrier-capable, multirole fighter aircraft derived from the McDonnell Douglas F/A-18 Hornet. The single-seat E model and two-seat F model are larger and more capable evolutions of the original Hornet, with about 25% more wing area, larger engines, and greater fuel capacity.

Development

The Super Hornet program began in the early 1990s as the U.S. Navy sought a replacement for aging strike aircraft, including the A-6 Intruder and later the F-14 Tomcat. Designed by McDonnell Douglas and produced after the Boeing merger, the aircraft first flew in November 1995 and entered fleet service in 1999, achieving initial operating capability in 2001.

Design

The Super Hornet is powered by two General Electric F414 turbofans and incorporates enlarged leading-edge extensions, an active electronically scanned array (AESA) radar in later production blocks, eleven weapon stations, and measures to reduce radar signature. The two-seat F model and the derived EA-18G Growler electronic-warfare aircraft share the airframe.

Service

The Super Hornet forms the backbone of U.S. Navy carrier air wings and has flown combat missions in Iraq, Afghanistan, Syria, and elsewhere. Export customers include Australia, which operates the F/A-18F and EA-18G, and Kuwait. Successive Block II and Block III upgrades have added conformal fuel tanks, advanced displays, and improved networking.
