The General Electric F414 is an American afterburning turbofan engine in the 22,000-pound (98 kN) thrust class produced by GE Aerospace. The F414 originated from GE's widely used F404 engine, enlarged to provide roughly 35 percent more thrust for the Boeing F/A-18E/F Super Hornet.

Development

GE developed the F414 from the F404-GE-402, the engine of the legacy F/A-18 Hornet. A larger fan, new materials, and a redesigned core allowed the engine to meet the Super Hornet's increased weight and thrust requirements while retaining much of the F404's architecture and maintainability.

Applications

Two F414-GE-400 engines power every F/A-18E/F Super Hornet and EA-18G Growler. Variants also power Sweden's Saab JAS 39 Gripen E/F models, South Korea's KF-21 Boramae, and India's HAL Tejas Mark 2, making the F414 one of the most widely used fighter engines of its generation.

Specifications (F414-GE-400)

The engine produces about 13,000 lbf of dry thrust and 22,000 lbf with afterburner. It is a two-spool axial-flow turbofan with a three-stage fan and seven-stage high-pressure compressor, with an overall pressure ratio around 30:1.
