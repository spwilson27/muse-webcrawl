The Boeing EA-18G Growler is an American carrier-based electronic warfare aircraft, a specialized version of the two-seat F/A-18F Super Hornet. The EA-18G replaced the Northrop Grumman EA-6B Prowlers in service with the United States Navy. The Growler's electronic warfare capability is primarily provided by Northrop Grumman.

Development

The Growler was developed as a replacement for the aging EA-6B Prowler, which had served as the U.S. Navy's principal electronic attack aircraft since the Vietnam War era. Using the Super Hornet airframe gave the new aircraft commonality with the fleet's strike fighters, simplifying logistics and allowing the Growler to be escorted by, or itself fly alongside, strike packages at comparable speeds.

Design

The EA-18G retains roughly 90 percent commonality with the F/A-18F. Its internal 20 mm cannon is removed and replaced with electronic warfare equipment, while wingtip pods housing ALQ-218 receivers and ALQ-99 jamming pods provide its principal electronic attack capability. Two crew members — a pilot and an electronic countermeasures officer — operate the systems.

Operational history

The Growler entered operational service in 2009 and saw its first combat deployment in support of Operation Odyssey Dawn over Libya in 2011. It has since been exported to Australia, the only foreign operator, and remains the cornerstone of U.S. airborne electronic attack.

Specifications

The aircraft is powered by two General Electric F414 turbofan engines, has a maximum speed of Mach 1.8, and a combat radius of roughly 390 nautical miles on electronic warfare missions.
