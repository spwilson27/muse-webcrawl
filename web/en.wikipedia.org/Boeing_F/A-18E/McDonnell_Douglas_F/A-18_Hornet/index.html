The McDonnell Douglas F/A-18 Hornet is an all-weather supersonic, twin-engine, carrier-capable, multirole combat aircraft, designed as both a fighter and attack aircraft (hence the F/A designation). Designed by McDonnell Douglas and Northrop, the F/A-18 was derived from the latter's YF-17 lightweight fighter prototype that was part of the Lightweight Fighter Program.

Design and development

The Hornet was conceived to complement the larger F-14 Tomcat aboard U.S. Navy carriers and to replace the A-7 Corsair II in the light attack role. Its twin General Electric F404 engines — the predecessors of the F414 — large leading-edge extensions, and digital fly-by-wire controls gave it excellent high angle-of-attack performance and maintainability.

Operational history

The F/A-18 first saw combat in 1986 strikes against Libya and was used extensively in the Gulf War, Iraq War, and numerous other conflicts. It served with the U.S. Navy until 2019 in front-line roles and continues with the U.S. Marine Corps and air forces of Canada, Spain, Switzerland, Finland, Malaysia, Kuwait, and Australia (which retired its legacy Hornets in 2021).

Variants and legacy

The design was enlarged and extensively redesigned into the F/A-18E/F Super Hornet, which entered service in 1999 and became the basis for the EA-18G Growler electronic warfare aircraft. The Hornet also flew for over three decades as the aircraft of the U.S. Navy's Blue Angels flight demonstration squadron.
