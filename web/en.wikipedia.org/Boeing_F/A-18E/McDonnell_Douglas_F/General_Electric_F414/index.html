The General Electric F414 is an American afterburning turbofan engine in the 22,000-pound (98 kN) thrust class produced by GE Aerospace. The F414 originated from GE's widely used F404 engine, which powers the F/A-18 Hornet, and was developed to power the larger F/A-18E/F Super Hornet.

Compared with the F404, the F414 delivers roughly 35 percent more thrust, with a larger fan, redesigned compressor, and full-authority digital engine control (FADEC). Variants include the F414-GE-400 used in the Super Hornet and Growler, the F414G for Sweden's Gripen E/F, and the F414-INS6 selected for India's Tejas Mk2 and AMCA programs.

Enhanced engine

GE has demonstrated an enhanced F414 variant offering up to 18–20 percent more thrust through advanced materials and cooling in the core, and increased durability versions intended to reduce life-cycle costs for existing operators.
