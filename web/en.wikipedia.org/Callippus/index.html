Callippus

Bornc. 370 BC, Cyzicus
Diedc. 300 BC
Known forCallippic cycle; homocentric spheres

Callippus of Cyzicus (Greek: Κάλλιππος; c. 370 BC – c. 300 BC) was a Greek astronomer and mathematician. Born at Cyzicus on the Sea of Marmara, he studied under Eudoxus of Cnidus at the Academy of Plato in Athens and also worked with Aristotle at the Lyceum.

Astronomy

Callippus refined Eudoxus's model of homocentric spheres to better explain the motions of the planets, adding spheres to a total of 34: he added two spheres each for the Sun and Moon and one each for Mercury, Venus, and Mars to the 27 of Eudoxus. His observations of the unequal lengths of the seasons, made from the Hellespont, improved the parameters used in Greek astronomy.

Callippic cycle

Callippus is best known for the Callippic cycle, proposed about 330 BC: a period of 76 years equal to 940 lunar months and 27,759 days, refining the 19-year Metonic cycle by dropping one day from four Metonic cycles. The cycle began at the summer solstice of 330 BC and was used by later astronomers including Hipparchus and Ptolemy for dating observations, and in the Antikythera mechanism.

Legacy

The lunar crater Callippus is named in his honour. His calendar work fed into the long tradition of Greek and Hellenistic calendrical astronomy.
