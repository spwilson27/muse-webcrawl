Vexillology

Vexillology is the study of the history, symbolism and usage of flags or, by extension, any interest in flags in general. The word is a synthesis of the Latin vexillum (a kind of small square flag used by Roman cavalry) and the Greek suffix -logia ("study"). A person who studies flags is a vexillologist; one who designs flags is a vexillographer; and the art of designing flags is called vexillography.

History

Although the study of flags is much older, vexillology as a formal discipline was pioneered in the 1960s by the American scholar Whitney Smith, who founded the Flag Research Center and coined the term in 1957. The International Federation of Vexillological Associations (FIAV) coordinates national associations and holds biennial international congresses. Flags are described using a technical vocabulary (canton, hoist, fly, fess, pale, bend) and represented in heraldic blazon.

Design principles

Vexillologists popularised guidelines for good flag design — codified in the North American Vexillological Association's booklet Good Flag, Bad Flag: keep it simple, use meaningful symbolism, use two or three basic colours, avoid lettering and seals, and be distinctive. Flag types include national, civil, state, military, maritime (ensigns and jacks) and signal flags.
