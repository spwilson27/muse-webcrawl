Federal Aviation Administration

The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) is the largest transportation agency of the U.S. government and regulates all aspects of civil aviation in the country as well as over surrounding international waters. Its powers include air traffic control, certification of personnel and aircraft, setting standards for airports, and protection of U.S. assets during the launch or re-entry of commercial space vehicles.

FormedAugust 23, 1958

Parent agencyU.S. Department of Transportation
EmployeesAbout 45,000

History

The FAA was created by the Federal Aviation Act of 1958 as the Federal Aviation Agency, following a series of mid-air collisions, and became part of the Department of Transportation in 1967. It took over air traffic control from the Civil Aeronautics Administration and progressively absorbed safety rulemaking functions.

Responsibilities

The FAA issues pilot and mechanic certificates, certifies aircraft designs and production, operates the national air traffic control system, licences commercial space launch sites, and conducts safety oversight of airlines. Its administrator serves a five-year term, appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate.
