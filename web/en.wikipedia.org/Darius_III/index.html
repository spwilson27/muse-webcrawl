Darius III (Old Persian: Dārayavaʰuš; c. 380 – 330 BC), originally named Artashata and called Codomannus by the Greeks, was the last king of the Achaemenid Empire of Persia, reigning from 336 BC until his death in 330 BC. His reign ended with the conquest of Persia by Alexander the Great.

Accession

Darius was a distant member of the royal house who rose as satrap of Armenia. After the eunuch Bagoas poisoned Artaxerxes III and his son Arses, Darius was placed on the throne in 336 BC. When Bagoas attempted to poison him as well, Darius forced the eunuch to drink the poison himself.

War with Alexander

Alexander invaded Asia in 334 BC. After the Macedonian victory at the Granicus, Darius took personal command and confronted Alexander at the Battle of Issus (333 BC), where the confined terrain nullified his numerical advantage; Darius fled, leaving his family to be captured. Despite generous peace offers, Alexander pressed on, and at the Battle of Gaugamela (331 BC) Darius's great army was decisively defeated and he fled again, to Ecbatana and then eastward.

Death

In 330 BC Darius was arrested and deposed by his kinsman Bessus, satrap of Bactria, who proclaimed himself king. As Alexander's cavalry closed in near Hecatompylos, Bessus's men stabbed Darius and abandoned him in a wagon, where Alexander found him dying. Alexander gave him a royal burial in the necropolis at Persepolis and later punished Bessus.

Assessment

Greek sources portray Darius as a weak and cowardly figure, but modern historians note that he inherited a fractious empire, repeatedly raised new armies after defeats, and faced one of the most formidable commanders in history.
