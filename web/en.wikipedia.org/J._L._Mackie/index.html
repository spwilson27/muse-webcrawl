John Leslie Mackie (25 August 1917 – 12 December 1981) was an Australian philosopher, known for his contributions to metaethics, philosophy of religion, and metaphysics. He taught at universities in Australia, New Zealand, and England, finishing his career as a fellow of University College, Oxford.

Moral philosophy

In Ethics: Inventing Right and Wrong (1977), Mackie defended moral scepticism in the form of an "error theory": ordinary moral judgements purport to refer to objective values, but no such values exist, so those judgements are systematically false — morality is something humans invent rather than discover. His "argument from queerness" held that objectively prescriptive values would be metaphysically and epistemologically unlike anything else in the universe.

Other work

Mackie also wrote influentially on causation (The Cement of the Universe, 1974), where he developed the notion of an INUS condition; on Hume; and on the problem of evil, arguing in The Miracle of Theism (1982, posthumous) against the existence of God. He is also associated with an early formulation of responses to the paradox of omnipotence.
