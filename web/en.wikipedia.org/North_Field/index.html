North Field

The North Field (Arabic: حقل الشمال) is an offshore natural gas field in the Persian Gulf, operated by Qatar. It is the world's largest single natural gas field, and is shared with Iran, where the corresponding portion is called South Pars.

Geology and reserves

Discovered in 1971 by Shell, the field covers about 9,700 square kilometres (6,000 in Qatari waters) northeast of the Qatar Peninsula. Its recoverable reserves are estimated at around 900 trillion cubic feet, making up the bulk of Qatar's roughly 25 trillion cubic metres of proven gas — the third-largest national reserves in the world.

Development

Production began in 1991 for domestic use; from the late 1990s the field underpinned Qatar's rise to become one of the world's leading exporters of liquefied natural gas (LNG) through Qatargas and RasGas (now QatarEnergy LNG). After a self-imposed moratorium from 2005, Qatar launched the North Field East and North Field South expansion projects, intended to raise LNG capacity from 77 million tonnes per year to about 142 million tonnes by 2030.
