Hymenaea protera is an extinct prehistoric leguminous tree in the family Fabaceae, described from fossil material preserved in Dominican amber. The species was formally described by George Poinar Jr. in 1991. It is considered the source plant of Dominican amber and possibly also of Mexican (Chiapas) amber, both of which are fossilised resins produced by this tree roughly 15–20 million years ago during the Miocene.

Description

The species is known from flowers, leaves, and other plant fragments trapped in amber, as well as from the chemistry of the resin itself, which closely matches that of the living East African and Neotropical species Hymenaea verrucosa and Hymenaea courbaril. H. protera grew in a tropical moist-forest environment on the island of Hispaniola.

Fossil insects and DNA claims

Amber from H. protera preserves an exceptional fossil ecosystem, including insects, spiders, and plant debris. In the 1990s, researchers reported extracting ancient DNA, including DNA attributed to the extinct stingless bee Proplebeia dominicana preserved in Dominican amber; such claims of DNA surviving tens of millions of years have since been widely questioned and attributed to contamination. The species also features in discussions of Jurassic Park-style de-extinction scenarios, since dinosaur-era ambers derive from different source plants.
