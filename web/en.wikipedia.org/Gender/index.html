Gender

Gender includes the social, psychological, cultural and behavioural aspects of being a person. It is typically associated with socially constructed norms, behaviours and roles linked to being a woman, man, girl or boy, as well as with identities that do not fit within the female–male binary. In many social sciences and in everyday usage since the mid-20th century, gender is distinguished from sex, which refers to biological attributes such as chromosomes, hormones and reproductive anatomy; although how the two relate is a matter of continuing scholarly and public debate.

Concepts

Gender identity is a person's internal sense of their own gender; gender expression covers the external presentation (clothing, voice, mannerisms); gender roles are the behaviours and expectations a society associates with a given gender. A person whose gender identity aligns with their sex assigned at birth is described as cisgender; one whose does not is transgender. Some cultures recognise specific third-gender categories, such as the hijra of South Asia, fa'afafine of Samoa, or Two-Spirit people in some Indigenous North American communities. Non-binary identities fall outside the woman/man dichotomy.

Formation and debate

Scholars disagree over the relative contributions of biology and socialisation. Feminist theorists such as Simone de Beauvoir, and later Judith Butler — who argued gender is "performative", produced through repeated acts — emphasised its constructed character, while psychologists and biologists point to prenatal hormonal influences and cross-cultural consistencies. Money and Ehrhardt's mid-20th-century work popularised the modern sense of "gender role".

Gender and society

Gender structures labour markets (the gender pay gap), political representation, family life and health. The World Health Organization and the United Nations study gender norms as determinants of well-being, and "gender mainstreaming" has been adopted as a policy strategy by many institutions. Attitudes toward gender diversity, and its legal recognition, vary widely by country and over time.
