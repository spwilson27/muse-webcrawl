The Battle of the Aegates (also called the Battle of the Aegusa Islands) was a naval battle fought on 10 March 241 BC off the Aegates Islands, west of Sicily. A Roman fleet under the consul Gaius Lutatius Catulus destroyed the Carthaginian relief fleet of Hanno, bringing the First Punic War to an end after 23 years of fighting.

Background

By 242 BC the war in Sicily had become a stalemate centred on the Carthaginian strongholds of Lilybaeum and Drepanum. Bankrupt and having lost several fleets to storms, Rome made one final effort: wealthy citizens funded by subscription a new fleet of some 200 quinqueremes, modelled on a captured Carthaginian vessel, whose crews Catulus trained rigorously through the winter.

The battle

Carthage sent Hanno with a fleet laden with supplies to relieve Lilybaeum and embark the general Hamilcar Barca's troops for a decisive engagement. Catulus intercepted first, choosing to fight despite rough seas. The Carthaginian ships, heavily loaded, undermanned and crewed by raw levies, were overwhelmed by the drilled Roman crews: about 50 Carthaginian ships were sunk and some 70 captured, with around 10,000 prisoners taken, according to Polybius.

Aftermath

With its fleet destroyed and no money to build another, Carthage authorised Hamilcar to negotiate peace. By the Treaty of Lutatius, Carthage evacuated Sicily, paid a large indemnity, and surrendered the islands between Sicily and Italy. Sicily became Rome's first overseas province, marking the beginning of Roman expansion beyond the Italian peninsula.
