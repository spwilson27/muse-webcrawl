Durham Report

The Durham Report, formally the Report on the Affairs of British North America, was an influential document submitted in 1839 by John George Lambton, 1st Earl of Durham, who had been sent to British North America as Governor General following the Rebellions of 1837.

Background

After armed rebellions broke out in both Upper Canada (Ontario) and Lower Canada (Quebec), Durham was dispatched to investigate the causes of the unrest and to recommend reforms. He arrived in Canada in 1838 and spent several months touring the colonies before returning to Britain to write his report.

Findings

Durham famously described Lower Canada as "a struggle, not of principles, but of races," and blamed the conflict on tensions between the French-Canadian majority and the English-speaking minority. In Upper Canada he criticized the oligarchic Family Compact that dominated government.

Recommendations

The report made two principal recommendations: the union of Upper and Lower Canada into a single province, and the introduction of responsible government, under which the executive council would be accountable to the elected assembly. The union was implemented by the Act of Union 1840, creating the Province of Canada; responsible government followed later in the decade.

Legacy

The report is widely regarded as one of the foundational documents of the modern Canadian state and a landmark in the development of self-government within the British Empire, though its call for the assimilation of French Canadians remains controversial.
