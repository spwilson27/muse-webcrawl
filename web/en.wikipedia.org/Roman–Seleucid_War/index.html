Roman–Seleucid War

The Roman–Seleucid War (192–188 BC), also known as the Aetolian War or Syrian War, was a military conflict fought between the Roman Republic and its allies on one side, and the Seleucid Empire under Antiochus III the Great and the Aetolian League on the other.

Following the Roman victory over Macedon in the Second Macedonian War, the Aetolian League, dissatisfied with the settlement, invited Antiochus III to liberate Greece from Roman influence. Antiochus landed in Greece in 192 BC with a small force, but the Romans and their allies defeated him decisively at the Battle of Thermopylae in 191 BC and drove him back to Asia.

Course of the war

Roman forces under Lucius Cornelius Scipio, advised by his brother Scipio Africanus, crossed into Asia Minor in 190 BC. Antiochus was defeated at sea at Myonessus and finally in the war's decisive land battle, the Battle of Magnesia (December 190 BC or January 189 BC), where the large but unwieldy Seleucid army was routed by the Roman-led coalition.

Treaty of Apamea

The Treaty of Apamea (188 BC) imposed harsh terms on the Seleucids: Antiochus renounced all claims to territory west of the Taurus Mountains, surrendered his war elephants and most of his fleet, and agreed to pay a war indemnity of 15,000 talents over twelve years. Pergamum and Rhodes were rewarded with Seleucid territory. The settlement established Roman predominance in the eastern Mediterranean.
