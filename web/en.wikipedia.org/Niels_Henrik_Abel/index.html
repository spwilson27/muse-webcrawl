Niels Henrik Abel

Niels Henrik Abel (5 August 1802 – 6 April 1829) was a Norwegian mathematician who made pioneering contributions to a variety of fields despite dying of tuberculosis at age 26. His name survives in numerous terms: abelian groups, abelian functions, Abel's theorem, and the Abel Prize, established by Norway in 2002 as a counterpart to the Nobel Prize in mathematics.

Work

Abel proved the impossibility of solving the general quintic equation in radicals (the Abel–Ruffini theorem). On a study trip to Berlin and Paris in 1825–27 he developed the theory of elliptic functions in parallel with Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi, and presented a celebrated memoir to the Paris Academy that was mislaid and published only posthumously. In poverty and ill health, he returned to Norway; a letter offering him a professorship in Berlin arrived two days after his death.

Legacy

The mathematician Charles Hermite remarked that Abel left mathematicians "enough to keep them busy for five hundred years." His collected works and tragic early death made him a national hero of Norway.
