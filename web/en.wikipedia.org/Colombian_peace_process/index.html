Colombian peace process

The Colombian peace process refers to negotiations between the Government of Colombia and the Revolutionary Armed Forces of Colombia (FARC) that culminated in the Final Agreement signed in 2016, ending over fifty years of armed conflict.

Negotiations

Talks began secretly in 2011 and publicly in Havana, Cuba, in 2012, with Cuba and Norway as guarantors. The agenda covered rural development, political participation, illicit drugs, victims' rights and disarmament. A first accord was narrowly rejected in a plebiscite on 2 October 2016; a revised agreement was signed on 24 November and ratified by Congress.

Implementation

Some 13,000 FARC combatants demobilised under UN verification, and the movement became a political party. Implementation of rural reform and security guarantees has been slow, and dissident factions and other armed groups remain active. President Juan Manuel Santos received the 2016 Nobel Peace Prize for his efforts.
