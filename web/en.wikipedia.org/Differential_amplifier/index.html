Differential amplifier

A differential amplifier is a type of electronic amplifier that amplifies the difference between two input voltages while suppressing any voltage common to the two inputs. It is the fundamental building block of the operational amplifier and of most analogue integrated circuits.

Operation

The classic circuit, the long-tailed pair, uses two transistors sharing a constant-current tail. Equal (common-mode) input changes cancel, while differential inputs produce proportional output. Performance is described by differential gain and the common-mode rejection ratio (CMRR), which good designs push to 100 dB or more.

Applications

Differential pairs appear in op-amps, comparators, instrumentation amplifiers, ECL logic, and balanced audio and data transmission, where rejecting interference picked up equally on both lines is essential.
