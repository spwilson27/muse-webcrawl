Canada Reads is an annual "battle of the books" competition organized by the Canadian Broadcasting Corporation (CBC), first broadcast in 2002. Five celebrity panellists each champion a Canadian book, debate its merits over several days of radio and television broadcasts, and vote one book off each day until a single winner remains — promoted as the book that all of Canada should read that year.

Format and impact

The shortlist is announced in the winter, often organised around a theme such as "one book to connect us all" or stories of resilience. The debates air on CBC Radio One and CBC Television, with recent editions hosted by comedian and writer Ali Hassan. Winning titles characteristically receive a large boost in sales and print runs, and libraries and book clubs across the country read along. Early winners included Michael Ondaatje's In the Skin of a Lion (2002), Hubert Aquin's Next Episode, and Rohinton Mistry's A Fine Balance; later winners have included The Orenda by Joseph Boyden, Broken Glass by Alain Mabanckou in translation, and Five Little Indians by Michelle Good.

Related editions

A French-language counterpart, Le Combat des livres, has aired on Radio-Canada. School and regional variants, and the CBC's related reading initiatives, have made the programme one of the most prominent vehicles for promoting Canadian literature.
