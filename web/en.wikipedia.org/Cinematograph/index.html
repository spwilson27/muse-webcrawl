Cinematograph

A cinematograph is a motion picture film camera and projector combined in a single device. The best-known example, the Cinématographe, was patented by the Lumière brothers, Auguste and Louis Lumière, in 1895 and used for the first public screening of projected motion pictures to a paying audience, in Paris on 28 December 1895.

Design

The Cinématographe was a hand-cranked machine that could record, develop and project film. It used 35 mm film with a single round perforation on each side of each frame, transported by a claw mechanism adapted from the sewing machine. Running at about 16 frames per second, it was much lighter than Thomas Edison's Kinetograph, and because the same machine served as camera and projector, the Lumières' operators could travel the world filming and showing their short actuality films.

Impact

The Lumière programme of ten short films, including Workers Leaving the Lumière Factory and The Arrival of a Train at La Ciotat, is traditionally taken as the birth of cinema as a public medium. The word "cinematograph" gave many languages their word for the cinema itself.
