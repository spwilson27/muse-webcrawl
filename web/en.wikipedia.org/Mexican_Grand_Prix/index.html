The Mexican Grand Prix (Spanish: Gran Premio de México) is a motor racing event that has been held intermittently since 1962 and forms part of the Formula One World Championship. It is staged at the Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez in Mexico City, a high-altitude circuit situated roughly 2,285 metres (7,500 feet) above sea level, which presents unusual challenges for engine performance, cooling and aerodynamics because of the thin air.

History

The first Mexican Grand Prix was run in 1962 as a non-championship race, overshadowed by the death of local hero Ricardo Rodríguez in practice. The event became a championship round from 1963 until 1970, when crowd control problems — including spectators spilling onto the track at the final running — led to its removal from the calendar. The race returned between 1986 and 1992 at the renamed Hermanos Rodríguez circuit, before another long absence.

After a 23-year gap, the Mexican Grand Prix returned to the Formula One calendar in 2015 following an extensive renovation of the circuit by Hermann Tilke. The redesigned track retained the famous Peraltada corner in modified form and added a stadium section through the Foro Sol baseball stadium, notable for its podium ceremony atmosphere. From 2021 the event was officially renamed the Mexico City Grand Prix, though the Mexican Grand Prix name remains in common usage and was restored in later seasons.

Characteristics

The altitude of Mexico City reduces air density by roughly a quarter compared with sea level, cutting aerodynamic downforce and drag and forcing teams to run maximum wing angles while still recording some of the highest top speeds of the season. Turbocharged engines and brakes are also harder to cool.
