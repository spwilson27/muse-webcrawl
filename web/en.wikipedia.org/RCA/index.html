RCA

RCA Corporation
Founded: 1919 (as Radio Corporation of America)
Defunct: 1986 (broken up)
Headquarters: New York City
Known for: NBC, colour television, RCA Records

RCA, originally the Radio Corporation of America, was a major American electronics and communications company founded in 1919 and a dominant force in radio, television, recorded music and broadcasting for much of the twentieth century. Under its long-serving president David Sarnoff, it created the NBC broadcasting network, pioneered compatible colour television in the United States, and operated the record label RCA Records.

History

RCA was organised in 1919 at the urging of the United States Navy, taking over the American Marconi wireless business to keep radio technology under American control, with General Electric and later Westinghouse and AT&T as owners. It became independent in 1932 following antitrust action. RCA demonstrated electronic television at the 1939 New York World's Fair, and its colour system was adopted as the NTSC standard in 1953.

Decline and breakup

From the 1960s RCA diversified unsuccessfully into computers, car rental and frozen foods, and lost ground in consumer electronics to Japanese competitors. General Electric reacquired RCA in 1986 and broke it up: NBC remained with GE, the RCA consumer electronics business passed to Thomson, and RCA Records to Bertelsmann. The RCA name survives as a licensed trademark on consumer products.
