Constantine X Doukas (Greek: Κωνσταντῖνος Δούκας, 1006 – 23 May 1067) was Byzantine emperor from 1059 to 1067. A leading member of the civil aristocracy and the Doukas family, he succeeded Isaac I Komnenos, who abdicated after illness and designated Constantine as his successor.

Reign

Constantine reversed Isaac's military retrenchment and economising. He reduced military expenditure and disbanded parts of the army — measures blamed by later historians, including his contemporary Michael Psellos's successors and modern scholars, for weakening the empire's defences at a critical moment. His reign saw mounting pressure on all frontiers: the Seljuk Turks raided Armenia and Anatolia (Ani fell in 1064), the Pechenegs and Ouzes crossed the Danube in 1064–65, and the Normans under Robert Guiscard continued conquering Byzantine southern Italy.

Constantine favoured the civil bureaucracy and senatorial class, raised taxes to replenish the treasury, and survived a conspiracy in 1061. He was noted for his interest in law and philosophy and his friendship with Michael Psellos.

Death and succession

Constantine died in May 1067. On his deathbed he extracted an oath from his wife Eudokia that she would not remarry and would rule as regent for their sons Michael, Andronikos and Konstantios. Eudokia soon married the general Romanos Diogenes (Romanos IV), a decision with fateful consequences culminating in the Battle of Manzikert (1071).
