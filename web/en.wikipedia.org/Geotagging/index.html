Geotagging

Geotagging, also called geotagging or geocoding in some contexts, is the process of adding geographical identification metadata to media such as photographs, videos, websites, text messages, QR codes and RSS feeds. The metadata usually consists of latitude and longitude coordinates, and may also include altitude, bearing, distance, accuracy data and place names.

Methods

Geotags are added either automatically or manually. GPS-equipped cameras and smartphones record position automatically at the moment of capture; dedicated GPS loggers record a track that can later be synchronised with photo timestamps in software such as GeoSetter or Adobe Lightroom. Where no GPS data exist, users can geotag manually by placing media on a map, a feature offered by photo-sharing services such as Flickr and Panoramio.

Formats and standards

For images, GPS coordinates are stored in the file's Exif metadata. GeoRSS extends web feeds with location, the KML format encodes geographic annotation for map applications, and microformats such as the geo microformat mark up coordinates in web pages. Social media platforms apply the term more loosely to location labels attached to posts, such as place check-ins.

Uses

Geotagging enables searching and browsing media by location, mapping photo collections, finding images taken near a given point, and documenting field research, journalism and citizen science. Location-based games and services, including geocaching, depend on geotagged data.

Privacy and safety concerns

Because geotags can reveal home addresses, routines and the locations of children or of sensitive wildlife, they raise privacy and safety issues. Photographs of military personnel and equipment have inadvertently disclosed positions, and images of endangered species posted publicly have been used by poachers. Many social platforms strip location metadata from uploaded images, and security guidance commonly advises disabling automatic geotagging.
