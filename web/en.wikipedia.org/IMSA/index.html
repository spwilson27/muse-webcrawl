IMSA

The International Motor Sports Association (IMSA) is a North American sports car racing sanctioning body based in Daytona Beach, Florida. It was founded by John Bishop, a former SCCA executive, and his wife Peggy in 1969 with assistance from NASCAR founder Bill France Sr. IMSA is the sanctioning body of the IMSA SportsCar Championship, the premier sports car racing series in North America, and is owned by NASCAR Holdings. It is also the U.S. representative and recognized sanctioning body affiliated with the Automobile Competition Committee for the United States (ACCUS) and the FIA.

History

IMSA's first race was held at Pocono Raceway in October 1969. Through the 1970s and 1980s its Camel GT Series became a leading showcase for sports car racing, and the GTP prototypes of the 1980s — from manufacturers such as Porsche, Jaguar, Nissan and Toyota — are regarded as a golden era. After ownership changes and competition from rival series in the 1990s, Bishop's organisation evolved into Professional Sports Car Racing and later the American Le Mans Series, while a separate IMSA-branded entity continued under different management.

Reunification and modern era

In 2012, NASCAR Holdings acquired the American Le Mans Series and merged it with the rival Grand-Am Rolex Sports Car Series to form the United SportsCar Championship, launched in 2014 and renamed the IMSA SportsCar Championship in 2016, reviving the IMSA name. The series' crown-jewel events are the 24 Hours of Daytona (Rolex 24), the 12 Hours of Sebring, the Six Hours of The Glen, and Petit Le Mans at Road Atlanta.

Classes and series

The SportsCar Championship features multi-class racing: GTP hybrid prototypes (built to LMDh regulations, introduced 2023), LMP2 prototypes, and GTD Pro and GTD classes for GT3-based production cars. IMSA also sanctions the Michelin Pilot Challenge, VP Racing SportsCar Challenge, and single-make series for Ferrari, Lamborghini, Mazda MX-5 Cup, Ford Mustang Challenge and Porsche Carrera Cup North America.
