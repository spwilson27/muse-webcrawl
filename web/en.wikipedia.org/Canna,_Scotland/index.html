Canna (Scottish Gaelic: Canaigh, Eilean Chanaigh) is the westernmost of the Small Isles archipelago in the Scottish Inner Hebrides. It is linked to the neighbouring island of Sanday by a road bridge and by sandbanks at low tide. The island is about 7 kilometres long and 1.5 kilometres wide, and is known for its basalt columns, seabird colonies, and rich Gaelic cultural heritage.

History

Canna shows evidence of settlement from the Mesolithic and Neolithic periods, and its early Christian carved stones suggest an early monastic site linked to Iona. The island passed through the hands of clan chiefs and later proprietors, including the MacNeils of Barra and the Clanranald family. In the 19th and 20th centuries the island was owned by the Thom family, and then by the Gaelic scholar John Lorne Campbell, who, together with his wife Margaret Fay Shaw, documented Gaelic folklore and song. Campbell gifted the island to the National Trust for Scotland in 1981.

Wildlife and culture

Canna is noted for its colonies of puffins, razorbills, and Manx shearwaters (the latter shared with neighbouring Rùm), and for white-tailed eagles reintroduced to the region. Canna House preserves the Campbell archive of Gaelic song and folklore, one of the most important collections of its kind. A regular ferry from Mallaig serves the island, which has no public road network beyond its few tracks and relies substantially on renewable energy.
