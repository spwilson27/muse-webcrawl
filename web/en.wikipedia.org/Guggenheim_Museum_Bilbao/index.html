The Guggenheim Museum Bilbao is a museum of modern and contemporary art in Bilbao, Spain, designed by Canadian-American architect Frank Gehry. Opened in 1997 along the Nervión river on a former industrial waterfront, its sweeping titanium-clad forms made it an icon of deconstructivist architecture and one of the most admired buildings of the late 20th century.

Design and construction

Gehry's design was developed with CATIA aerospace software, allowing its random-seeming curves to be built economically. The building is clad in glass, limestone and some 33,000 thin titanium sheets that shimmer with changing light. Inside, a soaring atrium connects nineteen galleries, including a vast column-free hall 130 metres long housing Richard Serra's The Matter of Time.

The "Bilbao effect"

The museum drew over a million visitors in its first years and generated economic returns far exceeding its construction cost, revitalising the declining industrial city. The phenomenon of a landmark cultural building regenerating a city became known worldwide as the "Bilbao effect", and many cities have since attempted to replicate it. Outside stands Jeff Koons's floral sculpture Puppy.
