Gérard Genette (1930–2018)

Gérard Genette (7 June 1930 – 11 May 2018) was a French literary theorist, associated in particular with the literary movement known as structuralism and with the creation of the modern discipline of narratology. He is the author of the influential five-volume Figures (1966–1972), of Narrative Discourse: An Essay in Method (1972), and of works that founded the study of the "paratext" and of transtextuality.

Life and career

Born in Paris, Genette studied at the École Normale Supérieure and taught at the Sorbonne before becoming director of studies at the École Pratique des Hautes Études. He was a founding figure, with Roland Barthes and others, of the journal Poétique (founded 1970) and of the structuralist poetics movement. In his youth he was briefly a member of the French Communist Party and contributed to the group Socialisme ou Barbarie.

Narratology

In Narrative Discourse, drawing chiefly on Marcel Proust's In Search of Lost Time, Genette systematised the analysis of narrative through categories that became standard: order (anachronies such as analepsis and prolepsis), duration (summary, scene, ellipsis, pause), frequency, mood (distance and focalization—zero, internal, external), and voice (narrating instance, homo- and heterodiegetic narrators). His distinction between story, narrative, and narrating remains foundational to narrative theory.

Paratext and transtextuality

In Palimpsests (1982) Genette mapped "transtextuality"—intertextuality, paratextuality, metatextuality, hypertextuality, and architextuality. In Paratexts: Thresholds of Interpretation (1987) he analysed the elements that surround a text—titles, prefaces, notes, interviews, covers—as a "threshold" mediating between text and reader.
