Animal agriculture

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Animal agriculture is the branch of agriculture concerned with the breeding, raising and care of farm animals — livestock — for products such as meat, milk, eggs, wool, leather and other materials. It includes pastoralism, mixed farming and intensive livestock production, and employs a large share of the world's agricultural land.

Sectors

Major sectors include cattle (beef and dairy), poultry, pigs, sheep, goats and aquaculture. Global meat production has risen steadily with population growth and rising incomes, exceeding 350 million tonnes per year in the early 2020s. Most production in industrialised countries now takes place in intensive systems, sometimes called factory farms.

Environmental impact

Livestock farming is a significant source of greenhouse gas emissions — the FAO has estimated the sector's share at roughly 14.5% of human-caused emissions — as well as a driver of land use, deforestation, water consumption and nutrient pollution. Ruminants produce methane through enteric fermentation. Animal agriculture is also a leading subject of ethical debate concerning animal welfare, and of public-health concern regarding antibiotic resistance and zoonotic disease.

Alternatives

Responses include improved feed and manure management, precision livestock farming, plant-based diets, and novel foods such as cultivated meat and insect protein.
