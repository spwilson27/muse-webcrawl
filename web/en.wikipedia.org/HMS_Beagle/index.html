HMS Beagle was a Cherokee-class 10-gun brig-sloop of the Royal Navy, launched in 1820. She is famous as the ship that, on her second survey voyage (1831–1836), carried the young naturalist Charles Darwin around the world — the voyage that shaped his theory of evolution by natural selection.

Voyages

Her first voyage (1826–1830), under Pringle Stokes and then Robert FitzRoy, surveyed the coasts of southern South America in company with HMS Adventure. The second voyage, again commanded by FitzRoy, left Plymouth in December 1831 and charted the South American coast, crossing the Pacific to Tahiti, New Zealand and Australia, and visiting the Galápagos Islands, where Darwin's observations of finches and tortoises later proved significant. A third voyage (1837–1843) surveyed much of the Australian coast under John Clements Wickham and John Lort Stokes; the port of Darwin in the Northern Territory is named for the naturalist.

Later service

From 1845 the Beagle served as a static coastguard watch vessel in Essex, moored in the River Roach. She was sold in 1870 and broken up. In 2020, radar survey work identified remains consistent with her dock at Paglesham.
