Convex combination

In convex geometry and vector algebra, a convex combination is a linear combination of points (which can be vectors, scalars, or more generally points in an affine space) where all coefficients are non-negative and sum to 1. Formally, given points x₁, …, xₙ and coefficients α₁, …, αₙ ≥ 0 with α₁ + ⋯ + αₙ = 1, the convex combination is α₁x₁ + ⋯ + αₙxₙ.

Geometry

A convex combination of two points lies on the line segment between them; of three points, anywhere in the triangle they span; and in general, within the convex hull of the set of points. A set is convex precisely when it contains every convex combination of its points. Points expressible as convex combinations with all coefficients strictly positive lie in the relative interior of the convex hull.

Applications

Convex combinations appear throughout mathematics and its applications: as weighted averages and expected values in probability (a probability distribution over outcomes yields a convex combination), in the barycentric coordinates used in geometry and computer graphics, and in game theory, where a mixed strategy is a convex combination of pure strategies.
