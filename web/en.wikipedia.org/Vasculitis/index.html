Vasculitis

Vasculitis is a group of disorders characterised by inflammation of blood vessels. Inflammation damages vessel walls, which may weaken them (risking aneurysm and bleeding) or narrow them (reducing blood flow and causing tissue ischaemia and organ damage). Vasculitides can affect vessels of any size and almost any organ system.

Classification

The Chapel Hill Consensus Conference nomenclature classifies vasculitides mainly by vessel size. Large-vessel vasculitis includes giant cell arteritis and Takayasu arteritis; medium-vessel vasculitis includes polyarteritis nodosa and Kawasaki disease; small-vessel vasculitis includes the ANCA-associated vasculitides (granulomatosis with polyangiitis, microscopic polyangiitis, eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis) and immune-complex diseases such as IgA vasculitis (Henoch–Schönlein purpura).

Signs and symptoms

General features include fever, weight loss, fatigue and raised inflammatory markers. Specific features depend on the organs involved: rashes and palpable purpura in the skin, glomerulonephritis in the kidneys, nerve damage (mononeuritis multiplex), lung haemorrhage, or jaw claudication and visual loss in giant cell arteritis.

Treatment

Most vasculitides are autoimmune in nature, and treatment usually involves immunosuppression with glucocorticoids combined with agents such as cyclophosphamide, methotrexate or rituximab. Some cases are secondary to infection, drugs or other diseases and are treated by addressing the underlying cause.
