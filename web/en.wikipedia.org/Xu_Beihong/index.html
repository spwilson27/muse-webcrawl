Xu Beihong (Chinese: 徐悲鴻; Wade–Giles: Hsü Pei-hung; 19 July 1895 – 26 September 1953) was a Chinese painter, born in Yixing, Jiangsu province. He was primarily known for his Chinese ink paintings of horses and birds and was one of the first Chinese artists to articulate the need for artistic expressions that reflected a modern China at the beginning of the 20th century. He was also regarded as one of the first Chinese artists to pioneer large-scale oil paintings reflecting historical themes.

Life and training

Xu learned classical Chinese calligraphy and painting from his father, Xu Dazhang, a portrait painter, from the age of six. After early study in Shanghai, he travelled to Japan in 1917 and then to France in 1919 on a scholarship, studying at the Académie Julian and the École Nationale Supérieure des Beaux-Arts in Paris, and touring Europe to study the Old Masters. Returning to China in 1927, he taught at National Central University in Nanjing and later in Beiping (Beijing). During the Second Sino-Japanese War he held fundraising exhibitions of his work in Southeast Asia and India to support Chinese war relief.

Work and influence

Xu argued that Chinese painting should be reformed by incorporating Western techniques — accurate anatomy, perspective, and sketching from life — into the ink tradition. His large oil paintings on historical and literary subjects, such as Tian Heng and His Five Hundred Retainers (1928–30) and The Foolish Old Man Who Removed the Mountains (1940), embodied this programme. His most beloved works are his ink horses, galloping with sweeping, calligraphic brushstrokes informed by anatomical study; the galloping horse became a national symbol of vigour and resilience.

After 1949 Xu became the first president of the Central Academy of Fine Arts in Beijing and chairman of the China Artists Association, positions that made his realist pedagogy — drawing from life as the foundation of all art — the orthodox basis of art education in the People's Republic. He died of a stroke in 1953. His Beijing home is preserved as the Xu Beihong Memorial Museum, which houses a major collection of his works.
