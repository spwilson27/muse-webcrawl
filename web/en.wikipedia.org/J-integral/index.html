J-integral

The J-integral is a quantity in fracture mechanics that characterises the energy release rate and the intensity of stress and strain fields near the tip of a crack in a nonlinear elastic or elastic-plastic material. It was introduced by James R. Rice in 1968.

Definition

The J-integral is defined as a path-independent contour integral around the crack tip, involving the strain energy density and the traction and displacement gradients along the contour. For linear elastic materials it equals the energy release rate G, and it is related to the stress intensity factor K. For a crack in mode I, J = K²/E′ under linear elastic conditions, where E′ depends on plane stress or plane strain.

Applications

Because it remains valid when substantial plastic deformation occurs at the crack tip, the J-integral underlies elastic-plastic fracture mechanics. The critical value JIc, measured in standardised tests, serves as a fracture toughness parameter for ductile materials, and J is used in failure assessment of structures containing cracks.
