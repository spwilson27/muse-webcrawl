Afghanistan is a linguistically diverse nation. The two official languages are Dari (Afghan Persian) and Pashto, both Indo-European languages of the Iranian branch, and bilingualism is common. More than 40 languages are spoken in the country, reflecting its position at the crossroads of Central, South, and West Asia.

Major languages

Dari is the first language of roughly half the population and serves as the country's main lingua franca in government, media, and interethnic communication; it is the native tongue of Tajiks, Hazaras (who speak the Hazaragi dialect), and Aimaks in the north, west, and centre. Pashto, spoken natively by the Pashtun plurality (around 35–40% of the population), predominates in the south and east and across the border in Pakistan.

In the north, Turkic languages are widespread: Uzbek (the third most spoken language, around 10%) and Turkmen. Other languages include Balochi in the far south, Pashayi and the Nuristani languages in the mountainous east, and small pockets of Kyrgyz in the Wakhan Corridor, as well as Brahui, Hindi/Urdu, and Arabic.

Language policy

The 2004 constitution names Pashto and Dari as official languages and allows Uzbek, Turkmen, Balochi, Pashayi, Nuristani, and Pamiri languages official status in regions where they predominate. Education and publishing in minority languages have been constrained by war and limited resources, and both the pre-2021 republic and the Taliban authorities have been accused by critics of favouring one official language over the other in schools and administration.

Writing systems

Dari and Pashto are written in modified Perso-Arabic scripts; Uzbek in Afghanistan traditionally uses the Arabic script as well, unlike the Latin and Cyrillic scripts used in Uzbekistan. Literacy rates remain low by global standards, estimated at roughly a third of adults.
