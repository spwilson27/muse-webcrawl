Shor's algorithm

Shor's algorithm is a quantum algorithm for finding the prime factors of an integer, developed by mathematician Peter Shor in 1994. It runs in polynomial time on a quantum computer, whereas the best-known classical factoring algorithms run in super-polynomial time — an exponential speedup that threatens widely used public-key cryptography such as RSA.

Description

The algorithm reduces factoring to the problem of finding the period of a modular exponential function, which a quantum computer solves efficiently using the quantum Fourier transform. Given the period, the factors are recovered with elementary number theory and the Euclidean algorithm.

Significance

Because RSA and related cryptosystems rely on the practical difficulty of factoring large numbers, a sufficiently large, error-corrected quantum computer running Shor's algorithm could break them. This prospect has driven the development of post-quantum cryptography.

Implementations

Small demonstrations have factored numbers such as 15 and 21 on experimental quantum hardware, but scaling to cryptographically relevant sizes remains a major engineering challenge.
