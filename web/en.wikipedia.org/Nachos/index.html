Nachos are a Tex-Mex dish consisting of tortilla chips or totopos covered with cheese or a cheese sauce, often served as a snack or appetizer. More elaborate versions add other ingredients such as refried beans, guacamole, salsa, sour cream, olives, jalapeño peppers and seasoned meat, and may be served as a main dish. Nachos were created in 1940 by Ignacio "Nacho" Anaya in Piedras Negras, Coahuila, Mexico.

History

According to the popular account, in 1940 Anaya, the maître d'hôtel at the Victory Club in Piedras Negras, was asked to serve a snack to a group of wives of American soldiers stationed at nearby Fort Duncan, Texas, after the kitchen had closed. He improvised by melting grated Wisconsin cheddar cheese over freshly made totopos and topping them with slices of pickled jalapeño. He named the dish nachos especiales after his own nickname. The dish spread across the border into Texas and the American Southwest; a recipe appeared under his name in a 1949 cookbook, and the dish was popularised internationally in the 1970s when Frank Liberto began selling nachos with a processed-cheese sauce at sporting events in Arlington, Texas.

Variations

Common variants include "loaded" nachos with beans, meat and vegetables; barbecue nachos with pulled pork (a Memphis specialty); and dessert nachos with cinnamon sugar, chocolate and fruit. The International Day of the Nacho is celebrated on 21 October; Piedras Negras hosts an annual Nacho Festival with the world's largest plate of nachos.
