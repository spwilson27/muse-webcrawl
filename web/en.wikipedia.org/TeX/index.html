TeX

TeX (pronounced "tekh"; the name derives from the Greek τέχνη, "art/craft", and is written with a lowered E) is a typesetting system created by computer scientist Donald Knuth of Stanford University. Knuth began work in 1977, dissatisfied with the poor typesetting of the second edition of his book The Art of Computer Programming, and released the first version in 1978; the system reached its essentially final form in 1982 and has been famously stable since.

Features

TeX excels at typesetting mathematics and complex documents. It treats a paragraph as a whole and chooses line breaks to optimise the entire paragraph, using the sophisticated hyphenation and justification algorithms developed by Knuth with his students. Together with the companion font-design system METAFONT and the Computer Modern fonts, TeX produces output of high typographic quality. It is a macro-based, Turing-complete typesetting language.

Versioning and stability

TeX's version numbers famously converge to π: after version 3 they proceed 3.1, 3.14, 3.141, and so on; at Knuth's death the version is to become exactly π and no further changes made. Knuth offered monetary rewards for bugs found in his software, with cheques becoming prized collectors' items.

Related systems

Most users meet TeX through macro packages such as LaTeX (created by Leslie Lamport), which provides document structure, and ConTeXt. Modern engines including pdfTeX, XeTeX, and LuaTeX extend TeX with PDF output and Unicode fonts. TeX remains the standard for publishing in mathematics, physics, and computer science.
