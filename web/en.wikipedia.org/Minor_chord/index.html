Minor chord

In music theory, a minor chord is a chord that has a root, a minor third, and a perfect fifth. When a chord consists of these three notes alone it is called a minor triad. For example, the A minor triad consists of the notes A, C and E.

Structure

A minor triad can be understood as a stack of a minor third (three semitones) below a major third (four semitones), spanning a perfect fifth overall. In just intonation its frequency ratios approximate 10:12:15. In chord notation a minor chord is often indicated by a lowercase "m" or "min" after the root, as in Am.

Character and use

Minor chords are commonly described by listeners as sounding sad, dark or serious relative to major chords, an association deeply embedded in Western musical culture (though perception is partly learned). The minor triad is the tonic chord of minor scales and appears throughout harmony as ii, iii and vi chords in major keys. Extended minor chords, such as minor sevenths and minor ninths, are staples of jazz, soul and popular music.
