Summability

In mathematics, summability concerns the assignment of sums to series, including series that diverge in the ordinary sense. A summation method or summability method is a function from a set of sequences or series to values, extending the ordinary notion of sum while ideally agreeing with it whenever the series converges.

Classical methods

The best-known method is Cesàro summation, which takes the limit of the arithmetic means of the partial sums; a series that converges is Cesàro summable to the same value, and some divergent series, such as Grandi's series 1 − 1 + 1 − 1 + ⋯, are Cesàro summable to 1/2. Abel summation uses the limit of a related power series as its variable approaches 1.

Properties

A summation method is called regular if it sums every convergent series to its ordinary limit, stable if dropping finitely many terms shifts the sum accordingly, and linear if it respects addition and scalar multiplication. Silverman–Toeplitz-type theorems characterise the matrix methods with these properties.

Applications

Summability theory is used in Fourier analysis, the theory of divergent series, and in physics through regularisation techniques such as zeta-function regularisation.
