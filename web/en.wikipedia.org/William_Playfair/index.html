William Playfair

William Playfair (22 September 1759 – 11 February 1823) was a Scottish engineer and political economist best known for having invented several types of diagrams: the line chart, bar chart and pie chart, as well as popularising the use of statistical graphics.

Life

Born near Dundee, Playfair was apprenticed as a millwright and draughtsman, worked for Boulton and Watt in Birmingham, and later lived in London and Paris, where he witnessed the storming of the Bastille in 1789. His varied career included manufacturing, banking schemes, pamphleteering and some time in debtors' prison.

The Statistical Atlas and Elements of Geography

In The Commercial and Political Atlas (1786) he presented economic time series as engraved line and bar charts — the first major work of data visualisation in book form. His Statistical Breviary (1801) introduced the pie chart and the circle diagram.

Influence

Playfair is considered the founder of statistical graphics. His charts of British trade, national debt and prices demonstrated that quantities over time and proportions of a whole could be grasped far more readily in pictures than in tables, an insight foundational to modern information design.
