The Church of Scotland (Scots: The Scots Kirk; Scottish Gaelic: Eaglais na h-Alba) is the national church in Scotland, founded in the Scottish Reformation of 1560. It is Presbyterian in government and Reformed in theology, and is informally known by its Scots language title, "the Kirk".

History

The church was founded in 1560 when Scotland broke with the papacy under the leadership of John Knox and adopted a Reformed confession of faith. Its position was contested through the seventeenth century, alternating between Presbyterian and Episcopal government, until Presbyterianism was finally secured after the Glorious Revolution of 1688.

The Disruption of 1843, a schism over patronage and state interference, led about a third of ministers to form the Free Church of Scotland. Most later reunions culminated in the 1929 union, and the Church of Scotland Act 1921 had already recognised the church's spiritual independence.

Government

The church has no bishops; it is governed by a hierarchy of courts: kirk sessions at parish level, presbyteries regionally, and the General Assembly nationally, which meets annually in Edinburgh and is presided over by a Moderator serving a one-year term. The monarch is represented by a Lord High Commissioner but is not head of the church.

Doctrine and membership

The church's subordinate standard is the Westminster Confession of Faith (1647). Women have been ordained as ministers since 1969. Membership has declined markedly since the mid-twentieth century, though the Kirk retains a central role in Scottish public life, education historically, and social care through CrossReach.
