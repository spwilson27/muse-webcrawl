Bullying

Bullying
DefinitionRepeated aggressive behaviour involving an imbalance of power, intended to hurt another person physically, mentally or emotionally
FormsPhysical, verbal, relational (social), cyberbullying
SettingsSchools, workplaces, online, prisons, homes

Bullying is the use of force, coercion, hurtful teasing or threats to abuse, aggressively dominate or intimidate another person. The behaviour is typically repeated and habitual, and involves a real or perceived imbalance of physical or social power. Researcher Dan Olweus is widely credited with defining bullying through three criteria: hostile intent, an imbalance of power, and repetition over time.

Types

Physical bullying includes hitting, kicking, pushing and damaging property. Verbal bullying includes name-calling, insults and threats. Relational or social bullying harms reputation or relationships through exclusion, gossip and public embarrassment. Cyberbullying takes place over digital devices and social media, and can occur at any hour, reach wide audiences, and leave a permanent record.

Effects

Victims of bullying are at increased risk of anxiety, depression, low self-esteem, sleep and health problems, reduced academic achievement and, in severe cases, self-harm and suicide. Those who bully also face higher risks of later antisocial behaviour, substance abuse and criminality. Bystanders can experience guilt and anxiety as well.

Prevention

Effective prevention programmes, such as the Olweus Bullying Prevention Program developed in Norway, work at the level of the whole school community: clear rules, consistent adult responses, classroom discussion, and support for both victims and perpetrators. Many jurisdictions have enacted anti-bullying legislation and require schools to maintain formal policies. Workplace bullying is addressed through organisational policies, grievance procedures and, in some countries, specific law.
