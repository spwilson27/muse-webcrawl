The Magi Chapel is a chapel in the Palazzo Medici Riccardi of Florence, Italy, famous for the cycle of frescoes The Journey of the Magi painted by the Renaissance artist Benozzo Gozzoli around 1459–1461. The frescoes are considered among the masterpieces of early Renaissance secular painting and a celebration of the Medici family's wealth, piety, and political prestige.

The frescoes

The chapel, designed by Michelozzo as the family's private oratory, is covered on three walls with the cavalcade of the Three Kings winding through an idealized Tuscan landscape of hills, castles, hunting scenes, and flowering plants. Each king's retinue occupies one wall: the youngest king, Caspar, on the east wall; the middle king, Melchior, on the south; and the eldest, Balthasar, on the west. The procession is generally read as evoking the Council of Florence of 1439 and the splendid public processions staged by the Florentine Compagnia dei Magi, to which the Medici belonged.

Portraits

Gozzoli filled the cavalcade with recognizable contemporaries. Caspar was long identified with the young Lorenzo de' Medici, and the train includes portraits of Piero and Giovanni de' Medici, the Byzantine emperor John VIII Palaiologos, and Patriarch Joseph II of Constantinople; Gozzoli included his own self-portrait, his name inscribed in Greek letters on his red cap. The altarpiece, Filippo Lippi's Adoration in the Forest, of which the original is now in Berlin, completed the program.

Later history

The palace passed to the Riccardi family, who enlarged it in the seventeenth century; the chapel survived with its decoration substantially intact and remains the centerpiece of the museum of the Palazzo Medici Riccardi.
