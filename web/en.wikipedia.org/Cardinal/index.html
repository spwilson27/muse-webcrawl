Cardinal

Cardinal is a word with many meanings, from the Latin cardinalis, "principal, pivotal", itself from cardo, "hinge" — that on which something turns. This article is a summary of the principal senses; full treatment is given in the linked articles.

[Image: The northern cardinal, the bright red songbird named after the colour of a Catholic cardinal's vestments]

Religion

In the Roman Catholic Church, a cardinal is a senior church official appointed by the Pope, ranking immediately below him. Cardinals form the College of Cardinals, whose chief function is to elect a new pope in conclave. They are distinguished by their scarlet vestments, the colour symbolising willingness to shed blood for the faith. Historically powerful cardinal-ministers include Richelieu and Mazarin in 17th-century France.

Mathematics and language

In mathematics, cardinal numbers measure the size of sets, including infinite sets, following the work of Georg Cantor. In linguistics and everyday usage, cardinal numbers are the counting numbers (one, two, three…), as opposed to ordinal numbers (first, second, third…). Grammar also recognises the four cardinal directions — north, south, east and west — and in Catholic moral teaching the four cardinal virtues: prudence, justice, fortitude and temperance.

Nature

The northern cardinal (Cardinalis cardinalis) is a crested North American songbird whose male is brilliant crimson; it takes its name from the vestments of the churchmen and is the state bird of seven US states. The cardinalfish family and cardinal tetra are named for the same colour.
