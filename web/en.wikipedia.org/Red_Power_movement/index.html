Red Power movement

The Red Power movement was a social and political movement led by Native American youth in the United States in the late 1960s and 1970s that demanded self-determination for Native peoples, the honoring of treaty rights, and an end to the federal policies of termination and forced assimilation. The phrase "Red Power" echoed the era's "Black Power" slogan and signalled a more confrontational, pan-Indian activism than that of older tribal leadership.

Background and major events

The movement emerged in reaction to the termination policy of the 1950s, under which the federal government sought to end its recognition of tribes and relocate Native people to cities, and drew energy from the wider civil rights era. The National Indian Youth Council was founded in 1961, and the American Indian Movement (AIM) was founded in Minneapolis in 1968, initially to address police brutality and urban Indian poverty.

Signature actions included the occupation of Alcatraz Island (1969–1971) by the group Indians of All Tribes, who claimed the island under the Treaty of Fort Laramie; the Trail of Broken Treaties caravan and occupation of the Bureau of Indian Affairs headquarters in Washington in 1972; and the 71-day occupation of Wounded Knee, South Dakota, in 1973 by AIM members and Oglala Lakota residents of the Pine Ridge Reservation, which ended after a siege by federal forces.

Legacy

The movement shifted federal policy toward tribal self-determination, embodied in the Indian Self-Determination and Education Assistance Act of 1975, and influenced later legislation on religious freedom, child welfare, and gaming, as well as subsequent generations of Indigenous activism.
