Rhône wine

Rhône wine is produced in the Rhône Valley of south-eastern France, along the Rhône river between Vienne in the north and Avignon in the south. The region is divided into two distinct sub-regions with different traditions: the Northern Rhône, where Syrah dominates the red wines, and the Southern Rhône, where blends based on Grenache predominate and which includes the famous appellation of Châteauneuf-du-Pape. The Rhône is France's second-largest wine-producing region by volume under appellation contrôlée, after Bordeaux.

Northern Rhône

The northern Rhône stretches about 60 km from Vienne to Valence. Its red wines are made from Syrah, sometimes co-fermented with a small proportion of white grapes. Appellations include Côte-Rôtie, Condrieu (white, from Viognier), Saint-Joseph, Hermitage and Crozes-Hermitage, Cornas, and Saint-Péray. Hermitage, grown on a single granite hill above Tain-l'Hermitage, is among the most celebrated and long-lived red wines of France.

Southern Rhône

The southern Rhône, around Orange and Avignon, has a more Mediterranean climate and produces mainly red blends of Grenache, Syrah, Mourvèdre and other varieties. The generic appellation is Côtes du Rhône, with Côtes du Rhône Villages ranking above it, and a number of village-level crus including Châteauneuf-du-Pape — whose vineyards, covered in large rounded stones (galets roulés), were historically associated with the papal court at Avignon — Gigondas, Vacqueyras, Lirac and the rosé appellation of Tavel. The area also produces white wines and the fortified vins doux naturels of Muscat de Beaumes-de-Venise and Rasteau.

History

Viticulture in the valley dates to Roman times, and wine production expanded under the Romans along the river trade route. In the 14th century the papal court at Avignon promoted the wines of Châteauneuf-du-Pape; in 1936 the area became one of the first to receive AOC status, and Baron Pierre Le Roy's rules for Châteauneuf were a model for the French appellation system.

"Rhône Rangers"

The term "Rhône Rangers" was coined in the 1980s for a group of Californian winemakers who championed Rhône grape varieties and blends in the United States; the name has since been used by producers of Rhône-style wines in Australia, South Africa and elsewhere.
