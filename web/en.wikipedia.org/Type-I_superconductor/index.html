Type-I superconductor

Type-I superconductor
Behaviour: complete Meissner effect up to a single critical field Hₓ
Examples: lead, mercury, tin (pure elements)
Ginzburg–Landau parameter: κ < 1/√2

Type-I superconductors are superconductors that exhibit a single, sharp transition to the normal state when an applied magnetic field exceeds a critical value. Below that field they show the complete Meissner effect, expelling magnetic flux from their interior.

Characteristics

They are generally pure elemental metals with relatively low critical fields and temperatures, which limits practical applications. In the Ginzburg–Landau classification they have κ < 1/√2, meaning the surface energy between normal and superconducting regions is positive, so the sample stays in a single domain state (apart from geometric intermediate-state patterns in non-ellipsoidal samples).

Contrast with type-II

Type-II superconductors, by contrast, admit partial flux penetration in the form of vortices between lower and upper critical fields, reaching much higher usable fields.
