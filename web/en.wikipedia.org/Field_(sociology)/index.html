Field (sociology)

In sociology, a field is a social arena in which agents and organisations compete for capital and position, following the usage developed by Pierre Bourdieu. Fields — such as the artistic, academic, economic, or political field — are relatively autonomous microcosms with their own rules, stakes, and forms of authority.

Bourdieu's theory

Bourdieu defined a field as a structured social space with its own rules of functioning and its own institutions, in which agents struggle according to their habitus and their endowment of economic, cultural, social, and symbolic capital. Positions within a field are relational: each agent's standing depends on its relation to all others.

Field and habitus

The concept is inseparable from habitus, the durable dispositions agents acquire through socialisation, and from doxa, the unquestioned beliefs taken for granted within a field. Struggles within a field concern both material and symbolic stakes, including the power to define what counts as legitimate within it.
