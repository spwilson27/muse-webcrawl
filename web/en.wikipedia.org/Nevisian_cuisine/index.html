Nevisian cuisine

The cuisine of Nevis, the smaller of the two islands comprising the Caribbean federation of Saint Kitts and Nevis, reflects the island's West African, British, and Creole heritage, built on fresh seafood, tropical produce and locally raised livestock. Nevisian cooking closely resembles that of neighbouring Leeward Islands but preserves distinctive dishes of its own.

Signature dishes

The best-known Nevisian dish is goat water, a rich, spicy stew of goat meat cooked with breadfruit, green pawpaw (papaya), dumplings, tomatoes, onions and hot peppers; despite its name it is thick rather than watery, and is traditionally served at gatherings and celebrations. Other staples include saltfish with dumplings, fungi (a cornmeal and okra dish similar to cou-cou), stewed conch, curried goat, rice and peas, and johnny cakes. Seafood — lobster, whelks, conch and reef fish — is central, as are ground provisions such as yams, sweet potatoes, tannia and breadfruit.

Ingredients and drinks

Island markets supply mangoes, soursop, guava, coconut, plantains and the famously sweet Nevis mango varieties. Meals are washed down with fresh juices such as sorrel, tamarind and passionfruit, ginger beer, or rum-based drinks; the island is known for the "Killer Bee" rum punch served at a famous beach bar on Pinney's Beach. Seasonal food festivals and the annual Nevis Mango and Food Festival celebrate the island's produce and chefs.
