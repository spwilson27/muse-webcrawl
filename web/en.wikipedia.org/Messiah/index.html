Messiah

In Abrahamic religions, a messiah or messias is a saviour or liberator of a group of people. The concept of messianism originated in Judaism, where an mashiach ("anointed one") is a king or high priest traditionally anointed with holy anointing oil.

In Judaism

In Jewish eschatology, the Messiah is a future Jewish king from the Davidic line, expected to rule the Jewish people during the Messianic Age and in the world to come. He is considered to be the future redeemer who will accomplish the ingathering of the exiles and the rebuilding of the Temple in Jerusalem.

In Christianity

Christians identify Jesus of Nazareth as the Messiah (the Christ), believing that his crucifixion and resurrection fulfilled the messianic prophecies of the Hebrew Bible and that he will return in a Second Coming to complete them.

In Islam

In Islam, Isa ibn Maryam (Jesus, son of Mary) is recognised as al-Masih, the Messiah, who is held to have been a prophet and to have performed miracles, and who will return before the Day of Judgement.
