Alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase complex

Reaction: α-ketoglutarate + NAD+ + CoA → succinyl-CoA + CO2 + NADH
Location: Mitochondrial matrix
Pathway: Citric acid cycle
Subunits: E1, E2, E3

The alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase complex (also called the oxoglutarate dehydrogenase complex) is a multi-enzyme complex in the mitochondrial matrix that catalyses a key step of the citric acid cycle: the oxidative decarboxylation of α-ketoglutarate to succinyl-CoA, generating NADH and releasing carbon dioxide. The reaction is essentially irreversible and is one of the cycle's principal control points.

Structure and mechanism

Like the related pyruvate dehydrogenase complex, it consists of multiple copies of three enzymes: E1 (α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase, requiring thiamine pyrophosphate), E2 (dihydrolipoyl succinyltransferase, bearing a swinging lipoamide arm), and E3 (dihydrolipoyl dehydrogenase, an FAD-containing enzyme shared with other complexes). The complex channels intermediates between active sites without releasing them into solution.

Regulation and disease

The complex is activated by calcium and ADP and inhibited by its products NADH and succinyl-CoA, linking its activity to the cell's energy state. Reduced activity has been reported in several neurodegenerative conditions, and inherited deficiencies cause metabolic acidosis and neurological symptoms.
