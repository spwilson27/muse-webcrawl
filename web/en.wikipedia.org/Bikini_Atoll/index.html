Bikini Atoll is a coral atoll in the Marshall Islands consisting of 23 islands surrounding a 594 km² central lagoon. It was the site of 23 nuclear weapons tests conducted by the United States between 1946 and 1958. The atoll, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2010, gave its name to the bikini swimsuit, introduced days after the first postwar test in 1946.

Nuclear testing

The islanders, led by Chief Juda, agreed to relocate in 1946, initially to Rongerik Atoll, and were later moved to Kili Island. Operation Crossroads opened the test series in July 1946 with two detonations. The largest test, Castle Bravo in 1954 — at 15 megatons the biggest US explosion — spread fallout over inhabited atolls including Rongelap, whose people were evacuated days later.

Aftermath

Resettlement attempts in the 1970s were reversed when radiation levels in local food proved unsafe. The Bikini islanders pursued claims through the Nuclear Claims Tribunal, and a trust fund supports the displaced community. Parts of the atoll remain restricted, although the lagoon — containing the wrecks of target ships sunk in 1946 — is a celebrated scuba diving destination.

Heritage

UNESCO inscribed Bikini Atoll Nuclear Test Site on the World Heritage List as testimony to the dawn of the nuclear age. The sunken fleet, including the carrier Saratoga, and the Bravo crater are among its most significant features.
