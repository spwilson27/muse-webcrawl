Gigantism

Gigantism results from excess growth hormone before the growth plates close.

Gigantism
SpecialtyEndocrinology
CauseExcess growth hormone, usually from a pituitary adenoma, in childhood
Related conditionAcromegaly (onset after growth-plate fusion)

Gigantism is a condition characterised by excessive growth and height significantly above average, caused by overproduction of growth hormone (GH) in childhood, before the fusion of the epiphyseal (growth) plates. In most cases the cause is a benign tumour (adenoma) of the pituitary gland.

Signs and symptoms

Children with gigantism grow unusually fast and tall, with proportional enlargement of bones and soft tissues. Without treatment, excessive height may exceed 2.4 m, accompanied by headaches, visual-field defects from pressure on the optic chiasm, delayed puberty, and later cardiovascular and metabolic complications.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis relies on elevated levels of GH and insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1), failure of GH suppression after a glucose load, and MRI imaging of the pituitary. Treatment is usually transsphenoidal surgery to remove the adenoma; medications such as somatostatin analogues or the GH-receptor antagonist pegvisomant, and occasionally radiotherapy, are used when surgery is insufficient.

Notable cases

Robert Wadlow (1918–1940), the tallest person in recorded history at 2.72 m (8 ft 11 in), had pituitary gigantism. Historically, many exceptionally tall individuals exhibited the condition before effective treatment existed.
