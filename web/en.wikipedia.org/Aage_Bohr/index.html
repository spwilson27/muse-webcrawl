Aage Niels Bohr (19 June 1922 – 8 September 2009) was a Danish nuclear physicist who shared the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1975 with Ben Mottelson and James Rainwater "for the discovery of the connection between collective motion and particle motion in atomic nuclei and the development of the theory of the structure of the atomic nucleus based on this connection". He was the fourth son of the physicist Niels Bohr, making them one of the few father–son pairs both to win Nobel Prizes.

Early life and education

Born in Copenhagen, Aage Bohr grew up surrounded by the physicists who frequented his father's Institute for Theoretical Physics (now the Niels Bohr Institute). He studied physics at the University of Copenhagen, interrupting his studies during the German occupation of Denmark: in 1943 the Bohr family escaped to Sweden and then Britain, where the young Aage accompanied his father as assistant and secretary, including on visits connected with the Manhattan Project. He completed his master's degree in 1946 and his doctorate in 1954.

Work on nuclear structure

Experiments in the late 1940s showed that nuclei could not be fully described by the shell model alone. Building on James Rainwater's suggestion that nucleons could cooperate to deform the nucleus, Aage Bohr developed — in close collaboration with Ben Mottelson — the collective model, which unified single-particle and collective (rotational and vibrational) motion of nuclei. The resulting Bohr–Mottelson model successfully explained a wide range of nuclear spectra and became foundational in nuclear structure physics. Their two-volume monograph Nuclear Structure (1969, 1975) remains a standard reference.

Career

Bohr became professor at the University of Copenhagen in 1956 and later directed the Niels Bohr Institute (1970–1975) and the Nordic Institute for Theoretical Atomic Physics (Nordita). He died in Copenhagen on 8 September 2009.
