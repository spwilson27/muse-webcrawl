Saunders Mac Lane (1909–2005) was an American mathematician who, with Samuel Eilenberg, founded category theory, one of the most unifying frameworks in modern mathematics. He spent most of his career at the University of Chicago.

Saunders Mac Lane, co-founder of category theory

Career

Mac Lane studied at Yale and the University of Chicago, then completed his doctorate in Göttingen in 1934 under Hermann Weyl and Paul Bernays, returning to the United States as Nazi purges emptied German universities. During the Second World War he worked on applied mathematics for the war effort.

Category theory

In a 1945 paper, "General Theory of Natural Equivalences", Eilenberg and Mac Lane introduced categories, functors and natural transformations, motivated by problems in algebraic topology. Mac Lane's textbook Categories for the Working Mathematician (1971) became the standard reference. He also co-authored, with Garrett Birkhoff, the influential textbooks A Survey of Modern Algebra (1941) and Algebra.
