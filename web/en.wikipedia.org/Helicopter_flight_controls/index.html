Helicopter flight controls

Main controlsCyclic, collective, anti-torque pedals, throttle
ActuationSwashplate (main rotor); pitch links (tail rotor)

Helicopter flight controls are the cockpit controls with which a pilot commands the rotor system of a helicopter. A conventional helicopter has four primary controls: the cyclic stick, the collective lever, the anti-torque pedals and the throttle.

The controls

The cyclic, usually a stick between the pilot's knees, tilts the main rotor disc in the direction the stick is moved, by varying blade pitch cyclically through the swashplate; this pitches and rolls the aircraft and so controls its direction of travel. The collective, a lever on the pilot's left, raises and lowers all blade pitches together ("collectively"), increasing or decreasing total lift, and a twist-grip throttle on its end controls engine power — in most modern helicopters a governor or FADEC system keeps rotor speed automatically constant. The anti-torque pedals change the pitch of the tail rotor blades, varying tail rotor thrust to control yaw and to counter the torque of the main rotor; helicopters with NOTAR, fenestron or coaxial configurations achieve the same end differently.

Characteristics

Because helicopter controls interact strongly — raising the collective also affects torque and rotor speed, for example — flying a helicopter requires continuous coordination of all four controls. Control forces are generally assisted by hydraulic servos in larger machines, and stability augmentation or autopilot systems are common.
