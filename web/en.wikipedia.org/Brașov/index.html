Brașov

Brașov (German: Kronstadt; Hungarian: Brassó) is a city in Transylvania, Romania, and the administrative centre of Brașov County. With a population of roughly 280,000, it is one of Romania's largest cities, located in the Carpathian Mountains about 166 km north of Bucharest.

History

Founded by the Teutonic Knights in 1211 and settled by Transylvanian Saxons, Brașov became a fortified trading centre between the Ottoman Empire and Western Europe. In 1541 Johannes Honterus established one of the first printing presses in Transylvania here; the city was also home to the first Romanian school.

Landmarks

The Black Church (Biserica Neagră), a Gothic hall church begun in 1383, is the city's symbol. Other sights include Council Square, Catherine's Gate, the narrow Rope Street, and Mount Tâmpa overlooking the old town. Nearby stands Bran Castle, popularly associated with the Dracula legend.

Tourism and economy

Brașov is a gateway to the ski resort of Poiana Brașov and to the Prahova Valley. Its economy includes automotive components, manufacturing, and a growing IT sector.
