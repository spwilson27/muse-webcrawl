The AK-47, officially known as the Avtomat Kalashnikova (Russian: Автома́т Кала́шникова, lit. 'Kalashnikov's automatic [rifle]'), is a gas-operated assault rifle chambered for the 7.62×39mm cartridge. Developed in the Soviet Union by Russian small-arms designer Mikhail Kalashnikov, it is the originating firearm of the Kalashnikov rifle (or "AK") family. It was adopted by the Soviet Army in 1949.

Famous for its durability, reliability in harsh conditions, loose tolerances, ease of use, and low production cost, the AK-47 is the most widely used assault rifle in the world. It has been manufactured in many countries, licensed and unlicensed, and has served as the standard service rifle of numerous armies, as well as the weapon of insurgent and guerrilla forces worldwide. Its silhouette appears on the flags and emblems of several states and organizations.

History

Kalashnikov, a tank commander wounded in the Second World War, began designing firearms while convalescing. After earlier submachine gun and carbine efforts, he produced the prototype AK-46 in 1946 and the refined AK-47 in 1947, winning the Soviet trials. Influences included the German StG 44 and the intermediate 7.92×33mm Kurz concept, the American M1 Garand's trigger and rotating bolt ideas, and the Soviet SKS carbine.

Design

The AK-47 uses a long-stroke gas piston system in which the piston is permanently attached to the bolt carrier, generous internal clearances, a chrome-lined barrel, and a two-lug rotating bolt. The distinctive curved magazine accommodates the heavily tapered 7.62×39mm cartridge. Early production rifles used stamped receivers that proved problematic; the milled-receiver AK became standard until the improved AKM of 1959 introduced a refined stamped receiver, a rate reducer, and a slant muzzle compensator.

Variants and derivatives

The family includes the AKS (side-folding stock), the RPK light machine gun, the AKM, the smaller-caliber AK-74 in 5.45×39mm, the AK-100 series, and the modern AK-12. Foreign copies include China's Type 56, Yugoslavia's Zastava M70, East Germany's MPi-KM, Finland's Valmet Rk 62, and Israel's Galil, which drew on the AK action.

Users and cultural impact

An estimated 75 million AK-47s have been produced, and Kalashnikov-pattern weapons account for roughly one fifth of all firearms in existence. The rifle appears on the flag of Mozambique and the emblems of Hezbollah and other groups, and it has become a potent cultural symbol of revolution, resistance, and armed struggle, as well as a major factor in the global small-arms trade and conflicts in Africa, the Middle East, and Asia.
