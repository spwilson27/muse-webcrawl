House of Councillors

The House of Councillors (参議院, Sangiin) is the upper house of the National Diet of Japan. It consists of 248 members who serve six-year terms; half of the membership is elected every three years in staggered elections, and the house cannot be dissolved.

The House of Councillors was created by the Constitution of Japan, which came into effect in 1947, replacing the pre-war House of Peers of the Imperial Diet. Under the constitution, the lower House of Representatives has supremacy in passing budgets, treaties, and designating the prime minister; if the two houses disagree on such matters, the decision of the House of Representatives prevails. For ordinary legislation, a bill rejected by the House of Councillors can still be enacted if repassed by a two-thirds majority of the House of Representatives.

Composition and elections

Of the 248 members, 148 are elected from prefectural districts by single non-transferable vote, and 100 are elected by proportional representation from a single nationwide district, in which voters may vote for a party list or a specific candidate. Members must be at least 30 years old, ten years older than the minimum age for the lower house, reflecting the intended role of the chamber as a "house of wisdom" or deliberative revising body.

History

The first House of Councillors election was held in April 1947. In recent decades, elections have occasionally produced a "twisted Diet" (nejire kokkai), in which different parties control the two houses, complicating government legislation.
