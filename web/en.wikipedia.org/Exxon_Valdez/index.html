Exxon Valdez

Exxon Valdez was an oil tanker that gained notoriety after running aground in Prince William Sound, Alaska, on 24 March 1989, spilling approximately 10.8 million US gallons (about 41 million litres) of crude oil — one of the most environmentally damaging oil spills in history.

Exxon Valdez
Type: Very large crude carrier
Launched: 1986
Spill: 24 March 1989, Bligh Reef

The spill

The tanker struck Bligh Reef shortly after midnight after leaving the shipping lane to avoid icebergs. The spill spread over roughly 1,300 miles of coastline and killed large numbers of seabirds, sea otters, and other wildlife.

Aftermath

The disaster led to the US Oil Pollution Act of 1990, which mandated double hulls for tankers in US waters, and to long-running litigation over punitive damages. The vessel was repaired, renamed, and eventually scrapped in 2012.
