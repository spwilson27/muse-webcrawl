Human resources (HR) is the set of people who make up the workforce of an organization, business sector, industry, or economy. More commonly, the term refers to the department within an organization charged with managing those people: recruiting and hiring employees, administering pay and benefits, training and development, performance management, and ensuring compliance with employment law.

History

Personnel management emerged in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries as large industrial firms created welfare and employment offices. The term "human resources" gained currency in the mid-twentieth century, reflecting a view of employees as assets whose value could be developed. Human resource management as an academic and professional discipline grew rapidly from the 1980s onward.

Activities

Core HR activities include workforce planning, job analysis and design, recruitment and selection, onboarding, training and development, performance appraisal, compensation and benefits administration, employee relations, and health and safety. HR departments also maintain personnel records and advise management on organizational policy.

Roles and structure

HR work may be performed by generalists in small organizations or by specialists in larger ones. Common roles include HR managers, recruiters, compensation analysts, and training specialists. Professional bodies such as the Society for Human Resource Management (SHRM) and the Chartered Institute of Personnel and Development (CIPD) offer certifications and standards of practice.

Criticism

Critics argue that HR can prioritize the interests of management over those of employees, and debate continues over how well common HR practices are supported by evidence. The field has also been reshaped by outsourcing, HR information systems, and analytics-driven decision making.
