Bell state measurement at the heart of teleportation.

A Bell state measurement (BSM) is a joint quantum measurement performed on two qubits that projects them onto one of the four maximally entangled Bell states. It is a central primitive of quantum information processing: it is the key step in quantum teleportation, entanglement swapping, and the operation of quantum repeaters and many quantum network protocols.

Bell states

The four Bell states are the maximally entangled two-qubit states (|00⟩+|11⟩)/√2, (|00⟩−|11⟩)/√2, (|01⟩+|10⟩)/√2 and (|01⟩−|10⟩)/√2. A complete BSM distinguishes all four outcomes; in linear optics without auxiliary photons only two of the four can be identified, limiting success probability to 50%.

Applications

In teleportation, a BSM on an unknown state and one half of an entangled pair transfers the state to the remote partner, contingent on two classical bits of measurement outcome. Measurement-device-independent quantum key distribution also relies on BSM performed by an untrusted relay.
