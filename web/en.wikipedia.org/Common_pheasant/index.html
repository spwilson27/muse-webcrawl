Common pheasant

The common pheasant (Phasianus colchicus) is a bird in the pheasant family (Phasianidae). The genus name comes from Latin phasianus, "pheasant". The species name colchicus is Latin for "of Colchis" (modern Georgia), a country at the Black Sea where pheasants became known to Europeans. It is native to Asia and parts of Europe and has been widely introduced elsewhere as a game bird.

Description

There are many colour forms of the common pheasant; males are brightly coloured with an iridescent green head, red facial wattles, and in many populations a white neck ring, while the larger body plumage is golden-brown barred with black. Females are mottled brown and much less showy. Males measure 60–89 cm (24–35 in) in length including the long barred tail, females 50–63 cm (20–25 in).

Distribution and human use

Common pheasants inhabit grassland, farmland, and woodland edges near water. Introduced populations are established across Europe, North America (where it is the state bird of South Dakota), Hawaii, Chile, Japan, and New Zealand. Enormous numbers are reared and released annually for shooting as game.
