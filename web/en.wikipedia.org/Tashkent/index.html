Tashkent

Tashkent (Uzbek: Toshkent; Russian: Ташкент) is the capital and largest city of Uzbekistan, with a population of roughly 3 million, making it the most populous city in Central Asia. Located in the northeast of the country near the border with Kazakhstan, in the valley of the Chirchiq River west of the Chatkal Mountains, it has been an oasis and trading centre on routes between Samarkand and the steppe for two millennia. Its name derives from Turkic tash (stone) and Persian kent (city): "Stone City".

History

The oasis was settled by the 5th–3rd centuries BCE and became known to Chinese sources as a dependency of Kangju; Arab armies conquered it in 751 CE, and it later passed among Samanid, Karakhanid, Khwarazmian, Mongol, Timurid, and Shaybanid rulers, developing as a caravan city famed for its crafts and markets. The Russian Empire annexed Tashkent in 1865 under General Chernyaev, making it the capital of Russian Turkestan. In the Soviet period it became the USSR's fourth-largest city and a major industrial and evacuation centre during the Second World War.

The 1966 earthquake

On 26 April 1966 a magnitude-5.1 earthquake at shallow depth devastated much of the old city, leaving some 300,000 people homeless. Tashkent was rebuilt in the Soviet modernist style with labour and funds from across the USSR, leaving a distinctive legacy of socialist-modernist architecture alongside surviving mahalla (neighbourhood) districts of the Old City.

Landmarks and culture

Key sights include the Khast Imam complex, which houses one of the oldest surviving Qurans (the Uthman Quran), the Chorsu Bazaar beneath its green-tiled dome, the Kukeldash Madrasa, and the 375-metre Tashkent Tower (1985). The Tashkent Metro, opened in 1977 as Central Asia's first, is famed for its ornately decorated stations. The city also hosted the historic Tashkent Declaration of 1966, which ended the Indo-Pakistani War of 1965.
