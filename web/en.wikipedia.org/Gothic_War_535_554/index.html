Gothic War (535–554)

The Gothic War between the Byzantine (Eastern Roman) Empire and the Ostrogothic Kingdom of Italy, fought from 535 to 554, was a central part of Emperor Justinian I's project of reconquering the lost western provinces of the Roman Empire (renovatio imperii). The long war devastated Italy, ending Ostrogothic rule but leaving the peninsula ruined; many historians regard it as a Pyrrhic victory that left Italy vulnerable to the Lombard invasion of 568.

Outbreak and first phase

The pretext came in 535 with the murder of the pro-Byzantine Ostrogothic regent Amalasuntha by her co-ruler Theodahad. Justinian's general Belisarius, fresh from reconquering Vandal North Africa, invaded Sicily and then Italy, taking Naples and Rome (536). The Goths deposed Theodahad and elected Vitiges, who besieged Belisarius in Rome in 537–538 without success. Belisarius advanced north and captured Ravenna in 540, taking Vitiges captive; Justinian recalled him to fight Persia.

Totila's revival

Under the energetic King Totila (from 541), the Goths recovered most of Italy, retaking Rome twice (546 and 550) while plague and war ground down the population. The city of Rome changed hands repeatedly, and Italy's economy and urban life collapsed.

Narses and the end

In 552 the elderly eunuch general Narses arrived with a large, well-funded army. He defeated and killed Totila at the Battle of Taginae (552) and destroyed the last Gothic army under Teia at the Battle of Mons Lactarius near Mount Vesuvius (552/553). Organized resistance ended by 554, when the Pragmatic Sanction reorganized Italy as a Byzantine province — though pockets of Gothic and Frankish resistance were mopped up into the early 560s.
