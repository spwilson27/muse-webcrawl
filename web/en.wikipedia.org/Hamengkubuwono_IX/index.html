Hamengkubuwono IX

Hamengkubuwono IX (1912–1988) was the Sultan of Yogyakarta and the second Vice President of Indonesia.

Sri Sultan Hamengkubuwono IX (12 April 1912 – 2 October 1988), often abbreviated as HB IX, was an Indonesian statesman and a Javanese royal who was the ninth Sultan of the Yogyakarta Sultanate, the first Governor of the Special Region of Yogyakarta, the second Vice President of Indonesia (1973–1978), and Indonesia's first Minister of Defence. He played a central role in the Indonesian National Revolution and is honoured as a National Hero of Indonesia.

Early life and accession

Born Gusti Raden Mas Dorodjatun in Yogyakarta, he was educated in the Netherlands, studying at the University of Leiden. He succeeded his father, Hamengkubuwono VIII, and was crowned on 18 March 1940 — famously insisting during negotiations with the Dutch colonial administration on terms emphasising his duty to his people ("Tahta untuk Rakyat", "The throne for the people").

Role in the Revolution

After Sukarno and Hatta proclaimed Indonesian independence on 17 August 1945, Hamengkubuwono IX declared the Sultanate part of the Republic of Indonesia, and Yogyakarta became the republican capital from 1946 to 1949. He provided funds and shelter to the revolutionary government and sheltered its leaders during the second Dutch military offensive of December 1948. He is credited, with General Sudirman and others, with planning the General Offensive of 1 March 1949 (Serangan Umum), which briefly retook Yogyakarta and demonstrated to the world that the Republic still existed — a turning point in securing international recognition of Indonesian sovereignty.

Later career

Hamengkubuwono IX served in numerous cabinet posts, including Defence Minister, and in the 1950s and 1960s held economic portfolios, heading the State Audit Board and serving as Coordinating Minister for Economy, Finance and Industry under Suharto. In 1973 he was elected Vice President by the People's Consultative Assembly, serving alongside President Suharto until 1978, when he declined re-election, privately expressing concern over the direction of the New Order regime. He was also deeply involved in the Scout movement, serving as chairman of the Indonesian Scout Movement and earning the nickname "Father of Indonesian Scouting".

He died on 2 October 1988 in Washington, D.C., while on a visit to the United States, and was buried in the royal cemetery at Imogiri. He was succeeded as Sultan by his son, Hamengkubuwono X. His portrait appears on the 10,000 rupiah banknote.
