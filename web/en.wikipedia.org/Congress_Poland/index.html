Congress Poland

Congress Poland
1815–1867 (nominally to 1915)
Capital: Warsaw
Status: Personal union with the Russian Empire
Ruler: Tsar of Russia as King of Poland

Congress Poland, formally the Kingdom of Poland, was a Polish political entity created at the Congress of Vienna in 1815 from the territory of the Napoleonic Duchy of Warsaw. It was ruled in personal union by the Russian emperor, who held the title King of Poland, and it is often called "Congress Poland" to distinguish it from earlier kingdoms of Poland.

Status and constitution

The kingdom initially possessed considerable autonomy: a liberal constitution (1815), its own parliament (the Sejm), army, currency (the złoty), and administration, with Polish as the language of government. Its territory covered about 127,000 km² with a population growing from roughly 3.3 million to about 10 million by the end of the century, making Warsaw a major centre of industry and culture in the Russian Empire.

Uprisings and loss of autonomy

Russian violations of the constitution provoked the November Uprising of 1830–31; after its defeat, the constitution was replaced by the Organic Statute of 1832, the Sejm and separate army were abolished, and repression intensified. The January Uprising of 1863–64 led to still harsher measures: the kingdom's remaining separate institutions were dismantled in 1867, its name was unofficially replaced by "Vistula Land", Russian became the language of administration and education, and the territory was fully incorporated into the empire. The arrangement lasted until World War I, when German and Austro-Hungarian forces occupied the territory in 1915.
