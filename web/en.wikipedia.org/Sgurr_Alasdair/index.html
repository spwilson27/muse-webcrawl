Sgurr Alasdair

Sgurr Alasdair, highest point of the Black Cuillin.

Sgurr Alasdair is the highest peak of the Black Cuillin mountains on the Isle of Skye, Scotland, rising to 992 metres (3,255 feet) above sea level. It is classified as a Munro and is the highest point on Skye and in the Inner Hebrides.

Name and first ascent

The mountain was named after Alexander Nicolson of Husabost, a local sheriff and mountaineer, who made its first recorded ascent in 1873; sgurr is Gaelic for a sharp, rocky peak. Nicolson climbed the mountain with a guide and reportedly did not realise it was the Cuillin's highest summit at the time.

Climbing

The usual route begins at Glen Brittle and ascends Coire Lagan, passing the small lochan beneath the towering cliffs, before tackling the Great Stone Chute, a long scree gully that leads to a bealach near the summit. A short but exposed scramble along the ridge reaches the top. Although it avoids the technical rock climbing found elsewhere on the Cuillin ridge, the route involves loose rock and is a serious undertaking in poor weather. Sgurr Alasdair forms part of the full Cuillin traverse, one of the most demanding mountaineering challenges in the British Isles.
