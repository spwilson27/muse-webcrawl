Menai Strait

The Menai Strait (Welsh: Afon Menai, "river Menai") is a narrow stretch of shallow tidal water about 25 km (16 mi) long that separates the island of Anglesey from the mainland of Wales. It connects the Irish Sea near Beaumaris with Conwy Bay, and is famous for its strong tidal currents and for the two bridges that cross it.

Geography

The strait occupies a glacially deepened channel along a fault line. Tidal ranges of up to about 8 metres and the narrowing of the channel produce currents of up to 8 knots, especially in the treacherous central section known as the Swellies (Pwll Ceris), where reefs and islets make navigation hazardous. The town of Menai Bridge stands on the Anglesey shore.

Bridges

Thomas Telford's Menai Suspension Bridge, opened in 1826, was the first major modern suspension bridge and carried the London–Holyhead road (A5) across the strait. Robert Stephenson's Britannia Bridge of 1850 carried the Chester and Holyhead Railway on rectangular wrought-iron tubes; after a fire in 1970 it was rebuilt with steel arches and now also carries the A55 road.

Ecology

The strait's varied tidal habitats — sandbanks, rocky shores and mudflats — support rich birdlife and marine species. Much of it is protected within designated conservation areas, and its mussel fisheries in the eastern strait are among the most productive in Wales.
