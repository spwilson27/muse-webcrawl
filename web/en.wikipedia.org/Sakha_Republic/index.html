Sakha Republic

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Sakha Republic, also known as Yakutia, is a republic of Russia located in the Russian Far East, in north-eastern Siberia. It is the largest federal subject of Russia by area — about 3.08 million square kilometres, roughly a fifth of the country's territory — and one of the largest subnational entities in the world, yet its population is only around one million. Its capital is Yakutsk.

Geography and climate

Sakha stretches from the Arctic Ocean deep into Siberia and includes the Lena, Yana, Indigirka, and Kolyma river basins. Much of it lies on permafrost and is covered by taiga and tundra. It is one of the coldest inhabited regions on Earth: the village of Oymyakon and the town of Verkhoyansk have recorded temperatures near −67 °C (−89 °F), among the lowest ever measured in inhabited places — the "Pole of Cold."

History and people

The Turkic-speaking Sakha (Yakut) people migrated north to the middle Lena by roughly the 13th–14th centuries. Russia annexed the region in the 17th century. The Yakut Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic was created in 1922, and the present republic dates from the end of the Soviet Union.

Economy

Sakha produces a large share of the world's diamonds through the company ALROSA, alongside gold, coal, oil, and natural gas. Reindeer herding, horse and cattle breeding, hunting, and fishing remain important for Indigenous communities, which include Evenks, Evens, Yukaghirs, and Chukchi.
