The Three Treasures in Taoist inner alchemy

The Three Treasures (Chinese: 三寶; pinyin: sānbǎo) are the three essential energies or substances of the human being in Taoism and traditional Chinese medicine: jing (精, "essence"), qi (氣, "vital energy" or "breath"), and shen (神, "spirit"). Practitioners cultivate and refine them to maintain health, longevity and spiritual development.

The three treasures

Jing is the dense essence inherited from one's parents and stored in the kidneys; it governs growth, reproduction and development, and is depleted by stress and excess. Qi is the vital energy that animates the body, produced from food and air and circulating through the meridians. Shen is the spirit or mind, housed in the heart, manifesting as consciousness, wisdom and emotional balance. A phrase in Chinese medicine holds: "Jing is the root, qi is the stem, shen is the flower."

Cultivation

In internal alchemy (neidan), the adept reverses the natural flow of generation: refining jing into qi, qi into shen, and shen into emptiness (xu), the so-called three flowers gathering at the top of the head. Practices such as qigong, tai chi, meditation and dietary regulation aim to conserve jing, smooth the flow of qi and calm the shen. Some Taoist writers also use "Three Treasures" for the ethical virtues of compassion, frugality and humility named in chapter 67 of the Tao Te Ching.

Relation to Chinese medicine

Diagnosis in traditional Chinese medicine often assesses the state of the three treasures: deficiency of jing underlies developmental and ageing disorders, disordered qi produces pain and dysfunction, and disturbed shen appears as insomnia or anxiety.
