Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile

FIA
Founded: 20 June 1904 (as AIACR)
Headquarters: Place de la Concorde, Paris, France
Type: Sports federation

The Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (English: International Automobile Federation), commonly known by its French initials FIA, is an international organisation that governs many forms of motor racing worldwide and represents the interests of motoring organisations and motor car users. It is best known as the sanctioning body for Formula One, the World Rally Championship, the World Endurance Championship and Formula E.

History

The association was founded in Paris on 20 June 1904 as the Association Internationale des Automobile Clubs Reconnus (AIACR). It took its present name in 1946. In 1950 it organised the first Formula One World Championship. For much of the late twentieth century the federation was led by Jean-Marie Balestre and then Max Mosley (1993–2009), followed by Jean Todt (2009–2021) and Mohammed Ben Sulayem.

Structure and role

The FIA brings together more than 240 national motoring and sporting organisations from over 140 countries. Its World Motor Sport Council approves regulations for international series, and the FIA issues the International Sporting Code under which events are run. Since the early 2000s, the commercial rights to Formula One have been held separately by Formula One Group under a long-term agreement, while the FIA remains the regulator.

Mobility and safety

Beyond sport, the FIA campaigns for road safety, including support for the United Nations' road-safety goals, and represents touring and automobile clubs on mobility policy. Its affiliated institutes and foundations support research into racing safety, which has produced advances such as the halo cockpit protection device introduced in 2018.
