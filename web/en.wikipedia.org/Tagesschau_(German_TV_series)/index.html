Tagesschau (German for "View of the Day") is the flagship national television news program of Germany's public broadcaster ARD, shown on Das Erste and several regional channels. Produced by ARD-aktuell at the NDR studios in Hamburg, its main 20:00 edition is the most-watched news broadcast in Germany.

History

The program first aired on 26 December 1952 from Hamburg. Initially broadcast only a few times per week, it became a daily fixture as television spread through West Germany. Its sober, factual presentation style — newsreaders rather than anchor personalities — became a hallmark of German public-service journalism, and the distinctive opening fanfare and clock countdown to 8 p.m. are widely recognized cultural symbols.

Format

The main edition at 20:00 runs about fifteen minutes, covering politics, business, culture, and sport, followed by the weather. Shorter editions air throughout the day, and the brand extends to the in-depth Tagesthemen late-evening program, the Nachtmagazin, and the 24-hour tagesschau24 channel and online service.

Influence

With nightly audiences commonly in the millions, Tagesschau remains a central source of news in Germany despite competition from private broadcasters and online media. The phrase "20 Uhr Tagesschau" is a fixed point of the German evening schedule.
