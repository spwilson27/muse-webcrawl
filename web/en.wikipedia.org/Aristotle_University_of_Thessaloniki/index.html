Aristotle University of Thessaloniki
Type: Public university
Established: 1925
Location: Thessaloniki, Greece
Named after the philosopher Aristotle

The Aristotle University of Thessaloniki (AUTH) is the largest university in Greece and in the Balkans. It was founded in 1925, during the premiership of Alexandros Papanastasiou, and was the second Greek university to be established after the University of Athens.

History

The university opened its doors in the academic year 1926–1927, initially with a Faculty of Philosophy housed in the Villa Allatini. It is named after the philosopher Aristotle, who was born in nearby Stageira.

Organisation

The university comprises numerous faculties and schools, including Philosophy, Sciences, Engineering, Medicine, Law, Economics and Political Sciences, Agriculture, Forestry, Education, Fine Arts, and Theology. Its campus covers about 334,000 square metres in the centre of Thessaloniki.

Students and staff

The university enrolls tens of thousands of undergraduate and postgraduate students and employs thousands of teaching and administrative staff. Its library system is one of the largest in Greece.
