Digital Audio Broadcasting

Digital Audio Broadcasting (DAB) is a digital radio standard for broadcasting digital audio radio services in many countries, mainly in Europe, Asia-Pacific, and parts of Africa. Developed under the European Eureka 147 project in the 1980s and 1990s, it offers more stations per frequency band and resistance to multipath fading compared with analogue FM.

Technology

DAB uses COFDM modulation and multiplexes several services into an "ensemble" occupying about 1.5 MHz of bandwidth. The original standard used the MPEG-1 Audio Layer II codec; the later DAB+ standard, released in 2007, uses HE-AAC v2 for greater efficiency and stronger error correction, and has been adopted by most newer deployments.

Adoption

Norway became the first country to switch off national FM radio, completing the process in 2017. DAB has wide coverage in the United Kingdom, Germany, Australia, and the Netherlands, though adoption has been slower elsewhere, and the United States uses the incompatible HD Radio system instead.
