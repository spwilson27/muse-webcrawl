Waikiki

Waikiki (Hawaiian: Waikīkī, "spouting fresh water") is a neighborhood of Honolulu on the southern shore of the island of Oʻahu in Hawaiʻi. Famous for its crescent beach backed by high-rise hotels and the volcanic tuff cone of Diamond Head (Lēʻahi), Waikiki is Hawaiʻi's best-known tourist destination and one of the most recognized beach resorts in the world.

History

For centuries Waikiki was a retreat for Hawaiian royalty, who surfed its long, rolling breaks; the area's wetlands and taro fields were fed by streams from the Mānoa and Palolo valleys. Tourism began in earnest with the Moana Hotel (now the Moana Surfrider) in 1901, and the district boomed after statehood in 1959 as jet travel made Hawaiʻi accessible. The Ala Wai Canal, completed in the 1920s, drained the wetlands for development.

Tourism and beach

Waikiki Beach's gentle surf makes it a center for beginner surfing and outrigger canoeing; a statue of Duke Kahanamoku, the Olympic swimmer and surfing ambassador, stands at Kūhiō Beach. Because the beach is largely artificial and eroding, it has repeatedly been renourished with pumped sand. Kalākaua Avenue, the main thoroughfare, is lined with hotels, shops, and restaurants, and the district accounts for a large share of Hawaiʻi's visitor industry revenue.
