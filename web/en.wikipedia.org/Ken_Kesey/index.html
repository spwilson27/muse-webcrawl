Ken Elton Kesey
Born: September 17, 1935, La Junta, Colorado, U.S.
Died: November 10, 2001, Eugene, Oregon, U.S.
Occupation: Novelist, essayist
Notable works: One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest, Sometimes a Great Notion

Ken Elton Kesey (September 17, 1935 – November 10, 2001) was an American novelist, essayist and countercultural figure. He is best known for his novel One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest (1962) and for his role as a bridge between the Beat Generation of the 1950s and the hippie movement of the 1960s.

Early life and Cuckoo's Nest

Kesey grew up in Oregon and attended the University of Oregon, then Stanford University's creative writing program. While at Stanford he volunteered as a test subject in government-funded experiments with psychedelic drugs at the Menlo Park veterans' hospital, and worked the night shift as an aide on a psychiatric ward. Both experiences fed directly into One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest, narrated by "Chief" Bromden and centred on the rebellious patient Randle Patrick McMurphy and the authoritarian Nurse Ratched. The novel was adapted into a successful stage play and the 1975 film that swept the five major Academy Awards.

The Merry Pranksters

With the proceeds of his literary success Kesey bought property in La Honda, California. In 1964 he and a group of friends calling themselves the Merry Pranksters drove across the United States in a converted, psychedelically painted school bus named Furthur, a trip chronicled by Tom Wolfe in The Electric Kool-Aid Acid Test (1968). The Pranksters went on to stage the Acid Tests, multimedia happenings at which LSD was distributed to audiences, featuring early performances by the Grateful Dead.

Later life

Kesey's second novel, Sometimes a Great Notion (1964), a saga of an Oregon logging family, is regarded by many critics as his finest work. After legal troubles over marijuana possession, he settled on a farm near Eugene, Oregon, where he lived for the rest of his life, publishing story collections, the collaborative novel Caverns, and the prison-written Kesey's Jail Journal. He died in 2001 following surgery for liver cancer.
