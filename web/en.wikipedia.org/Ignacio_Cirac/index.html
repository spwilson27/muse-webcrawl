Ignacio Cirac

Juan Ignacio Cirac Sasturain (born 11 October 1965) is a Spanish physicist, recognised as one of the pioneers of quantum computing and quantum information theory. He is a director at the Max Planck Institute of Quantum Optics in Garching, Germany, where he heads the Theory Division.

Career and research

Cirac studied at the Complutense University of Madrid, receiving his doctorate in 1991. After positions in Madrid, at the University of Colorado (working with Peter Zoller), and at the University of Innsbruck, he joined the Max Planck Institute in 2001. With Zoller he proposed in 1995 the first concrete scheme for a quantum computer based on trapped ions — the Cirac–Zoller controlled-NOT gate — which became a landmark of the field. His research also covers quantum simulation with cold atoms in optical lattices, tensor network methods such as matrix product states, quantum optics and quantum communication protocols.

Awards

Among many honours, Cirac received the Prince of Asturias Award for Technical and Scientific Research (2006, shared with Peter Zoller), the BBVA Foundation Frontiers of Knowledge Award (2008), the Benjamin Franklin Medal in Physics (2010), the Wolf Prize in Physics (2013, shared with Peter Zoller) and the Max Planck Medal (2026). He is a member of numerous academies and one of the most highly cited physicists in his field.
