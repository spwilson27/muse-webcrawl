Five-star rank

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A five-star rank is the highest military rank in many countries, corresponding to NATO rank code OF-10. The rank is that of a field marshal or equivalent and is typically denoted by an insignia of five stars arranged in a pattern. Five-star officers are addressed by the title of their specific rank — for example, General of the Army or Fleet Admiral in the United States, or Field Marshal in the British Army.

United States

The United States created its five-star ranks in December 1944, during World War II, so that American commanders would not be outranked by Allied field marshals. The Army rank of General of the Army was awarded to George C. Marshall, Douglas MacArthur, Dwight D. Eisenhower, and Henry H. Arnold (later also the only General of the Air Force); Omar Bradley was promoted in 1950, the last American appointment to the rank. The Navy's Fleet Admiral rank went to William D. Leahy, Ernest King, Chester W. Nimitz, and William Halsey Jr. No five-star appointments have been made since, and the grades, while still authorized in wartime, are effectively dormant; officers holding them remained on active duty for life.

Other countries

Many armed forces maintain or historically maintained an OF-10 rank: the British Field Marshal and the Royal Air Force's Marshal of the Royal Air Force, the Australian Field Marshal, India's Field Marshal, and equivalents such as Pakistan's Field Marshal. In some countries the rank has been held by heads of state or awarded ceremonially. Because the rank is associated with command of very large formations in major wars, appointments outside wartime are rare and controversial.

Insignia

Five-star insignia generally arrange five stars in a circle or pentagon (as in the United States, where the stars point inward toward a central shield on shoulder boards), or use crossed batons and wreaths, as in the Commonwealth field marshal's badge. The number of stars follows the general system in which flag and general officers' rank is expressed by stars, culminating at five.
