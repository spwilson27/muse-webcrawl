Nuclear weapon

Nuclear weapon
First test: Trinity, 16 July 1945
Used in warfare: Hiroshima and Nagasaki, August 1945
Types: Fission bombs; thermonuclear (fusion) bombs
Delivery: Bombs, missiles, artillery, torpedoes

A nuclear weapon is an explosive device that derives its destructive force from nuclear reactions, either fission (fission bomb) or a combination of fission and fusion reactions (thermonuclear bomb), producing a nuclear explosion. Both bomb types release large quantities of energy from relatively small amounts of matter.

History

Nuclear weapons were first developed by the United States during World War II under the Manhattan Project, with contributions from the United Kingdom and Canada. The first test, Trinity, took place on 16 July 1945 in New Mexico. In August 1945 the United States bombed the Japanese cities of Hiroshima and Nagasaki — the only uses of nuclear weapons in warfare. The Soviet Union tested its first bomb in 1949, followed by the United Kingdom (1952), France (1960), China (1964), India (1974), Pakistan (1998), and North Korea (2006).

Effects

A nuclear explosion produces a blast wave, intense thermal radiation, ionizing radiation, and radioactive fallout. Large-scale nuclear war could cause catastrophic loss of life and climatic effects such as nuclear winter.

Strategy and control

Nuclear strategy centres on deterrence, including mutually assured destruction. International treaties, from the Non-Proliferation Treaty to bilateral arms-reduction agreements, seek to limit the spread and numbers of nuclear weapons. As of the 2020s, nine states possess nuclear weapons, with a combined stockpile of roughly 12,000 warheads.
