Village green

A village green is a common open area of grass within a village or other settlement, traditionally used for grazing, sports, fairs and community gatherings. Village greens are a characteristic feature of English settlements and of colonial New England towns in the United States, where they are often called a common.

History and law

Greens originated as shared grazing land at the heart of a settlement, often with a pond for livestock. In England and Wales, village greens enjoy legal protection: the Commons Registration Act 1965 and the Commons Act 2006 allow registration of land used by local inhabitants for lawful sports and pastimes, protecting it from development.

Notable examples include the greens at Finchingfield in Essex and the town commons of New England such as New Haven Green in Connecticut.
