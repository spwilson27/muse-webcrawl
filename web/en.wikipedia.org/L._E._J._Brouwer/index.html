Luitzen Egbertus Jan Brouwer (27 February 1881 – 2 December 1966), usually cited as L. E. J. Brouwer, was a Dutch mathematician and philosopher who worked in topology, set theory, measure theory and complex analysis. He is regarded as the founder of intuitionism, a philosophy of mathematics that challenged the formalist programme of David Hilbert.

Mathematics

Brouwer made foundational contributions to topology, including the Brouwer fixed-point theorem, the invariance of dimension and of domain, and the hairy ball theorem's lineage of results. His topological work underlies much of modern algebraic topology.

Intuitionism

In his philosophy, Brouwer held that mathematics is a free creation of the human mind and rejected the unrestricted use of the law of excluded middle for infinite sets, demanding constructive proofs of existence. This provoked the famous Grundlagenstreit (foundations dispute) with Hilbert, who vowed that "no one shall expel us from the paradise that Cantor has created for us". Intuitionistic logic, developed by Brouwer's student Arend Heyting, grew out of this programme and influenced later constructive mathematics and computer science.
