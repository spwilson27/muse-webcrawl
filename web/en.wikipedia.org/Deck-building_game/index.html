A deck-building game is a card game or board game where the construction of a deck is a main element of gameplay. It is similar to collectible card games (CCGs) in that each player has their own personal deck; however, unlike CCGs, the cards are not sold in randomised packs, and instead of building a deck before the game begins, players build their decks during the game from a common pool of cards.

History

The genre was defined by Dominion, designed by Donald X. Vaccarino and published by Rio Grande Games in 2008. Dominion won the Spiel des Jahres in 2009 and inspired a wave of imitators and successors. The deck-building mechanism had earlier antecedents in the computer game Magic: The Gathering drafts and in games where deck construction occurred between scenarios, but Dominion was the first to make in-game deck construction the central mechanic.

Gameplay

Typically, each player starts with an identical, weak deck of basic cards. On each turn a player draws a hand, plays cards for currency or effects, and uses that currency to buy stronger cards from a face-up supply, adding them to their discard pile. When the draw pile is exhausted, the discard pile is shuffled to form a new deck — so purchased cards enter circulation gradually. Players must balance buying powerful cards against thinning and streamlining the deck, and most games award victory points through certain cards or accomplishments.

Notable games

Ascension: Chronicle of the Godslayer (2010) replaced the fixed supply with a shifting centre row. Thunderstone (2009) added a dungeon theme. Later designs combined deck-building with other mechanisms: Clank! (2016) with board movement, Aeon's End (2016) with cooperative play and a no-shuffle rule, and the video game Slay the Spire (2019), which fused deck-building with roguelike progression and popularised the genre in digital form. Related "bag-building" and "pool-building" games use tokens or dice in place of cards.
