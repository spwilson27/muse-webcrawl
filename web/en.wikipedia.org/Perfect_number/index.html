Perfect number

In number theory, a perfect number is a positive integer that is equal to the sum of its positive proper divisors, that is, the sum of its positive divisors excluding the number itself. Equivalently, a number is perfect if it is half the sum of all of its positive divisors, or σ(n) = 2n, where σ is the sum-of-divisors function.

The first perfect number is 6: its proper divisors are 1, 2 and 3, and 1 + 2 + 3 = 6. The next is 28 = 1 + 2 + 4 + 7 + 14.

Even perfect numbers

Euclid proved that if 2p − 1 is prime (a Mersenne prime), then 2p−1(2p − 1) is an even perfect number. More than two thousand years later, Leonhard Euler proved the converse: every even perfect number has this form. Thus even perfect numbers and Mersenne primes correspond one-to-one. As of recent counts, 52 Mersenne primes are known, and hence 52 even perfect numbers; the largest known has tens of millions of digits.

Odd perfect numbers

It is unknown whether any odd perfect numbers exist. Many necessary conditions have been established: an odd perfect number must exceed 101500, must not be divisible by 3, must have at least eight distinct prime factors, and must have a prime factor greater than 108. None has ever been found, and the question remains one of the oldest unsolved problems in mathematics.

Related concepts

Numbers whose proper divisors sum to less than the number are called deficient; those whose divisors sum to more are called abundant. Pairs of numbers each equal to the sum of the other's proper divisors are called amicable numbers.
