Mikhail Leonidovich Gromov (Russian: Михаил Леонидович Громов; born 23 December 1943) is a Russian-French mathematician whose work transformed large parts of geometry in the second half of the twentieth century. This article gives his full name form; he is most widely known simply as Mikhail Gromov.

Education

Gromov studied at Leningrad State University, where he earned his candidate of sciences degree in 1969 and his doctorate in 1973 under Vladimir Rokhlin. His early work on partial differential relations produced the h-principle, which he later developed in the influential 1986 book Partial Differential Relations.

Career

After positions in Leningrad and at the State University of New York at Stony Brook, Gromov emigrated to France in 1982 and became a permanent professor at the Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques. He became a naturalised French citizen in 1992 and also held a professorship at the Courant Institute in New York.

Contributions

His contributions include the Gromov–Hausdorff distance between metric spaces, filling invariants and systolic geometry, pseudoholomorphic curves and Gromov–Witten theory in symplectic geometry, the theory of hyperbolic groups at the foundation of geometric group theory, and the theorem that groups of polynomial growth have a nilpotent subgroup of finite index.

Honours

Among many honours, Gromov received the Oswald Veblen Prize (1981), the Wolf Prize (1993), the Kyoto Prize (2002) and the Abel Prize (2009).
