Sexism

DefinitionPrejudice or discrimination based on sex or gender
Term coined1960s (popularised by Pauline Leet, 1965)
FormsHostile, benevolent, institutional, internalised
Related conceptsGender inequality, misogyny, patriarchy, feminism

Sexism is prejudice or discrimination based on one's sex or gender. It can affect anyone, but it primarily affects women and girls, and has been linked to gender roles and stereotypes and to belief systems holding one sex as intrinsically superior to another. Sexism ranges from individual attitudes and behaviours to institutional practices that produce unequal outcomes in employment, pay, education, political representation, healthcare and personal safety.

Forms and concepts

Psychologists Peter Glick and Susan Fiske distinguish hostile sexism (overt antagonism toward women, especially those challenging male power) from benevolent sexism (subjectively positive but patronising attitudes that idealise women in traditional roles); together they form "ambivalent sexism". Feminist theory analyses sexism as embedded in patriarchal structures, while the concept of institutional sexism addresses rules and norms that systematically disadvantage a sex, such as historical bars on women's suffrage, property ownership and university admission. Internalised sexism describes members of the disadvantaged group adopting sexist beliefs themselves.

Manifestations and responses

Documented manifestations include the gender pay gap, occupational segregation, under-representation in leadership and STEM fields, gender-based violence and harassment, and discriminatory law. Responses have included the women's suffrage movements, equal-pay and anti-discrimination legislation (such as the US Equal Pay Act 1963 and Title VII of the Civil Rights Act 1964), the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women (CEDAW, 1979), and campaigns such as #MeToo. Critics of particular remedies debate causes and solutions, but the empirical study of sex-based disparities remains a large field in sociology, psychology and economics.
