Bid rigging

Bid rigging is a form of fraud and collusion in which bidders in an auction or tender conspire to determine the winning bidder in advance, typically by agreeing that one party will submit the lowest bid while others submit artificially high "cover" bids or refrain from bidding. Bid rigging is a form of price fixing and market allocation, and it is an illegal practice under the competition laws of most countries, including the Sherman Act in the United States.

Forms

  • Cover bidding: competitors submit bids known to be too high, or with unacceptable terms, to create the appearance of competition.
  • Bid suppression: one or more competitors agree not to bid, or withdraw a previously submitted bid.
  • Bid rotation: competitors take turns being the designated low bidder on successive contracts.
  • Market allocation: competitors agree to divide markets by customer or territory and not bid against each other in allocated areas.

Detection and enforcement

Competition authorities such as the US Department of Justice, the European Commission, and the OECD publish guidance on detecting bid rigging, noting warning signs such as identical pricing, unusual bid patterns, and the same firm repeatedly winning in one area. Public procurement, especially construction and defence contracting, is a common target; in Japan the practice known as dango has been a persistent concern. Penalties can include heavy fines and imprisonment for individuals.
