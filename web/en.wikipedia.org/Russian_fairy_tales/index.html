Russian fairy tales

Illustration in the style of Ivan Bilibin, famed for his fairy-tale artwork.

Russian fairy tales (Russian: русские народные сказки) form a rich tradition of folklore passed down orally for centuries before being collected in the 19th century. The most famous collection is that of Alexander Afanasyev, whose Russian Fairy Tales (1855–1863) gathered hundreds of tales and is often compared to the work of the Brothers Grimm.

Recurring characters

Well-known figures include the witch Baba Yaga, who lives in a hut on chicken legs; the villain Koschei the Deathless, who hides his death in a needle inside an egg; the hero Ivan Tsarevich; the wise heroine Vasilisa the Beautiful; and the Firebird, a magical glowing bird sought by heroes. Other figures include the frost spirit Morozko and the water spirit Vodyanoy.

Themes and structure

Russian fairy tales commonly feature quests, magical helpers, transformations, and the rule of three. Many begin with the formula "In a certain kingdom, in a certain state..." and end with the narrator claiming to have been present at a feast. Scholars such as Vladimir Propp analysed their structure in his Morphology of the Folktale (1928), identifying a sequence of narrative functions shared across tales.

Collections and influence

Beyond Afanasyev, important collectors include Vladimir Dal. The tales influenced Russian literature, opera and ballet, inspiring works such as Stravinsky's The Firebird and illustrations by Ivan Bilibin.
