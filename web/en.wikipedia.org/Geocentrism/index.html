Geocentrism

Geocentrism
BeliefEarth is the centre of the universe

Geocentrism is the belief that the Earth is fixed at the centre of the universe, with the Sun, Moon, stars and planets revolving around it. As an astronomical doctrine it is embodied in the geocentric model, systematised by Ptolemy and dominant in Western, Islamic and Chinese astronomy for well over a millennium. Since the scientific revolution, "geocentrism" has chiefly referred to the continued advocacy of an Earth-centred cosmos, usually on religious grounds, long after mainstream science adopted heliocentrism and, later, the modern view that the universe has no centre.

A medieval conception of the cosmos with Earth at its centre, ringed by the spheres of the planets and the fixed stars.

History

Geocentric ideas appear in early Greek cosmology (Anaximander placed Earth at the centre of a cylindrical cosmos), were given physical foundations by Aristotle — heavy elements naturally seek the centre — and received their definitive mathematical form in Ptolemy's Almagest. Medieval scholasticism absorbed the doctrine, and passages of Scripture (e.g. Joshua commanding the Sun to stand still) were read as confirming it. The Copernican revolution, Galileo's trial of 1633, and eventually stellar aberration (Bradley, 1729) and stellar parallax (Bessel, 1838) dismantled the scientific case for a fixed central Earth.

Modern geocentrism

A small minority continues to advocate geocentrism. Some fundamentalist Christians and ultra-Orthodox Jewish writers cite scripture; the "Galileo Was Wrong" conference (2010) and the 2014 film The Principle promoted modern geocentric claims. Polls have periodically found that a notable share of the general public in some countries endorses the statement that the Sun goes around the Earth, though such answers may reflect question framing as much as conviction. Physicists note that coordinate systems centred on Earth are legitimate for calculation (and are used daily in astronomy), but that this is a choice of reference frame, not evidence that Earth is dynamically central — the dynamics of the Solar System are vastly simpler and more predictive in a Sun-centred frame, and general relativity offers no privileged centre at all.
