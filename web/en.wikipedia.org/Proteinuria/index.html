Proteinuria

Proteinuria is the presence of excess protein in the urine. Healthy kidneys filter blood while retaining most protein, so normal urine contains only small amounts (less than about 150 mg per day). Persistent proteinuria is a key sign of kidney damage and an independent risk factor for the progression of chronic kidney disease and cardiovascular disease.

Causes

Proteinuria most often results from damage to the glomerular filtration barrier in diseases such as diabetic kidney disease, glomerulonephritis, and hypertension. Heavy proteinuria (over 3.5 g/day) with low blood albumin, swelling, and high cholesterol defines nephrotic syndrome. Transient or benign proteinuria can follow fever, exercise, or standing (orthostatic proteinuria).

Diagnosis

Screening is usually by urine dipstick, with quantification by a urine albumin-to-creatinine or protein-to-creatinine ratio, or a timed urine collection. Albuminuria, the presence of the protein albumin specifically, is the most sensitive early marker of kidney damage in diabetes.

Management

Treatment targets the underlying cause, together with blood-pressure control and drugs that reduce protein leakage, notably ACE inhibitors and angiotensin receptor blockers.
