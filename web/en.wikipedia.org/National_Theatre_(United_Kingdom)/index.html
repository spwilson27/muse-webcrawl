National Theatre (United Kingdom)

The term National Theatre in the United Kingdom most commonly refers to the Royal National Theatre in London, a major publicly funded theatre company and venue on the South Bank. However, the United Kingdom has several national theatre companies reflecting its constituent nations.

England

The Royal National Theatre, founded in 1963 with Laurence Olivier as its first director, is England's flagship subsidised theatre. Its South Bank building, designed by Denys Lasdun and opened in 1976, houses the Olivier, Lyttelton, and Dorfman auditoria. The company is funded by Arts Council England and is known for productions that transfer to the West End and Broadway, as well as the National Theatre Live cinema broadcasts.

Scotland

The National Theatre of Scotland, established in 2006, is distinctive in having no permanent theatre building; instead it commissions and tours productions in partnership with venues across Scotland and internationally. Its acclaimed productions have included Black Watch by Gregory Burke.

Wales

National Theatre Wales, launched in 2009, similarly operates without a single home venue, creating site-specific and touring work across Wales. The Welsh-language company Theatr Genedlaethol Cymru serves as the national Welsh-language theatre.

Background

The idea of a British national theatre was debated from the 1840s onward, promoted by figures such as Effingham Wilson, William Archer, and Harley Granville-Barker. Government funding was eventually authorised after the Second World War, leading to the founding of the London company in 1963.
