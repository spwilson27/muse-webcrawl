The Galveston Seawall is a seawall in Galveston, Texas, built after the catastrophic Galveston hurricane of 1900 for protection from future hurricanes. Construction began in September 1902, and the initial segment was completed on July 29, 1904. From 1904 to 1963, the seawall was extended from 3.3 miles (5.3 km) to over 10 miles (16 km).

Background

The 1900 hurricane remains the deadliest natural disaster in United States history, killing at least 6,000 people and destroying much of the city. In response, Galveston undertook two monumental engineering projects: the seawall itself, with a curved face designed to deflect waves, and a grade raising that lifted some 500 city blocks and thousands of structures by up to 17 feet, sloping down from the wall toward the bay.

Performance and legacy

The wall proved itself in the 1915 hurricane, which struck with comparable strength but killed far fewer people in the city. Seawall Boulevard, along the crest, became the city's principal promenade, lined with hotels and attractions; the wall is listed on the National Register of Historic Places and designated a National Historic Civil Engineering Landmark. Hurricane Ike in 2008 nevertheless showed the wall's limits, as surge waters overtopped it and flooded Galveston from the bay side.
