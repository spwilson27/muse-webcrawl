Central nervous system

Central nervous system
Components: Brain, spinal cord
System: Nervous system
Protection: Skull, vertebrae, meninges, CSF

The central nervous system (CNS) consists of the brain and spinal cord. It integrates sensory information, coordinates activity throughout the body, and is the seat of cognition, emotion and memory in vertebrates. It is distinguished from the peripheral nervous system of nerves outside the brain and cord.

Structure

The brain comprises the cerebrum, cerebellum and brainstem. The spinal cord carries signals between brain and body and mediates reflexes. CNS tissue contains neurons and glial cells, is bathed in cerebrospinal fluid, protected by the meninges and the blood–brain barrier, and contains grey and white matter.

Development and disease

The CNS develops from the neural tube in the embryo. Disorders include stroke, multiple sclerosis, meningitis, trauma and neurodegenerative diseases. See Brain and Cerebellum.
