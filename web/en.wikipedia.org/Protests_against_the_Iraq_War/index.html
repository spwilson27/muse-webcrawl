Protests against the Iraq War

Beginning in 2002, and continuing after the 2003 invasion of Iraq, large protests against the Iraq War were held in many cities worldwide, often coordinated to occur simultaneously around the world. After the biggest series of demonstrations, held on 15 February 2003, the New York Times writer Patrick Tyler described them as evidence that there were two superpowers on the planet: the United States and worldwide public opinion.

Scale

The 15 February 2003 protests involved between six and ten million people in more than 600 cities, making them the largest anti-war protests in history. In Rome, around three million people marched, which the Guinness Book of Records lists as the largest anti-war rally ever. London saw around one million demonstrators, and Madrid between 660,000 and 990,000.

Chronology

Protests began in 2002 as the United States and United Kingdom built the case for invading Iraq over alleged weapons of mass destruction. They continued through the invasion in March 2003, the occupation, and the Iraq insurgency, with anniversaries of the invasion marked by further demonstrations in subsequent years.

Opposition and slogans

Common slogans included "Not in my name", "No blood for oil", "Make tea, not war", and "Drop Bush, not bombs". Despite the scale of the protests, the invasion proceeded on 20 March 2003, leading to discussion about the effectiveness of mass protest in influencing government policy.
