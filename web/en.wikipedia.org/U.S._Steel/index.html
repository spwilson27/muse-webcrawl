The United States Steel Corporation, commonly known as U.S. Steel, is an American integrated steel producer headquartered in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. Formed in 1901 by banker J. P. Morgan through the merger of Andrew Carnegie's Carnegie Steel Company with other producers, it was the first corporation in the world with a market capitalisation exceeding one billion dollars, and it controlled roughly two-thirds of American steel production.

History

The combination assembled Carnegie's mills with the Federal Steel Company and National Steel Company, among others, in a $1.4 billion capitalisation. U.S. Steel supplied the material skeleton of twentieth-century America — rails, bridges, skyscrapers, ships and armaments in both world wars — and its Gary Works in Indiana was long the largest steel mill in the world. Over the later twentieth century the company faced import competition, ageing plant and labour conflicts; it diversified into oil (Marathon Oil) and chemicals, sold its rail and other assets, and became the smaller X Corporation before resuming the U.S. Steel name in 1999.

Recent developments

In December 2023 U.S. Steel agreed to be acquired by Japan's Nippon Steel for about $14.9 billion. The transaction became a major political issue in the 2024 US election and was blocked on national-security grounds by President Joe Biden in January 2025, before a revised arrangement was subsequently approved under the Trump administration with a US government "golden share". The company remains a major American producer, operating integrated mills in the United States and a large plant in Košice, Slovakia, alongside mini-mills such as Big River Steel in Arkansas.
