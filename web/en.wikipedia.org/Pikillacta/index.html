Pikillacta

LocationLucre District, Quispicanchi Province, Cusco Region, Peru
CultureWari (Huari)
Periodc. 550–1100 AD
Areac. 30–50 hectares (core)

Pikillacta (Quechua piki "flea" + llaqta "town", hence "flea town") is a large archaeological site of the pre-Inca Wari (Huari) culture located about 20 kilometres east of Cusco, in the Lucre Basin of the southern Peruvian highlands. Occupied roughly between AD 550 and 1100, it is one of the most important Wari administrative centres outside the Wari heartland near Ayacucho, and one of the few Wari sites in the Cusco region to have been extensively excavated.

Description

The site is characterised by its highly planned, rectilinear layout. Its core consists of large rectangular enclosures divided by straight streets and walls built of rough fieldstone set in mud mortar, some standing two storeys high. More than 700 buildings, along with canals, reservoirs, terraces and storage facilities, have been recorded across the complex. The largest sector, known as Sector 2, contains some forty large rectangular structures arranged in a grid-like pattern, interpreted as a planned administrative and storage compound.

History and function

Pikillacta is generally interpreted as an administrative centre through which the Wari state managed the production and storage of goods—especially maize—in the Lucre Basin, as well as labour obligations from subject populations. The site appears to have been largely emptied of portable artefacts before its abandonment, suggesting an orderly evacuation rather than a violent destruction. Offerings of turquoise figurines and other objects buried at the site have been taken as evidence of ritual closure ceremonies.

Excavations

The site was investigated by researchers including Gordon McEwan, who directed excavations in the late 1970s and 1980s, and Mary Glowacki. Their work established the site's chronology and its role within the Wari imperial system. A small site museum displays artefacts recovered from the excavations.
