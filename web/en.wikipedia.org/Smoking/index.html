Smoking

Smoking is the practice of burning a substance, most commonly tobacco, and inhaling the resulting smoke, which is absorbed into the bloodstream through the lungs. The practice is primarily performed as a route of administration for recreational drugs, as the combustion releases active substances such as nicotine and converts them into an inhalable aerosol.

Health effects

Tobacco smoking is a leading preventable cause of death worldwide. It is a major risk factor for lung cancer, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, cardiovascular disease and many other cancers and conditions. Exposure to second-hand smoke also harms non-smokers, including children. Health authorities estimate smoking kills more than 8 million people each year globally.

Prevalence and regulation

Smoking rates have declined in many countries since the mid-20th century due to taxation, advertising bans, smoke-free laws, graphic warnings and public health campaigns, though more than a billion people still smoke. Common cessation aids include nicotine replacement therapy, medications such as varenicline, and behavioural support.

History

Smoking of tobacco spread from the Americas after European contact in the late 15th century. Mass-produced cigarettes in the 20th century made smoking widespread before evidence of its harms was firmly established and publicised from the 1950s onward.
