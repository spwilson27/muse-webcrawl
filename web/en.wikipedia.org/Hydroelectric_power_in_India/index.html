India is one of the world's largest producers of hydroelectric power. Hydroelectricity has been a cornerstone of the country's power sector since the late 19th century, when a small plant at Sidrapong near Darjeeling was commissioned in 1897 — among the first in Asia. The assessed hydroelectric potential of India is about 148,700 MW, concentrated largely in the Himalayan states and the north-eastern region.

Major projects

Major large hydroelectric stations include the Tehri Dam on the Bhagirathi River (1,000 MW, with the full complex planned at 2,400 MW), the Nathpa Jhakri project on the Sutlej (1,500 MW), the Sardar Sarovar project on the Narmada, the Koyna project in Maharashtra (1,960 MW), and the Subansiri Lower project on the border of Arunachal Pradesh and Assam (2,000 MW, under construction). The Bhakra Dam on the Sutlej was described by Jawaharlal Nehru as one of the "temples of modern India".

Small hydro

Plants up to 25 MW are classified as small hydro and fall under the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy. India's small-hydro potential is estimated at around 21,000 MW, of which roughly 5 GW had been developed by the early 2020s.

Challenges

Development of hydro potential has been slowed by geological surprises in the Himalayas, environmental concerns, displacement of local communities, seismic risk, and inter-state water disputes. The share of hydro in India's total installed capacity has declined steadily as thermal and, more recently, solar and wind capacity have grown faster.
