House of Hohenzollern

Hohenzollern Castle in Baden-Württemberg, the ancestral seat of the dynasty.

The House of Hohenzollern is a German royal dynasty whose members ruled Prussia, the German Empire, and Romania at various times. The family emerged in Swabia in the 11th century, taking its name from Hohenzollern Castle near Hechingen.

Branches

The dynasty divided into two principal branches. The Franconian-Brandenburg branch acquired the Margraviate of Brandenburg in 1415 and later ruled the Duchy of Prussia; its members became Kings in Prussia in 1701, Kings of Prussia, and German Emperors from 1871 to 1918. The Swabian branch retained lands in southwestern Germany, including Hohenzollern-Sigmaringen and Hohenzollern-Hechingen, and a cadet line reigned as kings of Romania from 1881 to 1947.

Rise of Prussia

Under the Great Elector Frederick William and King Frederick the Great, Brandenburg-Prussia rose to become a great European power. In 1871, Wilhelm I was proclaimed German Emperor at Versailles, uniting most German states under Hohenzollern leadership.

End of the monarchies

The German monarchy collapsed in the November Revolution of 1918, and Wilhelm II abdicated, ending Hohenzollern rule in Germany. In Romania, King Michael I was forced to abdicate in 1947. Descendants of the family remain prominent, and the Prussian branch has pursued restitution claims for property expropriated after 1945.

Notable members

  • Frederick William, the Great Elector
  • Frederick the Great
  • Wilhelm I, first German Emperor
  • Wilhelm II, last German Emperor
  • Carol I and Ferdinand I, kings of Romania
