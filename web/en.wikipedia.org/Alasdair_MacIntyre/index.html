Alasdair MacIntyre

Born: 12 January 1929, Glasgow, Scotland

Died: 21 May 2025

Field: Moral and political philosophy

Notable work: After Virtue (1981)

Tradition: Virtue ethics, Thomism

Alasdair Chalmers MacIntyre (12 January 1929 – 21 May 2025) was a Scottish-American philosopher best known for his book After Virtue (1981), one of the most influential works of moral philosophy of the twentieth century, which revived interest in Aristotelian virtue ethics and criticized the moral culture of modernity.

Life and career

Educated in London and Manchester, MacIntyre taught at universities in Britain before moving to the United States in 1970, eventually holding chairs at Vanderbilt University, Duke University (where he taught for many years), and the University of Notre Dame. Early in his career he was associated with Marxism and wrote Marxism and Christianity (1953); he later converted to Roman Catholicism and developed a Thomistic-Aristotelian position.

Philosophy

In After Virtue, MacIntyre argued that modern moral discourse is a fragmented set of remnants from older traditions, and that emotivism — treating moral judgments as expressions of preference — dominates contemporary culture. He defended the Aristotelian concepts of virtue, practice, narrative unity of a human life, and tradition as the context of rational inquiry. Later works, including Whose Justice? Which Rationality? (1988), Three Rival Versions of Moral Enquiry (1990), and Dependent Rational Animals (1999), extended this project. He is associated with communitarian critiques of liberalism.
