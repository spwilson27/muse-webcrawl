The Porsche 956 was a Group C sports-prototype racing car designed by Norbert Singer and built by Porsche in 1982 for the FIA World Sportscar Championship. It was the first Porsche racing car with an aluminum monocoque and the first to exploit ground-effect aerodynamics.

Racing record

The 956 dominated endurance racing in the mid-1980s, winning the 24 Hours of Le Mans four consecutive times (1982–1985) and multiple World Championship titles, in the hands of drivers including Jacky Ickx, Derek Bell, Jochen Mass, and Stefan Bellof. Bellof's qualifying lap of the Nürburgring Nordschleife in 1983 — 6 minutes 11.13 seconds — stood as an outright record for 35 years.

Technical details

Power came from a twin-turbocharged 2.65-litre flat-six engine producing roughly 635 horsepower. The 956 was succeeded by the 962, developed for IMSA regulations, which continued the design's success into the 1990s.
