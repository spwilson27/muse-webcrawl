Taedong River

Native name대동강 (Taedong-gang)
LocationNorth Korea
Length439 km (273 mi)
SourceRangrim Mountains
MouthKorea Bay, Yellow Sea

The Taedong River (Korean: 대동강) is a large river in North Korea. It rises in the Rangrim Mountains in the north of the country and flows southwest through the capital, Pyongyang, before emptying into Korea Bay on the Yellow Sea at Nampo. At about 439 kilometres long, it is one of the longest rivers on the Korean Peninsula and is navigable for much of its lower course.

The Taedong River in Pyongyang, with the Juche Tower on the eastern bank

Course

The river's headwaters lie in the mountainous interior of South Hamgyong province. Flowing through deep valleys, it passes the industrial city of Hamhung's hinterland before turning toward Pyongyang, where its banks are lined with monuments including the Juche Tower and the May Day Stadium on Rungna Island. Below the capital the river broadens and is crossed by the West Sea Barrage near its estuary at Nampo, a major flood-control and irrigation structure completed in 1986.

History

The Taedong basin was a cradle of early Korean civilization; the ancient kingdom of Goguryeo had its capital at Pyongyang on the river's banks from 427 CE, and the earlier Lelang Commandery was centered nearby. In 1866 the American merchant schooner General Sherman was burned and its crew killed after sailing up the river to Pyongyang in an attempt to force trade—an incident still commemorated in North Korea. The USS Pueblo, captured in 1968, was for many years moored as a museum ship on the river at Pyongyang.
