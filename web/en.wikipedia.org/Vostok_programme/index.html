The Vostok programme (Russian: Восток, "East") was a Soviet spaceflight project to put the first humans into space. Using the Vostok spacecraft, a derivative of the Zenit reconnaissance satellite, the programme flew six crewed missions between 1961 and 1963 and achieved a series of firsts in the Space Race against the United States.

Flights

After uncrewed test flights — including the Korabl-Sputnik missions carrying the dogs Belka and Strelka, who were safely recovered in August 1960 — Vostok 1 carried Yuri Gagarin on 12 April 1961, making him the first human in space and the first to orbit the Earth, in a flight of 108 minutes. Vostok 2 (Gherman Titov, August 1961) lasted a full day. Vostok 3 and 4 flew a joint mission in 1962, passing within a few kilometres of each other, and Vostok 5 and 6 repeated the feat in June 1963, with Vostok 6 carrying Valentina Tereshkova, the first woman in space.

Spacecraft

The Vostok consisted of a spherical descent module (nicknamed "sharik") about 2.3 m in diameter housing the cosmonaut, who sat in an ejection seat — cosmonauts ejected at about 7 km altitude and landed separately from the capsule, a fact initially concealed to secure world records — and a conical instrument module carrying batteries, thrusters and the retrorocket. The design could sustain one person for up to about 10 days.

Legacy

The programme was succeeded by the multi-crew Voskhod programme and then by Soyuz, whose basic configuration remains in use. Gagarin's flight is commemorated worldwide on 12 April as Yuri's Night and the International Day of Human Space Flight.
