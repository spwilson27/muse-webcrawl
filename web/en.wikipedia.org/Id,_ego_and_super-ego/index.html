Id, ego and super-ego

The id, ego and super-ego are the three agencies of the human personality in Sigmund Freud's structural model of the psyche, set out in his 1923 work The Ego and the Id.

The three agencies

The id is the wholly unconscious reservoir of instinctual drives, present from birth and operating on the pleasure principle. The ego, partly conscious, mediates between the id, reality, and the super-ego according to the reality principle. The super-ego embodies internalised moral standards and ideals acquired from parents and society, and produces feelings such as guilt and pride.

Functioning

In Freud's model, mental conflict arises when the demands of the id, the constraints of reality, and the prohibitions of the super-ego come into opposition; anxiety signals this conflict, and the ego deploys defence mechanisms in response.

Criticism

The structural model remains influential in cultural and literary theory, though its scientific status has been widely questioned by later psychologists.
