Rotoscoping

Rotoscoping is an animation technique that animators use to trace over motion picture footage, frame by frame, to produce realistic action. Originally, live-action film images were projected onto a glass panel and traced by the animator; the projected-image device was called a rotoscope, patented by Max Fleischer in 1917.

History

Fleischer developed rotoscoping for his Out of the Inkwell series, featuring Koko the Clown. The Fleischers' rival, Walt Disney, used the technique for realistic human figures in Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs (1937). Ralph Bakshi used extensive rotoscoping in Wizards (1977) and The Lord of the Rings (1978). In the digital era, Bob Sabiston's interpolated rotoscoping software powered Richard Linklater's Waking Life (2001) and A Scanner Darkly (2006).

In visual effects

In modern VFX, "roto" refers to manually drawing mattes around elements frame by frame so they can be composited or removed — a labour-intensive but essential task, now assisted by tracking and machine-learning tools.
