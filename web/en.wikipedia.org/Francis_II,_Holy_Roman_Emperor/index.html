Francis II (German: Franz II; 12 February 1768 – 2 March 1835) was the last Holy Roman Emperor, reigning from 1792 until the dissolution of the empire on 6 August 1806. As Francis I he was also the first emperor of the Austrian Empire, a title he assumed in 1804, and he ruled as King of Hungary and Croatia and King of Bohemia.

Early life

Francis was the eldest son of Emperor Leopold II and Maria Luisa of Spain, and the nephew of Marie Antoinette. Born in Florence, where his father ruled as Grand Duke of Tuscany, he was brought to Vienna in 1784 by his uncle Joseph II, who oversaw his education and intended him as heir.

Reign and the Napoleonic Wars

Francis succeeded his father in 1792, just as revolutionary France declared war on Austria. His reign was dominated by the French Revolutionary and Napoleonic Wars. After repeated defeats, including at Austerlitz in 1805, Napoleon forced the final reorganisation of Germany; anticipating the empire's collapse, Francis proclaimed the hereditary Austrian Empire in 1804 and abdicated as Holy Roman Emperor in 1806, ending the thousand-year-old imperial office.

Later years

Working closely with his minister Klemens von Metternich, Francis hosted the Congress of Vienna in 1814–15 and became a leading symbol of conservative reaction in Europe, maintaining a police state and censorship in his Austrian lands. In 1810 his daughter Marie Louise married Napoleon. He died in Vienna in 1835 and was succeeded by his son Ferdinand I.
