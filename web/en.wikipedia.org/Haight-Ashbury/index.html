Haight-Ashbury

Haight-Ashbury is a district of San Francisco, California, named for the intersection of Haight and Ashbury streets. It is famed as the centre of the 1960s hippie counterculture and the 1967 "Summer of Love", when tens of thousands of young people converged on the neighbourhood.

History

Originally a streetcar suburb of Victorian houses, the area's low rents in the 1960s attracted students, artists, and musicians. Psychedelic rock bands including the Grateful Dead, Jefferson Airplane, and Janis Joplin's Big Brother and the Holding Company lived in or near the district, and the neighbourhood hosted free concerts, the Haight-Ashbury Free Clinic, and the Diggers' free stores.

Later years

Overcrowding and hard drugs strained the scene by the end of 1967, symbolically closed by the "Death of Hippie" ceremony. The district later gentrified but retains psychedelic murals, vintage shops, and its countercultural identity.
