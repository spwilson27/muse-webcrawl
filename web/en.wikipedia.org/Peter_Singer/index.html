Peter Singer

Peter Albert David Singer (born 6 July 1946) is an Australian moral philosopher, author and emeritus professor at Princeton University. He specialises in applied ethics from a utilitarian standpoint, and is best known for his work on animal rights, global poverty and bioethics.

Animal ethics

His book Animal Liberation (1975) is regarded as a founding text of the modern animal rights movement. Singer argues that the capacity to suffer, not species membership, grounds moral consideration, and coined the popular use of the term "speciesism" for discrimination against non-human animals. He supports vegetarianism and veganism and opposes factory farming.

Global poverty and effective altruism

In his 1972 essay "Famine, Affluence, and Morality", Singer argued that affluent people are obliged to donate much of their wealth to relieve suffering, using the famous drowning-child analogy. His books The Life You Can Save (2009) and The Most Good You Can Do (2015) helped inspire the effective altruism movement; he founded the organisation The Life You Can Save to promote effective charities.

Bioethics and controversy

Singer's utilitarian views on abortion, euthanasia and infanticide in cases of severe disability have drawn sustained protest and debate. He taught at Oxford, Monash and Melbourne universities before Princeton (1999–2024). He co-founded the Journal of Controversial Ideas and continues to write widely on ethics, climate change and philanthropy.
