Kinhin

Kinhin
Japanese経行
MeaningWalking meditation / sutra walk
TraditionZen Buddhism
Related practiceZazen (seated meditation)

Kinhin (Japanese: 経行) is the walking meditation practised in Zen Buddhism between periods of seated meditation (zazen). Practitioners walk slowly and mindfully around the meditation hall, synchronising steps with the breath.

Practice

The hands are held in the shashu position — the left fist, thumb enclosed, resting against the sternum and covered by the right hand. In the Sōtō school the pace is extremely slow, advancing roughly half a foot-length with each full breath; in the Rinzai school kinhin is often brisk and energetic. The walk is usually clockwise around the room.

Purpose

Kinhin relieves stiffness and drowsiness accumulated during sitting while preserving meditative attention. It is treated not as a break from meditation but as meditation in motion, applying the same awareness of posture and breath used in zazen to walking.

History

Walking meditation has roots in early Indian Buddhism, where monks walked back and forth on a designated path (caṅkama). The practice passed through Chinese Chan Buddhism into Japanese Zen, where it was formalised as part of monastic training and sesshin retreats.
