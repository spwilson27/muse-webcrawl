Yokohama (Japanese: 横浜, lit. "horizontal beach") is the capital of Kanagawa Prefecture and the most populous municipality in Japan, with about 3.7 million inhabitants. It lies on Tokyo Bay, south of Tokyo, and forms part of the Greater Tokyo Area, the world's most populous metropolitan region.

History

Yokohama was a small fishing village until 1859, when it opened as one of Japan's first treaty ports following Commodore Matthew Perry's arrival and the Convention of Kanagawa. It grew rapidly as the centre of Japan's foreign trade and silk exports, acquiring a foreign settlement, one of Japan's oldest Chinatowns, and early railways — Japan's first line, to Tokyo, opened in 1872. The city was devastated by the 1923 Great Kantō earthquake and again by firebombing in 1945, and was rebuilt after the war.

Landmarks

The Minato Mirai 21 waterfront district includes the Landmark Tower, once Japan's tallest building, and the Cosmo World Ferris wheel. Other landmarks include the Yokohama Marine Tower, the Red Brick Warehouse, Chinatown, the historic Yamate bluff district, and Yokohama Stadium. The port remains one of Japan's largest, handling a major share of the country's container traffic.
