United States Army Special Operations Command

The United States Army Special Operations Command (USASOC) is the command charged with overseeing the various special operations forces of the United States Army. Headquartered at Fort Liberty (formerly Fort Bragg), North Carolina, it is the largest component of the U.S. Special Operations Command (USSOCOM), with roughly 33,000 personnel. USASOC was activated on 1 December 1989.

Subordinate units

  • U.S. Army Special Forces Command (Airborne) — the seven Special Forces groups (the "Green Berets"), conducting foreign internal defense and unconventional warfare.
  • 75th Ranger Regiment — the Army's premier light-infantry direct-action force.
  • 160th Special Operations Aviation Regiment (Airborne) — the "Night Stalkers," providing dedicated helicopter support.
  • 1st Special Forces Operational Detachment–Delta — Delta Force, the Army's tier-one counterterrorism unit (operationally controlled by JSOC).
  • Military Information Support and Civil Affairs commands — the 4th and 8th Psychological Operations Groups, 95th Civil Affairs Brigade, and 528th Sustainment Brigade.
  • U.S. Army John F. Kennedy Special Warfare Center and School — the training and doctrine center.

Roles

Army special operations forces perform missions including unconventional warfare, foreign internal defense, special reconnaissance, direct action, counterterrorism, civil affairs, and military information support operations. Since 2001 they have been continuously deployed in Afghanistan, Iraq, Syria, Africa, and elsewhere, making USASOC one of the most heavily tasked formations in the Army. The command's soldiers are all volunteers who pass lengthy selection and qualification pipelines, including language and cultural training for Special Forces detachments.
