Grass snake

KingdomAnimalia
FamilyColubridae
GenusNatrix
SpeciesN. natrix
Binomial nameNatrix natrix (Linnaeus, 1758)

The grass snake (Natrix natrix), sometimes called the ringed snake or water snake, is a Eurasian non-venomous snake of the colubrid family. It is widely distributed across mainland Europe and into western Asia, and is strongly associated with water and wetlands.

Description

Grass snakes are typically olive green, grey or brown with dark markings along the body, and a distinctive yellow or whitish collar behind the head, bordered in black. Females are larger than males and can exceed 110 cm in length, occasionally reaching considerably more. The barred grass snake of Britain was recognised as a separate species, Natrix helvetica, in 2017.

Behaviour and diet

Grass snakes are strong swimmers and feed almost exclusively on amphibians, especially frogs and toads, which they swallow whole, together with some fish and small mammals. They are not venomous and rely on defence strategies rather than biting: a threatened grass snake may hiss, feign death — rolling onto its back with mouth open — or release a foul-smelling musk from its cloaca.

Reproduction and conservation

Females lay clutches of eggs in warm, decomposing vegetation such as compost heaps, which provide the heat needed for incubation. The species hibernates underground during winter. Grass snakes are protected by law in some countries, including the United Kingdom, where their amphibian prey and wetland habitats have declined.
