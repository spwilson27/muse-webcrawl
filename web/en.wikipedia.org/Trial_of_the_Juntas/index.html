Trial of the Juntas

Dates22 April – 9 December 1985
LocationBuenos Aires, Argentina
Defendants9 former junta members

The Trial of the Juntas (Spanish: Juicio a las Juntas) was the judicial trial of the members of the de facto military government that ruled Argentina during the dictatorship of the Proceso de Reorganización Nacional (el Proceso), which lasted from 1976 to 1983. It is often regarded as having been a landmark in the history of civil rights because it was the first time in Latin America that a civilian court had prosecuted the members of a military dictatorship for crimes against humanity committed against its own citizens. The trial took place in 1985, two years after the return of democratic rule under President Raúl Alfonsín.

Background

After taking office in December 1983, Alfonsín ordered the prosecution of the nine leading members of the first three military juntas and established the National Commission on the Disappearance of Persons (CONADEP), whose report Nunca Más ("Never Again") documented thousands of disappearances. When the Armed Forces' Supreme Council refused to convict the commanders, the case passed to the Federal Criminal Appeals Court in Buenos Aires.

The trial

The trial was largely oral and public. Prosecutor Julio César Strassera and his deputy Luis Moreno Ocampo presented around 700 cases drawn from the CONADEP record, and hundreds of witnesses testified. In his closing argument, Strassera concluded with the phrase "Nunca más" ("Never again"). On 9 December 1985 the court sentenced Jorge Rafael Videla and Emilio Eduardo Massera to life imprisonment, Roberto Eduardo Viola to 17 years, Armando Lambruschini to 8 years, and Orlando Ramón Agosti to 4½ years; the remaining four defendants were acquitted.

Legacy

The verdicts were partly undone by the pardons of President Carlos Menem in 1989–1990, but the trial remained a landmark of transitional justice and is widely credited with consolidating Argentine democracy. Its record later supported renewed prosecutions after the amnesty laws were annulled in the 2000s.
