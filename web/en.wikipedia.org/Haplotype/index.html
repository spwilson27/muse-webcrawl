Haplotype

A haplotype is a group of alleles in an organism that are inherited together from a single parent — a specific combination of variants at multiple loci on a single chromosome that tend to be transmitted as a unit.

Concepts

The term is a contraction of "haploid genotype". Because recombination breaks up haplotypes over generations, shared haplotype blocks reveal ancestry and population history. The International HapMap Project catalogued common haplotypes across human populations.

Applications

Haplotypes are used in genome-wide association studies, tracing migration and ancestry, Y-chromosome and mitochondrial haplogroups, and pharmacogenetics. See also Genotype and Linkage disequilibrium.
