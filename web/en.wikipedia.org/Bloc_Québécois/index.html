Bloc Québécois

The Bloc Québécois (BQ; French: [blɔk kebekwa]) is a federal political party in Canada devoted to Quebec nationalism and the promotion of Quebec sovereignty. It was formed in 1991 by Members of Parliament who defected from the Progressive Conservative and Liberal parties during the failure of the Meech Lake Accord, under the leadership of Lucien Bouchard. The party runs candidates only in Quebec.

History

In the 1993 federal election, the Bloc won 54 seats and formed the Official Opposition — a regional sovereigntist party holding that role being a first in Canadian history. After the narrow defeat of the sovereignty option in the 1995 Quebec referendum, the party's fortunes declined, and it was reduced to four seats in the 2011 "orange wave" election. Under Yves-François Blanchet, who became leader in 2019, the Bloc resurged to 32 seats in the 2019 and 2021 elections.

Ideology and role

The Bloc advocates Quebec sovereignty but in practice focuses on defending Quebec's interests and autonomy within federal politics, supporting social-democratic policies, environmental measures, and the protection of the French language. It frequently holds the balance of power in minority parliaments.
