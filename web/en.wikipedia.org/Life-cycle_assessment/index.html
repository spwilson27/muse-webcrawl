Life-cycle assessment

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Life-cycle assessment evaluates environmental impacts from "cradle to grave".

Life-cycle assessment (LCA), also known as life-cycle analysis, is a methodology for assessing the environmental impacts associated with all stages of the life cycle of a commercial product, process, or service—from raw material extraction ("cradle") through production, use, and disposal or recycling ("grave"). For a manufactured product, the analysis includes the energy and materials used to make its parts, packaging, transport, the consumer's use of it, and its eventual end of life.

Phases

The ISO 14040 and 14044 standards define four main phases:

  1. Goal and scope definition — specifying the product system, the functional unit (the quantified service the product provides, used as the basis of comparison), and the system boundary.
  2. Life-cycle inventory (LCI) — compiling inputs of energy and materials and outputs of emissions and wastes for each stage.
  3. Life-cycle impact assessment (LCIA) — translating inventory flows into potential impacts such as climate change, eutrophication, acidification, resource depletion and human toxicity.
  4. Interpretation — evaluating results, identifying significant issues, and drawing conclusions consistent with the goal.

Applications and criticism

LCA is used in product design (ecodesign), environmental product declarations, carbon footprinting, and policy-making. Results depend heavily on boundary choices, allocation rules for multi-output processes, and data quality, so comparative LCAs intended for public claims are subject to critical review. LCA does not by itself capture economic or social impacts, though life-cycle costing and social LCA extend the framework.
