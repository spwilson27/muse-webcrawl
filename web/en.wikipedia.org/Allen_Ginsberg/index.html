Allen Ginsberg

Irwin Allen Ginsberg (June 3, 1926 – April 5, 1997) was an American poet and writer. As a student at Columbia University in the 1940s, he began friendships with William S. Burroughs and Jack Kerouac, forming the core of the Beat Generation. He vigorously opposed militarism, economic materialism, and sexual repression, and he embodied various aspects of this counterculture with his views on drugs, sex, multiculturalism, hostility to bureaucracy, and openness to Eastern religions.

Best known for his poem “Howl”, Ginsberg denounced what he saw as the destructive forces of capitalism and conformity in the United States. San Francisco police and US Customs seized copies of the poem, and the 1957 obscenity trial of its publisher, Lawrence Ferlinghetti, drew widespread publicity. In “Howl”, Ginsberg criticized the United States and described encounters with mental illness, including that of his mother, Naomi, who was confined to a mental institution.

Biography

Ginsberg was born into a Jewish family in Newark, New Jersey, and grew up in nearby Paterson. His father, Louis Ginsberg, was a schoolteacher and published poet; his mother, Naomi, suffered from paranoid schizophrenia. As a young man he attended Columbia University, where he met Kerouac, Burroughs, and Neal Cassady.

Beat Generation and “Howl”

On October 7, 1955, Ginsberg performed the first part of “Howl” at the Six Gallery reading in San Francisco, an event often regarded as the launch of the Beat movement in the public eye. Published in 1956 as part of Howl and Other Poems, the poem's first line—“I saw the best minds of my generation destroyed by madness”—became famous.

Later life

Ginsberg traveled widely, experimented with and later renounced heavy drug use, and became a practicing Buddhist. He co-founded the Jack Kerouac School of Disembodied Poetics at Naropa Institute in Boulder, Colorado. His later collections include Kaddish and Other Poems (1961) and The Fall of America (1973), which won the National Book Award. He died in New York City in 1997 of liver cancer.
