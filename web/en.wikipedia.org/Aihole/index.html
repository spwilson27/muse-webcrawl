Aihole

LocationBagalkot district, Karnataka, India
SignificanceEarly Chalukya temple architecture, c. 5th–8th centuries CE
MonumentsOver 120 Hindu, Jain and Buddhist temples and cave shrines
Famous structuresDurga temple, Lad Khan temple, Meguti Jain temple, Ravanaphadi cave

Aihole is a historic village and archaeological site in the Bagalkot district of Karnataka, India, on the banks of the Malaprabha river. Sometimes called the "cradle of Indian temple architecture", it preserves more than 120 stone temples and cave shrines built mainly under the Early Chalukya dynasty between about the 5th and 8th centuries CE, experimenting with the forms that later matured at Badami and Pattadakal.

Monuments

The apsidal Durga temple (named after the nearby fort, durg, not the goddess) blends a chaitya-hall plan with a Nagara-style shikhara and a colonnaded ambulatory. The Lad Khan temple, among the oldest, resembles a village assembly hall with heavy stone lattice windows. The rock-cut Ravanaphadi cave contains celebrated Shaivite sculpture, including dancing Shiva and the Saptamatrikas. On Meguti hill stands a Jain temple bearing the famous Aihole inscription (prashasti) of Pulakeshin II, composed by the court poet Ravikirti and dated to 634–635 CE, which records Chalukya victories, including over Harshavardhana.

History

Aihole served as an early capital of the Chalukyas before Badami, and as a centre of the Aihole guild of merchants and artisans (the Ayyavole guild), whose network traded across the Deccan and beyond. The site is protected by the Archaeological Survey of India and is visited together with Badami and the UNESCO-listed Pattadakal.
