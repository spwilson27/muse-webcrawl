Viktor Andriyovych Yushchenko (born 23 February 1954) is a Ukrainian politician who served as the third president of Ukraine from 2005 to 2010. A former central banker and prime minister, he came to office after the Orange Revolution that followed the disputed 2004 presidential election.

Banking and prime ministership

Yushchenko trained as an economist and worked in the Soviet banking system. As governor of the National Bank of Ukraine from 1993, he oversaw the introduction of the hryvnia in 1996 and helped stabilise the currency after hyperinflation. Appointed prime minister in 1999, his government implemented economic reforms and began repaying wage and pension arrears before he was dismissed by parliament in 2001.

2004 election and dioxin poisoning

During the 2004 presidential campaign against Viktor Yanukovych, Yushchenko fell seriously ill; medical tests later confirmed dioxin poisoning, which left his face disfigured. The election runoff was marred by fraud, and mass protests in Kyiv forced a Supreme Court-ordered re-vote, which Yushchenko won.

Presidency (2005–2010)

Yushchenko's presidency pursued closer ties with the European Union and NATO and commemorated the Holodomor as genocide, but was marked by persistent conflict with Prime Minister Yulia Tymoshenko and by the effects of the 2008 financial crisis and gas disputes with Russia. He lost the 2010 election, receiving about five per cent of the vote in the first round.
