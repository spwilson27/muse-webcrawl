Consumer rights

Consumer rights include the right to redress when goods are faulty.

Consumer rights are the legal and moral entitlements of people who buy goods and services, protected through consumer protection law. The modern articulation of these rights is commonly traced to a speech by United States President John F. Kennedy on 15 March 1962, now marked as World Consumer Rights Day.

The basic rights

Kennedy identified four rights: the right to safety, the right to be informed, the right to choose, and the right to be heard. Consumers International later expanded the list to eight, adding the rights to satisfaction of basic needs, to redress, to consumer education, and to a healthy environment. These principles informed the United Nations Guidelines for Consumer Protection, first adopted in 1985 and expanded since.

Legal protection

In practice, consumer rights are implemented through rules on product safety and liability, prohibitions on misleading advertising and unfair contract terms, cooling-off periods for distance and doorstep selling, warranties and rights to repair or refunds, and regulation of credit and financial services. The European Union maintains an extensive body of consumer law, and in the United Kingdom the Consumer Rights Act 2015 consolidated much of the law on goods, services, and digital content.

Enforcement is shared among regulators, trading standards bodies, ombudsmen, courts, and consumer organisations such as Consumer Reports.
