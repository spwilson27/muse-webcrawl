Cognitive distortion

Cognitive distortions are exaggerated or irrational thought patterns that are hypothesised to perpetuate psychological states, especially depression and anxiety. The concept was developed within cognitive therapy by Aaron T. Beck and popularised in self-help form by his student David D. Burns.

Common types

Frequently listed distortions include all-or-nothing thinking (seeing things in black-and-white categories), overgeneralisation (drawing sweeping conclusions from a single event), mental filtering (dwelling on negatives while ignoring positives), jumping to conclusions (mind reading and fortune telling), catastrophising (magnifying the importance of negative events), personalisation (assuming excessive responsibility or blame), emotional reasoning (treating feelings as evidence of truth), and "should" statements directed at oneself or others.

Role in therapy

Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) teaches clients to identify automatic negative thoughts, examine the evidence for and against them, and generate more balanced alternatives — a process called cognitive restructuring. Identifying distortions is a standard early step. Critics note that the distortion framework can be overly individualistic, and that some catalogued "distortions" overlap with ordinary cognitive biases studied in judgement and decision-making research.
