The River Lee (Irish: An Laoi) is a river in County Cork, Ireland. It rises in the Shehy Mountains on the Cork–Kerry border, close to Gougane Barra, and flows eastwards for about 90 kilometres through the city of Cork before entering the sea at Cork Harbour, one of the largest natural harbours in the world.

Course

From its source the Lee passes Lough Allua and Ballingeary, then flows through a deep glacial valley impounded by two hydroelectric reservoirs, Carrigadrohid and Inniscarra, built in the 1950s to supply Cork with electricity and water. Below Inniscarra Dam the river becomes tidal and enters Cork, where it divides into north and south channels around the island on which the city centre stands, a pattern reflected in the city's name — Corcaigh, "marshy place".

Flooding and ecology

The Lee has flooded Cork repeatedly, most severely in November 2009, when discharges from Inniscarra during extreme rainfall contributed to extensive inundation of the city centre. A major flood relief scheme has since been designed for the lower Lee. The river supports salmon, brown trout, and lamprey, and its estuarine mudflats in Cork Harbour are protected as a special protection area for birds.
