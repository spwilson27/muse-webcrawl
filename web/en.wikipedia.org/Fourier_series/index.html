Fourier series
Introduced byJoseph Fourier (1807, published 1822)
FieldHarmonic analysis, signal processing
RelatedFourier transform, DFT

A Fourier series is an expansion of a periodic function into a sum of trigonometric functions. It is named after French mathematician Joseph Fourier, who showed that representing a function as a sum of sines and cosines greatly simplifies the study of heat transfer.

Definition

For a periodic function f(x) with period 2π, the Fourier series is f(x) = a₀/2 + Σ [aₙ cos(nx) + bₙ sin(nx)], where the coefficients are found by integrating f(x) against cos(nx) and sin(nx) over one period. In complex form, f(x) = Σ cₙ einx with cₙ = (1/2π) ∫ f(x) e−inx dx.

History

Fourier presented his ideas in 1807 in a memoir on heat conduction, controversial at the time because Lagrange objected that arbitrary functions could not be so represented. The theory was published in the Théorie analytique de la chaleur (1822). Dirichlet later gave precise conditions for convergence (1829).

Properties and applications

Partial sums converge to the function at points of continuity and to the midpoint at jump discontinuities, with overshoot near jumps known as the Gibbs phenomenon. Fourier series underpin signal processing, acoustics, image compression, solving partial differential equations, and quantum mechanics.
