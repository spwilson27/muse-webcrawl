Ligurian cuisine

Ligurian cuisine is the traditional cooking of Liguria, the narrow coastal region of northwestern Italy centred on Genoa. It is a Mediterranean cuisine built on olive oil, herbs, vegetables, and seafood, and is famous as the home of pesto and focaccia.

Signature dishes

Pesto alla genovese — basil, pine nuts, garlic, Parmigiano-Reggiano, Pecorino, and Ligurian extra-virgin olive oil pounded to a sauce — traditionally dresses trofie or trenette pasta served with green beans and potatoes. Other staples include focaccia (including the cheese-filled focaccia di Recco), farinata (a chickpea-flour flatbread), pansoti with walnut sauce, cappon magro (an elaborate seafood and vegetable salad), stuffed vegetables, and dishes of anchovies and other fish from the Ligurian Sea. The region's delicate, slightly grassy olive oil and wines such as Vermentino and Pigato accompany the food.

Character

Ligurian cooking reflects a mountainous coastline with small terraced farms: it makes intensive use of wild herbs, garden vegetables, chestnuts, and preserved foods carried by Genoese sailors. It is often described as one of Italy's most refined regional cuisines despite its humble ingredients.
