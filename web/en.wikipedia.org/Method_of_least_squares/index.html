Method of least squares

Least squares
Published byAdrien-Marie Legendre (1805)
Used earlier byCarl Friedrich Gauss (from 1795)
FieldStatistics, regression analysis

The method of least squares is a standard approach in regression analysis to approximate the solution of overdetermined systems — sets of equations with more equations than unknowns — by minimising the sum of the squares of the residuals made in the results of each individual equation. The most important application is in data fitting, where the best fit to a dataset reduces exactly to least squares.

History

Adrien-Marie Legendre published the method in 1805 in connection with determining the orbits of comets. Carl Friedrich Gauss claimed to have used it since 1795 and applied it in 1801 to predict the position of the dwarf planet Ceres; he published his account in 1809, provoking a priority dispute. Gauss later connected the method to the normal distribution of errors, giving it a probabilistic foundation.

Formulation

For a linear model with parameters estimated from observations, the least-squares solution satisfies the normal equations and, under the Gauss–Markov assumptions, gives the best linear unbiased estimator. Extensions include weighted least squares, total least squares, non-linear least squares, and regularised variants such as ridge regression. The method underlies much of statistics, geodesy, signal processing, and machine learning.
