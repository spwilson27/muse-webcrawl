Rump (animal)

Rump (animal)

FieldAnimal anatomy
RegionPosterior dorsal part of the trunk
Related termsCroup (horses), uropygium (birds)

The rump or croup, in the external morphology of an animal, is the portion of the posterior dorsum — that is, behind the loins and in front of the tail. Anatomically, the rump corresponds to the sacrum and the musculature overlying the pelvis. The term is used in descriptions of quadrupeds such as horses, cattle and dogs, where the shape and slope of the croup are important points of conformation judged in breeding and showing.

In horses

In equine anatomy the croup runs from the lumbosacral joint to the tailhead. A level, well-muscled croup is generally desired; a steep ("goose") rump or an excessively flat croup can affect gait and jumping ability. The highest point of the croup may stand level with or above the withers in young horses.

In birds

In ornithology, the rump is the area above the tail and below the back, often a key field mark in identification — for example, the white rump of the northern harrier or the wheatears. The uropygium, bearing the oil gland, lies at the base of the tail.

In other animals

Rump colouration plays a signalling role in some mammals: the white rump patch of pronghorn flares when the animal is alarmed. In meat animals, the rump gives rise to valued cuts of beef, and "rump" is itself the name of a cut taken from this region.
