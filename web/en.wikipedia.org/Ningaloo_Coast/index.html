The Ningaloo Coast is a World Heritage-listed area on the remote western coast of Australia, in the state of Western Australia. Its centrepiece, Ningaloo Reef, is one of the world's largest fringing coral reefs, stretching some 260 kilometres along the North West Cape peninsula and lying unusually close to shore—in places just a few metres from the beach.

The reef supports about 300 species of coral and more than 500 species of fish. It is best known for seasonal aggregations of whale sharks, which arrive to feed on plankton blooms following the autumn coral spawning, and for visiting manta rays and humpback whales.

Conservation

The area includes Cape Range National Park and the Ningaloo Marine Park, near the town of Exmouth. The Ningaloo Coast was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2011 for its superlative natural beauty and biological diversity, including subterranean cave fauna in the limestone of Cape Range. A major development proposal for a salt works at Exmouth Gulf was rejected in the 2000s after public campaigning under the 'Save Ningaloo' banner.
