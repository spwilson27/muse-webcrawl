Quadjet

A quadjet is a jet aircraft powered by four engines. Four-engine designs were common in the early jet age, when individual engines were relatively low-powered and less reliable, and extra engines provided the thrust and redundancy needed for long overwater routes.

Design

Quadjets typically mount their engines either in pods under the wings (as on the Boeing 747 and Airbus A380) or in pairs at the rear fuselage or tail (as on the Ilyushin Il-62 and Vickers VC10). Four engines offer a lower workload per engine and graceful degradation after a failure, but carry penalties in fuel burn, maintenance and complexity compared with twin-engine designs.

Examples

  • Boeing 747 — the original "jumbo jet", in production 1968–2022
  • Airbus A340 and A380
  • Boeing 707, Douglas DC-8 and Convair 880/990 (first-generation jetliners)
  • Avro RJ / BAe 146 — a four-engine regional jet
  • Antonov An-124 and Lockheed C-5 Galaxy military transports
  • De Havilland Comet — the first commercial jetliner

Decline

Improvements in engine reliability and the extension of ETOPS rules to long-haul twins allowed twinjets such as the Boeing 777 and Airbus A350 to take over most long-haul roles. Production of the A380 ended in 2021 and of the 747 in 2022, leaving quadjets mainly in cargo, military and head-of-state service.
