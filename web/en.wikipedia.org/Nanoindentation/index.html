Nanoindentation

Nanoindentation, also called instrumented indentation testing, is a hardness test applied to small volumes: a tip of known geometry (commonly a Berkovich diamond) is pressed into a material while load and displacement are continuously recorded, yielding elastic modulus and hardness at nanometre scales.

Nanoindentation
Measures: Hardness, elastic modulus
Tip: Berkovich, Vickers, spherical
Analysis: Oliver–Pharr method

Method

The Oliver–Pharr method (1992) extracts contact stiffness from the unloading curve to compute contact area, hardness H = P/A and reduced modulus. Continuous stiffness measurement varies load dynamically for depth profiles.

Applications

Thin films, coatings, polymers, biomaterials, MEMS and single grains or phases in alloys — anywhere conventional hardness tests lack spatial resolution.
