Ascospore

An ascospore is a spore contained in an ascus, the sac-like cell in which sexual reproduction occurs in fungi of the phylum Ascomycota. Ascospores are formed by the process of "free cell formation" inside the ascus after meiosis and, in most species, a further mitotic division.

Formation

During sexual reproduction, a dikaryotic ascogenous hypha gives rise to an ascus in which the two parental nuclei fuse (karyogamy), immediately followed by meiosis. Typically eight ascospores result within a single ascus, though the number ranges from one to over a thousand depending on the species. The spores are delimited by membranes and develop specialised, often ornamented, walls.

Release and germination

In many ascomycetes the ascus acts as a tiny pressure gun, forcibly discharging its spores into the air when mature; in others, such as truffles, the spores are released when the fruiting body is eaten or decays. On landing on a suitable substrate, an ascospore germinates to produce a new haploid mycelium.

Significance

Ascospore size, shape, colour and septation are important characters in fungal taxonomy. Heat-resistant ascospores of some moulds can survive pasteurisation and cause spoilage of fruit products.
