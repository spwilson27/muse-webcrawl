Hot desking

Hot desking
TypeWorkplace organisation system
Origin of termNaval "hot racking"
RelatedHotelling, activity-based working

Hot desking (sometimes "hotdesking") is a workplace organisation system in which multiple workers use a single physical workstation during different time periods, rather than having an assigned desk. The term derives from the naval practice of "hot racking", in which sailors on different shifts share bunks. The practice became common in the 1990s and expanded greatly with hybrid working after 2020.

Operation

Employees typically book a desk through software or take any free workstation, keeping personal items in lockers. A related model, hotelling, requires advance reservation. Employers adopt hot desking to cut real-estate costs when desks would otherwise sit empty due to travel, shifts or remote work.

Reception

Surveys frequently find employee dissatisfaction: time lost finding desks and equipment, reduced sense of belonging and team cohesion, and hygiene concerns. Supporters cite cost savings, flexibility and opportunities for cross-team contact. Successful implementations usually provide booking systems, adequate meeting space and neighbourhoods where teams sit together.
