Ethiopian Red Terror

Amharic nameQey Shibir (ቀይ ሽብር)
Date1976–1978 (peak 1977–78)
PerpetratorThe Derg regime of Mengistu Haile Mariam
TargetsOpposition movements (chiefly the EPRP), students, suspected sympathizers
Estimated deathsTens of thousands to several hundred thousand

The Ethiopian Red Terror, known in Amharic as Qey Shibir, was a violent political campaign carried out by the Derg, the Marxist military government of Ethiopia under Mengistu Haile Mariam, against rival political groups and civilians between roughly 1976 and 1978. It followed the 1974 Ethiopian Revolution that deposed Emperor Haile Selassie.

Background

After seizing power, the Derg faced armed and political opposition from groups including the Ethiopian People's Revolutionary Party (EPRP), which accused the regime of betraying the revolution and responded with assassinations of officials — a campaign sometimes called the "White Terror." In early 1977 Mengistu consolidated control of the Derg and publicly proclaimed a campaign of "revolutionary defence" against counter-revolutionaries.

The campaign

Urban dwellers' associations (kebeles) and newly formed defence squads were armed and authorized to identify, arrest, and kill alleged opponents. Night-time executions were common; victims' bodies were frequently left in public places, and families were sometimes charged a fee — notoriously, the price of the bullet — to reclaim bodies for burial. Universities and secondary schools were heavily targeted, and tens of thousands of young people were killed, imprisoned, or fled. Estimates of the death toll vary widely, from around 30,000 to figures of 500,000 or more advanced by opposition and human-rights groups; Amnesty International estimated at least 150,000.

Aftermath

The Red Terror decimated the EPRP and other opposition organizations, though armed insurgencies in Eritrea and Tigray continued and ultimately brought down the Derg in 1991. After the regime's fall, Mengistu fled to Zimbabwe; Ethiopian courts tried Derg officials for genocide and other crimes, and in 2006 Mengistu was convicted in absentia. A Red Terror Martyrs' Memorial Museum opened in Addis Ababa in 2010 to commemorate the victims.
