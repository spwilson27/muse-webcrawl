The Ordnance ML 3 inch mortar was the standard medium mortar of the British Army and Commonwealth forces during the Second World War and for some years afterwards. It provided battalion- and brigade-level indirect fire support, firing high-explosive, smoke, and illuminating bombs.

Design

Like most infantry mortars of its era, the weapon was a muzzle-loaded, smooth-bore, high-angle-of-fire weapon derived from the Stokes mortar of the First World War. It consisted of a barrel, a bipod with elevating and traversing gear, and a baseplate to absorb recoil. The crew of three loaded bombs through the muzzle; the bomb's tail cartridge struck a fixed firing pin at the base of the barrel. Early marks had a maximum range of only about 1,460 metres (1,600 yards), but a stronger barrel and improved charges in later marks roughly doubled this.

Service

The 3-inch mortar served in every theatre in which British and Commonwealth forces fought, from the deserts of North Africa to the jungles of Burma. Mortar platoons were integral to infantry battalions, and in larger operations mortars were massed to provide concentrations of fire. Post-war it remained in service into the 1960s before being replaced by the 81 mm L16 mortar.
