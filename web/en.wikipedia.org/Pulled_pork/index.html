Pulled pork

Pulled pork is a method of cooking pork in which a tough cut of meat, usually the shoulder (often sold as pork butt or Boston butt), is slow-cooked at low temperature until the connective tissue breaks down and the meat becomes tender enough to be easily "pulled" or shredded into strands. It is a signature preparation of American barbecue, particularly in the Southern United States.

Preparation

The pork shoulder is typically coated with a dry rub of salt, sugar, paprika, and spices, then cooked for many hours in a smoker over hardwood such as hickory or oak, or in an oven or slow cooker. Internal temperatures near 195–205 °F (90–96 °C) render the collagen into gelatin, allowing the meat to be shredded by hand or with forks. The cooking liquid, drippings, or a finishing sauce may be mixed back into the shredded meat.

Regional styles

In eastern North Carolina, a whole hog or shoulder is dressed with a thin vinegar-and-red-pepper sauce. In the Lexington style of western North Carolina, only shoulders are used and served with a tomato-tinged vinegar dip. South Carolina's "Carolina Gold" uses a mustard-based sauce. Memphis and Kansas City versions commonly finish with sweeter tomato-based barbecue sauces, while Alabama sometimes pairs pulled pork with a white mayonnaise-based sauce.

Serving

Pulled pork is most often served on a soft bun as a sandwich, frequently topped with coleslaw, and accompanied by pickles, hushpuppies, or baked beans. It is also used as a filling for tacos and burritos, a topping for nachos and baked potatoes, and a component of Brunswick stew and hash.
