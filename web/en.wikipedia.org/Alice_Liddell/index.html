Alice Liddell

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Born: 4 May 1852, Westminster, London, England

Died: 16 November 1934 (aged 82), Westerham, Kent, England

Known for: Inspiration for Alice's Adventures in Wonderland

Spouse: Reginald Hargreaves

Alice Pleasance Liddell (4 May 1852 – 16 November 1934), known in adult life as Alice Hargreaves, was an English woman who, as a young girl, was the friend and photographic subject of Charles Lutwidge Dodgson — Lewis Carroll — and the principal inspiration for the heroine of his books Alice's Adventures in Wonderland (1865) and Through the Looking-Glass (1871).

Early life

Alice was the fourth of ten children of Henry Liddell, dean of Christ Church, Oxford, and co-editor of the great Greek–English lexicon. The Liddell family became friends with Dodgson, a mathematics lecturer at the college, who often took Alice and her sisters Lorina and Edith on outings. On a boat trip up the Thames on 4 July 1862, Dodgson improvised the story that became Alice's Adventures in Wonderland; at Alice's request he wrote it out, presenting her with a handwritten, self-illustrated copy titled Alice's Adventures Under Ground in November 1864. Dodgson also photographed Alice and her siblings many times, producing some of the most famous images of Victorian childhood. The family's friendship with Dodgson cooled after 1863 for reasons that remain debated.

Later life

In 1880 Alice married Reginald Hargreaves, a cricketer and former pupil of Dodgson's, and settled at Cuffnells in the New Forest. They had three sons, two of whom were killed in the First World War. After her husband's death, the cost of maintaining Cuffnells forced her to sell the manuscript of Alice's Adventures Under Ground at Sotheby's in 1928, where it fetched £15,400. In 1932, for the centenary of Carroll's birth, she travelled to New York and received an honorary doctorate from Columbia University. She died in 1934.
