International Wushu Federation

The International Wushu Federation (IWUF) is the international governing body for the sport of wushu, the modern competitive form of Chinese martial arts. Founded in Beijing in 1990, the federation oversees the standardised taolu (forms) and sanda (sparring) disciplines and organises the World Wushu Championships.

History

Efforts to standardise wushu for international competition began in China in the 1980s. The IWUF was officially established on 3 October 1990 in Beijing, and the first World Wushu Championships were held in Beijing the following year. The federation gained recognition from the International Olympic Committee in 2002 and became a member of the Association of IOC Recognised International Sports Federations.

Competitions

The flagship event is the biennial World Wushu Championships, covering both taolu routines — judged performances of forms such as changquan, nanquan, taijiquan and weapon events — and sanda, a full-contact fighting discipline fought on a raised platform called a leitai. The federation also runs World Cups, junior championships and traditional kung fu festivals.

Structure

The IWUF is composed of continental federations for Asia, Europe, Africa, the Americas and Oceania, with roughly 150 national member federations. Its congress meets every two years in conjunction with the world championships.
