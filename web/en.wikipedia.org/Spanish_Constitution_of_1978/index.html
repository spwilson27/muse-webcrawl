Spanish Constitution of 1978

Created1978
Ratified6 December 1978 (referendum)
Effective29 December 1978
SystemParliamentary constitutional monarchy

The Spanish Constitution of 1978 is the supreme law of the Kingdom of Spain. It was enacted after a referendum on 6 December 1978, during the Spanish transition to democracy following the death of Francisco Franco, and it established Spain as a parliamentary constitutional monarchy.

Drafting

The text was drafted by a committee of seven deputies, known as the padres de la Constitución, drawn from across the political spectrum. The constitution declares Spain a social and democratic state subject to the rule of law, guarantees fundamental rights, and proclaims the indissoluble unity of the Spanish nation while recognising the right to self-government of the nationalities and regions that compose it.

Structure

The constitution is divided into a preliminary title and ten parts covering rights and duties, the Crown, the Cortes Generales, government, the judiciary, territorial organisation, and the Constitutional Court. It has been amended only a few times since 1978.
