The Book of Jeremiah is the second of the Major Prophets in the Christian Old Testament and part of the Latter Prophets in the Hebrew Bible. It records the words and career of the prophet Jeremiah, active from the thirteenth year of Josiah (c. 627 BCE) through the fall of Jerusalem to Babylon in 586 BCE and beyond.

Jeremiah warned Judah that its covenant-breaking would bring Babylonian conquest; he was persecuted, imprisoned and lowered into a cistern for his message. His "confessions" — anguished personal prayers — are among the most intimate passages in prophetic literature, and his letter to the exiles ("seek the welfare of the city") and promise of a new covenant (chapter 31) became central texts for Judaism and Christianity alike.

Text and structure

The book exists in two forms: the longer Hebrew (Masoretic) text and a shorter Greek (Septuagint) version about one-eighth shorter, with the oracles against the nations placed differently. Qumran fragments show both forms circulated in antiquity. Much of the narrative prose is associated with the circle of Baruch son of Neriah, Jeremiah's scribe, who wrote out the prophet's scroll — which King Jehoiakim cut up and burned.
