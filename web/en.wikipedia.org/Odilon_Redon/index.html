Odilon Redon

Odilon Redon (born Bertrand Redon; 20 April 1840 – 6 July 1916) was a French Symbolist artist. He is known for his pastels, prints, and charcoal drawings, which explore dreamlike and fantastic subjects drawn from literature, mythology, and natural science.

Redon's charcoal "noirs" explored dreamlike imagery.

Early work: the noirs

After studying in Bordeaux and Paris, Redon worked for decades in black — charcoal and lithography — producing his noirs, images of disembodied heads, eyeball balloons, and hybrid creatures. His lithograph albums included Dans le Rêve (1879) and homages to Edgar Allan Poe and Gustave Flaubert.

Later colour work

From the 1890s Redon turned increasingly to colour, producing luminous pastels and oils of flowers, butterflies, and serene female profiles, as well as decorative panels for patrons such as Baron Robert de Domecy. His work influenced the Nabis and was celebrated by the Surrealists as a precursor of dream art.
