Finno-Ugric languages

Finno-Ugric is a traditional grouping of the languages in the Uralic language family other than the Samoyedic languages. Its principal branches are Ugric (Hungarian and the Ob-Ugric languages Khanty and Mansi), Finnic (including Finnish and Estonian), Sami, Mordvinic, Mari, and Permic (including Komi and Udmurt).

Speakers and distribution

The Finno-Ugric languages have around 25 million speakers, the great majority of them Hungarian (about 13 million), Finnish (about 5 million), and Estonian (about 1 million). They are spoken across a vast, discontinuous area from Norway and the Baltic in the west, through Finland and European Russia, to western Siberia. Hungarian is geographically separated from its closest relatives, Khanty and Mansi, spoken along the Ob River.

Classification debate

Traditional scholarship treated Finno-Ugric as a primary branch of Uralic, coordinate with Samoyedic, reconstructing a Proto-Finno-Ugric language. Many modern Uralicists question the validity of some intermediate nodes, including Finno-Ugric itself, and regard several branches as having split off successively from Proto-Uralic; the label remains in widespread use as a convenient collective term. Characteristic features shared by many of these languages include agglutinative morphology, extensive case systems, postpositions, and the absence of grammatical gender.
