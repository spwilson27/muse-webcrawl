Mátyás Rákosi

Mátyás Rákosi
Born: 9 March 1892, Ada, Austria-Hungary
Died: 5 February 1971, Gorky, USSR
Office: General Secretary of the Hungarian Working People's Party; de facto leader of Hungary (1945–1956)

Mátyás Rákosi (1892–1971) was a Hungarian communist politician who led the Hungarian People's Republic from 1945 to 1956 as the country's Stalinist dictator. He proudly described his method of destroying non-communist opposition as "salami tactics" — slicing it away piece by piece.

Rise to power

A veteran of the 1919 Hungarian Soviet Republic, Rákosi spent sixteen years in Horthy's prisons before being exchanged to the Soviet Union in 1940. He returned with the Red Army in 1945 and, as party leader, consolidated power through manipulated elections, the forced merger of parties and show trials, including that of László Rajk in 1949.

Rule and fall

His regime imposed rapid nationalisation, collectivisation and purges, and built a personality cult around him as "Stalin's best pupil". After Stalin's death he lost the premiership to Imre Nagy but clung to the party leadership until July 1956, when Soviet pressure forced his removal — a prelude to the Hungarian Revolution of 1956. He lived in exile in the Soviet Union until his death.
