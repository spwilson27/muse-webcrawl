Tombs of the Kings (Jerusalem)

Tombs of the Kings
Hebrew: קברי המלכים, Kivrei HaMelakhim
Location: East Jerusalem
Period: 1st century AD
Associated with: Helena of Adiabene

The Tombs of the Kings (Hebrew: קברי המלכים, Kivrei HaMelachim) are a rock-cut funerary complex in Jerusalem, located in the Sheikh Jarrah neighbourhood north of the Old City walls. Despite their name, the tombs are not those of the kings of Judah but are believed to belong to Queen Helena of Adiabene and her family, converts to Judaism who ruled the kingdom of Adiabene in the 1st century AD.

History

The complex was constructed around AD 50. Josephus records that Helena, having converted to Judaism, came to Jerusalem and built palaces there, and that she and her sons were buried in pyramids she had erected north of the city. The sarcophagi found within — including one bearing a bilingual inscription naming "Queen Saddan" — are now held in the Louvre Museum in Paris.

Description

A monumental stairway descends into a sunken courtyard before a facade carved into the bedrock, decorated with a frieze of fruit and foliage. Behind it lies a labyrinth of chambers with kokhim (burial niches), arcosolia and stone sarcophagi — some thirty chambers in total, making it one of the largest ancient tomb complexes in Jerusalem.

Ownership

The site was purchased in the 19th century by the French Jewish Pereire family and later transferred to the French government; it is administered by the French Consulate. The tombs, long closed to the public, reopened for visits in 2019.
