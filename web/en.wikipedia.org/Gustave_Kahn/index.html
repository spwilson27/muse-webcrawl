Gustave Kahn

Kahn in the 1890s, during the height of the symbolist movement.

Gustave Kahn (21 December 1859 – 5 September 1936) was a French Symbolist poet and art critic. He is best known as the principal theorist and populariser of vers libre (free verse) in French poetry, claiming — alongside Jules Laforgue — to have invented the form.

Career

Born in Metz, Kahn studied in Paris and spent several years in North Africa with the army before joining the literary circles of the capital. In 1886 he co-founded the review La Vogue, which published Rimbaud's Illuminations, and later directed Le Symboliste with Jean Moréas and Paul Adam. His first collection, Les Palais nomades (1887), was the first book of poems in sustained free verse. His essay-preface to that volume laid out a theory of rhythm based on the musical phrase rather than fixed metre.

Other work

Kahn also wrote novels, including Le Roi fou (1896), art criticism on the Impressionists and Post-Impressionists, and memoirs of the symbolist era, Symbolistes et décadents (1902). He championed Seurat and the Neo-Impressionists and wrote extensively on Jewish themes later in life.
