Adjoint representation

In mathematics, the adjoint representation is a way of representing a Lie group or Lie algebra as linear transformations of its own Lie algebra. For a Lie group G, it is the action of G on its Lie algebra by conjugation.

Definition

For g in G, define Ad(g)(X) = d/dt at t=0 of g exp(tX) g-1. The derivative at the identity gives the adjoint representation ad of the Lie algebra, ad(X)(Y) = [X, Y].

Properties

The kernel of Ad is the centre of G. The image lies in the automorphism group of the Lie algebra. The Killing form is invariant under the adjoint action.

Applications

Adjoint representations are fundamental to root systems, the classification of semisimple Lie algebras, and gauge theory in physics. See also Lie group, Lie algebra and Killing form.
