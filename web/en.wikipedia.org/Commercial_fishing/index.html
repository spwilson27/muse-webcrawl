Commercial fishing

Commercial fishing is the activity of catching fish and other seafood for commercial profit, mostly from wild fisheries. It supplies a large share of the world's animal protein, employing tens of millions of people directly and supporting processing, distribution, and shipbuilding industries. China, Indonesia, Peru, India, and Russia are among the largest producers.

Methods

Major methods include trawling (dragging nets through the water or along the seabed), purse seining (encircling schools of fish), longlining (baited hooks on lines kilometres long), gillnetting, and trapping in pots. Factory ships can process and freeze catches at sea for months at a time.

Economics and regulation

Because wild fish stocks are a common-pool resource, governments regulate catches through quotas, licences, closed seasons, and exclusive economic zones established under the law of the sea. Subsidies, illegal and unreported fishing, and disputes over quotas remain persistent problems.

Sustainability

Overfishing has depleted many major stocks, prompting certification schemes, marine protected areas, and a rapid growth of aquaculture, which now supplies more than half of the fish eaten by humans.
