CARICOM

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Caribbean Community, commonly known as CARICOM, is an intergovernmental organisation of Caribbean states founded in 1973 to promote economic integration, cooperation and foreign-policy coordination among its members. It comprises fifteen member states and five associate members, most of them island nations in the Caribbean Sea, together with Belize in Central America and Guyana and Suriname in South America.

History

CARICOM was established by the Treaty of Chaguaramas, signed on 4 July 1973 by Barbados, Guyana, Jamaica and Trinidad and Tobago, succeeding the earlier Caribbean Free Trade Association (CARIFTA). The Revised Treaty of Chaguaramas (2001) created the CARICOM Single Market and Economy (CSME), intended to allow the free movement of goods, services, capital and, for certain categories of workers, people.

Institutions

Supreme authority rests with the Conference of Heads of Government. The CARICOM Secretariat is based in Georgetown, Guyana. Associated institutions include the Caribbean Court of Justice (inaugurated 2005), the University of the West Indies, the Caribbean Development Bank and the Caribbean Examinations Council.

Members

Full members include Antigua and Barbuda, the Bahamas, Barbados, Belize, Dominica, Grenada, Guyana, Haiti, Jamaica, Montserrat, Saint Kitts and Nevis, Saint Lucia, Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, Suriname, and Trinidad and Tobago, representing a combined population of roughly 19 million.
