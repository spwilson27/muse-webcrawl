The Holocaust in Lithuania

The Holocaust in Lithuania resulted in the near-total destruction of Lithuanian Jews (Litvaks) living under Nazi occupation from June 1941 to 1945. Of roughly 208,000–210,000 Jews, an estimated 190,000–195,000 were murdered, one of the highest victim rates in Europe.

Course of events

Following the German invasion of the Soviet Union (Operation Barbarossa), Einsatzgruppen and Lithuanian auxiliaries carried out mass shootings, notably at Ponary (Paneriai) near Vilnius and the Ninth Fort near Kaunas. Ghettos were established in Vilnius, Kaunas, Šiauliai and Švenčionys; most were liquidated by 1943–44, survivors deported to concentration camps in Estonia and Latvia or to Stutthof.

Collaboration and rescue

Historians debate the extent of Lithuanian collaboration; units such as the Ypatingasis būrys participated in killings, while other Lithuanians rescued Jews — over 900 Lithuanians are recognized as Righteous Among the Nations. See also The Gulag Archipelago.
