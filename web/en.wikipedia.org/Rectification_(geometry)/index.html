Rectification (geometry)

Hero image (placeholder):

In Euclidean geometry, rectification, also known as complete truncation, is an operation that can be performed on a regular polytope or tiling. It truncates a polytope at the midpoints of its edges, resulting in a new polytope whose vertices lie at the edge midpoints of the original. The operation is represented in Schläfli symbols by the letter r.

Examples

Rectifying a cube produces a cuboctahedron, while rectifying an octahedron also produces a cuboctahedron, since the two polyhedra are duals. Rectifying a tetrahedron yields an octahedron. A second rectification is called birecification. Rectification can be applied in any dimension to polytopes and to honeycombs and tilings.

Notation

In Conway polyhedron notation, rectification is denoted by a for ambo. The rectified form of a regular polytope {p, q} has vertex configuration p.q.p.q.
