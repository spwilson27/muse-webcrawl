Protein NMR

Protein nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy (protein NMR) is a technique of structural biology in which NMR spectroscopy is used to obtain information about the structure and dynamics of proteins, nucleic acids and their complexes.

Protein NMR

Related: NMR spectroscopy, structural biology

Method

Isotopically labelled protein (¹³C, ¹⁵N) in solution is placed in a strong magnetic field. Multidimensional spectra (HSQC, triple-resonance experiments) assign resonances to atoms; NOE distance restraints, dihedral angles and residual dipolar couplings are used to calculate an ensemble of structures.

Advantages and limits

Unlike X-ray crystallography, NMR studies proteins in solution and reveals dynamics, but size limits (historically ~25–35 kDa, extended by TROSY and deuteration) apply. Kurt Wüthrich shared the 2002 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for developing the method.
