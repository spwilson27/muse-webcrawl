Life Is a Dream

Life Is a Dream (Spanish: La vida es sueño) is a Spanish-language play by the playwright Pedro Calderón de la Barca, first performed in 1635 and published in 1636. It is regarded as one of the masterpieces of the Spanish Golden Age theatre and of Baroque literature generally.

Plot

Prince Segismundo of Poland has been imprisoned in a tower since birth because astrologers prophesied he would become a cruel tyrant and bring disaster to the kingdom. His father, King Basilio, decides to test the prophecy: Segismundo is drugged, brought to the palace, and told he is the prince. His violent, uncontrolled behaviour appears to confirm the prophecy, and he is returned to the tower and told the palace experience was a dream. Later freed by rebels, Segismundo — reflecting that even in dreams one should do good — shows mercy, forgives his father, and rules wisely.

Themes

The play explores the conflict between free will and predestination, the illusory nature of earthly life, honour, and self-knowledge. Segismundo's famous soliloquy concludes that all life is a dream, and dreams themselves are only dreams.

Legacy

The work has been widely translated and adapted, influencing later drama and philosophy, and remains a staple of Spanish classical theatre repertoires worldwide.
