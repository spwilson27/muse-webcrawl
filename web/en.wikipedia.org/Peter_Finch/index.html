Peter Finch

Frederick George Peter Ingle Finch (28 September 1916 – 14 January 1977) was an English-Australian actor of theatre, film and radio. He won the Academy Award for Best Actor for his portrayal of the unravelling television news anchor Howard Beale in Network (1976) — the first actor to win an acting Oscar posthumously.

Early life and career

Born in London, Finch was raised in France, India and Australia, where he began acting after working in various jobs. He was spotted by Laurence Olivier during an Australian tour and brought to London, becoming a stage star in the late 1940s.

Film career

Finch's films include A Town Like Alice (1956), The Nun's Story (1959), The Trials of Oscar Wilde (1960), No Love for Johnnie (1961), Sunday Bloody Sunday (1971) — for which he received an Oscar nomination for a pioneering sympathetic portrayal of a gay man — and The Abdication (1974). He won five BAFTA Awards for Best Actor, a record for the category in his day.

Death and posthumous award

Finch died of a heart attack in Beverly Hills in January 1977 while promoting Network. His widow Eletha accepted his Academy Award on stage — his "I'm as mad as hell, and I'm not going to take this anymore!" speech in Network is ranked among the most famous in film history.
