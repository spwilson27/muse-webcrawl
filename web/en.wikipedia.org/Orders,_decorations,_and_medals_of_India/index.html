Orders, decorations, and medals of India

Indian honours system
Country: Republic of India
Highest civilian award: Bharat Ratna
Highest military award: Param Vir Chakra

The orders, decorations, and medals of India form the Indian honours system, by which the Republic of India recognises distinguished service by civilians and members of the armed forces and police. The system comprises civilian awards, gallantry awards, distinguished service decorations, and campaign and service medals.

Civilian awards

The highest civilian award is the Bharat Ratna, instituted in 1954, followed by the Padma awards: Padma Vibhushan (exceptional and distinguished service), Padma Bhushan (distinguished service of a high order) and Padma Shri (distinguished service in any field). The Padma awards are announced annually on the eve of Republic Day.

Military awards

Wartime gallantry awards head the military hierarchy: the Param Vir Chakra is India's highest military decoration for conspicuous bravery in the presence of the enemy, followed by the Maha Vir Chakra and Vir Chakra. Peacetime gallantry is recognised by the Ashoka Chakra, Kirti Chakra and Shaurya Chakra. Distinguished service decorations include the Param Vishisht Seva Medal and its counterparts, and the Sena, Nao Sena and Vayu Sena Medals.

Other decorations

Police and fire services have their own medals, including the President's Police Medal for Gallantry. Campaign medals commemorate participation in operations such as those of 1965, 1971 and the Kargil conflict, and long-service medals mark years of service.
