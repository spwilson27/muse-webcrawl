Probability distribution

In probability theory and statistics, a probability distribution is the mathematical function that gives the probabilities of occurrence of possible outcomes for an experiment or random phenomenon. It fully describes a random variable.

Terminology

A distribution is discrete if its outcomes are countable (e.g. die rolls), described by a probability mass function; examples include the binomial, Poisson and geometric distributions. A distribution is continuous if outcomes lie on a continuum, described by a probability density function whose integral over an interval gives the probability of that interval; the normal (Gaussian), exponential and uniform distributions are continuous. The cumulative distribution function F(x) = P(X ≤ x) exists for all cases.

Properties

Distributions are summarised by measures such as mean (expected value), median, variance and standard deviation, skewness and kurtosis, and by moment-generating and characteristic functions. Joint, marginal and conditional distributions extend the concept to multiple variables. The central limit theorem explains why the normal distribution arises so widely in nature: sums of many independent random effects tend towards normality.

Applications

Probability distributions underpin statistical inference, hypothesis testing, reliability engineering, actuarial science, finance and machine learning.
