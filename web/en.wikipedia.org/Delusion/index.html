Delusion

Delusions are a cardinal symptom of psychotic disorders such as schizophrenia and delusional disorder.
Delusion
SpecialtyPsychiatry
SymptomsFixed false belief held despite contradictory evidence
Associated conditionsSchizophrenia, delusional disorder, bipolar disorder, dementia, substance use

A delusion is a fixed belief that is not amenable to change in light of conflicting evidence. As a pathology, it is distinct from a belief based on false or incomplete information, confabulation, dogma, illusion, hallucination, or other effects of perception, and from culturally shared religious or political convictions. Delusions are a hallmark feature of psychosis and occur in schizophrenia, delusional disorder, the manic phase of bipolar disorder, dementia, and substance-induced states. A person with a delusion will hold firmly to the belief regardless of evidence to the contrary; delusions can occur in illogical forms ("bizarre delusions") or be plausible but false ("non-bizarre delusions").

Types

Common delusional themes include persecutory delusions (belief that one is being targeted or harmed — the most common type), grandiose delusions (inflated worth, power or identity), erotomanic delusions (belief that another person, often of higher status, is in love with one), somatic delusions (concerning bodily function or sensations), delusions of reference, and delusions of control. Named syndromes include Capgras delusion (belief that a familiar person has been replaced by an impostor), Cotard delusion (belief that one is dead or does not exist), and folie à deux (shared delusions).

Causes and theories

Karl Jaspers classified "true" delusions as primary phenomena not understandable from prior experience. Modern theories invoke aberrant salience and dopamine dysregulation, predictive-processing failures, jumping-to-conclusions reasoning biases, and deficits in theory of mind. The philosopher-psychiatrist distinction between form and content — the delusional form being universal while content is culturally shaped — remains influential.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is clinical, under criteria such as those of the DSM-5. Treatment targets the underlying disorder, commonly with antipsychotic medication; psychological approaches include cognitive behavioral therapy for psychosis (CBTp), which gently tests belief flexibility rather than directly confronting the belief.
