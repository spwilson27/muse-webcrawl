Möbius transformation

In geometry and complex analysis, a Möbius transformation of the complex plane is a rational function of the form f(z) = (az + b)/(cz + d), where a, b, c, d are complex numbers satisfying ad − bc ≠ 0. Möbius transformations are named after the German mathematician August Ferdinand Möbius.

Properties

A Möbius transformation can be extended to the Riemann sphere, where it acts as a bijection. The set of all Möbius transformations forms a group under composition, isomorphic to the projective special linear group PSL(2, ℂ). These transformations preserve angles (they are conformal) and map the set of circles and lines to itself — circles to circles, lines to lines, with lines treated as circles through infinity.

Classification

According to their fixed points and trace, Möbius transformations are classified as elliptic, parabolic, hyperbolic, or loxodromic. Every Möbius transformation is a composition of translations, rotations and scalings, and inversions in the unit circle.

Applications

Möbius transformations are fundamental in hyperbolic geometry, where they give the isometries of the Poincaré disc model, and in electrical engineering through the Smith chart. They also appear in special relativity, where the restricted Lorentz group is isomorphic to the Möbius group.
