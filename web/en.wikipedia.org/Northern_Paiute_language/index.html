The Northern Paiute language is a member of the Western Numic branch of the Uto-Aztecan language family, traditionally spoken by the Northern Paiute people of the Great Basin in what is now eastern California, western Nevada, south-eastern Oregon and southern Idaho. It is closely related to Mono and is traditionally described as a dialect chain stretching from the Mono Lake–Owens Valley area north to the Burns Paiute community in Oregon.

Features

Like other Numic languages, Northern Paiute is agglutinative, with complex verb morphology and a system of consonant alternation (gemination and spirantisation) at morpheme boundaries that is a hallmark of the family. Historically, speakers were highly mobile hunter-gatherer bands, and regional varieties differ mainly in vocabulary and pronunciation.

Endangerment and revitalisation

Colonisation, forced schooling and boarding-school policies sharply reduced intergenerational transmission during the twentieth century; most fluent speakers are now elders. Tribal communities including the Pyramid Lake Paiute Tribe, the Burns Paiute Tribe and the Fort McDermitt Paiute and Shoshone Tribe have established language classes, master–apprentice programmes and digital archives to revitalise the language.
