Madame de Pompadour

Jeanne-Antoinette Poisson, Marquise de Pompadour (29 December 1721 – 15 April 1764), commonly known as Madame de Pompadour, was a member of the French court and the official chief mistress of King Louis XV from 1745 until her death in 1764. Even after she ceased to be the king's lover, she remained an influential adviser and patron for nearly two decades.

Rise at court

Born into a bourgeois Parisian family and educated to a high standard, she was introduced to the king at a masked ball at Versailles in 1745. Louis made her his mistress and granted her the title Marquise de Pompadour, along with an estate. Beautiful, intelligent, and accomplished in music and art, she quickly became indispensable to the king.

Influence

Pompadour exerted real political influence, championing ministers and policies and promoting the alliance with Austria that preceded the Seven Years' War. She was an outstanding patron of the arts and letters: she protected Voltaire and the contributors to the Encyclopédie, supported painters such as François Boucher, and founded the Sèvres porcelain manufactory. Her taste shaped the Rococo style in France.

Legacy

She died of tuberculosis at Versailles, aged 42. Though resented by many courtiers and blamed in popular pamphlets for royal policy and extravagance, historians credit her with substantial cultural and political significance. The famous phrase "Après nous, le déluge" is attributed to her.
