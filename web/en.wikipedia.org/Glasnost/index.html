Glasnost
Meaning"Openness" (Russian: гласность)
Introduced byMikhail Gorbachev, 1985–86
Related policyPerestroika

Glasnost (Russian: гласность, "openness" or "publicity") is a concept relating to openness and transparency. It has been used in Russian politics since at least the end of the 18th century, but became internationally famous as the slogan of Soviet leader Mikhail Gorbachev, who introduced a policy of increased openness and transparency in government institutions and activities in the Soviet Union in the second half of the 1980s.

Policy

Gorbachev announced glasnost alongside perestroika (restructuring) after becoming General Secretary of the Communist Party in 1985. The policy loosened censorship, encouraged public discussion of corruption, bureaucracy and historical crimes, and freed many political prisoners and dissidents, including Andrei Sakharov in 1986. Reporting on the Chernobyl disaster of April 1986, initially delayed, became a catalyst for more open media coverage.

Effects

Under glasnost, previously suppressed works were published, archives were partially opened, and debates about Stalinism, the economy and national grievances in the republics entered the open press. Historians argue that glasnost, by exposing the system's failures and unleashing nationalist movements, contributed significantly to the revolutions of 1989 in Eastern Europe and the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991 — outcomes Gorbachev had not intended.
