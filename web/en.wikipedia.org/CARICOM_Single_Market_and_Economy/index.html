CARICOM Single Market and Economy

The CARICOM Single Market and Economy (CSME) is an integrated development strategy envisioned at the 10th Meeting of the Conference of Heads of Government of the Caribbean Community (CARICOM) in 1989, intended to create a single economic space among member states.

Background

The CSME was established by the Revised Treaty of Chaguaramas, signed in 2001, which replaced the original 1973 treaty founding CARICOM. Its goal is the full integration of national markets into one unified market in which goods, services, capital and skilled labour move freely.

The Single Market

The CARICOM Single Market was formally inaugurated on 30 January 2006 in Kingston, Jamaica, with an initial group of member states; others joined later that year. Key elements include the free movement of goods, services, capital, and certain categories of skilled nationals, a common external tariff, and harmonised laws.

The Single Economy

The single economy phase, envisaging harmonised fiscal and monetary policies and eventually a single currency, has proceeded more slowly and remains incomplete. Implementation has been uneven, with freedom of movement gradually expanded to additional categories of workers.
