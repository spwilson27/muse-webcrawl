Fatah (Arabic: فتح, Fatḥ, "conquest"), formally the Palestinian National Liberation Movement, is a Palestinian nationalist social democratic political party and the largest faction of the confederated Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO). The name is a reverse acronym of the Arabic Ḥarakat al-Taḥrīr al-Waṭanī al-Filasṭīnī ("Palestinian National Liberation Movement"); read forward, fatḥ also evokes the early Islamic conquests.

History

Fatah was founded in Kuwait in 1959 by members of the Palestinian diaspora, including Yasser Arafat, Khalil al-Wazir (Abu Jihad), and Salah Khalaf (Abu Iyad). Its armed wing, al-Assifa, launched its first operation against Israel on 1 January 1965. After the 1967 Six-Day War, Fatah's stand at the Battle of Karameh in 1968 greatly raised its prestige, and in 1969 Arafat became chairman of the PLO, which Fatah thereafter dominated. The movement relocated from Jordan to Lebanon after Black September in 1970–71, and to Tunisia after Israel's 1982 invasion of Lebanon.

Peace process and governance

In 1993 Fatah, through the PLO, signed the Oslo Accords with Israel, renouncing armed struggle and recognizing Israel's right to exist; Arafat became president of the newly created Palestinian Authority in 1994. Fatah governed the Palestinian territories until losing the 2006 legislative elections to Hamas, after which it retained control of the West Bank while Hamas took Gaza in 2007. Mahmoud Abbas, Fatah's leader since Arafat's death in 2004, serves as President of the State of Palestine.
