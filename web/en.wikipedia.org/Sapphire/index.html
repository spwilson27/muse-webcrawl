Sapphire

A cut blue sapphire.

Sapphire is a precious gemstone, a variety of the mineral corundum (aluminium oxide, α-Al2O3). Sapphires are typically blue, though they occur in nearly every colour — yellow, pink, green, purple, orange, and colourless — owing to trace elements such as iron, titanium, chromium, and vanadium. Red corundum is classified separately as ruby. Sapphire is the birthstone for September and a traditional gift for 45th anniversaries.

Properties

Corundum has a hardness of 9 on the Mohs scale, making sapphires suitable for everyday jewellery and for industrial uses. The most prized colour is a vivid, velvety cornflower blue; stones from Kashmir, Burma (Myanmar), and Sri Lanka are especially valued. Some sapphires show asterism — a star effect caused by needle-like rutile inclusions — and are cut as cabochons. The rare orange-pink variety is called padparadscha.

Sources

Historic sources include Sri Lanka (Ceylon), Myanmar's Mogok valley, and Kashmir; modern production comes from Australia, Madagascar, Thailand, Vietnam, and Montana in the United States. Famous stones include the Star of India (563 carats) and the Logan Sapphire.

Synthetic sapphire

Synthetic corundum has been produced since 1902 by the Verneuil flame-fusion process. Because of its hardness and optical transparency, synthetic sapphire is used for watch crystals, scratch-resistant windows, barcode scanners, and substrates for blue LEDs and semiconductor devices. Sapphire wafers underpin much of the gallium nitride industry.
