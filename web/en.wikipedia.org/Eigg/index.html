Eigg (Scottish Gaelic: Eige) is one of the Small Isles in the Scottish Inner Hebrides, lying south of the Isle of Skye and north of the Ardnamurchan peninsula. It is known for its dramatic geology, its wildlife, and its pioneering community ownership and renewable-energy system.

Geography and history

The island is dominated by An Sgùrr, a dramatic ridge of columnar pitchstone lava, the largest of its kind in Europe. Eigg has been inhabited since the Bronze Age; it was a centre of early Christianity associated with St Donnán, martyred with his monks in 617, and later passed between clan control and a succession of private landlords. In the 1990s, after years of neglect under an eccentric owner, islanders, the Scottish Wildlife Trust and Highland Council formed the Isle of Eigg Heritage Trust, which bought the island on 12 June 1997 — one of Scotland's landmark community buyouts.

Energy and community

In 2008 Eigg launched the world's first community-owned, off-grid electricity system combining wind, hydro, solar power and battery storage (with diesel backup), supplying the whole island and capping household consumption. The project won international awards and made Eigg a model for community renewables. The island is car-limited for visitors, reached by Caledonian MacBrayne ferries from Mallaig, and its economy rests on crofting, tourism and small businesses.
