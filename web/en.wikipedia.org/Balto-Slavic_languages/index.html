Balto-Slavic languages

The Balto-Slavic languages form a branch of the Indo-European language family, traditionally comprising the Baltic languages (Lithuanian, Latvian, and the extinct Old Prussian) and the Slavic languages. Most Indo-Europeanists accept that Baltic and Slavic descend from a common Proto-Balto-Slavic proto-language.

Shared features

Balto-Slavic is supported by a series of shared phonological and morphological innovations, including a distinctive accentual system (preserved in Lithuanian and parts of Slavic), Winter's law, Hirt's law, and the merger of Proto-Indo-European short o and a. The group also shares a large body of common vocabulary not found in other Indo-European branches.

History

Proto-Balto-Slavic is usually dated to the second millennium BCE, spoken around the upper Dnieper and Vistula basins. The Baltic and Slavic branches diverged over the following centuries; Slavic expanded dramatically across eastern and central Europe in the 6th–7th centuries CE, while Baltic territory contracted to the eastern Baltic coast, where Lithuanian and Latvian survive today.
