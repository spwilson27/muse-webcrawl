Lorelei

The Lorelei (also spelled Loreley) is a steep slate rock on the right bank of the River Rhine near St. Goarshausen in Rhineland-Palatinate, Germany, rising some 132 metres above the water. The narrow, fast-flowing stretch below the rock was long hazardous to shipping, and the place is famous for an echo effect.

In German legend the Lorelei is a siren-like maiden who sat on the rock combing her golden hair, distracting sailors so that their boats wrecked on the rocks. The legend was popularised by Clemens Brentano's ballad (1801) and Heinrich Heine's poem "Die Lore-Ley" (1824), the latter set to music by Friedrich Silcher in 1837 and now one of Germany's best-known folk songs.

The rock lies within the Upper Middle Rhine Valley UNESCO World Heritage Site.
