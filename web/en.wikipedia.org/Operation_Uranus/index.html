Operation Uranus

Soviet armoured forces advance during the counteroffensive at Stalingrad.

Date19–23 November 1942
LocationNear Stalingrad, Soviet Union
ResultSoviet victory; encirclement of the German 6th Army
Soviet commandersGeorgy Zhukov, Aleksandr Vasilevsky, Konstantin Rokossovsky, Nikolay Vatutin, Andrey Yeryomenko

Operation Uranus (Russian: Операция «Уран») was the codename for the Soviet counteroffensive of 19–23 November 1942 during the Battle of Stalingrad. In a double envelopment, the Red Army smashed through the weaker Romanian armies guarding the flanks of the German 6th Army and linked up at Kalach-on-Don, trapping some 300,000 Axis soldiers inside Stalingrad.

Planning

The plan was developed in September 1942 by Generals Georgy Zhukov and Aleksandr Vasilevsky. While the German 6th Army under Friedrich Paulus was ground down in street fighting inside the city, the Soviets secretly amassed over a million men, some 1,500 tanks and nearly 1,400 aircraft on the flanks, where the overstretched Romanian Third and Fourth Armies held the line with inadequate anti-tank weapons.

The offensive

On 19 November 1942, after a massive artillery bombardment, the Southwestern and Don Fronts attacked from bridgeheads across the Don River. The following day the Stalingrad Front attacked from the south. Romanian resistance collapsed within days, and on 23 November Soviet spearheads met at Kalach, closing the ring. Attempts to relieve the pocket — notably Operation Winter Storm — failed, and the encircled 6th Army surrendered in early February 1943.

Significance

Operation Uranus is widely regarded as a turning point of the Second World War on the Eastern Front. It demonstrated the maturity of Soviet operational art — deep battle conducted at army-group scale — and shifted the strategic initiative decisively to the USSR.
