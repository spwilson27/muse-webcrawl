Proportional navigation

Proportional navigation is a guidance law used by most homing air target missiles. It is based on the fact that two vehicles are on a collision course when their direct line-of-sight does not change direction as the range closes. The missile commands acceleration proportional to the rotation rate of the line of sight multiplied by the closing velocity, steering to maintain a constant bearing to the target.

Principle

In true proportional navigation, the commanded acceleration equals N × Vc × (line-of-sight rate), where N is the navigation constant (typically 3 to 5) and Vc is the closing speed. The missile's velocity vector rotates at a rate proportional to the line-of-sight rotation, converging on an interception course that generally requires less maneuvering than pure pursuit.

Applications

Proportional navigation and its variants underlie the guidance of many air-to-air and surface-to-air missiles. The principle also appears in nature: predators such as dragonflies and falcons use motion-camouflage interception strategies analogous to constant-bearing approach.
