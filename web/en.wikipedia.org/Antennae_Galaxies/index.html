Antennae Galaxies

The Antennae Galaxies (also known as NGC 4038/NGC 4039 or Caldwell 60/61) are a pair of interacting galaxies in the constellation Corvus. They are currently going through a starburst phase, in which the collision of clouds of gas and dust, with entangled magnetic fields, causes rapid star formation. They were discovered by William Herschel in 1785.

General information

The Antennae Galaxies are undergoing a galactic collision. Located in the NGC 4038 group of galaxies, they lie about 45 to 65 million light-years from Earth. Originally two separate spiral galaxies, they began interacting a few hundred million years ago, making them one of the nearest and youngest examples of a pair of colliding galaxies. Their nuclei are in the process of joining to become one giant nucleus, and long tidal tails of stars and gas — which give the system its antenna-like appearance — stretch hundreds of thousands of light-years into space.

Starburst and future

The collision has triggered the formation of millions of new stars in dense super star clusters. Computer simulations suggest the Antennae are a preview of the future collision between the Milky Way and the Andromeda Galaxy; the pair will eventually merge into a single elliptical galaxy.
