The Phasianidae are a family of heavy, ground-living birds in the order Galliformes, which includes pheasants, partridges, junglefowl, chickens, turkeys, Old World quail, and peafowl. The family is large, with about 185 species divided into 54 genera, native mainly to the Old World (Europe, Asia, Africa) with turkeys and some quail in the Americas. Several members are among the world's most important domestic and game birds.

Characteristics

Phasianids are generally plump birds with short, rounded wings adapted for brief, explosive flight, strong legs for scratching the ground in search of seeds, shoots, and invertebrates, and short, stout bills. Many show strong sexual dimorphism: males of pheasants and peafowl are brilliantly coloured with elaborate tails and ornaments, while females are cryptically brown. Sizes range from the tiny Asian blue quail (~10 cm) to the Indian peafowl, whose males exceed 2 m including the train.

Taxonomy

Modern classifications divide the family into subfamilies including Phasianinae (pheasants and allies), Pavoninae (peafowl, junglefowl, and in many treatments turkeys and grouse following molecular studies), and Rollulinae (partridge-like birds of Asia and Africa). The domestic chicken descends primarily from the red junglefowl (Gallus gallus), and the turkey from the wild turkey of North America.

Relationship with humans

The chicken is the world's most numerous domestic bird, central to global food production. Pheasants, partridges, and grouse are important game birds, widely introduced outside their native ranges for shooting. Conversely, habitat loss and hunting have left many wild phasianids threatened — the family contains a disproportionate share of endangered bird species, including several Asian pheasants and tragopans.
