Yajna

Yajna (Sanskrit: yajña, 'sacrifice, devotion, worship') is a central ritual of Vedic religion and Hinduism: an offering, typically of ghee, grains and other substances into a consecrated fire, accompanied by the chanting of mantras.

Meaning and history

In the Vedas, yajna sustains the cosmic order (rita) and the gods, to whom Agni, the fire god, carries offerings. The concept was internalised in later texts — the Bhagavad Gita treats selfless action itself as sacrifice — and elaborate royal rites such as the Ashvamedha (horse sacrifice) and Rajasuya marked sovereignty, while daily domestic fire offerings (agnihotra) continue in some households.

Practice

Modern yajnas range from small home homas to large public ceremonies performed for prosperity, health or world peace, conducted by priests around a fire altar with precise recitation.
