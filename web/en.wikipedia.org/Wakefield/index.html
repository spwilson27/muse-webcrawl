Wakefield

Wakefield is a cathedral city in West Yorkshire, England, on the River Calder. Historically the county town of the West Riding of Yorkshire and a market and woollen-textile centre, it is the administrative seat of the City of Wakefield metropolitan district. The city's population was about 109,000 at the 2021 census.

History

The Battle of Wakefield in 1460, during the Wars of the Roses, was fought at nearby Sandal Magna; Richard, Duke of York, was killed in the battle. Wakefield grew as an inland port on the navigable Calder and a centre of the wool and grain trades; its Georgian prosperity is reflected in buildings around Wood Street, and Wakefield Cathedral's crocketed spire — at 247 feet the tallest in Yorkshire — dominates the city. The district later became a coal-mining area, and the city was long known for liquorice growing and for the Wakefield Mystery Plays, a cycle of medieval biblical dramas revived in modern times.

Culture

Wakefield forms the "Yorkshire Sculpture Triangle" with Leeds, Halifax, and the Yorkshire Sculpture Park at nearby West Bretton; The Hepworth Wakefield gallery, opened in 2011 and named for sculptor Barbara Hepworth, who was born in the city, anchors its waterfront regeneration. Sculptor Henry Moore was born in nearby Castleford.
