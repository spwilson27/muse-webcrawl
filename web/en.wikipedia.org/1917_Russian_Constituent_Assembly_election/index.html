1917 Russian Constituent Assembly election

1917 Constituent Assembly election
Date: 25 November 1917 (main voting day)
Turnout: ~64% (of ~80 million eligible in many districts)
Largest party: Socialist Revolutionary Party (~40%)
Bolsheviks: ~24%

The 1917 Russian Constituent Assembly election was held on 25 November 1917 (with voting spread over several weeks in some regions), to elect the Russian Constituent Assembly promised after the February Revolution. It is regarded as the first free election in Russian history, with universal suffrage including women.

Results

More than 40 million votes were counted. The Socialist Revolutionary Party (SRs) won a plurality with roughly 40 percent, drawing on peasant support, while the Bolsheviks took about 24 percent, dominating in Petrograd, Moscow, the Baltic Fleet and the armies on the northern and western fronts. The liberal Kadets and the Mensheviks polled poorly.

Dissolution

The election took place after the Bolsheviks had seized power in the October Revolution. When the Assembly finally met in Petrograd on 18 January 1918, it refused to recognise Soviet authority; Lenin's government dispersed it after a single day, ending Russia's brief experiment with parliamentary democracy.
