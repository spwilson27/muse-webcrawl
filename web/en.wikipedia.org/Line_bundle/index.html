Line bundle

In mathematics, a line bundle is a vector bundle of rank one: a space which locally looks like the product of a base space X with a one-dimensional vector space (a "line"), but which may be twisted globally. Line bundles are among the simplest vector bundles, yet they carry rich information about the geometry of the base space and are central objects in differential geometry, algebraic geometry and topology.

Formally, a (real or complex) line bundle is a vector bundle π : E → X whose fibres are one-dimensional vector spaces, with structure group GL(1) — the nonzero scalars. Line bundles are classified by transition functions taking values in this group, and isomorphism classes of complex line bundles over a space X are classified by the first Chern class in the cohomology group H²(X; ℤ).

Examples

The canonical example of a nontrivial real line bundle is the Möbius bundle over the circle: its total space is the (open) Möbius strip. The tautological line bundle over projective space — whose fibre over a point (a line through the origin) is that line itself — is the fundamental example in algebraic geometry, usually denoted O(−1); its dual O(1) is the hyperplane bundle, an ample line bundle whose powers give embeddings into projective space.

Operations and the Picard group

Line bundles can be tensored together and dualised, and under tensor product the isomorphism classes of line bundles on a variety form an abelian group called the Picard group, Pic(X). The trivial bundle is the identity element, and dualisation gives inverses. On a smooth projective curve the Picard group splits into the degree map and the Jacobian variety; line bundles of degree zero correspond to points of the Jacobian.

Sections and divisors

A global section of a line bundle on an algebraic variety vanishes along an effective divisor, and (on smooth varieties) this sets up a correspondence between line bundles up to isomorphism and divisor classes, giving the isomorphism between the Picard group and the divisor class group. A line bundle with "enough" sections is called very ample if its sections embed the variety into projective space, or ample if some positive tensor power does so.

Related notions

In differential geometry, a line bundle's curvature and Chern class measure its twisting; the canonical bundle of a complex manifold — the top exterior power of the cotangent bundle — is a distinguished line bundle governing integration and, in string theory, the Calabi–Yau condition. A linearisation of a group action is an action lifted to a line bundle, fundamental in geometric invariant theory.
