Coordination complex

A coordination complex is a chemical compound consisting of a central atom or ion, usually a metal, bonded to a surrounding array of molecules or ions known as ligands. The ligands donate electron pairs to the metal through coordinate (dative covalent) bonds. Coordination complexes are central to inorganic chemistry, catalysis, and biology: haemoglobin, chlorophyll, vitamin B12, and many industrial catalysts are coordination complexes.

Structure and bonding

The number of donor atoms attached to the metal is the coordination number, commonly 4 or 6. Geometries include octahedral (6), tetrahedral and square planar (4), and linear (2). Ligands may be monodentate (water, ammonia, chloride), bidentate (ethylenediamine), or polydentate (EDTA); multidentate ligands form chelate complexes of enhanced stability (the chelate effect). Bonding is described by crystal field theory and ligand field theory, which explain the colours and magnetism of complexes through the splitting of the metal's d-orbitals.

History

Alfred Werner's coordination theory (1893) — postulating primary and secondary valences and specific spatial arrangements — displaced the chain theory of Blomstrand and Jørgensen and earned Werner the 1913 Nobel Prize in Chemistry. The theory explained isomerism in complexes such as the cobalt ammines: identical compositions with different properties, including geometric (cis/trans) and optical isomers.

Isomerism and nomenclature

Complexes display ionisation, linkage, geometric, and optical isomerism. Systematic names list ligands alphabetically before the metal, with prefixes (di-, tri-, tetra-) and suffixes indicating charge — for example [Co(NH3)5Cl]Cl2 is pentaamminechloridocobalt(III) chloride.

Applications

Coordination compounds serve as catalysts (hydroformylation, olefin polymerisation, cross-coupling), pigments (Prussian blue), analytical reagents, metal-extraction agents, and drugs — most famously the anticancer agent cisplatin. EDTA is widely used to sequester metal ions in water treatment and medicine (chelation therapy).
