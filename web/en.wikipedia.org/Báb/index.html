Báb

Born: ʿAlí Muḥammad, October 20, 1819, Shiraz, Qajar Persia

Died: July 9, 1850 (aged 30), Tabriz, Qajar Persia

Known for: Founder of Bábism; central figure of the Baháʼí Faith

The Báb (born ʿAlí Muḥammad Shírází; October 20, 1819 – July 9, 1850) was an Iranian religious leader who founded Bábism and is a central figure of the Baháʼí Faith. He took the title "Báb" (Arabic for "Gate") and, in 1844, declared himself the bearer of a new revelation from God, superseding Islamic law and preparing humanity for the imminent appearance of "Him Whom God shall make manifest", a promised messianic figure.

Life

Born to a merchant family of Shiraz descended from Muhammad through Husayn, ʿAlí Muḥammad worked in the family trading business before turning to religious study. On May 22, 1844, he declared his mission to Mullá Husayn, the first of the "Letters of the Living", his initial disciples. His teachings spread rapidly through Persia, alarming both the Shi'a clergy and the Qajar state.

Teachings

The Báb's principal works include the Qayyúmu'l-Asmáʼ and the Persian Bayán, which set out a new religious calendar, laws and social teachings, and emphasized the coming of a greater figure after him. Baháʼís regard him as the forerunner of Baháʼu'lláh and an independent Manifestation of God in his own right.

Imprisonment and execution

The Báb was arrested, exiled within Persia and imprisoned in the fortresses of Maku and Chihriq in Azerbaijan. As Bábí uprisings broke out, the government ordered his execution. On July 9, 1850 he was shot by a firing squad in the barracks square of Tabriz. His remains were later interred, after decades of concealment, in the Shrine of the Báb on Mount Carmel in Haifa, Israel.
