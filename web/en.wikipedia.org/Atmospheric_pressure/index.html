Atmospheric pressure

Atmospheric pressure

Standard sea-level value: 101,325 Pa (1 atm)

Other units: 760 mmHg; 1,013.25 mbar

Measured by: Barometer

Atmospheric pressure, also known as air pressure or barometric pressure, is the pressure within the atmosphere of Earth. It is the force per unit area exerted by the weight of the column of air above a given point. The standard atmosphere (atm) is defined as 101,325 pascals, the average pressure at mean sea level.

Variation with altitude

Atmospheric pressure decreases with increasing elevation, approximately exponentially: near sea level it falls by about 1 hPa for every 8–9 metres of ascent, and at 5.5 km altitude it is roughly half its sea-level value. Because of this, aircraft altimeters infer height from pressure, and boiling points of liquids fall with altitude.

Weather

Local variations in pressure are central to meteorology. Areas of low pressure are associated with rising air, cloud, and unsettled weather; high-pressure areas with sinking air and clear skies. Weather maps chart pressure using isobars, and the difference in pressure between two locations — the pressure gradient — drives wind.

Measurement and history

The mercury barometer was invented by Evangelista Torricelli in 1643, demonstrating both atmospheric pressure and the possibility of a vacuum. His contemporary Blaise Pascal showed that pressure fell with height by having a barometer carried up the Puy de Dôme in 1648. Modern instruments include aneroid barometers and electronic sensors.
