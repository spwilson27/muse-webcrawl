Battle of the Neva

Date15 July 1240
LocationConfluence of the Izhora and Neva rivers, near modern Saint Petersburg
ResultNovgorodian victory
BelligerentsNovgorod Republic vs. Swedish forces
CommandersPrince Alexander Yaroslavich

The Battle of the Neva was fought on 15 July 1240 between the army of the Novgorod Republic, led by the young Prince Alexander Yaroslavich, and a Swedish expeditionary force that had landed at the mouth of the Izhora river where it joins the Neva. The Novgorodian victory checked the Swedish advance and earned Alexander his famous epithet "Nevsky" — "of the Neva".

Alexander's surprise attack on the Swedish camp, July 1240.

Background

Sweden and Novgorod had long competed for control of Finland and the lands around the Gulf of Finland, with Novgorod seeking to protect its trade routes to the Baltic. In the summer of 1240 a Swedish fleet — in some accounts accompanied by Norwegian and Finnish contingents — sailed into the Neva, and its troops encamped near the Izhora confluence, apparently intending to seize the Neva mouth and advance on Ladoga.

The battle

Alexander, only about twenty years old, moved swiftly with a small force of Novgorodians and Ladogans. According to the Novgorod chronicle and Alexander's later Life, he attacked without waiting for reinforcements from his father in Vladimir, falling on the enemy camp in a surprise assault. The Swedes were driven back to their ships with heavy losses; tradition holds that Alexander personally wounded the Swedish leader. The scale of the battle is debated by historians, since Swedish sources do not mention it, but its place in Russian tradition is immense.

Aftermath

Two years later Alexander defeated the Livonian Knights at the Battle on the Ice on Lake Peipus (1242). Together the victories secured Novgorod's western frontier for a generation and made Alexander Nevsky a national hero and, eventually, an Orthodox saint.
