Queen regnant

A queen regnant (plural: queens regnant) is a female monarch who rules a kingdom in her own right, as opposed to a queen consort, who is the wife of a reigning king, or a queen regent, who rules temporarily on behalf of a child monarch. A queen regnant possesses and exercises sovereign authority equal to that of a king, is typically crowned and anointed in her own right, and is addressed as "queen" — a word that in many languages is simply the feminine form of the word for a ruler.

History

Queens regnant have arisen where succession laws permitted female inheritance, either generally or in the absence of male heirs. Examples span ancient Hatshepsut of Egypt (who ruled as pharaoh), Wu Zetian, the only female emperor of China, and European rulers such as Isabella I of Castile, Mary I and Elizabeth I of England, Christina of Sweden, Maria Theresa of Austria, Catherine the Great of Russia, and Queen Victoria of the United Kingdom.

In the modern era most remaining European monarchies have adopted absolute primogeniture, so eldest children inherit regardless of sex; Margrethe II of Denmark (reigned 1972–2024) was Europe's longest-serving queen regnant of recent times, while Elizabeth II (reigned 1952–2022) became the longest-reigning queen regnant in world history. Japan's imperial succession remains restricted to males, despite historical reigning empresses.

Titles and spouses

The husband of a queen regnant does not ordinarily share her title or rank and is usually styled a prince or prince consort, as with Prince Albert, husband of Victoria, and Prince Philip, husband of Elizabeth II. Exceptionally, a husband might be made king jure uxoris, sharing the title by right of his wife.
