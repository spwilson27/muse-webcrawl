Prince consort

Albert, Prince Consort to Queen Victoria — the archetypal holder of the title.

A prince consort is the husband of a reigning queen regnant who is himself a prince. Because a husband who is also a king in his own right would ordinarily outrank his wife, the consort of a female monarch is usually styled a prince rather than a king, unless he reigns jointly as a jure uxoris monarch. The female equivalent, the wife of a reigning king, is generally a queen consort.

Usage

The best-known holder of the title is Prince Albert of Saxe-Coburg and Gotha, husband of Queen Victoria, to whom the formal title "Prince Consort" was granted by letters patent in 1857 — uniquely capitalised in the British case, having been conferred as a substantive title rather than merely a description. Prince Philip, Duke of Edinburgh, husband of Elizabeth II, was a prince consort in function but was not formally granted the title, being styled a prince of the United Kingdom instead.

Other countries

In Denmark, Prince Henrik, husband of Margrethe II, held the title Prince Consort from 2005, though he publicly expressed dissatisfaction at not being styled king. In the Netherlands, the husbands of Queens Wilhelmina, Juliana, and Beatrix were styled Prince of the Netherlands. In Spain, by contrast, the husband of a queen regnant may be styled king consort, as was Francis, Duke of Cádiz, husband of Isabella II. In Sweden, Prince Daniel, husband of Crown Princess Victoria, will become prince consort when she accedes.

Role

A prince consort has no constitutional role by virtue of the title alone, but many have exercised substantial informal influence as advisers, patrons of the arts and sciences, and representatives of the crown. Albert's work on the Great Exhibition of 1851 is a prominent example.
