Flow network

In graph theory, a flow network (also known as a transportation network) is a directed graph where each edge has a capacity and each edge receives a flow. The amount of flow on an edge cannot exceed the capacity of the edge. A flow must satisfy the restriction that the amount of flow into a node equals the amount of flow out of it, except for the source, which has only outgoing flow, and the sink, which has only incoming flow.

Max-flow problem

The maximum flow problem asks for the largest feasible flow from source to sink. The max-flow min-cut theorem states that the maximum flow equals the total capacity of the edges in a minimum cut separating source from sink. Classic algorithms include the Ford–Fulkerson method, the Edmonds–Karp algorithm, and Dinic's algorithm; modern algorithms achieve near-linear running times.

Applications

Flow networks model traffic in computer networks, fluid in pipelines, currents in electrical circuits, goods in supply chains, and matching problems in bipartite graphs, among many other applications in operations research and computer science.
