Alvin Toffler

Alvin Toffler (October 4, 1928 – June 27, 2016) was an American writer and futurist known for works discussing the digital revolution, communications, and the accelerating pace of social change. His best-known book, Future Shock (1970), coined the term for the disorientation caused by "too much change in too short a period of time."

Works

With his wife and collaborator Heidi Toffler, he wrote The Third Wave (1980), which described history as three waves of civilization — agricultural, industrial, and the emerging information age — and Powershift (1990), on the changing sources of power from violence and wealth toward knowledge. His books sold millions of copies worldwide and influenced business and political leaders, despite criticism that his forecasts were impressionistic.
