Battle of Korsun

Battle of Korsun
Date26 May 1648
LocationKorsun, present-day Ukraine
ResultCossack–Tatar victory
BelligerentsZaporozhian Cossacks and Crimean Tatars; Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth
CommandersBohdan Khmelnytsky; Mikołaj Potocki, Marcin Kalinowski

The Battle of Korsun was fought on 26 May 1648 during the Khmelnytsky Uprising. A Cossack army led by Bohdan Khmelnytsky, allied with Crimean Tatars under Tugay Bey, destroyed a Polish–Lithuanian Crown army near Korsun in Ukraine.

Following their victory at Zhovti Vody, the Cossacks advanced on the main Polish force commanded by Grand Crown Hetman Mikołaj Potocki and Field Hetman Marcin Kalinowski. Khmelnytsky laid an ambush in a wooded ravine known as Horokhova Dibrova, where his men dug trenches and ditches across the road. The retreating Polish wagon column was caught in the defile, and Tatar cavalry attacks on the flanks turned the retreat into a rout. Both Polish hetmans were captured and handed over to the Tatars.

Significance

Together with Zhovti Vody, Korsun shattered the Commonwealth's military presence in Ukraine and opened the way for the Cossack rebellion to spread across the region, leading eventually to the Treaty of Pereyaslav and decades of conflict known in Polish historiography as the Deluge.
