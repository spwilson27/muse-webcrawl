Rōnin

A rōnin (浪人, literally "wave man") was a samurai in feudal Japan who had no lord or master — because his lord died, was deposed, or dismissed him, or because he chose to sever the bond. Rōnin occupied an ambiguous, often precarious social position, barred from many privileges of retained samurai.

History

The numbers of rōnin swelled after battles such as Sekigahara (1600) and during the Tokugawa peace, when diminished domains left many warriors masterless. Some became mercenaries, bodyguards, teachers or townsmen; others turned to banditry. The most famous episode is that of the forty-seven rōnin, who avenged their lord Asano Naganori in 1703 and were ordered to commit seppuku — a story retold in the play Chūshingura.

Modern usage

In contemporary Japan, "rōnin" also denotes a student who has failed a university entrance examination and spends a year studying to retake it, or a salaried worker between jobs. The figure of the wandering masterless swordsman, exemplified by Miyamoto Musashi, remains a staple of fiction and film. See also samurai and bushidō.
