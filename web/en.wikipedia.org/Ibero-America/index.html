Ibero-America

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ibero-America (Spanish: Iberoamérica, Portuguese: Ibero-América) is a term used since the second half of the 19th century to designate the Spanish- and Portuguese-speaking countries of the Americas. It is sometimes used interchangeably with Latin America, although the latter term is broader and also includes French-speaking territories such as Haiti, French Guiana, and the French Caribbean.

The term derives from the Iberian Peninsula, home to Spain and Portugal, whose languages and cultures spread throughout the Americas during the colonial period. Ibero-America is sometimes contrasted with Anglo-America, the English-speaking region of the continent.

Countries

The countries generally considered part of Ibero-America include Argentina, Bolivia, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Costa Rica, Cuba, the Dominican Republic, Ecuador, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, Mexico, Nicaragua, Panama, Paraguay, Peru, Puerto Rico (a territory of the United States), Uruguay, and Venezuela. Together they comprise a population of more than 600 million people.

Regional organisations

Since 1991, the heads of state and government of the Ibero-American countries have met at the Ibero-American Summit (Cumbre Iberoamericana), which also includes Spain, Portugal, and Andorra. The Organization of Ibero-American States (OEI) promotes cooperation in education, science, and culture among its member states.
