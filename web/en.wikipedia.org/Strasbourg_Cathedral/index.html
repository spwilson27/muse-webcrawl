Strasbourg Cathedral

Strasbourg Cathedral or the Cathedral of Our Lady of Strasbourg (French: Cathédrale Notre-Dame de Strasbourg; German: Straßburger Münster), also known as Strasbourg Minster, is a Catholic cathedral in Strasbourg, Alsace, France. Although considerable parts of it are still in Romanesque architecture, it is widely considered to be among the finest examples of Rayonnant Gothic architecture. Architect Erwin von Steinbach is credited for major contributions from 1277 to his death in 1318, and beyond through his son Johannes von Steinbach, and his grandson Gerlach von Steinbach, who succeeded him as chief architects.

At 142 metres (466 feet), the cathedral's single spire was the world's tallest building from 1647 to 1874, when it was surpassed by St. Nikolai's Church in Hamburg. Today it is the sixth-tallest church in the world and the tallest extant structure built entirely in the Middle Ages.

History

A Romanesque cathedral was begun on the site in 1015, built by Bishop Werner of Habsburg, but much of it burned in 1176. Rebuilding began in the Gothic style, influenced by Chartres Cathedral. The nave was completed in the 1270s, and the famous west facade — a great carved screen of pink Vosges sandstone, described by Victor Hugo as a "gigantic and delicate marvel" — rose thereafter. The single spire was completed in 1439 by Ulrich d'Ensingen. During the French Revolution the cathedral was briefly dedicated to the Cult of Reason; at the Reformation it had earlier been a Protestant church for over a century before returning to Catholic use in 1681.

Features

The cathedral is famed for its astronomical clock, whose current mechanism dates to 1843 and which includes a perpetual calendar and an orrery. Other highlights include the Pillar of Angels (Pilier des Anges), a sculpted column of c. 1230 depicting the Last Judgment; the great rose window; the stained glass windows, many dating from the 12th to 14th centuries; and the suspended pipe organ. The church contains the tomb of Marshal Maurice de Saxe.
