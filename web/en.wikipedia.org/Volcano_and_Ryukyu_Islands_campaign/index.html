Date19 February – 21 June 1945
LocationVolcano Islands and Ryukyu Islands, Japan

The Volcano and Ryukyu Islands campaign was a series of battles and engagements between Allied forces and Imperial Japanese forces in the Pacific Ocean campaign of World War II, between February and June 1945. It was the penultimate campaign of the Pacific War, bringing American forces to the doorstep of the Japanese home islands.

Course of the campaign

The campaign began with the Battle of Iwo Jima (19 February – 26 March 1945), in which U.S. Marines captured the Volcano Islands airfields needed to support bombing raids on Japan — the battle famous for the flag raising on Mount Suribachi. The larger Battle of Okinawa (1 April – 22 June 1945), codenamed Operation Iceberg, was the biggest amphibious assault of the Pacific war and the last major battle: it involved massive kamikaze attacks, the sinking of the battleship Yamato in Operation Ten-Go, and casualties that included well over 100,000 Japanese soldiers and a huge number of Okinawan civilians.

Significance

Possession of Okinawa and Iwo Jima gave the United States air bases within range of Kyushu for the planned invasion of Japan, Operation Downfall. The ferocity and casualty toll of the campaign — including an estimated 12,500 American dead on Okinawa alone — weighed heavily in American deliberations in the summer of 1945 over invasion versus blockade and atomic bombing.
