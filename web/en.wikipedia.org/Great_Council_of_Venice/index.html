The Great Council or Major Council (Italian: Maggior Consiglio) was the political body that governed the Republic of Venice from 1172 until the republic's fall in 1797. Formally the sovereign body of the state, it comprised all adult male members of the Venetian patriciate and was responsible for electing the Doge and filling the republic's magistracies.

History

The council emerged in 1172 as an assembly electing the Doge. The Serrata ("locking") of 1297 restricted membership to families that had sat in the council, turning the patriciate into a closed hereditary caste recorded in the Golden Book (Libro d'Oro). Membership grew so large — eventually over two thousand — that day-to-day power passed to smaller bodies such as the Senate and the Council of Ten, while the Great Council retained elections and sovereignty in theory.

Elections and the Doge

The ducal election procedure was famously elaborate, alternating rounds of sortition (drawing lots) and voting through multiple electoral colleges to frustrate faction and bribery. The council met in the vast Sala del Maggior Consiglio in the Doge's Palace, beneath Tintoretto's Paradiso. On 12 May 1797 the council, under the last Doge, Ludovico Manin, voted to dissolve the republic in the face of Napoleon's invasion.
