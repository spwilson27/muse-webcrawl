Cyberwarfare

Major states now maintain dedicated military cyber commands alongside land, sea, air and space forces.

Cyberwarfare is the use of cyber attacks against an enemy state, causing comparable harm to actual warfare and/or disrupting vital computer systems. It encompasses operations by nation-states and state-sponsored groups against military systems, critical infrastructure, financial networks and government services, for purposes of espionage, sabotage, propaganda and disruption. There is significant debate among experts as to whether any incident to date qualifies as "war" in the strict sense, and the term is often used loosely for state cyber operations of all kinds.

Types of operation

  • Espionage — long-term intrusion into networks to steal state and commercial secrets.
  • Sabotage — damaging or destroying equipment or data, as in the Stuxnet attack on Iranian centrifuges discovered in 2010.
  • Denial of service — flooding services to make them unavailable, as in the 2007 attacks on Estonia.
  • Influence operations — hacking and leaking, and coordinated disinformation, aimed at elections and public opinion.
  • Infrastructure pre-positioning — quietly maintaining access to power, water or transport networks for possible future use.

Notable incidents

The 2007 cyberattacks on Estonia, following the Bronze Soldier riots, overwhelmed government and banking websites and prompted NATO to establish its Cooperative Cyber Defence Centre of Excellence in Tallinn. Stuxnet (2010) demonstrated that malware could physically destroy equipment. The 2015 and 2016 attacks on Ukraine's power grid caused blackouts and are widely attributed to the Russian Sandworm group. NotPetya (2017), nominally ransomware aimed at Ukraine, spread worldwide and caused an estimated $10 billion in damage, the costliest cyber attack then known. State-sponsored espionage campaigns, such as the SolarWinds supply-chain compromise disclosed in 2020, have also reshaped defensive practice.

Military organisation and doctrine

The United States established U.S. Cyber Command in 2009, elevated to a unified combatant command in 2017. China integrates cyber capabilities within the People's Liberation Army; Russia's operations are associated with its military intelligence and security services; the United Kingdom created the National Cyber Force in 2020. Doctrine debates centre on deterrence, attribution, proportionality and whether the law of armed conflict — as analysed in the Tallinn Manual — governs operations in cyberspace.
