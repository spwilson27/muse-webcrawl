Handshaking lemma

In graph theory, the handshaking lemma states that in every finite undirected graph, the sum of the degrees of all vertices equals twice the number of edges: Σ deg(v) = 2|E|. The name comes from the analogy of handshakes at a gathering, where each handshake contributes two to the total count of hands shaken.

Corollary

An immediate consequence is that the number of vertices with odd degree is always even. Applied to a party, this says an even number of people have shaken hands an odd number of times. The result was known to Leonhard Euler, appearing in his 1736 paper on the Seven Bridges of Königsberg, often regarded as the founding work of graph theory.

Proof

The proof is a double-counting argument: count the pairs (v, e) where vertex v is an endpoint of edge e. Summing over vertices gives Σ deg(v); summing over edges gives 2|E|, since each edge has exactly two endpoints (loops contributing two to a vertex's degree).

Applications

The lemma is used in parity arguments throughout combinatorics and computer science, in proofs about trees and Eulerian trails, and in analysing the structure of regular graphs.
