Epic Games v. Apple

Epic Games v. Apple was a United States antitrust lawsuit arising from Apple's removal of Fortnite from the App Store in August 2020. Epic Games had introduced a direct payment option that bypassed Apple's in-app purchase system and its commission, prompting removal and litigation.

Background

Epic challenged Apple's rules requiring most digital goods in iOS apps to use Apple's payment system and pay a commission, commonly 30 percent. Apple argued that the rules funded the App Store, protected users, and were comparable to other platforms.

Trial and judgment

The case was tried in the Northern District of California in 2021. The court rejected Epic's central claim that Apple was an unlawful monopolist in the relevant market, but found Apple's anti-steering provisions unlawful under California's Unfair Competition Law and issued an injunction allowing developers to inform users of other payment options.

Appeals and impact

The Ninth Circuit largely upheld the judgment in 2023, and the U.S. Supreme Court declined review in 2024. The dispute became a major test of app-store competition policy and coincided with regulatory pressure in the European Union and elsewhere. See also Epic Games, App Store, and Fortnite.
