Zhu Rongji

Zhu Rongji (Chinese: 朱镕基; born 23 October 1928) is a retired Chinese politician who served as the fifth premier of the People's Republic of China from 1998 to 2003 and as a member of the Politburo Standing Committee. Earlier he was vice premier under Li Peng, mayor and party secretary of Shanghai, and governor of the People's Bank of China.

Zhu is widely credited with steering China's economy through a period of sweeping reform: restructuring state-owned enterprises, overhauling the tax and banking systems, fighting inflation, and negotiating China's accession to the World Trade Organization in 2001. Known for a blunt, no-nonsense manner and for anti-corruption rhetoric, he became one of the most internationally prominent Chinese leaders of his era.

Reforms

As central bank governor in the mid-1990s, Zhu tightened credit and brought inflation down from over 24 percent to single digits without a hard landing. As premier, he oversaw the restructuring of state enterprises that shed tens of millions of jobs while preparing large firms for market competition, a major fiscal recentralisation, housing privatisation, and the abolition of the state grain monopoly.

Later life

Zhu retired in 2003 and was succeeded by Wen Jiabao. He has since published collections of his speeches and letters, including Zhu Rongji on the Record, which became bestsellers in China.
