NCAA Division I Football Bowl Subdivision


Labelled image: FBS postseason bowl game

The NCAA Division I Football Bowl Subdivision (FBS), formerly known as Division I-A, is the highest level of college football in the United States. It comprises more than 130 schools in the National Collegiate Athletic Association (NCAA). Unlike the lower NCAA divisions, FBS historically did not use a tournament sanctioned by the NCAA to determine its national champion; instead, champions were recognized through polls and, since 2014, the College Football Playoff.

Structure

FBS programs must meet minimum attendance requirements and may award up to 85 full athletic scholarships. Most belong to conferences grouped informally into the "Power Four" (following the realignment of the 2020s) — the ACC, Big Ten, Big 12, and SEC — and the "Group of Five" conferences: the American, Conference USA, MAC, Mountain West, and Sun Belt, plus independent programs such as Notre Dame. Teams play a twelve-game regular season, conference championship games, and, if eligible with at least six wins, postseason bowl games.

Postseason

The first bowl game, the Rose Bowl, dates to 1902. For decades the national championship was decided by the AP and Coaches polls; the Bowl Championship Series (1998–2013) attempted to match the top two teams. The College Football Playoff began in 2014 with four teams and expanded to twelve teams for the 2024 season, using automatic berths for conference champions and at-large selections made by a committee. The system remains distinctive: the NCAA does not officially award the FBS championship.
