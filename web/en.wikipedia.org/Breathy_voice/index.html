ExamplesGujarati, Hmong, Sindhi contrasts

Breathy voice (also called murmured voice) is a phonation type in phonetics in which the vocal folds vibrate, as in normal modal voicing, but are held apart so that a greater volume of air escapes, producing a sighing or whispery quality in the sound. The arytenoid cartilages are separated, allowing air to flow through a triangular opening at the back of the glottis.


Breathy phonation shows increased noise and weaker higher harmonics.

Phonetic properties

Compared with modal voice, breathy voice has a steeper spectral tilt (a relatively strong fundamental frequency and weaker upper harmonics), more aspiration noise, and often a lower pitch. In the International Phonetic Alphabet it is indicated by a subscript diaeresis, as in [b̩].

In languages

Breathy phonation is phonemic in several languages. Gujarati contrasts breathy ("murmured") consonants with plain ones; Hmong has breathy-voiced consonants; and Sindhi murmur forms part of its larger stop series. Breathy vowels occur in languages such as Jalapa Mazatec and some varieties of Khmer, where "register" complexes combine phonation with vowel quality.

Expressive use

Sociophonetically, breathiness is associated in many cultures with intimacy, softness, or femininity, and it is used expressively in singing styles ranging from jazz to contemporary pop.
