Clementine (spacecraft)

Clementine
Operators: BMDO / NASA
Launch: 25 January 1994
Launch vehicle: Titan II(23)G Star-37FM
Mission: Lunar mapping; asteroid flyby (failed)
Status: Ended 1994

Clementine, officially the Deep Space Program Science Experiment (DSPSE), was a joint space project between the Ballistic Missile Defense Organization (BMDO) and NASA. Launched on 25 January 1994, its mission was to test sensors and spacecraft components under extended exposure to the space environment and to make scientific observations of the Moon and the near-Earth asteroid 1620 Geographos.

Mission

Clementine entered lunar orbit on 19 February 1994 and mapped the Moon's surface over about two months in 11 wavelengths, including ultraviolet and infrared. It carried a lidar altimeter and produced a global digital elevation model, as well as data that suggested the presence of water ice in permanently shadowed craters at the lunar south pole, interpreted from bistatic radar measurements.

Asteroid phase and end

After departing lunar orbit in May 1994, a computer malfunction caused a thruster to fire until its fuel was exhausted, leaving the spacecraft spinning and unable to perform the planned Geographos flyby. Contact was eventually lost, and the spacecraft remains in a geocentric orbit.

Legacy

The mission demonstrated that advanced sensors developed for missile defense could be adapted for civil space science, and its lunar data were widely used by later missions. Clementine was named after the folk song "Oh My Darling, Clementine," because, like the song's subject, it would be "lost and gone forever" after its mission.
