Coal mining is the process of extracting coal from the ground or from a mine. Coal is valued for its energy content and since the 1880s has been widely used to generate electricity; it is also essential in steel and cement production. Coal has been mined since Roman times, and it fuelled the Industrial Revolution, transforming industry, transport and society.

Methods

Coal is extracted either by surface mining (including open-pit, strip mining and mountaintop removal) when seams lie near the surface, or by underground mining for deeper seams. The two principal underground techniques are room-and-pillar mining, in which pillars of coal are left to support the roof, and longwall mining, in which a mechanised shearer cuts coal from a long face while hydraulic supports hold the roof and the void behind collapses. After extraction, coal is washed, crushed and sorted at preparation plants before transport, overwhelmingly by rail.

History

Coal was mined in Britain from Roman times and became central in the 18th and 19th centuries to steam engines, iron and steel, and railways. Mechanisation — steam-powered winding engines, ventilation, safety lamps (after disasters caused by firedamp), and later powered cutting and conveying — transformed productivity. The 20th century saw the industry expand across the United States, Germany, the Soviet Union, and, later, China, which today accounts for more than half of world production.

Safety and health

Coal mining has historically been among the most dangerous occupations. Hazards include roof falls, methane and coal-dust explosions, flooding, and long-term exposure to coal dust causing pneumoconiosis ("black lung disease"). Major disasters such as Courrières (France, 1906), Benxihu (China, 1942), and Upper Big Branch (United States, 2010) spurred progressively stricter safety regulation. Modern mines employ continuous gas monitoring, ventilation, dust suppression and rock-dusting to reduce risks.

Environmental impact

Burning coal is the largest single source of anthropogenic carbon dioxide emissions, and mining itself causes land disturbance, subsidence, acid mine drainage and methane release. Many countries are phasing out coal-fired power for climate reasons, although global production reached record levels in the early 2020s as demand in Asia grew.
