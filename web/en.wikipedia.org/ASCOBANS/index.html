ASCOBANS is the Agreement on the Conservation of Small Cetaceans of the Baltic, North East Atlantic, Irish and North Seas. It is a regional agreement concluded under the auspices of the Convention on the Conservation of Migratory Species of Wild Animals (CMS, or Bonn Convention). The agreement aims to achieve and maintain a favourable conservation status for small cetaceans — dolphins, porpoises and small toothed whales — in northern and western European waters.

Background

Migratory species cross national borders, so their protection requires international cooperation. The original agreement, covering the Baltic and North Seas, was concluded in 1991 and entered into force on 29 March 1994. In 2003 the agreement area was extended south-westwards to cover the North East Atlantic and Irish Sea; the amended agreement entered into force on 3 February 2008 and the name was updated accordingly.

Species and threats

The agreement covers all species of toothed whales (Odontoceti) in the area except the sperm whale. Key species include the harbour porpoise, bottlenose dolphin, common dolphin, white-beaked dolphin, Atlantic white-sided dolphin, striped dolphin, Risso's dolphin, killer whale, long-finned pilot whale and beaked whales. The main threats addressed are bycatch in fisheries, habitat degradation, marine pollution, acoustic disturbance (including underwater noise from shipping, seismic surveys and construction), prey depletion and climate change.

Conservation plans

ASCOBANS parties adopt conservation and management plans, including specific recovery plans for harbour porpoises in the Baltic Sea (the Jastarnia Plan) and conservation plans for the North Sea and for the common dolphin. The ASCOBANS Secretariat is provided by the United Nations Environment Programme and is co-located with the CMS Secretariat in Bonn.
