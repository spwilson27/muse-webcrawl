The Archers

The Archers is a British radio soap opera broadcast on BBC Radio 4. First broadcast in 1951, it is the world's longest-running radio drama and, by episode count, the longest-running drama in any medium, with more than 20,000 episodes. Its strapline describes it as "an everyday story of country folk".

Setting and origins

The programme is set in the fictional village of Ambridge in the fictional county of Borsetshire, modelled on the countryside around Inkberrow in Worcestershire. It was created by Godfrey Baseley, partly as a vehicle for conveying farming information to farmers in the post-war years of rationing and agricultural modernisation. The Archer family farms Brookfield Farm; other focal families include the Aldridges at Home Farm and the Grundys, and the village pub is The Bull.

Storylines

Though rooted in farming life, the serial has tackled contemporary rural and social issues: foot-and-mouth disease, rural poverty, addiction, domestic abuse and coercive control — the 2013–16 Rob Titchener and Helen Archer storyline drew national attention and measurably increased calls to abuse helplines. The death of Nigel Pargetter in 2011 and the trial of Helen Archer in 2016 were widely covered events.

Broadcast

Episodes are 13 minutes long, broadcast six days a week with a Sunday omnibus. The theme tune is "Barwick Green", a maypole dance from Arthur Wood's suite My Native Heath.
