Ex-voto
TypeVotive offering
TraditionsChristianity (especially Roman Catholicism), ancient religions

An ex-voto is a votive offering given to a saint or divinity in fulfilment of a vow, or in gratitude for a favour received, such as recovery from illness or survival of an accident. The term derives from the Latin phrase ex votum susceptum, "from the vow made".

Forms

Ex-votos take many forms: small paintings depicting the miraculous event, silver or wax models of healed body parts, crutches left at shrines, candles, plaques inscribed with thanks, and objects such as ships' models offered by sailors. In Mexico and other parts of Latin America, painted ex-voto panels on tin, called retablos, vividly narrate the dangers survived by the donor.

History

The practice descends from ancient Greek and Roman votive offerings deposited in temples and healing sanctuaries such as that of Asclepius at Epidaurus. It became widespread in Catholic Europe from the medieval period onwards and remains common at Marian shrines and pilgrimage churches today.
