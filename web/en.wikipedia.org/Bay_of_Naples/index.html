Bay of Naples

The Bay of Naples (Italian: Golfo di Napoli), also called the Gulf of Naples, is a roughly 15-kilometre-wide gulf located along the south-western coast of Italy (Metropolitan City of Naples, Campania region). It opens to the west into the Tyrrhenian Sea and is bordered on the north-west by the island of Ischia and on the south-east by the Sorrentine Peninsula. The city of Naples lies on its northern shore, dominated by the volcano Mount Vesuvius, whose eruption in AD 79 destroyed the Roman towns of Pompeii and Herculaneum on the bay's eastern shore.

Geography

The bay extends from Cape Miseno in the north-west to Punta Campanella in the south-east. The Phlegraean Fields (Campi Flegrei), a restless volcanic caldera, form its northern shore — an area of bradyseism that has lifted and lowered the ground at Pozzuoli since antiquity. Offshore lie the islands of Procida, Ischia, and Vivara in the north and Capri at the southern entrance.

History

Greek colonists founded Cumae and Naples (Neapolis) on the bay in the first millennium BC; under Rome the coast became a fashionable resort, lined with villas at Baiae, Stabiae, and Oplontis. The eruption of Vesuvius in AD 79 buried Pompeii and Herculaneum, whose excavation from the 18th century made the bay a centrepiece of the Grand Tour. Goethe, Turner, and countless painters made its view — Vesuvius across the water — an icon of the picturesque. In the Second World War Naples was heavily bombed and was liberated after the Four Days of Naples uprising in 1943.

Tourism and environment

Today the bay is one of Italy's most visited coastal regions, with ferries linking Naples to Capri, Ischia, Procida, and Sorrento. The marine protected area of Punta Campanella guards the southern entrance, while pollution and uncontrolled coastal development remain longstanding concerns.
