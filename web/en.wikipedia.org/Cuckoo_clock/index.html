Cuckoo clock
Origin: Black Forest, Germany
Period: 18th century–present
Mechanism: pendulum-regulated, weight-driven

A cuckoo clock is a pendulum-regulated clock, usually weight-driven, that strikes the hours with a sound like a common cuckoo's call accompanied by a mechanical cuckoo bird that moves out of a small door. The traditional carved style originated in the Black Forest region of Germany in the 18th century.

Styles

The best-known form is the carved "hunter style" chalet case decorated with leaves, birds, and animals; a plainer "railroad house" (Bahnhöfle) style was designed by Friedrich Eisenlohr in 1850. Modern quartz cuckoo clocks use electronic movements and recorded calls.

Mechanism

The cuckoo's call is produced by two tiny bellows and pipes (gedackt flute pipes). The bird's movement, dancers, water wheels, and other animations on elaborate clocks are driven by the going or strike train. Weights are typically cast in the shape of pine cones, and the pendulum bob often resembles a carved leaf.
