Barack Obama

Barack Hussein Obama II (born 4 August 1961) is an American politician who served as the 44th president of the United States from 2009 to 2017, the first African American president. A member of the Democratic Party, he previously served as a US senator from Illinois (2005–2008) and Illinois state senator.

Early life and career

Born in Honolulu, Hawaii, to a Kenyan father and American mother, Obama graduated from Columbia University and Harvard Law School, where he was the first Black president of the Harvard Law Review. He worked as a community organiser and civil rights attorney in Chicago and taught constitutional law at the University of Chicago.

Presidency

Obama's signature achievements include the Affordable Care Act ("Obamacare"), the Dodd–Frank financial reforms, the Paris Agreement on climate change, the Iran nuclear deal, and normalisation of relations with Cuba. He ordered the 2011 raid that killed Osama bin Laden. He was awarded the 2009 Nobel Peace Prize.

Post-presidency

With Michelle Obama, he founded the Obama Foundation in Chicago. His memoir A Promised Land was published in 2020.
