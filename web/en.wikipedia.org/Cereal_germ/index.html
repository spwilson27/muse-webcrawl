Cereal germ

The germ of a cereal grain is its reproductive part — the embryo — which germinates to grow into a new plant. Together with the bran and the starchy endosperm, it is one of the three parts of a whole grain. Though small (about 2–3% of a wheat kernel by weight), the germ is rich in nutrients, containing B vitamins, vitamin E, minerals, protein, and unsaturated fats.

Processing

Conventional milling removes the germ and bran to produce white flour, partly because the germ's oils shorten shelf life by going rancid. Whole-grain products retain the germ. Wheat germ is also sold separately as a food supplement and nutritional ingredient, and wheat germ oil is extracted for culinary and cosmetic use. Corn (maize) germ is a major source of corn oil.

Nutrition

Wheat germ is one of the richest natural sources of vitamin E and folate, and provides significant amounts of thiamine, magnesium, zinc, and essential fatty acids. Because of its nutrient density, germ is added to cereals, baked goods, and health foods.
