Operator (physics)

In quantum mechanics, physical observables are represented by linear operators acting on a Hilbert space.

In physics, an operator is a function over a space of physical states onto another space of physical states. The simplest example of the utility of operators is the study of symmetry, which explains why the concept is useful in classical mechanics. In quantum mechanics, operators are central to the mathematical formulation of the theory: every observable, such as position, momentum, or energy, corresponds to a linear, Hermitian operator acting on the state space.

Classical mechanics

In classical mechanics, operators such as the time-evolution generator appear through Poisson brackets; the Liouvillian generates the flow of a system in phase space. Symmetry operations, such as translations and rotations, can likewise be expressed as operators acting on functions of the phase-space variables.

Quantum mechanics

In the Schrödinger representation, the position operator acts by multiplication by x, while the momentum operator acts as −iħ d/dx. The Hamiltonian operator governs time evolution through the Schrödinger equation. Operators that do not commute, such as position and momentum, obey canonical commutation relations that underlie the Heisenberg uncertainty principle.

Types of operators

  • Hermitian (self-adjoint) operators, representing observables
  • Unitary operators, representing symmetry transformations and time evolution
  • Creation and annihilation operators in quantum field theory
  • Projection operators, representing measurement outcomes
