Sanctuary of Fátima

The Sanctuary of Fátima (Portuguese: Santuário de Fátima) is a Roman Catholic pilgrimage site in Fátima, Portugal, built at the Cova da Iria where, in 1917, three shepherd children — Lúcia dos Santos and her cousins Francisco and Jacinta Marto — reported apparitions of the Virgin Mary, venerated as Our Lady of Fátima.

History

Following the apparitions of May to October 1917, a small chapel was built at the site in 1919. The great Basilica of Our Lady of the Rosary, in neo-baroque style, was constructed between 1928 and 1953; the tombs of the three visionaries lie inside. The vast pilgrimage square can hold close to a million people.

Principal buildings

The complex includes the Chapel of the Apparitions, built on the exact spot of the apparitions, the Basilica of Our Lady of the Rosary, and the modern Basilica of the Holy Trinity, consecrated in 2007 and among the largest Catholic churches in the world. Major pilgrimages take place on 12–13 May and 12–13 October each year, drawing millions of visitors annually.
