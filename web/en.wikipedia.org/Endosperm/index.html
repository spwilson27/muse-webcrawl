Endosperm

The endosperm is a tissue produced inside the seeds of most flowering plants (angiosperms) following double fertilization. It surrounds and nourishes the embryo as it develops, supplying starch, oils, and proteins.

Formation

During double fertilization, one sperm cell fertilizes the egg to form the zygote, while a second sperm fuses with the two polar nuclei of the central cell to form a triploid (3n) primary endosperm cell. This cell divides repeatedly to produce the endosperm tissue.

Economic importance

Endosperm forms the edible bulk of cereal grains such as wheat, rice, and maize, making it the world's most important food source for humans. In wheat milling, the starchy endosperm becomes white flour, while the bran and germ are separated. Coconut "meat" and liquid are also forms of endosperm.
