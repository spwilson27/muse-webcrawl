Frankfurter Würstchen

Frankfurter Würstchen, thin parboiled pork sausages
TypeSausage
Place of originGermany (Frankfurt am Main)
Main ingredientsPork, water, salt, spices
Similar dishesWiener Würstchen

Frankfurter Würstchen ("little sausages of Frankfurt") are thin parboiled sausages made purely of pork, lightly smoked and traditionally served poached in hot water. They originate from Frankfurt am Main, Germany, where sausages of this kind have been known since the Middle Ages and were served at imperial coronations in the city.

Production

The sausage meat is a fine brät of pork with water (or ice), curing salt and spices such as white pepper, mace and coriander, filled into thin sheep's casings. The sausages are first scalded and then lightly smoked over beechwood, which gives them their pale golden colour. They are only gently heated in water for a few minutes before serving — never boiled vigorously or grilled — and are typically eaten with bread, mustard or horseradish, and potato salad.

Name protection

The name has been protected within Germany since the nineteenth century: only sausages produced in the Frankfurt area (notably in Neu-Isenburg and Dreieich) may be sold as genuine Frankfurter Würstchen. Closely related is the Austrian Frankfurter, which in Austria contains a mixture of pork and beef and gave its name, abroad, to the generic "frankfurter" or hot dog sausage.
