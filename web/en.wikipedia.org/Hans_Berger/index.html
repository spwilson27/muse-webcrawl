Hans Berger

Hans Berger (21 May 1873 – 1 June 1941) was a German psychiatrist best known as the inventor of electroencephalography (EEG) and the discoverer of the alpha wave rhythm in the human brain.

Career

Berger studied medicine and became director of the psychiatric clinic at the University of Jena. Motivated by an interest in the relationship between mind and brain activity, he spent years attempting to record electrical activity from the human scalp.

Electroencephalography

In 1924 Berger made the first human EEG recording, and he published his findings in 1929. He identified the alpha rhythm (8–13 Hz), also called Berger's wave, which appears when a subject is relaxed with eyes closed. His work laid the foundation for clinical neurology and brain research.
