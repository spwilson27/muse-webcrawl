Winnipeg River

SourceLake of the Woods
MouthLake Winnipeg
Length235 km (146 mi)
BasinHudson Bay watershed

The Winnipeg River is a Canadian river that flows roughly northwest from Lake of the Woods in the province of Ontario to Lake Winnipeg in Manitoba. It drops about 85 metres (280 ft) over its course and was historically part of a major fur-trade route connecting the Great Lakes with the interior of Canada.

History

The river was used by First Nations peoples for thousands of years and later by voyageurs of the fur trade. The first European to see it is thought to have been one of the La Vérendrye party in the 1730s. Portages around its many rapids and falls carried York boats and canoes between Lake of the Woods and Lake Winnipeg.

Hydroelectric development

Several hydroelectric generating stations were built on the river in Manitoba and Ontario during the 20th century, including plants at Kenora, Whitedog Falls, Pointe du Bois, Slave Falls, Seven Sisters Falls, McArthur Falls, Great Falls, and Pine Falls.
