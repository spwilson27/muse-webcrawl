Lefkada (Greek: Λευκάδα), also known as Leucas or Lefkas, is a Greek island in the Ionian Sea off the western coast of mainland Greece. It is connected to the mainland by a causeway and floating bridge, making it one of the few Greek islands accessible by road. The island has an area of about 336 square kilometres and a population of roughly 22,000.

Geography

Lefkada is mountainous, with its highest peak, Mount Elati, rising to 1,158 metres. Its western coast is famed for dramatic white cliffs and turquoise beaches such as Porto Katsiki and Egremni, while the gentler eastern shore faces the satellite islands of Skorpios, Meganisi and others. The capital, Lefkada town, sits at the northern end near the mainland causeway, beside a large lagoon.

History and culture

In antiquity the island was a Corinthian colony and later part of Roman, Byzantine, Venetian and Ottoman spheres; like the other Ionian Islands it passed through French, Russo-Ottoman and British hands before union with Greece in 1864. Wilhelm Dörpfeld excavated on the island and argued it was the Homeric Ithaca. Lefkada was the birthplace of the poet Aristotelis Valaoritis and is associated with Angelos Sikelianos. The island's name derives from the Greek lefkos, "white", after its white cliffs.
