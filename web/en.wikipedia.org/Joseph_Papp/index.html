Joseph Papp

Born: Joseph Papirofsky, 22 June 1921, Brooklyn, New York, US
Died: 31 October 1991 (aged 70), New York City, US
Occupation: Theatre producer and director
Known for: Founder of The Public Theater (1954) and Shakespeare in the Park

Joseph Papp (born Joseph Papirofsky; June 22, 1921 – October 31, 1991) was an American theatrical producer and director who created The Public Theater and the free Shakespeare in the Park festival in New York City. Driven by the conviction that theatre should be accessible to everyone, he built one of the most influential non-profit theatre institutions in the world.

Early life

Papp was born in Brooklyn to Jewish immigrants from Lithuania and worked in a navy yard and ship repair before serving in the U.S. Navy during the Second World War, where he directed shows for servicemen. After the war he studied acting and worked as a stage manager in television, losing his CBS job after refusing to name names before the House Un-American Activities Committee — a stand from which he later won reinstatement.

The Public Theater and Shakespeare in the Park

In 1954 Papp founded the Shakespeare Workshop, touring free Shakespeare productions to neighbourhoods and parks. In 1957 he began presenting summer productions in Central Park, defying parks commissioner Robert Moses's attempts to charge admission or end the shows; the Delacorte Theater opened in the park in 1962 and free Shakespeare in the Park continues to this day. In 1967 he established The Public Theater in the former Astor Library building on Lafayette Street, developing new plays and musicals with a deliberately diverse, democratic mission. Under Papp the Public premiered Hair (1967), A Chorus Line (1975) — whose Broadway profits subsidised the Public for years — and works by David Rabe, Sam Shepard, Ntozake Shange (for colored girls...), Václav Havel and many others. From 1973 to 1977 he also ran the theatre programme at Lincoln Center.

Legacy

Papp died of prostate cancer in 1991. The Public Theater continues as a leading American theatre institution, and his model of publicly supported, free and adventurous theatre shaped regional theatre across the United States.
