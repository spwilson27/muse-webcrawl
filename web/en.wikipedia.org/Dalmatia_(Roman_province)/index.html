Dalmatia (Roman province)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Dalmatia was a Roman province on the eastern coast of the Adriatic Sea, covering roughly the territory of modern coastal Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Montenegro, and parts of Serbia and Albania. It took its name from the Delmatae, an Illyrian tribe, and its capital was Salona, near modern Split.

Conquest

Rome intervened on the Adriatic's eastern shore from the third century BC in the Illyrian Wars against Queen Teuta's kingdom. The Delmatae proved stubborn opponents through a series of campaigns in the second and first centuries BC. Full pacification came only with the suppression of the Great Illyrian Revolt (Bellum Batonianum, AD 6–9), a massive uprising led by Bato the Daesitiate that Tiberius and Germanicus put down with difficulty; Augustus's reorganisation had earlier divided the region into the provinces of Illyricum Superius — which became Dalmatia — and Pannonia.

Administration and society

Dalmatia was governed by a senatorial legate of consular rank, reflecting its military importance on the Danube frontier's flank. The province supplied prized soldiers to the legions and auxiliaries and was rich in mines, timber, and cattle. Coastal cities such as Salona, Narona, Epidaurum, and Iader (Zadar) were heavily Romanised, organised into conventus (assize districts), while the mountainous interior retained tribal structures under Roman supervision. Diocletian, born near Salona, retired to his vast palace there around AD 305 — the structure that forms the core of modern Split.

Late antiquity

Under Diocletian's reforms Dalmatia belonged to the Diocese of Pannonia (later Illyricum), and a strip of its south was detached as the province of Praevalitana. After the fall of the West, the province passed through Ostrogothic, Byzantine, and eventually Slavic and Croatian control, while the Latin-speaking coastal cities preserved the Dalmatian language into the modern era — its last native speaker died in 1898.
