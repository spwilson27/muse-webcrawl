Isaac Hayes

Born20 August 1942, Covington, Tennessee
Died10 August 2008 (aged 65), Memphis, Tennessee
OccupationsSinger, songwriter, producer, actor
GenresSoul, funk, disco

Isaac Lee Hayes Jr. (August 20, 1942 – August 10, 2008) was an American singer, songwriter, actor, composer and producer. A key creative force at Stax Records in Memphis, he helped define Southern soul in the 1960s and became a solo star with his lush, orchestral brand of soul music in the 1970s.

Career

Hayes began as a session musician and, with partner David Porter, wrote hits for Sam & Dave including "Soul Man" and "Hold On, I'm Comin'". His solo breakthrough came with the album Hot Buttered Soul (1969), featuring extended, orchestral reworkings of popular songs. In 1971 his "Theme from Shaft", written for the film Shaft, became a number-one hit and won the Academy Award for Best Original Song, making Hayes the first African American to win in that category and a cultural icon of the blaxploitation era.

His mid-1970s albums as "Black Moses" established his deep-voiced, romantic persona. Later generations discovered him as the voice of Chef on the animated series South Park (1997–2006). Hayes was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2002.

Personal life and death

Hayes was a prominent Scientologist and a humanitarian; he was named an honorary king in Ghana for his development work there. He died of a stroke at his home near Memphis in August 2008, shortly before his 66th birthday.
