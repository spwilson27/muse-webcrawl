The United States Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) is an independent agency of the United States government charged with protecting the public from unreasonable risks of injury or death associated with consumer products. Created by the Consumer Product Safety Act of 1972, it regulates the manufacture and sale of more than 15,000 types of products.

Responsibilities

The Commission issues and enforces mandatory safety standards, can require recalls of defective products, and bans products that present an unreasonable risk when no standard would adequately protect the public. It administers statutes covering flammable fabrics, poison prevention packaging, children's products, refrigerators, and swimming pools, among others. CPSC also operates SaferProducts.gov, a public database of product-related incident reports.

Notable actions

The agency is known for recalls of products ranging from children's toys with lead paint or small magnets to faulty lithium-ion batteries, cribs and all-terrain vehicles. Its oversight of children's products was greatly strengthened by the Consumer Product Safety Improvement Act of 2008, passed after a wave of recalls of imported toys, which set strict limits on lead and phthalates and required third-party testing.

Structure

The CPSC is led by five commissioners appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate for staggered seven-year terms; no more than three may belong to the same political party. Its headquarters and laboratory are in Maryland.
