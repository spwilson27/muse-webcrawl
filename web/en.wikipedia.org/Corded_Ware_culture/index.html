Corded Ware culture

Corded Ware culture
PeriodLate Neolithic / Copper Age, c. 3000–2350 BC
RegionNorthern and Central Europe, from the Rhine to the Volga
Diagnostic traitsCord-impressed pottery, stone battle axes, single burials under barrows

The Corded Ware culture (c. 3000–2350 BC) was a Late Neolithic and early Bronze Age archaeological culture of northern and central Europe, stretching from the Rhine valley to the Volga. It is named for its pottery, decorated by pressing twisted cord into the wet clay. Closely associated regional variants include the Single Grave culture of Denmark and the Battle Axe culture of Scandinavia.

Corded Ware beaker and a boat-shaped stone battle axe, hallmarks of the culture.

Characteristics

Corded Ware communities buried their dead singly in pit graves under round barrows, typically in a crouched position with sex-differentiated orientation, accompanied by beakers, amphorae, and perforated stone battle axes modelled on metal prototypes. The economy was based on cattle herding and small-scale cereal farming; wheeled vehicles and wool sheep appear in this horizon, and the culture plausibly helped spread wheeled transport across Europe.

Origins and genetics

Ancient DNA studies published from 2015 onward showed that Corded Ware populations carried a large component of ancestry from the Yamnaya steppe pastoralists of the Pontic-Caspian steppe — in some regions around three-quarters of their ancestry — indicating a substantial migration from the east. This genetic evidence strongly supports the steppe hypothesis, associating the Corded Ware horizon with the dispersal of Indo-European languages across Europe, from which most later European Indo-European-speaking cultures partly descend.
