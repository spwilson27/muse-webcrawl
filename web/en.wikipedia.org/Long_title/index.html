Long title

Long title
TypePart of a statute
PurposeDescribes the purpose of an Act
Contrasts withShort title

In United Kingdom parliamentary practice, the long title of a bill or Act of Parliament is the formal heading that describes in a sentence or two the purpose and scope of the legislation. It begins with words such as "An Act to make provision about…" and appears above the enacting formula.

Function

The long title defines the permissible scope of a bill: amendments offered during its passage through Parliament must fall within the scope of the long title to be in order. Since the mid-nineteenth century, courts have also allowed the long title to be used as an aid to interpreting the statute's meaning. It is distinct from the short title, the brief name (such as "Companies Act 2006") by which the Act is cited.

Modern practice

Long titles traditionally recited every major purpose of the Act, making some of them very long. Modern drafting tends toward shorter, more general long titles, allowing flexibility for amendment. Some Commonwealth legislatures, including those of Australia and Ireland, follow the same convention, while other jurisdictions use a purpose clause within the body of the law instead.
