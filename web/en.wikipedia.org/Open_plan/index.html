Open plan

Open plan is the generic term used in architectural and interior design for any floor plan that makes use of large, open spaces and minimises the use of small, enclosed rooms such as private offices. The term can also refer to landscaping of housing estates, removing individual gardens in favour of short, trimmed lawns. In residential design, open plan — or open concept — typically combines kitchen, dining, and living areas into a single great room.

Offices

The open-plan office emerged in early twentieth-century design, from Frank Lloyd Wright's Larkin Building (1906) with its vast central atrium of desks, through the German Bürolandschaft ("office landscape") movement of the 1950s, which arranged desks in organic groups broken by plants and screens. In the United States the concept took a different turn with Herman Miller's Action Office system (1964, redesigned 1968), intended as a flexible, humane workspace; cost-driven interpretation of it produced the cubicle farm, which in turn gave way from the 1990s to fully open layouts popularised by technology companies, with bench desks, hot-desking, and shared amenities.

Advantages and criticism

Advocates claim open plans encourage collaboration, communication, transparency, and efficient use of space. Research and employee experience are more mixed: studies have found that removing walls can paradoxically reduce face-to-face interaction while increasing electronic communication, and open offices are associated with noise, distraction, reduced privacy, higher stress, and more sick leave. The COVID-19 pandemic and hybrid work prompted many employers to reconsider density and reintroduce focus rooms and partitions.

Residential open plan

In homes, open-concept layouts became dominant in late twentieth-century design, merging kitchen and living space to suit informal entertaining and to make smaller houses feel larger. Criticisms include cooking noise and smells spreading through the living area, lack of acoustic separation, and heating inefficiency; recent design trends show renewed interest in "broken plan" layouts that use partial walls, screens, and level changes to define zones within an open space.
