Uric acid

Uric acid is a heterocyclic compound of carbon, nitrogen, oxygen and hydrogen with formula C₅H₄N₄O₃. It is the final breakdown product of purine metabolism in humans and other primates, excreted mainly by the kidneys.

Uric acid
Formula: C₅H₄N₄O₃
Molar mass: 168.11 g/mol
Source: Purine catabolism
Enzyme: Xanthine oxidase

Biology

Purines from diet and cell turnover are converted via xanthine to uric acid by xanthine oxidase. Most mammals convert it further to allantoin with uricase, an enzyme humans lost in evolution. In birds and reptiles uric acid is the main nitrogenous waste, excreted as a paste to conserve water.

Health

Excess uric acid (hyperuricemia) can crystallise as monosodium urate in joints, causing gout, and form kidney stones. It is associated with metabolic syndrome alongside high triglycerides. Treatments include allopurinol and febuxostat, which inhibit xanthine oxidase, and dietary changes.
