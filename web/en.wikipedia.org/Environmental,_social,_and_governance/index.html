Environmental, social, and governance

Environmental, social, and governance (ESG) is a framework used by investors and companies to evaluate nonfinancial risks and impacts. Environmental criteria consider emissions, resource use, and climate exposure; social criteria cover labor, communities, product safety, and human rights; governance concerns boards, audits, executive pay, and shareholder rights.

Use in investing

ESG approaches range from excluding controversial sectors to integrating sustainability data into valuation and actively engaging company management. Ratings agencies score firms using public disclosures and estimates, but methodologies differ and scores for the same company can diverge.

Standards and disclosure

Frameworks associated with the Global Reporting Initiative, the Sustainability Accounting Standards Board, the Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures, and the International Sustainability Standards Board seek more comparable reporting. Regulators in several jurisdictions have moved toward mandatory climate and sustainability disclosure for large firms.

Criticism and debate

Supporters argue ESG identifies material risks and long-term value; critics question greenwashing, inconsistent metrics, and the political use of investment mandates. See also socially responsible investing and corporate social responsibility.
