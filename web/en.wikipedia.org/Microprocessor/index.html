Microprocessor

A microprocessor is a computer processor in which the data processing logic and control are contained on a single integrated circuit (IC), or a small number of ICs. The microprocessor contains the arithmetic, logic, and control circuitry required to perform the functions of a computer's central processing unit (CPU). Microprocessors form the basis of microcomputers and are embedded in countless devices, from personal computers and smartphones to automobiles, appliances, and industrial controllers.

History

The first commercial microprocessor, the Intel 4004, was released in 1971. Designed by Federico Faggin, Ted Hoff, Stanley Mazor, and Masatoshi Shima, the 4-bit 4004 contained about 2,300 transistors and was originally developed for a calculator. It was followed by the 8-bit Intel 8008 (1972) and the influential 8080 (1974), which powered early personal computers such as the Altair 8800.

The late 1970s and 1980s saw the rise of processors such as the MOS Technology 6502, Zilog Z80, Motorola 68000, and Intel's x86 line beginning with the 8086. In the 1990s and 2000s, clock speeds climbed into the gigahertz range and designs shifted toward superscalar pipelines, out-of-order execution, and eventually multiple cores per chip as transistor scaling continued under Moore's law.

Structure and operation

A microprocessor repeatedly executes a cycle of fetching an instruction from memory, decoding it, and executing it. Core components include:

  • Arithmetic logic unit (ALU) — performs arithmetic and logical operations.
  • Control unit — directs data flow and sequences operations.
  • Registers — small, fast storage locations holding operands and results.
  • Cache memory — on-chip memory that reduces access time to main memory.
  • Bus interface — connects the processor to memory and input/output devices.

Modern processors add branch prediction, speculative execution, simultaneous multithreading, and integrated graphics processing units.

Embedded microprocessors

Most microprocessors are not used in general-purpose computers but as embedded systems, controlling functions in washing machines, microwave ovens, cars, medical devices, and industrial equipment. Embedded chips are often packaged as microcontrollers, which integrate memory and peripherals on the same die. Billions of embedded processors are produced each year, far outnumbering PC processors.
