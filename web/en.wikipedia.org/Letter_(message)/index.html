Letter (message)

A letter is a written message conveyed from one person (or group of people) to another through a medium. Letters can be formal, semi-formal or informal, and have served as a principal means of communication for individuals and businesses for centuries.

History

Written correspondence is as old as writing itself; the ancient Egyptians, Romans and Chinese all exchanged letters on papyrus, parchment and bamboo. Cicero's and Pliny's letters are major historical sources. The development of postal systems — from the Persian mounted relay praised by Herodotus to Rowland Hill's Penny Post of 1840 — made letter writing a mass practice in the 19th and 20th centuries before telephone and email displaced it for most purposes.

Types and form

Common types include personal letters, business letters, letters of recommendation, cover letters, letters to the editor and open letters. A formal business letter traditionally includes the sender's address, date, recipient's address, salutation, body, complimentary close and signature. Letters are commonly sent in envelopes by post, though the term now extends to electronic equivalents such as email.

Cultural role

Collections of letters are a literary genre — the epistolary novel (such as Pamela) imitates them, and published correspondence of figures such as Vincent van Gogh or Emily Dickinson is valued as literature and biography.
