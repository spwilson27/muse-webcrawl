Ensign

Ensign is a military rank and also the name of a type of flag. As a rank, it has historically denoted a junior commissioned officer in armies and navies; as a flag, an ensign is flown to indicate nationality, especially on ships.

Military rank

In the United States Navy and Coast Guard, ensign is the most junior commissioned officer rank (pay grade O-1), equivalent to a second lieutenant in the Army, Marine Corps, and Air Force. Officers are commissioned as ensigns on graduating from the Naval Academy, NROTC, or Officer Candidate School, and are typically promoted to lieutenant (junior grade) after about two years. Historically in the British Army, an ensign was the lowest commissioned rank in an infantry regiment — the officer who carried the regimental colours (the "ensign") — until the rank was replaced by second lieutenant in 1871. Several other countries retain ensign or equivalent ranks (such as the French enseigne de vaisseau in naval service).

Flags

In vexillology, an ensign is a flag flown at the stern of a ship or used as an emblem of a nation or organization. Examples include the British Red Ensign, Blue Ensign, and White Ensign, which combine the Union Jack in the canton with red, blue, or white fields and are used by merchant, government, and naval vessels respectively; many Commonwealth countries' flags derive from these. The United States uses its national flag as its naval ensign.
