Arktika-class icebreaker

Arktika class
Type: Nuclear-powered icebreaker
Operators: Soviet Union / Russia
Lead ship: Arktika (launched 1972)
Ships built: 6 (Project 10510/10520 series predecessor designations)

The Arktika class is a class of nuclear-powered icebreakers built for the Soviet Union and later operated by Russia. They were for decades the largest and most powerful icebreakers in the world, designed to keep the Northern Sea Route open along Russia's Arctic coast.

History

The lead ship, Arktika, was commissioned in 1975. On 17 August 1977 she became the first surface ship to reach the North Pole. Sister ships include Sibir, Rossiya, Sovetskiy Soyuz, Yamal, and 50 Let Pobedy, the last completed in 2007 and fitted for Arctic tourist cruises to the North Pole.

Design

The ships are powered by two nuclear reactors driving steam turbines and electric propulsion motors. Their hulls are specially strengthened for breaking ice up to about 3 metres thick, using a spoon-shaped bow that rides up onto the ice and crushes it under the ship's weight.

Successors

A new generation, the Project 22220 icebreakers (also called the LK-60Ya class, lead ship also named Arktika), began entering service in 2020.
