Modulo

In computing and mathematics, the modulo operation finds the remainder after division of one number by another, called the modulus. Given two positive numbers a and n, a modulo n (written a mod n) is the remainder of the Euclidean division of a by n, where a is the dividend and n is the divisor.

Definitions

For positive integers, a mod n lies between 0 and n − 1 inclusive. Programming languages differ when operands are negative: some define the result to have the sign of the divisor (floored division, as in Python), others the sign of the dividend (truncated division, as in C). Mathematicians generally fix the remainder in the range [0, n).

Applications

Modular reduction underlies clock arithmetic, check digits such as ISBN and IBAN codes, hash functions, cyclic counters, and public-key cryptography, where modular exponentiation with very large moduli secures systems such as RSA and Diffie–Hellman key exchange. The related congruence relation of modular arithmetic was systematised by Carl Friedrich Gauss in his Disquisitiones Arithmeticae (1801).
