Bill Evans

Bill Evans
Born: 16 August 1929, Plainfield, New Jersey
Died: 15 September 1980, New York City
Genres: Jazz, modal jazz, cool jazz
Instrument: Piano

William John Evans (16 August 1929 – 15 September 1980) was an American jazz pianist and composer who worked primarily as the leader of his trio. His use of impressionist harmony, interpretation of traditional repertoire, block chords, and trademark melodic lines continues to influence jazz pianists today.

Career

Classically trained, Evans moved to New York in the 1950s and joined Miles Davis's sextet in 1958, contributing to the landmark modal jazz album Kind of Blue (1959), for which he co-wrote "Blue in Green" and "Flamenco Sketches". Leaving Davis, he formed a trio with bassist Scott LaFaro and drummer Paul Motian whose interplay — documented on Sunday at the Village Vanguard and Waltz for Debby (1961) — redefined the jazz piano trio before LaFaro's death in a car accident.

Style and later work

Evans's harmony drew on Debussy, Ravel, and Chopin, and his voicings for the left hand became a template for modern jazz piano. Later trios, notably with bassist Eddie Gómez, and solo and duo recordings sustained his reputation through the 1970s despite chronic health problems and drug addiction, which contributed to his death in 1980.

Legacy

Evans won seven Grammy Awards during his career and was inducted into the Down Beat Jazz Hall of Fame. Compositions such as "Waltz for Debby", "Time Remembered", and "Peace Piece" are standards, and pianists from Herbie Hancock and Chick Corea to Keith Jarrett and Brad Mehldau cite him as a central influence.
