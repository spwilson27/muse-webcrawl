Polish Armed Forces in the West

The Polish Armed Forces in the West (Polish: Polskie Siły Zbrojne na Zachodzie) were the military forces of the Polish government-in-exile, loyal to the Western Allies, during World War II. Formed in France and the United Kingdom after Poland's defeat in 1939, they fought alongside British and Commonwealth forces in nearly every major theatre of the war in the West.

Polish troops fought with the Western Allies from 1939 to 1945.

Formation

Under agreements with France (1939) and Britain (the Sikorski–Mayski context and the 1940 Allied Forces Act), Poland rebuilt army, air force, and navy units abroad. After France fell in 1940, some 85,000 Polish personnel were evacuated to Britain. General Władysław Anders later formed an army from Poles released from Soviet captivity in 1941–42, evacuated via Iran; it became the Polish II Corps.

Major operations

Polish airmen were among the highest-scoring Allied pilots in the Battle of Britain, with No. 303 (Kościuszko) Squadron the highest-scoring Hurricane squadron. Polish forces fought at Narvik, Tobruk, Monte Cassino (captured by II Corps in May 1944), Falaise, Arnhem (1st Independent Parachute Brigade under Sosabowski), and in the liberation of the Netherlands, including Breda. The Polish Navy and merchant marine served in Atlantic convoys, and Polish intelligence made major contributions, including pre-war work on Enigma and the 1944 recovery of V-2 rocket components.

Aftermath

At war's end the forces numbered over 200,000. With Poland under a Soviet-backed government, Western recognition of the government-in-exile was withdrawn in 1945, and most servicemen faced a hard choice between return and exile. The Polish Resettlement Act 1947 helped some 200,000 settle in Britain; the forces were disbanded by 1947, and Poles were controversially excluded from the 1946 London Victory Parade.
