Warren Farrell, American author and activist.

Warren Thomas Farrell (born June 26, 1943) is an American political scientist, activist and author of books on men's and women's issues. He came to prominence in the 1970s as a supporter of second-wave feminism, serving on the board of the New York City chapter of the National Organization for Women (NOW), before becoming a leading figure in the men's movement and an advocate on issues concerning boys and fathers.

Early life and feminism

Farrell earned a bachelor's degree from Montclair State University and a PhD in political science from New York University. In the 1970s he was elected three times to the board of directors of the New York City chapter of NOW and founded a men's group within the feminist movement. His first book, The Liberated Man (1974), argued that rigid sex roles harmed both women and men.

Shift toward men's issues

From the 1980s onward Farrell increasingly focused on disadvantages he argued men face, in books such as Why Men Are the Way They Are (1986), The Myth of Male Power (1993) and Women Can't Hear What Men Don't Say (1999). He has written extensively on father absence, custody, workplace deaths and boys' educational outcomes. His 2018 book The Boy Crisis, co-authored with John Gray, argues that fatherlessness is a central driver of boys' difficulties in school, mental health and employment, and proposes policy remedies including substantially expanded father involvement.

Criticism

Farrell's work has been praised by some readers of varied political persuasions and criticized by some feminists and scholars, who dispute his framing of gender and power. Farrell has described his approach as seeking a "gender liberation movement" addressing the concerns of both sexes.
