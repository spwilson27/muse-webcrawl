The Basque Country (Basque: Euskadi; Spanish: País Vasco) is an autonomous community in northern Spain. It includes the Basque provinces of Álava, Biscay and Gipuzkoa. Its capital is Vitoria-Gasteiz, while Bilbao is its largest city. The autonomous community is bordered by the Bay of Biscay to the north, France to the northeast, Navarre to the east, La Rioja to the south and Cantabria and Castile and León to the west.

The Basque Country is home to about 2.2 million people. The Basque language (Euskara), a language isolate unrelated to any other known language, is co-official with Spanish and is spoken by a substantial minority, with its use promoted by immersion schooling and public media. The region has a strong industrial tradition — steel, shipbuilding, machine tools and energy — and in recent decades has reinvented cities such as Bilbao through cultural investments like the Guggenheim Museum Bilbao, opened in 1997.

Basque autonomy is founded on the Statute of Gernika (1979), and the community enjoys an unusually high degree of fiscal self-government under the Concierto Económico, by which it collects its own taxes. The Basque Government (Gobierno Vasco) is headed by a president known as the Lehendakari. Basque nationalism, historically associated with the Basque Nationalist Party (PNV), remains a major political force; the armed group ETA, which sought independence, declared a definitive cessation of armed activity in 2011 and dissolved in 2018.
