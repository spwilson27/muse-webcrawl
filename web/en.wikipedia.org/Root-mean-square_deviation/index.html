Root-mean-square deviation

The root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) or root-mean-square error (RMSE) is a widely used measure of the difference between values predicted by a model or estimator and the values actually observed. The RMSD represents the square root of the second sample moment of the differences between predicted and observed values, or the quadratic mean of those differences. These deviations are called residuals when calculated over the sample used for estimation, and errors (or prediction errors) when computed out of sample.

Formula

For predictions ŷi and observed values yi over n observations:

RMSD = √( (1/n) · Σ (ŷi − yi)² )

That is, the errors are squared, averaged, and the square root of the average is taken — yielding a measure in the same units as the quantity being estimated. The normalised form, NRMSD, divides by a range or mean of observed values to allow comparisons across scales.

Uses

In meteorology and climate science, RMSE is a standard measure of forecast skill. In structural biology and computational chemistry, RMSD measures the average distance between atoms of superimposed molecules — for example, the backbone atoms of a modelled protein versus an experimental structure — and values near zero indicate close agreement, though the measure is size-dependent. In machine learning, RMSE is a common loss and evaluation metric for regression, though it penalises large errors more heavily than the mean absolute error because of squaring.

Related measures

The RMSD of an estimator with respect to an estimated parameter is closely related to the mean squared error, which decomposes into the estimator's variance plus the square of its bias. When the deviations are measured against the sample mean, the RMSD equals the population standard deviation.
