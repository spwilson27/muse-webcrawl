Chicago school of economics

The Chicago school of economics is a neoclassical school of economic thought associated with the faculty of the University of Chicago, known for its strong belief in free markets, monetarism and scepticism toward government intervention. Its leading figures have included Frank Knight, Milton Friedman, George Stigler, Gary Becker and Ronald Coase, and its members have won numerous Nobel Memorial Prizes in Economic Sciences.

Ideas

Chicago economists developed the permanent income hypothesis, the theory of human capital, the economic analysis of regulation and of law, and the efficient-market hypothesis. Friedman's monetarism held that inflation is primarily a monetary phenomenon and challenged Keynesian fiscal activism; the school's emphasis on price theory and empirical testing reshaped macroeconomics, finance and public policy from the 1970s onward.

Influence

The school influenced deregulation and market-oriented reforms in the United States and Britain, and, through the "Chicago Boys", economic policy in Chile under Augusto Pinochet. Critics associate it with widening inequality and the 2008 financial crisis, debates that remain active.
