Grand Slam of Ultrarunning

Four classic American 100-mile races in a single summer

The Grand Slam of Ultrarunning is the completion, in a single summer, of four of the oldest and most prestigious 100-mile trail races in the United States: the Western States Endurance Run in California, the Vermont 100 Endurance Race, the Leadville Trail 100 in Colorado, and the Wasatch Front 100 Mile Endurance Run in Utah.

History

The challenge was first completed in 1986 by Tom Green. Because entry to Western States is by lottery, few runners can attempt the slam in any given year; finishers receive a commemorative trophy at the Wasatch awards ceremony. The order and timing are brutal: the four races fall within roughly ten weeks, including two at high altitude in the Rocky Mountains.

Other slams

The format has inspired regional variants, including the Last Great Race — all six of the oldest US 100-milers (adding Old Dominion and Angeles Crest) — and national series in other countries. Some versions of the Grand Slam have substituted races in years when an event was cancelled, for instance by wildfire or the COVID-19 pandemic.
