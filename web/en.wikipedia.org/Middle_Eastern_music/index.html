Middle Eastern music

Middle Eastern music encompasses the musical traditions of the Arab world, Iran, Turkey, Israel, and neighbouring regions spanning North Africa and Western and Central Asia. Despite the area's diversity, the musics of the region share important features, including the use of melodic modes (such as the Arabic maqam and Turkish makam), prominent improvisation, and characteristic instruments.

Characteristics

Middle Eastern music is typically monophonic and melody-focused, with ornamented melodic lines supported by rhythmic cycles (such as wahda in Arabic music). Common instruments include the oud, qanun, ney, riq, darbuka, santur, setar, bağlama, and violin, the latter adapted to local tuning systems that employ microtones not found in Western equal temperament.

Genres

Regional genres include Arabic tarab and pop, Turkish classical and arabesque music, Persian classical music, Andalusian music of the Maghreb, and the diverse Jewish and liturgical traditions of the region.
