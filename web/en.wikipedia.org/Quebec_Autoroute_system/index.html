Quebec Autoroute system

The Quebec Autoroute system (French: réseau autoroutier du Québec) is a network of expressways and freeways in the Canadian province of Quebec, managed by Transports Québec. Autoroutes are identified by blue-and-red shield markers and are numbered similarly to U.S. Interstate Highways, to which several connect at the border.

Characteristics

Autoroutes are controlled-access highways generally built to a speed limit of 100 km/h (with a widely observed practice that enforcement tolerates up to about 118 km/h). Odd-numbered autoroutes generally run north–south and even-numbered ones east–west, mirroring the Interstate grid, although the St. Lawrence River's diagonal course distorts the pattern. Most autoroutes are free of tolls; exceptions include Autoroute 25's Olivier-Charbonneau Bridge and Autoroute 30's Saint-Laurent bridge crossings, tolled electronically.

Main routes

  • A-20 — the main Montreal–Quebec City south-shore corridor, continuing west to Ontario as Highway 401
  • A-40 — the north-shore Montreal–Quebec City route via Trois-Rivières
  • A-15 — Montreal to the Laurentian mountains and south to the U.S. border (Interstate 87)
  • A-10 — Montreal to the Eastern Townships
  • A-73 — Quebec City south toward the Maine border
  • A-85 — the Trans-Canada link between Rivière-du-Loup and New Brunswick

History

The first autoroute, the Laurentian Autoroute north of Montreal, opened in 1958 as a toll road. Construction accelerated in the 1960s under the Quiet Revolution and in preparation for Expo 67 and the 1976 Montreal Olympics.
