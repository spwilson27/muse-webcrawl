Heart rate

Heart rate is the frequency of the cardiac cycle, measured as the number of heartbeats per unit of time — usually beats per minute (bpm). It can be felt as the pulse at the wrist, neck, or other points where an artery lies close to the skin. Heart rate is one of the vital signs, together with respiratory rate, blood pressure, and body temperature.

Normal values

The normal resting heart rate for adults ranges from 60 to 100 bpm. A resting rate below 60 bpm is called bradycardia and above 100 bpm tachycardia; neither is necessarily pathological — trained athletes commonly have resting rates of 40–60 bpm. Heart rate is controlled by the autonomic nervous system: sympathetic activity accelerates it, while the vagus nerve slows it. It rises with exercise, fever, stress, and excitement, and falls during sleep.

Maximum and target rates

Maximum heart rate declines with age and is often estimated as 220 minus age, though individual variation is large. Exercise intensity is frequently prescribed as a percentage of maximum or of heart-rate reserve (the Karvonen formula), and heart-rate variability — the variation in intervals between beats — is used as a marker of autonomic function and fitness.

Measurement

Heart rate can be measured manually by palpating the radial or carotid pulse, electrically by electrocardiography (ECG), and optically by photoplethysmography in pulse oximeters and wearable fitness trackers. Abnormal rhythms (arrhythmias) may indicate underlying cardiac disease and require further investigation.
