Digital billboard

A digital billboard is a billboard that displays digital images that are changed by a computer every few seconds. Digital billboards are primarily used for advertising, but they can also serve public-service purposes, such as displaying emergency alerts, traffic information, and missing-person notices. The images are typically generated by LED displays and controlled remotely over a network connection.

Advantages and operation

Unlike static billboards, which require crews to replace printed posters, a digital billboard can rotate advertisements from several advertisers in a single location, change messages by time of day, and be updated within minutes. Advertisers can buy short time slots rather than a whole sign face, lowering the cost of entry.

Regulation and criticism

Digital billboards have been criticized for driver distraction, light pollution, and energy consumption, and some jurisdictions have banned or restricted them; São Paulo's 2007 "Clean City Law" famously removed most outdoor advertising. Industry and regulatory bodies have responded with rules on brightness, dwell time between messages, and prohibitions on animation near roadways. Highway authorities also use the displays for AMBER Alerts and road-safety messages.
