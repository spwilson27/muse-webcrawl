Corporate communication

Corporate communication is the set of activities involved in managing and orchestrating all internal and external communications aimed at creating a favourable point of view among stakeholders on which a company depends. It is the practice of developing and maintaining a corporate identity or brand image, and encompasses functions such as media relations, internal communication, investor relations, government affairs and crisis communication.

Scope

Corporate communication includes management communication with employees and external audiences, marketing communication such as advertising and promotion, and organisational communication in a formal sense. It aims to build, maintain and protect the company's reputation with the public, investors, employees and other stakeholders.

Functions

Typical functions include developing the corporate identity and brand, coordinating press releases and media enquiries, producing annual reports, managing internal channels such as intranets and newsletters, and preparing executives for public appearances. In a crisis, the corporate communication function coordinates the organisation's response to protect its reputation.

History

The discipline grew out of public relations in the twentieth century as large corporations sought a coherent voice across growing numbers of channels and audiences. Scholars such as Cees van Riel helped formalise it as an academic field in the 1990s, emphasising the alignment of all organisational communication.
