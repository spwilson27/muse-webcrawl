Minamata disease

Minamata disease is a neurological syndrome caused by severe mercury poisoning, first discovered in Minamata City, Kumamoto Prefecture, Japan, in 1956. It resulted from the release of methylmercury in industrial wastewater from the Chisso Corporation's chemical factory.

Cause and symptoms

Methylmercury discharged from acetaldehyde production accumulated in fish and shellfish in Minamata Bay, which local people ate. Symptoms include numbness in the hands and feet, muscle weakness, narrowing of the field of vision, damage to hearing and speech, paralysis, and in severe cases insanity, coma, and death. Congenital cases affected children born to exposed mothers.

Aftermath

The disease was officially recognized by the Japanese government in 1968. Victims fought decades of court battles for compensation from Chisso and the state. A similar outbreak occurred in Niigata Prefecture in 1965. The global Minamata Convention on Mercury, adopted in 2013, is named after the disaster.
