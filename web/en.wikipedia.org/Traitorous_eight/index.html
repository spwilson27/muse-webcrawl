The traitorous eight were eight employees who left Shockley Semiconductor Laboratory in 1957 to found Fairchild Semiconductor. They were Julius Blank, Victor Grinich, Jean Hoerni, Eugene Kleiner, Jay Last, Gordon Moore, Robert Noyce, and Sheldon Roberts. William Shockley, their former employer and co-inventor of the transistor, reportedly coined the label.

Background

Shockley had recruited the group of young scientists to his Mountain View laboratory in 1956 to work on silicon devices, but his erratic and authoritarian management drove them to seek backing elsewhere. Sherman Fairchild funded their new venture through Fairchild Camera and Instrument.

Fairchild and beyond

At Fairchild the group pioneered the planar process (Hoerni) and the first commercially practical integrated circuits (Noyce). The company became the seedbed of Silicon Valley: its alumni — the "Fairchildren" — went on to found dozens of firms. Noyce and Moore co-founded Intel in 1968; Kleiner co-founded the venture capital firm Kleiner Perkins; Last and Hoerni were among the founders of Amelco, ancestor of Teledyne's semiconductor operations.

Legacy

The episode is regarded as a founding moment of Silicon Valley, exemplifying the region's pattern of employee spin-offs and venture financing.
