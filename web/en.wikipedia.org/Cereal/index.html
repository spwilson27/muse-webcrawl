Cereal

Cereals
Family: Poaceae (grasses)
Main crops: Wheat, rice, maize
Use: Staple foods, fodder, brewing

A cereal is any grass cultivated for its edible grain, composed of the endosperm, germ and bran. Cereals are the world's most important food crops, providing more calories to humanity than any other crop group; the term also extends loosely to pseudocereals such as quinoa and buckwheat.

History

Wheat and barley were domesticated in the Fertile Crescent around 10,000 years ago, rice in China and sorghum and millets in Africa. Maize was domesticated from teosinte in Mexico. The Green Revolution of the 20th century greatly raised cereal yields.

Production and nutrition

Maize, rice and wheat together supply roughly half of global food calories. Whole grains provide fibre, B vitamins and minerals; refined grains lose much of the bran and germ. See Wheat, Rice, Maize.
