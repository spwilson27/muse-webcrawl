User experience

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A UX designer sketching wireframes — placeholder]

User experience (UX) encompasses all aspects of the end-user's interaction with the company, its services, and its products. The first requirement for an exemplary user experience is to meet the exact needs of the customer, without fuss or bother. Next comes simplicity and elegance that produce products that are a joy to own and use.

Definition

ISO 9241-210 defines UX as a person's perceptions and responses that result from the use or anticipated use of a product, system, or service. UX includes the practical, experiential, affective, meaningful, and valuable aspects of human–computer interaction, and covers task completion, aesthetics, accessibility, and emotional response. It is broader than usability, which concerns efficiency and effectiveness, and is shaped by marketing, documentation, and support as well as by the interface itself.

Elements and practice

Practitioners such as Don Norman — who coined the term at Apple in the 1990s — and Jesse James Garrett (whose Elements of User Experience model distinguishes strategy, scope, structure, skeleton, and surface) have shaped the discipline. Common activities include user research, personas, journey mapping, wireframing, prototyping, and usability testing. The honeycomb model of UX lists the qualities a good experience should have: useful, usable, desirable, findable, accessible, credible, and valuable.
