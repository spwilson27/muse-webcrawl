Battle of Gazala

Battle of Gazala
Date: 26 May – 21 June 1942
Location: Near Tobruk, Libya
Result: Axis victory
Part of the North African campaign of the Second World War

The Battle of Gazala was fought from 26 May to 21 June 1942 in the Libyan desert during the Western Desert campaign of the Second World War. The Axis forces of the Panzerarmee Afrika, commanded by Erwin Rommel, outflanked and defeated the British Eighth Army under General Neil Ritchie, and went on to capture Tobruk — one of the most complete Axis victories of the desert war.

Background

The Eighth Army held the Gazala Line, a series of defended "boxes" behind minefields running south from the coast at Gazala into the desert as far as Bir Hakeim, held by Free French forces. Rommel planned to sweep around the desert flank with his mobile divisions while Italian infantry fixed the line frontally.

The battle

Rommel's flanking columns ran into unexpected resistance and supply trouble and were briefly trapped against the minefields in an area known as "the Cauldron", but he held off piecemeal British counterattacks and broke into the line from the east, destroying much of the British armour in mid-June (notably on "Black Saturday", 13 June). The Free French evacuation of Bir Hakeim on 10–11 June opened his supply route. With the line broken, the Eighth Army retreated into Egypt; Tobruk, left isolated, fell on 21 June with some 35,000 prisoners.

Aftermath

Rommel was promoted to field marshal and pressed on to El Alamein, where he was halted in July. The Gazala defeat led to Ritchie's replacement by Claude Auchinleck and contributed to the command changes that brought Bernard Montgomery to the Eighth Army in August.
