Qinghai–Tibet railway

The Qinghai–Tibet railway or Qingzang railway is a high-elevation railway in China that connects Xining, Qinghai Province, to Lhasa, Tibet Autonomous Region. It was the first railway to connect Tibet with the rest of China. The line includes the Tanggula Pass, at 5,072 m (16,640 ft) above sea level the world's highest point on a railway, and the 1,338 m (4,390 ft) Fenghuoshan tunnel, the highest railway tunnel in the world. The railway opened in 2006 and is operated by China Railway.

Construction

The line was built in two sections. The section between Xining and Golmud was completed in 1984. The 1,142 km Golmud–Lhasa section was constructed between 2001 and 2006 across extremely difficult terrain: about 550 km of the line lies on permafrost, requiring special engineering techniques such as crushed-rock embankments, thermosyphons and elevated viaducts to keep the ground frozen and stable. Roughly 960 km of the line lies at elevations above 4,000 m. Passenger carriages are pressurised and equipped with supplementary oxygen to counteract altitude sickness.

Impact

The railway dramatically reduced the cost and time of travel and freight transport between Tibet and inland China, stimulating tourism and trade while also drawing criticism over its environmental effects on the fragile plateau ecosystem and its impact on Tibetan culture and demographics. Wildlife crossings were built at dozens of locations to allow the migration of Tibetan antelope and other species. An extension from Lhasa to Shigatse opened in 2014, and the line has since been connected to further railways in the region.
