The Worldwide Governance Indicators (WGI) are a long-standing research dataset produced by the World Bank that reports aggregate and individual governance indicators for over 200 countries and territories. Developed by Daniel Kaufmann, Aart Kraay, and Massimo Mastruzzi, the project has published estimates since 1999, covering the period from 1996 onward.

Dimensions

The WGI measure six dimensions of governance, summarizing the views of enterprises, citizens, and expert respondents drawn from more than 30 underlying data sources:

  1. Voice and Accountability
  2. Political Stability and Absence of Violence/Terrorism
  3. Government Effectiveness
  4. Regulatory Quality
  5. Rule of Law
  6. Control of Corruption

Each country receives an estimate on a scale from approximately −2.5 (weak) to +2.5 (strong), along with percentile ranks and margins of error derived from an unobserved components model.

Uses and criticism

The indicators are widely used in academic research, by investors assessing country risk, and — historically — in aid allocation frameworks such as the Millennium Challenge Corporation's eligibility criteria and the World Bank's own discontinued Doing Business ecosystem debates. Critics have questioned the heavy reliance on perception-based sources, the statistical significance of small year-to-year changes, and the composite methodology; the project itself cautions users to take margins of error into account when comparing countries.
