El Greco

Doménikos Theotokópoulos
Born: 1 October 1541, Heraklion, Crete
Died: 7 April 1614, Toledo, Spain
Known for: Painting
Movement: Spanish Renaissance, Mannerism

Doménikos Theotokópoulos (1541 – 7 April 1614), most widely known as El Greco ("The Greek"), was a Greek painter, sculptor and architect of the Spanish Renaissance. Trained in the Byzantine icon tradition of his native Crete and in the workshops of Venice and Rome, he settled in Toledo, Spain, in 1577 and there produced his mature works.

View of Toledo (c. 1599–1600), one of El Greco's best-known works.

Life

Born in Heraklion, then part of the Republic of Venice, Theotokópoulos trained as an icon painter before travelling to Venice around 1567, where he absorbed the colour and composition of Titian and Tintoretto. In Rome he studied Michelangelo and the Mannerists. Failing to win a major court commission from Philip II of Spain—his Martyrdom of St Maurice displeased the king—he settled in Toledo, which remained his home for the rest of his life.

Style and works

El Greco's mature style is marked by elongated figures, expressive hands, brilliant and often clashing colour, and a spiritual intensity blending Byzantine, Italian Mannerist, and Spanish Counter-Reformation influences. His masterpieces include The Burial of the Count of Orgaz (1586–1588), View of Toledo, The Opening of the Fifth Seal, and numerous penetrating portraits such as the Nobleman with his Hand on his Chest.

Legacy

Neglected for centuries after his death, El Greco was rediscovered in the 19th and early 20th centuries; his distortions of form and colour profoundly influenced modern artists including Cézanne, Picasso, and the Expressionists. He is now regarded as one of the greatest painters of the Spanish school.
