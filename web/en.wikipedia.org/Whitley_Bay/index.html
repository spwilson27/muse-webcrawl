Whitley Bay
Seaside town in Tyne and Wear, England
Population: approx. 39,000
Historic county: Northumberland

Whitley Bay is a seaside town in the Metropolitan Borough of North Tyneside, Tyne and Wear, England. Situated on the North Sea coast, it is around 10 miles (16 km) north-east of Newcastle upon Tyne. The town developed as a popular holiday resort in the Victorian era and remains a residential and leisure destination.

History

The area was known as early as the 12th century, when it was associated with Tynemouth Priory; the name derives from the "white lee" or clearing belonging to the monks. For centuries Whitley was a small agricultural and quarrying village. The arrival of the railway in 1882 transformed it into a fashionable resort for Tyneside holidaymakers, and large hotels, promenades, and attractions followed.

The town's most famous landmark, St Mary's Lighthouse, was completed in 1898 on St Mary's Island, which is accessible at low tide by a short causeway. The Spanish City, a pleasure complex opened in 1910 with a distinctive dome, became an emblem of the town; mentioned in Dire Straits' song "Tunnel of Love," it was restored and reopened in 2018 after a major regeneration programme.

Geography and amenities

Whitley Bay has a long sandy beach and promenade stretching from St Mary's Island southwards towards Cullercoats and Tynemouth. The town is served by stations on the Tyne and Wear Metro. Whitley Park and the seafront have undergone extensive refurbishment as part of coastal regeneration, and the town hosts events such as the Whitley Bay Film Festival.

Sport

Whitley Bay F.C., nicknamed the Seahorses, play at Hillheads Park and famously won the FA Vase in three consecutive years (2009, 2010, and 2011). The town also has a long ice hockey tradition through the Whitley Warriors.
