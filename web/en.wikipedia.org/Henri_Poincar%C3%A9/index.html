Jules Henri Poincaré (29 April 1854 – 17 July 1912) was a French mathematician, theoretical physicist, engineer, and philosopher of science. He is often described as a polymath and is regarded as one of the founders of topology and of the qualitative theory of dynamical systems.

Mathematics

Poincaré founded algebraic topology, studied automorphic functions, and in his work on the three-body problem discovered the first instance of deterministic chaos. In 1904 he posed the Poincaré conjecture, one of the most famous problems in mathematics, finally proved by Grigori Perelman in 2003.

Physics

Poincaré made fundamental contributions to celestial mechanics, electromagnetism, and relativity; in 1905 he published key results on the Lorentz transformations and the relativity principle, independently of Einstein's paper of the same year.
