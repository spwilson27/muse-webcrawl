Yellow perch

Yellow perch
Scientific namePerca flavescens
FamilyPercidae
RangeFresh waters of North America
Conservation statusLeast Concern (IUCN)

The yellow perch (Perca flavescens), commonly referred to as simply perch, is a freshwater perciform fish native to much of North America. It is a close relative of the European perch (Perca fluviatilis) and is recognisable by its golden-yellow flanks marked with six to eight dark vertical bars, and orange-red lower fins.

Distribution and habitat

Yellow perch are found from the Hudson Bay drainage and the Great Lakes–St. Lawrence system south to the Gulf Coast's river drainages, and west to the Great Plains; they have been widely introduced elsewhere. They inhabit lakes, ponds, and slow rivers, preferring clear water with moderate vegetation, and form schools that move between deep and shallow water over the day.

Biology and fishery

Adults typically reach 15–30 cm and feed on invertebrates and small fish. Females lay long, gelatinous ribbons of eggs over vegetation in spring. Yellow perch are a popular panfish with anglers and support commercial fisheries, notably in Lake Erie, though Great Lakes populations have fluctuated with invasive species and changes in the food web. It is the state fish of several US states' recognition lists and a common target of ice fishing.
