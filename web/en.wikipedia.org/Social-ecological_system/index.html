Social-ecological system

A social-ecological system (SES) consists of "a coherent system of biophysical and social factors that regularly interact in a resilient, sustained manner", defined at several spatial, temporal, and organisational scales. The concept emphasises that the delineation between social and natural systems is artificial and arbitrary: ecosystems are shaped by human action, and human societies depend on ecosystem services.

A social-ecological system links resource systems, users, and governance

The SES framework

Elinor Ostrom formalised a general framework for analysing the sustainability of social-ecological systems in a 2009 paper in Science. It organises variables into tiers: resource systems, resource units, governance systems, and users, interacting to produce outcomes, set within broader social, economic, and political settings and related ecosystems. The framework was designed to enable comparison of case studies of common-pool resources such as fisheries, irrigation systems, and forests.

Resilience and adaptive cycles

SES research draws on resilience theory associated with C. S. Holling and the Resilience Alliance, including panarchy and adaptive cycles of growth, conservation, release, and reorganisation. Work in this tradition, by scholars such as Fikret Berkes and Carl Folke, studies how systems absorb disturbance, self-organise, and learn — and how they can transform when existing configurations become untenable.
