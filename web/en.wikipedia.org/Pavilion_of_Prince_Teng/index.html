Pavilion of Prince Teng


The Pavilion of Prince Teng beside the Gan River, Nanchang

The Pavilion of Prince Teng (Chinese: 滕王閣; pinyin: Téngwáng Gé) is a famous tower in Nanchang, Jiangxi province, China, on the east bank of the Gan River. First built in 653 during the Tang dynasty by Li Yuanying, Prince Teng, it is classed with the Yellow Crane Tower and Yuewang Tower as one of the Three Great Towers of southern China.

History

The pavilion became immortal in Chinese literature through the "Preface to the Pavilion of Prince Teng" (Tengwang Ge Xu), composed in 675 by the young poet Wang Bo at a banquet at the tower. The essay — which includes the celebrated line "the setting sun and the solitary wild goose fly together; the autumn waters and the long sky share one hue" — has been memorized by generations of Chinese schoolchildren.

Reconstruction

The pavilion was destroyed and rebuilt many times over the centuries, by war, fire, and neglect. The present structure, a nine-story reinforced-concrete tower clad in traditional Song-dynasty style, was completed in 1989 on a site near the original, on the design of architect Liang Sicheng's earlier plans. It now houses exhibitions on its own history and functions as a landmark of Nanchang.
