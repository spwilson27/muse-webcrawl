Elections in Mexico

Elections in Mexico are held to elect the President of Mexico, members of the Congress of the Union, state governors, state legislatures, and municipal officials. Federal elections are organized by the National Electoral Institute (INE).

Federal elections are held every six years for the presidency and every three years for the Chamber of Deputies.

Presidential elections

The President of Mexico serves a single six-year term (sexenio) and cannot be re-elected. The president is chosen by plurality vote in a single round. The current system dates to the democratic transition of the late 20th century; the Institutional Revolutionary Party (PRI) dominated Mexican politics for most of the 20th century before losing the presidency in 2000 to Vicente Fox of the National Action Party (PAN).

Congress

The Congress of the Union is bicameral. The Senate has 128 members serving six-year terms, and the Chamber of Deputies has 500 members serving three-year terms. Both chambers combine first-past-the-post seats with proportional representation seats.

Electoral system

Mexico uses a mixed electoral system. Deputies are elected 300 by single-member districts and 200 by proportional representation from five multi-member regions. Senators are elected 64 by plurality in each state, 32 by first minority, and 32 by national proportional representation.

Recent elections

Recent general elections include 2018, when Andrés Manuel López Obrador of Morena won the presidency, and 2024, when Claudia Sheinbaum of Morena won, becoming Mexico's first woman president.
