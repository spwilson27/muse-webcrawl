S-Bahn

A Berlin S-Bahn train. The S-Bahn is a hybrid urban–suburban rail system.

S-Bahn is a type of hybrid urban and suburban rail system serving a metropolitan region, primarily in German-speaking countries. The name is an abbreviation of Stadtschnellbahn ("city rapid railway") or Stadtbahn. S-Bahn systems combine features of rapid transit (frequent service, city-centre tunnels or elevated sections) and commuter rail (longer distances into the suburbs and surrounding towns).

Characteristics

S-Bahn networks typically feature dedicated tracks, high-frequency service (often every 10–20 minutes), through-running across the city centre, and integration with other public transport in fares and ticketing. Trains are usually electric multiple units with high acceleration and many wide doors for rapid boarding.

History

The first system to bear the name was the Berlin S-Bahn, whose electrified network took shape in the 1920s and which adopted the name in 1930. The Hamburg S-Bahn followed in 1934. After the Second World War, large new S-Bahn networks were built in West German cities including Munich (opened for the 1972 Olympics), Frankfurt, Stuttgart, and the Rhine-Ruhr conurbation. In East Germany, S-Bahn networks were developed in cities such as Dresden, Leipzig, and Rostock.

Networks

S-Bahn systems operate in most large German cities, as well as in Austria (Vienna, Salzburg, Graz, Innsbruck, Linz, and others) and Switzerland (where they are also called S-Bahn in German-speaking cantons, e.g. the Zürich S-Bahn, the largest Swiss network). Similar systems elsewhere are often compared to the S-Bahn, such as the RER in Paris.
