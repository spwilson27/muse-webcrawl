Raymond Kurzweil (born February 12, 1948) is an American inventor and futurist. A pioneer in optical character recognition (OCR), speech recognition, and text-to-speech synthesis, he founded several technology companies and is widely known for his writings on artificial intelligence and the technological singularity, notably The Age of Intelligent Machines (1990), The Singularity Is Near (2005), and The Singularity Is Nearer (2024).

Inventions and companies

After graduating from MIT, Kurzweil founded Kurzweil Computer Products in 1974. Its Kurzweil Reading Machine (1976), combining OCR with flatbed scanning and text-to-speech, let blind people read ordinary printed books; Stevie Wonder was its first customer and began a long collaboration that led to the Kurzweil K250 synthesizer (1984). Kurzweil later founded Kurzweil Applied Intelligence (speech recognition) and Kurzweil Educational Systems. In 2012 he joined Google as a director of engineering working on machine intelligence and natural language.

Predictions

Kurzweil's "law of accelerating returns" argues that technological change is exponential. He predicted that computers would defeat the best human chess players by 1998 (achieved by Deep Blue in 1997) and forecasts human-level AI around 2029 and a singularity by 2045. His predictions have drawn both influence and criticism from researchers who question exponential extrapolation and his health regimen — he has written longevity books including Transcend (2009).

Honours

Kurzweil received the National Medal of Technology and Innovation in 1999 and was inducted into the National Inventors Hall of Fame in 2002.
