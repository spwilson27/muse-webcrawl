Onam

Onam (ഓണം)
Observed by: Malayalis worldwide
Type: Harvest festival
When: Month of Chingam (Aug–Sep), 10 days
Key day: Thiruvonam

Onam is an annual harvest and cultural festival celebrated by the people of Kerala, India, and by Malayalis around the world. Falling in the Malayalam month of Chingam (August–September), its ten days of festivities culminate on Thiruvonam and commemorate the legendary King Mahabali, whose annual return from the netherworld the festival welcomes.

Legend

According to tradition, the benevolent Asura king Mahabali ruled a golden age in which all were equal and happy. The gods, alarmed at his power, sent Vishnu in the form of the dwarf Vamana, who asked for three paces of land and grew to cover heaven and earth in two strides; Mahabali offered his own head for the third. Pleased with his devotion, Vishnu granted him the boon of visiting his people once a year — the visit Onam celebrates.

Celebrations

Homes are decorated with pookkalam, intricate floral carpets that grow each day of the festival. The centrepiece is the Onam sadhya, a vegetarian feast of more than two dozen dishes served on banana leaves. Traditional festivities include the Vallam Kali snake-boat races (notably at Aranmula), Pulikali "tiger dance," Kaikottikali dances by women in the cream-and-gold kasavu sari, masked Kathakali performances, and games known as Onakalikal. People wear new clothes (onakkodi) and exchange gifts.

Significance

Onam is Kerala's state festival, celebrated across religious communities, and is marked by government-sponsored cultural programmes during Tourism Week in Thiruvananthapuram.
