Spanish (español) or Castilian (castellano) is a Romance language of the Ibero-Romance group that evolved from Vulgar Latin in the north of the Iberian Peninsula and spread with the expansion of the Crown of Castile and the Spanish Empire. With roughly half a billion native speakers — concentrated in Spain and Spanish America, above all Mexico — it is the world's second-most spoken native language after Mandarin Chinese, and an official language of the United Nations.

Spanish is official across most of Latin America and in Spain.

History

Spanish developed from the Latin spoken in Hispania, absorbing vocabulary from Basque, Visigothic and especially Arabic during the centuries of al-Andalus — some 4,000 words, including aceite, ojalá and many place names. Alfonso X of Castile promoted Castilian for administration and learning in the 13th century; Antonio de Nebrija's Gramática of 1492 was the first grammar of a modern European language. Colonisation carried Spanish throughout the Americas and the Philippines.

Features

Spanish is a phonetic language with five pure vowels, a trilled /r/, and largely regular spelling governed by the RAE. Its verb system marks mood (indicative/subjunctive) and aspect richly. Notable regional features include distinción vs. seseo (the /θ/ sound for c/z in most of Spain), voseo (use of vos in the Río de la Plata and elsewhere), and vocabulary borrowed from Indigenous American languages such as Nahuatl (chocolate, tomate) and Quechua.

Geographic distribution

Mexico has the largest Spanish-speaking population, followed by Colombia, Spain and Argentina; the United States has more Spanish speakers than Spain. Equatorial Guinea is the only African country where Spanish is official. Literature in Spanish, from Cervantes's Don Quixote to Gabriel García Márquez, is among the world's most influential.
