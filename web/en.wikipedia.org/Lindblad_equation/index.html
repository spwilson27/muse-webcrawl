Lindblad equation

In quantum mechanics, the Lindblad equation (also called the Gorini–Kossakowski–Sudarshan–Lindblad equation, or GKSL equation) is a Markovian master equation describing the time evolution of the density matrix of an open quantum system — a system interacting with an environment that causes decoherence and dissipation.

Form

The equation has the form dρ/dt = −(i/ħ)[H, ρ] + Σk (Lk ρ Lk† − ½{Lk†Lk, ρ}), where ρ is the density matrix, H the system Hamiltonian, and the operators Lk are "Lindblad operators" (jump operators) encoding the system's coupling to its environment. The first term gives ordinary unitary (von Neumann) evolution; the remaining "dissipator" terms produce non-unitary effects such as relaxation and dephasing.

Properties

The Lindblad form is the most general form of a master equation that is Markovian and generates a completely positive, trace-preserving dynamical semigroup. Göran Lindblad derived the result in 1976 for bounded generators, independently of work by Vittorio Gorini, Andrzej Kossakowski, and George Sudarshan.

Applications

Lindblad equations are widely used in quantum optics (cavity QED, spontaneous emission), quantum information (modelling noise in qubits), and condensed-matter physics wherever weak coupling to a bath and memoryless (Born–Markov) approximations are justified.
