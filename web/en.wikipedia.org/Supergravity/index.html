Supergravity

Supergravity (often abbreviated SUGRA) is a field theory that combines the principles of supersymmetry and general relativity. In supergravity, the supersymmetry is a local symmetry, and the theory necessarily includes a spin-3/2 particle, the gravitino, as the superpartner of the spin-2 graviton.

History

The first supergravity theory in four dimensions was formulated in 1976 by Daniel Freedman, Peter van Nieuwenhuizen, and Sergio Ferrara, and independently by Stanley Deser and Bruno Zumino. Eleven-dimensional supergravity, constructed by Eugène Cremmer, Bernard Julia, and Joël Scherk in 1978, later drew attention as the low-energy limit related to M-theory.

Features

Supergravity theories were once candidates for a finite theory of quantum gravity, although the simplest versions were eventually shown to diverge at higher loop orders. They remain important in string theory compactifications and in the Anti-de Sitter / conformal field theory correspondence.
