Space warfare


Artist's concept of an anti-satellite interception

Space warfare is combat in which one or more belligerents are situated in outer space. The scope of space warfare therefore includes ground-to-space warfare, such as attacking satellites from the Earth, as well as space-to-space warfare, such as satellites attacking satellites. It does not include the use of space for communications or reconnaissance in support of terrestrial operations, sometimes called space-enabled warfare, nor does it include the purely hypothetical conduct of wars between planets.

History

Military interest in space began with the earliest rockets: Germany's V-2 of the Second World War was the first object to cross into space in military service, and the Cold War superpowers developed intercontinental ballistic missiles that travel through space. From the 1960s both the United States and the Soviet Union tested anti-satellite (ASAT) weapons, including co-orbital interceptor satellites and, in the United States, nuclear-armed systems such as Program 437. The Soviet Union also flew the Almaz military space stations, one of which test-fired a cannon in orbit.

Anti-satellite weapons

ASAT weapons include direct-ascent missiles, co-orbital systems, directed-energy weapons, and electronic jamming and spoofing. Notable debris-generating tests include China's destruction of the Fengyun-1C weather satellite in 2007, which created thousands of tracked debris fragments, the US shootdown of USA-193 in 2008, India's Mission Shakti test in 2019, and Russia's destruction of Kosmos 1408 in 2021, which forced the crew of the International Space Station to shelter.

Law and doctrine

The Outer Space Treaty of 1967 prohibits placing weapons of mass destruction in orbit and military installations on celestial bodies, but does not ban conventional weapons in space. Several states have established dedicated space forces, including the United States Space Force (2019), and strategists debate deterrence, escalation, and the vulnerability of the satellites on which modern militaries depend for navigation, communications, and missile warning.
