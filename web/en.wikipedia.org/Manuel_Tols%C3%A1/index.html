Manuel Tolsá

Born4 May 1757, Enguera, Spain
Died24 December 1816, Mexico City
OccupationArchitect and sculptor

Manuel Tolsá (1757–1816) was a Spanish architect and sculptor who worked in New Spain (Mexico) and was the leading exponent of Neoclassical art in colonial Mexico.

Career

Trained at the Royal Academy of Fine Arts of San Carlos in Valencia, Tolsá arrived in Mexico City in 1791 to direct the sculpture programme of the Academia de San Carlos. He completed the towers and dome of Mexico City Metropolitan Cathedral and designed the Palacio de Minería (1797–1813), considered his masterpiece, as well as the Hospicio Cabañas in Guadalajara.

El Caballito

His best-known sculpture is the monumental bronze equestrian statue of King Charles IV of Spain, popularly called El Caballito ("The Little Horse"), cast in 1803 and now standing in the plaza that bears his name in Mexico City.
