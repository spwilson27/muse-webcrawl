Lead and Copper Rule

The Lead and Copper Rule (LCR) is a United States federal regulation issued by the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) under the Safe Drinking Water Act. First published in 1991, it aims to protect public health by minimizing lead and copper levels in drinking water, primarily by reducing water corrosivity and requiring water systems to control the leaching of these metals from plumbing materials into tap water.

Requirements

Unlike most drinking-water standards, which set maximum contaminant levels measured at the treatment plant, the Lead and Copper Rule is a treatment technique regulation: it sets "action levels" of 15 parts per billion (ppb) for lead and 1.3 parts per million (ppm) for copper, based on the 90th percentile of tap water samples collected at high-risk homes. If more than 10 percent of samples exceed the action level, the water system must take additional actions, including corrosion control treatment, public education, and lead service line replacement.

Revisions

The rule was substantially revised in the wake of water crises such as the one in Flint, Michigan. The Lead and Copper Rule Revisions (LCRR) were finalized in 2021, and the Lead and Copper Rule Improvements (LCRI), finalized in 2024, lower the lead action level to 10 ppb and require most water systems to replace all lead service lines within ten years.

Health basis

Lead is a potent neurotoxin, particularly harmful to infants and children, and no level of lead exposure is considered safe. Copper in excess can cause gastrointestinal distress and, over time, liver or kidney damage.
