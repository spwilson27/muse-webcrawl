Antarctic

The Antarctic region comprises the continent of Antarctica and surrounding ocean and islands

The Antarctic (US spelling Antarctic; from Greek antarktikos, "opposite to the Arctic") is a polar region around the South Pole, comprising the continent of Antarctica, the Kerguelen Plateau, and other island territories located in the Antarctic Plate or south of the Antarctic Convergence. The Antarctic region is often defined politically by the area covered by the Antarctic Treaty System, south of 60°S latitude.

Geography and climate

The region is the coldest, driest and windiest on Earth. The Antarctic Convergence — where cold Antarctic waters meet warmer subantarctic waters — forms a natural biological boundary roughly between 48°S and 61°S. South of it, ecosystems are dominated by krill, penguins, seals and seabirds. Sea ice extent around the continent doubles its effective size in winter.

Wildlife

Terrestrial life is sparse — mosses, lichens and two flowering plants (Antarctic hair grass and Antarctic pearlwort) — while marine life is rich. Emperor and Adélie penguins breed on the continent; leopard seals, Weddell seals and orcas patrol the waters.

Governance

Under the Antarctic Treaty (1959), the region is reserved for peaceful scientific research, military activity is prohibited, and territorial claims are held in abeyance.
