Indicator species

Indicator species
FieldEcology, environmental monitoring
PurposeAssess environmental conditions or changes in an ecosystem
ExamplesLichens (air quality), amphibians (wetland health), mayflies (water quality)

An indicator species is an organism whose presence, absence, abundance, or condition reflects a specific environmental condition or change in an ecosystem. Because such species are sensitive to particular factors—such as pollution, pH, temperature, or habitat disturbance—monitoring them can provide early warning of environmental problems more cheaply or quickly than direct chemical measurement.

Types

Indicator species are sometimes grouped by function. Bioindicators show the quality of the environment; biomonitors are used quantitatively to measure pollutant exposure; and sentinel species are organisms, sometimes deliberately placed, that accumulate toxins or show measurable responses to contamination. Some classifications distinguish plant indicators, animal indicators, and microbial indicators.

Examples

Lichens are classic indicators of air quality because they absorb water and nutrients directly from the atmosphere and are highly sensitive to sulfur dioxide. Aquatic macroinvertebrates such as mayflies, stoneflies, and caddisflies indicate clean, oxygen-rich water, whereas their replacement by pollution-tolerant worms and midges signals degradation. Amphibians, with their permeable skin and biphasic life cycles, are widely used as indicators of wetland and ecosystem health, and their global declines have drawn attention to environmental stressors.

Limitations

No single species reflects all environmental conditions, and indicator responses can be confounded by disease, predation, or natural variability. Monitoring programmes therefore often use indices based on whole communities of indicator taxa rather than a single species.
