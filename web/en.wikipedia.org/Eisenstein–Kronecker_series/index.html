Eisenstein–Kronecker series

In mathematics, the Eisenstein–Kronecker series, also called Kronecker's double series, are generalisations of the Eisenstein series Ek to two complex variables. They are sums over a lattice in the complex plane depending on two parameters — points of the associated complex torus — and they interpolate between the classical Eisenstein series and the Weierstrass elliptic functions, playing a central role in the analytic theory of elliptic curves, complex multiplication, and the arithmetic of elliptic units.

Definition

Let Λ = ℤω1 + ℤω2 be a lattice in the complex plane. For integers a, b ≥ 0 (not both zero), complex parameters u, v, and Re(s) sufficiently large, the Eisenstein–Kronecker series is defined by a double sum over the nonzero lattice elements w ∈ Λ, schematically

Ea,b(u, v, s) = Σ′ (ū + w̄)a(v + w)b / |u + w|2s,

with suitable shifts of the summation variables by the parameters u and v and the product taken over the lattice with the singular term omitted. The series admits analytic continuation in s, and its special values recover the classical objects: setting the parameters to zero yields Eisenstein series, while differentiating in the parameters produces the Weierstrass ℘-function and its derivatives.

Properties and applications

The Eisenstein–Kronecker series satisfy distribution and functional relations generalising those of the Eisenstein series, and they appear as the coefficients of the Kronecker theta function. André Weil's Elliptic Functions according to Eisenstein and Kronecker (1976) revived the subject, presenting a unified treatment of the theory as developed from the manuscripts of Gotthold Eisenstein and Leopold Kronecker.

In modern number theory, special values of these series at torsion points of elliptic curves with complex multiplication give rise to elliptic units, which Kronecker dreamed of using for explicit class field theory — his famous Jugendtraum. The p-adic analogues of the Eisenstein–Kronecker series are used to construct p-adic L-functions for imaginary quadratic fields, and the series also occur in the theory of real-analytic Eisenstein series and in string theory amplitudes.
