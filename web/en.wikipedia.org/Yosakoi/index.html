Yosakoi (Japanese: よさこい) is a highly energetic style of festival dance that originated in Kōchi, Japan, in 1954 as a modernised offshoot of the traditional Awa Odori. Teams of up to about 150 dancers perform synchronised choreography in vivid costumes while clicking small wooden clappers called naruko, originally used by farmers to scare birds from rice fields.

Rules and style

Under the original Kōchi rules, choreography is free but music must incorporate the melody of the folk song "Yosakoi Bushi", which recounts the love story of a monk and a merchant's daughter at Harimayabashi bridge. Teams parade and perform on stages to arrangements blending folk melody with rock, samba, hip-hop and other genres, often accompanied by decorated trucks (jigatasha) carrying singers and musicians.

The Yosakoi Festival

The Yosakoi Matsuri in Kōchi, held each August since 1954, draws some 200 teams and 20,000 dancers. The style spread across Japan and internationally; the Yosakoi Soran Festival in Sapporo, founded in 1992 and fusing yosakoi with Hokkaido's soran bushi, attracts over a million spectators. Yosakoi teams now perform in dozens of countries.
