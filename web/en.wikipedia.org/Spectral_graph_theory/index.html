Spectral graph theory

FieldMathematics, combinatorics
ObjectsEigenvalues of adjacency and Laplacian matrices
Key textChung, Spectral Graph Theory (1997)

Spectral graph theory is the study of the properties of a graph in relation to the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of matrices associated with it, such as the adjacency matrix and the graph Laplacian.

Main ideas

The eigenvalues of the Laplacian bound a graph's connectivity, expansion, and mixing behaviour; the spectral gap of a random walk controls how quickly it converges. Cheeger's inequality relates the second-smallest Laplacian eigenvalue to the graph's isoperimetric number, and expander graphs — sparse graphs with large spectral gaps — have become central objects in combinatorics and computer science.

Applications

Spectral methods are used in clustering (spectral clustering), ranking, network analysis, and the study of vibrational modes of molecules, where the field has roots in quantum chemistry.
