Illegal drug trade

The illegal drug trade, drug trafficking, or narcotrafficking, is a global black market dedicated to the cultivation, manufacture, distribution, and sale of prohibited drugs. Most jurisdictions prohibit trade in many types of drugs through drug prohibition laws. The United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime (UNODC) has estimated the annual value of the trade in the hundreds of billions of dollars, making it one of the largest illicit markets in the world.

Major traded drugs

The principal commodities of the trade include cannabis, cocaine, heroin and other opiates, and synthetic drugs such as methamphetamine and MDMA. Cocaine production is concentrated in the Andean region of South America, and opium poppy cultivation historically in Afghanistan and the Golden Triangle of Southeast Asia. Trafficking routes run through Central America, the Caribbean, West Africa, and the Balkans toward consumer markets in North America and Europe.

Effects and enforcement

The trade is closely associated with organised crime, including cartels in Mexico and Colombia, and with corruption, violence, and money laundering. Governments respond through interdiction, crop eradication, and international treaties such as the 1961 Single Convention on Narcotic Drugs. Since the late twentieth century there has been ongoing debate over prohibition, decriminalisation, and legalisation as policy alternatives, sometimes described as the "war on drugs".
