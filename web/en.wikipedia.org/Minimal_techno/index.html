Minimal techno
Stylistic originsDetroit techno, house, minimalism
Cultural originsEarly 1990s, Detroit and Berlin
Key artistsRobert Hood, Richie Hawtin, Ricardo Villalobos

Minimal techno is a minimalist subgenre of techno music characterized by a stripped-down aesthetic that emphasizes repetition, subtle variation and understated development. It is thought to have emerged in the early 1990s through the work of Detroit producers such as Robert Hood and Daniel Bell.

Characteristics

Minimal techno tracks typically revolve around a small number of sounds — a kick drum, a bassline, sparse percussion and small textural fragments — arranged over long durations. Rather than dramatic build-ups, interest is generated by micro-level changes in rhythm, timbre and effects such as delay and reverb.

History

Robert Hood's 1994 album Minimal Nation is regarded as a landmark. In the 2000s the style became hugely popular in European clubs, particularly in Berlin, with labels such as Kompakt, Perlon and Minus and artists like Richie Hawtin and Ricardo Villalobos. A related commercial offshoot was dubbed "mnml", and by the late 2000s some critics felt the genre's sound had become formulaic, though it continued to evolve and influence tech house and microhouse.
