Limits to growth

The limits to growth are the concept that exponential growth of population and industrial output on a finite planet will eventually encounter resource and environmental limits. The idea was brought to worldwide attention by The Limits to Growth, a 1972 report commissioned by the Club of Rome and written by a team at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology led by Donella Meadows, Dennis Meadows, Jørgen Randers, and William W. Behrens III.

The World3 model

The report used the World3 system-dynamics model to simulate interactions among population, industrial capital, food production, nonrenewable resources, and pollution through to 2100. In its "standard run", growth overshot carrying capacity and was followed by decline in the mid-21st century; stabilisation scenarios showed that sustainability could be achieved through deliberate policies.

Reception and updates

The book sold millions of copies and sparked intense debate: critics argued it underestimated technology, substitution, and market adaptation, while supporters saw it as a pioneering warning. Updated editions (1992's Beyond the Limits and a 30-year update in 2004) revisited the modelling, and later comparisons have found historical data broadly tracking the standard run.
