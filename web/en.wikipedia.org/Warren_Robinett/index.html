Warren Robinett, designer of the Atari 2600 game Adventure.

John Warren Robinett Jr. (born December 25, 1951) is an American video game designer and programmer best known for creating Adventure (1979) for the Atari 2600, one of the first action-adventure games, and for hiding within it the first widely known video game Easter egg: a secret room bearing the message "Created by Warren Robinett".

Career at Atari

Robinett earned a master's degree in computer science from the University of California, Berkeley, and joined Atari in the late 1970s. His first game was Slot Racers (1978). He then developed Adventure, adapting the text adventure Colossal Cave Adventure into a graphical game with a roaming player character, objects, monsters (dragons) and mazes — a major technical achievement within the 2600's 4 KB cartridge limit. The game sold around a million copies.

The Easter egg

Atari at the time did not credit its game designers and paid them modest salaries. Robinett hid a secret room in Adventure, reachable only by carrying an invisible one-pixel object (the "dot") to a specific wall, displaying his name. Management discovered it only after a young player wrote in about it; rather than remove it, Atari's management reportedly embraced the idea of hidden surprises in games, popularizing the term "Easter egg" for such secrets.

Later work

After leaving Atari, Robinett co-founded The Learning Company in 1980, where he created the educational game Rocky's Boots (1982), which won multiple awards. He later worked in virtual reality research, including at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill and with NASA projects. The Easter egg in Adventure plays a prominent role in Ernest Cline's novel Ready Player One and its film adaptation.
