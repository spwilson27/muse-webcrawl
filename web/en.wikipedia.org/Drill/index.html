Drill

A drill is a tool used for making round holes or driving fasteners. It is fitted with a bit — either a drill bit for cutting holes or a driver bit for screws — attached to a rotating chuck. Drills are among the most common power and hand tools.

History

Hand-powered boring tools date to prehistory: the bow drill and pump drill were used from the Stone Age for both fire-making and hole-boring. The brace, a cranked hand tool, appeared in the 15th century. The first electric drill was invented in 1889 by Arthur James Arnot and William Brain in Melbourne, Australia; portable electric drills followed in the early 20th century, and cordless battery drills became widespread from the 1960s onward.

Types

  • Hand drills and braces — manually cranked.
  • Corded and cordless power drills — general-purpose drilling and screwdriving; most have variable speed, reversible rotation, and an adjustable clutch.
  • Hammer drills — add a percussive action for masonry.
  • Rotary hammers — heavier pneumatic hammering for concrete.
  • Drill presses — fixed, bench- or floor-mounted machines for precise holes.
  • Impact drivers — deliver rotational impacts for driving large fasteners.

Use

The correct drill bit and speed depend on the material: twist bits for metal and wood, spade and auger bits for large wood holes, masonry bits (often with hammer action) for brick and concrete, and hole saws for large diameters. Cutting fluid, pilot holes, and backing material improve results and safety.
