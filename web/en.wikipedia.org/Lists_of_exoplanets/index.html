Lists of exoplanets

More than 5,000 exoplanets — planets orbiting stars other than the Sun — have been confirmed since the first discoveries around the pulsar PSR B1257+12 in 1992 and the first around a Sun-like star, 51 Pegasi b, in 1995.

Notable lists

Exoplanets are listed by year of discovery, by detection method (transit, radial velocity, direct imaging, microlensing and timing), by host star and by physical characteristics. Specialised lists cover exoplanets in the habitable zone, potentially habitable worlds, multiplanet systems, disintegrating and rogue planets, and records such as the nearest (Proxima Centauri b), the largest, smallest, hottest and most distant.

Surveys

The Kepler space telescope found thousands of candidates by the transit method between 2009 and 2018; its successor TESS surveys bright nearby stars, while ground-based radial-velocity instruments such as HARPS and ESPRESSO measure masses. Direct imaging has captured young giant planets in systems such as HR 8799.
