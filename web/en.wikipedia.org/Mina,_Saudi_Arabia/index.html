Mina, Saudi Arabia

Region: Makkah Province

Country: Saudi Arabia

Significance: Hajj pilgrimage site

Mina (Arabic: مِنَى) is a valley and neighbourhood located five kilometres east of Mecca in western Saudi Arabia. It is best known for its role in the Hajj pilgrimage, during which it hosts a vast "tent city" of air-conditioned, fireproof tents accommodating millions of pilgrims.

Role in the Hajj

Pilgrims spend several nights in Mina during the Hajj. Its central ritual is the ramy al-jamarāt, the symbolic stoning of the devil, in which pebbles are thrown at three pillars (now walls) at the Jamarat Bridge, commemorating Abraham's rejection of temptation. Pilgrims also perform the Eid al-Adha animal sacrifice in or near Mina.

Safety

The concentration of enormous crowds in Mina has led to several tragic crowd disasters, most notably in 2015. Since then Saudi authorities have expanded the Jamarat Bridge into a multi-level structure and introduced strict scheduling of pilgrim movements.
