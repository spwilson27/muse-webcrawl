Sixth-generation jet fighter

A sixth-generation jet fighter is a conceptual class of fighter aircraft design more advanced than the fifth-generation jets in service, such as the F-35 Lightning II and Su-57. Programs under development in the United States, Europe, China, Japan and elsewhere aim to field such aircraft in the 2030s.

Sixth-generation fighter

Preceded by: Fifth-generation fighter
Expected service: 2030s
Programs: NGAD, GCAP, FCAS, and others

Characteristics

Although no single definition is agreed, commonly cited features include advanced stealth shaping and materials, optional manning, adaptive-cycle engines, directed-energy weapons, and the ability to command loyal-wingman drones. Sixth-generation concepts emphasise sensor fusion and networking — the fighter as a node in a wider combat cloud rather than a standalone platform — along with modular open-system architectures for rapid upgrades.

Programs

The United States is pursuing the Air Force's Next Generation Air Dominance (NGAD) programme and a separate Navy effort. The UK, Japan and Italy merged their work into the Global Combat Air Programme (GCAP), while France, Germany and Spain cooperate on the Future Combat Air System (FCAS). China has flown demonstrator aircraft widely reported as sixth-generation prototypes, and Russia has discussed the MiG-41 interceptor concept.
