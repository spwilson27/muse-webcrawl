Netscape

Netscape Communications Corporation was an American independent computer services company founded in 1994, best known for the Netscape Navigator web browser, which dominated the market in the mid-1990s and helped popularise the World Wide Web.

Founding and Navigator

Founded by Marc Andreessen, co-author of the Mosaic browser, and Jim Clark, the company released Netscape Navigator in late 1994. Its spectacular initial public offering in August 1995 is often seen as a spark of the dot-com boom.

Browser wars

Microsoft responded with Internet Explorer, bundled with Windows, in the "browser wars" that eroded Netscape's market share. The practices involved became a central issue in the United States antitrust case against Microsoft.

Acquisition and legacy

AOL acquired Netscape in 1999. In 1998 Netscape had released its browser source code as open source, creating the Mozilla project, from which the Firefox browser descends. The Netscape brand was discontinued in 2008.
