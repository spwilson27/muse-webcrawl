TypeSeasoning / rice wine
OriginJapan

Mirin (味醂 or みりん) is an essential condiment in Japanese cuisine: a sweet rice wine similar to sake but with lower alcohol content and higher sugar content, the sugar formed naturally through fermentation rather than added.

Production and types

Traditional hon-mirin is made from steamed glutinous rice, rice kōji and shōchū, matured for weeks to months, and contains about 14 percent alcohol and roughly 40–50 percent sugars. Cheaper "mirin-like seasonings" (mirin-fū chōmiryō) contain little or no alcohol with sugar and flavorings added, while salted cooking sake labelled shio-mirin avoids liquor tax.

Culinary use

Mirin adds sweetness, umami, gloss and glaze to dishes such as teriyaki, balances the saltiness of soy sauce in simmering liquids, helps mask fishy odors, and keeps ingredients from breaking down during cooking. It is also a component of tare sauces and noodle dipping broths.
