Lectio Divina

Lectio Divina (Latin for "divine reading") is a traditional monastic practice of scriptural reading, meditation and prayer intended to promote communion with God and to increase the knowledge of God's word. It does not treat scripture as a text to be studied analytically, but as the living word to be savoured slowly and prayerfully.

The four movements

The practice was classically described by the Carthusian Guigo II in the twelfth century as a ladder of four steps:

  1. Lectio — reading the passage slowly and attentively, several times.
  2. Meditatio — meditating on the text, dwelling on a word or phrase that stands out.
  3. Oratio — prayer, responding to God in light of the text.
  4. Contemplatio — contemplation, resting silently in God's presence.

History and modern use

Rooted in the Rule of Saint Benedict, which prescribed daily periods of prayerful reading, lectio divina was preserved in Benedictine and other monastic communities for centuries. Since the Second Vatican Council, which commended the practice in Dei Verbum, it has spread widely among lay Catholics and other Christians, and it is also practised in forms adapted by other traditions.
