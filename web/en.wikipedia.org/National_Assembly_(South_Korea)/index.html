National Assembly (South Korea)

National Assembly of the Republic of Korea

Type: Unicameral legislature
Seats: 300
Meeting place: National Assembly Building, Yeouido, Seoul
Term: Four years

The National Assembly of the Republic of Korea (Korean: 대한민국 국회) is the unicameral national legislature of South Korea. It consists of 300 members elected to four-year terms: 254 are elected in single-member constituencies and 46 by proportional representation. The Assembly meets in the National Assembly Building on Yeouido island in Seoul.

History

The first National Assembly was elected in 1948 under the new constitution of the Republic of Korea. Under the authoritarian governments of the 1960s and 1970s its powers were curtailed, but democratisation in 1987 restored the Assembly as a central institution of Korean politics, with powers to impeach officials, approve budgets, and override presidential vetoes by a two-thirds vote.

Powers and functions

The Assembly enacts legislation, approves the national budget and treaties, audits government agencies, and confirms certain appointments. It may recommend the removal of the prime minister and can impeach the president, with the final decision resting with the Constitutional Court. Sessions are known for vigorous, sometimes physical, debate.

Elections

Elections are held every four years. A mixed-member supplementary system combines constituency seats with proportional representation seats awarded by party vote, a system reformed in 2019 to increase proportionality.
