Toyota in Formula One refers to the participation of Japanese manufacturer Toyota as a Formula One constructor from 2002 to 2009, racing as Panasonic Toyota Racing. Based in Cologne, Germany, the team operated one of the largest budgets in the sport but never won a Grand Prix, recording 13 podium finishes and three pole positions in 139 races before withdrawing at the end of 2009 amid the global financial crisis.

History

Toyota announced its Formula One entry in 1999 and spent 2001 testing the TF101 test car before debuting with Mika Salo and Allan McNish in 2002. Despite enormous resources and its own engine programme, results were modest in the first seasons. The team's best year was 2005, when Jarno Trulli and Ralf Schumacher took five podiums and Toyota finished fourth in the constructors' standings with the TF105.

Subsequent seasons brought frustration: expensive driver line-ups and management changes — including the departure of technical director Mike Gascoyne — failed to deliver a victory. Highlights included pole position and a podium for Trulli at the rain-affected 2009 Japanese Grand Prix, and Timo Glock's podiums in 2008–2009. On 4 November 2009, Toyota announced its immediate withdrawal from Formula One, citing economic conditions; its Cologne facility and TF110 design were sold on, and the team never raced again. Toyota's motorsport focus shifted to the World Endurance Championship and World Rally Championship under Toyota Gazoo Racing, where it achieved the championship success that eluded it in F1.

Records

Toyota remains one of the most successful Formula One constructors never to win a race, and its spending without a victory is often cited as a cautionary tale about manufacturer involvement in the sport. Its drivers across eight seasons were Mika Salo, Allan McNish, Cristiano da Matta, Olivier Panis, Ricardo Zonta, Ralf Schumacher, Jarno Trulli, Timo Glock and Kamui Kobayashi.
