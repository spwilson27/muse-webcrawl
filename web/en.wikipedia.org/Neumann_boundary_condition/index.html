Neumann boundary condition

In mathematics, the Neumann boundary condition (or boundary condition of the second kind) is a type of boundary condition, named after Carl Neumann. When imposed on an ordinary or a partial differential equation, the condition specifies the values of the derivative applied at the boundary of the domain.

Definition

For a differential equation defined on a domain Ω, a Neumann boundary condition specifies the outward normal derivative of the unknown function on the boundary ∂Ω. For example, for Laplace's equation, the condition takes the form ∂u/∂n = f on ∂Ω, where n is the outward unit normal.

Applications

Neumann conditions appear in many physical problems. In heat conduction they prescribe the heat flux across a boundary; a zero Neumann condition represents a perfectly insulated boundary. In electrostatics they specify the normal component of the electric field at a surface.

Comparison with other conditions

The Neumann condition contrasts with the Dirichlet boundary condition, which specifies the value of the function itself on the boundary, and with the Robin boundary condition, which is a weighted combination of the two. Problems combining Dirichlet conditions on part of the boundary and Neumann conditions elsewhere are called mixed boundary value problems.
