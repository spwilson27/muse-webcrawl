Business Model Canvas

The Business Model Canvas is a strategic management template for developing new business models and documenting existing ones. It was proposed by Swiss business theorist Alexander Osterwalder, based on his earlier work with Yves Pigneur, and popularised in the 2010 book Business Model Generation.

The nine blocks

The canvas arranges a firm's model on a single page in nine building blocks: (1) customer segments, (2) value propositions, (3) channels, (4) customer relationships, (5) revenue streams, (6) key resources, (7) key activities, (8) key partnerships, and (9) cost structure. The right side focuses on value and the customer; the left side on the business's infrastructure and efficiency.

Use

The canvas is widely used by startups and established companies to visualise, test, and iterate business ideas, often in workshops with sticky notes on a printed poster. It influenced derivative tools such as the Lean Canvas, adapted for early-stage ventures in the lean startup movement.
