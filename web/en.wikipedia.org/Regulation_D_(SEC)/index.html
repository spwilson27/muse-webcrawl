Regulation D (SEC)

Regulation D (Reg D) is a regulation issued by the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) under the Securities Act of 1933. It provides exemptions that allow companies to raise capital by selling securities without registering them with the SEC, provided certain conditions are met. Reg D offerings — commonly called private placements — are a principal channel for venture capital and private equity fundraising.

Rules 504, 506(b) and 506(c)

Rule 504 permits offerings of up to $10 million in a 12-month period. Rule 506(b), the most widely used exemption, allows unlimited capital to be raised from an unlimited number of accredited investors and up to 35 sophisticated non-accredited investors, but prohibits general solicitation or advertising. Rule 506(c), created under the JOBS Act of 2012, permits general solicitation if all purchasers are accredited investors and the issuer takes reasonable steps to verify their status.

Compliance

Issuers must file a notice on Form D with the SEC within 15 days of the first sale. Securities sold under Reg D are "restricted securities" and generally cannot be resold without registration or another exemption. State securities ("blue sky") laws are largely pre-empted for Rule 506 offerings, though states may still require notice filings and fees.
