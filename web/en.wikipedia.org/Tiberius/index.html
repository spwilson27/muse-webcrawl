Tiberius (42 BC – 37 AD) was the second Roman emperor, reigning from the death of Augustus in 14 AD until his own death in 37 AD. A stepson of Augustus and a member of the Julio-Claudian dynasty, he had been one of Rome's most successful generals before his accession, conquering Pannonia, Dalmatia, Raetia and parts of Germania.

Rise and reign

Son of Tiberius Claudius Nero and Livia Drusilla, Tiberius was compelled by Augustus to divorce his wife Vipsania and marry the emperor's daughter Julia. After the deaths of Augustus's grandsons, he was adopted as heir in 4 AD. As emperor he proved a capable but austere administrator who left the empire's finances strong and its frontiers largely stable, halting expansion in Germania after the Varian disaster era and governing through competent subordinates.

Withdrawal and final years

Growing suspicion and the treason (maiestas) trials darkened the reign. In 26 AD Tiberius withdrew to the island of Capri, leaving administration in Rome to his praetorian prefect Sejanus, whose accumulation of power ended with his arrest and execution in 31 AD. Ancient historians—Tacitus, Suetonius and Cassius Dio—depict the later Tiberius as a reclusive tyrant, though modern scholars partly rehabilitate his record. He died at Misenum in 37 AD and was succeeded by his grand-nephew Caligula.
