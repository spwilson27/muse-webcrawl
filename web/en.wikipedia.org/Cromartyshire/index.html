Cromartyshire

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cromartyshire (Scottish Gaelic: Siorrachd Chromba) is a historic county in the Scottish Highlands, notable for having consisted of a number of detached fragments scattered within and around the much larger county of Ross-shire. Its county town was Cromarty, a burgh and port on the Cromarty Firth at the tip of the Black Isle peninsula.

History

The sheriffdom of Cromarty originated in the lands surrounding the royal castle of Cromarty. Over the centuries, estates acquired by the Earls of Cromartie in distant parts of Ross — including enclaves around Coigach in the far north-west and on the Tarbat peninsula — were annexed to Cromartyshire, leaving it as a patchwork of more than twenty separate parcels. This arrangement produced one of the most anomalous county geographies in Britain.

The anomaly was resolved by the Local Government (Scotland) Act 1889, which merged Cromartyshire with Ross-shire into the single county of Ross and Cromarty in 1890. The name survives in that combination and in the modern Highland council area, where the former county forms part of the ward and local identity of the Black Isle and Easter Ross.

Geography

The principal portion of the old county comprised the parish of Cromarty and parts of the Black Isle near the entrance to the Cromarty Firth, an important deep-water anchorage. Detached portions extended as far as the Coigach peninsula on the Atlantic coast. The landscape ranges from fertile lowland around the firth to rugged moorland and mountain terrain in the west.
