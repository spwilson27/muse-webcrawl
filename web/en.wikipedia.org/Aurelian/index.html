Aurelian

Reign: 270 – 275 AD

Born: c. 214 AD, Moesia

Died: 275 AD, Caenophrurium, Thrace

Predecessor: Quintillus

Successor: Tacitus

Lucius Domitius Aurelianus (c. 214 – 275), commonly known as Aurelian, was Roman emperor from 270 to 275. During his short reign he reunited the Roman Empire, which had fragmented into three competing polities amid the Crisis of the Third Century, earning the title Restitutor Orbis — "Restorer of the World".

Reunification

Rising through the cavalry ranks under Gallienus and Claudius Gothicus, Aurelian became emperor in 270. He drove invading Juthungi and Vandals from Italy, built the Aurelian Walls around Rome, and decisively defeated Queen Zenobia of the Palmyrene Empire at Antioch and Emesa in 272, retaking the eastern provinces. In 274 he defeated Tetricus of the Gallic Empire at Châlons, restoring the western provinces and ending nearly fifteen years of division. He also withdrew Roman administration from Dacia to a more defensible Danube frontier.

Reforms and death

Aurelian reformed the coinage, promoted the cult of Sol Invictus, and strengthened the annona (grain supply) of Rome. While marching east to campaign against Persia in 275, he was assassinated by officers near Byzantium, apparently after a secretary, fearing punishment, forged a list of men marked for execution.
