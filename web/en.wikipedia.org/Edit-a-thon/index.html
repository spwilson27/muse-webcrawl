Edit-a-thon

[Image: images/Edit-a-thon.png — An edit-a-thon in progress at a library]

An edit-a-thon (sometimes written editathon) is an organised event where editors of online communities such as Wikipedia, OpenStreetMap or LocalWiki come together in one place — or online — to edit and improve a specific topic or type of content. Edit-a-thons generally include training for new editors, and are frequently hosted by cultural institutions such as libraries, archives, museums and universities, often in partnership with Wikimedia chapters and user groups.

Format

Events are usually themed: participants create and improve articles on a subject area, drawing on the host institution's collections and reference materials, which are made available to attendees. Organisers typically prepare lists of articles to create or expand, provide accounts and training for newcomers, and track outputs on an event page. The term blends "edit" with "marathon", by analogy with hackathons.

Notable series

The GLAM-Wiki movement (galleries, libraries, archives and museums) has run edit-a-thons since the early 2010s. The annual Art+Feminism campaign, begun in 2014, holds edit-a-thons around International Women's Day to improve coverage of women and the arts and to recruit women editors, addressing Wikipedia's gender gap. Other recurring initiatives include Wiki Loves Monuments editing events, Black Lunch Table edit-a-thons documenting Black artists, and subject-focused events on science, local history and under-represented languages.
