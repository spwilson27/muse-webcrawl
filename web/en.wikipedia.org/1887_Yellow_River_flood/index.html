1887 Yellow River flood
Date: September–October 1887
Location: Henan Province, Qing China
Deaths: 900,000–2,000,000 (estimated)
Area flooded: ~50,000 sq mi

The 1887 Yellow River flood was a devastating flood on the Yellow River (Huang He) in Qing dynasty China that began in late September 1887 after days of heavy rain. It is regarded as one of the deadliest natural disasters in recorded history.

Event

The river, elevated above the surrounding plain by centuries of silt deposition between levees, breached its dikes near Zhengzhou in Henan. The water spread over some 50,000 square miles of the North China Plain, inundating thousands of villages and the city of Kaifeng's surroundings, and destroying crops across a vast agricultural region.

Toll and aftermath

Estimates of deaths range from 900,000 to two million, including victims of the ensuing famine and disease. The breach was not fully closed until 1889. The disaster, following the river's catastrophic 1855 course change, reinforced the Yellow River's epithet "China's Sorrow."
