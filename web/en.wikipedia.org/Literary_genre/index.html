Literary genre

A literary genre is a category of literature identified by shared conventions of form, style, subject matter or technique. Genre helps readers, writers, booksellers and critics organise works and set expectations.

Classical divisions

Aristotle's Poetics distinguished epic, tragedy and comedy. A traditional threefold scheme divides imaginative literature into poetry, drama and prose fiction, each with many subgenres. Major fiction genres include the novel, novella and short story by length; and by content, mystery, romance, thriller, western, horror, fantasy and science fiction, together with historical, crime and adventure fiction. Nonfiction genres include biography, memoir, essay, history and travel writing.

Function

Genres are not fixed: they evolve, hybridise and fall in and out of fashion, and a single work may belong to several. Genre theory studies how conventions shape both writing and reading.
