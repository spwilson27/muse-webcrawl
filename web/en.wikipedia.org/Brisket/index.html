Brisket

Smoked brisket, sliced against the grain

Brisket is a cut of meat from the breast or lower chest of beef or veal, comprising the pectoral muscles. Because these muscles support much of the animal's weight, brisket contains abundant connective tissue and is tough if cooked quickly; it becomes tender when cooked slowly with moist heat or smoked low and slow, which converts collagen to gelatin.

Cuts

A whole packer brisket has two parts: the leaner "flat" (deep pectoral) and the fattier "point" (superficial pectoral), separated by a layer of fat. In the United Kingdom the same part of the animal is often sold rolled as a boned and rolled brisket for pot-roasting. Brisket is also the cut used for corned beef and for pastrami (in the United States, the navel end is also used).

Cookery

In Jewish cuisine, brisket is braised as a pot roast for holidays such as Passover and Rosh Hashanah, often with onions, carrots, and wine or tomato. In central Texas barbecue, whole briskets are seasoned with salt and black pepper and smoked over oak for twelve hours or more until a dark bark forms; the point may be cubed and returned to the smoker as "burnt ends," a speciality also associated with Kansas City. Korean yangji and Vietnamese phở use brisket sliced and simmered in broth.
