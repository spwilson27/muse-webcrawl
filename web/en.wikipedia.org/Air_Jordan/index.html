Air Jordan is a line of basketball shoes produced by Nike, Inc. and endorsed by former professional basketball player Michael Jordan. The flagship model, the Air Jordan I, was released to the public on April 1, 1985, and was designed for Jordan, who played for the Chicago Bulls of the National Basketball Association (NBA). The shoes were designed for Nike by Peter Moore, Tinker Hatfield, and Bruce Kilgore.

The Air Jordan line became one of the most successful and influential sneaker franchises in history, spawning dozens of numbered models, retro re-releases, and a vast secondary market. The brand is credited with helping to create modern sneaker culture and collectible footwear markets.

History

Nike signed Michael Jordan to an endorsement deal in 1984, shortly after he was drafted by the Chicago Bulls. The Air Jordan I was famously banned by the NBA for violating the league's uniform rules, which required shoes to be predominantly white; Nike reportedly paid the $5,000-per-game fines, though the "banned" shoe was actually the Nike Air Ship in a similar colorway. The marketing campaign turned the controversy into a major selling point.

The Air Jordan III, designed by Tinker Hatfield and released in 1988, introduced the visible Air unit and the Jumpman logo. In 1997, Jordan Brand was established as a subsidiary of Nike.

Models

The numbered line runs from the Air Jordan I through the Air Jordan XXXIX and beyond, with annual flagship releases. Retro versions of earlier models are regularly reissued and remain among the most sought-after sneakers in the world.
