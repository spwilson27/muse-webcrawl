The 2008 Formula One season was the 59th season of Formula One motor racing, contested over eighteen Grands Prix. Lewis Hamilton won the Drivers' Championship by a single point from Felipe Massa, becoming at the time the youngest world champion in the sport's history at 23 years old. Ferrari won the Constructors' Championship.

Season summary

Hamilton and Massa traded wins throughout the season, with Robert Kubica (BMW Sauber) and defending champion Kimi Räikkönen also in contention at mid-season. The inaugural Singapore Grand Prix — Formula One's first night race — was later tainted by the "Crashgate" scandal, in which Renault driver Nelson Piquet Jr. was revealed to have crashed deliberately to help team-mate Fernando Alonso win. Sebastian Vettel became the youngest-ever Grand Prix winner at the time with his wet-weather victory for Toro Rosso at Monza.

The Interlagos finale

The title was decided in extraordinary circumstances at the Brazilian Grand Prix. Massa won the race and, as he crossed the line, led the championship standings. Hamilton, struggling on worn tyres in late rain, had dropped to sixth — one place short of the fifth he needed. On the final lap, at the last corners, he passed the dry-tyre-shod Toyota of Timo Glock, reclaiming fifth place and the championship by one point, seconds after the Ferrari garage had begun celebrating. The finish is routinely cited as the most dramatic in Formula One history.

Aftermath

The Super Aguri team withdrew after four rounds amid the global financial crisis, and Honda pulled out at season's end, its team surviving as Brawn GP. The 2008 season was also the last with grooved tyres and the last before major aerodynamic rule changes in 2009.
