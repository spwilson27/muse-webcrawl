Paul Warren Taylor (1920–2015) was an American philosopher best known for his work in environmental ethics, in particular the biocentric theory of "respect for nature" developed in his book Respect for Nature: A Theory of Environmental Ethics (1986), one of the most systematic defenses of the moral standing of all living things.

Biocentrism

Taylor argued that every individual organism is a "teleological center of life" pursuing its own good, and that humans are members of Earth's community of life on equal terms with other species. From the attitude of respect for nature he derived duties toward wild living things, including rules of nonmaleficence, noninterference, fidelity, and restitutive justice, together with priority principles for resolving conflicts between human and nonhuman interests.

Other work

Earlier in his career Taylor wrote on normative ethics and metaethics, including Normative Discourse (1961) and Principles of Ethics: An Introduction (1975). He taught for many years at Brooklyn College of the City University of New York.
