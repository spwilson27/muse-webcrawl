Beer

TypeAlcoholic beverage
Main ingredientsWater, malted barley, hops, yeast
Typical strength4–6% alcohol by volume
OriginsMesopotamia and Egypt, at least 5th millennium BC

Beer is one of the oldest and most widely consumed alcoholic drinks in the world, and the most popular after water and tea. It is produced by the brewing and fermentation of starches from cereal grains — most commonly malted barley, though wheat, maize and rice are also used — and is flavoured with hops, which add bitterness and act as a preservative.

Brewing

Brewing involves mashing the grain to produce a sugary wort, boiling it with hops, cooling, and fermenting with yeast. Beers divide broadly into ales, fermented warm with top-fermenting yeast, and lagers, fermented cool with bottom-fermenting yeast and stored ("lagered") to mature. Styles range from pale lagers and pilsners to stouts, porters, wheat beers and sour beers.

History and industry

Evidence of beer brewing reaches back to ancient Mesopotamia, where the Sumerian "Hymn to Ninkasi" describes a recipe. Monasteries refined techniques in medieval Europe; the Bavarian Reinheitsgebot of 1516 restricted ingredients to water, barley and hops. Industrialisation, refrigeration and Pasteur's work on yeast transformed production in the 19th century. Today brewing is dominated by multinational groups such as AB InBev, alongside a large craft brewing sector that expanded from the late 20th century. See also Brewing and Hops.
