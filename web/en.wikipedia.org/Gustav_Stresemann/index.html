Gustav Stresemann (1878–1929) was a German statesman who served briefly as Chancellor in 1923 and then as Foreign Minister of the Weimar Republic from 1923 until his death. His policy of reconciliation with the Western powers and fulfilment of Germany's treaty obligations restored the country's international standing after the First World War and earned him the Nobel Peace Prize.

Career

A National Liberal deputy before 1914, Stresemann supported the war effort and then adapted to the republic, founding the German People's Party. As Chancellor during the crisis year of 1923 he ended passive resistance in the Ruhr and introduced the Rentenmark, halting hyperinflation. As Foreign Minister he negotiated the Dawes Plan (1924), the Locarno Treaties (1925) — for which he shared the 1926 Nobel Peace Prize with Aristide Briand — Germany's admission to the League of Nations (1926), the Kellogg–Briand Pact (1928) and the Young Plan (1929).

Legacy

Stresemann's sudden death in October 1929, weeks before the Wall Street Crash, removed Weimar's most skilled statesman; many historians see his passing as a turning point toward the republic's collapse. His strategy of revising Versailles by negotiation rather than confrontation remains a landmark of interwar diplomacy.
