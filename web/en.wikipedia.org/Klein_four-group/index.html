Klein four-group


The Klein four-group is the smallest non-cyclic group.

In mathematics, the Klein four-group is a group with four elements, in which each element is self-inverse (when combined with itself, it produces the identity) and in which combining any two of the three non-identity elements produces the third one. It can be described as the symmetry group of a non-square rectangle (with the identity operation, the horizontal and vertical reflections, and the 180-degree rotation), as the group Z2 × Z2 of bit strings of length 2 under bitwise XOR, or more abstractly as the cyclic group decomposition Z2 × Z2.

The Klein four-group, with operation *, has the Cayley table:

*eabc
eeabc
aaecb
bbcea
ccbae

Properties

Apart from the identity element, all elements of the Klein four-group have order 2. It is the smallest non-cyclic group (the only other group of order four, the cyclic group Z4, is cyclic) and it is abelian. It is named after the German mathematician Felix Klein, who used it in his Erlangen programme context of symmetries.

The Klein four-group appears as a normal subgroup in many larger groups; for example, it is isomorphic to the group of symmetries of a rectangle and appears as a subgroup of the alternating group A4, where it is a normal subgroup whose quotient is Z3.

Examples

Concrete realisations include the set {1, −1, i, −i} under multiplication restricted appropriately in spirit, the symmetries of a rectangle, and the group of translations by half-periods among elliptic functions. In chemistry, the point group of certain planar molecules is the Klein four-group.
