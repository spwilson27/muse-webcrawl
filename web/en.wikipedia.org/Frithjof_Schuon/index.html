Frithjof Schuon

Frithjof Schuon (18 June 1907 – 5 May 1998) was a Swiss philosopher and spiritual leader, a leading figure of the Traditionalist or Perennialist school of thought founded by René Guénon. He wrote more than twenty books in French on metaphysics, religion, and spirituality, and founded the Maryamiyya Sufi order.

Life

Schuon was born in Basel to a German father, a concert violinist, and a Swiss mother. After his father's death the family moved to Mulhouse, and Schuon later settled in Paris, where he worked as a textile designer and studied Arabic and religion. In 1932 he travelled to Algeria, where he was initiated into the Alawiyya Sufi order and received the name Shaykh ʿĪsā Nūr al-Dīn. He later lived in Lausanne, Switzerland, for some forty years, and in 1980 emigrated to Bloomington, Indiana, in the United States, where he died in 1998.

Thought

Schuon developed Guénon's Traditionalism into a comprehensive perennial philosophy, arguing that behind the diversity of the world's religions lies a single primordial tradition and an esoteric unity. His first major book, The Transcendent Unity of Religions (1948), introduced the distinction between the exoteric and esoteric dimensions of religion. His writings cover Hinduism, Buddhism, Islam, Christianity, and the spirituality of the Plains Indians, in whose traditions he took a deep interest and by whom he was adopted.
