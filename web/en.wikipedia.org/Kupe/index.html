Kupe is a legendary figure in Māori tradition, credited as the discoverer of Aotearoa (New Zealand). According to the traditions of many iwi (tribes), Kupe was a great chief and navigator from Hawaiki, the ancestral Polynesian homeland, who voyaged across the Pacific in the ocean-going canoe (waka) Matahourua and reached the islands of New Zealand, which he explored and named many places around.

The voyage

Accounts differ between tribes. In many versions, Kupe pursued a giant octopus, Te Wheke-a-Muturangi, which had been stealing bait and fish from his people's lines in Hawaiki, and followed it across the ocean to Aotearoa. His wife, Kuramārōtini, is credited with sighting the long white cloud over the land and giving the country its name Aotearoa, commonly rendered "land of the long white cloud".

Exploration and naming

Kupe is said to have circumnavigated the islands and named numerous places, particularly around the Wellington region and Cook Strait (Te Moana-a-Raukawa), including the harbour Te Whanganui-a-Tara. Rocks and landmarks in Wellington Harbour are associated with his daughters or companions in some traditions. In most accounts, Kupe returned to Hawaiki and gave sailing directions that guided later migration voyages, though he did not himself settle permanently.

Historical interpretation

Kupe belongs to oral tradition rather than documented history, and details vary widely between tribal versions. Early European writers assembled a "great fleet" migration narrative from such traditions in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, an interpretation later scholars have largely rejected, preferring to treat each tribe's accounts on their own terms.
