Into the Woods is a 1987 musical with music and lyrics by Stephen Sondheim and a book by James Lapine. It intertwines the plots of several Brothers Grimm fairy tales — "Little Red Riding Hood", "Jack and the Beanstalk", "Rapunzel" and "Cinderella" — linked by an original story about a childless Baker and his Wife who set out to break a Witch's curse.

Plot

In the first act, the characters pursue their wishes and apparently achieve them: Cinderella marries her prince, Jack climbs the beanstalk and kills the Giant, and the Bakers lift the curse and have a child. The second act explores the consequences: a second Giant's widow descends seeking revenge, marriages fray, characters die, and the survivors must cooperate to defeat the Giant and accept responsibility — concluding with the ensemble number "Children Will Listen" and the warning "Careful the things you say".

Productions and film

The musical premiered at the Old Globe Theatre in 1986 and opened on Broadway in 1987, winning Tony Awards including Best Score and Best Book, in competition with The Phantom of the Opera. It has been revived frequently, including a 2002 Broadway revival and a 2022 Broadway revival that transferred from Encores!. Walt Disney Pictures released a film adaptation in 2014, directed by Rob Marshall and starring Meryl Streep as the Witch, which altered some of the darker elements while retaining most of the score.
