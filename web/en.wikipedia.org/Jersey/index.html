Jersey


Jersey, officially the Bailiwick of Jersey, is a self-governing Crown Dependency in the Channel Islands.

Jersey, officially the Bailiwick of Jersey, is a self-governing parliamentary democracy under a constitutional monarchy, with its own financial, legal, and postal systems and its own courts. It is a British Crown Dependency, located near the coast of north-west France. It is the largest of the Channel Islands and has a population of about 103,000. Its capital is Saint Helier.

Geography

Jersey lies in the English Channel, about 19 kilometres (12 mi) from the Cotentin Peninsula in Normandy, France, and about 160 kilometres (100 mi) south of mainland Britain. The island has an area of about 119 square kilometres (46 sq mi), with a coastline of some 70 kilometres. Its climate is temperate with mild winters and cool summers, influenced by the Atlantic.

History

The island was annexed to the Duchy of Normandy in 933 and its duke, William the Conqueror, became king of England in 1066. Jersey remained part of the English Crown after Normandy was lost to France in 1204, and the Channel Islands are among the last remnants of the medieval Duchy of Normandy. During the Second World War, Jersey was occupied by German forces from 1940 to 1945; Liberation Day is celebrated on 9 May.

Economy

Jersey's economy is based largely on international financial services, which account for a large share of its economic output, along with tourism, e-commerce, and agriculture — including the famous Jersey Royal potatoes and Jersey cattle, a breed known worldwide for the rich milk it produces. Jersey issues its own pound sterling notes and coins, which circulate alongside UK currency.
