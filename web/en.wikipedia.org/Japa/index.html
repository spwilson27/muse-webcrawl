Japa

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A japa mala (prayer beads) used for counting repetitions]

Japa (Sanskrit: जप) is the meditative repetition of a mantra or a divine name, a practice found in Hinduism, Jainism, Sikhism, and Buddhism. The repetition may be spoken aloud, whispered, or recited silently in the mind.

Practice

Practitioners commonly use a japa mala, a string of 108 beads, to count repetitions, with additional rounds of 108 treated as a standard unit. Mantra japa is often performed at a set time daily, sometimes before an image of a deity. Hindu tradition distinguishes three forms: vachika (spoken), upamshu (whispered), and manasika (mental), with mental repetition often considered the most powerful. The goal is sustained one-pointed focus on the divine name or formula, calming the mind and accruing spiritual merit.

In different traditions

In Sikh practice, repetition of the divine name (Nam Japna) is one of the central pillars of the faith. In Pure Land and other Buddhist traditions, recitation of a Buddha's name (such as the Japanese nembutsu) serves a comparable role. Famous examples of japa mantras include "Om", the Gayatri Mantra, and the Hare Krishna maha-mantra.
