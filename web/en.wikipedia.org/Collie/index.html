Collie

The Collie is a type of herding dog that originated in Scotland and Northern England. The name encompasses several related breeds and landraces, most famously the Rough Collie — popularised worldwide by the fictional dog Lassie — and the Smooth Collie, which differ mainly in coat length. Collies are medium-to-large, lightly built dogs noted for their long, narrow heads, intelligence, and sensitivity.

History

Collies were developed as working sheepdogs in the Scottish Highlands and Borders; the word "collie" may derive from the Scots word for coal, from the black-faced Colley sheep they tended, or from Gaelic terms for something useful. Queen Victoria's fondness for the breed in the 1860s transformed it from an obscure farm dog into a fashionable show dog, and Albert Payson Terhune's novels and Eric Knight's Lassie Come-Home (1940) cemented its image as a loyal family companion.

Characteristics

Rough Collies have a long, dense double coat in sable and white, tricolour, or blue merle; Smooth Collies share the same standard with a short coat. The dogs are generally eager to learn and highly trainable, though they can be vocal and prone to herding children and other animals. Known health issues include collie eye anomaly, progressive retinal atrophy, and a mutation in the MDR1 gene that causes sensitivity to certain drugs such as ivermectin.

Related breeds

Other collie-type breeds include the Border Collie, the Bearded Collie, the Shetland Sheepdog, and the Australian Shepherd, all sharing ancestry in British herding stock.
