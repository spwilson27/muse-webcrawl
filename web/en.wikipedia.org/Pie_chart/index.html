Pie chart

A pie chart (or circle chart) is a circular statistical graphic divided into slices to illustrate numerical proportions. In a pie chart, the arc length and central angle of each slice are proportional to the quantity it represents. The chart is named for its resemblance to a sliced pie.

A simple pie chart showing proportions of a whole.

History

The earliest known pie chart is credited to William Playfair, who included one in his Statistical Breviary of 1801. Florence Nightingale popularised a variant, the polar area diagram (or "coxcomb"), in 1858 to dramatise the causes of mortality in the Crimean War.

Variants

Variants include the doughnut chart, which has a hollow centre often used to display a total; the exploded pie chart, in which one or more slices are separated for emphasis; and three-dimensional pie charts, which are common in business presentations but often criticised for distorting proportions.

Criticism

Statisticians frequently caution against pie charts because readers judge angles and areas less accurately than lengths, making comparisons between slices — and especially between separate pies — difficult. Bar charts usually convey the same information more precisely. Pie charts work best for showing a few categories that sum to a meaningful whole.
