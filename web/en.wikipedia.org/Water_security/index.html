Water security links reliable supply with protection against floods, droughts and pollution.

Water security is the capacity of a population to safeguard sustainable access to adequate quantities of acceptable quality water for sustaining livelihoods, human well-being and socio-economic development, for ensuring protection against water-borne pollution and water-related disasters, and for preserving ecosystems, in a climate of peace and political stability. The concept, formalised by UN-Water in 2013, extends beyond simple water scarcity to encompass availability, quality, risk and governance.

Dimensions

Water security is commonly analysed across several dimensions: drinking water and human well-being; economic activities including agriculture, energy and industry; ecosystems; and resilience to hazards such as floods, droughts and contamination. A community may have abundant water yet be water-insecure because of poor quality, unreliable infrastructure, unaffordable prices or vulnerability to disaster. Access is also uneven within societies, with women and girls bearing much of the burden of water collection in many regions.

Scale of the problem

UN assessments estimate that around 2 billion people live in countries experiencing high water stress, and about 4 billion experience severe water scarcity during at least one month of the year. Billions more lack safely managed drinking water or sanitation. Climate change intensifies the hydrological cycle, increasing both droughts and floods, while population growth, urbanisation and groundwater depletion add pressure. Water insecurity is associated with food insecurity, disease, displacement and, in some analyses, a heightened risk of local conflict, particularly over shared transboundary basins.

Responses

Approaches include integrated water resources management (IWRM), investment in storage, treatment and distribution, water conservation and reuse, ecosystem restoration, early-warning systems, and cooperative agreements over the roughly 260 international river basins of the world. Water security is embedded in Sustainable Development Goal 6 and was the subject of the UN 2023 Water Conference, the first such conference in nearly half a century.
