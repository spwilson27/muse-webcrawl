The Boeing Company is an American multinational corporation that designs, manufactures, and sells airplanes, rotorcraft, rockets, satellites, and missiles worldwide. Founded by William E. Boeing in Seattle in 1916, the company grew into the largest American exporter by dollar value and, together with Europe's Airbus, one of the two dominant manufacturers of large commercial jet airliners.

History

William Boeing, a timber merchant who became fascinated with aviation, founded the Pacific Aero Products Co. in 1916; it was renamed the Boeing Airplane Company the following year. The company supplied seaplanes to the U.S. Navy during the First World War and built mail planes and early airliners in the interwar period, including the Model 247, often considered the first modern airliner. During the Second World War Boeing built the B-17 Flying Fortress and B-29 Superfortress bombers.

In the postwar era Boeing led the transition to jet air travel with the 707 (introduced in 1958), followed by the 727, the 737 (which became the best-selling commercial jetliner in history), and the wide-body 747 in 1970. Boeing absorbed rival McDonnell Douglas in 1997. Later programs included the 777 and the composite-bodied 787 Dreamliner.

Recent challenges

The 737 MAX was grounded worldwide in 2019–2020 after two fatal crashes (Lion Air Flight 610 and Ethiopian Airlines Flight 302) killed 346 people, an episode that cost the company tens of billions of dollars and led to criminal and regulatory proceedings. A further crisis followed a January 2024 mid-air door plug blowout on an Alaska Airlines 737 MAX 9, prompting production limits, leadership changes, and renewed safety oversight. Boeing also pleaded guilty in 2024 (an agreement later renegotiated) in connection with the MAX crashes.

Structure

Boeing operates three main business units: Boeing Commercial Airplanes; Boeing Defense, Space & Security, whose products include the F/A-18 Super Hornet, KC-46 tanker, P-8 Poseidon, and the Starliner spacecraft; and Boeing Global Services, covering maintenance and parts. The company moved its headquarters from Seattle to Chicago in 2001 and to Arlington, Virginia, in 2022. Major assembly plants remain at Everett and Renton in Washington State, and at North Charleston, South Carolina.
