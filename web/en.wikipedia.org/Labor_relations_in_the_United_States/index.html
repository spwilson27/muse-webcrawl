Labor relations in the United States

Labor relations in the United States concern the relationship between employers, employees and trade unions, governed principally by the National Labor Relations Act of 1935 (Wagner Act), which protects private-sector workers' rights to organise and bargain collectively, and amended by the Taft–Hartley Act of 1947, which restricted some union practices.

Framework

The National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) conducts union representation elections and adjudicates unfair labor practices. Federal law preempts most state regulation of private-sector collective bargaining, though states may pass "right-to-work" laws banning agreements that require union fees. Public-sector labour relations are governed by separate federal and state statutes, notably state laws following the model tested in cases such as Janus v. AFSCME (2018).

Union density

Union membership peaked at about 35% of the workforce in the 1950s and has declined since; by the 2020s roughly 10% of US workers (about 6% in the private sector) were union members, though union approval ratings and organising activity rose in the early 2020s.
