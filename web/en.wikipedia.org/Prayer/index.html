Prayer

TypeReligious practice
FormsPetition, praise, thanksgiving, confession, intercession
Found inMost world religions

Prayer is an invocation or act that seeks to activate a rapport with an object of worship through deliberate communication. In its most general sense, prayer may involve petition, praise, confession or thanksgiving, and may be practised individually or in community, silently or aloud, in set words or spontaneously.

Forms of prayer

Common forms include prayers of petition (asking for oneself), intercession (asking for others), thanksgiving, adoration and confession. Many traditions use fixed liturgical forms—such as the Lord's Prayer in Christianity or the five daily prayers (Salah) in Islam—while others emphasise extemporaneous or contemplative prayer, including meditation-like practices.

Across religions

Prayer is central to the Abrahamic religions: Judaism has set daily services centred on the Amidah; Christianity includes liturgical, devotional and charismatic traditions; Islam prescribes ritual prayer five times daily facing Mecca. Prayer also features in Hinduism (such as puja and mantra recitation), Buddhism, Sikhism and many Indigenous and new religious movements, though its theological meaning varies widely.

Posture and aids

Kneeling, prostration, folded or raised hands, and bowed heads are common postures. Prayer aids include rosaries, prayer beads, prayer wheels, icons and prayer books.

Studies of efficacy

Scientific studies of intercessory prayer, particularly for healing, have produced mixed and contested results; large controlled trials have not demonstrated consistent effects. Prayer is also studied by psychologists for its effects on the person praying, including coping and wellbeing.
