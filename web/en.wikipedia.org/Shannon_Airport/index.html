Shannon Airport (Irish: Aerfort na Sionainne) (IATA: SNN, ICAO: EINN) is an international airport in County Clare, in the Republic of Ireland. It is in the townland of Rineanna, close to the town of Shannon and the Shannon Estuary, and lies roughly 24 km west of Limerick. The airport is a historic gateway between Europe and North America and was long a compulsory refuelling stop for transatlantic flights; it hosts United States border preclearance facilities and one of the world's first duty-free shops.

History

A site at Rineanna was chosen in the 1930s as the land base for transatlantic flying boats operating from Foynes on the estuary. The airfield opened in 1942, and in October 1945 a Pan American World Airways flight from New York made Shannon the destination of the first scheduled transatlantic landplane service. Because early airliners lacked the range to fly deep into Europe, Shannon became the standard western-European refuelling point, and the Irish government made it a free port; in 1947 Brendan O'Regan established the world's first duty-free shop there, and Irish coffee is credited to the airport's chef Joe Sheridan, who served it to chilled flying-boat passengers at Foynes and then Shannon.

The famous "Shannon stopover" rule long required most transatlantic flights serving Ireland to land at Shannon; the requirement was phased out under the EU–US Open Skies agreement by 2008. Long-range aircraft also reduced Shannon's refuelling role, but the airport retained transatlantic services by Aer Lingus, United, Delta, and others, and in 2009 became the first airport outside North America and the Caribbean to offer full US customs and border preclearance. US military stopovers and charter traffic have been a persistent feature — and a source of political controversy in Ireland.

Facilities

Shannon has a single 3,199 m runway (06/24), one of Ireland's longest, capable of handling the largest aircraft; it was a designated emergency landing site for NASA's Space Shuttle. The main terminal opened in 2000. The airport anchors the Shannon Free Zone, one of Ireland's pioneering industrial estates, with aviation leasing, maintenance, and technology firms clustered nearby.

Airlines and destinations

Scheduled services connect Shannon with London Heathrow and other British cities, New York JFK, Boston, Newark, Chicago (seasonally), and European leisure destinations, with Ryanair and Aer Lingus the principal carriers. Passenger numbers peaked at about 3.6 million in 2006 and have since fluctuated between roughly 1.4 and 2 million annually.

Accidents and incidents

Early transatlantic flying was hazardous: notable crashes at or near Shannon include the loss of a KLM Constellation in the estuary in 1954 and an Alitalia DC-7 in 1960. The airport also features in UFO lore through various reported sightings, and hosted emergency landings of several spaceflight-related transports over the decades.
