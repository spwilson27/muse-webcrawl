Drum and bass

Drum and bass (commonly abbreviated as DnB, D&B, or drum n bass) is a genre of electronic dance music characterised by fast breakbeats (typically 165–185 beats per minute) with heavy bass and sub-bass lines, samples, and synthesizers. The genre grew out of the UK's jungle scene in the early 1990s and became one of the most popular electronic music styles worldwide.

History

Drum and bass evolved from rave, hardcore and jungle, with producers such as Goldie — whose 1995 album Timeless brought the sound mainstream attention — Roni Size and Reprazent, who won the 1997 Mercury Prize, LTJ Bukem, and Andy C. By the late 1990s it had split into numerous subgenres, from jazzy, atmospheric "liquid funk" to aggressive techstep and neurofunk, jump-up, and the stripped-back minimal style of the 2000s.

Characteristics

Tracks are built around sampled and chopped breakbeats, most famously the "Amen break" from the Winstons' 1969 recording "Amen, Brother", layered over deep synthesized basslines, especially the distorted "Reese" bass. MCs often rap over sets in clubs, where the genre's culture centres on DJ performance, sound system power, and rewinds of popular tracks.
