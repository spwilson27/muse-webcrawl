Castile-La Mancha (Spanish: Castilla-La Mancha) is an autonomous community of Spain, located in the central part of the Iberian Peninsula. It comprises the provinces of Albacete, Ciudad Real, Cuenca, Guadalajara, and Toledo, the latter serving as the regional capital. It is the setting immortalised in Miguel de Cervantes' novel Don Quixote.

Geography

The region occupies much of the southern sub-plateau of the Meseta Central, a vast, sparsely populated plain bordered by mountain ranges including the Sierra Morena and the Iberian System. The Tagus and Guadiana rivers cross its territory.

History

Formed from the historic region of New Castile together with the province of Albacete, Castile-La Mancha became an autonomous community under its Statute of Autonomy in 1982. The windmills of La Mancha and the historic cities of Toledo and Cuenca, both UNESCO World Heritage Sites, are among its best-known landmarks.

Economy

Agriculture, including vineyards, saffron, olives, and Manchego cheese, is prominent, alongside renewable energy, particularly wind power, and a growing services sector centred on Toledo and Albacete.
