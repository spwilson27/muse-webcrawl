Waitomo


Country: New Zealand
Region: Waikato
District: Waitomo
Known for: Waitomo Glowworm Caves
Name: Māori wai (water) + tomo (hole, shaft)

Waitomo is a rural community in the King Country of the Waikato region, in New Zealand's North Island. It is world-famous for its extensive limestone cave systems, above all the Waitomo Glowworm Caves, whose ceilings are lit by thousands of living glowworms. The name combines the Māori words wai, water, and tomo, a hole or shaft, referring to the stream that disappears into the caves.

Caves

The caves were formed in Oligocene limestone over millions of years as groundwater dissolved passages through the rock. The Glowworm Cave was known to local Māori for generations before being surveyed in 1887 by chief Tane Tinorau and English surveyor Fred Mace; guided tours began shortly afterwards and have run ever since. Visitors travel by boat beneath strands of the endemic fungus gnat Arachnocampa luminosa, whose larvae hang sticky silk threads from the cave roof and glow to attract prey.

Nearby systems include Ruakuri Cave, known for its spiral entrance and glowworms, Aranui Cave with its rich stalactite formations, and the region also offers black-water rafting (cave tubing) through underground streams. The village of Waitomo, home to a few hundred people, supports the cave tourism industry and includes the Waitomo Caves Discovery Centre.

Local government

Waitomo gives its name to the surrounding Waitomo District, administered from Te Kuiti, and to the Taranaki-King Country parliamentary electorate area in which it sits.
