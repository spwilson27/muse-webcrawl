Colour

Colour (or color in American English) is the visual perception resulting from the way an object reflects or emits light and the way the human visual system detects that light. It is determined by the spectral composition of the light reaching the eye and is experienced through attributes such as hue, saturation, and lightness.

Perception

Human colour vision is normally trichromatic: three types of cone cells in the retina respond preferentially to short, medium, and long wavelengths, loosely corresponding to blue, green, and red. The brain compares their signals to produce the sensation of colour. Objects appear coloured because their surfaces absorb some wavelengths and reflect others; the sky appears blue because air molecules scatter short wavelengths most strongly.

Measurement and models

Isaac Newton showed that white sunlight can be split into a spectrum of hues and recombined. Modern colorimetry describes colours numerically in spaces such as CIE XYZ and its derivatives. Artists and printers use subtractive mixing of pigments, conventionally red, yellow, and blue or cyan, magenta, and yellow, while screens use additive mixing of red, green, and blue light.

Culture

Colours carry strong symbolic associations that vary between cultures: red may signify luck and celebration in China but danger or mourning elsewhere, and white is traditional for weddings in the West yet associated with funerals in parts of Asia.
