Wave packet

A wave packet (or wave train) is a localized burst of wave action that travels as a unit. It can be analysed as a superposition of sinusoidal waves with different wavenumbers, whose interference produces an envelope that is nonzero only in a limited region of space.

Description

Mathematically, a wave packet is formed by integrating plane waves over a range of wavenumbers, weighted by a coefficient function — often Gaussian, producing a Gaussian wave packet. The packet moves at the group velocity, while the individual wave crests within it move at the phase velocity.

Role in quantum mechanics

In quantum mechanics, a particle is represented by a wave packet of probability amplitude. Because waves of different wavenumbers travel at slightly different speeds, packets generally spread over time — a manifestation of dispersion. The tradeoff between localization in position and spread in wavenumber is an expression of the Fourier uncertainty principle, closely related to Heisenberg's uncertainty principle.
