Kempeitai

The Kempeitai (憲兵隊, "Military Police Corps") was the military police arm of the Imperial Japanese Army from 1881 to 1945. It functioned not only as a military police force but also as a secret police, exercising extensive powers over both soldiers and civilians in Japan and in occupied territories, and is widely associated with repression, torture and war crimes during the Second World War.

History

Established in 1881 on the model of the French gendarmerie, the Kempeitai grew into a powerful organisation of tens of thousands of personnel by the 1930s and 1940s. Its civilian counterpart in political policing was the Tokkō (Special Higher Police). Under the wartime government of Hideki Tojo — himself a former commander of the Kwantung Army's Kempeitai — the force enforced conformity at home and policed occupied Asia.

Atrocities

The Kempeitai was responsible for the arrest, torture and execution of suspected dissidents, spies and resistance members across occupied Asia. It participated in the Double Tenth massacre in Singapore (1943), supplied prisoners and guarded facilities connected with Unit 731's human experiments, administered prisoner-of-war and civilian internment camps with notorious brutality, and enforced the system of "comfort women". After Japan's surrender in 1945, the Kempeitai was disbanded by the Allied occupation; a number of its officers were tried and executed for war crimes.
