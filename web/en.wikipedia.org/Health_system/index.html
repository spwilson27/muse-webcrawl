Health system

A health system, health care system or healthcare system is the organization of people, institutions, and resources that delivers health care services to meet the health needs of target populations. The World Health Organization (WHO) defines a health system as consisting of all organizations, people and actions whose primary intent is to promote, restore or maintain health, and evaluates systems by the goals of improving health, responding to people's expectations, and providing financial protection against the costs of ill-health.

Functions and building blocks

WHO's framework of six "building blocks" comprises: (1) service delivery; (2) the health workforce; (3) health information systems; (4) access to essential medicines, vaccines and technologies; (5) health financing; and (6) leadership and governance. Services are usually organized in levels — primary care as the first point of contact, secondary care in general hospitals, and tertiary specialized care — with referral systems linking them. Public health functions such as immunization, sanitation, health promotion and disease surveillance are also part of the wider system.

Financing models

Nations fund health care through several classic models. In the Beveridge model (United Kingdom, Spain, New Zealand and others), care is funded from general taxation and largely publicly provided, aiming at universal coverage free at the point of use. The Bismarck model (Germany, France, Japan) uses payroll-funded social health insurance through regulated non-profit sickness funds and mixed public-private providers. The national health insurance model (Canada, Taiwan, South Korea) combines tax-funded single-payer insurance with largely private delivery. The United States relies on a mixed multi-payer system combining employer-based private insurance, public programs (Medicare and Medicaid) and out-of-pocket payment, and spends a higher share of GDP on health care than any other country. Many low- and middle-income countries depend heavily on out-of-pocket payments and donor funding, and are pursuing universal health coverage as part of the Sustainable Development Goals.

Performance

Comparisons of health systems weigh outcomes such as life expectancy, infant and maternal mortality, access, equity, quality and cost. WHO's World Health Report 2000 controversially ranked 191 systems (placing France first and the United States 37th), and later efforts by the Commonwealth Fund, OECD and academic institutes continue to compare performance, generally finding that systems with strong primary care and universal financial coverage achieve good outcomes at lower cost.
