Field electron emission

Field electron emission, also known as field emission (FE) and electron field emission, is the emission of electrons induced by an electrostatic field. The most common context is electron emission into vacuum from the surface of a solid under a strong electric field, where quantum tunnelling lets electrons escape through a narrowed potential barrier.

Theory

The phenomenon was explained in 1928 by Fowler and Nordheim, who showed that the current density depends exponentially on the local field strength. The Fowler–Nordheim equation is the standard theoretical description, and a plot of ln(I/V²) against 1/V (a Fowler–Nordheim plot) is used to characterise emitters.

Applications

Field emission is the operating principle of field-emission displays, electron sources in electron microscopes, and cold-cathode X-ray tubes. Sharp tips, nanowires, and carbon nanotubes are used to achieve high local fields at modest voltages.
