Spheroplast

A spheroplast is a microbial cell from which the cell wall has been almost completely removed, for example by the action of lysozyme or penicillin. The term is usually applied to Gram-negative bacteria, which retain their outer membrane; the corresponding form in Gram-positive bacteria and fungi, lacking the wall entirely, is called a protoplast.

Properties

Spheroplasts are osmotically fragile and must be kept in isotonic solutions. Because they can take up foreign DNA and can fuse with one another, spheroplasts and protoplasts are widely used in genetic engineering, transformation experiments, and patch-clamp studies of membrane channels.
