Omnivore

An omnivore is an animal whose diet consists of both plant and animal matter. Omnivores occupy a middle position in the food web, consuming both primary producers and other consumers. Humans, bears, pigs, crows, and many rodents are familiar examples.

Characteristics

Omnivores typically possess digestive systems and dentition adapted to a varied diet: humans and bears, for instance, have both sharp teeth for tearing meat and flat molars for grinding plant material. Behavioural flexibility is a hallmark of omnivory, allowing animals to exploit seasonally available foods. Some species are obligate omnivores, needing both food types, while others are opportunistic generalists that shift proportions with availability.

Ecology

Because they feed at multiple trophic levels, omnivores can stabilise food webs but may also complicate simple models of energy flow. The term derives from the Latin omnis ("all") and vorare ("to devour").
