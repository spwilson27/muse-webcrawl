World line

The world line (or worldline) of an object is the path that an object traces in 4-dimensional spacetime. It is an important concept in theoretical physics, particularly in the theory of relativity, because it encodes the history of an object as a single geometric curve.

In a Minkowski diagram, an object's world line traces its path through space and time.

Usage in physics

In a spacetime diagram, the world line of a particle at rest is a vertical line, while a uniformly moving particle has a straight world line inclined at an angle determined by its velocity. Accelerating particles have curved world lines. Distances along a world line are measured by proper time, the time measured by a clock moving with the object.

World lines in special relativity

In special relativity, world lines are classified as timelike, lightlike (null), or spacelike depending on whether their slope corresponds to motion slower than, equal to, or faster than light. Observed objects follow timelike world lines; light follows lightlike ones.

History

The concept was introduced by Hermann Minkowski in his 1908 formulation of spacetime geometry following Albert Einstein's 1905 paper on special relativity.
