Behavioral targeting

Behavioral targeting is a method used by online advertisers and publishers to increase the effectiveness of their campaigns by collecting information about a user's browsing behaviour — such as pages visited, searches made, links clicked, and purchases — and using it to display advertisements matched to the user's inferred interests.

How it works

Data are typically collected with HTTP cookies, web beacons, and tracking pixels placed across many websites by ad networks and analytics companies. Users are grouped into audience segments (for example, "in-market for a car") and advertisers bid to reach those segments, often through real-time bidding exchanges. Retargeting is a related technique that shows ads for products a user has previously viewed.

Onsite targeting

Publishers can also use behaviour on their own site — pages read, items viewed, time spent — to personalise content and offers without third-party data, a practice sometimes called onsite behavioural targeting.

Privacy concerns

Behavioural targeting has been widely criticised for opaque tracking and profiling. Responses have included do-not-track initiatives, browser restrictions and the phase-out of third-party cookies, and regulations such as the European Union's ePrivacy rules and the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), which require consent for most tracking.
