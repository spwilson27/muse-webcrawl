2014 Isla Vista killings

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The 2014 Isla Vista killings were a series of attacks that took place on the evening of 23 May 2014 in Isla Vista, California, near the campus of the University of California, Santa Barbara. The perpetrator, 22-year-old Elliot Rodger, murdered six people and injured fourteen others before killing himself.

Attacks

Rodger first stabbed to death his two housemates, Cheng Yuan Hong and George Chen, and their friend Weihan Wang, in his apartment. He then drove to a sorority house near the UCSB campus, where, unable to gain entry, he shot three women outside, killing Katherine Cooper and Veronika Weiss. He continued driving through Isla Vista, shooting at pedestrians and striking people with his car, killing Christopher Michaels-Martinez at a delicatessen and wounding many others. After exchanging gunfire with sheriff's deputies, Rodger crashed his car and shot himself.

Motives and manifesto

Before the attacks, Rodger uploaded a video titled "Elliot Rodger's Retribution" to YouTube and emailed a lengthy autobiographical document, often described as a manifesto, to family members and his therapist. In these, he expressed hatred toward women who had rejected him and envy of sexually active men, framing the attack as revenge. The case brought wide attention to misogynistic online subcultures; Rodger later became an influential figure for parts of the so-called "incel" community.

Aftermath

Richard Martinez, father of victim Christopher Michaels-Martinez, became a prominent advocate for gun control, repeatedly declaring "Not one more." California subsequently enacted a gun violence restraining order law allowing families and law enforcement to petition courts to temporarily remove firearms from individuals considered a risk. A memorial paddle-out and anniversary commemorations have been held in Isla Vista.
