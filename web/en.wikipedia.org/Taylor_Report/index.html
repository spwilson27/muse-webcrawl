Taylor Report

The Taylor Report is the name given to the official inquiry into the Hillsborough disaster of 15 April 1989, in which 97 Liverpool football supporters were fatally injured in a crush at Sheffield Wednesday's Hillsborough Stadium during an FA Cup semi-final. The inquiry was led by Lord Justice Peter Taylor. His interim report, published in August 1989, identified the main cause of the disaster as a failure of police control; his final report of January 1990 transformed English football by requiring top-division stadiums to become all-seater.

Interim findings

Taylor's interim report rejected attempts to blame Liverpool supporters, finding instead that the immediate cause was the decision to open exit Gate C and allow an uncontrolled influx into already-full central pens of the Leppings Lane terrace, and that the underlying cause was "a failure to adopt a proper plan, to monitor the pens, and to control the crowd" by South Yorkshire Police. He also condemned the club's inadequate signage, crush barriers, and turnstile arrangements, and the delayed emergency response.

Recommendations

The final report made 76 recommendations. Its most famous required all clubs in the top two divisions of English and Scottish football to convert to all-seater stadiums, abolishing standing terraces at that level, and urged the removal of perimeter fences. It also recommended maximum terrace capacities calculated to stricter safety standards, improved ticketing and crowd management, upgraded communications, and a national advisory body on stadium design. The report further criticised the proposed football membership (ID card) scheme, which the government subsequently dropped.

Impact

The all-seater requirement reshaped English football grounds in the 1990s, improved safety and comfort but also raising ticket prices and changing supporter culture. Although "safe standing" areas with rail seating were later permitted in England from 2022, the Taylor Report remains the foundational document of modern stadium safety. Taylor himself pointedly declined to say that seating alone would have prevented Hillsborough, emphasising failures of policing and management above stadium design.
