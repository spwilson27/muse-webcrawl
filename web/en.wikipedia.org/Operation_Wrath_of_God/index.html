Operation Wrath of God

The 1972 Munich massacre prompted Israel's retaliatory assassination campaign.

Date1972 – c. 1988
TypeCovert assassination campaign
Conducted byMossad (Israel)
TargetsIndividuals linked to the Munich massacre and Palestinian militant groups

Operation Wrath of God (Hebrew: מבצע זעם האל, Mivtza Za'am Ha'el), also known as Operation Bayonet, was a covert campaign carried out by the Israeli intelligence agency Mossad in response to the Munich massacre of September 1972, in which eleven members of the Israeli Olympic team were killed by the Palestinian group Black September.

The campaign

Prime Minister Golda Meir authorised the operation and appointed a secret committee to oversee it; Mossad director Zvi Zamir and future prime minister Ehud Barak were among those involved in its execution. Over the following years, Israeli teams assassinated a series of Palestinians in Europe and the Middle East who were alleged to have planned or carried out the Munich attack, including Wael Zwaiter in Rome (1972), Mahmoud Hamshari in Paris (1972), and several targets in the April 1973 raid on Beirut known as Operation Spring of Youth.

Lillehammer affair

The operation's most notorious episode occurred in July 1973 in Lillehammer, Norway, where Mossad agents mistakenly killed Ahmed Bouchikhi, a Moroccan waiter, believing him to be Ali Hassan Salameh, the Black September operations chief. Several agents were arrested and tried by Norway, straining relations between the two countries. Salameh himself was eventually killed by a car bomb in Beirut in 1979.

Legacy

The campaign remains controversial: supporters credit it with disrupting Palestinian militant networks, while critics question its legality and the killing of bystanders and misidentified targets. It has been dramatised in the films Sword of Gideon (1986) and Steven Spielberg's Munich (2005).
