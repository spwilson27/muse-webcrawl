Siddha

A siddha is, in Indian religions, a person who has attained a state of spiritual perfection and mastery of extraordinary powers (siddhis). The term is used in Hinduism, Jainism, Buddhism and especially in tantric traditions.

In Hinduism

Siddhas are liberated beings who have overcome ignorance and ego. The 84 Mahasiddhas of medieval India, including figures such as Gorakhnath and Matsyendranath, are central to the Nath tradition and to Hatha yoga. In Tamil tradition, the eighteen Siddhars are associated with medicine, alchemy and yoga.

In Jainism and Buddhism

In Jainism, siddhas are souls that have shed all karma and reside eternally at the top of the universe, invoked in the Namokar Mantra. In Tibetan Buddhism, mahasiddhas are tantric masters credited with songs of realization and unconventional conduct.
