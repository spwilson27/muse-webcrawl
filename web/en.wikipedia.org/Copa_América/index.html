Copa América

The Copa América is the senior men's football championship of South America, organised by CONMEBOL. Founded in 1916 as the South American Championship, it is the oldest international football tournament in the world. The competition determines the continental champion of South America and has traditionally been contested by CONMEBOL's ten member nations, frequently with invited guest teams from other confederations.

History

The first tournament was held in Argentina in 1916 to celebrate the centenary of Argentine independence, with Argentina, Brazil, Chile and Uruguay taking part; Uruguay won and has been a dominant force ever since. The championship was played at irregular intervals — annually at first, then sporadically — and from 1975 it took the name Copa América, adopting for a time a home-and-away format without a host country before returning to a single-host tournament in 1987. Since the 1990s guest nations, most often Mexico and the United States, have been invited; the centenary edition, Copa América Centenario in 2016, was staged in the United States with sixteen teams.

Format

The modern tournament brings together the ten CONMEBOL members plus invitees, divided into groups followed by knockout rounds culminating in a final. Recent editions have aligned the tournament cycle with the UEFA European Championship, held every four years.

Records

Argentina and Uruguay have been the most successful nations, each winning the title fifteen or more times across the tournament's history, followed by Brazil. Argentina's Lionel Messi and others hold individual appearance and scoring records; the all-time top scorers include Norberto Méndez of Argentina and Zizinho of Brazil with seventeen goals each.
