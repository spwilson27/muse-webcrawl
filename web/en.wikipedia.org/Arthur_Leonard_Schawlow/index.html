Arthur Leonard Schawlow

Born: 5 May 1921, Mount Vernon, New York

Died: 28 April 1999, Palo Alto, California

Fields: Physics, laser spectroscopy

Awards: Nobel Prize in Physics (1981)

Arthur Leonard Schawlow (5 May 1921 – 28 April 1999) was an American physicist best remembered for his work on lasers and laser spectroscopy. He shared the 1981 Nobel Prize in Physics with Nicolaas Bloembergen and Kai Siegbahn for contributions to the development of laser spectroscopy.

Career

Schawlow studied at the University of Toronto and worked at Bell Laboratories, where, with his brother-in-law Charles H. Townes, he wrote the landmark 1958 paper proposing the optical maser — the laser — and derived the Schawlow–Townes linewidth limit. In 1961 he joined the faculty of Stanford University, where he remained for the rest of his career.

Laser spectroscopy

At Stanford, Schawlow pioneered methods for using lasers to make extremely precise spectroscopic measurements, including saturation spectroscopy and two-photon spectroscopy, enabling scientists to probe atomic and molecular structure with unprecedented resolution. He was renowned as a teacher and demonstrator, and his playful demonstrations with lasers became legendary.

Personal life

Schawlow married Aurelia Townes, sister of Charles Townes. Their son had autism, and Schawlow became an advocate involved in founding a school for autistic children. He died of leukaemia in 1999.
