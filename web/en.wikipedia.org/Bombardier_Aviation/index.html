Bombardier Aviation is the aircraft manufacturing division of the Canadian company Bombardier Inc. Formerly known as Bombardier Aerospace, it was for decades the world's third-largest aircraft manufacturer and the leader in regional aircraft and business jets. After a series of divestitures between 2018 and 2021 — including the sale of its commercial airliner programs — Bombardier became a "pure-play" business jet manufacturer.

History

Bombardier entered aviation in 1986 by acquiring the state-owned Canadair, maker of the Challenger business jet and CL-215 water bomber. It subsequently purchased Short Brothers in Northern Ireland (1989), the bankrupt Learjet Corporation (1990), and de Havilland Canada (1992), assembling an aviation group spanning business, regional, and amphibious aircraft.

The CRJ family of regional jets, launched in the 1990s, became the best-selling regional jet program of its era, while the Q400 turboprop served regional airlines worldwide.

Divestitures

The ambitious CSeries airliner — rebranded the Airbus A220 after Airbus acquired a majority stake in the program in 2018 — strained the company's finances. Bombardier sold the Q400 program to Longview Aviation Capital in 2019, the CRJ program to Mitsubishi Heavy Industries in 2020, and its rail division to Alstom in 2021, leaving business aviation as its core. Learjet production ended in 2021.

Current products

Bombardier's present lineup consists of the medium-cabin Challenger 3500 and the large-cabin Global family, including the flagship Global 7500 — noted for a range of about 7,700 nautical miles and the largest cabin in its class — and the Global 8000. The company also operates a large global service and support network.
