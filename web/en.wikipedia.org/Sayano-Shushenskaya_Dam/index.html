Sayano-Shushenskaya Dam

LocationYenisei River, Khakassia, Russia
TypeArch-gravity dam
Height242 m
Installed capacity6,400 MW
Construction1963–1978

The Sayano-Shushenskaya Dam is a large hydroelectric power station on the Yenisei River in Siberia, Russia. Its arch-gravity dam, 242 metres high, is the tallest of its type in the world, and the plant's installed capacity of 6,400 MW makes it Russia's largest power station and one of the largest hydroelectric facilities globally.

The dam across the Yenisei River

Construction

Construction began in 1963 in a narrow gorge of the Yenisei, and the first generator was commissioned in 1978. The reservoir extends for hundreds of kilometres upstream. The plant historically supplied energy-intensive aluminium smelters in Khakassia.

2009 accident

On 17 August 2009, turbine 2 catastrophically failed: the turbine cover was torn off by water pressure, flooding the machine hall, destroying or damaging most of the ten turbines and killing 75 people. It was the worst accident in the history of Russian hydropower. Investigations blamed metal fatigue in the stud bolts securing the turbine lid, aggravated by vibration and maintenance failures. The station was progressively rebuilt with new turbines, returning to full capacity in 2014.
