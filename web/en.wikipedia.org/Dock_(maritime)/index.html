Dock (maritime)

A vessel in a dry dock

A dock is an enclosed area of water in a port used for the loading, unloading, repair, and building of ships. The term is used in various senses: in British English it generally denotes the water area between quays or piers, while in American English it often refers to the pier or wharf structure itself.

Types

A wet dock maintains a constant water level, often behind lock gates, allowing ships to remain afloat regardless of tides. A dry dock (graving dock) is a basin that can be pumped dry so that a vessel rests on blocks, enabling work on the hull below the waterline. A floating dock is a submersible pontoon structure that lifts ships out of the water. Docks are also distinguished as fitting-out docks, loading docks, and repair docks.

History

Enclosed docks developed in the great trading ports of the 17th and 18th centuries; Liverpool's Old Dock of 1715 is regarded as the world's first commercial wet dock. Docklands districts in cities such as London, Liverpool, Hamburg, and Rotterdam grew around extensive dock systems, many of which were redeveloped for housing and commerce in the late 20th century as container shipping moved cargo handling to larger out-of-town terminals.
