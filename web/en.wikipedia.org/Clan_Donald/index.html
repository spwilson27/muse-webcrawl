Clan Donald (Scottish Gaelic: Clann Dòmhnaill) is one of the largest and most historically significant Scottish clans, claiming descent from Domhnall mac Raghnuill (Donald of Islay), a twelfth-century descendant of Somerled, the Norse-Gaelic Lord of the Isles. At its height, the clan's chiefs — as Lords of the Isles — ruled virtually the whole of the Hebrides and large parts of the western Scottish mainland, rivalled only by the kings of Scotland.

The Lordship of the Isles

The Lordship of the Isles was established by Somerled's descendants and became the most powerful semi-independent power in medieval Scotland, with its seat at Finlaggan on Islay, where the Lords of the Isles were crowned on a stone at the Council Isle. The Lordship was forfeited to the Scottish crown in 1493 after the rebellion of John of Islay, Earl of Ross.

Branches

Clan Donald comprises numerous major branches, including Clan Macdonald of Sleat, Clan Macdonald of Clanranald, Clan Macdonell of Glengarry, Clan Macdonald of Keppoch and Clan Macdonald of Dunnyveg (or Clan Donald South). The chiefship is vested in the Macdonald of Macdonald (the High Chief of Clan Donald), currently recognised by the Lord Lyon.

Later history

Clan Donald fought at the Battle of Harlaw (1411) against the royal forces of Scotland and later supported the Jacobite risings, with many clansmen dying at Culloden in 1746. The clan diaspora has carried the name MacDonald and its septs worldwide.
