Popol Vuh

The Popol Vuh (also Popul Vuh or Pop Vuj; K'iche': "Book of the Mat" or "Book of the Community") is a text recounting the mythology and history of the K'iche' people of the Guatemalan highlands. It is the most famous work of classic Maya literature preserved in alphabetic writing, containing creation myths, the epic of the Hero Twins, and a dynastic history of the K'iche' kingdom up to the Spanish conquest.

Contents

The text opens with the creation of the world by the gods Heart of Sky and the Sovereign Plumed Serpent, and the successive attempts to create humanity — from mud, then wood — before the first true humans are fashioned from maize dough. The middle section tells of the Hero Twins, Hunahpu and Xbalanque, who descend to the underworld of Xibalba, outwit its lords in trials and in the ballgame, die and are reborn, and finally defeat the death gods. The final part recounts the migration of the K'iche' from the mythical Tulan, the founding of their capital Q'umarkaj, and the succession of their kings down to the era of Pedro de Alvarado's conquest (1524).

Manuscript and transmission

The alphabetic text was composed by anonymous K'iche' nobles around 1554–1558, probably drawing on a hieroglyphic original that has not survived. Between 1701 and 1703 the Dominican friar Francisco Ximénez copied the K'iche' text and translated it into Spanish in Chichicastenango; his manuscript is now held by the Newberry Library in Chicago. Influential modern translations include those of Adrián Recinos (Spanish, 1947), Dennis Tedlock (English, 1985), and Allen J. Christenson (2003).

Significance

The Popol Vuh is a central source for Maya mythology, religion, and history, and a national symbol of Guatemala. Episodes such as the Hero Twins also appear in Classic-period Maya art a thousand years earlier, demonstrating deep continuity in Maya narrative tradition.
