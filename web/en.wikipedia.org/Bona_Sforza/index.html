Bona Sforza

Born: 2 February 1494, Vigevano, Italy

Died: 19 November 1557, Bari, Italy

Reign: Queen consort of Poland, 1518–1548

Spouse: Sigismund I the Old

Issue: Sigismund II Augustus; Anna Jagiellon; Catherine, Queen of Sweden

Bona Sforza d'Aragona (1494–1557) was Queen of Poland and Grand Duchess of Lithuania as the second wife of King Sigismund I the Old, and Duchess of Bari and Rossano in her own right. A member of the powerful Milanese House of Sforza, she was one of the most politically influential women in sixteenth-century Poland, remembered for her economic reforms, patronage of Renaissance culture, and conflicts with the Polish nobility.

Early life and marriage

Bona was the daughter of Gian Galeazzo Sforza, Duke of Milan, and Isabella of Aragon. After her father's early death and the loss of Milan to France, she was raised in Bari, the Italian duchy her mother held. Educated in Naples and Bari in the Renaissance humanist tradition, she married the widowed Sigismund I in Kraków in April 1518, bringing a large dowry and the revenues of her Italian duchies.

Queenship

Bona worked to strengthen royal power and finances. She bought up or redeemed pledged crown estates, reformed taxation, and in the Grand Duchy of Lithuania promoted agricultural reform — the measurement and reorganisation of peasant holdings later codified in the Volok Reform of 1557. She founded towns, hospitals, and churches, and surrounded her court with Italian artists, architects, and musicians, helping to spread Renaissance culture in Poland. She is also credited with popularising Italian vegetables, remembered in Polish as włoszczyzna.

Later life and death

After Sigismund's death in 1548, Bona quarrelled with her son Sigismund II Augustus, particularly over his marriage to Barbara Radziwiłł. In 1556 she left Poland for Bari, taking with her a vast fortune. She died there the following year, possibly poisoned by agents of the Habsburgs, amid disputes over her Italian inheritance and the large loans she had made to Philip II of Spain, known as the "Neapolitan sums."
