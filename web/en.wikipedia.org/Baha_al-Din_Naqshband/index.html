Baha al-Din Naqshband

Bahāʾ al-Dīn Naqshband (1318–1389) was a Central Asian Sufi saint and the eponymous founder-figure of the Naqshbandi order, one of the largest and most influential Sufi orders (tariqas) in Islam. He lived near Bukhara, in present-day Uzbekistan, where his tomb complex remains a major pilgrimage site.

Baha al-Din Naqshband
Born: 1318, near Bukhara
Died: 1389, Bukhara
Tradition: Sufism (Hanafi Islam)
Order: Naqshbandiyya

Life

Muhammad ibn Muhammad Bahāʾ al-Dīn was born in the village of Qasr al-ʿĀrifān (Qasr-i Hinduvan) near Bukhara. The name "Naqshband" — associated with embroidery or imprinting an image — is traditionally linked to his family's craft and interpreted spiritually as the imprinting of God's name on the heart. He studied under Sufi masters including Amir Kulal and was strongly influenced by the earlier Central Asian master Yusuf Hamadani and by Abdul Khaliq Ghijduwani, whose principles of silent remembrance (dhikr) and "solitude within society" he revived.

Legacy

Although the chain of teaching predates him, the order was reorganised and spread under his name, later carried by figures such as Khwaja Ubaydullah Ahrar and, in India, Ahmad Sirhindi. The Naqshbandi path became dominant across Central Asia, the Ottoman world, and much of South and Southeast Asia. The Bahauddin Naqshband memorial complex outside Bukhara, built around his tomb, is one of Uzbekistan's most visited shrines.
