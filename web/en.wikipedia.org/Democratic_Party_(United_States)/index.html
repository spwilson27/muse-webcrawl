The Democratic Party is one of the two major contemporary political parties in the United States, alongside the Republican Party. It is the world's oldest active political party, tracing its heritage to the Democratic-Republican Party of Thomas Jefferson and James Madison and taking its modern form under Andrew Jackson in 1828. Its unofficial symbol is the donkey, and its associated colour in media coverage is blue.

History

The party of Jackson championed the common (white male) citizen against the "money power", opposed a national bank, and supported westward expansion and — increasingly fatefully — the institution of slavery in the South. The slavery question split the party in 1860, enabling Abraham Lincoln's Republican victory and the Civil War; Democrats then dominated the post-Reconstruction South for a century. In the 20th century the party was transformed by Franklin D. Roosevelt's New Deal coalition — labour unions, urban machines, farmers, intellectuals, and the Solid South — and by Lyndon B. Johnson's Great Society. Democratic support for civil rights legislation in the 1960s triggered a long realignment in which the South moved to the Republicans while Black voters became a core Democratic constituency.

Philosophy and platform

Modern Democratic philosophy rests on social liberalism: a mixed economy with regulation and social programmes (Social Security, Medicare, the Affordable Care Act), support for labour unions and a higher minimum wage, environmental protection and action on climate change, abortion rights, LGBTQ rights, stricter gun control, and a foreign policy emphasising diplomacy, alliances, and multilateral institutions. The party is a broad coalition, with progressive, liberal, and moderate/centrist wings that contest its direction in primary elections.

Presidents

Democratic presidents have included Andrew Jackson, Martin Van Buren, James K. Polk, Franklin Pierce, James Buchanan, Grover Cleveland, Woodrow Wilson, Franklin D. Roosevelt, Harry S. Truman, John F. Kennedy, Lyndon B. Johnson, Jimmy Carter, Bill Clinton, and Barack Obama.
