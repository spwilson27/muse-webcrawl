Haag–Kastler axioms

The Haag–Kastler axioms are a formulation of algebraic quantum field theory introduced by Rudolf Haag and Daniel Kastler in 1964. Rather than assigning fields to spacetime points, the framework assigns to each bounded region of spacetime an algebra of observables, and the theory is defined by the network of these local algebras and their relations.

The axioms

The axioms require: isotony (larger regions have larger algebras), locality or Einstein causality (algebras of spacelike-separated regions commute), Poincaré covariance (a unitary representation of the Poincaré group acts compatibly on the net), and the existence of a unique invariant vacuum state satisfying the spectrum condition. Under these assumptions one can derive general results such as the Reeh–Schlieder theorem and the CPT theorem.

Significance

The Haag–Kastler framework underlies the mathematically rigorous study of quantum field theory, including the Doplicher–Haag–Roberts theory of superselection sectors and the analysis of thermal (KMS) states in quantum statistical mechanics.
