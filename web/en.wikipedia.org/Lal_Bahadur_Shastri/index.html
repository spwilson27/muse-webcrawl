Lal Bahadur Shastri (2 October 1904 – 11 January 1966) was an Indian politician and statesman who served as the second prime minister of India from 1964 to 1966. He previously served as home minister from 1961 to 1963. Shastri led India during the Indo-Pakistani War of 1965 and coined the slogan "Jai Jawan, Jai Kisan" ("Hail to the soldier; Hail to the farmer").

Early life and freedom struggle

Shastri was born in Mughalsarai (now Pandit Deen Dayal Upadhyaya Nagar), Uttar Pradesh, into a Kayastha family. He joined the Indian independence movement in the 1920s, drawn by Mahatma Gandhi, and was imprisoned repeatedly by the British — spending nearly nine years in jail in total. He earned the title "Shastri" (scholar) from the Kashi Vidyapith. After independence he became a protégé of Jawaharlal Nehru, serving as minister of railways and transport; he resigned as railway minister in 1956, accepting moral responsibility for a train accident — a rare act in Indian politics.

Prime ministership

On Nehru's death in May 1964, the Congress Party chose Shastri as a consensus prime minister. Mild-mannered but firm, he promoted the White Revolution for milk production and laid foundations for the Green Revolution in foodgrains. When Pakistan launched its 1965 offensive, Shastri authorised counterattacks across the international border toward Lahore, surprising observers with his resolve. The war ended with a UN-mandated ceasefire.

Death at Tashkent

Shastri travelled to Tashkent in the Soviet Union for talks mediated by Premier Alexei Kosygin. The Tashkent Declaration was signed on 10 January 1966, restoring the pre-war status quo. Hours later, in the early morning of 11 January 1966, Shastri died — officially of a heart attack, though the absence of a post-mortem and lost medical records have fuelled lasting speculation. He was posthumously awarded the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian honour.
