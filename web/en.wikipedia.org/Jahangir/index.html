Jahangir (31 August 1569 – 28 October 1627), born Prince Salim, was the fourth Mughal emperor, reigning from 1605 until his death. His imperial name Jahangir means "conqueror of the world" in Persian. He was the eldest surviving son of Akbar the Great.

Reign

Jahangir largely continued his father's policies of administrative consolidation and relative religious tolerance, though he could be severe with rivals: he ordered the execution of Guru Arjan, the fifth Sikh guru, in 1606, and suppressed rebellions by his own sons Khusrau and Khurram (the future Shah Jahan). The empire expanded modestly, with the submission of Mewar in 1615, while Kandahar was lost to Persia in 1622.

Court and culture

Jahangir was a notable patron of the arts; Mughal painting reached a celebrated peak of naturalism under his ateliers. His wife Nur Jahan exercised extraordinary political influence in the later years of the reign. Jahangir kept a detailed memoir, the Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri, and installed a famous "chain of justice" at Agra so petitioners could appeal to him directly. Addicted at times to alcohol and opium, his health declined, and he died in 1627 while traveling from Kashmir; he is buried at Shahdara near Lahore.
