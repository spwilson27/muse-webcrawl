The Battle of Blenheim (German: Zweite Schlacht bei Höchstädt) was a major battle of the War of the Spanish Succession, fought on 13 August 1704 near the village of Blindheim in Bavaria. Forces of the Grand Alliance under the Duke of Marlborough and Prince Eugene of Savoy decisively defeated a Franco-Bavarian army, saving Vienna and knocking Bavaria out of the war.

Background

In 1704 French and Bavarian forces threatened the Habsburg capital. Marlborough marched his army in secret from the Low Countries to the Danube — a feat of logistics and deception — and joined Eugene. After storming the Schellenberg heights at Donauwörth, the allies confronted Marshal Tallard's army along the Nebel stream.

The battle

While Eugene engaged the Franco-Bavarian right, Marlborough pinned the enemy wings with attacks on Blenheim and Oberglau, then massed his infantry and cavalry to smash through the weakened French centre. The French army disintegrated; Tallard was captured, and some 30,000 French and Bavarian troops were killed, wounded or taken prisoner.

Significance

Blenheim shattered the reputation of French military invincibility established under Louis XIV and shifted the war's momentum. Marlborough was rewarded with the royal manor of Woodstock, where Blenheim Palace was built in his honour.
