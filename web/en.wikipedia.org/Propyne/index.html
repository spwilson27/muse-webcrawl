Propyne

Propyne (methylacetylene) is a three-carbon alkyne.

Propyne (also called methylacetylene) is an alkyne with the chemical formula CH3C≡CH and molar mass 40.06 g/mol. It is a colourless gas with a faint petroleum-like odour, boiling at −23.2 °C. It is the second-simplest alkyne after acetylene and exists in equilibrium with its isomer propadiene (allene); mixtures of the two are sold as MAPD gas.

Uses

Propyne mixed with propadiene and propane was marketed as MAPP gas, a fuel gas once widely used for brazing and welding because it burns hotter than propane, though production for that use has ended in North America. Solid propyne is also of interest as a rocket fuel component, and propyne is used in organic synthesis as a building block.

Occurrence

Propyne is a component of cracked petroleum gases and has been detected in the atmosphere of Titan and in interstellar clouds, where its chemistry is studied as a model for hydrocarbon formation in space.
