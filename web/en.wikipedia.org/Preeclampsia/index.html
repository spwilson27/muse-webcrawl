Preeclampsia

Preeclampsia
SpecialtyObstetrics
OnsetAfter 20 weeks of pregnancy (also postpartum)
SymptomsHigh blood pressure, proteinuria; headaches, visual changes, upper abdominal pain in severe disease
Risk factorsFirst pregnancy, previous preeclampsia, chronic hypertension, diabetes, multiple pregnancy, obesity, age
TreatmentDelivery of baby and placenta; antihypertensives; magnesium sulfate
Frequencyc. 2–8% of pregnancies worldwide

Preeclampsia is a hypertensive disorder of pregnancy characterised by new-onset high blood pressure after 20 weeks of gestation, together with protein in the urine or other signs of organ dysfunction. It is a leading cause of maternal and perinatal morbidity and mortality worldwide. If seizures occur, the condition is termed eclampsia.

Signs and pathophysiology

Preeclampsia is thought to originate in abnormal development of the placenta, in which the spiral arteries fail to remodel properly, leading to placental underperfusion and release of factors — including antiangiogenic proteins such as sFlt-1 — that damage the maternal endothelium. The result is widespread vasoconstriction and capillary leak affecting the kidneys, liver, brain and blood. Severe features include blood pressure of 160/110 mmHg or higher, low platelets, impaired liver function, kidney failure, pulmonary oedema and cerebral symptoms. The related HELLP syndrome combines haemolysis, elevated liver enzymes and low platelets. Preeclampsia can also arise in the postpartum period.

Management

The only definitive cure is delivery of the baby and placenta; timing balances the risks of prematurity against deterioration of the mother. Antihypertensive drugs such as labetalol, nifedipine and methyldopa control severe hypertension, and magnesium sulfate is given to prevent eclamptic seizures. Low-dose aspirin started in early pregnancy reduces the risk in women identified as high-risk, and adequate calcium intake may help where dietary calcium is low. Women who have had preeclampsia have a higher lifetime risk of cardiovascular disease and are advised to have long-term follow-up.
