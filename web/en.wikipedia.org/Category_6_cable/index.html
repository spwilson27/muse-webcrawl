Category 6 cable

Category 6 cable (Cat 6) is a standardised twisted-pair cable for Ethernet and other network physical layers, defined by the ANSI/TIA and ISO/IEC cabling standards. It is backward compatible with Category 5 and 5e and supports data rates up to 1 Gbit/s over 100 metres, and 10 Gbit/s over shorter runs (up to about 55 metres in favourable conditions).

Characteristics

Cat 6 cable consists of four pairs of copper wires, twisted more tightly than in Cat 5e and often separated by a plastic spline running the length of the cable to reduce crosstalk. It is specified to a bandwidth of 250 MHz, compared with 100 MHz for Cat 5e. Conductors are usually 23 AWG, thicker than the 24 AWG common in Cat 5e, which makes Cat 6 cables stiffer and increases the minimum bend radius.

Variants

Category 6A ("augmented", standardised in 2008) doubles the bandwidth to 500 MHz and supports 10GBASE-T over the full 100-metre channel, with improved alien-crosstalk performance. Cat 6 installations terminate in 8P8C ("RJ45") modular connectors and patch panels and use the T568A or T568B wiring schemes. Like other balanced twisted-pair cables, it can carry Power over Ethernet (PoE).
