Doping at the Olympic Games

The use of performance-enhancing substances — doping — has been a recurring feature of competition at the Olympic Games. Athletes have used stimulants since the earliest modern Games: marathon winner Thomas Hicks was given strychnine and brandy during the 1904 race. The problem grew with the introduction of anabolic steroids in the 1950s and 1960s, and the death of Danish cyclist Knud Enemark Jensen at the 1960 Rome Games helped spur the International Olympic Committee to begin drug testing in 1968.

Notable cases

The most famous individual case is Canadian sprinter Ben Johnson, stripped of his 100 metres gold medal at Seoul 1988 after testing positive for stanozolol. State-sponsored programs have produced the largest scandals: East Germany systematically doped its athletes during the 1970s, and investigations after 2014 exposed a state-run doping program in Russia, culminating in the McLaren reports, the stripping of numerous Sochi 2014 medals, and sanctions requiring Russian athletes to compete under neutral designations at subsequent Games. Retesting of stored samples from Beijing 2008 and London 2012 later disqualified more than a hundred athletes.

Anti-doping system

Since 1999, the World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA) has harmonized prohibited lists, testing standards, and laboratory accreditation; samples from Olympic Games are stored for up to ten years for reanalysis as detection methods improve.
