The United Nations Foundation is an independent charitable organization created to support the work of the United Nations and to promote global cooperation. It was founded in 1998 with a US$1 billion gift from media entrepreneur and philanthropist Ted Turner, at the time the largest single donation of its kind.

History

Turner announced the gift at a UN dinner in September 1997, in part to spur the United States to pay its arrears to the UN. The Foundation was established as a public charity to channel Turner's grant — paid over ten years — and contributions from others toward UN causes. Timothy E. Wirth, a former US senator, served as its first president; subsequent leadership has included Kathy Calvin and Elizabeth Cousens.

Work

The Foundation builds partnerships between the UN, governments, businesses and civil society in areas including global health (polio and measles campaigns, malaria), climate change and clean energy, girls' and women's empowerment, data for development, and advocacy for the Sustainable Development Goals. Initiatives associated with it include the Measles & Rubella Initiative, Nothing But Nets, Girl Up, the Clean Cooking Alliance and the Better World Campaign, which advocates for US engagement with the UN.

It also administers the UN Fund for International Partnerships (UNFIP), the UN office that handles the Foundation's grant relationship with the UN system, and convenes events such as the Social Good Summit.
