Unique games conjecture

Unique games conjecture
Field: Computational complexity theory
Proposed by: Subhash Khot (2002)
Status: Unproven conjecture

The unique games conjecture (UGC) is a conjecture in computational complexity theory proposed by Subhash Khot in 2002. It asserts, roughly, that determining whether a "unique game" — a system of equations over a graph with one constraint per edge — has an almost perfectly satisfiable assignment or is not even slightly satisfiable is NP-hard for sufficiently large alphabets.

Implications

If true, the UGC implies that many well-known approximation algorithms for problems such as Max-Cut, vertex cover, and others are already optimal under the assumption P ≠ NP: no polynomial-time algorithm can achieve a better approximation ratio. Work on the conjecture's consequences earned Khot the Nevanlinna Prize in 2014. In 2018, proofs of the related 2-to-2 games conjecture resolved a major case, though the full conjecture remains open.
