Identity (social science)

Identity is the set of qualities, beliefs, personality traits, appearance, and expressions that characterise a person or a group. In the social sciences the term covers both personal identity — what makes an individual distinct — and social identity, derived from membership in groups such as nations, professions, ethnicities, and communities.

Formation

Psychologists such as Erik Erikson described identity formation as a central developmental task of adolescence, involving exploration and commitment. James Marcia elaborated this into identity statuses: diffusion, foreclosure, moratorium, and achievement.

Social identity theory

Social identity theory, developed by Henri Tajfel and John Turner, holds that part of the self-concept derives from group memberships, and that categorisation into in-groups and out-groups shapes perception and behaviour even in minimal groups.

Contemporary study

Identity is studied across sociology, anthropology, psychology, and political science, including work on cultural, gender, national, and online identities, and on how identities intersect.
