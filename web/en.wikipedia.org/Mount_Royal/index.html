Mount Royal

Elevation233 m (764 ft)
LocationMontreal, Quebec, Canada
GeologyMonteregian Hills; igneous intrusion (gabbro)

Mount Royal (French: Mont Royal) is a mountain in the city of Montreal, immediately west of Downtown Montreal, Quebec, Canada. The city's name is derived from the mountain's name; Jacques Cartier, who visited the site in 1535, named it Mont Royal. The mountain is part of the Monteregian Hills situated between the Laurentian Mountains and the Appalachians. It gave rise to the city's highest point and is the site of Mount Royal Park, one of Montreal's largest greenspaces.

Geology

Mount Royal is a deeply eroded igneous intrusion, formed about 125 million years ago when magma pushed up through the sedimentary rock of the St. Lawrence lowlands without reaching the surface. The mountain has three summits: Colline de la Croix (Mount Royal proper) at 233 metres, Colline d'Outremont at 211 metres, and Westmount Summit at 201 metres.

Mount Royal Park

The park was inaugurated in 1876 and designed by Frederick Law Olmsted, who also co-designed New York's Central Park. Olmsted envisioned the mountain's natural character preserved, with winding paths following its contours. Key features include Beaver Lake (Lac aux Castors), the Smith House, and the Kondiaronk Belvedere lookout in front of the Mount Royal Chalet, offering a celebrated view of the downtown skyline.

Landmarks

The 31.4-metre Mount Royal Cross, first erected in 1924 and illuminated at night, crowns the northeastern summit; tradition traces its origin to a wooden cross raised by Paul de Chomedey, Sieur de Maisonneuve, founder of Montreal, in 1643. The mountain also hosts two large cemeteries — Mount Royal Cemetery (1852) and Notre Dame des Neiges Cemetery, Canada's largest — Saint Joseph's Oratory, and the campus areas around Université de Montréal and McGill University at its foot.
