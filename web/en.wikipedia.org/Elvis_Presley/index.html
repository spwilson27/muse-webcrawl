Elvis Aaron Presley (January 8, 1935 – August 16, 1977) was an American singer and actor. Known as the "King of Rock and Roll", he is regarded as one of the most significant cultural figures of the 20th century. Presley's sexually provocative performance style, combined with a mix of influences across colour lines during the transformative era of race relations, brought both great success and controversy.

Career

Born in Tupelo, Mississippi, Presley moved to Memphis, Tennessee, as a teenager and began recording at Sun Studio in 1954. Signed by RCA Victor in 1955, he topped the charts with "Heartbreak Hotel" in 1956 and starred in films beginning with Love Me Tender. After two years of military service in Germany, he returned to music, later staging a celebrated 1968 television comeback special and acclaimed Las Vegas concert residencies. His recordings span rockabilly, pop, country, blues, and gospel; he won three Grammy Awards, all for gospel performances.

Death and legacy

Presley died at his Graceland estate in Memphis in 1977 at the age of 42. He is the best-selling solo music artist of all time, with estimated record sales in the hundreds of millions, and Graceland remains one of the most visited private homes in the United States.
