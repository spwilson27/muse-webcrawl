Van der Waerden's theorem

Van der Waerden's theorem is a theorem in the branch of mathematics called Ramsey theory. It states that for any given positive integers r and k, there is some number N such that if the integers {1, 2, …, N} are coloured, each with one of r different colours, then there are at least k integers in arithmetic progression all of the same colour. The smallest such N is called the van der Waerden number W(r, k).

History

The theorem was conjectured by Baudet and proved in 1927 by B. L. van der Waerden, working from a hint by Emil Artin. It was one of the founding results of Ramsey theory and inspired later work, including Szemerédi's theorem, which extended the result to sets of positive density.

Van der Waerden numbers

Only a few van der Waerden numbers are known exactly. For two colours, W(2, 3) = 9, W(2, 4) = 35, W(2, 5) = 178, and W(2, 6) = 1132. General upper bounds were long extremely large; the best current bounds come from the work of Gowers on Szemerédi's theorem.
