Professional gamer

A professional gamer, commonly shortened to pro gamer, is a player who plays video games professionally, usually as a member of an organized esports team, earning income through salaries, tournament prize winnings, sponsorships, and media streaming. Professional gaming is the competitive apex of esports, a form of competition using video games that has grown into a global spectator industry with leagues, world championships, and audiences in the hundreds of millions.

Pro gamers typically specialize in a single game title — such as League of Legends, Counter-Strike 2, Dota 2, StarCraft II, Valorant, or fighting games such as Street Fighter — and train for many hours daily, studying strategy, practicing mechanics, and reviewing match footage with coaches and analysts, much like athletes in traditional sports.

History

Competitive video gaming dates to the early 1980s with arcade high-score competitions and events such as the Space Invaders Championship of 1980. The modern professional scene emerged in South Korea in the late 1990s around real-time strategy games, particularly StarCraft, where televised leagues and salaried teams first appeared. The 2010s saw explosive growth driven by streaming platforms such as Twitch, franchised leagues, and multimillion-dollar tournaments such as The International for Dota 2, whose 2021 prize pool exceeded US$40 million.

Career

Professional players are usually signed to organizations that provide salaries, housing in team "gaming houses," coaching, and travel. Careers are often short, with peak performance commonly in the late teens and twenties, after which players may transition into coaching, commentary ("casting"), or full-time streaming. Income streams include team salary, prize money, personal sponsorships, and advertising revenue from streamed content.

Skills and training

Elite play demands exceptional hand–eye coordination, reaction speed — often measured in actions per minute (APM) in strategy games — deep strategic knowledge, teamwork and communication, and mental resilience under tournament pressure. Pro gamers train in structured regimens including scrimmages ("scrims") against other teams, individual practice drills, and video review.

Recognition

Esports has gained formal recognition in several countries; professional players have received athlete visas, and esports has appeared as a medal event at the Asian Games. The audience and revenues of the industry rival those of established sports leagues.
