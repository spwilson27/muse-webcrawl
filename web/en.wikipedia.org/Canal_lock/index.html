Canal lock

Lock
A chamber with gates used to raise and lower boats between stretches of water of different levels.

A lock is a device used for raising and lowering boats, ships, and other watercraft between stretches of water of different levels on river and canal waterways.

Operation

A boat entering a lock is sealed in the chamber by closing the gates behind it. Valves (paddles) then admit or release water so the chamber level matches the level of the pound the boat is heading to, after which the far gates open and the boat proceeds.

History

The chamber lock was developed in China during the Song dynasty (the pound lock is credited to Qiao Weiyue in 984 CE) and in medieval Europe. The mitre gate, whose paired doors point upstream so water pressure seals them, is often attributed to Leonardo da Vinci's designs in the late 15th century.
