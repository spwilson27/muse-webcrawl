Tesla, Inc. (formerly Tesla Motors) is an American multinational automotive and clean energy company headquartered in Austin, Texas. Tesla designs, manufactures and sells battery electric vehicles (BEVs), stationary battery energy storage devices, solar panels and solar shingles, and related products and services. Named after electrical engineer Nikola Tesla, the company was incorporated in 2003 by Martin Eberhard and Marc Tarpenning. Elon Musk, who joined the following year as an investor and chairman, has served as chief executive since 2008 and is the public face of the company.

History

Tesla's first product, the Roadster sports car, launched in 2008 and demonstrated that lithium-ion battery technology could produce a desirable, long-range electric car. The Model S luxury sedan followed in 2012, then the Model X crossover (2015), the mass-market Model 3 sedan (2017), and the Model Y crossover (2020), which became the world's best-selling car in 2023. The company went public in 2010, the first American carmaker to do so since Ford in 1956, and grew to become the world's most valuable automaker by market capitalisation, briefly exceeding US$1 trillion in 2021.

Products and technology

Tesla's vehicle line-up includes the Model S, Model 3, Model X, Model Y, the Cybertruck pickup (2023), and the Semi truck. Its energy products include the Powerwall home battery, Megapack grid-scale battery, solar panels, and the Solar Roof. Tesla operates "Gigafactory" plants in the United States, China, and Germany, and sells cars directly to consumers rather than through franchised dealerships. Its driver-assistance systems, marketed as Autopilot and "Full Self-Driving", have attracted both acclaim and regulatory scrutiny.

Criticism

The company has faced criticism over workplace safety, labour relations, product quality, Musk's public statements, and the safety record of its driver-assistance technology.
