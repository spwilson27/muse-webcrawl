Naukratis

Naucratis or Naukratis (Ancient Greek: Ναύκρατις) was a city and trading-port in ancient Egypt, located on the Canopic (westernmost) branch of the Nile in the western Nile Delta. Founded in the 7th century BC, it was the first and, for much of its early history, the only permanent Greek colony in Egypt, serving as a centre of trade and cultural exchange between ancient Greece and Egypt.

History

According to Herodotus, the pharaoh Amasis (26th Dynasty) gave Naukratis to the Greeks as a commercial headquarters, though Greek settlement at the site probably began in the later 7th century BC, possibly under Psammetichus I. Ionian and other Greek cities maintained temples and trading posts there, including the famous Hellenion precinct.

Trade and significance

Naukratis monopolised trade between Greece and Egypt: Greek merchants imported silver, wine, oil and pottery, and exported Egyptian grain, linen, natron and papyrus. The city is also known for its pottery workshops and faience production.

Excavations

The site, near the modern village of Kom Gi'eif, was excavated by Flinders Petrie in 1884–1885 and has been the subject of an ongoing British Museum research project.
