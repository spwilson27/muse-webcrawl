Sognefjord

The Sognefjord or Sognefjorden, nicknamed the "King of the Fjords" (Norwegian: Fjordenes konge), is the largest and deepest fjord in Norway and the second-longest in the world, after Scoresby Sound in Greenland. Located in Vestland county in Western Norway, it stretches about 205 kilometres (127 mi) inland from the ocean to the small village of Skjolden in the municipality of Luster.

The fjord reaches a maximum depth of about 1,308 metres (4,291 ft) below sea level, and its mouth is roughly 4.5 kilometres (2.8 mi) wide. The inner end of the fjord lies southeast of the Jotunheimen mountain range, home to Norway's highest peaks.

Geography

The Sognefjord penetrates deep into the Scandinavian Mountains. Its many branches include the Fjærlandsfjord, Sogndalsfjord, Lustrafjord, Årdalsfjord, Lærdalsfjord, Aurlandsfjord, and the famous Nærøyfjord, which together with the Geirangerfjord was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2005 as part of the West Norwegian Fjords.

The fjord was carved by glacial erosion during the ice ages. In places the bedrock beneath the water lies more than 1,000 metres below sea level while the surrounding mountains rise over 1,000 metres above it, so the total relief exceeds 2 kilometres.

Climate and settlement

The climate along the fjord varies from a mild, wet coastal climate at the mouth to a much drier, more continental climate at the inner branches around Årdal and Lærdal. Fruit growing, especially apples, plums, and cherries, is common on the sunny slopes of the inner fjord. Towns and villages along the fjord include Balestrand, Leikanger, Sogndal, Kaupanger, and Flåm, the latter famous as the terminus of the Flåm Railway.

Transport

There are no bridges across the main fjord; crossings are by car ferry. Several road tunnels, including the Lærdal Tunnel — the world's longest road tunnel at 24.5 kilometres — pass through the mountains near the fjord's inner reaches. Express boats connect Bergen with Sogndal and Flåm.

Tourism

The Sognefjord is one of Norway's most visited natural attractions. Cruise ships sail its length in summer, and tourists combine fjord cruises with the Flåm Railway and visits to stave churches such as those at Borgund and Urnes, the latter a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
