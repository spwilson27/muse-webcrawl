The Darien scheme was an unsuccessful attempt, backed largely by investors in the Kingdom of Scotland, to gain wealth and influence by establishing New Caledonia, a colony on the Isthmus of Panama, in the late 1690s. The plan was for the colony, located on the Gulf of Darién, to establish an overland route linking the Pacific and Atlantic Oceans — an early precursor of the Panama Canal route.

Background

The scheme was promoted by William Paterson, a founder of the Bank of England, through the Company of Scotland, chartered by the Scottish Parliament in 1695. Scots from all classes invested an estimated £400,000 — perhaps a quarter to a half of all the money circulating in Scotland. English and Dutch investors were forced out by political pressure from the English Parliament and the East India Company, leaving Scotland to fund the venture alone.

The expeditions

The first expedition of five ships and about 1,200 settlers sailed in July 1698 and founded New Edinburgh on a swampy, disease-ridden site. The colonists struggled: the land was unsuitable for agriculture, trade goods proved unsellable, the local Indigenous peoples declined English gifts, and Spanish colonists, who claimed the territory, were hostile. Supplies from Scotland failed to arrive, and English colonies were forbidden to help. Disease, especially dysentery and fever, killed around ten settlers a day. The colony was abandoned in July 1699.

A second expedition of some 1,300 people arrived in November 1699, unaware of the colony's fate. After rebuilding, the settlers repelled a Spanish attack in March 1700 but were blockaded and starved into surrender. Only a few hundred of the roughly 2,500 who sailed ever returned to Scotland.

Consequences

The disaster ruined many Scottish investors and intensified resentment of England, whose government had undermined the scheme. Compensation for the shareholders was written into the financial settlement accompanying the Acts of Union 1707, which united the Scottish and English parliaments — leading some historians to argue that Darien helped pave the way to the Union.
