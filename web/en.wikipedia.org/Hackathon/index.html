Hackathon

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A hackathon (also known as a hack day, hackfest, datathon or codefest; a portmanteau of hack and marathon) is an event where people engage in rapid and collaborative engineering over a relatively short period of time, such as 24 or 48 hours. Participants — typically programmers, designers and project managers — form teams to build working software or hardware prototypes by the end of the event, often competing for prizes.

History

The term was coined in the late 1990s; early uses include an OpenBSD cryptographic development event in Calgary in 1999 and a Sun Microsystems event at the JavaOne conference the same year. Hackathons became widespread in the 2000s and 2010s as technology companies, universities and civic organisations adopted them for recruiting, community building and rapid prototyping. Corporations such as Facebook institutionalised internal hackathons, and some well-known products and features began as hackathon projects.

Structure

Events typically open with pitches of ideas, followed by team formation, an intensive building period with mentoring and workshops, and closing demonstrations judged against criteria such as innovation, execution and impact. Many hackathons focus on a theme — an application programming interface, a social cause, a programming language or a demographic group.

Criticism

Hackathons have been criticised for promoting unsustainable "crunch" work, producing throwaway prototypes, and, in corporate settings, extracting unpaid speculative labour. Others defend them as voluntary, social and educational events.
