Warwickshire (abbreviated Warks) is a ceremonial county in the West Midlands of England. Its county town is Warwick, and its largest town is Nuneaton. The county is best known as the birthplace of William Shakespeare at Stratford-upon-Avon, and it is often marketed as "Shakespeare's County". Other principal towns include Rugby, Leamington Spa, Kenilworth and Bedworth.

Geography

Warwickshire is a largely rural county of about 1,975 km², bordered by Staffordshire, Leicestershire, Northamptonshire, Oxfordshire, Gloucestershire, Worcestershire and the West Midlands metropolitan county. The River Avon flows through the south of the county, passing Stratford-upon-Avon, Evesham (in Worcestershire) and Warwick. The south includes the edge of the Cotswolds, while the north historically contained the Forest of Arden, a heavily wooded area that features in Shakespeare's As You Like It.

History

The county takes its name from Warwick, a Saxon burh fortified against the Danes. Warwick Castle, founded in the Norman period, became the seat of the Earls of Warwick, including Richard Neville, "the Kingmaker", in the 15th century. The Battle of Edgehill (1642), the first major battle of the English Civil War, was fought in the county. Rugby is famous as the birthplace of rugby football, said to have originated at Rugby School in 1823, and the town of Rugby grew as a railway and engineering centre. The north of the county was formerly industrial, with coal mining around Nuneaton and Bedworth; Coventry and Birmingham were historically within Warwickshire before becoming part of the West Midlands county in 1974.

Economy and transport

Tourism centred on Stratford-upon-Avon, Warwick and Kenilworth castles is a major industry. The county is crossed by the M40, M6 and M42 motorways and by the West Coast Main Line through Rugby. The Grand Union and Oxford canals pass through the county. Jaguar Land Rover has facilities at Gaydon, and the area forms part of the wider automotive and technology economy of the West Midlands.
