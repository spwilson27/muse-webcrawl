Lentivirus

Lentivirus is a genus of retroviruses characterised by long incubation periods (Latin lentus, "slow"). Lentiviruses can deliver a significant amount of viral RNA into the DNA of host cells and can infect non-dividing cells, a distinctive feature among retroviruses.

Members and significance

The best-known lentivirus is the human immunodeficiency virus (HIV), which causes AIDS. Others include simian, feline and equine immunodeficiency/infectious anaemia viruses, and visna-maedi virus of sheep. In biotechnology, lentiviral vectors derived mainly from HIV are widely used to deliver genes into cells in research and gene therapy.
