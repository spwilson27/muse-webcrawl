Correspondence theory of truth

The correspondence theory of truth states that a proposition is true if and only if it corresponds to the facts of the world. It is the classical view, traceable to Aristotle's formulation that to say of what is that it is, is true.

History

Versions were developed by Aquinas, Descartes, Locke, Bertrand Russell and J. L. Austin. Alfred Tarski's semantic theory gave a formal account for formal languages.

Objections

Critics question how a linguistic item can correspond to a non-linguistic fact, and how correspondence can be verified independently. Rival accounts include coherence, pragmatist and deflationary theories of truth.
