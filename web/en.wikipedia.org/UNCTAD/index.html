UN Trade and Development (UNCTAD)

The United Nations Conference on Trade and Development (UNCTAD), now styled UN Trade and Development, is an intergovernmental organization within the United Nations Secretariat that promotes the interests of developing countries in world trade. It was established in 1964 by the United Nations General Assembly and is headquartered at the Palais des Nations in Geneva, Switzerland. Its 195 member states meet in a full conference every four years.

Work

UNCTAD combines research and policy analysis, intergovernmental consensus-building, and technical cooperation. Its flagship publications include the Trade and Development Report, the World Investment Report, and the Review of Maritime Transport, and it maintains statistical databases such as UNCTADstat. It administers tools used worldwide, including the ASYCUDA customs automation system and the DMFAS debt-management software, and it has a special mandate for the concerns of least developed countries, landlocked developing countries, and small island developing states.

History

UNCTAD emerged from developing countries' dissatisfaction with the postwar trading order embodied in the GATT, which they saw as favoring industrialized nations. Its first conference, held in Geneva in 1964 under Secretary-General Raúl Prebisch, produced the Group of 77 coalition and led to the Generalized System of Preferences, under which developed countries grant tariff preferences to developing-country exports. UNCTAD was a central forum for the 1970s campaign for a New International Economic Order.
