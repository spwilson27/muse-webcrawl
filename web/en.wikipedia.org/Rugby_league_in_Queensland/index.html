Rugby league is one of the most popular sports in Queensland, Australia. The Queensland Rugby Football League was formed in 1908, the same year the code took hold in Sydney, after local rugby union players broke away over demands for compensation for time lost from work. The game is governed state-wide by the Queensland Rugby League.

History

The first Queensland club competition began in Brisbane in 1909. Queensland teams long contested the interstate series against New South Wales under residency rules, which disadvantaged them as star players moved to wealthy Sydney clubs. The introduction of the State of Origin format in 1980, in which players represent the state of their origin, revitalised the rivalry and became the sport's premier domestic spectacle.

State of Origin

The Queensland Maroons have produced legendary figures including Wally Lewis, Mal Meninga, Allan Langer, Darren Lockyer, Johnathan Thurston, Cameron Smith and Billy Slater. Queensland dominated the series from 2006 to 2013 with eight consecutive series wins under coach Mal Meninga. Matches at Brisbane's Lang Park are famous for their atmosphere.

Club football

Queensland's National Rugby League clubs are the Brisbane Broncos (founded 1988), the North Queensland Cowboys (1995, based in Townsville), the Gold Coast Titans (2007) and the Dolphins (2023, based at Redcliffe). The QRL also runs the Queensland Cup, a statewide second-tier competition rooted in the old Brisbane club league.
