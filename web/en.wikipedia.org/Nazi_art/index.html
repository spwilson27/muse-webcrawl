Art in Nazi Germany

The art of Nazi Germany was the state-approved art produced in Germany between 1933 and 1945. The Nazi regime promoted heroic, realistic art celebrating Aryan ideals, rural life, military strength and the state, while condemning modernist art as "degenerate" (entartet).

Official style

Officially favoured art drew on neoclassicism and romantic realism: monumental sculpture, heroic male nudes, idealised peasant scenes and heroic landscapes. Figures such as the sculptor Arno Breker and the painter Adolf Ziegler exemplified the approved style shown at the annual Great German Art Exhibitions in Munich.

Degenerate art

In 1937 the regime confiscated thousands of modern works from museums and staged the mocking "Degenerate Art" exhibition in Munich, which drew far larger crowds than the official show. Many works were sold abroad or destroyed, and persecuted artists emigrated or were banned from working.

Legacy

After 1945 Nazi monumental art was widely removed or destroyed, though surviving buildings and some works remain the subject of historical study and debate.
