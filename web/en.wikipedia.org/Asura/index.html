Asuras (Sanskrit: असुर) are a class of beings in Indic religions. They are described as power-seeking beings, generally opposed to the benevolent devas. In Hinduism, the asuras are often portrayed as competitors and enemies of the gods, though their character varies greatly across texts: some asuras are virtuous devotees, others arrogant tyrants. In Buddhism, the asuras form one of the six realms of rebirth in samsara, a realm of beings driven by jealousy, pride, and constant conflict with the gods of the Trāyastriṃśa heaven.

Etymology and origins

The word asura is related to Avestan ahura, as in Ahura Mazda of Zoroastrianism. In the earliest layers of the Rigveda, asura is an honorific applied to powerful gods such as Varuna, Mitra, and Agni, meaning "lord". Only in later Vedic and post-Vedic literature does the term consistently denote beings opposed to the devas, a shift sometimes explained by the folk etymology that parses a-sura as "non-god" or "anti-god".

In Hinduism

Hindu mythology is rich in stories of the eternal struggle between devas and asuras, who are frequently described as cousins — both descending from the sage Kashyapa. Famous asuras include Hiranyakashipu, slain by the avatar Narasimha; Mahishasura, defeated by Durga; Ravana, the antagonist of the Ramayana; and Vritra, the serpent who withheld the waters of the world until Indra slew him. The churning of the ocean of milk (Samudra Manthana) is a celebrated episode in which devas and asuras cooperate — pulling opposite ends of the serpent Vasuki — before quarrelling over the nectar of immortality.

Not all asuras are evil: Prahlada, a devotee of Vishnu, and the generous king Bali are revered examples of righteous asuras.

In Buddhism

In Buddhist cosmology the asuras inhabit one of the six domains of rebirth. They enjoy long lives and pleasures greater than those of humans but are afflicted by envy of the devas, with whom they wage perpetual war. Their condition is a karmic result of good deeds performed with jealousy and ill intent. In Mahayana traditions, asuras may also appear as wrathful protectors at the gates of temples.

In Jainism

Jain cosmology includes asuras (Asura Kumaras) among the Bhavanapati, the lowest class of heavenly beings residing in the upper levels of the netherworld, where they serve and torment one another according to their karma.
