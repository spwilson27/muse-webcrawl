Giant Wild Goose Pagoda

The Giant Wild Goose Pagoda (Chinese: Da Yan Ta, "Big Wild Goose Pagoda") is a monumental Buddhist pagoda in Xi'an, Shaanxi, China. Built during the Tang dynasty, it is one of the most famous landmarks of Xi'an and a symbol of the ancient capital, and it is distinguished from the nearby Small Wild Goose Pagoda.

History

The pagoda was first built in 652, during the reign of Emperor Gaozong of Tang, within the Da Ci'en Temple complex. Its purpose was to house the Buddhist sutras, statues, and relics that the monk Xuanzang had brought back from his pilgrimage to India along the Silk Road. Xuanzang supervised the project and translated many of the texts stored there.

The original structure, built of rammed earth with a stone exterior, collapsed within decades and was rebuilt in brick between 701 and 704 under Empress Wu Zetian. Earthquakes damaged it over the centuries — notably in the great Shaanxi earthquake of 1556 — and it was repaired repeatedly, taking its present seven-storey form in the Ming dynasty.

Architecture

The pagoda is a square, tapering brick tower about 64 metres (210 feet) high, with seven storeys. Each floor is slightly smaller than the one below, and the structure rests on a square base. Inside, a staircase allows visitors to climb to the top for views over Xi'an. The surrounding temple, Da Ci'en Temple, remains an active Buddhist site, and a large square with musical fountains lies to the north of the pagoda.

In 2014 the pagoda was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as part of the "Silk Roads: the Routes Network of Chang'an-Tianshan Corridor" site.
