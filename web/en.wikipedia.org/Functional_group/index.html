Functional group

Functional group
FieldOrganic chemistry
ExamplesHydroxyl –OH, carbonyl C=O, carboxyl –COOH, amino –NH2
RoleDetermines characteristic reactions of a molecule

In organic chemistry, a functional group is a specific group of atoms within a molecule that is responsible for the molecule's characteristic chemical reactions. The same functional group behaves similarly regardless of the size of the molecule it is part of, so organic compounds are classified into families — alcohols, amines, carboxylic acids and so on — by the functional groups they contain.

Common groups

Hydrocarbons: alkyl (C–C), alkene (C=C), alkyne (C≡C), aryl (aromatic rings). Oxygen-containing: hydroxyl (alcohols), ether, carbonyl (aldehydes and ketones), carboxyl (carboxylic acids), ester and anhydride. Nitrogen-containing: amino, amide, nitrile, nitro. Others: halogeno (haloalkanes), thiol (–SH), phosphate. Some groups combine features, as in amino acids, which carry both amino and carboxyl groups.

Use in synthesis and analysis

Chemists plan syntheses as interconversions of functional groups while treating the carbon skeleton as a scaffold, a perspective formalised in retrosynthetic analysis. Functional groups give characteristic signals in infrared spectroscopy and NMR, allowing structure identification.
