Temperature record of the last 2,000 years

The temperature record of the last 2,000 years describes reconstructions of past climate based on climate proxy data such as tree rings, ice cores, corals, and lake and ocean sediments. Because instrumental records of global temperature only extend back to about 1850, proxy reconstructions are used to place recent warming in the context of the last two millennia.

Reconstructions

Multiple independent research groups have produced reconstructions of Northern Hemisphere and global surface temperature. These studies generally show relatively warm conditions in parts of the world during the Medieval Climate Anomaly (roughly 950–1250), cooler conditions during the Little Ice Age (roughly 1450–1850), and rapid warming from the late 19th century onward, with recent decades the warmest of the past two thousand years. The prominent "hockey stick graph", first published by Mann, Bradley and Hughes in 1998–1999, illustrated this pattern and became a focus of public debate, though subsequent studies using different methods have produced broadly similar results.

Assessment

The IPCC Sixth Assessment Report concluded that global surface temperature has increased faster since 1970 than in any other 50-year period over the last 2,000 years, and that recent warmth is unprecedented in that timespan at the global scale.
