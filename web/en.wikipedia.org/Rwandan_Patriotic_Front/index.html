The Rwandan Patriotic Front (French: Front Patriotique Rwandais, FPR), commonly known by its English abbreviation RPF, is the ruling political party of Rwanda and the movement that has governed the country since the end of the Rwandan genocide in 1994.

Origins

The RPF grew out of the Rwandan Alliance for National Unity (RANU), an organisation founded in the late 1970s by Rwandan refugees — chiefly Tutsi who had fled pogroms in 1959 and afterwards and were living in Uganda and elsewhere in the diaspora. RANU was renamed the Rwandan Patriotic Front in 1987. Its military wing, the Rwandan Patriotic Army (RPA), drew heavily on Rwandan officers and soldiers who had served in Uganda's National Resistance Army during the Ugandan Bush War.

The civil war

On 1 October 1990 the RPA invaded northern Rwanda from Uganda, opening the Rwandan Civil War. The offensive was checked by the Rwandan Armed Forces, backed by France and Zaire, but the RPF regrouped in the Virunga mountains under the leadership of Paul Kagame, who succeeded Fred Rwigyema after his death early in the invasion. The Arusha Accords of August 1993 provided for a power-sharing transitional government, but implementation stalled.

The genocide and the RPF victory

After the assassination of President Juvénal Habyarimana on 6 April 1994 triggered the genocide against the Tutsi, in which some 800,000 people were murdered in about one hundred days, the RPF resumed its offensive. By mid-July 1994 it controlled most of Rwanda, including the capital, Kigali, and declared victory. A Government of National Unity was formed with Pasteur Bizimungu as president, though real power lay with the RPF and Kagame, who became president in 2000.

In government

Under the RPF, Rwanda has pursued rapid economic development, administrative reform and a policy of national unity that suppresses public ethnic identification. The party has won successive multiparty elections with large majorities; critics, including human rights organisations, have accused it of restricting political opposition, press freedom and civil liberties, and of involvement in the wars in the neighbouring Democratic Republic of the Congo. The RPF dominates the Chamber of Deputies, in coalition with several smaller parties.
