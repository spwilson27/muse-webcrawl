Gianni Agnelli

Giovanni "Gianni" Agnelli (12 March 1921 – 24 January 2003) was an Italian industrialist and the principal shareholder and head of Fiat, the automobile company founded by his grandfather Giovanni Agnelli. Known in Italy as l'Avvocato ("the Lawyer"), he was for decades one of the most powerful and richest men in Italy and a symbol of Italian style and capitalism.

Early life

Agnelli was born in Turin into Italy's most prominent industrial family. His father, Edoardo Agnelli, died in a seaplane accident in 1935, and his mother Virginia died in 1945. Gianni studied law at the University of Turin — the source of his nickname — and served as a cavalry officer during the Second World War.

Fiat

After a period as vice-chairman, Agnelli took control of Fiat in 1966 and led the company for thirty years. Under his leadership Fiat expanded into a diversified industrial group with interests in automobiles, trucks, tractors, steel, publishing (including the newspaper La Stampa), and insurance. He negotiated major deals including the 1969 acquisition of Lancia and a stake in Ferrari, and a landmark agreement to build a giant car plant in the Soviet Union at Tolyatti. In the 1980s Fiat was briefly Europe's largest carmaker.

Other interests and later life

Agnelli's family also controlled the Juventus football club, with which he was closely associated. A celebrated figure of the international jet set, he was regarded as a style icon, famous for wearing his wristwatch over his shirt cuff. In 1991 he was appointed a senator for life by the President of Italy. He stepped down as Fiat chairman in 1996, remaining honorary chairman until his death in Turin in 2003.
