Michael D. Higgins

Michael Daniel Higgins (Irish: Mícheál Dónal Ó hUigínn; born 18 April 1941) is an Irish politician, poet, sociologist and broadcaster who served as the ninth President of Ireland from 2011 to 2025. A longtime Labour Party figure, he was first elected in the 2011 presidential election and re-elected in 2018 with the largest personal mandate in Irish electoral history.

Early life and career

Born in Limerick and raised in County Clare, Higgins studied at University College Galway, where he later lectured in sociology and political science. He served as a Teachta Dála (member of parliament) for Galway West and as a senator, and was Mayor of Galway twice. As Minister for Arts, Culture and the Gaeltacht (1993–1997), he established the Irish-language television channel TG4 and revived the Irish film industry.

Presidency

As president, a largely ceremonial role, Higgins was noted for his speeches on ethics, inequality, memory, and Ireland's colonial history — including the state commemoration programme for the decade of centenaries — and for his advocacy on climate change and human rights. He and his wife Sabina are also well known for their Bernese Mountain dogs, including Síoda, Misneach and Bród.

Writing

Higgins has published several collections of poetry, including The Betrayal (1990) and An Arid Season (2004), and essays on politics and culture.
