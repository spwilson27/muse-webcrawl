Battle of Rorke's Drift

The Battle of Rorke's Drift, also known as the Defence of Rorke's Drift, was an engagement in the Anglo-Zulu War, fought on 22–23 January 1879. A small British garrison of about 150 men, commanded by Lieutenants John Chard and Gonville Bromhead, successfully defended the mission station at Rorke's Drift in Natal against repeated attacks by 3,000–4,000 Zulu warriors — on the same day that a much larger British force was destroyed at Isandlwana.

Background

Rorke's Drift was a mission station and supply depot on the Buffalo River at the border of Zululand. Warned by survivors of the approaching Zulu impi — a reserve force that had missed the main battle — Chard improvised defences of mealie bags and biscuit boxes around the storehouse and hospital.

The battle

Attacks began in the late afternoon and continued through the night. The Zulus set fire to the hospital, and patients and defenders fought room to room to escape the flames. Behind their barricades the British delivered disciplined volley fire with Martini-Henry rifles. By dawn the Zulus, exhausted and having lost several hundred men, withdrew as a British relief column approached. Seventeen defenders were killed.

Legacy

Eleven Victoria Crosses were awarded for the action — the most ever given for a single engagement — and the defence was widely celebrated in Britain, partly offsetting the shock of Isandlwana. The battle was dramatised in the 1964 film Zulu.
