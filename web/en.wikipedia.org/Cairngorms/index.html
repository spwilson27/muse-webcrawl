The Cairngorms (Scottish Gaelic: Am Monadh Ruadh, "the red hills") are a mountain range in the eastern Highlands of Scotland, closely associated with the mountain range of the same name. The Cairngorms form part of the Grampian Mountains and contain the highest, coldest, and snowiest plateaux in the British Isles.


The Cairngorm plateau, among the highest land in Britain.

The range is centred on the Cairngorms National Park, established in 2003 and the largest national park in the United Kingdom. Its high plateau is one of the most extensive areas of land above 1,000 metres in Britain and has a tundra-like climate, with persistent snow patches that sometimes survive the summer.

Geography

The Cairngorms consist of a granite massif of broad, rounded plateaux cut by deep glacial troughs and corries. Five of Scotland's six highest mountains lie here: Ben Macdui (1,309 m), Braeriach (1,296 m), Cairn Toul (1,291 m), Sgor an Lochain Uaine (1,258 m), and Cairn Gorm (1,245 m). The Lairig Ghru pass, rising to 835 m, cuts through the range between Deeside and Strathspey. Major rivers rising in the range include the Dee, the Spey, and the Don.

Climate and snow

The Cairngorms have the coldest average temperatures in the United Kingdom; Braemar, at the range's edge, has recorded temperatures below −27 °C. Snow patches in sheltered corries, such as those on Braeriach, have survived entire summers, melting completely only in exceptional years. The Cairngorm Mountain ski area, served by the Cairngorm Mountain Railway funicular (opened 2001), is one of Scotland's main winter sports centres.

Flora and fauna

The range hosts the largest remaining fragments of Caledonian pinewood, notably at Rothiemurchus and Abernethy, home to Scottish crossbills, capercaillie, crested tits, and red squirrels. The high plateau supports mountain hare, ptarmigan, dotterel, and snow bunting, and Britain's only free-ranging herd of reindeer, introduced in 1952, grazes the hills. The Cairngorms are also noted for semi-precious cairngorm quartz (smoky quartz) found in the granite.

Human history and recreation

The area has long been used for deer stalking estates, hill farming, and whisky distilling in surrounding glens. Aviemore is the main resort town. The Cairngorms are popular for hillwalking, climbing, skiing, and mountain biking; bothies and mountain rescue teams support an often harsh environment in which sudden weather changes are a recognised hazard.
