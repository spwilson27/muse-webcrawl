Yalta is a resort city on the south coast of the Crimean Peninsula, surrounded by the Black Sea. It lies within the Crimean Mountains and has long been a popular holiday destination, known for its mild climate, promenades, and nearby palaces.

History

The area was settled in antiquity and later came under Byzantine, Genoese, Ottoman, and Russian imperial control. In February 1945, Yalta hosted the Yalta Conference, at which Franklin D. Roosevelt, Winston Churchill, and Joseph Stalin met to plan the postwar order of Europe. The conference was held at the Livadia Palace, the former summer retreat of the Russian tsars.

Geography and climate

Yalta sits in an amphitheatre-like bay sheltered by mountains, giving it a humid subtropical climate unusual for its latitude. Winters are mild and summers warm, supporting vineyards and subtropical plantings in the Nikita Botanical Garden, founded in 1812.

Landmarks

Notable sights include the Swallow's Nest castle perched on a cliff near the town, the Vorontsov Palace at Alupka, and the Massandra Palace and winery. The city remains a centre of tourism on the Black Sea.
