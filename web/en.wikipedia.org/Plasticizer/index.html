Plasticizer

A plasticizer (or plasticiser) is a substance added to a material to make it softer, more flexible and easier to process. Plasticizers are most widely used with polyvinyl chloride (PVC), which is rigid in its pure form but becomes pliable when plasticized, as in cables, flooring, tubing and synthetic leather.

How they work

Plasticizer molecules embed between polymer chains, increasing the space between them and lowering the glass transition temperature, so the material bends rather than cracks. The most common class historically has been the phthalate esters, such as DEHP, alongside adipates, citrates and other alternatives.

Health and environment

Because plasticizers are not chemically bound to the polymer, they can migrate and leach out over time. Concerns that some phthalates act as endocrine disruptors have led to restrictions in toys, food contact materials and medical devices, and wider use of alternative plasticizers.
