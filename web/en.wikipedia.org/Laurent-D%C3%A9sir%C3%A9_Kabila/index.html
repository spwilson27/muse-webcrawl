Laurent-Désiré Kabila (27 November 1939 – 16 January 2001) was a Congolese politician and rebel leader who overthrew Mobutu Sese Seko and became president of the Democratic Republic of the Congo in 1997.

Early life and rebellion

Kabila was a longtime opponent of Mobutu, leading a small Marxist guerrilla movement in eastern Congo from the 1960s. His forces remained marginal until the aftermath of the Rwandan genocide reshaped regional politics.

Presidency

Backed by Rwanda and Uganda, his AFDL alliance marched across Zaire in the First Congo War and took Kinshasa in May 1997; Kabila renamed the country the Democratic Republic of the Congo. In 1998 his former allies invaded, sparking the Second Congo War. Kabila was assassinated by one of his bodyguards on 16 January 2001 and succeeded by his son Joseph Kabila.
