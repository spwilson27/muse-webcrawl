Raymond A. Moody Jr. (born 30 June 1944) is an American philosopher, psychiatrist and author, best known for his 1975 book Life After Life, in which he coined the term "near-death experience" (NDE). Moody trained in philosophy at the University of Virginia, earning bachelor's, master's and doctoral degrees, and later completed a medical degree and residency in psychiatry.

Work

In Life After Life, Moody collected accounts from more than a hundred people who had been resuscitated after being close to death, identifying common elements such as a sense of peace, a tunnel, an out-of-body perspective, meeting beings of light, and a panoramic life review. The book sold millions of copies and is credited with founding the modern field of near-death studies. Moody emphasised that he was not claiming scientific proof of an afterlife, but presenting the reports for consideration. He later wrote Reflections on Life After Life (1977), The Light Beyond (1988) and Reunions (1993), the latter describing a "psychomanteum" technique intended to evoke visionary encounters with deceased loved ones.

Reception

Moody's work has been influential in popular culture and in hospice and bereavement circles, but has been criticised by some scientists and sceptics, who attribute NDE reports to neurochemical and psychological causes and question his anecdotal methodology. Moody has held academic posts and has been associated with the study of anomalistic psychology and consciousness.
