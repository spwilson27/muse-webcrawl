Baldwin, a pioneer of found-footage cinema

Craig Baldwin (born 1952) is an American experimental filmmaker. He uses found footage from educational films, industrials, newsreels and B-movies, combined with original material, to create dense collage essays on American politics, history and media culture.

Work

Baldwin studied with Bruce Conner at the San Francisco Art Institute. His best-known works include Tribulation 99: Alien Anomalies Under America (1991), a pseudo-documentary montage about US intervention in Latin America narrated in the style of a conspiracy exposé; ¡O No Coronado! (1992); Sonic Outlaws (1995), about the band Negativland and culture jamming; and Spectres of the Spectrum (1999). His feature Mock Up on Mu (2008) blends science fiction, occultism and the history of California aerospace.

Baldwin founded Other Cinema, a long-running microcinema and distribution label in San Francisco's Mission District, which presents experimental film and releases archival oddities on DVD. His practice is closely associated with debates over appropriation, fair use and copyright in collage art.
