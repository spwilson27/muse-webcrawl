Bloom syndrome

Bloom syndrome (BS), also known as Bloom–Torre–Machacek syndrome, is a rare autosomal recessive genetic disorder characterised by short stature, a sun-sensitive red rash on the face, and a greatly increased susceptibility to cancer. It is caused by mutations in the BLM gene, which encodes a RecQ-family DNA helicase involved in maintaining genome stability.

Signs and symptoms

Affected individuals are small at birth and grow slowly, remaining well below average height as adults, usually under 150 cm (4 ft 11 in). A characteristic butterfly-shaped rash (telangiectatic erythema) develops across the cheeks and nose after sun exposure, and may also affect the hands and forearms. Other features include a high-pitched voice, distinctive facial features, frequent infections from mild immune deficiency, feeding difficulties in infancy, and reduced fertility. People with Bloom syndrome develop cancers of many types and at unusually young ages; cancer is the leading cause of death, and average life expectancy is reduced.

Genetics and mechanism

The condition is most common in people of Ashkenazi Jewish ancestry, in whom a single founder mutation accounts for most cases. Loss of BLM helicase function leads to excessive crossing-over between chromosomes — cells show a characteristically high rate of sister chromatid exchange — resulting in genomic instability. Diagnosis is confirmed by this cytogenetic finding or by genetic testing.
