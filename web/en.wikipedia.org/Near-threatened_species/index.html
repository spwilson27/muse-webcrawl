Near-threatened species

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A near-threatened species is a species which has been assessed by the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) as being close to qualifying for, or likely to qualify for, a threatened category (vulnerable, endangered, or critically endangered) in the near future. It is one of the categories of the IUCN Red List, sitting between Least Concern and the threatened categories.

Criteria

A taxon is Near Threatened when it has been evaluated against the Red List criteria but does not qualify for Critically Endangered, Endangered or Vulnerable now, but is close to qualifying or is likely to qualify soon — for example, because its population is declining, its habitat is shrinking, or it depends on conservation measures that, if stopped, would cause it to qualify as threatened within five years. Such taxa previously identified as "conservation dependent" are now included in this category.

Examples

Examples have included the jaguar, the emperor penguin (moved toward this status as sea-ice loss threatens breeding colonies), the narwhal, and various species of bat, shark, and tree. Thousands of species carry the NT assessment, and they are monitored so that deteriorating taxa can be uplisted. See also IUCN Red List and Vulnerable species.
