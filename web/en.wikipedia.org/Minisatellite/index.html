Minisatellite

A minisatellite is a tract of repetitive DNA in which a short sequence motif (typically 10–60 base pairs long) is tandemly repeated many times, forming arrays from about 0.5 to 30 kilobases. Minisatellites are a class of variable number tandem repeats (VNTRs) and are concentrated near the ends of chromosomes, in telomeric regions.

Properties

The number of repeats at a minisatellite locus varies greatly between individuals because unequal crossing over and gene conversion during meiosis change the array length. This hypervariability made minisatellites the basis of DNA fingerprinting, developed by Alec Jeffreys in 1984, which revolutionised forensic science and paternity testing before being largely superseded by microsatellite (STR) profiling.

Biological significance

Certain minisatellites influence gene expression — for example, the repeat upstream of the human insulin gene is associated with susceptibility to type 1 diabetes — and elevated germline mutation at a mouse minisatellite has been used to study the effects of radiation exposure.
