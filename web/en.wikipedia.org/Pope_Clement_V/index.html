Pope Clement V (born Raymond Bertrand de Got, c. 1264 – 20 April 1314) was head of the Catholic Church from 1305 to his death. A Frenchman and former Archbishop of Bordeaux, he is remembered for moving the papal court to Avignon — beginning the Avignon Papacy — and for suppressing the Knights Templar under pressure from King Philip IV of France.

Pontificate

Elected at Perugia after an eleven-month deadlock, Clement never resided in Rome; in 1309 he settled at Avignon, then a papal fief near France. In 1307, at Philip IV's urging, he ordered the arrest of the Templars throughout Christendom, and in 1312, by the bull Vox in excelso, he dissolved the order after the Council of Vienne. He also issued the Clementine Constitutions, an important body of canon law, and founded the universities of Perugia and Orléans.

Death and legend

Clement died in April 1314 at Roquemaure. According to a famous legend, the Templar Grand Master Jacques de Molay, burned at the stake a month earlier, summoned both pope and king to meet him before God's tribunal within a year — both men died within that span, a coincidence that nourished the myth of the Templar curse. Dante placed Clement among the simoniacs in the Inferno.
