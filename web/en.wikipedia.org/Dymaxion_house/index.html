Dymaxion house

The Dymaxion house was a prefabricated housing design developed by American inventor and architect R. Buckminster Fuller. Conceived beginning in the late 1920s and developed as a prototype in the 1940s, it embodied Fuller's "Dymaxion" philosophy of doing more with less.

Design

The house was a circular structure of lightweight aluminium, suspended from a central mast, weighing only about three tons — far less than a conventional house. Rooms were arranged around the mast like slices of pie, and the design incorporated rotating closets, a water-conserving "fogger" shower and other space-age features. It was intended to be mass-produced in aircraft factories, delivered by air and assembled in about two days.

History

Fuller developed the design with Beech Aircraft during the Second World War; only two prototypes were built, in 1946. Production never began owing to financing and management problems. The two prototypes were later combined into one structure, which survives at the Henry Ford Museum in Dearborn, Michigan, following a major restoration completed in 2001.
