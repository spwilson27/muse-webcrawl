Munster

The countryside of County Clare, in the province of Munster

Munster (Irish: Mumhain) is one of the four traditional provinces of Ireland, located in the southwest of the island. It comprises the counties of Clare, Cork, Kerry, Limerick, Tipperary, and Waterford. Munster is the largest of Ireland's provinces by land area, with a population of roughly 1.37 million.

History

In the early centuries AD the region was dominated by the Eóganachta dynasty, whose kings ruled from the Rock of Cashel. In the 10th century the rival Dál gCais, led by Brian Boru, rose to prominence from Thomond. The province later saw the arrival of the Normans, the Desmond Rebellions, and the Munster Plantation of the 16th century. During the Irish War of Independence and Civil War, Munster was a stronghold of anti-Treaty republican forces, briefly styled the "Munster Republic".

Geography

Munster contains Ireland's highest mountain, Carrauntoohil (1,038 m) in County Kerry, and is drained by the River Shannon, Ireland's longest river. Major cities include Cork, Limerick, and Waterford.

Culture

The province is noted for traditional Irish music and sport. Munster Rugby is one of the four professional provincial teams, and the province has a strong tradition in Gaelic games, particularly hurling. Parts of west Munster form Gaeltacht areas where Irish is spoken daily.
