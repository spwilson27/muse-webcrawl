Formed: 3 April 1995
Headquarters: Lubyanka Building, Moscow
Predecessor: FSK; ultimately the KGB

The Lubyanka Building in Moscow, headquarters of the FSB and formerly of the KGB.

The Federal Security Service of the Russian Federation (FSB; Russian: Federalnaya sluzhba bezopasnosti) is the principal security and counterintelligence agency of Russia and the main successor agency to the Soviet Union's KGB. Established in its present form in 1995, the FSB is responsible for counterintelligence, internal security, counterterrorism, border security, and surveillance within Russia. It is headquartered in the Lubyanka Building in Moscow, the former headquarters of the KGB, and reports directly to the President of Russia.

History

After the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991, the KGB was broken up; its foreign intelligence arm became the SVR, while its domestic functions passed through a succession of bodies — the Ministry of Security and then the Federal Counterintelligence Service (FSK) — before President Boris Yeltsin reorganised the FSK as the FSB in 1995, expanding its staff and powers. Vladimir Putin, himself a former KGB officer, directed the FSB in 1998–1999 before becoming prime minister and president; under his presidency the service's influence within the Russian state grew markedly, and FSB veterans (the siloviki) came to occupy many senior government posts.

Roles and powers

The FSB combines functions comparable to those of the FBI, domestic intelligence services, and border guards in Western states. It conducts counterintelligence against foreign spies, investigates terrorism, organised crime, and treason cases, operates the border guard service, protects state secrets, and runs extensive electronic surveillance under the SORM system. Critics, including Russian opposition figures and Western governments, accuse the FSB of suppressing dissent, persecuting independent journalists and religious minorities, and involvement in poisonings of exiles and opposition leaders — allegations the Russian government denies.

Structure

The service is headed by a Director appointed by the President; Alexander Bortnikov has held the post since 2008. Its departments cover counterintelligence, counterterrorism (including the elite Alpha and Vympel special units), military counterintelligence, economic security, and border service. Personnel numbers are classified but are generally estimated in the hundreds of thousands including border troops.
