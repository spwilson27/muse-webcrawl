Mozarabic rite
Also calledHispanic rite, Visigothic rite, Rite of Toledo
OriginVisigothic Iberia, by the 6th–7th century
Surviving useCorpus Christi Chapel, Toledo Cathedral

The Mozarabic rite, also called the Hispanic rite, is a liturgical rite of the Latin Church once used generally throughout Visigothic Hispania and later preserved by the Mozarabs — Christians living under Muslim rule in al-Andalus.

History

The rite developed in late antiquity and was codified at councils of Toledo. After the Christian reconquest, the Roman rite was imposed in Castile and Aragon from the 11th century (following the Council of Burgos in 1080), but six parishes in Toledo retained the old rite by privilege. Cardinal Cisneros revived and printed its books around 1500 and founded the Mozarabic Chapel in Toledo Cathedral, where the rite is still celebrated daily.

Characteristics

The Hispanic liturgy differs from the Roman in its prayers, calendar of saints, and structure of the Mass, with distinctive features such as the illatio (preface), the placing of the kiss of peace before the Canon, and nine prayers in the eucharistic celebration. Its chant, known as Mozarabic chant, survives in notation that is only partially decipherable.
