Sots Art

MovementConceptual art, postmodernism
OriginSoviet Union, 1972
FoundersVitaly Komar and Alexander Melamid
RelatedSocialist realism, Pop art, Moscow Conceptualism

Sots Art (Russian: Соц-арт) is a style of Soviet conceptual and postmodern art that emerged in the early 1970s. The term, coined by the artists Vitaly Komar and Alexander Melamid in 1972, fuses "Sots" (from sotsialisticheskiy, socialist) with "art", consciously echoing American Pop art.

Description

Where Pop art ironically recycled the imagery of consumer capitalism, Sots Art appropriated the over-saturated visual language of Soviet ideology — heroic posters, slogans, portraits of leaders, and Socialist Realist clichés — and pushed it to absurdity. The result was at once parody and earnest engagement with the only "mass culture" the USSR possessed.

Artists and works

Besides Komar and Melamid, key figures include Erik Bulatov, whose canvases overlay monumental slogans on sky and landscape; Ilya Kabakov; Leonid Sokov; and Alexander Kosolapov, whose juxtapositions of Lenin with Western brand logos epitomise the genre. Barred from official exhibition, Sots Art circulated in unofficial apartment shows until perestroika.

Legacy

After 1991 the style influenced China's Political Pop, and its works entered major Western collections as documents of late-Soviet visual culture.
