Battle of Djerba

The Battle of Djerba was a naval battle fought in May 1560 near the island of Djerba off the coast of Tunisia, in which an Ottoman fleet commanded by Piyale Pasha overwhelmingly defeated a Christian fleet assembled by Spain and its Italian allies. It was one of the greatest Ottoman naval victories of the sixteenth century.

Background

Spain's Philip II organised a large expedition — some 90–120 vessels from Spain, Genoa, Naples, Sicily, Malta and others — to recapture Tripoli from the Ottomans. The force landed on Djerba in March 1560 and built a fort, but delays and disease weakened it. In May, Piyale Pasha arrived from Istanbul with unexpected speed with a fleet of about 120 galleys.

The battle

Caught by surprise, the Christian fleet attempted to escape but was overwhelmed on 14 May 1560. Roughly half the fleet was sunk or captured and thousands of men were killed or enslaved. The surviving garrison of the Djerba fort held out until July before surrendering; its commanders were executed and, according to tradition, the Ottomans built a pyramid of the defenders' skulls that stood on the island until the nineteenth century.

Consequences

The disaster stripped Spain and its allies of experienced crews and galleys they could not quickly replace, marking the peak of Ottoman naval dominance in the Mediterranean. The balance only began to shift with the Siege of Malta in 1565 and the Battle of Lepanto in 1571.
