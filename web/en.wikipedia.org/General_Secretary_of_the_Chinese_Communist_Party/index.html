The General Secretary of the Central Committee of the Chinese Communist Party is the highest-ranking official of the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) and the de facto paramount leader of the People's Republic of China. The officeholder chairs the Politburo Standing Committee, the party's top decision-making body, and usually also serves as Chairman of the Central Military Commission and as President of the People's Republic of China.

History

The early CCP was led by a Secretary of the Central Bureau, first Chen Duxiu from 1921. For much of the Mao Zedong era the senior party post was the Chairman of the Central Committee, held by Mao from 1945 until his death in 1976, with the General Secretary a subordinate administrative role (held notably by Deng Xiaoping in the 1950s). After Hua Guofeng, the chairmanship was abolished in 1982 and the General Secretary became the party's top office. Holders since then have been Hu Yaobang, Zhao Ziyang, Jiang Zemin, Hu Jintao and Xi Jinping.

Powers

Formally elected by the Central Committee, the General Secretary presides over the Politburo and its Standing Committee, directs the work of the Secretariat, and speaks for the party. In practice the holder, especially when also commanding the military through the Central Military Commission, is the most powerful figure in China's political system. Xi Jinping additionally took the title of "core leader" and, after the abolition of presidential term limits in 2018, began a third term in 2022 — unprecedented since Mao.
