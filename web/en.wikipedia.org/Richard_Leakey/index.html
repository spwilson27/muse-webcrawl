Richard Leakey

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Richard Erskine Frere Leakey (19 December 1944 – 2 January 2022) was a Kenyan paleoanthropologist, conservationist, and politician. The second son of the archaeologists Louis and Mary Leakey, he directed expeditions around Lake Turkana in northern Kenya that produced major fossil discoveries of early humans, and later led Kenya's wildlife conservation efforts as head of the Kenya Wildlife Service.

Paleoanthropology

Leakey had no formal university degree but learned the family trade on expeditions to Olduvai Gorge. In 1968 he led an expedition to the Koobi Fora region east of Lake Turkana, where his teams found thousands of fossils. In 1972 his team discovered the skull KNM-ER 1470, attributed to Homo habilis (later Homo rudolfensis). In 1984 Kamoya Kimeu, a member of his team, found the nearly complete skeleton of a Homo erectus youth known as Turkana Boy (KNM-WT 15000), the most complete early human skeleton ever discovered.

Conservation and politics

Appointed director of the Kenya Wildlife Service in 1989, Leakey mounted an aggressive campaign against elephant poaching, raising international funds and presiding over the public burning of a stockpile of confiscated ivory in Nairobi National Park. He survived a 1993 plane crash in which he lost both legs below the knee. He co-founded the Safina Party in 1995, served as head of Kenya's civil service (1999–2001), and in 2015 was appointed chairman of the Kenya Wildlife Service board. He co-founded the Turkana Basin Institute and the conservation organization WildlifeDirect.
