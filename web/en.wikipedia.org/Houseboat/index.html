Houseboat

A houseboat is a boat designed or modified to be used primarily as a dwelling. Houseboats range from converted barges and canal narrowboats to purpose-built floating homes and the ornate kettuvallam of Kerala and the shikara-linked houseboats of Kashmir's Dal Lake.

Residential houseboats line canals in Amsterdam and many other cities.

Types and regions

In the Netherlands some 10,000 houseboats are moored in canals, many of them converted freight barges (spits). Britain's canal network hosts thousands of narrowboats used as homes. In the United States, floating homes cluster in places such as Sausalito, California, and Seattle's Lake Union, while recreational houseboats — rented for holidays — are common on Lake Powell and the Murray River in Australia.

Kashmir and Kerala

The stationary cedar houseboats of Dal Lake in Srinagar, built during the British Raj when foreigners could not own land, are famed for carved woodwork and are now largely tourist accommodation. In Kerala, kettuvallam — traditional rice barges with thatched roofs — serve as cruising houseboats on the backwaters.

Regulation and living

Houseboat living raises distinctive issues: mooring rights and fees, connection to water, sewerage and electricity, hull maintenance and insurance, and a legal status that may fall between vessel and dwelling. Advocates cite lower costs and waterside settings; critics point to environmental concerns about waste discharge.
