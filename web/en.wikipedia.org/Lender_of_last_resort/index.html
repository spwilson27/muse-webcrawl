Lender of last resort

A lender of last resort is an institution, usually a country's central bank, that offers loans to banks or other financial institutions that are experiencing difficulty and cannot obtain funds elsewhere, in order to prevent a liquidity crisis from spreading through the financial system.

Doctrine

The classical doctrine, articulated by Walter Bagehot in Lombard Street (1873), holds that the lender of last resort should lend freely, at a penalty rate, against good collateral, to solvent but illiquid institutions. Debate continues over moral hazard — that such support may encourage excessive risk-taking — and over how far the function extends to non-bank institutions and foreign-currency crises, where international bodies such as the IMF may play a related role.
