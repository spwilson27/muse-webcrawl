Intermediate cartridge

An intermediate cartridge is a rifle and carbine cartridge that has a muzzle energy between that of a full-power rifle cartridge and a pistol cartridge. Such rounds made the assault rifle possible, providing controllable automatic fire out to a few hundred metres.

History

Development began before and during the Second World War, when analysis showed most infantry combat occurred under 400 metres. Germany's 7.92×33mm Kurz powered the StG 44, and the Soviet 7.62×39mm followed. NATO later standardised the 5.56×45mm, while the Soviet Union adopted the 5.45×39mm.

Characteristics

Intermediate cartridges are lighter than full-power rounds, letting soldiers carry more ammunition, and produce less recoil, improving controllability in automatic fire — at the cost of reduced long-range performance.
