Engaged column

An engaged column is a column embedded in a wall and partly projecting from its surface. Unlike a freestanding column, it is partly built into the wall itself, and unlike a pilaster, it is round in plan and projects by roughly half its diameter or more.

History and use

Engaged columns appear in ancient Egyptian and Roman architecture and were widely used in Romanesque and Gothic churches, where clustered engaged columns (demicolumns and shaft bundles) articulate piers and carry vault ribs. In classical architecture they give a wall the rhythm of a colonnade while adding little or no structural support; their role is mainly decorative and proportional.
