Dolby Vision

Dolby Vision
Developer: Dolby Laboratories
Type: High dynamic range video format
Introduced: 2014

Dolby Vision is a set of technologies developed by Dolby Laboratories for high dynamic range (HDR) video. It covers content creation, distribution, and playback, and includes dynamic metadata that define the aspect ratio and adjust the picture based on a display's capabilities on a per-shot or even per-frame basis, in a way standard HDR formats such as HDR10 cannot.

Technical details

Dolby Vision supports a maximum resolution of 8K, a peak brightness of up to 10,000 nits, and a color depth of up to 12 bits per channel. In practice, consumer content is typically mastered at up to 4,000 nits. Unlike HDR10, which uses static metadata set once for an entire program, Dolby Vision carries dynamic metadata describing optimal tone mapping for each scene.

Licensing and adoption

Dolby Vision is a proprietary format: content creators and device manufacturers pay licensing fees to Dolby. It competes with the royalty-free HDR10+ format developed by Samsung and others. The format is supported by major streaming services including Netflix and Disney+, and by televisions from LG, Sony, TCL, and other manufacturers. Apple devices such as the iPhone 12 and later can record video in Dolby Vision.
