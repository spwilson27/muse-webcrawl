Chile

Chile, officially the Republic of Chile, is a country in western South America. It occupies a long, narrow strip of land between the Andes mountains and the Pacific Ocean, stretching about 4,300 km (2,670 mi) north to south but averaging only about 177 km (110 mi) in width. Chile borders Peru, Bolivia, and Argentina, and its capital and largest city is Santiago. Its population is about 19 million.

Geography and climate

Chile's territory spans dramatic extremes: the Atacama Desert in the north, among the driest places on Earth and a premier site for astronomical observatories; a Mediterranean central valley where most Chileans live; the lakes and rainforests of the south; and the fjords and glaciers of Patagonia. Chile also claims Easter Island and Juan Fernández Islands in the Pacific.

History

Indigenous peoples, including the Mapuche, who long resisted Inca and Spanish expansion, inhabited the region before colonization. Chile declared independence from Spain in 1818 under Bernardo O'Higgins and José de San Martín. In the War of the Pacific (1879–1884) Chile gained nitrate-rich northern territories from Peru and Bolivia. The 20th century saw the election of socialist president Salvador Allende in 1970, his overthrow in the 1973 military coup led by Augusto Pinochet, and a return to democracy following the 1988 plebiscite.

Economy

Chile is the world's largest copper producer and a major exporter of lithium, wine, fruit, and salmon. It is widely regarded as one of Latin America's most prosperous and open economies, though inequality remains a central political issue, visible in the 2019 social protests and subsequent (ultimately rejected) constitutional processes.
