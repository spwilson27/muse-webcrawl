FamilyNorthwest Caucasian (Abkhaz–Adyghe)
SpeakersRoughly 500,000–600,000 worldwide

Adyghe (Адыгабзэ, Adygabze), also known as West Circassian, is a Northwest Caucasian language spoken by the Adyghe people in the North Caucasus and by a large Circassian diaspora, principally in Turkey, Jordan, Syria and Israel. Together with Kabardian (East Circassian) it forms the Circassian branch of the family. Adyghe is an official language of the Republic of Adygea in Russia, alongside Russian.

Phonology and grammar

Adyghe is famous for a very large consonant inventory — around 50 to 60 consonants in the literary dialect, including ejectives, labialised and palatalised series — but only a small vowel system, usually analysed as three vowels. The language is ergative and strongly agglutinative, with polypersonal verbs that can agree with subject, object and indirect object, and word order is typically subject–object–verb.

Dialects and writing

Major dialects include Temirgoy (the basis of the literary standard), Abdzakh, Bzhedug and Shapsug. Adyghe was written with Arabic script before the 1920s, then with a Latin alphabet, and since 1938 with a Cyrillic-based alphabet. In Turkey, diaspora communities increasingly use a Latin-based orthography.
