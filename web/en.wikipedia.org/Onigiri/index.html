Onigiri

Onigiri

Japanese: おにぎり

Also called: Omusubi

Origin: Japan

Main ingredients: Rice, nori, filling (e.g. umeboshi, salmon, tuna mayonnaise)

Onigiri (おにぎり), also called omusubi (おむすび), is a Japanese food made from white rice shaped into triangular or cylindrical forms and often wrapped in nori (seaweed). Traditionally, an onigiri is filled with pickled ume (umeboshi), salted salmon, katsuobushi (bonito flakes), kombu, tarako (salted roe), or any other salty or sour ingredient used as a natural preservative. Onigiri is a quintessential item of Japanese convenience stores, bento lunches, and picnics.

History

The origins of onigiri reach back more than a thousand years. In the eleventh-century diary of Murasaki Shikibu, author of The Tale of Genji, there is mention of rice balls eaten at outdoor picnics. Samurai carried rice balls wrapped in bamboo sheaths as quick meals on the battlefield, and farmers took them to the fields. The triangular shape is said to imitate mountains, which were objects of worship. In the Edo period, onigiri spread as a portable food for travellers.

Forms and fillings

Onigiri are shaped by hand, lightly salted, with the filling placed in the centre. Common shapes are triangular, round, cylindrical, and the tawara (rice-bale) shape. Popular modern fillings include:

  • Umeboshi (pickled plum)
  • Shake (salted grilled salmon)
  • Tuna mayonnaise
  • Okaka (bonito flakes with soy sauce)
  • Kombu (seasoned kelp)
  • Mentaiko (spicy cod roe)

The nori wrapping both keeps the rice from sticking to the hand and adds flavour; convenience-store onigiri use a special packaging that keeps the nori separate from the rice until the moment of eating, so it stays crisp.

Onigiri and sushi

Although it is sometimes mistaken for a type of sushi, onigiri is distinct: sushi rice is seasoned with vinegar, sugar, and salt, whereas onigiri rice is ordinarily plain or lightly salted. Onigiri also has a much older history than most forms of sushi.
