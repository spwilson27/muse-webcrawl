Bihar

Country: India
Capital: Patna
Population: about 104 million (2011), third most populous Indian state

Bihar is a state in eastern India, bordered by Nepal to the north and by the Indian states of Uttar Pradesh, Jharkhand, and West Bengal. Its capital and largest city is Patna. The name derives from the Sanskrit vihara, meaning monastery, reflecting the region's many Buddhist monasteries in ancient times.

History

Bihar was a cradle of early Indian civilisation: the Magadha kingdom and the Maurya and Gupta empires were centred at Pataliputra (modern Patna). It is sacred to several religions — the Buddha attained enlightenment at Bodh Gaya, and Mahavira, the 24th Jain Tirthankara, was born and died in the region. The ancient university of Nalanda drew scholars from across Asia. In 2000 the southern part of Bihar became the separate state of Jharkhand.

Geography and economy

The fertile Gangetic plain is split by the Ganges river into northern and southern Bihar and is intensively farmed, though prone to flooding from Himalayan tributaries. Bihar is among India's least urbanised and poorest states, with a large share of its workforce migrating to other states; in recent years it has recorded rapid economic growth from a low base.

Languages

Hindi is the official language; Urdu has second official status, and regional languages include Maithili, Magahi, and Bhojpuri.
