Washington (state)

Capital: Olympia
Largest city: Seattle
Admitted: November 11, 1889 (42nd state)
Population: approx. 7.8 million

Washington is a state in the Pacific Northwest region of the United States, bordered by Oregon to the south, Idaho to the east, the Canadian province of British Columbia to the north, and the Pacific Ocean to the west. Named for George Washington, it is the only U.S. state named after a president. Its capital is Olympia and its largest city is Seattle.

Geography

Washington is divided by the Cascade Range into a rainy western lowland and a drier eastern plateau. It contains several prominent volcanoes, including Mount Rainier, the state's highest point at 14,411 feet (4,392 m), and Mount St. Helens, which erupted catastrophically in 1980. Puget Sound, a complex inlet of the Pacific, dominates the northwest.

Economy

The state has major industries in aerospace (Boeing), technology (Microsoft and Amazon are headquartered in the Seattle area), agriculture — it leads the nation in apple production — timber, and hydroelectric power from dams on the Columbia River.

History

Indigenous peoples inhabited the region for thousands of years before European exploration. Washington Territory was created in 1853 from the Oregon Territory, and statehood followed in 1889.
