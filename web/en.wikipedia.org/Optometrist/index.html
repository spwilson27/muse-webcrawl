Optometrist

An optometrist is a healthcare professional who provides primary eye and vision care, including eye examinations, detection and management of eye diseases, and the prescription and fitting of corrective lenses and contact lenses. In many countries optometrists hold a Doctor of Optometry (OD) degree or equivalent professional qualification.

OccupationEye care professional
QualificationDoctor of Optometry (OD) or BSc Optometry
DutiesRefraction, eye health screening, prescribing lenses

Role

Optometrists measure refractive errors (myopia, hyperopia, astigmatism and presbyopia) and prescribe spectacles and contact lenses. They screen for conditions such as glaucoma, cataract, diabetic retinopathy and macular degeneration, referring patients to ophthalmologists for surgery or specialist treatment. Depending on jurisdiction, they may prescribe therapeutic drugs for certain eye conditions.

Training

In the United States and Canada, optometry requires a four-year doctoral programme following undergraduate study. In the United Kingdom, optometrists complete an accredited degree and a supervised pre-registration year before registering with the General Optical Council. Scope of practice varies widely between countries.

Related professions

Optometrists work alongside ophthalmologists (medical doctors specialising in eyes), orthoptists, and dispensing opticians, who fit and supply spectacles from a prescription.
