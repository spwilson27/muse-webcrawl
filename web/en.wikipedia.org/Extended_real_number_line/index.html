Extended real number line

The extended real number line, or affinely extended real number system, is obtained from the real numbers ℝ by adding two elements, +∞ and −∞, which are greater and less than every real number respectively. The resulting system, written [−∞, +∞], is fundamental in measure theory and integration, where it allows functions and measures to take infinite values in a controlled way.

Arithmetic

Many ordinary arithmetic rules extend naturally: a + ∞ = +∞ for finite a, a · (+∞) = +∞ for positive a, and a/∞ = 0. Other expressions — ∞ − ∞, 0 · ∞, ∞/∞ and 1∞ — are left undefined as indeterminate forms. The extended line is not a field, but it is a complete lattice: every subset has a supremum and an infimum, which makes it convenient for stating results about limits and integrals.

Topology and analysis

With the order topology, [−∞, +∞] is homeomorphic to the closed interval [0, 1], and convergence to ±∞ is expressed in the familiar language of limits "tending to infinity". In measure theory, measurable functions are commonly allowed to take the value +∞, and measures of sets may be infinite; the convention 0 · ∞ = 0 is usually adopted in that context, in contrast to the indeterminate treatment in calculus.

Related constructions

The projectively extended real line adds a single unsigned infinity, closing the line into a circle, while the extended complex plane (the Riemann sphere) adds one point at infinity to the complex numbers.
