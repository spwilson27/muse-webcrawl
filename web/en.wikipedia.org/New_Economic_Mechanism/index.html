New Economic Mechanism

The New Economic Mechanism (NEM) was a major economic reform launched in Hungary on 1 January 1968, under the leadership of János Kádár. It introduced elements of market socialism into the centrally planned economy and formed the economic basis of so-called "goulash communism".

Features

Mandatory plan indicators for enterprises were abolished and replaced by indirect regulators — taxes, credit and partially freed prices. Firms gained freedom to decide output, investment and some foreign trade, and small private businesses were tolerated.

Course and legacy

The reform improved living standards and consumer supply through the 1970s but was repeatedly diluted under pressure from conservatives and the Soviet Union; external debt and inefficiency grew in the 1980s, yet the NEM's experiments eased Hungary's transition to a market economy after 1989.
