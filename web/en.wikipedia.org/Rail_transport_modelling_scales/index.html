Rail transport modelling scales

Rail transport modelling scales are standardized proportions used in model railroading, relating the size of the model to the full-size (prototype) railway. Each scale is paired with one or more track gauges representing standard, narrow, or broad gauge prototypes. Standards are maintained by bodies such as the NMRA in North America and MOROP (NEM standards) in Europe.

Gauges and narrow gauge

Within a scale, narrower track gauges model narrow-gauge prototypes: for example, HOn3 represents 3 ft gauge in HO scale, and On30 models 30-inch gauge in O scale using HO track. The distinction between scale (size ratio) and gauge (distance between rails) is fundamental to the hobby's terminology.

History

Standardized scales emerged in the early 20th century as manufacturers such as Märklin and Hornby rationalized their ranges. The "gauge" designations (0, 1, 2, 3) gradually gave way to letter scales as smaller sizes became feasible with improved electric motors.
