The Metropolitan Police Service (MPS or "the Met") is the territorial police force responsible for law enforcement and crime prevention in Greater London, excluding the square mile of the City of London, which has its own force. Founded in 1829, it is the largest police service in the United Kingdom and one of the largest in the world, and it also carries national responsibilities, including coordinating counter-terrorism policing and protecting the Royal Family and senior government figures.

History

The force was created by Home Secretary Sir Robert Peel under the Metropolitan Police Act 1829 — hence the nicknames "bobbies" and (more pejoratively) "peelers" for police constables. Its first headquarters at Whitehall Place backed onto Great Scotland Yard, giving "Scotland Yard" its enduring name. Peel's principles emphasised prevention, public approval and minimal force — "the police are the public and the public are the police". Early constables wore blue (to distinguish them from red-coated soldiers), carried truncheons and rattles, and were initially unpopular, but professional policing gradually replaced the old watch system and Bow Street Runners.

Organisation

The Met is headed by the Commissioner, appointed by the Crown on the advice of the Home Secretary, and is overseen by the Mayor's Office for Policing and Crime. London's boroughs are grouped into Basic Command Units for neighbourhood policing, while specialist commands handle homicide (made famous by "Murder Squad" detectives), firearms (SCO19), roads, aviation security, and the Parliamentary and Diplomatic Protection command. Famous headquarters buildings include the rotating New Scotland Yard sign on Broadway and, since 2016, offices on the Victoria Embankment.

Reputation and controversy

The Met's detectives at Scotland Yard are a fixture of world fiction, from Sherlock Holmes stories onward. In recent decades the force has faced major controversies, including the Stephen Lawrence inquiry's finding of institutional racism (1999), the phone-hacking scandal, and the 2023 Casey Review, which found the Met institutionally racist, misogynistic and homophobic after the murder of Sarah Everard by a serving officer — findings the Commissioner accepted while pledging reform.
