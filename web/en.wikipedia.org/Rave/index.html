A rave is a dance party at a warehouse, club, public or private property, typically featuring performances by DJs and sometimes live performers playing electronic dance music. The style is associated with the acid house scene that exploded in Britain in the late 1980s, building on the house and techno music developed in Chicago and Detroit. Raves are known for all-night dancing, powerful sound systems, laser light shows and visual effects, and a distinctive culture of inclusiveness summed up in the acronym PLUR (Peace, Love, Unity, and Respect).

History

The word "rave" was first used in 1960s London for wild bohemian parties, and reggae sound-system culture and Northern soul all-nighters laid groundwork in Britain. The modern rave era began in the "Second Summer of Love" (1988–89), when acid house and the drug MDMA (ecstasy) spread from clubs such as Shoom and Trip to huge unlicensed orbital raves around London's M25 motorway, drawing tens of thousands. Organisers publicised venues by pirate radio and telephone hotlines to evade police. Moral panic in the press led to the UK's Criminal Justice and Public Order Act 1994, which famously targeted gatherings playing music "characterised by the emission of a succession of repetitive beats". The scene then moved into licensed clubs and festivals, and spread to the United States, continental Europe (notably Berlin techno and Dutch gabber), and worldwide — mutating into the commercial EDM festival culture of the 2000s–2010s, with mega-events such as Electric Daisy Carnival and Tomorrowland.

Music and culture

Rave music emphasises rhythm and texture over song structure: acid house's squelching Roland TB-303 basslines, Detroit techno's futurism, breakbeat hardcore and its descendants jungle and drum and bass, trance's euphoric build-ups, and hard, fast styles like gabber and happy hardcore. DJs mix records seamlessly into continuous sets. Fashion has run from smiley-face T-shirts, baggy clothes, whistles, and glow sticks to today's festival wear; participants trade beaded "kandi" bracelets in North American PLUR culture. Harm-reduction organisations such as DanceSafe promote drug checking, hydration, and rest, while deaths linked to adulterated pills and overheating have repeatedly brought regulation and controversy.
