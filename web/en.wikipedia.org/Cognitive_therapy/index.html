Cognitive therapy

Cognitive therapy (CT) is a form of psychotherapy developed by American psychiatrist Aaron T. Beck in the 1960s. It is based on the cognitive model: the idea that how a person perceives a situation influences their emotional and behavioural reactions more than the situation itself, and that psychological disorders involve distorted or unhelpful thinking.

Method

Cognitive therapy is structured, time-limited and collaborative. Therapist and client identify automatic thoughts and underlying beliefs (the "cognitive triad" of negative views of oneself, the world and the future in depression), test them against evidence, and practise alternative responses through techniques such as thought records, behavioural experiments and activity scheduling. Beck's approach was developed first for depression and later extended to anxiety disorders, personality disorders, substance use and other conditions.

Legacy

Cognitive therapy merged with behaviour therapy to form cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT), now among the most researched and widely practised forms of psychotherapy, with an extensive evidence base from randomised controlled trials. Beck founded the Beck Institute for Cognitive Behavior Therapy in 1994.
