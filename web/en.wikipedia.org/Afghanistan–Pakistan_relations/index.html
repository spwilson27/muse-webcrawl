Afghanistan–Pakistan relations


The Durand Line forms the disputed international border

Afghanistan–Pakistan relations concern the bilateral relationship between the Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan and the Islamic Republic of Pakistan. Relations have historically been strained by the dispute over the Durand Line, the 2,640-kilometre (1,640-mile) border drawn in 1893, which Afghanistan has never formally recognised and which divides ethnic Pashtun and Baloch populations.

History

Afghanistan was the only country to vote against Pakistan's admission to the United Nations in 1947, over the Pashtunistan question. During the Soviet–Afghan War, Pakistan hosted millions of Afghan refugees and, together with the United States and Saudi Arabia, funnelled support to the mujahideen. In the 1990s Pakistan backed the Taliban, while Afghanistan's Northern Alliance was supported by Iran, India and Russia. After 2001, relations were complicated by accusations that Pakistan sheltered Taliban and Haqqani network militants, and by cross-border militancy affecting both countries.

Contemporary issues

Following the Taliban takeover of Kabul in August 2021, tensions persisted over the Tehrik-i-Taliban Pakistan (TTP) insurgency, border fencing, trade disputes and the deportation of Afghan refugees from Pakistan beginning in 2023. Periodic border clashes have occurred at crossings such as Torkham and Chaman.
