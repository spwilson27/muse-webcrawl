SQ3R

TypeReading comprehension method
Introduced byFrancis P. Robinson (1946)
StepsSurvey, Question, Read, Recite, Review

SQ3R is a reading comprehension method intended to improve the understanding and retention of textbook material. The name is an acronym of its five steps: Survey, Question, Read, Recite and Review. It was introduced by the American education philosopher Francis P. Robinson in his 1946 book Effective Study.

The five steps

Survey: skim the chapter to gain an overview — headings, summaries, illustrations and questions. Question: turn headings into questions to be answered, creating a purpose for reading. Read: read the section actively, looking for answers to those questions. Recite: after each section, look away and restate the main points and answers in one's own words, noting them down. Review: after finishing, revisit the material, checking one's notes and re-answering the questions, ideally repeatedly over subsequent days.

Variants and reception

Numerous adaptations exist, such as SQ4R (adding "Reflect" or "Record") and PQRST. The method is widely taught in study-skills courses; its components align with later research on retrieval practice and spaced review, although controlled studies of the complete package are limited.
