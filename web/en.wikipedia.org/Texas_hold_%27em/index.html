Texas hold 'em

Texas hold 'em (also known as Texas holdem, hold 'em, and holdem) is one of the most popular variants of the card game of poker. Two cards, known as hole cards, are dealt face down to each player, and then five community cards are dealt face up in three stages. The stages consist of a series of three cards ("the flop"), later an additional single card ("the turn" or "fourth street"), and a final card ("the river" or "fifth street"). Each player seeks the best five-card poker hand from any combination of the seven cards: the five community cards and their two hole cards. Players have betting options to check, call, raise, or fold, in rounds before the flop and after each subsequent deal.

History

Although little is known about the invention of Texas hold 'em, the Texas Legislature officially recognizes Robstown, Texas, as the game's birthplace, dating its origin to the early 1900s. The game was brought to Las Vegas in 1963 by Corky McCorquodale and popularised by players such as Doyle Brunson. The World Series of Poker, founded in 1970, adopted no-limit hold 'em for its Main Event, and televised poker in the early 2000s — aided by the hole-card camera and Chris Moneymaker's 2003 Main Event win — triggered the "poker boom".

Rules

  1. The two players to the left of the dealer button post forced bets (the small blind and big blind).
  2. Each player is dealt two hole cards face down.
  3. A round of betting occurs, beginning with the player after the big blind.
  4. The dealer deals the three-card flop, followed by another betting round.
  5. A single turn card and then a river card are dealt, each followed by betting.
  6. Remaining players show down their cards; the best five-card hand wins the pot. Suits have no relative rank.

Strategy and study

Hold 'em strategy rests on probability, position, pot odds and reading opponents. Game-theory-optimal (GTO) play and solver software have reshaped professional strategy since the 2010s. In 2017 the heads-up no-limit variant was essentially solved in a weak sense by the DeepStack and Libratus computer programs defeating professional players.
