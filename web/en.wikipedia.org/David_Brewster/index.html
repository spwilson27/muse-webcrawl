Sir David Brewster KH PRSE FRS (11 December 1781 – 10 February 1868) was a Scottish scientist, inventor, author, and academic administrator. In his early career he worked as a Presbyterian clergyman, but devoted himself to natural philosophy and became a prominent figure in British science, working principally in the field of physical optics.

Brewster is remembered chiefly for his experimental work in physical optics, including the discovery of Brewster's angle, the description of the biaxial character of certain crystals, and studies of the polarization of light and photoelasticity. He invented the kaleidoscope and the binocular prism camera, and made important improvements to the stereoscope and to lighthouse lenses.

Life and career

Brewster was born in the Canongate, Jedburgh, in the Scottish Borders, the son of a rector of the grammar school. He was destined for the church and entered the University of Edinburgh at the age of twelve, graduating with an MA in 1800. He was licensed as a preacher in 1804 but never took a charge, turning instead to science and writing.

From 1808 he edited the Edinburgh Encyclopaedia, and he founded or edited several scientific journals. In 1819 he co-founded the Edinburgh Philosophical Journal with Robert Jameson. Brewster was a founder of the British Association for the Advancement of Science in 1831 and served as its president. He was knighted in 1831, became principal of the United College at the University of St Andrews in 1838, and in 1859 became principal and vice-chancellor of the University of Edinburgh.

Optical work

Brewster's best-known discovery is the relation now called Brewster's angle: when light strikes a transparent surface at a particular angle, the reflected beam is completely plane-polarized, and the tangent of that angle equals the refractive index of the medium. He also discovered the laws governing the optical properties of biaxial crystals and investigated the temporary double refraction produced in isotropic materials under mechanical strain, laying the foundations of photoelasticity.

Inventions

In 1816 Brewster patented the kaleidoscope, an optical toy that became an enormous popular success, though he derived little financial benefit because of piracy of the design. He also improved the lenticular stereoscope, proposed the binocular camera, and worked on improvements to Fresnel lenses for lighthouses.
