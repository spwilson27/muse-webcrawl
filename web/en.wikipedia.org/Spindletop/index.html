Spindletop

Spindletop is an oil field located in the southern portion of Beaumont, Texas, in the United States. The Spindletop dome was derived from the Louann Salt evaporite layer of the Jurassic geologic period. On January 10, 1901, a well at Spindletop struck oil, creating a "gusher" that launched the modern petroleum industry.

The Lucas gusher

The well drilled by the Guffey Petroleum Company under the supervision of Anthony F. Lucas blew out and produced an estimated 100,000 barrels of oil per day, more than all the other wells in the United States combined at the time. The fountain flowed for nine days before it was capped. The discovery tripled United States oil production overnight and launched the Texas oil boom.

Legacy

Spindletop spurred the founding or growth of companies including Gulf Oil, Texaco, and Humble Oil (later Exxon). Production peaked in 1927, and renewed drilling with better technology found deeper oil in the 1950s. The site is a National Historic Landmark, and a replica boomtown museum stands near the original location.
