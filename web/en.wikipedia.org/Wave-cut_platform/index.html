Wave-cut platform

A wave-cut platform, also called a shore platform or abrasion platform, is a gently sloping, flat rock surface at the base of a sea cliff, formed by erosion from waves. Platforms typically slope seaward at less than a few degrees and are exposed at low tide, often covered by rock pools and marine algae.

Formation

As waves attack the base of a cliff they cut a wave-cut notch through hydraulic action and abrasion. The undercut cliff eventually collapses, and repeated collapse causes the cliff to retreat inland while the platform widens at its base. Because wave energy decreases across a wider platform, platform growth slows over time; widths are commonly limited to a few hundred metres. Platform development depends on rock type, tidal range, and wave energy, and prominent examples occur along the coasts of Britain, Australia, and New Zealand.

Significance

Raised wave-cut platforms are used by geologists as markers of past sea levels, helping reconstruct coastal uplift and sea-level change over time.
