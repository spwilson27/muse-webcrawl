Sildenafil

Sildenafil, sold under the brand name Viagra among others, is a medication used to treat erectile dysfunction and pulmonary arterial hypertension. It is also sometimes used off-label for other conditions. It is taken orally; onset is typically within 20 minutes and duration is around two hours.

Medical uses

Sildenafil is a phosphodiesterase type 5 (PDE5) inhibitor. By blocking PDE5, it increases the effect of nitric oxide on cyclic GMP in the corpus cavernosum of the penis, increasing blood flow in the presence of sexual stimulation. For pulmonary arterial hypertension it is sold as Revatio and relaxes blood vessels in the lungs. Common side effects include headache, flushing, indigestion, nasal congestion, and visual disturbances; it must not be combined with nitrates because of the risk of dangerous hypotension.

History

Sildenafil was synthesized by Pfizer scientists at Sandwich, Kent, England, and was originally studied as a treatment for hypertension and angina pectoris. Phase I trials under the direction of Ian Osterloh suggested little effect on angina but noted that it could induce marked penile erections. Pfizer therefore decided to market it for erectile dysfunction; it was patented in 1996 and approved by the US Food and Drug Administration on 27 March 1998, becoming the first oral treatment for erectile dysfunction approved in the United States and a major commercial success, with annual sales peaking above US$2 billion. Generic versions became available after patent expiry in many markets around 2013–2020.

Society and culture

Viagra's launch transformed public discussion and treatment of erectile dysfunction, attracted extensive advertising and media coverage, and spawned widespread recreational use and a large counterfeit trade. It is among the most counterfeited drugs in the world.
