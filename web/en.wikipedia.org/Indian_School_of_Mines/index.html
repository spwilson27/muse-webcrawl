The Indian School of Mines (ISM), now the Indian Institute of Technology (Indian School of Mines), Dhanbad, is a public technical university located in Dhanbad, Jharkhand, India. It was founded on 9 December 1926, modelled on the Royal School of Mines in London, to train mining engineers and geologists for India's coal and mineral industries in the heart of the Damodar Valley coalfields.

History

The school was formally inaugurated by Lord Irwin, then Viceroy of India. It initially offered courses in mining engineering and applied geology, later adding petroleum engineering (1957) and a range of other disciplines. In 1967 it was granted deemed-university status, and in 2016 the Union Cabinet approved its conversion to an Indian Institute of Technology, making it IIT (ISM) Dhanbad under the Institutes of Technology (Amendment) Act, 2016.

Academics

The institute offers undergraduate, postgraduate, and doctoral programmes across engineering, applied sciences, management, and humanities. Its oldest departments include Mining Engineering, Petroleum Engineering, Applied Geology, and Applied Geophysics. Admission to B.Tech programmes is through JEE (Advanced).

Campus and student life

The campus covers roughly 218 acres in Dhanbad and includes heritage buildings such as the Main Building and the Penman Auditorium. The annual techno-cultural fest is known as Srijan, and the institute hosts Concetto, its technical festival.
