A Christmas Carol. In Prose. Being a Ghost Story of Christmas, commonly known as A Christmas Carol, is a novella by Charles Dickens, first published in London by Chapman & Hall in 1843 and illustrated by John Leech. It recounts the story of Ebenezer Scrooge, an elderly miser who is visited by the ghost of his former business partner Jacob Marley and the spirits of Christmas Past, Present and Yet to Come. After their visits, Scrooge is transformed into a kinder, gentler man.

Plot

On Christmas Eve, the cold-hearted moneylender Ebenezer Scrooge refuses charity collectors and grudgingly allows his clerk Bob Cratchit Christmas Day off. That night Marley's ghost, dragging heavy chains, warns him to change his ways. Three spirits follow: the Ghost of Christmas Past shows Scrooge his lonely childhood and lost fiancée Belle; the Ghost of Christmas Present reveals the humble but joyful Cratchit family and their frail son Tiny Tim; and the Ghost of Christmas Yet to Come shows a future in which an unnamed dead man is unmourned and Tiny Tim has died. Scrooge awakens on Christmas morning resolved to change: he sends the Cratchits a prize turkey, gives generously, and becomes a second father to Tiny Tim, who survives.

Publication and influence

Dickens wrote the novella in six weeks, partly in response to a government report on child labour and after a visit to Manchester. Published on 19 December 1843, the first edition sold out by Christmas Eve; Dickens, however, made little money because of the lavish production and his insistence on a low price. The book has never been out of print, popularised phrases such as "Merry Christmas" and "Bah! Humbug!", and came to define the spirit of the Victorian Christmas revival. It has been adapted countless times for stage, film, opera and television, and the name "Scrooge" has entered the language as a synonym for a miser.
