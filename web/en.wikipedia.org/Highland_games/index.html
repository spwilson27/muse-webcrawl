Highland games

Highland games (Scottish Gaelic: geamannan Gàidhealach) are outdoor events held in spring and summer in Scotland and in other countries with Scottish diaspora communities, celebrating Scottish and Celtic culture — particularly that of the Scottish Highlands — through athletic contests, piping and drumming, dancing and clan gatherings. Some events, such as the Cowal Highland Gathering in Dunoon and the Braemar Gathering, attract tens of thousands of spectators.

History

The origins of the heavy events are often traced to clan gatherings at which chiefs tested the strength of their men for roles as bodyguards, porters and messengers; legend associates a caber-like contest with the court of Malcolm III in the 11th century. The modern games date from the Highland revival of the early 19th century, after the repeal of the Dress Act that had banned Highland dress. Queen Victoria's patronage of the Braemar Gathering from 1848 helped popularise the format. Scottish emigrants carried the games abroad: the Caledonian Games began in the United States in the 1860s, and long-running games are now held in Canada, Australia, New Zealand and elsewhere.

Heavy events

  • Caber toss — the athlete lifts a long tapered pine log (typically 5–6 m and 40–70 kg), balances it upright, and throws it so it turns end over end; a "twelve o'clock" turn, landing directly away from the thrower, is perfect.
  • Stone put — throwing a heavy stone (the open stone and the heavier Braemar stone, thrown without run-up).
  • Scottish hammer throw — a metal ball on a rigid shaft, whirled and released from a stationary stance in hammer boots.
  • Weight throws — for distance (light and heavy weights on chains) and for height over a bar.
  • Sheaf toss — hurling a burlap sheaf with a pitchfork over a bar.
  • Maide Leisg — a trial of strength in which two competitors pull on a short stick while seated.

Other elements

Games also feature solo piping and pipe-band competitions, Highland dancing contests (including the Highland fling and sword dance), clan tents, tug o' war, track and hill races, and livestock and dog displays. Competitors in the heavy events traditionally wear kilts, and tartan is ubiquitous among spectators and officials.
