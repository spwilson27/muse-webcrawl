The Cell Broadband Engine, commonly called the Cell, is a multi-core microprocessor architecture developed jointly by Sony, Toshiba, and IBM (the "STI" alliance). It is best known as the CPU of Sony's PlayStation 3 game console, released in 2006.

Architecture

The Cell combines one general-purpose PowerPC-based core, the Power Processing Element (PPE), with multiple specialised Synergistic Processing Units (SPUs) — SIMD coprocessors each with its own local memory, fed by explicit DMA transfers. In the PlayStation 3, seven of eight SPUs are available to games (one disabled for yield, one reserved for the OS).

Reception and use

The architecture offered very high floating-point throughput for its time but was notoriously difficult to program, contributing to challenging early PS3 development. Cells also powered IBM blade servers, supercomputers such as Roadrunner (the first petaflop machine), Toshiba televisions, and distributed-computing projects like Folding@home via PS3.

Legacy

Sony moved to an x86 architecture for the PlayStation 4, ending Cell's console line, though the chip remains a notable experiment in heterogeneous computing.
