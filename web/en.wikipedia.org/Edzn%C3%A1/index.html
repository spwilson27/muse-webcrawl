Edzná is a Maya archaeological site in the north of the Mexican state of Campeche, about 50 km southeast of the city of Campeche. The name is often interpreted as "House of the Itzá" or "House of Echoes".

Site

The most prominent structure is the Platform of the Knives and the main acropolis, crowned by the Building of the Five Floors (Edificio de los Cinco Pisos), a stepped pyramidal palace about 40 m high combining temple and residential functions. The site's Puuc-style architecture includes the Temple of the Masks, where stucco masks of the sun god Kinich Ahau flank a staircase. Edzná was sustained by an extensive hydraulic system of canals and reservoirs (chultuns) that collected rainwater in this dry region.

History

The valley was settled by about 600 BC; Edzná became a regional capital in the Late Classic (c. 600–900 AD) with a population perhaps of 25,000, before declining and being largely abandoned by around 1500.
