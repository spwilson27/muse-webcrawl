Canut

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The canuts were Lyon silk workers, predominantly master weavers and journeymen working on hand looms in the 19th century, concentrated in the Croix-Rousse district of the city. The term is thought to derive from an abbreviation of the French expression "Voici les cannes nues!" ("Look at those bare canes!") or possibly from canette, the bobbin used in weaving.

The Canut revolts

The canuts are famous for their revolts of 1831 and 1834, among the earliest industrial workers' uprisings in Europe. In November 1831, after merchants refused to honour a minimum rate for silk work agreed with the workers, the canuts rose under the banner "Vivre en travaillant ou mourir en combattant" ("Live working or die fighting") and briefly controlled Lyon. The second revolt, in April 1834, was bloodily suppressed.

Legacy

The revolts influenced later labour movements and social thinkers, and the canuts' conditions were documented by early sociologists. The Croix-Rousse neighbourhood retains traboules — covered passageways used to carry silk — and a museum, the Maison des Canuts, commemorates the trade.
