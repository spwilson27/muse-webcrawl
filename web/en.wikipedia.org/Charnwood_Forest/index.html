Charnwood Forest is an upland tract in north-western Leicestershire, England, situated between Loughborough, Shepshed, Markfield and Coalville. Famous for its ancient Precambrian rocks, the area contains some of the oldest fossil-bearing strata in Britain and is regarded as the birthplace of British Precambrian palaeontology following the discovery of Charnia in 1957.

Geology

The Charnian Supergroup consists of volcanic and sedimentary rocks around 600 million years old, among the oldest in England. In 1957 schoolboy Roger Mason found an impression fossil in a quarry near Charnwood Lodge; described by Trevor Ford as Charnia masoni, it was one of the first convincing Precambrian macrofossils and helped overturn the belief that complex life began in the Cambrian. The rocks are quarried for roadstone at sites such as Mountsorrel and Bardon.

Landscape and history

The forest was a royal hunting forest in the medieval period, though never densely wooded across its whole extent. Bardon Hill, at 278 metres, is the highest point in Leicestershire. Notable sites include Bradgate Park, once home to Lady Jane Grey, Swithland Wood, Beacon Hill, and the preserved Great Central Railway. The area is designated a National Character Area and much of it lies within the National Forest.
