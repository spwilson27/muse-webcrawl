La Violencia

The Bogotazo, the riots following Gaitán's assassination, ignited La Violencia.

La Violencia ("The Violence") was a period of civil conflict in Colombia between the Liberal and Conservative parties, roughly from 1948 to 1958, in which an estimated 200,000 people were killed, overwhelmingly peasants in the countryside. It remains one of the deadliest conflicts in the history of the Americas and laid the groundwork for Colombia's later armed conflicts.

Course

Tensions between the two traditional parties had simmered since the Conservatives returned to power in 1946. The assassination in Bogotá on 9 April 1948 of the Liberal populist leader Jorge Eliécer Gaitán triggered the Bogotazo, massive riots that destroyed much of the capital's centre, and unleashed nationwide partisan warfare. Conservative governments used police and party paramilitaries (chulavitas and pájaros) against Liberal peasants, who formed guerrilla bands and self-defence groups; atrocities and massacres depopulated whole regions and displaced perhaps two million people.

In 1953 General Gustavo Rojas Pinilla seized power in a coup and offered amnesties that briefly pacified many guerrillas, but violence resumed and Rojas himself was overthrown in 1957. The conflict formally ended with the National Front pact of 1958, in which Liberals and Conservatives agreed to alternate the presidency and share power for sixteen years. Some self-defence groups refused to demobilize, however, and their descendants — attacked by the army in the 1960s — became the nucleus of the FARC guerrilla movement, extending Colombia's conflict for another half-century.
