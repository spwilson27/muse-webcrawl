Nawab of Awadh

Nawab of Awadh
Region: Awadh (Oudh), northern India
First holder: Saadat Ali Khan I (1722)
Last holder: Wajid Ali Shah (deposed 1856)
Capital: Faizabad, later Lucknow

The Nawabs of Awadh (also spelled Oudh) were the rulers of the kingdom of Awadh in north India, in the region of the present-day state of Uttar Pradesh. The dynasty was founded by Saadat Ali Khan I (Burhan-ul-Mulk), a Persian adventurer appointed governor (subadar) of Awadh by the Mughal emperor Muhammad Shah in 1722. As Mughal authority collapsed in the 18th century, the nawabs became effectively independent hereditary rulers.

History

Saadat Ali Khan's successors — Safdar Jang, Shuja-ud-Daula, and Asaf-ud-Daula — consolidated the state. Shuja-ud-Daula fought the British East India Company at the Battle of Buxar (1764) and thereafter Awadh fell under growing British influence, bound by subsidiary alliances and ceding territory in 1801. Asaf-ud-Daula moved the capital from Faizabad to Lucknow in 1775, and Lucknow flowered as a centre of Urdu poetry, music, cuisine, and Shia Islamic culture. In 1819 Ghazi-ud-Din Haidar assumed the title of King of Awadh, renouncing even nominal Mughal suzerainty.

Annexation and legacy

Claiming misgovernment, the East India Company annexed Awadh in 1856 under Governor-General Lord Dalhousie; the last ruler, Wajid Ali Shah, was deposed and exiled to Calcutta. The annexation was a major grievance contributing to the Indian Rebellion of 1857, in which Awadh was a principal theatre. The nawabs are remembered for their refined court culture, monumental architecture (such as the Bara Imambara), and patronage of the arts.
