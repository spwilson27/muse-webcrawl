The Doge's Palace (Italian: Palazzo Ducale) is a palace built in Venetian Gothic style, and one of the main landmarks of the city of Venice in northern Italy. The palace was the residence of the Doge of Venice, the supreme authority of the former Venetian Republic, and housed its government offices, law courts, and prisons.

History

A palace first stood on the site in the ninth century, but the present building was constructed principally from 1340 onward, beginning with the waterfront wing containing the hall of the Great Council (Sala del Maggior Consiglio). Fires in 1574 and 1577 gutted parts of the palace, which were rebuilt in Renaissance style to designs by Antonio da Ponte, retaining the Gothic exterior. After the fall of the Republic in 1797 the palace served French and Austrian administrations and later the Italian state; it has been a public museum since 1923.

Architecture and art

The palace is famous for its two-tone facade of white Istrian stone and pink Verona marble, its delicate Gothic arcades, and interiors decorated by Tintoretto, Veronese, Titian and other Venetian masters. Veronese and Tintoretto painted vast canvases for the Great Council hall, including Tintoretto's Paradise, among the largest oil paintings on canvas in the world. The state rooms include the Senate chamber, the Hall of the Council of Ten, and the Doge's apartments. The palace connects across a canal to the New Prisons via the Bridge of Sighs, named for the sighs of prisoners glimpsing Venice for the last time.
