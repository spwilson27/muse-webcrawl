Leptogenesis
Hypothetical process in physical cosmology generating an asymmetry between leptons and antileptons in the early universe.

In physical cosmology, leptogenesis is the generic term for hypothetical physical processes that produced an asymmetry between leptons and antileptons in the very early universe, resulting in the present-day dominance of leptons over antileptons. Through sphaleron processes, which conserve B − L but violate B + L, a lepton asymmetry is partially converted into the observed baryon asymmetry of the universe.

Mechanism

The most studied scenario, proposed by Masataka Fukugita and Tsutomu Yanagida in 1986, involves heavy right-handed Majorana neutrinos. If neutrinos are Majorana particles, the seesaw mechanism explains their small masses and introduces lepton-number-violating decays. The out-of-equilibrium, CP-violating decays of the heavy neutrinos in the early universe generate a net lepton number, satisfying Sakharov's conditions.

Connection to baryogenesis

Leptogenesis is attractive because the baryon asymmetry can be explained without introducing baryon-number violation directly: electroweak sphalerons in the Standard Model convert part of the lepton asymmetry into baryon asymmetry. The final baryon-to-photon ratio predicted is of the order observed, η ≈ 6×10−10.

Experimental status

Direct evidence is lacking. Indirect support would come from establishing that neutrinos are Majorana particles, for example through observation of neutrinoless double beta decay, and from measuring CP violation in the lepton sector.
