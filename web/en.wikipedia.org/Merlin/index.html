Merlin

Merlin (Welsh: Myrddin, Cornish: Marzhin, Breton: Merzhin) is a mythical figure prominently featured in the legend of King Arthur and best known as a magician (also known as a prophet and madman). His standard depiction, based on an amalgamation of historic and legendary figures, was introduced by the 12th-century British pseudo-historian Geoffrey of Monmouth.

Origins

Geoffrey combined the Welsh bard and wild man Myrddin Wyllt with the Romano-British war leader Ambrosius Aurelianus to create Merlin Ambrosius. In Geoffrey's Historia Regum Britanniae, Merlin is the fatherless child who prophesies before King Vortigern about the red and white dragons, and later serves as adviser to Uther Pendragon and Arthur.

In medieval romance

Robert de Boron elaborated Merlin's demonic birth — the son of an incubus and a mortal woman, baptized at birth so that he used his supernatural knowledge for good — and made him the architect of Arthur's world, including the Round Table and the Grail quest. In later tradition Merlin falls in love with the Lady of the Lake (Viviane/Nimue), who traps him in a crystal cave, hawthorn tree, or tower of air.

Legacy

Merlin remains the archetype of the wise wizard in Western literature, appearing in works from Malory's Le Morte d'Arthur to modern novels, films, and television. The name "Merlin" also denotes a small falcon (Falco columbarius), unrelated in origin.
