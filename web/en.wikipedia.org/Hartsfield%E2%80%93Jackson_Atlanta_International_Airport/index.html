Hartsfield–Jackson Atlanta International Airport

Hartsfield–Jackson Atlanta International Airport (IATA: ATL) is the primary international airport serving Atlanta, Georgia, United States. Located about 10 miles south of downtown Atlanta, it has been among the world's busiest airports by passenger traffic since 1998 and is the largest hub of Delta Air Lines.

History

The airport began in 1925–26 as Candler Field on the site of an abandoned racetrack. It was renamed for former mayor William B. Hartsfield in 1971, and in 2003 the name of the late mayor Maynard Jackson was added. The current terminal complex, opened in 1980, uses parallel concourses connected by an underground people mover known as the Plane Train.

Operations

The airport has five runways and two terminal complexes: the Domestic Terminal and the Maynard H. Jackson Jr. International Terminal, opened in 2012. Its scale as a connecting hub makes it a major driver of the regional economy, and it is frequently cited as the world's busiest airport by both passengers and aircraft movements.
