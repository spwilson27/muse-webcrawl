The United Center on Chicago's Near West Side.

The United Center is an indoor arena on the Near West Side of Chicago, Illinois. Home to the Chicago Bulls of the NBA and the Chicago Blackhawks of the NHL, it opened in 1994 as the replacement for the Chicago Stadium, which stood across Madison Street. It is named for its corporate sponsor, United Airlines.

History

The arena was a joint venture of Bulls owner Jerry Reinsdorf and Blackhawks owner William Wirtz. Ground was broken in 1992 and the building opened on August 18, 1994. It was the home court for the final three championships of the Bulls' 1990s dynasty, and a statue of Michael Jordan, The Spirit, stands outside the arena. The Blackhawks won the Stanley Cup on its ice in 2015.

Events

Beyond basketball and hockey, the United Center is one of the busiest concert venues in the United States and has hosted the Democratic National Convention twice, in 1996 and 2024. With standing room, its basketball capacity of 23,500 makes it the largest arena by capacity in the NBA.
