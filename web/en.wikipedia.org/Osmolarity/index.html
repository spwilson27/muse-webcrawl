Osmolarity

Osmolarity is the measure of solute concentration, defined as the number of osmoles of solute per litre of solution. It is a colligative property: it depends on the number of dissolved particles rather than their identity.

Definition and units

Osmolarity is expressed in osmoles per litre (Osm/L) or, more commonly in clinical practice, milliosmoles per kilogram conventions aside, as mOsm/L. It differs from osmolality, which is measured per kilogram of solvent and is independent of temperature and pressure.

Clinical significance

Normal plasma osmolarity is about 275–295 mOsm/L, mainly determined by sodium, glucose, and urea. It can be estimated with the formula 2[Na+] + [glucose]/18 + [BUN]/2.8. The kidneys regulate plasma osmolarity through antidiuretic hormone (ADH) and thirst.
