A myoblast is a type of embryonic progenitor cell that differentiates into muscle cells. During development, myoblasts arise from the mesoderm, proliferate, and then fuse with one another to form multinucleated myotubes, which mature into skeletal muscle fibres. In adult muscle, a resident population of myoblast precursors — satellite cells, located beneath the basal lamina of fibres — is activated to repair damage and support growth.

Differentiation

Myoblast fate is controlled by the myogenic regulatory factors. Pax3 and Pax7 mark early progenitors; Myf5 and MyoD commit cells to the myogenic lineage, while myogenin and MRF4 drive terminal differentiation, cell-cycle exit and fusion. Fusion itself requires specific membrane proteins, including myomaker and myomerger. Cultured myoblasts, such as the mouse C2C12 cell line, are widely used as a model system for studying muscle development and differentiation.

Clinical relevance

Myoblast transplantation has been investigated as a therapy for muscular dystrophies such as Duchenne muscular dystrophy, in which healthy or corrected donor myoblasts would fuse with host fibres and restore dystrophin expression. Early trials showed poor cell survival and distribution, and research has since broadened to other stem and progenitor cell sources.
