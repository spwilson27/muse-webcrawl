Wrocław

Wrocław (German: Breslau) is a city in southwestern Poland and the largest city in the historical region of Silesia. It lies on the banks of the Oder (Odra) River and is the capital of the Lower Silesian Voivodeship. With a population of roughly 670,000, it is the third-largest city in Poland and a major centre of culture, education, industry and technology.

History

Founded around a stronghold on Cathedral Island (Ostrów Tumski), the city is first documented in the year 1000 when a bishopric was established. Over the centuries Wrocław passed among Polish, Bohemian, Austrian Habsburg and Prussian/German rule; as Breslau it became one of the largest cities of the German Empire and a noted centre of learning, home to a university founded in 1702.

In 1945, as a declared fortress (Festung Breslau), the city endured a brutal siege and was largely destroyed. After the war it was transferred to Poland under the Potsdam Agreement; its German population was expelled westward and the city was resettled by Poles, many of them displaced from Lwów (Lviv). Rebuilt over decades, the historic Old Town — including its vast Market Square, the Gothic Town Hall, and Centennial Hall (1913), a UNESCO World Heritage Site — has been extensively restored.

Modern city

Wrocław is known for its bridges over the Oder's channels, its university and technical university, and whimsical bronze dwarf figurines scattered across the city. It was a host city for UEFA Euro 2012, European Capital of Culture in 2016, and the host of the 2017 World Games. Major employers span IT, finance and manufacturing, and the city consistently ranks among Poland's most liveable.
