The New Development Bank (NDB), formerly known as the BRICS Development Bank, is a multilateral development bank established by the BRICS states (Brazil, Russia, India, China, and South Africa). According to the agreement reached at the 2014 BRICS summit in Fortaleza, Brazil, the bank was created to mobilise resources for infrastructure and sustainable development projects in BRICS and other emerging economies and developing countries.

The bank is headquartered in Shanghai, China. Its first regional office was opened in Johannesburg, South Africa, followed by offices in São Paulo, Moscow, and New Delhi (GIFT City, Gujarat).

History

The idea of a development bank funded by the BRICS nations was first discussed at the 2012 summit in New Delhi. The agreement establishing the bank was signed on 15 July 2014 at the sixth BRICS summit in Fortaleza, together with the treaty for a Contingent Reserve Arrangement. The bank's Articles of Agreement entered into force in July 2015, and the NDB began operations in 2016, approving its first set of loans that year.

The bank's initial authorised capital was US$100 billion, with US$50 billion in paid-in capital subscribed equally by the five founding members, each holding equal voting power — an unusual feature among multilateral lenders, where voting shares typically reflect capital contributions weighted by economic size.

Membership

Membership is open to all members of the United Nations. In 2021 the bank admitted its first new members beyond the founders: Bangladesh, the United Arab Emirates, and Uruguay, with Egypt admitted shortly afterwards. The expansion signalled the bank's ambition to broaden its shareholder base beyond the original BRICS five.

Activities

The NDB lends primarily for infrastructure and sustainable development: transport, water and sanitation, clean energy, urban development, and social infrastructure. It has also issued bonds in international and local-currency markets, including green bonds, and has approved emergency assistance lending, notably during the COVID-19 pandemic. The bank emphasises lending in members' local currencies to reduce borrowers' exchange-rate risk.

Governance

The bank is governed by a Board of Governors, a Board of Directors, and a president who serves a five-year term and is selected on a rotational basis from among the founding members. Indian banker K. V. Kamath was the first president, followed by Marcos Troyjo of Brazil, and subsequently Dilma Rousseff, the former president of Brazil, who took office in 2023.
