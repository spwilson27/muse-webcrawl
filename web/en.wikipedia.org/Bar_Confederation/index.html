The Bar Confederation (Polish: Konfederacja barska) was an alliance of Polish nobles formed on 29 February 1768 at the fortress of Bar in Podolia to defend the independence of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth against Russian interference, the policies of King Stanisław August Poniatowski, and the civil rights newly granted to non-Catholic "dissenters" under Russian and Prussian pressure. In the Polish tradition a confederation was a legal form of armed association of the nobility (szlachta) for a stated public purpose.

Background

Russia had effectively made the Commonwealth a protectorate; in 1767–68 the Russian ambassador Nikolai Repnin compelled the Sejm to accept the so-called Cardinal Laws and equal rights for Orthodox and Protestant dissenters, abducting senators who resisted. In response, Catholic nobles led by figures such as Józef Pułaski and Bishop Adam Stanisław Krasiński formed the confederation under religious slogans ("for faith and freedom").

Course of the war

The confederates fought a guerrilla and positional war against Russian forces and troops loyal to the king for four years. Notable episodes include the defence of Berdyczów and Bar itself, campaigns in Greater Poland and Lithuania, and the defence of the Jasna Góra monastery at Częstochowa under Kazimierz Pułaski. France sent officers, money and a small expedition (including Charles François Dumouriez) to support the confederates, and the Ottoman Empire declared war on Russia in 1768 partly in response to events in Poland. An audacious confederate attempt to kidnap the king in Warsaw in 1771 failed and damaged their reputation abroad.

Outcome

By 1772 the confederation was crushed. Its failure provided the pretext for Prussia, Russia and Austria to carry out the First Partition of Poland (1772), in which the Commonwealth lost about a third of its territory and population. Kazimierz Pułaski later became a hero of the American Revolutionary War, dying at Savannah in 1779. The Bar Confederation is remembered in Poland as the first national uprising in defence of independence.
