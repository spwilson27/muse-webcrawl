Irreligion is the absence of religion, an indifference toward religion, or hostility toward religion. When characterized as the rejection of religious belief, it encompasses atheism; when characterized as a lack of belief without active rejection, it may include agnosticism; and when described as indifference, it includes apatheism. Irreligion may also include forms of theism, such as deism, depending on the religious context against which it is defined — for example, in 18th-century Europe the epitome of irreligion was deism.

People who identify as irreligious are sometimes called the "religiously unaffiliated" or "nones" in survey research. In 2015 the Pew Research Center estimated that about 16 percent of the world's population — some 1.2 billion people — were religiously unaffiliated, making them collectively the third-largest group after Christians and Muslims, although many of them hold some religious or spiritual beliefs.

Types of irreligion

Atheism

Atheism, in the broad sense, is an absence of belief in the existence of deities. In a narrower sense it is specifically the position that there are no deities. Atheism is contrasted with theism, the belief in at least one deity.

Agnosticism

Agnosticism is the view that the existence of God, the divine, or the supernatural is unknown or unknowable. The term was coined by the biologist Thomas Huxley in 1869.

Secular humanism

Secular humanism is a philosophy that embraces human reason, ethics, and philosophical naturalism while specifically rejecting religious dogma, supernaturalism, and superstition as the basis of morality and decision making.

Demographics

Worldwide, the religiously unaffiliated are most concentrated in the Asia-Pacific region, largely because of China, where official state atheism under the Communist Party and longstanding folk-religious traditions that do not map neatly onto survey categories produce very large counts. In Europe, countries such as the Czech Republic and Estonia have among the highest proportions of irreligious people; a majority of the population of the Netherlands has also been described as non-religious in recent surveys.

In the United States, the share of adults describing themselves as atheist, agnostic, or "nothing in particular" rose steadily in the early 21st century, reaching roughly a quarter to a third of adults in surveys of the early 2020s, with markedly higher rates among younger generations.

History

Ideas recognizably similar to irreligion appear in ancient philosophy: the Indian Cārvāka school rejected supernaturalism, the Greek philosopher Theodorus of Cyrene was nicknamed "the Atheist", and Roman author Lucretius argued that religion arose from fear of natural phenomena. The Enlightenment of the 17th and 18th centuries, the publication of openly atheist works such as Baron d'Holbach's System of Nature (1770), and the separation of church and state in the American and French revolutions created social and legal space for non-religion. In the 20th century several communist states promoted state atheism, while in many Western democracies declining church attendance and confidence in religious institutions accelerated secularization.
