Unexploded ordnance

Unexploded ordnance (UXO)
Includes: Bombs, shells, grenades, land mines, cluster munitions
Disposal: Explosive ordnance disposal (EOD) units

Unexploded ordnance (UXO) is explosive weapons — bombs, shells, grenades, land mines, naval mines, and cluster munitions — that did not explode when employed and remain a risk of detonation decades after they were placed or dropped. Failures may result from faulty fuzes, soft impact surfaces, or deliberate burial.

Extent of the problem

Large areas of Europe remain contaminated by ordnance from the two world wars; construction work in German and British cities regularly uncovers unexploded bombs requiring mass evacuations. Laos is the most heavily bombed country per capita in history and retains millions of unexploded cluster bomblets. Similar contamination affects Vietnam, Cambodia, Iraq, Afghanistan, and Ukraine.

Clearance

Detection typically uses magnetometers and ground-penetrating radar. Items are destroyed in place by explosive ordnance disposal teams when they cannot be moved safely. International efforts are coordinated under treaties such as the Ottawa Treaty (landmines) and the Convention on Cluster Munitions.
