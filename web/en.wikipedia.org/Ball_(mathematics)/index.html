Ball (mathematics)

Ball
Field: Topology, metric geometry
Boundary: Sphere
Notation: Br(p)

In mathematics, a ball is the region in space comprising all points within a given distance (the radius) of a fixed central point. The boundary of a ball is a sphere. Balls are fundamental objects in topology and analysis, generalising the everyday notion of a solid ball to arbitrary metric spaces and dimensions.

Definitions

In a metric space (X, d), the open ball of radius r centred at p is the set of points x with d(x, p) < r; the closed ball uses d(x, p) ≤ r. In Euclidean three-dimensional space, the open ball is the interior of a sphere, and its volume is (4/3)πr³.

General topology

Open balls form a basis for the topology induced by a metric. In normed vector spaces, the unit ball is the ball of radius 1 centred at the origin, and its shape characterises the norm: a cube in the maximum norm, a diamond (octahedron in 3D) in the taxicab norm.

Properties

In higher dimensions, balls display counter-intuitive behaviour: most of the volume of a high-dimensional ball lies near its surface, and balls can be packed in surprising ways. Open balls need not be "round" in general metric spaces, and in ultrametric spaces every point of a ball can serve as its centre.
