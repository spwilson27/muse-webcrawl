Rio Tinto

IndustryMetals and mining
Founded1873
HeadquartersLondon, United Kingdom; Melbourne, Australia
ProductsIron ore, aluminium, copper, lithium, borates, diamonds

Rio Tinto Group is a British-Australian multinational company that is the world's second-largest metals and mining corporation (after BHP). It was founded in 1873 when a group of investors purchased a mine complex on the Río Tinto ("red river"), in Huelva, Spain, from the Spanish government and reorganised it as the Rio Tinto Company.

Through a long series of mergers and acquisitions, the company grew to produce mainly iron ore, as well as aluminium, copper, lithium, borates and (historically) diamonds and uranium. The group operates through a dual-listed company structure: Rio Tinto plc in London and Rio Tinto Limited in Melbourne, managed as a single economic unit.

Operations

Rio Tinto's largest business is iron ore from the Pilbara region of Western Australia, shipped mainly to Chinese steel mills. Other major assets include the Escondida copper mine in Chile (a minority stake), the Oyu Tolgoi copper-gold mine in Mongolia, bauxite and alumina operations in Australia and elsewhere, and the Simandou iron ore project in Guinea, one of the world's largest untapped high-grade deposits.

Juukan Gorge controversy

In May 2020 the company legally destroyed two rock shelters at Juukan Gorge in the Pilbara, sites of deep significance to the Puutu Kunti Kurrama and Pinikura peoples with evidence of human occupation dating back about 46,000 years. The destruction provoked a parliamentary inquiry in Australia, investor backlash, and the resignations of chief executive Jean-Sébastien Jacques and two senior executives; it spurred company-wide reforms of heritage management.
