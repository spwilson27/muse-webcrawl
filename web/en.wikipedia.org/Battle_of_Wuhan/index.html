Battle of Wuhan

Battle of Wuhan
Date11 June – 27 October 1938
LocationWuhan and surrounding provinces, China
ResultJapanese victory, at heavy cost
BelligerentsRepublic of China; Empire of Japan
CommandersChiang Kai-shek, Chen Cheng, Bai Chongxi; Yasuji Okamura, Prince Higashikuni

The Battle of Wuhan, popularly known in China as the Defense of Great Wuhan, was a major campaign of the Second Sino-Japanese War fought from June to October 1938. More than a million Chinese troops defended the temporary capital of Wuhan in central China against a Japanese offensive up the Yangtze River and overland from the north.

The campaign ranged over a vast area of Anhui, Henan, Jiangxi and Hubei. Chinese forces flooded the Yellow River in a desperate measure that slowed the northern Japanese advance but killed hundreds of thousands of civilians. In the south, Chinese troops won a notable local victory at Wanjialing, nearly annihilating a Japanese division. The fighting saw the use of poison gas by Japanese forces and heavy air battles in which Soviet volunteer pilots supported China.

Outcome

Wuhan fell on 25–27 October 1938 after the Chinese command withdrew its forces in good order. Japan had taken the city but at a cost of well over 100,000 casualties and with its strategic goal — destroying the Chinese army and forcing a negotiated peace — unachieved. The war accordingly settled into the prolonged stalemate phase, with the Chinese government entrenched at Chongqing far upriver.
