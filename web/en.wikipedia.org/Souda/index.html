Souda


Souda Bay, seen from the Akrotiri peninsula

Souda (Greek: Σούδα) is a town and former municipality in the Chania regional unit of Crete, Greece. Since the 2011 local government reform it has been a municipal unit of the municipality of Chania. The town lies on the southern shore of Souda Bay, about 6 kilometres east of Chania town, and functions as its principal port.

Souda Bay

Souda Bay is one of the largest and deepest natural harbours in the Mediterranean, roughly 15 kilometres long, protected by the Akrotiri peninsula. Its strategic position in the eastern Mediterranean has made it a military anchorage since antiquity. Venetian fortifications, including the fortress on Souda islet at the bay's entrance, date from the period of Venetian rule over Crete.

Naval facilities

The bay hosts the Souda Bay Naval Base of the Hellenic Navy and Naval Support Activity Souda Bay, a United States naval facility used by the US Navy and NATO since the early 1950s. The Marathi pier area also serves NATO maritime forces. During the Second World War the bay was a Royal Navy anchorage and was attacked during the Battle of Crete in May 1941; the heavy cruiser HMS York was disabled there by Italian explosive motor boats in March 1941.

Port and cemeteries

Souda's commercial port provides ferry connections to Piraeus. Nearby at Souda Bay is the Souda Bay War Cemetery, maintained by the Commonwealth War Graves Commission, containing the graves of more than 1,500 Commonwealth servicemen, most from the Battle of Crete.
