Upekkha

Upekkhā (Pali: උපේක්ඛා; Sanskrit: उपेक्षा, upekṣā) is the Buddhist concept of equanimity. It is one of the four divine abidings (brahmavihāra), along with loving-kindness (mettā), compassion (karuṇā) and sympathetic joy (muditā). Upekkhā is also one of the seven factors of enlightenment (bojjhaṅga) and one of the ten perfections (pāramī) of Theravāda Buddhism.

Meaning

The word literally suggests "to look over" or "to look on with equipoise" — seeing situations impartially, without being drawn into attraction or aversion. In the Pali Canon, upekkhā is described as an even-minded state of mental balance, "neither thought nor non-thought", in which the mind is free from discrimination arising from likes and dislikes. The Visuddhimagga distinguishes it from mere indifference, which is called its "near enemy": true equanimity is a balanced, caring impartiality, whereas indifference is a cold unconcern rooted in ignorance.

In meditation

Upekkhā is characteristic of the fourth jhāna, the deepest of the meditative absorptions of form, where mindfulness is purified by equanimity. As the fourth brahmavihāra, it is cultivated through contemplation of beings as owners of their kamma: all beings experience the results of their own actions, which tempers both attachment and resentment toward them.

As an enlightenment factor

Among the seven factors of enlightenment, upekkhā arises when the preceding factors — mindfulness, investigation, energy, joy, tranquillity and concentration — are mature, and it balances the mind so that the other factors function evenly. It is also listed among the mental factors present in wholesome consciousness in the Abhidhamma analysis.
