Communes of Chile

Communes of Chile
Number: 346
Level: third, lowest administrative division
Governments: municipalities (municipalidades)
Head: mayor (alcalde) and council

A commune (comuna) is the smallest administrative subdivision in Chile. There are 346 communes, each administered by a municipality headed by a directly elected mayor (alcalde) and a municipal council elected every four years.

Administrative structure

Chile is divided into regions, which are divided into provinces, which in turn consist of communes. A single municipality may administer one or more communes, although most administer exactly one. Communes are created, modified or abolished by law.

Functions

Municipalities are responsible for local services including primary health care, public education, waste collection, local roads, social assistance and urban planning within their territories.

Examples

The commune of Santiago contains the historic centre of the capital, while greater Santiago comprises dozens of communes such as Providencia, Las Condes, Maipú and Puente Alto. The least populous communes are remote territories such as Antártica, which administers Chile's Antarctic claims.
