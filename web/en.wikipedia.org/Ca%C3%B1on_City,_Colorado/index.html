Cañon City, Colorado

Cañon City is a home rule municipality in and the county seat of Fremont County, Colorado, United States. Located in south-central Colorado along the Arkansas River, it is known as a centre for tourism, rafting, and as the location of numerous state and federal correctional facilities.

History

The town was laid out in 1860 during the Colorado gold rush, taking its name from the nearby canyon (cañón in Spanish) of the Arkansas River. It competed with Colorado City and Pueblo as a supply point for mining districts in the mountains. In 1871 the territorial prison was established there, beginning the corrections industry that remains a major employer.

Geography and attractions

The city lies at the mouth of the Royal Gorge, crossed nearby by the Royal Gorge Bridge, one of the highest suspension bridges in the world. The Arkansas River through the gorge is popular for whitewater rafting. Other attractions include the Royal Gorge Route Railroad, the Museum of Colorado Prisons, and nearby dinosaur fossil sites, including Garden Park, known for Jurassic-era finds.

Prisons

Cañon City is sometimes called the "Prison Capital of Colorado". The area hosts a complex of state correctional facilities and the federal Administrative Maximum Facility (ADX) at nearby Florence, the only federal supermax prison in the United States.
