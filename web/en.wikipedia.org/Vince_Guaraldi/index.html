Vincent Anthony Guaraldi (born Dellaglio; 17 July 1928 – 6 February 1976) was an American jazz pianist best known for composing music for animated adaptations of the Peanuts comic strip, including the theme "Linus and Lucy", and for his Grammy Award-winning instrumental "Cast Your Fate to the Wind".

Early career

Guaraldi was born in San Francisco's North Beach neighbourhood and began performing professionally while at San Francisco State College. He worked with Cal Tjader's groups in the early 1950s and formed his own trio later in the decade, recording for Fantasy Records.

"Cast Your Fate to the Wind" and bossa nova

His 1962 album Jazz Impressions of Black Orpheus, inspired by the film Black Orpheus, included his original composition "Cast Your Fate to the Wind", which became a surprise hit single and won the Grammy Award for Best Original Composition. The album helped popularise bossa nova in the United States.

Peanuts scores

Producer Lee Mendelson heard "Cast Your Fate to the Wind" and hired Guaraldi to score the 1963 documentary A Boy Named Charlie Brown. Guaraldi went on to compose music for A Charlie Brown Christmas (1965) and more than a dozen subsequent Peanuts television specials, creating a distinctive jazz sound — including "Linus and Lucy" and "Skating" — closely identified with Charles M. Schulz's characters. He died suddenly of a heart attack in 1976.
