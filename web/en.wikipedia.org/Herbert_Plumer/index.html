Herbert Charles Onslow Plumer, 1st Viscount Plumer (13 March 1857 – 16 July 1932) was a senior British Army officer of the First World War. After commanding in Sudan and the Boer War, he led the Second Army on the Western Front, winning the Battle of Messines in 1917, and was later British commander in Italy and commander of the Army of the Rhine. He ended his career as High Commissioner of the British Mandate for Palestine.

Early career

Educated at Eton and Sandhurst, Plumer was commissioned into the 65th (York and Lancaster) Regiment in 1876. He served in Sudan (1884), in Southern Africa during the Second Matabele War (1896) — raising a mounted force in Rhodesia — and in the Second Boer War, where he commanded a column in the relief operations and earned a reputation for methodical planning and care for his men.

First World War

Plumer took command of V Corps and then Second Army in the Ypres Salient in 1915. His masterpiece was the Battle of Messines (June 1917): after nearly two years of tunnelling, 19 huge mines exploded under the German lines, and Plumer's troops — famous for his maxim that his plans relied on preparation rather than dash ("bite and hold") — seized the Messines Ridge with limited objectives. He then directed the later stages of Third Ypres (Passchendaele), winning methodical victories at Menin Road, Polygon Wood, and Broodseinde in autumn 1917.

Sent to Italy after the Caporetto disaster in November 1917, he returned to Second Army in March 1918 and helped halt the German Lys offensive. In the Hundred Days he took part in the final advance into Belgium.

Later life

Plumer commanded the British Army of the Rhine (1919–1924), was promoted field marshal, and became a viscount in 1929. From 1925 to 1928 he served as High Commissioner for Palestine, where his firm but impartial administration kept relative calm. He died in London in 1932 and was buried in Westminster Abbey.
