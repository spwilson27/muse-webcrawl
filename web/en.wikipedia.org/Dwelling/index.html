Dwelling

In law, a dwelling (also known as a residence or abode) is a self-contained unit of accommodation used by one or more households as a home — such as a house, apartment, mobile home, houseboat, vehicle, or other "substantial" structure. The concept of a dwelling has significance in the context of search and seizure, conveyancing of real property, burglary, and housing statistics.

Types

Dwellings range from rudimentary shelters such as huts and tents to complex permanent structures of wood, brick, concrete, or other materials containing plumbing, electrical, and heating systems. Categories used in official statistics include detached and semi-detached houses, terraced houses, apartments or flats, maisonettes, and temporary or mobile structures.

Legal significance

Many legal systems afford dwellings special protection. In English common law, the home has long been treated as inviolable ("an Englishman's home is his castle"), and entering a dwelling to commit an offence constitutes the distinct crime of burglary in many jurisdictions. Constitutional protections, such as those in the Fourth Amendment to the United States Constitution, apply with particular force to searches of a home.

Housing statistics

Censuses and housing surveys count "dwelling units" as a basic measure of housing stock, distinguishing occupied from vacant dwellings and recording tenure — owner-occupied, privately rented, or social housing.
