1,2-Dichloroethane

Chemical compound
FormulaC2H4Cl2
Molar mass98.96 g/mol
AppearanceColourless liquid
Boiling point83.5 °C

The chemical compound 1,2-dichloroethane, commonly known as ethylene dichloride (EDC), is a chlorinated hydrocarbon. It is a colourless liquid with a chloroform-like odour, produced on a large scale for use in industry.

Production and uses

1,2-Dichloroethane is manufactured by the direct chlorination or oxychlorination of ethylene. The overwhelming majority is converted into vinyl chloride monomer, the precursor to PVC plastic. It was historically used as a solvent, degreaser and lead scavenger in petrol, but these uses have declined because of its toxicity.

Safety

The compound is toxic, flammable, and classified as a probable human carcinogen; exposure is regulated in most jurisdictions.
