San Gabriel Valley

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

San Gabriel Valley

Location: Los Angeles County, California

Named for: Mission San Gabriel Arcángel (1771)

Principal cities: Pasadena, Alhambra, Monterey Park, San Gabriel, Arcadia, Rosemead, El Monte

Bounded by: San Gabriel Mountains (north), downtown Los Angeles (west)

The San Gabriel Valley is one of the principal valleys of Southern California, lying generally to the east of the city of Los Angeles and occupying much of the southeastern part of Los Angeles County. It takes its name from the San Gabriel River that flows southward through the middle of the valley and from Mission San Gabriel Arcángel, founded in 1771. The valley is enclosed by the San Rafael Hills and San Gabriel Mountains to the north and the Puente Hills to the south.

Communities and people

The valley’s more than thirty incorporated cities include Pasadena, famous for the Rose Parade and Rose Bowl and for Caltech and the Jet Propulsion Laboratory; Alhambra; Monterey Park; San Gabriel; Arcadia; Temple City; Rosemead; and El Monte. The population is roughly two million. Since the 1980s the western San Gabriel Valley has become one of the largest centres of Asian American life in the United States: Monterey Park was the first mainland US city with an Asian-majority population, and the valley’s Chinese, Taiwanese, Vietnamese and other Asian communities support renowned concentrations of restaurants and businesses along Valley Boulevard and in the “626” (the valley’s historic area code).

History

The Tongva (Gabrieleño) people inhabited the valley for millennia before Spanish missionaries founded Mission San Gabriel Arcángel in 1771, at a site later moved to present-day San Gabriel. Under Mexican rule the mission lands were secularised and divided into ranchos; after the American conquest the valley became famous for citrus groves, vineyards and walnuts, and then, after the Second World War, for rapid suburban development along the San Bernardino and Foothill freeways.

Geography

The San Gabriel River drains the mountain watershed and passes through the valley via the Santa Fe Dam Recreational Area before reaching the San Gabriel Canyon and, eventually, the sea at Long Beach. The valley is traversed by Interstate 10, Interstate 210, State Route 60 and the Metro A Line light rail.
