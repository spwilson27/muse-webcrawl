Positive mass theorem

In general relativity, the positive mass theorem states that, assuming the dominant energy condition, the mass (or ADM mass) of an asymptotically flat spacetime is non-negative; furthermore, the mass is zero only for Minkowski spacetime. The theorem is a scalar curvature comparison theorem in Riemannian geometry with applications in differential geometry and mathematical physics.

History

The result was conjectured on physical grounds for many years. Richard Schoen and Shing-Tung Yau gave the first proof in 1979 using minimal surfaces and geometric analysis, work that contributed to their receiving major honours in mathematics. Edward Witten gave a different proof in 1981 using spinors, which was later made rigorous by mathematical physicists.

Statement and extensions

For an asymptotically flat initial data set satisfying the dominant energy condition, the ADM energy-momentum vector is timelike or null, and in particular the total energy is greater than or equal to the magnitude of the total momentum. Extensions include the positive mass theorem with charge, versions for asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds, and the rigidity statement that zero mass implies flatness.

Significance

The theorem confirms the physical intuition that gravity is attractive and that an isolated system obeying reasonable energy conditions cannot have negative total energy. It underlies results on the stability of Minkowski spacetime and the Penrose inequality.
