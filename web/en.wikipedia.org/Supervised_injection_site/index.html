Supervised injection site

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A supervised injection site, also called a supervised consumption site or overdose prevention centre, is a legally sanctioned facility where people can consume pre-obtained illicit drugs under the supervision of trained staff, as part of a harm reduction strategy.

Services

Facilities typically provide sterile injecting equipment, a hygienic supervised space, monitoring and emergency response to overdoses (including naloxone and oxygen), basic medical care, and referrals to addiction treatment, housing, and social services. Staff do not supply drugs and generally do not handle clients' substances.

History and distribution

The first such facility opened in Bern, Switzerland, in 1986. Sites now operate in a number of European countries, Canada — where Vancouver's Insite, opened in 2003, was the first in North America — Australia (in Sydney and Melbourne), and, since 2021, New York City in the United States, where officially recognised overdose prevention centres opened despite contested federal legal status.

Evidence and debate

Numerous peer-reviewed studies associate the facilities with reduced overdose deaths in their vicinity, fewer publicly discarded syringes, lower transmission of HIV and hepatitis C, and increased uptake of treatment, without measurable increases in local crime. Opponents argue that the sites enable drug use, attract disorder, and conflict with prohibition laws, and their legality has been litigated in several jurisdictions.
