Timeline of programming languages

The following is a timeline of programming languages, recording notable first releases and designs of influential languages from the 19th century precursors to the present.

Precursors and 1940s

  • 1843 – Ada Lovelace publishes an algorithm for Babbage's Analytical Engine, often called the first computer program.
  • 1943–45 – Konrad Zuse designs Plankalkül, the first high-level programming language (published later).
  • 1949 – Short Code, one of the first languages implemented on an electronic computer.

1950s – The first high-level languages

  • 1952 – Autocode (Alick Glennie).
  • 1957 – FORTRAN, developed at IBM by John Backus's team, the first widely used compiled language.
  • 1958 – Lisp (John McCarthy) and ALGOL 58.
  • 1959 – COBOL, designed for business data processing; RPG.

1960s

  • 1960 – ALGOL 60, a landmark in language design.
  • 1962 – APL; Simula, introducing object concepts.
  • 1964 – BASIC (Kemeny and Kurtz) and PL/I.
  • 1967 – Simula 67 formalizes classes and objects; Logo for education.
  • 1969 – B, predecessor of C, at Bell Labs.

1970s

  • 1970 – Pascal (Niklaus Wirth).
  • 1972 – C (Dennis Ritchie); Smalltalk; Prolog; SQL's predecessors.
  • 1973 – ML, pioneering type inference.
  • 1975 – Scheme, a minimalist Lisp dialect.
  • 1978 – FP and the influential Turing Award lecture by John Backus.

1980s

  • 1980 – Smalltalk-80; Ada (standardized 1983).
  • 1983 – C++ (Bjarne Stroustrup, as "C with Classes" from 1979).
  • 1984 – Common Lisp; MATLAB commercial release.
  • 1986 – Erlang; Objective-C gains prominence.
  • 1987 – Perl (Larry Wall).

1990s

  • 1990 – Haskell, named for Haskell Curry.
  • 1991 – Python (Guido van Rossum); Visual Basic.
  • 1993 – R, for statistical computing; Lua.
  • 1995 – Java, JavaScript, PHP, and Ruby all appear.
  • 1996 – OCaml.

2000s–present

  • 2000 – C# (Microsoft .NET).
  • 2001 – Scala development begins (released 2004).
  • 2003 – Groovy.
  • 2007 – Clojure, a Lisp for the JVM.
  • 2009 – Go (Google).
  • 2010 – Rust (Mozilla; 1.0 in 2015).
  • 2011 – Kotlin; Dart.
  • 2012 – TypeScript; Julia; Elixir.
  • 2014 – Swift (Apple).
  • 2022 – Carbon announced by Google as an experimental successor to C++.
