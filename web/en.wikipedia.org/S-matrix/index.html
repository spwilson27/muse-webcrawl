S-matrix

FieldQuantum mechanics, quantum field theory
Introduced byJohn Archibald Wheeler (1937); developed by Werner Heisenberg (1943)
RelatesInitial and final states of a scattering process
Key propertyUnitarity

In physics, the S-matrix or scattering matrix relates the initial state and the final state of a physical system undergoing a scattering process. It is used in quantum mechanics, scattering theory and quantum field theory. The S-matrix is the unitary operator that transforms a state describing particles far apart and non-interacting in the distant past into the state describing them long after their interaction, so that the probability amplitude for an initial state to evolve into a given final state is given by a matrix element of S.

History

The concept first appeared in a 1937 paper by John Archibald Wheeler on the resonating-group method in nuclear physics. Werner Heisenberg independently introduced and developed the idea in 1943, proposing that observable scattering amplitudes — rather than unobservable fields — should be the fundamental objects of theory. This idea flowered into the S-matrix programme of the 1950s and 1960s, led by figures such as Geoffrey Chew, which sought to construct strong-interaction physics from general principles of unitarity, analyticity and crossing symmetry — the "bootstrap" programme.

Formalism

For a system with Hilbert space of asymptotic "in" and "out" states, the S-matrix elements Sfi = ⟨f,out|S|i,in⟩ give transition amplitudes; the cross-sections measured in experiments are proportional to the squared moduli of these elements. Unitarity, S†S = 1, expresses conservation of total probability and leads to the optical theorem relating the imaginary part of the forward-scattering amplitude to the total cross-section. In perturbative quantum field theory the S-matrix is computed order by order using Feynman diagrams, an approach equivalent to the LSZ reduction formalism. Although quantum field theory supplanted the autonomous S-matrix programme for strong interactions, interest in S-matrix principles has revived in modern work on scattering amplitudes and the conformal bootstrap.
