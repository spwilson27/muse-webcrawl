Jigsaw (tool)


A jigsaw is a saw with a reciprocating blade used to cut curves and irregular shapes.

A jigsaw is a saw which uses a reciprocating blade to cut irregular curves, such as stencilled designs, in wood, metal, or other materials. Hand-held electric jigsaws combine a motor with a short, narrow blade that moves rapidly up and down; the operator guides the tool along a marked line. Jigsaws are well suited to cutting curves, circles, and bevels, and with specialist blades can cut sheet metal, laminate, ceramic tile, and plastics.

History

The tool descends from treadle-powered scroll saws and the hand-held "jigsaw" used to cut wooden jigsaw puzzles. The modern electric jigsaw is commonly credited to Albert Kaufmann, an engineer at the Swiss firm Scintilla AG, who in 1946 replaced the needle of his wife's sewing machine with a saw blade. Scintilla, later part of Bosch, began selling the tool in 1947 under the name "Lesto jigsaw," and it became a standard workshop and DIY power tool.

Design

Most jigsaws have variable speed controls, a tilting baseplate (foot) for bevel cuts up to about 45 degrees, and orbital (pendulum) blade action settings for faster cutting in wood. Blades are classified by their shank — T-shank and U-shank are the common standards — and by teeth per inch: coarse blades cut wood quickly, fine blades are used for metal and smooth finishes in sheet goods. Compared with a circular saw, a jigsaw cuts more slowly and less accurately on straight lines, but is far more versatile for shapes and internal cut-outs begun from a drilled hole.

Use

Typical applications include cutting sink and hob openings in kitchen worktops, curved furniture parts, floorboards around pipes, and sheet materials on site. Safety practice emphasises securing the workpiece, letting the blade reach full speed, and keeping hands clear of the stroke path.
