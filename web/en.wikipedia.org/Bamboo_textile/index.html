Bamboo textile

Bamboo textile is any clothing, yarn or fabric made from bamboo fibres. Most bamboo textile sold commercially is bamboo viscose (rayon), produced by dissolving bamboo pulp in chemical solvents and regenerating it as cellulose fibre; a smaller amount is made by mechanical processing of the plant, closer to linen production from flax.

Production

In the viscose process, bamboo is chipped, softened in sodium hydroxide, and treated with carbon disulfide to produce a spinnable solution. Regulators in several countries require such products to be labelled "viscose from bamboo" rather than simply "bamboo", because the chemical process destroys most of the plant's original properties.

Properties and criticism

Bamboo fabrics are marketed as soft, breathable and absorbent. Claims that finished bamboo rayon retains the raw plant's antimicrobial properties have been disputed by regulators and researchers, and its environmental profile is debated: bamboo grows rapidly with little irrigation, but the viscose process uses toxic chemicals and significant water.
