Lactobacillus

Lactobacillus is a genus of gram-positive, aerotolerant anaerobes or microaerophilic, rod-shaped, non-spore-forming bacteria. They are a major part of the group of lactic acid bacteria, so named because most of their members convert lactose and other sugars to lactic acid.

In humans, lactobacilli are a significant component of the microbiota at a number of body sites, including the digestive system and the female genital system.

Taxonomy

Until 2020 the genus Lactobacillus comprised over 260 phylogenetically diverse species, an unusually broad range that was widely regarded as taxonomically unsatisfactory. In 2020 a major reclassification split the genus into 25 genera, including the emended Lactobacillus, Lacticaseibacillus, Limosilactobacillus and Lactiplantibacillus.

Metabolism

Lactobacilli are classified as homofermentative, converting sugars almost entirely to lactic acid, or heterofermentative, producing lactic acid together with carbon dioxide, acetate or ethanol. Many species are auxotrophic, requiring rich media with amino acids, vitamins and fermentable carbohydrates.

Uses

Several species are used commercially in the production of yoghurt, cheese, sauerkraut, pickles, beer, wine, cider, kimchi, cocoa and sourdough, and as probiotics. A small number of species are associated with disease in severely immunocompromised people, but lactobacilli are generally regarded as safe.
