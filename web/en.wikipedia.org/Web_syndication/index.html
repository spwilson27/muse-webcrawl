Web syndication

Web syndication is a form of syndication in which website material is made available to multiple other sites or to individual subscribers. Most commonly, a site publishes a web feed—a machine-readable summary of its newest content in formats such as RSS or Atom—which reader software (an aggregator) polls regularly and presents to the user in one place.

Formats and mechanics

RSS emerged from Netscape's 1999 "Rich Site Summary" and was developed by several groups in competing versions (RSS 0.9x, 1.0, and 2.0). The Atom format was standardized by the IETF in 2005 (RFC 4287) to resolve RSS's ambiguities. A feed lists items with titles, links, dates, and summaries or full text; podcasting extended the model with media enclosures. Publishers benefit by reaching readers without requiring visits to the site itself, and content can also be re-published between sites under license.

History

Feeds became central to the blogging era of the 2000s; Google Reader, shut down in 2013, was the dominant aggregator. Although algorithmic social media timelines displaced feed reading for many users, RSS remains in wide use for podcasts, news alerts, and software such as Feedly and Inoreader, and is maintained by most blogging and publishing platforms.
