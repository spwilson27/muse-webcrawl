Drogden Tunnel

The Drogden Tunnel (Danish: Drogdentunnelen) is a combined road and rail immersed tube tunnel forming the western part of the Øresund fixed link between Denmark and Sweden. It runs for about 4,050 metres (13,290 ft) from the Danish coast at Kastrup to the artificial island of Peberholm in the Øresund strait, where the link continues eastward across the Øresund Bridge to Malmö.

Construction

The tunnel was built as an immersed tube: twenty prefabricated concrete elements, each about 176 metres long and among the largest of their kind, were cast in a dry dock, floated out, and lowered into a dredged trench in the seabed, then joined and covered. Construction took place in the second half of the 1990s as part of the Øresund link project, which opened to traffic on 1 July 2000.

A tunnel rather than a bridge was chosen for the Danish section because a bridge near Copenhagen Airport would have obstructed flight paths, and because the tunnel avoids hindering shipping in the Drogden channel. The tunnel carries a four-lane motorway (part of European route E20) and a double-track railway used by Øresundståg and freight services.

Operation

The tunnel is operated as part of the Øresund connection by the Øresundsbro Konsortiet. Extensive ventilation, drainage, and safety systems serve the tubes, and the link as a whole is tolled, with most revenue collected at the toll station on the Swedish side.
