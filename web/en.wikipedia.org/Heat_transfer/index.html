The three modes of heat transfer
ModesConduction, convection, radiation (plus phase-change processes)
Governing lawsFourier's law, Newton's law of cooling, Stefan–Boltzmann law
FieldsThermodynamics, mechanical and chemical engineering

Heat transfer is a discipline of thermal engineering concerned with the generation, use, conversion, and exchange of thermal energy (heat) between physical systems. Heat transfer is driven by temperature differences and always flows from hotter to colder bodies, in accordance with the second law of thermodynamics. It is classified into three fundamental modes: conduction, convection and thermal radiation; in practice, processes such as boiling and condensation are treated as specialised forms of convection.

Conduction

Thermal conduction is the diffusion of heat through a material or between materials in contact, without bulk motion. Its rate is described by Fourier's law: heat flux is proportional to the temperature gradient and the material's thermal conductivity, which is high for metals such as copper and aluminium and low for insulators such as mineral wool or aerogel.

Convection

Convection transfers heat between a surface and a moving fluid, combining conduction at the interface with bulk fluid motion. It may be natural (buoyancy-driven, as in air rising over a radiator) or forced (a fan or pump). Its rate is commonly modelled by Newton's law of cooling using a heat transfer coefficient.

Radiation

Thermal radiation is the emission of electromagnetic waves by all matter above absolute zero; it requires no medium, which is how the Sun warms the Earth. The Stefan–Boltzmann law gives the radiated power as proportional to the fourth power of absolute temperature, scaled by surface emissivity.

Applications

Heat transfer analysis underpins the design of heat exchangers, boilers, refrigeration and HVAC systems, building insulation, electronics cooling, power generation and spacecraft thermal control, and it informs the study of weather and climate.
