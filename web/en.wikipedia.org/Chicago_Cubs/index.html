Chicago Cubs

The Chicago Cubs are an American professional baseball team in Major League Baseball's National League Central division, playing at Wrigley Field since 1916. Founded in 1876 as the Chicago White Stockings, they are one of the NL's charter franchises.

Famous for a 108-year championship drought after 1908, the Cubs finally won the World Series in 2016, defeating Cleveland in seven games — one of the most celebrated titles in American sports.
