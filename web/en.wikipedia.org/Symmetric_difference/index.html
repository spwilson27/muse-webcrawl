Symmetric difference

Symmetric difference
Field: Set theory
Notation: A Δ B or A ⊕ B
Definition: Elements in exactly one of the sets

In mathematics, the symmetric difference of two sets is the set of elements which are in either of the sets, but not in their intersection. The symmetric difference of sets A and B is commonly denoted A Δ B or A ⊕ B.

Properties

The symmetric difference is commutative and associative, and the empty set is its identity element; every set is its own inverse. Intersection distributes over symmetric difference, so the power set of a set, equipped with symmetric difference and intersection, forms a Boolean ring. Symmetric difference corresponds to exclusive or (XOR) in logic and to addition modulo 2 of characteristic functions.

Examples and applications

For example, the symmetric difference of {1, 2, 3} and {3, 4} is {1, 2, 4}. In measure theory, the symmetric difference gives rise to natural notions of distance between sets, and in computing the operation appears in bit manipulation and database comparisons.
