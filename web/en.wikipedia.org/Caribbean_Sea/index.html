The Caribbean Sea is a sea of the Atlantic Ocean in the tropics of the Western Hemisphere. It is bounded by Mexico and Central America to the west and southwest, to the north by the Greater Antilles, to the east by the Lesser Antilles, and to the south by the northern coast of South America. Its surface area is about 2,754,000 km², making it one of the largest seas in the world.

The Caribbean Sea, bounded by the Antilles and the American mainland.

Geography

The deepest point is the Cayman Trough, between Cuba and Jamaica, at 7,686 m below sea level. The Caribbean Current flows northwestward through the sea from the Atlantic, passes through the Yucatán Channel into the Gulf of Mexico, and there becomes the Loop Current feeding the Gulf Stream.

Geology

The sea lies mainly on the Caribbean Plate, a mostly oceanic tectonic plate. It is separated from the ocean by island arcs and is geologically young in places; the sea floor is estimated to be between 160 and 180 million years old in its oldest basins.

History

The name derives from the Carib, an Indigenous people of the Lesser Antilles. European colonization began after Columbus reached the region in 1492, and the sea became a major theatre of trade, piracy, and naval warfare among Spain, Britain, France, and the Netherlands. In the modern era, disputes from coastal states are heard by institutions such as the Caribbean Court of Justice.

Ecology

The Caribbean hosts the Mesoamerican Barrier Reef, the second-largest barrier reef system in the world, along with extensive coral reefs, mangroves, and seagrass beds. The region is a biodiversity hotspot but is threatened by warming waters, coral bleaching, hurricanes, and invasive species such as lionfish.
