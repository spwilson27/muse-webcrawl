Lebanese Civil War

The Lebanese Civil War (1975–1990) was a multifaceted armed conflict in Lebanon, fought between rival sectarian militias and political factions and repeatedly involving foreign powers including Syria, Israel and the Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO).

The war grew out of tensions in Lebanon's confessional political system, demographic change, and the armed presence of Palestinian factions after their expulsion from Jordan. Fighting began in April 1975 between Christian Phalangist militiamen and Palestinian and leftist Muslim forces. The conflict passed through many phases: Syrian intervention from 1976, Israel's 1978 and major 1982 invasions — the latter leading to the PLO's evacuation from Beirut and the Sabra and Shatila massacre — a multinational peacekeeping deployment, the 1983 Beirut barracks bombings, the War of the Camps, and the rise of Hezbollah.

An estimated 120,000–150,000 people were killed and Beirut was devastated and divided. The Taif Agreement of 1989 rebalanced the confessional system, and the war ended in 1990 with Syrian predominance and the disarmament of most militias.
