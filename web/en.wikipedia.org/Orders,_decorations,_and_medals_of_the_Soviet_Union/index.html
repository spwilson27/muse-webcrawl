The orders, decorations, and medals of the Soviet Union were state awards established by the government of the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics to reward citizens and foreigners for military valour, outstanding service to the state, and contributions to science, industry, culture and sport. The system grew from a single revolutionary order in the 1920s into one of the largest award systems in the world, and was largely inherited, in altered form, by the Russian Federation after 1991.

History

The first Soviet order, the Order of the Red Banner, was instituted in September 1918 by the Russian SFSR and extended to the whole USSR in 1924. The Order of Lenin, created in 1930, became the country's premier civil and military decoration. During the Great Patriotic War (1941–1945) a large family of campaign and command orders appeared, including the Order of Victory, reserved for the highest commanders, and the Orders of Suvorov, Kutuzov and Alexander Nevsky for officers.

Principal orders

Senior awards included the Order of Victory, the Order of Lenin, the Order of the October Revolution, the Order of the Red Banner, the Order of the Red Banner of Labour, the Order of the Badge of Honour, the Order of Friendship of Peoples and the orders named for Russian military heroes. The title Hero of the Soviet Union, accompanied by the Gold Star medal and the Order of Lenin, was the highest distinction, while Hero of Socialist Labour was its civilian counterpart.

Medals

Below the orders stood hundreds of medals: for military merit, for the defence, liberation and capture of cities during the Second World War, for labour distinction, for long service, and commemorative jubilee medals marking anniversaries of the armed forces, of Lenin's birth and of Victory Day.

Legacy

After the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991, several Soviet orders were retained in the award systems of Russia and other successor states, while others were abolished or redesigned.
