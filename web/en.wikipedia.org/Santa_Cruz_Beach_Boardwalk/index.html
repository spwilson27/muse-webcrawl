The Santa Cruz Beach Boardwalk is an oceanfront amusement park in Santa Cruz, California, founded in 1907. Operated by the family-owned Santa Cruz Seaside Company, it is California's oldest surviving amusement park and one of the last classic seaside boardwalks on the Pacific coast, stretching along Main Beach beside Monterey Bay.

Attractions

The park's centrepiece is the Giant Dipper, a wooden roller coaster built in 1924 by Arthur Looff that is a National Historic Landmark and one of the world's most admired wooden coasters. The Looff Carousel, built in 1911 by Charles I. D. Looff, still features its original 1894 Ruth & Sohn band organ and a working ring-toss game in which riders grab steel rings and throw them at a clown target. Other attractions range from the Haunted Castle and Cave Train to modern thrill rides, mini-golf, laser tag and the Casino Arcade.

History

Entrepreneur Fred W. Swanton promoted the resort and opened the boardwalk's casino, pier and plunge in 1904–1907, modelled on Coney Island. The park survived the decline of American boardwalks, the 1989 Loma Prieta earthquake — its epicentre only a few miles away — and remains free-admission, charging per ride. The Cocoanut Grove ballroom within the complex has hosted bands from Benny Goodman to modern rock acts, and the boardwalk stages free summer concerts on the beach.
