Lord of the Dance (hymn)

Lord of the Dance is a hymn with words written by English songwriter Sydney Carter in 1963. The melody is adapted from the American Shaker tune "Simple Gifts". The hymn is well known for its chorus, which features dancing imagery and the refrain "Dance, then, wherever you may be" and "I am the Lord of the Dance, said he."

Background

Carter wrote the lyrics in 1963, drawing on the traditional English carol "Tomorrow Shall Be My Dancing Day" and on the Shaker melody "Simple Gifts". Carter later recounted that he was partly inspired by a statue of the Hindu god Shiva Nataraja ("Lord of the Dance") displayed at the British Museum, and he framed the hymn as a way of presenting the Christian story through dance imagery encompassing the creation, the life of Christ, the crucifixion, and the resurrection.

Content

The verses follow a first-person narrative voice identified with Christ, beginning "I danced in the morning when the world was begun". Successive verses describe dancing with "the Scribes and the Pharisees", being scourged and crucified by those who would not dance, and rising again: "I am the life that'll never, never die". The hymn's imagery of a dance that persists through betrayal and death made it popular at Easter services, weddings, and funerals.

Reception and use

The hymn appears in many modern hymnals, including British, American, and Australian collections. It has been recorded by numerous artists and adapted in other registers, including Michael Flatley's stage show Lord of the Dance, which took its title from the hymn. Carter maintained mixed feelings about its popularity, once remarking that congregations sometimes missed that the "dance" includes suffering as well as celebration.
