Private investigator

A private investigator (often abbreviated to PI or private detective) is a person who can be hired by individuals or groups to undertake investigatory law services. Private investigators often work for attorneys in civil and criminal cases.

History

The profession began in 1833 when Eugène François Vidocq founded Le Bureau des Renseignements in Paris, generally considered the first private detective agency. In the United States, Allan Pinkerton founded the Pinkerton National Detective Agency in 1850, which became famous for guarding Abraham Lincoln and pursuing outlaws.

Work and licensing

Duties include surveillance, background checks, locating missing persons, investigating insurance fraud, and gathering evidence for legal proceedings. Most jurisdictions require licensing, though requirements vary widely; private investigators generally have no police powers and are bound by the same laws as other citizens.

In popular culture

The hardboiled detective of fiction — Sam Spade, Philip Marlowe, and Sherlock Holmes (a "consulting detective") — has shaped the popular image of the profession far beyond its everyday reality.
