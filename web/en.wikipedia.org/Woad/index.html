Woad

SpeciesIsatis tinctoria
FamilyBrassicaceae
UsesBlue dye, traditional medicine

Woad (Isatis tinctoria), also called dyer's woad or glastum, is a flowering plant in the family Brassicaceae native to the steppe and desert zones of the Caucasus, Central Asia, Siberia, and Western Asia. For centuries it was grown in Europe as the principal source of the blue dye indigotin.

History

Woad was used in ancient times by peoples including the Egyptians and, famously, British Celtic tribes, who painted or tattooed their bodies blue before battle — a practice Julius Caesar described in his account of Britain. Leaves are harvested, dried, powdered, and fermented to produce the dye.

Decline

From the 16th century, imports of true indigo from Indigofera tinctoria grown in Asia and the Americas displaced woad in European dyeing, and the last commercial woad mills in England closed in the early 20th century. The root of the plant (banlangen) is used in traditional Chinese medicine.
