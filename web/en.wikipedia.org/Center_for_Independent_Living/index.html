Center for Independent Living

The Center for Independent Living (CIL) in Berkeley, California, founded in 1972, was the first center for independent living in the United States: a community-based, non-residential organisation run by and for people with disabilities. It became the model for hundreds of similar centers across the United States and inspired the worldwide independent living movement.

History

The CIL grew out of activism by students with disabilities at the University of California, Berkeley, including Ed Roberts, who had fought for admission and accessible housing in the 1960s. Frustrated by the lack of services that respected the autonomy of disabled people, Roberts and his colleagues — known as the "Rolling Quads" — established the center to provide peer support, advocacy, and practical services such as wheelchair repair and attendant referral.

Services and influence

Centers for independent living provide core services including information and referral, independent living skills training, peer counseling, individual and systems advocacy, and transition services. The Rehabilitation Act of 1973 and its 1978 amendments created federal funding for such centers, and the model has spread internationally, influencing disability policy and the philosophy that people with disabilities are the best experts on their own needs.
