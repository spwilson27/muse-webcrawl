United States Department of Defense

The United States Department of Defense (DoD) is an executive branch department of the federal government charged with coordinating and supervising the six US armed services: the Army, Navy, Air Force, Marine Corps, Space Force and Coast Guard. Led by the Secretary of Defense, it is headquartered at the Pentagon in Arlington County, Virginia, and is the world's largest employer, with roughly 3.4 million employees — about 1.3 million active-duty service members, over 800,000 civilians, and the rest in the Guard and Reserve. Its annual budget is over $800 billion, larger than the next several countries' military spending combined.

History

For most of US history the armed services were run by two independent cabinets — the Department of War (1789) and the Department of the Navy (1798). Inter-service rivalry during World War II led President Harry S. Truman to push for unification. The National Security Act of 1947 created the National Military Establishment under the first Secretary of Defense, James Forrestal, and created the independent Air Force; amendments in 1949 renamed it the Department of Defense and strengthened the Secretary's authority. The Goldwater–Nichols Act of 1986 reorganised the department, strengthening the Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff and the unified combatant commands.

Structure

The Department's civilian leadership comprises the Office of the Secretary of Defense (including the Deputy Secretary, under secretaries for policy, intelligence and security, acquisition and sustainment, and comptroller), the Joint Staff, and the military departments, each with its own civilian secretary. Operational command runs from the President through the Secretary of Defense to the commanders of the eleven unified combatant commands (CENTCOM, EUCOM, INDOPACOM, NORTHCOM, SOUTHCOM, AFRICOM, SPACECOM, CYBERCOM, SOCOM, STRATCOM and TRANSCOM). The DoD also operates hundreds of agencies and activities, from the Defence Intelligence Agency and National Security Agency to DARPA, the Defense Logistics Agency and the Missile Defense Agency.
