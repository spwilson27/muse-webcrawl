International Seabed Authority

Established1994
HeadquartersKingston, Jamaica
BasisUN Convention on the Law of the Sea

The International Seabed Authority (ISA) is an international organisation established under the 1982 United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea to organise and control mineral-related activities in the international seabed area — the ocean floor beyond national jurisdiction — for the benefit of humankind as a whole.

Mandate

The Authority issues exploration contracts for polymetallic nodules, polymetallic sulphides and cobalt-rich crusts, and develops regulations for their eventual exploitation, alongside requirements for environmental protection of the marine environment.

Debate

Prospective deep-sea mining has drawn criticism from scientists and states concerned about damage to little-understood ecosystems; calls for a moratorium have grown as exploitation regulations are negotiated.
