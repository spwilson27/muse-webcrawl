Dioscuri

The Dioscuri (also Dioskouroi) are the twin brothers Castor and Pollux (Polydeuces) of Greek and Roman mythology. Their mother was Leda, but they had different fathers: Castor was the mortal son of Tyndareus, king of Sparta, while Pollux was the divine son of Zeus, who had seduced Leda in the guise of a swan. They were brothers of Helen of Troy and Clytemnestra.

Castor and Pollux, patrons of sailors and horsemen.

Mythology

The twins were famed horsemen and hunters who took part in the Calydonian boar hunt and the Argonauts' voyage. When Castor was killed, Pollux asked Zeus to share his own immortality with his brother, and the two were transformed into the constellation Gemini, spending alternate periods in Olympus and the underworld. They were regarded as helpers of mankind, patrons of travelers and sailors, appearing as St. Elmo's fire to ships in storms.

Cult

The twins were especially venerated at Sparta and in Rome, where their temple stood in the Forum and where they were said to have fought beside the Romans at the Battle of Lake Regillus (c. 496 BCE). Their feast day in Rome was July 15. The cult reflects an Indo-European divine-twin motif found in other traditions.
