The Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency (DARPA) is a research and development agency of the United States Department of Defense responsible for the development of emerging technologies for use by the military. Originally known as the Advanced Research Projects Agency (ARPA), it was created in February 1958 by President Dwight D. Eisenhower in response to the Soviet launch of Sputnik 1.

Mission and organization

DARPA's mission is to make pivotal investments in breakthrough technologies for national security — in its own phrasing, to "prevent strategic surprise" and to create surprise for adversaries. The agency is deliberately small and flat: roughly 100 program managers, hired for limited terms of typically three to five years, each run a portfolio of funded projects performed by universities, industry, and government laboratories. DARPA owns no laboratories of its own.

Notable projects

DARPA-funded research underlay the ARPANET and hence the Internet, packet switching, stealth aircraft technology (Have Blue, leading to the F-117), precision-guided munitions, unmanned aerial vehicles, the Global Positioning System's miniaturized receivers, computer graphics and time-sharing, Siri's predecessor CALO, autonomous vehicles (through the DARPA Grand Challenges of 2004–2007), and early mRNA vaccine platform research. Famous programs include Project Orion's early studies, the Saturn V-era space work transferred to NASA, and the modern Liberty Lifter, AI Cyber Challenge, and hypersonics programs.
