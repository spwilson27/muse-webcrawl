Orders of magnitude (length)

An order of magnitude is a factor of ten. This page lists lengths by their order of magnitude in metres, from the smallest scales contemplated by physics — the Planck length, about 1.6 × 10−35 m — to the diameter of the observable universe, about 8.8 × 1026 m. It provides a way of comparing the sizes of objects across the whole range of known scales.

Subatomic and atomic scales

Below 10−15 m (one femtometre) lie quarks, electrons (point-like as far as experiments can tell) and the Planck length. The proton is about 1.7 fm across. Atoms range from roughly 60 to 520 picometres (10−10 m), and molecules such as DNA are a few nanometres wide; the wavelength of visible light spans about 380 to 750 nm.

Human scales

From millimetres (insects, grains of sand) through metres (human height, about 1.7 m) to kilometres (Mount Everest, 8,849 m), this is the range of direct everyday experience. The largest structures, such as the Great Wall of China and the largest dams, extend to hundreds of kilometres.

Planetary and astronomical scales

Earth's diameter is about 1.27 × 107 m; the Sun's is about 1.4 × 109 m. The astronomical unit is about 1.5 × 1011 m and a light-year 9.46 × 1015 m. The Milky Way is roughly 1021 m across, and the observable universe about 8.8 × 1026 m.
