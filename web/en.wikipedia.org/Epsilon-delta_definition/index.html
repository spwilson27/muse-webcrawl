Epsilon-delta definition of limit

(ε, δ)-definition of limit

Field
Mathematical analysis
Developed by
Cauchy; rigorised by Weierstrass (19th century)

In calculus, the (ε, δ)-definition of limit ("epsilon–delta definition of limit") is a formalisation of the notion of limit. It states that the limit of a function f(x) as x approaches a point c equals L if, for every ε > 0, there exists a δ > 0 such that whenever 0 < |x − c| < δ, it follows that |f(x) − L| < ε.

Interpretation

Informally: the values of f(x) can be made arbitrarily close to L (within any prescribed tolerance ε) by taking x sufficiently close to, but not equal to, c (within a distance δ). The definition underlies rigorous treatment of continuity, derivatives, and integrals.

History

Newton and Leibniz used infinitesimals informally; Augustin-Louis Cauchy gave a verbal definition of limit in 1821, and Karl Weierstrass recast it in the quantified ε–δ form that became standard, eliminating reliance on infinitesimals in standard analysis. Bernard Bolzano had anticipated a rigorous arithmetical definition in 1817.
