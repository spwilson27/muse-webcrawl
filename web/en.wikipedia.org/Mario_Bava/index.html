Mario Bava

Born31 July 1914, San Remo, Italy
Died25 April 1980, Rome
OccupationFilm director, cinematographer

Mario Bava (31 July 1914 – 25 April 1980) was an Italian filmmaker who worked across many genres, directing some of the most influential horror films of the twentieth century. Trained as a painter and cinematographer, he was renowned for creating striking effects with tiny budgets and vivid coloured lighting.

Career

Bava began as a cinematographer, frequently completing films for other directors, before his official directorial debut with Black Sunday (1960), a Gothic horror classic starring Barbara Steele. Black Sabbath (1963), an anthology introduced by Boris Karloff, followed.

Giallo and influence

With The Girl Who Knew Too Much (1963) and especially Blood and Black Lace (1964), Bava codified the giallo thriller, while A Bay of Blood (1971) pioneered slasher-film conventions later adopted by the Friday the 13th series. His other notable works include Planet of the Vampires (1965), Kill, Baby, Kill (1966), Danger: Diabolik (1968) and Lisa and the Devil (1973).

Legacy

Directors including Martin Scorsese, Quentin Tarantino, Tim Burton and Guillermo del Toro have cited Bava as an influence. His son Lamberto Bava also became a horror director, collaborating with Dario Argento.
