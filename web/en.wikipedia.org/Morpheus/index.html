Morpheus (Ancient Greek: Μορφεύς, "fashioner" or "shaper", derived from μορφή, morphē, "form, shape") is a god associated with sleep and dreams in Greek mythology. In Ovid's Metamorphoses he is the son of Somnus (Hypnos) and appears in dreams in human form. From his name the morphine alkaloid and the phrase "in the arms of Morpheus" (to be asleep) are derived.

Mythology

Morpheus is one of the thousand sons of Hypnos, the god of sleep, collectively called the Oneiroi. Ovid distinguishes three of them: Morpheus, who appears in dreams in the shape of men; Phobetor (or Icelus), who takes the form of animals; and Phantasos, who assumes the shape of inanimate objects. Morpheus, the most powerful, is entrusted with the dreams of kings and heroes.

In Book 11 of the Metamorphoses, the goddess Juno sends Iris to the cave of Somnus to ask that Morpheus appear to Alcyone in a dream in the form of her drowned husband Ceyx, to tell her of his death. Morpheus duly performs the imitation of Ceyx's voice and bearing.

In later culture

Morpheus has been reimagined in modern literature and media, notably as Dream of the Endless in Neil Gaiman's comic series The Sandman (1989–1996), and as the character Morpheus, played by Laurence Fishburne, in The Matrix films.
