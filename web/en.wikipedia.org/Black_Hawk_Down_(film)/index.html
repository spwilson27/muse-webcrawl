Black Hawk Down is a 2001 war film directed by Ridley Scott and produced by Jerry Bruckheimer. It is based on the 1999 non-fiction book of the same name by journalist Mark Bowden, which recounts the 1993 Battle of Mogadishu during the U.S. military intervention in Somalia.

Plot

In October 1993, a force of U.S. Army Rangers, Delta Force operators, and 160th SOAR helicopter crews launches a daylight raid into Mogadishu to capture lieutenants of warlord Mohamed Farrah Aidid. When two UH-60 Black Hawk helicopters are shot down by rocket-propelled grenades, the mission becomes a desperate overnight rescue and survival battle through the city's streets.

Production

The film was shot principally in Morocco, standing in for Mogadishu, with cooperation from the U.S. Department of Defense, which supplied helicopters and personnel. The ensemble cast trained with the actual military units depicted.

Reception and awards

The film was praised for its technical realism and intense combat sequences, and criticized in some quarters for its portrayal of Somalis. It won two Academy Awards, for Best Film Editing and Best Sound, from four nominations.
