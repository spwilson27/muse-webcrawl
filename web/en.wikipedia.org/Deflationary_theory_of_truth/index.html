Deflationary theory of truth

Portrait associated with the deflationary approach to truth

The deflationary theory of truth, also called deflationism, is a family of views holding that truth is not a substantive property requiring philosophical analysis. On this view, to assert that a statement is true is simply to assert the statement itself: "'Snow is white' is true" says no more than "snow is white."

Variants

Forms of deflationism include the redundancy theory associated with Frank Ramsey, disquotationalism developed by W. V. O. Quine, and the prosentential theory of Robert Brandom and Dorothy Grover. Paul Horwich's minimalism is a closely related position.

Criticism

Critics argue that deflationism cannot explain the use of truth as a goal of inquiry or in generalizations such as "everything the witness said is true."
