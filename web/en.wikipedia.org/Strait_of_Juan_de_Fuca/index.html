Strait of Juan de Fuca

The Strait of Juan de Fuca (officially named Juan de Fuca Strait in Canada) is a body of water about 154 km (96 mi) long that is the Salish Sea's outlet to the Pacific Ocean. The international boundary between Canada and the United States runs down its centre. It was named in 1788 by the English captain John Meares, of the ship Felice, for Juan de Fuca, the Greek navigator who sailed for Spain and claimed to have discovered the passage in 1592.

Geography

The strait separates Vancouver Island (British Columbia) to the north from the Olympic Peninsula (Washington state) to the south. At its western end it opens into the Pacific Ocean; at its eastern end it branches into Haro Strait and Rosario Strait, leading to the Strait of Georgia, and south into Puget Sound via Admiralty Inlet. Ports on the strait include Victoria, British Columbia, and Port Angeles, Washington, connected by a vehicle ferry.

Salish Sea

In 2009 the strait was formally included, with Puget Sound and the Strait of Georgia, in the name "Salish Sea", recognising the connected marine ecosystem and the Coast Salish peoples who have lived along its shores for millennia.

Climate and geology

The strait was carved by glacial action during the last ice age. It funnels Pacific weather systems eastward and experiences strong tidal currents and frequent fog. The Juan de Fuca tectonic plate offshore takes its name from the strait.
