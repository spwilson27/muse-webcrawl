Sports car racing

Sports car racing is a form of motorsport road racing that uses sports cars equipped with two seats and enclosed wheels. Cars are divided into purpose-built sports prototypes and production-based grand tourer (GT) cars, which often race together in multiple classes on the same track.

Characteristics

Sports car races are frequently endurance events lasting from several hours to 24 hours, with teams of two or more drivers sharing each car. Races take place on road courses rather than ovals, and strategy involving pit stops, driver changes, and fuel and tyre management is central. Multiple classes mean faster prototypes continually overtake slower GT traffic, making traffic management a key skill.

Major series and races

The most prestigious event is the 24 Hours of Le Mans in France, first held in 1923, which together with the Monaco Grand Prix and the Indianapolis 500 forms the Triple Crown of Motorsport. Other classic endurance races include the 24 Hours of Daytona, the 12 Hours of Sebring, the 24 Hours of Spa, and the Nürburgring 24 Hours. The FIA World Endurance Championship (WEC) and the IMSA SportsCar Championship are the leading contemporary series.

History

Sports car racing flourished in the 1950s and 1960s with marques such as Ferrari, Jaguar, Aston Martin, Maserati, and Porsche, and reached a famous peak with the Ford–Ferrari rivalry at Le Mans. The Group C era of the 1980s and the current Hypercar and LMDh regulations have revived factory competition at the top level.
