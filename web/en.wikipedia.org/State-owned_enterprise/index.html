State-owned enterprise

State-owned enterprises are prominent in energy, transport, and finance.

A state-owned enterprise (SOE) is a business enterprise where the government or state has significant control through full, majority, or substantial minority ownership. SOEs are common in strategic sectors such as energy, utilities, transport, defence, and banking, where governments seek to guarantee services, control natural monopolies, or pursue policy objectives beyond profit.

Characteristics and rationale

Governments create or retain SOEs to provide public goods, develop infrastructure, manage natural resources, and stabilize employment or prices. SOEs range from fully nationalized industries to partially privatized listed companies with government golden shares. Critics argue that SOEs can suffer from political interference, soft budget constraints, and inefficiency, while supporters point to public-service obligations and long-term strategic investment.

Examples

Large SOEs include Saudi Aramco, China's state-owned banks and energy giants such as Sinopec and State Grid, Norway's Equinor, and India's public sector undertakings. Waves of privatization in the 1980s and 1990s reduced SOEs' share in many economies, though they remain a major feature of the global economy, particularly in emerging markets.
