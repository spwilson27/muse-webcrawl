Wigwam

A wigwam, also called a wickiup, is a domed or cone-shaped dwelling used by certain Native American tribes and First Nations peoples, particularly in the northeastern woodlands of North America. The term comes from the Eastern Algonquian word for "their house." Wigwams are semi-permanent structures, distinct from the portable tipi of the Plains peoples.

Construction

A wigwam is built around a framework of bent saplings tied together to form an arched dome, then covered with woven mats of cattail or reed, bark sheets (often birch or elm), grass, or animal hides. A hole at the top lets out smoke from the central fire. A single wigwam might house one family, while larger versions, sometimes called longhouses in elongated form, sheltered several families.

Peoples and regions

Wigwams were used by Algonquian-speaking peoples including the Abenaki, Ojibwe, Kickapoo, and Wampanoag, and in the Southwest the similar wickiup was used by Apache and other peoples. The structure's materials and shape varied with the local environment. Wigwams continue to be built today for ceremonial and educational purposes.
