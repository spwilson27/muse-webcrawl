Minangkabau people

Minangkabau
Region: West Sumatra, Indonesia
Population: ~6.5 million
Language: Minangkabau (Malayic)
Religion: Islam (majority)
Known for: the world's largest matrilineal society

The Minangkabau (Minangkabau: Urang Minang) are an Austronesian ethnic group native to the Minangkabau Highlands of West Sumatra, Indonesia. They are the world's largest matrilineal society, in which property and family names pass through the female line, coexisting with Islam, the religion of nearly all Minangkabau since the 16th–17th centuries.

Culture and social organization

Minangkabau society is organized by adat (customary law) alongside Islamic law, expressed in the maxim adat basandi syarak, syarak basandi Kitabullah — "custom founded upon sharia, sharia founded upon the Qur'an." Ancestral property (harta pusaka), including rice land and the great communal houses called rumah gadang, is owned by women and transmitted from mother to daughter, while clan leadership roles (penghulu) are held by men chosen by the women of the clan. The distinctive rumah gadang, with its multiple upswept gables said to resemble buffalo horns, reflects the legendary origin of the name Minangkabau—menang kerbau, "victorious buffalo."

Merantau and diaspora

A defining institution is merantau, the tradition of voluntary out-migration by young men to study, trade, and seek fortune abroad, returning with wealth and experience. Minangkabau migrants spread across Indonesia and Malaysia (notably Negeri Sembilan), where they are prominent in commerce, politics, and cuisine; Minang (Padang) restaurants, serving dishes such as rendang and nasi padang, are ubiquitous across Indonesia. Minangkabau intellectuals were central to Indonesian nationalism and early republican politics, including figures such as Mohammad Hatta, the country's first vice president, and Tan Malaka; the celebrated author Abdul Muis and the modernist scholar Hamka also came from this tradition. The Padri War (1803–1837) pitted Islamic reformists against adat traditionalists before Dutch colonial intervention.
