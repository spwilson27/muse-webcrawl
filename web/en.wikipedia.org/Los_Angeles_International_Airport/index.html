Los Angeles International Airport

Los Angeles International Airport (IATA: LAX), commonly referred to by its airport code, is the primary international airport serving Los Angeles and the surrounding metropolitan area in California. Located in the Westchester neighborhood along the Pacific coast, it is one of the busiest airports in the world by passenger traffic, handling over 75 million passengers in peak years before 2020 and serving as a major hub for several U.S. airlines.

History

The airport opened in 1928 as Mines Field, a dirt strip on farmland, and became the city's municipal airport. The distinctive Theme Building, with its futuristic arches resembling a flying saucer, was completed in 1961 for the jet age. LAX expanded repeatedly, adding the Tom Bradley International Terminal in 1984 ahead of the Los Angeles Olympics. A multi-billion-dollar modernization program in the 2010s and 2020s added the Midfield Satellite Concourse, extensive terminal renovations, and an automated people mover connecting to rail transit.

Facilities

LAX has nine passenger terminals arranged in a U-shape around a central parking and road system, plus four parallel runways. It is a major international gateway with nonstop service to destinations across North America, Europe, Asia, Latin America, and Oceania.
