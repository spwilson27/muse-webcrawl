Chandela dynasty

The Chandela (also spelled Chandella, Chandel) dynasty was an Indian Rajput clan that ruled much of the Bundelkhand region of central India (then called Jejakabhukti) between the 9th and the 13th centuries. The Chandelas are celebrated as the builders of the magnificent temple complex at Khajuraho, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and as defenders of central India against the early Muslim invasions from the northwest.

History

The dynasty was founded by Nannuka in the early 9th century, initially as feudatories of the Gurjara-Pratihara empire. His descendants, including Vakpati and the father-son pair Jayashakti and Vijayashakti, consolidated power around their strongholds at Khajuraho, Mahoba and Kalinjar. Yashodharman (Lakshmanavarman) expanded the kingdom, and under Dhanga (r. c. 950–999) the Chandelas became fully independent. Vidyadhara (r. c. 1017–1029), one of the most powerful rulers, successfully resisted Mahmud of Ghazni's attacks on Kalinjar in 1019 and 1022 and commissioned the great Kandariya Mahadeva Temple at Khajuraho.

Later Chandelas fought the Kalachuris, the Paramaras and the Ghurid armies. Paramardi (r. c. 1165–1203), the last important Chandela king, appears in bardic epics such as the Alha-Khand as the overlord of the heroes Alha and Udal; he was defeated by Prithviraj Chauhan and then by Qutb ud-Din Aibak, losing Kalinjar in 1203. The dynasty lingered in reduced form into the 13th and possibly 14th centuries before disappearing from record.

Art and architecture

Chandela rule is remembered above all for the Western Group of Temples at Khajuraho (c. 950–1050), built in the Nagara style with soaring shikharas, intricate stone carving and famous erotic sculpture. Other Chandela monuments include temples and waterworks at Mahoba, Ajaygarh and Kalinjar, and the Chausath Yogini and Lalgun Mahadev temples among the earliest surviving structures of the group.
