Nakhodka is a port city in Primorsky Krai, Russia, located on the Trudny Peninsula jutting into Nakhodka Bay of the Sea of Japan, about 85 kilometres (53 mi) east of Vladivostok. It is the third-largest city in Primorsky Krai after Vladivostok and Ussuriysk, and is notable for its ice-free port, one of the most important commercial harbours on the Russian Pacific coast.

History

The bay around Nakhodka was first charted by Russian sailors in 1859, and a hydrographic post and settlement were established in the 1860s. The modern city grew rapidly after the Second World War, when Nakhodka was developed as an alternative deep-water port to Vladivostok, which was closed to foreign shipping as a naval base. Nakhodka received town status in 1950. During the Soviet era it became the principal Pacific gateway for trade with Japan and for ferry and container traffic; the nearby port of Vostochny, one of Russia's largest container terminals, was built in the 1970s.

Economy

Nakhodka's economy centres on its port complex, which handles coal, containers, timber, metals and petroleum products. The specialised terminals at Vostochny Port and the oil terminal at Kozmino, the eastern terminus of the Eastern Siberia–Pacific Ocean oil pipeline, are among the largest on Russia's Pacific coast. Fishing, ship repair and fish processing are also significant employers.

Geography and climate

The city occupies a hilly coastal area around the sheltered Nakhodka Bay. It has a monsoon-influenced humid continental climate, with cold, dry winters and warm, humid summers; the bay rarely freezes completely, keeping the port usable year-round. The twin hills known as Sestra and Brat ("Sister" and "Brother") are local landmarks overlooking the mouth of the bay.

Transport

Nakhodka is connected by rail to the Trans-Siberian Railway network and by road to Vladivostok. Passenger ferries have historically linked the city with ports in Japan and South Korea.
