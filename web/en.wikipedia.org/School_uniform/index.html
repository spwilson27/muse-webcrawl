School uniform


Students in school uniform

A school uniform is a uniform worn by students primarily for school or other educational institutions. They are common in primary and secondary schools in many countries, including the United Kingdom, Japan, Australia, India and much of Africa and the Caribbean, while practice in North America and continental Europe varies widely.

History

Uniforms are often traced to 16th-century England, where charity schools such as Christ's Hospital dressed pupils in distinctive clothing. In the 19th and 20th centuries uniform dress spread with mass public education, often modelled on military or clerical dress, with blazers, ties, skirts or trousers in school colours.

Arguments

Supporters argue uniforms promote equality by reducing visible differences in wealth, simplify mornings for families, foster school identity and may improve behaviour and focus. Critics contend they restrict self-expression, can be costly from designated suppliers, raise gender-related concerns, and lack clear evidence of academic benefit. Legal cases in several countries have addressed uniforms in relation to religious dress, hairstyles and freedom of expression.

Variations

Styles range from formal blazer-and-tie ensembles to simple polo shirts with a school logo. Some schools enforce detailed dress codes rather than a full uniform, and many systems allow exemptions or adjustments on religious or medical grounds.
