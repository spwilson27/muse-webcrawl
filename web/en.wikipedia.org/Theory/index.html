Theory

A theory is a rational type of abstract thinking about a phenomenon, or the results of such thinking. The process of contemplative and rational thinking is often associated with processes such as observational study or research. Theories may be scientific, belong to a non-scientific discipline, or be informal.

Scientific theory

In science, a theory is a well-substantiated explanation of some aspect of the natural world, based on a body of facts that have been repeatedly confirmed through observation and experiment. Such fact-supported theories are not "guesses" but reliable accounts of the real world. Examples include the theory of evolution by natural selection, the germ theory of disease, the theory of relativity, and the theory of plate tectonics.

Scientific theories are testable and make falsifiable predictions. They describe the causal elements responsible for a particular natural phenomenon and are used to explain and predict aspects of the physical universe or specific areas of inquiry. A theory differs from a hypothesis, which is a proposed explanation that has yet to be rigorously tested, and from a scientific law, which describes an observed pattern without explaining it.

Other uses

Outside science, "theory" names bodies of abstract or speculative thought: literary theory, music theory, mathematical theory (where a theory is a set of theorems within a formal system, as in number theory or group theory), philosophical theories, and legal theory. In everyday speech the word often means little more than an untested hunch, a sense quite distinct from its scientific usage.

Etymology

The word derives from the Greek theōria, "contemplation" or "speculation", literally a looking at or viewing.
