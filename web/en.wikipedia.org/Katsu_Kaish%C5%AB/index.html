Katsu Kaishū (勝 海舟, 12 February 1823 – 19 January 1899), born Katsu Yoshikuni and later known as Katsu Awa, was a Japanese naval officer and statesman of the Bakumatsu and early Meiji periods. He is best remembered as the commissioner who negotiated the bloodless surrender of Edo Castle in 1868, an act widely credited with sparing the city from destruction during the Boshin War.

Career

Katsu rose through the Tokugawa shogunate's navy, training in Western naval science and commanding the warship Kanrin Maru on its 1860 voyage to San Francisco — the first Japanese vessel to cross the Pacific. He later served as a commissioner of the shogunate navy and as army minister.

Later life

After the Meiji Restoration, Katsu served the new government as a privy councillor and naval minister, and was raised to the rank of count in the kazoku peerage. His memoirs and diary remain important sources for the period.
