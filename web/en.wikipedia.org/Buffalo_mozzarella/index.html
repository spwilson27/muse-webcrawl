Buffalo mozzarella (Italian: mozzarella di bufala) is a mozzarella made from the milk of the Italian Mediterranean buffalo. It is a dairy product traditionally manufactured in Campania, especially in the provinces of Caserta and Salerno. The term "mozzarella" derives from the Italian verb mozzare, "to cut off", referring to how the stretched curd is cut into individual balls.

Production

Buffalo milk is much richer than cow's milk, with roughly double the fat and higher protein and lactose. It is pasteurised or used raw, curdled with natural whey starter and calf rennet, and the curd is then stretched in hot water (pasta filata method), shaped, firmed in cold water and brined. The finished cheese has a thin, glossy rind, a stringy layered texture and a slightly tangy, rich flavour. It is perishable and traditionally eaten within a few days of production.

Protected status

Mozzarella di bufala Campana has held Denominazione di Origine Protetta (DOP) status in Italy since 1993 and EU protected designation of origin status since 1996. Production is restricted to defined municipalities in Campania, Lazio, Apulia and Molise. In 2008 a dioxin contamination scare in Campania temporarily damaged exports, and producers have campaigned against imitation "buffalo mozzarella" made from cow's milk or produced outside the designated area.

Culinary use

Buffalo mozzarella is prized in Caprese salad with tomato and basil, on Neapolitan pizza, and simply eaten fresh with olive oil. Compared with cow's-milk mozzarella (fior di latte), it is softer, creamier and more flavourful.
