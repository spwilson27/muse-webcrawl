Number-one record

FieldMusic industry
Related chartsBillboard Hot 100, UK Singles Chart

A number-one record is a recording — a single or an album — that occupies the top position on a national or genre music chart for at least one week. Reaching number one is widely treated as a benchmark of commercial success, and artists' career summaries conventionally tally the number of chart-toppers they have achieved and the total weeks spent at the summit.

Charts

In the United States, the benchmark singles chart is the Billboard Hot 100 (launched in 1958), which blends sales, radio airplay, and streaming; albums are ranked on the Billboard 200. In the United Kingdom, the Official Singles Chart, compiled since 1952 (and by the Official Charts Company in the modern era), plays the equivalent role. Many other countries maintain their own charts, historically published by trade magazines and now by industry bodies and streaming-era compilers.

Records and milestones

The Beatles hold the record for the most number-one singles on the Billboard Hot 100, with twenty. On the UK chart, Elvis Presley leads with twenty-one. Among the longest-running number ones are "Old Town Road" by Lil Nas X, which spent nineteen weeks atop the Hot 100 in 2019, and "(Everything I Do) I Do It for You" by Bryan Adams, which held the UK summit for sixteen weeks in 1991. A "one-hit wonder" is an act whose only significant chart success is a single hit — sometimes itself a number-one record.

Cultural significance

Seasonal chart battles, most famously the race for the UK Christmas number one, have become media events, and chart positions feature prominently in marketing, obituaries, and record-company histories. Changes in consumption — from sheet music and vinyl to downloads and streaming — have repeatedly prompted changes in chart methodology, and therefore in what a number-one record represents.
