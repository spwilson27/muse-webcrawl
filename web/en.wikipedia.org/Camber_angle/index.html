Camber angle

Camber angle is one of the three basic angles used in suspension alignment for vehicles — the angle made by the wheels relative to the vertical axis of the vehicle, viewed from the front or rear. If the top of the wheel leans outward, away from the vehicle, the camber is positive; if the top leans inward toward the vehicle, the camber is negative.

Effects

Camber affects the tyre's contact patch and therefore grip and wear. Negative camber on the front wheels is commonly used on performance and racing cars: when the car body rolls in a corner, the loaded outside tyre gains a more favourable contact patch, increasing cornering grip. However, excessive negative camber reduces straight-line braking and acceleration traction and causes accelerated wear on the inner shoulder of the tyre. Positive camber is sometimes used on heavily loaded vehicles so that the wheels become vertical under load, and was common on early automobiles to reduce steering effort and account for crowned roads.

Adjustment

Camber is set during wheel alignment, together with caster and toe. Depending on suspension design, it may be adjusted by eccentric bolts, shims, slotted strut mounts or adjustable control arms. Some suspension types, such as double wishbones, gain negative camber as they compress; MacPherson struts gain relatively little. Uneven camber side-to-side can cause the vehicle to pull toward the side with more positive camber.
