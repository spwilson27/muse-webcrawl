Indus river dolphin

Scientific namePlatanista minor
HabitatIndus River system, Pakistan
Populationc. 1,800–2,000 (surveys 2017)
StatusEndangered (IUCN)

The Indus river dolphin (Platanista minor), locally called bhulan, is a freshwater dolphin endemic to the Indus River system of Pakistan. Functionally blind, it navigates and hunts almost entirely by echolocation in the river's turbid waters. It is one of the world's rarest cetaceans and a flagship species for the Indus ecosystem.

Description

Adults reach about 2–2.6 metres and have a long, slender beak lined with many sharp teeth, a stocky grey-brown body, tiny eyes lacking a lens, and a low triangular dorsal hump instead of a fin. They often swim on their sides, trailing a flipper along the bottom, and surface with a characteristic loud exhalation.

Distribution and threats

Once ranging from the Indus delta up the main stem and tributaries, the dolphin was fragmented by irrigation barrages built during the 20th century into isolated subpopulations, the largest between the Sukkur and Guddu barrages in Sindh. Threats include entanglement in fishing gear, water abstraction, pollution, and stranding in irrigation canals, from which rescue programmes return animals to the river.

Conservation

Protected in Pakistan since the 1970s, the species has slowly recovered; surveys estimated roughly 1,800–2,000 individuals in 2017, up from about 1,200 in 2001. The Indus Dolphin Reserve in Sindh and community-based monitoring underpin conservation efforts.
