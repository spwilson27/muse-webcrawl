House of Godwin

The House of Godwin (or the Godwins) was an Anglo-Saxon family that was one of the most powerful noble houses in England during the late Anglo-Saxon period, in the first half of the 11th century. Its founder was Godwin, Earl of Wessex, who rose to prominence under King Cnut and became the richest and most influential magnate in the kingdom after the king himself.

Godwin's sons included Harold Godwinson, who became King Harold II of England in 1066, and Tostig Godwinson, Earl of Northumbria. His daughter Edith married King Edward the Confessor. Members of the family held several of the great earldoms of England, including Wessex, East Anglia, Northumbria, Kent, and Herefordshire.

Rise and fall

The family was exiled by Edward the Confessor in 1051 but returned by force the following year. After Edward's death in January 1066, Harold was crowned king. Tostig, who had been exiled from Northumbria, allied with Harald Hardrada of Norway against his brother; both were killed at the Battle of Stamford Bridge. Harold himself was defeated and killed at the Battle of Hastings on 14 October 1066 by William of Normandy, ending the power of the House of Godwin and Anglo-Saxon rule in England.
