Ayran
TypeYogurt drink
Main ingredientsYogurt, water, salt
RegionTurkey, Iran, Balkans, Central Asia
ServedChilled, sometimes with mint

Ayran is a cold savoury yogurt-based beverage made by mixing yogurt with water and salt. It is popular across Turkey, Iran (where it is called doogh), Armenia (tahn), Azerbaijan, the Balkans and Central Asia, and is widely regarded as the national drink of Turkey.

Preparation

Traditionally ayran was made by churning yogurt in skins or wooden churns, which also yielded butter. The modern preparation simply blends yogurt, cold water and a pinch of salt until frothy; variations add mint, cucumber juice or a splash of sparkling water. In some regions it is made with sheep's or goat's milk yogurt, giving a tangier flavour.

Consumption

Ayran is served chilled with grilled meats, kebabs and pastries such as börek, and is sold everywhere from street stalls to packaged bottles in supermarkets. In Iran, doogh is frequently carbonated and flavoured with dried mint. The drink's salt content makes it a refreshing accompaniment in hot climates, and it also features in rural hospitality customs across Anatolia.

Related drinks

Similar diluted yogurt drinks include Indian lassi (often sweetened), Bangladeshi borhani, and the strained-yogurt drink tan in Armenia.
