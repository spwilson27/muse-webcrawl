Saeculum

The saeculum marked the longest span of a human lifetime and the renewal of an age.

A saeculum is a length of time roughly equal to the potential lifetime of a human being, or equivalently the complete renewal of a human population — the time by which everyone alive at one moment has died. The word comes from Latin, originally meaning a generation or an age.

Roman usage

Among the Etruscans and Romans the saeculum was a civilizational period, calculated variously at 100 or 110 years. Its end was marked by the Ludi Saeculares, the Secular Games, celebrated in Rome with sacrifices and theatrical performances. Augustus staged celebrated games in 17 BCE, for which Horace composed the Carmen Saeculare; later emperors including Claudius, Domitian, and Septimius Severus held their own, reckoning the cycle from different starting points. Legend held that the saeculum's span was announced by portents and recorded in the Sibylline Books.

Modern usage

In geology and cosmology the term is occasionally used for long epochs. Historians William Strauss and Neil Howe borrowed "saeculum" in their generational theory for a full cycle of four generations, roughly 80 to 100 years, comprising a recurring sequence of social eras or "turnings." In medieval Latin, saeculum also gave rise to the concept of the "secular" — that which belongs to the present age or world, as opposed to the eternal realm of the Church.
