Chester A. Arthur

Chester Alan Arthur (October 5, 1829 – November 18, 1886) was the 21st president of the United States, serving from 1881 to 1885. He succeeded to the presidency upon the assassination of James A. Garfield and, despite his background in machine politics, surprised contemporaries by championing civil service reform.

Early life and career

Arthur was born in Fairfield, Vermont, the son of a Baptist preacher, and graduated from Union College in 1848. He became a lawyer in New York City, working with the firm of Erastus D. Culver, where he participated in notable civil rights cases. During the Civil War he served as quartermaster general of New York. After the war he became a leader of the Republican Stalwart faction associated with Senator Roscoe Conkling, and President Ulysses S. Grant appointed him Collector of the Port of New York in 1871, one of the most lucrative patronage posts in the country.

Presidency

Arthur was elected vice president on the 1880 Republican ticket and became president on September 20, 1881, after Garfield's death. His administration signed the Pendleton Civil Service Reform Act of 1883, which began replacing political patronage with merit-based appointments. Arthur also oversaw naval modernization, vetoed an early version of the Chinese Exclusion Act before signing a revised version, and pressed for tariff reform.

Later life

Failing to win the 1884 Republican nomination, Arthur retired to New York City. He died of Bright's disease on November 18, 1886. Historians generally credit him with presiding honestly and ably despite low initial expectations.
