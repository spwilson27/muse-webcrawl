Shoegaze

Stylistic originsAlternative rock, indie rock, dream pop, noise pop, post-punk
Cultural originsLate 1980s, Ireland and the United Kingdom
Key actsMy Bloody Valentine, Slowdive, Ride, Lush, Cocteau Twins (precursors)

Shoegaze (originally called shoegazing) is a subgenre of indie and alternative rock that emerged in Ireland and the United Kingdom in the late 1980s. It is characterised by ethereal, heavily processed vocals buried in the mix, guitar distortion and feedback, and dense layers of effects — especially reverb, delay and tremolo — that blur instruments into a shimmering "wall of sound." The name derives from performers' tendency to stand still on stage, heads bowed, gazing down at the effects pedals at their feet; the British music press coined it, initially half-mockingly, around 1990–91.

History

Shoegaze grew from the legacy of the Velvet Underground, the Jesus and Mary Chain's feedback-drenched Psychocandy (1985), the Cocteau Twins' dream pop, and American noise rock such as Sonic Youth and Dinosaur Jr. Its landmark recordings include My Bloody Valentine's Isn't Anything (1988) and the genre-defining Loveless (1991), whose painstaking production — built from glide-guitar, sampling and studio layering — nearly bankrupted Creation Records and became one of the most acclaimed albums of the decade. Other central bands were Slowdive, Ride, Lush, Chapterhouse, Swervedriver and Moose. The scene that celebrated itself, as one magazine jibe had it, was displaced in the mid-1990s by Britpop in the UK and grunge in the US, and most key bands split or changed direction.

Revival

From the 2000s onward, shoegaze was revived and reassessed: My Bloody Valentine reformed and released m b v (2013), Slowdive and Ride reunited to acclaim, and a new generation — including American and international acts blending shoegaze with metal ("blackgaze"), electronic music and emo — made it one of the most influential guitar styles of the 21st century.
