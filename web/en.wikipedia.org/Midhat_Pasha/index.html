Ahmed Şefik Midhat Pasha (18 October 1822 – 8 May 1883) was an Ottoman statesman, reformer, and grand vizier, best known as one of the leading architects of the Tanzimat reforms and the principal author of the Ottoman constitution of 1876, the first constitution in the empire's history, which inaugurated the First Constitutional Era. He is often regarded as a founder of modern Ottoman reformist and liberal thought.

Early life and career

Midhat was born in Istanbul in 1822, the son of a judge (kadı), Hacı Eşref Efendi, originally from Rusçuk (Ruse) in Bulgaria. Educated partly at home and partly in medreses, he entered the imperial bureaucracy at a young age, serving in the offices of the grand vizier and in provincial postings in Anatolia and Rumelia. He attracted notice for his energy and administrative talent, worked under the reforming statesmen Mustafa Reşid Pasha and Mehmed Emin Âli Pasha, and undertook fact-finding missions in Europe.

Provincial governorships

Midhat's reputation rests heavily on his governorships. As governor of Niš (1861–1864) he built roads, schools, hospitals, and model farms. Appointed first governor of the newly created Danube Vilayet (1864–1868), he undertook a celebrated programme of development — roads and bridges, agricultural credit cooperatives (the origin of the Ottoman agricultural bank), orphanages, and a bilingual Turkish-Bulgarian newspaper. As governor of Baghdad (1869–1872) he reasserted Ottoman authority in Iraq, reformed land tenure and taxation, founded schools and a printing press, and projected Ottoman power toward the Persian Gulf.

Grand vizier and the constitution

Midhat first became grand vizier in 1872 but was dismissed after only a few months. In the crisis of 1876 — amid bankruptcy, revolt in the Balkans, and the deposition of Sultan Abdülaziz — he emerged as the dominant reformist figure. He helped engineer the accession of Abdülhamid II on the promise of a constitution, became grand vizier again in December 1876, and the constitution (Kanun-i Esasi) was promulgated on 23 December 1876, establishing a parliament with an elected chamber.

Downfall and death

Abdülhamid soon turned against him. Midhat was dismissed and exiled in February 1877, on the eve of the Russo-Turkish War. After a period in Europe he was permitted to return and served as governor of Syria (1878–1881) and of Aydın. In 1881 he was arrested, tried at Yıldız Palace on charges of complicity in the death of Abdülaziz, and sentenced to death; the sentence was commuted to exile at Taif in the Hejaz, where he was strangled on 8 May 1883, almost certainly on orders from the palace. His remains were later transferred to Istanbul and buried at the Freedom Monument (Âbide-i Hürriyet) in 1951.

Legacy

Midhat Pasha is remembered in Turkey as a pioneer of constitutional government and in Bulgaria and Iraq for his energetic provincial administration. Markets, schools, and streets across the former Ottoman lands bear his name, including Baghdad's Midhat Pasha market traditions and the covered bazaar Souk Midhat Pacha in Damascus.
