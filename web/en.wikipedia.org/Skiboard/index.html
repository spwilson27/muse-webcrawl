Skiboard

Skiboards are a type of short ski, generally between 75 and 110 cm long — roughly double the length of a ski boot and far shorter than conventional skis — with wide, twin-tipped construction. The sport of using them is called skiboarding, and participants are known as skiboarders. Skiboards resemble a cross between skis and skates and are ridden without poles, allowing spins, tricks and skating-style manoeuvres.

History

Short skis have existed in various forms (such as Austrian Firngleiter glacier skis), but modern skiboarding emerged commercially in the 1990s. Early plastic products were followed by wood-core boards from companies such as Line and Salomon (whose "Snowblade" became a genericised name). The sport featured in the Winter X Games (as "skiboard" slopestyle) around 1999–2000 before being dropped, and survives as a niche discipline with its own world cup circuit.

Equipment

Skiboards use either non-release bindings with hard ski boots, or releasable bindings. Their width and short turning radius make them easy for beginners to learn on, but they are less stable than long skis at speed and in deep powder, and some resorts and insurers treat non-release bindings with caution.
