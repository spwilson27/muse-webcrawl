Boeing 777

The Boeing 777, commonly referred to as the Triple Seven, is an American long-range wide-body airliner developed and manufactured by Boeing Commercial Airplanes. The 777 is the world's largest twinjet and the most-built wide-body airliner. The jetliner was designed to bridge the gap between Boeing's other wide body airplanes, the twin-engined 767 and quad-engined 747, and to replace aging DC-10s and L-1011 TriStars. Developed in consultation with eight major airlines, the 777 program was launched in October 1990, the prototype first flew in June 1994, and the type entered service with United Airlines in June 1995.

Development

The 777 was the first commercial airliner designed entirely by computer (using Dassault's CATIA software) and features fly-by-wire controls, a circular fuselage cross-section, and the largest turbofan engines of any aircraft of its era. Initial variants—the 777-200 and stretched 777-300—were followed by the long-range 777-200ER, 777-200LR (once the world's longest-range airliner), the 777-300ER, and the 777F freighter. The GE90 engines powering the longer-range models have fan diameters exceeding the fuselage width of a Boeing 737 and produce up to about 115,000 pounds of thrust.

Variants

The 777-300ER became the backbone of many long-haul fleets, prized for carrying roughly 400 passengers with twin-engine economics. Emirates is the largest operator. The next-generation 777X family—the 777-8 and 777-9, launched in 2013—adds larger composite wings with folding wingtips, new GE9X engines, and a wider cabin; the 777-9 first flew in January 2020, though certification and entry into service have been repeatedly delayed.

Operational history and safety

The 777 has an excellent safety record overall but has been involved in several notable accidents, including British Airways Flight 38 (2008, fuel icing crash-landing at Heathrow), Asiana Airlines Flight 214 (2013, crash-landing at San Francisco), and the disappearance of Malaysia Airlines Flight 370 (2014), still one of aviation's greatest mysteries; MH17 was shot down over Ukraine later that year. A 2021 engine failure on United Airlines Flight 328 led to groundings of PW4000-powered aircraft pending inspections.

Specifications (777-300ER)

Typical three-class seating is about 365 passengers; maximum range is roughly 7,370 nautical miles (13,650 km); cruising speed is Mach 0.84. The aircraft is 73.9 m long with a wingspan of 64.8 m.
