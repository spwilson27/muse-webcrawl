Raoul Hausmann, co-founder of Berlin Dada and pioneer of photomontage.

Raoul Hausmann (12 July 1886 – 1 February 1971) was an Austrian artist and writer, a central figure of Berlin Dada and, with Hannah Höch, a pioneer of photomontage. Calling himself the "Dadasophe", he helped found Club Dada in Berlin in 1918 and edited the journal Der Dada.

Work

Hausmann developed the photomontage technique — cutting and recombining press photographs and typography — in works such as The Art Critic and the self-portrait collage ABCD. He also created sound poems and "optophonetic" experiments linking speech sounds and visual forms, and produced assemblages including Mechanical Head (The Spirit of Our Time) (c. 1920), a mannequin head fitted with measuring instruments. With Kurt Schwitters he organised the Merz/Dada "Anti-Dada" tours, and he collaborated with Höch and others on satirical attacks on Weimar society and militarism.

Later life

Driven from Germany after the Nazi rise to power, Hausmann lived in Ibiza, Zurich, Prague and finally Limoges, France, working on photography, painting and writing, including his memoir Courier Dada (1958). He died in Limoges in 1971.
