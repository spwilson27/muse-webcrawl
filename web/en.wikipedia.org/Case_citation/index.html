Case citation

Case citation is a system used by legal professionals to identify past court case decisions, whether in series of books called reporters or law reports, or in a neutral fashion that identifies a decision regardless of where it is reported.

Elements

A typical citation to a printed reporter gives the volume number, the reporter abbreviation, and the page on which the decision begins, followed by the court and year in parentheses. For example, Marbury v. Madison, 5 U.S. (1 Cranch) 137 (1803) means the case appears at page 137 of volume 5 of the United States Reports. Pinpoint citations add the page supporting a specific proposition, and parallel citations reference the same decision in multiple reporters.

Styles

In the United States, citation form is governed principally by The Bluebook; in the United Kingdom, the Oxford Standard for the Citation of Legal Authorities (OSCOLA) is standard. Many jurisdictions now assign vendor-neutral citations at the moment of decision, incorporating the year and a sequential paragraph number, as with UK Supreme Court neutral citations such as [2019] UKSC 41.
