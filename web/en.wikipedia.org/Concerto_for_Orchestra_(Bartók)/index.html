Concerto for Orchestra (Bartók)

The Concerto for Orchestra, Sz. 116, BB 123, is a five-movement orchestral work composed by Béla Bartók in 1943. Written in the United States during Bartók's exile from wartime Hungary, it was commissioned by the Koussevitzky Music Foundation in memory of Natalie Koussevitzky and premiered by the Boston Symphony Orchestra under Serge Koussevitzky on 1 December 1944. It has become Bartók's most popular work and a staple of the modern orchestral repertoire.

Composition

Bartók, ill with leukaemia and in financial difficulty, composed the work in the summer of 1943 at Saranac Lake, New York, working with remarkable speed. He explained that he called it a concerto rather than a symphony because of the way individual instruments and groups of instruments are treated in a soloistic, virtuoso fashion throughout — a concertante principle applied to the whole orchestra.

Movements

The five movements are: Introduzione; Giuoco delle coppie ("Game of Pairs"), in which pairs of wind instruments present a theme at fixed intervals; Elegia, a sombre nocturne forming the emotional heart of the work; Intermezzo interrotto, whose lyrical melody is interrupted by a crude parody of a theme from Shostakovich's Seventh Symphony; and a brilliant Finale in the spirit of a folk dance. Bartók revised the ending in 1945, shortly before his death.
