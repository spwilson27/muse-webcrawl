Lilliput and Blefuscu

Lilliput and Blefuscu are two fictional island nations that appear in the first part of Jonathan Swift's 1726 satirical novel Gulliver's Travels. Both are inhabited by tiny people, about six inches (15 cm) tall, roughly one-twelfth the height of a normal human.

In the novel

Lemuel Gulliver is shipwrecked on Lilliput, where he is held captive, then serves the emperor by wading across the channel and seizing the fleet of Blefuscu, Lilliput's rival across the strait. The two empires are divided by a bitter dispute over which end of an egg should be broken — the Big-Endians and Little-Endians — a satire of religious and political conflicts, widely read as mocking the wars and doctrinal quarrels between Britain and France and between Protestant and Catholic.

Satire and legacy

The episode satirises court intrigue, party politics (the high-heeled and low-heeled factions), and the pettiness of wars fought over trivial differences. The word "Lilliputian" has entered English to mean tiny or trivial.
