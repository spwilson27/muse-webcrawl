Bollandists

Named afterJean Bolland (1596–1665)
FieldHagiography
Principal workActa Sanctorum
HeadquartersBrussels, Belgium

The Bollandists (Latin: Bollandistae) are an association of scholars, philologists, and historians (originally all Jesuits, though the modern group includes non-Jesuits) who since the early 17th century have studied hagiography and the cult of the saints in Christianity. Their most influential publication is the Acta Sanctorum, a critical edition of saints' lives arranged by feast day.

History

The project began with Heribert Rosweyde, whose plans were taken up by Jean Bolland, after whom the group is named. The first volumes of the Acta Sanctorum appeared in Antwerp in 1643, covering saints of January. Work continued through the suppression of the Jesuit order in the 18th century and was revived in Brussels in the 19th century.

Work

Bollandists pioneered critical methods in hagiography: collecting manuscripts across Europe, comparing versions, and distinguishing historical evidence from legend. Their journal, Analecta Bollandiana (founded 1882), and reference works such as the Bibliotheca Hagiographica Latina remain standard scholarly resources.

Legacy

The Société des Bollandistes in Brussels continues the work today. The Bollandists' rigorous source criticism helped found modern hagiographical scholarship and influenced historical method more broadly.
