Forms of government

Countries of the world by form of government.

This page lists the forms of government used by the world's states. Governments are conventionally classified along several independent dimensions: whether the head of state is a monarch or a republican officeholder; how the executive relates to the legislature; whether power is centralized or federal; and how free and competitive political life is. A single state can therefore be described by several terms at once — for example, a federal parliamentary constitutional monarchy (Canada, Belgium) or a unitary presidential republic (the United States is federal and presidential).

Lists

Wikipedia maintains country-by-country lists including the list of countries by system of government, the list of current heads of state and government, and the list of republics and monarchies. Roughly 150 of the world's states are republics of some kind, while about 40 retain monarchs, most of them constitutional. Counts by regime type depend on measures such as the Economist Intelligence Unit's Democracy Index and Freedom House ratings, which classify states as democracies, hybrid regimes or authoritarian regimes regardless of their formal constitutional labels.
