René II, Duke of Lorraine

Born: 2 May 1451, Angers
Died: 10 December 1508, Fains
Reign: 1473–1508
Spouse: Philippa of Guelders
House: Lorraine (Vaudémont)

René II (2 May 1451 – 10 December 1508) was Count of Vaudémont from 1470, Duke of Lorraine from 1473, and Duke of Bar from 1483 to 1508. He is remembered above all for defeating Charles the Bold, Duke of Burgundy, at the Battle of Nancy in 1477, in which Charles was killed.

René II secured Lorraine's independence from Burgundian expansion.

Early life and claim

René was the son of Yolande of Anjou, daughter of René I of Naples, and Frederick II, Count of Vaudémont. Raised at the Angevin court in Angers and Provence, he inherited Vaudémont on his father's death in 1470. When his cousin Nicholas I, Duke of Lorraine, died childless in 1473, the duchy passed to his aunt Yolande, who immediately abdicated in René's favour.

War with Burgundy

Lorraine lay across the path of Charles the Bold's ambition to build a middle kingdom between France and the Empire. Charles invaded Lorraine in 1475 and captured Nancy. René allied himself with the Swiss Confederates and the Alsatian towns, whose infantry had already shattered Burgundian armies at Grandson and Morat (Murten) in 1476. At the Battle of Nancy on 5 January 1477, René's army, strengthened by Swiss pikemen, destroyed the Burgundian force; Charles the Bold's frozen body was found days later in a pond near the battlefield. The victory ended Burgundian power and preserved Lorraine as a distinct duchy.

Later life

René later supported claims to the Kingdom of Naples inherited from his grandfather but abandoned the expedition in 1488. He founded the church of Saint-Nicolas-de-Port and rebuilt the ducal palace at Nancy. His marriage to Philippa of Guelders produced twelve children, including Antoine, his successor, and Claude, the first Duke of Guise. He died at Fains in 1508.
