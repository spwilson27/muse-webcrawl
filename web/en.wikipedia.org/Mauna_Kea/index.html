Mauna Kea

Mauna Kea is a dormant shield volcano on the island of Hawaiʻi. Its peak is 4,207.3 m (13,803 ft) above sea level, making it the highest point in the U.S. state of Hawaii. Measured from its base on the ocean floor, it rises over 10,210 m (33,500 ft), taller than Mount Everest measured from sea level.

Astronomy

The summit's dry, stable atmosphere and isolation make it one of the world's best sites for astronomical observation. The Mauna Kea Observatories host thirteen major telescopes, including the Keck telescopes, the Subaru Telescope, and the Gemini North telescope. Plans for the Thirty Meter Telescope have been the subject of protest by Native Hawaiians, for whom the mountain is sacred.

Geology and ecology

Mauna Kea last erupted about 4,600 years ago. Its slopes host rare ecosystems, including the wēkiu bug, and Lake Waiau near the summit, one of the highest lakes in the United States.
