Renin–angiotensin system

Renin–angiotensin system
TypeHormonal cascade
FunctionRegulation of blood pressure and fluid balance
Key componentsRenin, angiotensinogen, ACE, angiotensin II, aldosterone
Drug targetsACE inhibitors, ARBs, direct renin inhibitors

The renin–angiotensin system (RAS), or renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system (RAAS), is a hormone cascade that regulates blood pressure, fluid and electrolyte balance, and systemic vascular resistance. When blood volume or sodium levels fall, or blood pressure drops, the kidneys release the enzyme renin, setting off a chain of reactions that produce the potent vasoconstrictor angiotensin II and the steroid hormone aldosterone, which together restore pressure and volume.

The cascade

Juxtaglomerular cells in the kidney release renin in response to low renal perfusion pressure, low sodium chloride delivery to the macula densa, or sympathetic stimulation. Renin cleaves angiotensinogen, a protein produced by the liver, to form angiotensin I. Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE), found especially on pulmonary endothelium, converts angiotensin I to angiotensin II.

Effects of angiotensin II

Angiotensin II acts mainly through AT1 receptors to constrict blood vessels (raising blood pressure), stimulate the adrenal cortex to release aldosterone (which increases sodium and water reabsorption in the kidneys), promote release of antidiuretic hormone (vasopressin) and thirst, and enhance sympathetic activity. A counter-regulatory arm of the system — ACE2, angiotensin-(1-7) and the Mas receptor — opposes these effects with vasodilation.

Clinical significance

Overactivity of the system contributes to hypertension, heart failure and kidney disease. Several major drug classes block the cascade: ACE inhibitors (such as lisinopril), angiotensin II receptor blockers (ARBs, such as losartan), direct renin inhibitors (aliskiren), and mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (such as spironolactone). These agents are mainstays of cardiovascular and renal medicine.
