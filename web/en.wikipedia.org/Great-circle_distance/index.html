Great-circle distance

[ Image placeholder: A great circle drawn on a globe, with the shorter arc between two points highlighted ]

The great-circle distance, orthodromic distance, or spherical distance is the distance between two points on a sphere, measured along the great circle passing through them. It is the shortest path between the two points on the surface of the sphere, in contrast to a straight line through the sphere's interior.

Formulae

For two points with latitudes φ₁, φ₂ and longitude difference Δλ, the central angle Δσ can be found from the spherical law of cosines, Δσ = arccos(sin φ₁ sin φ₂ + cos φ₁ cos φ₂ cos Δλ), and the distance is d = r Δσ for sphere radius r. Because the law of cosines is numerically ill-conditioned for small distances, navigators traditionally used the haversine formula, which remains stable for points close together; the Vincenty formula for a spherical earth is accurate at all separations.

Applications

Great-circle routes govern long-distance air and sea navigation: the shortest flight path between distant cities follows (approximately) a great circle, which is why flights between Europe and western North America pass near Greenland. The concept generalises geodesics on ellipsoids, used for precise geodesy on the Earth's actual shape.
