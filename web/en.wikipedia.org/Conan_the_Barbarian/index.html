Conan the Barbarian

Conan the Barbarian (also known as Conan the Cimmerian) is a fictional sword-and-sorcery hero created by American author Robert E. Howard. He debuted in the short story "The Phoenix on the Sword" in Weird Tales magazine in 1932, and became one of the most iconic figures in fantasy literature.

The character

Conan is a native of Cimmeria, a cold northern land of the Hyborian Age, Howard's invented prehistoric epoch set after the sinking of Atlantis. Over his life Conan passes through many roles — thief, pirate, mercenary, and finally king of Aquilonia, the most powerful kingdom of the Hyborian west. Howard wrote 17 short stories and four novels featuring the character, including The Hour of the Dragon.

Legacy

After Howard's death in 1936, other authors including L. Sprague de Camp and Lin Carter continued the saga. Conan became a major figure in comics, notably Marvel Comics' Conan the Barbarian (from 1970), and in film, played by Arnold Schwarzenegger in Conan the Barbarian (1982) and Conan the Destroyer (1984). The character helped define the sword-and-sorcery subgenre and influenced fantasy literature, role-playing games, and popular culture broadly.
