Bias tire

A bias tire (also called a cross-ply or diagonal-ply tire) is a pneumatic tire whose carcass plies are laid at diagonal angles, typically 30–40 degrees, to the direction of travel, with successive plies running in opposite directions to form a crisscross pattern.

Characteristics

Bias construction gives a stiff sidewall and good durability at low cost, but produces more heat and rolling resistance at speed and less precise handling than radial tires, whose plies run perpendicular to the tread with stabilising belt layers.

Uses

Bias tires dominated until the spread of radial tires from the 1970s onward. They remain common on motorcycles, trailers, agricultural and aviation applications, and on vintage vehicles, where their flexibility and load behaviour can be advantageous.
