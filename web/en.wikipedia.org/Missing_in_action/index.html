Missing in action

Missing in action (MIA) is a casualty classification assigned to combatants, military chaplains, combat medics, and prisoners of war who are reported missing during wartime or a ceasefire. Such personnel may have been killed, wounded, captured, executed, or deserted; if deceased, neither their remains nor their grave have been positively identified. Becoming missing in action has been called an occupational hazard of military service.

History

The problem of missing combatants became acute in the era of mass warfare. The First World War left hundreds of thousands of soldiers with no known grave, commemorated by monuments such as the Menin Gate and Thiepval Memorial. During the Second World War and the Korean and Vietnam wars, large numbers of American servicemen were listed as missing; the Vietnam-era POW/MIA issue became a lasting political and cultural cause in the United States, symbolised by the POW/MIA flag.

Identification efforts

Agencies such as the US Defense POW/MIA Accounting Agency work to recover and identify remains using forensic anthropology and DNA analysis. The Commonwealth War Graves Commission and similar bodies maintain records for British and Commonwealth forces. Advances in DNA extraction from aged remains have enabled identifications decades after conflicts ended.

In law

The Geneva Conventions require parties to a conflict to search for and record information about the missing and deceased. Families of the missing may seek legal declarations of death after prescribed periods of absence.
