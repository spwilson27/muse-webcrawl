National Tourist Routes of Norway

The National Tourist Routes of Norway (Norwegian: Nasjonale turistveger) are eighteen selected highways and roads across Norway, designated by the Norwegian Public Roads Administration for their exceptional landscapes and enhanced with architecture, viewpoints, rest areas and art installations designed to enrich the driving experience.

The routes

The routes run from Varanger in the far north-east to Jæren in the south-west, and include the Atlantic Road (Atlanterhavsvegen), the Trollstigen–Geiranger route, the Sognefjellet mountain road — the highest in Northern Europe — the old Strynefjell road, Rondane, Hardanger, Ryfylke, the Lofoten route and Senja. Together they total roughly 1,850 kilometres (1,150 miles).

Design programme

Beginning in 1994 and greatly expanded after 2005, the project commissioned leading Norwegian and international architects — including Snøhetta, Jensen & Skodvin, Reiulf Ramstad and Peter Zumthor — to build striking rest stops, bridges, walkways and memorials, such as the Stegastein viewpoint over the Aurlandsfjord and the Steilneset Memorial in Vardø. The programme is a celebrated example of infrastructure-led tourism and contemporary landscape architecture.
