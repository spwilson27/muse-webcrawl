The Maliki school (Arabic: مَالِكِيّ) is one of the four major schools of Islamic jurisprudence (madhhab) within Sunni Islam. Founded by Malik ibn Anas in the 8th century CE in Medina, it is the predominant school across North and West Africa and was historically dominant in Islamic Spain (al-Andalus).

Principles

Maliki jurisprudence draws on the Qur'an and hadith, and gives distinctive weight to ʿamal—the living practice of the people of Medina—as a source of law, on the reasoning that it preserves the Prophet's community's custom. It also employs maṣlaḥa (public interest) and local custom (ʿurf) as supplementary principles.

Key texts

Malik's Al-Muwaṭṭaʾ is among the earliest surviving works of Islamic law. Later foundational works include the Mudawwana of Sahnun and the Mukhtaṣar of Khalil ibn Ishaq, which became the standard manual in West Africa.

Distribution

Today Malikis form the majority in Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Libya, Mauritania, Mali, Niger, Nigeria and Sudan, and are present in the UAE, Kuwait and Bahrain.
