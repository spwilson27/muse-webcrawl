Naval Battle of Guadalcanal

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Naval Battle of Guadalcanal, sometimes referred to as the Third and Fourth Battles of Savo Island, the Battle of the Solomons, the Battle of Friday the 13th, or, in Japanese sources, the Third Battle of the Solomon Sea, took place from 12 to 15 November 1942 and was the decisive engagement in a series of naval battles between Allied (primarily American) and Imperial Japanese forces during the months-long Guadalcanal campaign in the Solomon Islands during World War II.

Background

Since landing on Guadalcanal in August 1942, Allied forces had contested Japanese control of Henderson Field, the airfield whose aircraft dominated the surrounding seas by day. At night Japanese warships bombarded the field and delivered reinforcements — runs the Americans called the "Tokyo Express" — while suffering losses they could ill afford to replace.

The battle

In a chaotic series of night surface actions beginning on Friday, 13 November, American cruisers and destroyers intercepted a Japanese bombardment force, losing Rear Admirals Daniel Callaghan and Norman Scott but preventing the shelling. Over the following two days, aircraft from Henderson Field and the carrier Enterprise sank the battleship Hiei and numerous transports, and on the night of 14–15 November the battleship Washington sank the battleship Kirishima.

Outcome

The battle turned back Japan's last major attempt to dislodge Allied forces from Guadalcanal. Combined with the attrition of Japanese naval aviation, it marked a decisive turning point: Japan evacuated its surviving troops in February 1943 and never regained the strategic initiative in the Pacific.
