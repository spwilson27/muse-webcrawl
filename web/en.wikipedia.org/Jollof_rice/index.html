Jollof rice

Jollof rice is a rice dish from West Africa. The dish is typically made with long-grain rice, tomatoes, chilies, onions, spices, and sometimes other vegetables and/or meat in a single pot, although its ingredients and preparation methods vary across different regions. The dish's origins are traced to the Wolof people of Senegal and the Gambia, and it is a staple across West Africa, especially in Nigeria, Ghana, and Senegal, where regional rivalry over the best version — the so-called "jollof wars" — is a popular cultural phenomenon.

History

Jollof derives from thieboudienne, the national dish of Senegal, created in Saint-Louis in the 19th century and named for the Wolof empire of Jolof. As the dish spread through the region with trade and migration, local variations developed: Senegalese and Gambian versions often include broken rice and vegetables, Nigerian "party jollof" is known for a smoky flavour from cooking over firewood, and Ghanaian jollof typically uses basmati rice and a spicier tomato base.

Preparation

Rice is cooked in a blended sauce of tomatoes, red peppers, onions, and tomato paste with curry powder, thyme, bay leaves, and stock, absorbing the sauce to give an orange-red colour. It is served at celebrations and everyday meals alike, often with fried plantain, chicken, or beef. In 2022, UNESCO recognized Senegalese thieboudienne on its Intangible Cultural Heritage list.
