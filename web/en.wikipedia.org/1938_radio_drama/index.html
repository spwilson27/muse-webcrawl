"The War of the Worlds" is an episode of the American radio drama anthology series The Mercury Theatre on the Air, directed and narrated by Orson Welles and broadcast live as a Halloween episode on October 30, 1938, over the CBS Radio network. An adaptation of H. G. Wells's 1898 novel, it was presented largely as a series of simulated news bulletins describing a Martian invasion of New Jersey.

Public reaction

The realistic format caused confusion among some listeners who tuned in late and missed the opening announcement that it was fiction. Newspaper reports the following day claimed mass panic, though later scholarship has argued the scale of the panic was greatly exaggerated by the press, which had commercial motives to depict radio as irresponsible.

Legacy

The broadcast made Welles famous and remains the most celebrated radio drama ever aired. It has been restaged and adapted many times, and its "panic" became a landmark case study in media effects research.
