Biholomorphism

In the mathematical theory of functions of one or more complex variables, and also in complex algebraic geometry, a biholomorphism or biholomorphic function is a bijective holomorphic function whose inverse is also holomorphic. Two domains admitting a biholomorphism between them are called biholomorphically equivalent or biholomorphic.

One complex variable

In one variable, a biholomorphism is a conformal map. By the Riemann mapping theorem, every simply connected proper open subset of the complex plane is biholomorphic to the open unit disk. In contrast to the one-dimensional case, biholomorphic equivalence in several complex variables is highly restrictive: the unit ball and the polydisk in two or more variables are not biholomorphic, a result associated with Poincaré.

Properties

If a holomorphic bijection exists between two domains, and one of the domains is considered with the standard complex structure, then the inverse is automatically holomorphic in one variable; in several variables this follows from deeper results of complex analysis. Biholomorphisms preserve the complex structure and are the natural isomorphisms in the category of complex manifolds.

Applications

The classification of domains up to biholomorphism is a central problem of several complex variables. Biholomorphic invariants include the Bergman kernel and metric, automorphism groups, and boundary geometry, and they distinguish domains that are equivalent as smooth manifolds but not as complex manifolds.
