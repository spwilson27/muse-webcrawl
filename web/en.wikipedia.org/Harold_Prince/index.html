Harold Smith Prince (born Harold Smith; January 30, 1928 – July 31, 2019), commonly known as Hal Prince, was an American theatrical producer and director associated with many of the best-known Broadway musical productions of the 20th century. Over the course of his career he won 21 Tony Awards, more than any other individual, including eight for directing, eight for producing, two as a producer of the year's Best Musical, and three special awards.

Early life

Prince was born in New York City and adopted by Milton A. Prince, a stockbroker, and Blanche Stern. He attended the Dwight School and graduated from the University of Pennsylvania at age 19. After serving two years in the U.S. Army in postwar Germany, he began working in theatre as an assistant stage manager for producer George Abbott, whom he regarded as a mentor.

Career

Prince first rose to prominence as a co-producer (with Robert E. Griffith) of successful musicals in the 1950s and 1960s, including The Pajama Game (1954), Damn Yankees (1955), West Side Story (1957), Fiorello! (1959), and Fiddler on the Roof (1964).

His most significant collaboration was with composer-lyricist Stephen Sondheim. Beginning with Company (1970), Prince directed a landmark series of "concept musicals" with scores by Sondheim, including Follies (1971), A Little Night Music (1973), Pacific Overtures (1976), and Sweeney Todd: The Demon Barber of Fleet Street (1979). These productions combined innovative staging with integrated design and are widely credited with reshaping the Broadway musical.

Prince also directed two of the most successful musicals of all time: Andrew Lloyd Webber's Evita (1978 in London, 1979 on Broadway) and The Phantom of the Opera (1986 in London, 1988 on Broadway), the latter becoming the longest-running show in Broadway history. His later credits included revivals such as Show Boat (1994), which won the Tony Award for Best Revival of a Musical, and Kiss of the Spider Woman (1993). He also directed opera productions for companies including the New York City Opera and the Metropolitan Opera.

Legacy

Prince received the Kennedy Center Honors in 1996 and the National Medal of Arts in 2000. The Harold Prince Theatre at the University of Pennsylvania's Annenberg Center is named for him, and in 2019 Broadway theatres dimmed their lights in his memory. The musical revue Prince of Broadway, celebrating his career, opened in Japan in 2015 and on Broadway in 2017. He died in Reykjavík, Iceland, on July 31, 2019, aged 91.
