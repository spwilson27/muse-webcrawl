Franz Joseph Haydn (31 March 1732 – 31 May 1809) was an Austrian composer of the Classical period. He was instrumental in the development of chamber music such as the string quartet and piano trio, and his contributions lead to his epithets "Father of the Symphony" and "Father of the String Quartet". He spent most of his career as a court musician for the wealthy Esterházy family at their palace in Eisenstadt.

Early life

Haydn was born in Rohrau, the son of a wheelwright. Spotted for his fine treble voice, he was recruited at about age eight to the choir of St Stephen's Cathedral in Vienna, and after his voice broke he survived as a freelance musician and teacher, studying counterpoint from Fux's Gradus ad Parnassum and receiving informal guidance from Nicola Porpora. In 1761 he entered the service of the Esterházy princes, becoming Kapellmeister in 1766, a position he retained — with increasing European fame — for nearly three decades at their remote estate of Eszterháza, later remarking that the isolation "forced [him] to become original".

Mature career and London

Haydn's output for the Esterházys spans the development of the Classical style: some 104 symphonies (including the "Farewell", "Oxford" and "Surprise"), dozens of string quartets (Opp. 33, 64 and 76, the last containing the "Emperor" Quartet, whose slow movement became the Austrian and later German national anthem), piano sonatas, trios, operas and masses. A close friend of Mozart and an early teacher of Beethoven, he made two triumphant visits to London (1791–92 and 1794–95) at the invitation of the impresario Johann Peter Salomon, composing the twelve "London" symphonies.

Later years

Freed from full-time court duties after 1790, Haydn in his final decade wrote his greatest choral works: the oratorios The Creation (1798) and The Seasons (1801), and six late masses. Enfeebled after 1802, he died in Vienna on 31 May 1809, during Napoleon's occupation of the city; Napoleon ordered a guard of honour at his house. His skull was stolen by phrenologists and only reunited with his remains in 1954.

Style and influence

Haydn's music is characterised by wit, surprise (the famous fortissimo chord in the "Surprise" Symphony), economy of thematic material, and a mastery of monothematic sonata form. His development of the symphony and string quartet provided the foundation on which Mozart and Beethoven built, and his works are catalogued in the Hoboken catalogue (Hob.).
