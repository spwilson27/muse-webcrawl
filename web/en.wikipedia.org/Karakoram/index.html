The Karakoram is a great mountain system spanning the borders of Pakistan, India, and China, at the western end of the Himalayan arc. It contains the densest concentration of very high peaks on Earth, including K2 (8,611 m), the world's second-highest mountain, and four other eight-thousanders: Gasherbrum I, Broad Peak, Gasherbrum II, and (just outside the main range) Nanga Parbat is in the Himalaya proper. The name derives from the Turkic for "black gravel," after the Karakoram Pass, an ancient trade route between Central Asia and the Indian subcontinent.

Geology and glaciation

The Karakoram was uplifted by the collision of the Indian and Eurasian plates and remains tectonically active. Outside the polar regions it is the most heavily glaciated mountain system in the world: the Siachen Glacier (about 76 km) and the Biafo–Hispar system (over 100 km combined) are among the longest glaciers on Earth. Unusually, many Karakoram glaciers were stable or advancing in the early twenty-first century — the so-called "Karakoram anomaly."

Mountaineering and access

The range is reached by the Karakoram Highway, one of the highest paved international roads, linking Pakistan with China over the Khunjerab Pass (4,693 m). K2, first climbed in 1954 by an Italian expedition, is famed for its difficulty and danger. The Baltoro Glacier trek to Concordia is among the world's celebrated high-mountain journeys.
