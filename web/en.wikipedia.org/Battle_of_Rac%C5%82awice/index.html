The Battle of Racławice was one of the first battles of the Kościuszko Uprising against the Russian Empire on 4 April 1794, near the village of Racławice in Lesser Poland. The Polish forces under Tadeusz Kościuszko defeated a Russian detachment commanded by General Alexander Tormasov.

Background

Kościuszko had proclaimed the uprising in Kraków on 24 March 1794 and moved north toward Warsaw with a small regular force reinforced by some 2,000 peasant volunteers armed with war scythes — the famous kosynierzy (scythemen).

The battle

The Russians held a strong position with artillery on rising ground. Kościuszko sent the scythemen, led in person, in a bayonet-like charge against the Russian guns; the peasants overran the artillery in hand-to-hand fighting, and the Russians retreated. The peasant Wojciech Bartos distinguished himself and was ennobled under the name Głowacki.

Aftermath

Although not strategically decisive, Racławice was a powerful moral victory that spread the uprising across Poland and inspired national memory — notably the monumental Racławice Panorama painting in Wrocław.
