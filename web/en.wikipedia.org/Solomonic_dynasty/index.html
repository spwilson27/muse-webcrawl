Solomonic dynasty

The Solomonic dynasty was a ruling dynasty of the Ethiopian Empire (and previously the Kingdom of Abyssinia) founded in 1270 by Yekuno Amlak, who claimed to restore a line descended from the biblical King Solomon and the Queen of Sheba. It ruled Ethiopia, with interruptions and civil conflicts, until the deposition of Emperor Haile Selassie in 1974 — a span of roughly seven centuries.

Origins and legend

According to the Kebra Nagast ("Glory of the Kings"), Ethiopia's national epic composed in the 14th century, the Queen of Sheba (Makeda) visited Solomon in Jerusalem and bore him a son, Menelik I, who later brought the Ark of the Covenant to Ethiopia. The dynasty's legitimacy rested on this claimed descent. In 1270 Yekuno Amlak overthrew the last Zagwe king, presenting his rule as a "restoration" of the Solomonic line, reportedly with the support of the monk Tekle Haymanot.

Medieval period

Under Amda Seyon I (r. 1314–1344) the empire expanded greatly through campaigns against Muslim sultanates to the east and south. Zara Yaqob (r. 1434–1468) strengthened the monarchy, reformed the church, and promoted literature. In the 16th century the empire was nearly destroyed by the invasions of Ahmad ibn Ibrahim al-Ghazi (Ahmad Gragn) of Adal (1529–1543), surviving with Portuguese assistance, after which Jesuit influence led to Emperor Susenyos's conversion to Catholicism and civil war, and his successor Fasilides expelled the Jesuits and founded Gondar as a fixed capital.

Gondarine period and Zemene Mesafint

From the 1630s Gondar served as capital, and emperors such as Iyasu I presided over a flourishing court culture. After the mid-18th century, imperial power declined during the Zemene Mesafint ("Era of the Princes," 1769–1855), when regional warlords ruled and emperors became figureheads.

Modern period

Tewodros II reunified the empire in 1855 but was not of the main Solomonic line; Yohannes IV and then Menelik II (r. 1889–1913), who traced Solomonic descent through the Shewan branch, expanded the empire to roughly its modern borders and defeated Italy at the Battle of Adwa in 1896. Menelik's daughter Zewditu reigned as empress (1916–1930), followed by Haile Selassie (r. 1930–1974), who led Ethiopia through the Second Italo-Ethiopian War, exile, restoration in 1941, and postwar modernization.

End of the dynasty

Haile Selassie was deposed by the Derg military junta on 12 September 1974, and the monarchy was formally abolished in 1975. Crown Prince Asfaw Wossen was proclaimed king-in-exile as Amha Selassie. Descendants of the imperial family survive, and the dynasty remains central to Ethiopian national identity and to the Rastafari movement, which regards Haile Selassie as divine.
