Carabinieri

The Carabinieri (formally the Arma dei Carabinieri, "Arm of Carabineers") is the national gendarmerie of Italy. It is one of Italy's primary law enforcement agencies and, since 2000, one of the four branches of the Italian Armed Forces, alongside the Army, Navy, and Air Force. The Carabinieri perform both military and civil police duties.

History

The corps was founded by King Victor Emmanuel I of Sardinia on 13 July 1814, modelled on the French gendarmerie, and named for the carbine each member carried. After Italian unification the Carabinieri became the senior corps of the Royal Italian Army. Its members fought in both world wars; Vice-Brigadier Salvo D'Acquisto, who saved 22 civilians by falsely confessing to sabotage in 1943, is among its most honoured heroes.

Organisation and duties

The Carabinieri are organised territorially into legions, groups, companies, and local stations (stazioni), which provide policing even in small rural communities — a presence that distinguishes them from the Polizia di Stato, which is concentrated in larger towns. Specialist units include the ROS (anti-organised crime), the GIS special forces group, the 1st Paratroopers Regiment "Tuscania", cultural-heritage protection (TPC), environmental and food-safety commands, and the mounted regiments in Rome.

Appearance

The distinctive uniform is black with red piping and a red stripe down the trousers. The corps' emblem, a flaming grenade (fiamma), is worn on headgear. Its motto is Nei secoli fedele — "Faithful through the centuries".

International service

Carabinieri serve in military police and stability-policing roles in peacekeeping missions abroad, and helped found the European Gendarmerie Force (EUROGENDFOR).
