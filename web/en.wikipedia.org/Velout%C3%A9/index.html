Velouté sauce

TypeSauce
Place of originFrance
Main ingredientsLight stock (chicken, veal, or fish), blond roux

Velouté sauce (French pronunciation: [vəluˈte]) is one of the five "mother sauces" of French cuisine codified by Auguste Escoffier. The name means "velvety" in French, describing the sauce's smooth texture. It is made from a light stock — chicken, veal, or fish — thickened with a blond roux of butter and flour cooked just long enough to lose its raw taste without browning.

Preparation

A blond roux is prepared by melting butter, stirring in an equal weight of flour, and cooking briefly. Hot light stock is then whisked in gradually and the mixture is simmered until it coats the back of a spoon. Unlike béchamel, which uses milk, velouté is based on stock, giving it a more delicate, savoury flavour.

Derivative sauces

Velouté serves as the base for many secondary sauces. Sauce suprême is a chicken velouté enriched with cream and mushroom liquor; allemande sauce adds egg yolk and lemon; a fish velouté combined with white wine, shallots, and cream yields white wine sauce. Velouté is also the foundation of many soups and gravies in classical French cooking.
