Safe deposit box

A wall of safe deposit boxes in a bank vault.

A safe deposit box, also known as a safety deposit box, is an individually secured container, usually held within a larger safe or bank vault. They are rented by banks and other institutions to customers for the storage of valuable possessions, currency, documents, and data media that need protection from theft, fire, flood, or tampering.

Operation

Boxes are typically opened with two keys: a guard key held by the bank and a renter's key, so both parties must be present. Sizes range from narrow boxes a few centimetres high suitable for documents, to large boxes for bulky items. A renter pays an annual fee; if rent goes unpaid for a statutory period, the contents may be treated as unclaimed property and escheated to the state after notice.

Protection and limitations

Contrary to common belief, in the United States the contents of safe deposit boxes are not insured by the FDIC, because the bank neither knows nor takes custody of the contents — the relationship is landlord-tenant rather than deposit. Losses have occurred through bank errors, vault thefts, fires, and floods, and renters are often advised to carry private insurance and maintain an inventory. Access can be restricted on the death of a renter until an executor is appointed. Use of safe deposit boxes has declined in the digital era as documents are stored electronically, and many new bank branches are built without vaults.
