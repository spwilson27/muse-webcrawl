Wired (magazine)

Founded1993, San Francisco, US
FoundersLouis Rossetto, Jane Metcalfe
PublisherCondé Nast
LanguageEnglish (with international editions)

Wired is an American magazine and website focused on how emerging technologies affect culture, the economy and politics. Founded in 1993 by Louis Rossetto and Jane Metcalfe with backing from figures including Nicholas Negroponte, it became the chronicle of the digital revolution and of Silicon Valley.

History and style

The magazine's early years under editor Kevin Kelly combined techno-utopian optimism with striking, fluorescent, digitally processed design. Marshall McLuhan was listed as its "patron saint". Wired launched HotWired, one of the first commercial websites, which pioneered the banner advertisement in 1994. Condé Nast acquired the print magazine in 1998 and the website in 2006, reuniting them.

Influence

Wired popularised terms such as "the Long Tail" (a 2004 essay by longtime editor-in-chief Chris Anderson) and "crowdsourcing" (Jeff Howe, 2006). Under editors including Nicholas Thompson it expanded into long-form investigative and political reporting. International editions appear in the UK, Italy, Japan and elsewhere.
