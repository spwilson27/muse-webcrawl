The Labrador Sea (Inuktitut: imiujjuaq, lit. "big water") is an arm of the North Atlantic Ocean between the Labrador Peninsula and Greenland. The sea is flanked by continental shelves to the southwest, northwest, and northeast. It connects to the north with Baffin Bay through the Davis Strait. It is a marginal sea of the Atlantic.

Geography

The Labrador Sea is about 840 km (521 mi) wide and 3,381 metres (11,092 ft) deep. It is surrounded by the continental shelves of southwestern Greenland, eastern Labrador, and northeastern Newfoundland. The sea is named after the Labrador Peninsula; the name derives from the Portuguese explorer João Fernandes Lavrador.

Oceanography

The sea is one of the few places in the world ocean where deep water is formed by convection: during winter, intense cooling makes surface water dense enough to sink to great depths, producing Labrador Sea Water, a distinct water mass that spreads through the North Atlantic and contributes to the Atlantic meridional overturning circulation.

Wildlife

The northern and western shores of the Labrador Sea are ice-covered for much of the year. The sea supports important fisheries, including Atlantic cod, shrimp, and Greenland halibut, and is a feeding ground for whales and seabirds.
