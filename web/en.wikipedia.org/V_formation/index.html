V formation

A V formation is a symmetric V-shaped or chevron-shaped flight formation. In nature, it occurs in the flight of geese, swans, ducks, cranes and other migratory birds. The formation is thought to improve energy efficiency and endurance, and it inspired similar formations used by military aircraft.

Aerodynamics

Each bird flies slightly above and behind the bird in front, positioned in the upwash of the wingtip vortex shed by the bird ahead. This reduces the induced drag on the following bird, lowering the energy cost of flight. Studies of northern bald ibises equipped with data loggers showed that the birds precisely position themselves and time their wingbeats to capture the maximum benefit from the upwash, and they avoid the downwash region directly behind another bird. Flying in formation can substantially extend the flock's range compared with flying alone.

Other benefits

The formation also allows birds to maintain visual contact with one another and to communicate, helping the flock stay together and navigate. The lead position is the most energetically demanding, and birds take turns at the front, dropping back when tired.

Military use

The "V" or vic formation was a standard flight formation for fighter and bomber aircraft, particularly in the early twentieth century and the Second World War, valued for mutual observation and signalling before reliable radio. Modern tactical formations have largely replaced it, but the V remains common in ceremonial flypasts.
