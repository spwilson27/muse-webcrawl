Babinski sign

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Babinski sign (or Babinski reflex) is a clinical sign observed when the sole of the foot is firmly stroked: in a positive response the big toe extends upward and the other toes fan out. A positive Babinski sign in adults indicates damage to the corticospinal (pyramidal) tract, while in healthy adults the normal plantar response is flexion of the toes downward. The sign is named after the French neurologist Joseph Babinski, who described it in 1896.

Clinical significance

In infants, whose corticospinal tracts are not yet fully myelinated, an extensor response is normal up to about 12–24 months of age. In older children and adults, a positive sign suggests an upper motor neuron lesion from causes such as stroke, multiple sclerosis, spinal cord compression or motor neuron disease, and it forms a standard part of the neurological examination. Responses can be equivocal, and related variants bear other eponyms, including the Chaddock, Oppenheim and Gordon signs, used when the standard stimulus is unsuitable.

History

Babinski presented the phenomenon in a brief communication to the Société de Biologie in Paris in 1896, noting its absence in health and in hysteria and its presence in organic hemiplegia. The sign became one of the most famous in clinical neurology for reliably distinguishing organic from functional weakness.
