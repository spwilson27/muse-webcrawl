Agustín de Iturbide

Born: 27 September 1783, Valladolid (now Morelia), New Spain
Died: 19 July 1824, Padilla, Tamaulipas
Reign: Emperor of Mexico, 19 May 1822 – 19 March 1823

Agustín Cosme Damián de Iturbide y Arámburu (27 September 1783 – 19 July 1824) was a Mexican army general and politician who successfully led the movement for Mexican independence from Spain. Under his Plan of Iguala, Mexico achieved independence in 1821; he then became the first and only emperor of the short-lived First Mexican Empire, reigning as Agustín I from 1822 to 1823.

Early career

Born to a wealthy Spanish Basque family in Valladolid, Michoacán, Iturbide joined the royalist army as a young officer. During the war of independence begun by Miguel Hidalgo in 1810, he fought against the insurgents and gained a reputation as an effective, if ruthless, commander, though he was also accused of profiteering and was briefly suspended.

Independence

In 1820, alarmed by the liberal turn in Spain, Iturbide negotiated with the last major insurgent leader, Vicente Guerrero. In February 1821 they proclaimed the Plan of Iguala, guaranteeing religion, independence and union (equality between Spaniards and Americans). The merged Army of the Three Guarantees marched into Mexico City on 27 September 1821, Iturbide's 38th birthday.

Empire and downfall

After a popular demonstration, Congress proclaimed Iturbide emperor in May 1822, and he was crowned in July. His empire quickly faltered amid fiscal crisis, conflict with Congress (which he dissolved) and regional revolts; Antonio López de Santa Anna's Plan of Casa Mata rallied opposition. Iturbide abdicated on 19 March 1823 and went into exile in Europe. Unaware that Congress had sentenced him to death should he return, he sailed back to Mexico in 1824, was arrested on landing, and was executed by firing squad at Padilla on 19 July 1824.

His remains were later reburied with honours in Mexico City Cathedral, and his legacy remains contested: he is credited as the architect of independence yet remembered as an anti-democratic ruler. The city of Iturbide and the former Mexican imperial flag's colours, retained in the modern flag, trace back to his Three Guarantees.
