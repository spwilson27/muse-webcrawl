Pontryagin duality

In mathematics, specifically in harmonic analysis and the theory of topological groups, Pontryagin duality explains the general properties of the Fourier transform on locally compact abelian groups, such as the real numbers, the circle, or finite cyclic groups. The Pontryagin duality theorem, due to Lev Pontryagin (1934) and Egbert van Kampen (1935), states that locally compact abelian groups identify naturally with their bidual groups (the dual of the dual).

The dual group

For a locally compact abelian group G, the dual group Ĝ consists of all continuous homomorphisms from G into the circle group T (the characters of G), equipped with the compact-open topology. The duality swaps familiar pairs of groups: the dual of the real line R is R itself; the dual of the circle T is the integers Z; the dual of Z is T; and the dual of a finite abelian group is a (non-canonically) isomorphic finite group. Compact groups have discrete duals and vice versa.

The duality theorem

The natural evaluation map from G to its double dual Ĝ̂, sending each element g to the character "evaluate at g", is an isomorphism of topological groups. This provides the framework for the abstract Fourier transform: functions on G transform to functions on Ĝ, and under suitable conditions (for example for L¹ functions, and for Schwartz–Bruhat functions) the Fourier inversion formula and Plancherel's theorem hold. For G = R this recovers ordinary Fourier analysis; for G = T it gives Fourier series; for finite abelian groups it gives the discrete Fourier transform.

Applications

Pontryagin duality underlies the theory of Tate's thesis in number theory, the harmonic analysis of adeles, Fourier analysis on profinite groups, and the structure theory of locally compact abelian groups. It is also the prototype for more general dualities in mathematics, including Tannaka–Krein duality for compact groups.
