Vaccinia

Vaccinia virus particles under the electron microscope.

Vaccinia virus (VACV or VV) is a large, complex poxvirus belonging to the family Poxviridae and the genus Orthopoxvirus. It is famous as the virus used in the vaccine that eradicated smallpox: vaccination with vaccinia provides cross-protective immunity against variola virus. The origin of vaccinia is uncertain — it is neither clearly the cowpox virus Jenner used nor a direct descendant of variola — and it may derive from a now-extinct horsepox-like virus.

Biology

Vaccinia has a brick-shaped virion about 360 × 270 nm with a large double-stranded DNA genome of roughly 190 kilobases encoding around 250 genes. Unusually for a DNA virus, it replicates entirely in the cytoplasm using its own transcription machinery. Strains such as Western Reserve and Copenhagen became workhorse laboratory models for poxvirus biology.

Uses

Beyond smallpox vaccination (the Dryvax and later ACAM2000 vaccines, and the non-replicating MVA strain now also used against mpox), vaccinia has been engineered as a vector for recombinant vaccines, including a successful oral rabies vaccine for wildlife, and as an oncolytic virus in cancer research. The vaccine itself can cause rare serious adverse events, a key reason routine smallpox vaccination ceased after eradication in 1980.
