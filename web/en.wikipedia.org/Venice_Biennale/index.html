Venice Biennale

The Venice Biennale (Italian: La Biennale di Venezia) is an international cultural exhibition hosted annually — in alternating years — by the Biennale Foundation in Venice, Italy. Founded in 1895 as an international art exhibition, it is one of the world's oldest and most prestigious cultural events, and its model of national pavilions has been imitated by biennales worldwide.

History and structure

The first International Art Exhibition opened on 30 April 1895 in the Giardini, a park where nations built permanent pavilions. Over the 20th century the Biennale expanded to include the Venice Film Festival (founded 1932, the world's oldest film festival), the Music and Theatre festivals, the Architecture Biennale (1980), and a Dance festival. Exhibitions also occupy the vast Arsenale shipyards.

Each Art Biennale centres on a curated international exhibition alongside dozens of national pavilions; the top prizes are the Golden Lions for best national participation and best artist. Editions draw hundreds of thousands of visitors and set agendas for contemporary art.
