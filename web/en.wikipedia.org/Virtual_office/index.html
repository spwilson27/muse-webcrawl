Virtual office

Virtual offices provide a prestigious business address without a permanent desk

Concept coined: 1980s–1990s

Key services: Business address, mail handling, call answering, meeting rooms

A virtual office is a service that provides businesses with a physical mailing address, office-related services and communication facilities without requiring them to lease a dedicated office space. The concept allows employees and business owners to work remotely — from home or anywhere — while still maintaining a professional presence in a desirable location, a business postal address, telephone answering services, and access to meeting rooms.

History

The idea of the virtual office developed from serviced offices and executive suites. Ralph Gregory is credited with founding "The Virtual Office, Inc." in 1994, while companies such as Regus (now IWG) popularized flexible office services internationally from 1989 onward. The growth of the internet, cloud computing, and video conferencing accelerated adoption, particularly among startups, freelancers, and small firms.

Services

Typical virtual office packages include a business address for registration and correspondence, mail collection and forwarding, a local telephone number with live receptionist answering, voicemail, and pay-as-you-go access to meeting rooms and day offices. Some providers add virtual assistants and administrative support.

Uses

Virtual offices are used by home-based businesses seeking credibility, companies expanding into new cities or countries without the cost of a lease, and remote teams. Regulators in some jurisdictions scrutinize virtual addresses used for company registration, money services, or licensing, and some industries restrict their use.
