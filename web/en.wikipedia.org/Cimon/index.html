Cimon

Cimon (Greek: Κίμων, Kímōn; c. 510 – 450 BC) was an Athenian statesman and general (strategos) in mid-5th century BC Greece and a leading figure of the conservative aristocratic faction at Athens. The son of Miltiades, the victor of Marathon, he rose to prominence during the wars against Persia and helped build the Athenian-led Delian League into an empire.

Early career

Cimon inherited his father's debts and reputation to repair. He distinguished himself at the battles of Salamis and Plataea and, as strategos, led campaigns against Persian garrisons in Thrace and the Aegean. At the Eurymedon River in Pamphylia (c. 466 BC) he won a decisive double victory over the Persian fleet and army, one of the greatest triumphs of the Greco-Persian Wars.

Politics

At home Cimon championed friendship with Sparta and opposed the democratic reforms of Ephialtes and Pericles. When he led an Athenian force to aid Sparta against the helot revolt on Mount Ithome in 462 BC and was dismissed by the Spartans, his standing collapsed; the reformers stripped the Areopagus of its powers, and Cimon was ostracized in 461 BC.

Recall and death

Recalled early — traditionally after only five years of his ten-year exile — Cimon negotiated a five-year truce with Sparta and led a final expedition to Cyprus to aid the Egyptian and Cypriot struggles against Persia. He died during the siege of Citium in Cyprus around 450 BC, reputedly ordering his death concealed so the army would fight on believing him alive.
