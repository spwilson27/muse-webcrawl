Monsanto Company was an American agrochemical and agricultural biotechnology corporation founded in 1901 and headquartered in Creve Coeur, Missouri. It was a leading producer of genetically engineered seed and of the herbicide glyphosate, sold under the Roundup brand. In June 2018 it was acquired by the German company Bayer AG, which retired the Monsanto name.

History

John Francis Queeny founded Monsanto in 1901 to manufacture the artificial sweetener saccharin, naming the company after his wife's maiden name. Over the 20th century it grew into a major chemical producer — making products including plastics, PCBs, and (through a contractor) the defoliant Agent Orange used in Vietnam — before reinventing itself in the 1980s and 90s as an agricultural biotechnology company. It commercialised recombinant bovine growth hormone and, from 1996, "Roundup Ready" crops genetically engineered to tolerate glyphosate. By the 2000s, after spinning off its industrial chemicals business as Solutia, Monsanto focused on seeds and traits, acquiring seed companies such as DeKalb and Seminis.

Controversies

Monsanto was among the most controversial corporations of its era: critics attacked its patent enforcement against farmers, its influence on regulation, and the safety of its products, while the company pointed to scientific consensus on the safety of approved GM foods. After the acquisition, Bayer faced tens of thousands of lawsuits alleging Roundup caused non-Hodgkin lymphoma, leading to multi-billion-dollar settlements. The Monsanto name itself became a focal point of global protests against GM crops, including the worldwide "March Against Monsanto".
