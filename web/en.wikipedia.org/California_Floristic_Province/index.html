California Floristic Province

TypeFloristic province / biodiversity hotspot
LocationCalifornia (US) and Baja California (Mexico)
Area~293,804 km²

The California Floristic Province (CFP) is a floristic province with a Mediterranean-type climate located on the Pacific Coast of North America, covering most of the U.S. state of California and part of northwestern Baja California in Mexico. It is recognized as a world biodiversity hotspot, defined by its exceptionally high plant endemism and the severe habitat loss it has experienced.

Flora

The province is home to more than 3,400 plant taxa found nowhere else, an endemism rate approaching 60%. Iconic plants include the giant sequoia (Sequoiadendron giganteum), the tallest and largest trees on Earth, and the coast redwood (Sequoia sempervirens). Major vegetation types include chaparral, oak woodland, coastal sage scrub, grassland, redwood forest, and mixed evergreen forest.

Boundaries

The province is bounded by the Pacific Ocean to the west, the Sierra Nevada and Cascade ranges to the east, the Klamath Mountains and southern Oregon to the north, and merges into desert floras in Baja California to the south. The Great Central Valley lies at its heart.

Threats

More than 70% of the province's original habitat has been lost to agriculture, urbanization, and development, and remaining habitats face pressure from invasive species, altered fire regimes, and climate change. Hotspot designation by Conservation International in 1996 highlighted it as one of 36 global regions of priority for conservation.
