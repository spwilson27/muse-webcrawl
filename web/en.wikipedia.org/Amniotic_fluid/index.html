Amniotic fluid

Amniotic fluid cushions and protects the developing fetus inside the amniotic sac.

The amniotic fluid is the protective liquid contained by the amniotic sac of a gravid amniote. This fluid serves as a cushion for the growing fetus, but also serves to facilitate the exchange of nutrients, water, and biochemical products between mother and fetus.

Development

Amniotic fluid is present from the formation of the gestational sac. At first it is derived mainly from maternal plasma passing through fetal membranes; later in pregnancy, fetal urine becomes a major component as the fetus swallows and re-circulates the fluid. The volume increases steadily, reaching roughly 800 mL by the 28th week and about 1,000 mL at its peak, then decreases somewhat toward term.

Functions and clinical significance

The fluid protects the fetus from impacts, pressure, and infection; it maintains a stable temperature; it permits fetal movement and symmetrical growth; and its passage through the fetal lungs and gut supports the development of those systems. Abnormal volumes are clinically important: too little fluid (oligohydramnios) can indicate problems with the fetal kidneys or placenta, while too much (polyhydramnios) may accompany diabetes or fetal anomalies. Sampling the fluid by amniocentesis allows genetic and biochemical testing of the fetus.
