John Dickson Carr

John Dickson Carr (November 30, 1906 – February 27, 1977) was an American author of detective stories, who also published under the pen names Carter Dickson, Carr Dickson, and Roger Fairbairn. He is generally regarded as one of the greatest writers of so-called "Golden Age" mysteries — complex, seemingly impossible plots, especially the locked-room mystery. In 1970 he received the Mystery Writers of America's Grand Master Award.

Carr was born in Uniontown, Pennsylvania, and moved to England in the 1930s, where most of his fiction is set. His best-known series detectives are Dr. Gideon Fell (under his own name) and Sir Henry Merrivale (as Carter Dickson). His novel The Hollow Man (1935), also published as The Three Coffins, contains Fell's famous "locked-room lecture" and was voted the best locked-room mystery of all time in a 1981 poll of critics.

Other work

Carr wrote historical novels such as The Bride of Newgate and Fire, Burn!, scripted BBC radio dramas including the series Appointment with Fear, and authored an authorised biography of Sir Arthur Conan Doyle (1949). He was admitted to the Detection Club and was a devoted admirer of G. K. Chesterton, on whom Fell was partly modelled. Carr returned to the United States in 1948 and died in Greenville, South Carolina.
