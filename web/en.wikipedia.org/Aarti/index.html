Aarti

Aarti (Hindi: आरती, ārtī) is a Hindu religious ritual of worship in which light — from camphor or from wicks soaked in ghee or oil — is offered to one or more deities, accompanied by songs sung in praise of the god. Aarti also refers to the songs sung during the ritual and to the plate (thali) holding the lamp, flowers, and other offerings. It is performed in temples and homes, typically morning and evening, and concludes most pujas.

The ritual

The aarti plate carries a lit lamp (deepak), often with five wicks or tiered flames, together with flowers, incense, and sometimes water and food. The worshipper or priest rotates the plate in a clockwise circular motion before the deity while ringing a bell and singing the aarti hymn, after which devotees cup their hands over the flame and touch their foreheads to receive the light as blessing. The circling is understood as a symbolic offering of the five elements and of the devotee's senses to the divine. In homes, small lamps on a simple plate are used; in major temples, aartis draw large congregations.

Notable aartis

Famous examples include the Ganga Aarti performed on the ghats of Varanasi, Rishikesh, and Haridwar — at Har Ki Pauri, large fire bowls are set afloat and raised in unison — and the aartis of the Golden Temple tradition (also practiced in Sikh worship as aarti with the hymn "Gagan mein thaal", attributed to Guru Nanak). Popular devotional aartis include "Om Jai Jagdish Hare" (Vishnu), "Om Jai Shiv Omkara" (Shiva), and "Sukhkarta Dukhharta" (Ganesha, in Marathi). The ceremony of the beating retreat at the Wagah–Attari border is sometimes informally likened to a martial aarti, and aarti forms part of the liturgy in Swaminarayan and ISKCON temples worldwide.

Etymology and history

The word derives from Sanskrit ārārtika, connected with the removal of darkness (tamas). Fire offerings descend from Vedic homa rituals, adapted in temple worship (agamic puja) into the offering of light (deepa) as one of the sixteen upacharas (services) to the deity.
