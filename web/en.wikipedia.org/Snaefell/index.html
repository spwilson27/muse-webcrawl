Snaefell

Snaefell (Manx: Sniaul) is the highest mountain on the Isle of Man, standing 621 metres (2,037 ft) above sea level. Its name derives from Old Norse snæfell, "snow hill". On a clear day, it is said that six kingdoms can be seen from the summit: the Isle of Man, England, Ireland, Scotland, Wales, and Heaven.

Access

The summit can be reached by the Snaefell Mountain Railway from Laxey, an electric railway opened in 1895, unique in the British Isles for using a Fell centre rail for braking. The A18 Snaefell Mountain Road, part of the Isle of Man TT Mountain Course, passes the Bungalow station near the summit.

Summit features

A summit hotel and café, a weather station, and telecommunications masts occupy the top. The Victorian-era summit hotel served the tourist trade brought by the railway. The mountain weather station has recorded some of the strongest winds in the British Isles.
