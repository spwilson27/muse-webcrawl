Dominant (music)

In music theory, the dominant is the fifth scale degree of a diatonic scale, and the chord built upon it — in major keys a major triad, and in minor keys usually a major triad or dominant seventh chord raised by the harmonic minor. It is abbreviated V (or V7) and is the second most important harmonic function after the tonic.

Function

The dominant has a strong tendency to resolve to the tonic, driven by the leading tone (the seventh scale degree), which pulls upward by semitone, and by the tritone within the dominant seventh chord that demands resolution. The progression V–I forms the authentic cadence, the principal means of establishing and confirming a key in common-practice harmony.

Extensions and substitutes

Dominant harmony generates a family of related sonorities: secondary dominants tonicise chords other than the tonic; the dominant seventh, ninth, and altered dominants add tension; the Neapolitan chord functions as a predominant preparing the dominant; and the tritone substitution replaces a dominant with another dominant a tritone away — a staple of jazz harmony. The "dominant" pedal point, sustained beneath changing harmonies, builds suspense before a recapitulation in sonata form.

Historical usage

The term derives from the dominanta of medieval chant theory, the reciting tone of a psalm tone, and was systematised by Jean-Philippe Rameau in the 18th century as one of the pillars of tonal harmony alongside the tonic and subdominant.
