Meaning24 progressive frames per second
OriginStandard frame rate of 35 mm film

24p refers to a video format that operates at 24 progressive frames per second (with "p" standing for progressive scan). It is derived from the frame rate long standard for motion picture film, and it is widely used to give digital video the motion characteristics — often called the "film look" — associated with cinema.

History and use

Twenty-four frames per second became the standard for sound film in the late 1920s, as the slowest rate that provided acceptable sound quality and economy of film stock. When digital cameras for independent filmmakers appeared in the early 2000s, 24p modes (such as those on the Panasonic DVX100) were celebrated for letting low-budget productions resemble shot-on-film features.

Distribution quirks

In NTSC regions, 24p material was historically transferred to 60 Hz video using 3:2 pulldown, which distributes film frames unevenly across video fields and can introduce judder. On Blu-ray and modern streaming platforms, true 24p (or 23.976p) output is preserved directly. Some viewers dislike the strobing of fast pans at 24 frames per second, a debate renewed by high-frame-rate releases such as The Hobbit trilogy at 48 frames per second.
