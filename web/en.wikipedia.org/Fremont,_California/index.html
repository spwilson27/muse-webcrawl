Fremont is a city in Alameda County, California, located in the East Bay region of the San Francisco Bay Area. It was incorporated in 1956 from the merger of five small communities: Centerville, Niles, Irvington, Mission San José, and Warm Springs. With a population of about 230,000, it is the fourth most populous city in the Bay Area.

History

The area was home to the Ohlone people, and Mission San José was founded in 1797. The city is named after explorer John C. Frémont. Niles became an early centre of the silent film industry, where Charlie Chaplin made films including The Tramp. The city's modern growth followed postwar suburban development and its position at the edge of Silicon Valley.

Economy

Fremont hosts major technology and manufacturing employers, most notably the Tesla Fremont Factory, one of the world's largest electric vehicle plants, along with numerous electronics and clean-tech firms. The city is one of the most ethnically diverse in the United States and has the largest Afghan American community in the country, centred in the Little Kabul area.

Landmarks

Landmarks include Mission Peak, a popular hiking destination; Lake Elizabeth and Central Park; the historic Niles district; and Mission San José. The city is served by two BART stations.
