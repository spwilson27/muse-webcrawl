The Battle of Tripoli (Arabic: معركة طرابلس) was a major engagement of the First Libyan Civil War fought in August 2011 for control of Libya's capital. Anti-Gaddafi rebel forces, coordinated with NATO air support, captured the city and effectively ended Muammar Gaddafi's 42-year rule.

Background

The uprising against Gaddafi began in February 2011. After months of fighting, rebel forces in the Nafusa Mountains broke through at Zawiya west of Tripoli, while NATO enforced a no-fly zone and struck loyalist forces under a United Nations mandate.

The battle

On the night of 20–21 August, rebel cells inside Tripoli rose up as fighters advanced from Zawiya and landed by sea from Misrata. Fighting centred on Gaddafi's Bab al-Azizia compound, which fell on 23 August after heavy bombardment. Gaddafi himself escaped; loyalist pockets around Abu Salim and the airport held out for several more days before the city was declared under rebel control around 28 August.

Aftermath

The fall of Tripoli ended the Gaddafi regime in all but name. Gaddafi was captured and killed near Sirte in October 2011, and the National Transitional Council assumed control of the country, though Libya subsequently descended into renewed instability and a second civil war.
