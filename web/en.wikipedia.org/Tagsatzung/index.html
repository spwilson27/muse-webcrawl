Tagsatzung

The Tagsatzung was the federal diet of the Old Swiss Confederacy, the assembly of delegates from the member cantons that served as the confederation's principal governing and diplomatic body from the early 15th century until 1848.

Function

Each canton sent envoys (usually two) with instructions from their home governments; decisions required broad consensus, and delegates often had to refer matters back. The diet met in various cities, frequently Baden and later Frauenfeld, and dealt with common affairs: war and peace, alliances, shared bailiwicks, and disputes between cantons.

History

Regular meetings developed after 1415. The Tagsatzung managed the jointly ruled subject territories and represented the confederation abroad. After the Sonderbund War of 1847, the federal constitution of 1848 replaced it with the modern Federal Assembly, turning Switzerland into a federal state.
