Māori culture

Māori culture (Māori: māoritanga) is the customs, cultural practices, and beliefs of the Māori, the indigenous Polynesian people of mainland New Zealand (Aotearoa). It originated with settlers from East Polynesia, who arrived in waves of waka (canoe) voyages between roughly 1320 and 1350, and developed a distinct culture over centuries of isolation.

Social organisation

Traditional society is organised around whānau (extended family), hapū (subtribe), and iwi (tribe). The marae, with its carved meeting house, remains the centre of communal life, hosting gatherings, funerals (tangihanga), and formal welcomes conducted under tikanga (customary protocol).

Language and arts

Te reo Māori, an Eastern Polynesian language, is an official language of New Zealand and has undergone a major revitalisation since the 1980s. Traditional arts include whakairo (carving), raranga (weaving), tā moko (tattooing), waiata (song), and the haka, a posture dance made world-famous by the All Blacks rugby team. Kapa haka (performing arts groups) flourish at regional and national competitions.

Beliefs and values

Core concepts include mana (prestige and authority), tapu (sacred restriction), mauri (life force), and kaitiakitanga (guardianship of the environment). Genealogy (whakapapa) links people to ancestors, land, and the natural world.
