Jon Kabat-Zinn (born Jon Kabat; June 5, 1944) is an American professor emeritus of medicine and the creator of the Stress Reduction Clinic and the Center for Mindfulness in Medicine, Health Care, and Society at the University of Massachusetts Medical School. He is credited with bringing mindfulness meditation into mainstream Western medicine and psychology.

Work

Trained in molecular biology at MIT under Salvador Luria, Kabat-Zinn was introduced to meditation by Zen missionary Philip Kapleau and studied with teachers including Thich Nhat Hanh and Seung Sahn. In 1979 he founded the Stress Reduction Clinic, where he developed Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR), an eight-week programme that adapts Buddhist mindfulness practices, stripped of religious framing, to help patients cope with chronic pain, stress and illness.

Influence and writings

MBSR became the template for a family of mindfulness-based interventions studied in hundreds of clinical trials and taught in hospitals and institutions worldwide. Kabat-Zinn's books include Full Catastrophe Living (1990), a manual of the MBSR programme, the bestseller Wherever You Go, There You Are (1994), Coming to Our Senses (2005) and Mindfulness for Beginners. He defines mindfulness as "the awareness that arises through paying attention, on purpose, in the present moment, non-judgmentally."
