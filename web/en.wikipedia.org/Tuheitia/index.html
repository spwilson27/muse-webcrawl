Tuheitia Paki (21 April 1955 – 30 August 2024), crowned as Kīngi Tuheitia, was the seventh Māori King, head of the Kīngitanga (Māori King movement) in New Zealand. He succeeded his mother, Dame Te Atairangikaahu, on her death in August 2006 and reigned for 18 years until his death in 2024, shortly after celebrating the eighteenth anniversary of his coronation (Koroneihana).

Life and reign

Tuheitia was the son of Te Atairangikaahu and Whatumoana Paki. He was educated in the Waikato and worked in various jobs, including as a truck driver and in cultural education, before his unexpected selection as his mother's successor — the Māori monarch is chosen by the leaders of the tribes involved in the Kīngitanga rather than by strict primogeniture. He was crowned at Tūrangawaewae Marae in Ngāruawāhia on the day of his mother's funeral, 21 August 2006.

As king, Tuheitia worked on Māori economic development, environmental advocacy (particularly concerning the Waikato River), and building relationships with indigenous peoples across the Pacific. He represented the Kīngitanga at events abroad and was a prominent, usually quietly spoken, presence in New Zealand public life. He underwent treatment for diabetes and kidney disease and received a kidney transplant in 2014.

Death and succession

Tuheitia died in Hamilton on 30 August 2024 following heart surgery, days after the Koroneihana celebrations marking 18 years of his reign. His tangihanga (funeral) at Tūrangawaewae drew tens of thousands, including Pacific royalty and the King and Queen of the Realm of New Zealand. On the final day of the tangi, his youngest child and only daughter, Ngā Wai Hono i te Pō, was chosen and crowned as the eighth Māori monarch.
