Thrissur Pooram is an annual Hindu temple festival held at the Vadakkunnathan Temple in Thrissur, Kerala, and is regarded as the largest and most famous of all poorams (temple festivals) in India, drawing hundreds of thousands of spectators.

[Image: Rows of caparisoned elephants at Thrissur Pooram — hero image placeholder]

History

The festival was organised in its present form in the 1790s by Sakthan Thampuran, the ruler of Cochin, after local temples were reportedly denied entry to another festival for arriving late. He united ten temples into two rival groups — the western group led by Paramekkavu Bhagavathi Temple and the eastern group led by Thiruvambadi Sri Krishna Temple — in friendly competition before the presiding deity Shiva of Vadakkunnathan.

Events

Over roughly 36 hours, processions of caparisoned elephants carrying golden nettipattam ornaments converge on the temple grounds. Signature events include the Ilanjithara Melam, a massed percussion ensemble of some 250 artists; the Kudamattam, a rapid-fire competitive exchange of colourful parasols atop elephants; and a celebrated pre-dawn fireworks display. The festival is notable for participation across religious communities in its organisation.
