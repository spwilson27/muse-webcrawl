Aldosterone

Aldosterone is the main mineralocorticoid steroid hormone. It is produced by the zona glomerulosa of the adrenal cortex and plays a central role in regulating blood pressure and electrolyte balance.

Aldosterone
Formula: C21H28O5
Produced in: Zona glomerulosa, adrenal cortex
Regulated by: Renin–angiotensin system, potassium
Target: Kidney collecting ducts

Function

Aldosterone acts on the kidneys to increase reabsorption of sodium ions and water and excretion of potassium ions. As part of the renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system, it is released when blood pressure or sodium falls, or potassium rises, restoring blood volume and pressure and helping prevent cardiovascular disease complications from chronic hypertension when in excess.

Disorders

Primary aldosteronism (Conn syndrome), usually from an adrenal adenoma or hyperplasia, causes hypertension, low potassium and suppressed renin. Low aldosterone occurs in Addison disease. Aldosterone was isolated in 1953 by Sylvia and James Tait with Tadeus Reichstein.
