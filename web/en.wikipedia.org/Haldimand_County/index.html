Haldimand County

Haldimand County is a rural city-status single-tier municipality on the Niagara Peninsula in Southern Ontario, Canada, on the north shore of Lake Erie and the Grand River. It had a population of about 46,000 in the 2016 census.

History

The county takes its name from Frederick Haldimand, governor of Quebec, whose 1784 Haldimand Proclamation granted lands along the Grand River to the Six Nations. The modern municipality was created in 2001 by the amalgamation of the former towns of Haldimand, Dunnville and part of Nanticoke.

Communities

The largest communities include Caledonia, Hagersville, Cayuga (the administrative centre), Dunnville and Jarvis. The economy combines agriculture, the Nanticoke industrial area on Lake Erie, and commuting to Hamilton.
