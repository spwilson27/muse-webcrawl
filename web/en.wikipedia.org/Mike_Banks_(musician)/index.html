Mike Banks, commonly known as "Mad" Mike Banks, is an American record producer and a central figure in Detroit techno. He co-founded the record label and music collective Underground Resistance with Jeff Mills and Robert Hood in 1989, and later founded the Submerge distribution company and Somewhere in Detroit (SID) label.

Underground Resistance

Underground Resistance adopted a militant, anonymous ethos — members performed masked and avoided publicity — while releasing politically charged techno records under Banks's direction. After Mills and Hood departed in the early 1990s, Banks became the collective's leader, steering projects such as Galaxy 2 Galaxy, which fused techno with live jazz instrumentation, and the Drexciya-associated electro of his label-mates' generation.

Submerge and community work

From his headquarters on East Grand Boulevard in Detroit, Banks built Submerge into a distribution hub for independent Detroit electronic labels and a de facto museum and community space. He is known for mentoring younger Detroit artists and for insisting on the African American roots of techno music against its commercialization overseas.

Style and influence

Banks's productions combine hard-edged machine funk with jazz harmony and science-fiction themes. Under aliases and group names he helped define the second wave of Detroit techno, influencing generations of producers worldwide while remaining deliberately outside the mainstream music industry.
