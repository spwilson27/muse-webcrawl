The Yalta Conference, codenamed Argonaut, was a meeting of the heads of government of the United States, the United Kingdom, and the Soviet Union held from 4 to 11 February 1945 to discuss the postwar reorganization of Germany and Europe. Held at the Livadia Palace near Yalta in Crimea, it was attended by President Franklin D. Roosevelt, Prime Minister Winston Churchill, and General Secretary Joseph Stalin.

Key agreements

The conference produced agreements on several major questions of the closing phase of World War II:

  • Germany: Nazi Germany would be demilitarized and denazified, divided into four occupation zones administered by the US, UK, USSR, and France, and would pay reparations.
  • United Nations: The leaders agreed to establish the United Nations, with a founding conference to open in San Francisco in April 1945 and a Security Council voting formula giving the great powers a veto.
  • Poland: Poland's eastern border would follow the Curzon Line, with territorial compensation from Germany in the west; the Soviet-backed Lublin government would be reorganized to include democratic leaders, with free elections promised.
  • Declaration on Liberated Europe: The three powers pledged to help liberated peoples establish democratic governments through free elections.
  • Far East: In a secret protocol, the Soviet Union agreed to enter the war against Japan two to three months after Germany's surrender, in return for territorial concessions including southern Sakhalin and the Kuril Islands.

Legacy

Yalta became a symbol of great-power diplomacy. In the West it was later criticized as a "betrayal" of Eastern Europe because Soviet domination of Poland and the region followed despite the promises of free elections; defenders noted that the Red Army already occupied most of the territories concerned. The conference was followed by the Potsdam Conference in July–August 1945.
