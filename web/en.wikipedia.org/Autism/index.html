Autism

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Repetitive behaviour is a core feature

Autism spectrum disorder (ASD), commonly known as autism, is a neurodevelopmental condition characterized by differences or difficulties in social communication and interaction, a preference for predictability and routine, sensory processing differences, focused interests, and repetitive behaviours. Features are present from early childhood and vary greatly between individuals.

Diagnosis

Since 2013, the DSM-5 has subsumed autistic disorder, Asperger syndrome, and pervasive developmental disorder not otherwise specified under the single diagnosis of autism spectrum disorder, graded by support needs. Diagnosis is behavioural, usually in early childhood, though many adults are diagnosed later. Prevalence estimates in many countries are around 1% or higher, with boys diagnosed more often than girls.

Causes

Autism is strongly heritable, with hundreds of associated genetic variants, and is understood as a difference in brain development rather than the result of parenting or vaccines — the claimed link to the MMR vaccine, based on a retracted 1998 paper by Andrew Wakefield, has been thoroughly discredited.

Support and perspectives

Support is individualized and may include speech and occupational therapy, educational accommodations, and treatment of co-occurring conditions such as anxiety or epilepsy. The neurodiversity movement and many autistic self-advocates frame autism as a natural form of human variation and emphasize acceptance and accommodations over cure.
