Point-to-point (telecommunications)

In telecommunications, a point-to-point connection is a communications connection between two communication endpoints or nodes, as opposed to a point-to-multipoint or broadcast connection, in which many nodes may receive information from one node. The term is also used in computer networking and computer architecture for a dedicated link between two systems.

Characteristics

A point-to-point link may be a physical circuit — such as a leased line, a microwave relay hop between two towers, a free-space optical link, or a pair of directional antennas — or a logical connection established over a shared medium, such as a PPP session or a VPN tunnel.

Old telephone service

Before shared switches, "point-to-point" described direct connections between two telephones or telegraph stations. The modern Plain Old Telephone Service is, by contrast, switched, though each call is a temporary end-to-end circuit.

Wireless point-to-point

Fixed wireless point-to-point links are widely used to extend networks between buildings or to provide backhaul in rural areas where fiber is uneconomical. They require line of sight between directional antennas and commonly operate in license-free bands.
