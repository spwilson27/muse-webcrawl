LGBT rights by country or territory

LGBT rights by country or territory vary widely around the world. This article summarises the legal status of lesbian, gay, bisexual and transgender people across sovereign states and territories, covering the legality of same-sex sexual activity, recognition of same-sex relationships, adoption, military service, anti-discrimination protections, and laws concerning gender identity and expression.

Global overview

As of the mid-2020s, marriage equality is legal in more than 35 countries, beginning with the Netherlands in 2001 and extending across much of Western Europe, the Americas, South Africa, Taiwan, Thailand and others. Civil unions or other partnership recognition exist in additional jurisdictions. Conversely, consensual same-sex activity remains criminalised in roughly 60 countries, mostly in Africa, the Middle East, Asia and the Caribbean, and is punishable by death in a small number of states including Iran, Saudi Arabia, Uganda (for "aggravated homosexuality") and parts of Nigeria and Somalia.

Regional summaries

Africa: South Africa is the continent's outlier, with constitutional protection and marriage equality since 2006; several states have decriminalised since 2019, while others have tightened penalties. Asia: Taiwan (2019), Nepal and Thailand (2025) lead on marriage; much of the region has no recognition, though India decriminalised homosexuality in 2018 (Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India). Europe: see LGBT rights in Europe. Americas: Canada, the United States, Mexico, Brazil, Argentina and others permit same-sex marriage nationwide. Oceania: Australia and New Zealand have marriage equality, while several Pacific island states retain colonial-era prohibitions.
