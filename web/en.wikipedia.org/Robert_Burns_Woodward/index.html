Robert Burns Woodward

Robert Burns Woodward (April 10, 1917 – July 8, 1979) was an American organic chemist widely regarded as the foremost synthetic organic chemist of the twentieth century. He received the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1965 "for his outstanding achievements in the art of organic synthesis".

Career

Woodward took his doctorate at MIT at the age of twenty and spent his career at Harvard University. He achieved landmark total syntheses of quinine, cholesterol and cortisone, strychnine, lysergic acid, cephalosporin C, and — with Albert Eschenmoser — vitamin B12, one of the most complex syntheses ever completed. With Roald Hoffmann he formulated the Woodward–Hoffmann rules governing pericyclic reactions.

Legacy

Woodward's insistence on planning syntheses with rigorous attention to stereochemistry and mechanism raised organic synthesis to an intellectual art form, and his school trained generations of leading chemists.
