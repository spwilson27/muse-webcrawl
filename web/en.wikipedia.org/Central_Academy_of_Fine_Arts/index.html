The Central Academy of Fine Arts (CAFA; Chinese: 中央美术学院) is a public art academy in Beijing, China, administered by the Ministry of Education. It is widely regarded as China's most prestigious institution for fine-art education and is the only institution of higher education devoted exclusively to fine arts under the Ministry's direct administration. Competition for places is intense, with tens of thousands of applicants taking its entrance examinations each year.

History

CAFA traces its origin to the National School of Fine Arts founded in Beijing in 1918, the first national art school in modern China. The artist and educator Xu Beihong — renowned for his ink paintings of horses and for promoting realist academic training — became its leading figure and, after the founding of the People's Republic in 1949, the first president of the newly organized Central Academy of Fine Arts (formally named in 1950). The academy played a central role in developing socialist realism in Chinese art and later, after the reform era began in 1978, in the emergence of contemporary Chinese art.

Academics

The academy comprises schools and departments covering Chinese painting, oil painting, printmaking, sculpture, mural painting, experimental art, design, architecture, art history and theory, and art management. The CAFA Art Museum, designed by Japanese architect Arata Isozaki, opened on the Wangjing campus in 2008 and holds a major collection including thousands of Ming and Qing dynasty scrolls.

Notable alumni and faculty

Graduates and teachers associated with CAFA include Xu Beihong, Wu Zuoren, Jin Shangyi (a former president), and prominent contemporary artists such as Fang Lijun, Yue Minjun, and Xu Bing (who served as vice-president). CAFA alumni have been central figures in movements from socialist realism to the avant-garde Cynical Realism of the 1990s.
