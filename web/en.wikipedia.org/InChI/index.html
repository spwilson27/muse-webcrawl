InChI

The International Chemical Identifier (InChI, pronounced "IN-chee") is a textual identifier for chemical substances, designed to provide a standard and human-readable way to encode molecular information and to facilitate the search for such information in databases and on the web. Initially developed by the International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry (IUPAC) and the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) during 2000–2005, the format and algorithms are non-proprietary.

Structure

Every InChI begins with the string "InChI=" followed by a version number (currently "1"), a letter "S" for the standard InChI, and a series of layers separated by slashes describing the chemical formula, atom connections, hydrogen atoms, charge, stereochemistry, and isotopic composition. For example, the standard InChI for ethanol is:

InChI=1S/C2H6O/c1-2-3/h3H,2H2,1H3

InChIKey

The InChIKey is a fixed-length (27-character) hashed representation of the InChI, designed for web searching and database indexing, since the full InChI can be very long for large molecules. The InChIKey is not unique in the strictest sense — collisions are theoretically possible — but is effectively unique for practical purposes. The InChIKey for ethanol is LFQSCWFLJHTTHZ-UHFFFAOYSA-N.

Development and adoption

Since 2009 the InChI Trust, a not-for-profit organization of which IUPAC is a member, has overseen development of the standard. InChI identifiers are widely used by chemical databases such as PubChem, ChemSpider, and ChEMBL, and by regulatory and publishing organizations.
