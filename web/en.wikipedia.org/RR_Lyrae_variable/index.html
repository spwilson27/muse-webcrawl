RR Lyrae variables
Pulsating variable stars
Periods typically 0.2–1 day
Used as standard candles

RR Lyrae variables are periodic variable stars, commonly found in globular clusters. They are used as standard candles to measure (relatively) nearby cosmic distances. This class is named after the prototype and brightest example, RR Lyrae, a star in the constellation Lyra.

Characteristics

RR Lyrae stars are pulsating horizontal branch stars of spectral class A or F, with masses of around 0.65 solar masses. They pulsate with periods typically between 0.2 and 1 day, and amplitudes of 0.3 to 2 magnitudes in visible light. They are old, metal-poor Population II stars that have left the red giant branch and are fusing helium in their cores.

Classification

The class is traditionally divided into subtypes: RRab stars pulsate in the fundamental mode with asymmetric light curves and periods of 0.4–1 day; RRc stars pulsate in the first overtone with shorter periods and more sinusoidal curves; and RRd stars pulsate in both modes simultaneously.

Period–luminosity relation

RR Lyrae stars obey a period–luminosity relation in infrared bands and a luminosity–metallicity relation in visible light, with absolute magnitudes near +0.6. Because they are abundant in globular clusters and the galactic halo, they serve as key distance indicators within the Milky Way and to nearby galaxies.
