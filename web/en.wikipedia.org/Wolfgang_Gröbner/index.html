Wolfgang Gröbner (11 February 1899 – 20 August 1980) was an Austrian mathematician whose name is associated with the Gröbner basis, a fundamental tool in computational algebra. His work laid groundwork that, decades later, became central to computer algebra systems.

Life and career

Born in Gossensaß (Colle Isarco) in South Tyrol, Gröbner studied engineering at the Technical University of Graz before turning to mathematics, completing his doctorate at the University of Vienna in 1932 under Philipp Furtwängler with a dissertation on polynomial ideals. After periods in Göttingen and Rome — where he worked with Francesco Severi — and dismissal from Vienna in the political turmoil of the late 1930s, he was appointed professor at the University of Innsbruck in 1947, remaining there until retirement.

Gröbner bases

In his 1949 habilitation work and later writings, Gröbner developed methods for computing with polynomial ideals, including what is now called a Gröbner basis. His doctoral student Bruno Buchberger formalised the concept, named it after Gröbner, and in 1965 gave an algorithm to compute such bases — Buchberger's algorithm — enabling effective computation in polynomial rings for solving systems of equations, robotics, coding theory, and automated theorem proving.
