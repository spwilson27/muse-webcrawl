Great Bear Rainforest

The Great Bear Rainforest is a temperate rain forest on the Pacific coast of British Columbia, Canada, comprising 6.4 million hectares. It is part of the larger Pacific temperate rain forest ecoregion, the largest coastal temperate rain forest in the world.

Ecology

The forest is home to old-growth stands of Sitka spruce, western red cedar and western hemlock, and to wildlife including grizzly bears, black bears, wolves and the Kermode bear — a rare white-coloured subspecies of black bear, sometimes called the "spirit bear". Its rivers support abundant salmon runs.

Conservation

After years of campaigning by First Nations, environmental groups and industry, the governments of British Columbia and First Nations announced land-use agreements in 2006, expanded in 2016, that protect most of the region from industrial logging and place it under ecosystem-based management.
