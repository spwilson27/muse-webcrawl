Logical connective

In logic, a logical connective (also called a logical operator) is a symbol or word used to connect two or more sentences in a grammatically valid way, such that the truth value of the compound sentence depends on the truth values of its parts. The most common connectives are conjunction ("and"), disjunction ("or"), negation ("not"), material implication ("if... then") and the biconditional ("if and only if").

Properties

Connectives may be truth-functional, as in classical propositional logic, where each connective is defined by a truth table. The set {¬, ∧} or even the single Sheffer stroke (NAND) is functionally complete: every truth function can be expressed with it. Natural-language counterparts of the connectives often carry extra pragmatic meaning beyond the truth-functional core, a topic studied in the philosophy of language.
