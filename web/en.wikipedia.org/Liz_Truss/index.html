Mary Elizabeth Truss (born 26 July 1975) is a British politician who served as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom and Leader of the Conservative Party from September to October 2022 — the shortest-serving prime minister in British history, at 49 days (seven weeks). She previously held cabinet posts including Foreign Secretary under Boris Johnson.

Early life and career

Truss was born in Oxford and raised in Paisley and Leeds; her parents were left-wing activists, and as a teenager she was active in the Liberal Democrats, even speaking for the abolition of the monarchy at their 1994 conference. She read Philosophy, Politics and Economics at Merton College, Oxford, then worked for Shell and Cable & Wireless and at the think tank Reform. Elected MP for South West Norfolk in 2010, she rose through government as Education minister, Environment Secretary, Justice Secretary, Chief Secretary to the Treasury, and International Trade Secretary before becoming Foreign Secretary in 2021.

Premiership

Truss won the 2022 Conservative leadership election against Rishi Sunak, promising tax cuts and deregulation, and became prime minister on 6 September 2022, two days before the death of Queen Elizabeth II. On 23 September her chancellor, Kwasi Kwarteng, delivered a "mini-budget" with £45 billion of unfunded tax cuts. Financial markets reacted violently: the pound fell to a record low against the dollar, gilt yields spiked, and the Bank of England intervened to prevent a collapse of pension funds. Truss reversed the tax cuts, sacked Kwarteng, and appointed Jeremy Hunt, but her authority collapsed. She resigned on 20 October 2022 and was succeeded by Sunak.

After Downing Street

Truss remained an MP and published a memoir, Ten Years to Save the West (2024), blaming institutional resistance for her downfall. She lost her seat in the 2024 general election, in which the Conservatives were heavily defeated.
