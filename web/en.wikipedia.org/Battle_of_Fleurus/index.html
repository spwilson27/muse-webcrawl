Battle of Fleurus

The Battle of Fleurus, fought on 26 June 1794, was a major engagement of the French Revolutionary Wars between the French Army of the Sambre and Meuse under General Jean-Baptiste Jourdan and the Coalition army commanded by Prince Josias of Saxe-Coburg-Saalfeld. The French victory led to the permanent loss of the Austrian Netherlands to the Coalition and marked a turning point in the war.

LocationFleurus, Austrian Netherlands (present-day Belgium)

Background

In the spring of 1794 the French Republic, having raised mass armies through the levée en masse, launched offensives on all fronts. Jourdan's army crossed the Sambre several times and besieged Charleroi. Coburg marched to relieve Charleroi, unaware that the fortress had surrendered the day before the battle.

The battle

The Coalition attacked the French positions around Fleurus in five columns along a wide front. The fighting was hard and prolonged; French positions at Lambusart collapsed under pressure from the Prince of Orange's column and Coburg nearly broke the French right. Jourdan committed his reserves and held on. The French also made the first significant military use of aerial reconnaissance: the observation balloon l'Entreprenant, flown by the Compagnie d'Aérostiers, reported Coalition movements from above the battlefield. Learning of Charleroi's fall and fearing encirclement, Coburg ordered a general retreat in the late afternoon.

Aftermath

Fleurus opened Belgium to French conquest and effectively knocked Austria out of the Low Countries for the remainder of the war. The battle is often seen as a triumph of the new French military system—large conscript armies fighting with ideological commitment—and its use of the observation balloon symbolised the dawn of air warfare.
