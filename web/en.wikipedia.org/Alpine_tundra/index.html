Alpine tundra

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Alpine tundra is a natural region of high mountain terrain lying above the tree line, where the climate is too cold and windy for trees to grow. Unlike arctic tundra, alpine tundra occurs at high elevations at all latitudes and generally lacks permafrost, with better-drained soils.

Climate and vegetation

The growing season is short, with strong sunlight, large daily temperature swings, and frequent frost. Vegetation consists of cushion plants, dwarf shrubs, grasses, sedges, lichens, and mosses adapted to wind and cold. Species differ by mountain range; examples include mountain avens, moss campion, and alpine forget-me-not.

Fauna

Animal life includes marmots, pikas, mountain goats, ibex, chamois, and ptarmigan, along with visiting summer migrants. Many species migrate downslope in winter.

Distribution

Alpine tundra is found in ranges such as the Rocky Mountains, the Alps, the Andes, the Himalayas, and the high mountains of East Africa, where afroalpine ecosystems occur.
