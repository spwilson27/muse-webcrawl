Glicko rating system

The Glicko rating system and its successor Glicko-2 are methods for assessing a player's strength in zero-sum games such as chess and Go, developed by Mark Glickman. They improve on the Elo rating system by tracking not only a rating but also a ratings deviation (RD), which measures the uncertainty of the rating estimate.

How it works

A player new to the system, or one who has not played for some time, has a large RD and an unreliable rating; as they play more games the RD shrinks and the rating becomes more certain. Results against opponents with high RD move a player's rating less than results against reliably rated opponents. Glicko-2 adds a third parameter, the rating volatility, which estimates the degree of expected fluctuation in a player's performance over time.

Use

The systems are used by chess bodies such as the Australian Chess Federation, by online game servers, and — in adapted form — in competitive video games and matchmaking systems. Glickman's papers describing the methods were published in 1995 and 2013.
