Lexicon

A lexicon is the vocabulary of a language or branch of knowledge (such as nautical or medical). In linguistics, a lexicon is a language's inventory of lexemes, and the mental lexicon is the store of words in a speaker's mind. The word also denotes a dictionary, especially of an ancient language such as Greek or Hebrew.

Linguistic theory

In generative linguistics, the lexicon is the component listing all the unpredictable properties of morphemes and words — their sounds, meanings, and category — while the grammar supplies the regular rules combining them. Lexical entries encode phonological, morphological, syntactic, and semantic information. Debates in the field concern how much information the lexicon contains and how lexical knowledge interacts with syntax.

Size and acquisition

The lexicon of a language comprises tens of thousands of lexemes; an educated adult English speaker may know 17,000–20,000 word families. Children acquire vocabulary rapidly during the "vocabulary spurt" of early childhood, and second-language learners treat building a lexicon as a central task.

Computational lexicons

Resources such as WordNet organise lexicons as networks of semantic relations and are used in natural language processing for word-sense disambiguation, search, and language modelling.
