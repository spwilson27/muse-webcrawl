Roussillon

The Roussillon plain and Mount Canigou

Roussillon (Catalan: Rosselló) is a historical and cultural region of southern France, roughly corresponding to the modern department of Pyrénées-Orientales. A former province of the Ancien Régime, it comprises the fertile Roussillon plain around its capital, Perpignan, together with the neighbouring districts of Vallespir, Conflent, Capcir and Cerdagne (the latter shared with Spain), forming the greater part of what is often called Northern Catalonia or French Catalonia.

Geography

The region is a Mediterranean lowland hemmed in by the Pyrenees to the south and west, the Corbières hills to the north, and the sea to the east. It is dominated by the peak of Canigou (2,784 m), a symbol of Catalan identity visible across the plain. Rivers—the Têt, Tech and Agly—water intensively farmed land producing wine (notably the sweet wines of Rivesaltes and Maury and the table wines of Côtes du Roussillon), early vegetables and fruit, especially apricots and peaches. The climate is among the sunniest and driest in France, swept by the cold north-westerly tramontane wind.

History

Roussillon was successively part of Roman Hispania, the Visigothic kingdom, and the Carolingian Spanish March, before becoming a county of the Catalan-Aragonese world. It passed between the Crown of Aragon and France during the late medieval and early modern wars; by the Treaty of the Pyrenees (1659), Spain ceded Roussillon and northern Cerdanya to France, fixing the border largely where it remains. The region's Catalan language and culture persist, though Catalan usage has declined since the 20th century under pressure from French-language schooling and media.

Identity

Roussillon retains distinctive Catalan traditions: the sardana dance, castells (human towers), Catalan-language festivals, and the red-and-gold stripes of the Senyera in its arms. Perpignan, described by Salvador Dalí as the "centre of the world" in homage to its railway station, is home to the Palace of the Kings of Majorca, a legacy of the 13th–14th-century Kingdom of Majorca.
