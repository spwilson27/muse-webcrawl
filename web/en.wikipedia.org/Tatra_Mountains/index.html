The Tatra Mountains are a mountain range on the border between northern Slovakia and southern Poland, forming the highest part of the Carpathian chain. Although compact in area, they display a pronounced alpine character, with granite peaks, glacial tarns, and deep valleys.

Divisions and peaks

The range is traditionally divided into the High Tatras in the east, the Western Tatras, and the Belianske Tatras. The highest summit is Slovakia's Gerlachovský štít at 2,655 m; the highest peak in Poland is Rysy (2,499 m on the border), and the most famous is Lomnický štít, served by a cable car and long home to an observatory.

Nature protection

Both countries protect the range as national parks — Tatra National Park in Slovakia (founded 1949) and Tatrzański Park Narodowy in Poland (1955) — jointly recognized by UNESCO as a transboundary biosphere reserve. The mountains support chamois, marmots, brown bears, lynx, and golden eagles. Resorts such as Zakopane in Poland and Štrbské Pleso and Tatranská Lomnica in Slovakia are major centers for hiking and skiing.
