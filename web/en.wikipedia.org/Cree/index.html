Cree

The Cree are a North American Indigenous people, one of the largest groups of First Nations in Canada. Their traditional lands span a vast territory from the Rocky Mountains to the Atlantic coast, across present-day Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, Ontario, Quebec, and the Northwest Territories. In the United States, Cree people live in Montana, notably on the Rocky Boy's Indian Reservation.

Language

The Cree language is an Algonquian language with a continuum of dialects; it is the most widely spoken Indigenous language in Canada. Cree is written in Latin script and in Canadian Aboriginal syllabics, a writing system developed in the 19th century.

History

The Cree were deeply involved in the fur trade with both the Hudson's Bay Company and French traders, and played central roles in the Métis history of the Canadian West. In the 1970s, the Grand Council of the Crees of Quebec successfully negotiated the James Bay and Northern Quebec Agreement, a landmark modern treaty.
