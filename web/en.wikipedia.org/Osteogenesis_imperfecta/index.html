Osteogenesis imperfecta

Osteogenesis imperfecta (OI), colloquially known as brittle bone disease, is a group of genetic disorders that mainly affect the bones, causing them to fracture easily, often with little or no apparent trauma. It affects roughly 1 in 15,000–20,000 births.

Cause

Most cases are caused by mutations in the COL1A1 or COL1A2 genes, which encode type I collagen, the main structural protein of bone. The condition is usually inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern, though rarer recessive forms exist.

Signs and management

Features can include blue sclerae (whites of the eyes), short stature, hearing loss, and dentinogenesis imperfecta affecting the teeth. Severity ranges from mild (type I) to perinatally lethal (type II). There is no cure; treatment involves fracture care, physical therapy, bisphosphonates, and sometimes rodding surgery to reinforce long bones.
