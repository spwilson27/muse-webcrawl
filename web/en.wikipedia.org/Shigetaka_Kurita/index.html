Shigetaka Kurita

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: portrait of Shigetaka Kurita]

Shigetaka Kurita (Japanese: 栗田 穣崇; born 9 May 1972) is a Japanese interface designer, best known as the creator of the first set of emoji. In 1999, while working for NTT Docomo on the i-mode mobile Internet service, Kurita designed a set of 176 pictograms, each 12 by 12 pixels, intended to convey information such as weather, traffic, and emotions in a compact visual form.

Kurita drew inspiration from manga, Japanese weather pictograms, and kanji characters. His original emoji set included symbols for the sun, rain, a heart, and facial expressions, and was displayed on the limited-resolution monochrome screens of early Japanese mobile phones. The set is now held in the permanent collection of the Museum of Modern Art in New York.

Career

After his work at NTT Docomo, Kurita joined the video game company Bandai Namco, and later worked with Dwango, the company behind the video-sharing site Niconico. He has spoken widely about the design constraints that shaped the original emoji and about their evolution into the rich, standardised sets maintained by the Unicode Consortium.
