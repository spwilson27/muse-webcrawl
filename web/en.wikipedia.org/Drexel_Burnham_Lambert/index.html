Drexel Burnham Lambert

IndustryInvestment banking
Founded1973 (as Drexel Burnham & Co.)
Defunct1990
HeadquartersNew York City, U.S.

Drexel Burnham Lambert was an American investment banking firm best known for its dominant position in the high-yield ("junk") bond market of the 1980s, and for its spectacular collapse in 1990 following a federal investigation into securities fraud.

Rise

The firm's fortunes were transformed by Michael Milken, who joined in 1969 and built its high-yield bond department, based in Beverly Hills. Drexel underwrote bonds for companies unable to borrow from traditional lenders and used the proceeds to finance the wave of corporate takeovers and leveraged buyouts of the 1980s, including the hostile bids of raiders such as T. Boone Pickens and Carl Icahn. By 1986 the firm was the most profitable on Wall Street.

Collapse

In 1986 arbitrageur Ivan Boesky agreed to cooperate with federal prosecutors and implicated Drexel in insider trading. In 1988 the firm was indicted under the RICO Act and pleaded guilty to six counts of securities and mail fraud, agreeing to pay $650 million in fines and penalties. Milken was indicted in 1989 and pleaded guilty to securities violations. Weakened by the charges and the collapse of the junk bond market, Drexel filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on 13 February 1990, then the largest bankruptcy of a Wall Street firm.
