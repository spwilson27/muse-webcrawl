Albrecht Dürer (1471–1528) was a German painter, printmaker, and theorist of the German Renaissance. Born in Nuremberg, he established his reputation across Europe in his twenties through his high-quality woodcut prints, and he is generally regarded as the greatest artist of the Northern Renaissance.

Career

Apprenticed to his goldsmith father and then to the painter Michael Wolgemut, Dürer travelled in Germany, the Netherlands, and twice to Italy, where he absorbed Renaissance ideas of perspective and proportion. His print series such as the Apocalypse (1498) and the Large Passion made him famous; his engravings, including Knight, Death and the Devil, Saint Jerome in His Study, and Melencolia I (all 1513–14), display unmatched technical mastery. His woodcut Rhinoceros (1515), drawn from a description of an animal he never saw, remained the standard image of the species for centuries.

Theory and legacy

Dürer wrote treatises on geometry (Underweysung der Messung, 1525), fortification, and human proportion, bringing mathematical rigour to Northern art. He worked for the Emperor Maximilian I and corresponded with leading humanists. His famous monogram "AD" became an early example of an artist's trademark.
