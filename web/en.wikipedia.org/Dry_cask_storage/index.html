Dry cask storage


Illustration: Dry cask storage

Dry cask storage is a method of storing high-level radioactive waste, such as spent nuclear fuel, after it has spent about a year cooling in a spent fuel pool. The fuel is sealed inside massive steel and concrete casks that passively dissipate decay heat through natural air circulation.

Design

A typical cask consists of a welded stainless-steel canister holding the fuel assemblies, placed inside a thick outer overpack of steel, concrete or cast iron that provides radiation shielding and protection against impacts, floods and earthquakes. Casks are licensed for decades of service and are monitored where they stand on concrete pads at reactor sites.

Role in waste management

Dry cask storage is an interim measure used worldwide while permanent deep geological repositories are developed. The first casks were licensed in the United States in the 1980s at the Surry Power Station in Virginia.
