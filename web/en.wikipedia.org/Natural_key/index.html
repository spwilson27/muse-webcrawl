Natural key

A natural key (also called a domain key) is a type of unique key in a relational database formed from attributes that already exist in the real world and have a logical relationship to the rest of the data — for example, a person's tax identification number, an ISBN for a book, or a vehicle identification number.

Comparison with surrogate keys

Natural keys contrast with surrogate keys, which are artificial identifiers (such as a sequential integer) with no business meaning. Supporters of natural keys argue they enforce real-world uniqueness and avoid extra indexes; critics note that real-world attributes can change, be reused, or prove not to be unique, which can force cascading updates through dependent tables. In practice, many database designs use a surrogate primary key together with a unique constraint on the natural key.
