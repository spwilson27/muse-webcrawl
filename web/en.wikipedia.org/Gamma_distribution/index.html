In probability theory and statistics, the gamma distribution is a versatile two-parameter family of continuous probability distributions defined on the positive real line. Its probability density function is f(x) = xk−1 e−x/θ / (Γ(k) θk) for x > 0, where k is the shape parameter, θ the scale parameter, and Γ the gamma function. An equivalent parameterisation replaces θ with a rate β = 1/θ.

Special cases and relationships

With k = 1 the gamma distribution reduces to the exponential distribution; with integer k it is the Erlang distribution, the distribution of the waiting time for the k-th event in a Poisson process. The chi-squared distribution is a gamma distribution with k = ν/2 and θ = 2. Sums of independent gamma variables with a common scale are again gamma-distributed, making the family closed under convolution.

Applications

The gamma distribution models waiting times, rainfall amounts, insurance claim sizes, and lifetimes of components. It is the conjugate prior for several likelihoods (including the Poisson rate and the exponential rate), giving it a central role in Bayesian statistics, and it supplies the error distribution in gamma generalised linear models. In queueing theory and reliability engineering, the Erlang special case models service times and times to the k-th failure.

Characterisation

For shape k > 1 the density has a single mode at (k − 1)θ; for k ≤ 1 it decreases monotonically from the origin. Skewness is 2/√k, so as k grows the distribution becomes increasingly symmetric and approaches a normal distribution, consistent with the central limit theorem since a gamma variable can be viewed as a sum of k exponential variables.
