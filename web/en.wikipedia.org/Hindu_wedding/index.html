Hindu wedding

A Hindu wedding, or vivaha, is the traditional Indian wedding ceremony of Hindus, and one of the most important of the sixteen samskaras (sacraments) in a Hindu's life. Ceremonies vary considerably by region, caste, and family custom, and celebrations may extend over several days of rituals, music, and feasting.

Pre-wedding rituals

Festivities commonly begin with an engagement ceremony and rituals such as the haldi, in which turmeric paste is applied to the bride and groom, the mehndi, in which henna designs are drawn on the bride's hands and feet, and the sangeet, an evening of music and dance.

The wedding ceremony

The central ceremony is held under a canopy (mandap) before a sacred fire, which serves as the divine witness (Agni). Key rites include kanyadana, the giving away of the bride by her father; panigrahana, the joining of hands; the exchange of garlands (jaimala); and the saptapadi, in which the couple take seven steps together, each accompanied by a vow. In many traditions the groom ties a sacred necklace (mangalsutra) around the bride's neck and applies vermilion (sindoor) to her hair parting.
