Drifting (motorsport)

Drifting is a driving technique and motorsport in which the driver intentionally oversteers, causing the rear wheels to lose traction while maintaining control of the car through a corner. In competition, drivers are judged on line, angle, speed, and style rather than simply on finishing time.

History

Drifting originated in Japan in the 1970s, when racers such as Kunimitsu Takahashi used controlled slides in touring car races, and street racers on mountain passes (touge) refined the technique. Keiichi Tsuchiya, known as the "Drift King," popularised drifting through videos and exhibition events in the 1980s. The D1 Grand Prix, founded in Japan in 2000, became the first major professional drifting series, and the sport spread worldwide, with Formula Drift established in the United States in 2004.

Techniques

A drift can be initiated by several methods: the handbrake (e-brake) turn, clutch kicking, power-over slides, braking drifts, and the feint or Scandinavian flick, in which the car is briefly steered away from the corner to shift weight before turning in. Once sliding, the driver counter-steers and modulates the throttle to hold the desired slip angle.

Competition

Modern events typically feature solo qualifying runs followed by tandem battles, in which a lead driver sets the line and a chase driver attempts to mirror it as closely as possible while maintaining proximity. Judges score angle, line, speed, and overall impression. Drift cars are usually rear-wheel-drive, heavily modified for steering angle, cooling, and durability, and they consume large numbers of rear tyres.

In popular culture

Drifting has featured prominently in films such as The Fast and the Furious: Tokyo Drift (2006), in anime and manga including Initial D, and in numerous video games.
