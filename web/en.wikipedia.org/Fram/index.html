Fram

Fram ("Forward") is a Norwegian polar exploration vessel built in 1892, famous for three great expeditions: Fridtjof Nansen's Arctic drift of 1893–1896, Otto Sverdrup's survey of the Canadian Arctic islands of 1898–1902, and Roald Amundsen's expedition to the South Pole in 1910–1912. Designed by Colin Archer with a rounded hull to ride up on the ice rather than be crushed, she is preserved at the Fram Museum in Oslo.

Design

Archer gave Fram an exceptionally strong, smooth, rounded hull so that squeezing ice would lift her instead of crushing her, shallow draft, a retractable rudder and propeller, and a windmill-driven generator providing electric light during the polar night.

Expeditions

Nansen deliberately froze Fram into the Siberian pack ice in 1893 and drifted across the Arctic Ocean for three years, proving the transpolar drift current; Nansen and Hjalmar Johansen meanwhile reached a record 86°13.6′N by sledge. Sverdrup charted some 260,000 km² of the Canadian high Arctic. Amundsen used the ship to reach Antarctica's Bay of Whales, from which his party reached the South Pole on 14 December 1911.

Legacy

Fram has sailed farther north and farther south than any other wooden ship. Since 1936 she has been housed in her own museum at Bygdøy, Oslo, alongside the Gjøa and the Kon-Tiki raft.
