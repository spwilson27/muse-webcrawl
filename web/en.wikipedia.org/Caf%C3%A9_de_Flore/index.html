Café de Flore

The Café de Flore is one of the oldest coffeehouses in Paris, celebrated for its famous clientele. It is located at the corner of Boulevard Saint-Germain and Rue Benoît in the 6th arrondissement, in the Saint-Germain-des-Prés quarter.

History

The café opened in the 1880s, during the Third Republic, taking its name from a sculpture of the goddess Flora on the opposite side of the boulevard. Around the turn of the century it attracted right-wing literary circles around Charles Maurras, who wrote his book Sous le signe de Flore there.

Existentialist era

In the mid-twentieth century the Café de Flore became a centre of Parisian intellectual life. Jean-Paul Sartre and Simone de Beauvoir wrote at its tables during and after the Second World War, and it was frequented by Albert Camus, Pablo Picasso, and other artists and writers associated with existentialism and the post-war Left Bank scene.

Today

The café retains a classic Art Deco interior, largely unchanged since the war, and remains a fashionable, if expensive, Parisian institution. Since 1994 it has awarded the annual Prix de Flore literary prize for young authors.
