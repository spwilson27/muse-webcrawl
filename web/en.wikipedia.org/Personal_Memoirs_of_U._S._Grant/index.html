Personal Memoirs of U. S. Grant

The Personal Memoirs of U. S. Grant is an autobiography by Ulysses S. Grant, commanding general of the Union Army and 18th President of the United States, focusing mainly on his military career in the Mexican–American War and the American Civil War. It was published in 1885–86 by Mark Twain's publishing firm, Charles L. Webster and Company.

Personal Memoirs
Author: Ulysses S. Grant
Published: 1885–1886 (2 volumes)
Publisher: Charles L. Webster & Co.
Subject: Mexican–American War, Civil War

Background

Financially ruined by fraud, and dying of throat cancer, Grant wrote the memoirs in a race against death to provide for his family, completing the manuscript days before he died in July 1885. Twain, his friend, offered generous terms and organised subscription sales.

Reception

The memoirs were a critical and commercial triumph, earning Grant's widow substantial royalties, and are regarded among the finest military memoirs in English for their clarity, candour and modesty. Grant deliberately ended the narrative with the end of the Civil War, saying little about his presidency.
