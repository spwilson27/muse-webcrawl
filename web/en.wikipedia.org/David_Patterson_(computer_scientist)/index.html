David Patterson (computer scientist)

David Andrew Patterson (born 16 November 1947) is an American computer scientist and academic who was a professor at the University of California, Berkeley from 1976 to 2016. He is a pioneer of reduced instruction set computer (RISC) architecture and co-inventor of RAID storage. With John Hennessy he shared the 2017 ACM Turing Award for their systematic, quantitative approach to computer architecture; their textbooks reshaped the teaching of the field.

RISC and Berkeley

In the early 1980s Patterson led the Berkeley RISC project, which demonstrated that a processor with a small, simple instruction set could outperform the complex-instruction-set designs then dominant. The Berkeley work, alongside parallel efforts at Stanford, helped launch the RISC revolution and fed into the commercial SPARC architecture. Patterson also led the Network of Workstations project and contributed to the design of scalable systems.

RAID and textbooks

With Garth Gibson and Randy Katz, Patterson published the landmark 1988 paper defining RAID (redundant array of inexpensive disks), which became the standard architecture for reliable, high-performance storage. His books with Hennessy, Computer Architecture: A Quantitative Approach and Computer Organization and Design, are among the most widely used texts in computing education.

Later work

Patterson served as president of the ACM (2004–2006) and chaired the Computing Research Association. After retiring from Berkeley he joined Google as a Distinguished Engineer, working on Tensor Processing Units for machine learning, and became a leading advocate of the open RISC-V instruction set architecture, co-founding the RISC-V movement through Berkeley's later projects.
