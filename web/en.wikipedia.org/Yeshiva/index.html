Yeshiva

Image placeholder: Students studying Talmud in a yeshiva beit midrash

A yeshiva (Hebrew: ישיבה) is a Jewish educational institution that focuses on the study of traditional religious texts, primarily the Torah and Talmud. Yeshiva education is typically for males, though parallel institutions for women exist.

Structure

Study takes place in a communal hall, the beit midrash, often in pairs (chavruta) who read and argue over texts aloud, interspersed with lectures (shiurim) given by the rosh yeshiva. The curriculum centers on Talmudic analysis, Jewish law, and ethics (musar).

History

The modern yeshiva model developed in 19th-century Lithuania, notably the Volozhin Yeshiva (founded 1803), considered the mother of Lithuanian yeshivas. Today major yeshivas operate in Israel, the United States, and elsewhere, and advanced married students study in a kollel.
