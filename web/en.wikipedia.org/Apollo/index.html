Apollo is one of the Olympian deities in classical Greek and Roman religion and mythology. The son of Zeus and Leto and the twin brother of Artemis, he is the god of archery, music and dance, truth and prophecy, healing and diseases, the Sun and light, and poetry. He is one of the most widely worshipped of the Greek gods, and the only major deity whose name passed unchanged into Roman religion.

Mythology

Apollo was born on the island of Delos, where his mother Leto, pursued by the jealous Hera, found refuge. Four days after his birth he slew the serpent Python at Delphi and took possession of its oracle, becoming the patron of prophecy; the Pythia delivered his oracles there. He is the patron of the Muses and leader of their choir, inventor of the lyre (which he received from Hermes), and a formidable archer whose arrows brought plague, as at the opening of the Iliad. His loves, mostly ill-fated, include Daphne, who was transformed into a laurel tree to escape him, and Hyacinthus; his sons include Asclepius, god of medicine, and the musician Orpheus in some traditions.

Worship

Major sanctuaries of Apollo stood at Delphi, Delos, Didyma, and Claros. The Pythian Games at Delphi ranked second only to the Olympic Games. In later antiquity Apollo was increasingly identified with the sun god Helios, and his oracle and healing cults remained influential into the Roman Empire.
