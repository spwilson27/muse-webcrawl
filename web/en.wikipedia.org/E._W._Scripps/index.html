E. W. Scripps

Edward Willis Scripps (June 18, 1854 – March 12, 1926) was an American newspaper publisher who built one of the first modern mass-media empires, the Scripps newspaper chain, and founded United Press (later United Press International) in 1907. He was also the co-founder, with his sister Ellen Browning Scripps, of the Scripps Institution of Oceanography in La Jolla, California.

Career

Scripps began working at the Detroit News, published by his half-brother James Edmund Scripps, and in 1878 founded the Penny Paper in Cincinnati (later the Cincinnati Post), using inexpensive papers aimed at working-class readers. He went on to acquire or found papers in Cleveland, St. Louis, Toledo, Chicago and many other cities, pioneering the chain model with centralized management and local editorial control.

In 1907 he merged three regional news services into United Press, breaking the Associated Press's restrictions on which papers could buy news. In 1922 he founded the Science Service to popularize science journalism, and in 1921 his son-in-law Roy W. Howard became his partner in the Scripps-Howard chain.

Philanthropy

With his sister Ellen, Scripps financed the Scripps Institution of Oceanography (1903) in La Jolla, now part of the University of California, San Diego. He spent his last years at his estate, Miramar, near San Diego, and died aboard his yacht Ohio off Liberia in 1926.
