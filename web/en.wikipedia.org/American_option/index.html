American option

An American option is a style of financial option contract that allows the holder to exercise the option at any time up to and including the expiration date. This contrasts with a European option, which can only be exercised at expiration. Most exchange-traded equity options are American-style.

Characteristics

Because of the flexibility of early exercise, an American option is worth at least as much as an otherwise identical European option. Early exercise is never optimal for a call option on a non-dividend-paying stock, but may be optimal for puts, or for calls on assets paying dividends shortly before expiration.

Valuation

There is no general closed-form solution for American options comparable to the Black–Scholes formula for European options. They are typically priced with numerical methods such as binomial or trinomial trees, finite difference methods, or approximations such as the Barone-Adesi and Whaley method. The names "American" and "European" refer only to exercise style, not geography.
