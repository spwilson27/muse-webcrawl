Stadthagen is a town in the district of Schaumburg, in Lower Saxony, Germany. It is situated approximately 40 km west of Hanover and serves as the administrative seat (Kreisstadt) of the Schaumburg district.

History

Stadthagen was founded in the early 13th century by Count Adolf III of Holstein-Schaumburg, who built a castle and granted the settlement town rights around 1224. It developed as a planned town around its market place and the church of St Martini. In the 17th century it briefly became the seat of a university: the Alma Ernestina, founded in nearby Rinteln, held its beginnings in Stadthagen's gymnasium, and the town was a residence of the counts of Schaumburg-Lippe before the court moved to Bückeburg.

Sights

Notable buildings include the former castle of the counts of Schaumburg (now housing the tax office), the St Martini church with the attached mausoleum of the princely house of Schaumburg-Lippe — a domed building designed by Giovanni Maria Nosseni and considered a masterpiece of the German Renaissance — and the old town hall. The mausoleum's resurrection monument by Adriaen de Vries is a significant work of bronze sculpture.

Economy and transport

Stadthagen lies on the Hanover–Minden railway line and the Bundesstraße 65. Local industry has historically included glassworks, machinery, and food processing, and the town functions as a service and shopping centre for the surrounding Schaumburg region.
