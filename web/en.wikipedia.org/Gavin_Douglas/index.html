Gavin Douglas (c. 1474 – September 1522) was a Scottish bishop, makar and translator. Though a churchman and statesman, he is chiefly remembered as a major figure of the Scottish Renaissance in literature, particularly for his Eneados, the first complete translation of Virgil's Aeneid into a vernacular language of the British Isles (Middle Scots), completed in 1513.

Life

Douglas was the third son of Archibald Douglas, 5th Earl of Angus. He studied at the University of St Andrews and possibly in Paris. He became provost of St Giles in Edinburgh and was later appointed Bishop of Dunkeld in 1515, though political turmoil delayed his possession of the see. Caught up in the factional struggles following the death of James IV at Flodden, he spent his last years in exile in England, where he died of the plague in London in 1522.

Works

His major works include The Palice of Honour (c. 1501), an allegorical dream-vision poem, and the Eneados, whose prologues — original essays in verse preceding each book — are regarded as among the finest achievements of Middle Scots poetry and an important early work of literary criticism.
