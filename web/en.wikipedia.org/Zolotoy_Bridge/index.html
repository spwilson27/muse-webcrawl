Zolotoy Bridge

The Zolotoy Bridge (Russian: Золотой мост, "Golden Bridge") is a cable-stayed bridge across the Zolotoy Rog (Golden Horn Bay) in Vladivostok, Russia. It links the city centre with the Churkin Cape district, whose residents previously faced a long drive or ferry ride around the bay. The bridge was opened to traffic on 11 August 2012, in time for the APEC summit held in Vladivostok that year.

Design

The bridge is 1,388 metres long with a main span of 737 metres, making it, at the time of its opening, one of the longest cable-stayed spans in the world. Its two M-shaped pylons rise about 226 metres above the bay, and the fan-arranged stays support a roadway carrying six lanes of traffic. Construction began in 2008 as part of a package of summit infrastructure that also included the Russky Bridge to Russky Island and a new airport terminal.

Name

Proposals to bridge the Golden Horn date back to the late 19th century and were repeatedly shelved, including because of the Russo-Japanese War and, later, the Second World War. The completed bridge takes its name from the bay it crosses, itself named for its resemblance to the Golden Horn of Istanbul.
