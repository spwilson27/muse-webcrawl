Web archiving

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Web archiving is the process of collecting portions of the World Wide Web to ensure the information is preserved in an archive for future researchers, historians and the public. Web archivists typically use web crawlers for automated capture, due to the massive size and amount of information on the web. The largest web archiving organisation is the Internet Archive, whose Wayback Machine holds hundreds of billions of captures.

Methods

Remote harvesting with crawlers such as Heritrix is the most common technique: a crawler follows links from seed URLs and stores pages and their embedded resources, usually in the WARC format. Other approaches include transaction archiving, which records exchanges between server and browser; client-side capture using browser-based tools that preserve interactive and social-media content; and database archiving of the content behind dynamic sites.

Motivations and challenges

Web content is ephemeral: studies of link rot show that a large fraction of cited web pages change or vanish within a few years. National libraries in many countries archive their national web domains under legal deposit laws. Difficulties include the sheer scale of the web, dynamically generated and personalised pages, content behind logins, streaming media, and legal questions over copyright and privacy.

Organisations

Besides the Internet Archive, major programmes include the UK Web Archive, the National Library of Australia's Pandora and Trove services, and the International Internet Preservation Consortium (IIPC), founded in 2003 to coordinate member institutions.
