The Arctic Ocean is the smallest and shallowest of the world's five oceanic divisions. It covers an area of about 14,056,000 square kilometres (5,427,000 sq mi) and is known as the coldest of all the oceans. The International Hydrographic Organization (IHO) recognizes it as an ocean, although some oceanographers call it the Arctic Mediterranean Sea. It has also been described as an estuary of the Atlantic Ocean. It is sometimes considered the northernmost part of the all-encompassing world ocean.

The Arctic Ocean includes the North Pole region in the middle of the Northern Hemisphere and extends south to about 60°N. It is surrounded by the land masses of Eurasia and North America, and the borders follow topographic features: the Bering Strait on the Pacific side and the Greenland Scotland Ridge on the Atlantic side. It is partly covered by sea ice throughout the year and almost completely in winter.

Geography

The Arctic Ocean occupies a roughly circular basin and covers an area of about 14,056,000 square kilometres, almost the size of Russia. The coastline is 45,389 kilometres long. It is surrounded by the land masses of Eurasia, North America, and Greenland, and several islands, including the second-largest island in the world, Greenland, and Iceland. It includes Baffin Bay, Barents Sea, Beaufort Sea, Chukchi Sea, East Siberian Sea, Greenland Sea, Hudson Bay, Hudson Strait, Kara Sea, Laptev Sea, White Sea, and other tributary bodies of water. It is connected to the Pacific Ocean by the Bering Strait and to the Atlantic Ocean through the Greenland Sea and Labrador Sea.

Climate

The Arctic Ocean is characterized by a polar climate, with persistent cold and relatively narrow annual temperature ranges. Winter is marked by the polar night, extreme cold, frequent low-level temperature inversions, and stable weather conditions; summer is characterized by continuous daylight (the midnight sun), and air temperatures can rise slightly above 0 °C. In recent decades the ocean has warmed and its summer sea ice extent has declined markedly, a trend attributed to climate change, with consequences for the Northern Sea Route and Northeast Passage shipping lanes.

Marine life

Endangered marine species in the Arctic Ocean include walruses and whales. The fragile ecosystem is slow to change and slow to recover from disruptions. The lion's mane jellyfish is abundant in Arctic waters, and the banded gunnel is the only species of gunnel living in the ocean. The bowhead whale, narwhal, beluga whale, and polar bear are emblematic species strongly associated with the region.

Natural resources

Petroleum and natural gas fields, placer deposits, polymetallic nodules, and sand and gravel aggregates occur within the Arctic Ocean basin. The United States Geological Survey has estimated that the Arctic may hold a substantial share of the world's undiscovered petroleum resources, though extraction is constrained by ice, remoteness, and environmental concerns.
