Carnac stones

LocationCarnac, Brittany, France
PeriodNeolithic (c. 4500–3300 BC)
FeaturesAlignments, dolmens, tumuli, menhirs

The Carnac stones (French: Alignements de Carnac) are an exceptionally dense collection of megalithic sites in Brittany in northwestern France, consisting of stone alignments (rows), dolmens (stone tombs), tumuli (burial mounds) and single menhirs (standing stones). More than 10,000 Neolithic stones were erected by pre-Celtic people around Carnac and neighboring communes.

Alignments

The most famous alignments—Ménec, Kermario, and Kerlescan—comprise over a thousand stones each arranged in converging rows up to about a kilometre long. The stones were hewn from local granite outcrops during the Neolithic, roughly 4500–3300 BC, making them older than Stonehenge's main phases and the Egyptian pyramids.

Purpose

Many explanations have been proposed—astronomical observatories, religious processions, memorials to ancestors, or earthquake detectors—none universally accepted. In 2025 the megaliths of Carnac and of the southern Morbihan coast were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.

Legends

Local legend says the stones are a Roman legion turned to stone by the wizard Merlin, or by Saint Cornelius, whose relics lie in Carnac's church.
