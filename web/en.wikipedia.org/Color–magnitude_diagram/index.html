Color–magnitude diagram

A color–magnitude diagram (CMD) is a scatter plot used in astronomy that shows the relationship between the absolute magnitude (or luminosity) of stars and their color (related to surface temperature). It is the observational counterpart of the theoretical Hertzsprung–Russell diagram, with the stellar color index — commonly B−V, the difference between blue and visual magnitudes — plotted on the horizontal axis instead of spectral type or temperature.

Features

When stars of a cluster are plotted, clear structures emerge: a diagonal main sequence of hydrogen-burning stars; a turnoff point where stars are exhausting core hydrogen; a red giant branch rising to bright, cool stars; a horizontal branch; and white dwarfs at the faint, blue corner. Because all cluster members share distance and age, the CMD of a cluster displays stellar evolution almost at a glance.

Uses

Fitting theoretical isochrones to a cluster's CMD yields its age (from the main-sequence turnoff), distance (by main-sequence fitting), chemical composition and interstellar reddening. CMDs from surveys such as Hipparcos and Gaia, which plot millions of field stars, have revealed fine structure in the Milky Way's stellar populations, including the white dwarf sequence and signatures of past galactic mergers. Color–magnitude diagrams are also fundamental tools for studying galaxies, whose integrated light can be modelled as sums of stellar populations.
