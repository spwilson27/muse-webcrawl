Canada lynx

The Canada lynx (Lynx canadensis) is one of the four living species in the genus Lynx. It is a medium-sized wild cat characterized by long, dense fur, triangular ears with black tufts at the tips, and broad, snowshoe-like paws. Its range spans Canada and Alaska, with populations extending into parts of the northern United States.

Characteristics

The Canada lynx stands about 48–56 cm at the shoulder and weighs 8–17 kg. First described by Robert Kerr in 1792, it is closely related to the Eurasian lynx and the bobcat. Three subspecies have been proposed, though their validity is debated.

Ecology and behaviour

The Canada lynx is a specialist predator of the snowshoe hare, and its populations follow the roughly ten-year boom-and-bust cycle of hare numbers in the boreal forest. When hares are scarce, lynx may take rodents, grouse, and other prey. Canada lynx are solitary and primarily nocturnal, occupying large home ranges in dense northern forests.

Conservation

The species is listed as Least Concern on the IUCN Red List because of its wide distribution in Canada and Alaska, but the contiguous United States distinct population segment was listed as Threatened under the U.S. Endangered Species Act in 2000, mainly owing to habitat fragmentation and the loss of connected forest habitat.
