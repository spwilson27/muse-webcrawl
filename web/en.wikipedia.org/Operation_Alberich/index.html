Operation Alberich

Operation Alberich
Date: 9 February – 20 March 1917
Location: Northern France
Result: German retirement to the Hindenburg Line completed

Operation Alberich (German: Unternehmen Alberich) was the code name of a German military operation in France during the First World War. Two salients had been formed during the Battle of the Somme in 1916 between Arras and Saint-Quentin and Bapaume and Noyon. Alberich was planned as a strategic withdrawal to new positions on the shorter, more easily defended Hindenburg Line (German: Siegfriedstellung).

The withdrawal

The retirement took place between 9 February and 20 March 1917. As they withdrew, German forces conducted a scorched-earth campaign: roads, bridges, and railways were destroyed, wells were poisoned, trees felled, and buildings demolished, and booby traps were left behind. Around 125,000 French civilians were evacuated from the area, many deported to work in Germany. The devastated region included the towns of Bapaume and Péronne.

Consequences

The withdrawal shortened the German front by about 40 kilometres, freeing roughly a dozen divisions for other duties, and disrupted Allied plans for a spring offensive. The deliberate destruction of the abandoned territory caused outrage in Allied countries and was later cited as an example of German misconduct in the war.
