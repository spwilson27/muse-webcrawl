The Academy Award for Best Picture is one of the Academy Awards ("Oscars") presented annually by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences (AMPAS) since the awards debuted in 1929. It is generally regarded as the most prestigious award of the ceremony and the final one presented; uniquely among the categories, it is voted on by the entire Academy membership and is awarded to the film's producers rather than to an individual artist.

History

At the first ceremony there were two top awards: Outstanding Picture, won by Wings, and Unique and Artistic Picture, won by Sunrise; the latter was discontinued and Wings is retrospectively counted as the first Best Picture. The category's name changed several times before settling on "Best Picture" in 1962. The number of nominees varied: five for most of the award's history, ten from 2009 (a change prompted partly by the omission of popular films such as The Dark Knight), then a variable number under a preferential ballot, and a fixed ten again from 2021. The preferential (instant-runoff) voting system, used since 2009, has produced notable outcomes including the 2017 ceremony, when La La Land was mistakenly announced before the true winner, Moonlight, was revealed. Milestones include Midnight Cowboy (the only X-rated winner), The Silence of the Lambs (a horror film), Parasite (2019; the first non-English-language winner) and CODA (the first winner distributed primarily by a streaming service).

Records

Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer is the studio with the most wins historically. Three films — Ben-Hur, Titanic and The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King — share the record of eleven Academy Awards overall, each including Best Picture.
