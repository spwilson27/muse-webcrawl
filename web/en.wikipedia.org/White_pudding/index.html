White pudding

RegionScotland, Ireland, parts of England
Main ingredientsOatmeal, suet, onion ( pork in Irish versions)
Similar dishesBlack pudding, haggis, skirlie

White pudding is a meat dish popular in Scotland and Ireland. It is very similar to black pudding, but does not include blood. Scottish versions consist of suet, oatmeal, onions, and spices stuffed into a casing, while many Irish versions include pork and pork fat.

History

Like black pudding, white pudding descends from medieval dishes that combined grain, fat, and offal in a gut casing to preserve scarce ingredients. Recipes appear in British cookery from the early modern period onward, with regional variations across the British Isles and in areas of Scottish and Irish settlement abroad, including Atlantic Canada.

Preparation and serving

White pudding is usually sliced and fried, and is a traditional component of a full Scottish or Irish breakfast alongside black pudding, eggs, bacon, and sausage. In Scottish fish and chip shops, a whole white pudding may be battered and deep-fried and served as a "white pudding supper" with chips.
