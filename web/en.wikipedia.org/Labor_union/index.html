Trade union

A trade union march; unions bargain collectively over wages and conditions.

A trade union (in American English, labor union) is an organization formed by workers in a particular trade, industry, or company to protect and advance their rights and interests. Through collective bargaining, unions negotiate with employers over wages, working hours, benefits, health and safety, and job security, and they may organize strikes or other industrial action when negotiations fail.

History

Modern unions grew out of the guilds and combination societies of the industrial revolution. In Britain, unions were legalized by legislation of the 1870s after decades of repression epitomized by the Tolpuddle Martyrs case (1834). In the United States, national organizations such as the Knights of Labor and the American Federation of Labor (1886) were followed in the 1930s by the Congress of Industrial Organizations and landmark laws such as the National Labor Relations Act of 1935. Union density peaked in many Western countries in the mid-twentieth century; it has since declined in the private sector in countries such as the US and UK, while remaining high in Scandinavia, where unions administer unemployment insurance under the Ghent system.

Structure and types

Unions may be organized as craft unions (workers of one skill), industrial unions (all workers in an industry), or general unions. Local unions commonly belong to national federations — such as the AFL–CIO in the United States, the Trades Union Congress in Britain, or the DGB in Germany — and internationally to bodies like the International Trade Union Confederation. Union governance is typically democratic, with elected shop stewards and officers, funded by members' dues.

Effects and debates

Economists find that unions raise members' wages (the "union wage premium"), compress wage inequality, and improve benefits and safety, while critics argue they can reduce employment flexibility and productivity in some settings; evidence on productivity effects is mixed. Unions have also been major political actors, founding or supporting labour and social-democratic parties in many countries.
