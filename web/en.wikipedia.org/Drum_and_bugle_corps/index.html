Drum and bugle corps

A drum and bugle corps is a marching ensemble composed of brass instruments, percussion instruments, and color guard. Modern corps are independent, nonprofit youth organizations that perform elaborate field shows in competition, most prominently under Drum Corps International (DCI), founded in 1971. The name survives from an era when the brass instruments were valveless or two-valved bugles in the key of G.

History

Drum corps grew out of American veterans' organizations such as the American Legion and VFW after World War I, which sponsored corps for parades and contests. Over the twentieth century the activity evolved from military-style exhibitions into highly theatrical field productions with front ensembles (stationary percussion), electronic instruments (permitted in DCI from 2009), and brass in B♭ and F.

Competitive structure

DCI World Class corps tour the United States each summer, with members typically aged up to 21 ("aging out" after their 22nd birthday), rehearsing and travelling for roughly ten weeks. Shows are scored by judges on music, visual, and general-effect captions, culminating in the DCI World Championships in Indianapolis each August. Leading corps include the Blue Devils, Carolina Crown, Bluecoats, and Santa Clara Vanguard.

Instrumentation

The brass line uses trumpets, mellophones, baritones, euphoniums, and tubas (contras); the battery includes snare drums, tenor drums (quints), and bass drums; the front ensemble and color guard complete the production.
