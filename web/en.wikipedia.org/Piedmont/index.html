Piedmont

Piedmont (Italian: Piemonte) is one of the twenty regions of Italy, located in the country's northwest, with an area of 25,402 km² and a population of about 4.25 million. Its capital and largest city is Turin. Surrounded on three sides by the Alps — including Monte Viso, at whose foot the Po River rises — the region's name means "foot of the mountain" in Italian. It borders France, Switzerland, and the Italian regions of Lombardy, Liguria, Emilia-Romagna, and Valle d'Aosta.

History

Inhabited in antiquity by Celtic-Ligurian peoples such as the Taurini, Piedmont was later Romanised. In the Middle Ages it came under the House of Savoy, whose Kingdom of Sardinia — governed from Turin — led the unification of Italy in 1861; the first Italian capital was Turin, and the Savoy kings became kings of Italy. Piedmontese is spoken by some two million people in addition to Italian.

Economy

Piedmont is one of Italy's most industrialised and wealthiest regions, home to Fiat (Stellantis) and a strong automotive supply chain, as well as food industries such as Ferrero (Nutella) and Lavazza coffee. The Langhe, Roero, and Monferrato wine regions — a UNESCO World Heritage Site — produce celebrated wines including Barolo and Barbaresco, and Alba is famed for white truffles. Turin hosted the 2006 Winter Olympics.
