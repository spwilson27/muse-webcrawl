Chicago is the most populous city in the U.S. state of Illinois and in the Midwestern United States. With a population of about 2.6 million, it is the third-most populous city in the United States, after New York City and Los Angeles, and its metropolitan area of roughly 9.5 million people is the third-largest in the country. Located on the shore of Lake Michigan, Chicago is an international hub for finance, culture, commerce, industry, education, technology, telecommunications, and transportation.

History

The name Chicago derives from a French rendering of the Miami-Illinois word shikaakwa, referring to wild garlic or onion that grew along the Chicago River. The area was home to a succession of indigenous peoples before the first permanent non-indigenous settler, Jean Baptiste Point du Sable, a trader of African and French descent, established a settlement in the 1780s. Chicago was incorporated as a town in 1833 and as a city in 1837. Its position between the Great Lakes and the Mississippi River watershed, and later its role as a railroad hub, made it grow explosively. The Great Chicago Fire of 1871 destroyed much of the central city, but Chicago rebuilt rapidly, pioneering the skyscraper: the steel-frame Home Insurance Building (1885) is often called the first. The city hosted the World's Columbian Exposition in 1893 and the Century of Progress in 1933–34.

Geography and climate

Chicago sits on a continental divide at the southern shore of Lake Michigan, which moderates its humid continental climate. Winters are cold and snowy; summers are warm and humid. The "Windy City" nickname is most often attributed to its blustery lake-effect weather and, in popular lore, to its boastful 19th-century politicians.

Economy

The Chicago metropolitan economy is among the world's largest and most diversified, with major strengths in financial services (home to the CME Group and the Chicago Board of Trade), manufacturing, printing and publishing, food processing, and transportation and distribution. O'Hare International Airport has long ranked among the world's busiest airports.

Culture

Chicago is renowned for its architecture — the birthplace of the Chicago school of architecture and of Frank Lloyd Wright's Prairie style nearby — its blues, jazz and house music traditions, improvisational comedy (The Second City), deep-dish pizza and the Chicago-style hot dog, and major institutions including the Art Institute of Chicago, the Field Museum, the Museum of Science and Industry, and the Chicago Symphony Orchestra. Its sports teams include the Bears (NFL), Bulls (NBA), Cubs and White Sox (MLB), Blackhawks (NHL), and Fire (MLS).
