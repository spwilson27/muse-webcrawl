798 Art Zone

798 Art Zone (Chinese: 798艺术区), or Dashanzi Art District, is a district in the Chaoyang District of Beijing known for its concentration of contemporary art galleries, studios, and design shops. It occupies a decommissioned military factory complex built in the 1950s with East German assistance, whose Bauhaus-influenced industrial architecture gives the area its distinctive character.

History

The complex began as the 718 Joint Factory, a state-owned electronics plant built under the slogan "718" and designed by German architects in the Bauhaus style, with vaulted ceilings and north-facing skylights. As production declined in the 1990s, artists began renting the cheap, spacious halls; the Central Academy of Fine Arts established a temporary campus nearby and the area rapidly grew into Beijing's leading contemporary art hub.

Development

Anchored by institutions such as the Ullens Center for Contemporary Art (opened 2007), 798 hosts hundreds of galleries as well as cafés, bookshops, and fashion boutiques. It is one of Beijing's most visited cultural sites and hosts the annual 798 Art Festival.

Criticism

Rising rents and commercialization have pushed many artist studios to cheaper districts, prompting debate about whether 798 remains a working art community or has become primarily a tourist and retail destination.
