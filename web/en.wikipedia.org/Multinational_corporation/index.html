Multinational corporation


Abbreviation: MNC (also MNE, TNC)
Definition: Company operating in multiple countries with a home-country headquarters

A multinational corporation (MNC), also called a multinational enterprise or transnational corporation, is an organization that owns or controls production or services in at least one country other than its home country. The largest MNCs have revenues comparable to the GDP of mid-sized nations and play a central role in globalization and foreign direct investment.

History

Chartered companies such as the Dutch East India Company (founded 1602) are often considered precursors. Modern multinationals expanded rapidly after the Second World War, first American firms, then European and Japanese ones, and increasingly firms headquartered in emerging economies such as China, India, and Brazil.

Structure and strategy

MNCs may be organized with a strong central headquarters, decentralized national subsidiaries, or integrated global networks. Market-seeking, resource-seeking, and efficiency-seeking motives explain why firms establish foreign operations; internalization theory explains why they own subsidiaries rather than licensing local firms.

Criticism and regulation

Debates surround MNCs' tax planning and transfer pricing, labour and environmental standards, influence over host governments, and cultural effects. International responses include OECD guidelines, UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights, and global minimum corporate tax agreements.
