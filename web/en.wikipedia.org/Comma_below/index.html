Comma below

The comma below is a diacritical mark placed beneath a letter, used in several languages to modify its pronunciation. It appears, for example, in the Romanian letters Ș/ș and Ț/ț, and in Latvian letters such as Ģ/ģ, Ķ/ķ, Ļ/ļ, Ņ/ņ, and Ŗ/ŗ, where it indicates palatalisation.

Romanian usage

Romanian uses the comma below on S and T to represent the sounds /ʃ/ and /ts/ respectively. For many years these characters were commonly printed with a cedilla instead because of limitations in typefaces and encodings, but the comma below is the correct form, and modern Unicode distinguishes the two marks explicitly. The confusion persisted in computing until dedicated code points and improved fonts became widespread.

Encoding

Unicode provides a combining comma below (U+0326) that can be added to any base letter, as well as precomposed characters for the Romanian and Latvian letters. In Latvian typography the comma below on the letter ģ is conventionally placed above the lowercase letter to avoid colliding with its descender.
