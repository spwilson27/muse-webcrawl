Daniel J. Bernstein, cryptologist and software author

Daniel Julius Bernstein (born October 29, 1971), known as djb, is an American mathematician, cryptologist and computer scientist. He is a professor at the University of Illinois at Chicago and at Ruhr University Bochum, and is known for the software packages qmail, djbdns and daemontools, for the Salsa20 and ChaCha stream ciphers, for Curve25519, and for the Bernstein v. United States case that challenged US export controls on cryptography.

Early life and education

Bernstein graduated from New York University in 1991 and received his PhD in mathematics from the University of California, Berkeley in 1995, under Hendrik Lenstra. As a student he became a prominent voice in the cypherpunks movement and in debates over cryptography regulation.

Bernstein v. United States

In 1995 Bernstein sued the US government, arguing that export regulations treating cryptographic source code as a munition violated the First Amendment. In 1999 the Ninth Circuit agreed that the regulations were an unconstitutional prior restraint on speech. The case was a landmark in establishing that source code is protected speech and contributed to the liberalisation of US cryptography exports.

Software and cryptography

Bernstein's software is noted for its security-focused design and for his standing offers of cash rewards for anyone finding security holes in qmail and djbdns. In cryptography he designed the Snuffle/Salsa20 and ChaCha ciphers, the Poly1305 authenticator, the Curve25519 elliptic-curve Diffie–Hellman function and Ed25519 signatures — all now ubiquitous in protocols such as TLS, SSH and Signal. He co-authored the post-quantum cryptosystem Classic McEliece and has been a leading researcher on high-speed cryptography with Tanja Lange in the Coding and Cryptography group at Eindhoven and Bochum.
