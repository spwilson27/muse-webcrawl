Lucien Febvre

Born: 22 July 1878, Nancy, France
Died: 25 September 1956 (aged 78)
Occupation: Historian
Known for: Co-founding the Annales school

Lucien Febvre (22 July 1878 – 25 September 1956) was a French historian, co-founder with Marc Bloch of the Annales school of history.

Career

Educated at the École Normale Supérieure, Febvre taught at Dijon and Strasbourg, where he met Bloch. In 1929 they founded the journal Annales d'histoire économique et sociale, advocating interdisciplinary, problem-oriented history drawing on geography, economics, psychology and sociology, and studying long-term structures rather than only political events.

Works

Major works include Philippe II et la Franche-Comté (1911), A Geographical Introduction to History (1922), The Problem of Unbelief in the Sixteenth Century (1942) – a study of Rabelais arguing that atheism was conceptually unavailable in its period – and The Mediterranean and the Mediterranean World tradition he fostered through Fernand Braudel.

Legacy

Febvre helped found the VIe Section of the École Pratique des Hautes Études, later EHESS, and reshaped French historiography toward histoire des mentalités.
