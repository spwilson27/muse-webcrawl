Gordium (Greek: Γόρδιον) was the capital city of ancient Phrygia. It was located at the site of modern Yassıhöyük, about 70–80 km southwest of Ankara, in the immediate vicinity of Polatlı in Turkey, near the confluence of the Sakarya and Porsuk rivers.

History

Gordium reached its greatest prominence under the Phrygian kings Gordias and Midas in the 8th century BC. It was destroyed by the Cimmerians around 696 BC, later rebuilt, and subsequently came under Lydian, Persian, Macedonian, Galatian, and Roman rule. The city was abandoned after an earthquake in late antiquity or the early medieval period.

The Gordian Knot

Gordium is famous as the site of Alexander the Great's legendary encounter with the Gordian Knot in 333 BC. According to tradition, an oracle declared that whoever untied the intricate knot fastening the yoke of Gordias's ox-cart would rule all of Asia; Alexander is said to have cut it with his sword, giving rise to the expression "to cut the Gordian knot" for solving a problem by bold action.

Excavations

The so-called Midas Mound, a huge royal tumulus excavated in 1957, contained a wooden burial chamber with rich grave goods, believed to date to about 740 BC.
