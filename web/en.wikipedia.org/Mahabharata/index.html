Mahabharata

The Mahabharata is one of the two major Sanskrit epics of ancient India, traditionally attributed to the sage Vyasa. At roughly 100,000 shlokas it is among the longest epic poems ever written.

Story

It tells of the rivalry between the Pandava and Kaurava cousins for the throne of Hastinapura, culminating in the eighteen-day Kurukshetra War. Embedded within it is the Bhagavad Gita, Krishna's dialogue with Arjuna on duty and dharma; Krishna is an avatar of Vishnu.

Structure

The text is divided into eighteen parvas (books), from the Adi Parva to the Svargarohana Parva, plus the Harivamsha appendix. It includes famous sub-stories such as Nala and Damayanti, Savitri, and Shakuntala.

Significance

Composed over centuries (roughly 400 BCE–400 CE in its surviving form), the Mahabharata is a central source on Hindu dharma, law and philosophy, retold across South and Southeast Asia alongside the Ramayana.
