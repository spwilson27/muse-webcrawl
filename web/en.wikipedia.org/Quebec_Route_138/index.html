Quebec Route 138

Lengthabout 1,400 km (including disconnected segments)
DirectionWest–east along the St. Lawrence River
FromElgin (New York border area)
ToNatashquan / Blanc-Sablon segments

Quebec Route 138 is a major highway in the Canadian province of Quebec that follows the north shore of the St. Lawrence River from the Montreal area eastward through Quebec City, the Charlevoix region and the Côte-Nord to Natashquan, with additional isolated segments farther east on the Lower North Shore.

Route

Beginning near the United States border southwest of Montreal, Route 138 runs through Montreal (where it follows streets including Sherbrooke Street), Trois-Rivières and Quebec City, where it is a scenic road through Charlevoix. East of Tadoussac it crosses the Saguenay River by ferry and continues through Baie-Comeau and Sept-Îles to Natashquan; beyond Kegashka the road continues in separated segments serving isolated communities toward Blanc-Sablon.

History

Parts of the route follow the Chemin du Roy, the historic road built in the 1730s between Montreal and Quebec City under French rule. Long a priority for the Côte-Nord, extensions of the highway eastward have been built progressively.
