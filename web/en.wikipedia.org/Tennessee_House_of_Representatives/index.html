Tennessee House of Representatives

The Tennessee House of Representatives is the lower house of the Tennessee General Assembly, the state legislature of the U.S. state of Tennessee. It consists of 99 members elected from single-member districts of roughly equal population for two-year terms. The House meets in the Tennessee State Capitol in Nashville.

Composition and elections

Representatives must be at least 21 years old, citizens of the United States, residents of Tennessee for at least three years, and residents of their county for at least one year before election. Elections are held every two years in November of even-numbered years. There are no term limits. Since 2011 the Republican Party has held a majority, and in recent assemblies a supermajority, in the chamber.

Leadership

The Speaker of the House is the presiding officer, elected by the full membership at the opening of each General Assembly. The Speaker appoints committee chairs and members and controls the flow of legislation. Other officers include the Speaker Pro Tempore, the majority and minority leaders, and the caucus chairs of each party. Cameron Sexton, a Republican from Crossville, has served as Speaker since August 2019, succeeding Glen Casada.

Powers and procedure

Under the Tennessee Constitution, revenue bills must originate in the House. The House shares ordinary legislative power with the Tennessee Senate; a bill must pass both chambers in identical form and be signed by the governor (or passed over a veto by a majority of both houses) to become law. The House alone may bring articles of impeachment, which are tried by the Senate. Sessions of the General Assembly begin in January; the House typically adjourns by spring.
