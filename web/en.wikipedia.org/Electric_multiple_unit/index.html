Electric multiple unit
TypeSelf-propelled passenger train
Power supplyOverhead line or third rail
First use1890s

An electric multiple unit (EMU) is a multiple-unit train consisting of self-propelled carriages using electricity as the motive power. Unlike locomotive-hauled trains, an EMU requires no separate locomotive, since electric traction motors are incorporated within one or more of the carriages.

Characteristics

EMUs are popular on commuter and suburban rail networks because of their fast acceleration and pollution-free operation at the point of use. Cars within a set are often semi-permanently coupled and driven from cabs at each end, allowing quick turnarounds. Power is drawn from an overhead catenary via a pantograph or from a third rail via collector shoes.

History

Multiple-unit control was pioneered in the 1890s, notably by Frank Sprague, whose system allowed several powered cars to be controlled from a single driving position. Early applications included elevated railways in Chicago and the Liverpool Overhead Railway.

Types

EMUs range from metro trains and airport expresses to long-distance high-speed trainsets such as the Japanese Shinkansen, French TGV derivatives, and German ICE trains, in which power is distributed along the train.
