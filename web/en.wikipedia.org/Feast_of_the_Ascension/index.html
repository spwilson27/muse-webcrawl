Feast of the Ascension

The Feast of the Ascension of Jesus Christ, also called Ascension Day, Ascension Thursday, or sometimes Holy Thursday, commemorates the Christian belief in the bodily Ascension of Jesus into Heaven. It is one of the ecumenical (shared among many denominations) feasts of Christian churches, ranking with the feasts of the Passion and Pentecost.

Observed byChristians worldwide
TypeChristian feast
Date39 days after Easter Sunday

History

Following the account in the Gospel of Luke and the Acts of the Apostles, the Ascension is believed to have occurred on the fortieth day after the Resurrection, on the Mount of Olives near Jerusalem. The feast itself is of great antiquity: Eusebius mentions its celebration in the fourth century, and Augustine of Hippo regarded it as of apostolic origin.

Observance

Ascension Day is traditionally celebrated on a Thursday, the fortieth day of Easter (counting both Easter Sunday and Ascension Thursday), though some denominations move it to the following Sunday. It is a public holiday in many European countries. Customs include processions, the extinguishing of the Paschal candle, and in some places the blessing of beans and grapes.
