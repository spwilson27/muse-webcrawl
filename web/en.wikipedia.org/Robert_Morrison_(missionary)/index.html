Robert Morrison (missionary)

Born: 5 January 1782, Bullers Green, England

Died: 1 August 1834, Guangzhou, China

Robert Morrison was a Scottish Protestant missionary who was the first such missionary to China and a pioneering sinologist. Sent by the London Missionary Society, he arrived in Guangzhou (Canton) in 1807.

Work in China

Employed as a translator by the East India Company to conceal his mission work, Morrison learned Chinese, translated the entire Bible into Chinese (published 1823, with William Milne), compiled a monumental Chinese dictionary (1815–1823), and co-founded the Anglo-Chinese College in Malacca. He baptised his first Chinese convert, Cai Gao, in 1814.

Legacy

Morrison's translations and dictionary laid the foundations for Protestant missions and Western Chinese studies, influencing later figures such as James Hudson Taylor. He is buried in Macau.
