Clipfish

Clipfish (Norwegian: klippfisk, from klippe, cliff/rock, and fisk, fish) is fish — traditionally cod, but also haddock, pollock, ling and tusk — that has been salted and dried. After salting, the fish was historically dried on flat rocks and cliffs along the Norwegian coast; in modern production it is dried in indoor facilities with controlled temperature and humidity.

History and trade

Clipfish production became a major Norwegian export industry from the 17th and 18th centuries, especially around Kristiansund, Molde and Ålesund, supplying Catholic markets in Spain, Portugal, Italy and Latin America, where the product is known as bacalao or bacalhau. It differs from stockfish, which is dried without salt, and from the lightly salted, moister bacalao traditions of other countries.

Before eating, clipfish must be soaked in water for one or more days, with repeated changes of water, to remove salt and rehydrate the flesh.
