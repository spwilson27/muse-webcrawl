Corporate finance

Corporate finance is the area of finance that deals with the sources of funding, the capital structure of corporations, the actions that managers take to increase the value of the firm to the shareholders, and the tools and analysis used to allocate financial resources. Its primary goal is often stated as maximising shareholder value while managing the firm's financial risks.

Capital investment decisions

The capital budgeting process evaluates long-term investments such as new plants, machinery and acquisitions, using techniques including net present value (NPV), internal rate of return (IRR) and payback period. Projects are accepted when their expected return exceeds the firm's cost of capital.

Capital structure

Firms choose a mix of debt and equity financing. The Modigliani–Miller theorem provides a benchmark showing that, under idealised conditions, the value of a firm is independent of its capital structure; in practice, taxes, bankruptcy costs and agency costs shape the choice. Dividend policy — how much profit to return to shareholders versus reinvest — is closely related.

Working capital management

Short-term financial management focuses on cash, inventory, receivables and payables, ensuring the firm can meet its obligations while avoiding excessive idle balances. The chief financial officer (CFO) typically oversees the whole corporate finance function.
