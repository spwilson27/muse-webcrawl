Ian Foster

Ian Tremere Foster (born 1959) is a New Zealand-American computer scientist best known as a pioneer of grid computing, a field he helped define with Carl Kesselman and Steve Tuecke. He is the Arthur Holly Compton Distinguished Service Professor of Computer Science at the University of Chicago, director of the Data Science and Learning Division at Argonne National Laboratory, and a co-founder of the Globus project and Globus Alliance, which produced widely used software for distributed scientific computing.

Career

Foster earned a BSc from the University of Canterbury and a PhD in computer science from Imperial College London in 1988, working on parallel logic programming; his Strand language and the book Strand: New Concepts in Parallel Programming (with Stephen Taylor) won the British Computer Society's Lovelace Medal (shared, 1995). At Argonne from 1989, he turned to the problem of coupling geographically distributed resources — supercomputers, instruments, databases — into unified "virtual organizations".

Grid computing and Globus

With Kesselman and Tuecke, Foster articulated grid computing in the 1998 book The Grid and the influential papers "The Anatomy of the Grid" (2001) and "The Physiology of the Grid" (2002). The Globus Toolkit became the standard middleware for scientific grids worldwide, underpinning projects in physics (including LHC data analysis), earth science and astronomy. In 2010 the team launched Globus as a cloud-based research data management service operated by the University of Chicago. His approach to federated infrastructure contrasts with the fully decentralised model of peers such as Ian Clarke of Freenet fame.

Later work and honours

Foster's more recent work covers cloud computing for science, machine learning systems, and research automation; he co-authored Cloud Computing for Science and Engineering (2017). He is a fellow of the ACM, IEEE, AAAS and the British Computer Society, and received the 2019 IEEE Computer Society Harry H. Goode Memorial Award and the ACM-IEEE CS Ken Kennedy Award (2022).
