Piper Alpha

Piper Alpha was a North Sea oil production platform operated by Occidental Petroleum (Caledonia) Limited, located about 190 km northeast of Aberdeen, Scotland. On 6 July 1988 it was destroyed by a series of explosions and fires, killing 167 men — the world's deadliest offshore oil disaster.

The disaster

A condensate leak from a pump whose pressure safety valve had been removed for maintenance — and whose status was not communicated across shifts — ignited on the evening of 6 July. Successive explosions ruptured gas risers from connected platforms, and the platform was largely destroyed within hours. Only 61 of the 228 men aboard survived.

Consequences

The public inquiry led by Lord Cullen produced 106 recommendations that transformed North Sea safety regulation, including the safety case regime and the transfer of regulatory oversight away from the energy department. A memorial stands in Aberdeen's Hazlehead Park.
