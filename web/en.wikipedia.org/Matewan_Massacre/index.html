Matewan Massacre

The Matewan Massacre, also called the Battle of Matewan, was a gunfight in Matewan, West Virginia, on 19 May 1920, between local coal miners and detectives of the Baldwin-Felts Agency hired by a coal company. Ten men were killed — seven detectives, including brothers Albert and Lee Felts, the mayor of Matewan, Cabell Testerman, and two miners. The shootout became a landmark episode of the West Virginia coal wars.

Background

Miners in the Tug Fork coalfield who joined the United Mine Workers were evicted from company-owned houses. On 19 May, Baldwin-Felts agents arrived by train, evicted several families at Stone Mountain Coal Corporation property, and were confronted in town by Police Chief Sid Hatfield and Mayor Testerman over their authority to carry out the evictions.

The shootout

As the detectives headed back to the train, shooting erupted near the station; who fired first was disputed at the subsequent trials. Hatfield became a hero to miners and was acquitted of murder charges, but in August 1921 he and Ed Chambers were shot dead by Baldwin-Felts agents on the steps of the McDowell County courthouse, an act that helped provoke the miners' march culminating in the Battle of Blair Mountain.

Legacy

The events inspired John Sayles's 1987 film Matewan. Matewan's history is commemorated in the town, which is a National Historic Landmark district.
