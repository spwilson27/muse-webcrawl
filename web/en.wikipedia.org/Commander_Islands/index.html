Commander Islands

The Commander Islands or Komandorski Islands (Russian: Командорские острова, Komandorskiye ostrova) are a group of treeless, sparsely populated Russian islands in the Bering Sea located about 175 kilometres (109 mi) east of the Kamchatka Peninsula in the Russian Far East. The islands consist of Bering Island, Medny Island, and fifteen smaller ones, the largest of which are Toporkov Island and Ariy Kamen Island.

History

The islands are named after Commander Vitus Bering, the Danish-born Russian navigator whose ship, the St. Peter, was wrecked on the largest island in 1741; Bering died there along with many of his crew. Georg Wilhelm Steller, the expedition's naturalist, survived and wrote an account of the islands' wildlife, including the now-extinct Steller's sea cow.

Geography and wildlife

The archipelago is administered as part of Kamchatka Krai. Its only permanently inhabited settlement is Nikolskoye on Bering Island, with a population of under a thousand, largely Aleuts and Russians. The surrounding waters and islands form the Komandorsky Zapovednik, a strict nature reserve protecting fur seals, sea otters, seabird colonies, and other wildlife.
