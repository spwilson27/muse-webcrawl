John Frost (Chartist)

Born25 May 1784, Newport, Wales
Died27 July 1877, Stapleton, Bristol
Known forLeader of the Newport Rising (1839)

John Frost (25 May 1784 – 27 July 1877) was a prominent leader of the British Chartist movement in Wales and leader of the Newport Rising of 1839, the largest armed rebellion in mainland Britain of the 19th century.

Early life and politics

Frost ran a tailor's and draper's business in Newport, Monmouthshire. Radicalised by a legal dispute and imprisonment in the 1820s, he became a campaigner for parliamentary reform, was elected to Newport's town council, served as mayor of Newport in 1836, and became a magistrate — from which he was removed for his radical activities. He was a Welsh delegate to the Chartist Convention in London in 1839, advocating the People's Charter's demands of universal male suffrage, secret ballots and other reforms.

Newport Rising

On the night of 3–4 November 1839, Frost led thousands of miners and ironworkers from the South Wales Valleys in a march on Newport, intending to seize the town and free imprisoned Chartists including Henry Vincent. At the Westgate Hotel, where Vincent's followers were believed held, soldiers of the 45th Foot fired on the crowd, killing at least 22 Chartists and wounding many more; the rising collapsed. Frost and fellow leaders Zephaniah Williams and William Jones were arrested, tried for high treason at Monmouth, and sentenced to death, commuted to transportation for life to Van Diemen's Land (Tasmania).

Later life

Frost spent years as a convict in Tasmania, then lived in the United States after a conditional pardon in 1854. A full pardon in 1856 allowed his return to Britain, where he was received as a hero by reformers in Newport. He spent his final years at Stapleton near Bristol and died in 1877, aged 93. John Frost Square in Newport and schools and memorials in Wales commemorate him; campaigners have periodically sought a posthumous pardon.
