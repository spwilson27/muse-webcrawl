Unit record equipment

Unit record equipment was the family of electromechanical machines that processed data stored on punched cards before the era of stored-program electronic computers. Each punched card was a "unit record" holding one transaction or data item, and a card deck was processed by passing it through a sequence of specialised machines.

Machines

Typical equipment included keypunches (such as the IBM 026/029) for recording data onto cards, sorters that ordered decks on one column at a time, collators that merged and matched decks, reproducers that copied decks, interpreters that printed card contents on the card itself, and accounting machines or tabulators — such as the IBM 402 and the highly versatile IBM 407 — that read cards, accumulated totals, and printed reports. Machines were programmed by removable wired control panels.

History

Unit record equipment descended from Herman Hollerith's tabulating machines used in the 1890 US census and dominated business data processing from the early 20th century until the 1960s, when electronic computers displaced them; IBM was by far the largest manufacturer.
