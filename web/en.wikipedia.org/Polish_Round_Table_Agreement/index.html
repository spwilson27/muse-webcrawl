Polish Round Table Agreement

Date5 April 1989
LocationWarsaw, Poland
PartiesCommunist government; Solidarity opposition
OutcomeSemi-free elections of 4 June 1989

The Polish Round Table Agreement was an accord signed on 5 April 1989 between the communist government of the Polish People's Republic and leaders of the Solidarity opposition after negotiations held in Warsaw from 6 February to 5 April 1989. It paved the way for partially free elections and the peaceful end of communist rule in Poland.

Background

A new wave of strikes in 1988, combined with a deepening economic crisis and the reform climate created by Mikhail Gorbachev in the Soviet Union, persuaded General Wojciech Jaruzelski's government to negotiate with the outlawed Solidarity movement, led by Lech Wałęsa. The Catholic Church acted as observer and mediator.

Terms

The agreement re-legalized Solidarity, created the office of President, restored the Senate, and provided for elections on 4 June 1989 in which 35 percent of Sejm seats would be freely contested while 65 percent were reserved for the ruling coalition; Senate elections were fully free.

Consequences

Solidarity won all contested Sejm seats and 99 of 100 Senate seats. The result stunned the regime and inspired democratic movements across Eastern Europe; by August 1989 Tadeusz Mazowiecki had formed the first non-communist government in the Soviet bloc, accelerating the revolutions of 1989 and the fall of the Berlin Wall.
