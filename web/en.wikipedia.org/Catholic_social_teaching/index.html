Catholic social teaching

Catholic social teaching is the body of doctrine developed by the Catholic Church on matters of society, economics and justice. Rooted in Scripture and the Church's tradition, it addresses human dignity, work, poverty, the role of the state and the moral ordering of economic life. Its modern form is usually dated from Pope Leo XIII's encyclical Rerum novarum (1891), on the condition of the working classes.

Principles

Key principles include the inherent dignity of the human person; the common good; subsidiarity — that matters should be handled by the smallest competent authority; solidarity with others, especially the poor (the "preferential option for the poor"); the universal destination of goods, qualifying private property with social obligations; just wages and the rights of workers to organise; and, increasingly since the late twentieth century, care for creation (see Catholic Church and climate change).

Development

Successive documents built on Rerum novarum: Pius XI's Quadragesimo anno (1931), John XXIII's Mater et magistra (1961) and Pacem in terris (1963), the Second Vatican Council's Gaudium et spes (1965), Paul VI's Populorum progressio (1967), and John Paul II's Laborem exercens (1981), Sollicitudo rei socialis (1987) and Centesimus annus (1991). Benedict XVI's Caritas in veritate (2009) and Francis's Laudato si' (2015) and Fratelli tutti (2020) extended the tradition to globalisation, ecology and fraternity.
