Severe weather terminology (United States)

The National Weather Service issues standardized watches and warnings

This article describes the severe weather terminology used by the National Weather Service (NWS) of the United States. The NWS and related agencies issue a system of watches, warnings, advisories and statements to alert the public to hazardous weather, each term having a precise definition tied to expected impacts and certainty.

Watches and warnings

A watch means that conditions are favourable for a hazard to develop over the next several hours and that the public should monitor forecasts and be prepared. A warning means that a hazardous event is occurring, imminent or highly likely, and poses a threat to life and property, requiring immediate protective action. An advisory covers less serious conditions that may cause inconvenience or, if caution is not exercised, could become dangerous.

Severe thunderstorms and tornadoes

A severe thunderstorm is defined in the United States as one producing hail at least one inch (2.5 cm) in diameter, wind gusts of at least 58 mph (93 km/h), or a tornado. Tornado warnings are issued when a tornado is indicated by radar or reported by spotters; a rare tornado emergency designation is used when a confirmed, destructive tornado threatens a populated area. The Storm Prediction Center issues convective outlooks using risk categories from marginal to high risk.

Other hazards

Standardized terms also exist for winter storms and blizzards (snowfall combined with winds over 35 mph and visibility under a quarter mile), flash floods, hurricanes and tropical storms, extreme heat and cold, dense fog and high winds. Since the 2010s the NWS has supplemented these products with impact-based wording and wireless emergency alerts for the most dangerous events.
