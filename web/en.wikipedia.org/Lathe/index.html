Lathe

A lathe is a machine tool that rotates a workpiece about an axis while a cutting tool is moved against it to shape the material, typically producing rotationally symmetric parts. It is one of the oldest and most fundamental machine tools.

Operation

The workpiece is held in a chuck, between centres, or on a faceplate, and spun by the headstock spindle. Operations include turning (reducing diameter), facing, boring, drilling, threading, knurling and parting off. Woodturning lathes use hand-held tools rested on a tool rest; metalworking lathes move the tool mechanically via leadscrews and carriages.

History

Lathes date to ancient Egypt and were central to the Industrial Revolution; Henry Maudslay's screw-cutting lathe (c. 1800) made accurate interchangeable threads practical. Modern CNC lathes are computer-controlled and ubiquitous in manufacturing.
