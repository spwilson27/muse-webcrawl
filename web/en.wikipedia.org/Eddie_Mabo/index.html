Edward Koiki Mabo (29 June 1936 – 21 January 1992) was an Indigenous Australian man from the Torres Strait Islands known for his role in campaigning for Indigenous land rights in Australia, in particular the landmark decision of the High Court of Australia that overturned the legal fiction of terra nullius.

Life

Mabo was born on Mer (Murray Island) in the Torres Strait and was adopted by his uncle and aunt under traditional custom, taking the surname Mabo. He worked in various jobs — cane cutter, railway worker, and gardener at James Cook University in Townsville — where conversations with historians Henry Reynolds and Noel Loos made him aware that the law treated his people's land as Crown land. In 1981 he and four other Murray Islanders brought a claim in the High Court.

Mabo v Queensland

After a decade of litigation, on 3 June 1992 — months after Mabo's death from cancer — the High Court decided Mabo v Queensland (No 2), recognising that native title existed and that the Meriam people were entitled to possession of their lands. The decision overturned the doctrine of terra nullius and led to the Native Title Act 1993. 3 June is commemorated as Mabo Day in the Torres Strait.
