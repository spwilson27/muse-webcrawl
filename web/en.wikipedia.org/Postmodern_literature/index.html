Postmodern literature

Postmodern literature is a form of literature characterised by reliance on narrative techniques such as fragmentation, paradox, unreliable narrators and self-conscious metafiction, and by scepticism toward grand narratives and the distinction between high and low culture. It emerged after the Second World War as both a continuation of and reaction against modernism.

Common themes and techniques

Postmodern works frequently employ intertextuality, pastiche, irony, paranoia and magic realism, and blur genres — for example mixing history with fiction in historiographic metafiction. Max Bense, Ihab Hassan and Brian McHale catalogued its features; Jean-François Lyotard described the postmodern condition as "incredulity toward metanarratives".

Notable authors

Precursors include Jorge Luis Borges, Samuel Beckett and Vladimir Nabokov. Central figures of the flourishing period from the 1960s include Thomas Pynchon (Gravity's Rainbow), Don DeLillo (White Noise), Italo Calvino (If on a winter's night a traveler), Kurt Vonnegut (Slaughterhouse-Five), Umberto Eco, Salman Rushdie, David Foster Wallace (Infinite Jest), Kathy Acker, William Gaddis and John Barth. Debates continue over whether postmodernism has ended, succeeded by movements variously named metamodernism, post-postmodernism or "New Sincerity".
