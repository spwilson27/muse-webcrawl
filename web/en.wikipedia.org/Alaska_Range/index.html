The Alaska Range is a relatively narrow, 600-kilometre-long (400 mi) mountain range in the southcentral region of the US state of Alaska, running in an arc from Lake Clark at its southwest end to the White River in Yukon, Canada, at its southeast end. It contains Denali, at 6,190 metres (20,310 ft) the highest mountain in North America.

Geography

The range acts as a climatic barrier between the coastal regions of southcentral Alaska and the interior. Major peaks include Denali, Mount Foraker (5,304 m), Mount Hunter (4,442 m), Mount Hayes and Mount Blackburn (the latter in the connected Wrangell Mountains). Large glaciers, including the Kahiltna, Ruth, Tokositna and Susitna glaciers, flow from its southern slopes.

Several important rivers originate in the range, among them the Susitna, Nenana, Tanana (via its tributaries) and Kuskokwim headwaters. The range is crossed by the Parks Highway through Broad Pass and by the Alaska Railroad, and the Denali fault, a major strike-slip fault, runs along its southern side — source of the magnitude 7.9 Denali fault earthquake of 2002.

Protected areas

Much of the central range lies within Denali National Park and Preserve, established in 1917 as Mount McKinley National Park, and parts of the western range fall within Lake Clark National Park and Preserve.

Mountaineering

The Alaska Range is a major destination for mountaineering. Denali's West Buttress route draws hundreds of climbers each season, while Mount Hunter and the Ruth Gorge offer some of the most demanding alpine climbing in the world. The range was the scene of notable first ascents by climbers such as Bradford Washburn, who pioneered aerial photography and mapping of the region.
