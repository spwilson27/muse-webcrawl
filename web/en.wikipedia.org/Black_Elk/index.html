Black Elk

Born1 December 1863, Little Powder River, Wyoming
Died19 August 1950, Pine Ridge, South Dakota
TribeOglala Lakota
Known forBlack Elk Speaks (1932)

Black Elk (Lakota: Heȟáka Sápa, December 1, 1863 – August 19, 1950) was a Lakota holy man (wičháša wakȟáŋ) and healer, a second cousin of Crazy Horse. As a young man he witnessed the Battle of the Little Bighorn and survived the Wounded Knee Massacre of 1890.

Great Vision

At the age of nine, Black Elk experienced a profound vision in which he was visited by the Six Grandfathers, the ancestral spirits of the Lakota. The vision, which he kept largely private for decades, became the spiritual centre of his life and its narration forms the core of the book Black Elk Speaks (1932), recorded by the poet John Neihardt.

Later life

He toured with Buffalo Bill's Wild West show, converted to Catholicism in 1904 and served as a catechist, while continuing traditional Lakota practices. In his old age he revealed further teachings to Joseph Epes Brown, published as The Sacred Pipe (1953). The Diocese of Rapid City opened his cause for canonization in 2017.
