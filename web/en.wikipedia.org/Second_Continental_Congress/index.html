Second Continental Congress

The Second Continental Congress was the assembly of delegates from the Thirteen Colonies that met beginning on 10 May 1775 in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, shortly after the outbreak of the American Revolutionary War. It succeeded the First Continental Congress and effectively acted as the de facto national government of the United States during the war.

Congress met principally at the Pennsylvania State House (Independence Hall).

Conduct of the war

Congress created the Continental Army on 14 June 1775 and appointed George Washington its commander-in-chief. It issued paper currency, sought foreign alliances and loans, organized a navy and marine corps, and coordinated strategy, all without any formal legal authority beyond the consent of the colonies. In July 1775 it sent the Olive Branch Petition to King George III as a final attempt at reconciliation, even as fighting continued.

Independence

On 2 July 1776 Congress voted to declare independence from Great Britain, and on 4 July it adopted the Declaration of Independence, principally drafted by Thomas Jefferson. In November 1777 Congress approved the Articles of Confederation, which were ratified in 1781; under them, the body became known as the Congress of the Confederation. Presidents of Congress during this period included John Hancock and Charles Thomson served as secretary throughout.
