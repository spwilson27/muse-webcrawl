Iron-deficiency anemia

Iron-deficiency anemia is anemia caused by a lack of iron. Anemia is defined as a decrease in red blood cells or in the amount of hemoglobin in the blood. When the onset is slow, symptoms are often vague, including fatigue, weakness, shortness of breath, headaches, and a reduced ability to exercise; when onset is rapid, there may be confusion and fainting.

Causes

Iron deficiency develops when the body's requirements or losses exceed intake. Common causes include blood loss (from menstruation, gastrointestinal bleeding, or frequent blood donation), inadequate dietary intake, malabsorption (as in coeliac disease or after gastric surgery), and increased demand during pregnancy and growth. It is the most common cause of anemia worldwide, affecting roughly a billion people.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is by blood tests showing low hemoglobin with small (microcytic), pale (hypochromic) red cells and low ferritin. Treatment addresses the underlying cause and replenishes iron, orally with ferrous salts or intravenously when oral iron is ineffective or not tolerated. Symptoms such as pagophagia (ice craving) and koilonychia (spoon-shaped nails) are characteristic clues.
