Tropic of Capricorn


The Tropic of Capricorn circle of latitude

The Tropic of Capricorn (or Southern Tropic) is the circle of latitude that contains the subsolar point at the December solstice. It is the southernmost latitude at which the Sun can appear directly overhead, currently at approximately 23.44° south of the equator.

Name and movement

The tropic is named for the constellation Capricornus, in which the Sun appeared at the December solstice when the name was given; because of the precession of the equinoxes, the Sun now appears in Sagittarius at that time. Like the Tropic of Cancer, its latitude shifts gradually with changes in Earth's axial tilt, currently drifting northward.

Geography

The line crosses Namibia, Botswana, South Africa, Mozambique, and Madagascar in Africa; Australia; and Chile, Argentina, Paraguay, and Brazil in South America. Much of its length passes over ocean—the highest proportion of any circle of latitude. Places on the tropic experience the Sun directly overhead once a year, at local noon on the December solstice.
