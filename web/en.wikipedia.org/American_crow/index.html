American crow

The American crow (Corvus brachyrhynchos) is a large passerine bird species of the family Corvidae. It is a common bird found throughout much of North America, from Canada to Mexico. It is entirely black, from feathers to legs and bill, and measures roughly 40–50 cm (16–20 in) in length.

Behavior

American crows are highly social and intelligent birds, often gathering in large communal roosts in winter that can number in the tens of thousands. They are omnivorous, feeding on invertebrates, carrion, seeds, eggs, nestlings, grains, and human refuse. Crows are recognized for problem-solving ability and tool use, and family groups cooperate in raising young.

Voice and identification

The usual call is a loud, harsh "caw caw." The American crow is very similar in appearance to the fish crow, from which it is most reliably distinguished by voice, and to the common raven, which is larger with a heavier bill and wedge-shaped tail.
