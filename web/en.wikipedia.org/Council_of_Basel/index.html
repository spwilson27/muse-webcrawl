Council of Basel

Council of Basel–Ferrara–Florence
Date1431–1449
LocationsBasel; Ferrara; Florence; Lausanne
Convoked byPope Martin V; confirmed by Eugene IV
IssuesHussites, conciliarism, union with the Eastern Church

The Council of Basel was an ecumenical council of the Catholic Church held from 1431 to 1449. Convoked by Pope Martin V in accordance with the decree of the Council of Constance requiring periodic councils, it became the high point of the conciliar movement, which asserted that a general council was superior to the pope.

Course of the council

The council negotiated with the Hussites of Bohemia, reaching the Compacts of Basel (1436), which allowed communion under both kinds in Bohemia. Pope Eugene IV attempted to dissolve and transfer the council; a majority refused and eventually deposed him, electing Amadeus VIII of Savoy as Antipope Felix V in 1439. Meanwhile the papal council at Ferrara and then Florence achieved a short-lived union with the Byzantine Church in 1439. The rump council at Basel lost support and dissolved in 1449 after moving to Lausanne; Felix V abdicated, ending the schism.

Significance

The council's failure discredited radical conciliarism and strengthened the papal monarchy, though its reforming decrees influenced later church practice.
