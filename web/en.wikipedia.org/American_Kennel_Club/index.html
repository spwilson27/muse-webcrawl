The American Kennel Club (AKC) is a registry of purebred dog pedigrees in the United States. In addition to maintaining its pedigree registry, this kennel club also promotes and sanctions events for purebred dogs, including the Westminster Kennel Club Dog Show, an annual event which predates the official formation of the AKC.

The AKC is not affiliated with the Fédération Cynologique Internationale, the international federation of kennel clubs. It recognizes nearly 200 dog breeds, organized into groups including Sporting, Hound, Working, Terrier, Toy, Non-Sporting, and Herding, plus a Miscellaneous class.

History

In the early 1880s, a group of sportsmen formed what became the American Kennel Club in 1884, modeled in part on the Kennel Club of England. The AKC maintains the largest registry of purebred dogs in the world and oversees conformation shows, obedience trials, agility, rally, tracking, and performance events across the United States.

Registry and standards

The AKC publishes official breed standards describing the ideal characteristics of each recognized breed. Registration records a dog's pedigree rather than certifying health or breeding quality, a distinction that has periodically drawn criticism from animal-welfare advocates.
