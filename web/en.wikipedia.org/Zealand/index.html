Zealand (Danish: Sjælland), at 7,031 km², is the largest and most populous island in Denmark proper (thus excluding Greenland and Disko Island, which are larger in size). Zealand had a population of 2,382,184 on 1 January 2024. It is the 13th-largest island in Europe by area and the 4th most populous. It is connected to Sprogø and Funen by the Great Belt Fixed Link and to Amager by several bridges in Copenhagen. Indirectly, through the island of Amager and the Øresund Bridge, it is also connected to Scania in Sweden.

Zealand's capital and largest city is Copenhagen, which occupies the eastern coast of the island and parts of Amager. Other major towns include Roskilde, Helsingør (Elsinore), Hillerød, Næstved, Slagelse and Køge. The island is low-lying, with fertile soils formed from glacial deposits, and its coastline is indented by fjords such as Roskilde Fjord and Isefjord in the north.

History

The tribal Danes came from Zealand and Scania and spoke an early form of North Germanic. Historians believe that before their arrival, most of Jutland and the nearest islands were settled by tribal Jutes. Zealand was at the centre of the early Danish kingdom: the royal borough of Roskilde, founded in the Viking Age, served as an early capital and its cathedral became the burial place of Danish monarchs. Copenhagen, founded as a fishing village and fortified harbour in the 12th century under Bishop Absalon, grew to become the national capital.

Geography and transport

Zealand is bounded by the Kattegat to the north, the Øresund to the east, and the Great Belt to the west. Since 2000 the Øresund Bridge has linked Copenhagen with Malmö in Sweden, and the Great Belt Fixed Link (opened 1997–98) connects Zealand via Sprogø to Funen. A fixed link across the Fehmarn Belt to Germany is under construction.
