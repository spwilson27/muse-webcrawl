Eruption column

An eruption column, or eruption plume, is a cloud of super-heated ash and tephra suspended in gases emitted during an explosive volcanic eruption. The materials form a vertical column or plume that can rise tens of kilometres into the atmosphere.

Formation

Eruption columns form when fragmented magma and entrained air are heated and rise buoyantly. Above a certain height the column may spread laterally into an umbrella cloud. If the column becomes too dense it can collapse, generating pyroclastic flows.

Hazards

Columns produce ashfall over wide areas, disrupt aviation, and can inject aerosols into the stratosphere, affecting climate, as in the 1991 eruption of Mount Pinatubo.
