Leaf vegetable

Leaf vegetables, also called leafy greens, pot herbs, greens, or simply leafy vegetables, are plant leaves eaten as a vegetable, sometimes accompanied by tender petioles and shoots. Leaf vegetables eaten raw in a salad are sometimes distinguished as salad greens. Nearly one thousand species of plants with edible leaves are known, most of them from short-lived herbaceous plants such as lettuce and spinach, though leaves of woody plants — shrubs, trees and vines — are also eaten.

Nutrition

Leaf vegetables are typically low in calories and fat, and high in dietary fibre, folate, vitamin C, vitamin K, and minerals such as iron and calcium. Dark green leafy vegetables are among the most nutrient-dense foods commonly available. Some, such as spinach, contain oxalic acid, which can reduce mineral absorption and is relevant to people prone to kidney stones.

Common leaf vegetables

  • Spinach, kale, chard, collard greens, mustard greens
  • Lettuce, cabbage, bok choy, arugula, watercress
  • Beet greens, turnip greens, dandelion greens, sorrel
  • Regional greens such as callaloo, ewedu, and moringa leaves

Preparation

Leaf vegetables are eaten raw in salads, steamed, boiled, sautéed, stir-fried, or incorporated into soups, stews, curries and pies. Tender leaves require only brief cooking, while tougher cooking greens benefit from longer simmering, often with fat and aromatics.
