Street art

Street art is visual art created in public locations for public visibility, usually executed outside the context of traditional art venues and often unsanctioned. The term gained popularity during the graffiti art boom of the early 1980s. Forms include graffiti, stencil art, wheatpasted posters, sticker art, street installations, murals, and yarn bombing.

History

Modern street art emerged from the graffiti culture of Philadelphia and New York in the 1960s–70s, developing distinctive lettering styles on subway trains. Artists such as Jean-Michel Basquiat and Keith Haring moved between streets and galleries. From the 1990s, stencil and poster artists like Shepard Fairey and the anonymous Banksy brought street art global attention, and commissioned murals became a feature of urban regeneration.

Techniques and debate

Common techniques are spray paint, stencils, stickers, wheatpaste, mosaic tiling, and projection. Street art raises ongoing debates about vandalism versus public art, property rights, commercialization, and gentrification; works by famous artists have been cut from walls and sold at auction, sometimes against the artists' wishes.
