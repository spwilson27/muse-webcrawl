Arthur Guinness

Born: c. 1725, Celbridge, County Kildare, Ireland

Died: 23 January 1803, Dublin, Ireland

Occupation: Brewer, entrepreneur

Known for: Founding the Guinness brewery

Arthur Guinness (c. 1725 – 23 January 1803) was an Irish brewer, entrepreneur and philanthropist, best known as the founder of the Guinness brewery in Dublin. In 1759 he signed the famous 9,000-year lease on the St. James's Gate Brewery, where Guinness stout has been brewed ever since.

Early life and brewing

Guinness was born in Celbridge, County Kildare, where his father was land steward to the Archbishop of Cashel, Arthur Price. Legend holds that a bequest of £100 from the archbishop in 1752 provided the capital for Guinness's first brewing ventures, initially in Leixlip and then in Dublin.

St. James's Gate

On 31 December 1759 Guinness signed a lease for the disused brewery at St. James's Gate in Dublin for £45 per year for 9,000 years. He initially brewed ales but by the 1770s had shifted production to porter, and in 1799 the brewery resolved to brew only porter and stout porter — the dark beer that became synonymous with the Guinness name.

Family and philanthropy

Guinness married Olivia Whitmore in 1761; of their 21 children, 10 reached adulthood, and several sons joined and expanded the business. A man of civic standing, he supported Irish independence causes, opposed anti-Catholic discrimination, and contributed to charitable works in Dublin, beginning a tradition of Guinness family philanthropy.
