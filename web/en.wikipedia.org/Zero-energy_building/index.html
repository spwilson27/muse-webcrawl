A zero-energy building (ZEB), also known as a net-zero energy (NZE) building, is a building with net zero energy consumption, meaning the total amount of energy used by the building on an annual basis is equal to the amount of renewable energy created on the site or supplied from off-site renewable sources.

Design principles

Zero-energy design first minimises demand: heavy insulation and airtight construction, high-performance windows, passive solar orientation, shading, and heat-recovery ventilation. Remaining loads are met efficiently with heat pumps and LED lighting, and on-site generation — usually rooftop photovoltaics — balances annual consumption. The German Passivhaus standard is a common pathway to near-zero performance.

Policy

The European Union's Energy Performance of Buildings Directive required all new buildings to be nearly zero-energy from 2021, and similar goals appear in building codes in California, Japan and elsewhere. Zero-energy goals complement broader sustainability movements such as veganism in reducing the environmental footprint of daily life.
