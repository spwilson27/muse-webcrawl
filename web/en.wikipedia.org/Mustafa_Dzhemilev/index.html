Mustafa Dzhemilev

Mustafa Abduldzhemil Dzhemilev (born 13 November 1943), also known as Mustafa Jemilev, is a Ukrainian politician and the longtime leader of the Crimean Tatar national movement. He served as chairman of the Mejlis of the Crimean Tatar People from 1991 to 2013 and has been a member of the Ukrainian parliament since 1998.

Life and activism

Dzhemilev was born in Crimea shortly before the 1944 deportation of the entire Crimean Tatar people to Central Asia by Stalin's regime; he grew up in exile in Uzbekistan. As a young man he became a founder of the Crimean Tatar movement demanding the right of return, and spent about 15 years in Soviet prisons and labour camps. His 303-day hunger strike in 1975–76, during which he was force-fed, drew international attention; the physicist and dissident Andrei Sakharov campaigned for his release.

Return and politics

Dzhemilev returned to Crimea in 1989 as the Tatars were allowed to resettle their homeland. Elected head of the newly formed Mejlis in 1991, he advocated non-violent pursuit of Tatar rights. After Russia's annexation of Crimea in 2014, he was banned from entering Crimea for five years, and he has continued to represent Crimean Tatar interests from Kyiv as a member of the Verkhovna Rada and as Commissioner of the President of Ukraine for the Affairs of Crimean Tatars (2014–2019).
