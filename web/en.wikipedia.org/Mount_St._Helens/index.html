Mount St. Helens

Mount St. Helens is an active stratovolcano located in Skamania County, Washington, in the Pacific Northwest region of the United States. It lies 52 miles (83 km) northeast of Portland, Oregon, and 98 miles (158 km) south of Seattle. The mountain is part of the Cascade Volcanic Arc and is best known for its cataclysmic eruption on May 18, 1980, the deadliest and most economically destructive volcanic event in US history.

Geology

St. Helens is a relatively young volcano, formed within the last 40,000–50,000 years; the pre-1980 summit cone began growing only about 2,200 years ago. It takes its prominence and activity from the subduction of the Juan de Fuca Plate beneath North America.

Eruption of 1980

After two months of earthquakes and steam eruptions, a magnitude 5.1 earthquake on May 18, 1980 triggered the largest landslide in recorded history: the entire north flank slid away, uncorking a lateral blast that devastated 230 square miles (600 km²). Fifty-seven people were killed, including volcanologist David Johnston and innkeeper Harry R. Truman. The eruption column rose 80,000 feet (24 km), ash fell across 11 states, and the summit was reduced from 9,677 ft (2,950 m) to 8,363 ft (2,549 m), leaving a vast horseshoe-shaped crater containing a growing lava dome.

2004–2008 activity

The volcano reawakened in 2004 with a dome-building eruption that extruded new lava spines until 2008, but without major explosive events. The crater glacier that formed around the dome is one of the few growing glaciers in the contiguous United States.

Ecology and memorial

The Mount St. Helens National Volcanic Monument (established 1982) preserves the blast zone for research and education; its ecological recovery — from lupines and pocket gophers to returning elk herds — has become a landmark case study in primary succession.
