The Battle of Fontenoy was a major engagement of the War of the Austrian Succession, fought on 11 May 1745 near Tournai in the Austrian Netherlands. A French army under the command of Marshal Maurice de Saxe defeated the Allied "Pragmatic Army" led by the Duke of Cumberland.

Background

Following the French capture of Menin and Ypres in 1744, the French laid siege to Tournai in April 1745. The Allied army marched to relieve the town and encountered the French army entrenched around the village of Fontenoy.

The battle

Saxe had prepared a strong defensive position anchored on Fontenoy and the woods of Barry. Repeated Allied attacks on the village and redoubts failed, and a great Allied infantry column advanced into the centre of the French position. A counter-attack by French reserves, including the Irish Brigade and the Maison du Roi, broke the column and forced the Allies to retreat. The battle is remembered for an allegedly courteous exchange between officers inviting the other side to fire first.

Aftermath

Tournai fell to the French, whose victory opened the way for the conquest of much of the Austrian Netherlands during 1745 and 1746.
