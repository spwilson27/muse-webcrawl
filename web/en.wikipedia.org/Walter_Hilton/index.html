Walter Hilton, OSA (c. 1340/1345 – 24 March 1396) was an English Augustinian canon regular and mystic, one of the most widely read English spiritual writers of the later Middle Ages. He is best known as the author of The Scale of Perfection (Scala Perfectionis), a guide to the contemplative life written in Middle English.

Life

Little is known of Hilton's early life; he may have studied law at Cambridge. He spent a period as a hermit before entering the Augustinian priory of Thurgarton in Nottinghamshire, where he remained until his death in 1396.

Works

The first book of The Scale of Perfection, written for a female anchoress, treats the reforming of the soul "in faith"; the second book, composed later, develops the more advanced theme of reforming "in faith and in feeling," describing the soul's passage through darkness toward spiritual light and union with God. Hilton also wrote Latin treatises, including works defending the veneration of images, and a commentary on the psalm Qui habitat.

Influence

Hilton's measured, orthodox teaching made him one of the most popular of the fourteenth-century English mystics — a group that includes Richard Rolle, Julian of Norwich, and the anonymous author of The Cloud of Unknowing. The Scale of Perfection circulated widely in manuscript and was printed by Wynkyn de Worde in 1494; it remains in print in modern translation.
