Catenoid

A catenoid is a surface of revolution generated by rotating a catenary curve about an axis. It was described by Leonhard Euler in 1744 and was the first non-trivial minimal surface to be discovered after the plane: its mean curvature is zero everywhere, a property it shares with the helicoid, to which it is closely related by a continuous deformation (the Bonnet transformation).

Geometry

A standard parametrisation is x = c cosh(v/c) cos u, y = c cosh(v/c) sin u, z = v. Physically, a catenoid is the shape taken by a soap film stretched between two equal circular rings, provided the rings are not too far apart (beyond about 1.33 times the ring diameter the film collapses). Catenoid-like forms appear in architecture as cooling tower shapes and in tensile structures.
