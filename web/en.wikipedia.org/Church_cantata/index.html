Church cantata

Type: Sacred vocal music

Peak period: Baroque era

A church cantata is a sacred vocal composition with instrumental accompaniment, typically performed as part of a church service. Especially in Lutheran Germany, cantatas were written for particular Sundays and feasts in the liturgical year.

Form

A Baroque church cantata usually alternates choruses, recitatives, arias, duets, and a closing chorale. Texts combine biblical passages, devotional poetry, and hymn verses relating to the day's readings.

Composers

Johann Sebastian Bach composed several annual cycles in Leipzig, and more than 200 of his sacred cantatas survive. Important predecessors and contemporaries include Dieterich Buxtehude, Johann Pachelbel, and Georg Philipp Telemann, who wrote cantatas on an enormous scale.
