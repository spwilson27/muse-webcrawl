Scalar curvature

In Riemannian geometry, the scalar curvature (or Ricci scalar) is the simplest curvature invariant of a Riemannian manifold. It assigns to each point a single real number determined by the geometry near that point, defined as the trace of the Ricci curvature tensor with respect to the metric.

Scalar curvature measures the deviation of geodesic ball volumes from Euclidean space

Definition

If Ric denotes the Ricci tensor and g the metric, the scalar curvature is R = trg Ric, the sum of the sectional curvatures over all 2-planes spanned by pairs of an orthonormal basis (up to a conventional factor). In two dimensions it is twice the Gaussian curvature.

Interpretation

Geometrically, scalar curvature controls the growth of the volume of small geodesic balls: positive scalar curvature means balls have less volume than in Euclidean space of the same dimension, negative scalar curvature means they have more. In general relativity, scalar curvature appears in the Einstein–Hilbert action, whose variation yields Einstein's field equations. A central question in geometry, the Yamabe problem — solved in the 1980s — asked whether every conformal class of metrics contains one of constant scalar curvature.
