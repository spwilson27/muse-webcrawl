Año Nuevo State Park

Año Nuevo State Park
Location: San Mateo County, California, USA
Area: About 4,000 acres (plus marine reserve)
Known for: Northern elephant seal breeding colony

Año Nuevo State Park is a state park of California, in San Mateo County, encompassing Año Nuevo Island and the mainland point opposite it, about 55 miles (89 km) south of San Francisco. The park is famous for its breeding colony of northern elephant seals, among the largest mainland colonies of the species in the world.

History

The point was named Punta de Año Nuevo ("New Year Point") by the Spanish explorer Sebastián Vizcaíno, whose expedition sighted it around New Year's Day 1603. The area later formed part of Rancho Punta del Año Nuevo. A lighthouse was built on Año Nuevo Island in the 1870s; the island is now closed to the public and managed as a wildlife reserve.

Elephant seals

Each winter, thousands of northern elephant seals haul out on the park's beaches to breed. Bulls arrive in December and battle for dominance; females give birth and nurse pups through the season. During the breeding season, public access to the colony is by guided walk only. The colony, which began in the 1970s as the species recovered from near-extinction, is a major research site for marine mammalogists.
