Trader Vic's

Trader Vic's is a restaurant and tiki bar chain headquartered in Emeryville, California, United States. Victor Jules Bergeron, Jr. (December 10, 1902 – October 11, 1984) founded a chain of Polynesian-themed restaurants that bore his nickname, "Trader Vic". He was one of two people who claimed to have invented the Mai Tai cocktail in 1944. Alongside Donn Beach's Don the Beachcomber, Trader Vic's defined the mid-20th-century tiki culture craze.

History

Bergeron opened a small bar called Hinky Dinks in Oakland in 1934. After a trip to Cuba and Los Angeles — where he visited Don the Beachcomber — he rebranded it as Trader Vic's in 1937 with a South Seas theme. A San Francisco location followed in 1951. The chain expanded worldwide, often inside Hilton hotels, and became famous for its Chinese ovens, barbecued appetizers, and elaborate rum drinks served in ceramic tiki mugs and scorpion bowls.

The Mai Tai

Bergeron claimed to have created the Mai Tai in 1944 at the Oakland restaurant, saying he mixed Jamaican rum, lime, orange curaçao, orgeat, and rock candy syrup for friends visiting from Tahiti, who exclaimed "Maita'i roa a'e!" — "out of this world!" Donn Beach's competing claim has fuelled a longstanding dispute in cocktail history.

Locations

At its peak the chain had dozens of locations across the United States and abroad. Most U.S. locations closed over the decades; the Emeryville flagship remains, and international franchised locations continue in places such as London, Munich, Dubai, and Tokyo.
