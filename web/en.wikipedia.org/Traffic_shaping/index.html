Traffic shaping

Traffic shaping is a bandwidth management technique used on computer networks that delays some or all datagrams to bring them into compliance with a desired traffic profile. Traffic shaping is used to optimise or guarantee performance, improve latency, or increase usable bandwidth for some kinds of packets by delaying other kinds. It is often confused with traffic policing, in which excess packets are dropped or marked rather than delayed.

Mechanisms

Common algorithms include the leaky bucket and the token bucket, which meter traffic at an average rate while permitting controlled bursts. Implementations in routers and switches use queues and schedulers — such as weighted fair queuing — to prioritise latency-sensitive traffic like voice and video over bulk transfers.

Applications

Internet service providers use shaping to enforce service tiers and manage congestion; enterprises use it to guarantee bandwidth for critical applications. Shaping of particular peer-to-peer protocols by ISPs has been controversial in net neutrality debates.
