Peter Taylor, Baron Taylor of Gosforth

Peter Murray Taylor, Baron Taylor of Gosforth (1 May 1930 – 28 April 1997) was a British judge who served as Lord Chief Justice of England from 1992 to 1996. He is remembered in public life both for presiding over major criminal cases and for the Taylor Report into football stadium safety, published after the Hillsborough disaster of 1989, which transformed British football grounds.

Career

Taylor was educated at the Royal Grammar School, Newcastle, and Pembroke College, Cambridge, and was called to the Bar in 1954. Practising on the North Eastern Circuit, he became a Recorder and then a High Court judge in 1980, and a Lord Justice of Appeal in 1988. He was created a life peer as Baron Taylor of Gosforth in 1992 on becoming Lord Chief Justice, succeeding Lord Lane. Serious illness forced his retirement in 1996; he died the following year.

The Taylor Report

Appointed by the government to inquire into the Hillsborough disaster, in which 97 Liverpool supporters were eventually killed, Taylor's interim and final reports (1989–90) criticised police control and stadium design. The Final Report recommended that all major stadiums in the top divisions convert to all-seater accommodation, the removal of perimeter fences, and improvements to crowd management — recommendations that reshaped English football.

Notable cases

As a judge of the Court of Appeal, Taylor was involved in reviewing some of the era's controversial convictions, and as Lord Chief Justice he spoke publicly on sentencing and penal policy, including criticism of mandatory sentencing proposals.
