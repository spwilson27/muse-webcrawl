Scripps College

Scripps College is a private liberal arts women's college in Claremont, California, United States. Founded in 1926 by newspaper publisher and philanthropist Ellen Browning Scripps, it is a member of the Claremont Colleges consortium and is widely regarded as one of the leading women's colleges in the United States.

History

Ellen Browning Scripps, a journalist and philanthropist from the E. W. Scripps newspaper family, founded the college at the age of 89 as one of the first institutions in the American West dedicated to women's education. The campus, designed by architect Gordon Kaufmann in the Spanish Colonial Revival style with landscape design by Edward Huntsman-Trout, is listed on the National Register of Historic Places and is noted for its courtyards, gardens and the Margaret Fowler Garden with its murals by Alfredo Ramos Martínez.

Academics

Scripps offers a curriculum centred on an interdisciplinary humanities core sequence, "Culture, Knowledge, and Representation", and more than 50 majors, with students able to cross-register at the other Claremont Colleges. The college enrols roughly 1,100 undergraduates. Its graduates include notable figures in politics, the arts, science and business.

The Claremont Colleges

Scripps is one of five undergraduate colleges in the Claremont consortium, alongside Pomona, Claremont McKenna, Harvey Mudd and Pitzer colleges, sharing libraries, dining and some academic programmes while retaining separate campuses and identities.
