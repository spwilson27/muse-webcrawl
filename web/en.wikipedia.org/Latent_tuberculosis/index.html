Latent tuberculosis

Latent tuberculosis infection (LTBI) is infection with Mycobacterium tuberculosis without signs or symptoms of active disease; the person is not infectious. About a quarter of the world's population is estimated to be latently infected, with a lifetime risk of progression to active TB of roughly 5–15%, highest in young children and immunocompromised people.

Diagnosis

There is no direct test for latent bacilli; diagnosis relies on the tuberculin skin test or interferon-gamma release assays, after excluding active disease by history, examination and chest radiography.

Treatment

Preventive regimens include 6–9 months of isoniazid, 4 months of rifampicin, or 3 months of weekly rifapentine plus isoniazid, recommended for groups at high risk of progression.
