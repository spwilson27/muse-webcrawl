TX-2

TX-2
Type: transistorised experimental computer
Built: 1958, MIT Lincoln Laboratory
Word length: 36 bits
Memory: 65,536 words of core memory

The TX-2 was an experimental transistorised computer built at MIT Lincoln Laboratory and completed in 1958. It was the successor to the TX-0 and one of the earliest machines with large core memory — 65,536 words of 36 bits — and an advanced interactive display, making it a landmark in the development of interactive computing and computer graphics.

Significance

The TX-2 hosted Ivan Sutherland's Sketchpad program (1963), the pioneering interactive computer graphics system that introduced direct manipulation and object-oriented ideas and is regarded as the ancestor of modern CAD. The machine also supported early work on time-sharing, pen-based input, and the TX-2's programmer community included figures central to the early ARPANET era.

Engineers who worked with the TX-2, including Wesley A. Clark and Ken Olsen, carried its design philosophy into the commercial minicomputer industry; Olsen went on to found Digital Equipment Corporation, whose PDP series drew heavily on the TX lineage.
