Castello Sforzesco

The main entrance and Filarete Tower of the Castello Sforzesco

The Castello Sforzesco ("Sforza Castle") is a medieval fortification in Milan, northern Italy. It was built in the 15th century by Francesco Sforza, Duke of Milan, on the remnants of a 14th-century Visconti fortification. Later renovated and enlarged, it was one of the largest citadels in Europe in the 16th and 17th centuries. The castle now houses several of the city's museums and art collections.

History

The original fortification, the Castello di Porta Giovia, was built by Galeazzo II Visconti between 1360 and 1370 and was partly demolished by the republican government after the death of Filippo Maria Visconti. Francesco Sforza, having become duke in 1450, rebuilt it as his residence and fortress. His successors, including Ludovico Sforza ("il Moro"), embellished the castle; Leonardo da Vinci painted frescoes in several rooms, including the Sala delle Asse. Under Spanish rule in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries the castle was modernised with a star-fort system of bastions and served as a citadel and barracks. It suffered damage under Napoleonic, Austrian, and later occupations and was restored at the end of the nineteenth century by the architect Luca Beltrami, who rebuilt the Filarete Tower — named for the Renaissance architect Filarete — which had collapsed in 1521.

Museums

The castle complex today hosts several museums, including the Pinacoteca del Castello Sforzesco, the Museum of Ancient Art — which holds Michelangelo's final, unfinished sculpture, the Rondanini Pietà — the Egyptian Museum, the Museum of Musical Instruments, and collections of applied arts and prehistoric archaeology. Behind the castle extends Parco Sempione, Milan's large central park.
