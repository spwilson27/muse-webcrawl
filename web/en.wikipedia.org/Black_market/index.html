Black market

For other uses, see Black market (disambiguation).

A black market, underground economy, or shadow economy is a clandestine market or series of transactions that has some aspect of illegality or is characterized by noncompliance with an institutional set of rules. If the rule defines the set of goods and services whose production and distribution are prohibited or restricted by law, noncompliance with the rule constitutes a black market transaction since the transaction itself is illegal. Such transactions include the illegal drug trade, prostitution (where prohibited), illegal currency transactions, and human trafficking.

Violations of the tax code involving income tax evasion constitute membership in the unreported economy that is commonly called the "informal economy", the "shadow economy", or the "grey economy", depending on conventions of usage, whereas a black market is most characteristically associated with the sale of contraband goods.

Background

Black markets tend to appear when a government restricts transactions through rationing, price controls, prohibitions, or heavy taxation. During wartime rationing, for example, goods such as food, fuel, and rubber often circulate illegally at prices above the official ceiling. The term black market is thought to derive from traders operating "in the black", i.e. outside the notice of the authorities, and it was widely popularized during the Second World War.

Organized crime

People engaged in the black market usually run their business hidden under a front business, because participants cannot readily avail themselves of the legal system to settle disputes, they have recourse to a reputation for violence to settle their disputes. Organized crime groups frequently dominate black markets in narcotics, extortion, and smuggling.

Prices and supply

Because black-market goods are illegal, their supply is restricted and risky, which tends to push prices well above legal market levels. Conversely, goods that are merely taxed, such as cigarettes or alcohol sold without duty, may be cheaper on the black market than through legal channels. Economists debate the size of the shadow economy; estimates in developed countries often range from under 10% to over 20% of GDP.
