Catherine of Siena

Catherine of Siena (25 March 1347 – 29 April 1380) was an Italian mystic, Dominican tertiary and scholastic philosopher and theologian. Born Caterina di Giacomo di Benincasa in Siena, she experienced visions from childhood, took a vow of virginity and joined the Dominican third order. Her reputation for holiness and her forceful letters gave her a public role unusual for a medieval woman.

Life and influence

Catherine corresponded with rulers and clergy across Italy, urging peace among the Italian city-states and reform of the Church. She travelled to Avignon in 1376 and persuaded Pope Gregory XI to return the papacy to Rome. During the ensuing Western Schism she staunchly supported Pope Urban VI. Her spiritual masterpiece, the Dialogue of Divine Providence, dictated in ecstasy, alongside some 380 surviving letters, ranks among the classics of Italian literature and Catholic mysticism.

Canonisation

Catherine was canonised in 1461 by Pope Pius II. In 1970 Paul VI named her a Doctor of the Church — among the first women so honoured — and in 1999 John Paul II proclaimed her a co-patron saint of Europe. Her feast day is 29 April.
