Tsitsernakaberd


The memorial complex at Tsitsernakaberd

Tsitsernakaberd (Armenian: Ծիծեռնակաբերդ, "swallow's fortress") is a memorial complex in Yerevan, Armenia, dedicated to the victims of the Armenian genocide of 1915. It stands on a hill west of the city overlooking the Hrazdan River gorge.

The memorial

Completed in 1967, following mass demonstrations in Yerevan on the genocide's 50th anniversary in 1965, the complex consists of a 44-metre stele symbolizing the national rebirth of Armenians, split by a deep cleft; a circle of twelve inward-leaning slabs representing the lost provinces of Western Armenia, sheltering an eternal flame; and a 100-metre wall inscribed with the names of towns and villages where massacres occurred. The memorial was designed by architects Arthur Tarkhanyan and Sashur Kalashyan.

Museum and commemoration

The Armenian Genocide Museum-Institute was added in 1995, along with a park where foreign dignitaries plant memorial fir trees. Every year on 24 April, Armenian Genocide Remembrance Day, hundreds of thousands of people climb to the memorial and lay flowers around the eternal flame.
