James, brother of Jesus

James the Just, or James, brother of Jesus, was a first-century Jewish Christian leader and, according to most biblical scholars and the ancient sources, a brother of Jesus of Nazareth. He led the Jerusalem church after the departure of Peter and is traditionally credited with the New Testament Epistle of James. He was martyred in Jerusalem around 62 or 69 CE.

Sources

Paul calls him "James, the Lord's brother" (Galatians 1:19) and lists him among the witnesses of the risen Christ. The Gospels name James among Jesus' brothers. The Jewish historian Josephus records that the high priest Ananus had James stoned to death in 62 CE, an act that displeased many Jerusalemites. Later Christian writers, including Hegesippus and Eusebius, describe him as "the Just", famed for his piety and devotion to prayer.

Relationship to Jesus

The precise kinship is debated. The majority view among scholars is that James was a full or half-brother of Jesus; Catholic and Orthodox tradition, maintaining the perpetual virginity of Mary, generally understands the "brothers" as cousins or as Joseph's sons from an earlier marriage.

Role and legacy

James presided over the Jerusalem community and, in Acts 15, delivers the judgement of the Council of Jerusalem on admitting Gentile converts. He appears as an advocate of continued Torah observance for Jewish Christians. He is venerated as a saint, with feast days in both Eastern and Western churches, and is distinguished from James son of Zebedee and James son of Alphaeus.
