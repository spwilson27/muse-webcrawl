The Stone Roses is the debut studio album by the English rock band the Stone Roses, released on 2 May 1989 by Silvertone Records. Recorded at Battery and RAK Studios in London with producer John Leckie, it is regarded as one of the greatest British albums of all time and the defining record of the Madchester scene.

Music

The album fused the jangling guitar psychedelia of the Byrds and the Beatles with the grooves of acid house and funk, a synthesis embodied in tracks such as "I Wanna Be Adored," "She Bangs the Drums," "Waterfall," the nine-minute closing "I Am the Resurrection," and "Fools Gold" — released as a single in November 1989 and appended to later US editions. Guitarist John Squire designed the cover in the splattered style of Jackson Pollock, decorated with lemons, a reference to the Paris riots of May 1968, during which protesters used lemons against tear gas.

Release and reception

Commercial success was gradual: the band's feuds with Silvertone and Jive and an injunction delayed a follow-up for five years, while the album's reputation grew steadily through the 1990s. It has topped numerous critics' polls, including the NME's greatest-albums lists and a 2006 public vote for the greatest British album.

Legacy

The Stone Roses is credited with shaping Britpop and restoring guitar music to the centre of British youth culture after the acid-house moment; its influence is heard across Oasis, the Verve and much of the 1990s indie generation. The band played only one more album, Second Coming (1994), before splitting, reuniting for acclaimed stadium shows from 2011.
