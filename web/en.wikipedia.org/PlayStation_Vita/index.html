The PlayStation Vita (PS Vita) is a handheld game console developed and marketed by Sony Computer Entertainment. It was first released in Japan on 17 December 2011, followed by North America and Europe in February 2012, as the successor to the PlayStation Portable.

Hardware

The Vita featured a 5-inch OLED multi-touch capacitive touchscreen (replaced by an LCD in the later PCH-2000 "Slim" model), two analog sticks — a first for a dedicated handheld — a rear touchpad, front and rear cameras, and optional 3G connectivity. Games were distributed on proprietary flash memory cards and digitally via the PlayStation Store, and the system used proprietary memory cards for storage.

Reception and legacy

Although praised for its hardware and its library of Japanese role-playing games, indies, and PlayStation ports, the Vita struggled commercially against smartphones and the Nintendo 3DS. Sony ended production in 2019, and the system sold an estimated 10–15 million units. Its Remote Play feature, allowing it to stream PlayStation 4 games, anticipated the later PlayStation Portal.
