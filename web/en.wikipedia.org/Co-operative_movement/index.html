Co-operative movement

The co-operative movement is the social and economic movement centred on co-operatives: autonomous enterprises owned and democratically controlled by their members — consumers, workers, producers, or residents — for their mutual benefit.

Origins

Early experiments included the Fenwick Weavers' Society in Scotland (1769) and the reforms inspired by Robert Owen. The movement is usually dated from the Rochdale Society of Equitable Pioneers, founded in 1844 in Rochdale, England, whose Rochdale Principles — democratic member control, limited interest on capital, distribution of surplus to members, and education — still underpin co-operative practice.

Development

Consumer, agricultural, housing, worker, and credit co-operatives spread worldwide in the late 19th and 20th centuries. The International Co-operative Alliance, founded in 1895, coordinates the movement globally; the United Nations estimated that co-operatives provide livelihoods for a substantial share of the world's population.
