Dirac comb

The Dirac comb (also called the Shah function or impulse train) is a periodic tempered distribution consisting of Dirac delta functions spaced at regular intervals. It is written ШT(t) = Σ δ(t − nT), summed over all integers n, and is named for its resemblance to the Cyrillic letter sha (Ш).

The Dirac comb: an infinite train of unit impulses.

Fourier transform

A celebrated property of the Dirac comb is that its Fourier transform is another Dirac comb, scaled: sampling a function and periodising its spectrum are dual operations expressed through the comb. This fact underpins the mathematics of sampling theory and the Poisson summation formula.

Uses

The Dirac comb models ideal sampling: multiplying a continuous signal by a comb extracts its values at the sample instants. In frequency-comb spectroscopy and optics, the name also refers to the spectrum of a stabilised mode-locked laser—an array of evenly spaced spectral lines—which functions as a precise "ruler" for measuring optical frequencies, recognised by the 2005 Nobel Prize in Physics.
