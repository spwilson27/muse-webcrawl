Maus

Maus is a graphic novel by American cartoonist Art Spiegelman, serialized from 1980 to 1991. It depicts Spiegelman interviewing his father about his experiences as a Polish Jew and Holocaust survivor. The work employs postmodern techniques — most strikingly, it represents Jews as mice, Germans as cats, Poles as pigs, and Americans as dogs — and deals with the guilt and strained relationship between survivor and child. In 1992 it became the first (and to date only) graphic novel to win a Pulitzer Prize.

Synopsis

The narrative alternates between Vladek Spiegelman's account of life in Poland before and during the Second World War — ghettos, hiding, Auschwitz and Dachau — and the present-day frame story of Art's difficult relationship with his aging father in Rego Park, New York, including the suicide of Art's mother Anja.

Reception and influence

Maus is credited with bringing literary respectability to comics and with shaping Holocaust representation and memoir. It is widely taught in schools; in 2022 a Tennessee school board's removal of the book from an eighth-grade curriculum drew national controversy and sent sales surging.
