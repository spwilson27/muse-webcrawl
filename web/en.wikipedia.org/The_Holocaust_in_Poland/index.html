The Holocaust in Poland

Period1939–1945
LocationGerman-occupied Poland
VictimsApproximately 3 million Polish Jews murdered (90% of prewar population)

The Holocaust in Poland was the Nazi German persecution and systematic murder of Jews in occupied Poland during the Second World War. Poland's prewar Jewish population of about 3.3 million — the largest in Europe — was almost entirely destroyed; approximately three million Polish Jews were killed, alongside millions of other victims.

Occupation and ghettos

Following the German invasion of September 1939, Jews were subjected to forced labour, expropriation, and violent persecution, and were progressively confined to ghettos, the largest being the Warsaw Ghetto, which held over 400,000 people in desperate conditions of starvation and disease.

Extermination

In 1942 the Germans launched Operation Reinhard, the murder of the Jews of the General Government in purpose-built extermination camps at Bełżec, Sobibór, and Treblinka; Auschwitz-Birkenau, Majdanek, and Chełmno also operated on Polish soil. The Warsaw Ghetto Uprising of April–May 1943 was the largest act of Jewish armed resistance. By 1945, only a small fraction of Polish Jews — perhaps 50,000–120,000 — had survived in Poland itself, with others surviving in the Soviet Union or in hiding.

Memory

The Holocaust in Poland is commemorated by museums and memorials at the former camps and by institutions such as the POLIN Museum of the History of Polish Jews in Warsaw; the roles of perpetrators, collaborators, bystanders, and rescuers (Poles form the largest national group among the Righteous Among the Nations) remain subjects of historical research and public debate.
