Zoledronic acid

Image placeholder: Chemical structure of zoledronic acid

Zoledronic acid, also known as zoledronate and sold under brand names including Zometa and Reclast, is a bisphosphonate medication used to treat a number of bone diseases, including osteoporosis, high blood calcium due to cancer, and bone breakdown due to cancer.

Medical use

It is given by intravenous infusion — typically once a year for osteoporosis and more frequently for cancer-related indications. Zoledronic acid works by inhibiting osteoclast-mediated bone resorption; it binds strongly to bone mineral and is the most potent bisphosphonate in clinical use.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include fever, flu-like symptoms after the first dose, fatigue, and joint pain. Serious but rare adverse effects include osteonecrosis of the jaw and atypical femoral fractures. It was patented in 1986 and approved for medical use in the early 2000s.
