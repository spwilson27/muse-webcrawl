Vikramaditya

Vikramaditya (Sanskrit: Vikramāditya) was a legendary emperor of ancient India, often characterised as an ideal king known for generosity, courage and patronage of scholars. Tales of his court at Ujjain — including the Simhasana Dvatrimsika (Thirty-two Tales of the Throne) and Vetala Panchavimsati (Baital Pachisi) — remain popular in Indian folklore.

Historical basis

The title Vikramaditya was adopted by several historical kings, most notably the Gupta emperor Chandragupta II (r. c. 375–415), who defeated the Western Satraps, and the Chalukya king Vikramaditya VI. The Vikram Samvat calendar era, beginning in 57 BCE, is traditionally associated with the legendary king's victory over the Shakas.

According to tradition, his court was graced by the navaratna, the "nine gems" of scholarship, including Kalidasa.
