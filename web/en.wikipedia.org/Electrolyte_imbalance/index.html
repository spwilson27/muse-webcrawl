Electrolyte imbalance

Electrolyte imbalance, or water-electrolyte imbalance, is an abnormality in the concentration of electrolytes — electrically charged minerals such as sodium, potassium, calcium, magnesium, chloride, phosphate, and bicarbonate — in the body. Electrolytes are essential for nerve and muscle function, fluid balance, blood pressure, and pH regulation, and significant imbalances can be life-threatening.

Common disorders

Sodium: Hyponatremia (low sodium) is the most common electrolyte disorder in hospitalized patients and can cause confusion, seizures, and coma; hypernatremia usually reflects water loss and causes intense thirst and neurological symptoms. Potassium: Hypokalemia and hyperkalemia both risk cardiac arrhythmias; hyperkalemia in particular is a medical emergency, commonly arising from kidney failure or certain medications. Calcium and magnesium: abnormalities affect neuromuscular excitability — hypocalcemia can cause tetany, while hypomagnesemia often accompanies and worsens potassium and calcium disturbances.

Causes

Causes include kidney disease, vomiting and diarrhea, excessive sweating, burns, heart failure, endocrine disorders (such as SIADH or adrenal insufficiency), malnutrition and refeeding, and many drugs — diuretics, ACE inhibitors, and lithium among them.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is by blood tests (a basic metabolic panel), urine studies, and clinical assessment. Treatment addresses the underlying cause and corrects the abnormal level at a safe rate — dangerously fast correction of chronic hyponatremia, for example, can cause osmotic demyelination syndrome. Oral or intravenous fluids, electrolyte supplements, and dialysis are used as appropriate.
