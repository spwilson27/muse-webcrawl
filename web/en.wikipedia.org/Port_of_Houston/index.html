The Port of Houston is one of the world's largest ports, centred on the Houston Ship Channel, a 52-mile dredged waterway connecting Houston to the Gulf of Mexico. It is the busiest port in the United States by foreign waterborne tonnage and a hub for the petrochemical industry.

History

Houston voters approved bond issues to dredge a deepwater channel in the early 1900s; the port officially opened in 1914, weeks before the Panama Canal. Along its banks grew the largest petrochemical complex in the United States.

Facilities

Port Houston operates the Bayport and Barbours Cut container terminals and general cargo facilities; hundreds of private terminals line the channel. The Project 11 widening program is enlarging the channel to accommodate larger vessels.
