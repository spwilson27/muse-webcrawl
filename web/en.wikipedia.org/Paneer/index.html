Paneer

Paneer is a fresh, acid-set, non-melting cheese common in Indian cuisine. It is made by curdling milk with a food acid such as lemon juice, vinegar or citric acid, then draining and pressing the curds in muslin. Unlike most cheeses, its production uses no rennet, making it suitable for lacto-vegetarian diets.

Characteristics

Paneer is white, mild and milky, with a dense, crumbly yet sliceable texture. Because the curds are pressed and the cheese is not aged or salted heavily, it absorbs the flavours of the dishes it is cooked in. Crucially, it does not melt when heated, so cubes hold their shape in curries and on skewers.

Culinary use

Paneer is a major source of protein in vegetarian Indian cooking. Famous dishes include palak paneer (with spinach), paneer tikka (marinated and grilled), mattar paneer (with peas), shahi paneer, and paneer butter masala. It is also crumbled into paratha fillings and used in sweets. In Bengal, the related unsqueezed curd chhena forms the base of rasgulla and sandesh.
