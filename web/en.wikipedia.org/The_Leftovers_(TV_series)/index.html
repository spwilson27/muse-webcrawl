The Leftovers is an American drama television series created by Damon Lindelof and Tom Perrotta, based on Perrotta's 2011 novel of the same name. It aired on HBO from 2014 to 2017. The series begins three years after the "Sudden Departure," an unexplained global event in which 2% of the world's population — some 140 million people — vanished simultaneously, and follows those who were left behind as they struggle with grief, faith, and the collapse of ordinary explanations.

Premise and seasons

The first season is set in the fictional town of Mapleton, New York, and centers on police chief Kevin Garvey (Justin Theroux) and his fractured family, the grieving widow Nora Durst (Carrie Coon), the Reverend Matt Jamison (Christopher Eccleston), and the Guilty Remnant, a silent, chain-smoking cult dressed in white that seeks to keep the Departure from being forgotten. The second season moves the characters to Jarden, Texas, a town from which no one departed, advertised as a miracle. The third season shifts largely to Australia and builds toward the seventh anniversary of the Departure.

Themes

The series deliberately never explains the Departure, focusing instead on how people construct meaning after trauma — through religion, cults, denial, or storytelling. Critics praised its emotional intensity, Max Richter's score, and standout episodes such as "Guest," "International Assassin," and the finale, "The Book of Nora."

Reception

Although its first season received mixed notices for its bleak tone, the second and third seasons were acclaimed; The Leftovers is now frequently ranked among the greatest television series of the 2010s, and Carrie Coon won the Critics' Choice Television Award for her performance.
