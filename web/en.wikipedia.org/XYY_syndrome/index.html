XYY syndrome, also known as 47,XYY syndrome or Jacobs syndrome, is a genetic condition in which a male has an extra copy of the Y chromosome in each of his cells, giving a total of 47 chromosomes instead of the usual 46. It occurs in about 1 in 1,000 male births. Most affected individuals are taller than average and have no other obvious physical differences; many are never diagnosed. Some have an increased risk of learning difficulties, delayed speech and language skills, and behavioural problems.

Signs and symptoms

Boys with XYY syndrome tend to grow faster than average from early childhood and are typically several centimetres taller than expected as adults. Other physical features are usually unremarkable, although acne, an increased head size, and widely spaced eyes have been reported. Intelligence is generally in the normal range, though often somewhat lower than that of siblings, and there is an increased incidence of attention deficit hyperactivity disorder, autism spectrum features, and difficulties with impulse control.

Cause

The condition arises from nondisjunction of the Y chromosome during meiosis II in the father, producing a sperm cell with two Y chromosomes. It is not inherited and is not caused by anything the parents did. Unlike some other sex chromosome aneuploidies, it is not associated with advanced parental age.

History

The condition was first described in 1961 by Avery Sandberg and colleagues. It was named Jacobs syndrome after Patricia Jacobs, who in 1965 published a controversial study of men in a Scottish maximum-security hospital and special school, which found an over-representation of XYY men and suggested a link to aggressive behaviour. Later, better-controlled studies showed that the great majority of XYY men are not unusually aggressive, and the early "supermale" stereotype is now regarded as unfounded.

Prognosis

Life expectancy is generally normal or only slightly reduced. With early educational support, most individuals lead ordinary lives, and the condition often remains undetected.
