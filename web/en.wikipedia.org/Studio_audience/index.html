Studio audience

A studio audience is an audience present in a television or radio studio during the recording of a programme. The presence of a studio audience — audibly laughing, clapping and reacting — has long been a feature of sitcoms, variety shows, talk shows, game shows and panel shows. Their reactions give performers immediate feedback and provide home viewers with social cues that material is funny or dramatic; recordings "filmed before a live studio audience" have traditionally been announced as such in credits.

Practice

Members of the public typically attend free of charge, obtaining tickets in advance, and are seated by ushers. A warm-up comedian often entertains and instructs the audience between takes, and illuminated "APPLAUSE" signs may prompt clapping. For multi-camera sitcoms such as I Love Lucy (which pioneered the format in 1951), episodes are performed in sequence before the audience, whose genuine laughter is recorded. Where a live audience is absent, producers sometimes use a laugh track ("canned laughter") instead, a practice widely debated since the 1960s.

Developments

Some series have moved away from studio audiences toward single-camera production, while others, from late-night talk shows to political debate programmes, rely on them heavily. During the COVID-19 pandemic, productions used virtual audiences on screens, reduced-capacity seating, or audience-free formats before spectators returned.
