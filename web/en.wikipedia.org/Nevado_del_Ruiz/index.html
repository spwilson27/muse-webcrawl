The Nevado del Ruiz (Spanish pronunciation: [neˈβaðo ðel ˈrwis]), also known as La Mesa de Herveo, is a volcano on the border of the departments of Caldas and Tolima in Colombia, about 129 km west of the capital city Bogotá. It is a stratovolcano composed of many layers of lava alternating with hardened volcanic ash and other pyroclastic rocks, and at 5,321 metres it is the highest active volcano in Colombia's Cordillera Central. Its summit glacier has retreated markedly over recent decades.

1985 eruption and the Armero tragedy

On 13 November 1985, a relatively small eruption melted part of the summit glacier, generating lahars (volcanic mudflows) that swept down river valleys. The flows engulfed the town of Armero, killing roughly 20,000 of its 29,000 inhabitants, and destroyed other settlements including parts of Chinchiná; total deaths are estimated at about 23,000–25,000. The Armero tragedy was the second-deadliest volcanic disaster of the 20th century. Hazard maps predicting the flows had been published weeks earlier, but evacuation orders were confused and delayed — a failure that spurred major reforms in volcano monitoring and disaster preparedness, including the strengthening of the Colombian Geological Service's observatories.

Later activity

The volcano erupted again in 1989, and from 2012 onward entered a new period of unrest with ash emissions and dome growth, periodically prompting evacuations and aviation alerts. It lies within Los Nevados National Natural Park, a major watershed and tourist area.
