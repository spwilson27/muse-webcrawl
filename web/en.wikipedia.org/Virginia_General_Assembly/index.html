The Virginia General Assembly is the legislative body of the Commonwealth of Virginia, consisting of the Senate of Virginia (40 members) and the Virginia House of Delegates (100 members). Founded in 1619 as the House of Burgesses, it is the oldest continuously operating law-making body in the Western Hemisphere, and it meets at the Virginia State Capitol in Richmond.

History

The first representative assembly in English America convened at Jamestown on 30 July 1619, when Governor George Yeardley summoned elected burgesses from the colony's settlements. After the American Revolution, the General Assembly adopted the Virginia Declaration of Rights and the 1776 constitution. The legislature sat in Williamsburg before moving to Richmond in 1780.

Structure and sessions

Senators serve four-year terms and delegates two-year terms; both are part-time citizen legislators. The Assembly convenes annually in January — for 60 days in even-numbered years and 30 days (often extended) in odd-numbered years — to pass laws and a biennial budget. The Speaker of the House and the Senate's president pro tempore lead the chambers, while the lieutenant governor presides over the Senate.

Functions

In addition to legislating, the General Assembly elects judges and certain state officials, confirms some gubernatorial appointments, and proposes amendments to the Virginia constitution for voter approval. Its members have included eight U.S. presidents' early careers, and Thomas Jefferson, James Madison and Patrick Henry all served in its predecessor or early forms.
