Lyre

The lyre is a stringed musical instrument known for its use in Greek classical antiquity and later periods. It consists of a soundbox — often originally a tortoise shell — two arms and a crossbar, with strings running from the crossbar to the soundbox.

History

Lyres date back to at least 2700 BCE in Mesopotamia, including the ornate Lyres of Ur. In Greece the lyre was associated with Apollo and Orpheus and accompanied poetry recitation, giving rise to the term "lyric poetry". Related instruments spread across the ancient Near East and Mediterranean.

Construction

Strings, typically of gut, were plucked or strummed, often with a plectrum. Greek lyres commonly had seven strings.
