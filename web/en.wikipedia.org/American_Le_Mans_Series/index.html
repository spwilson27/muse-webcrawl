The American Le Mans Series (ALMS) was a sports car racing series based in North America. It was created by Don Panoz and ran from 1999 to 2013, sanctioned by the International Motor Sports Association (IMSA). The series used rules and car classes similar to those of the 24 Hours of Le Mans in France, organized under a license from the Automobile Club de l'Ouest.

Racing classes

Fields combined multiple classes racing simultaneously: Le Mans Prototype classes (LMP1, LMP2, and the spec LMPC) and Grand Touring classes (GT and the spec GTC). Signature events included the 12 Hours of Sebring and the Petit Le Mans at Road Atlanta, a 1,000-mile (or 10-hour) endurance race that closed most seasons.

Merger

In 2012 it was announced that the ALMS would merge with the rival Grand-Am Rolex Sports Car Series. The combined championship, the United SportsCar Championship (later the IMSA SportsCar Championship), began in 2014, ending the ALMS after the 2013 season.
