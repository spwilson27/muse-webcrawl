Restatement of the Law

Restatements of the Law are a set of treatises on legal subjects that seek to inform judges and lawyers about general principles of common law. They are produced by the American Law Institute (ALI), an organisation of judges, legal academics, and practitioners founded in 1923, and are one of the most influential forms of secondary legal authority in the United States.

Purpose and format

The Restatements aim to restate the existing law in clear, systematic propositions, while also indicating trends and, where the law is unsettled, the better-reasoned view. Each Restatement consists of black-letter rules, explanatory comments, illustrations based on hypothetical facts, and reporters' notes summarising the case law. Drafts are prepared by expert reporters and debated and approved by the ALI membership before publication.

Series

The first series, completed by the early 1940s, covered subjects such as contracts, torts, agency, property, and trusts. A second series followed in the 1950s–1980s, and third (and in some areas fourth) series continue. Well-known examples include the Restatement (Second) of Contracts, the Restatement (Second) and (Third) of Torts, and the Restatement (Third) of Property. Some Restatement provisions, such as section 402A of the Restatement (Second) of Torts on strict products liability, have been adopted by many state courts and have substantially shaped American law.

Influence

Although not binding authority, the Restatements are highly persuasive and are frequently cited by courts, particularly where a jurisdiction has not yet ruled on an issue.
