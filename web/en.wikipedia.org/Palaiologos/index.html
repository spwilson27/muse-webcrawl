Palaiologos

The House of Palaiologos (Greek: Παλαιολόγος, plural Παλαιολόγοι, Palaiologoi) was a Byzantine Greek imperial dynasty that ruled the Byzantine Empire from 1259 until its fall in 1453, the longest-lived dynasty in Byzantine history. Its members also ruled the Despotate of the Morea and held territories in Italy. The family name is traditionally explained as deriving from palaios logos ("old word" or "old account"), though its origin is uncertain.

Origins and rise

The Palaiologoi first appear in the 11th century as military aristocrats in Anatolia and Macedonia. Michael VIII Palaiologos, a successful general, became regent and then co-emperor, and in 1261 his forces recovered Constantinople from the Latin Empire, restoring Byzantine rule. He was crowned sole emperor in 1259 and founded the dynasty's rule.

Imperial period

Palaiologan emperors — including Andronikos II, Andronikos III, John V, John VI Kantakouzenos (by marriage into power), Manuel II, John VIII, and Constantine XI — presided over a shrinking empire beset by civil wars, plague, Serbian and Ottoman expansion, and religious controversy over union with the Roman Catholic Church. Despite political decline, the period saw a flourishing of art, scholarship, and literature known as the Palaiologan Renaissance, centred on Constantinople and Mistra.

Fall and later descendants

Constantine XI, the last emperor, died defending Constantinople against Mehmed II on 29 May 1453. His brothers Demetrios and Thomas ruled the Morea until 1460; Thomas's descendants in exile were recognised in the West as titular emperors. A junior branch, the Palaeologus-Montferrat line, ruled the Italian marquisate of Montferrat until 1533. Later families bearing the name, such as the Paleologu of Wallachia and Romania, claim descent from the imperial house.
