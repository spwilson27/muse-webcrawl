Reproducibility

Reproducibility, closely related to replicability and repeatability, is a major principle underpinning the scientific method. For the findings of a study to be reproducible means that results obtained by an experiment or an observational study, or in a statistical analysis of a data set, should be achieved again with a high degree of reliability when the study is replicated.

Terminology for reproducibility varies between fields and standards bodies.

Definitions

Usage of the terms has varied. In metrology and engineering, repeatability refers to the same measurement procedure, observers, and conditions over a short period, while reproducibility involves different locations, operators, and measuring systems. In the computational and empirical sciences since the 2010s, a common convention distinguishes reproducibility — obtaining consistent results using the same data and code — from replicability — obtaining consistent results with newly collected data. Terminology adopted by the US National Academies in 2019 follows this convention, though older sources often reverse it.

Replication crisis

Concerns about reproducibility rose sharply in the 2010s with the so-called replication crisis, after large-scale attempts to replicate published findings — most prominently in psychology, where the Open Science Collaboration (2015) replicated only about a third of 100 studies with significant results — and preclinical biomedical research, where Begley and Ellis reported in 2012 that only 6 of 53 landmark cancer studies could be confirmed. Causes identified include publication bias, p-hacking, low statistical power, and the file-drawer effect. Proposed remedies include preregistration, open data and code, registered reports, and larger collaborative replications.
