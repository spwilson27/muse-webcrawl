Semantics

FieldLinguistics, philosophy, logic, computer science
SubjectMeaning in language and other sign systems
Key figuresGottlob Frege, Alfred Tarski, Richard Montague, Charles Sanders Peirce

Semantics is the study of meaning, reference, or truth. In linguistics it concerns how languages organise expressions into meaningful units and how the meanings of words, phrases and sentences combine. The term was coined in the 19th century from the Greek sēmantikos, "significant", and the discipline is related to — and often contrasted with — syntax (the structure of expressions) and pragmatics (meaning in context).

Sense and reference

Gottlob Frege distinguished the sense of an expression (the mode of presentation) from its reference (the object it picks out): "the morning star" and "the evening star" have the same reference, Venus, but different senses. This distinction underlies much of modern semantic theory.

Formal semantics

Formal semantics uses tools from logic and mathematics to model meaning precisely. Richard Montague showed in the 1970s that fragments of natural language could be treated with the rigour of formal languages, using truth conditions and possible-worlds semantics. Alfred Tarski's work on truth in formalised languages is foundational.

Branches

Lexical semantics studies word meaning and relations such as synonymy, antonymy and hyponymy. Compositional semantics examines how sentence meaning arises from the meanings of parts and their arrangement. Cognitive semantics links meaning to human conceptualisation, while computational semantics applies semantic analysis in natural language processing.
