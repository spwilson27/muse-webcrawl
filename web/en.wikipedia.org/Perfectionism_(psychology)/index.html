Perfectionism (psychology)

Perfectionism, in psychology, is a broad personality trait characterised by a person's concern with striving for flawlessness and perfection, accompanied by critical self-evaluations and concerns regarding others' evaluations. It is best conceptualised as a multidimensional and multilevel personality trait.

Dimensions

Researchers such as Paul Hewitt and Gordon Flett distinguish self-oriented perfectionism (imposing unrealistic standards on oneself), other-oriented perfectionism (imposing them on others), and socially prescribed perfectionism (believing others demand perfection of oneself). The Frost Multidimensional Perfectionism Scale identifies dimensions such as concern over mistakes, personal standards, parental expectations, doubts about actions and organisation.

Adaptive and maladaptive forms

Some researchers distinguish "adaptive" perfection striving, associated with conscientiousness and achievement, from "maladaptive" evaluative concerns, which are linked to depression, anxiety, eating disorders, procrastination and obsessive-compulsive traits. Meta-analyses show socially prescribed perfectionism has risen among young people in recent decades.

Treatment

Cognitive behavioural therapy for perfectionism targets rigid standards, self-criticism and contingent self-worth, using behavioural experiments and cognitive restructuring. Perfectionism is also a transdiagnostic process implicated in multiple disorders, making it a common focus of clinical intervention.
