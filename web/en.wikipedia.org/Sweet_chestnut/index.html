Sweet chestnut

The sweet chestnut (Castanea sativa) is a species of tree in the family Fagaceae, native to Southern Europe and Asia Minor and widely cultivated for its edible nuts and durable timber.

Description

It is a large deciduous tree growing to 20–35 metres, with oblong-lanceolate, boldly toothed leaves and spirally fissured bark on old trunks. The nuts develop in spiny burrs and ripen in autumn; some ancient specimens, such as Sicily's Hundred-Horse Chestnut, are among the oldest and largest trees in Europe.

Uses

Chestnuts are eaten roasted, ground into flour, or used in confectionery such as marrons glacés. The rot-resistant wood serves for fencing, beams and barrels. The species was spread across Europe in Roman times and remains a staple food tree in Mediterranean mountain regions.
