Form W-9

Form W-9 collects a payee's taxpayer identification number for information reporting.

Form W-9, officially the Request for Taxpayer Identification Number and Certification, is an Internal Revenue Service form used in the United States by a person or business (the payer) to request the correct taxpayer identification number (TIN) and certification from a payee — typically an independent contractor, freelancer, vendor or bank customer — so that payments can be reported to the IRS on information returns such as Form 1099.

How it is used

Unlike most IRS forms, the W-9 is not sent to the IRS. The payee completes it with their name, business name if any, federal tax classification (individual, partnership, C corporation, S corporation, LLC and so on), address and TIN — either a Social Security number or an employer identification number — and signs a certification that the TIN is correct and that they are not subject to backup withholding. The payer keeps the form on file and uses the information to prepare Forms 1099-NEC, 1099-MISC, 1099-INT and similar returns.

Backup withholding

If a payee fails to furnish a correct TIN, the payer may be required to withhold a flat percentage — 24% in recent years — of reportable payments as backup withholding and remit it to the IRS. Certain payees, such as corporations for many payment types, are exempt and indicate this with an exempt payee code on the form.

Related forms

Foreign persons generally do not use Form W-9; instead they furnish one of the Form W-8 series (such as W-8BEN) to certify foreign status and claim treaty benefits where applicable.
