iPhone

The iPhone is a line of smartphones developed and marketed by Apple Inc. The first-generation iPhone was announced by co-founder Steve Jobs on January 9, 2007, and released on June 29, 2007. It combined a mobile phone, an iPod with a touchscreen interface, and an internet communicator in a single device, and it is widely credited with popularising the modern touchscreen smartphone and the app economy.

History

Development began in 2004 as the secretive "Project Purple", with a team of roughly a thousand employees. Jobs redirected work from a tablet prototype toward a phone after seeing the multi-touch technology developed from FingerWorks' research. At Macworld 2007 Jobs introduced the device as "an iPod, a phone, and an internet communicator" in one product. The original iPhone had a 3.5-inch display, a 2-megapixel camera, and no 3G or App Store; it initially ran a version of OS X.

The iPhone 3G (2008) added faster data and launched the App Store, opening the platform to third-party software. Successive models added the high-resolution Retina display (iPhone 4, 2010), the Siri voice assistant (4S, 2011), Touch ID (5S, 2013), larger displays (iPhone 6, 2014), Face ID and an edge-to-edge OLED screen (iPhone X, 2017), and Apple-designed silicon that has led the industry in performance.

Hardware and software

iPhones run Apple's iOS operating system, noted for its touch interface built around direct manipulation with multi-touch gestures. Hardware is designed by Apple around its own A-series system-on-a-chip processors. The closed integration of hardware and software, the App Store ecosystem, services such as iMessage and iCloud, and features including the camera system and Apple Pay are central to the product line.

Sales and influence

More than 2.3 billion iPhones had been sold by the early 2020s, making the line one of the most successful consumer products in history and Apple's largest source of revenue. The iPhone's design language — a large touchscreen slab with minimal physical controls — became the template for the smartphone industry, and its introduction is considered a turning point in mobile computing, mobile internet use and digital media.
