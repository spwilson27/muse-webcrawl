Germinal is a novel by French writer Émile Zola, published in 1885. It is the thirteenth novel in Zola's twenty-volume series Les Rougon-Macquart and is widely considered his masterpiece. The title refers to the seventh month of the French Republican Calendar, a spring month of germination and new growth; Zola uses it metaphorically for the budding of revolutionary hope among the oppressed miners of northern France whose lives the novel depicts.

Plot summary

The novel follows Étienne Lantier, a young unemployed railway worker who arrives in the coal-mining town of Montsou in northern France and finds work at the Le Voreux pit. He lodges with the Maheu family, whose men have mined for generations, and falls in love with Catherine Maheu. Étienne reads socialist and anarchist literature and, as the mining company cuts wages by manipulating the timbering payments, he organises the miners and founds a local section of the International Workingmen's Association.

When the strike comes, it spreads across the coalfield. Weeks of hunger drive the miners to violence: a march on the neighbouring pits turns into a riot, shops are looted, and soldiers fire on the crowd at Le Voreux, killing several strikers including the patriarch Toussaint Maheu. The strike collapses in starvation, and the miners return to work defeated. The anarchist Souvarine sabotages the pit's shaft casing, flooding Le Voreux and trapping Étienne, Catherine, and her brutal lover Chaval below ground. In the darkness Étienne kills Chaval; Catherine dies in his arms before rescuers reach them. Étienne leaves Montsou, walking toward Paris as spring arrives, convinced that an army of the oppressed is germinating beneath the earth and will one day burst forth.

Research and writing

Zola prepared for the novel with his customary documentary rigour. In February 1884 he travelled to the coalfields around Anzin, where a major strike was under way, and went down a mine to observe conditions at first hand. His dossiers included mining manuals, medical reports on miners' diseases, and parliamentary inquiries. The resulting depiction of the miners' material world — the heat, noise, and darkness of the pits, the company housing estates (corons), the truck shops and the rationing of food — is among the most celebrated achievements of literary naturalism.

Themes and reception

Germinal examines heredity and environment, the twin forces of Zola's naturalism, but it is animated by an almost biblical imagery of devouring pits, seeds, and resurrection: the mine Le Voreux ("the voracious one") crouches like a beast swallowing men in shifts. On publication the novel scandalised some readers with its violence and sexuality, but it was an immediate international success. At Zola's funeral in 1902, delegations of miners marched in the cortège chanting "Germinal! Germinal!" The novel has been adapted for the cinema several times, most notably in Claude Berri's 1993 film starring Gérard Depardieu.
