Skookum Jim

Skookum Jim Mason (c. 1855 – July 11, 1916), born Keish, was a Tagish First Nations prospector in the Yukon. He is credited, together with his party, with the discovery of gold that triggered the Klondike Gold Rush in 1896. His nickname came from the Chinook jargon word skookum, meaning strong — he was renowned for packing enormous loads, reportedly carrying 180 pounds over the Chilkoot Pass.

The Klondike discovery

In August 1896, Keish was travelling with his nephew Dawson Charlie (Káa Goox) and his brother-in-law George Carmack, Carmack's wife Kate, and others near Rabbit Creek (later Bonanza Creek), a tributary of the Klondike River. Gold was found in the creek gravels on August 16, 1896, and claims were staked the next day. Although Carmack registered the discovery claim in his own name, Tagish oral tradition holds that Keish was the one who actually found the gold. The discovery sparked a stampede of roughly 100,000 prospectors to the Klondike.

Later life

Keish became wealthy from the claims but struggled with alcohol and the dislocations that followed the rush; his fortune largely dissipated. He died in Whitehorse in 1916. He was inducted into the Canadian Mining Hall of Fame, and the Skookum Jim Friendship Centre in Whitehorse is named in his honour.
