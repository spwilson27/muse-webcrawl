Allyl isothiocyanate

Chemical formula: C4H5NS
Appearance: Colourless to pale yellow oil
Boiling point: 150–154 °C
Source: Mustard seed, horseradish, wasabi

Allyl isothiocyanate (AITC) is an organosulfur compound with the formula CH2CHCH2NCS. This colourless to pale yellow oil is responsible for the pungent taste and lachrymatory effect of mustard, horseradish, and wasabi. It is produced when the glucosinolate sinigrin, present in the seeds of plants in the family Brassicaceae, is hydrolysed by the enzyme myrosinase when plant tissue is damaged.

Properties and occurrence

AITC is sparingly soluble in water but miscible with most organic solvents. In the plant it serves as a chemical defence against herbivores; its release from crushed mustard seed is an example of the so-called "mustard oil bomb". Commercially it is obtained from mustard seed oil or synthesised from allyl chloride and an alkali thiocyanate.

Uses and biological activity

Allyl isothiocyanate is used as a flavouring, as a fumigant and insecticide, and in some traditional remedies. It activates the TRPA1 ion channel, producing its characteristic pungency and tearing effect. It has been studied for antimicrobial and possible anticancer properties, though evidence for therapeutic benefit in humans remains limited. Concentrated AITC is a skin and respiratory irritant.
