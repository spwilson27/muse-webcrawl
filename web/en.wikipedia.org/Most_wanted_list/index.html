Most wanted list

A most wanted list is a published list of criminals and fugitives maintained by a law enforcement agency. The purpose of such a list is typically to alert the public and enlist their assistance in locating suspects who are considered especially dangerous or significant.

Notable lists

The best-known example is the FBI Ten Most Wanted Fugitives list, created in 1950 by the United States Federal Bureau of Investigation following a newspaper story about the bureau's "toughest guys". Fugitives on the list are generally wanted for serious crimes, and rewards are offered for information leading to capture. Hundreds of fugitives have appeared on the list since its inception, and the great majority have been located, many as a direct result of public tips.

Other agencies maintain similar lists, including the U.S. Marshals Service (Fifteen Most Wanted), the Drug Enforcement Administration, Interpol (whose Red Notices serve a related alerting function), and Europol's "Europe's Most Wanted Fugitives" campaign. Some lists target specific categories, such as war criminals, terrorists, or cybercriminals.

Criteria and removal

Suspects are generally added because they pose a danger to the public and because publicity is judged likely to assist capture. A fugitive is removed when captured, when charges are dismissed, when they are confirmed dead, or when they no longer meet the criteria.
