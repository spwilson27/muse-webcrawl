Sámi people

Total populationc. 80,000–100,000
RegionsSápmi (Norway, Sweden, Finland, Russia)
LanguagesSámi languages, Norwegian, Swedish, Finnish, Russian

The Sámi people are an Indigenous people inhabiting Sápmi, a region spanning northern Norway, Sweden, Finland, and Russia's Kola Peninsula. Traditionally, many Sámi livelihoods centred on reindeer herding, coastal fishing, hunting, and trapping.

Culture

Sámi culture is expressed through the colourful gákti garment, the joik vocal tradition, and handicrafts known as duodji. The Sámi flag, adopted in 1986, uses red, blue, green, and yellow with a circle symbolising the sun and moon.

Politics

Sámi parliaments in Norway, Sweden, and Finland represent Sámi interests, and the Sámi are recognised as an Indigenous people with rights to land, water, and cultural self-determination, though the extent of these rights remains contested.
