First Sea Lord

The First Sea Lord and Chief of the Naval Staff (1SL/CNS) is a statutory position in the British Armed Forces, usually held by an admiral. As the most senior officer of the Royal Navy, the First Sea Lord is the professional head of the Naval Service and is a member of the Defence Council and the Chiefs of Staff Committee.

History

The title dates from 1828, when the senior Naval Lord of the Board of Admiralty became known as the First Naval Lord; the style First Sea Lord was adopted in 1904 under Admiral Sir John Fisher, the office's most famous holder, who drove the dreadnought revolution. During both world wars the First Sea Lord directed the Royal Navy's global operations from the Admiralty in London. In 1964 the Admiralty was absorbed into the Ministry of Defence, but the post continued as head of the Naval Service.

Role

The First Sea Lord is responsible for the fighting effectiveness, efficiency and morale of the Royal Navy, and advises the government on naval strategy. The post is supported by the Second Sea Lord and the Fleet Commander. Notable holders include Fisher, David Beatty, Andrew Cunningham, Louis Mountbatten (later Chief of the Defence Staff), and, in the modern era, admirals who have overseen the service's carrier strike and nuclear deterrent roles.

As of recent years the incumbent has been a full admiral, appointed typically for a term of about three years.
