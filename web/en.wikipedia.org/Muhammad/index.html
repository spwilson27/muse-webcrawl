Muhammad (Arabic: مُحَمَّد, romanized: Muḥammad; c. 570 – 8 June 632 CE) was an Arab religious, social, and political leader and the founder of Islam. According to Islamic doctrine, he was a prophet divinely inspired to preach and confirm the monotheistic teachings of Adam, Abraham, Moses, Jesus, and other prophets. He is believed to be the Seal of the Prophets within Islam, and the Qur'an is held by Muslims to be the final revelation delivered through him.

Life

Born in Mecca into the Banu Hashim clan of the Quraysh tribe, Muhammad was orphaned young and raised first by his grandfather and then his uncle Abu Talib. He worked as a merchant and, at about 25, married the merchant Khadija. Around 610 CE, according to Islamic tradition, he received his first revelation from the angel Gabriel in the cave of Hira and began preaching monotheism in Mecca, where his followers faced persecution. In 622 he emigrated to Medina — the Hijra, which marks the start of the Islamic calendar — and there established a polity uniting the tribes of Arabia. After a series of conflicts with Meccan forces (Badr, Uhud, the Trench), he returned and peacefully took Mecca in 630. By his death in Medina in 632, most of the Arabian Peninsula had embraced Islam.

Legacy

Muhammad's teachings and example (the sunnah, recorded in hadith literature) form, with the Qur'an, the basis of Islamic law and practice for nearly two billion Muslims. Historical study distinguishes between the Muhammad of faith and the reconstruction of historians from early sources such as the Sira of Ibn Ishaq (preserved by Ibn Hisham) and 7th–8th-century non-Muslim references.
