Muhammad ibn Ismail al-Bukhari (810–870 CE), commonly known as Imam al-Bukhari, was a Persian Islamic scholar from Bukhara who compiled the hadith collection known as Sahih al-Bukhari, regarded by Sunni Muslims as the most authentic (sahih) of all hadith compilations.

Life

Born in Bukhara in 810, al-Bukhari began studying hadith as a boy and is said to have memorised tens of thousands of traditions. He travelled widely across the Abbasid world — to Mecca, Medina, Iraq, Syria, Egypt and Khurasan — collecting reports and scrutinising their chains of transmission (isnad) for reliability.

Sahih al-Bukhari

Over some sixteen years, al-Bukhari selected roughly 7,275 traditions (including repetitions; about 2,600 unique) from a reported pool of hundreds of thousands, applying strict criteria for narrator integrity and unbroken transmission. The work is organised into books on topics from purification and prayer to Quranic commentary and history, and forms, with Sahih Muslim, the twin pillars of Sunni hadith literature.

Other works and death

He also wrote Al-Adab al-Mufrad on manners and a biographical dictionary of narrators, Al-Tarikh al-Kabir. Late in life a dispute with the governor of Bukhara led to his exile; he died in the village of Khartank near Samarkand in 870.
