Ramadan (Arabic: رمضان) is the ninth month of the Islamic calendar and the holiest month in Islam. It is observed by Muslims worldwide as a month of fasting (ṣawm), prayer, reflection and community. Fasting during Ramadan is one of the Five Pillars of Islam. According to Islamic tradition, the first verses of the Quran were revealed to Muhammad during this month, on the night known as Laylat al-Qadr (the Night of Decree), which falls within the last ten nights of Ramadan.

Fasting

Physically and mentally capable adult Muslims abstain from food, drink (including water) and sexual relations from dawn (fajr) until sunset (maghrib). The pre-dawn meal is called suḥūr; the evening meal that breaks the fast is ifṭār, often shared with family and community and traditionally begun with dates. Children, the elderly, travellers, pregnant or menstruating women and those who are ill are exempted, though missed fasts may be made up later or, in some cases, replaced by feeding the poor.

Practices and observances

Beyond fasting, Muslims increase prayer — including the special nightly tarāwīḥ prayers — recite the Quran, and give charity (including the obligatory zakāt al-fiṭr before the closing festival). Work and school schedules are adjusted in many Muslim-majority countries, and nights take on a festive character, with markets, lanterns (fanous) and communal meals.

Timing

Because the Islamic calendar is purely lunar, Ramadan lasts 29 or 30 days and migrates through the solar year, beginning about eleven days earlier each Gregorian year; Muslims may thus fast during long summer days or short winter days depending on era and latitude. The month begins and ends with the sighting of the crescent moon (hilāl), a practice that can cause the start date to differ between countries. Ramadan concludes with Eid al-Fitr, one of Islam's two great festivals.
