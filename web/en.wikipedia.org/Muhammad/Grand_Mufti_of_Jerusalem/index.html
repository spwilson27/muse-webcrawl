Grand Mufti of Jerusalem

The Grand Mufti of Jerusalem is the Sunni Muslim cleric in charge of Jerusalem's Islamic holy places, including the Al-Aqsa Mosque and the Dome of the Rock. The title is generally understood to have been created by the British military government in 1918 in Mandatory Palestine, modelled loosely on the Ottoman office of Shaykh al-Islām, and was first held by Kamil al-Husayni.

History of the office

Kamil al-Husayni served from 1918 until his death in 1921, when the British High Commissioner Herbert Samuel appointed his half-brother Mohammad Amin al-Husayni (commonly Haj Amin al-Husseini) to the post. Al-Husayni combined the muftiate with the presidency of the Supreme Muslim Council, giving him control over religious endowments (awqaf) and appointments, and he became the most prominent Palestinian Arab leader of the Mandate era. He led opposition to Zionist immigration, was implicated in the 1929 riots and the 1936–39 Arab revolt, and was exiled by the British in 1937. His wartime residence in Nazi Germany and recruitment for the Waffen-SS made him a deeply controversial figure.

Later officeholders

After 1948, Jordan appointed Husam al-Din Jarallah and later others to the Jerusalem muftiate; following the 1967 war and the 1994 creation of the Palestinian Authority, Yasser Arafat appointed Sheikh Ekrima Sa'id Sabri (1994–2006), followed by Muhammad Ahmad Hussein, who has held the post since 2006. The Grand Mufti issues fatwas, oversees Islamic courts in Jerusalem, and remains a central religious authority for Palestinian Muslims.
