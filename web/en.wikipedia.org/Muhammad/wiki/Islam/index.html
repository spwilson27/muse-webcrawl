Islam is an Abrahamic monotheistic religion centred on the Quran and the teachings of Muhammad, whom Muslims regard as the final prophet of God. With about two billion followers — roughly a quarter of the world's population — Islam is the world's second-largest religion and its fastest-growing major faith. The word Islam means "submission" (to the will of God); a follower is a Muslim, "one who submits".

Beliefs

The core doctrine is tawhid, the absolute oneness of God (Allah), who is merciful, omnipotent, and without partners or equals. Muslims believe God revealed his word through prophets including Adam, Abraham, Moses, and Jesus, culminating in Muhammad, the "Seal of the Prophets", to whom the Quran was revealed over 23 years beginning in 610 CE. The six articles of faith are belief in God, his angels, his revealed books, his prophets, the Day of Judgment, and divine decree. After death comes judgment and the possibility of paradise (Jannah) or hell (Jahannam).

Practices

The Five Pillars frame Muslim practice: the shahada (declaration of faith); salah, ritual prayer five times daily; zakat, obligatory almsgiving (typically 2.5% of savings annually); sawm, fasting from dawn to sunset during Ramadan; and the hajj, pilgrimage to Mecca once in a lifetime for those who are able. Congregational life centres on the mosque, with Friday noon prayer the week's focal gathering. Islamic law (Sharia), derived from the Quran and the Prophet's example (Sunnah, preserved in hadith), addresses worship, family, commerce, and ethics, and is interpreted by schools of jurisprudence (madhhabs).

History

Islam began in Mecca; the Hijrah to Medina in 622 CE founded the first Muslim community. After Muhammad's death in 632, the Rashidun, Umayyad, and Abbasid caliphates expanded Muslim rule from Iberia to Central Asia and built a civilisation that preserved and advanced Greek, Persian, and Indian learning. Disagreement over succession produced the Sunni–Shia divide. Later powers — the Fatimids, Seljuks, Ayyubids, Mamluks, Ottomans, Safavids, and Mughals — carried Islam across Africa, Asia, and southeastern Europe, and trade spread it to West Africa, South Asia, and Southeast Asia; today Indonesia has the largest Muslim population. The 20th and 21st centuries have seen revival movements, debates over Islam and modernity, and Muslim communities established worldwide through migration.

Branches

Sunni Islam, the majority tradition, recognises the first caliphs and the classical legal schools. Shia Islam holds that leadership belonged to Muhammad's cousin and son-in-law Ali and his descendants, the Imams; its largest branch is Twelver Shi'ism, dominant in Iran and Iraq. The Ibadi school, centred in Oman, predates both. Sufism, the mystical dimension, cuts across branches and has produced orders, poetry, and devotional practices influential far beyond Islam.
