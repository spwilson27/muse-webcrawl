Hijra

The Hijra
Arabicالهجرة (al-Hijra)
Date622 CE (traditionally September 622)
RouteMecca to Yathrib (Medina), c. 320 km
SignificanceMarks the founding of the first Muslim community; epoch of the Islamic calendar (1 AH)

The Hijra (Arabic: الهجرة, "migration" or "emigration"), also rendered Hegira, was the journey of the Islamic prophet Muhammad and his followers from Mecca to the city of Yathrib — thereafter known as Medina ("the City", short for Madīnat an-Nabī, "City of the Prophet") — in 622 CE. The event is so foundational in Islam that the Islamic (Hijri) calendar begins with it: the first year is designated 1 AH (Anno Hegirae).

Background

In Mecca, Muhammad and his small community of followers faced escalating hostility and persecution from the Quraysh, the city's dominant tribe. In 620 and 621, delegations from Yathrib, a settlement torn by feuding between the Aws and Khazraj tribes, met Muhammad at nearby Aqaba and pledged their support (the Pledges of al-Aqaba). Over the following months, most Meccan Muslims emigrated to Yathrib in small groups.

The journey

According to Islamic tradition, after learning of a plot to assassinate him, Muhammad slipped out of Mecca with his close companion Abu Bakr. The pair hid for three days in a cave on Mount Thawr south of the city — tradition holds that a spider spun a web across the cave entrance, misleading pursuers — before travelling north by an indirect coastal route with a guide, arriving at Quba on Medina's outskirts roughly eight days later, traditionally on 12 Rabi' al-Awwal (about 24 September 622).

Aftermath

In Medina, Muhammad established the Charter of Medina, a compact binding the emigrants (Muhajirun), the Medinan helpers (Ansar) and the city's Jewish tribes into a single community (umma). He oversaw the building of the Prophet's Mosque and, from this new base, led the community until his death ten years later. The migration is commemorated in the Quran and remains a powerful paradigm in Islamic thought of emigration for the sake of faith.
