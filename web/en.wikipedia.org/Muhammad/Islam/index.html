Islam is the world's second-largest religion.

Islam is an Abrahamic monotheistic religion centred on the Quran, which Muslims believe to be the verbatim word of God (Allah) as revealed to the prophet Muhammad, and on Muhammad's teachings and example (the sunnah, recorded in hadith). Adherents, called Muslims, number about two billion, making Islam the world's second-largest religion.

Beliefs

The central doctrine is absolute monotheism (tawhid): God is one and incomparable. Muslims believe in angels, revealed scriptures (including the Torah, Psalms and Gospel in their original forms), a line of prophets from Adam through Abraham, Moses and Jesus to Muhammad as the final prophet, the Day of Judgment, and divine predestination. Practice is framed by the Five Pillars: shahada (faith), salah (prayer), zakat (almsgiving), sawm (fasting Ramadan), and hajj (pilgrimage).

History

Islam began in Mecca and Medina in the 7th century CE. After Muhammad's death in 632, caliphates expanded Muslim rule across the Middle East, North Africa, Iberia and Central Asia; disputes over succession produced the Sunni–Shia split. Islamic civilisation made major contributions to mathematics, medicine, astronomy, philosophy and art, and Islam spread widely through trade into sub-Saharan Africa and Southeast Asia.

Law and branches

Sharia (Islamic law) is derived from the Quran and sunnah and interpreted through schools of jurisprudence (madhhab). The major branches are Sunni and Shia Islam; Sufism is the tradition's mystical dimension.
