Medina, officially Al-Madinah al-Munawwarah ("The Radiant City"), is a city in the Hejaz region of present-day Saudi Arabia and the second-holiest city in Islam, after Mecca. It was in Medina — then known as Yathrib — that the prophet Muhammad established the first Muslim community after the Hijra of 622 CE, and the city contains his tomb beneath the Green Dome of the Prophet's Mosque.

History

Before Islam, Yathrib was an oasis town inhabited by Arab tribes (notably the Aws and Khazraj) and several Jewish tribes, engaged in agriculture, especially date palms. Following the Hijra, Muhammad united the emigrants from Mecca (the Muhajirun) with the Medinan converts (the Ansar) under the agreement known as the Constitution of Medina. The city served as the capital of the early Muslim community and caliphate through the reigns of the first caliphs, until the Umayyads moved the capital to Damascus. Medina later came under the control of the sharifs of Mecca, then the Ottomans, and in the 20th century was incorporated into the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia.

Religious significance

The Prophet's Mosque (Al-Masjid an-Nabawi), built on the site of Muhammad's house, is one of the largest mosques in the world and a focus of pilgrimage; prayer there is held to be especially meritorious. The city also contains the Quba Mosque, the first mosque built in Islam, and the Baqi cemetery, where many of Muhammad's family and companions are buried. Like Mecca, the central sacred area of Medina is closed to non-Muslims.

Geography and modern city

Medina lies in a fertile basin surrounded by volcanic hills, including Mount Uhud, site of the Battle of Uhud (625 CE). The modern city has a population of well over a million and is served by the Haramain high-speed railway connecting it with Mecca and Jeddah (opened 2018), as well as Prince Mohammad bin Abdulaziz International Airport, both handling millions of pilgrims annually.
