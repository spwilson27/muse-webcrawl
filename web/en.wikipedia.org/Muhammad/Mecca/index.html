Mecca (officially Makkah al-Mukarramah) is a city in the Hejaz region of Saudi Arabia and the holiest city in Islam. It is the birthplace of the prophet Muhammad and the site of the Kaaba, the cubical shrine within the Masjid al-Haram (Grand Mosque) that Muslims face in prayer (qibla) and circumambulate during pilgrimage.

Religious significance

Muslim tradition associates Mecca with Abraham, Hagar and Ishmael, and with the well of Zamzam. Non-Muslims are prohibited from entering the city. Each year millions of pilgrims visit for the Hajj, and many more for the lesser pilgrimage, Umrah.

History

An ancient trading and pilgrimage centre, Mecca was ruled in Muhammad's time by the Quraysh tribe. Muhammad emigrated to Medina in 622 CE (the Hijra) and returned to conquer Mecca in 630. The city later came under caliphates, the Sharifate of Mecca, Ottoman rule, and finally the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia in 1924–25.

Modern city

Modern Mecca's economy revolves around pilgrimage. Vast construction — including the Abraj Al Bait clock tower complex overlooking the Grand Mosque — has transformed the skyline and drawn debate about the loss of historic sites.
