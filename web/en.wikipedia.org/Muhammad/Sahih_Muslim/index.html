Sahih Muslim is a collection of hadith compiled by the Persian scholar Muslim ibn al-Hajjaj (c. 815–875 CE). It is one of the six canonical hadith collections (Kutub al-Sittah) of Sunni Islam and is generally regarded as second in authenticity only to Sahih al-Bukhari; together the two are called "the two Sahihs" (Sahihayn).

Compilation and method

Muslim travelled extensively in Arabia, Iraq, Syria and Egypt collecting traditions, and is said to have selected his material from some 300,000 hadith. Distinctive features include grouping all the chains of transmission (isnad) for a single report together at one place — allowing easy comparison of variants — and a famous methodological introduction explaining his criteria and categories of narrators.

Contents and reception

The collection is arranged into books (kitab) by subject, from faith and purification to marriage, commerce, jihad and Quranic exegesis, and includes some 7,500 narrations with repetitions. Numerous commentaries have been written on it, most famously that of the Syrian scholar al-Nawawi.
