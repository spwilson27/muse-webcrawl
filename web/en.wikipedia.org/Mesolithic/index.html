Mesolithic

Period: c. 15,000 – 5,000 BP (varies by region)
Preceded by: Upper Paleolithic
Followed by: Neolithic

The Mesolithic ("middle stone") is the Old World archaeological period between the Upper Paleolithic and the Neolithic, spanning the transition from the last glacial period to the spread of farming. It began with the warming of the Holocene around 11,700 years ago and ended at different times in different regions as agriculture arrived.

Characteristics

Mesolithic hunter-gatherers adapted to post-glacial forests and coastlines with composite tools hafting small stone blades called microliths, and exploited a broader spectrum of foods — fish, shellfish, waterfowl, and wild plants — than their Paleolithic predecessors. Dugout canoes, fish traps, nets, and the bow and arrow became common. Sites such as Star Carr in England (c. 9300 BC) preserve antler headdresses and wooden platforms, while Scandinavian shell middens (køkkenmøddinger) show intensive coastal settlement.

Regional sequences

In the Near East the equivalent Epipaleolithic cultures, such as the Natufian (c. 15,000–11,500 BP), built the first semi-sedentary villages and harvested wild cereals, presaging domestication. In Europe, cultures include the Maglemosian, Kongemose, and Ertebølle in the north and the Sauveterrian and Castelnovian in the south.
