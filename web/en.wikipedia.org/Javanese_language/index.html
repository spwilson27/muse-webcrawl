Javanese (Basa Jawa) is a Malayo-Polynesian language spoken by the Javanese people, principally in the central and eastern parts of the island of Java in Indonesia, with speakers also in western Java, Suriname, and elsewhere. With about 68 million native speakers, it is the largest Austronesian language by number of native speakers, although it has no official status — Indonesian serves as the national language, and most Javanese are bilingual.

Speech levels

Javanese is famous for its system of speech levels reflecting social hierarchy and politeness. The principal registers are ngoko (informal, used to intimates and inferiors), madya (intermediate), and krama (polite, used to elders and superiors), with honorific vocabulary (krama inggil) raising the addressee and humble vocabulary lowering the speaker. The levels differ extensively in vocabulary, so speakers command large parallel lexicons.

History

Old Javanese, or Kawi, is attested in inscriptions from the 9th century CE and produced a rich court literature, including the Ramayana and Arjunawiwaha kakawin poems. Middle Javanese developed in the Majapahit era; modern Javanese emerged with the rise of the Mataram Sultanate in the late 16th century. Javanese was traditionally written in the Brahmic-derived Javanese script (hanacaraka) and, for Islamic texts, the Arabic-based Pegon; today the Latin alphabet predominates.

Phonology and grammar

Javanese has a typical Austronesian phonology with a contrast between dental and retroflex stops and "heavy" and "light" aspirated consonants. Grammar is agglutinative, with rich derivational affixation and a voice system, and basic word order is subject–verb–object. Regional varieties include the Surakarta–Yogyakarta standard, Banyumasan (often treated as a distinct dialect), and East Javanese (Arek) varieties of Surabaya and Malang.

Status

Although still spoken by tens of millions, Javanese is losing child speakers to Indonesian in urban areas, and full mastery of the krama register is declining. It is taught as a local-content subject in schools in Central and East Java and maintained in media, wayang (shadow-puppet) performance, and literature.
