Pepperoncino

Pepperoncino (plural pepperoncini) is the Italian name for hot chili peppers, and in particular for a sweet, mild variety of Capsicum annuum also known as Tuscan peppers, golden Greek peppers, or friggitello. In Italy, peperoncino generally refers to the small hot chilies especially associated with Calabria.

Culinary use

The peppers are commonly picked while still yellow-green and pickled in brine or vinegar, giving a tangy, mildly spicy flavour (around 100–500 Scoville units for the pickled Greek type). They are served in antipasti, salads, sandwiches, and on pizzas. Calabrian peperoncino, much hotter, flavours dishes such as 'nduja and arrabbiata-style sauces.

Varieties

Types include the friggitello, the Calabrian diavolicchio, and the golden Greek pepper popular in the United States, where pepperoncini are a common pizza topping and salad ingredient.
