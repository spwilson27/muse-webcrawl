Wurundjeri

PeopleWurundjeri Woi Wurrung
RegionMelbourne area, Victoria, Australia
LanguageWoi Wurrung

The Wurundjeri (Wurundjeri Woi Wurrung) are an Aboriginal Australian people of the Woi Wurrung language group, part of the Kulin alliance, whose traditional lands include the area now occupied by the city of Melbourne, Victoria, along the Birrarung (Yarra River) and its tributaries.

Culture and history

Wurundjeri society was organized in clans with custodial responsibilities for particular tracts of land. The name Wurundjeri derives from wurun, the manna gum, and djeri, the witchetty grubs found in association with it. Elders such as Simon Wonga and William Barak were prominent leaders and artists in the 19th century; Barak's drawings and ceremonial work are an important record of Wurundjeri culture.

Colonisation

In 1835 John Batman negotiated a purported "treaty" with Wurundjeri elders, exchanging goods for land around Melbourne; the treaty was repudiated by the colonial government. Colonisation brought dispossession, disease, and violence, and many Wurundjeri were later concentrated at Coranderrk mission station near Healesville. Today the Wurundjeri Woi Wurrung Cultural Heritage Aboriginal Corporation represents the community as a Registered Aboriginal Party under Victorian law, and Wurundjeri elders perform Welcome to Country ceremonies in Melbourne.
