Xenophon

Bust of Xenophon

Xenophon of Athens (c. 430 – c. 354 BC) was a Greek military leader, philosopher, and historian born in Athens. At the age of 30, he was elected commander of one of the biggest Greek mercenary armies of the Achaemenid Empire, the Ten Thousand, that marched on and came close to capturing Babylon in 401 BC. As the military commander of the retreat, Xenophon wrote Anabasis, his most famous work and one of the earliest surviving descriptions of the life of a military commander.

Life

Xenophon was a student of Socrates and an admirer of Sparta. Recruited by his friend Proxenus, he joined the expedition of Cyrus the Younger against his brother Artaxerxes II. After Cyrus was killed at Cunaxa and the Greek generals were treacherously murdered, Xenophon helped lead the Ten Thousand on their famous march to the Black Sea — commemorated in the cry "Thálatta! Thálatta!" ("The sea! The sea!"). Exiled from Athens, he settled on an estate at Scillus near Olympia granted by Sparta, where he wrote most of his works.

Works

Xenophon's writings span history (Hellenica, continuing Thucydides' history; the Anabasis; the Cyropaedia, a fictionalised biography of Cyrus the Great), Socratic works (Memorabilia, Apology, Symposium, Oeconomicus), and treatises on horsemanship, hunting, cavalry command, and Athenian revenues. His clear, plain Attic prose made him a standard model in Greek education for centuries.
