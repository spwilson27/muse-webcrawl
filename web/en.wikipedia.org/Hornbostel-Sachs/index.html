Hornbostel–Sachs

Hornbostel–SachsDevised by: Erich Moritz von Hornbostel and Curt Sachs
Published: 1914
Purpose: classification of musical instruments

Hornbostel–Sachs (or Sachs–Hornbostel) is a system of musical instrument classification devised by Erich Moritz von Hornbostel and Curt Sachs, first published in 1914 in the Zeitschrift für Ethnologie. It remains the most widely used scheme among ethnomusicologists and organologists, modelled on the Dewey Decimal Classification.

Categories

Instruments are classified by what vibrates to produce the sound. The four original classes are: idiophones (the instrument's own body vibrates, e.g. bells, xylophones), membranophones (a stretched membrane vibrates, e.g. drums), chordophones (strings vibrate, e.g. violins, pianos) and aerophones (a body of air vibrates, e.g. flutes, trumpets). A fifth class, electrophones, was added later for instruments producing sound electrically or electronically. Successive digits subdivide each class — for chordophones, into lutes, harps, lyres and zithers — generating hierarchical numeric codes; suffixes record details such as how the instrument is played.

Revisions

The system has been periodically updated, notably by the MIMO (Musical Instrument Museums Online) consortium in 2011, to accommodate new and hybrid instruments while preserving the original numbering.
