Conwy (Welsh pronunciation: [ˈkɔnʊɨ], sometimes Conway in English) is a walled market town, community and the administrative centre of Conwy County Borough in North Wales. The town, which faces Deganwy across the River Conwy, formerly lay in Gwynedd and prior to that in Caernarfonshire. The community, which also includes the neighbouring towns of Deganwy and Llandudno Junction, had a population of 14,753 at the 2011 census.

History

Conwy Castle and the town walls were built, on instruction from Edward I of England, between 1283 and 1289, as part of his conquest of the principality of Wales. Conwy was the original site of Aberconwy Abbey, founded by Llywelyn the Great. Edward and his troops took over the abbey site and moved the monks up the Conwy valley to a new site at Maenan, establishing Maenan Abbey. The parish church of St Mary and All Saints (Church in Wales) is still on the site of the ancient abbey.

The town walls form part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site, the Castles and Town Walls of King Edward in Gwynedd.

Landmarks

Conwy's quayside has been home to Conwy's mussel fishermen for centuries, and they can still be seen today. The smallest house in the United Kingdom, measuring 72 inches across by 10 feet high, stands on the quay and is painted red; it was occupied until 1900. Other notable buildings include Plas Mawr, a well-preserved Elizabethan townhouse, and the Telford suspension bridge over the River Conwy, completed in 1826.

Transport

Conwy is served by the North Wales Coast Line, with a station in the town. The A55 North Wales Expressway passes nearby.
