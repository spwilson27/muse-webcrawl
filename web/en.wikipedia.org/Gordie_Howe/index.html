Gordon Howe (31 March 1928 – 10 June 2016) was a Canadian professional ice hockey player. From 1946 to 1980, he played 26 seasons in the National Hockey League (NHL) and six seasons in the World Hockey Association (WHA), spending most of his career with the Detroit Red Wings. Nicknamed "Mr. Hockey", Howe is considered one of the greatest ice hockey players of all time.

Career

Howe won four Stanley Cups with Detroit, six Hart Memorial Trophies as league MVP, and six Art Ross Trophies as scoring leader. He held the NHL records for goals and points for decades until they were broken by Wayne Gretzky. Remarkably, he played professional hockey across five decades, finishing his career at age 52 alongside his sons Mark and Marty with the Hartford Whalers.

Legacy

The "Gordie Howe hat trick" — a goal, an assist, and a fight in a single game — commemorates his combination of scoring skill and toughness. He was inducted into the Hockey Hall of Fame in 1972, and the Gordie Howe International Bridge between Detroit and Windsor is named in his honour.
