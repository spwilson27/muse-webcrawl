French wine is produced in France, in several regions throughout the country, and France is one of the largest wine producers in the world, rivalled in most years only by Italy and Spain. French wine traces its history to the 6th century BC, with the founding of Massalia (Marseille) by Phocaean Greeks, and has long been central to French culture and gastronomy; in 2010 UNESCO added the French gastronomic meal, with wine at its centre, to its list of intangible cultural heritage.

Many of the world's most influential grape varieties — Cabernet Sauvignon, Chardonnay, Pinot noir, Sauvignon blanc, Syrah — originated in or were popularised by France, and French winemaking concepts such as terroir and the appellation system have been widely imitated.

History

Viticulture spread through Gaul under the Romans. Monasteries preserved and refined winemaking through the Middle Ages. Catastrophes such as the phylloxera epidemic of the late 19th century devastated vineyards and forced replanting on American rootstocks. Fraud scandals led to the creation of the appellation d'origine contrôlée (AOC) system in 1935, administered by the INAO, which codified permitted grape varieties, yields and methods by place.

Appellation system

France classifies wines under the EU framework of protected designations. The hierarchy runs from Vin de France (table wine) through IGP (regional) to AOP/AOC, the most strictly regulated. Famous classification systems include the Bordeaux Wine Official Classification of 1855 for Médoc estates and the grand cru hierarchies of Burgundy and Alsace.

Major regions

  • Bordeaux — Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot blends; Médoc, Saint-Émilion, Pomerol, Sauternes.
  • Burgundy — Pinot noir and Chardonnay from small, meticulously classified vineyards (climats).
  • Champagne — the world's benchmark sparkling wine, made by the traditional method.
  • Loire Valley — Sauvignon blanc, Chenin blanc, Cabernet Franc; "the garden of France".
  • Rhône Valley — Syrah in the north; Grenache-based blends such as Châteauneuf-du-Pape in the south.
  • Alsace — aromatic whites (Riesling, Gewürztraminer) labelled by variety.
  • Languedoc-Roussillon — France's largest vineyard area by volume.
  • Provence — noted for dry rosé.

Styles and grapes

France produces red, white, rosé, sparkling, sweet (including botrytised Sauternes) and fortified wines. Unlike New World practice, most French wines are labelled by place rather than grape variety, reflecting the terroir philosophy that soil, climate and tradition define a wine's character.
