Follicle-stimulating hormone

Follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) is a gonadotropin, a glycoprotein polypeptide hormone secreted by gonadotropic cells of the anterior pituitary gland in response to gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) from the hypothalamus. FSH regulates the development, growth, pubertal maturation and reproductive processes of the body. In women, it stimulates the growth and maturation of ovarian follicles; in men, it acts on Sertoli cells to support spermatogenesis.

Function

In the follicular phase of the menstrual cycle, FSH recruits a cohort of antral follicles and stimulates their granulosa cells to produce oestrogens, including aromatising androgens to estradiol; rising oestrogen and inhibin feed back to modulate FSH release. In males, FSH stimulates Sertoli cells in the seminiferous tubules to support sperm production and to secrete inhibin B, its principal negative feedback signal to the pituitary.

Clinical significance

Serum FSH measurement is used in evaluating infertility, hypogonadism, disorders of puberty and menopause — FSH rises markedly after menopause as ovarian feedback declines. Recombinant FSH preparations (follitropins) are widely used in assisted reproduction to induce controlled ovarian stimulation.
