Amazon Web Services

Amazon Web Services, Inc.
Type: Subsidiary of Amazon
Founded: 2002–2006 (services launched progressively)
Industry: Cloud computing
Key services: EC2, S3, RDS, Lambda

Amazon Web Services, Inc. (AWS) is a subsidiary of Amazon that provides on-demand cloud computing platforms and APIs to individuals, companies and governments, on a metered pay-as-you-go basis. AWS is the world's largest cloud infrastructure provider, with roughly a third of the global market, and for many years generated the majority of Amazon's operating profit.

History and services

AWS grew out of Amazon's internal infrastructure work in the early 2000s. Its landmark launches were the Simple Storage Service (S3) and Elastic Compute Cloud (EC2) in 2006, which let customers rent storage and virtual servers by the hour. The catalogue has since grown to more than 200 services spanning computing, storage, databases (such as RDS and DynamoDB), analytics, machine learning, networking and the Internet of Things, delivered from data-centre clusters grouped into Regions and Availability Zones worldwide. The serverless Lambda service (2014) popularised function-as-a-service computing; Amazon Mechanical Turk is also part of the AWS portfolio.

Significance

AWS underpinned the growth of many startups and large enterprises, hosts major streaming and web services, and provides extensive cloud services to governments. Its scale has drawn scrutiny over outages, market dominance and the energy use of its data centres.
