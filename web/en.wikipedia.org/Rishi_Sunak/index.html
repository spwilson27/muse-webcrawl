Rishi Sunak (born 12 May 1980) is a British politician who served as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom from 2022 to 2024 and Leader of the Conservative Party from 2022 to 2024. He previously served as Chancellor of the Exchequer under Boris Johnson from 2020 to 2022. Sunak was the first British Asian and the first Hindu prime minister of the United Kingdom, and at 42 the youngest person to take the office in more than two centuries.

Early life and career

Sunak was born in Southampton to Punjabi Hindu parents who had emigrated from East Africa; his father was a general practitioner and his mother a pharmacist. He was educated at Winchester College and read philosophy, politics and economics at Lincoln College, Oxford, before earning an MBA from Stanford University as a Fulbright scholar. He worked in finance at Goldman Sachs and the hedge fund The Children's Investment Fund Management, and co-founded an investment firm.

Political career

Elected MP for Richmond (Yorks) in 2015, Sunak rose quickly: he became a junior minister in 2018, Chief Secretary to the Treasury in 2019, and Chancellor in February 2020. Weeks into the job he designed the Coronavirus Job Retention Scheme ("furlough"), which paid the wages of millions during the COVID-19 pandemic. His resignation in July 2022 helped precipitate Johnson's fall. After losing the summer 2022 leadership contest to Liz Truss, he won the uncontested autumn contest following her resignation and became prime minister on 25 October 2022.

Premiership

Sunak pledged to restore economic stability after the turmoil of the Truss mini-budget; inflation fell sharply during his tenure. His government passed the Windsor Framework on post-Brexit trade in Northern Ireland, prioritised "stopping the boats" of small-boat Channel crossings — including the controversial Rwanda asylum plan — and called a general election for July 2024, in which the Conservatives suffered their worst-ever defeat, winning 121 seats. Sunak resigned as prime minister and later as party leader, remaining an MP on the backbenches.
