Representation of a Lie group

Finite-dimensional representations of compact Lie groups are classified by highest weights.

In mathematics and theoretical physics, a representation of a Lie group is a linear action of a Lie group on a vector space, described by a smooth group homomorphism from the group into the general linear group of the space. Representations allow the abstract symmetries encoded by a Lie group to be realised concretely as matrices, and they underpin the classification of elementary particles, whose quantum states transform under representations of symmetry groups such as the rotation group and SU(3).

Lie group versus Lie algebra representations

Differentiating a Lie group representation at the identity yields a representation of the corresponding Lie algebra. For simply connected groups, this correspondence is a bijection: every Lie algebra representation integrates to a group representation. For non-simply connected groups such as SO(3), only those Lie algebra representations satisfying additional integrality conditions descend to the group — which is why the spin representations exist for the double cover SU(2) but not for SO(3) itself.

Compact groups

For compact Lie groups, every finite-dimensional representation is completely reducible, decomposing into irreducible representations, by the Peter–Weyl theory of matrix coefficients. The irreducible representations of a connected compact group are classified by their highest weights, in parallel with the theory for semisimple Lie algebras. Infinite-dimensional unitary representations, central to harmonic analysis and quantum mechanics, are more subtle and are classified only in special cases.
