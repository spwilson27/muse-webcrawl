Ze'ev Jabotinsky

Ze'ev Jabotinsky (born Vladimir Yevgenyevich Zhabotinsky, 1880–1940) was a Russian-born Jewish writer, journalist, and Zionist leader. He founded the Revisionist Zionist movement and was instrumental in creating the Jewish Legion during the First World War.

Died1940, New York, United States

Early life

Born in Odessa, Jabotinsky became a celebrated journalist writing in Russian, Hebrew, and Yiddish, and translated works including Poe's "The Raven" into Hebrew. After the Kishinev pogrom of 1903 he became an active Zionist and advocated of Jewish self-defence.

Zionist activity

During the First World War he campaigned for the formation of Jewish units in the British Army, resulting in the Jewish Legion. In 1923 he broke with the mainstream Zionist Organization and in 1925 founded the Union of Revisionist Zionists, advocating a Jewish state on both banks of the Jordan and mass Jewish immigration. He also founded the Betar youth movement and wrote the influential 1923 essay "The Iron Wall".

Death and legacy

Jabotinsky died of a heart attack in New York in 1940 while visiting a Betar camp. His remains were reinterred on Mount Herzl in Jerusalem in 1964. He remains a foundational figure for the Israeli right.
