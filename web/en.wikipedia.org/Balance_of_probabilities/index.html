Balance of probabilities

The balance of probabilities, also called the preponderance of the evidence, is the standard of proof used in most civil cases in common law legal systems. A party bearing the burden of proof satisfies it by showing that a fact is more likely than not to be true — in informal terms, more than 50 percent probable.

Application

The standard contrasts with the criminal standard of "beyond reasonable doubt", which is far more demanding. In civil litigation — contract, tort, family and employment disputes, for example — the claimant must prove each element of the claim on the balance of probabilities. The United States generally uses the phrase "preponderance of the evidence" for the same standard, while "balance of probabilities" is the customary term in the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia and other Commonwealth jurisdictions.

Nuances

Courts have debated whether especially serious allegations in civil proceedings — such as fraud — require stronger evidence within the same standard. The prevailing view in English law, expressed by Lord Denning and later confirmed by the House of Lords and UK Supreme Court, is that there is a single civil standard, but that more improbable events inherently require more cogent evidence to be found more likely than not.
