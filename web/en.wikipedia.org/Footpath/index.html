Footpath

A footpath is a type of thoroughfare intended for use only by pedestrians, and sometimes by cyclists, but not by other traffic. Footpaths include urban pavements (sidewalks), rural paths across fields, trails through parks and nature reserves, and long-distance hiking routes. The term is particularly common in British English, where "public footpath" has a specific legal meaning as a public right of way on foot.

Rights of way

In England and Wales, public footpaths are recorded on definitive maps maintained by local authorities, and landowners must not obstruct them. The network totals well over 100,000 miles of rights of way. Scotland has a distinct tradition of access rights, codified in the Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2003. Campaigning organisations such as the Ramblers work to protect and promote the network.

Long-distance paths

Many countries maintain waymarked long-distance footpaths, such as Britain's National Trails, the Appalachian Trail in the United States, and pilgrimage routes like the Camino de Santiago in Spain. Urban footpaths and pedestrianised streets form part of wider walking infrastructure intended to encourage active travel.
