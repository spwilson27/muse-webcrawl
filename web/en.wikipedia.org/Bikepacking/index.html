Bikepacking

Bikepacking is a style of self-supported, multi-day cycling that combines bicycle touring with elements of backpacking. Unlike traditional touring with panniers on racks, bikepacking uses soft bags strapped directly to the frame, seatpost, and handlebars, keeping the bicycle light and manoeuvrable enough for singletrack trails, gravel roads, and remote backcountry routes.

Equipment

Typical setups use a frame bag inside the main triangle, a large seat pack, a handlebar roll, and accessory pouches for food and water. Riders carry ultralight camping gear — a bivvy bag or small tent, sleeping bag, and simple cooking equipment — aiming for a total load far lighter than a conventional touring setup.

Routes and racing

Dedicated bikepacking routes include the Great Divide Mountain Bike Route in North America, the Tour Divide race along it, and networks such as the Baja Divide and trans-European trails. Self-supported ultra-distance bikepacking races, in which riders may not receive outside assistance, have become a recognised discipline with events such as the Silk Road Mountain Race.

Popularity

The activity grew rapidly in the 2010s with the spread of gravel and adventure bikes, lightweight outdoor equipment, and online route-sharing communities.
