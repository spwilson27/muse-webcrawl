Special linear group

In mathematics, the special linear group SL(n, F) of degree n over a field F is the set of n × n matrices with determinant 1, with the group operations of ordinary matrix multiplication and matrix inversion. This is the normal subgroup of the general linear group given by the kernel of the determinant map.

Properties

The special linear group is a normal subgroup of the general linear group GL(n, F), and the quotient group GL(n, F) / SL(n, F) is isomorphic to the multiplicative group F× of non-zero elements of the field. When F is the real or complex numbers, SL(n, F) is a Lie group of dimension n2 − 1, and it is connected in the complex case. The corresponding Lie algebra consists of the trace-zero matrices.

Generators

SL(n, F) is generated by transvections (elementary shear matrices), except in a few small cases. Every element of determinant 1 can be reduced to the identity by row operations that do not change the determinant.

Applications

Special linear groups play a central role in group theory, representation theory and geometry: SL(2, R) acts on the upper half-plane by Möbius transformations and underlies the theory of modular forms, while SL(2, C) is the double cover of the Lorentz group and hence appears in relativistic physics.
