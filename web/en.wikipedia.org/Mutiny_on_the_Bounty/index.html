Mutiny on the Bounty

HMS Bounty in the South Pacific

The mutiny on the Bounty was a mutiny aboard the British Royal Navy ship HMS Bounty on 28 April 1789 in the South Pacific. Led by master's mate Fletcher Christian, the mutineers seized control of the ship from Lieutenant William Bligh, who had commanded an expedition to transport breadfruit plants from Tahiti to the West Indies.

Events

After a five-month stay in Tahiti, relations between Bligh and Christian deteriorated sharply on the return voyage. Christian and his followers set Bligh and 18 loyalists adrift in the ship's open launch. In an extraordinary feat of navigation, Bligh sailed the launch some 3,600 nautical miles (about 6,700 km) to Timor without charts, losing only one man.

Fate of the mutineers

Christian's party settled some mutineers at Tahiti — where most were later captured by HMS Pandora and tried in England, three being hanged — while Christian, eight other mutineers, and a group of Tahitians sailed to uncharted Pitcairn Island, burned the Bounty, and founded a settlement whose descendants still live on Pitcairn and Norfolk Island.

Legacy

The story has been retold in numerous books and films, including the 1935 film Mutiny on the Bounty and the 1962 and 1984 adaptations, shaping the popular image of Bligh, Christian, and the South Seas.
