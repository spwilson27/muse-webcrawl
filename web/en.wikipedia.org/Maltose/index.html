Maltose

FormulaC₁₂H₂₂O₁₁
TypeDisaccharide
Also calledMalt sugar

Maltose, also known as malt sugar, is a disaccharide formed from two units of glucose joined by an α(1→4) glycosidic bond. It is a reducing sugar and tastes moderately sweet, with roughly a third to a half the sweetness of sucrose.

Formation and digestion

Maltose is produced when the enzyme amylase breaks down starch, and it is abundant in germinating grains such as malted barley — the source of its name. In the intestine, the enzyme maltase hydrolyses maltose into two glucose molecules that are absorbed into the blood.

Uses

Maltose is used in brewing and baking, and in East Asian cooking as maltose syrup, a traditional sweetener and glaze (for example, on Peking duck).
