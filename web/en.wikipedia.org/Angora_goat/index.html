Angora goat

The Angora goat (Turkish: Ankara keçisi) is a breed of domestic goat named for Ankara, Turkey — historically known as Angora — that produces the lustrous fibre called mohair. Both sexes are horned and covered in long, silky, ringleted locks; the fleece is shorn twice a year, an adult yielding on average some 4–5 kg of mohair annually. Turkey, South Africa, the United States (especially Texas), Lesotho, Argentina and Australia are the main producing regions, with South Africa and Lesotho together accounting for roughly half of world mohair output.

History

Angora goats are among the oldest distinct goat types, kept in central Anatolia for centuries; tradition associates their introduction with the era of Suleiman the Magnificent, and attempts were made to keep the valuable animals from leaving the Ottoman Empire — a ban on exporting live goats held into the 19th century. Breeding stock reached South Africa in 1838 (founding the world's largest industry in the Eastern Cape) and the United States in 1849, where the Edwards Plateau of Texas became the centre of production. Crossing with local goats and selective breeding improved fleece weight and quality in the importing countries.

Characteristics

Angoras are small, delicate goats, considerably less hardy than meat or dairy breeds and vulnerable to cold after shearing and to internal parasites. Their fleece grows about 2 cm a month and must be shorn twice yearly to prevent matting and overheating. Mohair differs from sheep's wool in being smoother, more lustrous, stronger and lacking the scaly surface that causes felting and itching; kid mohair, the first clip, commands the highest prices for fine garments.

Management and industry

The goats browse shrubs and forbs more than they graze grass and thrive on semi-arid rangeland unsuitable for other livestock. World mohair production has declined from its mid-20th-century peak as synthetic fibres displaced natural ones, but demand for fine kid mohair in fashion and upholstery persists, and the industry has adopted responsible-mohair standards covering animal welfare and land management.
