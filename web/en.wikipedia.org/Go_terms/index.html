Go terms

The board game Go, originating in China and developed in Japan, Korea and elsewhere, has a large specialised vocabulary, much of it Japanese. This article lists common Go terms used by English-speaking players.

Terms

atari
The state of a stone or group having only one liberty left, so it can be captured on the next move.
dan / kyu
Ranking grades: kyu ranks count down toward 1-kyu for weaker players; dan ranks count up from 1-dan for stronger amateurs and professionals.
fuseki
The opening phase of the game, in which whole-board strategy dominates.
goban
The Go board, traditionally a 19x19 grid (smaller 13x13 and 9x9 boards are used for teaching).
handicap
Stones given to the weaker player before the game begins, to equalise the contest.
hoshi
The marked "star points" on the board, used as reference and handicap points.
joseki
A standardised sequence of moves in a corner, studied as locally balanced for both players.
ko
A position in which a capture would repeat the previous board position; the rules forbid an immediate recapture, forcing a "ko threat" elsewhere first.
komi
Compensation points given to White for the disadvantage of moving second, commonly 6.5 or 7.5 points.
liberty
An empty point adjacent to a stone or group; a group with no liberties is captured.
miai
Two points of equivalent value: if one player takes one, the opponent takes the other.
moyo
A large framework of potential territory, not yet securely enclosed.
sente / gote
A move that forces a reply (sente, "initiative") versus one that does not (gote).
tenuki
To play elsewhere, ignoring the opponent's last move.
tesuji
A clever, skilful tactical move — the "brilliant move" of Go.
tsumego
Life-and-death problems: composed positions testing whether a group lives or dies.
yose
The endgame phase, when the remaining moves chiefly settle boundaries and count small points.
