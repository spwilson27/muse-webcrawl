Rail transport in Switzerland

A Swiss Federal Railways train in the Alps.

Rail transport in Switzerland is among the densest, most heavily used and most punctual in the world. The network totals about 5,300 km (including some 3,250 km operated by Swiss Federal Railways, SBB) and in normal years Swiss residents travel more kilometres by rail per capita than any other nation. The system is notable for a coordinated clock-face timetable (the Taktfahrplan, introduced in 1982) integrating trains, buses and boats.

Network and infrastructure

Switzerland lies at the crossroads of European north–south transit. The New Railway Link through the Alps (NRLA) project built the Lötschberg (2007), Gotthard (2016, at 57 km the world's longest railway tunnel) and Ceneri (2020) base tunnels to shift transalpine freight from road to rail. Alongside standard-gauge lines (1,435 mm), extensive metre-gauge networks — including the Rhaetian Railway, whose Albula/Bernina lines are a UNESCO World Heritage Site — and rack railways serve the mountains.

Operation

Most services run on standard gauge electrified at 15 kV 16.7 Hz AC. Timetables, fares (including the GA travelcard and half-fare card) and Zurich's pioneering S-Bahn (1990) make public transport comprehensive. Famous scenic and tourist services include the Glacier Express and Bernina Express.
