Francis Jean Marcel Poulenc (1899–1963) was a French composer and pianist, a member of the group known as Les Six. His music combines wit, elegance, and deep lyricism, and he is regarded as one of the finest 20th-century composers of art song (mélodie).

Career

Largely self-taught as a composer, Poulenc gained early notice with the ballet Les biches (1924) for Diaghilev's Ballets Russes. In the 1930s a return to Roman Catholic faith inspired a distinguished body of sacred music, beginning with the Litanies à la Vierge Noire (1936) and including the Mass in G major and the Gloria (1959).

Major works

His works include the operas Les mamelles de Tirésias (1947), Dialogues des Carmélites (1957) — frequently staged worldwide — and the monodrama La voix humaine (1959); the Concerto for Two Pianos (1932) and Organ Concerto (1938); and celebrated song cycles setting poems by Apollinaire, Éluard, and others, often performed with the baritone Pierre Bernac.

Style

Poulenc's music moves fluently between the playful and the profound, drawing on music-hall, Stravinsky, and French tradition. He described himself as a composer of both faith and irreverence, and his harmonic language is tonal, personal, and immediately recognisable.
