Walking taco

Walking taco
Alternative namesTaco in a bag
RegionMidwestern United States
Main ingredientsCorn chips, seasoned ground beef, cheese, lettuce, tomato, salsa, sour cream

The walking taco, also known as a taco in a bag, is a popular snack in the Midwestern United States, particularly at high school concession stands, fairs, and sporting events. It consists of taco-seasoned ground beef and toppings served directly in a single-serving bag of corn chips, typically Fritos, and eaten with a fork while walking.

Preparation

A small bag of corn chips is crushed slightly and opened along the side. Seasoned taco meat is spooned into the bag, followed by shredded cheese, lettuce, diced tomato, salsa, and sour cream. The contents are mixed with a fork and eaten straight from the bag, requiring no plate or table.

History and popularity

The dish is strongly associated with school fundraisers, concession stands, county fairs, and church suppers in states such as Iowa, Minnesota, and the Dakotas. Its portability and low cost made it a staple of community events. Similar preparations exist elsewhere; in New Mexico, the related Frito pie is typically served in the same style.

Variations

Doritos are sometimes substituted for Fritos, giving rise to names such as "walking nachos." Vegetarian versions use seasoned beans or lentils in place of beef.
