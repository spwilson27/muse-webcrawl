The iPod is a discontinued series of portable media players designed and marketed by Apple Inc. First released in 2001 with the slogan "1,000 songs in your pocket," the iPod combined a hard-drive-based player, a scroll-wheel interface, and the iTunes software and music store into an ecosystem that dominated digital music sales and reshaped the recording industry. Apple discontinued the line in May 2022 after selling more than 450 million units.

History and models

The original iPod, developed in under a year under Jon Rubinstein with Tony Fadell leading the project, stored 5 GB on a 1.8-inch hard drive and connected by FireWire. Successive "classic" generations added larger capacities, video, and the touch-sensitive click wheel introduced with the fourth generation (2004). The line expanded into the miniature iPod Mini (2004), the screenless flash-based iPod Shuffle (2005), the video-capable iPod Nano (2005), and the touchscreen iPod Touch (2007), which ran iOS and foreshadowed the iPhone. The iTunes Store, launched in 2003, became the world's largest music retailer.

Decline and legacy

The launch of the iPhone in 2007 began the iPod's long decline, as smartphones absorbed its functions; the iPod Classic was discontinued in 2014 and the Nano and Shuffle in 2017, leaving only the iPod Touch until 2022. The iPod nevertheless remains a landmark of industrial design (by Jonathan Ive's team), of user-interface design, and of the legal digital-download business model, and it revived Apple's fortunes in the years before the iPhone.
