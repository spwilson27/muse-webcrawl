Shore Temple

The Shore Temple is an 8th-century structural stone temple at Mahabalipuram, Tamil Nadu, India.

The Shore Temple (c. 725 CE) is a complex of temples and shrines that overlooks the shore of the Bay of Bengal at Mahabalipuram (Mamallapuram), in the Kancheepuram district of Tamil Nadu, India. Built of cut granite stones under the Pallava king Narasimhavarman II (Rajasimha), it is one of the oldest structural (as opposed to rock-cut) stone temples in South India and a landmark of early Dravidian architecture.

Architecture

The complex comprises three shrines: two tall pyramidal towers (vimanas) dedicated to Shiva — the Rajasimhesvara and Kshatriyasimhesvara shrines — and a smaller shrine between them dedicated to a reclining Vishnu (Anantasayana). The Shiva towers have finely carved walls, lion pilasters and monolithic lion and Nandi sculptures, many eroded by centuries of sea air and spray. A surrounding compound wall, gopura and the remnants of a small port mark the site as a former harbour temple; laterite breakwaters offshore are said to protect the remains of submerged structures associated with the legend of the Seven Pagodas.

Heritage status

The Shore Temple forms part of the Group of Monuments at Mahabalipuram, inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1984. The site survived the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami, which briefly exposed additional submerged structures off the coast.
