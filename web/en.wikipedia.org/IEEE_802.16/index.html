IEEE 802.16

IEEE 802.16 is a series of wireless broadband standards developed by the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers for metropolitan area networks. The standards are commercially promoted under the name WiMAX (Worldwide Interoperability for Microwave Access).

Overview

The original standard, published in 2001, addressed fixed broadband wireless access in the 10–66 GHz range; later amendments such as 802.16e added support for mobility at frequencies below 6 GHz. WiMAX networks were deployed for last-mile broadband and as an early 4G technology, competing with LTE, which ultimately achieved wider adoption.

Features

IEEE 802.16 uses orthogonal frequency-division multiple access (OFDMA), supports quality of service classes for voice and video, and offers adaptive modulation to trade throughput against range.
