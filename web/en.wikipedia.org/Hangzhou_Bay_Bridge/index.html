Hangzhou Bay Bridge

The Hangzhou Bay Bridge is a long highway bridge with cable-stayed sections crossing the mouth of Hangzhou Bay in eastern China. It connects Jiaxing in the north with Ningbo in Zhejiang province in the south, and at 35.7 kilometres was the world's longest trans-oceanic bridge when it opened.

Construction and opening

Construction began in 2003 and the bridge opened to traffic on 1 May 2008. It is a six-lane motorway with a design speed of 100 km/h, built to withstand typhoons and the bay's powerful tides and currents. A service centre with viewing facilities stands on an offshore platform midway across.

Significance

The bridge cut the road journey between Ningbo and Shanghai by about 120 kilometres, strengthening the Yangtze River Delta economy by linking the ports and industrial cities around the bay rather than forcing traffic around its head at Hangzhou.
