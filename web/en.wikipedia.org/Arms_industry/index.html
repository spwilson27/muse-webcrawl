Arms industry

The arms industry, also known as the defence industry or military industry, is a global business that manufactures and sells weapons and military technology. It consists of a commercial industry involved in the research and development, engineering, production and servicing of military material, equipment and facilities. Arms-producing companies, also referred to as arms dealers or defence contractors, produce arms mainly for the armed forces of states, and the sector also supplies the police and private security markets.

Scale and major producers

Global military expenditure exceeds two trillion US dollars annually, and the hundred largest arms-producing companies sell several hundred billion dollars' worth of arms and military services each year. The largest defence contractors include Lockheed Martin, RTX (formerly Raytheon Technologies), Boeing, Northrop Grumman and General Dynamics in the United States, BAE Systems in the United Kingdom, and state-owned conglomerates in China and Russia. The United States, Russia, France, China and Germany are typically the largest exporters of major conventional weapons.

International arms trade

The trade in major weapons between states is monitored by research institutes such as the Stockholm International Peace Research Institute (SIPRI). Arms exports are politically sensitive and are regulated through national licensing systems and international instruments such as the Arms Trade Treaty, adopted by the United Nations in 2013. Critics of the industry raise concerns about corruption, the fuelling of conflicts and the diversion of resources from civilian needs, while governments defend domestic arms production as essential to national security and employment.
