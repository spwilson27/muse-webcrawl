Edward III (13 November 1312 – 21 June 1377) was King of England and Lord of Ireland from January 1327 until his death, a reign of fifty years. He restored royal authority after his father's disastrous reign, began the Hundred Years' War with France, and transformed England into a formidable military power.

Rise to power

Edward succeeded at age fourteen after his father Edward II was deposed and probably murdered. For three years power rested with his mother Isabella and her lover Roger Mortimer. In 1330 Edward seized control in a night coup at Nottingham Castle, had Mortimer executed and his mother confined, and began personal rule.

Wars

Edward invaded Scotland, installing Edward Balliol and defeating the Scots at Halidon Hill (1333). In 1337, claiming the French throne through his mother, he opened the Hundred Years' War; English victories at Sluys (1340), Crécy (1346) and Poitiers (1356), won by the longbow and the tactics of Edward, the Black Prince, made England the dominant military power in Western Europe. The Treaty of Brétigny (1360) gave England vast French territories, though most were lost in the war's later phase.

Domestic legacy

His reign saw the Black Death (1348–1349), the founding of the Order of the Garter (1348), the increasing authority of Parliament in taxation and law, and the early use of English in government. His many children and their descendants later gave rise to the rival houses of Lancaster and York.
