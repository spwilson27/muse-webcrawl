Latin
aequanimitas — "even mind"
In Buddhism

In Stoicism
Related to apatheia

Equanimity is a state of psychological stability and composure that remains undisturbed by the experience of emotions, pain, or other phenomena that may cause others to lose their balance. The word comes from the Latin aequus (equal, even) and animus (mind, spirit).

In religious traditions

In Buddhism, equanimity (upekkhā in Pali) is one of the four sublime states (brahmavihārās), along with loving-kindness, compassion, and sympathetic joy, and is a factor of enlightenment. In Hindu philosophy, equanimity toward pleasure and pain, gain and loss, is praised in the Bhagavad Gita. Stoic philosophy valued a related calm freedom from disturbance, and the Roman poet Horace's dictum aequam memento rebus in arduis servare mentem ("remember to keep an even mind in difficulties") made the concept a classical ideal.

In psychology

Modern psychology treats equanimity as an aspect of emotional regulation and mental health, cultivated in mindfulness-based therapies. It is distinguished from indifference: equanimity involves attentive engagement without being pulled into reactivity.
