Meme


A meme spreads by imitation from mind to mind.

A meme (pronounced meem) is an idea, behaviour, or style that spreads by means of imitation from person to person within a culture and often carries symbolic meaning representing a particular phenomenon or theme. The term was coined by evolutionary biologist Richard Dawkins in his 1976 book The Selfish Gene as an attempted unit of cultural transmission, analogous to the gene: memes, like genes, replicate, mutate, and respond to selective pressures.

Etymology and origin

Dawkins formed the word from the Greek mimēma, "imitated thing," shortened to rhyme with "gene." Examples he offered included tunes, catchphrases, fashions, religious ideas, and the technology of building arches. The study of memes — memetics — applies evolutionary models to cultural information transfer, though its status as a rigorous academic discipline has been debated; the Journal of Memetics ceased publication in 2005.

Transmission and evolution

Memes spread through speech, writing, gesture, ritual, and media. Because copying is imperfect, variation arises; because minds and media are selective, some variants propagate further ("survival of the fittest" in an attention economy). Dawkins later emphasised that memes can be understood as replicators whose success is measured by their own propagation, not necessarily by benefit to their hosts, and commentators have applied the idea to everything from chain letters to advertising slogans.

Internet memes

In popular usage, "meme" now most often denotes an internet meme: a humorous image, video, piece of text, or hashtag that is copied, remixed, and spread rapidly online. Formats such as image macros with superimposed captions, reaction images, and short-form videos evolve through user modification, and platforms such as Reddit, 4chan, Twitter, and TikTok act as major vectors. Internet memes function as in-group signals, political commentary, and marketing assets, and researchers study them as a form of participatory digital folklore.

Criticism

Critics of memetics argue that the meme–gene analogy is loose: cultural transmission lacks a clear copying mechanism comparable to DNA replication, and units of culture are hard to individuate. Defenders reply that the concept remains a useful lens even if the analogy is imperfect, and the word itself has proved to be an exceptionally successful meme.
