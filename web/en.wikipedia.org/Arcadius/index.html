Arcadius

Reign: 383 – 1 May 408
Born: c. 377, Hispania
Died: 1 May 408, Constantinople
Predecessor: Theodosius I
Successor: Theodosius II
Spouse: Aelia Eudoxia
House: Theodosian dynasty

Arcadius (c. 377 – 1 May 408) was Roman emperor from 383 to 408. The elder son of Theodosius I, he was proclaimed Augustus while still a child and in 395 became the first emperor of the Eastern Roman Empire after his father's death divided the empire between Arcadius in the East and his younger brother Honorius in the West.

Reign

Arcadius was dominated by powerful ministers and court figures. The praetorian prefect Rufinus governed during his early reign until his murder in 395, after which the eunuch chamberlain Eutropius rose to power. Eutropius fell in 399, amid the revolt of the Gothic general Gainas, who briefly dominated Constantinople in 400 before being expelled amid anti-Gothic riots in the city.

Relations between the eastern and western courts were frequently strained, especially after the western general Stilicho claimed that Theodosius had made him guardian of both sons. Gothic forces under Alaric raided the Balkans during Arcadius's reign, though the East avoided the catastrophic invasions later suffered by the West.

Marriage and family

In 395 Arcadius married Aelia Eudoxia, a match promoted by Eutropius. Eudoxia became a powerful figure at court and clashed with John Chrysostom, Patriarch of Constantinople, contributing to his exile in 404. She died later that year. The couple's children included Theodosius II, who succeeded his father, and Pulcheria, later a dominant figure in her own right.

Death and legacy

Arcadius died on 1 May 408 and was succeeded by his seven-year-old son Theodosius II. Later historians often portrayed Arcadius as a weak and pious ruler controlled by his court, though modern assessments note the eastern empire's relative stability and prosperity during his reign.
