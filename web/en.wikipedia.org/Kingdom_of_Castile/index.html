Kingdom of Castile

The Kingdom of Castile (Spanish: Reino de Castilla) was a medieval and early modern kingdom in the Iberian Peninsula. It emerged as a county of the Kingdom of León in the 9th century and became an independent kingdom in 1035, when Ferdinand I inherited it and united it with León.

History

Castile expanded southward during the Reconquista, taking Toledo in 1085 and, after the Christian victory at Las Navas de Tolosa (1212), conquering Córdoba (1236) and Seville (1248). In 1230 Ferdinand III permanently united the crowns of Castile and León. The Crown of Castile grew into one of Europe's most powerful monarchies; the marriage of Isabella I of Castile and Ferdinand II of Aragon in 1469 laid the foundations of Spain, and in 1492 Castile completed the conquest of Granada and sponsored Columbus's voyage to the Americas.

Under the Habsburgs and Bourbons the kingdom retained distinct institutions within the composite Spanish monarchy until the Nueva Planta decrees and later reforms of the 18th century integrated its administration.
