Yamaha XG

Yamaha XG (eXtended General MIDI) is an extension of the General MIDI standard developed by Yamaha and introduced in 1994. Where the original General MIDI specification defined a fixed set of 128 instrument sounds and a basic set of controllers, XG dramatically expands the palette: each instrument has multiple variation banks, drum kits are greatly extended, and a rich system of effects and parameter controllers is defined, all while remaining compatible with basic GM playback.

Features

XG defines three levels of compliance: Level 1 requires a minimum of 480 voices/instrument variations and 32-note polyphony; higher levels add more sounds and effects. XG adds:

  • Variation banks selected by bank-select messages, giving variants such as different piano, organ and synth timbres on every program number.
  • Effects processing: reverb, chorus and a multi-effect "variation" block plus insertion effects on flagship modules, with detailed per-channel control.
  • Extended controllers for filter cutoff and resonance, envelope attack and release, vibrato and portamento, allowing expressive shaping absent from the GM specification.
  • Multiple drum kits and SFX kits mapped in dedicated banks.

Products

XG debuted in Yamaha's MU80 tone generator (1994) and was implemented across the MU series of sound modules, the DB50XG and DB60XG daughterboards, SW1000XG PCI card, and many Yamaha keyboards and digital pianos. XG also underpinned Yamaha's software synthesisers (the S-YXG series bundled with PCs and games) and karaoke equipment. Yamaha's main competitor in extended MIDI standards was Roland's GS format, used in the Sound Canvas line.
