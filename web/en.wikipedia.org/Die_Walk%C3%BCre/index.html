Die Walküre

ComposerRichard Wagner
LibrettistRichard Wagner
Premiere26 June 1870, Munich
Part ofDer Ring des Nibelungen

Die Walküre (The Valkyrie), WWV 86B, is the second of the four music dramas that constitute Richard Wagner's Der Ring des Nibelungen. It was first performed at the National Theatre in Munich on 26 June 1870, against Wagner's wishes, at the insistence of his patron, King Ludwig II of Bavaria.

Plot

The opera tells of the twins Siegmund and Sieglinde, separated children of the god Wotan, who fall in love; of Wotan's Valkyrie daughter Brünnhilde, who defies her father to protect Siegmund; and of Siegmund's death at the hands of Hunding. Brünnhilde saves the pregnant Sieglinde and is punished by Wotan — stripped of her divinity and left asleep on a rock ringed by magic fire, in the famous closing scene.

Music

The score is celebrated for the "Ride of the Valkyries" opening the third act, the love music of Act 1, and Wotan's Farewell. Wagner's system of leitmotifs weaves themes associated with characters, objects, and ideas through the drama.

Composition

Wagner completed the music in 1856 but, disillusioned and in exile, put the Ring aside for more than a decade after finishing Act 2 of Siegfried; Die Walküre was finally performed as part of the complete cycle at the first Bayreuth Festival in 1876.
