William Macready

William Charles Macready
Born: 3 March 1793, London
Died: 27 April 1873, Cheltenham
Occupation: Actor

William Charles Macready (3 March 1793 – 27 April 1873) was an English stage actor, regarded in his time as the leading tragedian of the British stage. He restored Shakespeare's original text of King Lear, with the Fool restored, in place of Nahum Tate's altered version, and his management of Covent Garden and Drury Lane raised production standards across the Victorian theatre.

Career

The son of an actor-manager, Macready made his debut in Birmingham in 1810 and his London debut at Covent Garden in 1816. His reputation grew through roles such as Richard III, Hamlet, Macbeth, and Virginius (in Sheridan Knowles's play). As manager of Covent Garden (1837–1839) and Drury Lane (1841–1843), he insisted on ensemble rehearsals, historically careful staging, and full texts.

Rivalry with Forrest and the Astor Place Riot

Macready's rivalry with the American actor Edwin Forrest turned deadly in New York in 1849. During Macready's performance of Macbeth at the Astor Opera House on 10 May 1849, Forrest's working-class supporters rioted outside; the state militia fired on the crowd, killing more than twenty people. Macready finished the performance in dumbshow and left the United States soon after.

Later life

Macready gave his farewell performance at Drury Lane in 1851 and retired to Cheltenham. His diaries, published posthumously, are a celebrated record of Victorian theatrical life. Charles Dickens was a close friend and dedicated Nicholas Nickleby to him.
