Ancient Egyptian language

RegionAncient Egypt
FamilyAfroasiatic
StagesOld, Middle, Late Egyptian; Demotic; Coptic
ScriptsHieroglyphs, Hieratic, Demotic, Coptic

The Egyptian language is an extinct Afroasiatic language spoken in ancient Egypt from the late fourth millennium BCE until at least the 17th century CE, making it one of the longest-attested languages in history. Its final stage, Coptic, survives as the liturgical language of the Coptic Orthodox Church.

Stages and scripts

Scholars divide Egyptian into Old Egyptian, Middle Egyptian (long the classical literary standard), Late Egyptian, Demotic, and Coptic. It was written in the hieroglyphic script for monumental inscriptions, the cursive hieratic for everyday documents, the later Demotic script, and finally the Coptic alphabet adapted from Greek.

Decipherment

Knowledge of Egyptian was lost after antiquity until the decipherment of hieroglyphs in the 1820s, when Jean-François Champollion used the trilingual Rosetta Stone and his knowledge of Coptic to unlock the script. Egyptian grammar is characterized by a consonantal root structure, a verb–subject–object word order in its classical stage, and a rich system of suffix pronouns.
