Preventive healthcare

Preventive healthcare, or prophylaxis, is the application of healthcare measures to prevent diseases, as opposed to curing them or treating their symptoms. It consists of measures taken for disease prevention, as opposed to disease treatment. Just as health comprises a variety of physical and mental states, so do disease and disability, which are affected by environmental factors, genetic predisposition, disease agents, and lifestyle choices.

Vaccination is one of the most widely used forms of preventive healthcare.

Levels of prevention

Primary prevention aims to prevent disease before it occurs, through measures such as vaccination, health education, and changes to risky behaviours. Secondary prevention aims to detect disease early, before symptoms appear, through screening programmes such as mammography, blood pressure checks, and colonoscopy, enabling earlier and more effective treatment. Tertiary prevention aims to reduce the impact of established disease and prevent complications, for example through rehabilitation after a stroke. Some frameworks add primordial prevention, which addresses the underlying social and environmental conditions that give rise to risk factors.

Common interventions

Preventive services commonly include immunisations; screening for cancers, hypertension, diabetes, and depression; counselling on smoking cessation, diet, and physical activity; and preventive medications such as statins and aspirin for people at elevated cardiovascular risk. Bodies such as the U.S. Preventive Services Task Force grade the evidence for preventive services and issue recommendations used by clinicians and insurers.

Effectiveness and debate

Many preventive interventions are highly cost-effective, and some, such as childhood immunisation and tobacco control, save money overall. Others raise questions about overdiagnosis — the detection of abnormalities that would never have caused harm — and about the balance of benefits and harms of population screening. Debate continues over which services should be offered universally and at what ages and intervals.
