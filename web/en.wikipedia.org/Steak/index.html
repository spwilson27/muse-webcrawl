Steak

A steak is a thick cut of meat generally sliced across the muscle fibers, sometimes including a bone. It is normally grilled or fried. Steak can also be cooked in sauce, such as in steak and kidney pie, or minced and formed into patties, such as hamburgers. Although most often associated with beef, steaks can be cut from other animals including pork, lamb, venison, and fish such as salmon and tuna.

Beef cuts

Popular beef steak cuts include the rib eye, sirloin (strip), tenderloin (filet mignon), T-bone and porterhouse, flank, and skirt. Tenderness and flavour depend on the muscle, the amount of intramuscular fat (marbling), and ageing; dry-aged and wet-aged beef develop distinct tastes. Grading systems such as those of the USDA classify beef quality by marbling and maturity.

Cooking

Steaks are typically cooked over high heat to brown the exterior through the Maillard reaction, and served at degrees of doneness from rare to well done. Internal temperatures range roughly from 49 °C (120 °F) for rare to above 71 °C (160 °F) for well done. Resting after cooking allows juices to redistribute. Steak is a centrepiece of cuisines worldwide, from American steakhouses to Argentine asado and Japanese wagyu preparations.
