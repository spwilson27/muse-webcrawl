Safdie brothers

Josh (born 1984) and Benny Safdie (born 1986), American filmmakers from New York City.

Josh Safdie and Benny Safdie are American filmmakers and actors known as the Safdie brothers. Working with their production group, associated with the "mumblecore" generation and the company Elara Pictures, they developed a distinctive style of frenetic, street-level New York cinema shot on film with a mix of professional and non-professional actors.

Career

The brothers grew up in New York, sons of artist Alberto Safdie, and attended Boston University. Their early features The Pleasure of Being Robbed (2008), Daddy Longlegs (2009), and Lenny Cooke (2013) screened at Cannes and other festivals. Heaven Knows What (2014), adapted from Arielle Holmes's memoir of heroin addiction, won acclaim for its raw realism.

Their breakthrough came with Good Time (2017), a one-night crime odyssey starring Robert Pattinson, which premiered in competition at Cannes. Uncut Gems (2019), starring Adam Sandler as a gambling-addicted jeweler in Manhattan's Diamond District, became A24's highest-grossing film at the time and was widely regarded as containing Sandler's finest performance. Benny Safdie also acts, appearing in Licorice Pizza and as physicist Edward Teller in Oppenheimer (2023), and co-created and starred in the series The Curse (2023) with Nathan Fielder. In 2024 the brothers began pursuing solo projects, with Benny directing The Smashing Machine (2025) and Josh directing Marty Supreme (2025).
