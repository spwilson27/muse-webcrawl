National People's Congress

The National People's Congress (NPC) is the highest organ of state power and the national legislature of the People's Republic of China. Under the Constitution of China, the NPC exercises legislative power, amends the constitution, elects and removes top state officials, and decides on major national issues.

Composition

The NPC is composed of deputies elected by the people's congresses of provinces, autonomous regions and municipalities, by the People's Liberation Army, and by the special administrative regions of Hong Kong and Macau; a small number represent Taiwan province. The 14th NPC, elected in 2023, has 2,977 deputies serving five-year terms. Deputies are overwhelmingly members of the Chinese Communist Party or its allied democratic parties.

Sessions and Standing Committee

The full Congress meets in session once a year, usually in March in the Great Hall of the People in Beijing, at the same time as the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference — the two meetings are known jointly as the "Two Sessions". When the NPC is not in session, its permanent organ, the Standing Committee (about 175 members), exercises most legislative functions. The Standing Committee is led by a chairman, who ranks among the top leaders of China.

Powers

The NPC elects the President and Vice President of China, decides on the Premier and other leaders of the State Council, elects the chairperson of the Central Military Commission, and approves the national economic plan and state budget. Although formally supreme, its role in practice is shaped by the leadership of the Communist Party.
