Economic sanctions

Domain: International relations, trade policy

Forms: Embargoes, trade barriers, asset freezes, travel bans

Economic sanctions are commercial and financial penalties applied by states or institutions against states, groups, individuals, or industries. Such measures can include trade embargoes, import and export restrictions, asset freezes, exclusion from financial systems, and arms embargoes. Since the twenty-first century there has been a shift toward sanctions on individuals and entities — so-called "smart" or targeted sanctions — in an attempt to reduce collateral economic damage on the target population.

Purpose and rationale

Sanctions are used to coerce a change in behavior, to punish conduct such as military aggression or human rights abuses, to signal disapproval, or to constrain a target's capabilities, for example by limiting a weapons program. They are often presented as an alternative to military force, sitting between diplomacy and war.

History

Early examples include the Megarian Decree of ancient Athens and Napoleon's Continental System. The League of Nations imposed sanctions on Italy in 1935, and the United Nations Charter empowers the Security Council to order sanctions. Prominent modern cases include the long-standing United States embargo against Cuba, sanctions against apartheid-era South Africa, Iraq in the 1990s, Iran's nuclear program, and the extensive measures imposed on Russia following its 2022 invasion of Ukraine.

Effectiveness and criticism

Scholars debate whether sanctions achieve their stated goals; studies find they succeed in a minority of cases, more often at modest aims. Critics argue that broad sanctions can impoverish civilian populations while entrenching target regimes, while supporters note their signaling value and their role in upholding international norms.
