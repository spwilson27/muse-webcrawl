Guy Fawkes

Guy Fawkes
Born: 13 April 1570, York, England
Died: 31 January 1606, Westminster, London
Known for: Gunpowder Plot

Guy Fawkes (13 April 1570 – 31 January 1606), also known as Guido Fawkes, was an English Catholic conspirator who took part in the Gunpowder Plot of 1605, a failed attempt to assassinate King James I and destroy the Houses of Parliament in protest at the persecution of English Catholics.

The Gunpowder Plot

Fawkes, a soldier who had fought for Catholic Spain in the Low Countries, was recruited by Robert Catesby's group of conspirators and given charge of the explosives because of his military experience. The plotters rented a cellar beneath the House of Lords and stored some 36 barrels of gunpowder there. After an anonymous warning letter reached Lord Monteagle, the authorities searched the cellars and discovered Fawkes guarding the powder on the night of 4–5 November 1605.

Execution and legacy

Fawkes was tortured and, after confessing, tried and convicted of high treason. He was executed by hanging, drawing and quartering on 31 January 1606, though weakened, he jumped from the scaffold and broke his neck. The failure of the plot is commemorated annually on 5 November — Guy Fawkes Night or Bonfire Night — with fireworks and the burning of effigies. The stylised Guy Fawkes mask popularised by the comic and film V for Vendetta has become a symbol of protest movements.
