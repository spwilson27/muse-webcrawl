Antonio Vivaldi

Antonio Vivaldi, the "Red Priest," composer of The Four Seasons

Antonio Lucio Vivaldi (4 March 1678 – 28 July 1741) was an Italian Baroque composer, virtuoso violinist, teacher, and Catholic priest. Regarded as one of the greatest Baroque composers, he is best known for his instrumental concertos—around 500 survive—especially the set of four violin concertos The Four Seasons (1725), among the most popular works in the classical repertoire.

Life

Born in Venice, Vivaldi was taught violin by his father Giovanni Battista, a barber and professional violinist at St Mark's Basilica. Ordained a priest in 1703, he was nicknamed il Prete Rosso ("the Red Priest") for his red hair. For decades he worked at the Ospedale della Pietà, a Venetian home for orphaned girls whose music school and orchestra became famous throughout Europe. He also composed operas, producing dozens of them and working as an impresario in Venice, Mantua, and Rome.

Music

Vivaldi's published collections include L'estro armonico (1711), which strongly influenced Johann Sebastian Bach, and Il cimento dell'armonia e dell'inventione (1725), containing The Four Seasons. He wrote concertos for many instruments, solo and multiple, sacred vocal works such as the Gloria RV 589, and around 46 operas. His music is marked by brisk ritornello forms, brilliant string writing, and vivid programmatic depiction.

Death and rediscovery

Vivaldi's popularity waned late in life; he moved to Vienna seeking patronage and died there in poverty in 1741, receiving a pauper's burial. His music was largely forgotten until a twentieth-century revival, beginning with the recovery of manuscripts in Turin in the 1920s and recordings after the Second World War.
