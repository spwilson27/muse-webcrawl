Chichen Itza

Chichen Itza
LocationYucatán, Mexico
CultureMaya
PeriodLate Classic to Early Postclassic, c. 600–1200 CE
StatusUNESCO World Heritage Site (1988)

Chichen Itza was a large pre-Columbian city built by the Maya people of the Terminal Classic period. The archaeological site is located in Tinúm Municipality, Yucatán State, Mexico. It was one of the largest Maya cities and probably one of the great mythical cities (Tollans) of Mesoamerican literature, with a diverse population including Itza and Putun Maya groups.

Principal structures

The site is dominated by El Castillo (the Temple of Kukulcan), a 30-metre step pyramid whose stairways produce, at the equinoxes, the shadow effect of a serpent descending. Other structures include the Great Ball Court — the largest in Mesoamerica — the Temple of the Warriors, the Caracol observatory, the Ossuary, and the Sacred Cenote, a natural sinkhole into which offerings, including gold, jade and human remains, were cast.

History

Chichen Itza rose to regional dominance in the 9th–10th centuries. Its politics and even its name (the "well of the Itza") relate to the area's cenotes, crucial water sources in the dry northern Yucatán. Political collapse followed in the 13th century, though the site remained a place of pilgrimage. Designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1988, it was voted one of the New7Wonders of the World in 2007 and receives over two million visitors a year.
