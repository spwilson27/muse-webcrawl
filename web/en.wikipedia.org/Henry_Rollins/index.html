Henry Lawrence Garfield (born February 13, 1961), better known as Henry Rollins, is an American singer, writer, spoken-word artist, actor, and former radio host. He is best known as the lead vocalist of the hardcore punk band Black Flag (1981–1986) and of the Rollins Band (1987–2003), and for a long subsequent career of spoken-word tours, publishing, and broadcasting.

Early life

Rollins was born in Washington, D.C., the only child of Iris and Paul Garfield. After his parents divorced, he was raised by his mother in the Glover Park neighbourhood. He was educated at the Bullis School in Potomac, Maryland, an experience he later wrote about extensively.

Music career

A devoted fan of the Washington hardcore scene, Rollins joined the band State of Alert (S.O.A.) in 1980, which released the EP No Policy on Dischord Records. In 1981 he joined the California band Black Flag, moving to Los Angeles and touring relentlessly; albums from his tenure include Damaged (1981), My War (1984) and Slip It In (1984). After Black Flag disbanded in 1986 he formed the Rollins Band, whose albums The End of Silence (1992) and Weight (1994), featuring the song "Liar", brought mainstream success.

Spoken word, writing and media

Rollins founded the publishing company 2.13.61, named for his birth date, which published his books of journals, poetry and essays, including Get in the Van (1994), a memoir of the Black Flag years. His spoken-word albums earned a Grammy Award for Get in the Van in 1995. He has acted in films and television (including Sons of Anarchy), hosted radio shows on Los Angeles station KCRW and the television series The Henry Rollins Show, and written a long-running column for LA Weekly. He is known for political activism and extensive world travel documented in his books and talks.
