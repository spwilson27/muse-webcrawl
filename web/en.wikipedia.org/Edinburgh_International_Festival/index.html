Edinburgh International Festival

The Edinburgh International Festival is an annual festival of the performing arts held in Edinburgh, Scotland, over three weeks each August. Founded in 1947, it presents world-class theatre, opera, classical music, and dance, and is the original and most prestigious of the cluster of festivals that make Edinburgh the world's leading festival city.

A performance at the Usher Hall during the Festival

History

The festival was conceived by Rudolf Bing, general manager of Glyndebourne Opera, together with Harvey Wood of the British Council and civic leaders in Edinburgh, as a way to heal the wounds of the Second World War through culture — "a platform for the flowering of the human spirit." The first festival in August 1947 featured the Vienna Philharmonic, reunited with conductor Bruno Walter, who had fled Nazi persecution. Eight theatre companies, uninvited to the official programme, performed on the margins that same year, giving rise to the Edinburgh Festival Fringe.

Programme

By invitation of the festival director, artists present opera, theatre, orchestral and chamber concerts, recitals, and contemporary dance in venues including the Usher Hall, Festival Theatre, Royal Lyceum, and The Hub. The festival traditionally closes with the Fireworks Concert in Princes Street Gardens, performed by the Scottish Chamber Orchestra beneath Edinburgh Castle. In the 21st century the programme has expanded to include world music, spoken-word events, and large-scale free opening events.

Significance

Together with the Fringe, the Royal Edinburgh Military Tattoo, and the book, film, art, and jazz festivals, the August season draws audiences rivalling those of the Olympic Games or the FIFA World Cup. The International Festival remains its curated centrepiece and a benchmark for arts festivals worldwide.
