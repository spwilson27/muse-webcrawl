Þorrablót

Þorrablót is an Icelandic midwinter festival, named for the month of Þorri of the historical Icelandic calendar, corresponding roughly to mid-January to mid-February. It is commonly celebrated on the Friday between 19 and 25 January, and is associated with traditional foods collectively known as þorramatur.

History

The blót (sacrifice/feast) is mentioned in medieval sagas as a pagan midwinter sacrifice. The modern festival was revived in the mid-19th century by Icelandic students in Copenhagen and spread in Iceland from the 1870s, becoming linked with the independence movement's celebration of national traditions. From the 1960s, restaurants and associations popularised the þorramatur buffet.

Þorramatur

The traditional foods include cured and fermented items suited to old preservation methods: hákarl (fermented shark), svið (singed sheep's head), hrútspungar (rams' testicles, pickled), lundabaggi, blood pudding (blóðmör), liver sausage (lifrarpylsa), dried fish (harðfiskur) and rye bread baked in geothermal heat. The meal is often washed down with brennivín, Icelandic schnapps. See also Icelandic cuisine.
