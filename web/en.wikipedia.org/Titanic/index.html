Titanic

RMS Titanic was a British ocean liner that sank in the North Atlantic Ocean on 15 April 1912 after striking an iceberg during her maiden voyage from Southampton, England, to New York City, United States. Of the estimated 2,224 passengers and crew aboard, more than 1,500 died, making it the deadliest sinking of a single ship up to that time.

The ship

Titanic was the second of three Olympic-class liners built by Harland and Wolff in Belfast for the White Star Line. At 882 feet 9 inches (269.1 m) long and 46,328 gross register tons, she was the largest ship afloat when she entered service. She was widely publicised as "unsinkable" because of her watertight compartments, although she carried lifeboats for only about half of those aboard.

The disaster

On the night of 14 April 1912, lookouts sighted an iceberg directly ahead; despite evasive action, the ship's starboard side struck it, buckling hull plates and flooding five forward compartments — one more than she could survive. She sank in about two hours and forty minutes. The rescue ship Carpathia picked up about 710 survivors.

Legacy

The disaster led to major reforms in maritime safety, including the founding of the SOLAS convention and the International Ice Patrol. The wreck was located in 1985 by Robert Ballard at a depth of about 12,500 feet (3,800 m), and has been the subject of extensive exploration and many films and books, including the 1997 film Titanic.
