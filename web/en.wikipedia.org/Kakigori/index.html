Kakigōri

Kakigōri (かき氷) is a Japanese shaved ice dessert flavoured with syrup and often condensed milk. It is a quintessential summer treat in Japan, sold at festivals, tea houses, speciality shops and convenience stores throughout the warm months.

History

The origins of kakigōri date back to the Heian period (794–1185), when blocks of ice stored in ice houses during winter were shaved and served with sweet syrup to Japanese aristocracy during the summer. The author Sei Shōnagon mentioned the delicacy in The Pillow Book. In the Meiji period of the 19th century, kakigōri became widely available to the public; the first kakigōri shop is said to have opened in Yokohama in 1869. The dessert later spread across Asia and inspired related shaved-ice treats such as Korean bingsu and Filipino halo-halo.

Description

The traditional way of making kakigōri uses a hand-cranked machine to spin a block of ice over a blade, producing light, fluffy shavings. Modern machines are usually electric. Popular syrup flavours include strawberry, lemon, green tea (matcha), melon, Blue Hawaii, sweet plum and grape. Kakigōri is commonly topped with condensed milk, sweetened azuki beans (as in ujikintoki, with matcha syrup), mochi, fruit and sometimes ice cream. On festival stalls the shavings are often served in a cup; in cafés they are mounded in a glass bowl and eaten with a spoon.

Variations

Ujikintoki combines matcha syrup with azuki bean paste and shiratama dango. Shirokuma, a variety from Kagoshima, is topped with colourful fruits and beans so as to resemble a polar bear's face. A simpler children's version is sold in cups at summer festivals, often with multi-coloured syrups.
