Second Bulgarian Empire

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Tsarevets fortress in Veliko Tarnovo, the empire's capital.

The Second Bulgarian Empire (1185–1396) was a medieval Bulgarian state that existed between the 12th and 14th centuries. It arose after a successful uprising against the Byzantine Empire led by the brothers Peter and Asen in 1185, and its capital was Tarnovo (modern Veliko Tarnovo). It is considered the successor of the First Bulgarian Empire.

Under Tsar Ivan Asen II (reigned 1218–1241), Bulgaria reached its greatest territorial extent and cultural flourishing, dominating the Balkan Peninsula. The empire's later decades were marked by feudal fragmentation, Mongol and Ottoman pressure, and internal strife.

History

Foundation

In 1185, the brothers Peter and Asen of Tarnovo led a revolt against Byzantine taxation. After defeating Byzantine armies, their independence was recognized, re-establishing the Bulgarian state after nearly 170 years of Byzantine rule. Their brother Kaloyan (1197–1207) secured recognition from Pope Innocent III and defeated the knights of the Fourth Crusade at the Battle of Adrianople in 1205, capturing the Latin Emperor Baldwin I.

Golden age under Ivan Asen II

Ivan Asen II expanded Bulgaria to the Adriatic, Aegean, and Black Seas after his victory at the Battle of Klokotnitsa in 1230 over Theodore Komnenos Doukas of Epirus. Tarnovo became a major center of trade, culture, and Orthodox Christianity, and in 1235 the Bulgarian Patriarchate was restored with the consent of the other Eastern patriarchs.

Decline and fall

After Ivan Asen II's death, Mongol invasions, peasant unrest (notably the uprising of Ivaylo in the 1270s), and feudal division weakened the empire. In the 14th century Bulgaria split into rival tsardoms at Tarnovo and Vidin. The Ottomans captured Tarnovo in 1393, and the fall of Vidin in 1396 ended the empire, beginning nearly five centuries of Ottoman rule.

Culture

The Second Bulgarian Empire was a golden age of Bulgarian culture. The Tarnovo Literary School, patronized by Tsar Ivan Alexander, produced illuminated manuscripts and translations that influenced Slavic Orthodoxy across the Balkans and Rus'. Architecture flourished with churches in Tarnovo, Mesembria (Nesebar), and Cherven, and Bulgarian coinage and administration were restored to imperial standards.
