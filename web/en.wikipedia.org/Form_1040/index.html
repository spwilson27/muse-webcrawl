Form 1040

Form 1040, U.S. Individual Income Tax Return.

Form 1040, officially the U.S. Individual Income Tax Return, is an Internal Revenue Service (IRS) tax form used for personal federal income tax returns filed by United States taxpayers. The form calculates the total taxable income of the taxpayer and determines how much of that income is taxable, along with any payments, credits and the amount owed or refunded.

History

The first Form 1040 was issued in 1913 following ratification of the Sixteenth Amendment, which authorized a federal income tax. The original form was three pages long. Over the decades the form grew substantially: by 2017 the basic return ran to two pages supported by a large family of schedules and worksheets. The Tax Cuts and Jobs Act prompted a redesign for the 2018 tax year, when the IRS shrank the 1040 to roughly postcard size and replaced the former variants — 1040A and 1040EZ — with a single form supplemented by numbered schedules. A version for seniors, Form 1040-SR, with larger print, was introduced for tax year 2019.

Structure

The form collects filing status, dependents, income from wages, business activity, capital gains, retirement distributions and other sources, then computes adjusted gross income. Deductions (standard or itemized) and nonrefundable credits produce the tax liability; withholding and estimated payments are then applied to determine a refund or balance due. Attachments include Schedules A (itemized deductions), B (interest and dividends), C (business profit or loss), D (capital gains), and many others.

Filing

Most returns are now filed electronically through IRS e-file or commercial software; filing season typically ends on April 15 ("Tax Day"). Nonresident aliens use Form 1040-NR, and amended returns are filed on Form 1040-X. Approximately 150 million individual returns are filed each year.
