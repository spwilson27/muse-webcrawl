UK Singles Chart

The UK Singles Chart is a music chart compiled by the Official Charts Company on behalf of the British music industry, ranking the best-selling and most-streamed singles in the United Kingdom. The chart week now runs from Friday to Thursday, and the Top 40 is broadcast on BBC Radio 1.

History

The first singles chart in Britain was published by the New Musical Express (NME) on 14 November 1952, based on a telephone poll of record shops; the first number one was "Here in My Heart" by Al Martino. Rival charts published by Melody Maker and Record Retailer competed through the 1960s; the Record Retailer chart, adopted by the BBC, is now treated as canonical for that decade. From 1969 the chart was compiled for the British Market Research Bureau, later by Gallup (1983–1994), and since 1994 by the organisation now known as the Official Charts Company.

Methodology

The chart originally counted only physical single sales. Paid downloads were incorporated from 2005 and became eligible in their own right in 2007. Streaming was added in July 2014, initially at a ratio of 100 streams to one sale; since 2017 the ratio has differed between premium (100:1) and free (600:1) streams, with an "accelerated chart ratio" applying to tracks in decline. The chart currently publishes a Top 100.

Records

The Beatles have the most UK number-one singles by a group (17), while Elvis Presley leads solo artists (21). The longest-running number one is "One Moment in Time"-era records aside — Bryan Adams's "(Everything I Do) I Do It for You" spent 16 consecutive weeks at number one in 1991. The best-selling single in UK chart history is Elton John's "Candle in the Wind 1997", released after the death of Diana, Princess of Wales.
