The 1947–1949 Palestine war was a war fought in the territory of Palestine under the British Mandate. It is generally divided into two phases: the civil war in Mandatory Palestine (1947–48) between Jewish and Palestinian Arab forces following the adoption of the United Nations Partition Plan on 29 November 1947, and the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, which began after the establishment of the State of Israel on 14 May 1948 and the intervention of neighbouring Arab states.

Course of the war

The civil war phase saw escalating communal violence, the Haganah's offensives under Plan Dalet in spring 1948, and the capture of major mixed cities including Haifa, Jaffa, and Safed. After Israel's declaration of independence, armies from Egypt, Transjordan, Syria, Lebanon, and Iraq invaded. Israel, after initial setbacks, went on the offensive in operations that extended its control beyond the partition borders.

Outcome

The 1949 Armistice Agreements left Israel in control of about 78 percent of the former Mandate territory. Jordan annexed the West Bank and Egypt administered the Gaza Strip. More than 700,000 Palestinian Arabs fled or were expelled from their homes — an event known in Arabic as the Nakba — and were not permitted to return.
