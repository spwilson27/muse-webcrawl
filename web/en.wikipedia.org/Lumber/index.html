Lumber

Lumber
Also called: Timber (outside North America, for some uses)
Produced from: Logs sawn and processed at sawmills
Types: Softwood (pine, spruce, fir) and hardwood (oak, maple)
Uses: Construction framing, furniture, flooring, paper pulp
[Image placeholder: Stacks of sawn softwood boards drying at a sawmill]

Lumber (known as timber in some other English-speaking regions) is wood that has been processed into beams and planks. It is produced by sawing logs lengthwise at a sawmill, then planing, grading and drying the pieces to standard dimensions. Lumber is a stage in the production of wood products, distinct from the trees or felled logs it comes from.

Types

Softwood lumber, from conifers such as pine, spruce and Douglas fir, supplies most construction framing — sold in nominal sizes such as the "two-by-four", which in fact measures 1.5 by 3.5 inches (38 by 89 mm) after drying and planing. Hardwood lumber, from broadleaf trees such as oak, maple and walnut, is used for flooring, furniture and cabinetry and is graded by the proportion of clear, defect-free cuttings a board yields.

Processing and standards

Freshly sawn "green" lumber is kiln- or air-dried to reduce warping and decay, then graded for strength and appearance under national standards. Engineered wood products — plywood, glulam, laminated veneer lumber and cross-laminated timber — extend what lumber can do and increasingly substitute for large solid timbers.
