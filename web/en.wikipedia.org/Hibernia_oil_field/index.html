The Hibernia platform

Hibernia is an oil field in the North Atlantic Ocean, approximately 315 km (196 mi) east-southeast of St. John's, Newfoundland, Canada, in shallow waters of 80 m depth on the Grand Banks. It was the first major offshore oil development in Canada and one of the largest offshore projects in the world.

History

The field was discovered in 1979 by the drillship Sedco 445 for a consortium led by Mobil. Development was delayed by low oil prices, disputes between the federal and Newfoundland governments over resource ownership, and the 1982 Ocean Ranger disaster, which sank a drilling rig in the area with the loss of 84 lives. The Atlantic Accord of 1985 settled jurisdiction, and construction went ahead in the 1990s.

The platform

Production is from a massive gravity base structure (GBS) — a concrete caisson 111 m high resting on the seabed, designed to withstand impact from a 6-million-tonne iceberg, with storage for 1.3 million barrels of crude. The topsides, built at Bull Arm, Newfoundland, were mated with the GBS in 1997 before tow-out. First oil flowed on 17 November 1997, ahead of schedule. Crude is loaded to shuttle tankers for transport to the Newfoundland Transshipment Terminal at Whiffen Head.

Production

Peak production exceeded 200,000 barrels per day. Subsea developments such as Hibernia South Extension have prolonged the field's life well beyond original plans, and the project has been a mainstay of Newfoundland and Labrador's economy and government revenues.
