Joseph Radetzky von Radetz

Born: November 2, 1766, Třebnice, Bohemia, Holy Roman Empire
Died: January 5, 1858 (aged 91), Milan, Lombardy–Venetia
Allegiance: Habsburg monarchy; Austrian Empire
Rank: Field marshal
Battles: Napoleonic Wars; First Italian War of Independence

Johann Graf Radetzky von Radetz (2 November 1766 – 5 January 1858) was a Czech nobleman and Austrian field marshal. He served in the Austrian army for over 70 years, fought against Napoleon, and is best remembered for his victories in Italy during the Revolutions of 1848, which made him a symbol of Habsburg loyalism. Johann Strauss Sr. composed the famous Radetzky March in his honour.

Early career

Radetzky was born at Třebnice in Bohemia and entered the Austrian army as a cadet in 1784. He fought in the Austro-Turkish War and in the campaigns against Revolutionary France in the Low Countries and on the Rhine. At the Battle of Marengo (1800) he distinguished himself in the rearguard, and by 1805 he had risen to major general.

Napoleonic Wars

Radetzky served as chief of staff of the Army of Inner Austria under Archduke Charles in 1809 and was wounded at Wagram. As chief of staff to Prince Schwarzenberg in 1813–1814 he was deeply involved in planning the allied campaigns that led to the victory at Leipzig and the invasion of France. After the war he held a series of commands, though his advocacy of military reform won him enemies at court, and in 1831 he was given command of the Austrian army in Italy, a post some considered a sidelining.

Italy and 1848

In Italy, Radetzky trained his army rigorously and became a beloved father figure to his soldiers. When revolution broke out in March 1848 and Milan rose in the Five Days, the 81-year-old marshal withdrew his outnumbered troops to the Quadrilatero fortresses. Reinforced, he defeated the Piedmontese army of Charles Albert at Custoza in July 1848 and crushed it again at Novara in March 1849, restoring Austrian rule in Lombardy–Venetia. He then served as governor-general of the kingdom until 1857.

Legacy

Radetzky died in Milan in 1858, aged 91, still on active service. Revered in Austria as a loyal soldier of the old order, he is commemorated by the Radetzky March, played annually at the Vienna New Year's Concert, and by monuments in Vienna and Prague. Joseph Roth's novel The Radetzky March (1932) made his name emblematic of the vanished Habsburg world.
