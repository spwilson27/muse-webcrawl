67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko (often shortened to 67P or C-G) is a Jupiter-family comet originally from the Kuiper belt. It became famous as the target of the European Space Agency's Rosetta mission, which orbited the comet and deployed the Philae lander in 2014 — the first soft landing on a comet nucleus.

Discovery and orbit

The comet was discovered in 1969 by Soviet astronomers Klim Ivanovich Churyumov and Svetlana Ivanovna Gerasimenko on photographic plates taken in Almaty. Close encounters with Jupiter in 1840 and 1959 dramatically reduced its perihelion distance, bringing it into the inner Solar System.

Rosetta mission

Rosetta arrived at 67P in August 2014 after a ten-year journey, revealing a nucleus shaped like a rubber duck, with two lobes joined by a narrow neck — probably two comets that merged in a gentle collision. On 12 November 2014 the Philae lander touched down, bouncing before coming to rest in the shadow of a cliff; it briefly reawakened in 2015. Rosetta ended its mission with a controlled impact on the comet on 30 September 2016.

Findings

Measurements showed the comet's water has a deuterium-to-hydrogen ratio about three times that of Earth's oceans, suggesting Jupiter-family comets delivered less of Earth's water than once thought. Rosetta also detected molecular oxygen and the amino acid glycine in the coma.
