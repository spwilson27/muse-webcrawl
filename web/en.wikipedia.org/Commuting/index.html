Commuting
Definition: regular travel between home and work or study
Modes: car, train, bus, bicycle, walking
Average one-way time (US): roughly 27 minutes

Commuting is the process of travelling between one's home and place of work or study on a regular basis. People who do so are called commuters. The word derives from the "commutation tickets"—reduced-fare season tickets—sold by early railways.

History

Before mechanised transport most people worked at or near home. Steam railways and streetcars created the first suburbs and the modern commute in the 19th century; the private car generalised the pattern in the 20th, enabling sprawling metropolitan labour markets.

Patterns and impacts

Commutes concentrate into morning and evening rush hours, generating congestion and pollution. Longer commutes are associated in studies with stress, poorer health and lower life satisfaction. Reverse commuting, telecommuting and flexible hours have modified traditional patterns, and remote work expanded sharply after 2020.

Extreme commuting

"Super-commuters" travel very long distances, sometimes between cities, by train or air. Census bureaus in several countries record rising shares of workers commuting over an hour each way.
