The American Recovery and Reinvestment Act of 2009 (ARRA), commonly known as the Stimulus or the Recovery Act, was a stimulus package enacted by the 111th U.S. Congress and signed into law by President Barack Obama in February 2009. Developed in response to the Great Recession, the Act's primary objective was to save and create jobs and to spur economic activity and invest in long-term growth.

Provisions

The package combined tax cuts, expansion of unemployment benefits and other social welfare provisions, and domestic spending on education, health care, infrastructure, and the energy sector. It included the Making Work Pay tax credit, funding for "shovel-ready" infrastructure projects, investment in renewable energy and high-speed rail, and aid to state governments to prevent layoffs of teachers and public employees.

Impact and debate

The Congressional Budget Office estimated the Act increased employment and output relative to what would have occurred otherwise, though its precise effect remained a subject of political and economic debate. The Act also imposed new transparency requirements, tracked through the Recovery.gov website.
