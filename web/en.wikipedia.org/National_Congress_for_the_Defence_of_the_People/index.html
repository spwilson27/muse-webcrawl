National Congress for the Defence of the People

The National Congress for the Defence of the People (French: Congrès national pour la défense du peuple, CNDP) was a Congolese rebel group and later political party led by Laurent Nkunda, active in the Nord-Kivu province of the eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo from 2006 to 2009, during the Kivu conflict.

History

Nkunda, a former officer of the Rwandan-backed RCD-Goma and a general in the Congolese army, launched his rebellion in 2006 claiming to protect the Congolese Tutsi (Banyamulenge and related communities) against the FDLR, the Hutu militia descended from perpetrators of the 1994 Rwandan genocide. The CNDP fought the Congolese army (FARDC) and UN peacekeepers (MONUC), displacing hundreds of thousands of civilians, and in late 2008 advanced to the outskirts of Goma, provoking an international crisis.

Dissolution

In January 2009, after a deal between Kinshasa and Kigali, Nkunda was arrested in Rwanda by his own deputy Bosco Ntaganda, who announced the CNDP's integration into the Congolese army under a peace agreement signed on 23 March 2009. The CNDP became a political party, but many of its ex-members later mutinied in 2012 to form the M23 movement, citing the government's failure to implement the March 23 agreement.
