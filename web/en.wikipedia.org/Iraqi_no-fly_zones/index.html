Iraqi no-fly zones

The Iraqi no-fly zones were air exclusion zones proclaimed by the United States, United Kingdom, and France after the 1991 Gulf War and maintained until the 2003 invasion of Iraq. Operation Northern Watch protected the Kurdish population north of the 36th parallel, while Operation Southern Watch policed the south, protecting Shia populations from Saddam Hussein's forces.

Controversy and enforcement

The zones were justified by their sponsors by reference to UN Security Council Resolution 688, though the resolution did not explicitly authorize force, and their legality was disputed. Coalition aircraft patrolling the zones regularly struck Iraqi air defenses that engaged them, in an extended low-level air campaign sometimes described as the longest continuous U.S. combat operation since Vietnam.
