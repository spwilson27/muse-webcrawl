Svalbard Treaty

The Svalbard Treaty (Norwegian: Svalbardtraktaten), originally the Spitsbergen Treaty, is a treaty signed on 9 February 1920 in Paris that recognises the full and absolute sovereignty of Norway over the Arctic archipelago of Svalbard, while granting the nationals and companies of all signatory states equal rights of economic activity on the islands and in their territorial waters.

Background

Before the First World War, Svalbard was regarded as terra nullius — land belonging to no state — although it had been exploited for whaling, hunting and coal mining by several nations. Attempts to establish an international administration before 1914 failed. The question was taken up at the Paris Peace Conference in 1919, where Norway's claim was accepted, partly in recognition of Norwegian interests and activity in the archipelago.

Terms

The treaty grants Norway "full and absolute sovereignty" over Svalbard, subject to significant conditions. Nationals and companies of all parties enjoy equal rights of fishing, hunting, mining and other economic activity on land and in territorial waters. Norway may not levy taxes beyond what is required for the administration of the islands themselves, and the archipelago must not be used for "warlike purposes", making it a partially demilitarised zone. Norway is also obliged to preserve the natural environment of Svalbard.

Later disputes

Disagreement persists over whether the treaty's equal-rights provisions extend to the continental shelf and the 200-nautical-mile zone around Svalbard. Norway maintains that the shelf is subject to the ordinary rules of the law of the sea under Norwegian sovereignty, while some other parties argue that the treaty's non-discrimination principles should apply there too. The Soviet Union, and later Russia, maintained a mining settlement at Barentsburg under the treaty's terms, and the state mining company Trust Arktikugol operated on the islands for decades.

The treaty entered into force in 1925, when Norway formally took possession of the archipelago and the name was officially changed from Spitsbergen to Svalbard.
