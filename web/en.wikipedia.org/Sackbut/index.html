Sackbut

The sackbut is a brass instrument of the Renaissance and Baroque eras, the direct ancestor of the modern trombone. Its name derives from the French sacquer ("to pull") and bouter ("to push"), describing the action of its slide.

Construction

Compared with a modern trombone, a sackbut has a narrower bore, a smaller and less flared bell, and is built from thinner metal, giving it a softer, more vocal tone suited to consorts with voices and viols. It was made in alto, tenor, bass and great-bass sizes.

History

The instrument appeared in the late 15th century, probably in Burgundy, and was used across Europe in church, court and civic music — by composers such as Gabrieli, Schütz, Monteverdi and Praetorius. By the 18th century it had evolved into the trombone, and the sackbut itself was revived in the 20th century for historically informed performance.
