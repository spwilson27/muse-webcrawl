Famine in Yemen

The famine in Yemen is a humanitarian crisis that began with the escalation of the Yemeni Civil War in March 2015. The conflict between the Houthi movement and the internationally recognised government, backed by a Saudi-led coalition, combined with an air and sea blockade, the collapse of the economy, and the destruction of infrastructure to produce what the United Nations has repeatedly called the world's worst humanitarian crisis.

Scale

UN agencies have estimated that tens of millions of Yemenis—at times more than 20 million people—have faced acute food insecurity, with millions on the edge of famine (IPC Phase 5 pockets reported in several districts in 2018 and again in later years). Malnutrition among children under five has been among the highest recorded globally, and the war coincided with the largest documented cholera outbreak in modern history, with over two million suspected cases from 2016.

Causes

Key drivers include:

  • Fighting and the destruction of farms, markets, ports and water systems
  • Restrictions on imports through the port of Hudaydah, through which most of Yemen's food and fuel entered
  • The collapse of public salaries and the central bank split, destroying household purchasing power
  • Displacement of millions of people within the country

Response

The UN-led humanitarian operation in Yemen has been one of the largest in the world, though chronically underfunded. A nationwide truce beginning in April 2022 eased fighting and improved access, but food insecurity remained severe.
