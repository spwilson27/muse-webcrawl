Open water swimming

Swimmers racing in open water. Marathon swimming over 10 km is an Olympic discipline.

Open water swimming is a swimming discipline which takes place in outdoor bodies of water such as open oceans, lakes, and rivers. The beginning of the modern age of open water swimming is sometimes taken to be 3 May 1810, when Lord Byron swam several miles to cross the Hellespont (now known as the Dardanelles) in Turkey.

History

Long-distance swims captured public imagination in the nineteenth century, notably Matthew Webb's first successful crossing of the English Channel in 1875. Channel swimming became a popular endurance challenge, and marathon races were held in lakes and rivers around the world.

Competitions

The first Olympic open water event was the men's and women's 10 km marathon swim at the 2008 Beijing Olympics. FINA (World Aquatics) World Championships include 5 km, 10 km, and 25 km events. Open water swimming also forms the swim leg of most triathlons.

Techniques and hazards

Unlike pool swimming, open water swimming has no lane lines, so competitors must "sight" on landmarks to navigate. Swimmers often draft behind or beside others to conserve energy. Hazards include cold water and hypothermia, currents and tides, marine life such as jellyfish, and boat traffic. Wetsuits may be permitted or required depending on water temperature and governing-body rules.
