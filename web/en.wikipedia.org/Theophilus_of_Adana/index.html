Theophilus of Adana

Theophilus of Adana (died c. 538) is a figure of Christian legend, venerated as a saint in some traditions, who is said to have made a pact with the Devil to regain his ecclesiastical position. His story is the earliest recorded example of the "deal with the Devil" motif in Western literature, and a forerunner of the Faust legend.

Legend

According to the tale, Theophilus was an archdeacon of Adana in Cilicia (in modern Turkey) who was unjustly deposed by his bishop. Through a sorcerer he summoned the Devil and signed a contract renouncing Christ and the Virgin Mary in exchange for restoration to office. Stricken with remorse, Theophilus prayed and fasted before an icon of the Virgin, who interceded for him, retrieved the contract from Hell and returned it. Theophilus publicly confessed, was forgiven, and died in a state of grace.

Influence

The legend was widely retold in the Middle Ages — in Greek, Latin and vernacular versions, including a miracle play by Hrotsvitha and a poem by Gautier de Coinci — and appears frequently in Gothic sculpture and stained glass. See also Faust and Deal with the Devil.
