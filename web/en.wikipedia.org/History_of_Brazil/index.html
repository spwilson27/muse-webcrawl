The history of Brazil begins with Indigenous peoples who inhabited the land for thousands of years before the Portuguese fleet of Pedro Álvares Cabral reached its coast in 1500. Colonised by Portugal for over three centuries, Brazil became the seat of the Portuguese empire in 1808, an independent empire in 1822, and a republic in 1889.

Colonial period

Early colonisation ran on brazilwood, then sugar plantations worked first by enslaved Indigenous people and then by enslaved Africans — Brazil received more enslaved Africans than any other country in the Americas, some 4.8 million. Gold and diamonds in Minas Gerais in the 1690s pulled settlement inland; bandeirantes from São Paulo expanded the effective frontier far beyond the Treaty of Tordesillas line.

Empire and abolition

Fleeing Napoleon, the Portuguese court moved to Rio de Janeiro in 1808. In 1822 Prince Pedro declared independence ("Ipiranga cry") and became Emperor Pedro I. His son Pedro II reigned 1840–1889, a period of stability, coffee wealth and gradual abolition, completed by the Golden Law of 1888 — the last abolition in the Americas. A military coup ended the monarchy the next year.

Republic, Vargas and dictatorship

The Old Republic (1889–1930) was dominated by coffee oligarchies. Getúlio Vargas (1930–45, 1951–54) industrialised and reshaped labour law. After democratic years that built Brasília (1960), a 1964 coup brought a military dictatorship lasting until 1985, marked by repression and an "economic miracle" followed by debt crisis.

New Republic

The 1988 "Citizen Constitution" restored democracy. Fernando Henrique Cardoso's Real Plan tamed hyperinflation (1994); Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva (2003–2010) combined growth with expanded social programmes. Later years saw recession, the impeachment of Dilma Rousseff (2016), the presidency of Jair Bolsonaro (2019–2022), and Lula's return in 2023.
