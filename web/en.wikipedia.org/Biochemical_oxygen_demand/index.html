Biochemical oxygen demand

Biochemical oxygen demand (BOD) is the amount of dissolved oxygen needed (i.e. demanded) by aerobic biological organisms to break down the organic material present in a given water sample at a certain temperature over a specific time period. The BOD value is most commonly expressed in milligrams of oxygen consumed per litre of sample during 5 days of incubation at 20 °C (BOD5) and is often used as a surrogate measure of the degree of organic pollution of water.

Background

Most natural waters contain small quantities of organic compounds. Aquatic microorganisms use the dissolved oxygen in the water to metabolise these compounds, so higher organic loads lead to greater oxygen depletion, which can suffocate fish and other aquatic life. Measuring BOD helps regulators set and enforce discharge limits for wastewater treatment plants.

Typical values

Pristine rivers have a 5-day BOD below 1 mg/L; moderately polluted rivers range from 2 to 8 mg/L. Untreated sewage typically has BOD values of 100–400 mg/L, while treated effluent from modern plants is often below 20 mg/L. The test was standardised in the United Kingdom following the work of the Royal Commission on Sewage Disposal in the early 20th century. A related but faster measurement is chemical oxygen demand (COD), which chemically oxidises both organic and inorganic matter.
