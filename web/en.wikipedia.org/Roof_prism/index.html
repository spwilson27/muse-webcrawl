Roof prism

A roof prism is a reflective optical prism containing a section where two faces meet at a 90° angle, resembling the roof of a building. Reflection from the two roof faces reverses the image laterally across the roof axis, which allows the prism to erect an image and bend the light path while remaining compact.

Types

Common roof prisms include the Amici roof prism (a right-angle prism with a roof in place of the hypotenuse), the Schmidt–Pechan prism, the Abbe–Koenig prism, the Dach prism, and the roof pentaprism used in single-lens reflex cameras to present an upright, correctly oriented image in the viewfinder.

Applications

Roof prisms are widely used in binoculars, where their in-line optical path permits a slim, straight-barrel design, as well as in spotting scopes, rangefinders, and camera viewfinders. Because the roof edge must be manufactured to very tight tolerances and the two halves of the split beam must be phase-matched, high-quality roof prisms often use phase-correction coatings to preserve contrast.
