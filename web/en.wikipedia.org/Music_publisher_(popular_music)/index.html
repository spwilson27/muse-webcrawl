Music publisher (popular music)

Sheet music, historically the core product of music publishers

In popular music, a music publisher is a company that owns or administers the copyright in musical compositions (the songs themselves, as distinct from sound recordings), promotes them to recording artists and media, licenses their use, and collects royalties on behalf of songwriters.

Role

Publishers secure placements of songs with recording artists, license compositions for film, television, advertising, and games (synchronisation), arrange covers and translations, and register works with collecting societies such as ASCAP, BMI, and PRS. In a typical publishing deal, the publisher takes a share of the composition royalties — traditionally around half of publishing income in older deals, though modern deals vary widely.

History

Music publishing grew out of the sheet music trade of the 19th century; New York's Tin Pan Alley publishers dominated early popular song. As recordings replaced sheet music in importance, publishers' role shifted to licensing and royalty administration. The largest contemporary publishers include Universal Music Publishing Group, Sony Music Publishing, and Warner Chappell Music.
