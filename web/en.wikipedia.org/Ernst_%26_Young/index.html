Ernst & Young

Type: Network of member firms (EYGM Limited)
Founded: 1989 (merger; roots from 1849)
Headquarters: London, England
Services: Assurance, tax, consulting, strategy and transactions

Ernst & Young Global Limited, trade name EY, is a British multinational professional services partnership. EY is one of the largest professional services networks in the world and one of the "Big Four" accounting firms, alongside Deloitte, KPMG, and PwC.

History

The firm was formed in 1989 by the merger of Ernst & Whinney and Arthur Young & Co., whose founding firms date back to the nineteenth century; Alwin C. Ernst founded Ernst & Ernst in Cleveland in 1903 and Arthur Young founded his firm in Chicago in 1906. The network rebranded from Ernst & Young to EY in 2013.

Operations

EY is organised as a network of legally separate member firms employing roughly 400,000 people in more than 150 countries. Its service lines cover assurance (audit), tax, consulting, and strategy and transactions. In 2022–2023 EY pursued and then abandoned "Project Everest", a plan to spin off its consulting arm into a separate public company.

Criticism

EY has faced scrutiny over its audits of failed companies including Lehman Brothers and Wirecard, resulting in litigation and regulatory penalties.
