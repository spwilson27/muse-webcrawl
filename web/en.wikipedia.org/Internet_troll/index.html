Internet troll

In Internet slang, a troll is a person who posts inflammatory, insincere, digressive, extraneous, or off-topic messages in an online community (such as a forum, chat room, blog or social media), with the intent of provoking readers into displaying emotional responses, or manipulating others' perception. The activity is known as trolling.

Etymology

The term derives both from the fishing technique of trolling — trailing a baited line to lure fish — which was applied on early Usenet to the practice of posting a lure to bait newcomers into an argument, and from the troll of folklore that lurks under bridges. The Usenet expression "trolling for newbies" dates to the early 1990s.

Behaviour and motivations

Trolls commonly seek attention and amusement from others' anger or distress. Research in psychology has associated heavy trolling behaviour with traits such as sadism, narcissism, Machiavellianism and subclinical psychopathy (the "dark tetrad"), though situational factors such as anonymity and community norms also matter. The common online advice "do not feed the trolls" reflects the view that engaging with trolls rewards them.

Types and responses

Trolling ranges from playful provocation to harassment, "concern trolling" (pretending to support a cause while undermining it), and coordinated political trolling by organized groups. Platforms respond with moderation, reporting tools, rate limits and bans, while communities develop norms and filters to identify bad-faith participation.
