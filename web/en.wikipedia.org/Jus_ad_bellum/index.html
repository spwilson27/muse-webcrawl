Jus ad bellum


Jus ad bellum

Jus ad bellum is a set of criteria in just war theory that are to be consulted before engaging in war, in order to determine whether entering into war is permissible — that is, whether the resort to force is just. Together with jus in bello (just conduct within war) and jus post bellum (justice after war), it forms the main body of just war doctrine.

Criteria

Modern just war theorists typically list the following criteria: (1) just cause — usually defence against aggression; (2) right intention — the aim must be the just cause, not revenge or aggrandizement; (3) legitimate authority — war must be declared by a competent authority; (4) last resort — all peaceful alternatives must have been exhausted; (5) proportionality — the good expected must outweigh the harm; and (6) reasonable prospect of success.

Modern international law

In contemporary international law, the UN Charter prohibits the use of force except in self-defence against an armed attack (Article 51) or when authorized by the Security Council. Aggressive war was criminalized at the Nuremberg trials as a "crime against peace".
