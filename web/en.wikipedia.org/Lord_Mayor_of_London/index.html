Lord Mayor of London

The Lord Mayor of London is the mayor of the City of London — the historic central district ("the Square Mile") — and the leader of the City of London Corporation. The office is distinct from the directly elected Mayor of London, who governs the whole of Greater London; the Lord Mayor's role today is largely ceremonial and ambassadorial, promoting London's financial and professional services worldwide.

History and election

The mayoralty dates from 1189, when Henry Fitz-Ailwin became the first recorded mayor of London; the title "Lord Mayor" came into regular use in the 16th century. Since the 13th century the mayor has been formally admitted to office in the Silent Ceremony at Guildhall and presented to the sovereign's judges in the Lord Mayor's Show, an annual pageant that remains a major public spectacle.

The Lord Mayor is elected each Michaelmas by the liverymen of the City's livery companies from among the City's aldermen who have served as Sheriff of the City of London. The term is one year and is unpaid. The Lord Mayor resides during the term at Mansion House and presides over the Court of Aldermen and Court of Common Council, the Corporation's governing bodies.

Role

Within the City, the Lord Mayor takes precedence over all others and, by tradition, ranks immediately after the sovereign on occasions in the City. Beyond ceremony, the Lord Mayor acts as a global spokesperson for the UK's financial services industry, travelling extensively, and supports charitable appeals through the Lord Mayor's Appeal.
