Pipe bursting

Pipe bursting is a trenchless method of replacing buried pipelines, such as water, sewer and gas lines, in which the existing pipe is fractured and displaced outward by a bursting head drawn through it, while a new pipe of the same or larger diameter is pulled in behind.

Method

A conical bursting head, powered pneumatically or hydraulically (or pulled by a winch in static systems), breaks the host pipe and pushes fragments into the surrounding soil. Because the replacement follows the existing line, excavation is limited to insertion and reception pits, reducing surface disruption compared with open-cut replacement.

Applications and limits

Pipe bursting can upsize capacity while replacing deteriorated pipe, but it is less suitable where the host pipe is heavily collapsed, where nearby utilities lie close to the line, or where ground conditions cannot absorb the displaced fragments.
