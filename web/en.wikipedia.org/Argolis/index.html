Argolis

Argolis (Greek: Αργολίδα, Argolida) is one of the regional units of Greece. It forms part of the region of the Peloponnese, situated in the eastern part of the peninsula. Its capital is Nafplio. The region takes its name from the ancient city of Argos, which stood on the fertile Argive plain near the head of the Argolic Gulf.

Geography

Argolis occupies a peninsula between the Argolic Gulf to the south and the Saronic Gulf to the northeast. Much of the region is mountainous, with the fertile Argive plain forming its agricultural heartland. The regional unit includes several islands, among them Spetses at its southern tip and settlements along the coast such as Ermioni and Porto Heli.

History

Argolis was one of the most important regions of Bronze Age Greece. Mycenae, the legendary seat of Agamemnon, Tiryns, and Argos were major Mycenaean centres, and their Cyclopean walls and tholos tombs remain major archaeological sites. In classical antiquity, Argos was a significant city-state, often rivalling Sparta for influence in the Peloponnese. The sanctuary of Asclepius at Epidaurus, famous for its theatre, lies in the region, as does the stadium of Nemea, site of the Nemean Games. Nafplio served as the first capital of modern Greece from 1828 to 1834.
