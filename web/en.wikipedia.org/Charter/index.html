Charter

A charter is the grant of authority or rights, stating that the granter formally recognises the prerogative of the recipient to exercise the rights specified. Historically, charters were issued by monarchs or governments to towns, universities, guilds, colonies and corporations.

Famous examples include Magna Carta (1215), royal charters incorporating cities and trading companies such as the English East India Company, and colonial charters in North America. A municipal charter functions as a city's basic governing document or constitution.

In modern usage, "charter" also means the temporary hire of a vehicle, aircraft or vessel (a charter flight or yacht charter), and documents such as the Charter of the United Nations (1945) and the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms (1982).
