County Kildare (Irish: Contae Chill Dara) is a county in Ireland. It is in the province of Leinster and is part of the Eastern and Midland Region. It is named after the town of Kildare. The county has a population of 247,774 (2022), making it one of the most populous counties in the state, owing in part to its position within the Greater Dublin Area. The county town is Naas; other large towns include Celbridge, Leixlip, Maynooth, Newbridge, and Athy.

Geography

Kildare is an inland county bordered by Dublin, Meath, Wicklow, Carlow, Laois, Offaly, and Westmeath. The Rivers Liffey, Barrow, and Boyne flow through it, as do the Grand and Royal Canals. The flat limestone plain of the Curragh, in the county's centre, is famous for horse racing and military training, while the Bog of Allen extends across the northwest.

History

Kildare takes its name from Cill Dara, "church of the oak," associated with Saint Brigid, one of Ireland's patron saints, who founded a famous double monastery there in the 5th century. In the medieval period the county was a stronghold of the FitzGerald earls of Kildare, whose power rivalled the English crown's. The county saw heavy fighting in the 1798 rebellion and during the Irish Civil War.

Economy and education

Kildare is known as the "Thoroughbred County" for its stud farms, including the Irish National Stud at Tully and racecourses at the Curragh, Punchestown, and Naas. The county hosts major technology and pharmaceutical employers around Leixlip and Naas, and Maynooth University, one of Ireland's fastest-growing universities, together with St Patrick's Pontifical University.
