Harry S. Truman

Harry S. Truman (May 8, 1884 – December 26, 1972) was the 33rd president of the United States, serving from 1945 to 1953. A member of the Democratic Party, he served as the 34th vice president under Franklin D. Roosevelt and succeeded to the presidency upon Roosevelt's death in April 1945, in the final months of World War II. He presided over the end of the war, the decision to use atomic weapons against Japan, the founding of the United Nations, the Marshall Plan, the Truman Doctrine and the establishment of NATO, and the Korean War.

Early life and rise

Truman was born in Lamar, Missouri, and raised in Independence. He worked as a farmer, served as an artillery officer in France during World War I, and ran a men's clothing store in Kansas City that failed in the postwar recession. Backed by the Pendergast political machine, he was elected a county judge (administrator) in 1922 and to the US Senate in 1934. National prominence came from the Truman Committee, which investigated waste and profiteering in wartime defence contracts. Chosen as Roosevelt's running mate in 1944 to replace Henry A. Wallace, he was vice president for only 82 days before Roosevelt's death on April 12, 1945. The "S" in his name is a middle initial honouring both of his grandfathers, Anderson Shipp Truman and Solomon Young; it stands for no specific name.

Presidency

Truman authorised the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki in August 1945, hastening Japan's surrender. In 1947 he proclaimed the Truman Doctrine of containing communism and signed the Marshall Plan to rebuild Western Europe; in 1948 he recognised the State of Israel, ordered the racial desegregation of the armed forces, and supported the Berlin Airlift. Against nearly universal predictions — immortalised by the erroneous "Dewey Defeats Truman" headline — he won election in his own right in 1948. His second term was dominated by the creation of NATO (1949), the Soviet atomic test and the fall of China to communism, and the Korean War (1950–1953), in which he dismissed General Douglas MacArthur for insubordination. Domestically his "Fair Deal" programme achieved only partial success against a conservative Congress. Truman left office deeply unpopular amid war stalemate and corruption scandals, but historians have since consistently ranked him among the greatest American presidents.

Later life

Truman retired to Independence, Missouri, oversaw the construction of his presidential library, and published his memoirs. He died on December 26, 1972, and is buried at the Harry S. Truman Presidential Library and Museum.
