Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge

The Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge is a double-decked suspension bridge connecting the New York City boroughs of Staten Island and Brooklyn across the Narrows, the tidal strait between Upper and Lower New York Bay. Completed in 1964, at the time it had the longest main span of any suspension bridge in the world, at 4,260 feet (1,298 m). It is named for the Italian explorer Giovanni da Verrazzano, the first European to enter New York Harbor, in 1524.

History

Plans for a crossing of the Narrows dated to the early 20th century, including proposals for tunnels. The bridge was championed by master builder Robert Moses and designed by noted bridge engineer Othmar Ammann. Construction began in 1959; the upper deck opened in 1964 and the lower deck in 1969. Its towers, 693 feet tall, are so far apart that their tops are about 1⅝ inches farther apart than their bases because of the curvature of the Earth. The bridge held the world record for the longest suspension span until the Humber Bridge opened in England in 1981.

Use

The bridge carries Interstate 278 and is the only fixed road connection between Staten Island and the rest of New York City. It is a toll bridge, tolled in the Staten Island-bound direction, and has long had one of the highest tolls in the United States. Since 1976 the bridge has served as the starting point of the New York City Marathon.

Name

The bridge's name was long spelled "Verrazano" with one z, following a typographical error in the original contract; the explorer's name is properly spelled with two. Legislation signed in 2018 officially corrected the spelling to Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge, and signage was progressively updated.
