Maximilian I (22 March 1459 – 12 January 1519) was King of the Romans from 1486 and Holy Roman Emperor from 1508 until his death. A member of the House of Habsburg, he expanded the family's influence throughout Europe largely through dynastic marriages, earning the epithet "the last knight" (der letzte Ritter).

Early life and Burgundian marriage

Maximilian was the son of Emperor Frederick III and Eleanor of Portugal. In 1477 he married Mary of Burgundy, heiress to the Burgundian Netherlands and Franche-Comté, a union that brought the Habsburgs into conflict with France for two generations. Mary's death in a riding accident in 1482 left Maximilian regent for their son, Philip the Handsome.

Reign

Elected King of the Romans in 1486, Maximilian succeeded his father in 1493. He reformed the empire's institutions, creating the Reichskammergericht (Imperial Chamber Court) and the Reichskreise (imperial circles). In 1508, blocked from travelling to Rome, he assumed the title "Elected Roman Emperor" with papal consent. His wars in Italy against France and Venice were costly and only partly successful, but treaties and marriages — above all the 1515 double marriage arranged at Vienna — laid the groundwork for his grandchildren to rule Spain, the Netherlands, Austria, Bohemia and Hungary.

Patronage and legacy

A celebrated patron of the arts, Maximilian commissioned works from Albrecht Dürer, including the monumental Triumphal Arch woodcut, and promoted humanists such as Conrad Celtis. His grandson Charles V inherited a vast composite monarchy that made the Habsburgs the dominant European dynasty of the sixteenth century.
