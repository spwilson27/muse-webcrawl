The Weissenhof Estate (German: Weißenhofsiedlung) is a housing estate in Stuttgart, Germany, built in 1927 as part of the Werkbund exhibition "Die Wohnung" ("The Dwelling"). Commissioned by the city and organised by the Deutscher Werkbund under the artistic direction of Ludwig Mies van der Rohe, it comprised 21 buildings containing 63 dwellings, designed by many of the leading architects of European modernism.

Design

Participating architects included Le Corbusier and Pierre Jeanneret, Walter Gropius, J.J.P. Oud, Mart Stam, Bruno Taut, Hans Scharoun, Peter Behrens and Hans Poelzig. The flat-roofed, white-rendered houses demonstrated the principles of the emerging International Style and of Neues Bauen: functional plans, flexible interiors (Mies's apartment block used movable partitions), industrial construction methods and an architecture of light, air and hygiene intended as a model for mass housing. Le Corbusier's contribution included a house built on pilotis and the double-height "Maison Citrohan"-derived design now known as the Weissenhof Museum.

Later history

Attacked by conservatives as un-German, and targeted in Nazi propaganda, the estate survived the Second World War with losses — several buildings were destroyed — and post-war alterations. From the 1980s the surviving buildings were restored. In 2016 the two Le Corbusier houses at Weissenhof were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as part of the transnational serial listing "The Architectural Work of Le Corbusier". The estate remains residential, with one house devoted to the Weissenhof Museum.
