Flexible working

Flexible working is the name given to any type of working practice that deviates from the norm of a fixed nine-to-five day spent in a central workplace. It usually incorporates variations in working time, working location, or the pattern of work itself, and includes arrangements such as flexitime, part-time work, job sharing, compressed working weeks, annualised hours, term-time working, and remote work or teleworking from home.

The concept has grown steadily since the late twentieth century, driven by changing workforce demographics, information technology that makes location-independent work possible, and legislation in many countries giving employees a right to request flexible arrangements. In the United Kingdom, the right to request flexible working was first introduced for parents of young children in 2003 and was extended to all employees in 2014; the Employment Relations (Flexible Working) Act 2023 made the right a day-one entitlement from April 2024.

Types of flexible working

Common forms include flexitime, in which employees vary their start and finish times around core hours; part-time work; job sharing, where two people share the responsibilities of one full-time post; compressed hours, such as working a full-time load over four days; annualised hours, where hours are averaged over a year; and remote working, in which work is performed from home or another location away from the employer's premises, full-time or in a hybrid pattern.

Benefits and drawbacks

Supporters argue that flexible working improves work-life balance, widens the pool of available labour (including carers and people with disabilities), reduces commuting and its environmental impact, and can raise productivity and staff retention. Critics point to risks of overwork when home and work boundaries blur, difficulties in supervision and team cohesion, and unequal access between office-based and front-line occupations. The COVID-19 pandemic of 2020 dramatically accelerated the adoption of homeworking and hybrid patterns across many economies.
