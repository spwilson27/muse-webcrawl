Sarawak is a state of Malaysia on the northwest coast of the island of Borneo, the country's largest state by area. It borders the Malaysian state of Sabah to the northeast, Indonesia's Kalimantan to the south, and surrounds the independent sultanate of Brunei. Its capital and largest city is Kuching. Sarawak is known for its extraordinary ethnic diversity — Iban, Chinese, Malay, Bidayuh, Melanau, Orang Ulu and many other groups — and for rainforests that include the Gunung Mulu and Niah national parks.

History

The region was long part of the Bruneian empire. In 1841 the English adventurer James Brooke was granted rule over Kuching for helping suppress a rebellion, founding the Raj of Sarawak governed by the "White Rajahs" of the Brooke family for a century. Sarawak became a British Crown Colony in 1946 after Japanese occupation during the Second World War, and in 1963 it joined Malaya, Sabah, and Singapore to form Malaysia. The Malaysia Agreement 1963 (MA63) granted Sarawak significant autonomy, including over immigration, which remains a live political issue.

Geography and nature

Sarawak's low coastal swamps rise to mountain ranges along the Indonesian border, including Mount Murud, the state's highest peak. Its rivers, such as the Rajang — Malaysia's longest — are the main transport arteries. Gunung Mulu National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, contains vast cave systems including the Sarawak Chamber, among the world's largest. The state hosts orangutans, proboscis monkeys, and Rafflesia flowers, though logging and oil-palm expansion have pressured its forests.

Economy

Oil and natural gas (from Bintulu's LNG complex), palm oil, timber, and pepper are economic mainstays, alongside a growing hydropower programme on rivers such as the Batang Ai and Bakun dams.
