The Northern Sea Route (Russian: Северный морской путь, Severny morskoi put) is a shipping lane from the Atlantic to the Pacific Ocean along the Russian Arctic coast, running from Murmansk and the Barents Sea through Siberia to the Bering Strait and the Far East.

History

The route was explored in stages from the 16th century onward; the full passage was first completed by Adolf Erik Nordenskiöld's Vega expedition of 1878–79. Under the Soviet Union it became a domestic supply artery for Arctic settlements, supported by the world's largest icebreaker fleet.

Navigation

Historically ice-bound for much of the year, the route has become increasingly navigable in late summer as Arctic sea ice declines. Transit between northern Europe and north-east Asia via the route is roughly 40 per cent shorter than via the Suez Canal, but ice conditions, shallow straits and limited rescue infrastructure remain constraints.

Administration

Russia regulates traffic through its Northern Sea Route Administration; vessels typically require permits and may need icebreaker escort. Cargo volumes have grown mainly through destination traffic serving Arctic oil, gas and mining projects.
