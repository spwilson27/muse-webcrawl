Jane Eyre

Jane Eyre: An Autobiography is a novel by the English writer Charlotte Brontë, published under the pen name "Currer Bell" on 19 October 1847 by Smith, Elder & Co. of London. It is a Bildungsroman following the emotional and moral development of the orphaned Jane Eyre from childhood to her marriage to Edward Fairfax Rochester.

Author: Charlotte Brontë (as Currer Bell)

Published: 19 October 1847

Publisher: Smith, Elder & Co.

Genre: Bildungsroman, Gothic romance

Plot

Orphaned Jane Eyre is raised by her cruel aunt, Mrs Reed, at Gateshead, then sent to Lowood School, where she endures harsh conditions but gains an education and a friend, Helen Burns, who dies of consumption. Jane becomes a governess at Thornfield Hall, home of the brooding Edward Rochester, with whom she falls in love. On their wedding day she learns that Rochester is already married: his wife, Bertha Mason, is confined, mad, in the attic. Jane flees and is taken in by the Rivers family; she discovers she has inherited a fortune and that the Rivers are her cousins. The clergyman St John Rivers proposes a loveless marriage of missionary duty, which she refuses. Hearing Rochester's voice calling to her, Jane returns to find Thornfield burnt down by Bertha, who died in the fire; Rochester was blinded and maimed attempting to save her. Jane marries him ("Reader, I married him"), and his sight partly returns.

Themes and reception

The novel combines Gothic elements with an unusually direct first-person female voice, addressing class, gender, religion and independence. It was immediately successful and remains a staple of English literature, inspiring adaptations and Jean Rhys's prequel Wide Sargasso Sea (1966), which gives Bertha Mason a voice.
