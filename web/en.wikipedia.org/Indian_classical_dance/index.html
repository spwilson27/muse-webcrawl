Indian classical dance

Indian classical dance comprises the codified dance-drama traditions of India, whose theoretical foundations are traced to the ancient Sanskrit treatise Natya Shastra, attributed to Bharata Muni. The dances combine nritta (pure rhythmic movement), nritya (expressive storytelling), and natya (dramatic enactment), using elaborate hand gestures (mudras), facial expressions conveying the rasas (emotional flavors), and complex footwork set to classical music.

Principal forms

India's Sangeet Natak Akademi officially recognizes eight classical forms: Bharatanatyam (Tamil Nadu), Kathak (northern India), Kathakali and Mohiniyattam (Kerala), Kuchipudi (Andhra Pradesh), Odissi (Odisha), Manipuri (Manipur), and Sattriya (Assam, recognized in 2000). Each has distinct technique, costume, repertoire, and musical accompaniment — Carnatic music in the south and Hindustani music in the north.

History

The traditions developed in temples, royal courts, and monastic settings over many centuries. Several forms were sustained by hereditary communities, notably the devadasis for Bharatanatyam's predecessor sadir. In the 20th century, revivalists such as Rukmini Devi Arundale (who founded Kalakshetra in 1936) brought the dances to the proscenium stage and middle-class students, sometimes sanitizing elements of the repertoire. Today the forms are taught worldwide and performed on global stages, while retaining strong regional identities and guru–shishya lineages.
