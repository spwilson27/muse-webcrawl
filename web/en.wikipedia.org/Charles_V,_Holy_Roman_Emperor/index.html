Charles V (1500–1558) was Holy Roman Emperor and Archduke of Austria from 1519 to 1556, King of Spain (as Charles I) from 1516 to 1556, and Lord of the Netherlands from 1506 to 1555. Through inheritance he assembled the first empire on which "the sun never set": Spain and its American colonies, the Burgundian Netherlands, the Austrian hereditary lands, Naples, Sicily, and Milan.

Rise and reign

Born in Ghent and raised in Flanders, Charles inherited Spain and its growing American empire in 1516, then outbid Francis I of France for the imperial crown in 1519. His reign was consumed by interlocking conflicts: four wars with Valois France over Italy (including the Sack of Rome in 1527 by his mutinous troops and victory at Pavia in 1525, where Francis was captured); Ottoman expansion under Suleiman the Magnificent, checked at Vienna (1529) and contested across the Mediterranean; and the Protestant Reformation in Germany.

Reformation and Germany

Charles presided over the Diet of Worms (1521), which outlawed Martin Luther. Years of negotiation, the Augsburg Confession (1530), and finally war against the Schmalkaldic League — won at Mühlberg (1547) — failed to restore religious unity. Exhausted, Charles accepted the Peace of Augsburg (1555), allowing princes to choose Lutheranism or Catholicism for their territories.

Abdication and death

Worn by gout and the scale of his burdens, Charles abdicated in stages in 1555–1556: the Netherlands and Spain to his son Philip II, the imperial title to his brother Ferdinand I, permanently splitting the Habsburgs into Spanish and Austrian branches. He retired to the monastery of Yuste in Extremadura, dying there on 21 September 1558.

Legacy

Charles personified the ideal of universal monarchy and the defence of Catholic Christendom, yet his reign entrenched the confessional division of Europe, the Franco-Habsburg rivalry, and Spain's costly imperial commitments. Under him, Spanish conquistadors overthrew the Aztec and Inca empires, and Magellan's expedition — sailing for Charles — completed the first circumnavigation of the globe.
