Nymphalidae

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The monarch butterfly, a famous nymphalid

The Nymphalidae are the largest family of butterflies, with more than 6,000 species distributed throughout most of the world. They are commonly called brush-footed butterflies because in adults of most species the forelegs are greatly reduced and hairy, so that the butterflies appear to stand on only four legs.

Characteristics

Nymphalids range from small to large (wingspans roughly 2.5–18 cm) and are usually brightly coloured, though the undersides of the wings are often dull and cryptic, in some species resembling dead leaves. The reduced forelegs are useless for walking in males and only slightly less reduced in females. Many species hold their wings flat when basking.

Subfamilies

Major subfamilies include the Danainae (milkweed butterflies, including the monarch), Satyrinae (browns), Heliconiinae (longwings and fritillaries), Nymphalinae (admirals, tortoiseshells, and anglewings), Morphinae (morphos), and Limenitidinae (admirals and allies).

Ecology

Many nymphalids are strong migrants — the monarch's multi-generational migration across North America is the most famous. Adults feed on nectar, rotting fruit, tree sap, and dung; caterpillars of the Danainae sequester toxins from milkweeds, advertising their unpalatability with warning coloration.
