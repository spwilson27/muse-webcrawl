Su Song

Su Song (1020–1101) was a Chinese polymath of the Song dynasty: statesman, astronomer, cartographer, horologist, pharmacologist, and engineer. He is best known for the great astronomical clock tower he built in Kaifeng, powered by a waterwheel and employing an early escapement mechanism.

Life and career

Born near Quanzhou in Fujian, Su Song passed the imperial examinations and rose to high office, serving as an ambassador to the Liao dynasty and as a senior minister. His diplomatic missions also produced detailed maps of the route to the Liao capital.

Clock tower

Completed in 1092, Su Song's clock tower stood about 12 metres tall. It featured a rotating armillary sphere, a celestial globe, mechanically driven mannequins that struck bells and held time plaques, and the earliest known endless chain drive. Su documented the design in his treatise Xin Yixiang Fayao (1092).

Other work

He also directed an imperial pharmacopoeia project, the Bencao Tujing, and produced some of the earliest printed star maps based on careful astronomical observation.
