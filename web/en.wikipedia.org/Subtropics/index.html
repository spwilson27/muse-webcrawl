Subtropics

The subtropics are geographical and climate zones located roughly between the Tropic of Cancer and the Tropic of Capricorn and the temperate zones, approximately between 23.5° and 40° latitude in both hemispheres. Subtropical climates are characterised by hot summers and mild winters with infrequent frost.

Climate types

The best-known subtropical climate is the humid subtropical climate (Köppen Cfa), found on the south-eastern sides of continents — the south-eastern United States, southern China, northern India, southern Brazil, and eastern Australia — with hot humid summers and plentiful rainfall. On the western sides of continents, the Mediterranean climate (Csa) prevails, with dry summers and wet winters, as around the Mediterranean Sea, in California, central Chile, and south-western Australia. Highland and semi-arid variants occur in between.

The subtropics are dominated in summer by the descending limbs of the Hadley cells — the subtropical highs — which bring much of the world's great deserts, from the Sahara and Arabian deserts in the north to the Kalahari and Australian desert in the south. Many of the world's major cities lie in the subtropics, including Houston, Shanghai, Cairo, Sydney, and Buenos Aires.
