Khan Tengri is a mountain of the Tian Shan range in Central Asia, rising to 7,010 metres (22,999 ft) including its ice cap — 6,995 metres of rock — near the tripoint of Kyrgyzstan, Kazakhstan, and China. Its name means "King Heaven" or "Lord of the Skies" in Turkic languages, and its perfectly pyramidal marble peak is famous for glowing red at sunset.

Geography

Khan Tengri is the second-highest mountain in the Tian Shan after Jengish Chokusu (Pobeda Peak, 7,439 m), and the highest point in Kazakhstan. It stands on the Tengri Tag subrange between the North and South Inylchek glaciers; the South Inylchek, at about 60 km, is one of the longest glaciers outside the polar regions. Although its rocky summit is just below 7,000 m, the mountain is counted among the world's 7,000-metre peaks because of its ice cap.

Climbing history

The peak's altitude was long confused with that of Jengish Chokusu, which was mistakenly thought lower until surveys in the 1940s. The first ascent was made in 1931 by a Ukrainian team led by Mikhail Pogrebetsky, climbing from the south. Khan Tengri is one of the five former-Soviet "Snow Leopard" peaks and is considered one of the most beautiful — and, for its latitude, one of the most demanding — big mountains in the world, with severe weather and a remote approach by glacier.
