Operation Spring Awakening

Date6–16 March 1945
LocationLake Balaton region, Hungary
ResultSoviet victory; last major German offensive of the Second World War

Operation Spring Awakening (German: Unternehmen Frühlingserwachen) was the last major German offensive of World War II. It took place in Hungary in March 1945, around Lake Balaton, and was intended to recapture Budapest, secure the Nagykanizsa oil fields, and halt the Soviet advance toward Vienna.

Background

By early 1945 Germany's situation was desperate, but the Hungarian oil fields were among the last sources of petroleum available to the Reich. Adolf Hitler diverted the 6th SS Panzer Army, recently withdrawn after the failed Ardennes offensive, to Hungary for a counterattack against the Soviet 3rd Ukrainian Front under Marshal Fyodor Tolbukhin.

The offensive

The attack began on 6 March 1945 in muddy terrain that hampered armoured movement. German forces made limited gains but failed to break through the prepared Soviet defences. From 16 March the Red Army launched the Vienna Offensive, driving the Germans back beyond their start lines and advancing on Austria.

Aftermath

The failure of Spring Awakening exhausted Germany's remaining armoured reserves in the east. Soviet forces captured Vienna in April 1945, and the war in Europe ended the following month.
