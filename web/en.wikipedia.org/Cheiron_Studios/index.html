Cheiron Studios

Cheiron Studios was a recording studio located in the Kungsholmen district of Stockholm, Sweden. Founded in 1992 by Denniz PoP and Tom Talomaa, it was world-famous for the pop music it produced in the late 1990s for artists including the Backstreet Boys, Britney Spears, 'N Sync, Westlife, and Robyn. The studio closed in January 2001, but its sound and its songwriting team shaped mainstream pop for years afterwards.

History

Denniz PoP, a Swedish DJ and producer, founded Cheiron with Tom Talomaa after early success producing Ace of Base's The Sign. He recruited a team of young writers and producers — among them Max Martin, Herbie Crichlow, E-Type, and later Rami Yacoub, Jörgen Elofsson, Andreas Carlsson, and Kristian Lundin. The studio's breakthrough came with the Backstreet Boys' debut singles and Britney Spears's "...Baby One More Time" (1998), written by Max Martin.

Legacy

Denniz PoP died of stomach cancer in August 1998 at the age of 35, but the studio continued until the team decided to close it at the end of 2000, feeling the moment had passed. Many of its alumni formed new companies; Max Martin went on to become one of the most successful songwriters in chart history, and the Cheiron "factory" model — a stable of writers crafting songs for multiple artists — became the template for modern pop production.
