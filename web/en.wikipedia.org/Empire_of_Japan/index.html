The Empire of Japan was the historical nation-state and great power that existed from the Meiji Restoration in 1868 until the enactment of the post-war constitution in 1947. In less than half a century Japan transformed from an isolated feudal society into an industrialised empire, adopting Western technology, law and military organisation while preserving the emperor as head of state under the Meiji Constitution of 1889.

Expansion

Victories in the First Sino-Japanese War (1894–95) and the Russo-Japanese War (1904–05) brought Taiwan, Korea (annexed 1910) and influence in Manchuria into the empire. Japan fought on the Allied side in the First World War and acquired Pacific mandates. From 1931, with the invasion of Manchuria, militarism drove further expansion: full-scale war with China from 1937, and from December 1941 war against the United States and European colonial powers across the Pacific and Southeast Asia.

Defeat and end

By 1945 the empire was collapsing under Allied blockade and bombing; following the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki and the Soviet entry into the war, Japan announced its surrender on 15 August 1945. Under Allied occupation Japan lost all overseas territories, was demilitarised, and adopted the pacifist Constitution of 1947, formally ending the imperial state.
