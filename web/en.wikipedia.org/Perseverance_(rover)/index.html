Perseverance, nicknamed Percy, is a car-sized Mars rover designed to explore Jezero Crater as part of NASA's Mars 2020 mission. It was launched on 30 July 2020 and landed on 18 February 2021. Building on the design of the Curiosity rover, Perseverance carries a suite of instruments aimed at searching for signs of ancient microbial life, characterising Mars's geology and climate, and collecting samples for possible return to Earth.

Landing site

Jezero Crater, about 45 km in diameter, once held a lake and a river delta more than 3.5 billion years ago. Its sedimentary rocks and delta deposits are prime targets in the search for preserved biosignatures. The rover landed at a site later named for science-fiction writer Octavia E. Butler.

Science and technology

Key instruments include the Mastcam-Z panoramic cameras, the SuperCam laser spectrometer, the PIXL and SHERLOC instruments for fine-scale chemistry and mineralogy, and the MOXIE experiment, which successfully produced oxygen from the carbon-dioxide atmosphere — a first for another planet. A coring drill seals rock and regolith samples in titanium tubes; by 2023 the rover had completed a sample depot of ten tubes at a backup location while continuing to carry paired samples on board, as the core of the planned Mars Sample Return campaign. The Ingenuity helicopter, the first aircraft to fly on another world, made 72 flights between April 2021 and January 2024, far exceeding its five-flight demonstration plan.

Mission progress

After exploring the crater floor's igneous rocks, Perseverance ascended the delta front, sampling sedimentary rocks, and in 2024 climbed to the crater rim. In July 2024 NASA announced that a rock dubbed Cheyava Falls bore chemical and structural features potentially consistent with ancient microbial activity, while stressing that non-biological explanations remain possible and sample return is needed for certainty.
