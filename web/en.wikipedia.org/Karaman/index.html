Karaman is a city in south-central Turkey, capital of Karaman Province, lying on the northern foothills of the Taurus Mountains about 100 km south of Konya. Known in antiquity as Laranda, it became famous as the capital of the Karamanids, the Anatolian Turkish beylik that was the Seljuks' most persistent rival and the last to be absorbed by the Ottoman Empire.

History

The city took its modern name from Karaman Bey, founder of the Karamanid dynasty in the thirteenth century. In 1277 the Karamanid ruler Mehmed Bey issued a celebrated decree making Turkish the official language of state in place of Persian — an event commemorated annually in Karaman's Turkish Language Festival. The mystic poet Yunus Emre is traditionally said to be buried near the city. Karaman was incorporated into the Ottoman Empire in the late fifteenth century after prolonged resistance.

Sights

Landmarks include Karaman Castle, the Aktekke Mosque (containing the tomb of Rumi's mother), the Hatuniye Medrese, and numerous Karamanid-era mosques and caravanserais. The region is also a significant centre of Turkish biscuit and confectionery manufacture and of apple production.
