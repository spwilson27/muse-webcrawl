The Saffir–Simpson hurricane wind scale classifies hurricanes in the Atlantic and eastern Pacific into five categories based on their maximum sustained wind speed, estimating potential property damage. It was developed in 1971 by civil engineer Herbert Saffir and meteorologist Robert Simpson.

Categories

Tropical cyclones with winds below hurricane force are classified as tropical storms or depressions. Category 3 and above are termed major hurricanes. The scale is purely wind-based and does not account for storm surge, rainfall, or flooding, which historically cause most deaths.
