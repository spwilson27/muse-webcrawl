Salford

Salford Quays, redeveloped docklands on the Manchester Ship Canal.

Salford is a city in Greater Manchester, England, lying west of Manchester across the River Irwell. It is the main settlement of the wider City of Salford metropolitan borough. Historically part of Lancashire, Salford grew rapidly during the Industrial Revolution as a centre of textile manufacture and inland port activity.

History

The name Salford derives from Old English elements meaning "ford by the willows" (sealh and ford). The town became an important cotton-spinning and weaving centre, and Friedrich Engels wrote about its working-class districts in The Condition of the Working Class in England (1845). The opening of the Manchester Ship Canal in 1894 made Salford Quays one of the largest inland ports in Britain.

Regeneration

After the docks closed in 1982, the quays were redeveloped into Salford Quays, home to The Lowry arts centre, Imperial War Museum North (across the water in Trafford), and MediaCityUK, where the BBC and ITV relocated major operations from 2011.

Education and sport

The University of Salford received its charter in 1967 and has a strong reputation in built environment and media studies. Salford Cathedral, a Grade II* listed Gothic Revival church, is the seat of the Roman Catholic Diocese of Salford. The painter L. S. Lowry, famous for his industrial scenes of "matchstick men", lived and worked in the area for many years.
