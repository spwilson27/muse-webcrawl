Micro Four Thirds system

The Micro Four Thirds system (MFT or M4/3) is a standard for mirrorless interchangeable-lens cameras and lenses, announced in August 2008 by Olympus and Panasonic. It is a smaller variant of the earlier Four Thirds system, using the same 18 × 13.5 mm sensor (a nominal 4/3-inch format) but eliminating the mirror box and pentaprism, which allows much smaller and lighter camera bodies and lenses.

Design

Micro Four Thirds lenses have a flange focal distance of about 20 mm, roughly half that of Four Thirds, and the mount is 6 mm smaller in diameter. The 2× crop factor relative to 35 mm full frame means a 25 mm lens gives the field of view of a 50 mm lens on full frame. Electronic communication between body and lens supports autofocus, image stabilization coordination ("Dual IS") and aperture control; adapters allow Four Thirds and legacy manual lenses to be used.

Cameras and lenses

The first Micro Four Thirds camera was the Panasonic Lumix G1 (2008), followed by Olympus's PEN E-P1 (2009). The system grew to include the Olympus OM-D line, Panasonic's GH series — the GH4 (2014) and GH5 (2017) being especially popular with video creators — and cameras from DJI, Blackmagic Design (the Pocket Cinema Camera) and others. Dozens of lenses are offered by Olympus (later OM System), Panasonic Leica, Sigma, Tamron, Voigtländer and others.

Legacy

Micro Four Thirds effectively created the mirrorless camera category, prompting Canon, Nikon, Sony and Fujifilm to develop rival systems. Though larger-sensor systems later took much of the market, MFT remains valued for compact telephoto reach, weather-sealed bodies and hybrid photo-video features.
