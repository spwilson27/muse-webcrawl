Molecular orbital theory

In chemistry, molecular orbital theory (MO theory) is a method for describing the electronic structure of molecules using quantum mechanics. It represents electrons as occupying molecular orbitals that extend over the whole molecule, formed from linear combinations of atomic orbitals (LCAO).

Principles

When atomic orbitals combine, they form an equal number of molecular orbitals: bonding orbitals lower in energy and antibonding orbitals higher in energy. Electrons fill these orbitals according to the Aufbau principle, Pauli exclusion, and Hund's rule. The bond order is calculated as (bonding electrons − antibonding electrons) / 2.

Successes

MO theory correctly predicts the paramagnetism of the oxygen molecule (O2), which simple valence bond pictures miss, and explains properties of species such as the superoxide and peroxide ions and the colours of conjugated molecules via frontier-orbital (HOMO–LUMO) ideas.
