Role: Fighter aircraft
Manufacturer: North American Aviation

The North American F-86 Sabre, sometimes called the Sabrejet, is a transonic jet fighter aircraft produced by North American Aviation. It is best known as the United States' first swept-wing fighter and for its air combat duels with the Soviet-built MiG-15 during the Korean War.

Design and development

The Sabre was developed from North American's earlier straight-wing FJ-1 Fury. Incorporating captured German aerodynamic research, its designers gave it a 35-degree swept wing, which delayed the rise in drag at transonic speeds. The prototype XP-86 first flew on October 1, 1947, and in 1948 an F-86A broke the world air speed record at 671 mph (1,080 km/h).

Operational history

The F-86 entered service with the United States Air Force in 1949. In Korea it proved a close match for the MiG-15; American pilots, many of them aces from the Second World War, claimed a favorable kill ratio over the Yalu River's "MiG Alley." Variants included the radar-equipped F-86D Sabre Dog interceptor and the F-86F, and Canadair, Fiat, and others built the type under license. Sabres served with many air forces; the Pakistan Air Force used them effectively in the Indo-Pakistani wars.

Legacy

A total of 9,860 aircraft were built, making the Sabre one of the most-produced Western jet fighters. Surviving examples are popular warbirds at air shows.
