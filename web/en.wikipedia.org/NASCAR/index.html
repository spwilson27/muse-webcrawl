The National Association for Stock Car Auto Racing, LLC (NASCAR) is an American auto racing sanctioning and operating company best known for stock car racing. Privately owned by the France family, it was founded by Bill France Sr. in 1948, and his son Jim France has been the chief executive officer since August 2018. NASCAR is headquartered in Daytona Beach, Florida, with additional offices in Charlotte, Concord, and Conover, North Carolina, as well as New York, Los Angeles, and Bentonville, Arkansas.

NASCAR sanctions more than 1,500 races at over 100 tracks in 48 U.S. states as well as in Canada, Mexico, Brazil, and Europe. It broadcasts races in over 150 countries and 20 languages, making it one of the most widely televised motorsports in the world.

History

Stock car racing in the United States has its origins in Prohibition-era bootlegging, when drivers ran bootleg whiskey produced in the Appalachian region and modified their cars for speed and handling, as well as increased cargo capacity. On December 14, 1947, Bill France Sr. began talks with other influential race promoters and drivers at the Streamline Hotel in Daytona Beach, Florida, that ended with the formation of NASCAR on February 21, 1948.

The first NASCAR "Strictly Stock" race was held on June 19, 1949, at Charlotte Speedway. The series became the Grand National in 1950, and in 1971, when R. J. Reynolds Tobacco Company became title sponsor through its Winston brand, it was renamed the Winston Cup Series. A landmark came in 1979, when the Daytona 500 was televised live flag-to-flag for the first time, ending with a famous last-lap crash and fight between Cale Yarborough and the Allison brothers.

Series

NASCAR's three national series are the Cup Series (its premier division), the Xfinity Series, and the Craftsman Truck Series, the latter contested in pickup-truck-bodied vehicles. The Cup Series runs 36 points races plus exhibition events, concluding with a 10-race playoff known as the Playoffs (formerly the Chase). Famous Cup races include the Daytona 500, the Coca-Cola 600, the Southern 500, and the Brickyard 400.

Cars

Cup Series cars are purpose-built silhouettes resembling production models such as the Chevrolet Camaro, Ford Mustang, and Toyota Camry. The seventh-generation "Next Gen" car, introduced in 2022, features a 5.8-litre V8 producing roughly 670 horsepower at most tracks, a five-speed sequential gearbox, independent rear suspension, and a single center-locking wheel nut.

Safety

Following the death of Dale Earnhardt in the 2001 Daytona 500, NASCAR undertook major safety reforms, including mandatory head-and-neck restraints, the SAFER barrier at ovals, the Car of Tomorrow chassis, and a dedicated research and development centre in Concord, North Carolina.
