USS Johnston (DD-557)

USS Johnston (DD-557) was a Fletcher-class destroyer of the United States Navy, named for Lieutenant John V. Johnston. She is famous for her sacrificial charge against a vastly superior Japanese force at the Battle off Samar on 25 October 1944, one of the most celebrated actions in US naval history.

Service history

After commissioning in October 1943, Johnston served in the Marshall Islands campaign, providing shore bombardment at Kwajalein and Eniwetok, supported operations in the Solomons, and screened carriers during strikes on the Palaus and Hollandia. In May 1944 she bombarded Japanese positions during the campaign in the Marianas and fought off air attacks in defence of the escort carriers at Saipan.

Battle off Samar

On the morning of 25 October 1944, Johnston was part of Task Unit 77.4.3 ("Taffy 3"), a group of escort carriers and small escorts surprised off Samar by Admiral Kurita's Center Force, which included the battleship Yamato. Without orders, Commander Ernest E. Evans turned Johnston toward the enemy, laid a smoke screen and attacked, firing her torpedoes and crippling the heavy cruiser Kumano. Fighting at close range against battleships and cruisers, the outgunned destroyer bought time for the carriers to escape before she was overwhelmed and sunk. Of her crew of 327, 186 were lost, including Evans, who was posthumously awarded the Medal of Honor. Johnston received the Presidential Unit Citation and six battle stars.

Wreck

The wreck was located in 2019 and surveyed in 2021 at a depth of about 6,460 m (21,200 ft) in the Philippine Sea — among the deepest shipwrecks ever surveyed — with the hull number 557 still legible on the bow.
