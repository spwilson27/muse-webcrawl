Compressibility factor

The compressibility factor, usually denoted Z, is a correction factor describing how much a real gas deviates from ideal-gas behaviour at given pressure, temperature, and molar volume. It is defined as Z = pV/(nRT).

Behaviour

For an ideal gas Z equals 1. At moderate pressures attractive forces can make Z less than 1, while at high pressures molecular volume dominates and Z exceeds 1. Generalized charts plot Z against reduced pressure and temperature.

Applications

Engineers use compressibility factors in natural-gas metering, pipeline design, and any calculation where ideal-gas assumptions would introduce unacceptable error.
