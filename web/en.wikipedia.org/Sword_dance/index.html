Sword dance

Sword dances are ritual and folk dances performed with swords or sword-like implements, recorded throughout Europe, Asia and the Middle East from antiquity to the present day. They include solo dances over crossed blades, linked dances in which a team of dancers holds the tips of one another's swords, and mock-combat dances dramatising a fight or a ritual death and resurrection.

European traditions

In England, the principal forms are the longsword dance of Yorkshire and the rapper sword dance of Northumberland and County Durham, in which dancers, linked by flexible steel "rappers", weave intricate figures and form a star-shaped lock of swords. Morris teams also perform sword traditions in some regions. Comparable hilt-and-point dances survive in Spain (such as the paloteo), France, Italy, the Basque Country, Croatia — where the moreška dramatises a battle — and in Germany and Scandinavia. The Scottish Highland sword dance, performed over two crossed swords on the ground, is traditionally said to derive from a dance of victory after battle.

Beyond Europe

Sword dances are performed in China, where solo sword forms are also part of wushu and opera; in Korea; in the Caucasus and the Middle East, including the ardah of the Arabian Peninsula; and among many other cultures. Ancient Roman and Greek writers described armed dances, and Tacitus recorded a sword dance among Germanic peoples.

Symbolism

Many sword dances include a mock beheading or a ritual death-and-revival sequence, leading folklorists to connect them with seasonal fertility rites, though such interpretations are debated. Today they are maintained by folk dance teams and at festivals and competitions.
