John Searle

Born: 31 July 1932, Denver, Colorado, U.S.
Fields: Philosophy of mind, philosophy of language, social philosophy
Institutions: University of California, Berkeley
Notable ideas: Chinese room argument; speech acts; intentionality; social construction of reality

John Rogers Searle (born July 31, 1932) is an American philosopher widely noted for contributions to the philosophy of language, philosophy of mind and social philosophy. He taught at the University of California, Berkeley, for many years.

Speech acts

A Rhodes Scholar at Oxford, Searle developed J. L. Austin's theory of speech acts in Speech Acts (1969), analysing how utterances perform actions such as promising and asserting, governed by constitutive rules.

Chinese room

In 1980 Searle proposed the Chinese room thought experiment against "strong AI": a person who manipulates Chinese symbols by rulebook to produce sensible answers does not thereby understand Chinese; likewise, Searle argued, a computer running a program does not thereby have a mind. The argument remains one of the most debated in philosophy of mind.

Social reality and consciousness

Searle's The Construction of Social Reality (1995) explains institutions such as money and marriage as resting on collectively imposed status functions. He defended a view of consciousness as a biological phenomenon caused by brain processes, rejecting both dualism and eliminative materialism.

Later years

In 2019, UC Berkeley found that Searle had violated the university's sexual harassment policies in 2017, and he was stripped of emeritus status; the allegations were widely reported and ended his academic career.
