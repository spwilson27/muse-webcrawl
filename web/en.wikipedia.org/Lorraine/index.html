Lorraine is a former administrative region and a cultural and historical region of north-eastern France. It borders Belgium, Luxembourg and Germany, and the French regions of Alsace, Champagne-Ardenne and Franche-Comté. Since 2016 it has formed part of the region of Grand Est. Its name derives from Lotharingia, the kingdom of Lothair II in the ninth century.

History

Lorraine was contested for centuries between France and the German states. The Duchy of Lorraine was progressively absorbed by France, finally passing to the French crown in 1766 after the death of Stanisław Leszczyński, the former Polish king who held it for life. After the Franco-Prussian War of 1870–71, Germany annexed northern Lorraine (Moselle) together with Alsace; the territory returned to France in 1918, was annexed again in 1940, and liberated in 1944–45.

Geography and economy

The region includes the cities of Metz (its largest), Nancy, Thionville and Épinal. The Moselle river flows through it. Lorraine's coal, iron and steel industries, long its economic backbone, declined sharply in the later twentieth century. The cross of Lorraine — a double-barred cross — is the region's enduring symbol, associated with Joan of Arc and later with the Free French forces.
