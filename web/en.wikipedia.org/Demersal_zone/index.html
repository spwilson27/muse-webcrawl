Demersal zone

The demersal zone is the part of the sea or ocean (or deep lake) comprising the water column close to and significantly affected by the seabed and the benthos. The demersal zone is just above the benthic zone and forms a layer of the larger profundal zone.

Ecology

Organisms living in the demersal zone are called demersal species; the best known are demersal fish such as cod, haddock, flatfish, rays and many sharks, which feed on or near the bottom. They are contrasted with pelagic species, which live in the open water column away from the bottom. Demersal fish can be divided into benthic fish, which rest on the seafloor, and benthopelagic fish, which float just above it.

Fisheries

Many commercially important fisheries target demersal species, typically using bottom trawls, longlines and traps. Because these fisheries interact directly with seabed habitats, they raise management concerns about habitat damage and bycatch, and are often regulated by quotas and gear restrictions.
