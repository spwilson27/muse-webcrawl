The Paradox of Choice: Why More Is Less is a book written by American psychologist Barry Schwartz and first published in 2004 by Ecco, an imprint of HarperCollins. In the book Schwartz argues that eliminating consumer choices can greatly reduce anxiety for shoppers, challenging the orthodox economic assumption that more choice always increases welfare.

Argument

Schwartz draws on psychological research, including the famous jam study by Sheena Iyengar, in which shoppers offered 24 varieties of jam were far less likely to buy than those offered six. He distinguishes "maximizers", who seek the best possible option, from "satisficers", who settle for good enough, arguing maximizers suffer most from abundant choice.

Consequences of too much choice

Schwartz identifies choice paralysis, escalation of expectations, self-blame when choices disappoint, and opportunity costs as mechanisms by which abundance can reduce satisfaction and well-being.

Reception

The book was widely reviewed and popularised the idea of choice overload; Schwartz's related TED talk has been viewed millions of times. Some later research has questioned the size and universality of the choice-overload effect.
