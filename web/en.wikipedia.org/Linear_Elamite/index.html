Linear Elamite is a writing system used in ancient Elam, in what is now southwestern Iran, during the late third and early second millennia BC (roughly 2300–1880 BC). It is attested in a small corpus of inscriptions — on silver vessels ("gunagi" beakers), stone monuments, bricks and seals — amounting to only a few dozen known texts, many of them short. It developed in the same milieu as the earlier Proto-Elamite script, though the two are not simply related as parent and descendant.

History

The script was first identified from inscriptions found at Susa and other Elamite sites in the early twentieth century. Its most famous attestations include dedicatory inscriptions of the Elamite king Puzur-Inshushinak (c. 2100 BC) and texts associated with later rulers of the Sukkalmah period. For most of the twentieth century Linear Elamite resisted decipherment because of the brevity of the corpus and the lack of a long bilingual text.

Decipherment

Beginning in 2020, the French archaeologist François Desset and colleagues announced a substantially complete decipherment, arguing that Linear Elamite is a purely phonetic (syllabic) writing system — unusual for its era — with roughly seventy to eighty signs. The breakthrough relied on a series of silver beakers from a private collection, said to come from the Kam-Firouz region, bearing parallel formulaic inscriptions naming kings and gods, which could be matched against known Elamite royal names in cuneiform. Published in peer-reviewed form in 2022, the decipherment has been broadly welcomed, though specialists continue to refine sign values and debate some readings.

If sustained, the decipherment makes the Elamite language — a language isolate unrelated to Semitic or Indo-European neighbours — readable in its own script for the first time, and pushes back the history of purely phonetic writing.

Signary

The signs are "linear" in form — geometric, drawn with straight strokes — in contrast to the wedge-shaped impressions of cuneiform, which Elam also used. Writing appears to run generally left to right in horizontal lines, sometimes in columns.
