Babe Ruth

George Herman "Babe" Ruth (February 6, 1895 – August 16, 1948) was an American professional baseball player whose career in Major League Baseball spanned 22 seasons, from 1914 to 1935. Nicknamed "the Bambino" and "the Sultan of Swat", he is widely regarded as one of the greatest sports heroes in American culture.

Career

Ruth began as a star left-handed pitcher for the Boston Red Sox, winning three World Series, before being sold to the New York Yankees in 1920 — a transaction later blamed for the "Curse of the Bambino". As an outfielder with the Yankees he transformed the game with his home run hitting, setting records including 60 home runs in 1927 and 714 in his career.

Legacy

Ruth helped establish the Yankees dynasty of the 1920s and 1930s, winning seven World Series in total. He was among the first five players elected to the Baseball Hall of Fame in 1936. His career home run record stood until Hank Aaron surpassed it in 1974.

Personal life

Raised largely in a Baltimore orphanage and reformatory, Ruth became a national celebrity during the Roaring Twenties, famous for his appetite for food, drink and publicity as well as for his charitable visits to children's hospitals. He died of cancer in 1948.
