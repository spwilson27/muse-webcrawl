Preamble to the Constitution of India

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
The Preamble is the introductory statement of the Constitution of India, adopted in 1949.

The Preamble to the Constitution of India is the introductory statement of the Constitution, setting out its guiding purpose and principles. Based on the Objectives Resolution moved by Jawaharlal Nehru in the Constituent Assembly in December 1946, it declares India to be a sovereign, socialist, secular, and democratic republic and promises its citizens justice, liberty, equality, and fraternity. Its text reads:

Preamble (India)

Adopted: 26 November 1949 (in force 26 January 1950)

Amended: Once — the 42nd Amendment, 1976, added "socialist", "secular", and "integrity"

Calligraphy: Prem Behari Narain Raizada

WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a SOVEREIGN SOCIALIST SECULAR DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC and to secure to all its citizens:
JUSTICE, social, economic and political;
LIBERTY of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship;
EQUALITY of status and of opportunity;
and to promote among them all
FRATERNITY assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation;
IN OUR CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY this twenty-sixth day of November, 1949, do HEREBY ADOPT, ENACT AND GIVE TO OURSELVES THIS CONSTITUTION.

Status and interpretation

The Supreme Court of India initially held (in the Berubari Union case, 1960) that the Preamble was not part of the Constitution, but reversed itself in the landmark Kesavananda Bharati case (1973), holding that the Preamble is part of the Constitution and reflects its "basic structure", which Parliament may not amend away. It is not directly enforceable in court, but serves as a key to interpreting ambiguous provisions. The words "socialist" and "secular" and the phrase "unity and integrity" were inserted by the Constitution (42nd Amendment) Act of 1976 during the Emergency — the only amendment ever made to the Preamble. The original calligraphed Constitution page was illuminated by the artist Nandalal Bose and handwritten by Prem Behari Narain Raizada.
