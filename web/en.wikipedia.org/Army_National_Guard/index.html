Army National Guard

The Army National Guard (ARNG) is the land force component of the United States National Guard and a reserve component of the United States Army. It is organised in each US state, territory and the District of Columbia, with units that normally serve under the command of state governors but can be federalised and called into the service of the United States by the President.

Dual role

Under the dual-status system, Army National Guard units respond to state emergencies — natural disasters, civil disturbances and other crises — under the governor's authority (State Active Duty or Title 32 status), while also training to federal standards so that they can be mobilised under Title 10 for overseas combat and support missions. Guard combat brigades, aviation units and support formations have served extensively in the wars in Iraq and Afghanistan.

History

The Army National Guard traces its origins to the colonial militias; the date of 13 December 1636, when the Massachusetts Bay Colony organised its militia regiments, is celebrated as the National Guard's birthday. The modern structure was shaped by the Militia Act of 1903, which standardised the organised militia as the National Guard, and by legislation in the 20th century that integrated the Guard into the Army's total force.
