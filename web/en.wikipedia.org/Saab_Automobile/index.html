Saab Automobile

Saab Automobile AB was a car manufacturer founded in Sweden in 1945 when its parent company, Saab AB, began a project to design a small automobile. The first production model, the Saab 92, was launched in 1949. Known for idiosyncratic engineering derived from its aircraft heritage — front-wheel drive, turbocharging, wraparound windscreens, and ignition keys between the front seats — Saab developed a loyal following despite its small market share.

History

In 1968 the parent company merged with Scania-Vabis to form Saab-Scania. In 1989, General Motors took a 50 percent stake in the automobile division, gaining full control in 2000. GM sold Saab to Dutch sports car maker Spyker in 2010, but the company was declared bankrupt in December 2011 after financing efforts collapsed. Most of its assets were purchased by National Electric Vehicle Sweden (NEVS), which briefly resumed production of the 9-3 before losing the right to use the Saab name in 2014.

Models

Notable models include the Saab 92, 96, 99, 900 (particularly the classic 900 of 1978–1993), 9000, 9-3, and 9-5. The Saab 900 Turbo of the 1980s became an emblematic example of early mass-market turbocharging. Saab also supplied the basis for a modest rally and safety-innovation record, pioneering features such as headlamp wipers, heated seats, and side-impact protection systems.
