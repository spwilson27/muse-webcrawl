Samadhi

Samādhi (Sanskrit: समाधि), in Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism and yogic schools, is a state of meditative consciousness and the highest stage in the practice of meditation. In the yogic tradition it is the eighth and final limb of Patañjali's aṣṭāṅga yoga, in which the meditator's awareness of the distinction between subject and object dissolves.

In Hindu yoga

In the Yoga Sūtras, samādhi follows concentration (dhāraṇā) and meditation (dhyāna). Texts distinguish forms such as samprajñāta (with an object of awareness) and asamprajñāta (without object), and sabīja ("with seed") versus nirbīja ("seedless") samādhi. Attainment of samādhi is associated with the realization of the self (ātman) and with progress toward liberation (mokṣa).

In Buddhism

In Buddhism, samādhi is one of the three divisions of the Noble Eightfold Path — "right concentration" (sammā samādhi) — cultivated through the meditative absorptions known as the jhānas. It is developed together with morality (śīla) and wisdom (prajñā) as the ground from which insight into the nature of reality arises. In Indian usage, samādhi also denotes the tomb or memorial shrine of a saint.
