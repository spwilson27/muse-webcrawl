Thermal efficiency

In thermodynamics, thermal efficiency is a dimensionless measure of how effectively a heat engine converts heat input into useful work output, defined as the ratio of net work output to heat input. By the second law of thermodynamics it is always less than one.

Carnot efficiency

The maximum possible efficiency between two reservoirs is given by the Carnot efficiency, 1 − TC/TH, where temperatures are absolute. Real engines achieve less: typical petrol engines reach roughly 20–35%, diesel engines up to about 40%, and combined-cycle power plants around 60%.
