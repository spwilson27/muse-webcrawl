Hawker (trade)

A hawker is a vendor of merchandise that can be easily transported; the term is broadly similar to a peddler or costermonger. Hawkers typically move from place to place, selling goods from a cart, stall or basket in streets and public places, and commonly advertise their wares by shouting or chanting.

History

Street hawking is among the oldest forms of retail trade. Hawkers supplied towns with food, drink and small goods long before fixed shops were common, and they feature in literature and song — from London's costermongers to the cried trades recorded in street literature. In Britain, hawkers and pedlars were licensed under legislation from the 19th century.

Around the world

In Singapore and Malaysia, the word refers especially to food stalls gathered in hawker centres, a celebrated part of the local food culture inscribed by UNESCO in 2020. In cities across South Asia, Africa and Latin America, hawkers remain a major part of the informal economy, selling snacks, produce and household goods, though their right to occupy public space is often contested by municipal authorities.
