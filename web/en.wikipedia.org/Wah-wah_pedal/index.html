Wah-wah pedal

A wah-wah pedal (or simply wah pedal) is a guitar effects pedal that alters the tone and frequencies of the guitar signal to create a distinctive sound that mimics vowel-like, human-voice qualities — hence the onomatopoeic name "wah". Rocking the footplate sweeps a resonant peak filter up and down the frequency spectrum.

History

The wah circuit was developed in 1966 by engineer Brad Plunkett at Warwick Electronics, maker of Thomas Organ amplifiers, and marketed through Vox as the Clyde McCoy model (named for a trumpeter known for a wah-like muted style) and later the Cry Baby. The effect spread rapidly: Eric Clapton used it with Cream, and Jimi Hendrix made it central to recordings such as "Voodoo Child (Slight Return)".

Uses

Beyond expressive lead playing, rhythmically rocked wah became a signature of funk guitar in the 1970s — famously on Isaac Hayes's "Theme from Shaft" (1971), played by Charles Pitts. "Auto-wah" or envelope-filter variants sweep the filter in response to playing dynamics rather than a pedal. The wah remains one of the most iconic guitar effects, produced by Dunlop, Vox, Morley, and many others.
