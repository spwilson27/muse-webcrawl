Henry Moseley

Henry Gwyn Jeffreys Moseley (23 November 1887 – 10 August 1915) was an English physicist whose contribution to physics was the justification from physical laws of the concept of atomic number.

Henry Moseley

Born: 23 November 1887, Weymouth
Died: 10 August 1915, Gallipoli (killed in action)
Known for: Moseley's law, atomic number

Work

Working in Manchester under Ernest Rutherford in 1913–14, Moseley measured X-ray spectra of elements and found a systematic relation — Moseley's law — between frequency and atomic number, resolving anomalies in the periodic table (argon/potassium, cobalt/nickel) and predicting missing elements.

Death and legacy

Moseley was killed at Gallipoli in the First World War, aged 27. His death is often cited as a reason Britain later kept scientists out of combat roles; many believed he would have won the Nobel Prize in Physics.
