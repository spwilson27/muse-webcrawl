Agrology

Agrology is the branch of soil science concerned with the production of crops. Agrologists study the chemical, physical, and biological properties of soils in relation to plant growth, and apply that knowledge to farm management, land classification, and conservation. The term is used especially in Canada, where "professional agrologist" (P.Ag.) is a regulated designation in several provinces.

Scope

Agrology overlaps with agronomy but emphasizes the soil resource: soil survey and classification, fertility and fertilizer recommendations, irrigation and drainage, erosion control, and reclamation of disturbed land. Practitioners also advise on environmental compliance and land-use planning.

Professional regulation

In Canada, provincial institutes of agrologists — such as the British Columbia Institute of Agrologists and the Alberta Institute of Agrologists — set standards of practice and ethics, and the title is protected by law. Agrology degrees combine coursework in pedology, plant science, chemistry, and economics.

History

The discipline developed in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries alongside agricultural experiment stations and soil surveys. In the Soviet Union, "agrology" formed part of the state agricultural sciences; in North America the profession organized formally after the Second World War.
