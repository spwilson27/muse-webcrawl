Galdhøpiggen

Galdhøpiggen is the highest mountain in Norway, Scandinavia, and Northern Europe. It stands 2,469 metres (8,100 ft) above sea level in Lom Municipality in Innlandet county, within the Jotunheimen mountain range and Jotunheimen National Park.

Geography

The summit is surrounded by glaciers, including Styggebreen to the north, and is one of more than 250 peaks above 2,000 metres in the Jotunheimen area. A small cabin, built at the summit, serves hikers in the summer season. The mountain's prominence and isolation make it a landmark visible from great distances in clear weather.

Climbing

Galdhøpiggen was first climbed in 1850 by the schoolteacher Steinar Sulheim together with Ingebrigt Flotten and Lars Arnesen. The two standard routes begin either from Spiterstulen, crossing the Styggebreen glacier (usually with a guide), or from Juvasshytta, a shorter route across the glacier to the summit. Thousands of hikers ascend the mountain each summer, and it is also a venue for summer skiing at the nearby Galdhøpiggen Summer Ski Centre on the Juvassbreen glacier.

Name

The name combines gald, a steep mountain road or pass, with høpiggen, "the high peak" — the peak by the high pass.
