Michael Milken

Michael Robert Milken (born July 4, 1946) is an American financier and philanthropist. He is best known for his role in developing the market for high-yield bonds ("junk bonds") while at Drexel Burnham Lambert in the 1970s and 1980s, and for his conviction in 1990 on charges of securities and reporting violations.

Early life and education

Milken was born in Encino, California. He graduated from the University of California, Berkeley, in 1968 and received an MBA from the Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania, where he was influenced by studies showing that higher-risk bonds had historically outperformed investment-grade debt.

Drexel Burnham Lambert

At Drexel, Milken built the high-yield bond department into the dominant force in corporate finance, funding companies such as MCI Communications, McCaw Cellular and Turner Broadcasting, as well as many leveraged buyouts. By the mid-1980s his network of investors was so influential that the firm's annual "Predators' Ball" conference became a fixture of Wall Street culture.

Indictment, conviction and pardon

Milken was indicted in 1989 on 98 counts of racketeering and securities fraud. In 1990 he pleaded guilty to six counts of securities and reporting violations, was fined $600 million and sentenced to ten years in prison, later reduced; he served about two years. In February 2020, President Donald Trump granted him a full pardon.

Philanthropy and later work

After his release, Milken founded the Milken Institute, a nonprofit economic think tank known for its Global Conference, and Prostate Cancer Foundation, which has raised hundreds of millions of dollars for research. His Milken Family Foundation supports education initiatives.
