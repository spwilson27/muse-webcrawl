Hong Kong International Airport

Hong Kong International Airport (IATA: HKG, ICAO: VHHH) is an international airport on the island of Chek Lap Kok in western Hong Kong. The airport, also known as Chek Lap Kok Airport, opened in 1998, replacing the former Kai Tak Airport in Kowloon. It is a major hub for Cathay Pacific, Hong Kong Airlines, HK Express and other carriers, and is one of the world's largest passenger airports as well as the world's busiest cargo airport for much of the period since 2010.

History

The airport was built on a largely artificial island formed by levelling the islands of Chek Lap Kok and Lam Chau and reclaiming land from the sea, as the centrepiece of the Airport Core Programme, one of the most expensive construction projects of its time. It opened for commercial operations on 6 July 1998, though cargo operations suffered serious teething problems in its first months. The former airport at Kai Tak, constrained by its urban location and a famously demanding approach over Kowloon, closed the same day.

Facilities

Terminal 1, designed by Foster + Partners, was one of the largest airport terminal buildings in the world at opening; Terminal 2 and the SkyPier ferry terminal serve cross-boundary passengers to the Pearl River Delta. The airport has two parallel runways designated 07L/25R and 07R/25L, and a third runway (07C/25C) entered service under the Three-runway System expansion, completed in 2024 with the reconfiguration of the existing north runway. The Automated People Mover connects the terminals and the Midfield Concourse. The Airport Express railway links the airport to central Hong Kong in about 24 minutes, and the Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macau Bridge, opened in 2018, connects the airport island to Macau and Zhuhai.

Traffic

Before the COVID-19 pandemic, the airport handled about 74.7 million passengers in 2019 and over 4.8 million tonnes of cargo. It regularly ranks among the top airports worldwide in Skytrax surveys and has repeatedly been named the world's busiest cargo airport.
