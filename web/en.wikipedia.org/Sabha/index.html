Sabha

Sabha (Sanskrit: सभा) refers, in ancient Indian tradition, to an assembly, congregation, or council. The term appears in early Vedic literature, where the sabha was one of the principal political and social institutions of the tribe, alongside the samiti (a broader folk assembly). The sabha is generally described as a smaller, more select body — sometimes characterised as a council of elders or notables — that deliberated on matters of governance, justice, and ritual, and also served as a venue for social activity, debate, and games such as dice.

In the Vedic period

References to the sabha occur in the Rigveda and later Vedic texts. Scholars debate the exact distinction between the sabha and the samiti: the sabha is often understood as an elite council attached to the king's court, while the samiti represented the wider assembly of the people. Women appear to have participated in the sabha in the earliest period, though their role diminished over time.

Later usage

In later Indian history, sabha came to denote assembly halls and councils in general — including village assemblies in South India, seats of learned Brahmins, and in the present day, legislative and socio-religious bodies. The word survives in the names of India's parliamentary houses, the Lok Sabha (House of the People) and Rajya Sabha (Council of States), and in terms such as gram sabha (village assembly).
