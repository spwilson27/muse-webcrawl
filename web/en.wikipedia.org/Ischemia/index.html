Ischemia

Ischemia (also spelled ischaemia) is a restriction in blood supply to any tissue, muscle group or organ of the body, causing a shortage of oxygen needed for cellular metabolism. It is generally caused by problems with blood vessels — narrowing (stenosis), blockage by a clot (thrombosis) or embolism, or external compression — and results in damage or dysfunction of the affected tissue.

Types

Myocardial ischemia, caused by narrowed coronary arteries, produces angina pectoris and can lead to heart attack. Cerebral ischemia causes stroke. Peripheral ischemia affects the limbs, while mesenteric ischemia, affecting the intestines, is a surgical emergency.

Consequences and treatment

Beyond a certain duration without oxygen, tissue dies (infarction), and reperfusion after prolonged ischemia can itself cause injury. Treatment aims to restore blood flow — by medication, angioplasty, stenting or bypass surgery — and to address risk factors such as atherosclerosis, diabetes and smoking. Contrast with anaemia, which reduces the oxygen-carrying capacity of blood rather than its flow.
