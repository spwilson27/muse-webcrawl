Richard Montague

Richard Merritt Montague (September 20, 1930 – March 7, 1971) was an American mathematician and philosopher, noted for founding Montague grammar, a formal approach to natural language semantics.

Life and career

Born in Stockton, California, Montague earned his PhD at the University of California, Berkeley in 1957 under Alfred Tarski. He spent his career at the University of California, Los Angeles, where he supervised several influential students including Nino Cocchiarella and Hans Kamp.

Work

Montague made contributions to set theory, model theory and the foundations of logic. His approach to semantics, treating English as a formal language interpretable in model-theoretic terms, was laid out in papers including "English as a Formal Language" (1970), "Universal Grammar" (1970) and "The Proper Treatment of Quantification in Ordinary English" (1973), the last published posthumously.

Death

Montague was murdered in his Los Angeles home in 1971; the crime remains unsolved. His collected papers were published as Formal Philosophy (1974).
