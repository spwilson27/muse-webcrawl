Peter Jackson

Sir Peter Robert Jackson (born 31 October 1961) is a New Zealand filmmaker. He is best known as the director, writer and producer of The Lord of the Rings trilogy (2001–2003) and The Hobbit trilogy (2012–2014), both adapted from the novels by J. R. R. Tolkien. The Return of the King won eleven Academy Awards, including Best Picture and Best Director.

Early films

Jackson began with low-budget splatter comedies — Bad Taste (1987), Meet the Feebles (1989) and Braindead (1992) — before critical acclaim for Heavenly Creatures (1994), which earned an Academy Award nomination for its screenplay, co-written with his partner Fran Walsh.

Middle-earth and beyond

Filming the three Lord of the Rings films back-to-back in New Zealand was an unprecedented production that transformed the country's film industry and visual-effects company Weta Digital. Jackson followed with King Kong (2005) and The Lovely Bones (2009) before returning to Middle-earth for The Hobbit. His later documentary work includes the First World War film They Shall Not Grow Old (2018), which colourised and restored archive footage, and the Beatles documentary series Get Back (2021).
