The Gawhar Shad Mosque (Persian: مسجد گوهرشاد) is a grand congregational mosque in Mashhad, Razavi Khorasan Province, Iran. Built during the Timurid period, it stands within the precinct of the Imam Reza shrine complex and is regarded as one of the masterpieces of Persian architecture.

History

The mosque was commissioned in 1416 by Gawhar Shad, the wife of Shah Rukh, son and successor of Timur. Its architect was Qavam al-Din ibn Zayn al-Din Shirazi, one of the foremost architects of the era, and construction was completed in 1418. The calligraphic inscriptions were executed by Gawhar Shad's son, Baysunghur, himself a celebrated calligrapher.

The mosque survived earthquakes and periods of damage; during Safavid and later times it was repeatedly repaired and restored. In July 1935 the mosque's courtyard was the scene of the Goharshad Mosque rebellion, an uprising against the secularising dress laws of Reza Shah, which was bloodily suppressed by government forces.

Architecture

The mosque follows the classic Persian four-iwan plan arranged around a large marble-paved sahn (courtyard), roughly 50 by 55 metres. The south iwan leads to the main domed prayer hall (maqsurah), whose dome rises about 41 metres and is sheathed in turquoise and blue glazed tiles. Two slender minarets, about 43 metres high, flank the great iwan. The whole complex is renowned for its glazed tile mosaic work, elegant proportions and monumental Kufic and Thuluth inscriptions.
