Mount Redoubt, or Redoubt Volcano, is an active stratovolcano in the Aleutian Range of the U.S. state of Alaska. Located at the head of the Chigmit Mountains in Lake Clark National Park and Preserve, the mountain is just west of Cook Inlet, in the Kenai Peninsula Borough about 180 km (110 mi) southwest of Anchorage. At 10,197 feet (3,108 m), in just over 5 miles (8 km) Mount Redoubt rises 9,150 feet (2,700 m) above the surrounding valleys to the north, south and east.

Active for millennia, Mount Redoubt has erupted four times since first observation in 1902: in 1902, 1922, 1966, 1989 and 2009, with two questionable eruptions in 1881 and 1933. The eruption in 1989 spewed volcanic ash to a height of 45,000 ft (14 km) and caught KLM Flight 867 in flight, a Boeing 747 aircraft, which temporarily lost power in all four engines; the resulting damage cost US$80 million. The ash plume covered an area of about 7,700 sq mi (20,000 km²).

Description

Mount Redoubt is a steep-sided cone about 6 miles (10 km) across at its base and with a volume of 30 to 35 cubic kilometres (7.2 to 8.4 cu mi). Its summit hosts a crater about 1.8 km (1.1 mi) wide, breached to the north, holding a small crater lake and glaciers that spill down its flanks. The Drift River drains the north side of the volcano, and lahars from past eruptions have repeatedly threatened the Drift River oil terminal near its mouth.

2009 eruption

Following months of unrest, Mount Redoubt erupted explosively on 22–23 March 2009, and activity continued into early April. Ash fell on communities around Cook Inlet, including light dustings in Anchorage, and lahars inundated the Drift River valley. The Alaska Volcano Observatory monitored the eruption closely, issuing warnings that allowed aircraft and residents to prepare.

Monitoring

The volcano is monitored by the Alaska Volcano Observatory with seismometers, GPS stations, webcams and gas sensors. It is designated one of the highest-threat volcanoes in the United States because of its proximity to Anchorage, aviation routes across the North Pacific, and the oil facilities in Cook Inlet.
