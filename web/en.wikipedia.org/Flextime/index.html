Flextime

Flextime (also spelled flexitime) is a flexible hours schedule that allows workers to alter their workday start and finish times, typically within limits set by the employer. In contrast to traditional fixed schedules, employees under flextime choose when they work, subject to achieving the required total hours and being present during a designated "core time".

Origins

The concept was developed in Germany in the 1960s — the German term is Gleitzeit ("sliding time") — often credited to management consultant Christel Kämmerer, and was adopted by companies such as Messerschmitt-Bölkow-Blohm to ease commuting congestion. It spread through Europe and North America in the 1970s.

Variants and effects

Variants include flexitime banking systems, in which surplus hours are carried forward, and compressed workweeks. Studies generally associate flextime with improved job satisfaction and reduced absenteeism and commuting stress, though it requires careful coordination in team-based and customer-facing work.
