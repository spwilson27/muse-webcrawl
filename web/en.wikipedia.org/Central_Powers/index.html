The Central Powers, also known as the Central Empires, were one of the two main coalitions that fought in the First World War (1914–1918). It consisted of the German Empire, Austria-Hungary, the Ottoman Empire, and the Kingdom of Bulgaria; this system is also known as the Quadruple Alliance. The name "Central Powers" derives from the geographical position of these countries: all four were located between the Russian Empire in the east and France and the United Kingdom in the west.

Origins

The alliance grew out of the Dual Alliance of 1879 between Germany and Austria-Hungary, later joined by Italy in the Triple Alliance of 1882. Italy, however, declined to fight alongside its allies in 1914 and instead joined the Allies in 1915. The Ottoman Empire entered the war in late 1914, and Bulgaria joined in October 1915, attracted by the prospect of territorial gains at Serbia's expense.

Defeat and dissolution

The Central Powers were defeated in 1918. Bulgaria signed an armistice in September 1918, the Ottoman Empire followed in October, Austria-Hungary collapsed and signed the Armistice of Villa Giusti in November, and Germany signed the Armistice of 11 November 1918. The subsequent peace treaties — Versailles, Saint-Germain, Neuilly, Trianon, and Sèvres — dissolved the empires and redrew the map of Central and Eastern Europe.
