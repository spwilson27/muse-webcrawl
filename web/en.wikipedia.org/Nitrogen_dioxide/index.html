Nitrogen dioxide is a chemical compound with the formula NO₂. One of several nitrogen oxides, it is a reddish-brown, highly toxic gas with a pungent, acrid odor. Nitrogen dioxide is a major air pollutant, produced mainly by combustion of fossil fuels in vehicles, power plants, and industrial processes, and it plays a key role in the formation of photochemical smog and acid rain. It exists in equilibrium with its colorless dimer, dinitrogen tetroxide (N₂O₄), favored at lower temperatures.

Properties and structure

The NO₂ molecule is bent, with an O–N–O angle of about 134°, and is paramagnetic because of an unpaired electron. Below 21.15 °C it condenses to a brown liquid, and at still lower temperatures it dimerizes almost completely to N₂O₄. Nitrogen dioxide reacts with water to form nitric acid and nitric oxide: 3 NO₂ + H₂O → 2 HNO₃ + NO, a reaction central both to acid rain and to the industrial Ostwald process for nitric acid production.

Sources and environmental role

NO₂ is emitted during high-temperature combustion, including in internal combustion engines and gas-fired appliances. In the atmosphere it is part of the NOₓ family, which reacts in sunlight with volatile organic compounds to produce ground-level ozone and smog. Lightning also produces nitrogen oxides naturally. NO₂ contributes to the formation of fine particulate matter and to nitrogen deposition that eutrophies ecosystems.

Health effects

Exposure to nitrogen dioxide irritates the airways, aggravates asthma and other respiratory diseases, and is associated with reduced lung function and increased susceptibility to respiratory infection. Occupational exposure in silos ("silo filler's disease") can cause potentially fatal pulmonary edema. Regulatory agencies set ambient standards for NO₂ as a criteria air pollutant.

Uses

Nitrogen dioxide is an intermediate in the manufacture of nitric acid and is used as an oxidizer in rocket propellants (usually as N₂O₄), as a nitrating agent, and as a polymerization inhibitor for acrylates.
