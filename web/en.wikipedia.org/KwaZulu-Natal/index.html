KwaZulu-Natal (also referred to as KZN) is a province of South Africa created in 1994 by the merger of the province of Natal with the bantustan of KwaZulu. Situated in the southeast of the country, it borders the Indian Ocean and the countries of Mozambique, Eswatini, and Lesotho, as well as the South African provinces of Mpumalanga, Free State, and Eastern Cape.

Geography

The province rises from a subtropical coastal plain to the dramatic Drakensberg escarpment in the west, which includes Tugela Falls, among the tallest waterfalls in the world. The uKhahlamba Drakensberg Park and the iSimangaliso Wetland Park are UNESCO World Heritage Sites. Durban, on the coast, is South Africa's busiest port and a major industrial and tourist centre.

History

The region was the heartland of the Zulu Kingdom built by Shaka in the early nineteenth century. British colonists established the Colony of Natal in the 1840s; the Anglo-Zulu War of 1879, famous for the battles of Isandlwana and Rorke's Drift, ended with the annexation of Zululand. During apartheid, Zulu-speaking areas were consolidated into the KwaZulu homeland, led by Mangosuthu Buthelezi's Inkatha movement. The transition to democracy in the early 1990s was marked by severe political violence in the province before the first democratic elections of 1994.

Demographics and economy

KwaZulu-Natal is the traditional home of the Zulu people, the largest ethnic group in South Africa, and also has one of the country's largest Indian South African communities, descended largely from indentured labourers brought to work on sugar plantations from 1860. Sugar cane, forestry, and subtropical fruit dominate agriculture, while Durban's port, petrochemicals, and manufacturing anchor the economy; the province is also a leading domestic tourism destination.
