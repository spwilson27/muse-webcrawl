Producers Guild of America Awards

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The Producers Guild Awards ceremony

The Producers Guild of America Awards, originally known as the Golden Laurel Awards, are presented annually by the Producers Guild of America (PGA) to honour outstanding producers of film and television. The ceremony has been held annually since 1990, when the first Golden Laurel Awards were presented at the Beverly Wilshire Hotel. The show is usually held in January in the run-up to the Academy Awards.

Darryl F. Zanuck Award

The top film prize is the Darryl F. Zanuck Award for Outstanding Producer of Theatrical Motion Pictures. Because the Producers Guild uses a preferential ballot similar to the Academy's and shares much of its membership with the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences, its winner has historically been one of the strongest predictors of the Academy Award for Best Picture, matching the Oscar winner in a majority of years since the award's inception. Past winners include Driving Miss Daisy, The Silence of the Lambs, Forrest Gump, Gladiator, The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King, Slumdog Millionaire, The King's Speech, The Artist, Argo, 12 Years a Slave, Green Book, 1917, Nomadland, CODA, Everything Everywhere All at Once and Oppenheimer.

Other categories

Television honours include the Norman Felton Award for drama series, the Danny Thomas Award for comedy series, and awards for limited series, non-fiction, children's, sports and streamed or televised movies. The Guild also presents special honours such as the Milestone Award, the David O. Selznick Achievement Award in theatrical films, and the Norman Lear Achievement Award in television.
