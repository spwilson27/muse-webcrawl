Data protection

Data protection is the process of safeguarding important information from corruption, compromise or loss, and in legal usage, the body of law governing the processing of personal data. The term covers both operational practices — backup, encryption, access control — and the privacy rights of individuals whose data is collected and used by organizations.

Legal frameworks

The German state of Hesse enacted the world's first data protection law in 1970. The Council of Europe's Convention 108 (1981) was the first binding international treaty on the subject. In the European Union, the Data Protection Directive of 1995 was replaced in 2018 by the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), which grants individuals rights of access, rectification, and erasure, and imposes substantial fines for non-compliance. Other jurisdictions, including Brazil (LGPD), California (CCPA/CPRA), and Japan (APPI), have adopted their own comprehensive regimes.

Principles

Modern data protection law generally rests on principles of lawfulness, purpose limitation, data minimization, accuracy, storage limitation, integrity and confidentiality, and accountability. Organizations are typically required to have a lawful basis for processing, to protect data with appropriate technical and organizational measures, and to notify authorities and affected individuals of serious data breaches.

Technical measures

Technical data protection includes encryption in transit and at rest, pseudonymization, access controls, audit logging, and regular backups with tested recovery procedures — the "3-2-1" strategy of keeping three copies on two media with one off-site is a widely cited guideline.
