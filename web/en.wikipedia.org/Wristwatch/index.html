Wristwatch

A wristwatch is a portable timepiece designed to be carried or worn on the wrist, typically attached by a strap or metal bracelet. It normally displays the time of day, and sometimes the day, date and other information. Wristwatches may be powered by a wound mechanical movement, by automatic self-winding, or by a battery driving a quartz movement or electronic display.

History

Watches worn as bracelets were made for women as jewellery from the 19th century; one of the earliest references is a wristwatch commissioned by the Queen of Naples in 1810 from Breguet. Men long preferred pocket watches, regarding wristwatches (derisively called "wristlets") as effeminate. Practical demand came from soldiers and aviators: troops in the Boer War and the First World War found wristwatches far more usable in combat, and Alberto Santos-Dumont's need to time flights hands-free led Louis Cartier to create the Santos watch in 1904. After 1918 the wristwatch rapidly displaced the pocket watch for men.

Technology

Mechanical watches store energy in a mainspring that drives a gear train regulated by a balance wheel and escapement. The introduction of quartz watches — pioneered commercially by the Seiko Astron in 1969 — brought far greater accuracy and lower cost, provoking the "quartz crisis" in the Swiss industry. Radio-controlled and GPS watches synchronise with external time signals, and smartwatches, popularised by devices such as the Apple Watch (2015), combine timekeeping with computing, notifications and health sensing.

Styles and functions

Common specialised types include dive watches with high water resistance, chronographs with stopwatch functions, pilot's and field watches, and dress watches. Complications — extra functions beyond hours and minutes — range from calendars and moon phases to tourbillons and minute repeaters in high horology.
