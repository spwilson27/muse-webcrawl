Authenticated encryption

Authenticated encryption
FieldCryptography
ProvidesConfidentiality, integrity, authenticity
ExamplesAES-GCM, ChaCha20-Poly1305, AES-CCM, AES-OCB

Authenticated encryption (AE) is a form of encryption that simultaneously assures the confidentiality and authenticity of data. In addition to encrypting a plaintext, an AE scheme produces a short authentication tag that allows a receiver to verify that the ciphertext has not been altered and was produced by a holder of the secret key.

Approaches

Authenticated encryption can be built from generic composition of a cipher and a message authentication code — the encrypt-then-MAC construction is generally preferred — or from dedicated modes designed to provide both properties in one pass. Widely deployed AEAD (AE with associated data) modes include AES in Galois/Counter Mode (AES-GCM), ChaCha20-Poly1305, and CCM.

Associated data

AEAD schemes accept additional associated data that is authenticated but not encrypted, such as packet headers. AEAD interfaces are the standard way encryption is exposed in modern protocols such as TLS 1.3, where AEAD ciphers are mandatory.
