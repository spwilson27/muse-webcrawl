Gaiwan

A gaiwan (simplified Chinese: 盖碗; traditional Chinese: 蓋碗; pinyin: gàiwǎn) or zhong is a Chinese lidded bowl without handle, with a saucer, used for the infusion of tea leaves and the consumption of tea. It was invented during the Ming dynasty. It consists of a saucer, bowl, and lid, traditionally made of porcelain but also found in glass and other materials.

Use

Tea leaves are placed in the bowl, hot water is added, and the lid is used both to keep the infusion warm and to hold back the leaves while drinking directly from the bowl. The lid can also be used to stir the tea and to push leaves aside. Gaiwans are central to gongfu tea preparation, where their small size (typically 70–150 ml) allows repeated short infusions that reveal the changing character of the tea, especially oolong and pu'er teas.

History

Lidded tea bowls appeared under the Ming dynasty, when steeping loose leaves replaced whisked powdered tea. The gaiwan became strongly associated with Sichuan tea-house culture, where large three-piece gaiwans remain in everyday use.
