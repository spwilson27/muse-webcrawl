Steiff

A Steiff teddy bear with the company's trademark "button in ear".

Margarete Steiff GmbH is a German manufacturer of plush toys, best known for its teddy bears. The company was founded in 1880 by Margarete Steiff in Giengen an der Brenz, Germany. Steiff is famous for its high-quality materials, hand-finishing, and the distinctive metal button in the ear of its animals, a trademark introduced in 1904 that remains one of the most recognisable marks in the toy industry.

History

Margarete Steiff (1847–1909), who had contracted polio as a child and used a wheelchair, trained as a seamstress and began selling felt garments and household items from her workshop in Giengen. In 1880 she produced a small felt elephant based on a pattern she had seen, originally intended as a pincushion; children took to it as a toy, and the elephant became the company's first successful soft toy.

Margarete's nephew Richard Steiff joined the firm and, in 1902, designed a jointed bear based on sketches of bears at Stuttgart Zoo. The bear, known as the "55 PB", was shown at the Leipzig Toy Fair in 1903, where an American buyer ordered 3,000 of them. The animals were marketed in the United States as "teddy bears", after President Theodore Roosevelt, and became an international success.

Trademark

To distinguish genuine products from the many imitations that appeared, Franz Steiff devised the "button in ear" (Knopf im Ohr) in 1904: a small metal button bearing the Steiff name, later accompanied by an ear tag. The slogan "Only the best is good enough for children" guided the company's production philosophy.

Products

Steiff animals were traditionally made from mohair, alpaca, wool felt and woven plush, with wooden excelsior stuffing later replaced by synthetic fibres. The company produces both contemporary children's toys and limited-edition replicas of historic designs for collectors; antique Steiff bears regularly achieve high prices at auction.
