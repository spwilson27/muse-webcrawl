Freedmen's Bureau

The Bureau of Refugees, Freedmen, and Abandoned Lands, usually referred to as simply the Freedmen's Bureau, was a U.S. federal government agency created in 1865 to aid freed slaves (freedmen) and poor whites in the South during Reconstruction after the American Civil War.

Formed3 March 1865
Dissolved1872
Parent agencyUnited States Department of War
CommissionerOliver O. Howard

Work

Headed by General Oliver Otis Howard, the Bureau distributed food and medical aid, established hospitals and schools (including support for institutions such as Howard University and Fisk University), supervised labor contracts, reunited families, and operated special courts. At its peak it ran more than 4,000 schools serving roughly 250,000 students.

Opposition and end

The Bureau was chronically underfunded and understaffed and faced violent opposition from white Southerners, including the Ku Klux Klan. President Andrew Johnson vetoed bills extending it, though Congress overrode him in 1866. Most functions ended by 1869 and the Bureau was formally abolished in 1872.
