Niger, officially the Republic of the Niger, is a landlocked country in West Africa named after the Niger River. It is bordered by Libya to the northeast, Chad to the east, Nigeria to the south, Benin and Burkina Faso to the southwest, Mali to the west, and Algeria to the northwest. With an area of nearly 1.27 million square kilometres, it is the largest country in West Africa, though much of its territory lies in the Sahara Desert.

Niger's population is predominantly rural and includes Hausa, Zarma-Songhai, Tuareg, Fulani and Kanuri peoples. The economy depends on subsistence agriculture, livestock, and exports of uranium and oil. Niger is consistently ranked among the world's least developed countries and faces recurring drought and food insecurity.

History

Niger gained independence from France in 1960 under its first president, Hamani Diori. Its subsequent history has been marked by military coups, including those of 1974, 1996, 1999, 2010 and 2023, when the military junta known as the National Council for the Safeguard of the Homeland seized power and removed President Mohamed Bazoum. Niger subsequently withdrew from the Economic Community of West African States along with Mali and Burkina Faso, forming the Alliance of Sahel States.

Geography

The Niger River flows through the more fertile southwest, while the north is dominated by the Sahara and the Aïr Mountains. Niger is a major uranium producer, with mines around Arlit, and began exporting crude oil by pipeline through Benin. Lake Chad in the southeast has shrunk dramatically over recent decades.
