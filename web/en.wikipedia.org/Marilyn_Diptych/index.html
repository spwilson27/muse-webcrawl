Marilyn Diptych

The Marilyn Diptych is a 1962 silkscreen painting by the American pop artist Andy Warhol. It consists of fifty images of the actress Marilyn Monroe, arranged in two panels of twenty-five: the left panel in vivid colour, the right in black and white, with the images progressively fading and smudging. The work is held by Tate and is displayed at Tate Modern in London.

Creation

Warhol made the work in the months following Monroe's death on 5 August 1962, basing every image on a single publicity photograph for the film Niagara (1953). He silkscreened twenty-five reproductions onto each of two canvases. The repeated image, reproduced with increasing misregistration and ink loss on the right-hand panel, has been read as a meditation on celebrity, mass media and mortality — the format echoes religious diptychs, with the coloured Monroe as the icon in life and the fading panel as her deterioration and death.

Reception

The Marilyn Diptych is among Warhol's most famous early pop works and a defining image of the 1960s. In 2004 it was ranked third in a poll of art experts for The Guardian as one of the most influential works of modern art. Warhol's other Marilyn silkscreens, such as Shot Sage Blue Marilyn (1964) — which sold at auction in 2022 for $195 million — use the same source photograph.
