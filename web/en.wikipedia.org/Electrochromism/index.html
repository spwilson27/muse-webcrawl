Electrochromism

Electrochromism
TypeReversible optical phenomenon
Common materialsTungsten oxide, nickel oxide, viologens, conducting polymers
ApplicationsSmart windows, mirrors, displays

Electrochromism is a phenomenon in which a material exhibits a reversible change in colour or opacity when an electric voltage is applied. Electrochromic materials change their optical properties as ions and electrons are inserted into or extracted from them.

Mechanism

In transition-metal oxides such as tungsten oxide (WO3), applying a voltage drives small ions such as H+ or Li+ into the film; the accompanying electrons reduce metal ions, producing strong optical absorption. Reversing the voltage bleaches the film. Organic materials such as viologens and conducting polymers change colour through redox reactions.

Applications

Electrochromic devices include smart windows that tint to control glare and solar heat gain in buildings and aircraft (such as the dimmable windows of the Boeing 787), automatically dimming rear-view mirrors, low-power displays, and adaptive camouflage research.
