Video game console emulator

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A video game console emulator is a type of emulator that allows a computing device to emulate a video game console's hardware and play its games on a different platform. Emulators are most often used to play games from obsolete consoles on modern personal computers and smartphones, and they are also a tool for homebrew development, fan translations, ROM hacking, and game preservation.

Operation

An emulator replicates the behaviour of the console's central processing unit, graphics and sound hardware, memory and input devices in software. Games are supplied as ROM images — copies of cartridge data — or disc images for optical-media systems. Accuracy varies: cycle-accurate emulators such as higan aim to reproduce the hardware exactly, while high-level emulation trades accuracy for speed and enhancements such as higher resolutions, widescreen patches, save states, and online play via netplay.

History

Console emulation became widespread in the mid-1990s with projects such as NESticle (NES, 1997), ZSNES and Snes9x (Super NES), UltraHLE (Nintendo 64, 1999), and bleem! for the PlayStation. Later emulators tackled more powerful hardware: Dolphin for the GameCube and Wii, PCSX2 for the PlayStation 2, RPCS3 for the PlayStation 3, and Ryujinx and Yuzu for the Nintendo Switch. Manufacturers themselves use emulation in products such as Nintendo's NES and SNES Classic mini consoles and in backward-compatibility and virtual-console services.

Legal issues

The legality of emulation is generally accepted in many jurisdictions — U.S. courts upheld commercial emulators in Sony Computer Entertainment v. Connectix (2000) and Sony v. bleem! — but distributing copyrighted ROMs, disc images, or console firmware/BIOS files is typically infringement, and circumventing copy protection may violate anti-circumvention law. Users are generally advised to dump software from media they own. Lawsuits in the 2020s against the Switch emulator Yuzu led to its discontinuation in 2024.
