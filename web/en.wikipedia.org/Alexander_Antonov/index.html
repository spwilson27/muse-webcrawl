Alexander Antonov

Alexander Stepanovich Antonov (Russian: Алекса́ндр Степа́нович Анто́нов; 26 July 1885 – 24 June 1922) was a Russian revolutionary and member of the Socialist Revolutionary Party who became a leader of the Tambov Rebellion, one of the largest peasant revolts against the Bolshevik government during the Russian Civil War.

Early life

Antonov was born in Moscow and raised in Kirsanov, in Tambov Governorate. He joined the Socialist Revolutionary Party as a young man and took part in revolutionary expropriations; arrested by the tsarist authorities, he was sentenced to death, commuted to penal servitude in Siberia. Freed after the February Revolution of 1917, he returned to Tambov and became chief of the district militia in Kirsanov.

Tambov Rebellion

Antonov broke with the Bolsheviks after clashes over forced grain requisitioning (prodrazvyorstka) and in 1920 helped organize a peasant uprising in Tambov province. As a leading commander of the rebel "Blue Army," his forces, numbering in the tens of thousands, controlled much of the countryside and disrupted grain collection. The Red Army, under Mikhail Tukhachevsky, suppressed the rebellion in 1921–22 with overwhelming force, mass arrests, hostage-taking, and, according to orders issued during the campaign, the use of poison gas against rebels hiding in forests.

Death

Antonov went into hiding after the rebellion's defeat. On 24 June 1922, Cheka operatives located him and his brother Dmitri near the village of Nizhny Shibryay; both were killed in the ensuing shoot-out.
