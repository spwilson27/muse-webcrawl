Chiapas

Chiapas, officially the Free and Sovereign State of Chiapas, is one of the 32 federal entities of Mexico. It is the southernmost state in the country, bordering the states of Oaxaca, Veracruz, and Tabasco, the nation of Guatemala, and the Pacific Ocean. Its capital and largest city is Tuxtla Gutiérrez. Chiapas has a population of over five million, including a large proportion of indigenous peoples, many of Maya descent.

Geography

The state ranges from Pacific coastal plains through the central highlands around San Cristóbal de las Casas to the tropical lowlands and rainforests of the Lacandon Jungle in the east. Major natural features include the Sumidero Canyon and the Grijalva River, whose dams supply much of Mexico's hydroelectric power.

History

Chiapas was a center of Classic Maya civilization; the ruins of Palenque, Yaxchilán, and Bonampak, famed for their architecture and murals, lie within the state. After the Spanish conquest the region was administered from Guatemala until it joined Mexico in 1824. In 1994 the state drew world attention when the Zapatista Army of National Liberation (EZLN) launched an uprising protesting the treatment of indigenous peoples, coinciding with the start of the North American Free Trade Agreement.

Economy

Agriculture — including coffee, corn, cacao, and bananas — remains central to the economy, along with hydroelectric generation and tourism centered on Maya archaeological sites and colonial cities.
