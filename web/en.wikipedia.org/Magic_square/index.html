Magic square

In recreational mathematics, a magic square is a square grid filled with distinct positive integers such that each cell contains a different integer and the sum of the integers in each row, column, and diagonal is the same. This constant sum is called the magic constant.

The Lo Shu square

The best-known magic square is the 3×3 Lo Shu square from ancient China, using the numbers 1 through 9:

492
357
816

Every row, column, and diagonal sums to 15. For a normal n×n square using the numbers 1 to n², the magic constant is n(n² + 1)/2.

History

Magic squares were known in China by 190 BCE and spread through the Islamic world, where mathematicians developed systematic construction methods, and into India and Europe. Albrecht Dürer engraved a famous 4×4 magic square in his 1514 work Melencolia I, in which the two middle cells of the bottom row read 15 and 14, the date of the engraving.

Construction and types

Standard methods exist for odd, doubly even, and singly even orders; the Siamese method constructs odd-order squares by placing successive numbers diagonally upward. Squares may also be pandiagonal, most-perfect, or alphamagic. Benjamin Franklin devised celebrated 8×8 squares with additional "bent diagonal" properties.
