Sick building syndrome

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sick building syndrome (SBS) is a condition in which people in a building suffer from symptoms of illness or feel unwell for no apparent reason. The symptoms tend to increase in severity with the time people spend in the building, and improve over time or even disappear when people are away from the building.

Symptoms

Occupants may complain of headache, eye, nose or throat irritation, dry cough, dry or itchy skin, dizziness and nausea, difficulty concentrating, and fatigue. In most cases, relief occurs soon after leaving the room or building. SBS differs from "building-related illness", in which symptoms are diagnosable and directly attributable to airborne building contaminants, such as Legionnaires' disease.

Causes

No single cause has been identified. Suspected contributing factors include inadequate ventilation (a concern that grew after 1970s energy-conservation measures reduced fresh-air exchange in sealed buildings), chemical contaminants from indoor sources such as adhesives, carpeting, copy machines and cleaning agents emitting volatile organic compounds, biological contaminants such as mould and bacteria, and psychosocial factors including work stress and low job satisfaction.

Prevention and remediation

Improving ventilation rates and air distribution, removing or controlling pollutant sources, regular maintenance of HVAC systems, and cleaning or replacing water-damaged materials are standard remediation steps. The World Health Organization estimated in the 1980s that up to 30% of new and remodelled buildings worldwide might generate excessive health complaints.
