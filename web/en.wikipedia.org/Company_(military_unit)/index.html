Company (military unit)

A company is a military unit, typically consisting of 100–250 soldiers and usually commanded by a major or a captain. Most companies are formed of three to seven platoons, although the exact number may vary by country, unit type, and functional specialty. Companies are usually grouped into battalions or regiments.

Organisation

In the United States Army, a rifle company is typically commanded by a captain, assisted by an executive officer and a first sergeant, and consists of a headquarters and three rifle platoons plus a weapons platoon. In artillery, the equivalent unit is a battery; in armoured and cavalry units, a troop; in Commonwealth armies, sub-units of certain corps, such as engineers and signals, are called squadrons.

Historical development

The term derives from the late medieval company of soldiers serving under a captain, and the company became a standard tactical and administrative unit in European armies in the early modern period. It has remained the smallest unit normally capable of independent administrative functions, with its own headquarters and support staff.
