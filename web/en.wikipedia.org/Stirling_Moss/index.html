Stirling Moss

Born17 September 1929, West Kensington, London, England
Died12 April 2020 (aged 90), London
F1 career1951–1961; 66 Grands Prix, 16 wins

Sir Stirling Craufurd Moss (17 September 1929 – 12 April 2020) was a British Formula One racing driver, widely described as "the greatest driver never to win the World Championship", having finished runner-up four times between 1955 and 1958.

Career

Moss won 212 of the 529 races he entered across categories, including 16 Formula One Grands Prix for Mercedes-Benz, Maserati, Vanwall, Cooper, and Lotus. In 1955 he became the first Briton to win the British Grand Prix and won the Mille Miglia in record time — averaging nearly 98 mph over 1,000 miles of Italian public roads, guided by navigator Denis Jenkinson's pioneering pace notes. In 1958 he lost the championship to Mike Hawthorn by a single point, having sportingly defended Hawthorn against a penalty that would have handed him the title.

Retirement

A serious crash at Goodwood in 1962 left Moss in a coma and ended his top-level career after testing showed his reflexes had dulled. He remained a prominent ambassador for motorsport, was knighted in 2000, and was inducted into the International Motorsports Hall of Fame.
