Motel

A motel, also known as a motor hotel, motor lodge, or motor court, is a hotel designed for motorists, usually with each room opening directly onto a parking area rather than onto an interior corridor. The word is a blend of motor and hotel.

History

Motels arose in the United States in the 1920s as automobile travel became widespread along new highways. The Motel Inn of San Luis Obispo, California, opened in 1925, is often credited as the first establishment to use the name. Motels differed from earlier auto camps and tourist courts by offering private rooms and bathrooms under one roof, and from downtown hotels by their roadside locations, low-rise architecture, and free parking.

After the Second World War, motel numbers boomed along U.S. highways; chains such as Holiday Inn (founded 1952) and Motel 6 (founded 1962) standardized the format. The construction of the Interstate Highway System later bypassed many older roadside motels, contributing to the decline of independent properties, though motels remain a common form of budget lodging worldwide.

Characteristics

Motels are typically one or two storeys arranged around a parking court, with exterior room doors, a small office, and limited amenities such as an ice machine or swimming pool. In some countries, notably South Korea and Japan, "motels" have acquired distinct cultural associations as short-stay lodging rented by the hour.
