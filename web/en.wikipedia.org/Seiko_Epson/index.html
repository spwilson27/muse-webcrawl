Seiko Epson

Seiko Epson, headquartered in Suwa, Nagano, is known for printers, projectors and precision devices.

Seiko Epson Corporation, commonly known as Epson, is a Japanese electronics company and one of the world's largest manufacturers of computer printers, information- and imaging-related equipment, and projectors. Headquartered in Suwa, Nagano Prefecture, Japan, the company originated as a maker of watch components for the Seiko group and is still known for precision technology including quartz timing devices and industrial robots.

History

The company's roots go back to 1942, when K. Hattori & Co. established a watch-parts factory in Suwa. The firm developed timing equipment and was the official timekeeper of the 1964 Tokyo Olympics. The Epson name derives from "EP-101", a compact electronic printer developed for the Olympics ("EP" for electronic printer, plus "son"). In 1968 it launched the EP-101 mini-printer, and in 1975 the business was reorganised under the Epson name; Seiko Epson Corporation was formed in 1985 through a merger of the group's manufacturing companies.

Products

Epson is a leading maker of inkjet printers — including EcoTank refillable-tank models — scanners, point-of-sale systems, LCD projectors (where it has long held the largest world market share), large-format printers, and wearable and industrial devices. Its Micro Piezo printhead technology is central to its printing business. The company also manufactures semiconductors, crystal (quartz) devices, and SCARA industrial robots, and produces Epson-branded watches through its Orient subsidiary.

Relationship with Seiko

Although historically part of the Seiko Group and sharing the Seiko name, Seiko Epson is a separate publicly listed company from Seiko Group Corporation (the former Hattori Seiko). The two remain associated through shareholdings and heritage.
