Adder

The word adder most commonly refers to Vipera berus, the common European adder or common viper, a venomous snake distributed throughout Europe and into East Asia; it is the only venomous snake native to Britain. The name is also applied to several other snakes.

Common European adder

Vipera berus is a relatively small, thick-bodied viper, typically 60–90 cm long, recognizable by a dark zigzag stripe along the back and a V- or X-shaped marking on the head. Males are usually grey with black markings; females are browner with darker brown markings, and melanistic individuals occur. It inhabits heathland, moorland, woodland edges, and coastal dunes, feeding on small mammals, lizards, and amphibians. Females give birth to live young. Its bite is painful and medically significant but rarely fatal to humans.

Other uses

"Adder" also names the death adder of Australia (genus Acanthophis, actually an elapid), the puff adder of Africa, and the hognose snake of North America, sometimes called the puff adder or spreadin' adder. In computing and electronics, an adder is a digital circuit that performs addition of numbers, such as the half adder and full adder.
