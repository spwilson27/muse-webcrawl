Anat

Anat (also spelled Anath or Anata) was a goddess of war and hunting worshipped in the ancient Near East, particularly in Canaanite and Ugaritic religion. She is known principally from the texts discovered at Ugarit (modern Ras Shamra in Syria), written in the second millennium BCE, where she appears as the sister — and in some traditions consort — of the storm god Baal.

Mythology

In the Baal Cycle, Anat fiercely defends her brother, wading through the blood of battle and later helping to restore Baal after his death at the hands of Mot, the god of death, whom she seizes, splits, burns, and grinds. Epithets describe her as "the Virgin" (btlt) despite her martial ferocity, a combination scholars have discussed extensively.

Worship and influence

Anat's cult spread to Egypt during the New Kingdom, where she was adopted as a war goddess and associated with pharaohs such as Ramesses II, who named a daughter and a horse after her. Her name also appears in the Hebrew Bible in place names such as Anathoth and personal names such as Shamgar son of Anath. Some Jewish texts from Elephantine mention a deity Anat-Yahu, a subject of scholarly debate.
