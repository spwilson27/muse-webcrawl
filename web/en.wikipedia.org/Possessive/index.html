Possessive

A possessive is a linguistic construction expressing a notion of possession, ownership, or close association — more broadly, what traditional grammar calls a genitive relation. Languages express possession through possessive pronouns and determiners (my, your, his, their), through case marking (the genitive case), through affixes, or through constructions such as English of-phrases.

In English

English has two main possessive constructions: the s-genitive (the cat's bowl), formed with the clitic 's, and the of-genitive (the bowl of the cat). Possessive determiners (my, your, his, her, its, our, their) precede a noun, while possessive pronouns (mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs) stand alone. Style guides have long debated the apostrophe in forms such as its (possessive) versus it's ("it is"), and the formation of possessives of plural and singular nouns ending in s.

Alienable and inalienable possession

Many languages grammatically distinguish alienable possession (things that can be given away, like tools) from inalienable possession (body parts, kin relations), using different constructions for each. Possession in grammar also covers relations far beyond ownership: authorship (Shakespeare's plays), part-whole relations (the car's engine), and subjective/objective genitives (the enemy's destruction).
