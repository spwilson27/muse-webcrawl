Andrew Wakefield

Andrew Jeremy Wakefield
Born: 1956, Eton, Berkshire, England
Occupation: Former physician, researcher
Known for: Retracted 1998 Lancet MMR paper

Andrew Jeremy Wakefield (born 1956) is a British former physician whose name became attached to one of the most consequential episodes of scientific fraud in modern medicine. A 1998 paper in The Lancet, of which he was lead author, suggested a link between the MMR (measles, mumps, and rubella) vaccine, bowel disease, and autism. The paper was fully retracted in 2010, and Wakefield was struck off the UK medical register.

The 1998 paper and the scare

The case series described twelve children referred to the Royal Free Hospital in London with developmental disorders and gastrointestinal symptoms. At a press conference, Wakefield called for suspension of the combined MMR vaccine in favour of single vaccines. Extensive subsequent studies found no link between MMR and autism, but vaccination rates in Britain fell sharply, and measles—declared eliminated—returned to endemic circulation.

Investigations and retraction

Journalist Brian Deer's investigations, published from 2004, revealed undisclosed financial conflicts: Wakefield had received funding from lawyers preparing litigation against vaccine manufacturers and had filed a patent for a rival single measles vaccine. A General Medical Council panel found him guilty of serious professional misconduct, including dishonesty and unethical research on vulnerable children; The Lancet retracted the paper, and the BMJ later described the work as an “elaborate fraud” in which patient data had been falsified.

Later activities

Wakefield moved to the United States, where he remained a prominent campaigner against vaccination and directed the 2016 film Vaxxed. Health authorities worldwide cite the episode as a case study in how fabricated findings can damage public health for decades.
