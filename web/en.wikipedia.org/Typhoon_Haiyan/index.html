Typhoon Haiyan, known in the Philippines as Typhoon Yolanda, was one of the most powerful tropical cyclones ever recorded. It devastated portions of Southeast Asia, particularly the Philippines, in early November 2013. With maximum sustained winds estimated at 230 km/h (145 mph) by the Japan Meteorological Agency and gusts far higher, Haiyan is among the strongest landfalling storms in recorded history.

Impact

Haiyan made landfall in the Philippines at Guiuan, Eastern Samar, early on 8 November 2013, then crossed the Visayas, driving a storm surge of up to five metres into coastal communities. The city of Tacloban was catastrophically flooded. At least 6,300 people were confirmed killed in the Philippines, more than 28,000 were injured, and millions were displaced; the United Nations estimated around 11 million people were affected. The storm later struck northern Vietnam and southern China as a weaker system.

Response and aftermath

The scale of destruction overwhelmed local authorities and prompted one of the largest international relief operations of its kind, involving military assets from numerous countries. Criticism of the Philippine government's preparedness and response led to reforms in disaster management, and debates followed about the role of climate change in the storm's intensity. The name Haiyan was retired by the ESCAP/WMO Typhoon Committee and replaced with Bailu; the Philippine name Yolanda was also retired.
