Protein design

Protein design is the rational design of new protein molecules to perform a predetermined function or behavior. It is the inverse of protein structure prediction: instead of inferring structure from sequence, designers choose a target structure or function and search for sequences that will fold and perform accordingly.

Approaches

Classical approaches combine physics-based energy functions (e.g., the Rosetta software suite) with optimization of amino-acid sequences against a target backbone. Landmark successes include the design of Top7 (2003), the first protein with a novel fold not seen in nature, designed enzymes, and self-assembling protein nanomaterials. More recently, deep-learning methods — including diffusion-based generators such as RFdiffusion and sequence-design networks such as ProteinMPNN — have greatly expanded the reliability and scope of de novo design.

Applications

Designed proteins are used as therapeutics (including engineered cytokines and vaccine immunogens), biosensors, industrial enzymes, and nanomaterials.
