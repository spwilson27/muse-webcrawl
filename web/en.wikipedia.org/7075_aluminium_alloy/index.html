7075 is an aluminium alloy with zinc as the primary alloying element, together with magnesium and copper. It is one of the strongest aluminium alloys in common use, with strength comparable to many steels, and has been a staple of aircraft construction since the Second World War.

Properties

In the T6 temper, 7075 reaches a yield strength of about 503 MPa — far above general-purpose alloys such as 6061. The trade-offs are poor weldability (it is generally joined by fasteners or adhesive) and susceptibility to stress-corrosion cracking in peak-aged tempers; the overaged T73 temper sacrifices some strength to greatly improve corrosion resistance. It machines well and takes a high polish.

History and applications

The alloy descends from a secret Japanese naval alloy of 1936 used in the Mitsubishi A6M Zero fighter; Alcoa introduced the near-identical 75S (later 7075) in 1943. Today it is used for aircraft wing and fuselage structures, high-end bicycle components, climbing and archery equipment, firearm receivers, and precision tooling such as injection-mold plates.
