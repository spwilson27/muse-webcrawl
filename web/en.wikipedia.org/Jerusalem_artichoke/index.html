Jerusalem artichoke


The Jerusalem artichoke (Helianthus tuberosus) is a species of sunflower grown for its edible tubers; also called sunchoke.

The Jerusalem artichoke (Helianthus tuberosus), also spelled with "sunchoke" among its many names, is a species of sunflower native to central North America. It is cultivated widely across the temperate zone for its tuber, which is used as a root vegetable.

Description

The plant is a herbaceous perennial growing 1.5–3 metres tall with opposite, hairy leaves and bright yellow flower heads 5–10 cm across, appearing in late summer. Its underground tubers vary in colour — brown, white, red, or purple — and are knobbly and elongated, resembling ginger root. They store carbohydrate mainly as inulin rather than starch.

History and name

Native Americans cultivated the plant long before Europeans arrived; Samuel de Champlain sent tubers to France in the early 1600s, where they became popular. The "Jerusalem" of the name has nothing to do with the city: it is thought to be a corruption of Italian girasole ("sunflower"), while "artichoke" refers to the taste of the cooked tuber, which resembles globe artichoke.

Culinary use

The tubers can be eaten raw in salads, roasted, boiled, or made into soups. Because inulin is not digested in the stomach, the tubers have been promoted as a low-glycaemic food suitable for diabetics, though inulin fermentation in the gut can cause flatulence. The plant has also been investigated as a feedstock for ethanol production.
