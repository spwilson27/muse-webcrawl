Sex linkage

Sex linkage describes traits whose genes lie on a sex chromosome, most often the X chromosome (X-linkage). Sex-linked inheritance patterns differ from autosomal because males are hemizygous for X genes.

Sex linkage
Types: X-linked, Y-linked
Discovered: Thomas Hunt Morgan, 1910

Discovery

Thomas Hunt Morgan's white-eyed Drosophila experiments (1910) first linked a trait to a specific chromosome, founding modern genetics.

Patterns

X-linked recessive traits — haemophilia, colour blindness — affect males more; fathers cannot pass X traits to sons. X-linked dominant traits affect both sexes, with no male-to-male transmission. Y-linked (holandric) traits pass father to son only.
