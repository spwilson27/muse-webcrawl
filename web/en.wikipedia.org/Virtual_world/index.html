Virtual world

A virtual world is a computer-simulated environment which may be populated by many simultaneous users who can create a personal avatar and independently explore the world, participate in its activities, and communicate with others. Virtual worlds are designed for users to inhabit and interact, and may be used for gaming, socializing, education, training, or commerce.

History

Early examples were text-based MUDs beginning in 1978, followed by graphical worlds such as Lucasfilm's Habitat (1986) and 1990s chat worlds like WorldsAway and Active Worlds. The genre expanded with massively multiplayer online games (Ultima Online, EverQuest, World of Warcraft) and social worlds such as Second Life (2003), which attracted businesses, universities, and media attention for its user-created content and virtual economy. Later platforms include VRChat, Roblox, and Fortnite, and the concept underpins much discussion of the "metaverse".

Characteristics

Common features are persistence (the world continues when a user logs off), real-time interaction, shared space, customisable avatars, and user-generated content. Virtual worlds raise research and policy questions about identity, virtual property, governance, accessibility, and the psychological effects of immersion.
