January Uprising
Date22 January 1863 – 18 June 1864
LocationCongress Poland and western Russian Empire
ResultRussian victory
BelligerentsPolish National Government vs. Russian Empire

The January Uprising (Polish: powstanie styczniowe) was an insurrection against the Russian Empire in the former Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth territories, beginning on 22 January 1863. It was the longest-lasting Polish uprising of the partition era, fought largely as guerrilla warfare by some 200,000 insurgents over eighteen months before being crushed in 1864.

Causes

The uprising was triggered by the forced conscription of young Polish men into the Russian army, ordered by Aleksander Wielopolski in January 1863 to decapitate the independence movement. The clandestine Central National Committee responded by declaring a national uprising and issuing a manifesto promising peasants ownership of the land they worked.

Course

Insurgent bands fought over a thousand skirmishes against Russian forces across Poland, Lithuania, Belarus and parts of Ukraine. Leaders included Romuald Traugutt, who headed the underground National Government from October 1863. Despite appeals, no foreign intervention came.

Aftermath

Reprisals were severe: hundreds of executions, tens of thousands deported to Siberia, confiscation of estates, and intensified Russification. Congress Poland lost its remaining autonomy and was renamed the Vistula Land. The uprising nonetheless became a founding legend of modern Polish patriotism.
