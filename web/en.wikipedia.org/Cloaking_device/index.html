Cloaking device

A cloaking device is a fictional stealth technology that causes objects, such as spaceships or individuals, to be partially or wholly invisible to parts of the electromagnetic spectrum. The concept achieved wide currency in Star Trek, where Romulan and Klingon starships use cloaks to approach undetected, and it has since become a staple of science fiction and video games.

In fiction

The idea predates Star Trek — invisibility devices appear in earlier science fiction, and H. G. Wells's The Invisible Man (1897) explored individual invisibility — but the terminology and many conventions come from the 1966 Star Trek episode "Balance of Terror" and the 1968 episode "The Enterprise Incident". In the Star Trek universe, treaty law (the Treaty of Algeron) barred the Federation from developing cloaking technology. Other franchises featuring cloaks include Stargate, Star Wars (where cloaks are rare and double-blind), the Predator films' active camouflage, and the Harry Potter invisibility cloak.

Real-world research

Metamaterials research has demonstrated limited "invisibility cloaks" that bend microwaves — and later, narrow bands of visible light — around small objects, an application of transformation optics pioneered in 2006. These demonstrations work only for specific wavelengths, viewing conditions and object sizes, far from the broadband, mobile cloaks of fiction, but they are frequently reported under the cloaking-device label.
