Cerebrum

The cerebrum is the largest part of the brain in vertebrates. It is divided into left and right cerebral hemispheres, each covered by a layer of grey matter called the cerebral cortex. The cerebrum is responsible for higher brain functions including thought, voluntary movement, language, reasoning and perception.


The human cerebrum

Structure

The two hemispheres are connected by the corpus callosum, a thick bundle of nerve fibres. Each hemisphere is divided into four main lobes: the frontal, parietal, temporal and occipital lobes. Beneath the cortex lies white matter made of myelinated axons, together with subcortical structures such as the hippocampus, basal ganglia and olfactory bulb.

Function

The cerebrum integrates sensory information and coordinates voluntary actions. The cerebral cortex processes conscious thought and memory, while structures within the cerebrum, including the amygdala, contribute to emotion. In humans the cerebrum accounts for about 85% of the brain's total weight.

Development

The cerebrum develops from the prosencephalon, the most anterior vesicle of the embryonic neural tube. Neurogenesis, migration and cortical folding (gyrification) produce the characteristic wrinkled surface that greatly increases cortical area.
