Norway is a country in Northern Europe occupying the western part of the Scandinavian Peninsula. It shares land borders with Sweden, Finland, and Russia, and has an extremely indented coastline facing the Skagerrak, the North Sea, the Norwegian Sea, and the Barents Sea.

Terrain and fjords

Much of Norway is mountainous, dominated by the Scandinavian Mountains, whose highest peak is Galdhøpiggen at 2,469 metres. During the ice ages, glaciers carved deep U-shaped valleys that were flooded by the sea as the ice retreated, forming the famous fjords. The Sognefjorden is the longest and deepest fjord in Norway and the second longest in the world.

Norway has one of the longest and most rugged coastlines in the world, with well over 200,000 islands along it when all islets are counted. The archipelagos of Lofoten and Vesterålen in the north are notable for their dramatic peaks rising directly from the sea.

Climate

Despite its northern latitude, coastal Norway has a relatively mild climate for its position because of the North Atlantic Current. Winters are cold inland and in the far north, where the sun does not rise in midwinter, while summers bring the midnight sun north of the Arctic Circle.

Territories

The Kingdom of Norway includes the Arctic archipelago of Svalbard and the island of Jan Mayen, as well as the Antarctic dependencies of Bouvet Island, Peter I Island, and Queen Maud Land.
