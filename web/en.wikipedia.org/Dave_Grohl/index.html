Dave Grohl

David Eric Grohl (born January 14, 1969) is an American musician. He is the founder, lead vocalist, rhythm guitarist, and principal songwriter of the rock band Foo Fighters, and was the drummer for Nirvana from 1990 until the band's dissolution in 1994.

Born in Warren, Ohio, and raised in Springfield, Virginia, Grohl began playing in Washington, D.C. hardcore bands as a teenager, most notably Scream. At 21 he joined Nirvana, drumming on the landmark albums Nevermind (1991) and In Utero (1993).

Foo Fighters

Following Kurt Cobain's death in 1994, Grohl recorded a collection of his own songs, playing nearly all the instruments himself, and released it in 1995 as the debut album of Foo Fighters. The project grew into a full band that became one of the most successful rock acts of the following decades, with albums including The Colour and the Shape (1997), There Is Nothing Left to Lose (1999), and Wasting Light (2011).

Other work

Grohl has drummed for Queens of the Stone Age, formed the supergroup Them Crooked Vultures with Josh Homme and John Paul Jones, and collaborated with artists from Paul McCartney to Tenacious D. He directed the documentary Sound City (2013) and the series Sonic Highways (2014), and published the memoir The Storyteller in 2021. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame twice: with Nirvana in 2014 and with Foo Fighters in 2021.
