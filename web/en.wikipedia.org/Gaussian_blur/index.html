Gaussian blur

Example of an image before and after a Gaussian blur is applied.

A Gaussian blur (also known as Gaussian smoothing) is the result of blurring an image by a Gaussian function. It is a widely used effect in graphics software, typically to reduce image noise and detail. The visual effect of this blurring technique is a smooth blur resembling that of viewing the image through a translucent screen, distinctly different from the bokeh effect produced by an out-of-focus lens or the shadow of an object under usual illumination.

Gaussian blurring is commonly used when reducing the size of an image. When downsampling an image, it is common to apply a low-pass filter to the image prior to resampling, to ensure that spurious high-frequency information does not appear in the downsampled image (aliasing). Gaussian blurs are a particularly effective low-pass filter.

Mathematics

In one dimension, the Gaussian kernel is defined by the Gaussian function. Applying a Gaussian blur to an image is the same as convolving the image with a Gaussian function. Since the Fourier transform of a Gaussian is another Gaussian, applying a Gaussian blur has the effect of reducing the image's high-frequency components; a Gaussian blur is thus a low pass filter.

Because the Gaussian function in two dimensions can be separated into the product of two one-dimensional Gaussians, the blur can be computed efficiently by applying two successive one-dimensional convolutions, one horizontal and one vertical.

Applications

Gaussian blur is used in many areas of image processing, including scale space representation, edge detection preprocessing (for example in the Canny edge detector), depth of field simulation in photography post-processing, and as a component of unsharp masking workflows. It is also used to generate drop shadows and glow effects in user interface design.
