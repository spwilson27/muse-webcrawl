Refugium (population biology)

In population biology, a refugium is a location that supports an isolated or relict population of a once more widespread species. The term is especially used for glacial refugia — ice-free areas where temperate species survived the last glacial maximum, such as southern European peninsulas (Iberia, Italy, the Balkans) and Beringia.

After the ice retreated, populations expanded from refugia to recolonise continents, leaving genetic signatures that phylogeography uses to reconstruct their history. Refugia often harbour high genetic diversity and endemism, making them conservation priorities, and the concept now extends to climate-change refugia that buffer species against warming.
