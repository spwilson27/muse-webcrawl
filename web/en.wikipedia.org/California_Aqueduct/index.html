California Aqueduct

California Aqueduct
Official name: Governor Edmund G. Brown California Aqueduct
Length: approx. 444 mi (715 km) total
Constructed: 1963–1973 (main sections)
Operator: California Department of Water Resources

The California Aqueduct, formally the Governor Edmund G. Brown California Aqueduct, is a system of canals, tunnels and pipelines that conveys water collected from the Sierra Nevada Mountains and the valleys of Northern and Central California to Southern California. Built as the central feature of the California State Water Project, it is among the largest aqueduct systems in the world.

Route

The aqueduct begins at the Clifton Court Forebay in the Sacramento–San Joaquin Delta, where water is pumped into the Bethany Reservoir. From there the main canal runs south along the western side of the San Joaquin Valley, carrying water by gravity and a series of pumping plants. Near the southern end of the valley the massive Edmonston Pumping Plant lifts water nearly 2,000 feet over the Tehachapi Mountains — one of the largest pumping lifts of its kind. South of the mountains the aqueduct splits into West and East Branches serving the Los Angeles Basin, the Inland Empire and the Antelope Valley.

Construction and operation

Voters approved the Burns–Porter Act bonds funding the State Water Project in 1960, under Governor Pat Brown, for whom the aqueduct is named. Construction began in 1963, and water deliveries began in the late 1960s and early 1970s. The concrete-lined canal, typically about 12 m deep with widths up to roughly 30 m in the valley sections, is operated by the California Department of Water Resources and supplies water to 29 public agency contractors, serving irrigation in the San Joaquin Valley and municipal supplies for some 27 million people in total project-wide.

Issues

The aqueduct loses water to evaporation and seepage, and long reaches in the San Joaquin Valley have subsided as surrounding groundwater pumping lowers the land, reducing the canal's capacity — a problem addressed by ongoing repair projects.
