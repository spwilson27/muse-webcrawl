Swatch

Image placeholder: classic Swatch plastic watches in various colours.

Swatch is a Swiss watch brand founded in 1983 by Ernst Thomke, Elmar Mock and Jacques Müller. The name "Swatch" is a contraction of "second watch", as the watches were intended as casual, disposable accessories. The brand is owned by The Swatch Group.

History

Swatch was launched in response to the "quartz crisis" of the 1970s and 1980s, when inexpensive Japanese quartz watches threatened the Swiss watch industry. The first collection of twelve models was unveiled on 1 March 1983 in Zürich. The watches combined a radically simplified movement — about 51 components instead of the roughly 91 or more in a conventional watch — with a plastic case welded shut, making the product cheap to manufacture in Switzerland while remaining stylish and collectible.

The initial sales target of one million watches for 1983 was exceeded, and 2.5 million were sold the following year. Swatch's success is widely credited with reviving the Swiss watchmaking industry.

Design and marketing

Swatch watches became famous for bold colours, artistic collaborations and limited editions designed by artists such as Keith Haring and Kiki Picasso. Because the watches were inexpensive, owners often bought several, treating them as fashion accessories rather than timepieces. Swatch has also produced lines such as the metal "Irony" series and the Sistem51 automatic watch.

Sponsorship

Swatch has been a prominent sponsor of sporting events and served as official timekeeper at several Olympic Games. See also Swatch Group.
