Scheherazade, narrator of the One Thousand and One Nights

Scheherazade, Op. 35, is a symphonic suite composed by Nikolai Rimsky-Korsakov in 1888 and based on One Thousand and One Nights, the collection of Middle Eastern folk tales. It is one of the composer's most popular works, celebrated for its colourful orchestration.

Movements

The suite consists of four related movements: "The Sea and Sinbad's Ship"; "The Tale of the Kalendar Prince"; "The Young Prince and the Young Princess"; and "Festival at Baghdad. The Sea. The Ship Breaks against a Cliff Surmounted by a Bronze Horseman." A solo violin theme represents Scheherazade herself, recurring throughout the work, while a stern brass motif depicts the Sultan.

Background

Rimsky-Korsakov intended the music to evoke a general impression of the fairy-tale world rather than a literal programme, and later softened the movement titles. The work was premiered in St Petersburg in 1888 under the composer's direction. In 1910 it was adapted as a ballet by the Ballets Russes with choreography by Michel Fokine.

Reception

The suite is a staple of the orchestral repertoire and a showcase for orchestral colour and the concertmaster's solos. Its themes are widely recognised and have been used frequently in film, television and popular culture.
