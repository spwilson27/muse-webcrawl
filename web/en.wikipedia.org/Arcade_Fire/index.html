Arcade Fire is a Canadian indie rock band from Montreal, Quebec, consisting of husband and wife Win Butler and Régine Chassagne, alongside Richard Reed Parry, Tim Kingsbury and Jeremy Gara. The band's touring line-up has included former core member Sarah Neufeld and multi-instrumentalists including Owen Pallett and Colin Stetson.

Career

Founded in 2001, the band came to prominence in 2004 with the release of the critically acclaimed debut album Funeral. Their second album, Neon Bible (2007), followed; the third, The Suburbs (2010), won the Grammy Award for Album of the Year, the Juno Award for Album of the Year, and the Polaris Music Prize. Reflektor (2013), a double album produced with James Murphy, debuted at number one in several countries. Later albums include Everything Now (2017), WE (2022), and Pink Elephant (2025).

Known for their large multi-instrumental arrangements — guitar, drums, bass, piano, violin, viola, cello, double bass, xylophone, glockenspiel, keyboard, synthesizer, French horn, accordion, harp, mandolin and hurdy-gurdy — and for theatrical live performances, Arcade Fire are frequently described as one of the most influential indie rock bands of their generation. Their songs have featured in films and media including The Hunger Games soundtrack, for which they wrote "Abraham's Daughter".
