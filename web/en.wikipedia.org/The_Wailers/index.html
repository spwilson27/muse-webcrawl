The Wailers

The Wailers, known originally as the Teenagers, the Wailing Rudeboys and the Wailing Wailers, was a Jamaican ska, rocksteady and reggae band formed in Kingston in 1963. Its best-known line-up centred on Bob Marley, Peter Tosh and Bunny Wailer, and as Bob Marley and the Wailers it became the most famous reggae group in the world.

History

The group formed in the Trench Town district of Kingston, recording early ska hits such as "Simmer Down" for producer Coxsone Dodd's Studio One label. After Tosh and Wailer left in 1974 to pursue solo careers, Marley continued with a new line-up including the Barrett brothers and the I Threes vocal group. Albums such as Catch a Fire (1973), Burnin' (1973), Exodus (1977) and Legend brought reggae to a global audience.

Legacy

Following Marley's death in 1981, various line-ups have continued under the Wailers name. The original band's recordings remain foundational to reggae music. See also Bob Marley, Peter Tosh and Reggae.
