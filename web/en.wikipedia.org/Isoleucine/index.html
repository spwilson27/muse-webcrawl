Isoleucine

SymbolIle, I
FormulaC₆H₁₃NO₂
CodonsAUU, AUC, AUA
TypeEssential, branched-chain amino acid

Isoleucine (symbol Ile or I) is an α-amino acid used in the biosynthesis of proteins. It contains an α-amino group, an α-carboxylic acid group, and a branched hydrocarbon side chain (a sec-butyl group), making it a non-polar, aliphatic amino acid. It is essential in humans — the body cannot synthesise it — and is one of the three branched-chain amino acids (BCAAs), along with leucine and valine.

Biochemistry

Isoleucine is encoded by the codons AUU, AUC and AUA. It has two stereogenic centres, so four stereoisomers are possible, though nature uses only L-isoleucine. Because it is both glucogenic and ketogenic, its catabolism yields propionyl-CoA and acetyl-CoA. Plants and microorganisms synthesise it from pyruvate and α-ketobutyrate via a pathway that shares enzymes with valine and leucine synthesis.

Dietary sources and roles

Good dietary sources include eggs, meat, fish, dairy products, soy and legumes. Isoleucine is important for haemoglobin formation, blood-sugar regulation and muscle metabolism; BCAA supplements are popular among athletes. Deficiency is rare but can cause symptoms similar to hypoglycaemia. The metabolic disorder maple syrup urine disease impairs breakdown of isoleucine and the other BCAAs.

Discovery

Isoleucine was isolated by Felix Ehrlich in 1903 from fibrin and beet-sugar molasses; its structure and synthesis were established soon afterwards.
