Ob (river)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The Ob flowing past Novosibirsk

The Ob (Russian: Обь) is a major river in western Siberia, Russia, and the world's seventh-longest river system. It flows north and west across western Siberia and drains into the Gulf of Ob, an inlet of the Kara Sea in the Arctic Ocean.

Course

The Ob proper forms at the confluence of the Biya and Katun rivers near Biysk in the Altai Krai and flows some 3,650 km (2,270 mi) to its mouth. Its chief tributary, the Irtysh, rises in China and flows through Kazakhstan; the Ob–Irtysh system, about 5,410 km long, drains a basin of nearly 3 million km² — one of the largest in the world, covering much of the West Siberian Plain.

Geography and use

Major cities on its banks include Novosibirsk, Russia's third-largest city, Barnaul, Surgut, and Salekhard, which straddles the Arctic Circle. The river crosses taiga, marsh, and tundra; its vast floodplain wetlands include the Vasyugan Swamp, one of the world's largest bogs. The Ob is used for transport, hydroelectric power (the Novosibirsk reservoir), irrigation, and fishing, though it is frozen for much of the year in its lower reaches.

Ecology

The basin supports valuable fish species such as sturgeon, nelma, and muksun. Environmental concerns include pollution from oil extraction in the Khanty-Mansi region and the legacy of nuclear contamination in tributaries near Mayak and Tomsk.
