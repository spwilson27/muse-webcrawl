Game bird

Game birds are birds hunted for sport or food, typically galliforms (such as pheasants, grouse, partridges and quail) and waterfowl (ducks and geese). The hunting and shooting of game birds is regulated by game laws specifying open seasons, bag limits and permitted methods, and the meat is prized in cooking, often described as having a "gamey" flavour after hanging.

Types

Upland game birds include the common pheasant, red grouse, grey and red-legged partridges, quail, woodcock, snipe, wild turkey and guinea fowl. Waterfowl such as mallard, teal, wigeon, Canada goose and greylag goose are hunted over wetlands, while pigeons and doves are also widely shot. Many species are reared and released for driven shooting, particularly on estates in Britain and Europe.

Management and conservation

Game bird hunting supports habitat management on shooting estates and, in North America, funding for conservation through hunting licences and excise taxes on equipment. Controversies include the use of lead shot (increasingly restricted over wetlands), the release of large numbers of non-native pheasants, and the illegal persecution of birds of prey on grouse moors.

Culinary use

Game birds are traditionally hung for several days to tenderize the meat and develop flavour, then roasted or braised. Classic dishes include roast grouse, pheasant in wine sauces, and partridge stews.
