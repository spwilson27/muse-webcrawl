Flavius Stilicho (c. 359 – 22 August 408) was a military commander (magister militum) of partial Vandal descent who became the most powerful man in the Western Roman Empire during the reign of the emperor Honorius. He is regarded by many historians as the last great general of Rome.

Rise to power

Stilicho's father was a Vandal cavalry officer and his mother a Roman. He rose under the emperor Theodosius I, married the emperor's niece Serena, and was appointed guardian of Theodosius's young son Honorius. After Theodosius's death in 395, Stilicho effectively ruled the West as Honorius's father-in-law and commander-in-chief.

Campaigns

Stilicho repeatedly confronted Alaric and the Visigoths, fighting them in Greece (395–397) and in Italy, where he won the battles of Pollentia and Verona in 402. In 406 he defeated the Ostrogothic king Radagaisus at Fiesole. His defense depended heavily on Gothic and other federate troops, a policy that made him controversial at court.

Downfall and death

Following the crossing of the Rhine by Vandals, Alans, and Suebi in 406 and the usurpation of Constantine III in Britain, Stilicho's enemies at the court in Ravenna, led by the minister Olympius, convinced Honorius that he was plotting treason. Stilicho was arrested and executed on 22 August 408. His fall was followed by a massacre of the families of federate soldiers, driving many to join Alaric — who sacked Rome two years later, in 410.
