The Sovereign Military Order of Malta (SMOM), officially the Sovereign Military Hospitaller Order of Saint John of Jerusalem, of Rhodes and of Malta, is a Catholic religious military order founded by the Knights Hospitaller in Jerusalem around 1099 and recognised by Pope Paschal II in 1113. Unusually, it remains a sovereign entity under international law despite having no territory since 1798: it maintains diplomatic relations with over 100 states, holds permanent observer status at the United Nations, and issues its own passports, stamps, coins and licence plates.

History

The Hospitallers ran a hospital for pilgrims in Jerusalem before the First Crusade and militarised during the Crusader states period. After the fall of Acre (1291) they moved to Cyprus, then conquered Rhodes (1310), ruling it until Suleiman the Magnificent expelled them in 1522. In 1530 Charles V granted them Malta and Tripoli; they withstood the Great Siege of Malta in 1565 and became a naval power policing the Mediterranean. Napoleon seized Malta in 1798, ending their territorial rule. After years of wandering, the Order settled in Rome in 1834.

Modern role

Today the Order is principally a humanitarian organisation: with roughly 13,500 members, tens of thousands of volunteers and medical staff, it runs hospitals, clinics and disaster-relief operations in about 120 countries through bodies such as Malteser International. Its two Roman properties — the Magistral Palace and the Magistral Villa on the Aventine — enjoy extraterritorial status.

Sovereignty

International lawyers describe the Order as a sui generis sovereign subject of international law: its sovereignty is functional, tied to its mission rather than territory. It is not a member state of the UN but has held observer status since 1994.
