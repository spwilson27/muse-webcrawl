Picton, New Zealand

Picton (Māori: Waitohi) is a town in the Marlborough Region of New Zealand's South Island, near the head of Queen Charlotte Sound / Tōtaranui, about 25 km north of Blenheim. It is the South Island terminal for the Interislander and Bluebridge ferries across Cook Strait to Wellington.

History

The town was named after Sir Thomas Picton, a Welsh general killed at Waterloo. European settlement began in the 1840s–50s beside the earlier Māori settlement of Waitohi. Picton briefly served as capital of the Marlborough Province. The Edwin Fox, one of the world's oldest surviving merchant ships, is preserved at the Edwin Fox Maritime Museum on the waterfront.

Tourism and transport

Picton is a gateway to the Marlborough Sounds, the Queen Charlotte Track and dolphin-watching tours. The Main North Line railway terminates at the ferry terminal, linking rail networks between the two islands via train ferries (historically) and roll-on/roll-off freight services.
