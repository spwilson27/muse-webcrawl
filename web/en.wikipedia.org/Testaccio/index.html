Testaccio

Testaccio is the 20th rione (administrative district) of Rome, Italy, and the name of the working-class neighbourhood in its area, lying south of the Aventine Hill and along the east bank of the Tiber. Its name derives from Monte Testaccio, an artificial hill composed almost entirely of broken ancient Roman amphorae — testae in Latin — discarded from the nearby river port. Long a poor, industrial district associated with the city's slaughterhouse, Testaccio is known for its markets, nightlife, and Roman culinary traditions, especially offal-based dishes.

Monte Testaccio

Monte Testaccio is a mound about 35 metres high and roughly a kilometre in circumference, formed between roughly the 1st and 3rd centuries CE by the systematic disposal of an estimated 53 million amphorae, most of which carried olive oil from Baetica (southern Spain) to Rome. The sherds were stacked deliberately, in terraces, and limed to neutralise rancid oil. Because amphorae bear stamps, painted inscriptions (tituli picti), and consular dates, the hill is an exceptional archaeological archive for the economy of the Roman Empire.

History of the district

In antiquity the area housed the Emporium, Rome's main Tiber port, with great warehouses such as the Horrea Galbae. After long centuries as open ground inside the Aurelian Walls, the modern quarter was laid out from the 1870s as a working-class residential district; the municipal slaughterhouse (Mattatoio), opened in 1890, employed thousands and shaped the neighbourhood's identity until its closure in 1975. The district's football club, and the Campo Testaccio stadium, were beloved local institutions; the area was also a stronghold of AS Roma supporters.

Modern Testaccio

The former slaughterhouse now houses a campus of the MACRO museum of contemporary art, part of Roma Tre University, and cultural spaces such as the Città dell'Altra Economia. The Testaccio Market moved in 2012 into a modern building near the old market square. The neighbourhood retains a reputation for authenticity amid gentrification, and landmarks include the Pyramid of Cestius at its edge, the Protestant Cemetery (with the graves of Keats and Shelley), and the Fontana delle Anfore in Piazza Testaccio.
