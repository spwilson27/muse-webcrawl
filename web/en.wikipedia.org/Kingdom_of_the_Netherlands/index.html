The Kingdom of the Netherlands is a sovereign state and constitutional monarchy with territory in Western Europe and the Caribbean. Since 2010 it has consisted of four constituent countries: the Netherlands proper, and the Caribbean islands of Aruba, Curaçao and Sint Maarten, each with its own government, under a single monarch as head of state.

Structure

The Charter for the Kingdom of the Netherlands (1954) is the kingdom’s highest legal instrument, regulating affairs of the kingdom such as defence, foreign relations, nationality and the monarchy, which are handled by the Council of Ministers of the Kingdom. The Dutch monarch is head of state of the whole kingdom; in each Caribbean country the monarch is represented by a governor.

The Netherlands proper is by far the largest part in population and area. The special municipalities of Bonaire, Sint Eustatius and Saba are part of the Netherlands proper despite lying in the Caribbean.

History

The kingdom was proclaimed in 1815 after the Napoleonic Wars, initially including Belgium, which seceded in 1830. The colonial relationship with the Dutch East Indies ended with Indonesian independence in 1949; Suriname became a constituent country in 1954 and independent in 1975. The Netherlands Antilles was dissolved in 2010, producing the present four-country structure.
