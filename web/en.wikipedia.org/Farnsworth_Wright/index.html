Farnsworth Wright

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Farnsworth Wright (July 29, 1888 – June 12, 1940) was an American editor, writer and poet, best remembered as the editor of the pioneering pulp magazine Weird Tales during its heyday in the 1920s and 1930s. A graduate of the University of Washington and a veteran of the First World War, Wright worked as a music critic for the Chicago Inter Ocean before joining Weird Tales in 1924, succeeding the founding editor Edwin Baird.

Editorship of Weird Tales

Under Wright, Weird Tales published much of the foundational work of modern horror, fantasy and science fiction. He bought and printed most of the fiction of H. P. Lovecraft, published Robert E. Howard's Conan the Barbarian stories, and championed writers such as Clark Ashton Smith, Seabury Quinn, Robert Bloch and Tennessee Williams (whose first published story appeared in the magazine). Wright's relationship with his contributors was sometimes fraught — he famously rejected Lovecraft's "At the Mountains of Madness" and hesitated over "The Call of Cthulhu" — but his eclectic taste and eye for striking cover art, notably the paintings of Margaret Brundage, defined the magazine's identity.

Wright also edited the short-lived Oriental Stories (later The Magic Carpet Magazine). Afflicted by Parkinson's disease from his mid-twenties, he worked despite worsening illness until his health forced him to leave Weird Tales in 1940; he was succeeded by Dorothy McIlwraith and died the same year in New York City.
