Bandwidth (computing)

In computing, bandwidth is the maximum rate of data transfer across a given path. Bandwidth may be characterized as network bandwidth, data bandwidth, or digital bandwidth. It is typically measured in bits per second (bit/s) or multiples such as kbit/s, Mbit/s, and Gbit/s.

Usage

The term is derived from the width of the frequency band used by a radio signal, but in computing it generally refers to throughput rather than a frequency range. Actual achieved throughput is usually lower than the nominal bandwidth because of protocol overhead, contention, and latency. Network providers often advertise bandwidth as a measure of connection speed, as with broadband Internet access.

Bandwidth consumption

Methods to increase effective bandwidth include data compression, caching, and content delivery networks. Conversely, streaming video, large file transfers, and software updates are major consumers of bandwidth in modern networks.
