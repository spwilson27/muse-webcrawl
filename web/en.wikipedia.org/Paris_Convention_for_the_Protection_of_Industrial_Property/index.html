Paris Convention for the Protection of Industrial Property

Paris Convention Signed: 20 March 1883, Paris
Effective: 7 July 1884
Parties: 180 states
Administered by: WIPO
Scope: Patents, trade marks, industrial designs, utility models, trade names, geographical indications

The Paris Convention for the Protection of Industrial Property, signed in Paris, France, on 20 March 1883, was one of the first intellectual property treaties. It established a Union for the protection of industrial property and remains, under the administration of the World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO), a foundational instrument of the international intellectual property system, with 180 contracting states. It has been revised several times, most recently at Stockholm in 1967.

Key principles

National treatment. Each contracting state must grant the same protection to nationals of other contracting states that it grants to its own nationals, without requiring domicile or establishment in the country where protection is claimed.

Right of priority. An applicant who has filed for a patent, trade mark, or industrial design in one member country has a period — 12 months for patents and utility models, 6 months for designs and trade marks — within which to file in other member countries while retaining the original filing date. This "priority right" is the basis of international patent and trade mark filing practice.

Common rules. The Convention also sets substantive minimum standards, including rules on patentability conditions, compulsory licences, the independence of patents and marks obtained in different countries, protection of trade names without filing, and provisions against unfair competition (Article 10bis).

Relationship to later treaties

The Paris Convention underlies the Patent Cooperation Treaty (1970) and the Madrid system for trade marks, which simplify multi-country filings under its priority framework. Its obligations were incorporated by reference into the WTO's TRIPS Agreement (1994), making most of its substantive provisions enforceable among WTO members through trade dispute settlement.
