Paget's disease of bone

SpecialtyRheumatology, orthopedics
SymptomsOften none; bone pain, deformity
Named afterJames Paget
First described1877

Paget's disease of bone (commonly known as Paget's disease or, historically, osteitis deformans) is a chronic bone disorder involving the cellular remodelling of bone tissue. It occurs when the normal cycle of bone renewal — breakdown by osteoclasts and rebuilding by osteoblasts — becomes disrupted, leading to enlarged, weakened, and misshapen bones. It is the second most common metabolic bone disease after osteoporosis.

Signs and symptoms

Many people with Paget's disease have no symptoms, and the condition is discovered incidentally on an X-ray or blood test. When symptoms occur, they may include bone pain, joint stiffness, skeletal deformities such as bowing of the legs, fractures, and, when the skull is affected, hearing loss. The pelvis, spine, skull, and long bones of the legs are most commonly involved.

Causes and treatment

The cause is unknown, though both genetic factors (including mutations in the SQSTM1 gene) and viral influences have been proposed. Treatment is usually with bisphosphonates, which suppress the excessive activity of osteoclasts; zoledronic acid is often the treatment of choice. The disease was first described by Sir James Paget in 1877.
