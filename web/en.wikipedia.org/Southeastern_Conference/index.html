The Southeastern Conference (SEC) is an American college athletic conference whose member institutions are located primarily in the South Central and Southeastern United States. Founded in 1933, it is one of the "Power" conferences in NCAA Division I and is especially renowned for its dominance and passionate following in American football.

History

The SEC was formed when 13 members of the Southern Conference left to form a more geographically compact league, holding its first season in 1933. Charter members included Alabama, Auburn, Florida, Georgia, Kentucky, LSU, Ole Miss, Mississippi State, Tennessee and Vanderbilt. Expansion added Arkansas and South Carolina in 1991, Missouri and Texas A&M in 2012, and Oklahoma and Texas in 2024, bringing membership to sixteen.

Football and championships

SEC teams have won numerous national championships in football, including a string of consecutive titles in the BCS and College Football Playoff eras. The conference stages a championship game in Atlanta and runs the SEC Network, launched in 2014 in partnership with ESPN. It is also highly successful in men's and women's basketball, baseball, gymnastics and track and field.
