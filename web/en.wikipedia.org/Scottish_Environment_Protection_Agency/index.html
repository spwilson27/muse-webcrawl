The Scottish Environment Protection Agency (SEPA; Scottish Gaelic: Buidheann Dìon Àrainneachd na h-Alba) is Scotland's principal environmental regulator and flood warning authority. It is a non-departmental public body of the Scottish Government, established under the Environment Act 1995 and operational from 1 April 1996, when it took over the functions of river purification boards, pollution inspectorates and waste regulation authorities.

Responsibilities

SEPA regulates activities that may pollute water, land and air in Scotland, including industrial emissions, waste management, radioactive substances and water abstraction and discharges. It monitors the quality of Scotland's rivers, lochs, coastal waters and air, operates the national flood forecasting and warning service (Floodline), and advises on sustainable development and climate change adaptation. It works alongside counterpart bodies elsewhere in the United Kingdom: the Environment Agency in England, Natural Resources Wales and the Northern Ireland Environment Agency.

Organisation

SEPA employs around a thousand staff across offices throughout Scotland, with its head office at Strathallan House in Stirling. It faced a severe cyberattack in December 2020, attributed to an international ransomware group, which disrupted its systems for an extended period and led to the loss or theft of data.
