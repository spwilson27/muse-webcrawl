Pseudocereal

A pseudocereal or pseudograin is one of any non-grasses that are used much like cereals — their seeds can be ground into flour and otherwise used as cereals. Examples of pseudocereals include amaranth, quinoa, and buckwheat, as well as chia, breadnut, and wattleseed.

Characteristics

Unlike true cereals, which are grasses in the family Poaceae, pseudocereals come from broadleaf plants in various families. Many are naturally gluten-free, and several have nutritional profiles with high-quality protein; quinoa and amaranth notably contain all nine essential amino acids in useful proportions.

History and use

Quinoa and amaranth were staple foods of pre-Columbian Andean and Mesoamerican civilisations respectively, while buckwheat was domesticated in Asia and spread to Europe. Pseudocereals have grown in popularity in the 21st century, promoted as health foods and as climate-resilient alternative crops.
