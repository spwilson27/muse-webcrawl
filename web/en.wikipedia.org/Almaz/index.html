Almaz

The Almaz programme (Russian: Алмаз, "Diamond") was a series of Soviet military space stations launched during the 1970s under the cover name Salyut. Developed by Vladimir Chelomei's OKB-52 design bureau, the Almaz stations were intended for crewed reconnaissance, carrying large optical cameras and, uniquely, a defensive aircraft cannon.

Development

Almaz was conceived in the mid-1960s as a response to the United States Air Force's Manned Orbiting Laboratory. The station core, designated OPS, was about 14.5 metres long with a maximum diameter of 4.15 metres. Reconnaissance equipment included the Agat-1 telescope camera system. Almaz stations were to be serviced by TKS spacecraft, which combined a large pressurized return capsule (VA) with a functional cargo block.

Flights

The first Almaz hull flew as Salyut 2 in April 1973 but failed in orbit before a crew could be sent. Salyut 3 (1974) hosted one successful crew, Soyuz 14, and conducted the only known test-firing of an onboard cannon in space — a modified 23 mm Nudelman aircraft gun fired after the crew had departed. Salyut 5 (1976–77) hosted two crews before the crewed Almaz programme was cancelled in 1978 in favour of uncrewed radar reconnaissance satellites, which flew as Kosmos 1870 and Almaz-1 in the late 1980s and early 1990s. Two unfinished Almaz hulls were later sold and exhibited as the Excalibur Almaz project.
