Surveying

Surveying or land surveying is the technique, profession, art and science of determining the terrestrial two-dimensional or three-dimensional positions of points and the distances and angles between them. Such points are used to establish maps and boundaries for ownership of land, locations for construction, and other governmental or legal purposes.

History

Surveying has been practised since antiquity: Egyptian "rope stretchers" re-established field boundaries after the annual Nile flood, and Greek and Roman surveyors (agrimensores) laid out roads, aqueducts and colonial land divisions. Landmark undertakings of the modern era include the Great Trigonometrical Survey of India and the cadastral surveys that underpinned the US Public Land Survey System. Professional licensing of surveyors reflects the legal weight their boundary determinations carry.

Methods and instruments

Classical surveying rests on triangulation and traversing: measuring angles with a theodolite and distances with chains, tapes or, since the mid-20th century, electronic distance measurement. The total station combines an electronic theodolite with a distance meter and data recorder. Heights are established by differential levelling. Since the 1980s, satellite positioning (GPS and other GNSS) using real-time kinematic techniques has delivered centimetre-level positions, and laser scanning (lidar) and drone photogrammetry now capture dense three-dimensional point clouds.

Branches

Specialisations include cadastral (boundary) surveying, engineering and construction surveying, topographic mapping, hydrographic surveying of waters, mining surveying, and geodetic surveying, which accounts for the Earth's curvature in national control networks and coordinate systems.
