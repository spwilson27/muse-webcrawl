Sima Yi

Sima Yi (179–251), courtesy name Zhongda, was a Chinese military general, politician, and regent of the state of Cao Wei during the Three Kingdoms period of China. Famous for his patience and for defending Wei against Zhuge Liang's northern expeditions, he seized power in the Incident at Gaoping Tombs in 249, laying the foundation for his grandson Sima Yan to found the Jin dynasty.

Career

Reluctantly entering Cao Cao's service, Sima Yi rose under Cao Pi and Cao Rui. He repelled Zhuge Liang's campaigns, most famously by refusing battle during the fifth expedition until Zhuge Liang died at Wuzhang Plains in 234, and conquered the Gongsun clan in Liaodong in 238.

Coup and legacy

As co-regent for the young emperor Cao Fang, Sima Yi was sidelined by Cao Shuang. In 249 he staged a coup at the Gaoping Tombs, executed Cao Shuang, and took control of Wei. His sons Sima Shi and Sima Zhao consolidated power, and his grandson usurped the throne in 266, posthumously honouring Sima Yi as Emperor Xuan.
