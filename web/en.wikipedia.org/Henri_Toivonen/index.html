Henri Pauli Toivonen (25 August 1956 – 2 May 1986) was a Finnish rally driver born in Jyväskylä, the home of Rally Finland. The son of rally driver Pauli Toivonen and younger brother of Harri Toivonen, he was one of the most gifted drivers of the Group B era. His death, along with that of his co-driver Sergio Cresto, in a crash at the 1986 Tour de Corse led directly to the banning of the immensely powerful Group B cars.

Career

Toivonen began in circuit racing and ice racing before switching to rallying. In 1980, driving a Talbot Sunbeam Lotus, he won the RAC Rally at age 24, becoming the youngest driver to win a World Rally Championship round — a record that stood for nearly three decades. He drove for the Opel factory team in the early 1980s and tried his hand at sports car racing with Porsche before joining Lancia for 1985.

At Lancia he mastered first the rear-wheel-drive Lancia Rally 037, winning the 1985 Rallye Sanremo, and then the twincharged Delta S4, one of the most extreme Group B machines, winning the 1985 RAC Rally and the 1986 Monte Carlo Rally. By early 1986 he was regarded as the fastest driver in the championship and a title favourite.

Death and legacy

On 2 May 1986, while leading the Tour de Corse in Corsica — reportedly suffering from influenza and taking medication — Toivonen's Delta S4 left the road on a fast left-hand bend, plunged into a ravine, landed inverted, and burst into flames as its aluminium fuel tanks ruptured. Toivonen and Cresto were killed instantly. There were no witnesses to the crash itself.

Within hours, FISA president Jean-Marie Balestre announced the cancellation of Group B: the cars would be banned from 1987, and the proposed even faster Group S was cancelled outright. Toivonen's death, following Attilio Bettega's fatal crash at the same rally a year earlier and other accidents involving spectators, ended rallying's most spectacular and dangerous era. The Race of Champions, founded in his memory, is held in honour of Henri Toivonen.
