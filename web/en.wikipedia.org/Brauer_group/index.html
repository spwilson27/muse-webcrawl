Brauer group

Brauer group
FieldAlgebra, number theory
Named afterRichard Brauer
NotationBr(F)

The Brauer group of a field F, denoted Br(F), is an abelian group in abstract algebra that classifies finite-dimensional central division algebras over F up to a natural equivalence relation. It is an important invariant in algebraic number theory and algebraic geometry, and it is defined as a cohomology group, namely the second Galois cohomology group H² of the absolute Galois group of F with values in the multiplicative group of an algebraic closure.


Central simple algebras are classified by the Brauer group.

Definition

Two central simple algebras over F are equivalent if they become isomorphic after tensoring with matrix algebras. The set of equivalence classes forms a group under the tensor product, with the identity element given by matrix algebras (the split algebras) and inverses given by opposite algebras. Finite-dimensional central division algebras correspond to the torsion elements that can be represented concretely in this group.

Examples

The Brauer group of the real numbers is cyclic of order two, generated by the class of the Hamilton quaternions. The Brauer group of a finite field is trivial (Wedderburn's little theorem implies every finite division ring is a field), and the Brauer group of an algebraically closed field is likewise trivial. For a non-archimedean local field such as the p-adic numbers, the Brauer group is isomorphic to the rationals modulo the integers, Q/Z.

The Brauer–Hasse–Noether theorem

For a global field such as a number field, the Brauer group fits into an exact sequence (the Albert–Brauer–Hasse–Noether theorem) relating it to the Brauer groups of all of its completions, with the sum of the local invariants equal to zero. This local-global principle is a cornerstone of class field theory.

Applications

Brauer groups appear in the theory of Severi–Brauer varieties, in the period-index problem, in Galois cohomology, and in the study of rational points on varieties. The notion has been extended to schemes, where the Brauer group of a scheme is defined using Azumaya algebras or étale cohomology.
