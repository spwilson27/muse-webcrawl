Ignimbrite

Ignimbrite is a type of volcanic rock consisting of hardened tuff deposited by pyroclastic density currents (pyroclastic flows) from large explosive eruptions. Ignimbrites are typically composed of pumice fragments, ash, crystals, and lithic fragments in a fine-grained matrix, and may be welded where deposited hot.

Formation

When a pyroclastic flow comes to rest, the deposit may be hot enough for the glassy fragments to deform and fuse — welding — producing a dense rock resembling lava. Columnar jointing can form as thick sheets cool.

Occurrence

Large ignimbrite provinces are associated with caldera-forming eruptions, such as those of Yellowstone, the Taupō Volcanic Zone in New Zealand, and the Altiplano–Puna volcanic complex in South America. Individual sheets can cover thousands of square kilometres.

Use

Non-welded ignimbrite is soft and easily carved; it was used as a building stone, for example in the carved churches of Cappadocia and statuary on Easter Island quarried from tuff.
