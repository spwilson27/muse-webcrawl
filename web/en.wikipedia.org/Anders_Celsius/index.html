Anders Celsius (1701–1744) was a Swedish astronomer, physicist, and mathematician. He was professor of astronomy at Uppsala University from 1730 until his death and is best known for proposing the temperature scale that bears his name.

Career

Celsius travelled widely in Europe, visiting observatories in Germany, Italy, and France, and in 1736 joined the French expedition to Lapland led by Pierre Louis Maupertuis to measure the length of a degree of latitude, confirming Newton's prediction that the Earth is flattened at the poles. He founded the Uppsala Astronomical Observatory in 1741 and made extensive observations of the aurora borealis, publishing a collection of 316 observations and noting the connection between auroras and disturbances of the magnetic field.

Temperature scale

In 1742 Celsius proposed a centigrade scale divided into 100 degrees between the freezing and boiling points of water; in his original version, 0° marked boiling and 100° freezing. After his death the scale was inverted to its modern form, by Carl Linnaeus or others, and in 1948 the unit was officially renamed the degree Celsius.
