Bharatanatyam

OriginTamil Nadu, India
Textual sourceNatya Shastra (attributed to Bharata)
MusicCarnatic music
Key featuresAraimandi posture, mudras, abhinaya

Bharatanatyam is an Indian classical dance form originating in the Hindu temples of Tamil Nadu. It is among the oldest classical dance traditions of India, with roots in the temple dance of the sadir performed by devadasis and in the theoretical framework of the ancient Natya Shastra.

Technique

Bharatanatyam is distinguished by its fixed upper torso, bent knees in the araimandi half-sitting posture, spectacular footwork, and a vocabulary of hand gestures (mudras) and facial expressions (abhinaya) used to narrate stories from Hindu epics and puranas. Dancers perform to Carnatic music with a vocalist, mridangam, violin, and the cymbals of the nattuvanar (dance teacher-conductor).

Repertoire

The modern recital format, codified by the Tanjore Quartet in the early 19th century, proceeds through items of increasing complexity: alarippu, jatiswaram, shabdam, the expressive centre-piece varnam, padams and javalis on themes of love, and the brisk concluding tillana.

History and revival

Colonial-era stigma and the 1947 Madras Devadasis (Prevention of Dedication) Act nearly extinguished the form. Its 20th-century revival is credited above all to Rukmini Devi Arundale, who founded the Kalakshetra academy in 1936 and brought the dance to the proscenium stage, and to the scholarship of E. Krishna Iyer. Today Bharatanatyam is taught and performed worldwide.
