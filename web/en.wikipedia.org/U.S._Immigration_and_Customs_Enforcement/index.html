U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) is a federal law enforcement agency under the U.S. Department of Homeland Security, created in 2003. ICE is charged with enforcing immigration laws within the United States and investigating transnational crime.

Directorates

Its two principal components are Homeland Security Investigations (HSI), which investigates smuggling, human trafficking, financial crime, export violations and other transnational offences, and Enforcement and Removal Operations (ERO), which arrests, detains and removes noncitizens who violate immigration law.

History

ICE was established under the Homeland Security Act of 2002 from investigative and interior-enforcement elements of the former Immigration and Naturalization Service and the U.S. Customs Service; its detention and deportation policies have frequently been the subject of public and political debate.
