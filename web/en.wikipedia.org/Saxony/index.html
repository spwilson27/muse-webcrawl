Saxony (German: Sachsen; Upper Sorbian: Sakska), officially the Free State of Saxony, is a landlocked state of eastern Germany, bordering the states of Brandenburg, Saxony-Anhalt, Thuringia and Bavaria, as well as Poland and the Czech Republic. Its capital is Dresden and its largest city Leipzig; other major cities include Chemnitz and Zwickau.

History

The modern state's name descends from the medieval Duchy of Saxony, though the Saxons' original homeland lay far to the northwest (today's Lower Saxony). The territory of present Saxony was the Margraviate of Meissen; its Wettin rulers acquired the Saxon electoral dignity in 1423, and "Saxony" shifted southeast with them. The Electorate, and from 1806 Kingdom of Saxony, was a cultural powerhouse — Bach in Leipzig, Wagner and the court at Dresden, Meissen porcelain — and an early industrial centre ("the workshop of Germany").

After 1945 Saxony lay in the Soviet occupation zone and became part of East Germany, being dissolved into districts in 1952. It was re-established in 1990 upon German reunification. A Sorbian Slavic minority survives in Lusatia, in the east of the state.

Economy and culture

Today microelectronics ("Silicon Saxony" around Dresden), automotive plants (VW, BMW and Porsche in Leipzig) and tourism (Elbe Sandstone Mountains, Ore Mountains mining region, a UNESCO site) anchor the economy. Saxony is renowned for Christmas traditions — Stollen, nutcrackers and the Striezelmarkt in Dresden — and for Leipzig's role in the peaceful revolution of 1989.
