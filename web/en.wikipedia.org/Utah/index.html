Utah

Utah is a landlocked state in the Mountain West subregion of the Western United States. It is bordered by Colorado to the east, Wyoming to the northeast, Idaho to the north, Arizona to the south, and Nevada to the west. Its capital and largest city is Salt Lake City. Utah is the 13th-largest by area, the 30th-most-populous, and the 11th-least-densely populated of the 50 states, with a population of about 3.3 million.

The name "Utah" derives from the Ute people, one of several Native American peoples native to the region. Members of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (Mormons) settled the Salt Lake Valley beginning in 1847 under Brigham Young, and the church remains headquartered in Salt Lake City; about half of Utahns identify as Latter-day Saints.

Geography

Utah contains three distinct physiographic regions: the Rocky Mountains (including the Wasatch Range and the Uinta Mountains, among the few major ranges in the United States running east to west), the Basin and Range Province in the west, and the Colorado Plateau in the south and east, famous for its red-rock canyons and five national parks: Zion, Bryce Canyon, Arches, Canyonlands, and Capitol Reef — the "Mighty Five". The Great Salt Lake, the largest saltwater lake in the Western Hemisphere, lies in the north.

History

The region was explored by Spanish expeditions in the 18th century and ceded by Mexico to the United States in 1848 after the Mexican–American War. The proposed State of Deseret became the Utah Territory in 1850. After the church's 1890 Manifesto renouncing polygamy, Utah achieved statehood on 4 January 1896.

Economy

Mining (including the Bingham Canyon copper mine), tourism and outdoor recreation, technology services (the "Silicon Slopes" corridor), agriculture, and government services anchor the economy. Utah consistently ranks among the fastest-growing states.
