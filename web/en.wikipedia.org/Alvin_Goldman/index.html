Alvin Goldman

Alvin Ira Goldman (born 1938) is an American philosopher, a leading figure in contemporary epistemology known for developing reliabilism and for founding work in social epistemology. He spent much of his career at the University of Arizona and later Rutgers University.

Work

In his landmark 1967 paper "A Causal Theory of Knowing", Goldman argued that a belief counts as knowledge when it is caused, in an appropriate way, by the fact that makes it true — an early "externalist" challenge to internalist accounts of justification. His 1979 paper "What is Justified Belief?" introduced process reliabilism: a belief is justified if it is produced by a reliable cognitive process, such as perception under good conditions, regardless of whether the believer can articulate why.

Goldman's later book Knowledge in a Social World (1999) helped define social epistemology, evaluating how institutions — science, law, journalism, markets and democracies — promote or hinder truth-seeking. He also wrote influential work on the simulation theory of mindreading and on the epistemology of testimony.
