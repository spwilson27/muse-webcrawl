Battle of Boyacá

Battle of Boyacá
Date: 7 August 1819
Location: Boyacá, New Granada (modern Colombia)
Result: Decisive patriot victory
Part of the Spanish American wars of independence

The Battle of Boyacá was fought on 7 August 1819 between the Spanish royalist forces and the patriot army of Simón Bolívar near Tunja in the Viceroyalty of New Granada. It was the decisive battle of Bolívar's campaign to liberate New Granada and secured the independence of what became Colombia.

Background

In May 1819 Bolívar crossed the flooded llanos and the Andes at the Páramo de Pisba with an army of about 2,500 men, descending into New Granada in the rainy season and surprising the Spanish. After hard fighting at the Pantano de Vargas on 25 July, Bolívar manoeuvred to cut the royalist road to Santa Fe de Bogotá. The royalist commander José María Barreiro marched to intercept him, and the armies met at the bridge over the Teatinos River near Boyacá.

The battle

Patriot forces under Bolívar, Francisco de Paula Santander, and José Antonio Anzoátegui overwhelmed the royalist column, which was strung out in march order. The patriot vanguard captured the bridge while the main body struck the royalist divisions in detail; Barreiro and most of his army of some 2,900 men were captured with relatively light patriot losses.

Aftermath

The road to Bogotá lay open; the viceroy fled and Bolívar entered the capital on 10 August. Boyacá is celebrated as the founding victory of the Republic of Colombia, and 7 August is a national holiday. The battlefield bridge, the Puente de Boyacá, is a national monument.
