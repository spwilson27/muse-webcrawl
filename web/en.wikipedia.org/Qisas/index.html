Qisas

Qiṣāṣ (Arabic: قِصَاص) is an Islamic term meaning "retaliation in kind", "eye for an eye", or retributive justice. In classical Islamic law (sharia), qisas is the category of punishment for murder and intentional bodily injury, under which the victim or the victim's family may demand an equivalent retaliation against the perpetrator, accept monetary compensation (diya, "blood money") instead, or grant a pardon.

Scriptural basis

The Qur'an prescribes qisas in verses such as 2:178–179 ("O you who believe, qisas is prescribed for you in cases of murder...") and 5:45 ("a life for a life, an eye for an eye, a nose for a nose, an ear for an ear, a tooth for a tooth, and wounds likewise"). The texts simultaneously encourage forgiveness and acceptance of diya as preferable acts of charity.

Application

Classical jurisprudence distinguishes intentional killing (qatl al-ʿamd), where qisas applies, from accidental killing, where only diya and expiation are due. Because qisas is classed as a private right (a claim of the victim's heirs rather than a public hadd punishment), the heirs may waive it. In modern states that retain qisas provisions — including Iran and Saudi Arabia — high-profile cases have included court-ordered blinding and death sentences that were sometimes halted by last-minute pardons. The practice is criticised by human rights organisations and defended by supporters as scripturally mandated retributive justice.
