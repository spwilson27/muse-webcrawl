Isaac

Isaac (Hebrew: יִצְחָק, Yiṣḥāq, "he laughs") is one of the three patriarchs of the Israelites in the Hebrew Bible. He was the son of Abraham and Sarah, the father of Jacob (later called Israel) and Esau, and the grandfather of the twelve tribes of Israel. Isaac's story is told chiefly in the Book of Genesis, and he is also an important figure, as the prophet Isḥāq, in Islam.

The binding of Isaac (the Akedah) is among the most studied episodes of Genesis.

Birth and the binding

According to Genesis, Isaac was born to Abraham and Sarah in their old age, after God promised that Sarah — long considered barren — would bear a child; she laughed at the announcement, giving the child his name. The central episode of his life is the Akedah, the Binding of Isaac (Genesis 22): God commands Abraham to sacrifice Isaac on Mount Moriah, but at the last moment an angel stays Abraham's hand and a ram is offered instead. The story has been central to Jewish, Christian and Muslim theology, and a major subject of art and philosophy, including Kierkegaard's Fear and Trembling.

Life in Genesis

Isaac is the least active of the patriarchs. He marries Rebecca, brought from Abraham's kin in Mesopotamia; she bears the twins Esau and Jacob. Isaac re-digs his father's wells in the Negev and prospers at Gerar and Beersheba, where God renews with him the covenant made with Abraham. In old age, blind, he intends to bless Esau, but Jacob, coached by Rebecca, receives the blessing in disguise. Isaac dies at the age of 180 and is buried by his sons in the Cave of Machpelah beside Abraham and Sarah.

In other traditions

In the New Testament Isaac is cited as a child of promise and a type of Christ. In Islam, Ishaq is a prophet and righteous patriarch; notably, Islamic tradition differs over whether the intended sacrificial son was Isaac or his brother Ishmael, with much early and later opinion favouring Ishmael.
