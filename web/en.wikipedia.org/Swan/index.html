Swan

Swans are large waterfowl of the genus Cygnus in the family Anatidae, closely related to geese and ducks. Six or seven living species are recognised, including the mute swan of Europe, the trumpeter and tundra swans of North America, and the black swan of Australia.

Description

Swans are among the largest flying birds, with long necks held curved or straight, and they mate for life in most cases. Young swans are called cygnets. Adults are mostly white in the Northern Hemisphere species; the black swan and the black-necked swan occur in the south.

Cultural significance

Swans feature prominently in mythology and art — from the Greek myth of Leda to Tchaikovsky's ballet Swan Lake and Hans Christian Andersen's "The Ugly Duckling". In Britain, unmarked mute swans on open water traditionally belong to the Crown, and the annual Swan Upping ceremony still counts them on the Thames.
