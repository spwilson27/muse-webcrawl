Palestine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Palestine (Arabic: فلسطين, Filasṭīn) is a geographic region in West Asia, in the southern Levant, situated between the Mediterranean Sea to the west and the Jordan River to the east. The term has been used since classical antiquity — derived from the "Sea Peoples" known as the Philistines recorded in Egyptian sources — and was formalised in the Roman province of Syria Palaestina.

The name may refer to the historical region as a whole; to the territory of Mandatory Palestine under British rule (1920–1948); or to the State of Palestine, the partially recognised state claiming the West Bank and the Gaza Strip, with East Jerusalem as its intended capital.

Geography

The region extends from the Litani River in the north to the Negev desert in the south. Its landscapes include the fertile coastal plain, the central hill country of Judea and Samaria, the Jordan Valley — the lowest land on Earth along the Dead Sea shore — and the semi-arid south. Major cities historically include Jerusalem, Hebron, Nablus, Gaza, Jaffa, Haifa, Acre, and Bethlehem.

History

Palestine has been ruled in succession by Canaanites, Israelites, empires of Assyria, Babylon, Persia, Alexander the Great and his successors, Rome and Byzantium, the early Islamic caliphates, Crusader states, the Ayyubids and Mamluks, and the Ottoman Empire, which governed it for four centuries until the First World War. The British Mandate followed, and the 1948 Arab–Israeli war led to the establishment of Israel, the displacement of much of the Arab population (commemorated as the Nakba), and Jordanian and Egyptian administration of the West Bank and Gaza. Israel occupied those territories in 1967. The Palestine Liberation Organization (founded 1964) led the Palestinian national movement; the Oslo Accords of the 1990s created the Palestinian Authority, and the State of Palestine was proclaimed in 1988 and recognised as a non-member observer state by the United Nations in 2012.
