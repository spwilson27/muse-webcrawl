Mastic

Mastic (Greek: μαστίχα, masticha) is a resin obtained from the mastic tree, Pistacia lentiscus, an evergreen shrub of the Mediterranean. It is produced almost exclusively on the Greek island of Chios, where the resin is harvested as hardened droplets or "tears" that exude from cuts in the bark and branches during summer.

Production

Farmers make shallow incisions in the bark from July to October; the resin flows and solidifies into translucent pale-yellow tears, which are collected, washed and sorted by hand. The mastic villages (Mastichochoria) of southern Chios have cultivated the tree since antiquity, and the know-how of mastic cultivation on Chios was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2014. Chios mastic has EU protected designation of origin status.

Uses

Mastic was chewed in the ancient world as a breath freshener — an early chewing gum, reflected in the word's origin in the Greek verb for chewing. It flavours liqueurs such as mastika and Chios masticha, sweets, ice cream (dondurma style), the Easter bread tsoureki, cosmetics and varnishes, and it is used in traditional medicine; modern studies have investigated its effects on oral bacteria and stomach ulcers associated with Helicobacter pylori.
